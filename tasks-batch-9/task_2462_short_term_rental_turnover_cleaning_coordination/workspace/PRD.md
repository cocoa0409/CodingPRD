# Short-Term Rental Turnover Coordination API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Vacation-rental operators live and die by the gap between one guest walking out and the next walking in. That gap is rarely generous: a departing party lingers past the contracted hour, a channel pushes through a same-day reservation, a cleaner's van is two zones away, and the linen closet turns out to be one duvet cover short of a staged bed. This document specifies the Short-Term Rental Turnover Coordination API — the service of record for detecting those gaps, sizing the work that must fit inside them, putting the right cleaner in front of the door, proving through photographic evidence that the unit was actually staged and restocked, and paying the cleaner an amount that reflects what really happened rather than what was optimistically forecast at assignment time.

Four concerns drive nearly every rule in this specification. The first is **temporal**: a cleaning window is derived, never entered by hand, and it must be re-derived every single time one of its boundary instants moves — a granted late checkout, a granted early check-in, a recorded actual departure, or the cancellation of the arriving stay. The second is **evidentiary**: no turnover closes until every generated checklist task has been either verified against its photograph or explicitly waived by a coordinator, and supply consumption is booked into the stock ledger only at the moment a reviewer approves it. The third is **fiscal**: cleaner compensation is computed twice — once provisionally the instant an offer is accepted so the cleaner knows what the job is worth, and once definitively at completion using real lateness and real linen consumption — with a conserved payout ledger that must reconcile after every request. The fourth is **independence**: the quality auditor whose score moves a cleaner's rolling reputation and settled pay must not be the same operator who handed out the assignment or signed off on its checklist evidence.

The system is designed for four operator personas plus the cleaning workforce itself, and it exposes a single flat collection namespace: every business collection is addressable at the API root, with parent-child relationships carried as identifier fields in request payloads rather than as nested URL segments. Two reverse-lookup sub-resources are the sole exceptions. All temporal reasoning reads a server-side **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time, which makes the whole windowing, buffering, lateness and audit-eligibility surface deterministically testable.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, identity and timestamp conventions, the order in which rejections are evaluated, the temporal arithmetic that windows depend on, the concurrency guards, and the ledger disciplines that money and stock obey.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two mandatory HTTP headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. Five roles exist and their spellings are exact: `PlatformAdmin`, `Coordinator`, `Cleaner`, `QualityAuditor`, and `PropertyOwner`. A request that omits either header, or that supplies an `X-User-Role` value outside those five, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A syntactically valid request whose role is not listed among the authorized roles for the addressed endpoint is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Three of the five roles are operator identities and two are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to an entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Coordinator`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier; the server records it — as `assigned_by_user_id` on an `Assignment` it creates, and as `reviewed_by_user_id` on any `ChecklistTask` it reviews — but does not compare it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `QualityAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is again an opaque operator identifier, recorded as `auditor_user_id` on the `QualityAudit` it samples and compared thereafter only against other recorded operator identifiers, never against an entity `id`.

When `X-User-Role` is `Cleaner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Cleaner` record; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Cleaner` compare the resource's cleaner reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a cleaner may act only on an `Assignment` whose `cleaner_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`, and may act on a turnover, a checklist task or an issue report only when the covering non-terminal assignment on that turnover belongs to it; a violation is rejected with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNMENT_OWNER` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `PropertyOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Property`; ownership checks compare the addressed resource's property reference to `X-User-ID`, and on the list reads available to owners the `property_id` filter is mandatory and must equal `X-User-ID`. Addressing a resource outside its own unit, or omitting that mandatory filter, is rejected with `ERR_NOT_PROPERTY_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position within the ordered result set: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the permitted range, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). List responses report `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` counts every row matching the applied filters before the page window is taken and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page.

Unless a specific collection declares otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Several collections carry a domain-natural ordering that overrides the default, and in each case the tie-break is stated explicitly: a property's stock lines are ordered by `sku` ascending; the stock ledger is ordered by `created_at` ascending and then by `sequence_no` ascending; reservations are ordered by `checkin_at` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending; turnovers are ordered by `window_start_at` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending; a turnover's checklist tasks are ordered by `sequence_no` ascending; offers are ordered by `created_at` ascending and then by `sequence_no` ascending. Pay lines, issue reports, repair orders and quality audits all use the default `created_at` ascending order.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` in UUID form. Identifiers never carry a type prefix — an `id` is the bare UUID string and nothing else. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied identifier field in a request body is always a reference to some other resource, never a request to choose the new resource's own key. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was minted.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on each mutation that the specification declares to be a version-bumping change. The single exception is the stock ledger: a `StockMovement` is an append-only evidence row that carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version`, and no endpoint in this API may edit or delete one. Corrections to stock are always expressed as new compensating movements, never as edits.

All temporal reasoning in this system — window boundaries, overlap buffers, lateness, audit eligibility, and every `..._at` timestamp the server writes itself — reads a single server-side logical UTC clock rather than the host's wall clock. Before any client sets that clock it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real wall-clock time. An administrative test-driving capability moves the clock to a supplied instant. Timestamps that record "when something happened" — `accepted_at`, `declined_at`, `revoked_at`, `started_at`, `finished_at`, `submitted_at`, `owner_notified_at`, `completed_at`, `finalized_at`, `cleanliness_rated_at`, `last_turnover_completed_at` — are all readings of that logical clock taken at the moment the corresponding transition is applied.

### 2.5. Rejection Precedence: The Order in Which a Request Is Judged

Because a single malformed request can violate several rules at once, the order in which checks run is part of the contract, not an implementation detail. A request is evaluated through the following gates, and the **first** gate that fails determines the response; no later gate may mask an earlier one. The list is exhaustive and ordered from first to last.

1. **Header authentication.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` must both be present and `X-User-Role` must be one of the five declared roles, on every endpoint. Failure yields `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorization.** The acting role must appear in the addressed endpoint's authorized-role list, on every endpoint. Failure yields `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Request shape.** On every endpoint: every required input field must be present, every enum-typed value must be one of the declared members, every integer must sit inside its declared bounds, every datetime must parse as ISO 8601 UTC, quantities and scores must sit inside their declared ranges, and `restock_entries` must be well formed. Failure yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
4. **Cleaner pay-line scoping.** On the two pay-line reads (`GET /pay-lines` and `GET /pay-lines/{id}`), a `Cleaner` reading the collection must pass a `cleaner_id` filter equal to its own `X-User-ID`, and a `Cleaner` reading a single pay line may only address one whose `cleaner_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`. Failure yields `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
5. **Path existence, left to right.** On every endpoint carrying path parameters, each templated resource must exist, and the segments are resolved strictly left to right so that the outermost missing resource is the one reported. The applicable code is `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLEANER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TURNOVER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHECKLIST_TASK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPPLY_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ISSUE_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPAIR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUALITY_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STOCK_MOVEMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PAY_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, each at HTTP 404.
6. **Body reference existence.** Every identifier referenced in a request body must resolve: `property_id`, `outgoing_booking_id`, `incoming_booking_id`, `cleaner_id`, each member of `exclude_cleaner_ids`, `turnover_id`, and the `supply_line_id` of each entry inside `restock_entries`. This gate applies to `POST /bookings`, `POST /turnovers`, `POST /turnovers/{id}/assign`, `POST /issue-reports`, `POST /quality-audits` and `POST /checklist-tasks/{id}/submit`, and yields `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLEANER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TURNOVER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_SUPPLY_LINE_NOT_FOUND` (404).
7. **Property-owner scoping.** A `PropertyOwner` may only touch its own unit: `X-User-ID` must equal the addressed property's `id`, or the mandatory `property_id` filter on a list read. This gate guards sixteen operations — `GET /properties/{id}`, `GET /properties/{id}/supply-lines`, `GET /supply-lines/{id}`, `GET /bookings`, `GET /bookings/{id}`, `GET /turnovers`, `GET /turnovers/{id}`, `GET /stock-movements`, `GET /stock-movements/{id}`, `GET /issue-reports`, `GET /issue-reports/{id}`, `GET /repair-orders`, `GET /repair-orders/{id}`, `GET /quality-audits`, `GET /quality-audits/{id}` and `POST /issue-reports/{id}/dispatch` — and yields `ERR_NOT_PROPERTY_OWNER` (403).
8. **Cleaner work scoping.** A `Cleaner` may only act on its own work: the addressed assignment's `cleaner_id`, or the `cleaner_id` of the turnover's `ACCEPTED` or `ACTIVE` assignment, must equal the acting `X-User-ID`. This gate guards six operations — `POST /assignments/{id}/accept`, `POST /assignments/{id}/decline`, `POST /turnovers/{id}/start`, `POST /turnovers/{id}/complete`, `POST /checklist-tasks/{id}/submit` and `POST /issue-reports` — and yields `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNMENT_OWNER` (403).
9. **Audit ownership.** On `POST /quality-audits/{id}/finalize`, the audit's `auditor_user_id` must equal the acting `X-User-ID`. Failure yields `ERR_NOT_AUDIT_OWNER` (403).
10. **Single-property cross-references.** On `POST /turnovers` the incoming booking's property must be the same property as the outgoing booking's, and on `POST /checklist-tasks/{id}/submit` every restock entry's supply line must belong to the turnover's property. Failure yields `ERR_PROPERTY_MISMATCH` (422).
11. **Stale-version guards.** On `POST /turnovers/{id}/assign` and `POST /supply-lines/{id}/replenish`, the supplied expected-version field must equal the parent's current `version`, yielding `ERR_STALE_TURNOVER_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_SUPPLY_LINE_VERSION` (412) as described in §2.7.
12. **Target state.** On every action endpoint, the addressed resource must itself be in a state that permits the operation, yielding one of `ERR_INVALID_TURNOVER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_TASK_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ISSUE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_REPAIR_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_AUDIT_STATE`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_SCHEDULED` or `ERR_INVALID_CLEANER_STATE`, each at HTTP 409.
13. **Gating sibling state.** A *different* entity than the one addressed must also be in the required state: the property must be `ACTIVE` for a new turnover, the cleaner must be `ACTIVE` to be offered work or to accept it, the outgoing booking must be `CHECKED_OUT` before the clean starts, and the turnover must be `COMPLETED` before an audit may be sampled. The gate guards `POST /turnovers`, `POST /turnovers/{id}/assign`, `POST /assignments/{id}/accept`, `POST /turnovers/{id}/start` and `POST /quality-audits`, and yields `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_CLEANER_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_GUEST_NOT_CHECKED_OUT` or `ERR_TURNOVER_NOT_COMPLETED` (409).
14. **Segregation of duties.** On `POST /quality-audits`, the acting auditor's `X-User-ID` must differ from the turnover's `assigned_by_user_id` and from every `reviewed_by_user_id` recorded on that turnover's checklist tasks. Failure yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
15. **Photo evidence.** On `POST /checklist-tasks/{id}/submit`, `photo_uri` must be present when the task's `requires_photo` is true. Failure yields `ERR_PHOTO_REQUIRED` (422).
16. **Restock entries required.** On `POST /checklist-tasks/{id}/submit`, a task whose `category` is `RESTOCK` must carry at least one restock entry. Failure yields `ERR_RESTOCK_ENTRIES_REQUIRED` (422).
17. **Restock entries forbidden.** On the same submission endpoint, a task whose `category` is `CLEANING` or `STAGING` must carry no restock entries at all. Failure yields `ERR_RESTOCK_ENTRIES_NOT_ALLOWED` (422).
18. **Verification completeness.** On `POST /turnovers/{id}/complete`, every checklist task of the turnover must be `VERIFIED` or `WAIVED`. Failure yields `ERR_UNVERIFIED_TASKS_REMAIN` (409).
19. **Interval ordering.** Across `POST /bookings`, `POST /bookings/{id}/schedule-change`, `POST /bookings/{id}/guest-review` and `POST /turnovers`: a booking's `checkout_at` must be strictly later than its `checkin_at`; a `LATE_CHECKOUT` request's `new_time_at` must be strictly later than the contracted `checkout_at`; an `EARLY_CHECKIN` request's `new_time_at` must be strictly earlier than the contracted `checkin_at` and at or after the affected turnover's `window_start_at`; the incoming booking's `checkin_at` must be at or after the outgoing booking's `checkout_at`; and a guest review requires the logical clock to be at or after that booking's `checkin_at`. Failure yields `ERR_INVALID_TIME_INTERVAL` (422).
20. **Late-checkout ceiling.** On `POST /bookings/{id}/schedule-change`, a granted late checkout may not fall later than four hours — 240 minutes — after the contracted `checkout_at`. Failure yields `ERR_SCHEDULE_CHANGE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422).
21. **Occupancy overlap.** On `POST /bookings`, the new stay's occupancy interval must not overlap another `SCHEDULED` or `CHECKED_OUT` stay on the same property, with adjacency permitted. Failure yields `ERR_BOOKING_OVERLAP` (409).
22. **Positive window.** On `POST /turnovers` and `POST /bookings/{id}/schedule-change`, the derived cleaning window must be strictly positive. Failure yields `ERR_CLEANING_WINDOW_NON_POSITIVE` (422).
23. **Window sufficiency at creation.** On `POST /turnovers`, `window_minutes` must be greater than or equal to `required_minutes` unless an `override_reason` is supplied. Failure yields `ERR_WINDOW_OVERRIDE_REQUIRED` (422).
24. **Window sufficiency at schedule change.** On `POST /bookings/{id}/schedule-change`, a change that would compress an existing turnover's window below `required_minutes` is refused unless an `override_reason` is supplied. Failure yields `ERR_SCHEDULE_CHANGE_BLOCKS_TURN` (409).
25. **Cleaner schedule conflict.** On `POST /turnovers/{id}/assign` and `POST /assignments/{id}/accept`, the candidate cleaner's existing non-terminal assignment windows must not overlap this turnover's window under the zone-conditional buffer of §2.6. Failure yields `ERR_CLEANER_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` (409).
26. **Start not before the window opens.** On `POST /turnovers/{id}/start`, the logical clock must be at or after the turnover's `window_start_at`. Failure yields `ERR_TOO_EARLY_TO_START` (409).
27. **Audit sampling window.** On `POST /quality-audits`, sampling is permitted only within 72 hours — three days — of the turnover's `finished_at`. Failure yields `ERR_AUDIT_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422).
28. **Uniqueness.** A property's `code` must be unique across all properties; a supply line's `sku` must be unique within its property; a booking's `external_ref` must be unique within its property. The gate guards `POST /properties`, `POST /properties/{id}/supply-lines` and `POST /bookings`, and yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE_KEY` (409).
29. **One live turnover per departure.** On `POST /turnovers`, an outgoing booking may carry at most one turnover that is not `VOID`. Failure yields `ERR_TURNOVER_ALREADY_EXISTS` (409).
30. **One live audit per turnover.** On `POST /quality-audits`, a turnover may carry at most one audit that is not `VOIDED`. Failure yields `ERR_AUDIT_ALREADY_EXISTS` (409).
31. **One guest review per stay.** On `POST /bookings/{id}/guest-review`, a booking accepts exactly one cleanliness review. Failure yields `ERR_GUEST_REVIEW_ALREADY_SUBMITTED` (409).
32. **Explicit-cleaner eligibility.** On `POST /turnovers/{id}/assign` with a named cleaner, that cleaner must serve the property's zone — by home zone, by secondary zone, or by accepting cross-zone work — and must have a `skill_level` greater than or equal to the property's `min_skill_level`. Failure yields `ERR_CLEANER_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422).
33. **Daily load cap.** On `POST /turnovers/{id}/assign` and `POST /assignments/{id}/accept`, the cleaner's count of non-terminal assignments falling on the turnover's property-local calendar date must be strictly below `max_daily_turnovers`. Failure yields `ERR_CLEANER_DAILY_CAP_REACHED` (409).
34. **Non-empty candidate pool.** On `POST /turnovers/{id}/assign` without a named cleaner, the filtered candidate pool must contain at least one cleaner. Failure yields `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CLEANER` (422).
35. **Stock sufficiency.** On `POST /checklist-tasks/{id}/submit`, every restock entry's `qty_used` must be less than or equal to its supply line's `on_hand_qty`, and a replenishment quantity must be at least 1. Failure yields `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ON_HAND` (409).

### 2.6. Property-Local Dates, Half-Open Intervals and Zone Buffers

Turn-type classification is a **local-calendar** question, never a UTC-calendar one. Each unit declares `local_utc_offset_minutes`, a fixed offset from UTC that may run from negative twelve hours (−720 minutes) to positive fourteen hours (840 minutes) and defaults to 0. To obtain the property-local calendar date of any instant, that offset in minutes is added to the instant and the calendar date of the shifted value is taken. A departure at 23:30 UTC in a unit whose offset is −480 minutes therefore sits on the *previous* local date, which is exactly the kind of case that decides whether a turn is same-day or overnight.

Occupancy intervals are **half-open**: a stay occupies its property from `checkin_at` inclusive up to `checkout_at` exclusive. Two stays overlap when the new stay's `checkin_at` is strictly earlier than the existing stay's `checkout_at` and the existing stay's `checkin_at` is strictly earlier than the new stay's `checkout_at`. There is no occupancy buffer whatsoever — the buffer is zero minutes — so a new stay may begin at the exact instant a prior stay ends and that adjacency is permitted rather than rejected. The comparison always uses the existing stay's *contracted* `checkout_at`, never a granted `late_checkout_until_at`: a courtesy extension granted to one guest never retroactively invalidates an already-booked stay. Only stays whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CHECKED_OUT` participate; a `CANCELLED` stay never blocks anything.

Cleaner scheduling uses a second, quite different interval rule with a **zone-conditional symmetric buffer**. When comparing two turnover windows held by the same cleaner, the buffer is 15 minutes if the two turnovers' properties share the same `zone_code`, and 45 minutes otherwise. Note that the buffer is decided by the two *properties'* zone codes, not by the cleaner's `home_zone_code`. The two windows conflict when the first window's start is strictly earlier than the second window's end extended forward by the buffer, and simultaneously the second window's start is strictly earlier than the first window's end extended forward by the buffer. Both comparisons are strict, so a gap of exactly the buffer length does **not** count as a conflict. Only assignments whose `status` is `OFFERED`, `ACCEPTED` or `ACTIVE` are considered; `DECLINED`, `REVOKED` and `COMPLETED` assignments are excluded both from this overlap test and from the daily-load count.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Offer Creation and Replenishment

Two write operations are protected by optimistic concurrency, and on each of them the expected-version field is a required input rather than an optional one.

Creating a cleaner offer on a turnover requires `expected_turnover_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `Turnover`. If it does not equal the turnover's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TURNOVER_VERSION` at HTTP 412. This guard exists because offer creation is a deliberate supersede operation — a fresh offer revokes the turnover's outstanding `OFFERED` assignment by design — so a coordinator working from a stale reading of the turnover would silently discard an offer, an acceptance, or a window recomputation it never saw. The stale-version check converts that into a deterministic refusal.

Recording received stock on a supply line requires `expected_supply_line_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `SupplyLine`. If it does not equal the line's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SUPPLY_LINE_VERSION` at HTTP 412. Replenishment is purely additive and would otherwise always succeed, so a second restocking request built on a stale reading of `on_hand_qty` would double-count a delivery that a restock consumption or an earlier replenishment had already moved; the guard forces the client to re-read the line first.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field. Every operation not named in this subsection takes no expected-version input and is never rejected for a version mismatch.

### 2.8. Money, Precision and the Conserved Payout Ledger

All monetary amounts in this system are integer cents; no field carries a fractional currency value. Wherever a percentage or multiplier produces a fractional result, that result is rounded to whole cents using **round half up**, and no intermediate value is ever carried forward at sub-cent precision. Where the specification names a rounding step explicitly — each pay premium, the late-penalty cap, each restock line charge — the rounding happens at exactly that step and not at some later aggregate. Non-monetary computed numbers have their own declared precision: a cleaner's `quality_score` and an audit's `observed_score` and `resulting_quality_score` all carry two decimal places under round half up, while an assignment's `match_score` carries four decimal places under round half up. Durations are always whole minutes, and stock quantities are always whole units.

`Cleaner.pending_payout_cents` is a **conserved ledger**, not a counter that gets nudged. Its value is at all times the sum of `net_cents` over that cleaner's pay lines whose `status` is `PROVISIONAL`, `FINAL` or `ADJUSTED`. Pay lines in `VOIDED` are excluded from the sum but are never deleted — the row and its money fields are retained as evidence. This invariant is re-established after every request that creates a pay line, switches its variant, folds in an audit adjustment, or voids it, which means an accept-then-revoke pair returns the ledger to its exact pre-acceptance value. Implementations must recompute from the pay-line set rather than incrementing blindly.

### 2.9. The Append-Only Stock Ledger

Supply stock is never stored as a mutable number. A supply line's `on_hand_qty` is a pure projection: it equals the sum of `delta_qty` across every `StockMovement` bearing that line's `supply_line_id`, regardless of the movement's `reason`. Immediately after that projection is refreshed, `below_par` is recomputed as true exactly when `on_hand_qty` is less than or equal to `reorder_point`. Because the ledger is append-only, a correction is always a new compensating movement rather than an edit, and the invariant that `on_hand_qty` equals the summed `delta_qty` holds after every single request. On-hand quantity can never be driven below zero, because consumption is pre-checked against the line's current on-hand figure before any movement is appended. Within one supply line, `sequence_no` is a 1-based monotonic counter that gives a total order even among several movements minted inside a single request; the four permitted values of `reason` are `SEED`, `REPLENISH`, `RESTOCK_USE` and `REVERSAL`.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline, Version Bumps and Compensation

Many operations in this system touch more than the resource they address. Three disciplines govern that.

**Cascades are declared, not implied.** Where this document says that an operation revokes offers, voids pay lines, reverts a parent status, blocks a guest's arrival, or debits the payout ledger, every one of those secondary effects is part of the same request and must be applied before the response is emitted. The consistency model is immediate: there is no deferred or eventual settlement anywhere in this API.

**Version bumps follow the mutation, wherever it lands.** When a cascade mutates a resource other than the one addressed, that resource's own `version` increases by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed. The specification states each such bump explicitly at the point it occurs, including the cases where a bump deliberately does *not* happen — declining an offer, for instance, leaves the turnover's `status` and `version` untouched, in deliberate contrast to accepting one.

**Reversal is compensation, never erasure.** Releasing a hold restores the status the turnover was in before the hold rather than guessing a status; reversing a verified restock appends negating ledger movements rather than deleting the originals; voiding a pay line leaves its money fields readable. Counters that record history — `rejected_review_count`, `reject_count`, `rework_round`, `decline_count`, `strike_count` — are never walked backwards by a compensating action.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering Units, Onboarding Crews and Par-Level Stock

A `PlatformAdmin` bootstraps the world. Registering a rental unit requires an operator-facing `code` that must be unique across every property in the system, a display `name` for the unit, the `zone_code` naming the cleaning zone it sits in (an uppercase token between 1 and 16 characters, such as `ZONE_A`), the `size_tier` classifying the unit as `STUDIO`, `ONE_BR`, `TWO_BR` or `THREE_BR_PLUS`, and an `owner_contact_email` that becomes the notification target for damage findings. The physical inventory of the unit is described by three counts, each defaulting to 1: `bedroom_count` and `bathroom_count` may each run from 0 to 12, and `bed_count` may run from 0 to 24. Two contractual dials may also be supplied: `min_clean_minutes`, a floor on the derived cleaning requirement that defaults to 60 and may run from 0 to 600, and `min_skill_level`, the lowest cleaner certification tier permitted to work the unit, defaulting to 1 within a range of 1 to 5. The unit's `local_utc_offset_minutes` defaults to 0. `owner_notify_min_cost_cents` — the repair estimate at or above which the owner is told even about a minor finding — defaults to 5,000 cents, that is fifty dollars, and may not be negative.

A property's operational `status` may be `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED` or `OFFBOARDED`, and it defaults to `ACTIVE`. This dimension is configuration rather than workflow: there is no runtime transition for it, and the only way a unit comes to sit in `PAUSED` or `OFFBOARDED` is for the registering administrator to supply that `status` at creation time, producing the unit directly in that state. What the dimension *does* is gate new work — a unit that is `PAUSED` or `OFFBOARDED` refuses to open a new turnover and answers `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_ACTIVE`, while an `ACTIVE` unit accepts one. At registration the unit's `open_issue_count` is initialised to 0 and its `last_turnover_completed_at` is initialised to null; the former is maintained thereafter as the count of that property's issue reports whose `status` is anything other than `RESOLVED`, and the latter is stamped with the logical clock each time one of the property's turnovers reaches `COMPLETED`.

Onboarding a cleaner takes a `name`, a `home_zone_code`, and a `skill_level` between 1 and 5. Zone reach is described by two further inputs: `secondary_zone_codes`, an array of additional zone tokens served at reduced preference, which defaults to empty, may hold at most 5 entries, may not repeat a token, and may not contain the cleaner's own `home_zone_code`; and `accepts_cross_zone`, a boolean defaulting to false that, when true, makes the cleaner eligible for zones outside both the home and secondary sets. `max_daily_turnovers` caps how many non-terminal assignments the cleaner may hold on one property-local calendar date; it defaults to 2 and may run from 1 to 6. The optional `initial_quality_score` seeds the cleaner's rolling `quality_score`, which is otherwise 80.00; the score is a two-decimal figure bounded between 0 and 100, and after onboarding it is **only ever** recomputed by quality-audit finalization — no other operation writes it. A cleaner's employment `status` may be `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `OFFBOARDED` and defaults to `ACTIVE`; only an `ACTIVE` cleaner may be offered work or accept it. Onboarding also initialises four counters to zero: `pending_payout_cents`, `completed_turnover_count` (the number of this cleaner's assignments sitting in `COMPLETED`), `decline_count` (the number sitting in `DECLINED`), and `strike_count`.

Par-level stock is configured per unit, one line per stock keeping unit. Creating a line requires the `sku`, which must be unique within the property, a human-readable `name`, a `category` of `LINEN`, `CONSUMABLE` or `WELCOME`, the `unit_cost_cents` replacement cost per unit (never negative), and the `par_qty` target stock level per turn (never negative). Optionally the caller supplies `unit`, the unit of issue, which defaults to the string `each`; `reorder_point`, defaulting to 0 and never negative; `initial_on_hand_qty`, which seeds the line's stock; and `status`, which may be `ACTIVE` or `DISCONTINUED` and defaults to `ACTIVE`. Like a property's operational state, a supply line's `status` is configuration: `DISCONTINUED` arises only by supplying it at creation, and its effect is that the line is excluded from the estimated-linen sum used by provisional pay while still accepting restock consumption. Creating a line appends exactly one stock movement with `reason` `SEED`, `sequence_no` 1, and `delta_qty` equal to `initial_on_hand_qty` — or 0 when the caller omitted it — after which `on_hand_qty` and `below_par` are projected. A newly created line with no seeded stock therefore has `below_par` true, which is also the field's default.

Received stock is recorded against a line with a `qty` of at least 1, the mandatory `expected_supply_line_version` described in §2.7, and an optional `note` that is carried onto the resulting movement. The append carries `reason` `REPLENISH` and a `delta_qty` equal to the supplied `qty`, refreshes `on_hand_qty` and `below_par`, and increments the line's `version` by 1. Every movement also captures `unit_cost_cents_at_time`, the line's `unit_cost_cents` as it stood when the movement was minted, and a `charge_cents` figure that is 0 for `SEED` and `REPLENISH` movements. A movement caused by cleaning work additionally records the `turnover_id` and `checklist_task_id` responsible; both are null on `SEED` and `REPLENISH` movements, whose `note` is the only free-text field a client ever supplies to the ledger.

### 3.2. The Booking Calendar: Import, Departure and Guest Communication

Reservations arrive from channel calendars and are imported by a `Coordinator`, who supplies the `property_id` of the unit, the channel's `external_ref` reservation reference (unique within that unit), the `guest_name` of record, and the contracted `checkin_at` and `checkout_at` instants, where the departure must be strictly later than the arrival. Two optional inputs shape the work the stay will generate: `party_size`, the number of guests, defaulting to 1 within a range of 1 to 24; and `pet_stay`, a boolean defaulting to false that flags a pet in residence and, as §3.3 explains, inflates the cleaning requirement of the turnover that follows the stay. A fresh reservation lands with `status` `SCHEDULED`, `checkin_gate` `OPEN` and `guest_notice_code` `NONE`, and is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_OVERLAP` if its half-open occupancy interval collides with another `SCHEDULED` or `CHECKED_OUT` stay on the same unit under the zero-buffer rule of §2.6.

A booking carries **two independent state dimensions**. The occupancy dimension is `status`, which advances from `SCHEDULED` to either `CHECKED_OUT` or `CANCELLED`. The arrival-readiness dimension is `checkin_gate`, which advances `OPEN` → `BLOCKED` → `RELEASED`. The two evolve on entirely different triggers and never constrain one another; `guest_notice_code` rides the gate dimension and records the communication protocol the guest was last notified under, taking one of `NONE`, `LATE_CHECKOUT_GRANTED`, `EARLY_CHECKIN_LIMITED` or `CHECKIN_DELAYED`.

Recording the departing guest's actual departure moves the booking `SCHEDULED → CHECKED_OUT`, and requires that the booking was in `SCHEDULED` to begin with — otherwise `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_SCHEDULED`. The coordinator may supply `actual_checkout_at`; when omitted it defaults to the current logical clock. This is the one boundary movement that **never refuses**: every non-terminal turnover whose departing stay is this booking re-derives `window_start_at`, `window_end_at`, `window_minutes`, `slack_minutes`, `turn_type` and `is_tight`, and has its `version` incremented by 1, but a window that would come out non-positive is clamped to 0 rather than rejected. When the recomputed `window_minutes` falls below `required_minutes`, the turnover raises `escalation_required` to true. And when the recomputed `window_minutes` lands at exactly 0, a second hop reaches across to the turnover's arriving stay: that booking's `checkin_gate` moves `OPEN → BLOCKED`, its `guest_notice_code` becomes `CHECKIN_DELAYED`, and its own `version` increments by 1 — the guest is being told their arrival is delayed because the departing guest left the unit with no cleaning time at all.

Guest-facing schedule courtesies are granted through a single operation carrying a `change_type` of either `LATE_CHECKOUT` or `EARLY_CHECKIN`, the requested `new_time_at`, and an optional `override_reason`. Both variants require the booking to still be `SCHEDULED`, and both leave the gate dimension completely untouched — a granted courtesy is not a delay and never blocks an arrival. A `LATE_CHECKOUT` request writes `late_checkout_until_at` and sets `guest_notice_code` to `LATE_CHECKOUT_GRANTED`; its `new_time_at` must be strictly later than the contracted `checkout_at` (else `ERR_INVALID_TIME_INTERVAL`) and may not fall more than 240 minutes past it (else `ERR_SCHEDULE_CHANGE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`). An `EARLY_CHECKIN` request writes `early_checkin_at` and, when an `override_reason` was required to permit it, sets `guest_notice_code` to `EARLY_CHECKIN_LIMITED`; its `new_time_at` must be strictly earlier than the contracted `checkin_at` and at or after the affected turnover's `window_start_at`, failing which the answer is again `ERR_INVALID_TIME_INTERVAL`. Either variant then re-derives the affected non-terminal turnover's window and increments that turnover's `version` by 1. Unlike a recorded departure, a schedule change is held to the strict standard: a derived window that is not strictly positive draws `ERR_CLEANING_WINDOW_NON_POSITIVE`, and a window compressed below `required_minutes` without an `override_reason` draws `ERR_SCHEDULE_CHANGE_BLOCKS_TURN`.

Guest sentiment closes the loop between cleaning quality and revenue. A cleanliness review carries a single `cleanliness_rating` from 1 to 5 and is accepted once the logical clock has reached the booking's `checkin_at`, whether or not the stay has ended; an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_INTERVAL`. The rating and its `cleanliness_rated_at` logical-clock reading are recorded, and a booking accepts exactly one such review — a second attempt is refused with `ERR_GUEST_REVIEW_ALREADY_SUBMITTED`. Timing matters downstream in a way that is deliberate rather than incidental: a rating that lands *after* an audit on the relevant turnover has already been finalized is recorded on the booking but never retroactively re-scores that audit.

### 3.3. Opening a Turnover: Window Derivation and Work Sizing

A `Coordinator` opens a turnover by nominating the departing stay through `outgoing_booking_id` and, when an arriving stay is already known, the `incoming_booking_id`; omitting the latter declares a vacant gap. An optional `override_reason` may be supplied as justification for a window too short for the work. The turnover copies its `property_id` from the departing stay, lands with `status` `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT` and `version` 1, and refuses to be created at all if the property is not `ACTIVE` (`ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_ACTIVE`), if the departing stay already carries a turnover that is not `VOID` (`ERR_TURNOVER_ALREADY_EXISTS`), if the two nominated stays belong to different units (`ERR_PROPERTY_MISMATCH`), or if the arriving stay's `checkin_at` precedes the departing stay's `checkout_at` (`ERR_INVALID_TIME_INTERVAL`). Only bookings whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CHECKED_OUT` may serve as a boundary; a `CANCELLED` stay is never a boundary.

The **window** is derived, and re-derived on every event that moves one of its boundary instants — turnover creation, a granted schedule change, a recorded departure, and the cancellation of the arriving stay. Its opening instant, `window_start_at`, is the latest of three candidate instants on the departing stay, considering only those that are not null: the contracted `checkout_at`, the granted `late_checkout_until_at`, and the recorded `actual_checkout_at`. Its closing instant depends on whether an arriving stay exists. With no arriving stay, `window_end_at` is `window_start_at` advanced by 48 hours — two full days of vacant-gap latitude — and `turn_type` is `VACANT_GAP`. With an arriving stay, the effective arrival instant is the *earliest* of the two candidate arrival instants that are not null — the contracted `checkin_at` and the granted `early_checkin_at` — and `window_end_at` is that effective arrival instant pulled back by a 30-minute inspection buffer, so that a supervisor always has half an hour between the cleaner finishing and the guest arriving. Classification then compares the property-local calendar date of `window_start_at` with the property-local calendar date of the effective arrival instant, using the offset arithmetic of §2.6: when the two local dates are the same the `turn_type` is `SAME_DAY`, and when the arrival falls on a later local date it is `OVERNIGHT`.

The raw window length is the whole number of minutes from `window_start_at` to `window_end_at`. The **work requirement** is sized independently of it. Start from the size-tier base: 90 minutes for a `STUDIO`, 120 for a `ONE_BR`, 150 for a `TWO_BR` and 200 for a `THREE_BR_PLUS`. Add 12 minutes for each of the unit's bathrooms and 8 minutes for each of its beds. When the departing stay's `pet_stay` flag is true, multiply that running subtotal by 1.20, adding a fifth again to every minute of the clean. Take the ceiling of the result so that a fractional minute always rounds up to the next whole minute, and then `required_minutes` is the larger of that ceiling and the unit's contractual `min_clean_minutes` floor.

Two guards then apply on the creation path, and their boundary behaviour is exact. A raw window length of zero or less is refused with `ERR_CLEANING_WINDOW_NON_POSITIVE`. A raw window length strictly less than `required_minutes` is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_OVERRIDE_REQUIRED` unless an `override_reason` was supplied; a window exactly equal to `required_minutes` is acceptable and needs no override, because the comparison the guard makes is "at least". Once past the guards, `window_minutes` is the raw length with a floor of 0 applied, `slack_minutes` is `window_minutes` less `required_minutes` and may legitimately be negative, and `is_tight` is true whenever `slack_minutes` is strictly below 45 minutes — note that a comfortable window is not merely one that fits, but one that fits with three quarters of an hour to spare. `escalation_required` becomes true whenever `slack_minutes` is negative, and once raised it is never silently lowered by a later recomputation of the same window; the flag also rises when a recorded departure collapses the window and when rework exceeds its permitted rounds.

### 3.4. Offering the Work: Zone Fit, Skill Headroom and Daily Load

Work reaches a cleaner as an **offer**. A `Coordinator` may name the cleaner outright by supplying `cleaner_id`, which records `selection_mode` `EXPLICIT`, or omit it and let the server rank the eligible pool, which records `selection_mode` `SELECTOR`. In either mode the request must carry `expected_turnover_version`, and may carry `exclude_cleaner_ids`, a list of cleaners the coordinator wants kept out of the ranking. Each offer takes the next `sequence_no` within its turnover, starting at 1, and records `assigned_by_user_id` from the acting operator's `X-User-ID`. Because a fresh offer supersedes whatever came before it, creating one revokes the turnover's outstanding `OFFERED` assignment — that older offer moves `OFFERED → REVOKED` with `revoked_at` stamped from the logical clock, and because no pay line exists for a mere offer there is no ledger movement attached to that revocation. Offering also increments the turnover's `version` by 1, and requires the turnover to be in `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT`; any other status draws `ERR_INVALID_TURNOVER_STATE`.

Eligibility is where the two modes diverge sharply. The **candidate pool** for selector mode consists of cleaners whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose zone reach covers the property's `zone_code` (by home-zone match, by the property's zone appearing among their `secondary_zone_codes`, or by `accepts_cross_zone` being true), whose `skill_level` is at least the property's `min_skill_level`, who are not named in `exclude_cleaner_ids`, whose count of non-terminal assignments on the turnover's property-local date is strictly below their `max_daily_turnovers`, and whose existing non-terminal assignment windows do not overlap this turnover's window under the zone-conditional buffer. A cleaner failing any of those conditions is simply filtered out; if the filtering empties the pool, the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CLEANER`. In explicit mode nothing is filtered — each pool condition the named cleaner fails becomes an error in its own right instead: a suspended or offboarded cleaner draws `ERR_CLEANER_NOT_ACTIVE`, a cleaner outside the served zones or below the required skill tier draws `ERR_CLEANER_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, a cleaner already at the daily ceiling draws `ERR_CLEANER_DAILY_CAP_REACHED`, and a cleaner whose existing windows collide draws `ERR_CLEANER_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`.

Every offer, in both modes, records a `match_score` — the weighted fit of cleaner to job — together with the `zone_match_factor` that fed it. The zone factor is 1.0 when the cleaner's `home_zone_code` equals the property's `zone_code`, 0.4 when the property's zone appears among the cleaner's `secondary_zone_codes`, and 0.0 otherwise, the last case being reachable only for a cleaner who accepts cross-zone work. Two further terms enter the score. Normalised quality is the cleaner's `quality_score` divided by 100, placing it on a 0-to-1 scale. Skill headroom measures how far above the unit's floor the cleaner sits: take the cleaner's `skill_level`, subtract the property's `min_skill_level`, add 1, divide by 4, and cap the result at 1.0 — so a cleaner exactly at the floor scores 0.25 of headroom and a cleaner four tiers above it saturates. The score is then the sum of three weighted terms: normalised quality weighted at 0.50, the zone factor weighted at 0.30, and skill headroom weighted at 0.20. Because the three weights sum to exactly 1.00, the score always lies between 0 and 1 inclusive; it is rounded half up to four decimal places.

In selector mode the highest `match_score` wins, and ties are broken by a fixed chain applied in order: first the higher `skill_level`; then the lower count of the cleaner's non-terminal assignments on the same property-local date; then the earlier `created_at` on the cleaner record. The chain never falls back to comparing cleaner identifiers. The **daily load** referred to throughout is computed against the property-local calendar date of the turnover's `window_start_at`: it counts the cleaner's assignments whose `status` is `OFFERED`, `ACCEPTED` or `ACTIVE` and whose own turnover's `window_start_at`, converted using *that* turnover's property offset, falls on the same local date. The cap is reached when that count is greater than or equal to `max_daily_turnovers`.

### 3.5. Acceptance, Provisional Pay and Starting the Clean

Acceptance is the moment the job becomes real for both parties. Accepting an offer requires that the offer's `status` is `OFFERED` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`), that the offer's `cleaner_id` equals the acting `X-User-ID`, that the cleaner is `ACTIVE` (otherwise `ERR_CLEANER_NOT_ACTIVE`), that the turnover is still in `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TURNOVER_STATE`), that the cleaner is not already at the daily cap (`ERR_CLEANER_DAILY_CAP_REACHED`), and that this turnover's window does not overlap another of the cleaner's non-terminal assignment windows under the zone buffer (`ERR_CLEANER_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`). On success the offer moves `OFFERED → ACCEPTED` with `accepted_at` stamped from the logical clock, the turnover moves `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT → SCHEDULED` and its `version` increments by 1, a pay line is created in `status` `PROVISIONAL` under the `PROVISIONAL` variant, and the cleaner's `pending_payout_cents` increases by that pay line's `net_cents`.

Declining is the deliberately asymmetric sibling. It likewise requires an `OFFERED` offer owned by the acting cleaner, and it moves the offer `OFFERED → DECLINED` with `declined_at` stamped from the logical clock while incrementing the cleaner's `decline_count` by 1; an optional `decline_reason` may accompany the refusal. But the turnover is left entirely alone — it stays in `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT`, and crucially its `version` is **not** incremented. A coordinator holding a version reading taken before a decline can therefore still issue a fresh offer without re-reading, whereas the same reading taken before an acceptance is stale.

Beginning the clean advances the turnover `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS` and simultaneously moves the accepted offer `ACCEPTED → ACTIVE`. Four preconditions guard it: the turnover must be in `SCHEDULED`, the acting cleaner must own the turnover's `ACCEPTED` offer, the departing stay must already be `CHECKED_OUT` — a cross-entity gate answering `ERR_GUEST_NOT_CHECKED_OUT` when the guest has not left — and the logical clock must be at or after `window_start_at`, answering `ERR_TOO_EARLY_TO_START` when the cleaner arrives ahead of the window. On success `started_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the turnover's `version` increments by 1.

Starting the clean is also what **materialises the checklist**. Rows are projected from a fixed template, filtered to those whose applicable size tiers include the property's `size_tier`, and appended in ascending template order. Four rows apply to every tier: `STRIP_LINENS` at sequence 10, in `category` `CLEANING`, needing no photograph; `BATHROOM_DEEP_CLEAN` at sequence 20, in `CLEANING`, requiring a photograph; `BED_STAGING` at sequence 30, in `category` `STAGING`, requiring a photograph; and `SUPPLY_RESTOCK` at sequence 40, in `category` `RESTOCK`, requiring a photograph. A fifth row, `PATIO_STAGING` at sequence 50, in `STAGING` and requiring a photograph, applies only to a `THREE_BR_PLUS` unit. Each generated row copies its `task_code`, `category`, `sequence_no` and `requires_photo` from the template and lands in `status` `PENDING`. The turnover's `checklist_task_count` is then set to the number of rows appended — four for a `STUDIO`, a `ONE_BR` or a `TWO_BR`, and five for a `THREE_BR_PLUS`.

### 3.6. Checklist Execution, Photo Verification and the Rework Cycle

Each checklist row travels its own small lifecycle — `PENDING`, `SUBMITTED`, `VERIFIED`, `REJECTED`, `WAIVED` — and the parent turnover's status is a *function* of those children rather than something set directly. While the clean is underway, and **only** while the turnover's current status is one of `IN_PROGRESS`, `AWAITING_VERIFICATION` or `REWORK_REQUIRED`, the turnover's status is re-derived after every single child change by this ordered rule, first match winning: if any child sits in `REJECTED` the turnover is `REWORK_REQUIRED`; otherwise if any child sits in `PENDING` the turnover is `IN_PROGRESS`; otherwise — every child being `VERIFIED` or `WAIVED` — the turnover is `AWAITING_VERIFICATION`. A turnover in `ON_HOLD`, `COMPLETED` or `VOID` is never re-derived. Each re-derivation that actually changes the status increments the turnover's `version` by 1, bubbling the child's mutation up to the parent.

A cleaner submits a row by supplying, as appropriate, `photo_uri` as the photographic evidence, a free-text `note`, and `restock_entries` — a list in which each entry names a `supply_line_id` and the `qty_used` consumed from it. Submission is permitted from `PENDING` and from `REJECTED`, and requires that the acting cleaner owns the turnover's `ACTIVE` assignment and that the turnover's status is one of `IN_PROGRESS`, `AWAITING_VERIFICATION` or `REWORK_REQUIRED`; a task already `VERIFIED` or `WAIVED` cannot be submitted and draws `ERR_INVALID_TASK_STATE`. `photo_uri` must be present whenever the row's `requires_photo` is true, failing which the answer is `ERR_PHOTO_REQUIRED`. Restock entries must be present exactly when the row's `category` is `RESTOCK`: a restock row with none draws `ERR_RESTOCK_ENTRIES_REQUIRED`, and a `CLEANING` or `STAGING` row carrying any draws `ERR_RESTOCK_ENTRIES_NOT_ALLOWED`. Every entry's supply line must belong to the turnover's property (`ERR_PROPERTY_MISMATCH`) and its `qty_used` must not exceed that line's current `on_hand_qty` (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ON_HAND`). A successful submission records `photo_uri`, `note`, a `submitted_at` reading of the logical clock, and `restock_line_count` — the number of restock entries carried on this most recent submission — and re-derives the parent. Submitting from `PENDING` is the first pass; submitting again from `REJECTED` refreshes `submitted_at` and re-derives the parent, either continuing or closing the rework cycle. **No stock moves at submission time**: consumption is booked only when a reviewer approves, which is precisely what prevents a rejected-then-resubmitted row from double-counting stock.

Review is a coordinator's act and carries an `action` of `APPROVE`, `REJECT` or `WAIVE`, plus an optional `reject_reason`. Every review records `reviewed_by_user_id` from the acting operator's `X-User-ID` and then re-derives the parent turnover.

**Approval** moves the row `SUBMITTED → VERIFIED` and requires the row to have been `SUBMITTED` and the turnover to be in one of `IN_PROGRESS`, `AWAITING_VERIFICATION` or `REWORK_REQUIRED`. For a `RESTOCK` row, approval is the moment stock actually leaves the closet: one stock movement is appended per submitted restock entry, in the exact order the entries were supplied, each with `reason` `RESTOCK_USE` and a `delta_qty` equal to the negative of that entry's `qty_used`; each affected supply line then re-projects `on_hand_qty`, recomputes `below_par`, and has its `version` incremented by 1. Approval also computes the row's `restock_charge_cents`, the owner chargeback this row contributes. That chargeback is computed **per line and then summed**, and the order is normative: for each restock entry in the order supplied, multiply the supply line's `unit_cost_cents` by the entry's `qty_used` and then by a waste factor of 1.075 — a seven-and-a-half percent allowance for spoilage and partial packs — and round that product half up to whole cents; the row's `restock_charge_cents` is the sum of those already-rounded per-line charges. Rounding a pre-waste subtotal and applying the factor afterwards produces a different number and is wrong. Each appended movement carries its own per-line figure in `charge_cents`, so summing `charge_cents` across the ledger always reconciles with the surviving row charges.

**Rejection** moves the row `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` and, like approval, requires a `SUBMITTED` row and a turnover in one of the three underway statuses. It increments the row's `reject_count` by 1 and records the supplied `reject_reason`. It appends **no** stock movement at all. On the parent, `rejected_review_count` and `rework_round` each increase by 1, the re-derivation rule drives the turnover to `REWORK_REQUIRED` — most visibly as the `AWAITING_VERIFICATION → REWORK_REQUIRED` step when the last row had just been cleared — and the turnover's `version` increments by 1. The money follows immediately: the turnover's `PROVISIONAL` pay line recomputes `photo_penalty_cents`, clears `photo_pass_bonus_cents` to zero, re-derives `net_cents`, and the cleaner's `pending_payout_cents` moves by exactly that delta. When `rework_round` climbs strictly above 2 — that is, on the third and any subsequent entry into rework — the turnover sets `escalation_required` to true and the cleaner's `strike_count` increases by 1, once per turnover.

**Waiving** is the coordinator's escape hatch and is permitted from three source states: `PENDING → WAIVED`, `REJECTED → WAIVED` and `VERIFIED → WAIVED`, each requiring the turnover to be in one of `IN_PROGRESS`, `AWAITING_VERIFICATION` or `REWORK_REQUIRED`. Waiving a `PENDING` row simply retires it and re-derives the parent. Waiving a `REJECTED` row re-derives the parent and may close the rework cycle, but it does **not** decrease `rejected_review_count` — the penalty already booked stands. Waiving an already `VERIFIED` restock row is the compensation case: for every `RESTOCK_USE` movement that row booked, a movement with `reason` `REVERSAL` is appended carrying the opposite `delta_qty` and the negated `charge_cents`, in the same per-line order, so that each supply line's `on_hand_qty` returns exactly to its pre-approval value, `below_par` is recomputed and the line's `version` increments by 1; the row's `restock_charge_cents` returns to 0. Because `VERIFIED` is not a terminal state, this reversal can pull the turnover backwards from `AWAITING_VERIFICATION` to `IN_PROGRESS` when another child returns to `PENDING`, and the loop `AWAITING_VERIFICATION → REWORK_REQUIRED → IN_PROGRESS → AWAITING_VERIFICATION` may be traversed repeatedly. `WAIVED` is where a row finally comes to rest.

### 3.7. Damage Findings, Owner Notification and Repair Holds

Cleaners are the operator's eyes inside the unit, so filing a finding is available to a `Cleaner` working the turnover as well as to a `Coordinator`. A report names the `turnover_id` during which the issue surfaced, an `issue_type` of `DAMAGE`, `MAINTENANCE`, `SAFETY` or `CONSUMABLE_SHORTAGE`, a `severity` of `LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH` or `CRITICAL`, a `description` of what the team found, and an `estimated_cost_cents` repair estimate that may not be negative. The optional `guest_chargeable` flag, defaulting to false, marks a finding the departing guest may be billed for. The report copies `property_id` from the turnover, records `reported_by_cleaner_id` as the acting cleaner's `X-User-ID` — left null when a coordinator filed it — and increases the property's `open_issue_count` by 1.

Owner notification is decided at creation time rather than as a later step. The report is created directly in `OWNER_NOTIFIED`, with `owner_notified_at` stamped from the logical clock, when either the `severity` is `HIGH` or `CRITICAL`, or the `estimated_cost_cents` is greater than or equal to the property's `owner_notify_min_cost_cents` threshold. Failing both conditions it is created in `REPORTED` with `owner_notified_at` left null. This is why a unit's notification floor matters: a `LOW`-severity finding on a unit whose floor is the default fifty dollars still reaches the owner the moment the estimate touches 5,000 cents.

Dispatching a vendor is available to a `Coordinator` and also to the `PropertyOwner` of the affected unit, and it advances the report from `REPORTED → DISPATCHED` or from `OWNER_NOTIFIED → DISPATCHED`; a report already `DISPATCHED` or `RESOLVED` draws `ERR_INVALID_ISSUE_STATE`. The request names the `vendor_name` and the `scheduled_at` visit instant, and may set `blocks_checkin` — false by default. A repair order is created in `DISPATCHED`, copying `issue_report_id` from the report it was raised against and `property_id` from that report. When `blocks_checkin` is true and the associated turnover is non-terminal — that is, in any of `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT`, `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `AWAITING_VERIFICATION` or `REWORK_REQUIRED` — the turnover captures the status it is leaving into `pre_hold_status`, moves to `ON_HOLD`, records the new order in `blocking_repair_order_id`, and increments its `version` by 1; in a second hop the arriving stay's `checkin_gate` moves `OPEN → BLOCKED` with `guest_notice_code` `CHECKIN_DELAYED` and that booking's own `version` incremented by 1. When `blocks_checkin` is true but the turnover has already reached `COMPLETED` or `VOID`, no hold is placed and no gate changes.

Closing the repair is a coordinator's act, recording the invoiced `actual_cost_cents` and a `completion_note` vendor closeout, moving the order `DISPATCHED → COMPLETED` with `completed_at` stamped from the logical clock; an order already `COMPLETED` draws `ERR_INVALID_REPAIR_STATE`. Two effects always follow: the parent report moves `DISPATCHED → RESOLVED`, and the property's `open_issue_count` decreases by 1. A third effect is conditional — **only** when some turnover carries this very order as its `blocking_repair_order_id` does that turnover leave `ON_HOLD`. In that case the status is restored from `pre_hold_status`, which may be any of `IN_PROGRESS`, `AWAITING_VERIFICATION`, `REWORK_REQUIRED`, `SCHEDULED` or `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT`; both `pre_hold_status` and `blocking_repair_order_id` are then cleared, and the turnover's `version` increments by 1. The arriving guest is compensated in the same request: that booking's `checkin_gate` moves `BLOCKED → RELEASED`, its `guest_notice_code` returns to `NONE`, and its `version` increments by 1.

### 3.8. Completion, the Pay Model and the Live Pay Preview

Closing out a clean advances the turnover `AWAITING_VERIFICATION → COMPLETED` and is available to the working `Cleaner` and to a `Coordinator`; an optional `completion_note` may be supplied. Two preconditions apply: the turnover must be in `AWAITING_VERIFICATION`, and every one of its checklist rows must be `VERIFIED` or `WAIVED` — a row still `PENDING`, `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED` draws `ERR_UNVERIFIED_TASKS_REMAIN`. Completion stamps `finished_at` from the logical clock and derives `late_minutes` as the whole number of minutes by which `finished_at` exceeds `window_end_at`, or 0 when the clean finished at or before the window closed. The working offer moves `ACTIVE → COMPLETED` and the cleaner's `completed_turnover_count` increases by 1. The pay line recomputes under the `FINAL` variant, taking `status` `FINAL` and `variant` `FINAL`, and the cleaner's `pending_payout_cents` moves by the final `net_cents` less the provisional `net_cents`. The turnover's `restock_chargeback_cents` is set to the sum of `restock_charge_cents` across its `VERIFIED` restock rows — rows still `SUBMITTED`, or sitting in `REJECTED` or `WAIVED`, contribute nothing — and the property's `last_turnover_completed_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Finally the turnover's `version` increments by 1.

Cleaner compensation is a single seven-step calculation whose **step order is normative**; capping the late penalty before the linen adder has landed in the gross, or applying bonuses before the cap, yields different money for the same data. Each pay line records `assignment_id`, and copies `cleaner_id` and `turnover_id` from that offer.

*Step one, the base.* `size_base_cents` is fixed by the unit's `size_tier`: 4,550 cents for a `STUDIO`, 6,175 for a `ONE_BR`, 8,325 for a `TWO_BR` and 11,450 for a `THREE_BR_PLUS`.

*Step two, the premiums, each rounded independently off that base.* `rush_premium_cents` is the base multiplied by fifteen percent when the `turn_type` is `SAME_DAY`, by five percent when it is `OVERNIGHT`, and by zero when it is `VACANT_GAP`, rounded half up to whole cents. `tight_window_premium_cents` is the base multiplied by seven and a half percent, rounded half up, when the turnover's `is_tight` is true, and 0 otherwise. `cross_zone_premium_cents` is the base multiplied by four and a half percent, rounded half up, when the cleaner's `home_zone_code` differs from the property's `zone_code`, and 0 when they match — note this premium turns on a plain home-zone mismatch, so it is paid on a secondary-zone job as well as on a true cross-zone one.

*Step three, the linen units — the variant switch.* Under the `PROVISIONAL` variant, `linen_units` is the estimate obtained by summing `par_qty` across the property's supply lines whose `category` is `LINEN` and whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; a `DISCONTINUED` linen line contributes nothing. Under the `FINAL` variant, `linen_units` is the actual consumption: the sum of `qty_used` across restock entries on the turnover's `VERIFIED` restock rows whose supply line's `category` is `LINEN`. Either way, `linen_adder_cents` is 120 cents of handling per linen unit multiplied by `linen_units`.

*Step four, the gross.* `gross_cents` is `size_base_cents` plus `rush_premium_cents` plus `tight_window_premium_cents` plus `cross_zone_premium_cents` plus `linen_adder_cents`. This figure — and not some later net — is the cap base for the lateness charge.

*Step five, the penalties, capped against the gross just computed.* The pay line's own `late_minutes` is forced to 0 under the `PROVISIONAL` variant and is the turnover's real `late_minutes` under `FINAL`. The raw lateness charge is 250 cents for each started block of 15 minutes: divide `late_minutes` by 15, round that quotient up to a whole number, and multiply by 250 — so one minute late already costs a full block. `late_penalty_cents` is the smaller of that raw charge and thirty percent of `gross_cents` rounded half up. `photo_penalty_cents` is the smaller of 300 cents for each of the turnover's `rejected_review_count` rejections and an absolute ceiling of 900 cents, so the photo penalty saturates at the third rejection. `penalty_cents` is `late_penalty_cents` plus `photo_penalty_cents`.

*Step six, the bonuses.* `on_time_bonus_cents` is 800 cents when the pay line's `late_minutes` is 0 and 0 otherwise — which under the `PROVISIONAL` variant means it is always granted, because provisional lateness is forced to zero. `photo_pass_bonus_cents` is 500 cents when the turnover's `rejected_review_count` is 0 and 0 otherwise; photo penalties from rejections already booked apply under both variants, so a provisional line can carry a photo penalty and no photo-pass bonus. `bonus_cents` is `on_time_bonus_cents` plus `photo_pass_bonus_cents`.

*Step seven, the net.* `net_cents` is `gross_cents` plus `bonus_cents`, less `penalty_cents`, plus `audit_adjustment_cents` — the last term being 0 until a quality audit folds one in. A pay line's `status` runs `PROVISIONAL` at creation, becomes `FINAL` at completion, may become `ADJUSTED` after an audit, and becomes `VOIDED` if the underlying offer is revoked or the turnover is voided; only `PROVISIONAL`, `FINAL` and `ADJUSTED` count toward the cleaner's `pending_payout_cents`.

A live **pay preview** is available as a computed read on the turnover for a `PlatformAdmin`, a `Coordinator` or a `Cleaner`. It returns the pay breakdown the turnover would settle at right now, shaped exactly like a pay line but unpersisted: its `id` and its `status` are both null. While the turnover has not reached `COMPLETED` the preview uses the `PROVISIONAL` variant; once the turnover is `COMPLETED` it uses the `FINAL` variant. Because the two variants differ in both `linen_units` and `late_minutes`, and because the late-penalty cap is proportional to a gross that the linen adder feeds, the same turnover can preview materially different money before and after completion.

### 3.9. Quality Audit Sampling, Independence and Score Reconciliation

Independent inspection is what keeps the quality signal honest, so sampling belongs exclusively to a `QualityAuditor` and is fenced by segregation of duties. Sampling names the `turnover_id` under review, an inspection `score` between 0 and 100 inclusive, and optional `findings` notes. The turnover must have reached `COMPLETED`, otherwise `ERR_TURNOVER_NOT_COMPLETED`; sampling is permitted only within 72 hours — three days — of the turnover's `finished_at`, after which the answer is `ERR_AUDIT_WINDOW_CLOSED`; and a turnover may carry at most one audit that is not `VOIDED`, a second attempt drawing `ERR_AUDIT_ALREADY_EXISTS`. The independence rule is the sharpest of the four: the acting auditor's `X-User-ID` must differ both from the `assigned_by_user_id` recorded on the turnover's assignment and from every `reviewed_by_user_id` recorded on that turnover's checklist rows, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A successful sample lands in `status` `SAMPLED`, records `auditor_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and copies `cleaner_id` from the turnover's `COMPLETED` assignment. Nothing moves yet — neither `quality_score` nor any money — until the audit is finalized.

Finalization moves the audit `SAMPLED → FINALIZED` and may be performed only by the auditor who sampled it: a mismatch between `auditor_user_id` and the acting `X-User-ID` draws `ERR_NOT_AUDIT_OWNER`, and an audit already `FINALIZED` or `VOIDED` draws `ERR_INVALID_AUDIT_STATE`. The first thing finalization does is choose a variant, and it chooses on the evidence available **at that instant**. When the turnover's arriving stay already carries a `cleanliness_rating`, `variant_applied` is `BLENDED` and that rating is captured into `guest_rating_at_audit`. When there is no arriving stay at all — a vacant-gap turnover — or its review has simply not landed yet, `variant_applied` is `AUDIT_ONLY` and `guest_rating_at_audit` stays null.

Under the `BLENDED` variant, `observed_score` is the auditor's `score` weighted at sixty percent plus the guest's rating rescaled to the same 0-to-100 axis — multiplied by 20, so a 5 becomes 100 and a 3 becomes 60 — weighted at forty percent, with the result rounded half up to two decimal places. Under the `AUDIT_ONLY` variant, `observed_score` is simply the auditor's `score` carried onto the two-decimal scale. From there both variants converge: `resulting_quality_score` is seventy percent of the cleaner's current `quality_score` plus thirty percent of `observed_score`, rounded half up to two decimals, and the cleaner's `quality_score` is then replaced by that figure. The order sensitivity is deliberate — a guest review that arrives after finalization is recorded on the booking but never re-scores a `FINALIZED` audit, so the same audit data can yield two different rolling scores depending purely on whether the guest had reviewed yet.

Pay moves on the auditor's raw `score`, not on the blended `observed_score`. `pay_adjustment_cents` is positive 1,000 cents when the `score` is greater than or equal to 95, negative 1,200 cents when the `score` is strictly below 70, and 0 for any score inside that neutral band. When the adjustment is non-zero, the turnover's `FINAL` pay line folds it into `audit_adjustment_cents`, re-derives `net_cents` accordingly, moves `FINAL → ADJUSTED`, and the cleaner's `pending_payout_cents` moves by that same signed delta. `finalized_at` is stamped from the logical clock. A neutral score leaves the pay line in `FINAL` untouched.

### 3.10. Withdrawal Paths: Cancelled Stays, Suspended Cleaners and Clock Control

A stay is cancelled by a `Coordinator` with an optional `cancellation_reason`, moving the booking `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`; a booking that is not `SCHEDULED` draws `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_SCHEDULED`. What happens next depends entirely on which side of a turnover the cancelled stay occupied, and the two branches are genuinely different rather than variations on one theme.

When the cancelled stay is the **departing** side of a non-terminal turnover, the work itself evaporates. That turnover moves to `VOID` and its `version` increments by 1 — the transition applies identically from `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT`, `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `AWAITING_VERIFICATION`, `REWORK_REQUIRED` and `ON_HOLD`. Its assignments sitting in `OFFERED`, `ACCEPTED` or `ACTIVE` all move to `REVOKED` with `revoked_at` stamped; every `PROVISIONAL` pay line among them moves to `VOIDED` and the owning cleaner's `pending_payout_cents` decreases by that line's `net_cents`; and any audit on that turnover still sitting in `SAMPLED` moves to `VOIDED`, which touches neither `quality_score` nor money because an unfinalized audit had never moved either.

When the cancelled stay is the **arriving** side of a non-terminal turnover, the work survives and merely loses its deadline. That turnover clears `incoming_booking_id` to null, re-derives its window as a vacant gap — `turn_type` becomes `VACANT_GAP` and `window_end_at` becomes `window_start_at` advanced by 48 hours — clears `escalation_required` when the roomier window now covers `required_minutes`, and increments its `version` by 1. Its `status` is deliberately **not** changed: a cleaner mid-clean keeps cleaning. Under either branch, a turnover that has already reached `COMPLETED` or `VOID` is left entirely untouched.

Employment changes are a `PlatformAdmin` matter, driven by an `action` of `SUSPEND`, `REACTIVATE` or `OFFBOARD` with an optional `reason`. Suspension requires the cleaner to be `ACTIVE` and moves it `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`; reactivation requires `SUSPENDED` and moves it `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`; offboarding is reachable from both `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED`. An action that does not apply from the cleaner's current status draws `ERR_INVALID_CLEANER_STATE`, as does an offboarding attempt while the cleaner still holds an `ACTIVE` assignment — a cleaner mid-clean may not be offboarded out from under the job.

Suspension cascades downward through non-terminal children only. Every assignment of that cleaner sitting in `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED` moves to `REVOKED` with `revoked_at` stamped from the logical clock. For each such assignment whose turnover was `SCHEDULED`, that turnover returns `SCHEDULED → PENDING_ASSIGNMENT` and its `version` increments by 1, making it available for a fresh offer. Each `PROVISIONAL` pay line belonging to those assignments moves to `VOIDED`, and the cleaner's `pending_payout_cents` decreases by each voided line's `net_cents`, restoring the ledger exactly. Assignments already `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETED` are untouched, so a turnover already `IN_PROGRESS` keeps its cleaner. Offboarding from `ACTIVE` performs the identical revocation, turnover-reversion and payout-debit cascade. Offboarding from `SUSPENDED` performs no further cascade, because suspension already revoked the open offers. Reactivation restores nothing: the cleaner becomes eligible for new work again, but revoked assignments are never resurrected.

Finally, because every window, buffer, lateness and audit-eligibility rule reads the logical clock rather than wall time, a `PlatformAdmin` holds a test-driving capability that sets the server's logical UTC instant to a supplied `to_iso8601` value. No entity is mutated by that call and it returns no resource; subsequent requests simply observe the new instant.
