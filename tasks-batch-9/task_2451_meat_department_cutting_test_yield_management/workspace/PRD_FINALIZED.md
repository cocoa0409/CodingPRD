# Meat Department Yield Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A supermarket meat department is one of the few retail operations where the cost of goods is not known until after the goods have been cut. A primal arrives on the receiving dock with an invoice, a freight charge and possibly a vendor allowance; what leaves the bench is a scatter of ribeye steaks, roasts, grind trim, fat and bone. Until a cutter has broken the primal down on a scale and recorded what each subprimal weighed, nobody knows what a pound of that steak actually cost — and therefore nobody knows what it should sell for. Departments that guess end up either giving away margin on high-yielding primals or pricing themselves out of the case on low-yielding ones, and the shrink at period end never reconciles against anything.

This service is the system of record for that entire chain. It captures the landed cost of every received primal, records the cutting test that measures its true yield, allocates the landed cost across the subprimals by market value, and turns the resulting yield-adjusted cost into a shelf price at a target gross margin. Downstream it plans the service-day case by facing counts and forecast movement, schedules the grinder against country-of-origin segregation blocks, dates every package for sell-by, enforces the department's re-work and re-grind rules, monitors case temperature and halts sales when the cold chain fails, takes and settles holiday-roast pre-orders, and finally reconciles the period's shrink and markdown dollars back against the yield model that started the chain.

Two properties of the domain shape the whole design. First, the cost ledger must conserve: every cent of a primal's landed cost is either allocated to a cutting-test line or sits in an explicitly named residual, and superseding a cutting test must rewind that ledger with no drift. Second, food safety outranks merchandising: a single critical case temperature reading can suspend published plans, hold filled case lines and hold the customer orders staged against them, and no amount of margin pressure may bypass that gate. The API is mounted under `/meat-ops`, with one out-of-band test-driving path under `/_clock`.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section applies across the whole API and is contractual. Where §3 narrates a workflow, it does so on top of these rules; where the two appear to differ, this section governs.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming that operator's role. If either header is absent, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing. If both headers are present but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

There are exactly five roles: `StoreAdmin`, `MeatCutter`, `MeatManager`, `PricingAnalyst`, and `ServiceClerk`. A `StoreAdmin` provisions departments, seeds the retail item catalogue and drives the logical clock, and never touches yield, price activation or case execution. A `MeatCutter` works the bench: receiving primals, recording cutting tests, harvesting and re-working trim, running the grinder, filling the case, staging customer orders and posting temperature readings. A `MeatManager` owns every approval and every irreversible decision: approving or rejecting cutting tests, activating prices, the full case-plan lifecycle, writing off shrink, aborting grind runs, escalating and resolving case failures, forfeiting expired pre-orders and posting reconciliations. A `PricingAnalyst` proposes provisional prices, executes markdowns and drafts, disputes or redrafts reconciliations, but may never activate a price. A `ServiceClerk` takes, confirms, amends, picks up and cancels customer pre-orders and may post temperature readings.

All five roles are **opaque operator identities**. When `X-User-Role` is `StoreAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the same is true when `X-User-Role` is `MeatCutter`, `MeatManager`, `PricingAnalyst`, or `ServiceClerk`. The server records the identifier on the fields where a transition captures it — `submitted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a cutting test, `published_by_user_id` on a case plan, `started_by_user_id` on a grind run, `recorded_by_user_id` on a temperature reading, `reported_by_user_id` and `resolved_by_user_id` on a case failure, and `posted_by_user_id` on a reconciliation — but it never compares `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`, and there is no resource-ownership check anywhere in this API. What the recorded identifiers *are* used for is segregation of duties: two named steps require that the acting operator differ from the operator who performed the prior step, and a violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

Several endpoints accept a family of actions in an `action` field, and reaching such an endpoint is not the same as being allowed to request a given action. A caller whose role is listed for the endpoint but not for the specific action is rejected with `ERR_TRANSITION_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). Action ownership is as follows. On a cutting test, `SUBMIT` and `REVISE` belong to a `MeatCutter` while `APPROVE` and `REJECT` belong to a `MeatManager`. On a retail item, `SUSPEND` and `REINSTATE` belong to a `MeatManager`. On a case plan, `PUBLISH`, `SUSPEND`, `RESUME` and `CLOSE` all belong to a `MeatManager`. On a trim lot, `DISCARD` belongs to a `MeatCutter`. On a grind run, `START` and `COMPLETE` belong to a `MeatCutter` while `ABORT` belongs to a `MeatManager`. On a case failure, `CONTAIN` belongs to a `MeatCutter` while `ESCALATE` and `RESOLVE` belong to a `MeatManager`. On a pre-order, `CONFIRM`, `AMEND`, `PICK_UP` and `CANCEL` belong to a `ServiceClerk`, `STAGE` belongs to a `MeatCutter`, and `FORFEIT` belongs to a `MeatManager`. On a shrink reconciliation, `POST` belongs to a `MeatManager` while `DISPUTE` and `REDRAFT` belong to a `PricingAnalyst`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps how many rows come back; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. The `offset` parameter skips rows from the front of the result set and defaults to 0.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list endpoint returns its rows sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope described in §2.2.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every resource in this system carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Alongside it, most resources carry three further system-managed fields:

- `created_at` (string) — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, set when the row is first written.
- `updated_at` (string) — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the resource.
- `version` (integer) — a counter that increments by one on every mutation of the resource.

Departments, primal purchases, cutting tests, cutting-test lines, retail items, case plans, case-plan lines, trim lots, grind runs, grind inputs, case failures, pre-orders and shrink reconciliations all carry the full set. **Temperature readings are the sole exception**: a temperature log carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version`, because a reading is a fact that is never revised.

A mutation is any change to a resource's stored data, and that explicitly includes changes the server makes to *derived* fields on the caller's behalf. When approving a cutting test rewrites a retail item's cost basis, when a fill on one line rolls up into its plan's totals, or when a temperature reading moves a department's posture, the touched resource's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented exactly as if a client had written it directly.

Time in this system comes from a **logical clock**, never from wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to the host's real time. A `StoreAdmin` moves it by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting capability, and every subsequent date and window evaluation — pack dates, sell-by gates, case-failure escalation windows, pickup windows and reconciliation period membership — reads that instant.

### 2.5. Weights, Cents, and Rounding Discipline

Money is always carried in whole cents as an integer where a stored price or dollar total is concerned, and as a four-decimal number where a per-pound cost basis is concerned. Weights are pounds. Percentages and margins are stored as fractions, not as whole-number percents — a 32% target margin is stored as `0.32` — with the single exception of lean percentages (`lean_pct`, `target_lean_pct`, `lean_tolerance_pct`, `blended_lean_pct`), which are stored as percentage points on a 0-to-100 scale, so seventy-three percent lean is `73.0`.

Wherever a value must be rounded, the method is **round half up** unless a rule explicitly names a different method. The scale per family of fields is fixed:

- Pound quantities — `net_weight_lb`, `test_weight_lb`, `yield_weight_lb`, `total_yield_weight_lb`, `unaccounted_lb`, `required_fill_lb`, `actual_fill_lb`, `staged_lb`, `markdown_lb`, `shrink_lb`, `received_lb`, `allocated_lb`, `consumed_lb`, `discarded_lb`, `remaining_lb`, `weight_lb`, `batch_weight_lb`, `expected_movement_lb`, `requested_weight_lb`, `expected_salable_lb`, `physical_salable_lb`, `variance_lb`, `receiving_shrink_lb`, `gross_weight_lb`, and every plan total (`total_expected_movement_lb`, `total_required_fill_lb`, `total_actual_fill_lb`) — carry two decimal places.
- Whole-cent integers — `invoice_cost_cents`, `freight_cost_cents`, `vendor_allowance_cents`, `landed_cost_total_cents`, `allocated_cost_cents`, `unallocated_cost_cents`, `market_value_per_lb_cents`, `provisional_price_per_lb_cents`, `final_price_per_lb_cents`, `active_price_per_lb_cents`, `quoted_price_per_lb_cents`, `markdown_price_per_lb_cents`, `markdown_dollars_cents`, `shrink_dollars_cents`, `deposit_cents`, `deposit_applied_cents`, `deposit_refund_cents` and `deposit_forfeited_cents` — hold no fractional part at all.
- Four-decimal per-pound cost bases and ratios — `landed_cost_per_lb_cents`, `allocated_cost_per_lb_cents`, `cost_per_lb_cents`, `yield_adjusted_cost_per_lb_cents`, `blend_cost_per_lb_cents`, `salable_yield_pct`, `trim_yield_pct`, `loss_pct`, `pricing_yield_pct_snapshot`, `price_variance_pct` and `variance_pct` — carry four decimal places, rounded half up. `price_variance_pct` and `variance_pct` may be negative; so may `variance_lb` and `unaccounted_lb`.
- Lean percentages and Celsius readings — `blended_lean_pct` carries two decimal places; `lean_pct`, `target_lean_pct`, `lean_tolerance_pct`, `reading_c`, `peak_reading_c`, `max_safe_case_temp_c` and `critical_case_temp_c` carry one decimal place.
- Configuration fractions — `target_margin_pct`, `markdown_floor_pct`, `shrink_tolerance_pct` and `deposit_rate` — carry four decimal places.

Two places break from round-half-up and must be implemented exactly as stated. A pre-order deposit is always rounded **up** to the next whole cent. And a shelf price, after all margin arithmetic and rounding is finished, is nudged **up** to the next whole-cent value whose last digit is 9; that psychological adjustment happens strictly after rounding, never before, so `provisional_price_per_lb_cents` and `final_price_per_lb_cents` always end in the digit 9 whenever they are greater than zero.

Where a sum of rounded amounts is required, rounding happens **per component and then the components are summed** — never by summing exact values and rounding once at the end. This applies to the per-line cost allocation of a cutting test, to the per-event accumulation of markdown give-up, and to the per-line retail valuation of discarded pounds in a reconciliation. The two approaches produce different cents, and only the per-component approach is correct here.

### 2.6. The Landed-Cost Ledger and Its Conservation Invariant

A primal purchase is a small ledger. Its `landed_cost_total_cents` is the authoritative cost of the primal; `allocated_cost_cents` is the portion that an approved cutting test has pushed down onto individual subprimal lines; and `unallocated_cost_cents` is the residual, always exactly the total minus the allocated amount. The residual is never allowed to go negative, and `allocated_cost_cents` never exceeds `landed_cost_total_cents`. Before any cutting test is approved, the allocated amount is 0 and the residual carries the entire total.

Conservation must survive rewinds. Because a fresh cutting test on the same primal supersedes whatever test was already submitted or approved, approval is not a one-way door: superseding an approved test resets every one of its lines' `allocated_cost_cents` and `allocated_cost_per_lb_cents` to 0, returns the purchase's `allocated_cost_cents` to 0 and its `unallocated_cost_cents` to the full `landed_cost_total_cents`, and moves the purchase's `status` back to `RECEIVED`. The same discipline governs pounds in the grind schedule: aborting a run returns exactly the pounds it had committed from each trim lot, with zero drift, so that a lot's `received_lb` always equals `allocated_lb` plus `consumed_lb` plus `discarded_lb` plus `remaining_lb`. Deposits obey a comparable identity: once a pre-order has reached a terminal state, `deposit_cents` equals `deposit_applied_cents` plus `deposit_refund_cents` plus `deposit_forfeited_cents`.

### 2.7. Cold-Chain Posture as a Department-Wide Gate

Each department carries a derived `case_posture` with three values: `SALES_OPEN`, `SALES_RESTRICTED` and `SALES_HALTED`. Posture is never set directly by a client; it is recomputed from the department's temperature-reading history and its open case-failure counts, and it gates merchandising operations across the whole department rather than on a single case or item. While the posture is `SALES_HALTED`, adding a line to a case plan, publishing a plan, resuming a suspended plan, filling a case line and starting a grind run are all refused with `ERR_POSTURE_SALES_HALTED` (HTTP 409). While the posture is `SALES_RESTRICTED`, those operations proceed, but a newly created case-plan line's required fill is cut in half — see §3.7.

Because posture changes cascade, a halt is not merely a gate on future writes. It reaches into already-published plans, into the filled lines inside them, and into the customer orders staged against those lines, suspending and holding each in turn. §3.8 narrates the full cascade; §2.9 explains why the plan's fill accounting is untouched by it.

### 2.8. Country-of-Origin Segregation and the Re-Grind Prohibition

Country-of-origin labelling is a compliance obligation, not a merchandising preference. Every primal purchase declares a `country_of_origin` drawn from `USA`, `CAN`, `MEX` or `AUS`, and that declaration flows to every cut and every trim lot derived from the primal. A grind run declares the segregation block it runs under in `declared_coo`, which may be `USA`, `CAN`, `MEX`, `AUS` or `MULTIPLE`, and the label actually derived from the run's trim inputs (`coo_label`) must agree with that declaration before the run may complete. Two grind runs whose `declared_coo` values differ may not sit back-to-back on the grinder without a sanitation gap, and a retail item's printed `country_of_origin_label` may be `PENDING`, `USA`, `CAN`, `MEX`, `AUS` or `MULTIPLE`.

Re-work is permitted once and only once. A trim lot harvested directly from a cutting-test line carries `rework_generation` 0. A lot created by re-working an existing lot carries the source lot's generation plus one. Because the department's re-grind limit is one generation, re-working a lot that is itself already a re-work is prohibited outright and is rejected with `ERR_PROHIBITED_REGRIND` (HTTP 422); consequently `rework_generation` never exceeds 1. Re-worked trim is treated as ground product for dating purposes regardless of what it was cut from.

### 2.9. Two-Dimensional Plan State and Derived-Field Reconciliation

A case plan is unusual in carrying **two independent state dimensions**. Its `plan_status` (`DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED`, `SUSPENDED`, `CLOSED`) is the publication dimension, driven by manager actions and by the posture cascade. Its `fill_status` (`UNFILLED`, `PARTIAL`, `FILLED`) is the execution dimension, derived purely from `total_actual_fill_lb` against `total_required_fill_lb`. Neither dimension constrains the other: suspending a published plan holds its lines but leaves `fill_status` exactly where it was, and a plan reaching `FILLED` says nothing about whether it has been closed.

Derived fields are reconciled immediately, in the same request that causes them to move, and they cascade transitively. A fill on a line rewrites the line's `actual_fill_lb`, then the plan's `total_actual_fill_lb`, then the plan's `fill_status`. Approving a cutting test rewrites its lines' allocations, then the purchase's ledger and `status`, then each affected retail item's cost basis and final price, then the `price_stale` flag on published case-plan lines and the owning plans' `price_stale_line_count`. Each hop is a real mutation on a real resource and refreshes that resource's `updated_at` and `version` per §2.4. There is no deferred or batched settlement anywhere in this system.

### 2.10. Version Pinning and Stale-Parent Rejection

Two write operations rewind or overwrite state that a client may have read some time earlier, and both therefore require the client to prove which version of the parent it was looking at.

Creating a cutting test requires `expected_primal_purchase_version` as a non-optional field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target primal purchase. If that value does not equal the purchase's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The check cannot be replaced by a state precondition, because a new test legitimately supersedes an existing submitted or approved test and rewinds the purchase's cost ledger; the client must demonstrate that its supersede decision was based on the purchase it actually saw.

Activating a price requires `expected_retail_item_version` as a non-optional field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the retail item. A mismatch with the item's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Activation overwrites `active_price_per_lb_cents` and `price_variance_pct` from whichever cost basis the client evaluated, and a stale view must not be allowed to publish a price computed from a cost basis that has since been superseded.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field, and none performs a version check. Where a write is not listed above, the server simply applies it.

### 2.11. Path Resolution and Validation Precedence

When a request path carries an identifier, the server resolves it before doing anything else, and where a path carries several identifiers it resolves them strictly left to right, failing on the first one that does not exist. The 404 error names the entity that failed to resolve: `ERR_DEPARTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRIMAL_PURCHASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUTTING_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUTTING_TEST_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RETAIL_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_PLAN_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRIM_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GRIND_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GRIND_INPUT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEMPERATURE_LOG_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_FAILURE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_SHRINK_RECONCILIATION_NOT_FOUND`. The same catalogue applies to foreign-key identifiers supplied in a request body: `department_id`, `primal_purchase_id`, `cutting_test_id`, `cutting_test_line_id`, `retail_item_id`, `target_retail_item_id`, `case_plan_id`, `staged_case_plan_line_id`, `fill_case_plan_line_id`, `trim_lot_id`, `source_trim_lot_id`, `grind_run_id` and `trigger_temperature_log_id` each resolve to their own not-found error.

Validation is not a set of independent checks. It is a **strictly ordered ladder**, evaluated in ascending order, and the *first* rung a request violates determines the response. A request that would fail three checks reports only the lowest-numbered one; a later, more dramatic-sounding failure never masks an earlier one. The ladder is:

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. The caller's role is authorized for the endpoint — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. Every identifier in the request path resolves, left to right — otherwise the matching not-found error (404). Applies to every endpoint with a path identifier.
4. The request body conforms to the endpoint's input schema — required fields present, enum members recognized, numeric ranges honoured, dates and datetimes well-formed ISO 8601, `scheduled_end_at` strictly after `scheduled_start_at`, `pickup_window_end_at` strictly after `pickup_window_start_at`, `period_end_date` not earlier than `period_start_date`, exactly one of a trim lot's `cutting_test_line_id` and `source_trim_lot_id` supplied, `lean_pct` supplied only on a `GRIND_TRIM` cutting-test line, and `retail_item_id` supplied only on a `SALABLE_CUT` cutting-test line — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every write endpoint.
5. Every foreign-key identifier in the request body resolves — otherwise the matching not-found error (404). Applies to every write endpoint carrying foreign keys.
6. The requested `action` is permitted for the caller's role per §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_TRANSITION_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every action-driven transition endpoint.
7. The supplied `expected_primal_purchase_version` or `expected_retail_item_version` equals the parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (412). Applies to cutting-test creation and to price activation.
8. The requested transition is a declared transition out of the resource's current state — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409). Applies to every action-driven transition endpoint and to every sub-resource write endpoint.
9. A cost-basis-from-cutting-test price activation names a cutting test whose `status` is `APPROVED` — otherwise `ERR_SOURCE_TEST_NOT_APPROVED` (409). Applies to price activation.
10. A cost-basis-from-grind-blend price activation finds a `COMPLETED` grind run for the item — otherwise `ERR_COST_BASIS_UNAVAILABLE` (409). Applies to price activation.
11. The referenced retail item's `price_status` is one of `PROPOSED`, `PRICED` or `REPRICE_REQUIRED` — otherwise `ERR_ITEM_NOT_PRICED` (409). Applies to case-plan line creation, to the plan `PUBLISH` action, to the pre-order `CONFIRM` action and to the grind-run `START` action.
12. The owning case plan's `plan_status` is `PUBLISHED` — otherwise `ERR_CASE_PLAN_NOT_PUBLISHED` (409). Applies to case-line fills, markdowns and shrink events.
13. The grind run's `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` — otherwise `ERR_GRIND_RUN_NOT_OPEN` (409). Applies to grind-input creation.
14. The trim lot's `status` is `AVAILABLE` or `PARTIALLY_COMMITTED` — otherwise `ERR_TRIM_LOT_NOT_AVAILABLE` (409). Applies to grind-input creation, to trim-lot creation when a re-work source is named, and to trim-lot transitions.
15. The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the operator who performed the segregated prior step — the cutting test's `submitted_by_user_id` on `APPROVE` and `REJECT`, the case failure's `reported_by_user_id` on `RESOLVE` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
16. The department's `case_posture` is not `SALES_HALTED` — otherwise `ERR_POSTURE_SALES_HALTED` (409). Applies to case-plan line creation, to the plan `PUBLISH` and `RESUME` actions, to case-line fills and to the grind-run `START` action.
17. The case line's `sell_by_date` is not earlier than the logical clock's current date — otherwise `ERR_SELL_BY_EXPIRED` (422). Applies to the pre-order `STAGE` action and to case-line markdowns.
18. The named source trim lot's `rework_generation` is below the one-generation re-grind limit — otherwise `ERR_PROHIBITED_REGRIND` (422). Applies to trim-lot creation.
19. The grind run's derived `coo_label` equals its `declared_coo` — otherwise `ERR_COO_LABEL_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to the grind-run `COMPLETE` action.
20. Neither the case line's `line_status` nor the pre-order's `status` is `HELD` — otherwise `ERR_LINE_ON_HOLD` (409). Applies to case-line markdowns and to the pre-order `STAGE` and `PICK_UP` actions.
21. Every entity referenced in one request belongs to the same department — otherwise `ERR_DEPARTMENT_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to case-plan line creation, grind-run creation, grind-input creation, pre-order creation, price proposal, price activation and trim-lot creation.
22. Product classes are compatible — otherwise `ERR_PRODUCT_CLASS_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to grind-run creation, price proposal, price activation and the pre-order `STAGE` action. Concretely: a grind run must target a `GROUND` retail item; a cost-basis-from-cutting-test item must cite a primal purchase whose `product_class` equals the item's own `product_class` (`WHOLE_MUSCLE` or `VAC_PACKED`) on both proposal and activation; a `GROUND` item's provisional proposal may cite any primal purchase of the same department while its activation must cite a `COMPLETED` grind run; and a staged pre-order's case line must carry the ordered item.
23. Scoped uniqueness holds — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` (409). The scoped keys are `store_code` across all departments, `item_code` per department, `lot_code` per department, `service_date` per department, a cutting-test line's `line_seq` per test, a case-plan line's `line_seq` per plan, and a grind input's `input_seq` and `trim_lot_id` each per run. Applies to department creation, retail-item creation, trim-lot creation, case-plan creation, cutting-test line creation, case-plan line creation and grind-input creation.
24. The requested grinder window does not overlap another `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` run in the department — otherwise `ERR_GRINDER_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to grind-run creation.
25. A country-of-origin changeover between neighbouring grinder windows leaves at least the department's sanitation buffer of idle time — otherwise `ERR_SANITATION_BUFFER_REQUIRED` (409). Applies to grind-run creation.
26. Confirming a pre-order does not exceed the department's pickup-slot capacity — otherwise `ERR_PICKUP_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to the pre-order `CONFIRM` action.
27. A pickup happens inside the closed pickup window and a forfeiture only after it has closed — otherwise `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422). Applies to the pre-order `PICK_UP` and `FORFEIT` actions.
28. The cutting test's unaccounted pounds are within tolerance and its `test_weight_lb` equals the purchase's `net_weight_lb` — otherwise `ERR_YIELD_NOT_RECONCILED` (422). Applies to cutting-test creation and to the `SUBMIT` action.
29. The cutting test has at least one line whose `disposition` is `SALABLE_CUT` — otherwise `ERR_NO_SALABLE_YIELD` (422). Applies to the cutting-test `SUBMIT` action.
30. The revision or amendment budget is not exhausted — otherwise `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (422). Applies to the cutting-test `REVISE` action and the pre-order `AMEND` action.
31. The grind run's blended lean is within its tolerance of the target — otherwise `ERR_LEAN_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE` (422). Applies to the grind-run `COMPLETE` action.
32. The purchase's gross weight exceeds its receiving shrink — otherwise `ERR_NET_WEIGHT_NOT_POSITIVE` (422). Applies to primal-purchase creation.
33. No case plan of the department inside the reconciled period sits in `plan_status` `SUSPENDED` — otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_HAS_SUSPENDED_PLAN` (422). Applies to the reconciliation `POST` action.
34. No case-plan line of the plan is still in `line_status` `PLANNED` — otherwise `ERR_CASE_PLAN_HAS_UNFILLED_LINES` (422). Applies to the plan `CLOSE` action.
35. A trim lot with committed pounds may not be discarded — otherwise `ERR_TRIM_LOT_COMMITTED` (422). Applies to the trim-lot `DISCARD` action.
36. The pounds drawn into a grind run do not exceed the lot's `remaining_lb` — otherwise `ERR_TRIM_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Applies to grind-input creation.
37. The markdown price is at or above the department's yield-adjusted markdown floor — otherwise `ERR_MARKDOWN_BELOW_FLOOR` (422). Applies to case-line markdowns.
38. The fill does not push `actual_fill_lb` above the overage cap on `required_fill_lb` — otherwise `ERR_FILL_EXCEEDS_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to case-line fills.
39. Pounds requested against a case line are actually available, meaning `markdown_lb`, `shrink_lb` and staged pounds together never exceed `actual_fill_lb` — otherwise `ERR_LINE_POUNDS_UNAVAILABLE` (422). Applies to case-line markdowns, case-line shrink events and the pre-order `STAGE` action.
40. A reconciliation whose variance falls outside tolerance carries an `override_reason` — otherwise `ERR_SHRINK_EXCEEDS_TOLERANCE` (422). Applies to the reconciliation `POST` action.
41. The cutting test's value-bearing lines carry a positive market-value weight base — otherwise `ERR_NO_MARKET_VALUE_BASIS` (422). Applies to the cutting-test `SUBMIT` and `APPROVE` actions.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Provisioning a Department, Its Catalogue, and Receiving Primals

A `StoreAdmin` stands up a meat department by supplying a display `name` of 1 to 120 characters and a `store_code` of 2 to 16 uppercase characters that must be unique across every department in the system. Everything else is optional configuration, and each optional field has a default that the department behaves with until somebody overrides it. The margin target `target_margin_pct` defaults to `0.32` and must lie between 0 and 0.60 inclusive. Shelf lives are configured per merchandising class: `shelf_life_days_ground` defaults to 2 days over a 1-to-10 range, `shelf_life_days_whole_muscle` defaults to 5 days over a 1-to-20 range, and `shelf_life_days_vac_packed` defaults to 14 days over a 1-to-45 range. The cold-chain thresholds are `max_safe_case_temp_c`, defaulting to 4.4 degrees Celsius and required to be at least 0.0 and strictly below `critical_case_temp_c`, which defaults to 7.0 degrees Celsius and must sit strictly above the safe ceiling. `pickup_slot_capacity` defaults to 2 over a 1-to-10 range, `grind_sanitation_buffer_min` defaults to 30 minutes over a 0-to-240 range, `markdown_floor_pct` defaults to `0.10` over a 0-to-0.50 range, `shrink_tolerance_pct` defaults to `0.015` and must be greater than 0 and no greater than 0.25, and `deposit_rate` defaults to `0.25` over a 0-to-1.0 inclusive range. A department is born operating normally: `case_posture` is `SALES_OPEN`, and `open_case_failure_count`, `contained_case_failure_count` and `consecutive_out_of_spec_readings` are all 0.

Seeding the case catalogue is shared between a `StoreAdmin` and a `MeatManager`. A retail item names its owning department, an `item_code` of 2 to 24 characters that must be unique within that department, the `subprimal_code` of 1 to 60 characters that names the cut or blend it is made from, and a merchandising `product_class` of `GROUND`, `WHOLE_MUSCLE` or `VAC_PACKED`. An item may carry its own `target_margin_pct` override in the same 0-to-0.60 range; when it is absent, the department's margin target applies instead. The merchandising class immediately determines where the item's cost basis will come from: `cost_basis_source` is set to `CUT_TEST_LINE` for a `WHOLE_MUSCLE` or `VAC_PACKED` item and to `GRIND_BLEND` for a `GROUND` item. A new item is `UNPRICED` with `country_of_origin_label` `PENDING`, and `pricing_yield_pct_snapshot`, `yield_adjusted_cost_per_lb_cents`, `provisional_price_per_lb_cents`, `final_price_per_lb_cents`, `active_price_per_lb_cents`, `price_variance_pct` and `shelf_life_days` all sit at 0.

Receiving is where the cost enters the system, and either a `MeatCutter` or a `MeatManager` may record it. A primal purchase names its `department_id`, the vendor's `primal_code` — one of `BEEF_RIBEYE_LIP_ON`, `BEEF_STRIP_LOIN`, `PORK_LOIN_BONELESS` or `BEEF_CHUCK_ROLL` — the packaging `product_class` of `WHOLE_MUSCLE` or `VAC_PACKED`, the `country_of_origin` of `USA`, `CAN`, `MEX` or `AUS`, the `vendor_name` as printed on the invoice (1 to 120 characters), the invoiced `gross_weight_lb` which must be greater than 0, the extended `invoice_cost_cents` which must be greater than 0, and the `received_at` receiving timestamp. Three fields are optional and default to 0: `freight_cost_cents` and `vendor_allowance_cents`, both of which must be at least 0, and `receiving_shrink_lb`, the pounds of purge and packaging discarded on the dock, which must also be at least 0.

Landing the cost is arithmetic done once, at receipt. The usable weight `net_weight_lb` is the gross weight less the receiving shrink; if that difference is not strictly positive the receipt is refused with `ERR_NET_WEIGHT_NOT_POSITIVE`. The total `landed_cost_total_cents` is the invoice cost plus the freight charge minus the vendor allowance. Dividing that total by the net weight and rounding half up to four decimal places gives `landed_cost_per_lb_cents`, the cost of a usable pound before any yield measurement. The ledger opens with `allocated_cost_cents` at 0 and `unallocated_cost_cents` equal to the full landed total, and the purchase's `status` begins at `RECEIVED`.

### 3.2. The Cutting Test Bench: Yield Lines, Metrics, and the Revision Cycle

Only a `MeatCutter` opens a cutting test, and only against a primal purchase that has already been received. The request names the `primal_purchase_id`, the `test_weight_lb` actually placed on the bench, and the version pin `expected_primal_purchase_version` described in §2.10. The bench weight is not free: it must equal the purchase's `net_weight_lb` exactly, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_YIELD_NOT_RECONCILED`. A test opens in `DRAFT`. Because a fresh test supersedes whatever came before it, opening a second test against the same purchase drives any existing test in `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED` straight to `SUPERSEDED`, zeroes that superseded test's line allocations, rewinds the purchase's ledger to `RECEIVED` per §2.6, and increments the purchase's `version` by one. Once a purchase has reached `RECONCILED` it is read-only and no further cutting test may be opened against it; the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Yield lines are added to a test while it is still `DRAFT`; adding a line to a test in any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Each line carries a `line_seq` between 1 and 40 that must be unique within the test and that pins the deterministic order in which cost is later allocated, a `subprimal_code` of 1 to 60 characters naming the cut or by-product, a `disposition` of `SALABLE_CUT`, `GRIND_TRIM` or `FAT_BONE_LOSS`, the `yield_weight_lb` read off the bench scale (at least 0), and a `market_value_per_lb_cents` that serves as the cost-allocation weight. The market value must be exactly 0 on a `FAT_BONE_LOSS` line and strictly greater than 0 on any other line; violating that shape is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Two further fields are conditional: `lean_pct` — between 50.0 and 99.0 with one decimal place — is required on a `GRIND_TRIM` line and forbidden elsewhere, and `retail_item_id` is required on a `SALABLE_CUT` line, naming the item that cut will price, and forbidden elsewhere.

Every time a line lands, the test's yield metrics are recomputed. `total_yield_weight_lb` is the sum of `yield_weight_lb` across all lines of the test regardless of disposition. `unaccounted_lb` is the bench weight minus that total, rounded half up to two decimal places, and it may be negative when the lines overweigh the bench. The three percentages each divide a disposition's pounds by `test_weight_lb` and round half up to four decimal places: `salable_yield_pct` from the `SALABLE_CUT` pounds, `trim_yield_pct` from the `GRIND_TRIM` pounds, and `loss_pct` from the `FAT_BONE_LOSS` pounds. Because lines become immutable the moment the test leaves `DRAFT`, no state filter is applied to this aggregation — every line of the test participates.

Submission is the cutter's assertion that the bench reconciles, and it is gated four ways. The test must have at least one line at all; it must have at least one line whose `disposition` is `SALABLE_CUT`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_NO_SALABLE_YIELD`; the magnitude of `unaccounted_lb` must not exceed five hundredths of a pound, or the submission is refused with `ERR_YIELD_NOT_RECONCILED`; and every line whose disposition is `SALABLE_CUT` or `GRIND_TRIM` must carry a `market_value_per_lb_cents` greater than 0, or the submission is refused with `ERR_NO_MARKET_VALUE_BASIS`. These do not fire in the order narrated here — they fire in the ladder order of §2.11, so the yield-reconciliation check at rung 28 is evaluated before the salable-yield check at rung 29, which is evaluated before the market-value-basis check at rung 41. An entirely empty test therefore surfaces whichever of those rungs it violates first. A successful submission stamps `submitted_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID` and re-runs the yield metrics, moving the test `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`.

Rejection and revision close a bounded loop. A `MeatManager` reviewing a submitted test may reject it, which requires a `rejection_reason` of 1 to 280 characters and requires that the manager's `X-User-ID` differ from the recorded `submitted_by_user_id`; a self-review is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Rejection stores the reason, moves the test `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, and leaves every line's `allocated_cost_cents` at 0 — no cost is ever pushed down by a rejection. The cutter may then revise, which returns the test `REJECTED → DRAFT`, increments `revision_count` by one, and clears both `rejection_reason` and `submitted_by_user_id` so the next submission captures a fresh submitter. The revision budget is two: revising is permitted only while `revision_count` is below 2, and the third attempt is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. Consequently `revision_count` never exceeds 2, and the `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → REJECTED → DRAFT` cycle can run at most twice. A submitted test may also be superseded directly, moving `SUBMITTED → SUPERSEDED` and incrementing the purchase's `version`, when a newer test is opened against the same purchase; `SUPERSEDED` is terminal.

### 3.3. Approval, Cost Allocation, and the Repricing Cascade

Approval is the single most consequential operation in the system, and it belongs to a `MeatManager` alone. Two preconditions guard it: the approving operator's `X-User-ID` must differ from the test's `submitted_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; and the parent purchase's `status` must still be `RECEIVED`. Approval stamps `approved_by_user_id`, moves the test `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, and then runs the cost allocation.

Allocation distributes the purchase's `landed_cost_total_cents` across the test's value-bearing lines in proportion to the market value they represent. The candidate set is every line whose `disposition` is `SALABLE_CUT` or `GRIND_TRIM`, taken in ascending `line_seq` order; `FAT_BONE_LOSS` lines are excluded from the weight base entirely and keep `allocated_cost_cents` at 0. Each candidate's value weight is its `market_value_per_lb_cents` multiplied by its `yield_weight_lb`, and the weight base is the sum of those weights over all candidates. If that base is zero the approval is refused with `ERR_NO_MARKET_VALUE_BASIS`. Otherwise, walking the candidates in ascending `line_seq` order, each line's `allocated_cost_cents` is the landed total multiplied by that line's weight, divided by the weight base, and rounded half up to a whole cent. Rounding happens **per line**, not once over a summed total, so the rounded allocations will not generally add up to the landed total; the difference — the landed total minus the sum of the per-line rounded allocations, which may be positive or negative — is a remainder that is assigned in full to exactly one line. That line is the candidate with the greatest `yield_weight_lb`, and when two candidates tie on weight the one with the lower `line_seq` takes the remainder. Neither `id` nor `created_at` is ever used to break that tie. Finally, every line's `allocated_cost_per_lb_cents` is its allocated cents divided by its `yield_weight_lb`, rounded half up to four decimal places, except that a line with zero pounds keeps a per-pound cost of 0.

From there the approval cascades four hops deep, and every hop is a real mutation that must land. The first hop recomputes the purchase's `allocated_cost_cents` as the sum of the approved test's line allocations and its `unallocated_cost_cents` as the landed total minus that sum, and moves the purchase `RECEIVED → CUT_TESTED`. The second hop reaches each `SALABLE_CUT` line's referenced retail item and rewrites that item's `yield_adjusted_cost_per_lb_cents` from the line's freshly computed `allocated_cost_per_lb_cents` — this is the moment a book-yield price becomes a measured-yield price. The third hop looks at each such item whose `price_status` is `PRICED` and recomputes its `final_price_per_lb_cents` under the final-price rules of §3.4; when the recomputed price differs from the currently charged `active_price_per_lb_cents` by more than two percent of that charged price, the item moves `PRICED → REPRICE_REQUIRED`, keeping the stale price live at the scale while flagging that the shelf tag is wrong. The fourth hop reaches every case-plan line of a `PUBLISHED` case plan that references an item just moved to `REPRICE_REQUIRED`, sets that line's `price_stale` flag to `true`, and increments the owning plan's `price_stale_line_count`.

Superseding an approved test — the `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED` move — unwinds all of that. Every line of the superseded test has `allocated_cost_cents` and `allocated_cost_per_lb_cents` reset to 0, the purchase's `allocated_cost_cents` returns to 0 and its `unallocated_cost_cents` returns to the full `landed_cost_total_cents`, the purchase moves `CUT_TESTED → RECEIVED`, and its `version` is incremented by one. That `CUT_TESTED → RECEIVED → CUT_TESTED` loop is the purchase-side cost-ledger cycle, and the residual must land on exactly the value it started from every time it is traversed. The purchase's only other exit is `CUT_TESTED → RECONCILED`, which happens when a reconciliation is posted for the same department over a period whose inclusive date range contains the calendar date of the purchase's `received_at`.

### 3.4. Shelf Pricing from Provisional Proposal to Final Activation

Pricing runs in two stages by design, because the department needs a shelf tag on day one and the measured cost basis does not exist until the bench work is approved. A `PricingAnalyst` opens the first stage by proposing a provisional price against a named `primal_purchase_id`. The cited purchase must belong to the item's own department, or the proposal is refused with `ERR_DEPARTMENT_MISMATCH`, and its packaging class must be compatible with the item's merchandising class, or the proposal is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_CLASS_MISMATCH`. Compatibility here is asymmetric: a `WHOLE_MUSCLE` or `VAC_PACKED` item must cite a purchase whose `product_class` matches the item's exactly, whereas a `GROUND` item — whose measured basis only ever arrives from a completed grind blend — may cite any purchase of its department, which is precisely what makes a ground item eligible for a grind run in the first place.

The provisional cost basis deliberately ignores this primal's measured performance. It is the purchase's `invoice_cost_cents` divided by its `net_weight_lb` — freight and vendor allowance excluded — then divided by the standard book salable yield of sixty-two hundredths, rounded half up to four decimal places. The item's `pricing_yield_pct_snapshot` records that book yield of `0.62`, and `shelf_life_days` is copied from whichever department shelf-life field matches the item's merchandising class: the ground shelf life for a `GROUND` item, the whole-muscle shelf life for a `WHOLE_MUSCLE` item, the vac-packed shelf life for a `VAC_PACKED` item. Pricing then runs and the result lands in `provisional_price_per_lb_cents`, is copied into `active_price_per_lb_cents`, and moves the item `UNPRICED → PROPOSED`.

The pricing arithmetic itself deserves care, because the margin tier depends on the price while the price depends on the margin, and the evaluation order is pinned to **exactly one pass**. The base margin is the item's own `target_margin_pct` when it is present and the department's `target_margin_pct` otherwise. A first-pass price is the cost basis divided by one minus that base margin, rounded half up to a whole cent. That first-pass price selects a margin bump and nothing else: below eight dollars per pound — that is, under 800 cents — the bump is zero; from 800 cents up to but not including 1600 cents the bump is two hundredths; at 1600 cents or above the bump is four hundredths. The effective margin is the base margin plus that bump, capped at sixty hundredths. A second-pass price is then the same cost basis divided by one minus the effective margin, again rounded half up to a whole cent. The tier is **never** re-evaluated against the second-pass price. Finally the shelf price is the second-pass price advanced upward to the nearest whole-cent value whose last digit is 9: if the second-pass price already ends in 9 it stands unchanged, otherwise it is increased by however many cents are needed to reach the next value ending in 9. That adjustment is applied strictly after rounding, never before.

Activation is the second stage, it belongs to a `MeatManager`, and it carries the version pin `expected_retail_item_version` of §2.10. The request may name a `cutting_test_id` or a `grind_run_id` as the cost-basis source. For an item whose `cost_basis_source` is `CUT_TEST_LINE`, the named cutting test's `status` must be `APPROVED`, or the activation is refused with `ERR_SOURCE_TEST_NOT_APPROVED`. For an item whose `cost_basis_source` is `GRIND_BLEND`, a `COMPLETED` grind run targeting the item must exist, or the activation is refused with `ERR_COST_BASIS_UNAVAILABLE`. The same department and product-class compatibility checks that guarded the proposal guard the activation too.

Activation replaces book assumptions with measurements. For a cut-test-based item, `pricing_yield_pct_snapshot` is overwritten with the approved test's `salable_yield_pct`; for a grind-blend item it stays at 0. The final cost basis is the item's `yield_adjusted_cost_per_lb_cents` — either the approved cutting-test line's `allocated_cost_per_lb_cents`, which already carries freight, allowance and this primal's measured yield, or the completed grind run's `blend_cost_per_lb_cents`. The same one-pass tiered arithmetic runs over that basis, and the result lands in `final_price_per_lb_cents` and is copied into `active_price_per_lb_cents`. `price_variance_pct` records the final price minus the provisional price divided by the provisional price, rounded half up to four decimal places, and is 0 when no provisional price was ever set. The item's `country_of_origin_label` is set from the cost-basis source, its `version` is incremented by one, and it moves `PROPOSED → PRICED`. Activation also clears the staleness flags it may have inherited: every case-plan line referencing the item has `price_stale` set back to `false` and the owning plan's `price_stale_line_count` is decremented.

An item flagged `REPRICE_REQUIRED` is repaired the same way. A second activation moves it `REPRICE_REQUIRED → PRICED`, lets `active_price_per_lb_cents` catch up to the already-refreshed `final_price_per_lb_cents`, clears `price_stale` on every case-plan line referencing the item while decrementing that plan's `price_stale_line_count`, and increments the item's `version` by one. Separately, a `MeatManager` may suspend an item from either `PROPOSED` or `PRICED` (`PROPOSED → SUSPENDED` or `PRICED → SUSPENDED`), provided no pre-order referencing that item currently sits in `STAGED`; a `SUSPENDED` item may not be referenced by a new case-plan line or a new pre-order, and such attempts are refused with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_PRICED`. Reinstating moves the item `SUSPENDED → PRICED` and restores `active_price_per_lb_cents` from `final_price_per_lb_cents`, which requires that `final_price_per_lb_cents` be greater than 0. An item has no terminal pricing state; it can cycle through repricing and suspension indefinitely.

### 3.5. Trim Lots, Re-Work, and the Grind Schedule

Grind trim harvested from the bench becomes a trim lot, created by a `MeatCutter` in the lot's `department_id` with a `lot_code` of 2 to 24 characters unique within that department, a `received_lb` greater than 0, and a `pack_date`. Exactly one of two sources must be named: `cutting_test_line_id`, pointing at the `GRIND_TRIM` line the trim came off, or `source_trim_lot_id`, pointing at the parent lot being re-worked. Supplying both, or neither, is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The lot inherits three attributes from whichever source it names — `country_of_origin`, `lean_pct` and `cost_per_lb_cents` — where a lot from a cutting-test line takes the country of origin of the purchase behind that line, the line's own `lean_pct`, and the line's `allocated_cost_per_lb_cents` as its cost per pound, while a re-worked lot copies all three from its parent lot. `rework_generation` is 0 for a lot off a cutting-test line and one more than the parent's generation for a re-worked lot; because the re-grind limit is a single generation, naming a source lot whose `rework_generation` is already 1 is refused with `ERR_PROHIBITED_REGRIND`. A named source lot must also be `AVAILABLE` or `PARTIALLY_COMMITTED`, or the creation is refused with `ERR_TRIM_LOT_NOT_AVAILABLE`. The lot's `sell_by_date` is its `pack_date` plus the department's `shelf_life_days_ground`, because re-worked trim is dated as ground product no matter what it was cut from. A new lot is `AVAILABLE` with `remaining_lb` equal to `received_lb` and `allocated_lb`, `consumed_lb` and `discarded_lb` all at 0.

The lot's four pound buckets are a closed ledger: `received_lb` always equals `allocated_lb` plus `consumed_lb` plus `discarded_lb` plus `remaining_lb`. Committing pounds to a run moves them from `remaining_lb` into `allocated_lb`; completing that run moves them from `allocated_lb` into `consumed_lb`; aborting it moves them back out of `allocated_lb` into `remaining_lb`. Status tracks the commitment: a lot goes `AVAILABLE → PARTIALLY_COMMITTED` when a commitment takes strictly less than its remaining pounds, `AVAILABLE → FULLY_COMMITTED` or `PARTIALLY_COMMITTED → FULLY_COMMITTED` when a commitment takes exactly its remaining pounds and drives `remaining_lb` to 0. Restoring pounds runs the same path backwards: an abort takes a lot `FULLY_COMMITTED → PARTIALLY_COMMITTED` when the restored pounds still leave it partly committed, and `PARTIALLY_COMMITTED → AVAILABLE` when restoring returns `allocated_lb` to 0, at which point `remaining_lb` is exactly `received_lb` minus `consumed_lb` minus `discarded_lb`. Completion consumes: a lot goes `FULLY_COMMITTED → CONSUMED` when the completing run holds an input on it and its `remaining_lb` is 0, and stays `PARTIALLY_COMMITTED → PARTIALLY_COMMITTED` when pounds remain after the run's pounds are consumed. A `MeatCutter` may also write a lot off entirely, moving it `AVAILABLE → DISCARDED` or `PARTIALLY_COMMITTED → DISCARDED`, which moves `remaining_lb` into `discarded_lb` and drives `remaining_lb` to 0; a lot with any committed pounds — that is, `allocated_lb` above 0 — may not be discarded and the attempt is refused with `ERR_TRIM_LOT_COMMITTED`. `CONSUMED` and `DISCARDED` are both terminal.

Scheduling the grinder is shared between a `MeatCutter` and a `MeatManager`. A run names its `department_id`, the `target_retail_item_id` it produces — which must be a `GROUND` item of the same department — the segregation block `declared_coo`, the occupation window `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at` where the end must fall strictly after the start, and the `target_lean_pct` the finished blend must hit, between 60.0 and 95.0 with one decimal place. Two fields are optional: `lean_tolerance_pct`, the allowed absolute deviation at completion, which defaults to 2.0 and must be greater than 0.0 and no greater than 10.0; and `fill_case_plan_line_id`, naming the case line the finished batch will fill on completion. Grinder windows are **half-open** on the start instant and exclusive of the end instant, so a run ending at the exact instant another begins does not overlap it; any true overlap with another `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` run in the department is refused with `ERR_GRINDER_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Segregation adds a second temporal rule: when the nearest neighbouring `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` run in the department carries a different `declared_coo`, the idle gap between the two windows must be at least the department's `grind_sanitation_buffer_min` minutes, with a gap exactly equal to the buffer accepted; a shorter changeover gap is refused with `ERR_SANITATION_BUFFER_REQUIRED`. A scheduled run starts with `status` `SCHEDULED`, `coo_label` `PENDING`, and `batch_weight_lb`, `blended_lean_pct` and `blend_cost_per_lb_cents` all at 0.

Trim is committed into a run by a `MeatCutter`, naming the `trim_lot_id`, an `input_seq` between 1 and 20 that must be unique within the run, and the `weight_lb` drawn from the lot, which must be greater than 0. A lot may appear at most once per run — a repeated `trim_lot_id`, like a repeated `input_seq`, is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The run must still be open, meaning its `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, or the request is refused with `ERR_GRIND_RUN_NOT_OPEN`; the lot must be `AVAILABLE` or `PARTIALLY_COMMITTED`, or refused with `ERR_TRIM_LOT_NOT_AVAILABLE`; the lot and the run must belong to the same department, or refused with `ERR_DEPARTMENT_MISMATCH`; and the pounds drawn must not exceed the lot's `remaining_lb`, or refused with `ERR_TRIM_INSUFFICIENT`.

Every committed input re-derives the blend, over all of the run's inputs in ascending `input_seq` order and never over any other run's inputs. `batch_weight_lb` is the sum of the inputs' `weight_lb`. `blended_lean_pct` is the sum over inputs of pounds multiplied by that input's lot `lean_pct`, divided by the batch weight, rounded half up to two decimal places, and it is 0 when the batch weight is 0. `blend_cost_per_lb_cents` is the sum over inputs of pounds multiplied by that input's lot `cost_per_lb_cents`, divided by the batch weight, rounded half up to four decimal places, and it too is 0 on an empty batch. `coo_label` is `PENDING` when the run has no inputs, the single shared value when every input lot carries the same `country_of_origin`, and `MULTIPLE` when two or more distinct countries are represented.

Starting a run is a `MeatCutter` action taking it `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS`. It requires that the department's `case_posture` not be `SALES_HALTED`, that `batch_weight_lb` be greater than 0, and that the target item's `price_status` be one of `PROPOSED`, `PRICED` or `REPRICE_REQUIRED` — a `UNPRICED` or `SUSPENDED` target is refused with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_PRICED`. Starting stamps `started_by_user_id` and refreshes the blend derivation.

From `IN_PROGRESS` the run forks, and the two branches have deliberately **disjoint** side-effect sets. Completing it — the `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` move, again a `MeatCutter` action — requires that the absolute difference between `blended_lean_pct` and `target_lean_pct` not exceed `lean_tolerance_pct`, or the completion is refused with `ERR_LEAN_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE`, and that `coo_label` equal `declared_coo` exactly, or refused with `ERR_COO_LABEL_MISMATCH`. A completion stamps `completed_at` from the logical clock; moves each input lot's run pounds from `allocated_lb` into `consumed_lb` and sets that input's `consumed` flag to `true`; overwrites the target item's `yield_adjusted_cost_per_lb_cents` with `blend_cost_per_lb_cents` and its `country_of_origin_label` with `coo_label`; recomputes the target item's final price when its `price_status` is `PRICED` and moves it to `REPRICE_REQUIRED` when that price moves by more than two percent of the charged price, which in turn flags the published case-plan lines referencing it exactly as the approval cascade does; and, when `fill_case_plan_line_id` was supplied, records `batch_weight_lb` as a fill on that case line, dating it with `pack_date` and `sell_by_date` if this is the line's first fill. Aborting instead — a `MeatManager` action available as either `IN_PROGRESS → ABORTED` or `SCHEDULED → ABORTED` and requiring an `abort_reason` of 1 to 280 characters — moves every input lot's run pounds out of `allocated_lb` back into `remaining_lb`, leaves each input's `consumed` flag at `false`, and applies **none** of the cost, price, label or fill effects of the completion branch. `COMPLETED` and `ABORTED` are both terminal, so a consumed input never reverts.

### 3.6. The Service-Day Case Plan: Facings, Fills, Markdowns, and Shrink

A `MeatManager` owns the case plan end to end. A plan is created for a `department_id` and a `service_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form that must be unique within the department; it opens with `plan_status` `DRAFT` and `fill_status` `UNFILLED`, the two dimensions of §2.9 that thereafter move independently.

Each plan line names the `retail_item_id` it merchandises, a `line_seq` between 1 and 60 unique within the plan, the `facings` allotted to the item between 1 and 12, and the forecast `expected_movement_lb` for the day, which must be greater than 0. The item must belong to the plan's department, or the line is refused with `ERR_DEPARTMENT_MISMATCH`, and its `price_status` must be `PROPOSED` or `PRICED` — an `UNPRICED` or `SUSPENDED` item is refused with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_PRICED`. Creating a line while the department's `case_posture` is `SALES_HALTED` is refused with `ERR_POSTURE_SALES_HALTED`.

Required fill is derived from four inputs and resolved once, at line creation. Presentation pounds are the `facings` multiplied by a per-facing capacity that depends on the item's merchandising class: four pounds a facing for a `GROUND` item, six pounds a facing for a `WHOLE_MUSCLE` item, eight pounds a facing for a `VAC_PACKED` item. Demand pounds are the `expected_movement_lb` multiplied by one and fifteen hundredths, the forecast safety uplift. Carrying-capacity pounds are the `expected_movement_lb` multiplied by the item's `shelf_life_days` and then by three tenths, which caps how much product the shelf life can carry. The base is the larger of the presentation pounds and the smaller of the demand pounds and the carrying-capacity pounds. When the department's posture at line-creation time is `SALES_OPEN`, `required_fill_lb` is that base rounded half up to two decimal places. When the posture is `SALES_RESTRICTED`, the base is rounded half up to two decimal places, then halved, then rounded half up to two decimal places again. When the posture is `SALES_HALTED` the line is refused outright. A posture change *after* the line exists never retroactively rewrites `required_fill_lb`. Every new line refreshes the plan's `total_expected_movement_lb` and `total_required_fill_lb` as the sums of its lines' `expected_movement_lb` and `required_fill_lb`.

Publishing a plan takes it `DRAFT → PUBLISHED`, requires at least one line, requires that the department's posture not be `SALES_HALTED`, and requires that every line's retail item sit in `PROPOSED` or `PRICED`. It stamps `published_by_user_id`, recomputes `total_expected_movement_lb` and `total_required_fill_lb`, and leaves `fill_status` alone on its own dimension — a freshly published plan is still `UNFILLED` until pounds arrive. Closing a plan takes it `PUBLISHED → CLOSED` and requires that no line still sit in `line_status` `PLANNED`; an unfilled line blocks the close with `ERR_CASE_PLAN_HAS_UNFILLED_LINES`. A closed plan is terminal on the publication dimension and becomes eligible for reconciliation scope. Suspension and resumption are covered in §3.8, since in practice they are driven by the cold chain.

Filling is a `MeatCutter` action carrying a single `filled_lb`. The owning plan must be `PUBLISHED`, or the fill is refused with `ERR_CASE_PLAN_NOT_PUBLISHED`; the department must not be `SALES_HALTED`; and the running `actual_fill_lb` plus the new pounds must not exceed `required_fill_lb` multiplied by one and twenty-five hundredths, the overage cap that allows a quarter more than the model asked for and no more — exceeding it is refused with `ERR_FILL_EXCEEDS_REQUIRED`. A fill adds its pounds to `actual_fill_lb`. On the **first** fill only, `pack_date` is stamped with the logical clock's current date and `sell_by_date` is set to that pack date plus the item's `shelf_life_days`, which is itself the department shelf life matching the item's merchandising class. A first fill takes the line `PLANNED → FILLED`; later fills keep it `FILLED → FILLED`. Either way the plan's `total_actual_fill_lb` is recomputed and `fill_status` follows from it: the plan moves `UNFILLED → PARTIAL` while total actual pounds are above 0 but still below total required pounds, and `PARTIAL → FILLED` once total actual pounds reach or exceed total required pounds.

Marking down is a `PricingAnalyst` action carrying the pounds to discount in `markdown_lb` and the discounted price in `markdown_price_per_lb_cents`. The plan must be `PUBLISHED`; the line's `line_status` must be `FILLED` — a line sitting in `HELD` is refused with `ERR_LINE_ON_HOLD`; the line's `sell_by_date` must not be earlier than the logical clock's current date, or the markdown is refused with `ERR_SELL_BY_EXPIRED`; and the accumulated `markdown_lb` plus the requested pounds must not exceed `actual_fill_lb` minus `staged_lb`, with the broader rule that `markdown_lb`, `shrink_lb` and staged pounds together never exceed `actual_fill_lb` — a request past that limit is refused with `ERR_LINE_POUNDS_UNAVAILABLE`. The price floor protects cost: the floor in cents is the item's `yield_adjusted_cost_per_lb_cents` multiplied by one plus the department's `markdown_floor_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent, and a `markdown_price_per_lb_cents` strictly below that floor is refused with `ERR_MARKDOWN_BELOW_FLOOR` while a price exactly at the floor is accepted. An accepted markdown adds its pounds to the line's cumulative `markdown_lb`, stores the event's price in `markdown_price_per_lb_cents`, and adds to the cumulative `markdown_dollars_cents` the give-up of this event: the pounds marked down in this event multiplied by the item's `active_price_per_lb_cents` minus the event's markdown price, rounded half up to a whole cent. That rounding is applied **per event** and never to the running total. A first markdown takes the line `FILLED → MARKED_DOWN`; a deeper markdown keeps it `MARKED_DOWN → MARKED_DOWN`.

Writing off discarded pounds is a `MeatManager` action carrying `shrink_lb`. The plan must be `PUBLISHED`, and the same pounds-availability rule applies in full: the accumulated `shrink_lb` plus the requested pounds must not exceed `actual_fill_lb` minus `staged_lb`, and the broader rule of §2.11 — that `markdown_lb`, `shrink_lb` and staged pounds together never exceed `actual_fill_lb` — binds a write-off too, so pounds already marked down are not available to write off. A request past either limit is refused with `ERR_LINE_POUNDS_UNAVAILABLE`. A partial write-off — one where the accumulated `shrink_lb` plus the requested pounds is strictly less than `actual_fill_lb` minus `staged_lb` — adds to `shrink_lb` and leaves the line's status where it was, `FILLED → FILLED` or `MARKED_DOWN → MARKED_DOWN`. A write-off that exactly empties the line — where accumulated `shrink_lb` plus requested pounds equals `actual_fill_lb` minus `staged_lb` — takes the line `FILLED → SHRUNK_OUT` or `MARKED_DOWN → SHRUNK_OUT`, which is terminal. That shrink-out boundary is measured against `actual_fill_lb` minus `staged_lb` alone and never deducts `markdown_lb`, so a line already carrying marked-down pounds is held below the boundary by the broader rule and stays where it was. Shrink events never touch `actual_fill_lb`, so they never move the plan's `fill_status` by way of the fill total; the plan's fill dimension is recomputed on a shrink-out but reads the same `total_actual_fill_lb` it read before.

### 3.7. Cold-Chain Compliance and Case-Failure Response

Temperature readings are posted by a `MeatCutter` or a `ServiceClerk`, naming the `department_id`, the `case_code` of the display case read (1 to 24 characters), and the `reading_c` in Celsius between -10.0 and 15.0 with one decimal place. Posting stamps `taken_at` from the logical clock and `recorded_by_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID`. A reading is out of spec when `reading_c` is **strictly above** the department's `max_safe_case_temp_c`; a reading exactly at the safe ceiling is in spec. The `out_of_spec` flag records that verdict.

Readings are applied in `created_at` ascending order, and they maintain a run counter. An in-spec reading resets the department's `consecutive_out_of_spec_readings` to 0. An out-of-spec reading raises it by one but never above 2, the consecutive-failure threshold. Whichever value results is copied onto the reading itself as `consecutive_index`, so a reading carries the run length as it stood immediately after that reading was applied.

A new case failure opens on either of two triggers: a single reading at or above the department's `critical_case_temp_c` — a spike, evaluated inclusively — or the second consecutive out-of-spec reading, meaning `consecutive_out_of_spec_readings` has reached 2. When a trigger fires and a `CONTAINED` failure already exists on the same `case_code`, that existing failure is **reopened** rather than duplicated: it returns `CONTAINED → OPEN`, its `reopen_count` is incremented by one, and its `peak_reading_c` is refreshed to the highest `reading_c` observed on the case while the failure has been open. Otherwise a new failure is created in `OPEN`, recording the `trigger_temperature_log_id` of the reading that opened it, copying that reading's `case_code`, stamping `opened_at` from the logical clock, capturing `reported_by_user_id` from the posting clerk, and recording `peak_reading_c`. Every new failure starts with `reopen_count` and `affected_line_count` at 0 and `disposition` `NONE`.

Posture then follows mechanically from the counters. The department is `SALES_HALTED` whenever `open_case_failure_count` is 1 or more, where that counter tallies failures in `OPEN` **or** `ESCALATED`. Failing that, it is `SALES_RESTRICTED` when `contained_case_failure_count` — failures in `CONTAINED` — is 1 or more, or when `consecutive_out_of_spec_readings` is 1 or more. Only when none of those hold is the department `SALES_OPEN`.

The moment a reading drives the posture to `SALES_HALTED`, the halt cascades three hops. Every case plan of the department whose `plan_status` is `PUBLISHED` and whose `service_date` is not earlier than the logical clock's current date moves `PUBLISHED → SUSPENDED`. Inside each suspended plan, every line whose `line_status` is `FILLED` or `MARKED_DOWN` records its current status in `held_from_status` and moves to `HELD` — that is, `FILLED → HELD` or `MARKED_DOWN → HELD` — and the triggering failure's `affected_line_count` is incremented for each line so held. Finally, every pre-order in `STAGED` whose `staged_case_plan_line_id` points at one of those held lines moves `STAGED → HELD` and is not pickable while held; an attempt is refused with `ERR_LINE_ON_HOLD`. Crucially, none of these hops touches the plan's `fill_status` — the execution dimension is preserved exactly as it stood, so that the pounds already in the case remain accounted for.

A `MeatManager` may also suspend a published plan by hand, provided its `service_date` is not earlier than the logical clock's current date, and the same line-hold and pre-order-hold hops run. Resumption reverses them: a manager resuming a suspended plan takes it `SUSPENDED → PUBLISHED`, provided the department's posture is not `SALES_HALTED`, and every `HELD` line returns to whatever `held_from_status` recorded — `HELD → FILLED` when it recorded `FILLED`, `HELD → MARKED_DOWN` when it recorded `MARKED_DOWN` — clearing `held_from_status` as it goes, while every held pre-order staged on those lines returns `HELD → STAGED` and becomes pickable again. Neither the suspend hop nor the resume hop moves `fill_status`.

Failure response is a `MeatCutter`-then-`MeatManager` sequence. Containment is the cutter's job: it requires a `containment_note` of 1 to 280 characters, moves the failure `OPEN → CONTAINED`, stamps `contained_at` from the logical clock, decreases `open_case_failure_count` by one and increases `contained_case_failure_count` by one, and recomputes posture — which typically means the department relaxes from `SALES_HALTED` to `SALES_RESTRICTED` once no other failure is open. Should another out-of-spec reading land on the same `case_code`, the failure reopens as described above, `open_case_failure_count` rises again, `contained_case_failure_count` falls, and the department returns to `SALES_HALTED`; that `OPEN → CONTAINED → OPEN` loop is unbounded and each traversal re-halts the department. A manager may instead escalate an open failure, moving it `OPEN → ESCALATED`, but only once the logical clock has reached or passed four hours after `opened_at`; escalating earlier is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Escalation stamps `escalated_at` and leaves the department `SALES_HALTED`, because an escalated failure still counts as open.

Resolution belongs to a `MeatManager` and closes the failure from either `CONTAINED` or `ESCALATED`, as `CONTAINED → RESOLVED` or `ESCALATED → RESOLVED`. It requires a `disposition` of `NONE`, `MARKDOWN` or `DISCARD` recording what happened to the product, and it requires that the resolving operator's `X-User-ID` differ from the `reported_by_user_id` captured when the failure opened; a self-resolution is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Resolution stamps `resolved_at` and `resolved_by_user_id`. Resolving from `CONTAINED` decreases `contained_case_failure_count` by one and returns the department to `SALES_OPEN` only when no failure remains and `consecutive_out_of_spec_readings` is 0; resolving from `ESCALATED` decreases `open_case_failure_count` by one and recomputes posture the same way. `RESOLVED` is terminal.

### 3.8. Special Orders, Holiday Roasts, and Deposit Settlement

A special order is taken by a `ServiceClerk` or a `MeatManager`, naming the `department_id`, the `retail_item_id` ordered, the `customer_name` of 1 to 120 characters, the `customer_phone` of 7 to 20 characters, the `requested_weight_lb` reserved which must be greater than 0, and the promised pickup window as `pickup_window_start_at` and `pickup_window_end_at`, where the end must fall strictly after the start. Unlike a grinder window, a pickup window is a **closed** interval: both endpoints belong to it. The item must belong to the same department, or the order is refused with `ERR_DEPARTMENT_MISMATCH`. A new order sits in `REQUESTED` with `quoted_price_per_lb_cents`, `pinned_retail_item_version`, `deposit_cents`, `deposit_applied_cents`, `deposit_refund_cents`, `deposit_forfeited_cents` and `amend_count` all at 0.

Confirmation is a `ServiceClerk` action taking the order `REQUESTED → CONFIRMED`. The ordered item's `price_status` must be `PROPOSED`, `PRICED` or `REPRICE_REQUIRED`, or the confirmation is refused with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_PRICED`. Capacity is also checked: the count of already `CONFIRMED` or `STAGED` pre-orders in the department whose own closed pickup interval intersects the requested interval must be strictly below the department's `pickup_slot_capacity`, and reaching that ceiling is refused with `ERR_PICKUP_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Confirmation pins the quote so later repricing cannot move it: `quoted_price_per_lb_cents` takes the item's `active_price_per_lb_cents` as of that instant and `pinned_retail_item_version` takes the item's `version`. It then collects the deposit, which is the pinned per-pound quote multiplied by `requested_weight_lb` to give the extended price, multiplied by the department's `deposit_rate`, and rounded **up** to the next whole cent — never half up.

Amending releases the pin. A `ServiceClerk` may take a confirmed order `CONFIRMED → REQUESTED`, which increments `amend_count` by one and returns `quoted_price_per_lb_cents`, `pinned_retail_item_version` and `deposit_cents` all to 0 so the next confirmation re-quotes from current prices. The amendment budget is two: amending is permitted only while `amend_count` is below 2, and the third attempt is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. `amend_count` therefore never exceeds 2, and the `REQUESTED → CONFIRMED → REQUESTED` cycle runs at most twice.

Staging reserves physical pounds and is a `MeatCutter` action taking the order `CONFIRMED → STAGED`. It requires a `staged_case_plan_line_id` naming a case line whose `line_status` is `FILLED` or `MARKED_DOWN`; a line in `HELD` is refused with `ERR_LINE_ON_HOLD`. That line's retail item must be the item this order names, or the staging is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_CLASS_MISMATCH`. The pounds must actually be there: `requested_weight_lb` must not exceed the line's `actual_fill_lb` minus its `staged_lb` minus its `markdown_lb` minus its `shrink_lb`, and a shortfall is refused with `ERR_LINE_POUNDS_UNAVAILABLE`. The line must also still be saleable — its `sell_by_date` must not be earlier than the logical clock's current date, or the staging is refused with `ERR_SELL_BY_EXPIRED`. Staging adds `requested_weight_lb` to the line's `staged_lb`, which is what makes those pounds unavailable to markdowns and shrink events.

Pickup is a `ServiceClerk` action taking the order `STAGED → PICKED_UP`, permitted only while the logical clock sits inside the closed pickup window, endpoints included; outside it the pickup is refused with `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Pickup sets `deposit_applied_cents` to the full `deposit_cents`, leaving refund and forfeiture at 0, and releases `requested_weight_lb` from **both** the line's `staged_lb` and its `actual_fill_lb`. Because the pounds leave the fill total, a pickup can drag the owning plan back down the fill dimension: the plan moves `FILLED → PARTIAL` when the departing pounds leave `total_actual_fill_lb` below `total_required_fill_lb`.

Cancellation is available to a `ServiceClerk` as `REQUESTED → CANCELLED`, `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED` or `STAGED → CANCELLED` — never from `PICKED_UP` — and the deposit split hinges on the pickup window. Cancelling strictly before `pickup_window_start_at` refunds the entire `deposit_cents` into `deposit_refund_cents` and leaves `deposit_forfeited_cents` at 0. Cancelling at or after that instant refunds half: `deposit_refund_cents` is `deposit_cents` multiplied by one half and rounded half up to a whole cent, and `deposit_forfeited_cents` takes the residual, the deposit minus that refund. Cancelling from `REQUESTED` leaves both at 0 because no deposit was ever collected. Cancelling from `STAGED` additionally releases `requested_weight_lb` from the line's `staged_lb`. Forfeiture is the manager's remedy for a no-show: a `MeatManager` may take a staged order `STAGED → FORFEITED` only after the logical clock has passed `pickup_window_end_at` — attempting it while the window is still open is refused with `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_CLOSED` — which moves the whole `deposit_cents` into `deposit_forfeited_cents`, leaves `deposit_refund_cents` at 0, and releases `requested_weight_lb` from the line's `staged_lb`. `PICKED_UP`, `CANCELLED` and `FORFEITED` are all terminal, and in each the deposit identity of §2.6 must hold.

### 3.9. Period Reconciliation of Shrink and Markdown

Reconciliation closes the loop back to the yield model. A `PricingAnalyst` drafts one for a `department_id` over an inclusive period bounded by `period_start_date` and `period_end_date`, where the end date must not be earlier than the start date, and selects a `basis` of either `YIELD_MODEL` or `PHYSICAL_COUNT`. The row is computed immediately and lands in `DRAFT`.

Scope is the same for both bases. It is every case plan of the department whose `plan_status` is `PUBLISHED` or `CLOSED` and whose `service_date` falls inside the inclusive period, and then every case-plan line of those plans, taken in ascending plan `service_date` order and, within a plan, ascending `line_seq` order. Plans in `DRAFT` are excluded, and plans in `SUSPENDED` are excluded too — although in practice a suspended plan inside the period blocks the posting outright rather than being quietly skipped.

What the two bases disagree about is which pounds count as expected and which count as physical, and on identical line data they produce different answers. Under the `YIELD_MODEL` basis, `expected_salable_lb` is the sum of `required_fill_lb` over the scope lines and `physical_salable_lb` is the sum of `actual_fill_lb` — the plan is being measured against what the yield model said the case needed, and neither markdowns nor discards are deducted from the physical side. Under the `PHYSICAL_COUNT` basis, `expected_salable_lb` is the sum of `actual_fill_lb` and `physical_salable_lb` is the sum, over the scope lines, of `actual_fill_lb` minus `markdown_lb` minus `shrink_lb` — what actually stayed salable measured against what was put in the case, with the `required_fill_lb` model ignored entirely.

From there the arithmetic is common. `variance_lb` is `physical_salable_lb` minus `expected_salable_lb`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and it may be negative. `variance_pct` is `variance_lb` divided by `expected_salable_lb`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and is 0 when `expected_salable_lb` is 0; it too may be negative. `within_tolerance` is `true` exactly when the absolute value of `variance_pct` is no greater than the department's `shrink_tolerance_pct` — a variance exactly at the tolerance is inside it. The row also carries two dollar figures: `markdown_dollars_cents` is the sum of each scope line's `markdown_dollars_cents`, and `shrink_dollars_cents` is the retail value of the discarded pounds, computed by multiplying each scope line's `shrink_lb` by its item's `active_price_per_lb_cents` and rounding half up to a whole cent **per line**, then summing those per-line values.

Posting is a `MeatManager` action taking the row `DRAFT → POSTED`. Two gates guard it. No case plan of the department whose `service_date` falls inside the period may sit in `plan_status` `SUSPENDED`, or the posting is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_HAS_SUSPENDED_PLAN` — the department must settle its cold-chain business before it settles its books. And `within_tolerance` must be `true`, unless the manager supplies an `override_reason` of 1 to 280 characters explaining the excursion; posting an out-of-tolerance row with no override is refused with `ERR_SHRINK_EXCEEDS_TOLERANCE`. Posting stamps `posted_by_user_id` and, as its cross-entity effect, moves every primal purchase of the department whose `status` is `CUT_TESTED` and whose `received_at` calendar date falls inside the inclusive period to `RECONCILED`, closing those purchases to further cutting tests.

Disputes reopen the books. A `PricingAnalyst` may take a posted row `POSTED → DISPUTED` by supplying a `dispute_reason` of 1 to 280 characters, which freezes the row's figures pending a redraft. The same analyst may then redraft it, taking the row `DISPUTED → DRAFT`, which clears `posted_by_user_id` and recomputes every figure — `expected_salable_lb`, `physical_salable_lb`, `variance_lb`, `variance_pct`, `within_tolerance`, `markdown_dollars_cents` and `shrink_dollars_cents` — from current line data under the row's stored `basis`. Reconciliation has no terminal state; the `DRAFT → POSTED → DISPUTED → DRAFT` cycle may run as often as the department needs.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is mounted under the `/meat-ops` prefix, with the single exception of the test-harness clock control, which is mounted at the server root. All endpoints require the `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` headers described in §2.1.

Endpoints that create a new resource respond with HTTP 201 and return the created resource in the `data` field. This includes the sub-resource creations that record an event against an existing parent — adding a cutting-test line, proposing or activating a price, adding a case-plan line, recording a fill, a markdown or a shrink event, and committing a grind input — because each of those brings a new row into being. Endpoints that drive a state machine, and the clock control, respond with HTTP 200 and return the updated resource. Every transition endpoint takes an `action` field naming the transition to perform, together with whatever fields that particular action requires as described in §3; an `action` that is not a legal move out of the resource's current state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409), and an `action` that is legal for the state but not permitted to the caller's role is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).

All list endpoints accept the `limit` and `offset` pagination parameters of §2.3 together with optional filters. Each filter is named exactly after the field it filters and matches it exactly: `status`, `price_status`, `plan_status`, `fill_status`, `line_status`, `disposition`, `basis`, `case_code`, `out_of_spec`, `price_stale`, `consumed`, `department_id`, `retail_item_id`, `cutting_test_id`, `case_plan_id`, `source_trim_lot_id`, `cutting_test_line_id`, `target_retail_item_id`, `fill_case_plan_line_id`, `grind_run_id`, `trim_lot_id`, `staged_case_plan_line_id` and `trigger_temperature_log_id`. Filters combine conjunctively, and an omitted filter imposes no restriction.
