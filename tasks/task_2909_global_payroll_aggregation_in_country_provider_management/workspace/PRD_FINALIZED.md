# Global Payroll Aggregation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A multinational employer that pays people in a dozen countries does not run one payroll; it runs a dozen legally distinct payrolls that must nevertheless close on one calendar, reconcile to one treasury position, and survive one audit. Each country brings its own in-country provider, its own statutory filing obligations, its own data-residency law, and its own definition of what a "material" gross-to-net movement looks like. The operational failure mode is always the same: a country's numbers arrive late or wrong, nobody notices until the funding has already moved, and the statutory deadline passes while the client and the provider argue about who owns the penalty.

This service is the aggregation layer that makes that estate governable. It holds the country-by-country picture — which provider serves which country, under what service scope, with what filing responsibility and what data-residency terms — and it drives each pay period through a single consolidated lifecycle: open the cycle, resolve the input cutoff against local holidays, standardize the HR extract, score the provider's gross-to-net results against the prior period, fund the requirement in the treasury's currency, lodge and confirm the statutory filings, and only then close the period with a complete evidence file. Every step that can go wrong is instrumented: service-level incidents deduct from a provider's score, a crossed score floor puts the provider on probation or suspends it and freezes the draft cycles that depend on it, and a missed filing accrues a penalty that is split between provider and client against a contractual indemnity cap.

The design constraints are as important as the features. Money never moves on one person's authority, and the person who supplied a country's payroll inputs is never the person who signs off on its results. Payroll personal data never crosses a residency boundary that either the country or the provider has not agreed to. Every amount is carried in whole minor currency units so that no cent is ever invented by a floating-point conversion. And because several of these figures are conserved aggregates — the treasury commitment of an engagement, the count of live filing obligations of a country — every reversal must return them exactly to the value they held before the action that changed them. The API is mounted under `/payroll`, with a single administrative logical-clock endpoint outside that prefix so that temporal behaviour can be driven deterministically.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint, including read-only ones, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting under. If either header is absent or empty, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs. If both headers are present but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being called, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Authentication is checked before authorization, and both are checked before the existence of any referenced resource. Header names are matched case-insensitively, as HTTP requires; the role value itself is case-sensitive and must be spelled exactly as listed below.

Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `PlatformAdmin`, `GlobalPayrollOps`, `PayrollController`, `TreasuryOfficer`, `ProviderUser`, and `ComplianceAuditor`.

`PlatformAdmin` registers the reference data of the payroll estate — country profiles, providers, engagements, local bank accounts — and adjudicates provider transitions. It is deliberately **not** an override role: a `PlatformAdmin` cannot submit or approve payroll results, cannot move money, and cannot decide an engagement's residency terms. `GlobalPayrollOps` runs the payroll calendar: it opens cycles, submits and validates HR extracts, locks inputs, cancels cycles, lodges client-responsible filings and sweeps for late obligations. `PayrollController` owns review and governance: it approves or returns gross-to-net results, closes cycles, manages service-level incidents, decides penalty disputes and writes provider transition plans; it may not approve or release funding. `TreasuryOfficer` coordinates funding, and because approval and release are the two halves of a dual-authorization control, two distinct `TreasuryOfficer` principals are always required before money moves. `ProviderUser` is the in-country provider itself: it submits gross-to-net results, lodges provider-responsible filings and disputes penalty shares. `ComplianceAuditor` owns payroll data privacy: it is the only role that may decide an engagement's residency terms or record the manual residency attestation, and it is read-only everywhere else.

Five of the six roles carry an opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `GlobalPayrollOps`, `PayrollController`, `TreasuryOfficer`, or `ComplianceAuditor`, the `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it — for example as `input_submitter_user_id` on a payroll cycle, as `approver_user_id` or `releaser_user_id` on a funding instruction, or as `recorded_by_user_id` on an audit evidence item — and it may compare two such recorded identifiers with each other for segregation-of-duties purposes, but it never compares them to any entity's `id`.

`ProviderUser` is the exception. When `X-User-Role` is `ProviderUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting provider entity; ownership checks on any resource that references a provider compare that resource's provider foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, when a `ProviderUser` submits or resubmits gross-to-net results for a cycle, the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `provider_id` of that cycle's engagement, and any other value is rejected with `ERR_PROVIDER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). The same ownership rule governs a `ProviderUser` lodging a filing or disputing a penalty share: it may only act on filings whose cycle belongs to an engagement whose `provider_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. When `limit` is omitted it defaults to 10; the smallest accepted value is 1 and the largest accepted value is 100. When `offset` is omitted it defaults to 0, and it may not be negative. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

Unless a subsection states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, so the oldest resource appears first and pagination is stable across calls. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` as they were applied, `total_count` giving the number of rows that match the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more`, which is true exactly when the sum of `offset` and the number of rows returned in this page is less than `total_count`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply an `id`. Timestamps are ISO 8601 UTC instants rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z`. Ten of the eleven resource kinds — country profiles, providers, engagements, payroll cycles, HR input files, gross-to-net runs, local bank accounts, funding instructions, statutory filings and service-level incidents — carry the full common property set: `created_at`, set once at creation; `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation of the row; and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Audit evidence items are the single exception: because they are append-only records of what happened, an audit evidence item carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and neither field appears in its representation.

Fields that hold a calendar date rather than an instant — a cycle's `period_start_date`, `period_end_date`, `pay_date`, a funding instruction's `value_date`, a transition plan's cutover dates, and the entries of a country's `local_holidays` — are plain `YYYY-MM-DD` strings with no time component. Wherever a date range is described in this document it is half-open, meaning the start date is included and the end date is excluded, unless the text explicitly says the bound is inclusive.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Every temporal rule in this specification — input cutoff enforcement, statutory due dates, filing sweeps, the rolling window of the provider score, and every server-stamped instant such as `activated_at`, `submitted_at`, `filed_at` and `occurred_at` comparisons — reads a single **logical clock** rather than the host wall clock. Before the clock has ever been set, it is fixed at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, so a client that never touches it still observes fully deterministic temporal behaviour. `POST /_clock/set_current_utc` accepts a `to_iso8601` instant and moves the clock to that value, where it remains until the next call. The endpoint is available to `PlatformAdmin`, `GlobalPayrollOps`, `PayrollController`, `TreasuryOfficer` and `ComplianceAuditor`, returns HTTP 200, and produces no resource payload of its own. Setting the clock does not retroactively recompute any stored value; it only changes what subsequent requests observe as "now".

### 2.6. Minor Units, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in **minor currency units** — cents, pence, or whatever the currency's smallest unit is — and every field whose name ends in `_minor` is such an integer. Amounts denominated in the payroll country's own currency are said to be in *local* minor units; amounts denominated in the currency the treasury funds from are in *funding-currency* minor units. The two are never mixed in one field, and a field's owning narrative always says which of the two it carries.

Percentage-bearing fields — `materiality_pct`, `indemnity_cap_pct`, `service_score`, `variance_pct`, `headcount_drift_pct`, `weight_points` — carry two decimal places. Exchange rates carry six decimal places. Wherever this document says a value is rounded, the rounding method is **half up**: a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero. Rounded percentages are rounded to two decimal places; rounded money amounts are rounded to a whole minor unit. Two rules about *when* rounding happens matter more than the method and are restated at each formula that depends on them: per-employee amounts used in variance normalization are exact rationals with no intermediate rounding, and each component of a currency conversion is converted and rounded individually before the components are summed.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency, because each of them overwrites state that the caller must have observed before deciding to write.

The first guards gross-to-net submission against a stale payroll cycle. `POST /payroll/gross-to-net-runs` requires a non-optional `expected_cycle_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target payroll cycle. If that value does not equal the cycle's current stored `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing at all is written — no run is created, no earlier run is superseded, and the cycle is untouched. A matching request succeeds and increments the cycle's `version` by 1, so any later request built on the pre-submission read is itself rejected. `POST /payroll/gross-to-net-runs/{id}/resubmit` is guarded by the same `expected_cycle_version` field and the same `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) rejection, and likewise increments the cycle's `version` by 1 on success. The reason is that creating a run supersedes every earlier run of the cycle and rewrites the cycle's result totals; a caller working from an older read would be discarding results it never saw.

The second guards transition planning against a stale engagement. `POST /payroll/engagements/{id}/transition-plan` requires a non-optional `expected_engagement_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the engagement. If it does not equal the engagement's current stored `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ENGAGEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no plan field changes. A matching request succeeds and increments the engagement's `version` by 1, so a subsequent plan built on the pre-plan read is rejected. A transition plan overwrites all four of the engagement's pending-plan fields at once, which is precisely what must not be done blindly.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field. In particular, cycle locking, review, funding approval, funding release, filing, confirmation, penalty disputes, incident handling and transition decisions carry no expected-version input, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.8. Validation Order and Error Precedence

Where several rules could reject the same request, exactly one error is returned: the one belonging to the highest-precedence rule that the request violates. The ladder is fixed and applies uniformly across the API.

Before any business rule runs, the request's **shape** is checked. A missing required field, a field of the wrong JSON type, an enum value that is not a declared member of its enumeration, a numeric field outside its declared range, or a date or instant that is not valid ISO 8601 all produce `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). Header authentication (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`) and endpoint role authorization (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`) precede even that.

After the shape gate, business rules are evaluated in this order:

1. **Existence of every referenced entity.** Any path identifier or body foreign key that names no row produces the matching not-found error — `ERR_COUNTRY_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INPUT_FILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GTN_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BANK_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FUNDING_INSTRUCTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FILING_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_SLA_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND` — each with HTTP 404.
2. **Authorization and segregation of duties**, covering provider ownership, the reviewer-versus-submitter rule, the treasury dual-authorization rule and the filing-responsibility actor rule.
3. **Data-residency and service-scope safety.**
4. **Entity state and cross-entity state.**
5. **Temporal windows and cutoffs.**
6. **Referential coherence between two rows that both exist.**
7. **Fiscal caps and aggregate limits.**
8. **Uniqueness and remaining shape constraints.**

So, for example, a request that both violates residency and names a state that forbids the operation reports the residency error, and a request that is both unauthorized for its actor and out of temporal window reports the authorization error. Optimistic-concurrency rejections are evaluated together with the state group: the version check runs after existence and authorization have passed and after the acting role's scope has been established, and before any temporal, referential, fiscal or uniqueness rule of the same request.

### 2.9. Data Residency and Payroll Privacy

Payroll personal data is treated as regulated data with a geographic home. Five residency regions exist: `EU`, `UK`, `US`, `APAC` and `LATAM`. A country profile declares the single region in which its payroll personal data must reside; a provider declares the set of regions it is certified to host data in; an engagement declares the contracted region for that country relationship; a local bank account declares the region its payment data resides in; and each HR extract declares the region it is transferred from and stored in.

The invariant is that all five must agree. An engagement's region must equal the region of its country profile and must also be one of the regions its provider is certified for; a bank account's region must equal the region of its engagement; an extract's transfer region must equal the region of its engagement; and an incoming provider named in a transition plan must be certified for the engagement's region. The first four of those, when violated, produce `ERR_DATA_RESIDENCY_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422). Residency is also the reason the extract's required data-element keys differ by region, and the reason an engagement cannot become operational until a `ComplianceAuditor` has recorded a data-processing-agreement reference against it.

### 2.10. Nested Paths and Sub-Resource Reads

Most collections in this API are peers under the `/payroll` prefix, linked by foreign keys in their payloads rather than by URL nesting. Three read paths are nested — an engagement's local bank accounts, a cycle's HR input files, and a cycle's audit evidence — and a further set of write actions are addressed as sub-paths of the resource they act upon. Wherever a path contains more than one segment that must resolve, the segments are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that names no row produces its own not-found error. A read of the bank accounts of an engagement that does not exist therefore reports `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND` and not an empty list; a read of the audit evidence of a cycle that does not exist reports `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`. Placeholder segments always address the resource named by the collection segment immediately before them.

Every foreign-key relationship in the system is readable from the parent side. Where there is no nested path, the child collection's list endpoint accepts the parent identifier as a filter — cycles are filterable by `engagement_id` and by `prior_cycle_id`, gross-to-net runs by `cycle_id` and `input_file_id`, funding instructions by `cycle_id` and `bank_account_id`, filings by `cycle_id`, incidents by `engagement_id` and `cycle_id`, and engagements by `country_profile_id`, by `provider_id` and by `transition_incoming_provider_id`. Every list endpoint additionally accepts a `status` filter over the enumeration of the resource it lists, and specific lists accept the extra filters named in their own narrative.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties Across the Payroll Chain

Two control points in the chain require two distinct human beings, and both are enforced on `X-User-ID` rather than on role.

The first is the input-to-approval separation. When an HR extract is submitted for a cycle, the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded on the cycle as `input_submitter_user_id`, and it is overwritten by each newly created extract so that it always names whoever supplied the most recent one. When a gross-to-net run of that cycle is later reviewed, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the cycle's `input_submitter_user_id`; if they are equal the review is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Note the consequence: submitting a second extract can move the prohibition onto a different person.

The second is treasury dual authorization. The principal that approves a funding instruction is recorded as `approver_user_id`; the principal that releases it is recorded as `releaser_user_id`. Release requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from `approver_user_id`, and an attempt by the approver to release its own instruction is rejected with `ERR_DUAL_AUTHORIZATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403). Both fields are stamped from the acting header, both belong to the `TreasuryOfficer` role, and no role — `PlatformAdmin` included — can collapse the two halves into one act.

A third, weaker separation is the filing-responsibility rule: the party named by a filing's `responsibility` must be the party that lodges it, which is described with the filing workflow.

### 2.12. Cascades, Conserved Aggregates and Reconciliation

Several figures in this system are **conserved quantities**: they are not free-standing counters but derived aggregates that must, at every observable moment, equal a recomputation from the rows they summarize. Two are stated here because they cut across workflows.

An engagement's `treasury_committed_minor` must always equal the sum of `funded_source_minor` over the funding instructions whose cycle belongs to that engagement and whose status is `RELEASED`, expressed in whole funding-currency minor units with no rounding anywhere in the aggregate. A release adds the instruction's `funded_source_minor`; a recall removes exactly the same amount; instructions in `DRAFT`, `APPROVED` or `VOIDED` never contribute. Releasing and then recalling one instruction must therefore return the engagement to precisely the commitment it had before, and the cycle's `funded_total_minor` must return to its prior value in the same request.

A country profile's `open_filing_obligation_count` must always equal the number of statutory filings whose cycle belongs to an engagement of that country and whose status is `PENDING` or `LATE`. It is a whole count and never negative. Minting obligations when a cycle locks its inputs increases it by the number minted; a filing moving from `PENDING` to `LATE` is count-neutral because both states are live; and a filing reaching `FILED`, `FILED_LATE` or `VOIDED` decreases it by 1. Lodging or voiding every obligation of a country therefore returns the count to its opening value.

Cascades are the other half of this principle. A single request in this system routinely mutates several rows: confirming a service-level incident rescores its provider, which may change the provider's standing, which may push each of that provider's live engagements into a remediation state, which may in turn freeze each of those engagements' draft cycles. Every row touched by such a cascade has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1, exactly as if it had been written directly, and every cascade described in this document runs to completion within the request that triggered it — there is no deferred or eventual settlement.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Estate: Countries, Providers and Local Accounts

Before any payroll can run, a `PlatformAdmin` registers the payroll countries. A country registration carries the `country_code`, which is exactly two uppercase letters following ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 and must be unique across all registered countries — reusing a code that already exists is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_COUNTRY_CODE` (HTTP 409). It carries `local_currency`, exactly three uppercase letters following ISO 4217, in which that country's payroll is denominated. It carries `data_residency_region`, one of `EU`, `UK`, `US`, `APAC` or `LATAM`, fixing where the country's payroll personal data must live. It carries `statutory_filing_deadline_days`, a whole number from 1 through 90 that says how many days after a cycle's pay date each of that country's filings becomes due. It carries the two materiality terms: `materiality_pct`, a percentage with two decimal places from 0.01 through 50.00 against which gross-to-net variance is judged, and `materiality_floor_minor`, an absolute floor in local minor units, at least zero, below which a variance is never treated as material no matter how large the percentage. It carries `penalty_daily_rate_minor`, strictly greater than zero, which is the local-minor-unit late-filing accrual for each late day. And it carries `required_filing_types`, between one and three distinct members of `INCOME_TAX_WITHHOLDING`, `SOCIAL_INSURANCE` and `YEAR_END`, which is the obligation set minted for every cycle of every engagement in that country.

The optional `local_holidays` list completes the picture. It holds between zero and forty distinct `YYYY-MM-DD` dates that are not working days in that country, and it defaults to an empty list when omitted. It exists for one purpose: cutoff arithmetic. A newly registered country starts with `open_filing_obligation_count` at 0; the value is maintained by the system and never supplied by a client. Reading a country profile back returns that live obligation count alongside the registered terms.

Providers are registered next, also by a `PlatformAdmin`. A provider carries its `legal_name`, between 1 and 120 characters. It carries `service_scope`, between one and four distinct members of `GROSS_TO_NET`, `STATUTORY_FILING`, `PAYMENT_DISBURSEMENT` and `ADVISORY`, describing what the provider is contracted to be *able* to deliver anywhere. It carries `data_residency_regions`, between one and five distinct members of the five region values, being the regions in which it is certified to host payroll personal data. And it carries the two indemnity terms: `annual_fee_minor`, strictly positive, the contracted annual fee expressed in EUR minor units, and `indemnity_cap_pct`, a percentage with two decimal places from 0.00 through 100.00 that caps the provider's cumulative penalty exposure as a fraction of that fee. From the declared scope the system derives `statutory_filing_capable`, which is true exactly when `service_scope` contains `STATUTORY_FILING` and false otherwise; clients never set it.

A freshly registered provider opens with `status` `ACTIVE`, a perfect `service_score` of 100.00, `score_variant_applied` set to `ENGAGEMENT_TO_DATE`, and `open_penalty_minor` at 0. Provider standing thereafter moves through `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION`, `SUSPENDED` and the terminal `OFFBOARDED` purely under system control, driven by service scoring and penalty exposure as described later; there is no endpoint that sets a provider's status directly. Reading a provider back exposes its current score, the score variant that produced it, and its open penalty exposure.

Local disbursement accounts are registered per engagement, again by a `PlatformAdmin`, and the payload names the `engagement_id` whose local payments will settle through the account. Each account carries an `account_ref` of 1 to 40 characters that must be unique within its engagement — reusing a reference within the same engagement is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACCOUNT_REF` (HTTP 409) — together with `account_currency`, exactly three uppercase letters, and `residency_region`, one of the five regions. Two coherence rules bind the account to its context: `residency_region` must equal the engagement's contracted residency region, and `account_currency` must equal the `local_currency` of the engagement's country. The residency rule is evaluated first, at the safety rank, and yields `ERR_DATA_RESIDENCY_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422); the currency rule is evaluated afterwards, at the referential-coherence rank, and yields `ERR_CURRENCY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422); the uniqueness of `account_ref` is checked last of the three. An account that offends against residency therefore never reports a currency problem.

Three further terms on the account govern how money may leave it. `daily_cap_minor`, strictly positive, is the maximum aggregate local requirement that may be released against that account for any single value date. `settlement_window_hours`, from 1 through 96, is how long a released instruction occupies the account. `buffer_hours`, from 0 through 48, is a mandatory idle gap that must separate two settlement windows on that account. A newly registered account opens in `status` `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, and no funding may be released from it in that state. Bank verification is a separate `PlatformAdmin` act that moves the account `PENDING_VERIFICATION → VERIFIED` and changes nothing else. The accounts of one engagement are readable as a nested sub-resource list, optionally narrowed by `status`.

### 3.2. Binding a Country to a Provider: Engagement Formation

An **engagement** is the junction row that makes one provider responsible for one country's payroll, and it is where country terms, provider capabilities and contractual choices are reconciled. A `PlatformAdmin` creates it by naming the `country_profile_id` and the `provider_id`, then declaring four contractual terms: the `service_scope` actually in scope for this country relationship, between one and four distinct members of `GROSS_TO_NET`, `STATUTORY_FILING`, `PAYMENT_DISBURSEMENT` and `ADVISORY`; the `filing_responsibility`, either `PROVIDER` or `CLIENT`, naming who must lodge statutory filings; the `data_residency_region`, one of the five regions; and the `pay_frequency`, one of `WEEKLY`, `BIWEEKLY`, `SEMI_MONTHLY` or `MONTHLY`, which constrains the length of every pay period this engagement will ever run.

Four checks guard the creation, and where a request offends against more than one, the earlier check in this list is the one reported. First, at the safety rank, three peer conditions are evaluated in the order given: the declared `data_residency_region` must equal the country profile's own region and must also be a member of the provider's `data_residency_regions`, otherwise `ERR_DATA_RESIDENCY_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422); every member of the engagement's `service_scope` must also be a member of the provider's `service_scope`, otherwise `ERR_SERVICE_SCOPE_NOT_COVERED` (HTTP 422); and if `filing_responsibility` is `PROVIDER`, the provider's `statutory_filing_capable` must be true, otherwise `ERR_FILING_CAPABILITY_MISSING` (HTTP 422). Second, at the state rank, a provider that has reached `OFFBOARDED` can no longer take new work, and naming one is rejected with `ERR_PROVIDER_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Last, at the uniqueness rank, no two engagements may pair the same country with the same provider unless the earlier one was rejected: if an engagement already exists for that `country_profile_id` and `provider_id` in any status other than `REJECTED`, the request is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENGAGEMENT` (HTTP 409).

A new engagement opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `treasury_committed_minor` at 0, `closed_cycle_count` at 0, `transition_required` false, and `activated_at`, `residency_terms_ref`, `transition_incoming_provider_id`, `transition_cutover_start_date`, `transition_cutover_end_date` and `transition_parallel_run_cycles` all null. A `DRAFT` engagement is inert: it cannot host a payroll cycle.

What makes an engagement operational is the **residency decision**, and only a `ComplianceAuditor` may take it. The decision body carries a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT`. On `APPROVE`, the auditor must supply `residency_terms_ref`, a data-processing-agreement reference of 1 to 60 characters, which is persisted on the engagement; the engagement moves `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, `activated_at` is stamped with the current logical-clock instant, and the engaged provider's `service_score` is recomputed — under the `ENGAGEMENT_TO_DATE` variant, because a brand-new engagement has `closed_cycle_count` at 0. On `REJECT`, the auditor must supply a `rejection_note` of 1 to 200 characters; the engagement moves `DRAFT → REJECTED`, `activated_at` stays null, and because `REJECTED` is terminal, no payroll cycle may ever be created for that engagement. Omitting the field the chosen decision requires is a request-shape failure and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). A residency decision attempted on an engagement whose `status` is anything other than `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).

Beyond `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` and `REJECTED`, an engagement's `status` may also be `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, `TRANSITION_PLANNED` or `TRANSITION_IN_PROGRESS`; those three states belong to provider-standing and transition narratives later in this section. Engagements are listable with filters on `country_profile_id`, `provider_id`, `transition_incoming_provider_id` and `status`, and a single engagement read exposes its treasury commitment and any pending transition plan.

### 3.3. Consolidating the Payroll Calendar: Cycles, Cutoffs and Holds

A **payroll cycle** is one pay period of one engagement, and `GlobalPayrollOps` opens it. The request names the `engagement_id`, gives the period a `period_label` of 1 to 20 characters such as a year-month string, and defines the period with `period_start_date` and `period_end_date` — half-open, so the start date is inside the period and the end date is the first date outside it. It supplies the local `pay_date`, on which both cutoff and statutory deadline arithmetic are anchored, and `cutoff_lead_working_days`, a whole number from 1 through 15. It may optionally supply `prior_cycle_id`, naming the cycle of the same engagement that serves as the variance baseline; a first cycle leaves it null.

Six checks guard the opening, and the first violated in this order is the only error returned. The engagement must be in a live state — one of `ACTIVE`, `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, `TRANSITION_PLANNED` or `TRANSITION_IN_PROGRESS` — and anything else, including a `DRAFT` or `REJECTED` engagement, yields `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). Next come the two temporal checks: `pay_date` must not be earlier than `period_start_date` and must not be later than `period_end_date` plus 30 calendar days, otherwise `ERR_PAY_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422); and the new half-open period must not overlap the half-open period of any other cycle of the same engagement whose status is not `CANCELLED`, otherwise `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Two periods that merely touch — one ending on the very date the next starts — do not overlap, because the end date is excluded. Then the referential check on the baseline: `prior_cycle_id`, when supplied, must name a cycle of the same engagement, must not name the cycle being created, and must name a cycle whose `period_end_date` is not after the new cycle's `period_start_date`, so that the baseline is genuinely chronologically prior; any of those three failures yields `ERR_PRIOR_CYCLE_ENGAGEMENT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Finally two constraint checks: the period's length must match the engagement's contracted `pay_frequency`, and the `period_label` must be unique within the engagement, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_PERIOD_LABEL` (HTTP 409).

The frequency band deserves spelling out, because it is counted on the half-open period. A `WEEKLY` engagement's period must be exactly 7 days long. A `BIWEEKLY` engagement's period must be exactly 14 days long. A `SEMI_MONTHLY` engagement's period must be at least 14 and at most 17 days long. A `MONTHLY` engagement's period must be at least 28 and at most 31 days long. A period whose day count falls outside the band for its engagement's frequency is rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_FREQUENCY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).

Opening the cycle resolves `input_cutoff_at`, the instant after which the HR system may no longer feed this period, and the resolution walks the country's working calendar backwards. Begin at the cycle's `pay_date` and set a counter of counted working days to zero. Repeatedly step back one calendar day at a time; each time you land on a day whose ISO weekday is Monday through Friday **and** which is not one of the country's `local_holidays`, increase the counter by one. Stop as soon as the counter equals `cutoff_lead_working_days`. The `input_cutoff_at` is that final landed date at 17:00:00.000Z. Two properties of this walk are load-bearing: the `pay_date` itself is never counted, even when it is an ordinary working day, because the first thing the walk does is step back a day; and `local_holidays` is consulted on every candidate day, so a public holiday sitting inside the walk is skipped without being counted and pushes the cutoff one further working day into the past. The resolved instant always carries millisecond precision and its time-of-day component is always exactly 17:00:00.000Z.

A new cycle opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `gross_total_minor` at 0, `funded_total_minor` at 0, `filings_confirmed_count` at 0, `hold_reason` null and `input_submitter_user_id` null. Over its life the cycle's `status` moves through `ON_HOLD`, `INPUTS_LOCKED`, `RESULTS_APPROVED` and `FUNDED` to the terminal `CLOSED`, or to the terminal `CANCELLED`. Cycles are readable individually — the read exposes the resolved cutoff and every derived total — and listable with filters on `engagement_id`, `prior_cycle_id` and `status`.

**Holds** are how provider trouble reaches the calendar. When the engaged provider's standing degrades, every cycle of the affected engagements that is still in `DRAFT` is moved `DRAFT → ON_HOLD` by the system, and `hold_reason` is set to `PROVIDER_PROBATION` when the provider went on probation or `PROVIDER_SUSPENSION` when the provider was suspended. While a cycle is `ON_HOLD` no HR extract may be created against it and no extract of it may be validated. Recovering the provider does **not** automatically release the hold: a held cycle stays held until `GlobalPayrollOps` or a `PayrollController` explicitly releases it, which moves the cycle `ON_HOLD → DRAFT` and clears `hold_reason` back to null. That release is only permitted while both causes of the hold are healthy again — the engaged provider must be back to `ACTIVE` and the engagement must be back to `ACTIVE` — and an attempt made while either is still unhealthy is rejected with `ERR_HOLD_CAUSE_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). `hold_reason` is null whenever the cycle's status is anything other than `ON_HOLD`.

### 3.4. Standardizing HR Input and Locking the Period

The HR system feeds a cycle through an **input file**: a standardized extract of the period's employee records. Either `GlobalPayrollOps` or a `PayrollController` may submit one. The payload names the `cycle_id` it feeds, declares the `source_system` it came from — one of `HRIS_CORE`, `HRIS_LEGACY` or `MANUAL` — and states the `record_count` of employee records and the `gross_declared_minor`, the gross pay the HR system believes is due, in local minor units and strictly greater than zero. It carries `country_elements`, a map of country-specific data-element keys to string values, and `residency_region_of_transfer`, the region the extract is transferred from and stored in.

Four checks guard submission, again first-violated-wins in this order. The extract's `residency_region_of_transfer` must equal the engagement's contracted residency region, otherwise `ERR_DATA_RESIDENCY_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422) — an extract may not be moved into a region the country relationship has not agreed to. The cycle must be in `DRAFT`: a cycle that is `ON_HOLD`, already locked, funded, closed or cancelled accepts no extract, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The current logical clock must not be strictly after the cycle's resolved `input_cutoff_at`; submitting at exactly the cutoff instant is accepted, and submitting even one millisecond later is rejected with `ERR_INPUT_CUTOFF_PASSED` (HTTP 409). And a `record_count` of zero is rejected with `ERR_EMPTY_INPUT_FILE` (HTTP 422); any accepted extract describes at least one employee record.

A submitted extract opens in `status` `RECEIVED` with `submitted_at` stamped from the logical clock, `rejection_code` null and `validated_record_count` at 0. The submission has one further effect that reaches beyond the extract: the acting `X-User-ID` is written onto the cycle as `input_submitter_user_id`, replacing whatever was there before. That write is a mutation of the cycle row like any other, so it refreshes the cycle's `updated_at` and increases the cycle's `version` by exactly 1; a cycle that has been opened and then fed one accepted extract, with nothing else having touched it, therefore stands at `version` 2. Validating or resubmitting an extract, by contrast, writes nothing to the cycle row and so leaves the cycle's `version` alone. That single assignment fixes which principal may no longer sign off on this cycle's results. The extracts of a cycle are readable as a nested sub-resource list, optionally narrowed by `status`.

Validation is a distinct act reserved to `GlobalPayrollOps`, and it is what decides whether the extract is fit to drive a payroll. It may only be run while the extract is `RECEIVED` and its cycle is `DRAFT`; run from any other extract status, or against a cycle that has left `DRAFT`, it is rejected with `ERR_INPUT_FILE_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The outcome is computed in two strictly ordered stages, and the second stage never runs if the first one fails.

The first stage is the country-element check. The set of keys the extract is required to carry is selected by the engagement's contracted residency region, not by the country code, and the five sets are these. An `EU` engagement requires `tax_id`, `social_insurance_no` and `iban`. A `UK` engagement requires `tax_id`, `ni_number`, `sort_code` and `account_number`. A `US` engagement requires `tax_id`, `ssn_last4` and `routing_number`. An `APAC` engagement requires `tax_id` and `national_id`. A `LATAM` engagement requires `tax_id`, `national_id` and `clabe`. If any required key is absent from `country_elements`, or is present but maps to an empty string, validation fails immediately: the extract moves `RECEIVED → REJECTED` with `rejection_code` set to `MISSING_ELEMENTS`, `validated_record_count` stays at 0, and no audit evidence is minted. Keys beyond the required set are permitted and ignored.

The second stage is the record-count drift check, and it only has anything to compare against when the cycle has a baseline. The baseline is the gross-to-net run in status `APPROVED` belonging to the cycle named by this cycle's `prior_cycle_id`. If the cycle has no `prior_cycle_id`, or that prior cycle has no approved run, validation passes with no drift computed at all. Otherwise, take the absolute difference between the extract's `record_count` and the baseline run's `employee_count`, divide by the baseline `employee_count`, multiply by 100, and round the result half up to two decimal places. If that rounded drift percentage is strictly greater than 20.00, validation fails: the extract moves `RECEIVED → REJECTED` with `rejection_code` set to `RECORD_COUNT_DRIFT`. Drift landing exactly on 20.00 is not greater than 20.00 and therefore passes.

When both stages pass, the extract moves `RECEIVED → VALIDATED`: `validated_record_count` is frozen at the extract's `record_count`, `rejection_code` is cleared to null, and any other extract of the same cycle that was already `VALIDATED` is moved `VALIDATED → SUPERSEDED` — its `validated_record_count` stays frozen at whatever it held, and it can no longer back a gross-to-net run. A successful validation also mints the cycle's input-integrity control evidence, recording an audit evidence item with `control_ref` `CTRL_INPUT_INTEGRITY`, but only when the cycle does not already carry such an item. `rejection_code` is null on any extract whose status is not `REJECTED`.

A rejected extract is not a dead end. Either `GlobalPayrollOps` or a `PayrollController` may resubmit it, supplying fresh values for `record_count`, `gross_declared_minor` and `country_elements`, which wholly replace the previous ones. The resubmission moves the extract `REJECTED → RECEIVED`, clears `rejection_code` to null and re-stamps `submitted_at` from the current logical clock, after which it can be validated again. Two guards apply in order: the extract must be in `REJECTED` and its cycle must still be in `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_INPUT_FILE_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409); and the current logical clock must not be strictly after the cycle's `input_cutoff_at`, otherwise `ERR_INPUT_CUTOFF_PASSED` (HTTP 409). The cutoff therefore closes the door on repair attempts as firmly as on first submissions.

**Locking inputs** is the act that ends the input phase, and only `GlobalPayrollOps` may perform it. The cycle must be in `DRAFT` — a cycle that is `ON_HOLD` or already past `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) — the owning engagement must be in `ACTIVE`, and anything else yields `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). Exactly one extract of the cycle must be in `VALIDATED`; if the cycle has no validated extract at all, the lock is rejected with `ERR_NO_VALIDATED_INPUT` (HTTP 409).

A successful lock does four things beyond moving the cycle `DRAFT → INPUTS_LOCKED`. It mints one statutory filing for every member of the country's `required_filing_types`, each opening in status `PENDING` with `responsibility` copied from the engagement's `filing_responsibility` and with a `due_at` resolved as described below. It increases the country profile's `open_filing_obligation_count` by the number of filings minted. It moves every extract of the cycle still sitting in `RECEIVED` to `SUPERSEDED`, since those extracts can now neither be validated nor resubmitted. And it increments the cycle's `version` by 1.

Each minted filing's `due_at` is resolved from calendar days, not working days: take the cycle's `pay_date`, add the country profile's `statutory_filing_deadline_days` as whole calendar days, and set the time of day to 23:59:59.999Z. Statutory deadlines never roll forward off a weekend or a local holiday, so `local_holidays` plays no part here — it matters only for the input cutoff. Every filing minted for one cycle shares the same `due_at`, and that instant is inclusive: lodging at exactly `due_at` is on time.

### 3.5. Gross-to-Net Results and Variance Analysis

Once a period's inputs are locked, the in-country provider computes the payroll and returns the results. A `ProviderUser` — and only a `ProviderUser` — submits them, and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `provider_id` of the cycle's engagement; a provider reaching into another provider's cycle is rejected with `ERR_PROVIDER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403) before any state or coherence rule is considered. The submission names the `cycle_id` whose results these are and the `input_file_id` of the validated extract they were computed from, then reports five figures: `gross_minor`, the employee gross pay, strictly positive; `net_minor`, the employee net pay, strictly positive; `employer_cost_minor`, the employer-borne contributions and taxes, at least zero; `provider_fee_minor`, the provider's service fee for this run, at least zero; and `employee_count`, the paid headcount, at least 1 — a headcount of zero is a shape failure and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). All four money figures are in local minor units. The submission also carries `expected_cycle_version`, described under optimistic concurrency above.

The guards on submission run in this order. Provider ownership comes first. Then the two state checks: the cycle must be in `INPUTS_LOCKED`, and any other cycle status is rejected with `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409); and the provider engaged on the cycle's engagement must not be `SUSPENDED`, because a suspended provider may not produce new payroll results, which is rejected with `ERR_PROVIDER_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 409). The stale-cycle-version check is evaluated with that state group, so a request that is both stale and aimed at a wrongly-stated cycle reports the cycle-state error. Then the referential check on the backing extract: the named extract must belong to this same cycle and must be in `VALIDATED`, and an extract of another cycle or one in `RECEIVED`, `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED` is rejected with `ERR_INPUT_FILE_CYCLE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Then the fiscal tolerance check: the absolute difference between the run's `gross_minor` and the backing extract's `gross_declared_minor` must not exceed one tenth of that declared gross, and a wider gap is rejected with `ERR_GROSS_TOTAL_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE` (HTTP 422) — the provider's arithmetic must be recognizably the same payroll the HR system described. And last, the internal coherence constraint: `net_minor` must not be greater than `gross_minor`, otherwise `ERR_GTN_TOTALS_INCONSISTENT` (HTTP 422).

A successful submission opens the run in `status` `SUBMITTED`, moves every earlier gross-to-net run of the same cycle that was in `SUBMITTED`, `CLEARED`, `ESCALATED` or `RETURNED` to `SUPERSEDED` so that it can no longer be reviewed or resubmitted, and increments the cycle's `version` by 1. Then, within the very same request, the variance analysis runs and the run immediately re-branches out of `SUBMITTED` — there is no observable resting state at `SUBMITTED` after a successful submission.

**Variance analysis** compares the run against the prior period's approved results, and it exists in two variants because a moderate headcount movement should be normalized away while a structural population change must be judged on absolute cash. The baseline is the gross-to-net run with status `APPROVED` whose cycle is the one named by this cycle's `prior_cycle_id`. If the cycle has no `prior_cycle_id`, or that prior cycle has no approved run, there is nothing to compare against: `variance_basis` is set to `NO_PRIOR_BASELINE`, `variance_pct` and `headcount_drift_pct` are both null, and `variance_material` is false.

When a baseline exists, first compute `headcount_drift_pct`: take the absolute difference between this run's `employee_count` and the baseline run's `employee_count`, divide by the baseline `employee_count`, multiply by 100, and round half up to two decimal places. That drift selects the variant. When the drift is at least 3.00 and at most 10.00 — both bounds inclusive — the `HEADCOUNT_NORMALIZED` variant applies. When the drift is below 3.00, or above 10.00, the `ABSOLUTE_TOTAL` variant applies. The selected variant's name is persisted in `variance_basis`.

Under `HEADCOUNT_NORMALIZED`, the comparison is per employee. Divide this run's `gross_minor` by its `employee_count` to obtain the per-head amount, and divide the baseline's `gross_minor` by the baseline's `employee_count` to obtain the baseline per-head amount. Both quotients are exact rational values and are **not** rounded at this stage. Subtract the baseline per-head amount from this run's per-head amount, divide the difference by the baseline per-head amount, multiply by 100, and round half up to two decimal places; that is `variance_pct`. Under `ABSOLUTE_TOTAL`, the comparison is on the totals: subtract the baseline's `gross_minor` from this run's `gross_minor`, divide by the baseline's `gross_minor`, multiply by 100, and round half up to two decimal places. In both variants `variance_pct` is signed, so a payroll that shrank carries a negative value. The two variants can disagree about more than the number: the same pair of runs can be immaterial when normalized per head and material on absolute totals, which is exactly why the drift band decides which reading governs.

Materiality then requires **both** thresholds of the country profile to be crossed. Let the gross delta be the absolute difference between this run's `gross_minor` and the baseline's `gross_minor`, taken unrounded. `variance_material` is true when the absolute value of the rounded `variance_pct` is greater than or equal to the country's `materiality_pct` **and** that gross delta is greater than or equal to the country's `materiality_floor_minor`. Either condition failing makes the variance immaterial; a spectacular percentage on a tiny payroll is not material, and a large absolute movement that is a small percentage is not material either.

The run's next status follows directly from materiality. An immaterial run moves `SUBMITTED → CLEARED`, and no service-level incident is created on that branch. A material run moves `SUBMITTED → ESCALATED`, and if in addition the absolute value of `variance_pct` is greater than or equal to twice the country's `materiality_pct`, a service-level incident is raised automatically for the cycle's engagement with `incident_type` `VARIANCE_ESCALATION`, `severity` `SEV3`, `status` `OPEN` and `occurred_at` stamped from the current logical clock. On both branches `variance_pct`, `variance_basis`, `headcount_drift_pct` and `variance_material` are persisted on the run. Runs are readable individually and listable with filters on `cycle_id`, `input_file_id` and `status`.

**Review** is the `PayrollController`'s act, and it is where segregation of duties bites. The body carries a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `RETURN`, optionally a `variance_justification`, and optionally a `return_reason`. Three guards apply in this order. First, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the cycle's `input_submitter_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Second, the run must be in `CLEARED` or `ESCALATED`; a run still `SUBMITTED`, already `APPROVED`, `RETURNED` or `SUPERSEDED` cannot be reviewed, and the attempt yields `ERR_GTN_RUN_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Third, approving a run whose status is `ESCALATED` requires a `variance_justification` of at least 10 characters; approving an escalated run without one, or with a shorter one, is rejected with `ERR_VARIANCE_JUSTIFICATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). Approving a `CLEARED` run needs no justification, and any justification supplied on that branch is simply recorded.

Approval carries the whole cycle forward. The run moves to `APPROVED` — from `CLEARED` or from `ESCALATED`, with the justification persisted in the latter case — the cycle moves `INPUTS_LOCKED → RESULTS_APPROVED` with `gross_total_minor` set to the approved run's `gross_minor`, and the cycle's variance-review control evidence is minted as an audit evidence item with `control_ref` `CTRL_VARIANCE_REVIEW`, `evidence_type` `SYSTEM_ATTESTATION` and a system attribution rather than the controller's own. `APPROVED` is terminal for the run.

Returning the run sends it back to the provider and records a service failure. A `RETURN` decision requires a `return_reason` of at least 10 characters, which is persisted; the run moves `CLEARED → RETURNED` or `ESCALATED → RETURNED`; a service-level incident is created for the cycle's engagement with `incident_type` `LATE_GTN_DELIVERY`, `severity` `SEV2` and `status` `OPEN`; and the cycle itself stays in `INPUTS_LOCKED`, since nothing has been approved.

A returned run is repaired by the provider through resubmission, which is again a `ProviderUser` act. The body replaces `gross_minor`, `net_minor`, `employer_cost_minor`, `provider_fee_minor` and `employee_count` with fresh figures and carries `expected_cycle_version` matching the cycle's current `version`. The run must be in `RETURNED`, and any other status is rejected with `ERR_GTN_RUN_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409); the owning cycle must still be in `INPUTS_LOCKED`. On success the run moves `RETURNED → SUBMITTED`, `return_reason` is cleared to null, the cycle's `version` is incremented by 1, and the variance is recomputed from the new figures, so the run immediately re-branches to `CLEARED` or `ESCALATED` exactly as a first submission would — including the possibility of raising a fresh `VARIANCE_ESCALATION` incident.

### 3.6. Funding, Treasury Dual Authorization and Recall

With results approved, the money has to be raised in the treasury's currency and landed in the country's local account. Either `GlobalPayrollOps` or a `TreasuryOfficer` drafts the **funding instruction**. The payload names the `cycle_id` being funded and the `bank_account_id` of the destination local account, sets the `value_date` of the transfer as a `YYYY-MM-DD` date, declares the `funding_currency` as exactly three uppercase letters, and supplies the `fx_rate`, a strictly positive rate carried to six decimal places and read as *local minor units obtained per one funding-currency minor unit*.

Four guards apply in this order. The cycle must be in `RESULTS_APPROVED`; drafting funding for a cycle that has not been approved, or that is already funded, closed or cancelled, is rejected with `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The named local account must belong to the same engagement as the cycle, otherwise `ERR_BANK_ACCOUNT_ENGAGEMENT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The `funding_currency` must differ from the account's `account_currency` — this system funds cross-currency, and a same-currency instruction is rejected with `ERR_CURRENCY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). And a cycle may carry only one live instruction at a time: if the cycle already has a funding instruction in `DRAFT`, `APPROVED` or `RELEASED`, the request is rejected with `ERR_FUNDING_ALREADY_EXISTS` (HTTP 409). A cycle whose only previous instruction was `RECALLED` or `VOIDED` may be funded again.

Drafting computes the conversion, and the **order of operations is mandatory**. First, the local requirement: `requested_local_minor` is the sum of `net_minor`, `employer_cost_minor` and `provider_fee_minor` taken from the cycle's approved gross-to-net run, in local minor units. Then each of those three components is converted on its own: divide `net_minor` by the `fx_rate` and round the quotient half up to a whole funding-currency minor unit; do the same for `employer_cost_minor`; do the same for `provider_fee_minor`. The three converted amounts are exposed as `component_source_minor`, an object keyed `net`, `employer_cost` and `provider_fee`. Sum those three already-rounded components to obtain the subtotal. Compute the FX buffer as 1.50 percent of that subtotal — multiply the subtotal by 1.50, divide by 100, and round half up to a whole minor unit. `funded_source_minor` is the subtotal plus that buffer. Converting the aggregate once instead of converting each component separately is wrong and can differ from the required answer by a minor unit; the buffer is likewise computed on the sum of the rounded components rather than on any unrounded intermediate.

Drafting also fixes the settlement window on the destination account. `window_start_at` is the `value_date` at 00:00:00.000Z and is the inclusive start of the window; `window_end_at` is `window_start_at` advanced by the account's `settlement_window_hours` and is the inclusive end. The instruction opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `approver_user_id`, `releaser_user_id` and `recall_reason` all null. Instructions are readable individually — the read exposes the per-component conversion breakdown and the settlement window — and listable with filters on `cycle_id`, `bank_account_id` and `status`.

Approval is the first of the two treasury authorizations and belongs exclusively to a `TreasuryOfficer`. The instruction must be in `DRAFT`, and any other status is rejected with `ERR_FUNDING_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409); the cycle must still be in `RESULTS_APPROVED`. On success the instruction moves `DRAFT → APPROVED` and `approver_user_id` is stamped with the acting `X-User-ID`. Nothing else changes; no money has moved.

Release is the second authorization, also a `TreasuryOfficer` act, and it is the point at which the money is committed. Five guards apply, in this order. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `approver_user_id`, and an approver releasing its own instruction is rejected with `ERR_DUAL_AUTHORIZATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403). The instruction must be in `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_FUNDING_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), and the cycle must be in `RESULTS_APPROVED`. The destination account must be in `VERIFIED`; releasing to an account still in `PENDING_VERIFICATION` is rejected with `ERR_BANK_ACCOUNT_NOT_VERIFIED` (HTTP 409). Then the temporal window check: treat this instruction's settlement interval as the closed interval running from `window_start_at` to `window_end_at`, and extend it by the account's `buffer_hours` at both ends; if that extended interval intersects the closed settlement interval of any other already-`RELEASED` instruction on the same `bank_account_id`, the release is rejected with `ERR_FUNDING_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Because both intervals are closed and the buffer is applied on both sides, two windows that merely touch after buffering do conflict. Finally the fiscal cap: sum the `requested_local_minor` of every already-`RELEASED` instruction on the same `bank_account_id` bearing the same `value_date`, add this instruction's own `requested_local_minor`, and if that total exceeds the account's `daily_cap_minor`, the release is rejected with `ERR_FUNDING_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). A total landing exactly on the cap is within it.

A successful release cascades widely. The instruction moves `APPROVED → RELEASED` and `releaser_user_id` is stamped with the acting `X-User-ID`, which by construction differs from `approver_user_id`. The engagement's `treasury_committed_minor` increases by this instruction's `funded_source_minor`. The cycle's `funded_total_minor` increases by the same `funded_source_minor` and the cycle moves `RESULTS_APPROVED → FUNDED`. And the cycle's funding dual-authorization control evidence is minted as an audit evidence item with `control_ref` `CTRL_FUNDING_DUAL_AUTH` under a system attribution.

Recall reverses every one of those effects. A `TreasuryOfficer` recalls a released instruction by supplying a `recall_reason` of at least 10 characters, which is persisted. The instruction must be in `RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_FUNDING_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), and the cycle must be in `FUNDED`. On success the instruction moves `RELEASED → RECALLED`, which is terminal; the engagement's `treasury_committed_minor` decreases by exactly the `funded_source_minor` it gained on release, restoring the conserved commitment to its pre-release value; the cycle's `funded_total_minor` decreases by the same amount, returning to 0 when this was the cycle's only release; and provided no other instruction of the cycle is still `RELEASED`, the cycle moves `FUNDED → RESULTS_APPROVED`. Both the settlement window and that value date's cap bucket on the account are freed, so another instruction may occupy them.

The remaining path out of a funding instruction is involuntary. When a cycle is cancelled, each of its instructions sitting in `DRAFT` or `APPROVED` is moved to the terminal `VOIDED` and can no longer be approved or released; an instruction voided out of `APPROVED` retains its `approver_user_id` for audit.

### 3.7. Statutory Filing, Late Sweeps and Penalty Ownership

Each statutory obligation of a period is a **filing**, minted by the system when the cycle locks its inputs; there is no endpoint that creates one. A filing carries `filing_type`, one of `INCOME_TAX_WITHHOLDING`, `SOCIAL_INSURANCE` or `YEAR_END`, taken from the country's `required_filing_types`, and no two filings of the same cycle share a `filing_type`. It carries `responsibility`, copied at minting from the engagement's `filing_responsibility` and therefore either `PROVIDER` or `CLIENT`, and the `due_at` computed from the pay date and the country's deadline days. A filing tracks **two independent state dimensions**: `status`, the lodgement dimension, which moves through `PENDING`, `LATE`, `FILED` and `FILED_LATE` to the terminal `CONFIRMED` or the terminal `VOIDED`; and `penalty_status`, the penalty dimension, which moves through `NONE`, `ASSESSED` and `DISPUTED` to the terminal `WAIVED`. The two evolve separately, and an action on one does not disturb the other unless this section says it does. Filings are readable individually and listable with filters on `cycle_id`, `status`, `penalty_status` and `filing_type`.

Lodging a filing is done by whoever the obligation names. The body supplies the authority's `submission_ref`, which is persisted, and `filed_at` is stamped with the current logical-clock instant. The actor rule is checked before any state rule: when `responsibility` is `PROVIDER`, the actor must be the `ProviderUser` of the engaged provider, and when `responsibility` is `CLIENT`, the actor must not be a `ProviderUser` — it is `GlobalPayrollOps` that lodges client obligations. A mismatch in either direction is rejected with `ERR_FILING_RESPONSIBILITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). Then the state rules: the filing must be in `PENDING` or `LATE`, and the owning cycle must be in `RESULTS_APPROVED` or `FUNDED`; a filing already lodged or confirmed, or a cycle that has not yet had its results approved, is rejected with `ERR_FILING_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). A `PENDING` filing may only be lodged while the current logical clock is at or before its `due_at`, in which case it moves `PENDING → FILED`; once the clock has passed `due_at` the obligation must first be swept to `LATE`, and lodging a still-`PENDING` filing after its deadline is rejected with `ERR_FILING_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). A filing already in `LATE` moves `LATE → FILED_LATE` with no clock condition at all. Either way, the country profile's `open_filing_obligation_count` decreases by 1, because the obligation is no longer live. Lodging never touches `penalty_status`: an obligation that accrued a penalty keeps it after it is finally lodged.

Confirmation records the authority's acknowledgement. Either `GlobalPayrollOps` or a `ProviderUser` may confirm, supplying a `confirmation_ref` of 1 to 60 characters, which is persisted. The filing must be in `FILED` or `FILED_LATE`; confirming from any other status is rejected with `ERR_FILING_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Both lodgement states lead to the same terminal `CONFIRMED`. Confirmation increases the cycle's `filings_confirmed_count` by 1 and, at the moment every non-`VOIDED` filing of the cycle has reached `CONFIRMED`, mints the cycle's filing-confirmation control evidence as an audit evidence item with `control_ref` `CTRL_FILING_CONFIRMATION`; earlier confirmations mint nothing.

The **filing sweep** is how missed deadlines are detected, and it is a per-cycle capability available to `GlobalPayrollOps` or a `PayrollController`. The cycle must be in `RESULTS_APPROVED` or `FUNDED`, otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The sweep examines every filing of the cycle still in `PENDING` and, for each whose `due_at` now lies before the current logical clock, moves it `PENDING → LATE` and assesses the penalty. Moving from `PENDING` to `LATE` leaves the country's `open_filing_obligation_count` unchanged, since both states count as live obligations. The sweep returns the collection of the cycle's filings.

Penalty assessment runs in seven steps for each newly late filing. **Late days** first: take the elapsed time from the filing's `due_at` to the current logical clock, express it in seconds, divide by 86400, and round *up* to the next whole number, so any positive remainder counts as another whole late day; the result is `late_days`. **Base accrual** second: `base_penalty_minor` is a statutory base of 25000 local minor units plus `late_days` multiplied by the country profile's `penalty_daily_rate_minor`. **Tier selection** third, and critically it reads the *uncapped* base: if `base_penalty_minor` is at most 50000 the surcharge rate is 0.00 percent; otherwise if it is at most 100000 the rate is 10.00 percent; otherwise the rate is 20.00 percent. `surcharge_minor` is `base_penalty_minor` multiplied by that rate, divided by 100, rounded half up to a whole local minor unit. **Total** fourth: `total_penalty_minor` is `base_penalty_minor` plus `surcharge_minor`. **Indemnity headroom** fifth: the provider's absolute cap is its `annual_fee_minor` multiplied by its `indemnity_cap_pct`, divided by 100, rounded half up to a whole minor unit, and the remaining headroom is that cap less the provider's current `open_penalty_minor`, floored at zero so it is never negative. **Split** sixth: when `responsibility` is `PROVIDER`, `provider_share_minor` is the smaller of `total_penalty_minor` and the remaining headroom; when `responsibility` is `CLIENT`, `provider_share_minor` is 0. `client_share_minor` is always `total_penalty_minor` less `provider_share_minor`, so the two shares sum exactly to the total with no rounding drift. **Ownership label** seventh: `penalty_owner` is `NONE` when the total is zero, `PROVIDER` when the provider's share equals the whole total, `CLIENT` when the provider's share is zero, and `SHARED` otherwise. Note the deliberate sequencing: because the tier is chosen from the uncapped base, a provider whose cap bites still bears the surcharge that the larger, uncapped base earned.

Assessment also moves the penalty dimension and reaches out of the filing. `penalty_status` moves `NONE → ASSESSED`, the provider's `open_penalty_minor` increases by the new `provider_share_minor`, and if that running exposure now exceeds 100000 local minor units the provider drops `ACTIVE → PROBATION`, which cascades to its live engagements and their draft cycles as described in the next subsection. That probation path leaves `service_score` untouched: penalty exposure and service quality are independent grounds for probation. Finally, every provider-responsible filing that went late also raises a service-level incident for the cycle's engagement with `incident_type` `FILING_MISS`, `severity` `SEV2` and `status` `OPEN`; client-responsible filings raise none.

A provider that believes an assessment is wrong may dispute its share. Disputing is a `ProviderUser` act carrying a `dispute_reason`, which is persisted. Two guards apply in order: the filing's `penalty_status` must be `ASSESSED` and its `provider_share_minor` must be greater than zero, otherwise `ERR_PENALTY_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) — a provider cannot dispute a penalty it does not bear; and `dispute_round`, which counts the dispute rounds already rejected, must be less than 2, so a provider whose `dispute_round` has reached 2 is rejected with `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409). A successful dispute moves `penalty_status` `ASSESSED → DISPUTED`, leaves every penalty amount unchanged, and leaves the lodgement `status` entirely alone.

A `PayrollController` decides the dispute, supplying a `decision` of `UPHOLD` or `REJECT` and optionally a `decision_note`. The filing's `penalty_status` must be `DISPUTED`, otherwise `ERR_PENALTY_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). A `REJECT` decision moves `penalty_status` `DISPUTED → ASSESSED`, increases `dispute_round` by 1 and clears `dispute_reason` to null; the provider may try again only while `dispute_round` remains below 2, which gives it at most two rejected rounds before the door closes. An `UPHOLD` decision moves `penalty_status` `DISPUTED → WAIVED`, which is terminal: the whole `provider_share_minor` is shifted into `client_share_minor` so that `total_penalty_minor` is preserved unchanged, `penalty_owner` becomes `CLIENT`, and the provider's `open_penalty_minor` decreases by the waived share — which, if the exposure falls back to 100000 or less, can recover the provider from `PROBATION` to `ACTIVE` and bubble that recovery back through its engagements.

Voiding is the last route out of the lodgement dimension. When a cycle is cancelled, each of its filings in `PENDING` or `LATE` moves to the terminal `VOIDED` and the country's `open_filing_obligation_count` decreases by 1 for each. Voiding a filing that had already been assessed retains its `penalty_status` and every penalty amount, and it does **not** reduce the provider's `open_penalty_minor`: cancelling the payroll period does not undo a penalty the authority has already earned.

### 3.8. Service Level Monitoring, Provider Standing and Transition

Service failures are recorded as **incidents** against an engagement. Either `GlobalPayrollOps` or a `PayrollController` may raise one, naming the `engagement_id`, the `incident_type` — one of `LATE_GTN_DELIVERY`, `VARIANCE_ESCALATION`, `FILING_MISS`, `FUNDING_MISS` or `DATA_INCIDENT` — the `severity` band, one of `SEV1`, `SEV2` or `SEV3`, and the `occurred_at` instant at which the failure happened. A `cycle_id` may be supplied when the failure relates to a specific period and is left null for engagement-level failures, and a free-text `note` may be attached. Two guards apply in order: `occurred_at` must not be strictly after the current logical clock, and a future-dated incident is rejected with `ERR_INCIDENT_FUTURE_DATED` (HTTP 422); and when `cycle_id` is supplied it must name a cycle of the same engagement, otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_ENGAGEMENT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Three of the five types are also raised automatically by the system — `VARIANCE_ESCALATION` on a heavily escalated gross-to-net run, `LATE_GTN_DELIVERY` when a run is returned to the provider, and `FILING_MISS` when a provider-responsible obligation goes late — while `FUNDING_MISS` and `DATA_INCIDENT` are only ever raised by an operator.

A new incident opens in `status` `OPEN` with `weight_points` at 0.00, `escalation_level` at 0 and `remediation_ref` null. An `OPEN` incident is a report, not yet a finding, and it deducts nothing from anybody's score. Incidents are readable individually and listable with filters on `engagement_id`, `cycle_id`, `severity` and `status`.

The `PayrollController` owns the incident lifecycle. **Confirming** an incident moves it `OPEN → CONFIRMED` and is where scoring begins: `weight_points` is set from the severity band, 25.00 for `SEV1`, 15.00 for `SEV2` and 5.00 for `SEV3`. Confirming an incident that is not `OPEN` is rejected with `ERR_INCIDENT_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). **Escalating** a confirmed incident moves it `CONFIRMED → ESCALATED`, sets `escalation_level` to 1, and multiplies the severity's base points by 1.5, rounding half up to two decimal places — so an escalated `SEV1` weighs 37.50, an escalated `SEV2` weighs 22.50 and an escalated `SEV3` weighs 7.50. An optional `escalation_note` may accompany the act. Escalating an incident that is not `CONFIRMED` is rejected with `ERR_INCIDENT_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). **Remediating** an incident is available from either `CONFIRMED` or `ESCALATED`, requires a `remediation_ref` of 1 to 60 characters which is persisted, and moves the incident to the terminal `REMEDIATED` with `weight_points` reset to 0.00; `escalation_level` is retained for audit rather than reset. Remediating from `OPEN` or from `REMEDIATED` is rejected with `ERR_INCIDENT_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Every one of these three acts recomputes the engaged provider's `service_score` immediately, within the same request.

**The provider service score** starts from a perfect 100.00 and is reduced by two kinds of failure. The qualifying incidents are the incidents of *every* engagement of that provider whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` or `ESCALATED` — `OPEN` and `REMEDIATED` incidents contribute nothing, which is why confirming and remediating both move the score. The qualifying late filings are the statutory filings of those engagements' cycles whose `late_days` is greater than zero. The deduction is the sum of `weight_points` over the qualifying incidents, plus 5.00 for each qualifying late filing. The `service_score` is 100.00 less that deduction, rounded half up to two decimal places and floored at 0.00, so it always lies between 0.00 and 100.00 inclusive.

Which incidents and filings *qualify* depends on a variant, and the variant is chosen by delivery history. If at least one engagement of the provider has a `closed_cycle_count` of 1 or more, the `ROLLING_WINDOW` variant applies; if no engagement of the provider has ever closed a cycle, the `ENGAGEMENT_TO_DATE` variant applies. The chosen variant's name is persisted on the provider as `score_variant_applied`. Under `ROLLING_WINDOW`, the window opens 90 calendar days before the current logical clock: an incident qualifies when its `occurred_at` is at or after that window start, and a late filing qualifies when its `due_at` is at or after that window start; anything older drops out of the reckoning. Under `ENGAGEMENT_TO_DATE` there is no time filter whatsoever — every qualifying incident and every late filing counts, including ones that occurred before the engagement's `activated_at`. The consequence is that the first cycle a provider closes can *raise* its score by aging out old failures, and that is precisely why closing a cycle is a scoring event.

Standing is re-derived from the recomputed score immediately after every recompute, and penalty exposure participates. A `service_score` below 40.00 makes the provider `SUSPENDED`. A score at least 40.00 but below 55.00 makes it `PROBATION`. A score of at least 55.00 makes it `ACTIVE` if and only if its `open_penalty_minor` is at most 100000; a score of at least 55.00 together with more than 100000 of unresolved penalty share still makes it `PROBATION`. A provider with a flawless 100.00 score and excessive open penalty exposure is therefore on probation, and waiving that exposure is what recovers it. `OFFBOARDED` is never entered or left through scoring.

Degradation cascades down three levels within the triggering request. When a provider becomes `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED`, every engagement of that provider whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` and has its `transition_required` set to true, and every cycle of those engagements sitting in `DRAFT` moves `DRAFT → ON_HOLD` with `hold_reason` set to `PROVIDER_PROBATION` when the provider is on probation or `PROVIDER_SUSPENSION` when the provider is suspended. A provider that slides further, `PROBATION → SUSPENDED`, upgrades the `hold_reason` of cycles already `ON_HOLD` to `PROVIDER_SUSPENSION`; a provider that partially recovers, `SUSPENDED → PROBATION`, downgrades the `hold_reason` of cycles still `ON_HOLD` to `PROVIDER_PROBATION`. Suspension carries one additional prohibition beyond the holds: no new gross-to-net run may be created for any cycle of a suspended provider, which is the `ERR_PROVIDER_SUSPENDED` rejection described with the results workflow.

Recovery cascades back up, but not completely. When a provider returns to `ACTIVE` — because its recomputed score reached at least 55.00, or because a waived penalty brought `open_penalty_minor` back to 100000 or less, with both conditions satisfied — every engagement of that provider sitting in `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` that has no remaining incident in `CONFIRMED` or `ESCALATED` moves `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED → ACTIVE` with `transition_required` cleared to false. An engagement that still carries an unremediated finding stays in remediation even though its provider is healthy. And cycles already `ON_HOLD` are **not** released automatically by any of this; they wait for an explicit hold release.

**Transition planning** is the escape hatch when a provider cannot be recovered, and a `PayrollController` writes the plan. The engagement must be in `ACTIVE` or `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, and any other status is rejected with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The plan names the `incoming_provider_id`, defines the cutover window with `cutover_period_start_date` and `cutover_period_end_date`, and states `parallel_run_cycles`, a whole number from 0 through 3 giving how many cycles will be run in parallel across the two providers. It also carries `expected_engagement_version`. After the state check and the stale-version check, two further guards apply in order: `cutover_period_end_date` must be strictly after `cutover_period_start_date`, and a window that is empty or inverted is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422); and the incoming provider must be a genuine alternative — it must differ from the engagement's current `provider_id`, its `status` must be `ACTIVE`, and its `data_residency_regions` must contain the engagement's `data_residency_region` — with any of those three failures rejected as `ERR_INCOMING_PROVIDER_INVALID` (HTTP 422).

A written plan moves the engagement to `TRANSITION_PLANNED` and populates the four plan fields from the request: `transition_incoming_provider_id` from `incoming_provider_id`, `transition_cutover_start_date` as the inclusive first date of the cutover window, `transition_cutover_end_date` as the exclusive last date of that window, and `transition_parallel_run_cycles` from `parallel_run_cycles`. The engagement's `version` increments by 1. Planning from `ACTIVE` and planning from `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` behave identically in every respect except one: a plan written out of `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` leaves `transition_required` true, because the underlying remediation has not been resolved by writing a plan.

**Transition decisions** belong to `PlatformAdmin` and carry a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `ABORT` or `COMPLETE`, plus an optional `knowledge_transfer_confirmed` flag. The state rule is decision-specific: `APPROVE` requires the engagement to be in `TRANSITION_PLANNED`, `COMPLETE` requires it to be in `TRANSITION_IN_PROGRESS`, and `ABORT` requires it to be in either `TRANSITION_PLANNED` or `TRANSITION_IN_PROGRESS`; a decision taken from the wrong state is rejected with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).

`APPROVE` moves the engagement `TRANSITION_PLANNED → TRANSITION_IN_PROGRESS` and changes no other field at all; from that point the plan fields are frozen. `ABORT` discards the plan: all four `transition_*` plan fields are reset to null and `provider_id` is left exactly as it was. Where the engagement lands depends on `transition_required` — an abort with `transition_required` false returns the engagement to `ACTIVE`, while an abort with `transition_required` true returns it to `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, the remediation state it came from. Both landings are reachable from `TRANSITION_PLANNED` and from `TRANSITION_IN_PROGRESS` alike.

`COMPLETE` is the cutover itself, and it is guarded twice more. First, at the state rank, no cycle of the engagement may be mid-flight: if any cycle of the engagement is in `INPUTS_LOCKED`, `RESULTS_APPROVED` or `FUNDED`, the completion is rejected with `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) — a period that is already running must finish or be cancelled before the provider changes hands. Second, at the constraint rank and therefore only after the live-cycle check has passed, `knowledge_transfer_confirmed` must be present and true; absent or false, the completion is rejected with `ERR_KNOWLEDGE_TRANSFER_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409). On success, the outgoing provider is noted, `provider_id` is reassigned to `transition_incoming_provider_id`, all four `transition_*` plan fields are reset to null, `transition_required` is cleared to false, and the engagement moves `TRANSITION_IN_PROGRESS → ACTIVE`. Two provider-level effects follow. The outgoing provider becomes `OFFBOARDED` — from `ACTIVE`, from `PROBATION` or from `SUSPENDED` alike — if and only if it has no other engagement left in `ACTIVE`, `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, `TRANSITION_PLANNED` or `TRANSITION_IN_PROGRESS`; its `service_score` is frozen at its last computed value and it may never again be named as an incoming provider, since `OFFBOARDED` is terminal. The incoming provider's `service_score` is recomputed for its newly enlarged engagement portfolio, with the same variant selection and the same status re-derivation as any other recompute.

### 3.9. Global Controls, Evidence, Closure and Cancellation

Auditability is not a report generated at the end; it accumulates as the period runs. Each **audit evidence item** attaches to one `cycle_id` and evidences one global control named by `control_ref`, which is one of `CTRL_INPUT_INTEGRITY`, `CTRL_VARIANCE_REVIEW`, `CTRL_FUNDING_DUAL_AUTH`, `CTRL_FILING_CONFIRMATION` or `CTRL_RESIDENCY_ATTESTATION`. No cycle may carry two items for the same control. Each item states its `evidence_type`, either `SYSTEM_ATTESTATION` or `DOCUMENT_HASH`, and a `payload_hash` of 1 to 80 characters identifying the evidenced artefact. Each item also records who put it there: `recorded_by_role` and `recorded_by_user_id`, stamped from the request headers for an operator-recorded item, and carrying the literal values `SYSTEM` and `system` respectively for an item minted by a system cascade. Finally each item carries `sequence_no`, a 1-based append position within its cycle assigned in ascending `created_at` order, so the evidence file reads as a chronological narrative. Audit evidence items are append-only: they have no `updated_at` and no `version`, and there is no endpoint that edits or deletes one. A cycle's evidence is readable as a nested sub-resource list in append order, optionally narrowed by `control_ref`.

Four of the five controls are minted by the system at the moment the control is actually exercised: `CTRL_INPUT_INTEGRITY` when an HR extract first validates for the cycle, `CTRL_VARIANCE_REVIEW` when the gross-to-net results are approved, `CTRL_FUNDING_DUAL_AUTH` when funding is released under two distinct treasury signatures, and `CTRL_FILING_CONFIRMATION` when the last non-`VOIDED` filing of the cycle is confirmed. Only `CTRL_RESIDENCY_ATTESTATION` is recorded by hand, and only a `ComplianceAuditor` may record it. Three guards apply in order. The cycle must still be open: posting evidence against a cycle in `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` is rejected with `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The `control_ref` must be `CTRL_RESIDENCY_ATTESTATION`; every other control is the system's to mint, and naming one manually is rejected with `ERR_MANUAL_CONTROL_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 422). And the control must not already be evidenced for that cycle, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_EVIDENCE_CONTROL` (HTTP 409).

**Closing a cycle** is the `PayrollController`'s final act on a period, and it is the strictest gate in the system. Three checks apply in order. The cycle must be in `FUNDED`; closing from any other status, including a cycle already `CLOSED`, is rejected with `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The statutory position must be complete: the cycle must have at least one statutory filing, and every non-`VOIDED` filing of the cycle must be in `CONFIRMED`; a cycle with no filing at all, or with any live filing short of confirmation, is rejected with `ERR_FILINGS_NOT_CONFIRMED` (HTTP 409). And the evidence file must be complete: the cycle must carry an audit evidence item for each of `CTRL_INPUT_INTEGRITY`, `CTRL_VARIANCE_REVIEW`, `CTRL_FUNDING_DUAL_AUTH` and `CTRL_FILING_CONFIRMATION`, and a cycle missing any of those four is rejected with `ERR_AUDIT_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409). The manual residency attestation is deliberately not among the four mandatory controls for closure.

A successful close moves the cycle `FUNDED → CLOSED`, which is terminal, and increases the owning engagement's `closed_cycle_count` by 1. That increment is a scoring event: the engaged provider's `service_score` is recomputed immediately, and because the provider now has an engagement with at least one closed cycle, `score_variant_applied` flips to `ROLLING_WINDOW` at the first such closure. Since the rolling window only counts incidents whose `occurred_at` and late filings whose `due_at` fall within the last 90 calendar days, the recomputed score can differ from the score the same provider carried a moment earlier, and the re-derived `Provider.status` can therefore change in either direction — cascading onward to that provider's engagements and their draft cycles exactly as any other standing change does.

**Cancelling a cycle** is the alternative ending, and `GlobalPayrollOps` performs it with a `cancel_reason` in the body. Cancellation is only available before the period has produced anything irreversible: the cycle's status must be `DRAFT`, `ON_HOLD` or `INPUTS_LOCKED`, no funding instruction of the cycle may be in `RELEASED`, and no filing of the cycle may be in `FILED`, `FILED_LATE` or `CONFIRMED`. Any of those three conditions failing rejects the request with `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) — money that has moved and obligations already lodged with an authority cannot be wished away by cancelling the period.

A successful cancellation moves the cycle to the terminal `CANCELLED` and cleans up everything downstream of it in the same request. If the cycle was `ON_HOLD`, `hold_reason` is cleared back to null. Every statutory filing of the cycle in `PENDING` or `LATE` moves to `VOIDED`, and the country profile's `open_filing_obligation_count` decreases by exactly that number of filings, restoring the conserved obligation count to the value it held before the cycle minted its obligations. Every funding instruction of the cycle in `DRAFT` or `APPROVED` moves to `VOIDED`. And every gross-to-net run of the cycle in `SUBMITTED`, `CLEARED`, `ESCALATED` or `RETURNED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, so none of them can be reviewed or resubmitted afterwards. A cancelled cycle's period no longer blocks a new cycle's period from overlapping it, which is how a mis-dated period is corrected.
