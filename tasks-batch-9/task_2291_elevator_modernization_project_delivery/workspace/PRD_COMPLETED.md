# Elevator Modernization Delivery API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Modernizing the elevators in a building that people still live and work in is a delivery problem before it is an engineering problem. A modernization contractor arrives at a bank of thirty-year-old cars, opens the controller cabinets and the machine room, writes down what it finds, and from that assessment proposes a depth of intervention — a new controller alone, a controller plus door equipment, a controller plus hoist machine, or a complete replacement. Every one of those choices drags along code obligations that the authority having jurisdiction will enforce whether or not they were in the original budget: firefighters' service, seismic restraint of the machine and counterweight, door reopening protection, brake and rope monitoring. And every one of them requires taking a car out of service for weeks in a building whose tenants expect to reach the fourteenth floor tomorrow morning.

This API is the system of record for that delivery. It carries the condition survey and the two different ways a partially-observed survey can be scored; the priced scope option with its pinned sequence of code surcharge, lead-time escalation and volume discount; the long-lead procurement orders and the finite square footage of staging yard they compete for; the car-by-car outage sequence with its group service floors, buffer separations and tenant notice obligations; the jurisdiction's inspection and acceptance testing rulings, including the reinspection failure that sends a car back into outage; and finally the warranty that rolls into an ongoing maintenance contract with a credit for the coverage the owner should not have to pay for twice.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and are worth stating up front. First, the numbers are pinned: where more than one plausible order of operations exists — round each line then sum, or sum then round; discount on the pre-escalation figure, or on the post-escalation subtotal — exactly one order is correct and the other is explicitly wrong. Second, the scheduling geometry is deliberately non-uniform: the staging yard is an additive pool over half-open intervals where touching holds are legal, while outage windows within one hoistway group are a pairwise conflict over buffer-inflated intervals where touching windows are illegal. Implementers who assume the two behave alike will pass roughly half the scheduling scenarios.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the authority under which they act. If either header is absent or empty, or if the value of `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names declared below, the request is refused with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH` and HTTP 401. When both headers are well formed but the named role does not appear in the target endpoint's authorized-role list, the request is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` and HTTP 403. This second check runs before any identifier in the request path is resolved, so a caller without permission on an endpoint cannot use 404-versus-403 differences to discover which rows exist.

The six roles are `ProjectManager`, `SurveyTechnician`, `BuildingRepresentative`, `AHJInspector`, `ServiceManager` and `SystemOperator`.

A `ProjectManager` is the modernization contractor's project manager. The role owns the project record, the hoistway groups, the car register, scope options and their pricing, code-trigger pricing, procurement, staging and outage planning; it may not approve or reject its own scope options and may not rule on an inspection. A `SurveyTechnician` is the field technician who assesses controller, machine and door equipment condition; two distinct technician identities are required to submit and to countersign one survey. A `BuildingRepresentative` acts for the building owner: approving and rejecting scope options, tightening a group's in-service floor, and signing off tenant communications. An `AHJInspector` acts for the authority having jurisdiction: scheduling inspections, ruling on acceptance tests, and issuing code waivers. A `ServiceManager` runs the contractor's service organisation — receiving and installing equipment, running the outage floor, and owning the warranty-to-maintenance handover — and may not create or price scope options. A `SystemOperator` is the platform operator: it registers jurisdictions and drives the logical clock, and holds no business authority over surveys, scope, procurement, outages, inspections or contracts.

Two of the six roles are backed by a persisted entity, and for those two `X-User-ID` is an ownership claim that the server verifies. When `X-User-Role` is `BuildingRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ModernizationProject`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `ModernizationProject` compare the resource's owning project — resolved through car to project, group to project, scope option to car to project, and so on — to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_SCOPE_MISMATCH` and HTTP 403. A representative of one building can therefore never act on another building's rows, even on endpoints their role permits, and their list reads are scoped to their own project. When `X-User-Role` is `AHJInspector`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Jurisdiction`; ownership checks compare the `jurisdiction_id` of the project owning the target resource to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_JURISDICTION_SCOPE_MISMATCH` and HTTP 403. An inspector cannot schedule, reschedule, waive or rule on work outside their own jurisdiction, and — exactly as for a representative — their list reads are filtered to the rows their claim covers, with the `total_count` and `has_more` of such a list computed over the filtered set rather than over the whole collection. Both ownership claims apply only where the target row resolves to an owning `ModernizationProject` at all: a jurisdiction row has none, so reading one is never refused on either ground.

The remaining four roles are not entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `ProjectManager`, `SurveyTechnician`, `ServiceManager` or `SystemOperator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — a survey stores the submitting technician's identifier as `surveyed_by_user_id` and the countersigning technician's as `countersigned_by_user_id` — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`. The one place where a recorded opaque identifier is enforced is segregation of duties on countersignature: the countersigning caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from the survey's `surveyed_by_user_id`, and a self-countersignature is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position in the result set: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds, or one that is not an integer at all, is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400. The optional filter parameters a collection endpoint declares divide the same way: an enum-valued or integer-valued filter whose value lies outside its declared set, or is not an integer where an integer is expected, is likewise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 400, whereas an identifier-valued filter naming no existing row is not an error at all and simply matches nothing, answering with an empty page and a `total_count` of 0.

Collections are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest row first — and this ordering is stable across pages so that a client walking a collection with successive offsets sees each row exactly once. Because `created_at` is read from the logical clock rather than from a wall clock, rows inserted without an intervening clock move carry an identical `created_at`; wherever rows are ordered by `created_at` — in a list response, and in the internal walks that take a car's latest survey, an option's synthesized triggers or a car's outage windows in order — such a tie is broken by insertion order, the row inserted first sorting first. Alongside the request identifier and timestamp, the `meta` object of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` as they were applied, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the request's filters before pagination, and `has_more`, which is true exactly when rows matching the filters remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Stamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource this API persists — jurisdictions, projects, hoistway groups, cars, surveys, scope options, code triggers, procurement orders, staging reservations, outage windows, inspection appointments, warranty transitions and maintenance contracts — carries the same four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, assigned at creation and never reused or reassigned; clients never supply it. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was inserted. `updated_at` is the same format and records the most recent mutation of the row, read from the same logical clock as `created_at`, so two mutations with no intervening clock move leave identical `updated_at` values; at creation it equals `created_at`. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations that reach it as a side effect of a write aimed at a different resource. No entity in this system opts out of these four properties.

Time in this system is not wall-clock time. Every lead-time comparison, expiry test, stamping operation and day count reads a single server-side **logical clock**. Before any request has moved it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to the host's real clock. A `SystemOperator` moves it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the new setting as `current_utc` together with `lapsed_warranty_transition_count`, the number of pending warranty transitions the move expired. Advancing the clock is not a passive act — it lapses warranties and re-evaluates every project's downtime posture, as described in §3.8.

Every instant this API emits on a resource row carries the same ISO 8601 UTC rendering as `created_at` and `updated_at`, with three fractional digits; there is no second timestamp format. A timestamp supplied by a client is echoed back on the stored row in that canonical rendering rather than in the client's own spelling of it, and an instant derived by whole-day or calendar-month arithmetic is rendered the same way. The one exception is an instant stamped directly from the logical clock at the moment of the write — `ordered_at`, `released_at`, `advance_notice_sent_at`, `activated_at`, `completed_at`, `warranty_start`, and the `current_utc` a clock read reports — which reproduces the clock's own current reading verbatim: exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` while the clock still stands where it started, and the three-fractional-digit rendering of the instant it was last set to once it has been moved.

### 2.5. Path Resolution and the Order in Which a Request Is Refused

Because most operations in this domain fail for more than one reason at once, the order in which checks run is part of the contract: the first band that rejects the request determines the error the caller sees, and no later band can mask an earlier one. The bands are evaluated in the following sequence.

1. **Header presence and role validity** — `ERR_MISSING_AUTH`, HTTP 401, on every endpoint.
2. **Role permission on the target endpoint** — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, HTTP 403, on every endpoint.
3. **Path identifier resolution** — every identifier embedded in the request path must resolve to an existing row, and the identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, so the leftmost unresolvable identifier is the one reported. Any unresolvable path identifier yields the generic `ERR_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404. The path identifiers this API uses are `jurisdiction_id`, `project_id`, `group_id`, `car_id`, `survey_id`, `scope_option_id`, `code_trigger_id`, `procurement_order_id`, `staging_reservation_id`, `outage_window_id`, `inspection_appointment_id`, `warranty_transition_id` and `maintenance_contract_id`.
4. **Body foreign-key resolution** — every foreign key supplied in a request body must resolve to an existing row, validated in the order the fields are listed in the endpoint's input, again reporting `ERR_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404. This band applies to `POST /projects`, `POST /elevator-groups`, `POST /elevator-cars`, `POST /equipment-surveys`, `POST /scope-options`, `POST /procurement-orders`, `POST /staging-reservations`, `POST /outage-windows`, `POST /inspection-appointments`, `POST /warranty-transitions` and `POST /maintenance-contracts`, and equally to the two transition endpoints that carry a foreign key in their body, `POST /procurement-orders/{procurement_order_id}/receive` and `POST /warranty-transitions/{warranty_transition_id}/enroll`.
5. **Building-representative project scope** — `ERR_PROJECT_SCOPE_MISMATCH`, HTTP 403, on every endpoint whose authorized roles include `BuildingRepresentative`.
6. **Inspector jurisdiction scope** — `ERR_JURISDICTION_SCOPE_MISMATCH`, HTTP 403, on every endpoint whose authorized roles include `AHJInspector`.
7. **Segregation of duties on countersignature** — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, HTTP 403, on `POST /equipment-surveys/{survey_id}/countersign` only.
8. **Request shape** — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, HTTP 400, on every endpoint. All required input fields must be present, no unknown fields may appear, enum-valued fields must carry a value from their declared set, integers must be non-negative, monetary amounts must be non-negative whole numbers of cents, timestamps must be valid ISO 8601 UTC, `hold_end` must be strictly after `hold_start`, `end_at` must be strictly after `start_at`, `seismic_zone` must lie between 0 and 4 inclusive, `warranty_months` must lie between 12 and 60 inclusive, and where a `basis` query parameter is required its value must be one of the declared basis names for that endpoint.
9. **Optimistic concurrency** — `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION`, HTTP 412, on `POST /scope-options` and `POST /staging-reservations`. It is deliberately checked after the request is known to be well formed but before any state precondition, so that a caller holding a stale view of the project is told to re-read rather than being told about a state it cannot yet see.
10. **State preconditions on the target row and its ancestors**, evaluated target-first: the row's own status must permit the requested transition, and where a transition depends on a parent, the parent's status is checked in this same band. Each entity reports its own identifier so the operator can diagnose the failure — `ERR_PROJECT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_CAR_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_SURVEY_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_SCOPE_OPTION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_CODE_TRIGGER_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PROCUREMENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_RESERVATION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_OUTAGE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_INSPECTION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_RESCHEDULABLE`, `ERR_SCOPE_OPTION_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_WARRANTY_STATE_INVALID` and `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`, each with HTTP 409. The project row counts as an ancestor for this purpose: a project that has reached `COMPLETED` is closed to new work, so registering a car, creating a scope option or planning an outage window against it is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_STATE_INVALID`.
11. **Cross-row coherence of rows that already exist**, HTTP 409: an outage window's declared predecessor must belong to a car of the same hoistway group (`ERR_CAR_GROUP_MISMATCH`); a staging reservation named in a receive call must belong to the procurement order being received (`ERR_RESERVATION_ORDER_MISMATCH`); a warranty transition's car must belong to the maintenance contract's project (`ERR_CONTRACT_PROJECT_MISMATCH`). This band guards `POST /outage-windows`, `POST /procurement-orders/{procurement_order_id}/receive` and `POST /warranty-transitions/{warranty_transition_id}/enroll`; the contract-versus-project comparison is made at enrolment only, that being the single write at which a transition and a contract are named together, so neither opening a transition nor activating a contract re-checks it.
12. **Code compliance and life-safety gates**, HTTP 409. These outrank every scheduling, capacity and money rule, because an elevator that is not code compliant may not carry passengers. The gates are: no code trigger may still be open at scope approval (`ERR_CODE_TRIGGER_OPEN`); an approved tier may not rank below the countersigned recommendation (`ERR_SCOPE_TIER_BELOW_RECOMMENDATION`); a group's service floor must still hold after an outage activation (`ERR_GROUP_SERVICE_FLOOR`); an occupied building's outage must declare a temporary service plan other than stairs only (`ERR_TEMP_SERVICE_PLAN_INADEQUATE`); a conditional inspection result must carry at least one punch-list item (`ERR_PUNCH_LIST_REQUIRED`); and a warranty transition may be opened only for a car that has been code accepted (`ERR_CAR_NOT_CODE_ACCEPTED`). This band guards `POST /scope-options/{scope_option_id}/approve`, `POST /outage-windows`, `POST /outage-windows/{outage_window_id}/activate`, `POST /inspection-appointments/{inspection_appointment_id}/record-result` and `POST /warranty-transitions`.
13. **Time and sequencing rules**, HTTP 409, checked after safety and before countable constraints, and internally in this order: first a declared predecessor window that is not yet finished, or a window that starts before its predecessor ends (`ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_COMPLETE`); then the hoistway group buffer conflict (`ERR_OUTAGE_BUFFER_CONFLICT`), so a pair that trips both reports the predecessor failure; then tenant notice that does not carry the required lead ahead of the window's `start_at` (`ERR_TENANT_NOTICE_LEAD_UNMET`); an inspection date that does not clear the jurisdiction's required lead (`ERR_INSPECTION_LEAD_UNMET`); and a window whose planned end has already been reached or passed on the logical clock (`ERR_WINDOW_EXPIRED`). This band guards `POST /outage-windows`, `POST /outage-windows/{outage_window_id}/activate`, `POST /inspection-appointments`, `POST /inspection-appointments/{inspection_appointment_id}/schedule` and `POST /inspection-appointments/{inspection_appointment_id}/reschedule`.
14. **Countable limits and posture locks**, HTTP 409: the project's concurrent outage cap (`ERR_CONCURRENCY_CAP_EXCEEDED`); a frozen project refusing new work (`ERR_PROJECT_FROZEN`); the per-appointment reschedule cap (`ERR_RESCHEDULE_LIMIT`); the per-option revision cap (`ERR_REVISION_LIMIT`); the requirement that every live procurement order be installed before acceptance testing begins (`ERR_PROCUREMENT_NOT_INSTALLED`); and the requirement that a contract activation have at least one enrolled warranty (`ERR_NO_ENROLLED_TRANSITIONS`). This band guards `POST /outage-windows/{outage_window_id}/activate`, `POST /staging-reservations`, `POST /inspection-appointments/{inspection_appointment_id}/reschedule`, `POST /scope-options/{scope_option_id}/revise`, `POST /elevator-cars/{car_id}/begin-testing`, `POST /maintenance-contracts/{maintenance_contract_id}/activate`, and — for the frozen-project lock alone — `POST /procurement-orders/{procurement_order_id}/issue` and `POST /procurement-orders/{procurement_order_id}/install`.
15. **Money and physical-capacity ceilings**, checked last, HTTP 409: the staging yard's pointwise capacity over the requested hold interval (`ERR_STAGING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`) and the project's contract value ceiling on the sum of approved option totals (`ERR_SCOPE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`). This band guards `POST /staging-reservations` and `POST /scope-options/{scope_option_id}/approve`.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on the Project Ledger

Two writes in this system are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because both of them move a shared, finite quantity that is recorded on the `ModernizationProject` row rather than on the resource being created.

Creating a scope option is the first. A `POST /scope-options` request must carry `expected_project_version` as a required, non-optional input field whose value is the `version` the client last observed on the target project — the value returned by reading that project. If the project's current `version` differs from the supplied `expected_project_version`, the create is refused with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The reason is that a newly created option legitimately supersedes the car's currently approved option and therefore moves `approved_scope_value_cents` on the project; a writer holding a stale view of that ledger would otherwise re-scope the car against a budget figure that no longer exists.

Creating a staging reservation is the second. A `POST /staging-reservations` request must likewise carry `expected_project_version` as a required, non-optional input field matching the project's current `version`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Staging square footage is a finite pool whose committed total lives on the project row as `staging_committed_sqft`, so a client that sized its hold against a version of the project it can no longer see would be sizing against capacity that has already been consumed or released.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field. Every other write is applied against whatever the current state happens to be, and the checks in §2.5 are what protect it.

### 2.7. Money, Percentages and the Order of Rounding

All monetary amounts in this API are integers denominated in whole cents; there are no fractional-cent balances anywhere. Wherever a monetary result comes out of a multiplication, it is rounded to a whole number of cents using round-half-up — a residue of exactly half a cent rounds away from zero, upward.

Percentage-valued fields — the jurisdiction's `permit_surcharge_pct` and a scope option's `escalation_pct` and `volume_discount_pct` — are exact decimal rates carried to four decimal places and are themselves never rounded; only the money that results from applying them is. `permit_surcharge_pct` is supplied by the operator, must lie between 0.0000 and 0.2500 inclusive, and defaults to 0.0000 when omitted. Composite condition scores (`provisional_score`, `final_score` and the car's `condition_score`) are decimals carried to two places, again round-half-up, and `notice_lead_hours` is likewise a two-decimal figure.

The single most consequential precision rule in this specification concerns *when* rounding happens relative to summation. Three aggregates in this system round each contributing line to whole cents first and only then add the rounded lines together: the code-upgrade total on a scope option, which rounds once per code trigger; the monthly maintenance total on a contract, which rounds once per covered car; and, by extension, any figure derived from those. Summing the unrounded products and rounding the single total is *not* the rule, is observably different by one cent per pair of half-cent residues, and is explicitly rejected. Conversely, the composite condition score has exactly one rounding step applied to the finished weighted sum — rounding each weighted component before adding them is not the rule and is likewise rejected.

### 2.8. The Project's Conserved Counters and Cascade Discipline

Four counters on `ModernizationProject` are not free-standing values that operations nudge up and down at will; they are definitions, and after any write completes each one must equal exactly what its definition says. `car_count` is the number of `ElevatorCar` rows whose `project_id` is this project. `approved_scope_value_cents` is the sum of `total_price_cents` over every scope option in status `APPROVED` whose car belongs to this project. `staging_committed_sqft` is the sum of `sqft` over every staging reservation in status `HELD` whose order chain — reservation to procurement order to scope option to car to project — resolves to this project; note that this ledger is deliberately interval-independent, counting every held reservation regardless of whether its interval is in the past, present or future. `active_outage_count` is the number of `OutageWindow` rows in status `ACTIVE` whose car belongs to this project. Two counters on `ElevatorGroup` are defined the same way: `car_count` is the number of cars whose `group_id` is this group, and `cars_in_service` is the number of cars in this group whose `work_status` is anything other than `OUTAGE_ACTIVE` — which means a car undergoing acceptance testing counts as in service, because testing runs on a car that has been returned to the hoistway for acceptance runs.

Because these are conserved quantities, every compensating action must return them to their pre-action value. Releasing a staging hold subtracts exactly the footage the hold added. Rescinding an approved scope option removes exactly the total it contributed to `approved_scope_value_cents`. Cancelling a procurement order releases whatever footage that order's hold was carrying. A ledger that drifts is a defect even when no individual operation looks wrong in isolation.

Mutation stamping follows the cascade rather than the request. When a write reaches a row — directly, or as the second, third or sixth link of a cascade — that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by 1. The `ModernizationProject` row in particular is touched by nearly every business write in this system, and each such write increments the project's `version` by exactly 1 regardless of how many child rows the write also modified. That single increment is what the two optimistic-concurrency guards in §2.6 measure. A minority of writes never reach the project row at all and therefore leave its `version` and `updated_at` untouched: declaring a hoistway group, submitting an equipment survey, planning an outage window, sending its tenant notice, cancelling it, requesting an inspection appointment, scheduling or rescheduling one, drafting a maintenance contract, and setting the logical clock. Conversely, an `ElevatorGroup` row is stamped only when the row itself is written — when its `min_cars_in_service` is adjusted — and never merely because its derived `car_count` or `cars_in_service` moved, so registering a car into a group, or taking one of its cars out of service, leaves that group's `updated_at` and `version` exactly as they were.

### 2.9. Downtime Posture: the Project-Level Delivery Gate

A project's `downtime_posture` is not an operator-set flag. It is derived, and it is recomputed after every write that can move a hoistway group's service level, the project's live outage count, or its committed staging footage: activating, completing or cancelling an outage window; creating or releasing a staging reservation; installing or cancelling a procurement order; rescinding a scope option; recording an inspection result; adjusting a group's in-service floor; and advancing the logical clock. It is also available as a computed read that re-evaluates the ladder and returns the posture together with the three inputs that decided it — `downtime_posture`, `active_outage_count`, `staging_committed_sqft`, and `groups_below_floor`, the count of this project's groups whose `cars_in_service` has fallen below their `min_cars_in_service` — without writing anything.

The posture is an ordered ladder and the first matching clause wins; no later clause can soften an earlier verdict. If any hoistway group of the project has fewer cars in service than its declared floor — that is, its `cars_in_service` is strictly less than its `min_cars_in_service` — the posture is `FROZEN`. Otherwise, if the project's `active_outage_count` has reached or passed its `max_concurrent_outages`, or if committed staging footage has passed the pressure line, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`, which is also the value a newly opened project starts with.

The staging pressure line sits at 85 percent of the yard and is crossed only strictly. To avoid any floating-point division, the comparison is stated as an integer cross-multiplication: `staging_committed_sqft` multiplied by 100 must be strictly greater than `staging_area_sqft` multiplied by 85 for the pressure clause to fire. A project sitting at exactly 85 percent of its yard is therefore not `RESTRICTED` on that account. The inputs to the whole ladder are all of the project's group rows together with their cars in every work status, plus the project's own two counters; cars belonging to other projects never contribute, and a project with no groups at all can never be frozen by the first clause.

The two non-`OPEN` postures bite differently. `FROZEN` is a hard lock: while a project is frozen, activating an outage window, creating a staging hold, issuing a procurement order, installing staged equipment, and returning a car to acceptance testing are all refused with `ERR_PROJECT_FROZEN`. `RESTRICTED` is much narrower — it does not by itself block a staging hold, and the concurrency cap it reflects is enforced separately by `ERR_CONCURRENCY_CAP_EXCEEDED`; its one substantive effect is on temporary service plans in occupied buildings, described in §3.6.

### 2.10. Two Interval Geometries: the Staging Yard and the Group Outage Buffer

This system schedules two scarce resources over time intervals, and it does so with deliberately different geometry. Implementers should treat them as unrelated algorithms that happen to share a vocabulary.

The **staging yard** is an additive, pointwise capacity over half-open intervals. Each staging reservation occupies the yard from `hold_start` inclusive to `hold_end` exclusive; the interval is written closed at the start and open at the end. Two holds overlap when the first starts strictly before the second ends and the second starts strictly before the first ends, which means a hold that begins at exactly the instant another hold ends does not overlap it at all. Adjacency is legal, touching holds may reuse the same square footage, and the capacity test is a sum over all simultaneously held footage rather than a pairwise veto. The scope of the pool is the whole project.

The **group outage buffer** is a pairwise, buffer-inflated conflict over closed intervals. Two outage windows on cars in the same hoistway group conflict when each one's start falls strictly before the other's end extended forward by the project's `outage_buffer_hours`. Because the buffer inflates both ends, adjacency is *illegal*: back-to-back outages in one group are rejected, and one single conflicting sibling is fatal — there is no summation and no capacity to spread across. The scope of the check is the `ElevatorGroup`, not the project, so windows on cars of a different group never conflict even when they overlap perfectly in time. The status filters differ too: the staging test looks only at reservations in status `HELD`, while the outage test looks only at windows in status `PLANNED` or `ACTIVE`.

### 2.11. Two Independent Status Dimensions on One Car

Every `ElevatorCar` advances along two status dimensions at once, and they never move each other. `work_status` is the physical delivery dimension: it runs `SURVEY_PENDING`, `SURVEYED`, `SCOPED`, `PROCURING`, `OUTAGE_ACTIVE`, `TESTING`, `ACCEPTED`, `IN_WARRANTY`, `UNDER_CONTRACT`, starting at `SURVEY_PENDING` and terminating at `UNDER_CONTRACT`. `compliance_status` is the code-compliance dimension: it takes the values `NOT_EVALUATED`, `TRIGGERS_OPEN`, `TRIGGERS_CLEARED` and `CODE_ACCEPTED`, starting at `NOT_EVALUATED`, and it is cyclic — a car can return to `TRIGGERS_OPEN` from `CODE_ACCEPTED` and can be reset all the way to `NOT_EVALUATED`.

The separation is strict. Physical delivery events — submitting a scope option, activating an outage, completing an outage, returning to testing, entering warranty — move `work_status` and leave `compliance_status` exactly where it was. Code-compliance events — synthesizing a trigger set, pricing a trigger, waiving a trigger — move `compliance_status` and leave `work_status` exactly where it was. Only three operations move both, and each is called out explicitly where it occurs: recording an inspection result, rescinding an approved scope option, and creating a scope option that supersedes an already approved one.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Delivery: the Jurisdiction, the Project, the Groups and the Cars

Before any building can be modernized, the authority having jurisdiction that will police the work must be on file. A `SystemOperator` registers one by supplying its display `name` and the `code_edition_year` of the elevator safety code it enforces, which must fall between 2000 and 2030 inclusive, together with three code posture flags: `fire_service_required`, the `seismic_zone` — a seismic design category between 0 and 4 inclusive — and `door_protection_required`. Four further settings are optional and take declared defaults when omitted. `inspection_lead_days` is the minimum number of whole days that must separate the logical clock from any requested or rescheduled inspection date, and defaults to 10. `witness_test_required` defaults to false; when a jurisdiction sets it true, an inspection request that does not state otherwise is opened with a witnessed acceptance test requested. `reinspection_extension_hours` is the number of hours a reopened outage window gains when an inspection is recorded as a failure, and defaults to 24 — one full day of extra outage as the standing price of a failed acceptance test. `permit_surcharge_pct` is the permit surcharge applied to each priced code trigger and defaults to 0.0000. Jurisdictions can be listed and optionally narrowed by `code_edition_year`.

A `ProjectManager` then opens a modernization project against one of those jurisdictions. The payload names the governing `jurisdiction_id`, the `building_name` the work is delivered in, and the `occupancy_type`, which is either `OCCUPIED` or `VACANT` and determines how forgiving the temporary-service rules will be. Three physical and commercial ceilings follow: `contract_value_cents`, the contracted ceiling that the sum of approved scope totals may not exceed; `staging_area_sqft`, the on-site staging floor area available for long-lead equipment, which must be at least 1; and `max_concurrent_outages`, the ceiling on simultaneously active outage windows across the whole project, which must also be at least 1. Two governance parameters are optional: `outage_buffer_hours`, the separation required between two outage windows on cars of the same hoistway group, defaulting to 4; and `tenant_notice_lead_hours`, the minimum advance notice an outage window must carry before it may be activated, defaulting to 72 — three full days, which is what most residential leases assume. A new project opens in phase `SURVEY` with a `downtime_posture` of `OPEN` and all four of its counters — `car_count`, `approved_scope_value_cents`, `staging_committed_sqft` and `active_outage_count` — at zero. Projects can be listed and narrowed by `jurisdiction_id` or by `status`, and a single project read returns the derived counters together with the current `version` that callers hand back as `expected_project_version` on the two concurrency-guarded writes.

The project's own `status` is never set directly; it is bubbled up from the child aggregates and moves through `SURVEY`, `SCOPING`, `EXECUTION`, `CLOSEOUT` and finally `COMPLETED`, which is terminal. `SURVEY → SCOPING` fires on the project's *first* countersigned equipment survey and only then: the project must still be in `SURVEY` and the countersigned survey's car must belong to this project. `SCOPING → EXECUTION` fires on the project's first scope approval, and requires that the project still be in `SCOPING`. `EXECUTION → CLOSEOUT` fires when an inspection is ruled either passed or conditional and, at that moment, the project has at least one car registered and every one of its cars stands at `ACCEPTED`, `IN_WARRANTY` or `UNDER_CONTRACT`. `CLOSEOUT → EXECUTION` is the revert leg: when an inspection is ruled failed while the project sits in `CLOSEOUT` and the failing appointment's car has consequently reverted to `OUTAGE_ACTIVE`, the project drops back to `EXECUTION` so the compound aggregate stays honest. `CLOSEOUT → COMPLETED` fires when a maintenance contract is activated while the project is in `CLOSEOUT` and that contract's `cars_covered` equals the project's `car_count`. Every one of these five phase moves refreshes the project's `updated_at` and increments its `version`.

Governance parameters can be retuned mid-flight. A `ProjectManager` may adjust any of `max_concurrent_outages`, `outage_buffer_hours`, `tenant_notice_lead_hours` and `contract_value_cents` on an existing project; all four are optional, an omitted one is left exactly as it stood, a payload naming none of them is still an accepted adjustment, and the adjustment is accepted in every project phase, `COMPLETED` included. The adjustment recomputes the project's downtime posture and increments the project's `version` by 1. Tightening `max_concurrent_outages` while outages are running can therefore move a project into `RESTRICTED` with no outage having changed at all.

Hoistway groups come next. A group is a bank of cars sharing a lobby and a dispatcher, and it is declared with the owning `project_id`, a `group_label` such as "Low Rise A", and `min_cars_in_service` — the service floor, the number of cars in that bank that must remain available to tenants, which may be zero. A new group starts with `car_count` and `cars_in_service` both at zero. Groups can be listed and narrowed by `project_id`. Either a `ProjectManager` or the building's `BuildingRepresentative` may raise or lower a group's `min_cars_in_service` later — a building owner tightening the service commitment for a busy retail season, for instance — and that single change recomputes the project's downtime posture and increments the project's `version` by 1. Raising the floor above the number of cars currently in service moves the project to `FROZEN` immediately, with no outage having been touched.

Cars are registered into a group by a `ProjectManager`, one row per physical elevator to be modernized. The payload carries the owning `project_id` and `group_id`, the `car_designation` painted on the car such as "Car 3", the `rated_load_lbs` capacity of the existing car which must be at least 500, the `controller_type_legacy` found on site — `RELAY`, `SOLID_STATE_ANALOG` or `MICROPROCESSOR` — the existing `machine_type`, which is `GEARED`, `GEARLESS` or `MRL_GEARLESS`, and a `duty_rank` of at least 1. The duty rank is the operator's sequencing preference within the group, lower numbers going out of service first; it is pure ordering input and never an identity or a foreign key. A newly registered car stands at `work_status` `SURVEY_PENDING` and `compliance_status` `NOT_EVALUATED`, with `condition_score`, `recommended_scope_tier` and `approved_scope_option_id` all null. Registering the car increments its group's `car_count` and `cars_in_service` by 1 each, increments the project's `car_count` by 1 and the project's `version` by 1, and recomputes the project's downtime posture. The two foreign keys are checked for resolvability only and never against each other, so a car may legally be registered with a `group_id` whose group belongs to a different project; the group's counters then follow the car's `group_id` while the project's `car_count` follows its `project_id`. Cars can be listed and narrowed by `project_id`, `group_id`, `work_status` or `compliance_status`, and a single car read returns both status dimensions along with its condition score. Registering a car into a project that has already reached `COMPLETED` is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.2. Surveying the Existing Equipment and Fixing the Binding Recommendation

A `SurveyTechnician` opens the equipment on site and records one condition survey per car, naming the `car_id` and grading three components: `controller_condition`, `machine_condition` and `door_equipment_condition`. Each grade is drawn from the same ordinal scale — `EXCELLENT`, `GOOD`, `FAIR`, `POOR`, `FAILED` or `NOT_OBSERVED` — where `NOT_OBSERVED` is the honest answer when a machine room was locked or a door operator could not be exercised. The survey records the submitting technician's `X-User-ID` as `surveyed_by_user_id`, opens in status `SUBMITTED` with `countersigned_by_user_id` null, and immediately computes a provisional score. Surveys can be listed and narrowed by `car_id` or by `status`.

The scoring converts each grade to points on a fixed scale: `EXCELLENT` is worth 100 points, `GOOD` 80, `FAIR` 55, `POOR` 25 and `FAILED` 0. The three components carry fixed weights — the controller assessment carries forty-five hundredths of the total, the hoist machine thirty hundredths, and the door equipment the remaining twenty-five hundredths. In the ordinary case where all three components were observed, the composite is the sum of each component's points multiplied by its own weight, and that finished sum is rounded half-up to two decimal places in exactly one rounding step. Only the three grades recorded on the survey row being scored contribute; no other survey of the same car participates, including any survey that has been voided.

`NOT_OBSERVED` is where the two scoring bases part company, and the difference is the single most consequential ambiguity in this domain. Under the **observed-only** basis, a component graded `NOT_OBSERVED` is dropped from both the numerator and the denominator: the score is the weighted sum over the observed components divided by the sum of the weights of those observed components, and that quotient is then rounded half-up to two decimal places. The observed weights are effectively renormalized, so a machine nobody could look at cannot drag the score down. When all three components are `NOT_OBSERVED` the denominator would be zero, and the score is then defined to be exactly 0.00 — an empty-set fallback, never an error and never null. Under the **imputed-failed** basis, a component graded `NOT_OBSERVED` scores 0 points and *keeps* its weight in the denominator, which stays at the full unity of the three weights combined. Anything the surveyor did not open is treated as failed for acceptance purposes. For identical grades the two bases return different numbers whenever at least one component is `NOT_OBSERVED`, and they can select different recommended tiers.

The score selects a recommended modernization tier by descending threshold, with each boundary inclusive at its lower edge. A score of 80.00 or more recommends `CONTROLLER_ONLY`. Otherwise a score of 60.00 or more recommends `CONTROLLER_DOOR`. Otherwise a score of 35.00 or more recommends `CONTROLLER_MACHINE`. Anything below 35.00 recommends `FULL_REPLACEMENT`. The comparison is performed against the already-rounded two-decimal score with no further rounding, so exactly 80.00 recommends `CONTROLLER_ONLY` while 79.99 recommends `CONTROLLER_DOOR`.

At submission the survey is scored on the observed-only basis and the result is written to `provisional_score`, with the tier it implies written to the survey's `recommended_scope_tier`. That provisional recommendation is advisory only and gates nothing. The survey's `final_score` and the car's `condition_score` and `recommended_scope_tier` all remain null at this point.

Countersignature is what makes the assessment binding, and it is deliberately a second pair of eyes: either another `SurveyTechnician` or a `ProjectManager` may countersign, and the caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from the survey's `surveyed_by_user_id` or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The survey must be in status `SUBMITTED`; countersigning a survey that is already `COUNTERSIGNED`, or one that has been voided, is refused with `ERR_SURVEY_STATE_INVALID`. On success the survey moves `SUBMITTED → COUNTERSIGNED`, stores the countersigning caller's identifier as `countersigned_by_user_id`, recomputes the composite on the imputed-failed basis into `final_score`, derives `recommended_scope_tier` from that final score, copies the final score onto the car as `condition_score` and the tier onto the car as `recommended_scope_tier`, advances the car `SURVEY_PENDING → SURVEYED` on the work dimension while leaving `compliance_status` untouched, moves the project `SURVEY → SCOPING` if this is the project's first countersignature, and increments the project's `version` by 1.

Surveys are replaceable rather than editable. When a technician submits a fresh survey for a car whose `work_status` is `SURVEY_PENDING` or `SURVEYED`, any prior survey of that car in status `SUBMITTED` or `COUNTERSIGNED` becomes `VOID`, the car's `condition_score` and `recommended_scope_tier` are reset to null, and the car returns to `work_status` `SURVEY_PENDING` — so a car whose latest survey has been voided has no score at all and must be countersigned afresh before it can be scoped. Submitting a survey for a car that has already advanced past `SURVEYED` is refused with `ERR_CAR_STATE_INVALID`. Both `SUBMITTED` and `COUNTERSIGNED` are exit points into `VOID`, and `VOID` and `COUNTERSIGNED` are terminal.

Because the two bases disagree and the disagreement is expensive, a read-only preview scores an existing survey under an explicitly named `basis` without writing anything. The `basis` query parameter takes either `OBSERVED_ONLY` or `IMPUTED_FAILED`, and any other value is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The preview echoes the `basis` it applied, the resulting `score` to two decimal places, the `recommended_scope_tier` that score selects, and `observed_weight_total` — the sum of the weights that actually entered the denominator, which is the full unity under the imputed-failed basis and something smaller under the observed-only basis whenever a component went unobserved.

### 3.3. Shaping a Scope and Discovering What the Code Will Demand

With a countersigned assessment in hand, a `ProjectManager` prices a modernization scope for the car. The payload names the `car_id`, declares the `tier` of intervention — `CONTROLLER_ONLY`, `CONTROLLER_DOOR`, `CONTROLLER_MACHINE` or `FULL_REPLACEMENT` — states the contractor's `base_price_cents` for that tier before any code upgrades, escalation or discount, and carries `expected_project_version` for the concurrency guard described in §2.6. The car must have a countersigned survey on file; scoping a car with no countersigned survey is refused with `ERR_CAR_STATE_INVALID`, and creating an option under a project that has reached `COMPLETED` is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_STATE_INVALID` — in that internal order, the car's own precondition being the one reported when both fail, and both being reached only once the `expected_project_version` guard has passed. The four tiers form an invasiveness ladder whose ranks matter later: `CONTROLLER_ONLY` ranks 1, `CONTROLLER_DOOR` ranks 2, `CONTROLLER_MACHINE` ranks 3 and `FULL_REPLACEMENT` ranks 4.

A new option is created in status `DRAFT` with `revision_count` at zero and `rejection_note` null, and it takes over as the car's one live option: any earlier option for the same `car_id` sitting in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED` or `APPROVED` becomes `SUPERSEDED`, which is a terminal status. When the option being superseded was itself `APPROVED`, three further things happen: its `total_price_cents` leaves the project's `approved_scope_value_cents`, the car's `approved_scope_option_id` is cleared, and the car moves `PROCURING → SCOPED` on the work dimension. The new option takes over the `SCOPED` slot directly, so no separate submission is needed to re-enter `SCOPED`. This legitimate supersession of an approved option is precisely why option creation is guarded against a stale project view. Creating the option increments the project's `version` by 1. Options can be listed and narrowed by `car_id` or `status`, and that list is also how a caller discovers which option a car's `approved_scope_option_id` points at, since that pointer is a denormalized convenience rather than a declared relationship. A single option read returns the full pricing breakdown.

Immediately after the option row is inserted, and exactly once for that option, the system synthesizes the option's code obligations from the governing jurisdiction, the declared tier, the car's `machine_type` and the `door_equipment_condition` recorded on the car's latest countersigned survey. Surveys still in `SUBMITTED`, and surveys that have been voided, are excluded from that input entirely. Four predicates are evaluated, and they are emitted in exactly the order given below so the resulting rows have a deterministic `created_at` ordering.

First, a trigger of `trigger_type` `FIRE_SERVICE_PHASE_I_II` is emitted whenever the jurisdiction's `fire_service_required` is true, regardless of tier, carrying the `source_rule` text `ASME A17.1 2.27.3 Phase I/II firefighters service`. Second, `SEISMIC_RESTRAINT` is emitted when the jurisdiction's `seismic_zone` is 2 or higher *and* the declared tier is one of the two machine-touching tiers, `CONTROLLER_MACHINE` or `FULL_REPLACEMENT`, with `source_rule` `ASME A17.1 8.4 seismic restraint of machine and counterweight` — a controller-only job in an earthquake zone does not disturb the machine and therefore does not trip the restraint requirement. Third, `DOOR_PROTECTION_REOPENING` is emitted when the jurisdiction's `door_protection_required` is true and the countersigned survey graded the door equipment `FAIR`, `POOR`, `FAILED` or `NOT_OBSERVED` — that is, anything short of `GOOD` or `EXCELLENT` — with `source_rule` `ASME A17.1 2.13.5 door reopening protective device`. Fourth, `MACHINE_BRAKE_MONITOR` is emitted when the tier is `FULL_REPLACEMENT`, or when the tier is `CONTROLLER_MACHINE` and the car's existing `machine_type` is `GEARED`, with `source_rule` `ASME A17.1 2.26.10 brake and rope monitoring`. Every emitted trigger is created against its owning `scope_option_id`, in status `REQUIRED`, with `cost_cents` of 0 and `waiver_reference` null.

The synthesis result drives the car's compliance dimension. When at least one trigger was emitted, the car's `compliance_status` is set to `TRIGGERS_OPEN` — ordinarily the move `NOT_EVALUATED → TRIGGERS_OPEN`, but the assignment is unconditional and overwrites whatever the dimension held, so a car standing at `TRIGGERS_CLEARED` or even `CODE_ACCEPTED` on account of the option this creation supersedes is driven back to `TRIGGERS_OPEN` — and its `work_status` is left exactly where it was; approval is then blocked by `ERR_CODE_TRIGGER_OPEN` until every emitted trigger has left status `REQUIRED`. When no predicate held and zero trigger rows were created — a lenient jurisdiction with no fire service requirement, seismic zone below 2, no door protection requirement, and a controller-only tier — the car's `compliance_status` is set to `TRIGGERS_CLEARED` — ordinarily the jump straight from `NOT_EVALUATED`, and again unconditionally from whatever it held — with `work_status` unchanged. The empty set is a legitimate outcome, not a skipped step: the option's `code_upgrade_cents` is then a genuine zero computed over an empty set.

Code triggers are created only as a cascade of option creation and are never returned in a creation response, so listing them — optionally narrowed by `scope_option_id`, `status` or `trigger_type` — is the only discovery channel. A trigger travels through `REQUIRED`, `PRICED`, `WAIVED` and `SATISFIED`; none of these is terminal, because a failed reinspection can drive a trigger back to `REQUIRED`.

A `ProjectManager` prices a trigger by attaching its `cost_cents`, the pre-surcharge cost quoted for the upgrade. The trigger must be in status `REQUIRED`; pricing a trigger that is not `REQUIRED` is refused with `ERR_CODE_TRIGGER_STATE_INVALID`. On success the cost is stored, the trigger moves `REQUIRED → PRICED`, the owning option's `code_upgrade_cents` and its downstream pricing are recomputed but *only* while that option's status is `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED`, and the project's `version` increments by 1.

An `AHJInspector` — and only the inspector for the jurisdiction governing that car's project — may instead record a waiver, supplying the `waiver_reference` naming the jurisdiction's waiver document. A waiver is accepted on a trigger in status `REQUIRED` or in status `PRICED`; waiving a trigger that is already `WAIVED` or already `SATISFIED` is refused with `ERR_CODE_TRIGGER_STATE_INVALID`. On success the reference is stored, the trigger moves to `WAIVED`, and its cost is excluded from `code_upgrade_cents` — a waived trigger contributes nothing to the option price even if it had already been priced, so waiving a priced trigger makes its surcharged amount drop out of the total while the option is still `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED`. The project's `version` increments by 1.

Either operation can be the one that clears the car. After a pricing or a waiver, if zero triggers of the option that owns the priced or waived trigger remain in status `REQUIRED`, the car moves `TRIGGERS_OPEN → TRIGGERS_CLEARED` with its `work_status` untouched — the test is scoped to that trigger's own option, which is ordinarily the car's live option and is not re-resolved to it when the trigger happens to hang off a superseded one. Until that happens the car stays `TRIGGERS_OPEN`, and the owner cannot approve the scope.

### 3.4. What a Scope Option Actually Costs

Option pricing is a chain of four figures — code upgrades, escalation, subtotal, discount — and the order in which they are computed is pinned, because each step consumes the previous step's output. Reading the rules in a different order produces a different total for the same inputs, and only one order is correct.

**Step one: the code-upgrade total.** `code_upgrade_cents` aggregates only those code triggers of this option whose status is `PRICED`. Triggers still in `REQUIRED` contribute nothing (they also block approval outright), triggers in `WAIVED` contribute nothing regardless of the `cost_cents` they may be carrying, and triggers in `SATISFIED` retain the contribution they last had when priced. For each contributing trigger, its `cost_cents` is multiplied by one plus the jurisdiction's `permit_surcharge_pct`, and **that per-trigger product is rounded half-up to whole cents before anything is added**. Only then are the rounded per-trigger lines summed. The alternative order — summing the unrounded surcharged amounts and rounding the single total once — is not the rule and can differ by one cent for each pair of half-cent residues; it is explicitly rejected. In a jurisdiction whose `permit_surcharge_pct` is 0.0000 the multiplication is the identity, per-line and total rounding coincide, and `code_upgrade_cents` is simply the plain sum of the priced costs. In a surcharge-bearing jurisdiction the per-line rounding becomes observable: two lines whose surcharged amounts each carry a fractional residue below half a cent each round down individually, while their combined residue would have rounded up, so the pinned order returns a total one cent lower. An option with no priced triggers has `code_upgrade_cents` of 0.

**Step two: the pre-escalation base.** The option's `base_price_cents` and its `code_upgrade_cents` are added together. Call this the pre-escalation amount.

**Step three: the escalation rate.** `max_lead_time_days` is the largest `lead_time_days` across this option's procurement orders whose status is anything other than `CANCELLED`; when that set is empty — no order was ever raised, or every order has been cancelled — `max_lead_time_days` is 0. Orders belonging to any other option of the same car never contribute. The rate `escalation_pct` is then selected by which bucket the longest lead falls into: at most 60 days carries 0.0000; from 61 through 120 days inclusive carries 0.0300; from 121 through 180 days inclusive carries 0.0650; and 181 days or more carries 0.1100. Because cancelled orders are excluded even when they were the longest-lead item, cancelling the long-lead controller can drop the option into a cheaper escalation bucket.

**Step four: the escalation fee.** `escalation_fee_cents` is the pre-escalation amount multiplied by `escalation_pct`, rounded half-up to whole cents. This is the first of exactly two rounding steps in the pricing chain.

**Step five: the post-escalation subtotal.** The pre-escalation amount plus `escalation_fee_cents`.

**Step six: the volume discount rate.** This is the trap. `volume_discount_pct` is keyed on the **post-escalation subtotal** — not on `base_price_cents`, and not on the pre-escalation amount. A subtotal below 15,000,000 cents (that is, below one hundred fifty thousand dollars) earns 0.0000. A subtotal at or above that line but below 30,000,000 cents earns 0.0200. A subtotal at or above 30,000,000 cents but below 60,000,000 cents earns 0.0450. A subtotal of 60,000,000 cents or more earns 0.0700. The pinned keying is observable whenever escalation carries the amount across a discount breakpoint: an option whose pre-escalation amount sits just under a breakpoint but whose subtotal lands at or above it earns the higher discount tier, where keying on the pre-escalation amount instead would award no discount or a lower one and produce a strictly larger total.

**Step seven: the discount.** `volume_discount_cents` is the post-escalation subtotal multiplied by `volume_discount_pct`, rounded half-up to whole cents. This is the second and last rounding step.

**Step eight: the total.** `total_price_cents` is the post-escalation subtotal minus `volume_discount_cents` — equivalently, `base_price_cents` plus `code_upgrade_cents` plus `escalation_fee_cents` less `volume_discount_cents`. Percentages are exact four-decimal constants throughout and are never themselves rounded.

The whole chain re-runs whenever one of its inputs moves while the option is still negotiable: on option creation, on trigger pricing, on trigger waiver, on option revision, when a procurement order is raised against the option, and when one of its procurement orders is cancelled. Once an option reaches `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED` the pricing is frozen — later procurement activity, however dramatic, does not move an approved price. An option with no live procurement orders is a fully priced option, not an unpriced one: its `max_lead_time_days` is the empty-set fallback of 0, its `escalation_pct` is 0.0000, its `escalation_fee_cents` is 0, and its discount tier is keyed on base plus code upgrades alone.

### 3.5. Negotiating the Scope with the Building Owner

A priced option is negotiated in a loop between the contractor and the owner. The option's own lifecycle runs `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, then either `APPROVED` or `REJECTED`, with `REJECTED` able to return to `DRAFT`; `SUPERSEDED` is the single terminal status and can be reached from `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED` or `APPROVED`.

Submission is the `ProjectManager`'s act. The option must be in `DRAFT`; submitting an option in any other status is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_OPTION_STATE_INVALID`. On success the option moves `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, the car advances `SURVEYED → SCOPED` on the work dimension with `compliance_status` untouched, and the project's `version` increments by 1.

Approval belongs exclusively to the `BuildingRepresentative`, whose `X-User-ID` must be the `id` of the project owning the car. The option must be in `SUBMITTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_OPTION_STATE_INVALID`. Three business gates then apply, and their relative order is fixed because the safety band outranks the money band: first, zero code triggers of the option may remain in status `REQUIRED`, and any still-open trigger refuses the approval with `ERR_CODE_TRIGGER_OPEN`; second, the option's declared `tier` must rank at or above the car's countersigned `recommended_scope_tier` on the four-step invasiveness ladder, and a tier ranking below the recommendation is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_TIER_BELOW_RECOMMENDATION` — a car whose imputed-failed score recommended `FULL_REPLACEMENT` cannot be discharged with a controller swap, however much cheaper that would be; third, and only after both safety gates have passed, the project's `approved_scope_value_cents` plus this option's `total_price_cents` must not rise strictly above the project's `contract_value_cents`, and an approval that would push the ledger strictly past the contracted ceiling is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. Landing exactly on the ceiling is permitted.

Upon a successful approval the option moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and its price is frozen from that instant. The option's `total_price_cents` is added to the project's `approved_scope_value_cents`, the car's `approved_scope_option_id` is set to this option, the car advances `SCOPED → PROCURING`, the project moves `SCOPING → EXECUTION` if this is the project's first approval, and the project's `version` increments by 1.

Rejection is also the representative's, and it carries a reason: the payload supplies `rejection_note`, which is stored on the option. The option must be in `SUBMITTED`. The option moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, the car's `work_status` is left exactly where it is — the car remains `SCOPED`, because nothing physical has changed — and the project's `version` increments by 1.

A rejected option is reopened for repricing by the `ProjectManager`. The option must be in `REJECTED`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_OPTION_STATE_INVALID`, and its `revision_count` must be strictly below 3; a fourth revision attempt is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT`, after which a fresh option must be created instead. A revision may optionally supply a replacement `base_price_cents`, which is substituted when present and left alone when omitted. The option moves `REJECTED → DRAFT`, `revision_count` increases by 1, `rejection_note` is cleared back to null, the car steps back `SCOPED → SURVEYED` on the work dimension, and both the code-upgrade total and the full pricing chain of §3.4 are recomputed. The synthesized trigger set itself is *not* re-derived — a revision reprices the same code obligations rather than rediscovering them. The project's `version` increments by 1.

Rescission is the heavy instrument, used when the owner defers a car to a later phase after the scope was already approved. Only a `ProjectManager` may rescind, and only an option in status `APPROVED`; rescinding anything else is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_OPTION_STATE_INVALID`. A rescission unwinds the whole procurement chain in six linked steps, and every step must be applied — the later links are consequences of the earlier ones, not optional extras.

The first link moves the option `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED` and removes its `total_price_cents` from the project's `approved_scope_value_cents`. The second link, caused by the first, cancels every procurement order of that option sitting in `DRAFT`, `ORDERED` or `STAGED`; orders already `INSTALLED` are left untouched, because the equipment is in the hoistway. The third link, caused by the second, releases every staging reservation of a cancelled order that was still in status `HELD`, and each released reservation's `sqft` leaves the project's `staging_committed_sqft`. The fourth link, caused by the third, recomputes the project's `downtime_posture` from the reduced staging commitment. The fifth link, caused by the first, moves the car `PROCURING → SCOPED` on the work dimension, clears its `approved_scope_option_id` back to null, and — because no option in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED` remains for that car after the rescission — resets `compliance_status` all the way back to `NOT_EVALUATED`, from `TRIGGERS_CLEARED` or from `TRIGGERS_OPEN` alike. The sixth link, caused by the fifth, cancels every outage window of that car still in status `PLANNED`. Any inspection appointment for that car still sitting in `REQUESTED` is cancelled as well, since there is no longer an approved scope to inspect. The project's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.6. Long-Lead Procurement and the Square Footage It Competes For

Controllers and machines are not off-the-shelf items; they are built to order and they arrive months later, in crates that have to sit somewhere. A `ProjectManager` raises a long-lead order against a scope option by naming the `scope_option_id`, the `item_type` — one of `CONTROLLER`, `MACHINE`, `DOOR_OPERATOR` or `POWER_UNIT` — the quoted `lead_time_days`, which may be zero or more, and the `required_staging_sqft` the delivered item will occupy on site, which may also be zero. The order is created in status `DRAFT` with `ordered_at`, `promised_delivery_date` and `staging_reservation_id` all null. Raising the order re-runs the option's pricing chain when the option is still `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` — this is how a long lead re-tiers the escalation — while an `APPROVED` option's price is left exactly as it was. The project's `version` increments by 1. Orders can be listed and narrowed by `scope_option_id`, `status` or `item_type`.

Issuing the order to the factory requires the parent option to have reached `APPROVED`; issuing against an option that has not been approved is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_OPTION_NOT_APPROVED`. The order itself must be in `DRAFT`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROCUREMENT_STATE_INVALID` — and that target-first check runs ahead of the parent option's, so an order that has already left `DRAFT` under an unapproved option reports `ERR_PROCUREMENT_STATE_INVALID` rather than `ERR_SCOPE_OPTION_NOT_APPROVED` — and the project must not be frozen, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_FROZEN`. On success the order moves `DRAFT → ORDERED`, `ordered_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `promised_delivery_date` is derived as `ordered_at` advanced by `lead_time_days` whole days, and the project's `version` increments by 1. An order's lifecycle runs `DRAFT`, `ORDERED`, `STAGED`, `INSTALLED`, with `CANCELLED` reachable from any of the first three; `INSTALLED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal.

Staging holds are booked separately, and they are the second concurrency-guarded write in this API. A `ProjectManager` or a `ServiceManager` reserves yard space by naming the `procurement_order_id` whose delivered item will occupy it, the `sqft` held, which must be at least 1, the `hold_start` and `hold_end` bounding the occupation with `hold_end` strictly after `hold_start`, and `expected_project_version`. The capacity test runs before the row is inserted and it is additive and pointwise over half-open intervals, as set out in §2.10: the candidate set is every staging reservation in status `HELD` whose ownership chain — through procurement order, scope option and car — resolves to this same project. Holds of any other project are excluded even when the two projects share a building, released reservations have already given their footage back and are excluded, and reservations whose order was cancelled have already been released by cascade and so are excluded too. For each instant within the new hold's own span, the footage of every candidate overlapping the new hold at that instant is summed; the peak of those sums across the new hold's span, plus the new hold's own `sqft`, is compared against the project's `staging_area_sqft`. The hold is refused with `ERR_STAGING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` only when that figure *strictly exceeds* the yard: a hold that fills the yard to exactly `staging_area_sqft` succeeds, and one additional square foot fails. Because the intervals are half-open, a new hold beginning at exactly the instant an existing hold ends sees zero overlap and has the whole yard available to it, even if the two holds together far exceed the yard's size; and a hold nested entirely inside a longer existing hold sees the longer hold's footage across its whole span, so nesting is never cheaper than partial overlap. A frozen project accepts no new hold at all, refusing it with `ERR_PROJECT_FROZEN`. An accepted hold is created in status `HELD` with `released_at` null, adds its `sqft` to the project's `staging_committed_sqft`, recomputes the project's `downtime_posture`, and increments the project's `version` by 1. Reservations can be listed and narrowed by `procurement_order_id` or `status`.

Receiving delivered equipment ties an order to a hold that already exists for it. The caller — a `ProjectManager` or a `ServiceManager` — supplies the `staging_reservation_id`, and three things must hold: the order must be in status `ORDERED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROCUREMENT_STATE_INVALID`; the named reservation must be in status `HELD`, one already `RELEASED` being refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_STATE_INVALID`; and the reservation must belong to the very order being received, or the request is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_ORDER_MISMATCH`. On success the order stores the reservation as its denormalized `staging_reservation_id` pointer, moves `ORDERED → STAGED`, and the project's `version` increments by 1.

Installation frees the yard. The order must be in `STAGED` and the project must not be frozen. On success the order moves `STAGED → INSTALLED`, every reservation of that order still in status `HELD` — ordinarily just the linked one — moves `HELD → RELEASED` with `released_at` stamped from the logical clock, the released `sqft` is subtracted from the project's `staging_committed_sqft`, the project's `downtime_posture` is recomputed against the freed capacity, and the project's `version` increments by 1.

An order that has not been installed can be cancelled by a `ProjectManager` from `DRAFT`, `ORDERED` or `STAGED`; cancelling an order that is already `INSTALLED` is refused with `ERR_PROCUREMENT_STATE_INVALID`. Cancellation moves the order to `CANCELLED`, releases any staging reservation of that order still in status `HELD` and subtracts its `sqft` from the project's `staging_committed_sqft`, re-runs the option's pricing chain when the parent option is still `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` — which is where cancelling the long-lead item can drop the escalation tier and change `max_lead_time_days` — recomputes the project's `downtime_posture`, and increments the project's `version` by 1.

A hold can also be given back early, by either a `ProjectManager` or a `ServiceManager`, without touching the order. The reservation must be in status `HELD`; releasing a reservation that is already `RELEASED` is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_STATE_INVALID`. Release is the exact compensating action for the original hold: the reservation moves `HELD → RELEASED`, `released_at` is stamped from the logical clock, precisely the `sqft` that the hold added is subtracted from the project's `staging_committed_sqft` so the ledger returns to its pre-hold value, the `downtime_posture` is recomputed, and the project's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.7. Taking Cars Out of Service, One at a Time

In an occupied building the outage plan is the part of the project the tenants actually experience, and it is planned car by car. A `ProjectManager` plans a window by naming the `car_id`, the planned `start_at` and `end_at` with the end strictly after the start, and the `temporary_service_plan` that will be in force while the car is down — `STAIRS_ONLY`, `ADJACENT_CAR` or `ATTENDANT_SERVICE`. Optionally the window declares a `predecessor_window_id`, the self-referential sequencing link that chains one car's outage behind another's; it is left null for the first car in a sequence. A planned window is created in status `PLANNED` with `extension_hours` at zero, `notice_count` at zero, and `advance_notice_sent_at`, `notice_lead_hours`, `activated_at` and `completed_at` all null. Windows can be listed and narrowed by `car_id`, by `status`, or by `predecessor_window_id` to walk the successors of a given window down the sequencing chain. A window cannot be planned against a project that has reached `COMPLETED`, which is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_STATE_INVALID`.

Three checks run at planning time. If the building's `occupancy_type` is `OCCUPIED`, the window may not declare `STAIRS_ONLY` as its temporary service plan — asking residents of an occupied tower to walk is not a plan — and such a request is refused with `ERR_TEMP_SERVICE_PLAN_INADEQUATE`. If a `predecessor_window_id` is declared, the predecessor must belong to a car of the same hoistway group as the new window's car, and a predecessor from another group is refused with `ERR_CAR_GROUP_MISMATCH`; the new window's `start_at` must also fall at or after the predecessor's `end_at`, and a window that starts before its predecessor finishes is refused with `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_COMPLETE`, which is reported ahead of any buffer conflict the same overlapping pair would also raise. Finally the group buffer conflict of §2.10 is evaluated against every window in status `PLANNED` or `ACTIVE` whose car shares the new window's group, the new window's own car included: a conflict exists when the new window's `start_at` falls strictly before a sibling's `end_at` extended forward by the project's `outage_buffer_hours`, and simultaneously that sibling's `start_at` falls strictly before the new window's `end_at` extended forward by the same buffer. A single conflicting sibling is fatal and the plan is refused with `ERR_OUTAGE_BUFFER_CONFLICT`. Because both ends are inflated, two back-to-back windows in one group conflict even though their raw intervals merely touch. Windows in status `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` never conflict, so a finished outage never blocks the next car, and windows on cars of another group are excluded even when they overlap perfectly in time.

Tenant communication is a separate act, performed by either a `ProjectManager` or the `BuildingRepresentative`, and it is gated on nothing at all: a notice is accepted against a window in any status, `ACTIVE`, `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` included. Sending the notice for an upcoming outage stamps `advance_notice_sent_at` from the logical clock *on the first notice only* and increments `notice_count` by 1. Every later notice for the same window increments `notice_count` again but never moves `advance_notice_sent_at` — a reminder sent the day before does not reset the clock on a notice that went out three weeks ago. From the first notice onward the window carries `notice_lead_hours`, the number of hours from `advance_notice_sent_at` to the window's `start_at`, carried to two decimal places; before the first notice it is null.

Activation is where the car actually comes out of service, and it is the most heavily guarded write in the system. Either a `ProjectManager` or a `ServiceManager` may activate. The window must be in status `PLANNED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_OUTAGE_STATE_INVALID`. Then the safety gates: the group's `cars_in_service` counted *after* this activation must still be at or above the group's `min_cars_in_service`, and an activation that would drop the bank below its service floor is refused with `ERR_GROUP_SERVICE_FLOOR`; and unless the building's `occupancy_type` is `VACANT`, it must not be the case that the project's posture is `RESTRICTED` while this window's `temporary_service_plan` is `STAIRS_ONLY` — a stairs-only outage is tolerable in a comfortable project but not in a project already under pressure — with that combination refused by `ERR_TEMP_SERVICE_PLAN_INADEQUATE`. Then the temporal gates: any declared `predecessor_window_id` must point at a window whose status is `COMPLETED`, and a predecessor that has not finished refuses the activation with `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_COMPLETE`; the group buffer conflict is re-evaluated, because the sibling set may have changed since the window was planned, refusing with `ERR_OUTAGE_BUFFER_CONFLICT`; `advance_notice_sent_at` must not be null and the window's `notice_lead_hours` must be at or above the project's `tenant_notice_lead_hours`, with an unsent or late notice refused by `ERR_TENANT_NOTICE_LEAD_UNMET`; and the logical clock must still be strictly before the window's `end_at`, since a window whose planned end has already arrived or passed can no longer be activated and is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Then the countable gates: the project's `active_outage_count` must be strictly below its `max_concurrent_outages`, and an activation at the cap is refused with `ERR_CONCURRENCY_CAP_EXCEEDED`; and the project's `downtime_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, or the activation is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_FROZEN`.

An accepted activation moves the window `PLANNED → ACTIVE`, stamps `activated_at` from the logical clock, advances the car `PROCURING → OUTAGE_ACTIVE` on the work dimension while leaving `compliance_status` alone — a car standing anywhere other than `PROCURING` keeps the `work_status` it had, the activation itself never being refused on that account — refreshes the group's `cars_in_service` (which drops by one, since the car is no longer available) and the project's `active_outage_count` (which rises by one), recomputes the project's `downtime_posture`, and increments the project's `version` by 1.

Completing an outage closes the window but does *not* return the car to service. The window must be in status `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_OUTAGE_STATE_INVALID`. On success it moves `ACTIVE → COMPLETED`, `completed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the project's `active_outage_count` and `downtime_posture` are refreshed — a refresh that reaches the project row itself, so the project's `version` increments by 1. The car's `work_status` stays exactly at `OUTAGE_ACTIVE`: physically the hoistway is closed until the equipment has been proven, and the car leaves `OUTAGE_ACTIVE` only when it is returned to acceptance testing. `COMPLETED` is deliberately re-enterable rather than terminal, because a failed reinspection reopens it.

A planned window that will not be used is cancelled by a `ProjectManager`. The window must be in status `PLANNED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_OUTAGE_STATE_INVALID`; on success it moves `PLANNED → CANCELLED`, which is terminal, and it immediately stops participating in buffer-conflict detection, freeing its span and its buffer shoulders for the other cars in the group.

Returning a car from outage to acceptance testing is the bridge between the physical work and the jurisdiction's sign-off, and either a `ProjectManager` or a `ServiceManager` may perform it. Four conditions must hold. The car's `work_status` must be `OUTAGE_ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CAR_STATE_INVALID`. The car's latest outage window — the one with the greatest `completed_at`, ties broken by the earlier `created_at`, chosen among only those windows that carry a `completed_at` at all — must be in status `COMPLETED`; a car with no such window, or one whose latest has since been reopened to `ACTIVE` by a failed reinspection, is refused with `ERR_OUTAGE_STATE_INVALID`. The car's approved scope option must have at least one procurement order whose status is not `CANCELLED`, and every such live order must be in status `INSTALLED`; a car whose equipment is still in crates, and equally an approved option with no procurement order at all, is refused with `ERR_PROCUREMENT_NOT_INSTALLED` — an approved option with zero live orders is a failure, not a vacuous success. And the project's `downtime_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_FROZEN`. On success the car advances `OUTAGE_ACTIVE → TESTING`, the group's `cars_in_service` rises by one because a car in `TESTING` counts as in service, the project's `active_outage_count` and `downtime_posture` are refreshed, and the project's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.8. Presenting the Car to the Jurisdiction

Acceptance testing is coordinated with the authority having jurisdiction, and the jurisdiction sets the pace. A `ProjectManager` or a `ServiceManager` requests an inspection by naming the `car_id` and the `requested_date` the contractor is asking the authority for, optionally stating whether a witnessed acceptance test is wanted via `witness_requested`. When `witness_requested` is omitted it takes the governing jurisdiction's `witness_test_required` as its value, so a jurisdiction that always witnesses gets a witnessed test without the contractor having to remember. The requested date must clear the jurisdiction's lead: the number of whole days from the logical clock to the `requested_date`, obtained by taking the elapsed seconds and discarding any remainder short of a full 86,400-second day, must be at or above the jurisdiction's `inspection_lead_days`, and a date that does not clear it is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_LEAD_UNMET`. That same whole-day figure is snapshotted for audit as `lead_days_at_request`. A new appointment opens in status `REQUESTED` with `scheduled_at` null, `reschedule_count` at zero and `punch_list_count` at zero, and its `recorded_by_jurisdiction_id` is set to the governing project's `jurisdiction_id` — which is also, by construction, the `X-User-ID` any inspector who later rules on it will present. Appointments can be listed and narrowed by `car_id` or `status`. An appointment's lifecycle runs `REQUESTED`, `SCHEDULED`, and then forks into `PASSED`, `CONDITIONAL` or `FAILED`, with `CANCELLED` reachable from `REQUESTED`; `PASSED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

Only the `AHJInspector` for that jurisdiction fixes the date. The appointment must be in status `REQUESTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_STATE_INVALID`, and the supplied `scheduled_at` must itself clear the jurisdiction's lead — the whole days from the logical clock to `scheduled_at`, computed the same way, must be at or above `inspection_lead_days`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_LEAD_UNMET`. On success `scheduled_at` is stored and the appointment moves `REQUESTED → SCHEDULED`, while `lead_days_at_request` keeps the figure snapshotted when the inspection was requested and is not recomputed here.

A scheduled appointment can be moved, by either the `AHJInspector` or a `ProjectManager`. This is a self-loop: the appointment must be in status `SCHEDULED` and stays `SCHEDULED`. An appointment that has already been ruled on — `PASSED`, `CONDITIONAL`, `FAILED` or `CANCELLED` — is not reschedulable at all, and the attempt is refused with the distinct `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_RESCHEDULABLE` so the operator learns that the ruling, not the request shape, closed the appointment. The new `scheduled_at` must clear the jurisdiction's lead exactly as at scheduling time, or `ERR_INSPECTION_LEAD_UNMET` applies, and only once it does is the cap consulted: `reschedule_count` must be strictly below 2, and a third move is refused with `ERR_RESCHEDULE_LIMIT`. On success the replacement `scheduled_at` is stored, `reschedule_count` increases by 1, and `lead_days_at_request` is recomputed against the new `scheduled_at`.

Ruling on the acceptance test belongs exclusively to the `AHJInspector` of the governing jurisdiction. The payload carries `result`, which is `PASSED`, `CONDITIONAL` or `FAILED`, and optionally `punch_list_count`, the number of open punch-list items being recorded. The appointment must be in status `SCHEDULED`, or the ruling is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_STATE_INVALID`, and the car must stand at `work_status` `TESTING` or `ACCEPTED`, the latter allowing a follow-up ruling on a car that was already accepted. Every branch increments the project's `version` by 1.

A **passed** ruling is the clean outcome. The appointment moves `SCHEDULED → PASSED` and records the ruling inspector's `X-User-ID` as `recorded_by_jurisdiction_id`; a `punch_list_count` supplied alongside a pass is stored as given, and one omitted leaves the appointment's count exactly where it stood. The car advances `TESTING → ACCEPTED` on the work dimension, and a car already sitting at `ACCEPTED` — for instance after an earlier conditional ruling — simply stays `ACCEPTED`. On the compliance dimension a car standing at `TRIGGERS_CLEARED` advances to `CODE_ACCEPTED`, and every code trigger of the car's live scope option in status `PRICED` or `WAIVED` becomes `SATISFIED`. If, at that moment, every car of the project stands at `ACCEPTED`, `IN_WARRANTY` or `UNDER_CONTRACT` and the project has at least one car, the project moves `EXECUTION → CLOSEOUT`.

A **conditional** ruling accepts the car with a snag list, and it is deliberately *not* the same as a pass. The `punch_list_count` must be at least 1; a conditional ruling with the count omitted or zero is refused with `ERR_PUNCH_LIST_REQUIRED`. The appointment moves `SCHEDULED → CONDITIONAL` and stores the supplied `punch_list_count`. The car advances `TESTING → ACCEPTED` on the work dimension. On the compliance dimension nothing moves at all: the car remains at `TRIGGERS_CLEARED` and is *not* code accepted, and its code triggers are left exactly as they were, none of them becoming `SATISFIED`. That deliberate asymmetry is what blocks the warranty handover in §3.9 until a clean pass is obtained. As with a pass, the project moves `EXECUTION → CLOSEOUT` once every car of the project stands at `ACCEPTED` or beyond.

A **failed** ruling is the origin of the reinspection cascade, and every link must be applied. The first link moves the appointment `SCHEDULED → FAILED` and stores the supplied `punch_list_count`. The second link, caused by the first, sends the car back into the hoistway: its `work_status` moves from `TESTING` — or from `ACCEPTED`, when the failure is a follow-up ruling on a car that had already been accepted — to `OUTAGE_ACTIVE`, its `compliance_status` moves to `TRIGGERS_OPEN` from either `TRIGGERS_CLEARED` or `CODE_ACCEPTED`, and every code trigger of the car's live option in status `PRICED` or `SATISFIED` reverts to `REQUIRED` while triggers in status `WAIVED` stay `WAIVED` — a waiver granted by the authority survives a failed test. The third link, caused by the second, reopens the car's outage window: the car's window in status `COMPLETED` with the greatest `completed_at`, ties broken by the earlier `created_at`, moves `COMPLETED → ACTIVE`, its `extension_hours` increases by the jurisdiction's `reinspection_extension_hours` and its `end_at` is pushed out by the same number of hours. This reopening bypasses the group buffer check entirely: the cascade is the consequence of a code failure rather than an operator's scheduling request, and a scheduling rule must never be able to block it. If the car has no completed window at all, nothing is reopened and the car still reverts to `OUTAGE_ACTIVE`. The fourth link, caused by the third, refreshes the group's `cars_in_service`, which drops by one, and the project's `active_outage_count`, which rises by one. The fifth link, caused by the fourth, recomputes the project's `downtime_posture`, which reaches `FROZEN` whenever the group has now fallen below its `min_cars_in_service`. The sixth link, caused by the second, moves the project `CLOSEOUT → EXECUTION` when the project had already reached `CLOSEOUT`.

Time itself is a `SystemOperator` capability. Setting the logical clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant has two standing consequences beyond the clock reading itself: every warranty transition in status `PENDING` whose `warranty_end` is at or before the new clock value moves to `LAPSED`, and the `downtime_posture` of every project is recomputed. The response reports the new `current_utc` together with `lapsed_warranty_transition_count`, the number of pending transitions the move expired.

### 3.9. From Warranty into a Maintenance Contract

A modernized car carries a manufacturer-style warranty from the contractor, and that warranty eventually folds into an ongoing maintenance agreement. Either a `ServiceManager` or a `ProjectManager` opens the handover record by naming the `car_id` and the `warranty_months`, a length between 12 and 60 calendar months inclusive. The car must stand at `work_status` `ACCEPTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CAR_STATE_INVALID`, and its `compliance_status` must be `CODE_ACCEPTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CAR_NOT_CODE_ACCEPTED` — which is exactly why a car accepted only conditionally cannot enter warranty until it has been passed cleanly. The transition opens in status `PENDING` with `maintenance_contract_id` null and both `provisional_credit_cents` and `final_credit_cents` at zero. `warranty_start` is stamped from the logical clock, and `warranty_end` is derived as `warranty_start` advanced by `warranty_months` calendar months, clamped to the last day of the target month when the target month is too short to contain the same day number. The car advances `ACCEPTED → IN_WARRANTY` and the project's `version` increments by 1. Transitions can be listed and narrowed by `car_id`, by `maintenance_contract_id` or by `status`, and a single read returns both credit figures. The transition lifecycle runs `PENDING` and then forks into either `ENROLLED` and onward to `UNDER_CONTRACT`, or into `LAPSED`; `UNDER_CONTRACT` and `LAPSED` are both terminal.

A `ServiceManager` drafts the maintenance contract separately, naming the `project_id` whose modernized cars it covers, the `monthly_base_cents` per-car monthly base rate before any tier multiplier or group factor, and the `coverage_start` instant, which serves both as the coverage anchor and as the anchor date for warranty-credit proration. A new contract opens in status `DRAFT` with `monthly_total_cents`, `cars_covered` and `total_final_credit_cents` all at zero, and its lifecycle is simply `DRAFT` then `ACTIVE`. Contracts can be listed and narrowed by `project_id` or `status`.

Enrolment attaches one warranty to one draft contract, and only a `ServiceManager` may do it. The payload names the `maintenance_contract_id`. The transition must be in status `PENDING`; enrolling a transition that is already `ENROLLED`, already `UNDER_CONTRACT`, or `LAPSED` is refused with `ERR_WARRANTY_STATE_INVALID`. The contract must still be in status `DRAFT`; enrolling into a contract that is already `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`. And the contract's project must be the project that owns the transition's car, with a mismatch refused by `ERR_CONTRACT_PROJECT_MISMATCH`. On success the contract identifier is stored on the transition, the unexpired warranty is valued on the provisional 30/360 day-count basis described below and written to `provisional_credit_cents`, the transition moves `PENDING → ENROLLED`, and the project's `version` increments by 1.

The credit exists because the owner should not pay twice for coverage they already own: the remaining, unexpired portion of the modernization warranty is credited against the maintenance contract. Both bases share the same skeleton. The proration anchor is the contract's `coverage_start`. The per-car rate is the car's own monthly maintenance rate, computed as described two paragraphs below. If the warranty's `warranty_end` falls at or before the anchor, the warranty is fully consumed before coverage begins and the credit is exactly 0. Otherwise a day count is taken between the anchor and `warranty_end`, and the credit is the per-car rate multiplied by that day count and divided by a divisor, with a single round-half-up to whole cents applied to the final figure.

The two bases differ in how they count days and what they divide by. Under the **30/360** basis, used for the provisional credit at enrolment, every month is treated as thirty days long: the day count is three hundred and sixty times the difference in years, plus thirty times the difference in months, plus the difference between the two day-of-month numbers each first capped at thirty, and the credit is the rate multiplied by that day count and divided by thirty. The result is insensitive to real month lengths and to leap days. Under the **actual-calendar** basis, used for the final credit at contract activation, the day count is the exact number of calendar days between the anchor and `warranty_end` including any leap day, and the credit is the rate multiplied by that day count, multiplied by twelve, and divided by three hundred and sixty-five. For identical inputs the two bases diverge whenever the span's real length differs from its thirty-day-month length, so the provisional and the final credit are genuinely different numbers and the difference is a real reconciliation the contract must carry. A read-only preview values an existing transition under an explicitly named `basis` — either `THIRTY_360` or `ACTUAL_CALENDAR`, anything else being refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and returns the `basis` applied, the `day_count` that basis produced between the contract's `coverage_start` and `warranty_end`, the `per_car_monthly_cents` rate it used, and the resulting `credit_cents`, without writing anything. The contract it anchors against is the one the transition is already attached to through its own `maintenance_contract_id`, so a transition that has not been enrolled has no anchor at all and the preview then reports `day_count`, `per_car_monthly_cents` and `credit_cents` all as 0. Where an anchor does exist but the warranty is already fully consumed at it, only the money is suppressed: the preview still reports the `day_count` that basis produced, zero or negative as the case may be, and the real `per_car_monthly_cents`, with `credit_cents` alone at 0.

Activating the contract is the `ServiceManager`'s closing act and it does five things at once. The contract must be in status `DRAFT`; activating a contract that is already `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_STATE_INVALID`. At least one warranty transition on the contract must be in status `ENROLLED`, and an activation with none is refused with `ERR_NO_ENROLLED_TRANSITIONS` rather than producing a zero-car contract. The covered set is exactly the transitions pointing at this contract whose status is `ENROLLED`: transitions still `PENDING` because they were never enrolled, transitions that have `LAPSED`, and transitions already `UNDER_CONTRACT` from an earlier activation are all excluded.

For each covered car the monthly rate is the contract's `monthly_base_cents` multiplied by a tier multiplier and by a group factor. The tier multiplier is keyed on the tier of the car's approved scope option: `CONTROLLER_ONLY` multiplies by 1.00, `CONTROLLER_DOOR` by 1.15, `CONTROLLER_MACHINE` by 1.30 and `FULL_REPLACEMENT` by 1.45 — deeper work carries more maintainable equipment; a covered car with no option in status `APPROVED` at that moment, its scope having since been rescinded or superseded, falls back to the 1.00 multiplier rather than failing the activation. The group factor is keyed on the `car_count` of the car's hoistway group: a group holding exactly one car takes 1.00, a group holding two or three cars takes 0.93, and a group holding four or more takes 0.88, reflecting the route density a technician gains from a larger bank. **That per-car product is rounded half-up to whole cents before summation, and the contract's `monthly_total_cents` is the sum of those already-rounded per-car rates.** Rounding the sum of unrounded products once is not the rule. `cars_covered` is the count of covered cars. Because the group factor is keyed on the group's `car_count` rather than on `cars_covered`, adding a car to a group lowers the per-car rate of cars that were already covered, on the next activation.

Activation then moves the contract `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, and for every enrolled transition it recomputes the credit on the actual-calendar basis into `final_credit_cents` and moves that transition `ENROLLED → UNDER_CONTRACT`. The contract's `total_final_credit_cents` is the sum of those recomputed final credits, and its `cars_covered` is the number of transitions now `UNDER_CONTRACT` on it. Every covered car advances `IN_WARRANTY → UNDER_CONTRACT`, which is the terminal state of the work dimension. If the project stood at `CLOSEOUT` and the contract's `cars_covered` equals the project's `car_count`, the project moves `CLOSEOUT → COMPLETED` and the delivery is finished. The project's `version` increments by 1.

Every path below is absolute; this API mounts its collections at the root rather than under a shared prefix, and foreign keys travel in request bodies rather than in nested URL segments. Endpoints that bring a new resource into existence answer with HTTP 201 on success; every other successful call — reads, state transitions, partial updates and the computed reads — answers with HTTP 200. Anything off this table is simply not there: an undeclared path, and equally a declared path addressed with a method it does not list, both answer `ERR_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404 rather than distinguishing the two.
