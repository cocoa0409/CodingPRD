# Reentry Employment Services API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Reentry employment work is a relay race in which every handoff can drop the baton. A case manager meets someone eight weeks before release, orders a birth certificate that arrives after the release date, builds a housing plan that a director never approves, and then discovers on release day that the participant has no state identification and therefore cannot be onboarded by the one employer who was willing to hire. Meanwhile a probation officer has authorised work only between certain hours, a funder's grant has quietly run out of uncommitted money, and a licensing board has asked for supplementary information on a petition nobody is tracking. The organisation's staff currently hold this coordination in spreadsheets and memory, and the seams show: money is double-committed, shifts are scheduled outside supervision windows, and funders receive retention figures that cannot be reproduced from the underlying records.

This document specifies the Reentry Employment Services API, the single system of record for that relay. It covers pre-release engagement (barrier assessment, vital-document gathering, and release planning inside the facility), the immediate post-release response (identification, housing, transportation, and health-coverage enrolment goals), employment readiness training with criminal-history disclosure coaching and interview practice, employer partnership development with federal fidelity bonding and Work Opportunity Tax Credit paperwork, record-relevant job matching, occupational-licensing barrier navigation through petitions for review, supportive services funded from funder grants, coordination of work schedules with probation and parole officers, and retention and recidivism outcome tracking for funders.

Three properties drive the design and should be read as non-negotiable. First, **every derived number is reconciled after every write** — a participant's barrier tier, a placement's subsidy projection, a grant's available balance, a job opening's remaining seats: none of these is a snapshot a client may compute for itself, and all of them must be correct the instant the responding write returns. Second, **money is never lost or double-spent**; the grant ledger obeys a stated conservation identity after every operation, and supportive-services spending passes through a two-person control where the submitter, the reviewer, and the settler must be three separable actors. Third, **all time is logical time**; a test-driving clock endpoint supplies the current UTC instant that every window, milestone, and staleness rule reads, so behaviour is reproducible and never depends on the host's wall clock.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries its caller in two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting operator, and `X-User-Role` names the role that operator is acting in. Both are required on every endpoint of this API with a single deliberate exception: the logical-clock endpoint that sets the server's current UTC instant is exempt from the authentication check so that a test harness may position time before any operator exists.

Authentication is the very first gate, evaluated before request-body parsing, before path resolution, and before any role check. If `X-User-ID` is absent, or present but an empty string, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The same code and status are returned when `X-User-ID` is present and non-empty but does not identify a registered user. A malformed body on an unauthenticated request still yields `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, never a validation error.

Authorisation is the second gate. Each endpoint declares the set of roles permitted to invoke it, listed in the endpoint table of section 4. A caller whose `X-User-Role` is outside that set is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). This check precedes request-shape validation, so a wrong-role caller who also sends a malformed body receives 403 and not 422.

Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `CaseManager`, `EmployerLiaison`, `ProgramDirector`, `SupervisionOfficer`, `Participant`, and `FunderAnalyst`.

A `CaseManager` is a reentry case manager who owns intake, barrier assessment, document gathering, release planning, job matching, work-shift scheduling, readiness-session facilitation, and supportive-services submission. An `EmployerLiaison` develops employer partnerships, posts job openings, records separations, and drives fidelity bonding. A `ProgramDirector` approves release plans, suspends and exits participants, and is both the reviewer and the settler of supportive-services money. A `SupervisionOfficer` is a probation or parole officer who owns a supervision case and its work-authorization window. A `Participant` acts for themselves and may only respond to their own placement offers. A `FunderAnalyst` sits on the funder side, records grants, and reads the ledger and outcome reporting; the analyst touches no participant, employment, or money workflow.

Identity semantics differ by role, and this distinction is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `Participant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Participant` entity; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Participant` compare the resource's participant foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a participant may only respond to a placement offer whose `participant_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_NOT_PLACEMENT_PARTICIPANT` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `CaseManager`, `EmployerLiaison`, `ProgramDirector`, `SupervisionOfficer`, or `FunderAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record such an identifier as a field on an entity — for example as `assigned_case_manager_user_id` on a participant, as `officer_user_id` on a supervision case, as `coach_user_id` on a readiness session, or as `submitted_by_user_id`, `reviewed_by_user_id`, and `settled_by_user_id` on a support request — and it may compare a stored operator identifier to `X-User-ID`, but it never compares an opaque identifier to any entity's `id`.

Two ownership rules follow from those opaque comparisons. A `SupervisionOfficer` may act only on a supervision case whose `officer_user_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`; acting on another officer's case is refused with `ERR_NOT_CASE_OFFICER` (HTTP 403), and this applies to both supervision-case action capabilities — the lifecycle-status transition and the work-authorization decision. Segregation of duties then governs supportive-services money: the `ProgramDirector` whose `X-User-ID` equals a support request's `submitted_by_user_id` may not review that request, and the director whose `X-User-ID` equals its `reviewed_by_user_id` may not settle it. Either violation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). Because ownership sits at the fifth precedence band and segregation of duties at the tenth (see section 2.5), a wrong-officer call on a non-existent case still reports the not-found error, while a self-review of a validly addressed request reports the segregation error.

Read access is broad but not universal. Every retrieval endpoint is open to all six roles except three: the grant ledger read and the funder outcome report are restricted to `ProgramDirector` and `FunderAnalyst`, and the match-preview scoring read is restricted to `CaseManager`, `EmployerLiaison`, and `ProgramDirector`. The logical-clock capability is available to all six roles.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted and has a minimum of 0; no upper bound applies to it. A `limit` below 1, a `limit` above 100, an `offset` below 0, or a non-integer value for either parameter is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending by default, and this ordering is stable across pages so that a client walking pages with a fixed `limit` sees each row exactly once. The one supplementary ordering rule concerns deferred supportive-services requests: when a support-request list is filtered to the deferred status, rows are additionally ordered by `deferred_at` ascending, which is the same queue order the deferral waterfall uses.

The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit` (the effective page size actually applied), `offset` (the effective offset actually applied), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied), and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page, false otherwise). Single-resource reads and writes omit these four fields from `meta`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Columns and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix and no client influence — a client-supplied `id` in a request body is ignored. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits, stamped once when the row is inserted and never modified. All ten entity types in this system additionally carry `updated_at` (an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every accepted mutation of that row) and `version` (an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly one on every accepted mutation of that row). No entity in this system is exempt from the `updated_at` and `version` columns.

`version` increments are not confined to writes a client aims directly at a row. A row mutated only as the downstream consequence of a cascade — a job opening whose seat counters shift because a placement was declined, a placement whose subsidy projection falls because a shift was cancelled, a grant whose ledger columns move because a request was voided — has both its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` advanced exactly as if it had been written directly. This is precisely what makes stale-version detection (section 2.6) meaningful across the graph.

Time is logical. The server holds a single current UTC instant, initialised at boot to `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and an administrative capability sets it to a supplied `current_utc` value. Every temporal rule in this specification — supervision work-window containment, shift-in-the-past refusal, session-start refusal, retention-milestone elapse, the `deferred_at` and `promoted_at` stamps, and `last_retention_check_at` — reads this logical clock and never the host's wall clock. Setting the clock mutates no entity and recomputes nothing retroactively: a shift that was legal when created stays legal after the clock moves past it, and a milestone that was not yet reached becomes reachable simply because the clock advanced.

### 2.5. Refusal Precedence: The Order in Which a Request Fails

A single request frequently violates more than one rule. The response is decided by a strict thirteen-band precedence ladder, evaluated in ascending band order; the first band whose condition holds produces the response, and no later band may pre-empt an earlier one. When two rules in the same band are both violated, either error is acceptable, but a rule in a lower-numbered band always wins over a rule in a higher-numbered band. The bands are:

1. **Authentication** — missing, empty, or unrecognised `X-User-ID`, yielding `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401), on every endpoint except the logical-clock capability.
2. **Role authorisation** — the caller's role is outside the endpoint's permitted set, yielding `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Request shape** — a required body field is missing, a field carries the wrong JSON type, an enumerated value falls outside its declared set, a string violates its length or character-set constraint, or a number falls outside its declared range. All shape failures yield `ERR_VALIDATION` (422), as do out-of-range pagination parameters.
4. **Path existence** — an identifier embedded in the URL path matches no row of the collection it addresses, yielding that collection's own not-found code with HTTP 404: `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPERVISION_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTNERSHIP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_JOB_OPENING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORK_SHIFT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GRANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPPORT_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND`.
5. **Ownership** — `ERR_NOT_CASE_OFFICER` and `ERR_NOT_PLACEMENT_PARTICIPANT`, both HTTP 403, as described in section 2.1.
6. **Body foreign keys** — a foreign key supplied inside the request body matches no row, yielding the referenced collection's not-found code with HTTP 404. Body foreign keys are resolved strictly after the path entity, so a request that carries both a bad path identifier and a bad body foreign key reports the path error.
7. **Optimistic concurrency** — a supplied expected parent version does not match the parent's current `version`, yielding the parent-specific stale-version code with HTTP 412 (section 2.6).
8. **Own-state gates** — the addressed entity's own state forbids the operation, yielding that entity's invalid-state code with HTTP 409 (section 2.8).
9. **Related-entity state gates** — some other entity the operation depends on is in a blocking state: `ERR_PARTICIPANT_SUSPENDED`, `ERR_PARTNERSHIP_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_WORK_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_INVALID_STATE` when a shift is scheduled against a non-active placement, and `ERR_GRANT_EXHAUSTED` — all HTTP 409.
10. **Safety, eligibility and segregation of duties** — `ERR_RECORD_CATEGORY_BARRED`, `ERR_LICENSE_RELIEF_REQUIRED`, `ERR_FIDELITY_BOND_UNAVAILABLE`, and `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, all HTTP 409.
11. **Temporal windows and overlaps** — `ERR_SHIFT_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`, `ERR_SHIFT_IN_PAST`, `ERR_SHIFT_OVERLAP`, `ERR_SHIFT_CONFLICTS_SESSION`, `ERR_COACH_DOUBLE_BOOKED`, `ERR_SESSION_ALREADY_STARTED`, and `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_REACHED`, all HTTP 409.
12. **Capacity, rounds and uniqueness** — `ERR_JOB_OPENING_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_PARTNERSHIP_CAPACITY_REACHED`, `ERR_SESSION_FULL`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLACEMENT`, `ERR_INFO_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, `ERR_PETITION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, `ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_REQUIRED`, `ERR_BOND_ALREADY_RECORDED`, `ERR_PARTNERSHIP_HAS_ACTIVE_PLACEMENTS`, `ERR_PARTICIPANT_HAS_ACTIVE_PLACEMENT`, `ERR_MILESTONE_ALREADY_RECORDED`, and `ERR_CATEGORY_NOT_FUNDABLE`, all HTTP 409.
13. **Fiscal caps** — `ERR_PARTICIPANT_CAP_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_SUBSIDY_CAP_EXCEEDED`, and `ERR_GRANT_FUNDS_INSUFFICIENT`, all HTTP 409.

Several consequences of this ordering are deliberate and are worth naming outright. A suspended participant sits in band nine, above every capacity gate in band twelve and every fiscal cap in band thirteen, so a supportive-services request for a suspended participant against a grant that would also breach the per-participant cap reports the suspension, not the cap. A barred record category sits in band ten, above the opening-capacity gate in band twelve, so a barred participant applying to a full opening reports the record bar. A stale job-opening version sits in band seven, above every business gate on that opening, so a stale version supplied against an already-filled opening reports the stale-version error and not the opening-not-open error. Work authorisation sits in band nine, above the window-containment check in band eleven, so an unauthorised participant whose proposed shift also falls outside a window reports the missing authorisation.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Three write operations in this system are guarded against writing on top of a parent record the client has not observed. Each guarded write requires a mandatory input field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent; the field is never optional, and its absence is a request-shape failure yielding `ERR_VALIDATION` (422). If the supplied value differs from the parent's current `version` at the moment the write is evaluated, the write is refused with the parent-specific stale-version code and HTTP 412.

The first guard protects release-plan revisions. Storing a release-plan revision requires `expected_participant_version` matching the target participant's current `version`. If any other writer has advanced that participant's version since the client last read it — most importantly another case manager who already stored a newer revision, advancing `release_plan_revision` and dropping `release_plan_status` back to `DRAFT` — the write is refused with `ERR_STALE_PARTICIPANT_VERSION` (HTTP 412), because the current writer's view of the participant is stale and its revision would silently supersede a plan it never saw.

The second guard protects supportive-services requests. Creating a support request requires `expected_grant_version` matching the target grant's current `version`. Every accepted create raises the grant's `open_request_count` and advances the grant's version, and every review moves `committed_cents`, so a mismatched version means the client chose which grant to charge from an outdated view of `available_cents` and `funding_posture`. The refusal is `ERR_STALE_GRANT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

The third guard protects placements. Creating a placement requires `expected_job_opening_version` matching the target job opening's current `version`. Each accepted create raises `offered_count` and lowers `remaining_openings`, so a mismatched version means the client selected the opening believing more seats were free than actually are. The refusal is `ERR_STALE_JOB_OPENING_VERSION` (HTTP 412), and because concurrency sits at band seven it is reported ahead of every business gate on that opening.

No other endpoint in this system accepts an expected-version input field. Sending one where it is not specified is not an error; the server simply ignores unknown keys that are not part of a declared payload.

### 2.7. Money, Rounding and Numeric Precision

All monetary amounts in this system are integer counts of cents. There are no floating-point currency fields, no decimal strings, and no currency codes: `authorized_cents`, `committed_cents`, `disbursed_cents`, `available_cents`, `per_participant_cap_cents`, `hourly_wage_cents`, `bond_coverage_cents`, `requested_cents`, `admin_fee_cents`, `total_charge_cents`, `projected_subsidy_cents`, `actual_subsidy_cents`, and `disbursed_subsidy_cents` are all integers.

Wherever a computation produces a fractional value that must be stored, the result is rounded half up — a value exactly on the halfway point rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. Money is rounded half up to whole cents. Percentage and score outputs that carry a fractional part — `doc_completeness_pct`, `barrier_score`, `retention_benchmark_pct`, and the two report rates `retention_rate_pct` and `recidivism_rate_pct` — are rounded half up to exactly one decimal place. `match_score` and its six breakdown components are whole integers, each component rounded half up to a whole number before any summation. `scheduled_hours` on a work shift is rounded half up to two decimal places.

Where rounding is applied matters as much as how. Two rules in this specification pin the rounding point explicitly, and an implementation that rounds at a different point produces observably different totals: supportive-services line awards are rounded per line and the resulting sum is never re-rounded, and wage-subsidy amounts are rounded once per shift after that shift's tier segments have been summed, never per segment.

### 2.8. Parallel State Dimensions, Transition Legality and Cascades

Several entities in this system carry more than one independent state dimension on the same row, and each dimension advances through its own transition graph without constraining the others except where a precondition explicitly says so. A participant carries three dimensions: `stage`, `suspension_status`, and `license_relief_status`. A supervision case carries two: `status` and `work_authorization_status`. A placement carries two: `employment_status` and `bonding_status`. Employer partnerships, job openings, work shifts, support requests, readiness sessions, and session enrollments each carry a single `status` dimension, and a funder grant carries a derived `funding_posture`.

A request that names a target state is legal only when the pair formed by the entity's current value in that dimension and the requested target is a declared transition of that dimension's graph. Anything else — a transition out of a terminal state, a transition that skips an intermediate state, or a re-application of a transition already taken — is refused at band eight with the addressed entity's own invalid-state code and HTTP 409: `ERR_PARTICIPANT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_SUPERVISION_CASE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PARTNERSHIP_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_WORK_SHIFT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_SUPPORT_REQUEST_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_SESSION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_INVALID_STATE`, or — for the placement bonding dimension specifically — `ERR_BONDING_INVALID_STATE`. A participant whose `stage` is `EXITED` is additionally frozen against all direct participant writes at band eight: updating intake attributes, storing a release-plan revision, recording an assessment, verifying a document, driving the licence-relief dimension, and suspending or reinstating are each refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_INVALID_STATE`.

Cascades are immediate and complete. When an operation drives a state change that other rows depend on, every downstream row is reconciled inside the same request, and the response reflects the fully settled world. Three cascade families run through this system, and each is described in full in the workflow narrative of section 3: supervision withdrawal (work-authorization suspension or case revocation conflicts a participant's future shifts, which drives placement compliance, which drives participant engagement posture, which drives the grant ledger, which may promote a deferred funding request); employment closure (a separation or a partnership termination cancels or declines downstream rows and settles subsidy money); and participant closure (a suspension or an exit withdraws enrollments and voids open funding requests). Because every cascaded row has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` advanced, a client that held a version of any touched row now holds a stale one.

### 2.9. Derived Fields and Conservation Invariants

No derived field is ever a client input. Every counter, percentage, score, tier, posture, compliance flag, and money column named as computed in this document is recalculated by the server at the end of any write whose inputs it depends on — including writes that reach the field only through a cascade — and the recalculated value is what the response carries. Two conservation identities on capacity counters must hold after every accepted write.

For a job opening, `openings_count` always equals `offered_count` plus `filled_count` plus `remaining_openings`. `offered_count` is the number of placements on that opening whose `employment_status` is `OFFERED`; `filled_count` is the number whose `employment_status` is `ACTIVE`; `remaining_openings` is `openings_count` less those two counts and is never negative, because the creating operation refuses the write before the remainder could go below zero. Placements that reach `DECLINED` or `SEPARATED` leave both counts and return their seat.

For a readiness session, `seat_capacity` always equals `enrolled_count` plus `remaining_seats`. `enrolled_count` is the number of enrollments on that session whose `status` is `ENROLLED`, `ATTENDED`, or `NO_SHOW` — an attended or no-show enrollment keeps the seat it consumed — and `remaining_seats` is `seat_capacity` less `enrolled_count`, never negative. Only a withdrawal returns a seat.

A third counter spans two entity types: an employer partnership's `active_placement_count` is the number of placements whose job opening belongs to that partnership and whose `employment_status` is `ACTIVE`. It rises when an offer is accepted and falls on separation, and it is the quantity the partnership's `max_concurrent_placements` ceiling is compared against.

The grant ledger carries its own identity, stated in section 3.8 and repeated here because it is the system's most important invariant: for every funder grant, `authorized_cents` equals `available_cents` plus `committed_cents` plus `disbursed_cents` after every write.

### 2.10. Path Addressing and Referential Resolution

Every collection in this API is mounted at the top level of the URL space, and relationships are expressed by foreign key rather than by URL nesting. Four sub-resource read paths and three sub-resource write paths nest one level deep for ergonomic reasons: a partnership's job openings, a placement's work shifts, and a session's enrollments are each reachable both as a filtered top-level list and as a nested list under their parent. Where a path carries more than one identifier segment, segments are validated left to right, and the leftmost segment that resolves to nothing produces the response.

Path identifiers are resolved before request bodies are interpreted for referential purposes, which is why band four precedes band six. A create call that supplies a `participant_id`, `partnership_id`, `job_opening_id`, or `grant_id` in its body against a non-existent row is refused with that collection's not-found code and HTTP 404, but only after the path entity itself resolved successfully.

Nested creation paths supply the parent foreign key rather than the body carrying it. A work shift created under a placement takes its `placement_id` from the path; an enrollment created under a readiness session takes its `session_id` from the path; a job opening takes its `partnership_id` from its create body. In every one of these cases the field is present on the stored resource and appears in read responses, and a client-supplied value for a path-derived field is ignored.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Pre-Release Engagement: Intake, Barrier Assessment and Vital Documents

Engagement begins inside the facility, weeks before anyone walks out of it. A `CaseManager` enrols a participant by supplying the person's legal name as `full_name`, a string of at least 1 and at most 120 characters; the correctional facility as `facility_name`; the facility's location as `facility_state`, which must be exactly two uppercase letters in the range A through Z, so that `CA` is accepted while `Ca`, `CAL`, and `C` are each refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`; and the scheduled release date as `expected_release_date`, an ISO 8601 calendar date with no time component. Two arrays complete intake. `record_categories` lists the participant's conviction categories and must be a non-empty array drawn from `DRUG_OFFENSE`, `PROPERTY_OFFENSE`, `VIOLENT_OFFENSE`, `FINANCIAL_OFFENSE`, `DUI`, and `OTHER`, with no value repeated. `required_document_types` lists the vital documents this person must gather before release and must be a non-empty array drawn from `STATE_ID`, `SSN_CARD`, `BIRTH_CERTIFICATE`, `WORK_HISTORY`, `MEDICAID_APPLICATION`, and `DD214`, again with no duplicates. An empty array where a non-empty one is declared, or a repeated element in either array, is a shape failure yielding `ERR_VALIDATION`. Intake may optionally name the owning case manager as `assigned_case_manager_user_id`, which is an opaque operator identifier the server stores and never compares to any entity's `id`.

A freshly enrolled participant is born in a fully determined state: `stage` is `PRE_RELEASE`, `suspension_status` is `NONE`, `license_relief_status` is `NONE`, `release_plan_status` is `NONE`, `release_plan_revision` is 0, `petition_rounds_used` is 0, `verified_document_types` is an empty array, `doc_completeness_pct` is 0.0, and `engagement_posture` is `OPEN`. The fields that only later work fills in are all null at this point: `actual_release_date`, `assessment_domain_scores`, `barrier_score`, `barrier_tier`, `housing_plan_type`, `transportation_plan_type`, `health_coverage_target`, `planned_release_date`, `suspension_reason`, `license_relief_occupation_code`, `exit_reason`, and `exit_date`.

Intake attributes remain editable. A `CaseManager` may revise any of `full_name`, `facility_name`, `facility_state`, `expected_release_date`, `record_categories`, and `required_document_types`, each independently optional in the update payload, and the same format and duplicate constraints apply to a revised array as to an original one. The update touches no state dimension — `stage`, `suspension_status`, and `license_relief_status` are untouched by it. It does, however, force a documentation recount: if `required_document_types` changes, any element of `verified_document_types` that is no longer required is dropped from `verified_document_types`, `doc_completeness_pct` is recomputed against the new denominator, and `engagement_posture` is recomputed. An exited participant cannot be updated at all, and the refusal is `ERR_PARTICIPANT_INVALID_STATE`.

The barrier assessment is a single structured write. The case manager submits `assessment_domain_scores`, an object carrying exactly five keys — `housing_score`, `transportation_score`, `health_score`, `work_history_score`, and `documentation_score` — each of which must be an integer from 0 through 4 inclusive. Omitting any of the five keys, carrying a key outside that set, or holding a value that is not an integer within 0 through 4 is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. From those five numbers the server derives the participant's `barrier_score` as follows: multiply `housing_score` by 0.30, `transportation_score` by 0.20, `health_score` by 0.15, `work_history_score` by 0.25, and `documentation_score` by 0.10, and add the five products together to obtain a weighted raw figure. Divide that raw figure by 4, multiply the quotient by 100, and round the result half up to one decimal place; the outcome always falls between 0.0 and 100.0. The normalisation by 4 and by 100 happens once, after the weighted sum, and never domain by domain — normalising per domain and then summing rounds differently and is wrong. The weights add to exactly 1.00, so no drift is tolerated: a participant scoring housing 3, transportation 2, health 1, work history 4, and documentation 2 yields a weighted raw figure of 2.65, which normalises to 66.25 and rounds to a `barrier_score` of 66.3. All fives at 2 give a raw 2.00, a score of 50.0; all fives at 1 give 25.0.

`barrier_tier` is banded off that score, evaluated top down: the tier is `HIGH` when `barrier_score` is at least 65.0, otherwise `MODERATE` when `barrier_score` is at least 35.0, and otherwise `LOW`. Both boundaries are inclusive, so a score of exactly 65.0 is `HIGH` and exactly 35.0 is `MODERATE`. The 66.3 example above therefore lands in `HIGH`, the 50.0 example in `MODERATE`, and the 25.0 example in `LOW`. Recording an assessment a second time simply overwrites the previous five scores and rescores the participant; there is no accumulation and no history. After the assessment lands, `engagement_posture` is recomputed and the release-readiness predicate is re-evaluated.

Documents are confirmed one at a time. Each verification names a single `document_type`, and the server appends it to `verified_document_types`. A `document_type` that is not an element of the participant's `required_document_types`, or that already appears in `verified_document_types`, is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_REQUIRED` — the system does not track documents it never asked for, and it does not double-count a confirmation. `doc_completeness_pct` is then recomputed as one hundred times the count of document types that appear in both `verified_document_types` and `required_document_types`, divided by the count of `required_document_types`, rounded half up to one decimal place. Document types verified but not required are ignored entirely, both as numerator members and as denominator members. Five required types with four verified therefore stand at exactly 80.0, three of five at 60.0, and two of three at 66.7.

Release readiness is a predicate the server re-evaluates at the end of every assessment write, every document verification, and every release-plan approval — and only at the end of those three. A participant in `PRE_RELEASE` advances to `RELEASE_READY` when all four of these hold simultaneously: `doc_completeness_pct` is at least 80.0, `barrier_score` is not null, `release_plan_status` is `APPROVED`, and `suspension_status` is `NONE`. The documentation boundary is inclusive, so a participant sitting at exactly 80.0 — four of five required documents verified — does advance, while three of five at 60.0 does not. When the transition fires, `engagement_posture` is recomputed as part of the same write.

A computed readiness read exposes the predicate and its inputs for a single participant without mutating anything: it reports `participant_id`, the current `stage`, `doc_completeness_pct`, `required_document_count` (the size of `required_document_types`), `verified_document_count` (the size of `verified_document_types`), `barrier_score`, `barrier_tier`, `release_plan_status`, and `readiness_met`, the boolean value of the four-part predicate above. Participants may also be listed with pagination and narrowed by `stage`, `barrier_tier`, `engagement_posture`, or `facility_state`.

### 3.2. Release Planning, Director Approval and the Walk into the Community

A release plan is a revision-numbered assertion about where someone will live, how they will travel, and how they will obtain health coverage. Each stored revision carries `housing_plan_type`, one of `FAMILY_REUNIFICATION`, `TRANSITIONAL_HOUSING`, `SHELTER`, or `INDEPENDENT_LEASE`; `transportation_plan_type`, one of `TRANSIT_PASS`, `OWN_VEHICLE`, `EMPLOYER_SHUTTLE`, `RIDE_SHARE_STIPEND`, or `NONE`; `health_coverage_target`, one of `MEDICAID`, `MARKETPLACE`, `EMPLOYER_PLAN`, `VA`, or `NONE`; and `planned_release_date`, the release date this revision asserts. The transportation choice is not merely documentation — it feeds the access component of the job-match score described in section 3.6, so a plan that names `OWN_VEHICLE` produces materially different match arithmetic than one that names `NONE`.

Every accepted revision increments `release_plan_revision` by exactly one and sets `release_plan_status` to `DRAFT`, whatever it was before. That is the mechanism by which a newer revision supersedes an earlier approved one: the previous plan snapshot is marked superseded as `release_plan_status` falls from `APPROVED` back to `DRAFT` while `release_plan_revision` advances, and the `SUPERSEDED` value of `release_plan_status` is reserved for exactly that displaced-plan condition. Because the readiness predicate requires an `APPROVED` plan, a participant who had reached `RELEASE_READY` on the strength of an approved plan walks backwards from `RELEASE_READY` to `PRE_RELEASE` the moment a fresh revision drops the plan to `DRAFT`. This revision write is the release-plan optimistic-concurrency guard: it requires `expected_participant_version` and is refused with `ERR_STALE_PARTICIPANT_VERSION` when that value does not match the participant's current `version`. It is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_INVALID_STATE` when the participant's `stage` is `EXITED`.

Approval belongs to the `ProgramDirector` alone and carries no payload. It sets `release_plan_status` to `APPROVED` and then re-evaluates release readiness, which is the moment a fully documented, fully assessed, unsuspended participant crosses `PRE_RELEASE → RELEASE_READY`. Approval is only meaningful against a plan that exists and is not already approved: when `release_plan_status` is `NONE` or already `APPROVED`, approval is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_INVALID_STATE`.

Recording the actual release is the case manager's job and moves the participant from `RELEASE_READY` to `POST_RELEASE`. The payload carries `actual_release_date`, which must be a calendar date falling on or before the logical clock's current UTC date — a release cannot be recorded for a future day. The transition additionally requires that `stage` is `RELEASE_READY` and `suspension_status` is `NONE`; a suspended participant, or one still in `PRE_RELEASE`, is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_INVALID_STATE`. On success `actual_release_date` is stored and `engagement_posture` is recomputed.

`engagement_posture` is the participant's single gate on new programme activity, and it is recomputed at the end of every write that touches any of its inputs, including writes that reach the participant only through a cascade. Its inputs are `suspension_status`, `stage`, `license_relief_status`, and the `schedule_compliance` of every placement belonging to the participant. It is evaluated strictly in this order, so the first matching condition wins: the posture is `FROZEN` when `suspension_status` is `SUSPENDED` or `stage` is `EXITED`; otherwise it is `RESTRICTED` when any placement of this participant has `schedule_compliance` of `OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE`, or when `license_relief_status` is `PETITION_DENIED`; otherwise it is `OPEN`. Because `FROZEN` is tested first, a suspended participant whose every placement is compliant is `FROZEN`, not `OPEN`. A participant with one conflicted shift on one placement is `RESTRICTED`; restoring that shift to a scheduled state returns the placement to compliance and the participant to `OPEN`.

### 3.3. Supervision Coordination and the Approved Work Window

Most participants leave custody under community supervision, and nothing about their working life can be scheduled without their officer's consent. A `SupervisionOfficer` opens a supervision case by naming the supervised person as `participant_id`, themselves as `officer_user_id`, their display name as `officer_name`, and the kind of supervision as `supervision_type`, which is one of `PROBATION`, `PAROLE`, or `POST_RELEASE_CONTROL`. An `expected_discharge_date` may optionally be recorded as the planned end of supervision. A `participant_id` that matches no participant is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`. A new case is born with `status` of `PENDING_CONTACT`, `work_authorization_status` of `NOT_REQUESTED`, `authorization_change_count` of 0, and both `approved_window_start_minute` and `approved_window_end_minute` null.

The case lifecycle is short and officer-owned. Supplying a `to_status` of `ACTIVE` moves the case from `PENDING_CONTACT` to `ACTIVE`, which is the only state in which the officer's work window constrains anything. From `ACTIVE`, a `to_status` of `DISCHARGED` closes the case normally and a `to_status` of `REVOKED` closes it adversely; both are terminal. Every one of these transitions requires that the caller's `X-User-ID` equals the case's `officer_user_id`, else `ERR_NOT_CASE_OFFICER`, and that the case is in the transition's declared source state, else `ERR_SUPERVISION_CASE_INVALID_STATE`.

Discharge is deliberately quiet. When a case moves `ACTIVE → DISCHARGED`, `work_authorization_status` is left exactly as it was — the field is not reset, not suspended, and not denied. A discharged case simply stops constraining new shift creation, because both the work-authorization requirement and the window-containment check consult only cases whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. Existing shifts are untouched by a discharge.

Revocation is loud. When a case moves `ACTIVE → REVOKED`, five things happen in the same request. First, `work_authorization_status` moves from `APPROVED` to `SUSPENDED`. Second, because authorisation was withdrawn, every work shift whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, whose `starts_at` is later than the logical clock, and which belongs to a placement of this participant whose `employment_status` is `ACTIVE` moves to `CONFLICTED` with `conflict_reason` set to `SUPERVISION_REVOKED`. Third, because those shifts became conflicted, each owning placement recomputes `schedule_compliance` to `OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE` and recomputes `projected_subsidy_cents` with those conflicted shifts removed from the projection input set. Fourth, because a placement of this participant is now out of compliance, the participant's `engagement_posture` recomputes to `RESTRICTED`. Fifth, because `projected_subsidy_cents` fell, the grant ledger of the funding grant re-runs, which lowers `committed_cents`, raises `available_cents`, and may flip `funding_posture` and promote one deferred supportive-services request as described in section 3.8.

Work authorisation is a separate dimension on the same case row, driven by a `decision` field. From `NOT_REQUESTED`, a `decision` of `APPROVED` grants permission and a `decision` of `DENIED` refuses it — `DENIED` is terminal and no path leads out of it. From `APPROVED`, a `decision` of `SUSPENDED` withdraws permission; from `SUSPENDED`, a `decision` of `APPROVED` restores it. Every decision requires the caller to be the case's own officer and requires the case's `status` to be `ACTIVE`; a decision on a case that is still `PENDING_CONTACT`, already `DISCHARGED`, or `REVOKED` is refused with `ERR_SUPERVISION_CASE_INVALID_STATE`, and a decision pair that is not one of the four declared transitions is refused with the same code. Every accepted decision increments `authorization_change_count` by exactly one, including a denial.

Granting or restoring authorisation stores the daily work window. `approved_window_start_minute` and `approved_window_end_minute` are minute offsets from UTC midnight, each an integer from 0 through 1440 inclusive, and the end must be strictly greater than the start. When a `decision` of `APPROVED` arrives with either minute absent, with either minute outside 0 through 1440, or with an end that is not strictly greater than the start, the request is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. A window from 480 to 1020 therefore authorises work from 08:00 to 17:00 UTC each day. The two window fields are only meaningful alongside an approval; a suspension or a denial does not carry them.

Restoration runs a compensating cascade that is the mirror image of suspension, and its scope is narrower than one might assume. When authorisation moves `SUSPENDED → APPROVED`, every work shift that is currently `CONFLICTED`, whose `conflict_reason` is `WORK_AUTHORIZATION_SUSPENDED`, whose `starts_at` is later than the logical clock, and which belongs to an active placement of this participant returns to `SCHEDULED` with `conflict_reason` cleared to null — but only if that shift's minute-of-day span still lies inside the newly stored window. A shift that no longer fits the new window stays `CONFLICTED`. Shifts whose `conflict_reason` is `SUPERVISION_REVOKED` are never restored by this cascade, because a revoked case cannot return to `ACTIVE`. As shifts return to `SCHEDULED`, each owning placement recomputes `schedule_compliance` back to `COMPLIANT` and recomputes `projected_subsidy_cents` back upward; with no placement out of compliance, the participant's `engagement_posture` recomputes to `OPEN`; and the grant ledger re-runs on the funding grant.

Suspension of authorisation, driven by a `decision` of `SUSPENDED` from `APPROVED`, produces exactly the same downstream chain as revocation, differing only in the `conflict_reason` written onto the affected shifts, which is `WORK_AUTHORIZATION_SUSPENDED` rather than `SUPERVISION_REVOKED`: future scheduled shifts on the participant's active placements become `CONFLICTED`, those placements become `OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE` and shed the conflicted shifts from `projected_subsidy_cents`, the participant becomes `RESTRICTED`, and the grant ledger re-runs — lowering `committed_cents`, raising `available_cents`, possibly flipping `funding_posture`, and possibly promoting one deferred request. Supervision cases may be read individually or listed with pagination and narrowed by `participant_id`, `status`, `work_authorization_status`, or `officer_user_id`.

### 3.4. Employer Partnership Development, Job Postings and Fidelity Bonding

An `EmployerLiaison` builds the employer side of the programme. Registering a partnership requires `employer_name`, the employer's legal name; `industry`, one of `MANUFACTURING`, `CONSTRUCTION`, `HOSPITALITY`, `LOGISTICS`, `HEALTHCARE`, `RETAIL`, or `SKILLED_TRADES`; and `max_concurrent_placements`, an integer of at least 1 that caps how many placements across all of this employer's openings may be simultaneously active. Two optional inputs shape eligibility later: `wotc_certified` declares whether the employer is enrolled for Work Opportunity Tax Credit paperwork and defaults to false, and `excluded_record_categories` lists conviction categories the employer refuses across every one of its openings, defaulting to an empty array. A new partnership starts with `status` of `PROSPECT`, `active_placement_count` of 0, `fidelity_bond_enabled` of false, and both `bond_coverage_cents` and `bond_effective_date` null.

Partnership development walks a short ladder driven by `to_status`. `PROSPECT → ENGAGED` records first substantive contact. `ENGAGED → ACTIVE` opens the partnership for business, and only from `ACTIVE` may placements be created against its openings. `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` pauses new business without disturbing anything already running: existing active placements are untouched, existing shifts keep running, and only new placement creation is refused, with `ERR_PARTNERSHIP_NOT_ACTIVE`. `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` is the recovery edge and restores placement creation. Any pair outside the declared set is refused with `ERR_PARTNERSHIP_INVALID_STATE`.

Termination is reachable from both `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED`, is terminal, and requires the partnership to be drained first: `active_placement_count` must be 0, and a termination attempted while any placement on any of the partnership's openings is still `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_PARTNERSHIP_HAS_ACTIVE_PLACEMENTS`. Once accepted, termination cascades identically from either source state. Every job opening of the partnership whose `status` is `OPEN` or `FILLED` moves to `CLOSED`. Because those openings closed, every placement on them whose `employment_status` is `OFFERED` moves to `DECLINED`, which decrements that opening's `offered_count` and raises its `remaining_openings`. Placements that had already reached `SEPARATED` or `DECLINED` are unaffected, and no money moves as a consequence of the closure itself.

Fidelity bonding is orthogonal to the development ladder and is recorded once. The bonding agreement write supplies `bond_coverage_cents`, the coverage amount in cents, and `bond_effective_date`, the first date the bond covers placements; it sets `fidelity_bond_enabled` to true and stores both values. It deliberately makes no status change — a `PROSPECT` or `ENGAGED` partnership may carry a bond just as an `ACTIVE` one can. It is refused with `ERR_PARTNERSHIP_INVALID_STATE` when the partnership's `status` is `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED`, and refused with `ERR_BOND_ALREADY_RECORDED` when `fidelity_bond_enabled` is already true. Whether a partnership carries bonding decides two later gates: whether a placement may be created on an opening that demands a bond, and whether bonding may be requested on a placement at all.

Job openings are posted under a partnership. The create payload names the employer as `partnership_id` and carries `title`, the job title; `occupation_code`, a Standard Occupational Classification code such as `51-4031`; `hourly_wage_cents`, the offered wage in cents per hour, which must be at least 725; `weekly_hours`, the scheduled hours per week, which must be strictly greater than 0 and at most 60; and `openings_count`, the total number of seats the posting can fill, an integer of at least 1. Five optional inputs describe the opening's restrictions and amenities: `barred_record_categories`, conviction categories barred for this specific opening in addition to the employer-level exclusions, defaulting to an empty array; `requires_license`, defaulting to false, marking the opening as gated by an occupational licence; `license_occupation_code`, defaulting to null, naming the Standard Occupational Classification code of the licence the opening requires; `requires_fidelity_bond`, defaulting to false, marking the opening as one whose placements may only proceed under a bonded partnership; and `transit_accessible`, defaulting to false, declaring the worksite reachable by public transit. A `partnership_id` matching no partnership is refused with `ERR_PARTNERSHIP_NOT_FOUND`. A new opening starts with `status` of `OPEN`, `offered_count` and `filled_count` of 0, and `remaining_openings` equal to `openings_count`.

An opening's own state is fully derived from its seat arithmetic in two directions. After any placement write that changes `offered_count` or `filled_count`, the server re-evaluates the opening: `OPEN → FILLED` when `remaining_openings` reaches 0, and `FILLED → OPEN` when `remaining_openings` becomes greater than 0 again because a placement left `OFFERED` or `ACTIVE` through a decline, a separation, or a termination cascade. Both edges fire as part of the triggering write, not lazily. While an opening is `FILLED`, further placement creation against it is refused with `ERR_JOB_OPENING_NOT_OPEN`, and the same refusal covers an opening whose `remaining_openings` has reached 0 or below or whose `status` is `CLOSED`. `CLOSED` is terminal and is reached only through partnership termination, from either `OPEN` or `FILLED`; in both cases the closure declines the opening's outstanding offered placements. Partnerships may be listed with pagination and narrowed by `status`, `wotc_certified`, or `fidelity_bond_enabled`; a partnership's openings are reachable both as a nested sub-resource list narrowed by `status` and as a top-level opening list narrowed by `partnership_id`, `status`, `requires_license`, or `transit_accessible`.

### 3.5. Readiness Sessions, Disclosure Coaching and Seat Management

Employment readiness is delivered in scheduled group sessions that a `CaseManager` books and facilitates. A session declares its `session_type`, one of `DISCLOSURE_COACHING` (rehearsing how to talk about a criminal record with an employer), `INTERVIEW_PRACTICE`, `RESUME_LAB`, or `DIGITAL_LITERACY`; the facilitating coach as `coach_user_id`, an opaque operator identifier; its `starts_at` and `ends_at` as ISO 8601 UTC instants with three fractional digits, where `ends_at` must be strictly after `starts_at` or the write is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`; and its `seat_capacity`, an integer of at least 1 and at most 30. A new session begins with `status` of `SCHEDULED`, `enrolled_count` of 0, and `remaining_seats` equal to `seat_capacity`.

A coach cannot be in two rooms at once. Booking is refused with `ERR_COACH_DOUBLE_BOOKED` when the proposed interval, treated as half-open — including its start instant and excluding its end instant — intersects the interval of another session whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` and whose `coach_user_id` is the same. No buffer is applied and no padding is added, so a session ending at 12:00:00.000Z and another beginning at exactly 12:00:00.000Z with the same coach are both accepted: back-to-back sessions are legitimate. Sessions that have already been held or cancelled do not occupy their coach, so only `SCHEDULED` sessions collide.

A session closes in one of two ways, selected by `to_status`. `SCHEDULED → HELD` records that the session actually ran and requires that `ends_at` is at or before the logical clock — a session cannot be marked held before it has finished. Holding a session sweeps attendance: every enrollment on it still in `ENROLLED` moves to `NO_SHOW`, while `enrolled_count` and `remaining_seats` do not move at all, because a no-show enrollment still occupies the seat it consumed. Enrollments already moved to `ATTENDED` or `NO_SHOW` during the session window are left exactly as they are. `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` calls the session off and requires that `starts_at` is strictly later than the logical clock — a session already under way cannot be cancelled. Cancellation moves every `ENROLLED` enrollment to `WITHDRAWN`, which drops `enrolled_count` to 0 and restores `remaining_seats` to the full `seat_capacity`. Both `HELD` and `CANCELLED` are terminal, and any other transition attempt is refused with `ERR_SESSION_INVALID_STATE`.

Enrolling a participant consumes a seat. The enrollment payload names the `participant_id` and may optionally set `disclosure_script_reviewed`, which defaults to false and records that the coach confirmed the participant rehearsed their criminal-history disclosure script; the `session_id` comes from the path rather than the body. A successful enrollment is created with `status` of `ENROLLED`, increments the session's `enrolled_count`, and decrements its `remaining_seats`. Five gates guard it, and they fire in precedence order: the session's own state must be `SCHEDULED`, else `ERR_SESSION_INVALID_STATE`; the participant must not have `suspension_status` of `SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_PARTICIPANT_SUSPENDED`; the session's `starts_at` must be strictly later than the logical clock, so a session whose `starts_at` is at or before the current instant is refused with `ERR_SESSION_ALREADY_STARTED`; `remaining_seats` must be greater than 0, else `ERR_SESSION_FULL`; and the participant must not already hold an enrollment on this same session whose `status` is `ENROLLED`, `ATTENDED`, or `NO_SHOW`, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT`. Because a withdrawn enrollment is excluded from that uniqueness set, a participant who withdrew may enrol again while seats remain. A newly created enrollment immediately joins the commute-buffer conflict set that constrains work-shift scheduling, described in section 3.7.

An enrollment's own lifecycle has three exits, all terminal, driven by `to_status`. `ENROLLED → ATTENDED` records attendance and requires that the session's `status` is either `SCHEDULED` or `HELD` and that the session's `starts_at` is at or before the logical clock; the seat counters do not move, because an attended enrollment keeps its seat. `ENROLLED → NO_SHOW` records absence under exactly the same two preconditions — session `SCHEDULED` or `HELD`, session `starts_at` at or before the logical clock — and likewise leaves both counters alone. `ENROLLED → WITHDRAWN` removes the participant and requires that the session's `status` is `SCHEDULED`; it is the only exit that decrements `enrolled_count` and increments `remaining_seats`, which is the compensating half of the seat-conservation identity. A transition that is not one of those three is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_INVALID_STATE`, and a transition blocked by the owning session's state is refused with `ERR_SESSION_INVALID_STATE`. Reaching `ATTENDED` matters beyond attendance records: from that point the enrollment begins contributing to the readiness component of the participant's job-match arithmetic. Sessions may be listed with pagination and narrowed by `status`, `session_type`, or `coach_user_id`; enrollments may be listed as a nested sub-resource of a session narrowed by `status`, or as a top-level list narrowed by `session_id`, `participant_id`, or `status`.

### 3.6. Record-Relevant Job Matching and the Placement Eligibility Ladder

A placement is the junction where a participant, a job opening, and — when the work is subsidised — a funder grant meet. A `CaseManager` creates one by naming the `participant_id`, the `job_opening_id`, the funding `grant_id`, the `subsidy_track` (either `NONE` for an unsubsidised hire or `SUBSIDIZED_WORK_EXPERIENCE` for a wage-subsidised work-experience slot), the opening's `expected_job_opening_version`, and `start_date`, the first scheduled work date as an ISO calendar date. The funding reference is conditional: `grant_id` is required when `subsidy_track` is `SUBSIDIZED_WORK_EXPERIENCE` and must be null when `subsidy_track` is `NONE`.

Creation runs the full eligibility ladder, and the order in which it refuses is as important as the refusals themselves. The stale-version guard comes first, so a mismatched `expected_job_opening_version` yields `ERR_STALE_JOB_OPENING_VERSION` even when the opening is also full. Then a participant whose `suspension_status` is `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_SUSPENDED`. Then a job opening whose employer partnership has a `status` other than `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_PARTNERSHIP_NOT_ACTIVE`. Next comes the record-relevance check: if any element of the participant's `record_categories` appears either in the opening's `barred_record_categories` or in the employer partnership's `excluded_record_categories`, the placement is refused with `ERR_RECORD_CATEGORY_BARRED` — the two exclusion lists are cumulative, and a single overlapping category on either list is enough. Then licensing: when the opening's `requires_license` is true, the placement proceeds only if the participant's `license_relief_status` is `RELIEF_GRANTED` **and** the participant's `license_relief_occupation_code` equals the opening's `license_occupation_code`; a participant without granted relief, or with relief granted for a different occupation code, is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_RELIEF_REQUIRED`. Then bonding: when the opening's `requires_fidelity_bond` is true and its employer partnership's `fidelity_bond_enabled` is false, the placement is refused with `ERR_FIDELITY_BOND_UNAVAILABLE`. Then uniqueness: a participant who already holds a placement on this same opening whose `employment_status` is `OFFERED` or `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLACEMENT`, while a prior `DECLINED` or `SEPARATED` placement on the opening does not block a fresh one. Last comes capacity: an opening whose `status` is not `OPEN`, or whose `remaining_openings` is 0 or below, is refused with `ERR_JOB_OPENING_NOT_OPEN`. Because the record bar sits above the capacity gate, a barred participant applying to a full opening is told about the record bar.

An accepted placement is created with `employment_status` of `OFFERED`, `bonding_status` of `NONE`, `schedule_compliance` of `COMPLIANT` (no shifts exist yet, so no conflicted shift exists), `projected_subsidy_cents` and `actual_subsidy_cents` of 0, `disbursed_subsidy_cents` of 0, `recidivism_event` of `NONE`, and null values for `separation_reason`, `separation_date`, `retention_30_outcome`, `retention_60_outcome`, `retention_90_outcome`, and `last_retention_check_at`. The opening's `offered_count` rises by one and `remaining_openings` falls by one, which may flip the opening from `OPEN` to `FILLED`.

Creation also pins the match arithmetic. `match_score` is an integer from 0 through 100 computed once at creation from the then-current participant, opening, and partnership values and never recomputed afterwards, and `match_breakdown` stores the six components under the keys `wage_component`, `hours_component`, `barrier_fit_component`, `access_component`, `readiness_component`, and `wotc_bonus`. Each component is rounded half up to a whole number before any summation, and each is derived as follows.

The `wage_component` rewards pay against a programme target wage of 2,000 cents an hour — twenty dollars. It is thirty multiplied by the opening's `hourly_wage_cents`, divided by 2,000, rounded half up to a whole number, and then capped at 30. The `hours_component` rewards hours, banded off the opening's `weekly_hours` and evaluated top down: 20 when `weekly_hours` is at least 30, otherwise 14 when `weekly_hours` is at least 20, otherwise 8 when `weekly_hours` is at least 10, otherwise 0. The `barrier_fit_component` rewards a lower barrier profile: it is twenty multiplied by the quantity one hundred less the participant's `barrier_score`, divided by one hundred, rounded half up to a whole number — so a participant at 0.0 earns 20 and a participant at 100.0 earns 0.

The `access_component` translates the participant's release-plan `transportation_plan_type` against the opening's `transit_accessible` flag: 15 when the plan is `OWN_VEHICLE`; 15 when the plan is `EMPLOYER_SHUTTLE`; 15 when the plan is `TRANSIT_PASS` and the opening's `transit_accessible` is true; 5 when the plan is `TRANSIT_PASS` and `transit_accessible` is false; 10 when the plan is `RIDE_SHARE_STIPEND`; and 0 when the plan is `NONE` or has not been set at all. The `readiness_component` rewards attended training: take five multiplied by the number of the participant's session enrollments whose `status` is `ATTENDED`, add a further 5 if at least one of those attended enrollments carries `disclosure_script_reviewed` of true, and cap the result at 15. The `wotc_bonus` is 5 when the employer partnership's `wotc_certified` is true and 0 when it is false.

`match_score` is the sum of those six components, capped at 100. Two independent caps therefore operate: the inner cap of 30 on `wage_component` alone, and the outer cap of 100 on the total. The outer cap is genuinely reachable, because the six components can sum to as much as 105. Consider a wage of 1,850 cents, 35 hours a week, a `barrier_score` of 66.3, a `TRANSIT_PASS` plan against a transit-accessible opening, two attended sessions one of which reviewed the disclosure script, and a tax-credit-certified employer: the components are 28, 20, 7, 15, 15, and 5, for a `match_score` of 90. The same case against an opening whose `transit_accessible` is false drops the access component to 5 and the score to 80. A wage of 2,400 cents, 40 hours a week, a `barrier_score` of 0.0, an `OWN_VEHICLE` plan, three attended sessions including a reviewed disclosure script, and a certified employer produces 30, 20, 20, 15, 15, and 5 — 105 before the outer cap and a stored `match_score` of exactly 100.

The same arithmetic is available as a dry run. A computed match-preview read scores a supplied `participant_id` against a job opening without creating anything, returning `job_opening_id`, `participant_id`, `match_score`, `match_breakdown`, `eligible`, and `blocking_error_id`. When `eligible` is true, `blocking_error_id` is null. When `eligible` is false, `blocking_error_id` names the very error identifier a real placement creation would return, chosen by the same precedence ladder — so a preview against a full opening for a barred participant reports `ERR_RECORD_CATEGORY_BARRED`, matching what creation would say. The preview is restricted to `CaseManager`, `EmployerLiaison`, and `ProgramDirector`.

Only the participant may answer their own offer, by supplying a `response` of either `ACCEPTED` or `DECLINED`; a caller whose `X-User-ID` does not equal the placement's `participant_id` is refused with `ERR_NOT_PLACEMENT_PARTICIPANT`. Acceptance moves `employment_status` from `OFFERED` to `ACTIVE` and requires four things beyond ownership: the placement is still `OFFERED`, else `ERR_PLACEMENT_INVALID_STATE`; the participant's `suspension_status` is `NONE`, else `ERR_PARTICIPANT_SUSPENDED`; the employer partnership's `status` is `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_PARTNERSHIP_NOT_ACTIVE`; and the partnership's `active_placement_count` is strictly less than its `max_concurrent_placements`, else `ERR_PARTNERSHIP_CAPACITY_REACHED`. On acceptance the opening's `offered_count` falls by one while `filled_count` rises by one, leaving `remaining_openings` unchanged; the partnership's `active_placement_count` rises by one; the participant's `stage` moves `POST_RELEASE → PLACED`; and both `projected_subsidy_cents` and `actual_subsidy_cents` are recomputed, which yields 0 for each until shifts exist. Declining moves `employment_status` from `OFFERED` to `DECLINED`, which decrements the opening's `offered_count` and raises `remaining_openings`, possibly flipping the opening `FILLED → OPEN`. `DECLINED` is also the landing state when a partnership termination cascade closes the opening under an outstanding offer. Both `DECLINED` and `SEPARATED` are terminal for the employment dimension.

Fidelity bonding runs as a second, independent dimension on the placement row, driven by `to_status` and touching no money — the bond is carried by the employer partnership, not by the grant. Its `bonding_status` takes the values `NONE`, `REQUESTED`, `BONDED`, `CLAIMED`, and `EXPIRED`, of which `CLAIMED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal. `NONE → REQUESTED` opens a bonding request and requires that `employment_status` is `OFFERED` or `ACTIVE`, so bonding may be requested while the offer is still outstanding; it additionally requires the employer partnership's `fidelity_bond_enabled` to be true, and a request against an unbonded partnership is refused with `ERR_FIDELITY_BOND_UNAVAILABLE`. `REQUESTED → BONDED` confirms coverage and likewise requires `employment_status` to be `OFFERED` or `ACTIVE`. From `BONDED` there are two terminal exits, both of which require the placement to have already reached `SEPARATED`: `BONDED → CLAIMED` files a claim and additionally requires `separation_reason` to be `TERMINATED_FOR_CAUSE`, since a bond answers for employee dishonesty and not for a layoff or a voluntary quit — a claim attempted on any other separation reason is refused with `ERR_BONDING_CLAIM_NOT_ELIGIBLE`; and `BONDED → EXPIRED` lets the coverage lapse after any separation reason. Any bonding transition outside those four declared pairs is refused with `ERR_BONDING_INVALID_STATE`. Placements may be read individually or listed with pagination and narrowed by `participant_id`, `job_opening_id`, `grant_id`, `employment_status`, `bonding_status`, or `schedule_compliance`.

### 3.7. Work Shifts, the Wage-Subsidy Ladder and Separation

Scheduling a subsidised shift is the most heavily guarded write in this system, because it must respect a probation officer's window, a participant's training commitments, and a funder's remaining money all at once. A `CaseManager` schedules a shift under a placement by supplying `starts_at` and `ends_at` as ISO 8601 UTC instants with three fractional digits; the shift's `placement_id` is taken from the path. If `ends_at` is not strictly after `starts_at` the write is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. From the accepted interval the server computes `scheduled_hours` as the number of minutes from `starts_at` to `ends_at` divided by sixty, rounded half up to two decimal places, and the shift is stored with `status` of `SCHEDULED` and `conflict_reason` of null.

Six business gates then apply, in this precedence order. The placement must be live: a placement whose `employment_status` is anything other than `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. Work must be authorised: the participant must hold a supervision case whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `work_authorization_status` is `APPROVED`, and if no such case exists the shift is refused with `ERR_WORK_NOT_AUTHORIZED`. Because authorisation ranks above the window check, an unauthorised participant whose proposed shift also falls outside any window is told about the missing authorisation.

The shift must fall inside the officer's window. Expressed as minutes from UTC midnight, the shift's span must be fully contained within `approved_window_start_minute` through `approved_window_end_minute` of that active supervision case, both endpoints inclusive; the shift must also lie entirely within a single UTC calendar day. A shift that starts before the window opens, ends after it closes, or crosses midnight into a second UTC calendar date is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`. The shift must be in the future: a `starts_at` at or before the logical clock's current UTC instant is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_IN_PAST`, so a shift beginning at exactly the current instant is not allowed.

The shift must not double-book the participant. Treating each interval as half-open — including its start instant and excluding its end instant — the proposed interval must not intersect the interval of any other work shift whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `CONFLICTED`, or `WORKED` and which belongs to any placement of the same participant, not merely to the same placement. Touching endpoints do not intersect, so a shift ending at 17:00:00.000Z and another beginning at exactly 17:00:00.000Z are both accepted. A genuine intersection is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_OVERLAP`.

The shift must also leave room for training, with travel time allowed for. Inflate the proposed shift's interval by forty-five minutes of commute buffer on **both** sides and treat the inflated interval as closed — including both of its endpoints. If that inflated interval intersects the interval of a readiness session whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` and in which this same participant holds an enrollment whose `status` is `ENROLLED`, the shift is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_CONFLICTS_SESSION`. Adjacency is a conflict here, unlike the shift-versus-shift rule: a session ending at 09:00:00.000Z and a shift starting at 09:45:00.000Z collide, because the buffered shift interval begins at exactly 09:00:00.000Z and the closed comparison counts that shared instant.

Finally two fiscal gates apply. If the placement's `projected_subsidy_cents`, recomputed with the new shift included, would exceed the per-placement subsidy ceiling of 120,000 cents — twelve hundred dollars of subsidy per placement — the shift is refused with `ERR_SUBSIDY_CAP_EXCEEDED`. And if the increase in `projected_subsidy_cents` that the new shift would cause exceeds the funding grant's `available_cents`, the shift is refused with `ERR_GRANT_FUNDS_INSUFFICIENT`. An accepted shift recomputes the placement's `projected_subsidy_cents` through the projected variant of the subsidy calculation, recomputes the placement's `schedule_compliance` (which stays `COMPLIANT` while no shift on the placement is `CONFLICTED`), and re-runs the grant ledger, raising the grant's `committed_cents` by the increase in `projected_subsidy_cents`.

The wage subsidy itself is a two-variant tiered calculation on the placement, and both variants read the same shift rows through different status filters. The **projected** variant, stored in `projected_subsidy_cents`, reads the placement's shifts whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `WORKED`; this is the figure the grant's `committed_cents` holds while the placement is active. The **actual** variant, stored in `actual_subsidy_cents`, reads only the shifts whose `status` is `WORKED`. Shifts that are `CONFLICTED` or `CANCELLED` are excluded from both variants. Both variants are rewritten on every placement or shift mutation. A placement whose `subsidy_track` is `NONE` carries 0 in both fields no matter how many shifts it holds; the tier ladder is walked only for a `subsidy_track` of `SUBSIDIZED_WORK_EXPERIENCE`.

Within a variant, the placement's qualifying shifts are processed in chronological order — by `starts_at` ascending, and where two shifts share a `starts_at`, by `created_at` ascending. A running cumulative-hours total starts at zero for the placement, and each shift's `scheduled_hours` are consumed against a three-step ladder measured in that cumulative total: while cumulative hours are from 0 up to but not including 80, the subsidy rate is 50 percent of the wage; from 80 up to but not including 160, the rate is 25 percent; from 160 hours onward, the rate is 0 percent. A shift whose hours straddle a boundary is split across the segments it spans and each segment is priced at its own rate. Each segment contributes its hours multiplied by the opening's `hourly_wage_cents` multiplied by that segment's rate; the segments of one shift are summed and only then rounded half up to whole cents, producing that shift's amount. The variant's result is the sum of all shift amounts, capped at 120,000 cents. Because the ladder is cumulative per placement and walked in `starts_at` order, shifts created out of chronological order must still be charged in chronological order.

Three worked figures pin the arithmetic. At an `hourly_wage_cents` of 1850 with three forty-hour shifts, the first shift sits entirely in the first tier and earns forty hours times 1850 times one half, or 37,000 cents; the second shift also sits entirely in the first tier, bringing the cumulative total to exactly 80 hours, and likewise earns 37,000; the third shift falls entirely in the second tier and earns forty times 1850 times one quarter, or 18,500 — a total of 92,500 cents. A single ninety-hour shift at the same wage straddles the first boundary: eighty hours at half rate yields 74,000 and the remaining ten hours at quarter rate yield 4,625, for 78,625 cents. Four fifty-hour shifts at an `hourly_wage_cents` of 1900 yield 47,500, then 38,000, then 23,750, then 4,750, for 114,000 cents. Rounding is applied once per shift after its segments are summed, never per segment: one shift of 6.25 hours at 1855 produces 5,796.875 cents, which is stored as 5,797.

A shift's own lifecycle has three operator-driven edges. `SCHEDULED → WORKED` records that the shift was worked and requires that the shift is `SCHEDULED`, that `ends_at` is at or before the logical clock, and that the owning placement's `employment_status` is still `ACTIVE`; it recomputes `actual_subsidy_cents` through the actual variant and leaves `projected_subsidy_cents` untouched. `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` calls a shift off before it happens and requires that `starts_at` is strictly later than the logical clock; it lowers `projected_subsidy_cents`, leaves `actual_subsidy_cents` untouched, and re-runs the grant ledger, which may flip `funding_posture` and promote one deferred supportive-services request. `CONFLICTED → CANCELLED` clears a shift that a supervision cascade has already conflicted and carries no clock precondition; it recomputes the owning placement's `schedule_compliance`, which returns to `COMPLIANT` once no conflicted shift remains on the placement. The two remaining edges, `SCHEDULED → CONFLICTED` and `CONFLICTED → SCHEDULED`, are driven only by the supervision cascades of section 3.3 and are never requested directly. `WORKED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal, and any transition outside the declared set is refused with `ERR_WORK_SHIFT_INVALID_STATE`.

A placement's `schedule_compliance` follows mechanically from its shift rows: it is `OUT_OF_COMPLIANCE` when any shift on the placement has a `status` of `CONFLICTED`, and `COMPLIANT` otherwise. Cancelled and worked shifts never make a placement non-compliant; only a live conflicted shift does. A placement with no shift rows at all, and a placement all of whose shifts were cancelled, are both `COMPLIANT` — the field carries no third "not yet evaluated" value.

Separation ends the employment relationship and settles its money, and it belongs to the `EmployerLiaison`. The payload names `separation_reason`, one of `VOLUNTARY_QUIT`, `TERMINATED_FOR_CAUSE`, `LAYOFF`, `SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`, `RETURNED_TO_CUSTODY`, or `CONTRACT_END`, and `separation_date`, a calendar date that must fall on or after the placement's `start_date` and on or before the logical clock's current UTC date. The placement's `employment_status` must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_PLACEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. Once accepted, the placement moves `ACTIVE → SEPARATED` and a seven-step cascade runs inside the same request. `separation_reason` and `separation_date` are recorded. Every shift on the placement whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `CONFLICTED` and whose `starts_at` is later than the logical clock moves to `CANCELLED`. `projected_subsidy_cents` recomputes to 0, `actual_subsidy_cents` is frozen at its current figure, and `disbursed_subsidy_cents` is set to that frozen `actual_subsidy_cents`. The grant ledger then re-runs on the funding grant: `committed_cents` drops by the placement's former projected amount and `disbursed_cents` rises by `disbursed_subsidy_cents`. Because `available_cents` rises, `funding_posture` is recomputed and may flip from `RESTRICTED` to `OPEN`, and the deferral waterfall of section 3.8 promotes at most one deferred request. The opening's `filled_count` falls by one, raising `remaining_openings` and possibly flipping the opening `FILLED → OPEN`, and the employer partnership's `active_placement_count` falls by one. Finally, if this was the participant's last placement with `employment_status` of `ACTIVE` — that is, the count of their active placements has become 0 — the participant's `stage` walks backwards, `PLACED → POST_RELEASE` or `RETENTION → POST_RELEASE`, and `engagement_posture` is recomputed. Shifts may be read individually, listed as a nested sub-resource of a placement ordered by `starts_at` ascending and narrowed by `status`, or listed at the top level narrowed by `placement_id`, `status`, or `conflict_reason`.

### 3.8. Funder Grants, Supportive Services and the Deferral Waterfall

A `FunderAnalyst` records the money. A grant declares `funder_name`, the funder's display name; `program_year`, the programme year the award belongs to, an integer from 2000 through 2100; `authorized_cents`, the total award, which must be strictly greater than 0; `allowed_categories`, a non-empty array naming which support categories this grant may fund, drawn from `TOOLS`, `UNIFORM`, `TRANSIT_PASS`, `ID_FEE`, `LICENSE_FEE`, and `WAGE_SUBSIDY`; `per_participant_cap_cents`, a ceiling on one participant's cumulative support charges against this grant, which must be strictly greater than 0; and `retention_benchmark_pct`, the funder's ninety-day retention benchmark expressed to one decimal place between 0.0 and 100.0 inclusive. A new grant opens with `committed_cents` and `disbursed_cents` of 0, `available_cents` equal to `authorized_cents`, `open_request_count` of 0, and `funding_posture` of `OPEN`.

The grant ledger is recomputed from scratch after every write that could move it, and it is defined by aggregation rather than by incremental bookkeeping. `committed_cents` is the sum of `total_charge_cents` across every support request on this grant whose `status` is `APPROVED`, plus the sum of `projected_subsidy_cents` across every placement on this grant whose `employment_status` is `ACTIVE`. `disbursed_cents` is the sum of `total_charge_cents` across every support request on this grant whose `status` is `DISBURSED`, plus the sum of `disbursed_subsidy_cents` across every placement on this grant whose `employment_status` is `SEPARATED`. `available_cents` is `authorized_cents` less `committed_cents` less `disbursed_cents`, which is exactly why the identity in section 2.9 holds after every write. `open_request_count` is the number of support requests on the grant whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, or `DEFERRED`. Requests in `SUBMITTED`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `DEFERRED`, `DENIED`, or `VOIDED` contribute nothing at all to the two money columns — only `APPROVED` (committed) and `DISBURSED` (disbursed) do. Deferred requests are counted in `open_request_count` while holding no money, and that is precisely what makes the promotion waterfall safe.

`funding_posture` is banded off `available_cents` against a restricted floor of fifteen percent of the award: the floor is `authorized_cents` multiplied by 0.15, rounded half up to whole cents. The posture is evaluated top down, so the first matching condition wins: `EXHAUSTED` when `available_cents` is 0 or below; otherwise `RESTRICTED` when `available_cents` is strictly less than the restricted floor; otherwise `OPEN`. The posture therefore moves between all three values in both directions as money is committed and released: `OPEN → RESTRICTED` when the balance falls below the floor while remaining above zero, `OPEN → EXHAUSTED` when a single commitment consumes everything at once, `RESTRICTED → EXHAUSTED` as the balance reaches zero or below, `RESTRICTED → OPEN` when a release brings the balance back to at least the floor, `EXHAUSTED → RESTRICTED` when a release brings the balance above zero but still below the floor, and `EXHAUSTED → OPEN` when a large release brings it to at least the floor. Every transition that raises `available_cents` — the three restoring edges — runs the deferral waterfall. While a grant is `EXHAUSTED`, new support-request creation against it is refused with `ERR_GRANT_EXHAUSTED`. While it is `RESTRICTED`, creation still succeeds but an approval that the balance cannot cover lands in `DEFERRED` rather than `APPROVED`. An award of 5,000,000 cents carries a restricted floor of 750,000 cents; with one approved request of 20,600 and one active placement projecting 92,500, `committed_cents` is 113,100, `disbursed_cents` is 0, `available_cents` is 4,886,900, and the posture is `OPEN`. Disbursing that request moves `committed_cents` to 92,500 and `disbursed_cents` to 20,600 while `available_cents` stays at 4,886,900 and the posture stays `OPEN`.

A `CaseManager` requests supportive services — tools, uniforms, transit passes, identification fees, licence fees — by naming the beneficiary `participant_id`, the funding `grant_id`, the request-level `category` (one of `TOOLS`, `UNIFORM`, `TRANSIT_PASS`, `ID_FEE`, or `LICENSE_FEE`), the grant's `expected_grant_version`, the itemised `line_items`, and a `justification` narrative for the funder file. `line_items` is a non-empty array of at most six elements, and each element carries a `description` string, a `unit_cost_cents` integer of at least 1, and a `quantity` integer from 1 through 20 inclusive; an empty array, a seventh element, a missing `description`, a `unit_cost_cents` below 1, or a `quantity` outside 1 through 20 is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. The array's order is the pinned evaluation order of the pricing calculation. A `category` that is not a member of the grant's `allowed_categories` is refused with `ERR_CATEGORY_NOT_FUNDABLE`; note that `WAGE_SUBSIDY` is an allowable grant category for funding placements but is never a support-request `category`.

Pricing is a two-stage calculation with a deliberately pinned rounding point. First the coverage rate is selected from the request-level `category` — not from any individual line — at 0.85 for `TOOLS`, 0.90 for `UNIFORM`, 1.00 for `TRANSIT_PASS`, 1.00 for `ID_FEE`, and 0.60 for `LICENSE_FEE`. One rate governs the whole request. Each line's award is then that line's `unit_cost_cents` multiplied by its `quantity` multiplied by the coverage rate, rounded half up to whole cents, and each line's result is echoed back in the response as `line_items[i].line_award_cents`. `requested_cents` is the sum of those per-line awards, and that sum is never re-rounded. Rounding per line and rounding the aggregate genuinely diverge: for a `TOOLS` request at 0.85 with four lines of `quantity` 1 and unit costs 4550, 5550, 3750, and 2570, the per-line awards are 3868, 4718, 3188, and 2185 for a `requested_cents` of 13,959 — whereas rounding the aggregate of 16,420 at 0.85 would have produced 13,957, a two-cent divergence that the per-line rule forbids.

The administrative fee is then tiered off `requested_cents` alone and never off the total. When `requested_cents` is at most 5,000, the fee rate is 0.00; when `requested_cents` is strictly greater than 5,000 and at most 20,000, the rate is 0.03; when `requested_cents` is strictly greater than 20,000, the rate is 0.05. `admin_fee_cents` is `requested_cents` multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents, and `total_charge_cents` is `requested_cents` plus `admin_fee_cents`. Selecting the tier from the total instead of from the request is observably wrong, because adding the fee can push the total past a boundary the request itself never crossed: a `requested_cents` of exactly 20,000 selects the middle tier, yielding a fee of 600 and a total of 20,600, whereas reading the tier from that 20,600 total would wrongly select 0.05 and produce a fee of 1,030. A `requested_cents` of 5,000 selects the lowest tier for a fee of 0 and a total of 5,000; 5,001 selects the middle tier for a fee of 150 and a total of 5,151; 13,959 selects the middle tier for a fee of 419 and a total of 14,378; and 57,143 selects the highest tier for a fee of 2,857 and a total of exactly 60,000.

The per-participant cap is the last gate on creation. Take the sum of `total_charge_cents` already held by this participant's support requests on this same grant whose `status` is `APPROVED`, `DISBURSED`, `DEFERRED`, `SUBMITTED`, or `INFO_REQUESTED`, and add this request's own `total_charge_cents`. If that sum is strictly greater than the grant's `per_participant_cap_cents`, the request is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Landing exactly on the cap is allowed; one cent past it is refused. Denied and voided requests are excluded from the running sum entirely. An accepted request is created with `status` of `SUBMITTED`, `info_rounds_used` of 0, `submitted_by_user_id` set from the caller's `X-User-ID`, and `reviewed_by_user_id`, `settled_by_user_id`, `review_note`, `settlement_note`, `deferred_at`, and `promoted_at` all null; the grant's `open_request_count` rises by one and the grant's `version` advances, while no money is committed yet.

Review belongs to the `ProgramDirector`, carries a `decision` and an optional `review_note`, and is barred to whoever submitted the request. A `decision` of `INFO_REQUESTED` moves the request `SUBMITTED → INFO_REQUESTED`, recording `review_note` and `reviewed_by_user_id`; it requires that fewer than two information-request rounds have been consumed, so a `decision` of `INFO_REQUESTED` when `info_rounds_used` has already reached 2 is refused with `ERR_INFO_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. A `decision` of `DENIED` moves the request to `DENIED` from either `SUBMITTED` or `INFO_REQUESTED`, recording `reviewed_by_user_id` and `review_note` and moving no money; `DENIED` is terminal.

A `decision` of `APPROVED` has two possible landing states, and the server — not the director — chooses between them. When the grant's `funding_posture` is not `EXHAUSTED` and its `available_cents` is at least the request's `total_charge_cents`, the request moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`: `reviewed_by_user_id` is recorded, the grant's `committed_cents` rises by `total_charge_cents`, and the ledger re-runs, lowering `available_cents` and possibly flipping `funding_posture` from `OPEN` to `RESTRICTED` or to `EXHAUSTED`. When instead the grant's `funding_posture` is `RESTRICTED` and its `available_cents` is strictly less than the request's `total_charge_cents`, the same `APPROVED` decision lands the request in `DEFERRED` rather than `APPROVED`: `reviewed_by_user_id` is recorded and `deferred_at` is stamped from the logical clock, but no money moves at all, because a deferred request commits nothing. The director's intent is recorded as approval; the server downgrades the landing state because funds are short.

Answering an information request belongs back to the `CaseManager`. A resubmission moves the request `INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, requires that `info_rounds_used` is below two, and increments `info_rounds_used` by exactly one — so the third resubmission attempt is refused with `ERR_INFO_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. It may optionally carry replacement `line_items` and a replacement `justification`; when `line_items` is supplied, `requested_cents`, each line's `line_award_cents`, `admin_fee_cents`, and `total_charge_cents` are all recomputed from the new array, the same shape constraints apply, `ERR_CATEGORY_NOT_FUNDABLE` remains reachable, and the per-participant cap is re-applied — with this request's own prior `total_charge_cents` excluded from the running sum, since the new total replaces it rather than adding to it.

Settlement also belongs to the `ProgramDirector`, carries a `to_status` and an optional `settlement_note`, and is barred to whoever reviewed the request. `APPROVED → DISBURSED` pays the money out: `settled_by_user_id` and `settlement_note` are recorded, `committed_cents` falls by `total_charge_cents` and `disbursed_cents` rises by the same amount, so `available_cents` and `funding_posture` are unchanged in value by this move. `APPROVED → VOIDED` cancels an approved request: `settled_by_user_id` is recorded, `committed_cents` falls by `total_charge_cents`, the ledger re-runs raising `available_cents` and possibly flipping `funding_posture` back toward `OPEN`, and because `available_cents` rose, the deferral waterfall runs. `DEFERRED → VOIDED` cancels a deferred request and moves no money at all, since a deferred request holds no commitment. All three of `DISBURSED`, `DENIED`, and `VOIDED` are terminal.

The deferral waterfall enforces strict queue discipline. It runs after any write that raises a grant's `available_cents` — a voided settlement, a work-shift cancellation, a placement separation, or a participant exit that voids approved requests. Order that grant's requests whose `status` is `DEFERRED` by `deferred_at` ascending and, where two share a `deferred_at`, by `created_at` ascending; the row `id` is never used as an ordering key. Look only at the head of that queue. If a head exists and its `total_charge_cents` is at most the grant's `available_cents`, promote that head from `DEFERRED` to `APPROVED`, stamp `promoted_at` from the logical clock, and add its `total_charge_cents` to `committed_cents`, then recompute the ledger and `funding_posture`. Otherwise promote nothing. At most one request is promoted per triggering write, even when the released amount would have covered two, and the waterfall never skips the head: given a queue of a request deferred at 09:00 totalling 80,000 and one deferred at 10:00 totalling 5,000, a void that releases 60,000 into a balance of 0 promotes nothing at all, and the smaller later request stays `DEFERRED`. A void releasing 150,000 instead promotes the first request only, leaving the second `DEFERRED` even though 70,000 remains available.

A computed ledger read exposes the money for one grant, restricted to `ProgramDirector` and `FunderAnalyst`: it reports `grant_id`, `authorized_cents`, `committed_cents`, `disbursed_cents`, `available_cents`, `restricted_floor_cents` (the fifteen-percent floor described above), `funding_posture`, `open_request_count`, and `deferred_request_count`, the last being the count of requests on the grant whose `status` is `DEFERRED`, which is reported by this read and never persisted on the grant row. Grants may also be listed with pagination and narrowed by `funding_posture` or `program_year`, and support requests may be listed narrowed by `participant_id`, `grant_id`, or `status`.

### 3.9. Suspension, Licence Relief, Retention Follow-Up, Exit and Funder Reporting

Programme suspension is the `ProgramDirector`'s emergency brake and moves along its own dimension, independently of `stage`. Driving `to_status` to `SUSPENDED` requires that the participant's `stage` is not `EXITED` and that `suspension_status` is currently `NONE`; it records the supplied `suspension_reason`, forces `engagement_posture` to `FROZEN`, and withdraws the participant from readiness training — every enrollment of this participant whose `status` is `ENROLLED` and whose session's `status` is `SCHEDULED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, decrementing that session's `enrolled_count` and incrementing its `remaining_seats`. Driving `to_status` to `LIFTED` requires that `suspension_status` is currently `SUSPENDED`; it clears `suspension_reason` back to null and recomputes `engagement_posture` to `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED`. Lifting a suspension does **not** restore the withdrawn enrollments: the seats were released to other participants and the withdrawals stand, so re-enrollment is a fresh act. A suspended participant keeps whatever `stage` it had, and while suspended it is barred from placement creation, offer responses, support requests, and enrollment with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_SUSPENDED`. Attempting to suspend an exited participant is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_INVALID_STATE`, and so is any suspension or lift that is not one of the two declared edges.

Occupational-licensing barrier navigation is a third participant dimension, and it is cyclic. The petition is opened by a `CaseManager` driving `to_status` to `PETITION_SUBMITTED` from `NONE`, which requires that `stage` is not `EXITED` and that `occupation_code` is present in the body; `license_relief_occupation_code` is stored from that `occupation_code` on this first submission. The licensing board's request for more information is recorded by a `CaseManager` driving `to_status` to `INFO_REQUESTED` from `PETITION_SUBMITTED`. The cycle edge returns `INFO_REQUESTED → PETITION_SUBMITTED`, requires that `petition_rounds_used` is strictly below two, and increments `petition_rounds_used` by exactly one — so the third re-submission is refused with `ERR_PETITION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. A `ProgramDirector` closes the petition favourably by driving `to_status` to `RELIEF_GRANTED` from `PETITION_SUBMITTED`, which is what makes placements on licence-gated openings whose `license_occupation_code` equals the participant's stored `license_relief_occupation_code` creatable at last, or unfavourably by driving `to_status` to `PETITION_DENIED` from `PETITION_SUBMITTED`, which pushes `engagement_posture` to `RESTRICTED`. A `CaseManager` may instead abandon a petition by driving `to_status` to `PETITION_WITHDRAWN`, which is legal only from `INFO_REQUESTED`. `RELIEF_GRANTED`, `PETITION_DENIED`, and `PETITION_WITHDRAWN` are all terminal, so relief cannot be re-petitioned once resolved, and every off-diagram move — including withdrawing directly from `PETITION_SUBMITTED` or granting relief from `INFO_REQUESTED` — is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_INVALID_STATE`. Every licence-relief write may also carry an optional `note` for the case file. This dimension is driven by both a `CaseManager` and a `ProgramDirector`, each on the edges named above.

The `stage` dimension also walks on its own, driven by employment rather than by any operator. A participant in `POST_RELEASE` advances to `PLACED` the moment any placement of theirs enters `ACTIVE`. A participant in `PLACED` walks backwards to `POST_RELEASE` when their last placement with `employment_status` of `ACTIVE` leaves that state through separation, so that the count of their active placements becomes 0; `engagement_posture` is recomputed on that revert. A participant in `RETENTION` reverts to `POST_RELEASE` on exactly the same condition — the loss of the last active placement — and never straight to `PLACED`. The forward edge into `RETENTION` is described next.

Retention follow-up is recorded by a `CaseManager` against a placement that has started, naming `milestone_days`, the `outcome`, and optionally a `recidivism_event`. `milestone_days` must be exactly 30, 60, or 90; `outcome` must be one of `RETAINED`, `SEPARATED`, or `UNREACHABLE`; and `recidivism_event`, when supplied, must be one of `NONE`, `NEW_ARREST`, `TECHNICAL_VIOLATION`, `REVOCATION`, or `RETURN_TO_CUSTODY`. Anything else is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. A check against a placement that never started — one whose `employment_status` is `OFFERED` or `DECLINED`, which therefore has no `start_date` to measure from — is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. The milestone must genuinely have elapsed: when the logical clock is earlier than the placement's `start_date` plus `milestone_days` days, the check is refused with `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_REACHED`, so a placement that started on the first of the month cannot record a thirty-day outcome until the clock reaches the thirty-first. Each milestone may be recorded once only: when the placement already carries an outcome for the supplied `milestone_days`, the check is refused with `ERR_MILESTONE_ALREADY_RECORDED`, and the elapse test is applied before the already-recorded test. An accepted check writes `retention_30_outcome`, `retention_60_outcome`, or `retention_90_outcome` according to `milestone_days`, stamps `last_retention_check_at` from the logical clock, and stores `recidivism_event` when supplied — overwriting whatever it held, since the field carries the latest value recorded by any check on that placement. When `milestone_days` is 30 and `outcome` is `RETAINED` and the checked placement is still `ACTIVE`, the participant's `stage` advances `PLACED → RETENTION`; a thirty-day check with any other `outcome`, or a sixty- or ninety-day check, moves `stage` not at all.

Case closure belongs to the `ProgramDirector` and is terminal for every participant dimension. The caller supplies the `exit_reason` — one of `EMPLOYED_STABLE`, `PROGRAM_COMPLETE`, `DISENGAGED`, `RETURNED_TO_CUSTODY`, or `OTHER` — and the calendar `exit_date`. Exit is legal from `PRE_RELEASE`, `RELEASE_READY`, `POST_RELEASE`, and `RETENTION`, and in every case requires that no placement of this participant has `employment_status` of `ACTIVE`; if one does, the exit is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_HAS_ACTIVE_PLACEMENT`, which is the programme's way of insisting that employment be separated before a case is closed. Exiting a participant who is already `EXITED` is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_INVALID_STATE`, since `EXITED` is terminal. Exit from any of the four permitted stages runs an identical cascade: `stage` becomes `EXITED`, `exit_reason` and `exit_date` are recorded, and `engagement_posture` becomes `FROZEN`; every enrollment of this participant whose `status` is `ENROLLED` and whose session's `status` is `SCHEDULED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with its seat returned, decrementing `enrolled_count` and incrementing `remaining_seats`; every support request of this participant whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `INFO_REQUESTED` moves to `VOIDED` with no ledger movement, because those states never held money; and every support request whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `DEFERRED` also moves to `VOIDED`, where each `APPROVED` void releases that request's `total_charge_cents` from its grant's `committed_cents`, re-runs that grant's ledger, may flip its `funding_posture`, and may promote one `DEFERRED` request per the waterfall of section 3.8. Support requests already `DISBURSED`, `DENIED`, or `VOIDED` are untouched. Once a participant is `EXITED`, the record is frozen against the direct writes listed in section 2.8, each refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_INVALID_STATE`.

Funder reporting closes the loop. A computed outcome report for one grant, restricted to `ProgramDirector` and `FunderAnalyst`, accepts an optional `variant` query parameter and reports `grant_id`, the echoed `variant`, `participants_served`, `denominator_placements`, `retained_placements`, `retention_rate_pct`, `recidivism_rate_pct`, `benchmark_met`, `subsidy_disbursed_cents`, and `support_disbursed_cents`. The two variants share every numerator definition and differ only in which placements enter the denominator. Under `COHORT` — the default, used when `variant` is omitted or equals `COHORT` — the input set is every placement on this grant whose `employment_status` is `ACTIVE` or `SEPARATED`, that is, every placement that actually started, regardless of whether the participant has exited. Under `EXIT_BASED` the input set is narrowed to those same started placements whose participant's `stage` is `EXITED`, which is the closed-case denominator funders use for final reporting. Placements in `OFFERED` or `DECLINED` are excluded from both variants, because they never started. Any `variant` value other than those two is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

Within the chosen input set, `denominator_placements` is the count of placements in it, and `retained_placements` is the count of those whose `retention_90_outcome` is `RETAINED` — the ninety-day column only, never the thirty- or sixty-day ones. `retention_rate_pct` is 100 multiplied by `retained_placements` divided by `denominator_placements`, rounded half up to one decimal place, and is exactly null — not 0.0, and not omitted from the body — when `denominator_placements` is 0. `participants_served` equals the count of distinct `participant_id` values in the input set, which is also the recidivism denominator; the recidivism numerator is the count of distinct `participant_id` values in the input set having at least one placement whose `recidivism_event` is anything other than `NONE`. `recidivism_rate_pct` is 100 multiplied by that numerator divided by that denominator, rounded half up to one decimal place, and is likewise exactly null when the participant denominator is 0. Both rates are distinct-participant versus per-placement measures, so a participant with two placements counts twice in `denominator_placements` but once in `participants_served`. `benchmark_met` is false whenever `retention_rate_pct` is null, and otherwise true exactly when `retention_rate_pct` is greater than or equal to the grant's `retention_benchmark_pct`, so landing exactly on the benchmark meets it. `subsidy_disbursed_cents` is the sum of `disbursed_subsidy_cents` over the variant's input set and therefore does move between variants. `support_disbursed_cents`, by contrast, is the sum of `total_charge_cents` across every support request on this grant whose `status` is `DISBURSED`; it is a grant-level column rather than a placement roll-up and is deliberately **not** variant-filtered, so it is identical under both variants. With three started placements of which one is retained at ninety days, and only two of the three participants exited — one of them being the retained one — `COHORT` reports a `retention_rate_pct` of 33.3 while `EXIT_BASED` reports 50.0 from the very same data. A grant with no started placements reports both rates as null and `benchmark_met` as false.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is rooted at the API root, and every path is absolute as written. A request that successfully creates a new resource returns HTTP 201 with the created resource in `data`; every other successful request — reads, lists, updates, state transitions, and computed reports — returns HTTP 200.
