# Retransmission Consent Negotiation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every three years a broadcast station group and every multichannel distributor that carries its signals must reopen the same argument. The station group owns local signals that the distributor's subscribers expect to find on channel three; the distributor owns the billing relationship with those subscribers and would rather not pay more for a signal it can already receive off-air. Between those two positions sits a statutory framework: the station may elect compulsory must-carry treatment and receive nothing, or it may elect retransmission consent and negotiate a per-subscriber fee — and a third party, the network the station is affiliated with, takes a contractual share of whatever the station collects. When the parties cannot agree, the law does not simply let the signal go dark; it requires documented good-faith conduct, and it requires that subscribers be warned in advance.

This document specifies the Retransmission Consent Negotiation API, the system of record a station group uses to run that cycle end to end. The API is mounted under the `/retrans` path prefix. It tracks which election was served on which distributor for which cycle, the subscriber counts the distributor reports and the station group's right to audit them, the round-by-round offer exchange and the two different fee ladders that exchange produces, the good-faith log and impasse machinery, the executed carriage agreement with its independent execution and network-approval dimensions, interim extensions that keep a signal up while the successor deal is unfinished, disputes over what "digital" or "streaming" actually means inside a grant, and finally the monthly fee settlement that splits gross carriage revenue with the network.

Three characteristics of this domain drive most of the system's complexity, and the requirements below should be read with them in mind. First, almost every number the system produces is a projection of a projection: an offer is priced against a subscriber count that may later be revised by audit, and the revision must flow forward into already-issued settlements without corrupting the historical record. Second, the same economics are computed two different ways on purpose — one ladder is quoted during negotiation, a materially different ladder is billed under the executed contract — and confusing the two is the single most consequential implementation error available. Third, binding the station group is a legal act, so the system enforces segregation of duties between the counsel who accepts an offer and the counsel who signs the resulting agreement, and it refuses to let anyone bind the group on a stale view of the negotiation record.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint. Where a rule here and a rule in Section 3 describe the same behaviour from different angles, both are normative and they do not conflict.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. Both must be present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` must name one of exactly six recognised roles: `CarriageAdmin`, `StationGroupNegotiator`, `GroupCounsel`, `DistributorRep`, `NetworkRepresentative`, and `ComplianceOfficer`. A request with a missing header, a header that is empty or contains only whitespace, or a role string outside that set of six is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. A request whose headers are well-formed but whose role does not appear in the target operation's authorised role list is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

Two of the six roles are entity-backed, meaning the header value is itself a foreign key into the data model. When `X-User-Role` is `StationGroupNegotiator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Station`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Station` compare the resource's station foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `DistributorRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Distributor`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Distributor` compare the resource's distributor foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. A failed ownership comparison on either of those two roles is reported as `ERR_NOT_COUNTERPARTY`. A `DistributorRep` whose `X-User-ID` resolves to no distributor at all is reported as `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_FOUND`.

The remaining four roles are opaque operator identifiers not tied to any persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `CarriageAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque platform-operator identifier; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `GroupCounsel`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque counsel identifier; the server records it (as `accepted_by_user_id` and `executed_by_user_id` on a carriage agreement, and as `issued_by_user_id` on a fee settlement) and compares those recorded values against each other to enforce segregation of duties, but it never compares them to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `NetworkRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque network affiliate-relations identifier which the server does not persist or compare. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque neutral-adjudicator identifier; the server may record the officer's role (as `actor_role` on a good-faith log entry) but does not compare the identifier to any entity's `id`.

`CarriageAdmin` is the platform operator: it registers stations, attaches network affiliation contracts, registers distributors, drives the time-based maintenance sweep, and sets the logical clock. `StationGroupNegotiator` acts as the station itself and owns the election, offer, audit-rights, dispute and extension levers. `GroupCounsel` binds and executes contracts and runs the settlement ledger. `DistributorRep` acts as the distributor itself, files subscriber reports and counters offers. `NetworkRepresentative` approves or rejects the rights scope the station group grants downstream. `ComplianceOfficer` is the neutral function that adjudicates audit findings and rights-definition disputes and records subscriber blackout notices.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` and continues with exactly twenty further characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the single instant in this system taken from the host's real clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.5, because it describes the transport exchange rather than any business fact.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints and omitted entirely from single-object responses. `total_count` is the number of rows that survive the operation's filters, not the number of rows in the underlying table, because filtering is applied before the page window is taken; `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of items actually returned is still less than `total_count`.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List operations are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip and defaults to 0; negative values are refused. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and so is either parameter supplied in a form that is not a base-ten integer literal.

Unless a specific operation states otherwise, every list is ordered by the resource's creation timestamp ascending. Because `created_at` is read from the logical clock rather than from real time, rows written without an intervening clock move share a creation timestamp; such ties are broken by a monotonic server-assigned insertion sequence, so the effective order is always the order in which the rows were actually written, and that same ordering is what "creation-timestamp ascending" means everywhere it governs a cascade. The good-faith conduct record is the one place where this ordering is also semantically load-bearing rather than merely conventional, because append position is itself recorded on each entry.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope described in §2.2.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every resource the API creates is addressed by an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix — clients never supply it and must not infer a resource's kind from its shape. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written; like every other domain instant it is read from the logical clock of §2.5 and never from the host's real clock, so it renders the clock's value at the moment of the write and every row written before the clock moves again carries the same `created_at`.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` and `version`. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, also with three fractional digits and likewise read from the logical clock, refreshed on every mutation of the resource, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade; on a freshly created row it equals `created_at`. `version` is an integer that stands at 1 on a freshly created row and increments by one on every mutation of the resource, and it is the value the optimistic-concurrency mechanism in §2.7 compares against. Recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation for both purposes: when a settlement's amounts are rewritten because a rights dispute resolved, that settlement's `updated_at` moves and its `version` advances, and the same is true of the parent agreement whose lifetime totals were re-derived.

Fixed three-digit rendering is confined to `created_at`, `updated_at` and the `api_request_timestamp` of §2.2. Every other instant-valued field in this system — every `*_utc` field on every resource, and the `current_utc` the clock endpoint answers with — is rendered at whatever precision the instant it holds actually carries: no fractional digits at all when the instant falls exactly on a whole second, and three fractional digits otherwise, any precision finer than a millisecond being truncated. Parsing on the way in is deliberately tolerant about how the instant is spelled: a supplied value may end in `Z`, carry an explicit numeric UTC offset, or omit the zone marker altogether, and it may be a bare calendar date with no time part; whatever arrives is normalised to UTC before it is stored, so an offset-bearing value round-trips as the equivalent `Z` instant rather than in the offset form it was sent in. A client-supplied instant therefore round-trips at the precision it arrived with rather than being rewritten into the `created_at` format; the fields stamped from the logical clock of §2.5 — `filed_at_utc`, `impasse_declared_at_utc`, `served_at_utc` and `opened_at_utc` — inherit whatever precision the clock itself carries, so they read `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` while the clock still sits at its initial value; and the instants the server derives by whole-day arithmetic — `extension_end_utc`, `blackout_notice_deadline_utc`, and the agreement `effective_end_utc` an interim extension pushes forward — keep the precision of the instant they were derived from.

`GoodFaithLog` is the sole exception. A good-faith conduct entry is an append-only record of something that already happened, so it is never edited after it is written. Entries of that kind carry only `id` and `created_at`; both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from a `GoodFaithLog` representation entirely.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and the Statutory Calendar

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. The server maintains a single logical clock, and every deadline, expiry, window and freshness comparison in this document evaluates against that clock. Before any client has set it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it never falls back to the host's real time. A `CarriageAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the test-driving clock endpoint, which answers with a `data` object carrying `current_utc` and nothing else, and performs no state transitions of its own — moving the clock forward past a batch of expiries does not itself expire anything. Any parseable instant is accepted whatever its relation to the clock's present value, so the clock may be wound backwards as freely as forwards.

Two fixed calendar constants govern the cycle. The election deadline, `ELECTION_DEADLINE_UTC`, is `2026-10-01T00:00:00Z`: a carriage election may be amended, filed or withdrawn only while the logical clock is at or before that instant, and acting exactly at the deadline instant is permitted. The audit-rights window runs for 180 days measured from a subscriber report's period end, and the boundary instant is likewise inside the window.

Deadline comparisons in this system are inclusive at the boundary unless stated otherwise: "at or before" means the boundary instant qualifies, and expiry sweeps fire when a term-end instant is at or before the clock rather than strictly before it.

### 2.6. Cents, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary amount in this system is an integer number of whole cents. There are no fractional-cent representations, no decimal strings, and no currency symbols; a per-subscriber-per-month rate of six dollars is the integer 600. Rates are capped: no per-subscriber rate may exceed 600 cents, and that ceiling applies not only to a proposed base rate but to every escalated rate the projected ladder produces.

Every proportion in this system is an integer number of basis points, where 10,000 basis points is the whole. An annual escalator of seven percent is the integer 700; a network split of fifty-five percent is 5,500; a penetration commitment covering forty percent of a distributor's video households is 4,000.

Where a computation must reduce a ratio to an integer, the rounding method is pinned per computation and is never left to the implementer's discretion. Unless a specific rule names a different method, the method is round half up: a value exactly halfway between two integers rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. Three computations deliberately use truncation toward negative infinity instead of round half up, and each says so explicitly at the point of use: the device-to-household conversion in the subscriber basis determination, the midpoint interim-extension rate, and the actual-penetration measurement in basis points. Rounding order is also pinned: where a formula rounds an intermediate and then multiplies, rounding the final product instead produces a different number and is incorrect.

### 2.7. Version Preconditions on Binding Writes

Two operations in this system commit the station group to something it cannot easily undo, and both therefore require the client to prove it is acting on a current view of the record rather than a stale one. On those two operations the version field is a required input, not an optional one; omitting it is a schema violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Accepting an outstanding offer requires `expected_negotiation_version`, carrying the `version` of the parent `Negotiation` the client last observed. If that value does not equal the negotiation's current `version` at the moment of the write, the acceptance is rejected with `ERR_STALE_NEGOTIATION_VERSION` and HTTP 412. This guard exists because countering does not terminate the counterparty's outstanding offer, so accepting an older offer that is still `SUBMITTED` remains legitimate purely on that offer's own state; the only thing the accepting counsel could be missing is a further round the negotiation has since recorded, and the stale version is the sole signal that this is happening.

Creating an interim extension requires `expected_agreement_version`, carrying the `version` of the parent `CarriageAgreement` the client last observed. If that value does not equal the agreement's current `version` at the moment of the write, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. This guard exists because a newly created extension pushes the agreement's effective term end forward and draws down a finite extension-day budget, so a second extension computed from the earlier view of the agreement would land a window and a budget draw the client never intended.

No other operation in this system accepts a version field. An operation not named in this subsection performs no version check, and supplying an unexpected version-shaped key to it is simply an unknown field.

### 2.8. Path Resolution and the Order of Rejection

When a path carries more than one identifier, identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that does not resolve to a live row determines the 404 that comes back. Each resource kind has its own not-found code: `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ELECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NEGOTIATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_FOUND`. A request naming a real negotiation under a non-existent election reports the election's absence, never the negotiation's.

Because many operations in this system are guarded by several rules at once, the order in which those rules are evaluated is itself part of the contract. A request that violates more than one rule must report the violation that comes first in the following sequence, and the checks are numbered so that a lower number always wins:

1. Header authentication, as described in §2.1 — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every operation.
2. Role authorisation against the target operation's role list — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Applies to every operation.
3. Request-shape validation — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Applies to every operation. This covers missing required fields, enum values outside their declared sets, non-integer or negative integers where a non-negative integer is required, a proposed base rate above 600 cents, an escalator above 1,000 basis points, a penetration commitment outside 0 through 10,000 basis points, a term length outside 12 through 120 months, an extension length outside 1 through 90 days, a reporting or settlement period whose end is not strictly after its start, a carriage term whose end is not strictly after its start, a `limit` outside 1 through 100, a negative `offset`, and any timestamp that does not parse as an ISO 8601 UTC instant. It also covers a required request body that is absent, blank, unparseable as JSON, or parses to something other than a JSON object. Unknown fields anywhere in a request body are ignored rather than rejected. The whole body is validated before any identifier in the path or in the body is resolved, so a malformed field is reported even when the path names a resource that does not exist.
4. Path identifier resolution, left to right — the 404 family listed above. Applies to every operation with a path parameter.
5. Body foreign-key resolution — `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate. Applies to the four operations that accept a foreign key in the body: opening a carriage election, filing a subscriber report, submitting an offer, and issuing a fee settlement. Where one body carries two foreign keys — `station_id` and `distributor_id` on a carriage election — they resolve in that order, so an unknown station is reported even when the distributor is unknown as well.
6. Counterparty ownership — `ERR_NOT_COUNTERPARTY`. Applies to eight operations: amending an election, filing an election, withdrawing an election, submitting an offer, rejecting an offer, opening an audit finding, creating an interim extension, and raising a rights dispute.
7. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Applies to two operations: executing an agreement and voiding a fee settlement.
8. Election is in `DRAFT` — `ERR_ELECTION_NOT_DRAFT`. Applies to amending an election and filing an election.
9. Election is withdrawable, meaning its status is `NEGOTIATING` or `ELECTION_EFFECTIVE` — `ERR_ELECTION_NOT_WITHDRAWABLE`. Applies to withdrawing an election.
10. Negotiation is still open, meaning its status is `OPEN`, `AWAITING_STATION_OFFER`, `AWAITING_DISTRIBUTOR_RESPONSE` or `IMPASSE` — `ERR_NEGOTIATION_NOT_OPEN`. Applies to four operations: submitting an offer, appending a good-faith log entry, declaring impasse, and serving a blackout notice. `CONSENTED` and `CANCELLED` negotiations accept none of the four.
11. Offer is outstanding, meaning its status is `SUBMITTED` — `ERR_OFFER_NOT_OUTSTANDING`. Applies to accepting an offer and rejecting an offer.
12. Agreement is in the required execution phase — `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_PENDING_EXECUTION`. Applies to five operations. Network approval and execution require `execution_status` equal to `PENDING_EXECUTION`; creating an interim extension, raising a rights dispute and issuing a fee settlement all require `execution_status` equal to `EXECUTED`.
13. Subscriber report is auditable, meaning its status is `REPORTED`, `UNDER_AUDIT` or `ADJUSTED` — `ERR_REPORT_NOT_AUDITABLE`. Applies to opening an audit finding; an `ACCEPTED` report is closed to audit.
14. Audit finding is `OPEN` — `ERR_AUDIT_FINDING_NOT_OPEN`. Applies to resolving an audit finding.
15. Rights dispute is still pending, meaning its status is `RAISED` or `IN_MEDIATION` — `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_PENDING`. Applies to resolving a rights dispute.
16. Fee settlement is in a state that accepts the requested action — `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_ISSUED`. Applies to truing up a settlement and voiding a settlement. True-up requires `ISSUED`; voiding accepts `ISSUED` or `TRUED_UP`; a `VOIDED` settlement accepts neither.
17. Must-carry carriage is compulsory, uncompensated and `BROADCAST_BASIC` only — `ERR_MUST_CARRY_NO_NEGOTIATION`. Applies to filing an election and submitting an offer.
18. An extension rate basis of `LAST_STATION_OFFER` or `MIDPOINT` requires a station-group offer on record — `ERR_NO_STATION_OFFER_ON_RECORD`. Applies to creating an interim extension.
19. True-up requires an audit adjustment to exist — `ERR_TRUE_UP_NOT_REQUIRED`. Applies to truing up a settlement.
20. The logical clock is at or before `ELECTION_DEADLINE_UTC` — `ERR_ELECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Applies to amending, filing and withdrawing an election.
21. The audit-rights window is still open — `ERR_AUDIT_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Applies to opening an audit finding.
22. The blackout notice is timely — `ERR_BLACKOUT_NOTICE_LATE`. Applies to serving a blackout notice.
23. Good-faith meetings are sufficient — `ERR_GOOD_FAITH_NOT_SATISFIED`. Applies to declaring impasse.
24. Offers have been exchanged by both sides — `ERR_NO_OFFERS_EXCHANGED`. Applies to declaring impasse.
25. Every right inside the offer is grantable under the station's affiliation contract — `ERR_RIGHTS_NOT_GRANTABLE`. Applies to two operations: submitting an offer, and approving an agreement on network approval, where the agreement's `granted_rights_scope` plays the part the offer's three rights flags play.
26. The station has an affiliation contract in status `ACTIVE` — `ERR_AFFILIATION_NOT_ACTIVE`. Applies to three operations: submitting an offer, executing an agreement, and approving an agreement on network approval.
27. Network approval permits execution — `ERR_NETWORK_APPROVAL_PENDING`. Applies to executing an agreement.
28. The offered tier is permitted by the affiliation contract — `ERR_TIER_NOT_PERMITTED`. Applies to submitting an offer.
29. No duplicate non-terminal election occupies the slot — `ERR_DUPLICATE_ELECTION`. Applies to opening a carriage election.
30. No duplicate reporting period exists — `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_DUPLICATE`. Applies to filing a subscriber report, and the same code reports a settlement period already used on the same agreement.
31. The carriage term does not overlap an existing one — `ERR_CARRIAGE_TERM_OVERLAP`. Applies to accepting an offer.
32. The extension window does not overlap an existing one — `ERR_EXTENSION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Applies to creating an interim extension.
33. No rate in the projected ladder exceeds the cap — `ERR_RATE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Applies to submitting an offer.
34. The extension-day budget is not exceeded — `ERR_EXTENSION_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. Applies to creating an interim extension.
35. The referenced subscriber report is priceable for this counterparty pair — `ERR_REPORT_NOT_PRICEABLE`. Applies to submitting an offer and issuing a fee settlement.
36. The affiliation contract's network matches the station's affiliation — `ERR_NETWORK_MISMATCH`. Applies to attaching an affiliation agreement to a station.
37. The disputed term is inside the agreement's granted rights scope — `ERR_TERM_NOT_IN_GRANT`. Applies to raising a rights dispute.
38. `expected_negotiation_version` matches the negotiation's current `version` — `ERR_STALE_NEGOTIATION_VERSION`. Applies to accepting an offer.
39. `expected_agreement_version` matches the agreement's current `version` — `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION`. Applies to creating an interim extension.

Note in particular that the two version preconditions sit at the very end of this sequence. A request that is both stale and substantively invalid reports the substantive problem, not the staleness; a stale version surfaces only once every other rule has passed.

### 2.9. Derived Counters and Cascade Discipline

A substantial number of fields in this system are not written by clients at all but re-derived by the server from the rows that exist at the moment of the write. `Station.filed_election_count` counts that station's elections in status `NEGOTIATING`, `ELECTION_EFFECTIVE` or `CONSENT_GRANTED`. `Negotiation.round_number` counts the offers on that negotiation, while `station_offer_count` and `distributor_offer_count` count them split by originating side, and `good_faith_meeting_count` counts only those good-faith entries whose event type records a meeting. `Distributor.open_true_up_count` counts the still-`ISSUED` settlements flagged for true-up that reach back to that distributor through their agreement's negotiation and election. `SubscriberReport.audit_adjustment_subscribers` sums the deltas of substantiated findings on that report. `CarriageAgreement.consumed_extension_days`, `rights_credit_bp_total`, `lifetime_gross_fee_cents` and `lifetime_net_retained_cents` are all sums over child rows in specific statuses, spelled out where each is discussed in Section 3. `Negotiation.notice_compliance_status` is derived from the negotiation's own state, the notices served on it, and the logical clock.

The governing discipline is that these fields are recomputed from scratch rather than incrementally adjusted. When a row leaves the status a counter filters on, the counter must fall; when it re-enters, the counter must rise again. This is what makes the compensating pairs in this system exact: filing an election raises `filed_election_count`, withdrawing it returns the count to its pre-filing value, and voiding a settlement returns the agreement's lifetime ledger to precisely the value it held before that settlement was issued.

Cascades in this system can run several levels deep, and every level is mandatory. A single client request may mutate a child, then its parent, then that parent's siblings, then a grandparent's aggregates — and every entity touched at every level has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` advanced, without any explicit client request naming it. Where a cascade rewrites several sibling rows, the order of processing is stated at the point of use, and where it is stated as creation-timestamp ascending it must be honoured, because the rounding inside each row's recomputation makes the order observable.

### 2.10. Ledger Visibility and Counterparty Confidentiality

Read access is broad but not universal, and two confidentiality boundaries are enforced on list and read operations rather than on writes.

The station group's reverse-compensation split with its network is not the distributor's business. A `DistributorRep` may read almost everything else about an agreement it is party to, but may not read the fee-settlement ledger issued under that agreement.

Conversely, a distributor's subscriber counts are not the network's business. A `NetworkRepresentative` may read stations, distributors, elections, negotiations, offers, agreements, extensions, disputes and the good-faith record, but has no visibility into subscriber reports, into the audit findings opened against them, or into the settlements that drew their basis from a given report. Attempts by either role to reach a restricted read are refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. The authorised-role column in Section 4 is the definitive enumeration of who may reach each operation.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Station Group and Its Network Obligations

Before any carriage can be negotiated, the platform operator must describe the station group's inventory. A `CarriageAdmin` registers each owned-and-operated **Station** with its `call_sign` — exactly four uppercase letters, and globally unique across the system, because two stations cannot share a call sign, a duplicate being a request-shape violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — together with the `market_code` of the designated market area it is licensed to serve, given as exactly three uppercase letters such as `SEA`. The market's Nielsen ranking is recorded as `dma_rank`, an integer of at least 1 and at most 210, where 1 denotes the largest market in the country. The number of television households in that market is recorded as `market_tv_households`, an integer of at least 1; it is carried purely as reporting context and never enters a fee computation. Finally, the station declares its `network_affiliation`, which is one of `ABC`, `CBS`, `NBC`, `FOX`, `CW`, or `INDEPENDENT`. A newly registered station begins with `filed_election_count` at 0.

An affiliated station is not free to sell whatever it likes. Its **network affiliation agreement** — the contract between the station and its network — is attached separately by a `CarriageAdmin`, and it is the document that constrains everything the station group may later grant a distributor. Nothing limits a station to a single affiliation agreement, and where more than one of a station's agreements sits in status `ACTIVE`, every lookup in this document that speaks of "the station's affiliation agreement in status `ACTIVE`" resolves to the earliest-created of them. The affiliation agreement names its `network_name`, drawn from `ABC`, `CBS`, `NBC`, `FOX` or `CW`, and that value must equal the parent station's declared `network_affiliation`. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_NETWORK_MISMATCH`, and so is any attempt to attach an affiliation agreement at all to a station whose `network_affiliation` is `INDEPENDENT` — an independent station has no network counterparty and therefore no affiliation contract to hold.

The affiliation agreement carries the economics of the station's obligation upstream. `reverse_comp_floor_per_sub_cents` is the per-subscriber-per-month amount the network is owed no matter how the tiered split turns out, and it is an integer of at least 0 and at most 400 cents. `split_premium_bp` is the number of basis points the network adds to whatever tiered split rate applies, an integer of at least 0 and at most 2,000 — that is, at most twenty additional percentage points. The affiliation also declares the term boundary as `term_end_utc`, the instant at which the affiliation term runs out; it is required, and it is accepted whatever its relation to the logical clock, so an agreement attached with an already-past term end is still created in `ACTIVE` and stays there until a maintenance pass expires it.

Three boolean permissions control what the station may put inside a downstream grant: `digital_rights_grantable`, `ooh_rights_grantable`, and `streaming_rights_grantable`. Each defaults to false when omitted, meaning that unless the network has explicitly licensed the station to pass a right along, the station may not offer it. Alongside these, `permitted_tiers` lists the tier placements the network will tolerate for the station's signal; it is a non-empty subset of `BROADCAST_BASIC`, `EXPANDED_BASIC` and `DIGITAL_TIER`. A repeated entry is not an error: duplicates are collapsed and the stored list keeps each tier at its first appearance, so the value read back is the de-duplicated list in the order supplied rather than a canonical ordering.

An affiliation agreement's `status` is one of `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `EXPIRED`, and it defaults to `ACTIVE`. Any of the three may be supplied explicitly at creation, `EXPIRED` included. `SUSPENDED` is reachable only by supplying it explicitly at creation time; it models a frozen affiliation for test purposes and has no transitions in or out. `ACTIVE` becomes `EXPIRED` when the time-driven maintenance pass finds the affiliation's `term_end_utc` at or before the logical clock. Because an affiliation in `SUSPENDED` or `EXPIRED` is not `ACTIVE`, a station in either condition can neither have offers submitted on its behalf nor have its agreements executed, and both refusals carry `ERR_AFFILIATION_NOT_ACTIVE`. Affiliation agreements attached to a station can be listed and filtered by `status`.

### 3.2. Distributors and the Measurement Problem

On the other side of the table, a `CarriageAdmin` registers each **Distributor** with its legal `name` — between 1 and 80 characters, and globally unique under exact, case-sensitive comparison, a duplicate being a request-shape violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and its `distributor_type`, one of `CABLE`, `DBS`, `TELCO`, or `VMVPD`. The last of these is a virtual distributor: a streaming service that delivers a channel lineup over the public internet rather than over its own plant. The distributor also declares `video_household_count`, an integer of at least 1, representing how many video households it serves in the station's market. That number is the denominator against which penetration commitments are later measured, and it is deliberately the distributor's own household count rather than the station's `market_tv_households`.

Measurement method is where virtual distributors diverge most sharply from traditional ones, and the distributor must declare which convention it uses as `subscriber_measurement_method`: `BILLED_HOUSEHOLDS`, `WEIGHTED_STREAMING_DEVICES`, or `AVERAGE_MONTHLY_UNIQUE`. This choice drives the billable-subscriber arithmetic described in §3.4 and is the single most consequential number a distributor supplies at registration.

Registration also derives `required_blackout_notice_days` from the distributor's technology class. A traditional distributor — `CABLE`, `DBS` or `TELCO` — must be given 30 days of advance subscriber notice before a signal may go dark. A `VMVPD` gets 14 days, on the theory that a streaming subscriber can change services within a billing cycle. A newly registered distributor begins with `open_true_up_count` at 0.

### 3.3. The Carriage Election Cycle

The **carriage election** is the junction record that joins one station to one distributor for one three-year cycle, and it is where the statutory fork lives. A `StationGroupNegotiator` opens one by naming the `station_id` and `distributor_id` it joins, a `cycle_label` of between 1 and 20 characters such as `2027-2029`, the `election_type` it intends, the `requested_tier` it wants its signal placed on, the `incumbent_expiry_utc` at which the existing carriage arrangement with this distributor lapses, and the `incumbent_rate_per_sub_cents` being paid under that expiring arrangement — an integer of at least 0 and at most 600 cents. The new election's `status` begins at `DRAFT`, its `filed_at_utc` stays null until it is actually served, and its `withdrawal_reason` likewise begins null.

Only one live election may occupy a given slot. If an election already exists for the same station, distributor and `cycle_label` combination whose status is neither `CONSENT_GRANTED` nor `ELECTION_LAPSED`, a second one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ELECTION`. A terminal election, by contrast, does not block a replacement for the same cycle — a lapsed election can be redrafted and served again.

While the election is still `DRAFT`, the negotiator may amend it, revising any of `election_type`, `requested_tier` or `incumbent_rate_per_sub_cents`. A JSON object body is required, but all three fields are optional inside it: a field that is absent or null is left exactly as it stands, and an amendment that revises nothing at all is still accepted and still counts as a mutation, so the election's `updated_at` moves and its `version` advances. Amendment is refused with `ERR_ELECTION_NOT_DRAFT` once the election has left `DRAFT`, and refused with `ERR_ELECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED` once the logical clock has passed the election deadline.

**Filing** is the fork, and the two branches produce genuinely different worlds. Filing requires that the election be in `DRAFT`, that the acting negotiator's `X-User-ID` equal the election's station, and that the logical clock be at or before the election deadline. Filing carries no request body of its own: an absent or empty body is accepted, and anything sent is ignored.

On the retransmission-consent branch, filing an election whose `election_type` is `RETRANS_CONSENT` advances it `DRAFT → NEGOTIATING`. This branch additionally requires, for any station whose `network_affiliation` is not `INDEPENDENT`, that the `requested_tier` be one of the tiers listed in `permitted_tiers` on that station's affiliation agreement in status `ACTIVE`; a request for a tier the network does not permit is refused with `ERR_TIER_NOT_PERMITTED`, and so is a station that holds no `ACTIVE` affiliation agreement at all — this operation reports a missing contract as `ERR_TIER_NOT_PERMITTED` rather than as `ERR_AFFILIATION_NOT_ACTIVE`, which it never raises. Filing on this branch stamps `filed_at_utc` with the current logical clock, creates exactly one **Negotiation** whose `election_id` points back at the election, whose `status` starts at `OPEN` and whose `round_number` starts at 0, computes that negotiation's `blackout_notice_deadline_utc` as described in §3.6, and recomputes the station's `filed_election_count`.

On the must-carry branch, filing an election whose `election_type` is `MUST_CARRY` advances it `DRAFT → ELECTION_EFFECTIVE`. Must-carry carriage is compulsory, uncompensated, and available on `BROADCAST_BASIC` only, because negotiated tier placement exists exclusively on the retransmission-consent path; filing a must-carry election whose `requested_tier` is anything other than `BROADCAST_BASIC` is refused with `ERR_MUST_CARRY_NO_NEGOTIATION`. This branch also stamps `filed_at_utc` and recomputes `filed_election_count`, but it creates no negotiation whatsoever — there is nothing to negotiate. The same rule refuses any offer submitted on a negotiation whose parent election's `election_type` has become `MUST_CARRY`, again with `ERR_MUST_CARRY_NO_NEGOTIATION`.

**Withdrawal** is the compensating counterpart to filing, and it closes the cycle back to where it started. A negotiator supplies a `withdrawal_reason` and the election returns to `DRAFT` from either `NEGOTIATING` or `ELECTION_EFFECTIVE`; withdrawal requires the acting negotiator to be the election's station and the logical clock to be at or before the election deadline. The reason is a required non-empty string with no upper length bound. The supplied reason is captured on the election as `withdrawal_reason` and `filed_at_utc` is cleared back to null, and the station's `filed_election_count` is recomputed — which, because filing raised it and withdrawal lowers it, returns the count to exactly its pre-filing value. `withdrawal_reason` is never cleared afterwards: a subsequent filing stamps `filed_at_utc` again but leaves the reason from the last withdrawal standing on the row. On the `NEGOTIATING → DRAFT` branch, a three-level cascade follows: the earliest-created negotiation on the election moves to `CANCELLED`; because that negotiation is now `CANCELLED`, every offer on it still in `SUBMITTED` moves to `EXPIRED` and every blackout notice on it still in `SERVED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`. On the `ELECTION_EFFECTIVE → DRAFT` branch there is no negotiation to cancel, so the cascade stops at the election itself, but `filed_at_utc`, `withdrawal_reason` and `filed_election_count` are handled identically. Withdrawing an election that is in `DRAFT` — which has nothing to withdraw — or one that has reached the terminal `CONSENT_GRANTED` or `ELECTION_LAPSED`, is refused with `ERR_ELECTION_NOT_WITHDRAWABLE`.

An election has two terminal outcomes. `NEGOTIATING → CONSENT_GRANTED` is a bubble-up: the parent election follows its child negotiation into a terminal state the moment that negotiation reaches `CONSENTED`, and the station's `filed_election_count` is recomputed as part of that movement. `NEGOTIATING → ELECTION_LAPSED` is the failure outcome, applied by the time-driven maintenance pass described in §3.9 when the election's `incumbent_expiry_utc` has arrived at or before the logical clock, no carriage agreement exists on the election's negotiation, and no interim extension in status `ACTIVE` covers the current logical clock for any agreement of that election. Lapsing cascades much the way withdrawal does on the negotiating branch: the earliest-created negotiation on the election moves to `CANCELLED`, every `SUBMITTED` offer on it moves to `EXPIRED`, and `filed_election_count` falls as a consequence of the election leaving `NEGOTIATING`. Two things withdrawal does are deliberately absent here. The station row itself is not touched by the sweep, so its `updated_at` and `version` do not move even though the count it reports has changed; and any blackout notice still in `SERVED` is left in `SERVED` rather than being superseded.

Elections can be listed and narrowed by `station_id`, `distributor_id`, `election_type`, `status` and `cycle_label`; the three free-text filters are matched literally with no existence check behind them, so an unknown station or distributor identifier yields an empty page rather than a 404, while `election_type` and `status` must name a value from their declared sets or the request is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The negotiations created by filing a given election can be listed from the election itself: none on the must-carry branch, and one per retransmission-consent filing. Filing never reuses a negotiation it previously cancelled, so an election that was withdrawn and filed again carries one negotiation row per filing and the list returns all of them — which is why the cascades above name the earliest-created negotiation specifically.

### 3.4. Subscriber Reports, Audit Rights and the Billable Basis

Every dollar in this system is per-subscriber-per-month, so the subscriber count is the foundation everything else rests on — and it is supplied by the party with the least incentive to inflate it. A `DistributorRep` files a **subscriber report** for one reporting period. The report names the `station_id` whose signal the counts relate to; its `distributor_id` is never accepted from the body but is taken from the reporting representative's own `X-User-ID`. Those two identifiers are resolved in that order, so an unknown `station_id` is reported as `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND` even when the acting representative's `X-User-ID` resolves to no distributor either. The period is described by a `period_label` of between 1 and 20 characters such as `2027-01`, together with `period_start_utc`, which is the inclusive start of the period, and `period_end_utc`, which is the exclusive end. At most one report may exist for a given station, distributor and `period_label` combination; a second is refused with `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_DUPLICATE`.

The counts themselves are `reported_subscriber_count`, the raw figure as measured by the distributor's own convention, which must be at least 0; and `excluded_free_trial_count`, the portion of that raw figure sitting in a promotional free trial and therefore excluded from the billable basis. The free-trial figure defaults to 0 when omitted, must be at least 0, and may not exceed `reported_subscriber_count`. At creation the report also freezes `measurement_method_snapshot` as a copy of the distributor's `subscriber_measurement_method` at that instant; a distributor that later changes how it counts does not retroactively rewrite reports it has already filed, and every basis computation on this report reads the snapshot rather than the distributor's current setting. A new report begins with `status` at `REPORTED` and `audit_adjustment_subscribers` at 0.

Determining the **billable subscriber basis** from those inputs proceeds in two stages, and the arithmetic is fully pinned. First, the adjusted subscriber count is derived: take `reported_subscriber_count`, subtract `excluded_free_trial_count`, and add `audit_adjustment_subscribers`. That value is recorded as `adjusted_subscriber_count`. Second, the measurement convention frozen in `measurement_method_snapshot` is applied to it. Under `BILLED_HOUSEHOLDS` the basis is the adjusted count unchanged, because a billed household already is a household. Under `WEIGHTED_STREAMING_DEVICES` the basis converts devices into households by multiplying the adjusted count by 10, dividing by 25, and truncating any remainder toward negative infinity — an integer-exact rendering of dividing by two and a half devices per household, so 25 devices yield 10 households and 24 devices also yield 9, never 10. Under `AVERAGE_MONTHLY_UNIQUE` the basis discounts the adjusted count to 8,800 basis points of itself — that is, to 88 percent — by multiplying by 8,800, dividing by 10,000, and rounding half up. Whichever branch applies, the final basis is then floored at zero: a negative intermediate becomes 0 rather than a negative subscriber count. Exactly one report row participates in this computation; no other report, offer or settlement enters it.

That computation is run twice, in two variants that differ in one input and nothing else, and both results are stored on the report. The `as_reported_basis` is the variant that forces `audit_adjustment_subscribers` to 0, so substantiated audit findings do not move it; it is the number a fee settlement snapshots at issuance. The `billable_subscriber_basis` is the variant that includes `audit_adjustment_subscribers` in full, so a substantiated undercount raises it and every downstream fee with it; it is the number an offer snapshots at submission and the number a settlement adopts at true-up. On a report with no substantiated findings the two are identical, which is precisely why the distinction is easy to miss and expensive to get wrong.

The station group's counterweight is its **audit right**. A `StationGroupNegotiator` — and it must be the station named on the report — opens an **audit finding** asserting a `adjustment_subscribers` delta together with a `basis_note` of between 1 and 500 characters recording the evidentiary basis for the assertion. The delta is signed: a positive value means the station group believes the distributor undercounted. It may not be zero, and its magnitude may not exceed 500,000 in either direction. A new finding begins with `status` at `OPEN`, has its `opened_at_utc` stamped from the current logical clock, and leaves `resolution_note` null.

Opening a finding is time-limited. The audit-rights window runs for 180 days from the report's `period_end_utc`, and a finding may be opened only while the logical clock is at or before that instant — the boundary day itself is inside the window. Past it, the request is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Opening a finding also moves the report under audit: a report in `REPORTED` advances `REPORTED → UNDER_AUDIT`, and — importantly — a report already in `ADJUSTED` from an earlier substantiated finding advances `ADJUSTED → UNDER_AUDIT` all over again. This is a genuine cycle, not a one-way pipeline; an already-adjusted report can be pulled back under audit as many times as the window allows. Opening a further finding while the report already sits in `UNDER_AUDIT` moves nothing on the report at all: it stays in `UNDER_AUDIT` and is left untouched, so its `updated_at` and `version` do not move. A report that has reached `ACCEPTED`, by contrast, is closed to audit and refuses the attempt with `ERR_REPORT_NOT_AUDITABLE`.

Adjudication belongs to the neutral function. A `ComplianceOfficer` resolves an open finding by supplying a `resolution` of either `SUBSTANTIATED` or `DISMISSED` along with a `resolution_note` — a required non-empty string with no upper length bound — which is recorded on the finding. Resolution requires the finding to be `OPEN` and its parent report to be `UNDER_AUDIT`, and those two conditions are tested in that order: a finding that is no longer `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_FINDING_NOT_OPEN`, while a still-`OPEN` finding whose report has meanwhile left `UNDER_AUDIT` — because a sibling finding on the same report was substantiated and drove it to `ADJUSTED` — is refused with `ERR_REPORT_NOT_AUDITABLE`.

A `SUBSTANTIATED` resolution moves the finding `OPEN → SUBSTANTIATED` and drives a three-level cascade. At the first level the finding's note is recorded and the parent report advances `UNDER_AUDIT → ADJUSTED`. At the second level the report recomputes `audit_adjustment_subscribers` as the sum of `adjustment_subscribers` over all findings on it whose status is `SUBSTANTIATED` — findings still `OPEN` and findings `DISMISSED` contribute nothing — and then recomputes `adjusted_subscriber_count` and `billable_subscriber_basis` under the audited variant. At the third level, because `audit_adjustment_subscribers` is now non-zero, every fee settlement that drew its basis from this report and is still in status `ISSUED` has `true_up_required` set to true, and the distributor's `open_true_up_count` is recomputed. The settlements themselves are not re-priced by this cascade; they are only flagged, and the actual re-pricing happens when a counsel trues them up as described in §3.9.

A `DISMISSED` resolution moves the finding `OPEN → DISMISSED`, records its `resolution_note`, and leaves `audit_adjustment_subscribers` untouched. If, after that dismissal, no finding on the report remains `OPEN` and no finding on it is `SUBSTANTIATED`, the report returns `UNDER_AUDIT → REPORTED` with `audit_adjustment_subscribers` still at 0 and `billable_subscriber_basis` still equal to `as_reported_basis`. If a substantiated finding does exist on the report, or another finding is still open, the report stays where it is and is left untouched, so its `updated_at` and `version` do not move either.

Reports finally settle when their audit window closes. The time-driven maintenance pass moves a report from `REPORTED`, `UNDER_AUDIT` or `ADJUSTED` to the terminal `ACCEPTED` once the logical clock has passed 180 days beyond `period_end_utc` and no finding on the report is still `OPEN`. Acceptance freezes `billable_subscriber_basis` permanently; no further finding may be opened against the report.

Subscriber reports can be listed and narrowed by `station_id`, `distributor_id`, `period_label` and `status` — the first three matched literally with no existence check behind them, so an unknown identifier yields an empty page rather than a 404; the findings opened against one report can be listed and narrowed by `status`; and the settlements that drew their basis from a given report can be listed from that report and narrowed by `status` as well.

### 3.5. Negotiation Rounds and the Economics of an Offer

Filing a retransmission-consent election opens a **negotiation**, and the negotiation is the record of turn-taking between the two counterparties. Its `status` begins at `OPEN` and cycles between two waiting states as offers cross the table: a station-group offer leaves the distributor to respond, and a distributor counter leaves the station group to respond. The negotiation also carries the derived counters described in §2.9 — `round_number`, `station_offer_count`, `distributor_offer_count` and `good_faith_meeting_count` — along with `impasse_declared_at_utc`, `blackout_notice_deadline_utc` and `notice_compliance_status`, all of which are covered in §3.6.

Submitting an **offer** is a single capability shared by both counterparties, and which side an offer came from is never an input. `offered_by` is derived from the caller's role: `STATION_GROUP` when the caller is a `StationGroupNegotiator`, `DISTRIBUTOR` when the caller is a `DistributorRep`. The negotiation the offer belongs to is likewise taken from the path rather than the body, and recorded on the offer as `negotiation_id`.

An offer's economic terms are `base_rate_per_sub_cents`, the year-one per-subscriber-per-month rate proposed, which must be at least 1 and at most 600; `escalator_bp_per_year`, the annual escalator in basis points, at least 0 and at most 1,000, so that 700 means seven percent per contract year; `term_months`, the proposed carriage term length, at least 12 and at most 120 months; `tier`, the placement offered for the signal, one of `BROADCAST_BASIC`, `EXPANDED_BASIC` or `DIGITAL_TIER`; and `penetration_commitment_bp`, the committed penetration expressed in basis points of the distributor's video households, at least 0 and at most 10,000. Three optional boolean flags declare what non-linear rights sit inside the grant — `includes_digital_rights`, `includes_ooh_rights` and `includes_streaming_rights` — each defaulting to false when omitted. Finally, the offer must name the `subscriber_report_id` of the verified subscriber count it prices against.

Turn order is enforced. A station-group offer may be submitted only while the negotiation is `OPEN`, `AWAITING_STATION_OFFER` or `IMPASSE`; a distributor offer only while the negotiation is `OPEN` or `AWAITING_DISTRIBUTOR_RESPONSE`. A side attempting to speak out of turn, and any attempt to submit on a `CONSENTED` or `CANCELLED` negotiation, is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_NOT_OPEN`. Beyond turn order, submission requires the parent election to be in `NEGOTIATING` with `election_type` equal to `RETRANS_CONSENT`, and requires the caller to be the correct counterparty — the station-group side's `X-User-ID` must equal the election's station, the distributor side's must equal the election's distributor, and a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_COUNTERPARTY`.

Four substantive gates guard the terms of an offer, and they are evaluated in the precedence order fixed in §2.8. First, every right inside the grant must be one the network has licensed the station to pass along: `includes_digital_rights` requires `digital_rights_grantable`, `includes_ooh_rights` requires `ooh_rights_grantable`, and `includes_streaming_rights` requires `streaming_rights_grantable`, each on the station's affiliation agreement in status `ACTIVE`; a grant reaching past those permissions is refused with `ERR_RIGHTS_NOT_GRANTABLE`. Second, the station must actually have an affiliation agreement in status `ACTIVE` at the moment of submission, failing which the refusal is `ERR_AFFILIATION_NOT_ACTIVE`. Third, the offered `tier` must appear in that affiliation agreement's `permitted_tiers`, or the refusal is `ERR_TIER_NOT_PERMITTED`. Fourth, the referenced subscriber report must belong to the same station-and-distributor pair as the parent election and must be in status `REPORTED`, `UNDER_AUDIT`, `ADJUSTED` or `ACCEPTED`; a report drawn from a different counterparty pair is refused with `ERR_REPORT_NOT_PRICEABLE`.

A successful submission creates one offer with `status` at `SUBMITTED` and `rejection_reason` null, increments the negotiation's `round_number` by one and stamps the resulting value onto the offer's own `round_number`, recomputes the side-specific offer counter, flips the negotiation's `status` to the counterparty's turn, and increments the negotiation's `version` by one. The turn flip is unconditional on the starting state: a station-group offer moves the negotiation to `AWAITING_DISTRIBUTOR_RESPONSE` whether it started at `OPEN`, at `AWAITING_STATION_OFFER` or at `IMPASSE`, and a distributor offer moves it to `AWAITING_STATION_OFFER` whether it started at `OPEN` or at `AWAITING_DISTRIBUTOR_RESPONSE`. It also freezes `projection_basis_subscribers` as a snapshot of the referenced report's `billable_subscriber_basis` at submission time; that snapshot is never re-read afterwards, so a later audit adjustment on the report leaves the offer's economics exactly where they were.

The offer's projected economics are then computed. The number of contract years in the term is the `term_months` value divided by twelve and rounded up to the next whole number, so a 30-month term spans three contract years. A per-year rate ladder is built with one entry per contract year. Under the **compound-annual** convention — the one quoted during negotiation, stored as `year_rate_schedule_compound_cents` — year one's rate is `base_rate_per_sub_cents` exactly, and each later year's rate is the previous year's *already-rounded* rate multiplied by ten thousand plus `escalator_bp_per_year`, divided by ten thousand, and rounded half up to whole cents. Because each year escalates off a rounded figure, rounding error carries forward and the ladder drifts upward relative to the alternative convention from year three onward. The number of months attributable to each contract year is twelve, except for the final partial year, where it is whatever remains: for contract year *n*, it is the smaller of twelve and the term length minus twelve times one less than *n*. The whole-term gross fee, stored as `projected_total_fee_cents`, is then the sum across contract years of that year's ladder rate multiplied by `projection_basis_subscribers` multiplied by that year's month count. The rounding order here is pinned and not negotiable: each per-year rate is rounded to whole cents *first*, and only then multiplied by basis and months and summed across years. Summing unrounded yearly rates and rounding the whole-term total once is forbidden and produces a different number from the same inputs.

The rate ceiling applies to the whole ladder, not only to what was typed in. If any per-subscriber rate in the projected ladder exceeds 600 cents, the offer is refused with `ERR_RATE_CAP_EXCEEDED` — a base rate that is itself compliant but whose year-N escalated rate breaches the ceiling is still rejected.

Countering does not kill the offer being countered. When a side responds with its own offer, every pre-existing offer from the counterparty stays at `SUBMITTED` and therefore remains acceptable, which is why more than one live offer can sit on the table at once and why the version precondition in §2.7 matters so much at acceptance time.

An offer may instead be **rejected** by the counterparty, who supplies a `rejection_reason` — a required non-empty string with no upper length bound — that is recorded on the offer as it moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`. Only the counterparty of `offered_by` may reject: a station-group offer may be rejected only by a `DistributorRep` whose `X-User-ID` is the election's distributor, and a distributor offer only by a `StationGroupNegotiator` whose `X-User-ID` is the election's station. Rejection requires the offer to be `SUBMITTED` and the parent negotiation to be neither `CONSENTED` nor `CANCELLED`. Notably, rejection ends nothing: the negotiation's `status`, its `round_number` and its `version` are all left exactly as they were, so a rejection is a position statement rather than a turn.

An offer's third terminal outcome is `SUBMITTED → EXPIRED`, which the system applies without any client request when a sibling offer is accepted, when the parent election is withdrawn, or when the maintenance pass lapses the election. An expired offer takes no further side effects; it is frozen for the audit record.

Offers on one negotiation can be listed and narrowed by `offered_by` and `status`, and offers can also be listed across negotiations and narrowed by `negotiation_id`, `subscriber_report_id`, `offered_by` and `status` — the two identifier filters being matched literally with no existence check behind them, so an unknown identifier yields an empty page rather than a 404, and the report filter being how a station group finds every offer that was priced against a count now known to have been wrong.

### 3.6. Good Faith, Impasse and Notice to Subscribers

The statutory requirement that both sides bargain in good faith is documented rather than inferred, and the **good-faith log** is where it lives. Any of the two counterparties or the `ComplianceOfficer` may append an entry to a negotiation's log, declaring an `event_type` of `MEETING_HELD`, `PROPOSAL_EXCHANGED`, `INFORMATION_REQUEST`, `INFORMATION_RESPONSE` or `DELAY_OBJECTION`, the `occurred_at_utc` instant the conduct actually took place as asserted by the logging party, and a `summary` of between 1 and 500 characters recording what happened. The entry is immutable once written: the server stamps `actor_role` from the caller's role — one of `StationGroupNegotiator`, `DistributorRep` or `ComplianceOfficer` — and assigns `sequence_index` as a 1-based append position within the negotiation, ordered by creation timestamp ascending. Entries whose `event_type` is `MEETING_HELD` feed the negotiation's `good_faith_meeting_count`; the other four event types are recorded but do not count toward it. Appending to a `CONSENTED` or `CANCELLED` negotiation is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_NOT_OPEN`. An append leaves the parent negotiation row itself untouched — its `updated_at` and `version` do not move, even though the `good_faith_meeting_count` it reports may have changed. The log can be read back in append order and narrowed by `event_type`.

**Impasse** is the station group's declaration that bargaining has failed, and it is deliberately hard to reach. A `StationGroupNegotiator` — who must be the election's station — declares it by supplying an `impasse_statement` — a required non-empty string with no upper length bound — and the negotiation moves to `IMPASSE` from `OPEN`, `AWAITING_DISTRIBUTOR_RESPONSE`, `AWAITING_STATION_OFFER`, or from `IMPASSE` itself: re-declaring impasse on a negotiation already sitting at `IMPASSE` is accepted rather than refused, and simply re-stamps `impasse_declared_at_utc` from the current clock and advances the negotiation's `version` again. Two independent preconditions guard the declaration. The negotiation's `good_faith_meeting_count` must be at least three, counting only `MEETING_HELD` entries; exactly three satisfies the rule, and fewer is refused with `ERR_GOOD_FAITH_NOT_SATISFIED`. Separately, at least one offer must exist from each side — at least one with `offered_by` of `STATION_GROUP` and at least one with `offered_by` of `DISTRIBUTOR` — and a negotiation where only one side has ever tabled anything is refused with `ERR_NO_OFFERS_EXCHANGED`. Declaring impasse stamps `impasse_declared_at_utc` from the current logical clock, recomputes `notice_compliance_status`, and increments the negotiation's `version` by one.

Once at impasse, the station group's remaining obligation is to the distributor's subscribers, who are about to lose a channel. The **blackout notice deadline** is derived when the negotiation is created and re-read whenever a notice is served: the required notice period is 30 days for a distributor whose `distributor_type` is `CABLE`, `DBS` or `TELCO`, and 14 days for a `VMVPD`, which is exactly the value carried on the distributor as `required_blackout_notice_days`; the deadline instant, `blackout_notice_deadline_utc`, is the parent election's `incumbent_expiry_utc` with that many whole days subtracted. Service is timely when the logical clock is at or before the deadline instant — landing exactly on the deadline instant counts as timely. Only the election's `incumbent_expiry_utc` and the distributor's `distributor_type` participate in this derivation; no other row is consulted.

A `ComplianceOfficer` records the notice, supplying `subscriber_notice_count` — the number of subscribers served, at least 1 — and the `notice_channel` it went out through, one of `ON_AIR_CRAWL`, `BILL_INSERT`, `EMAIL` or `WEBSITE`. The server stamps `served_at_utc` from the logical clock, snapshots `required_notice_days_snapshot` from the distributor's `required_blackout_notice_days` at service time so that a later reclassification of the distributor cannot rewrite history, and creates the notice with `status` at `SERVED`. Service after the deadline is refused with `ERR_BLACKOUT_NOTICE_LATE`, and service on a `CONSENTED` or `CANCELLED` negotiation with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_NOT_OPEN`. Service is not otherwise gated on the negotiation having reached `IMPASSE`: a negotiation still in `OPEN`, `AWAITING_STATION_OFFER` or `AWAITING_DISTRIBUTOR_RESPONSE` accepts a notice exactly as an `IMPASSE` one does. Recording a notice leaves the parent negotiation row untouched — its `updated_at` and `version` do not move, even though the `notice_compliance_status` it reports changes. Notices served on a negotiation can be listed and narrowed by `status`.

`notice_compliance_status` is the negotiation's own summary of where it stands on this obligation, and it takes one of four values, decided by tests applied in a fixed order. The presence of a notice is tested first: once any notice on the negotiation is in status `SERVED` it reads `NOTICE_SERVED`, whatever state the negotiation itself is in — so a notice served while the negotiation was still `OPEN` or awaiting a turn makes the status read `NOTICE_SERVED` and not `NOT_REQUIRED`. Failing that, it reads `NOT_REQUIRED` while the negotiation is in any state other than `IMPASSE`. At `IMPASSE` with no notice in status `SERVED` and the logical clock at or before `blackout_notice_deadline_utc`, it reads `NOTICE_PENDING`. And at `IMPASSE` with no notice in status `SERVED` and the logical clock past `blackout_notice_deadline_utc`, it reads `NOTICE_DEFICIENT` — the compliance failure state. The declaration of impasse itself therefore lands, when no notice is yet in `SERVED`, on `NOTICE_PENDING` when it happens at or before the deadline and on `NOTICE_DEFICIENT` when it happens after.

Impasse is not terminal, and the way out of it is the second cycle in the negotiation machine. A station-group offer submitted while the negotiation sits at `IMPASSE` advances it `IMPASSE → AWAITING_DISTRIBUTOR_RESPONSE`, creates the offer with `offered_by` of `STATION_GROUP`, increments `round_number` by one, moves every blackout notice on the negotiation still in `SERVED` to `SUPERSEDED`, resets `notice_compliance_status` to `NOT_REQUIRED`, and increments the negotiation's `version` by one. A notice moves `SERVED → SUPERSEDED` whenever its parent negotiation leaves `IMPASSE` — whether by a station offer resuming bargaining, by the parent election being withdrawn, or by an offer being accepted out of impasse — and each such move triggers a recomputation of the parent negotiation's `notice_compliance_status`.

### 3.7. Binding the Group, Network Approval, and Execution

Accepting an offer is the moment the station group becomes contractually bound, and it is reserved to `GroupCounsel`. The accepting counsel supplies `expected_negotiation_version` as described in §2.7, together with `effective_start_utc` — the inclusive start of the carriage term — and `effective_end_utc`, its exclusive end. Acceptance requires the target offer to be in `SUBMITTED`, its parent negotiation to be in neither `CONSENTED` nor `CANCELLED`, and the grandparent election to be in `NEGOTIATING` with `election_type` equal to `RETRANS_CONSENT`.

Carriage terms on a given station-and-distributor slot may not overlap. The requested term is treated as half-open — inclusive of `effective_start_utc`, exclusive of `effective_end_utc` — and is compared against every existing carriage agreement on that same slot whose `execution_status` is `PENDING_EXECUTION` or `EXECUTED`. An overlap is refused with `ERR_CARRIAGE_TERM_OVERLAP`. Because the interval is half-open, a term that starts at precisely the instant another one ends is adjacent rather than overlapping, and is allowed.

A successful acceptance advances the offer `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED` and creates exactly one **carriage agreement**. The new agreement records `negotiation_id` for the negotiation it came out of and `accepted_offer_id` for the offer whose economics it embodies, carries the supplied `effective_start_utc` and `effective_end_utc`, and starts with `execution_status` at `PENDING_EXECUTION`, `executed_by_user_id` null and `decision_note` — the field a later network decision writes its note into — null. Its `accepted_by_user_id` is stamped with the accepting counsel's `X-User-ID`, and that value is the comparand for the segregation-of-duties rule below. Its `network_approval_status` is set to `NOT_REQUIRED` when the station's `network_affiliation` is `INDEPENDENT` — there is no network to ask — and otherwise to `PENDING_NETWORK`. Its `granted_rights_scope` is derived from the accepted offer's three rights flags, mapping `includes_digital_rights` to `DIGITAL`, `includes_ooh_rights` to `OUT_OF_HOME` and `includes_streaming_rights` to `STREAMING`, and including only the terms whose flag was true. `rights_credit_bp_total`, `consumed_extension_days`, `lifetime_gross_fee_cents` and `lifetime_net_retained_cents` all start at 0.

Acceptance also closes the negotiation and its parent election. The negotiation moves to `CONSENTED` from whichever of `OPEN`, `AWAITING_DISTRIBUTOR_RESPONSE`, `AWAITING_STATION_OFFER` or `IMPASSE` it was in, and its `version` increments by one. Because the negotiation is now `CONSENTED`, the parent election moves `NEGOTIATING → CONSENT_GRANTED`; and because that election left `NEGOTIATING`, the station's `filed_election_count` is recomputed — and unlike the maintenance sweep of §3.3, this cascade does touch the station row itself, so the station's `updated_at` and `version` move as well. Every other offer on the negotiation still in `SUBMITTED` moves to `EXPIRED`. When the acceptance happened out of `IMPASSE`, every blackout notice on the negotiation still in `SERVED` additionally moves to `SUPERSEDED`.

The agreement's billing ladder is computed at acceptance, and it is **not** the ladder that was quoted during negotiation. `contract_year_rate_schedule_cents` uses the **simple-annual** convention: for contract year *n*, the rate is `base_rate_per_sub_cents` multiplied by ten thousand plus `escalator_bp_per_year` times one less than *n*, divided by ten thousand, and rounded half up to whole cents. Every year therefore escalates off the unrounded base with a linear multiplier, so no rounding error carries forward. The contrast with the negotiation ladder is concrete: for a base rate of 250 cents and an escalator of 700 basis points over a three-year term, the compound-annual ladder quoted during negotiation runs 250, 268, 287, while the simple-annual ladder the executed contract bills on runs 250, 268, 285. Contract year count and per-year month attribution are derived exactly as in §3.5. Alongside the ladder, the agreement exposes `current_year_rate_per_sub_cents`, which is the entry of `contract_year_rate_schedule_cents` at the contract-year index implied by the current logical clock — that index computed by the Step 0 arithmetic of §3.9 against the agreement's `effective_start_utc`, and then clamped into the ladder's own bounds, so a clock standing before `effective_start_utc` reads year one's rate and a clock past the term's final contract year reads the last year's rate rather than reporting nothing.

Both ladders can also be read side by side from the agreement as a computed projection, which reports `compound_total_fee_cents` and `simple_total_fee_cents` as the whole-term gross under each convention — both recomputed on the spot from the accepted offer's `base_rate_per_sub_cents`, `escalator_bp_per_year`, `term_months` and frozen `projection_basis_subscribers` by the whole-term arithmetic of §3.5, never from the agreement's own `effective_start_utc` and `effective_end_utc`, so an interim extension that pushed the term end forward leaves this read exactly where it was — `divergence_cents` as the compound total minus the simple total, and the two ladders themselves as `year_rate_schedule_compound_cents` and `year_rate_schedule_simple_cents`. Those five keys are the whole of the response: this projection is a computed read rather than a resource, so it carries no `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` or `version` of its own. This read exists precisely so that the two conventions can be reconciled by a human before anyone signs.

An agreement advances along **two independent dimensions**, and neither waits for the other. The execution dimension runs through `execution_status`, one of `PENDING_EXECUTION`, `EXECUTED` or `EXPIRED`. The network dimension runs through `network_approval_status`, one of `NOT_REQUIRED`, `PENDING_NETWORK`, `NETWORK_APPROVED` or `NETWORK_REJECTED`.

On the network dimension, a `NetworkRepresentative` supplies a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` together with a `decision_note` — a required non-empty string with no upper length bound, which lands on the agreement's own `decision_note` field, and does so on both branches alike rather than on rejection only. The decision requires `network_approval_status` to be `PENDING_NETWORK`, and — like the other four operations that address an agreement — requires the agreement to be in the right execution phase, which for network approval means `execution_status` equal to `PENDING_EXECUTION`, failing which the refusal is `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_PENDING_EXECUTION`. Those two conditions share that one refusal code: an agreement whose network dimension has already been decided, or which never had one to decide because it is `NOT_REQUIRED`, is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_PENDING_EXECUTION` exactly as one in the wrong execution phase is. An `APPROVE` decision additionally requires that the station hold an affiliation agreement in status `ACTIVE` — the earliest-created of them where more than one does, as in §3.1 — and that the agreement's `granted_rights_scope` be a subset of the rights that affiliation marks grantable; the subset test runs first, term by term in the order `granted_rights_scope` lists them, and a station holding no `ACTIVE` affiliation at all fails it as though nothing were grantable, so an `APPROVE` on a non-empty scope without an `ACTIVE` affiliation is refused with `ERR_RIGHTS_NOT_GRANTABLE` and the `ERR_AFFILIATION_NOT_ACTIVE` refusal is reached only when that scope is empty. An `APPROVE` that passes advances `PENDING_NETWORK → NETWORK_APPROVED`. A `REJECT` decision advances `PENDING_NETWORK → NETWORK_REJECTED` and records the same `decision_note`, and is subject to none of those affiliation or grantability tests; `execution_status` remains `PENDING_EXECUTION` and can never afterwards advance to `EXECUTED`, making network rejection a terminal outcome for the deal. Either decision leaves `execution_status` entirely untouched and increments the agreement's `version` by one.

On the execution dimension, a second `GroupCounsel` signs. Execution takes an `execution_note` — a required non-empty string with no upper length bound, which is validated and then discarded rather than persisted anywhere on the agreement — and advances `PENDING_EXECUTION → EXECUTED`, stamping `executed_by_user_id` with the executing counsel's `X-User-ID`. Four preconditions guard it, and they are evaluated in the §2.8 precedence order rather than the order they are recited here, so a counsel who both accepted and now attempts to execute is told about the segregation-of-duties violation at priority 7 even when the agreement has also left `PENDING_EXECUTION`, whose check sits at priority 12. The agreement must be in `PENDING_EXECUTION`. Its `network_approval_status` must be `NOT_REQUIRED` or `NETWORK_APPROVED` — both `PENDING_NETWORK` and `NETWORK_REJECTED` block execution, and the refusal is `ERR_NETWORK_APPROVAL_PENDING`. The station must hold an affiliation agreement in status `ACTIVE` at the moment of execution, failing which the refusal is `ERR_AFFILIATION_NOT_ACTIVE`. And the executing counsel's `X-User-ID` must differ from the agreement's `accepted_by_user_id`; the counsel who bound the group may not also sign for it, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Execution carries a cross-agreement cascade that is easy to overlook. Because this agreement is now `EXECUTED`, every interim extension in status `ACTIVE` sitting on any *other* carriage agreement that shares this agreement's station-and-distributor slot, and whose own closed window intersects this agreement's half-open term from `effective_start_utc` to `effective_end_utc`, moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` — the successor deal has taken over, so the stopgap is no longer operative. The candidate agreements are visited in creation-timestamp ascending order and each one's extensions likewise. And because those extensions left `ACTIVE`, each affected other agreement recomputes its `consumed_extension_days`, from which superseded days now drop out; that agreement's `updated_at` and `version` move exactly once however many of its extensions this single execution superseded, and an agreement on the slot none of whose extensions were superseded is not touched at all. This agreement's own `version` increments by one.

The final movement on the execution dimension is `EXECUTED → EXPIRED`, applied by the maintenance pass once the agreement's `effective_end_utc` is at or before the logical clock. No further fee settlement may be issued against an expired agreement.

Agreements can be listed and narrowed by `negotiation_id`, `accepted_offer_id`, `execution_status` and `network_approval_status` — the two identifier filters matched literally with no existence check behind them, so an unknown negotiation or offer identifier yields an empty page rather than a 404, while the two status filters must each name a value from their declared sets or the request is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 3.8. Keeping the Signal Up: Interim Extensions and Rights Disputes

When a successor deal is still unfinished but the incumbent arrangement is running out, the station group can buy time. A `StationGroupNegotiator` — who must be the station on the agreement's slot — creates an **interim extension** against an agreement whose `execution_status` is `EXECUTED`, supplying `expected_agreement_version` as described in §2.7, `extension_days` of at least 1 and at most 90, an `extension_start_utc` marking the inclusive start of the window, and an `extension_rate_basis` of `PRIOR_RATE`, `LAST_STATION_OFFER` or `MIDPOINT`. The extension's `extension_end_utc` is derived as `extension_start_utc` plus `extension_days` whole days, and the new extension begins with `status` at `ACTIVE`. `extension_start_utc` is accepted whatever its relation to the logical clock and whatever its relation to the agreement's own term, so a window wholly in the past, wholly in the future, or lying entirely outside `effective_start_utc` through `effective_end_utc` is created just the same; only the overlap and budget rules below constrain where it may sit. Its `agreement_id` is taken from the addressed agreement rather than from the request body, and a body that carries an `agreement_id` of its own does not override it.

Extension windows are treated as **closed at both endpoints** for overlap purposes, which is a deliberate contrast with carriage terms. A requested window that shares even a single instant — including just its boundary day — with an existing extension on the same agreement in status `ACTIVE` or `LAPSED` is refused with `ERR_EXTENSION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Extensions in `SUPERSEDED` are not consulted for overlap.

Extension days are also a finite budget. `consumed_extension_days` on the agreement is the sum of `extension_days` over that agreement's extensions in status `ACTIVE` or `LAPSED`, and the sum of that figure plus the newly requested `extension_days` may not exceed 90 days — a full quarter of interim carriage across the life of the agreement. Landing exactly on 90 is permitted; going past it is refused with `ERR_EXTENSION_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. Extensions in `SUPERSEDED` drop out of the sum and free their days for reuse.

The rate the interim window bills at is computed as `effective_rate_per_sub_cents`, and the selection of inputs is fully pinned. The prior rate is the `incumbent_rate_per_sub_cents` of the election reached from the agreement through its negotiation. The last station offer is selected from the offers on that agreement's negotiation whose `offered_by` is `STATION_GROUP` and whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED` or `EXPIRED` — every station offer ever tabled, regardless of how it ended. Those candidates are ranked by `round_number` descending, and where two candidates share a `round_number`, by creation timestamp descending; the `base_rate_per_sub_cents` of the top-ranked candidate is the last station offer rate. Under `PRIOR_RATE` the effective rate is the prior rate. Under `LAST_STATION_OFFER` it is the last station offer rate. Under `MIDPOINT` it is the prior rate plus the last station offer rate, divided by two, with any remainder truncated toward negative infinity — the midpoint always floors and never rounds half up. Because `PRIOR_RATE` never touches the offer pool, an empty candidate set is an error only for `LAST_STATION_OFFER` and `MIDPOINT`, and in those two cases the request is refused with `ERR_NO_STATION_OFFER_ON_RECORD`.

Creating an extension adds its `extension_days` to the agreement's `consumed_extension_days` and pushes the agreement's effective term end forward — `effective_end_utc` moves forward by exactly `extension_days` whole days from wherever it currently stands, the placement of the extension window itself never entering that arithmetic, so a window that sits nowhere near the term end still moves the term end by its full day count — and it increments the agreement's `version` by one.

An extension has two terminal outcomes. `ACTIVE → LAPSED` is applied by the maintenance pass once `extension_end_utc` is at or before the logical clock; lapsing deliberately leaves the parent agreement's `consumed_extension_days` unchanged, because lapsed days have genuinely been used and remain inside the budget sum. `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` is the cross-agreement cascade described in §3.7, triggered when a successor agreement on the same station-and-distributor slot is executed and its half-open term intersects the extension's closed window; superseding recomputes the parent agreement's `consumed_extension_days`, which now excludes this extension's days. Extensions on an agreement can be listed and narrowed by `status`, which must name `ACTIVE`, `LAPSED` or `SUPERSEDED` or the request is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Rights language is the other place a signed deal comes apart. What exactly counts as a "digital" retransmission, or where out-of-home viewing ends and streaming begins, is contested often enough that the system models it explicitly. Either counterparty — a `StationGroupNegotiator` or a `DistributorRep` — raises a **rights dispute** against an agreement whose `execution_status` is `EXECUTED`, naming the `disputed_term` at issue as one of `DIGITAL`, `OUT_OF_HOME` or `STREAMING`, and stating the `claimed_definition` the raising party asserts in between 1 and 500 characters. The `raised_by` field is derived from the caller's role — `STATION_GROUP` for a negotiator, `DISTRIBUTOR` for a distributor representative — and is never an input. A dispute may only contest a right that was actually granted: the `disputed_term` must be a member of the agreement's `granted_rights_scope` at creation, and a term that was never granted is refused with `ERR_TERM_NOT_IN_GRANT`. A new dispute begins with `status` at `RAISED`, `credit_bp_applied` at 0 and `resolution_note` null, and its `agreement_id` is taken from the addressed agreement rather than from the request body. Nothing limits an agreement to one dispute per term: while a term is still inside `granted_rights_scope` a second dispute naming that same term is accepted, and because the credit arithmetic is per dispute rather than per term, two disputes on one term that are both resolved `EXCLUDED` credit that term's basis points twice.

A `ComplianceOfficer` resolves the dispute, supplying a `resolution` of `MEDIATION`, `INCLUDED` or `EXCLUDED` together with a `resolution_note` — a required non-empty string with no upper length bound — which is recorded on the dispute whichever of the three resolutions was asked for. Resolution requires the dispute to be in `RAISED` or `IN_MEDIATION`; a dispute already at `RESOLVED_INCLUDED` or `RESOLVED_EXCLUDED` is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_PENDING`.

A `MEDIATION` resolution advances `RAISED → IN_MEDIATION`, records the note, and changes nothing economically: `credit_bp_applied` stays 0 and the agreement's `granted_rights_scope` is untouched. Asking for `MEDIATION` on a dispute already sitting at `IN_MEDIATION` is accepted rather than refused; it leaves the dispute where it is, overwrites `resolution_note` with the new one, and advances the dispute's `version` again.

An `INCLUDED` resolution advances either `RAISED → RESOLVED_INCLUDED` or `IN_MEDIATION → RESOLVED_INCLUDED`, and the two paths behave identically. The station group's definition stands: `credit_bp_applied` stays 0, `granted_rights_scope` keeps the `disputed_term`, and no settlement is recomputed.

An `EXCLUDED` resolution advances either `RAISED → RESOLVED_EXCLUDED` or `IN_MEDIATION → RESOLVED_EXCLUDED` — again identically on both paths — and drives the deepest cascade in the system, four levels of it. At the first level the dispute's `credit_bp_applied` is set to the rights credit for the excluded term: 800 basis points for `DIGITAL`, 500 basis points for `OUT_OF_HOME`, and 1,200 basis points for `STREAMING` — that is, eight percent, five percent and twelve percent of gross carriage revenue respectively. At the second level, because the dispute is now `RESOLVED_EXCLUDED`, the parent agreement removes the `disputed_term` from `granted_rights_scope`, recomputes `rights_credit_bp_total` as the sum of `credit_bp_applied` over its disputes in status `RESOLVED_EXCLUDED`, and increments its own `version` by one. At the third level, because the agreement's `rights_credit_bp_total` changed, every fee settlement on that agreement still in status `ISSUED` recomputes `rights_credit_cents`, `reverse_comp_cents`, `penetration_penalty_cents` and `net_retained_cents` by the split arithmetic in §3.9, processed in creation-timestamp ascending order. At the fourth level, because those settlements changed, the parent agreement re-derives `lifetime_gross_fee_cents` and `lifetime_net_retained_cents` from the settlements now in status `ISSUED` or `TRUED_UP`. Disputes on an agreement can be listed and narrowed by `status` and `disputed_term`, each of which must name a value from its declared set or the request is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 3.9. Monthly Settlement, the Network's Share, and Time-Driven Maintenance

Once an agreement is `EXECUTED`, money starts moving, one month at a time. A `GroupCounsel` issues a **fee settlement** against the agreement, naming the `period_label` for the month being settled — between 1 and 20 characters, and unique per agreement, so a second settlement for the same label on the same agreement is refused with `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_DUPLICATE`; that uniqueness is tested against every settlement the agreement carries whatever its status, so a `VOIDED` settlement keeps its label reserved rather than releasing it for reuse — together with `period_start_utc` as the inclusive start of the settlement period, `period_end_utc` as its exclusive end, and the `subscriber_report_id` of the report supplying the billable basis. As with an offer, the referenced report must belong to the same station-and-distributor pair the agreement reaches through its negotiation and election, and must be in status `REPORTED`, `UNDER_AUDIT`, `ADJUSTED` or `ACCEPTED`; otherwise the refusal is `ERR_REPORT_NOT_PRICEABLE`. The settlement's `agreement_id` is taken from the addressed agreement rather than from the request body, and it is that `agreement_id` — not the report's own linkage — that the uniqueness rule on `period_label` is scoped to. Beyond requiring its end to be strictly after its start, the settlement period is otherwise unconstrained: nothing requires it to fall inside the agreement's carriage term, and nothing requires it to coincide with — or even overlap — the reporting period of the report supplying the basis. Issuance stamps `issued_by_user_id` with the issuing counsel's `X-User-ID`, sets `status` to `ISSUED`, and leaves `true_up_required` at false.

The settlement's arithmetic proceeds in six ordered steps, and each step's output is stored on the settlement so the whole waterfall is auditable.

**Step 0 — which contract year is being billed.** The elapsed month count is the settlement period's start year minus the agreement's `effective_start_utc` year, multiplied by twelve, plus the settlement period's start month minus the agreement's `effective_start_utc` month. `contract_year_index` is that elapsed month count divided by twelve with the remainder truncated toward negative infinity, plus one, and it is stored on the settlement exactly as computed and never clamped — a settlement period beginning before the agreement's `effective_start_utc` therefore records a `contract_year_index` of zero or below, and one beginning past the term's final contract year records an index above the ladder's length. `rate_per_sub_cents` is then the entry of the agreement's `contract_year_rate_schedule_cents` at that index clamped into the ladder's own bounds: an index below 1 reads year one's rate and an index past the last contract year reads the final year's rate, so out-of-range periods are priced at the nearest ladder entry and the stored index and the stored rate can legitimately disagree. The ladder read is the simple-annual one, never the compound-annual one quoted during negotiation.

**Step 1 — gross for one month of carriage.** At issuance, `billable_subscriber_basis_snapshot` is taken from the referenced report's `as_reported_basis` and `basis_variant` is stamped `AS_REPORTED`. `gross_fee_cents` is `rate_per_sub_cents` multiplied by that snapshot basis.

**Step 2 — rights credits, rounded per dispute and then summed.** For each rights dispute on this agreement in status `RESOLVED_EXCLUDED`, iterated in creation-timestamp ascending order, take `gross_fee_cents`, multiply by that dispute's rights credit in basis points — 800 for `DIGITAL`, 500 for `OUT_OF_HOME`, 1,200 for `STREAMING` — divide by ten thousand, and round half up to whole cents. `rights_credit_cents` is the sum of those individually-rounded per-dispute credits. Disputes in `RAISED`, `IN_MEDIATION` or `RESOLVED_INCLUDED` contribute nothing. The rounding order matters: each credit is rounded on its own *before* summation, and summing unrounded credits and rounding once at the end is incorrect.

**Step 3 — the creditable base.** The creditable base is `gross_fee_cents` minus `rights_credit_cents`. It is not stored as a field of its own, but it is the operand Steps 4 through 6 work from.

**Step 4 — the network's split rate.** This is the one place in the system where a plausible reading is wrong and the correct reading must be stated flatly. The split tier looks as though it ought to key on what the station group nets, but net depends on the split, so that reading is circular. The pinned rule is that the tier keys on the **pre-credit per-subscriber rate** from Step 0 — `rate_per_sub_cents` — and never on the creditable base and never on `net_retained_cents`. A `rate_per_sub_cents` of at most 200 gives a tier of 4,000 basis points; a rate above 200 but at most 300 gives 5,000; a rate above 300 gives 5,500. `reverse_comp_split_bp` is then that tier plus the affiliation agreement's `split_premium_bp`, capped at 6,000 basis points — the network's share never exceeds sixty percent no matter how large the premium.

**Step 5 — what the network actually takes.** The network receives the greater of two amounts: its percentage share, computed as the creditable base multiplied by `reverse_comp_split_bp`, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to whole cents; or its per-subscriber floor, computed as the affiliation agreement's `reverse_comp_floor_per_sub_cents` multiplied by the snapshot basis. The larger of the two is stored as `reverse_comp_cents`. The affiliation agreement consulted here is the station's, in status `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED`; affiliations in `SUSPENDED` are excluded from this lookup, and their absence has already blocked execution upstream. The two admissible statuses rank equally and the earliest-created row among them wins, so a station holding an `EXPIRED` agreement created before its `ACTIVE` one has its split priced from that `EXPIRED` agreement's `split_premium_bp` and `reverse_comp_floor_per_sub_cents` even though execution consulted the `ACTIVE` one; and where the station holds no admissible affiliation at all — a condition an executed agreement cannot be reached from — the split proceeds with a premium and a floor of zero rather than failing.

**Step 6 — the penetration penalty and the residual.** The penetration test measures whether the distributor actually put the signal in front of the audience it committed to. `actual_penetration_bp` is the settlement's `billable_subscriber_basis_snapshot` multiplied by ten thousand, divided by the distributor's `video_household_count`, with the remainder truncated toward negative infinity — deliberately the distributor's own household count, never the station's `market_tv_households`. `penetration_shortfall_bp` is the accepted offer's `penetration_commitment_bp` minus `actual_penetration_bp`, floored at zero so that over-delivery never produces a negative shortfall. A shortfall of at most 50 basis points falls inside an inclusive grace band and yields a `penetration_penalty_cents` of 0 — being exactly half a percentage point short is free. A shortfall above 50 basis points yields a penalty of `gross_fee_cents` multiplied by `penetration_shortfall_bp`, multiplied by two, divided by ten thousand, and rounded half up to whole cents. Finally, `net_retained_cents` — what the station group keeps — is the creditable base minus `reverse_comp_cents` minus `penetration_penalty_cents`.

A conservation invariant must hold on every settlement row after every mutation of it: `gross_fee_cents` equals the sum of `net_retained_cents`, `reverse_comp_cents`, `rights_credit_cents` and `penetration_penalty_cents`. If it does not, the waterfall has been implemented in the wrong order.

Issuing a settlement adds its amounts into the parent agreement's ledger: `lifetime_gross_fee_cents` and `lifetime_net_retained_cents` are each the sum of the corresponding per-settlement amount over the agreement's settlements in status `ISSUED` or `TRUED_UP`. The agreement is the only other row issuance touches; the distributor is left alone, because a settlement issued with `true_up_required` at false leaves `open_true_up_count` where it was.

**True-up** is how an audit finding eventually reaches the money. A `GroupCounsel` trues up a settlement by supplying a `true_up_note`, a required non-empty string with no upper length bound that is validated and then discarded rather than persisted on the settlement; the settlement must be in `ISSUED`, and the referenced subscriber report's `audit_adjustment_subscribers` must be non-zero — an unadjusted report has nothing to true up, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_TRUE_UP_NOT_REQUIRED`. True-up advances the settlement `ISSUED → TRUED_UP`, flips `basis_variant` to `AUDITED`, and replaces `billable_subscriber_basis_snapshot` with the report's `billable_subscriber_basis` — the audited variant, which includes the substantiated adjustment. Every downstream amount is then recomputed from that new basis: `gross_fee_cents`, `rights_credit_cents`, `reverse_comp_split_bp`, `reverse_comp_cents`, `actual_penetration_bp`, `penetration_shortfall_bp`, `penetration_penalty_cents` and `net_retained_cents`, by the same six ordered steps. `true_up_required` is cleared back to false, the parent agreement's `lifetime_gross_fee_cents` and `lifetime_net_retained_cents` are re-derived, and the distributor's `open_true_up_count` is recomputed. A settlement may be trued up only once; a second attempt against a `TRUED_UP` settlement is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_ISSUED`.

**Voiding** is the compensating action for a settlement issued in error, and it enforces the same segregation of duties acceptance and execution do. A `GroupCounsel` supplies a `void_reason`, likewise a required non-empty string with no upper length bound that is validated and then discarded rather than persisted; the settlement may be in either `ISSUED` or `TRUED_UP`, and a `VOIDED` settlement accepts neither true-up nor a second void, both refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_ISSUED`. The voiding counsel's `X-User-ID` must differ from the settlement's `issued_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Voiding moves `status` to `VOIDED`, and because the agreement's lifetime totals sum only over settlements in `ISSUED` or `TRUED_UP`, this row's amounts drop straight out of both `lifetime_gross_fee_cents` and `lifetime_net_retained_cents`, restoring the ledger to exactly the value it held before that settlement was issued. The distributor's `open_true_up_count` is recomputed as well, that distributor being the one named on the voided settlement's own subscriber report. Settlements under an agreement can be listed and narrowed by `status`, `basis_variant` and `period_label` — the two status-shaped filters must each name a value from their declared sets or the request is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while `period_label` is matched literally.

**Time-driven maintenance** is a single explicit pass rather than a background job, so that test scenarios are fully deterministic. A `CarriageAdmin` triggers the expiration sweep, which advances every time-gated lifecycle in the system against the current logical clock and answers with five counts: `expired_agreement_count`, `lapsed_extension_count`, `lapsed_election_count`, `accepted_report_count` and `expired_affiliation_count`. Each count reports only the rows this pass actually transitioned, so a second sweep at the same clock value answers with five zeroes. The sweep does five things, in the order they are listed below and each as a complete phase before the next begins, and within each phase it visits candidate rows in creation-timestamp ascending order. Carriage agreements in `EXECUTED` whose `effective_end_utc` is at or before the clock move to `EXPIRED`. Interim extensions in `ACTIVE` whose `extension_end_utc` is at or before the clock move to `LAPSED`, leaving their days inside the extension budget. Elections in `NEGOTIATING` whose `incumbent_expiry_utc` is at or before the clock, which have no carriage agreement on their negotiation and no `ACTIVE` interim extension covering the clock, move to `ELECTION_LAPSED` — cancelling their negotiation and expiring that negotiation's `SUBMITTED` offers, and additionally, where the negotiation was sitting at `IMPASSE`, cancelling it from there and expiring its outstanding offers just the same. Subscriber reports past their 180-day audit window with no finding still `OPEN` move to `ACCEPTED`. Affiliation agreements in `ACTIVE` whose `term_end_utc` is at or before the clock move to `EXPIRED`, after which no carriage agreement of that station may be executed until an `ACTIVE` affiliation exists again.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that bring a new resource into existence — a station, an affiliation agreement, a distributor, an election, an offer, a good-faith entry, a blackout notice, a subscriber report, an audit finding, a carriage agreement created by accepting an offer, an interim extension, a rights dispute, or a fee settlement — return `201 Created`. Operations that modify an existing resource, including every state transition applied to a resource that already exists, and every read, return `200 OK`. Error responses carry the status code listed in Section 5 for the reported error identifier. A trailing slash on any path below resolves to the same operation as the path without it. Requests that never reach a declared operation are answered in the same error envelope with `error_id` of `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, but under the status code the transport calls for rather than the 400 Section 5 lists: an unrouted path answers 404, and a method not defined on an otherwise routable path answers 405.
