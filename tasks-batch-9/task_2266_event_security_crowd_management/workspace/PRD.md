# Event Security & Crowd Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An event security services firm lives or dies by two numbers it must produce before a single patron reaches a door: how many guards it will put on the ground, and where each of them will stand. Getting those numbers right means reading a venue's physical layout against an intelligence picture, a ticketing forecast and a historical incident record, then translating the resulting risk judgement into a concrete roster of perimeter, screening, floor, emergency-exit and supervisory posts that a real workforce — part employees, part subcontracted labour — can actually fill within an authorised labour budget. Today that translation happens in spreadsheets that nobody can audit, and the consequences surface on the night: an emergency exit left uncovered, a screening lane opened with no operator, a supervisor span nobody checked, an ejection ordered with no supervisor on duty to witness it.

This document specifies the API that replaces those spreadsheets. The system is deliberately opinionated about arithmetic and about ordering. Every staffing requirement is derived, not typed in; every cost is committed to a plan-level ledger the moment a shift is confirmed and released the moment it is cancelled; every credential lapse propagates outward through the roster, the posts, the plan's coverage figures and finally the event's own operating posture, so that a stale guard card can never quietly remain load-bearing in a live deployment. The system also refuses to guess. Where a formula could plausibly be evaluated in more than one order, this document pins the order; where a boundary could plausibly be inclusive or exclusive, this document says which.

The API is mounted under the `/event-security` prefix, with a single operator-facing clock control mounted at `/_clock/set_current_utc`. Readers should treat §2 as a contract and §3 as the domain narrative from which the behaviour of each endpoint is reconstructed.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401) before anything else is examined. Eight roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `SecurityDirector`, `RiskAnalyst`, `StaffingCoordinator`, `CredentialAuditor`, `PostSupervisor`, `IncidentCommander`, `SubcontractorLiaison`, and `SystemOperator`. A request whose role is not among those authorised for the target endpoint in §4 is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).

The roles divide along segregation-of-duties lines. A `SecurityDirector` is the accountable executive: they register venues and events, patch the event profile, drive the event lifecycle, approve or reject risk assessments, activate and lock staffing plans, and return or finalize after-action reviews. A `RiskAnalyst` authors threat, capacity and layout analysis and may submit or revise an assessment but may never approve one. A `StaffingCoordinator` builds the plan — posts, guard sourcing, assignments, screening lanes — and publishes or recalls it. A `CredentialAuditor` is the independent compliance function and is the only role that may verify, reject or disqualify a guard card, or approve, suspend or terminate a vendor. A `PostSupervisor` works the event on the night and drives the duty side of each assignment. An `IncidentCommander` runs the incident response protocol and authors the after-action review. A `SubcontractorLiaison` is vendor-side and may only source and re-submit that vendor's own guards. A `SystemOperator` drives the logical clock and holds no business authority.

Several endpoints accept an `action` value in the body, and authority is granted per action rather than per endpoint. When the acting role is permitted on the endpoint but not permitted to perform the requested `action`, the request is rejected with `ERR_ACTION_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_ROLE` (403). The action-level authority map is:

- Risk-assessment decisions: `SUBMIT` and `REVISE` belong exclusively to `RiskAnalyst`; `APPROVE` and `REJECT` belong exclusively to `SecurityDirector`.
- Staffing-plan lifecycle: `PUBLISH` and `RECALL` belong exclusively to `StaffingCoordinator`; `ACTIVATE`, `LOCK` and `CLOSE` belong exclusively to `SecurityDirector`.
- Post-assignment duty events: `CONFIRM` and `CANCEL` belong exclusively to `StaffingCoordinator`; `BRIEF`, `CHECK_IN`, `MARK_NO_SHOW` and `RELIEVE` belong exclusively to `PostSupervisor`.
- Guard credential verification: `SUBMIT_FOR_REVIEW` is shared by `StaffingCoordinator` and `SubcontractorLiaison`; `VERIFY`, `REJECT` and `DISQUALIFY` belong exclusively to `CredentialAuditor`.
- After-action review decisions: `SUBMIT` and `REVISE` belong exclusively to `IncidentCommander`; `RETURN` and `FINALIZE` belong exclusively to `SecurityDirector`.
- Incident actions (`TRIAGE`, `ESCALATE`, `MEDICAL_HANDOFF`, `EJECT`, `LE_HANDOFF`, `RESOLVE`, `CLOSE_UNFOUNDED`) are exercised by `IncidentCommander`, with `SecurityDirector` also authorised on the endpoint. Screening-lane status changes (`OPEN`, `HOLD`, `CLOSE`) are shared by `StaffingCoordinator` and `SecurityDirector`. Vendor decisions (`APPROVE`, `SUSPEND`, `TERMINATE`) belong to `CredentialAuditor` alone.

Two roles are entity-backed, and for them `X-User-ID` is not an opaque string. When `X-User-Role` is `PostSupervisor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Guard`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Guard` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `PostSupervisor` acting on a post assignment must either be that assignment's own guard — `X-User-ID` equal to the assignment's `guard_id` — or hold a `CONFIRMED` assignment on a `SUPERVISOR` post belonging to the same staffing plan; otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_POST_SUPERVISOR` (403). If the supplied `X-User-ID` is not the `id` of any guard at all, the request is rejected with `ERR_GUARD_NOT_FOUND` (404). When `X-User-Role` is `SubcontractorLiaison`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Subcontractor`; that liaison may source guards only with `subcontractor_id` equal to `X-User-ID`, and may re-submit for review only guards whose `subcontractor_id` equals `X-User-ID`.

The remaining six roles — `SecurityDirector`, `RiskAnalyst`, `StaffingCoordinator`, `CredentialAuditor`, `IncidentCommander` and `SystemOperator` — carry an opaque operator identifier in `X-User-ID` that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition calls for it (as `submitted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a risk assessment, as `reported_by_user_id` on a density reading or an incident, and as `authored_by_user_id` and `finalized_by_user_id` on an after-action review) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. Those recorded identifiers are what the segregation-of-duties rules compare: the `X-User-ID` performing an assessment `APPROVE` or `REJECT` must differ from that assessment's `submitted_by_user_id`, and the `X-User-ID` performing a review `RETURN` or `FINALIZE` must differ from that review's `authored_by_user_id`. A violation of either is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip and defaults to 0.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order.

Every list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Audit Fields and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Most resources also carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation — including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade, and including mutations that only change a derived field such as `coverage_ratio` or `gate_posture`. The one exception is `DensityReading`: a reading is immutable once recorded, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from it entirely.

Time in this system is a logical clock, not wall-clock time. Before any clock call is made, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `SystemOperator` moves it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the new `current_utc` together with `expired_guard_count` and `voided_assignment_count`, the two figures produced by the credential-expiry sweep that runs as part of the same call (see §3.5). Every field described in this document as being stamped "at" an action — `verified_at`, `briefed_at`, `check_in_at`, `relieved_at`, `reported_at`, `triaged_at`, `resolved_at` — is stamped with the logical clock value at the moment the action is processed, and every temporal precondition compares against the logical clock.

### 2.5. The Order in Which a Request Is Judged

Many requests fail more than one rule at once, and which failure the caller sees is part of the contract. Checks are evaluated strictly in the following order, and the first failure terminates the request. Later checks are never reached once an earlier one has fired.

1. **Header presence** — `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Endpoint role authority** — the acting role appears among the target endpoint's authorised roles in §4, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Action authority** — on the five multi-action endpoints `POST /event-security/risk-assessments/{id}/decision`, `POST /event-security/staffing-plans/{id}/lifecycle`, `POST /event-security/post-assignments/{id}/duty-events`, `POST /event-security/guards/{id}/credential-verification` and `POST /event-security/after-action-reviews/{id}/decision`, the submitted `action` is one the acting role may perform per the map in §2.1, else `ERR_ACTION_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_ROLE` (403).
4. **Supervisor standing** — on `POST /event-security/post-assignments/{id}/duty-events`, a `PostSupervisor` is either the assignment's own guard or a holder of a `CONFIRMED` assignment on a `SUPERVISOR` post of the same plan, else `ERR_NOT_POST_SUPERVISOR` (403).
5. **Body schema** — required fields present, enum values drawn from the declared member sets, integers and numbers inside their declared ranges, `window_end` strictly after `window_start`, `event_start_at` strictly after `doors_open_at`, `event_end_at` strictly after `event_start_at`, and a guard whose `employment_type` is `SUBCONTRACTED` carrying a `subcontractor_id` while a guard whose `employment_type` is `EMPLOYEE` carries none. Any violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
6. **Path existence** — each identifier in the path is resolved left to right, and the first one that does not resolve produces its own 404: `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RISK_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GUARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBCONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LANE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_READING_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint.
7. **Body foreign keys** — `zone_id`, `guard_id`, `post_id`, `risk_assessment_id`, `subcontractor_id`, `lane_id` and `reports_to_post_id` each resolve to an existing row, else the matching 404 from `ERR_ZONE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GUARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RISK_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBCONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_LANE_NOT_FOUND`. This check guards `POST /event-security/security-events`, `POST /event-security/security-events/{id}/staffing-plans`, `POST /event-security/staffing-plans/{id}/posts`, `POST /event-security/post-assignments`, `POST /event-security/security-events/{id}/screening-lanes`, `POST /event-security/security-events/{id}/density-readings`, `POST /event-security/security-events/{id}/incidents` and `POST /event-security/guards`.
8. **Referential scoping** — a referenced zone belongs to the event's venue, a referenced lane belongs to the same event, `reports_to_post_id` names a `SUPERVISOR` post in the same staffing plan, and `risk_assessment_id` belongs to the event named in the path. Any mismatch is `ERR_ZONE_VENUE_MISMATCH` (422). This check guards `POST /event-security/staffing-plans/{id}/posts`, `POST /event-security/security-events/{id}/screening-lanes`, `POST /event-security/security-events/{id}/density-readings`, `POST /event-security/security-events/{id}/incidents` and `POST /event-security/security-events/{id}/staffing-plans`.
9. **Version pinning** — the supplied `expected_event_version` or `expected_post_version` equals the parent's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_EVENT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_POST_VERSION` (412). This check guards `PATCH /event-security/security-events/{id}`, `POST /event-security/security-events/{id}/staffing-plans` and `PATCH /event-security/posts/{id}`.
10. **Plan not locked** — the owning staffing plan's `status` is not `LOCKED` for any roster mutation, else `ERR_PLAN_LOCKED` (409). This check guards `POST /event-security/staffing-plans/{id}/posts`, `PATCH /event-security/posts/{id}`, `POST /event-security/post-assignments`, and `POST /event-security/post-assignments/{id}/duty-events` for the `CONFIRM` and `CANCEL` actions.
11. **Plan currency** — the owning staffing plan's `is_current` is true, else `ERR_PLAN_NOT_CURRENT` (409). This check guards `POST /event-security/staffing-plans/{id}/posts`, `PATCH /event-security/posts/{id}`, `POST /event-security/post-assignments`, `POST /event-security/post-assignments/{id}/duty-events` and `POST /event-security/staffing-plans/{id}/lifecycle`.
12. **Transition legality** — the target resource's current state has an outgoing transition for the requested action, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to every action and lifecycle endpoint. One carve-out is narrower than this rule and is therefore evaluated in its place: a check-in against an assignment whose `assignment_status` is `CONFIRMED` but whose `duty_status` is still `PENDING_BRIEF` returns the more specific `ERR_GUARD_NOT_BRIEFED` (rank 19) rather than `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.
13. **Gate posture** — the owning event's `gate_posture` is not `FROZEN`, else `ERR_EVENT_GATE_FROZEN` (409). This check guards `POST /event-security/staffing-plans/{id}/posts`, `PATCH /event-security/posts/{id}`, `POST /event-security/post-assignments`, `POST /event-security/post-assignments/{id}/duty-events` for the `CONFIRM` and `CANCEL` actions, `POST /event-security/screening-lanes/{id}/status` for the `OPEN` action, `POST /event-security/staffing-plans/{id}/lifecycle`, and `POST /event-security/risk-assessments/{id}/decision` for the `SUBMIT` action.
14. **Assessment approval** — the plan's risk assessment has `status` equal to `APPROVED` before a publish, else `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_APPROVED` (409). Guards `POST /event-security/staffing-plans/{id}/lifecycle` for the `PUBLISH` action.
15. **Segregation of duties** — as described in §2.1, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Guards `POST /event-security/risk-assessments/{id}/decision` for `APPROVE` and `REJECT`, and `POST /event-security/after-action-reviews/{id}/decision` for `RETURN` and `FINALIZE`.
16. **Guard credential currency** — the guard's `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` and their `credential_expires_on` is strictly after the assignment's `window_end`, else `ERR_GUARD_CREDENTIAL_INVALID` (422). Guards `POST /event-security/post-assignments/{id}/duty-events` for `CONFIRM` and `POST /event-security/guards/{id}/credential-verification` for `VERIFY` (where the comparison is against the logical clock instead).
17. **Credential tier** — the guard's `credential_level` is at least the post's `min_credential_level`, else `ERR_CREDENTIAL_LEVEL_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Guards the `CONFIRM` action.
18. **Supervisor qualification** — a `SUPERVISOR` post requires a guard whose `credential_level` is at least 3, else `ERR_SUPERVISOR_NOT_QUALIFIED` (422). Guards the `CONFIRM` action.
19. **Briefing precondition** — a check-in requires `duty_status` to be `BRIEFED`, else `ERR_GUARD_NOT_BRIEFED` (409). Guards the `CHECK_IN` action.
20. **Vendor eligibility** — for a guard whose `employment_type` is `SUBCONTRACTED`, the vendor's `status` is `APPROVED` and its `insurance_expires_on` is strictly after the assignment's `window_end`, else `ERR_SUBCONTRACTOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422). Guards `POST /event-security/post-assignments/{id}/duty-events` for `CONFIRM`, `POST /event-security/guards/{id}/credential-verification` for `VERIFY`, and `POST /event-security/subcontractors/{id}/decision` for `APPROVE` — in the latter two cases the expiry is compared against the logical clock.
21. **Span of control** — the sum of `required_guard_count` over the posts reporting to a given `SUPERVISOR` post does not exceed 10, else `ERR_SPAN_OF_CONTROL_EXCEEDED` (422). Guards `POST /event-security/staffing-plans/{id}/posts` and `PATCH /event-security/posts/{id}`.
22. **Shift containment** — the assignment's `window_start` is at or after the post's `window_start` and its `window_end` is at or before the post's `window_end`, else `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_POST` (422). Guards `POST /event-security/post-assignments`.
23. **Guard double-booking** — as described in §3.6, else `ERR_GUARD_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Guards the `CONFIRM` action only.
24. **Zone slot capacity** — as described in §3.4, else `ERR_ZONE_POST_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Guards `POST /event-security/staffing-plans/{id}/posts` and `PATCH /event-security/posts/{id}`.
25. **Post headroom** — the number of assignments on the post whose `assignment_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED` and whose `duty_status` is anything other than `NO_SHOW` is strictly below the post's `required_guard_count`, else `ERR_POST_FULLY_STAFFED` (409). Guards the `CONFIRM` action.
26. **Licence uniqueness** — a `license_number` is globally unique across every guard and every vendor, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_LICENSE_NUMBER` (409). Guards `POST /event-security/guards` and `POST /event-security/subcontractors`.
27. **Non-empty plan** — a publish or an activate requires at least one post on the plan whose `status` is not `CLOSED`, else `ERR_PLAN_HAS_NO_POSTS` (422). Guards `POST /event-security/staffing-plans/{id}/lifecycle` for `PUBLISH` and `ACTIVATE`.
28. **Assessed floor on headcount** — a publish requires the plan's `required_total_staff` to be at least the assessment's `required_total_staff`, else `ERR_STAFFING_BELOW_ASSESSED_REQUIREMENT` (422). Guards the `PUBLISH` action.
29. **Emergency exits staffed** — as described in §3.7, else `ERR_EXIT_POSTS_UNSTAFFED` (409). Guards `POST /event-security/security-events/{id}/lifecycle` for the `DOORS_OPEN` action.
30. **Screening capacity** — the number of the event's lanes whose `status` is `OPEN` is at least the assessment's `required_screening_lanes`, else `ERR_SCREENING_CAPACITY_INSUFFICIENT` (409). Guards the `DOORS_OPEN` action.
31. **Lane staffing** — opening a lane requires its `assigned_operator_count` to be at least 1, else `ERR_LANE_UNSTAFFED` (409). Guards `POST /event-security/screening-lanes/{id}/status` for the `OPEN` action.
32. **Escalation direction** — an escalation requires the requested `severity` to sit strictly higher in the ordering `MINOR`, `MODERATE`, `SEVERE`, `CRITICAL` than the incident's current `severity`, else `ERR_SEVERITY_NOT_ESCALATING` (422). Guards `POST /event-security/incidents/{id}/actions` for the `ESCALATE` action.
33. **Supervisor on duty** — an ejection or a law-enforcement handoff requires at least one assignment on the event's current plan whose post has `post_type` `SUPERVISOR`, whose `assignment_status` is `CONFIRMED` and whose `duty_status` is `ON_POST`, else `ERR_NO_SUPERVISOR_ON_DUTY` (409). Guards `POST /event-security/incidents/{id}/actions` for the `EJECT` and `LE_HANDOFF` actions.
34. **Observation window** — a density reading's `observed_at` falls no earlier than two hours before the event's `doors_open_at` and no later than two hours after its `event_end_at`, else `ERR_READING_TIMESTAMP_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422). Guards `POST /event-security/security-events/{id}/density-readings`.
35. **Doors-open lead time** — the logical clock is at or after one hour before the event's `doors_open_at`, else `ERR_DOORS_OPEN_TOO_EARLY` (409). Guards the `DOORS_OPEN` action.
36. **Single open review** — at most one after-action review per event may exist with a `status` other than `FINALIZED`, else `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_EXISTS` (409). Guards `POST /event-security/security-events/{id}/after-action-reviews`.
37. **Budget floor** — a publish requires the plan's `budget_cents` to be at least the assessment's `minimum_staffing_cost_floor_cents`, else `ERR_PLAN_BUDGET_BELOW_FLOOR` (402). Guards the `PUBLISH` action.
38. **Budget headroom** — a confirmation requires the assignment's `line_cost_cents` to be at most the plan's `budget_remaining_cents`, else `ERR_PLAN_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (402). Guards the `CONFIRM` action.
39. **Readiness for briefing** — a briefing requires the event's current plan to have `readiness_posture` equal to `READY`, else `ERR_PLAN_NOT_READY` (409). Guards `POST /event-security/security-events/{id}/lifecycle` for the `BRIEF` action.

### 2.6. Version Pinning and Stale-Write Rejection

Three write endpoints participate in optimistic concurrency control, and on each of them the version field is a required input, not an optional courtesy.

`POST /event-security/security-events/{id}/staffing-plans` requires `expected_event_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `SecurityEvent`. If that value does not equal the event's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_EVENT_VERSION` (412). The rule exists because a new plan supersedes the event's prior `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED` plan; a writer holding a stale view of the event would silently discard planning work it never observed, and pinning the version makes that condition explicit and deterministic.

`PATCH /event-security/security-events/{id}` likewise requires `expected_event_version`, and a mismatch against the event's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_EVENT_VERSION` (412). Box-office updates to `ticketed_attendance` legitimately overwrite one another, so the value the client last observed is the only way to detect that the profile being edited has already moved.

`PATCH /event-security/posts/{id}` requires `expected_post_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Post`. A mismatch against the post's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POST_VERSION` (412). Successive re-profiles legitimately overwrite one another, and cascaded roster changes advance a post's `version` behind the client's back, so the observed version is the only safe basis for a re-profile.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Endpoints outside this list of three do not take one, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.7. Gate Posture: The Event's Master Interlock

Every `SecurityEvent` carries a derived `gate_posture` whose value is one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. It is never written directly by a client; it is recomputed whenever the event changes lifecycle state, whenever the current plan's readiness figures are recomputed, whenever an incident changes `status` or `severity`, and whenever a density reading is recorded. It is the system's master interlock: while an event's `gate_posture` is `FROZEN`, the child mutations listed at rank 13 of §2.5 are refused with `ERR_EVENT_GATE_FROZEN`.

Posture is resolved by a first-match ladder, where each clause is evaluated only when every earlier clause is false:

1. If `open_critical_incident_count` is greater than zero, the posture is `FROZEN`.
2. Otherwise, if `ingress_status` is `HOLD`, the posture is `FROZEN`.
3. Otherwise, if there is no current plan, or the current plan's `readiness_posture` is `AT_RISK` or `BLOCKED`, the posture is `RESTRICTED`.
4. Otherwise, if any density reading for this event has `effective_level` equal to `CRITICAL` and an `observed_at` no earlier than 15 minutes before the logical clock, the posture is `RESTRICTED`.
5. Otherwise, the posture is `OPEN`.

`open_critical_incident_count` is itself derived: it is the number of incidents on the event whose `severity` is `CRITICAL` and whose `status` is neither `RESOLVED` nor `CLOSED_UNFOUNDED`. The "current plan" is the event's staffing plan whose `is_current` is true.

The ladder has one variant, keyed on how far the event has travelled. While the event's `status` is `PLANNING` or `RISK_ASSESSED` — that is, before any plan has been activated — clause 3 is skipped entirely, because no plan is yet in force. The posture is therefore `OPEN` during those two states unless clause 1, clause 2 or clause 4 fires; this keeps planning-time writes reachable while still letting a `CRITICAL` density observation restrict the gate. Once the event's `status` is any of `STAFFED`, `BRIEFED`, `DOORS_OPEN`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUSPENDED`, `CLOSED` or `DEBRIEFED`, the full five-clause ladder applies, so a plan that drops out of `READY` immediately restricts the event.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline

This system is aggressively hierarchical, and a single request routinely mutates four levels of the graph. The canonical chain runs from an assignment upward: a change in roster membership changes the post's `filled_guard_count` and therefore the post's `status`; a change in post status changes the plan's `filled_total_staff`, `coverage_ratio`, `understaffed_post_count` and `readiness_posture`; a change in plan readiness changes the event's `gate_posture`. Every entity touched anywhere on that chain has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, even when the client's request named only the assignment.

Cascades also run downward. Superseding a plan closes its posts and cancels its pencilled-in and confirmed assignments; closing an event relieves its on-post guards, closes its lanes, closes its posts and closes the plan. Rejecting or expiring a guard card voids that guard's assignments and then re-derives every post, plan and event they touched. Suspending or terminating a vendor first pushes that vendor's verified guards back to review, then voids their assignments, then re-derives posts, plans and events.

Where a cascade touches more than one row of the same kind, the traversal order is pinned so that the single rounded result at the end is reproducible: affected assignments are processed in ascending `created_at` order, tie-broken by their post's ascending `created_at`; guards are swept in ascending `created_at` order; screening lanes are swept in ascending `created_at` order; posts are traversed in ascending `created_at` order.

### 2.9. Arithmetic, Precision and Rounding

All arithmetic in this system is exact decimal arithmetic. Binary floating point is not acceptable: fifty-five hundredths of two hundred is exactly one hundred and ten, and a computation that yields anything else is wrong.

Rounding is always half-up, and the number of retained places is fixed per field. Monetary values are whole cents; `composite_threat_score`, `scheduled_hours`, `billed_hours`, `committed_guard_hours`, `coverage_ratio`, `response_minutes`, `coverage_shortfall_minutes`, `prior_incident_rate`, `history_incident_rate`, `floor_area_sqm` and `markup_pct` carry two decimal places; `density_persons_per_sqm`, `occupancy_ratio` and `rolling_mean_persons_per_sqm` carry three decimal places; `no_show_rate` carries four decimal places. Where a formula in §3 calls for rounding down to a whole number, that is a floor; where it calls for rounding up, that is a ceiling; neither is affected by the half-up rule.

Rounding *order* is as load-bearing as rounding *precision*, and §3 pins it explicitly at every point where it matters. As a general principle, every per-row figure is rounded at the row level and aggregates then sum those already-rounded integers; summing unrounded products and rounding once at the aggregate level is never correct.

### 2.10. Nested Paths and Referential Scoping

When a path names more than one resource — a zone under a venue, an assessment or a lane or a reading under an event — the identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the 404 returned. A request for a screening lane under a nonexistent event yields `ERR_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, never `ERR_LANE_NOT_FOUND`. Body-borne foreign keys are resolved only after every path identifier has resolved.

Existence is not sufficiency. A resolvable reference must also be in the right scope, and scope violations are reported as `ERR_ZONE_VENUE_MISMATCH`: a zone referenced from an event's child must belong to that event's venue; a lane referenced from a post must belong to the same event; a `reports_to_post_id` must name a `SUPERVISOR` post inside the same staffing plan; and a `risk_assessment_id` supplied when creating a plan must belong to the event named in the path.

### 2.11. Conserved Quantities: Money and Labour Hours

Each staffing plan runs a two-column ledger, and both columns are conserved quantities that must balance after every single request. On the money side, `budget_remaining_cents` plus `committed_cost_cents` always equals `budget_cents`; confirming a shift and then cancelling the same shift restores `budget_remaining_cents` to exactly its prior value, to the cent. On the labour side, `committed_guard_hours` always equals the sum of `scheduled_hours` over the plan's committed roster, so a confirmation followed by a system void restores the pre-confirmation figure exactly.

The committed ledger recognises a shift only once it is contractually committed. Rows whose `assignment_status` is `TENTATIVE`, `CANCELLED` or `VOIDED` are outside the ledger set entirely — a pencilled-in guard moves no money. Rows whose `assignment_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED` are inside it, which means a completed shift stays in the committed columns after the night is over rather than falling out of them. Actual delivered labour is tracked separately in `billed_cost_cents`, which accumulates only from rows that reached `RELIEVED` duty.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Venue Layout and Occupancy Ceilings

A `SecurityDirector` registers a **Venue** with its trade `name` (non-empty, at most 120 characters), its licensed all-in `gross_capacity` (at least 1), the number of public ingress doors `door_count` (at least 1), the `egress_capacity_per_minute` it can discharge in a full evacuation (at least 1), and its outer `perimeter_length_m` in metres (at least 1). Two optional flags round out the record: `alcohol_licensed`, which defaults to false, and `prior_incident_rate`, the historical incidents per thousand attendees observed at this venue, which defaults to 0, must lie between 0 and 25 inclusive, and carries two decimal places. A venue's `status` is system-managed, starts at `ACTIVE`, and may also read `UNDER_RENOVATION`.

Inside a venue, a `SecurityDirector` or a `RiskAnalyst` declares **Venue Zones** — the layout units against which posts, lanes, readings and incidents are all located. A zone is created with a `zone_code` that must be unique within its owning venue (the zone's `venue_id` is taken from the path, not the body), a `zone_type` drawn from `PERIMETER`, `CONCOURSE`, `FLOOR`, `SEATED`, `EXIT_CORRIDOR` and `BACKSTAGE` that classifies the layout and indicates which post functions naturally belong there, a `floor_area_sqm` of usable standing or seating area that must be strictly greater than zero and carries two decimal places, a placarded `posted_capacity` of at least 1, an `exit_units` count of at least 1 recording how many standard 22-inch egress widths serve the zone, and a `post_slot_capacity` of at least 1 giving the maximum number of guard slots the zone can physically host at any single instant. The optional `is_ingress_zone` flag, defaulting to false, records whether patrons enter the venue through this zone.

At the moment a zone is created the system derives its `max_safe_occupancy`, which is the binding of three independent ceilings. The first is the placard itself, `posted_capacity`. The second is an area ceiling: the fire-code allowance is 0.65 square metres of usable floor per person, so the zone's `floor_area_sqm` divided by that allowance, rounded down to a whole person, gives the number the floor space can hold. The third is an egress ceiling: each exit unit can discharge 100 persons, so `exit_units` multiplied by 100 gives the number the exits can clear. `max_safe_occupancy` is the smallest of those three whole numbers. Because it is the denominator of the occupancy ratio used in crowd monitoring, it is derived once, at zone creation, from the zone's own inputs — no other row participates.

### 3.2. Standing Up an Event and Assessing Its Risk

A `SecurityDirector` creates a **SecurityEvent** against a venue, supplying `venue_id`, an `event_name`, an `event_type` drawn from `CONCERT`, `SPORTING`, `FESTIVAL`, `CONVENTION` and `CIVIC_PROTEST`, the three scheduled instants `doors_open_at`, `event_start_at` and `event_end_at` (each strictly later than the previous one), and an `expected_attendance` of at least 1 that may not exceed the venue's `gross_capacity`. Three optional inputs may be supplied at creation: `ticketed_attendance`, the confirmed tickets sold, which is null until the box office reports and which must likewise be at least 1 and no greater than the venue's `gross_capacity` when present; `alcohol_served`, defaulting to false; and `vip_present`, marking a protectee detail, also defaulting to false. A new event's `status` is `PLANNING`, its `ingress_status` is `NORMAL`, its `open_critical_incident_count` is 0, and its `gate_posture` is derived immediately under the pre-plan variant described in §2.7.

The box office reports late and reports repeatedly, so the attendance and flag profile stays editable throughout planning. A director may revise `ticketed_attendance`, `alcohol_served` and `vip_present` on an existing event, supplying `expected_event_version` as described in §2.6; a successful revision increments the event's `version` and re-derives `gate_posture`. Which attendance figure is authoritative is decided at assessment time, not here — see below.

A `RiskAnalyst` (or a director) authors a **RiskAssessment** revision against the event, recording the intelligence-derived `threat_level_input` from `LOW`, `GUARDED`, `ELEVATED`, `HIGH` and `SEVERE`; the `protest_likelihood` from `NONE`, `POSSIBLE` and `LIKELY`; the expected `crowd_profile` from `SEATED`, `FAMILY`, `GENERAL_ADMISSION` and `MOSH_PIT`; the `ingress_door_count` actually opened for this event (at least 1); a `layout_complexity_score` that is a whole number from 1 to 5 rating layout and sightline complexity; and a `history_incident_rate` of incidents per thousand attendees observed for comparable events, between 0 and 25 inclusive with two decimal places. The `event_id` comes from the path. A fresh assessment begins in `DRAFT` with `revision_number` 1, and **no scores are computed at creation** — every derived figure stays unpopulated until the assessment is submitted.

Submission by the analyst moves the assessment `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. It is permitted only while the event's `status` is `PLANNING` or `RISK_ASSESSED` — a replacement assessment may still be submitted after an earlier one was approved — and is otherwise refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; it is also subject to the gate interlock. On submission the assessment sets `attendance_basis` to `EXPECTED` and `score_basis_attendance` to the event's `expected_attendance`, stamps `submitted_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, recomputes the whole derived block (`composite_threat_score`, `risk_tier`, `required_perimeter_guards`, `required_ingress_guards`, `required_floor_guards`, `required_exit_guards`, `required_line_guards`, `required_supervisors`, `required_total_staff`, `required_screening_lanes` and `minimum_staffing_cost_floor_cents`) under the preliminary basis, and increments the assessment's `version`.

A director then either approves or rejects. Rejection moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, requires a `rejection_reason` in the body, stores it, and increments `version`; the acting user must differ from `submitted_by_user_id`. A rejected assessment can be revised back to `DRAFT` by the analyst, which increases `revision_number` by one, clears `submitted_by_user_id` back to null, clears `rejection_reason` back to null, and increments `version`. That revision loop — `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → REJECTED → DRAFT` — may repeat any number of times.

Approval moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and is the pivot of the whole planning phase. It requires an acting user different from `submitted_by_user_id`, and it requires the event's `status` to be `PLANNING` or `RISK_ASSESSED`: a replacement assessment may be approved while the event is already `RISK_ASSESSED`, but once a plan has been activated the event has left both states and no further assessment may be approved, which surfaces as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Approval performs a **basis switch**: if the event's `ticketed_attendance` is not null, `attendance_basis` becomes `TICKETED` and `score_basis_attendance` becomes that ticketed figure; if it is still null, both keep the `EXPECTED` basis. The entire derived block is then recomputed on the final basis — which is why an approval can move the score, the tier and every staffing requirement even though no analyst input changed. Approval also stamps `approved_by_user_id`, supersedes any other assessment on the same event that was already `APPROVED` by transitioning it `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED` and incrementing that assessment's `version`, cascades the event `PLANNING → RISK_ASSESSED` (incrementing the event's `version` and re-deriving its `gate_posture`), and increments the approved assessment's own `version`. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal for an assessment.

The `composite_threat_score` is a weighted score on a 0.00 to 100.00 scale, computed in a pinned order. First the **load ratio** is the basis attendance divided by the venue's `gross_capacity`, taken as an exact decimal division. The **density pressure** is that load ratio multiplied by 30 points-at-full-house, rounded half-up to two decimal places, and then capped at 25 — the rounding and the cap both happen here, before the term is used anywhere else. Next the **base points** are the sum of six contributions plus that density pressure: the threat contribution is 0 for `LOW`, 8 for `GUARDED`, 18 for `ELEVATED`, 30 for `HIGH` and 44 for `SEVERE`; the protest contribution is 0 for `NONE`, 6 for `POSSIBLE` and 15 for `LIKELY`; the crowd-profile contribution is 0 for `SEATED`, 2 for `FAMILY`, 10 for `GENERAL_ADMISSION` and 20 for `MOSH_PIT`; the layout contribution is 3 points for each level of `layout_complexity_score`; 7 points are added when the event's `alcohol_served` is true and nothing otherwise; and 5 points are added when the event's `vip_present` is true and nothing otherwise. Then the **crowd factor** is one plus four hundredths for each unit of `history_incident_rate`, capped at 1.35 — again, the cap is applied to the factor itself before it is used. Finally `composite_threat_score` is the base points multiplied by that capped crowd factor, rounded half-up to two decimal places, and capped at 100. Multiplying an uncapped factor by an unrounded density pressure and rounding only once at the end produces a different number and is not the specified behaviour.

The `risk_tier` follows immediately from the two-decimal score, by inclusive-at-the-bottom thresholds: a score of 70 or above is `T4`; otherwise 50 or above is `T3`; otherwise 30 or above is `T2`; otherwise `T1`. A score of exactly 50.00 is `T3`, and 49.99 is `T2`.

### 3.3. Turning the Score Into a Staffing Requirement

Once the tier is known, four further derivations run in sequence, each on the same basis attendance the score used.

The **line-guard matrix** starts from a raw line-guard figure: the basis attendance divided by 1000, multiplied by the tier's guard ratio, which is 1.5 guards per thousand for `T1`, 2.5 for `T2`, 4.0 for `T3` and 6.0 for `T4`. That raw figure is then split across four post functions by share — 30 percent perimeter, 25 percent ingress, 35 percent floor and 10 percent exit — and each share is rounded **up** to a whole guard **independently**. `required_perimeter_guards` is the ceiling of the raw figure times 0.30; `required_ingress_guards` is the ceiling of the raw figure times 0.25; `required_floor_guards` is the ceiling of the raw figure times 0.35. `required_exit_guards` is the larger of two numbers: the ceiling of the raw figure times 0.10, and the venue's `door_count` multiplied by one guard per door — the door count is a hard floor on emergency-exit staffing, never a ceiling. `required_line_guards` is the sum of those four already-rounded integers. Rounding the raw figure up first and then splitting that single integer across the shares yields a smaller total for almost every input and is not the specified behaviour.

The **supervisor span** looks circular — supervisors are staff, and staff need supervision — but the evaluation is pinned as a single downward pass over line guards only. The first-line supervisor count is `required_line_guards` divided by a span of control of 10, rounded up. A second line is added only when that first-line count exceeds a supervisor-to-supervisor span of 5: in that case the second-line count is the first-line count divided by 5, rounded up; otherwise it is zero. `required_supervisors` is the sum of the two lines, and the assessment's `required_total_staff` is `required_line_guards` plus `required_supervisors`. The fixed-point reading — solving for a supervisor count that covers line guards plus supervisors together — is explicitly not implemented; for 10 line guards the answer is exactly 1 first-line supervisor, because the ceiling of ten tenths is one and the span comparison is not a strict "greater than" on the guard count.

The **screening-lane requirement** models the ingress surge. The surge population is the basis attendance multiplied by 0.55, rounded up to a whole patron. Of that surge, 60 percent are assumed to carry a bag: the bag-carrying population is the surge population times 0.60, rounded up, and the no-bag population is the surge population minus the bag-carrying population. The clearance window is the 45-minute surge window plus a 20-minute maximum acceptable queue wait, giving 65 minutes. Lane capacities are computed by multiplying first and dividing by 60 last, so that they come out exact: a bag-check lane rated at 180 patrons per hour clears 180 times 65 divided by 60, which is exactly 195 patrons in the window, and a no-bag lane rated at 420 patrons per hour clears exactly 455. `required_screening_lanes` is the ceiling of the bag-carrying population divided by 195, plus the ceiling of the no-bag population divided by 455 — each stream is rounded up separately and only then are the two integers added.

The **minimum staffing cost floor** is deliberately crude, because it exists only to catch a plan that has been under-budgeted before anyone wastes time rostering it. Every member of `required_total_staff` is assumed to work a 4-hour minimum call at a floor rate of 18.00 per hour, so `minimum_staffing_cost_floor_cents` is `required_total_staff` multiplied by 4 hours multiplied by 1800 cents, in whole cents.

Every one of these figures is readable on the assessment itself, alongside `composite_threat_score`, `risk_tier` and the `attendance_basis` and `score_basis_attendance` pair that documents which attendance number produced them.

### 3.4. The Staffing Plan, Its Posts and the Span of Control

A `StaffingCoordinator` opens a **StaffingPlan** against an event, naming the `risk_assessment_id` the plan is sized from and an authorised labour `budget_cents` of at least 1, together with the `expected_event_version` required by §2.6. The plan's `event_id` comes from the path. Creating a plan is also the mechanism for revising one: any existing plan on the same event whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED` is superseded, and the new plan's `supersedes_plan_id` records that prior plan's identifier (it is null for an event's first plan). A superseded plan has its `status` set to `SUPERSEDED` and its `is_current` set to false, cancels every assignment on its posts whose `assignment_status` is `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED` with a `cancellation_reason` of `PLAN_SUPERSEDED`, closes every one of its posts, recomputes its own `committed_cost_cents`, `budget_remaining_cents`, `committed_guard_hours`, `coverage_ratio` and `readiness_posture`, and increments its `version`. The side-effect set is identical whether the superseded plan was `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED`. `is_current` is true for exactly one plan per event: it is true whenever `status` is anything other than `SUPERSEDED`. A new plan starts in `DRAFT` with `pinned_risk_assessment_version` null, `committed_cost_cents` and `billed_cost_cents` and `committed_guard_hours` at zero, `required_total_staff` and `filled_total_staff` and `understaffed_post_count` at zero, `coverage_ratio` at 0, and `readiness_posture` at `BLOCKED`.

Within a plan the coordinator declares **Posts**. A post is created with the `zone_id` it stands in, a `post_code` that must be unique within the plan, a `post_type` drawn from `PERIMETER`, `INGRESS_SCREENING`, `FLOOR`, `EMERGENCY_EXIT` and `SUPERVISOR`, a `required_guard_count` of at least 1 giving the slots the post must have to count as filled, a `min_credential_level` that is a whole number from 1 to 4, and a coverage window `window_start` to `window_end` where the end is strictly after the start. The plan's identifier arrives as `staffing_plan_id` from the path. Two optional references complete the structure: `reports_to_post_id` names the supervising `SUPERVISOR` post inside the same plan and defaults to null — it is null when a post has no supervising post, and it is always null on `SUPERVISOR` posts themselves — while `lane_id` names the screening lane an `INGRESS_SCREENING` post operates and is otherwise null. A new post starts with `filled_guard_count` at 0 and `status` at `OPEN`.

Two capacity rules bind at post declaration and at re-profiling alike. The **span-of-control rule** caps a supervisor's direct reports: summing `required_guard_count` over the posts whose `reports_to_post_id` names a given supervisor post must not exceed 10 guard slots, and a declaration that would breach it is refused with `ERR_SPAN_OF_CONTROL_EXCEEDED`. The **zone slot rule** caps concurrent physical occupancy of a zone by guards, and it uses closed-interval semantics with an aggregate sum. Consider every candidate instant drawn from the union of the `window_start` and `window_end` endpoints of the new post and of the zone's existing non-`CLOSED` posts on non-`SUPERSEDED` plans. At each such instant, sum `required_guard_count` over all posts — including the new one — whose closed coverage window, endpoints inclusive, contains that instant. If any of those sums exceeds the zone's `post_slot_capacity`, the request is refused with `ERR_ZONE_POST_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Note that under closed-interval semantics two windows that merely touch at an endpoint **do** overlap, and both posts count at that shared instant.

A coordinator may re-profile a post, adjusting `required_guard_count`, `min_credential_level`, `window_start` or `window_end` under the version pinning of §2.6. A re-profile increments the post's `version`, re-derives the post's `status`, then re-derives the plan's `required_total_staff`, `coverage_ratio`, `understaffed_post_count` and `readiness_posture`, and finally re-derives the event's `gate_posture`.

A post's `filled_guard_count` is the number of assignments on that post whose `assignment_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED` **and** whose `duty_status` is anything other than `NO_SHOW`. Rows that are `TENTATIVE`, `CANCELLED`, `VOIDED`, or marked `NO_SHOW` are all excluded. The post's `status` is then resolved by a first-match ladder, and the order matters because a post can satisfy more than one clause at once:

1. If the plan's `status` is `SUPERSEDED` or `CLOSED`, the post is `CLOSED`.
2. Otherwise, if `filled_guard_count` is at least `required_guard_count`, the post is `FILLED`.
3. Otherwise, if the plan's `status` is `ACTIVATED` or `LOCKED`, the post is `UNDERSTAFFED`.
4. Otherwise, if `filled_guard_count` is greater than zero, the post is `PARTIALLY_STAFFED`.
5. Otherwise the post is `OPEN`.

This ladder is what produces the post transitions the system performs on its own: `OPEN → PARTIALLY_STAFFED` when a partial fill lands while the plan is still `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED`; `OPEN → FILLED` and `PARTIALLY_STAFFED → FILLED` when the requirement is met; `FILLED → UNDERSTAFFED` and `OPEN → UNDERSTAFFED` and `PARTIALLY_STAFFED → UNDERSTAFFED` once the plan is live and the post falls short; `UNDERSTAFFED → FILLED` when the shortfall is closed; and `OPEN → CLOSED`, `PARTIALLY_STAFFED → CLOSED`, `FILLED → CLOSED` or `UNDERSTAFFED → CLOSED` when the plan is superseded or the event closes. `CLOSED` is terminal. Every one of these transitions increments the post's `version`, and every transition into or out of `FILLED` or `UNDERSTAFFED` bubbles up to the plan: reaching `FILLED` re-derives the plan's `filled_total_staff`, `coverage_ratio`, `understaffed_post_count` and `readiness_posture`; falling into `UNDERSTAFFED` raises the plan's `understaffed_post_count` by one and re-derives `coverage_ratio` and `readiness_posture`; recovering from `UNDERSTAFFED` to `FILLED` lowers `understaffed_post_count` by one and re-derives the same two figures.

The plan's coverage figures are derived over the plan's posts whose `status` is not `CLOSED`. `required_total_staff` is the sum of `required_guard_count` over that set; `filled_total_staff` is the sum of `filled_guard_count` over the same set; `understaffed_post_count` is how many of them are `UNDERSTAFFED`. `coverage_ratio` is `filled_total_staff` divided by `required_total_staff`, rounded half-up to two decimal places — and it is exactly 0.00 when `required_total_staff` is zero, since an empty plan covers nothing rather than everything. A separate boolean, `supervisor_coverage_ok`, is true when the number of assignments on the plan's `SUPERVISOR` posts with `assignment_status` in `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED` and `duty_status` other than `NO_SHOW` is at least the assessment's `required_supervisors`. `readiness_posture` then follows a pinned first-match ladder:

1. If `coverage_ratio` is at least 1.00, `supervisor_coverage_ok` is true, and `understaffed_post_count` is zero, the posture is `READY`.
2. Otherwise, if `coverage_ratio` is at least 0.90, the posture is `AT_RISK`.
3. Otherwise the posture is `BLOCKED`.

A ratio of exactly 0.90 is `AT_RISK` while 0.89 is `BLOCKED`, and a plan at exactly 1.00 coverage that still has a supervisor gap is `AT_RISK`, not `READY`. These figures, together with `committed_cost_cents`, `budget_remaining_cents` and `committed_guard_hours`, are also exposed as a dedicated computed read on the plan.

The plan lifecycle runs `DRAFT → PUBLISHED → ACTIVATED → LOCKED → CLOSED`, with a recall loop back to `DRAFT` and a supersession exit. Publishing, performed by the coordinator, requires the referenced assessment's `status` to be `APPROVED` (else `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_APPROVED`), at least one non-`CLOSED` post (else `ERR_PLAN_HAS_NO_POSTS`), the plan's `required_total_staff` to be at least the assessment's `required_total_staff` (else `ERR_STAFFING_BELOW_ASSESSED_REQUIREMENT`), and `budget_cents` to be at least the assessment's `minimum_staffing_cost_floor_cents` (else `ERR_PLAN_BUDGET_BELOW_FLOOR`); the gate interlock also applies. On success the plan becomes `PUBLISHED`, pins `pinned_risk_assessment_version` to the assessment's current `version` so the plan is permanently sized against that snapshot, recomputes the coverage and posture block, and increments `version`. Recall, also a coordinator action, returns `PUBLISHED → DRAFT`, but only while no assignment on the plan's posts has `assignment_status` equal to `CONFIRMED`; it clears `pinned_risk_assessment_version` back to null, re-derives `readiness_posture`, and increments `version`. The publish-recall cycle may repeat.

Activation is a director action moving `PUBLISHED → ACTIVATED`. It requires at least one non-`CLOSED` post and requires the event's `status` to be `RISK_ASSESSED`, and it respects the gate interlock. On activation every post on the plan has its `status` re-derived — which is when posts below their requirement flip to `UNDERSTAFFED`, since clause 3 of the post ladder becomes reachable — the plan's `coverage_ratio`, `understaffed_post_count` and `readiness_posture` are recomputed, the event cascades `RISK_ASSESSED → STAFFED` with its own `version` increment, and the plan's `version` increments. Locking, also a director action, moves `ACTIVATED → LOCKED` and requires the event's `status` to be one of `BRIEFED`, `DOORS_OPEN`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `SUSPENDED`; from that point every roster mutation on the plan's posts and assignments is refused with `ERR_PLAN_LOCKED`. The final step `LOCKED → CLOSED` is not requested directly on the plan: it happens as part of closing the event.

### 3.5. Sourcing and Credentialing the Guard Force

Labour comes from two channels, and both must clear the same compliance bar before anyone stands a post.

A **Subcontractor** is registered by a coordinator or a credential auditor with a `company_name`, the state security-agency `license_number`, the general-liability certificate expiry `insurance_expires_on`, and a contractual `markup_pct` between 0 and 0.60 inclusive carried to two decimal places. Licence numbers live in a single global namespace shared with guard cards, so a `license_number` already used by any guard or any other vendor is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LICENSE_NUMBER`. A new vendor's `status` is `PENDING` and its `verified_guard_count` — the number of its guards currently `VERIFIED` — is 0.

A `CredentialAuditor` alone moves a vendor. Approval, from either `PENDING` or `SUSPENDED`, requires `insurance_expires_on` to be strictly after the logical clock and sets `status` to `APPROVED`. Suspension moves `APPROVED → SUSPENDED` and runs a four-hop cascade: every guard of that vendor whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` is pushed `VERIFIED → PENDING_REVIEW`; because those guards have left `VERIFIED`, their assignments whose `assignment_status` is `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED` are voided; because those rows left the roster, each affected post's `filled_guard_count` and `status` are re-derived; because those posts changed, each affected plan's `coverage_ratio`, `readiness_posture` and cost-and-hours ledger are re-derived, and then each affected event's `gate_posture` is re-derived. The vendor's `verified_guard_count` is recomputed and its `version` increments. Re-approving a suspended vendor does **not** auto-re-verify anybody: each guard must be verified again individually. That approve-suspend cycle may repeat. Termination is reachable from `PENDING`, `APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED` and sets `status` to `TERMINATED`, which is terminal; when terminating from `APPROVED`, the identical four-hop suspension cascade is applied before the status is written.

A **Guard** is sourced by a coordinator or a vendor liaison with a `full_name`, an `employment_type` of either `EMPLOYEE` or `SUBCONTRACTED`, a `credential_level` that is a whole number from 1 to 4, the guard-card `license_number`, the card expiry `credential_expires_on`, and a straight-time `base_hourly_rate_cents` of at least 1. A `subcontractor_id` is required when `employment_type` is `SUBCONTRACTED` and must be absent when it is `EMPLOYEE`. The same global licence uniqueness applies. A new guard's `verification_status` is `UNVERIFIED`, `verified_at` is null, and `is_supervisor_qualified` is derived as true exactly when `credential_level` is 3 or higher — level 3 is the supervisor-qualified threshold.

Verification is where the compliance function bites. A coordinator or vendor liaison may request review, moving `UNVERIFIED → PENDING_REVIEW`. An auditor may verify, and there is a deliberate fast path: verification is reachable both from `PENDING_REVIEW` and straight from `UNVERIFIED` without any prior review request. Either way, verification requires `credential_expires_on` to be strictly after the logical clock (else `ERR_GUARD_CREDENTIAL_INVALID`) and, for a subcontracted guard, requires the vendor's `status` to be `APPROVED` with `insurance_expires_on` strictly after the logical clock (else `ERR_SUBCONTRACTOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`). Success sets `verification_status` to `VERIFIED`, stamps `verified_at` with the logical clock, recomputes the vendor's `verified_guard_count` when a vendor is involved, and increments the guard's `version`.

Rejection moves `PENDING_REVIEW → REJECTED`, clears `verified_at` back to null, and triggers the four-hop void cascade: every assignment for that guard whose `assignment_status` is `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED` is voided; each affected post's `filled_guard_count` and `status` are re-derived; each affected plan's `coverage_ratio`, `understaffed_post_count`, `readiness_posture`, `committed_cost_cents`, `committed_guard_hours` and `budget_remaining_cents` are re-derived; and each affected event's `gate_posture` is re-derived. Affected rows are processed in ascending assignment `created_at` order, tie-broken by ascending post `created_at`. A rejected guard may be disqualified by an auditor, moving `REJECTED → DISQUALIFIED`, which is terminal.

Re-certification is a loop. A coordinator or liaison may request review from `VERIFIED`, from `REJECTED` or from `EXPIRED`, and the cycle back to `VERIFIED` may repeat any number of times. The three entry points differ in exactly one respect each, and the difference matters. From `VERIFIED`, the request pushes the guard to `PENDING_REVIEW` but existing assignments are deliberately **not** voided — only a rejection or an expiry voids them. From `REJECTED`, the request pushes the guard to `PENDING_REVIEW` and overwrites `credential_expires_on` and `credential_level` when those values are supplied in the body. From `EXPIRED`, the request requires a `credential_expires_on` in the body that is strictly later than the logical clock, and stores it. All three increment the guard's `version`.

Expiry is not requested; it is swept. When a `SystemOperator` advances the logical clock, the same call runs a credential-expiry sweep over guards in ascending `created_at` order: every guard whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` and whose `credential_expires_on` is at or before the new clock value moves `VERIFIED → EXPIRED`, has `verified_at` cleared to null, and runs the identical four-hop void cascade described above — assignments voided, posts re-derived, plans re-derived across coverage and ledger alike, events' `gate_posture` re-derived. The call reports how many guards expired and how many assignments were voided as a result.

### 3.6. Rostering: Assignments, Cost and the Committed Ledger

A coordinator creates a **PostAssignment** by naming the `post_id`, the `guard_id`, and the shift window `window_start` to `window_end` (end strictly after start). The shift must sit inside the post's own coverage window — its start at or after the post's `window_start` and its end at or before the post's `window_end` — or the create is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_POST`. A new row starts `TENTATIVE` on the roster dimension and `PENDING_BRIEF` on the duty dimension, with `billed_hours`, `billed_cost_cents`, `radio_channel`, `training_module_code`, `briefed_at`, `check_in_at`, `relieved_at` and `cancellation_reason` all null.

**The two dimensions are independent.** `assignment_status` — `TENTATIVE`, `CONFIRMED`, `CANCELLED`, `VOIDED`, `COMPLETED` — is the roster dimension, owned by the coordinator. `duty_status` — `PENDING_BRIEF`, `BRIEFED`, `ON_POST`, `NO_SHOW`, `RELIEVED` — is the on-the-night dimension, owned by the supervisor. They evolve on the same row without either one forcing the other, and only their conjunction decides whether the row counts toward a post's `filled_guard_count`: a row counts if and only if its `assignment_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED` **and** its `duty_status` is not `NO_SHOW`. `CANCELLED`, `VOIDED` and `COMPLETED` are the terminal roster states.

Cost is computed at creation, on the scheduled window, and is what the ledger will later commit. The `scheduled_hours` figure is the total minutes between `window_start` and `window_end` divided by 60, rounded half-up to two decimal places. The `night_differential_applies` flag is true when the UTC hour-of-day of `window_start` is 22 or later, or earlier than 6. Straight-time hours are the lesser of `scheduled_hours` and an 8-hour overtime threshold; overtime hours are whatever exceeds 8, or zero. Rounding order is pinned at three levels, and each level rounds to whole cents. First, base pay is the guard's `base_hourly_rate_cents` times straight hours, rounded to a whole cent, **plus** the guard's `base_hourly_rate_cents` times an overtime multiplier of 1.5 times overtime hours, rounded to a whole cent separately — two roundings, then an addition. Second, the night component is 150 cents per hour times `scheduled_hours`, rounded to a whole cent on its own, when `night_differential_applies` is true, and zero otherwise. Third, the sourcing branch applies. For a guard whose `employment_type` is `SUBCONTRACTED`, `line_cost_cents` is the already-rounded sum of base and night multiplied by one plus the vendor's `markup_pct`, rounded again to a whole cent, and only then is a flat subcontract admin fee of 1200 cents added; `pay_basis` is `SUBCONTRACT`. For an `EMPLOYEE`, `line_cost_cents` is simply the sum of base and night, and `pay_basis` is `EMPLOYEE_OT` when there were any overtime hours and `EMPLOYEE_STRAIGHT` when there were none.

Creating an assignment moves no money. **Confirming** it does, and confirmation is the single most heavily guarded operation in the system. Beyond the plan-locked, plan-current and gate interlocks, confirmation requires: the plan's `status` to be `PUBLISHED` or `ACTIVATED` — a `LOCKED` plan yields `ERR_PLAN_LOCKED` and any other plan state yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the guard's `verification_status` to be `VERIFIED` with `credential_expires_on` strictly after the shift's `window_end`, else `ERR_GUARD_CREDENTIAL_INVALID`; the guard's `credential_level` to be at least the post's `min_credential_level`, else `ERR_CREDENTIAL_LEVEL_INSUFFICIENT`; for a `SUPERVISOR` post, the guard's `credential_level` to be at least 3, else `ERR_SUPERVISOR_NOT_QUALIFIED`; for a subcontracted guard, the vendor's `status` to be `APPROVED` with `insurance_expires_on` strictly after the shift's `window_end`, else `ERR_SUBCONTRACTOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`; no double-booking of the guard; headroom on the post, else `ERR_POST_FULLY_STAFFED`; and enough budget, else `ERR_PLAN_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`.

The **double-booking rule** uses half-open interval semantics and is enforced only at confirmation, never at creation — overlapping pencilled-in rows are allowed to exist and are resolved when someone tries to commit one of them. Confirmation is refused with `ERR_GUARD_WINDOW_CONFLICT` when some other assignment for the same `guard_id` whose `assignment_status` is `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED` starts strictly before this shift ends and also ends strictly after this shift starts. Two back-to-back shifts that merely touch, where the other shift's `window_end` equals this shift's `window_start`, are accepted. Note the deliberate contrast with the zone slot rule of §3.4, which uses closed intervals and therefore treats touching windows as overlapping.

A successful confirmation sets `assignment_status` to `CONFIRMED` and then runs three hops upward. Hop one raises the post's `filled_guard_count` by one and re-derives the post's `status`. Hop two, because the post's status changed, re-derives the plan's `filled_total_staff`, `coverage_ratio`, `understaffed_post_count` and `readiness_posture`. Hop three, because the plan's readiness changed, re-derives the event's `gate_posture`. In the same request the ledger is synchronised: the row's `line_cost_cents` is added to the plan's `committed_cost_cents`, the row's `scheduled_hours` is added to the plan's `committed_guard_hours`, and `budget_remaining_cents` is set to `budget_cents` minus `committed_cost_cents`. The assignment's `version` increments, as do the versions of every entity the hops touched.

**Cancellation** by the coordinator is reachable from both `TENTATIVE` and `CONFIRMED`, requires the plan not to be `LOCKED` and the gate not to be `FROZEN`, and — from `CONFIRMED` only — requires `duty_status` to be `PENDING_BRIEF` or `BRIEFED`, so a guard already on post cannot be cancelled off the roster. Either way `assignment_status` becomes `CANCELLED` and `cancellation_reason` is stored from the body. Cancelling a `TENTATIVE` row moves no money, because such a row was never committed. Cancelling a `CONFIRMED` row runs the compensating cascade: hop one lowers the post's `filled_guard_count` by one and re-derives the post's `status`, which typically means `FILLED → UNDERSTAFFED` once the plan is `ACTIVATED` or `LOCKED`; hop two re-derives the plan's `coverage_ratio`, `understaffed_post_count` and `readiness_posture`, which typically means `READY → BLOCKED` once `coverage_ratio` falls below 0.90; hop three re-derives the event's `gate_posture`, which typically means `OPEN → RESTRICTED`. The compensating ledger sync subtracts the row's `line_cost_cents` from `committed_cost_cents` and its `scheduled_hours` from `committed_guard_hours`, then recomputes `budget_remaining_cents`.

**Voiding** is never requested by a client. It is the system's response to a guard leaving `VERIFIED` — through a credential rejection, the clock-driven expiry sweep, or a vendor suspension or termination — and it applies to rows whose guard now has a `verification_status` of `REJECTED`, `EXPIRED` or `PENDING_REVIEW` after the triggering transition. A `TENTATIVE` row simply becomes `VOIDED` with `cancellation_reason` set to `CREDENTIAL_NOT_CURRENT`, with no ledger movement. A `CONFIRMED` row becomes `VOIDED` with the same reason and additionally runs the upward hops — post `filled_guard_count` and `status`, then plan coverage, understaffed count, readiness and ledger, then event `gate_posture` — with the compensating ledger sync subtracting `line_cost_cents` and `scheduled_hours` and recomputing `budget_remaining_cents`.

The plan-level ledger is a straight aggregation over the plan's committed set — the assignments on the plan's posts whose `assignment_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED`. `committed_cost_cents` is the sum of those rows' `line_cost_cents`; because each of those is already a whole-cent value rounded at the assignment level, the plan sums already-rounded integers rather than summing unrounded products and rounding once at the end, which would differ by up to a cent per assignment. `committed_guard_hours` is the sum of those rows' `scheduled_hours`, rounded half-up to two decimal places once, after summing. `budget_remaining_cents` is `budget_cents` minus `committed_cost_cents`. `billed_cost_cents` is the sum of `billed_cost_cents` over just the committed rows whose `duty_status` is `RELIEVED`. The committed set is summed in ascending assignment `created_at` order, tie-broken by ascending post `created_at`.

### 3.7. Event Night: Briefing, Duty Status, Doors and Screening Lanes

Between activation and doors, the event must be briefed. A `PostSupervisor` briefs each confirmed guard individually, moving that row `PENDING_BRIEF → BRIEFED`. Briefing requires the row's `assignment_status` to be `CONFIRMED` and the event's `status` to be `STAFFED` or `BRIEFED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and it requires both a `radio_channel` and a `training_module_code` in the body — the radio communication structure and the event-specific training module are acknowledged together, in one act. Both values are stored on the row along with `briefed_at`, stamped from the logical clock, and the row's `version` increments.

A `SecurityDirector` then briefs the event itself, moving `STAFFED → BRIEFED`. That transition requires the current plan's `status` to be `ACTIVATED`, requires the current plan's `readiness_posture` to be `READY` (else `ERR_PLAN_NOT_READY`, a cross-entity dependency of the event on its plan), requires that every assignment on the current plan's posts whose `assignment_status` is `CONFIRMED` has a `duty_status` other than `PENDING_BRIEF`, and requires the event's `gate_posture` not to be `FROZEN`. On success the event's `status` becomes `BRIEFED` and its `version` increments.

Opening the doors, `BRIEFED → DOORS_OPEN`, is the safety gate of the whole night, and it carries three preconditions plus the gate interlock. The logical clock must have reached at least one hour before the scheduled `doors_open_at`, else `ERR_DOORS_OPEN_TOO_EARLY`. Every post on the current plan whose `post_type` is `EMERGENCY_EXIT` must have a `filled_guard_count` of at least its `required_guard_count` **and** at least that many assignments actually standing there with `duty_status` equal to `ON_POST`, else `ERR_EXIT_POSTS_UNSTAFFED` — a roster on paper is not an exit covered. And the number of the event's screening lanes whose `status` is `OPEN` must be at least the `required_screening_lanes` figure carried on the pinned assessment, else `ERR_SCREENING_CAPACITY_INSUFFICIENT`. On success the event's `status` becomes `DOORS_OPEN`, its `ingress_status` is set to `NORMAL`, and its `version` increments. Starting the event, `DOORS_OPEN → IN_PROGRESS`, requires only that the logical clock have reached `event_start_at`.

**Screening lanes** are planned ahead of the night by a coordinator, who names the `zone_id` the lane sits in — an ingress zone of the event's venue — a `lane_type` drawn from `WALKTHROUGH_MAG`, `HANDHELD_WAND`, `BAG_CHECK` and `EXPRESS_NO_BAG` describing the screening procedure and prohibited-items check run there, a `nominal_throughput_pph` of at least 1 giving the rated patrons per hour, an `operator_capacity` of at least 1 giving the maximum concurrent operators the lane can physically host, and a planned opening window `open_from` to `open_to` with the close strictly after the open. The lane's `event_id` comes from the path. A new lane's `status` is `PLANNED`, its `assigned_operator_count` is 0 and its `effective_throughput_pph` is 0.

A lane's `assigned_operator_count` is the number of assignments on posts whose `lane_id` is this lane where `assignment_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED` and `duty_status` is exactly `ON_POST` — planned, briefed, no-show and relieved operators do not count; only bodies currently at the lane do. `effective_throughput_pph` is 0 whenever the lane's `status` is anything other than `OPEN`. When the lane is `OPEN`, a staffing factor is the lesser of 1 and `assigned_operator_count` divided by `operator_capacity`, so a half-crewed lane runs at half rate and an over-crewed one does not exceed its rating. A prohibited-item drag then applies: 4 percent of patrons trigger a secondary screen, and a secondary screen costs 2.5 times a normal screen, so the drag factor is one minus the product of those two, namely 0.90. `effective_throughput_pph` is `nominal_throughput_pph` multiplied by the staffing factor multiplied by the drag factor, rounded **down** to a whole patron per hour.

Lane state moves `PLANNED → OPEN`, cycles `OPEN ↔ HOLD`, and terminates at `CLOSED` from any of `PLANNED`, `OPEN` or `HOLD`. Opening a lane from `PLANNED` requires the event's `status` to be `BRIEFED`, `DOORS_OPEN` or `IN_PROGRESS` (else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`), requires `assigned_operator_count` of at least 1 (else `ERR_LANE_UNSTAFFED`), and respects the gate interlock; it sets `status` to `OPEN` and re-derives `effective_throughput_pph`. Re-opening from `HOLD` additionally requires the event's `open_critical_incident_count` to be zero and likewise requires at least one assigned operator. Placing a lane on `HOLD` — by a coordinator or director, or as a system cascade — sets `status` to `HOLD` and forces `effective_throughput_pph` to 0. Closing a lane sets `status` to `CLOSED` and forces `effective_throughput_pph` to 0. Every lane transition increments the lane's `version`. Because operator presence drives throughput, a check-in or a relief on a post carrying a non-null `lane_id` re-derives that lane's `assigned_operator_count` and `effective_throughput_pph`.

On the duty dimension of an assignment, **check-in** moves `BRIEFED → ON_POST`. It requires `assignment_status` to be `CONFIRMED`, requires `duty_status` to already be `BRIEFED` (a row still at `PENDING_BRIEF` yields the specific `ERR_GUARD_NOT_BRIEFED` rather than the generic state error), requires the event's `status` to be one of `BRIEFED`, `DOORS_OPEN`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `SUSPENDED`, and requires the logical clock to have reached at least half an hour before the shift's `window_start`. It stamps `check_in_at` from the logical clock and refreshes the lane figures where a `lane_id` applies.

**No-show** is reachable from either `PENDING_BRIEF` or `BRIEFED` and is a supervisor action with identical effects from both origins. It requires `assignment_status` to be `CONFIRMED` and requires the logical clock to have passed the shift's `window_start` by at least 15 minutes of grace. It sets `duty_status` to `NO_SHOW` while deliberately leaving `assignment_status` at `CONFIRMED` — the dimensions are independent, and the shift remains contractually committed. Because a `NO_SHOW` row stops counting toward fill, hop one re-derives the post's `filled_guard_count` and `status`, hop two re-derives the plan's `coverage_ratio`, `understaffed_post_count` and `readiness_posture`, and hop three re-derives the event's `gate_posture`. The plan's `committed_cost_cents` and `committed_guard_hours` are pointedly **not** reduced: the firm still owes for the shift it booked.

**Relief** moves `ON_POST → RELIEVED`, requires `assignment_status` to be `CONFIRMED` and `check_in_at` to be non-null, and stamps `relieved_at` from the logical clock. Relief recomputes the cost on actual delivered time rather than scheduled time: `billed_hours` is the total minutes between `check_in_at` and `relieved_at` divided by 60, rounded half-up to two decimal places, and `billed_cost_cents` is the same three-level cost computation described in §3.6 applied to those billed hours instead of the scheduled ones — the same overtime threshold, the same night-differential determination from the `window_start` hour, the same subcontract markup and admin fee. Relief then cascades on the roster dimension: `assignment_status` moves `CONFIRMED → COMPLETED`, the row's `billed_cost_cents` is added to the plan's `billed_cost_cents`, and the plan's `committed_cost_cents` and `committed_guard_hours` are deliberately left untouched, since a `COMPLETED` row stays inside the committed ledger set. Lane figures for a non-null `lane_id` are refreshed, and the row's `version` increments.

### 3.8. Crowd Density, Incident Response and Ingress Surge Mitigation

Crowd monitoring runs on **DensityReadings**, recorded by a `PostSupervisor`, an `IncidentCommander` or a `SecurityDirector` against a zone of the event's venue. A reading carries the `zone_id` observed, the `observed_at` instant, and the `observed_occupancy` head count, which must be zero or greater; the `event_id` comes from the path and `reported_by_user_id` is stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`. The `observed_at` instant must fall no earlier than two hours before the event's `doors_open_at` and no later than two hours after its `event_end_at`, else `ERR_READING_TIMESTAMP_OUT_OF_RANGE`. Readings are immutable, so a reading carries `id` and `created_at` but neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

Each reading derives five figures. `density_persons_per_sqm` is `observed_occupancy` divided by the zone's `floor_area_sqm`, rounded half-up to three decimal places. `occupancy_ratio` is `observed_occupancy` divided by the zone's `max_safe_occupancy`, likewise to three decimal places. `rolling_mean_persons_per_sqm` is the mean density over a rolling window of the latest three readings for the same event-and-zone pair **including the row being created**: order those readings by `observed_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as tie-break, take the last three of that ordering, average their `density_persons_per_sqm` values, and round the mean half-up to three decimal places once, after averaging and before any classification. Readings for other zones or other events never enter the window.

Classification uses inclusive-at-the-bottom thresholds: a density of 4.000 persons per square metre or more is `CRITICAL`; otherwise 3.200 or more is `RED`; otherwise 2.000 or more is `AMBER`; otherwise `GREEN`. Exactly 4.000 is `CRITICAL` and 3.999 is `RED`. `instantaneous_level` is that classification applied to `density_persons_per_sqm`; `rolling_level` is the same classification applied to `rolling_mean_persons_per_sqm`. Which of the two gates the event is decided by how much history exists: if the count of readings for this event-and-zone pair, counting the one being created, is three or more, then `effective_level` is `rolling_level`; if it is fewer than three, `effective_level` is `instantaneous_level`. The consequence is deliberate and asymmetric — early in the night a single spike immediately drives the event posture, whereas later the same spike observation can leave the event `OPEN` because two calmer prior readings average it down. Recording a reading re-derives the event's `gate_posture`, which is where clause 4 of the posture ladder in §2.7 picks up a recent `CRITICAL` reading.

**Incidents** are reported by a supervisor, commander or director against a zone, carrying the `zone_id`, a `category` from `MEDICAL`, `EJECTION`, `LAW_ENFORCEMENT`, `CROWD_SURGE` and `PROHIBITED_ITEM`, a `severity` from `MINOR`, `MODERATE`, `SEVERE` and `CRITICAL`, and a free-text `description`. On creation the `status` is `REPORTED`, `reported_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `reported_by_user_id` is stamped from `X-User-ID`, and `severity_at_report` captures the severity as first reported. The event's `open_critical_incident_count` and `gate_posture` are then re-derived, so reporting a `CRITICAL` incident freezes the gate immediately.

An `IncidentCommander` triages the incident, moving `REPORTED → TRIAGED` while the event's `status` is `BRIEFED`, `DOORS_OPEN`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `SUSPENDED`. Triage stamps `triaged_at` and computes the two response metrics. `response_minutes` is the total seconds between `reported_at` and `triaged_at` divided by 60, rounded half-up to two decimal places. `sla_breached` is true when `response_minutes` is strictly greater than the budget for the incident's `severity_at_report` — 15 minutes for `MINOR`, 10 for `MODERATE`, 5 for `SEVERE` and 3 for `CRITICAL`. The clock is keyed on `severity_at_report`, never on the possibly-escalated current `severity`, so escalating a minor incident to critical after a slow triage neither creates nor clears a breach. A `response_minutes` exactly equal to the budget is not a breach, because the comparison is strict.

Escalation is a self-transition available from both `REPORTED` and `TRIAGED`, with identical effects from either: it requires the requested `severity` to sit strictly higher in the ordering `MINOR`, `MODERATE`, `SEVERE`, `CRITICAL` than the current one (else `ERR_SEVERITY_NOT_ESCALATING`), overwrites `severity` with the requested value while leaving `severity_at_report` unchanged, re-derives the event's `open_critical_incident_count` and `gate_posture` — an open `CRITICAL` incident forces the posture to `FROZEN` — and increments the incident's `version`.

From `TRIAGED` the protocol **forks into three mutually exclusive handoff branches**, and the branches differ materially in what they touch. A **medical handoff**, `TRIAGED → MEDICAL_HANDOFF`, requires the incident's `category` to be exactly `MEDICAL` (else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`) and requires a `handoff_agency_ref` in the body naming the receiving agency, which is stored. This branch changes nothing else: `ingress_status`, lane statuses and `gate_posture` are all left alone. An **ejection**, `TRIAGED → EJECTION_IN_PROGRESS`, requires the `category` to be `EJECTION` or `PROHIBITED_ITEM` (else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`), requires an `ejection_patron_ref` in the body naming the patron, which is stored, and requires at least one supervisor actually on duty — an assignment on the event's current plan whose post has `post_type` `SUPERVISOR`, whose `assignment_status` is `CONFIRMED` and whose `duty_status` is `ON_POST` — else `ERR_NO_SUPERVISOR_ON_DUTY`. This branch too has no ingress or lane side effects. A **law-enforcement handoff**, `TRIAGED → LE_HANDOFF`, requires either a `category` of `LAW_ENFORCEMENT` or a `severity` of `SEVERE` or `CRITICAL` (else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`), requires a `handoff_agency_ref`, which is stored, and requires the same on-duty supervisor. This is the only branch that reaches ingress, and only at the top of the severity scale: when `severity` is `CRITICAL`, hop one sets the event's `ingress_status` to `HOLD`; hop two, because ingress went to `HOLD`, transitions every lane on the event whose `status` is `OPEN` to `HOLD`, sweeping lanes in ascending `created_at` order; hop three re-derives the event's `gate_posture`, which clause 2 of the posture ladder resolves to `FROZEN`. All three branches increment the incident's `version`.

The three branches join back at `RESOLVED`, which is also reachable directly from `TRIAGED`. Resolution requires a `resolution_note` in the body, sets `status` to `RESOLVED`, stamps `resolved_at` from the logical clock, and re-derives the event's `open_critical_incident_count` and `gate_posture`. Resolving from `LE_HANDOFF` additionally runs the **compensating release**: if the event's `open_critical_incident_count` becomes zero, the event's `ingress_status` is set back to `NORMAL`, and every lane on the event whose `status` is `HOLD` transitions `HOLD → OPEN` provided its `assigned_operator_count` is at least 1 — a lane that lost its crew during the hold stays on hold. The event's `gate_posture` is then re-derived. An incident may instead be closed as unfounded straight from `REPORTED`, which requires a `resolution_note`, sets `status` to `CLOSED_UNFOUNDED`, stamps `resolved_at`, and re-derives the event's `open_critical_incident_count` and `gate_posture`. `RESOLVED` and `CLOSED_UNFOUNDED` are the terminal incident states.

**Ingress surge mitigation** operates on the event's `ingress_status`, which reads `NORMAL`, `METERED` or `HOLD` and is the door-flow control used for queue management. It is set to `NORMAL` when the doors open, forced to `HOLD` by a suspension or a critical law-enforcement handoff, and returned to `NORMAL` by a resumption or by the compensating release above. A director may **suspend** a live event, `IN_PROGRESS → SUSPENDED`, with no preconditions: the suspension sets `status` to `SUSPENDED`, sets `ingress_status` to `HOLD`, transitions every lane on the event whose `status` is `OPEN` to `HOLD`, and thereby drives `gate_posture` to `FROZEN` through clause 2 of the ladder. **Resumption**, `SUSPENDED → IN_PROGRESS`, requires `open_critical_incident_count` to be zero: it sets `status` back to `IN_PROGRESS`, sets `ingress_status` back to `NORMAL`, transitions every lane whose `status` is `HOLD` back to `OPEN`, and re-derives `gate_posture`. The suspend-resume cycle may repeat any number of times, and every pass increments the event's `version`.

### 3.9. Close-Out, Debrief and the After-Action Review

A director closes the event with `IN_PROGRESS → CLOSED`, which requires only that the logical clock have reached `event_end_at`. Closing is the largest single cascade in the system. Every assignment on the current plan whose `duty_status` is `ON_POST` is relieved — `ON_POST → RELIEVED` with `relieved_at` stamped from the logical clock — and its roster dimension completes, `CONFIRMED → COMPLETED`. Every screening lane on the event whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `OPEN` or `HOLD` moves to `CLOSED`. The current staffing plan moves `LOCKED → CLOSED`, and if it was still `ACTIVATED` it first moves `ACTIVATED → LOCKED` and then to `CLOSED`. Every post on the plan moves to `CLOSED`. The event's own `status` becomes `CLOSED` and its `version` increments, as do the versions of every row the cascade touched.

An `IncidentCommander` or director then opens an **AfterActionReview** on the closed event, supplying a `narrative` body and a `corrective_action_count` of zero or more; `event_id` comes from the path and `authored_by_user_id` is stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`. Only one non-`FINALIZED` review may exist per event at a time, so a second attempt while one is still open is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_EXISTS`. A new review starts in `DRAFT` with `return_reason` and `finalized_by_user_id` null, `total_incident_count`, `sla_breach_count`, `coverage_shortfall_minutes` and `no_show_rate` at zero, and `peak_density_level` at `GREEN`.

Submission by the commander moves `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and requires the event's `status` to be `CLOSED` and every incident on the event to be in `RESOLVED` or `CLOSED_UNFOUNDED`; either failure is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Submission computes the review's five metrics. `total_incident_count` is the number of the event's incidents whose `status` is not `CLOSED_UNFOUNDED` — unfounded reports are excluded from the count entirely. `sla_breach_count` is how many of that same set have `sla_breached` true. `peak_density_level` is the highest `effective_level` across every density reading for the event, ranked `GREEN` below `AMBER` below `RED` below `CRITICAL`, and is `GREEN` when the event has no readings at all. `no_show_rate` is computed over the roster of the current plan — the assignments on that plan's posts whose `assignment_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED`, with `TENTATIVE`, `CANCELLED` and `VOIDED` rows excluded — as the count of rows whose `duty_status` is `NO_SHOW` divided by the count of the whole roster, rounded half-up to four decimal places, and is exactly 0.0000 when that roster is empty. `coverage_shortfall_minutes` measures unstaffed post-minutes: for each post on the current plan, take its `required_guard_count` minus the number of roster rows on that post whose `duty_status` is `ON_POST` or `RELIEVED`, clamp that shortfall at zero so an over-covered post contributes nothing, multiply by the post's coverage window length in minutes (the total seconds between its `window_start` and `window_end` divided by 60), sum across the plan's posts traversed in ascending `created_at` order, and round the single total half-up to two decimal places.

A director then returns or finalizes the review, and in both cases the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `authored_by_user_id`. A return moves `SUBMITTED → RETURNED`, requires a `return_reason` in the body, and stores it. The commander revises a returned review back to `DRAFT`, which clears `return_reason` to null; that `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → RETURNED → DRAFT` loop may repeat. Finalization moves `SUBMITTED → FINALIZED`, stamps `finalized_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, recomputes all five metrics a second time, and cascades the event `CLOSED → DEBRIEFED` with the event's `version` incremented. `FINALIZED` is terminal for a review and `DEBRIEFED` is terminal for an event.
