# Recruitment Marketing & Employer Brand Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Talent acquisition marketing teams operate a strange hybrid business: half of it is a media agency buying paid inventory against a fiscal-year budget, and half of it is a regulated publisher whose advertisements are legally binding statements about pay, benefits and equal opportunity. The team we are building for today runs both halves on spreadsheets. Persona research lives in one survey tool, career-site copy in a CMS, job-board and social spend in three vendor consoles, employer-review responses in a shared inbox, and campus-event logistics in a calendar. Nobody can answer the two questions that actually matter — *what did a qualified applicant cost us on this channel for this job family*, and *is every live advertisement currently cleared for compliance* — without a week of manual reconciliation.

This document specifies a single backend service that consolidates the whole program. It models the employer brand **Program** as the fiscal container that holds the paid-media pools and the annual nurture send quota; **job families** as the budgeting and reporting unit that carries a cost-per-qualified-applicant target; **candidate personas** backed by auditable channel-preference research; **job postings** that carry two independent status dimensions, one for editorial content and one for advertising compliance; **media flights** that are priced, funded through a strict budget waterfall, and paced against measured cost-per-qualified-applicant; **applications** and their **touchpoints**, which together produce multi-touch source-of-hire attribution; a consent-managed **talent community** with frequency-capped nurture campaigns; **employer reviews** with rating-driven response SLAs that feed a program-level brand health score; and **event presences** that compete with paid media for the same money.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, money is never implicit: every reservation and every release is written to an append-only budget ledger, and program-level and family-level availability are always derivable by replaying that ledger. Second, time is never ambiguous: the service runs on an explicit server-side logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so deadlines, decay windows and frequency caps are exactly reproducible. Engineers implementing this specification should treat the ledger and the logical clock as the two invariants that all other rules hang from.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request that is not explicitly open to the public carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting as. There are exactly eight roles in the access matrix, spelled precisely as follows: `BrandProgramLead`, `PersonaResearcher`, `ComplianceReviewer`, `MediaBuyer`, `CommunityManager`, `RecruitingOps`, `Candidate`, and `Anonymous`.

Their remits divide the program along segregation-of-duties lines. A `BrandProgramLead` owns the program itself — funding pools, job families, channels, career-site content, and the persona approval decision. A `PersonaResearcher` runs persona development and channel-preference research, drafting personas and retiring stale ones. A `ComplianceReviewer` is the independent gate for advertising accuracy, equal-opportunity statements and legal sign-off on employer-review responses. A `MediaBuyer` allocates paid media across job boards, social and programmatic inventory, records delivered spend, and coordinates event and campus presence. A `CommunityManager` runs the talent community's nurture campaigns and owns employer-review monitoring, triage and response drafting. `RecruitingOps` operates the application funnel — screening, qualification decisions, attribution ingestion — and owns the logical clock. A `Candidate` is a person who joined the talent community. `Anonymous` is an unauthenticated career-site visitor.

Identity semantics differ across the eight, and implementers must not conflate them. When `X-User-Role` is `Candidate`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `TalentCommunityMember`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `TalentCommunityMember` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, which is exactly how a consent change is bound to the member's own record. For all seven other roles — `BrandProgramLead`, `PersonaResearcher`, `ComplianceReviewer`, `MediaBuyer`, `CommunityManager`, `RecruitingOps`, and `Anonymous` — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records such identifiers where the workflow demands an audit trail — as `drafted_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` on a persona, as `optimized_by_user_id` and `cleared_by_user_id` on a posting, and as `drafted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on an employer review — but it never compares them to any entity's `id`.

Authentication and authorization failures are reported through two codes. A request that is missing `X-User-ID` or `X-User-Role`, that supplies either header empty, or that names a role outside the eight above, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) — but only on endpoints whose authorized-role list does not include `Anonymous`. Endpoints that do list `Anonymous` accept header-less requests. A request whose headers are well formed but whose role is not among the endpoint's authorized roles is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). A `Candidate` who attempts a consent change on a member record other than their own is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Finally, wherever a two-person rule applies, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the identifier that performed the paired upstream step, and a violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403) — the three guarded pairings are enumerated in §2.11.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection read accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` that is not an integer inside the inclusive range of 1 through 100, or an `offset` that is not an integer of zero or greater, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Successful list responses populate four pagination keys inside `meta`: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values applied to the query, `total_count` reports how many rows matched the filters before the page window was applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window.

The default ordering for every list endpoint is `created_at` ascending. A handful of collections carry a domain-natural ordering instead, and in each of those the listed ordering is normative: job families are ordered by `priority_rank` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; channel-preference signals by `collected_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending; media flights by `start_date` ascending then `created_at` ascending; touchpoints by `occurred_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending — deliberately the same order the attribution engine walks; talent-community members by `created_at` ascending then `email` ascending, again matching the order a nurture dispatch evaluates them in; nurture send-evaluation rows by `sequence_no` ascending; employer reviews by `posted_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending; event presences by `start_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending; and budget ledger entries by `sequence_no` ascending.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource the service persists is addressed by a server-minted `id`, formatted as a UUID with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`; they supply foreign keys that reference ids already minted. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits.

Beyond that, resources split into two families. Mutable resources — `Program`, `JobFamily`, `Channel`, `CandidatePersona`, `JobPosting`, `MediaFlight`, `Application`, `TalentCommunityMember`, `NurtureCampaign`, `EmployerReview` and `EventPresence` — additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same ISO 8601 UTC format and an integer `version`. On creation, `version` starts at 1 and `updated_at` equals `created_at`. Thereafter, every mutation that the service accepts refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version` by exactly 1, including mutations that a cascade performed rather than the caller requesting directly. Immutable event rows — `ChannelPreferenceSignal`, `Touchpoint`, `NurtureSend` and `BudgetLedgerEntry` — carry only `id` and `created_at`: for these four entity kinds `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the resource entirely, because the rows are written once and never edited.

Time in this system is a server-controlled logical clock, not the host's wall clock. Before any clock-setting call has been made, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time under any circumstance. `RecruitingOps` may move the clock forward or backward by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting capability, which is the mechanism the test harness uses to age reviews, expire flights and advance decay windows. Moving the clock is not a passive act: it re-evaluates `sla_status` on every `EmployerReview` — a review still sitting in an unresolved state whose `response_due_at` the clock has now passed flips from `PENDING` to `BREACHED` — and it recomputes the brand health score of every `Program`, because both the ninety-day review window and the per-review recency weights have shifted underneath. That pass writes the stored SLA verdict and nothing else: notwithstanding the audit rule above, it neither refreshes `updated_at` nor advances `version` on any `EmployerReview` or any `Program`, however many verdicts and scores it moves.

### 2.5. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this API are integer cents. There are no floating-point money fields, no currency codes, and no sub-cent amounts. All attribution and channel-mix shares are integer basis points, where ten thousand basis points represents one whole unit of credit.

Three rounding behaviours appear, and using the wrong one produces off-by-one divergences that ripple through the ledger. The volume discount on a media flight is **floored** — the fractional cent is discarded. The platform fee on a media flight, the blended cost-per-qualified-applicant target of a channel, the measured cost-per-qualified-applicant of a channel, and the pacing threshold derived from that target are all **rounded half up** to whole cents. The program's `brand_health_score` is the only non-integer stored value: it is a number rounded half up to exactly two decimal places and clamped to the inclusive interval from `0.00` to `100.00`. Conversion rate in basis points and the persona confidence percentage are **floored** to integers.

Where a formula divides, the division happens once, at the position the formula pins it to, and the rounding happens once, immediately after that division. Intermediate per-item terms are accumulated as exact values and are never rounded individually. Where a formula distributes a fixed total of ten thousand basis points across several rows, the distribution uses floor-then-largest-remainder: each row's exact share is floored, the shortfall between the sum of floors and ten thousand is counted, and that many single basis points are awarded to the rows with the largest fractional parts. Rounding each row's exact share half up independently is explicitly wrong — it can total 9,999 or 10,001 — and any implementation that does so will disagree with the specification.

### 2.6. The Budget Waterfall and the Ledger of Record

A program's paid-media money sits in five named buckets. Four of them live on the `Program`: `pool_job_board_cents`, `pool_social_cents` and `pool_programmatic_cents` are channel-type pools, each usable only by media on the matching channel family, and `flex_reserve_cents` is a last-resort bucket usable by any channel type and by event presences. The fifth is per-family: each `JobFamily` carries `dedicated_budget_cents`, money earmarked for that family alone. Whenever money must be committed, it is drawn through a **waterfall** — a strict, non-revisiting bucket order — and the draw is all-or-nothing.

For a media flight the bucket order is: the family-dedicated bucket first, then exactly one channel-type pool chosen by the channel's `channel_type` (the job-board pool when the type is `JOB_BOARD`, the social pool when it is `SOCIAL`, the programmatic pool when it is `PROGRAMMATIC`), then the flex reserve. For an event presence the bucket order is the family-dedicated bucket followed directly by the flex reserve — an event presence never touches a channel-type pool. Availability in the family-dedicated bucket is that family's `dedicated_budget_cents` less its `reserved_cents`. Availability in a channel-type pool is the corresponding program pool amount less the sum of `delta_cents` across every ledger entry of that program whose `bucket` is that pool, and availability in the flex reserve is computed the same way against `flex_reserve_cents`. Because release entries carry negative deltas, availability restores itself automatically as reservations unwind.

The walk itself is mechanical. Starting with the full amount still needed, each bucket in order contributes the smaller of the amount still needed and that bucket's availability, and never a negative amount; if a bucket's availability is zero or negative it contributes nothing and the walk moves on. Each positive contribution reduces the amount still needed. If the walk finishes the last bucket with any amount still needed, the whole request is rejected with `ERR_MEDIA_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409) and **nothing whatsoever is persisted** — no partial reservation, no partial ledger entry, no state change.

When the walk succeeds, one `BudgetLedgerEntry` is written per contributing bucket, in bucket order, with ascending `sequence_no`. `sequence_no` is a 1-based, monotonically increasing position of the entry inside its own program's ledger, and it is the deterministic replay order for budget reconciliation. Every entry names its `program_id` and its `job_family_id`, the `bucket` it moved, an `entry_type` of `RESERVE` for a positive `delta_cents` or `RELEASE` for a negative one, a `source_type` of `MEDIA_FLIGHT` or `EVENT_PRESENCE`, and a `source_id` that is polymorphically resolved by that `source_type` — the flight's `id` when the source type is `MEDIA_FLIGHT`, the presence's `id` when it is `EVENT_PRESENCE`. Each entry also carries a `reason` drawn from `FLIGHT_RESERVATION`, `FLIGHT_CANCELLED`, `FLIGHT_COMPLETED`, `FLIGHT_PACING_THROTTLE`, `FLIGHT_COMPLIANCE_SUSPENSION`, `EVENT_RESERVATION` or `EVENT_CANCELLED`, so the ledger explains itself without joining back to the source object.

Releases are always **mirrored**, never recomputed. When a reservation unwinds, the service walks that source's outstanding `RESERVE` entries in ascending `sequence_no` order and writes one `RELEASE` entry per original, reproducing the original `bucket` and negating the original `delta_cents`. This guarantees that money returns to precisely the buckets it came from, even if availability has shifted in the meantime. The two program-level aggregates follow directly: `committed_budget_cents` is the sum of `delta_cents` over every ledger entry of that program, and `available_budget_cents` is the sum of `pool_job_board_cents`, `pool_social_cents`, `pool_programmatic_cents`, `flex_reserve_cents` and the `dedicated_budget_cents` of every `JobFamily` belonging to that program, less `committed_budget_cents`. At the family level, `reserved_cents` is the sum of `delta_cents` over that family's entries whose `bucket` is `FAMILY_DEDICATED`, and `dedicated_available_cents` is `dedicated_budget_cents` less `reserved_cents`.

### 2.7. Version Guards on Budget and Content Writes

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because in both cases acting on a stale read would silently destroy work the client never saw.

Reserving a media flight requires the caller to supply `expected_program_version`, a non-optional field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the funding `Program`. If that value does not match the program's current `version`, the reservation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard exists precisely because the product *does* allow two flights to draw from the same waterfall buckets: without it, a second reservation built on an older read of the program would succeed against a budget picture the buyer never observed. The guard forces the buyer to re-read the program's remaining budget before committing further money. Note that program funding edits, flight reservations, flight activations and releases, and event-presence confirmations and cancellations all advance `Program.version`, so any of them invalidates a previously fetched version number.

Re-optimizing a job posting requires the caller to supply `expected_posting_version`, a non-optional field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `JobPosting`. If that value does not match the posting's current `version`, the optimization is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POSTING_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Each optimization legitimately supersedes the previous revision and resets compliance clearance, so an optimization built on a stale copy would discard a newer revision without trace; the guard converts that into a 412 the client resolves by re-reading the posting.

No other write in this API takes a version field. Activating, resuming, completing or cancelling a flight, publishing or archiving a posting, recording a compliance decision, driving the application funnel, changing consent, dispatching a campaign, handling an employer review and managing an event presence are all unguarded in this sense and must not require or accept a version input.

### 2.8. Resource Addressing and Foreign-Key Resolution

Every entity in this system is a peer collection mounted at the URL root; there are no nested resource paths. Parentage is expressed exclusively through foreign-key fields carried in request bodies — a persona names its job family, a flight names its channel, family and posting, a touchpoint names its application, channel and optionally its flight — never through URL segments. A request that supplies a path identifier resolves that identifier before anything else about the request body is considered, and where a request carries several identifiers they are resolved strictly left to right in the path and then in the order the fields appear in the endpoint's input list.

Each entity kind reports its own not-found code, and the code is chosen by the *kind of thing that failed to resolve*, whether it failed as a path segment or as a body foreign key. A program identifier that does not resolve yields `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`; a job family identifier — including `interest_job_family_id` on a talent-community member — yields `ERR_JOB_FAMILY_NOT_FOUND`; a channel identifier yields `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_FOUND`; a persona identifier yields `ERR_PERSONA_NOT_FOUND`; a posting identifier, including `job_posting_id`, yields `ERR_POSTING_NOT_FOUND`; a flight identifier, including `media_flight_id`, yields `ERR_FLIGHT_NOT_FOUND`; an application identifier, including `application_id`, yields `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`; a member identifier, including `member_id`, yields `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`; a campaign identifier, including `campaign_id`, yields `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`; an employer-review identifier yields `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`; an event-presence identifier yields `ERR_PRESENCE_NOT_FOUND`. The four immutable event collections — channel-preference signals, touchpoints, nurture send-evaluation rows and budget ledger entries — share a single code, `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, when the addressed row does not exist. All eleven of these codes carry HTTP 404.

### 2.9. Request Admission Order

Several checks frequently apply to the same request, and the order in which they are evaluated is part of the contract: a caller must be able to fix one problem at a time and see the next one surface. The service therefore evaluates checks in the following fixed priority order, stopping at the first failure. Text order in the rest of this document is *not* an ordering signal; this list is.

1. On every endpoint that does not list `Anonymous`, both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` must be present and non-empty, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. `X-User-Role` must name one of the eight roles in the access matrix, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
3. The acting role must appear in the called endpoint's authorized-role list, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
4. On every write endpoint, the request body must conform to the endpoint's input schema — required fields present, types correct, enum members valid, integer ranges respected, ISO 8601 datetimes well formed — else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422).
5. On every list endpoint, `limit` must be an integer from 1 through 100 and `offset` an integer of zero or more, else `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).
6. Every path identifier must resolve, left to right, with the per-kind codes of §2.8 (404).
7. Every foreign key supplied in a request body must resolve to an existing row of its declared parent entity, in the order the fields appear in the endpoint's input list, with the same per-kind codes (404).
8. A `Candidate` may act only on the `TalentCommunityMember` whose `id` equals the acting `X-User-ID`, on the consent-change operation, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
9. Segregation of duties on the three paired steps of §2.11, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
10. Scoped uniqueness on job-family creation, talent-community registration and employer-review ingestion, with the three codes of §2.11 (409).
11. `expected_program_version` must equal the funding program's current `version` on flight reservation, else `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` (412).
12. `expected_posting_version` must equal the posting's current `version` on optimization, else `ERR_STALE_POSTING_VERSION` (412).
13. A `CandidatePersona` must be in the state the requested transition departs from — submission requires `DRAFT` (`ERR_PERSONA_NOT_DRAFT`), a review decision requires `IN_REVIEW` (`ERR_PERSONA_NOT_IN_REVIEW`), retirement requires `APPROVED` (`ERR_PERSONA_NOT_APPROVED`) — all 409.
14. A posting's `content_status` must permit the requested content transition — optimization requires `DRAFT` or `OPTIMIZED` (`ERR_POSTING_NOT_OPTIMIZABLE`), publication requires `OPTIMIZED` or `SUSPENDED` (`ERR_POSTING_NOT_PUBLISHABLE`), archiving requires a posting that is not already `ARCHIVED` (`ERR_POSTING_ALREADY_ARCHIVED`) — all 409.
15. A posting's `compliance_status` must permit the requested compliance decision — a `CLEAR` decision requires `UNREVIEWED` or `FLAGGED` (`ERR_POSTING_ALREADY_CLEARED`), a `FLAG` decision requires `UNREVIEWED` or `CLEARED` (`ERR_POSTING_ALREADY_FLAGGED`) — both 409.
16. A flight's `state` must permit the requested transition — activation requires `RESERVED` (`ERR_FLIGHT_NOT_RESERVED`), resumption requires `PAUSED_PACING` or `SUSPENDED_CONTENT` (`ERR_FLIGHT_NOT_RESUMABLE`), completion requires `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED_PACING` (`ERR_FLIGHT_NOT_COMPLETABLE`), cancellation requires a non-terminal flight and spend recording requires `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED_PACING`, both rejecting a `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` flight with `ERR_FLIGHT_ALREADY_TERMINAL` — all 409.
17. An application's `status` must permit the requested transition — submission requires `STARTED` (`ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_STARTED`), screening requires `SUBMITTED` (`ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_SUBMITTED`), a `QUALIFIED` decision requires `SCREENED` (`ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_SCREENED`), a `DISQUALIFIED` decision requires `SCREENED` or `QUALIFIED` (`ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_DECIDABLE`) — all 409.
18. A member's `consent_status` must permit the requested action — confirmation requires `PENDING_CONFIRMATION` (`ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_RESOLVED`), a `PAUSE` action requires `SUBSCRIBED` and a `RESUME` action requires `PAUSED` (`ERR_CONSENT_TRANSITION_INVALID`), and an `UNSUBSCRIBE` action requires a member who is not already `UNSUBSCRIBED` (`ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_RESOLVED`) — all 409.
19. A campaign's `state` must permit the requested transition — scheduling requires `DRAFT` (`ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_DRAFT`), dispatch requires `SCHEDULED` (`ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_SCHEDULED`), cancellation requires `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED` (`ERR_CAMPAIGN_ALREADY_TERMINAL`) — all 409.
20. An employer review's `state` must permit the requested transition — triage requires `NEW` (`ERR_REVIEW_NOT_NEW`), drafting requires `TRIAGED` or `DRAFTING` (`ERR_REVIEW_NOT_DRAFTABLE`), submission to legal requires `DRAFTING` (`ERR_REVIEW_NOT_DRAFTING`), a legal decision requires `LEGAL_REVIEW` (`ERR_REVIEW_NOT_IN_LEGAL_REVIEW`), closing requires `TRIAGED` (`ERR_REVIEW_NOT_CLOSABLE`) — all 409.
21. An event presence's `state` must permit the requested transition — confirmation requires `PLANNED` (`ERR_PRESENCE_NOT_PLANNED`), completion requires `CONFIRMED` (`ERR_PRESENCE_NOT_CONFIRMED`), cancellation requires `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED` (`ERR_PRESENCE_ALREADY_TERMINAL`) — all 409.
22. The supplied `consent_token` must equal the member's stored `consent_token`, else `ERR_CONSENT_TOKEN_INVALID` (422).
23. `eeo_statement_included` must be true before a compliance `CLEAR` decision and before publication, else `ERR_EEO_STATEMENT_MISSING` (409).
24. When `superlative_claim_included` is true, `substantiation_url` must be present before a compliance `CLEAR` decision, else `ERR_UNSUBSTANTIATED_CLAIM` (409).
25. `compliance_status` must be `CLEARED` before publishing a posting, before activating or resuming a flight pointing at it, and before an application on it is submitted, else `ERR_POSTING_COMPLIANCE_NOT_CLEARED` (409).
26. At least one member of a dispatch audience must be `SUBSCRIBED`, else `ERR_NO_CONSENTED_AUDIENCE` (409).
27. `brand_health_score` must be at least `55.00` before a flight may be activated, else `ERR_BRAND_HEALTH_GATE` (409).
28. At least one persona of the posting's job family must be `APPROVED` before publication, else `ERR_NO_APPROVED_PERSONA` (409).
29. Retirement of the last `APPROVED` persona of a family is refused while that family still has a posting whose `content_status` is `PUBLISHED` or `SUSPENDED`, with `ERR_PERSONA_IN_USE` (409).
30. A paid flight's channel must have a `channel_type` of `JOB_BOARD`, `SOCIAL` or `PROGRAMMATIC`, else `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_PAYABLE` (409).
31. A posting's `content_status` must be `PUBLISHED` before a flight on it is activated or resumed and before an application on it is submitted, else `ERR_POSTING_NOT_PUBLISHED` (409).
32. `optimization_score` must be at least 70 before publication, else `ERR_OPTIMIZATION_SCORE_TOO_LOW` (409).
33. `pay_range_min_cents` and `pay_range_max_cents` must both be present, and the ceiling must be greater than or equal to the floor, on optimization and on publication, else `ERR_PAY_RANGE_INVALID` (409).
34. `flight_days` must be at least the channel's `min_flight_days`, else `ERR_FLIGHT_TOO_SHORT` (409).
35. At least one touchpoint of the application must fall inside the attribution lookback window when qualifying, else `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TOUCHPOINT` (409).
36. A persona's `signal_count` inside its research window must be at least 1, both when it is submitted for review and when a review decision is recorded on it, else `ERR_NO_RESEARCH_SIGNALS` (409).
37. A persona's `mix_confidence_pct` must be at least 40 when it is submitted for review, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RESEARCH_CONFIDENCE` (409).
38. Closing an employer review without a response requires a `rating` of at least 4, else `ERR_RESPONSE_REQUIRED` (409).
39. A `response_body` must already be stored before the response is sent to legal review, else `ERR_RESPONSE_BODY_MISSING` (409).
40. No other non-terminal flight on the same channel and job family may have a flight window intersecting the requested one, else `ERR_FLIGHT_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409).
41. Every other confirmed or completed presence of the same job family must be separated from the requested one by the recruiter-team buffer, else `ERR_EVENT_BUFFER_CONFLICT` (409).
42. `scheduled_for` must be at or after the logical clock when a campaign is scheduled, else `ERR_SCHEDULE_IN_PAST` (409).
43. The logical clock must be at or after `scheduled_for` when a campaign is dispatched, else `ERR_DISPATCH_TOO_EARLY` (409).
44. The logical clock must have reached the flight's `end_date` at midnight UTC before completion, else `ERR_FLIGHT_WINDOW_OPEN` (409).
45. The logical clock must be strictly after a presence's `end_at` before completion, else `ERR_EVENT_WINDOW_OPEN` (409).
46. The channel's `pacing_status` must not be `THROTTLED` when a flight on it is resumed, else `ERR_CHANNEL_THROTTLED` (409).
47. The requested `daily_cap_cents` plus the daily caps of every intersecting non-terminal flight on the same channel must not exceed the channel's `daily_capacity_cents`, else `ERR_CHANNEL_DAILY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
48. The budget waterfall must fund the requested amount in full, else `ERR_MEDIA_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` (409).
49. A campaign's `max_sends` must not exceed the program's remaining send quota, else `ERR_SEND_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` (409).
50. Recorded spend must not push a flight's `spent_cents` above its `total_cost_cents`, else `ERR_SPEND_EXCEEDS_RESERVATION` (409).

The practical consequence worth internalising is that authentication precedes authorization, authorization precedes schema validation, schema validation precedes existence, existence precedes ownership and two-person rules, those precede uniqueness, uniqueness precedes the version guards, the version guards precede state-machine gating, and every domain-specific business rule — compliance, quotas, temporal windows, capacity, budget — is evaluated only after the target's state has been confirmed to permit the transition at all.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Counter Propagation

Several single operations in this system mutate five or six entities. Three rules govern all of them.

**Cascades are synchronous and complete within the originating request.** There is no deferred queue and no eventual reconciliation. When a compliance flag suspends a posting, the posting, its flights, those flights' channels, the program and the ledger are all consistent by the time the response is written. Every entity a cascade mutates has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by exactly 1, and this applies as much to a channel touched incidentally as to the resource the caller addressed.

**Channel occupancy is a maintained counter, not a query.** `active_flight_count` on a `Channel` is the number of flights on that channel whose `state` is `ACTIVE`. It rises by one whenever a flight enters `ACTIVE` and falls by one whenever a flight leaves `ACTIVE`, whether it left by pausing for pacing, by suspension for content, by completion or by cancellation.

**`THROTTLED` outranks `IDLE` inside a single request.** When a flight departure drives a channel's `active_flight_count` to zero, the channel's `pacing_status` becomes `IDLE` and `idle_since` records the logical-clock instant at which that happened — *but only if* the pacing calculation did not already set that channel to `THROTTLED` earlier in the same request. A channel that was throttled and whose last active flight was then paused *because* of that throttle stays `THROTTLED`, and its `idle_since` stays null. This precedence rule applies identically to every path that removes an active flight: the pacing-throttle pause, the compliance suspension, flight completion and flight cancellation. Conversely, entering or re-entering `ACTIVE` clears `idle_since` back to null.

### 2.11. Two-Person Rules and Scoped Uniqueness

Three steps in this program are deliberately split between two humans, and in each case the service compares the acting `X-User-ID` against the identifier stored by the paired upstream step. A persona review decision is refused when the acting identifier equals the persona's `drafted_by_user_id`. A posting compliance decision — `CLEAR` or `FLAG` alike — is refused when the acting identifier equals the posting's `optimized_by_user_id`. A legal decision on an employer review response — `APPROVE` or `REJECT` alike — is refused when the acting identifier equals the review's `drafted_by_user_id`. All three refusals carry `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), and all three are evaluated before any state-machine or business rule on the same request.

Three uniqueness constraints are scoped rather than global. A `JobFamily`'s `code` must be unique within its program, and a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FAMILY_CODE`. A `TalentCommunityMember`'s `email` must be unique within its program, and a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_MEMBER_EMAIL`. An `EmployerReview`'s `external_ref` must be unique within its `platform`, and a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REVIEW_REF`. All three carry HTTP 409. Note the scoping carefully: the same family code in two different programs is legal, as is the same external reference on two different review platforms.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Program and Its Job Families

Everything begins with a `BrandProgramLead` creating the fiscal container. Registering a program takes a `name` between 1 and 160 characters, a required `fiscal_year` that must be an integer from 2000 through 2100 identifying the year the media budget belongs to, and five optional funding and quota figures. Four of those are the money buckets already described — `pool_job_board_cents`, `pool_social_cents`, `pool_programmatic_cents` and `flex_reserve_cents` — each an integer of zero or more cents defaulting to 0. The fifth is `send_quota_total`, the number of nurture messages the program may dispatch across the fiscal year, also an integer of zero or more defaulting to 0. The lead may also nominate an `attribution_model` of either `LINEAR` or `POSITION_BASED`; when the field is omitted the program is created with `LINEAR`.

A freshly created program is initialised to a known zero point: `committed_budget_cents` is 0, `send_quota_used` is 0, `brand_health_score` is `100.00`, `last_attribution_recompute_at` is null because no attribution run has happened yet, and `available_budget_cents` equals the sum of the three channel-type pools plus the flex reserve. Later on, a lead may adjust the program's funding, its attribution model or its send quota; each such adjustment recomputes `available_budget_cents` from the standing definition in §2.6, refreshes `updated_at` and advances `version` by 1. That version advance is not incidental — it is exactly the signal the media-flight reservation guard reads, so a funding edit invalidates every buyer's cached view of the budget.

Job families are the unit both the budget and the reporting hang off. Creating one names its funding program, a display `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a short `code` of 2 to 12 uppercase characters unique within that program, a `cpqa_target_cents` of at least 1 cent expressing the target cost per qualified applicant for that family, and a `priority_rank` of at least 1 that must be unique within the program — a lower rank wins wherever families of one program must be ordered deterministically. A family may also carry `dedicated_budget_cents`, an integer of zero or more cents defaulting to 0, which becomes the first bucket of the waterfall for anything that family funds. A new family starts with `reserved_cents` at 0 and `dedicated_available_cents` equal to its `dedicated_budget_cents`.

### 3.2. Channels and the Research Behind a Persona

Channels are the inventory surfaces the program markets on. Registering one names the owning program, a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, and a `channel_type` chosen from `JOB_BOARD`, `SOCIAL`, `PROGRAMMATIC`, `CAREER_SITE`, `EVENT` and `EMAIL`. Only the first three of those may ever carry paid media; the other three exist so that organic, event and email touches can be attributed to a real channel record. Two optional operational limits complete the registration: `daily_capacity_cents`, an integer of zero or more cents defaulting to 0 that ceilings the combined daily spend of intersecting flights on the channel, and `min_flight_days`, an integer of at least 1 defaulting to 1 that sets the shortest flight the channel will accept. A new channel reports `spent_cents` 0, `credited_qualified_bp` 0, `active_flight_count` 0, `pacing_status` `PACING_UNKNOWN`, `idle_since` null, and both `blended_target_cents` and `cpqa_cents` null.

A `PersonaResearcher` drafts a persona against a job family, giving it a `name` of 1 to 120 characters. Two research parameters shape how the persona's channel mix will be computed. `mix_method` selects the weighting variant and is either `SURVEY_WEIGHTED` or `ENGAGEMENT_WEIGHTED`, defaulting to `SURVEY_WEIGHTED`. `research_window_days` is the signal recency window, an integer of at least 1 defaulting to 180. A new persona is in state `DRAFT` with an empty `channel_mix` array, `mix_confidence_pct` 0, `signal_count` 0, `revision_count` 0, `reviewed_by_user_id` and `last_review_note` null, and `drafted_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID` — the value the later two-person rule will compare against.

Research arrives one signal at a time. Each `ChannelPreferenceSignal` is submitted with a payload naming the persona it informs as `persona_id` and the channel it scores as `channel_id`, and carries a `preference_score` from 0 through 100 taken from the research instrument, a `sample_size` of at least 1 respondent, an optional `engagement_events` count of zero or more observed interactions defaulting to 0, and a `collected_at` instant. Recording a signal immediately recomputes the persona's channel mix, overwriting `channel_mix`, `mix_confidence_pct` and `signal_count`, and refreshes the persona's `updated_at` while advancing its `version` by 1. Signals themselves are immutable once written.

**How the channel mix is computed.** The calculation starts by selecting the in-window signals of the persona: those whose `collected_at` is strictly later than the logical clock minus the persona's `research_window_days` and no later than the logical clock itself. The lower bound is exclusive, the upper inclusive — a signal collected at the very instant the window opens is outside it, and one collected at the current instant is inside. `signal_count` is the number of signals so selected. If that count is zero, `channel_mix` becomes an empty array and `mix_confidence_pct` becomes 0, and the calculation stops there.

Otherwise each selected signal receives a weight, and this is where the two variants diverge. Under `SURVEY_WEIGHTED`, a signal's weight is simply its `sample_size`, with no time decay at all — the practical consequence being that an old survey with a large respondent base can dominate the mix indefinitely. Under `ENGAGEMENT_WEIGHTED`, the signal's age in whole days is the elapsed time from `collected_at` to the logical clock divided by 86,400 seconds and rounded down; a decay percentage is then 100 less 10 for each complete 30-day step of that age, with the number of complete steps obtained by rounding the age in days divided by 30 down to a whole number, and the result held to a floor of 10 percent no matter how old the signal is; the signal's weight is its `engagement_events` multiplied by that decay percentage and divided by 100, kept as an exact value with no rounding. A signal with `engagement_events` of 0 therefore contributes nothing at all under this variant, even if its `preference_score` is 100.

Each channel's raw score is then the sum, over that channel's in-window signals, of the signal's weight multiplied by its `preference_score`, accumulated exactly. Let the grand total be the sum of those raw scores across all channels. When the grand total is zero, `channel_mix` becomes an empty array and `mix_confidence_pct` is still computed as described below — the signals count towards confidence even though they produce no mix. When the grand total is positive, exactly ten thousand basis points are distributed across the channels: each channel's exact share is ten thousand multiplied by that channel's raw score and divided by the grand total, each exact share is rounded down to a whole basis point, the shortfall between the sum of those floors and ten thousand is counted, and one additional basis point is awarded to each of that many channels with the largest fractional parts, ties broken by the channel's `created_at` ascending and then the channel's `name` ascending — never by the channel's `id`. The resulting `channel_mix` is an array of objects each pairing a `channel_id` with its `share_bp`, containing only channels whose raw score was greater than zero, ordered by `share_bp` descending and then by the channel's `created_at` ascending.

Confidence is independent of the mix. `mix_confidence_pct` is the total `sample_size` across all in-window signals divided by 10 and rounded down, then held to a ceiling of 100 — so ten respondents buy one percentage point of confidence, and a thousand respondents saturate the scale. This is the figure the submission gate reads.

**The persona review cycle.** Submitting a drafted persona for review moves it from `DRAFT` to `IN_REVIEW` and is a `PersonaResearcher` action. The recomputation happens *first*: the channel mix, `mix_confidence_pct` and `signal_count` are refreshed from the current logical clock before any gate is evaluated, so a persona whose signals have aged out of the window fails even though it passed yesterday. The persona must be in `DRAFT`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_PERSONA_NOT_DRAFT`. After the refresh, `signal_count` must be at least 1 or the submission is refused with `ERR_NO_RESEARCH_SIGNALS`, and `mix_confidence_pct` must be at least 40 — meaning a cumulative in-window respondent base of at least 400 — or the submission is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RESEARCH_CONFIDENCE`. A successful submission refreshes `updated_at` and advances `version` by 1.

A `BrandProgramLead` then records a `decision` on the persona under review, either `APPROVE` or `REQUEST_REVISION`, optionally attaching a `review_note`. Either decision requires the persona to be in `IN_REVIEW`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_PERSONA_NOT_IN_REVIEW`, and either decision requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from `drafted_by_user_id`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Both branches set `reviewed_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, store the supplied note in `last_review_note`, refresh `updated_at` and advance `version` by 1. `APPROVE` additionally requires `channel_mix` to be a non-empty array — a persona whose mix collapsed to nothing is refused with `ERR_NO_RESEARCH_SIGNALS` — and moves the persona `IN_REVIEW → APPROVED`. `REQUEST_REVISION` carries no such mix requirement, moves the persona `IN_REVIEW → DRAFT`, and additionally increments `revision_count` by 1. The `DRAFT` and `IN_REVIEW` pair therefore forms a loop that the reviewer alone bounds; there is no cap on `revision_count`.

Retirement closes the persona out. A `PersonaResearcher` retires an approved persona once its research is stale, moving it `APPROVED → RETIRED`; a persona in any other state is refused with `ERR_PERSONA_NOT_APPROVED`, and `RETIRED` is terminal. One referential guard applies: if no *other* persona of the same job family is in `APPROVED` and at least one job posting of that family has a `content_status` of `PUBLISHED` or `SUSPENDED`, the retirement is refused with `ERR_PERSONA_IN_USE`. In other words, live or suspended advertising must always be backed by at least one approved persona; retiring the family's last approved persona is only possible once the family has no such postings. A successful retirement refreshes `updated_at` and advances `version` by 1.

### 3.3. Career-Site Content: Optimization, Compliance Clearance and Publication

A job posting is the advertisement itself, and it deliberately carries **two independent status dimensions**. `content_status` tracks the editorial lifecycle through `DRAFT`, `OPTIMIZED`, `PUBLISHED`, `SUSPENDED` and the terminal `ARCHIVED`. `compliance_status` tracks the advertising-accuracy lifecycle through `UNREVIEWED`, `CLEARED` and `FLAGGED`. Neither dimension is a function of the other: clearing a posting for compliance never moves its content status, and optimizing its content never clears it. Publication is the one point where both dimensions must line up.

Creating a posting is a `BrandProgramLead` action naming the recruiting job family and carrying a `title` of 1 to 200 characters as advertised. The body's shape is described numerically rather than textually: `responsibility_count` and `benefit_count` are each integers from 0 through 40 defaulting to 0, counting the responsibility and benefit bullets in the description. The advertised annual pay range is given as `pay_range_min_cents` and `pay_range_max_cents`, each an integer of zero or more cents when present, with the ceiling never below the floor; either may be null when pay is undisclosed at draft time. Two compliance flags round it out: `eeo_statement_included`, defaulting to false, is true when the equal-opportunity statement appears in the posting body, and `superlative_claim_included`, defaulting to false, is true when the body advertises a superlative employer claim that requires substantiation. When such a claim is made, `substantiation_url` should carry a public evidence URL; it is null when absent. On creation the service derives `title_word_count` as the number of whitespace-delimited tokens in `title`, and initialises `content_status` to `DRAFT`, `compliance_status` to `UNREVIEWED`, `optimization_score` to 0, `apply_start_count`, `submitted_count` and `qualified_count` to 0, `conversion_rate_bp` to null, and `optimized_by_user_id`, `cleared_by_user_id` and `suspended_reason` all to null.

**Optimization revisions.** A lead improves a posting by submitting an optimization revision, which must carry `expected_posting_version` and may carry replacements for `title`, `responsibility_count`, `benefit_count`, `pay_range_min_cents`, `pay_range_max_cents`, `eeo_statement_included`, `superlative_claim_included` and `substantiation_url`. The posting's `content_status` must be `DRAFT` or `OPTIMIZED`, or the revision is refused with `ERR_POSTING_NOT_OPTIMIZABLE`; note that the `OPTIMIZED → OPTIMIZED` self-transition is entirely legal, each call being a new revision that supersedes the prior one. The version guard of §2.7 applies, and whenever both pay bounds are present after the edits, the ceiling must be at least the floor or the revision is refused with `ERR_PAY_RANGE_INVALID`.

A successful revision applies the supplied edits, recomputes `title_word_count` from the resulting title, recomputes `optimization_score`, moves `content_status` to `OPTIMIZED`, sets `optimized_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, refreshes `updated_at` and advances `version` by 1. It also has a compliance consequence that is easy to miss: if the posting's `compliance_status` was `CLEARED`, it is reset to `UNREVIEWED` and `cleared_by_user_id` is set back to null, because the text a reviewer cleared has just been superseded. A posting whose compliance status was `UNREVIEWED` or `FLAGGED` keeps that status through the revision.

**The optimization score.** The score is an integer from 0 through 100 assembled from five independent components, all read from the posting's own fields with no cross-entity data involved. The title component contributes 25 points when `title_word_count` is between 3 and 12 inclusive; failing that, it contributes 10 points when `title_word_count` is 20 or fewer — which includes very short titles of one or two words — and 0 points when the count exceeds 20. The responsibilities component contributes 20 points when `responsibility_count` is 5 or more, and otherwise contributes 4 points for each responsibility bullet present. The pay-transparency component contributes 15 points when `pay_range_min_cents` and `pay_range_max_cents` are both present and the ceiling is at least the floor, and 0 points otherwise. The equal-opportunity component contributes 15 points when `eeo_statement_included` is true and 0 when it is false. The benefits component contributes 5 points for each benefit bullet, counting at most five bullets, so it saturates at 25 points. `optimization_score` is the sum of the five, it stays 0 until the first optimization revision runs, and publication requires it to be at least 70 — a score of exactly 70 publishes.

**Compliance decisions.** A `ComplianceReviewer` records a `decision` of `CLEAR` or `FLAG` on a posting, optionally with a `note`. Neither decision is permitted on an `ARCHIVED` posting, which is refused with `ERR_POSTING_ALREADY_ARCHIVED`, and neither is permitted when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the posting's `optimized_by_user_id`, which is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

A `CLEAR` decision requires `compliance_status` to be `UNREVIEWED` or `FLAGGED`, refusing an already-cleared posting with `ERR_POSTING_ALREADY_CLEARED`. It further requires `eeo_statement_included` to be true, refusing otherwise with `ERR_EEO_STATEMENT_MISSING`, and it requires that either `superlative_claim_included` is false or `substantiation_url` is present, refusing an unsupported boast with `ERR_UNSUBSTANTIATED_CLAIM`. Clearing moves `compliance_status` to `CLEARED`, sets `cleared_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, refreshes `updated_at` and advances `version` by 1. It changes nothing about `content_status` — a posting cleared while `SUSPENDED` stays `SUSPENDED` until someone explicitly publishes it again.

A `FLAG` decision requires `compliance_status` to be `UNREVIEWED` or `CLEARED`, refusing an already-flagged posting with `ERR_POSTING_ALREADY_FLAGGED`. It has no equal-opportunity or substantiation requirement of its own — flagging is precisely the tool for a posting that fails those tests. The first hop of the flag sets `compliance_status` to `FLAGGED` and `cleared_by_user_id` back to null. What happens next depends on the content dimension, and this is the program's most consequential cascade.

**The five-hop compliance-flag cascade.** When the flagged posting's `content_status` is `PUBLISHED`, the flag drives it `PUBLISHED → SUSPENDED` as hop two, recording `suspended_reason` as `COMPLIANCE_FLAG` — the only value that field ever takes. Hop three follows because the posting is now suspended: every media flight pointing at this posting whose `state` is `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED_PACING` moves to `SUSPENDED_CONTENT`, with `release_reason` set to `COMPLIANCE_SUSPENSION` and `reserved_cents` set to 0. Hop four follows because those flights left `ACTIVE`: each affected channel has its `active_flight_count` decremented once per departing flight that had been `ACTIVE`, and any channel whose count thereby reaches zero moves to `pacing_status` `IDLE` with `idle_since` set to the logical clock — unless the pacing calculation had already marked that channel `THROTTLED` earlier in this same request, in which case `THROTTLED` stands, per §2.10. Hop five follows because each flight released its reservation: for every original `RESERVE` ledger entry of that flight, walked in ascending `sequence_no`, a mirroring `RELEASE` entry is written with the same `bucket` and the negated amount, which lowers `committed_budget_cents` on the program, lowers `reserved_cents` on the affected job families, and raises `available_budget_cents` correspondingly. Every entity the cascade touched — the posting, each mutated flight, each mutated channel and the program — has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` advanced by 1. When the flagged posting was not `PUBLISHED`, hops two through five simply do not fire; only the compliance dimension moves.

**Publication.** Publishing a posting is a `BrandProgramLead` action with no request body, and it is the gate where six conditions must hold simultaneously. The posting's `content_status` must be `OPTIMIZED` or `SUSPENDED`, refusing anything else with `ERR_POSTING_NOT_PUBLISHABLE`. At least one persona of the posting's job family must be in state `APPROVED`, or publication is refused with `ERR_NO_APPROVED_PERSONA`. `optimization_score` must be at least 70, or publication is refused with `ERR_OPTIMIZATION_SCORE_TOO_LOW`. `eeo_statement_included` must be true, or publication is refused with `ERR_EEO_STATEMENT_MISSING`. Both `pay_range_min_cents` and `pay_range_max_cents` must be present, or publication is refused with `ERR_PAY_RANGE_INVALID` — pay transparency is optional in a draft but mandatory in a live advertisement. And `compliance_status` must be `CLEARED`, or publication is refused with `ERR_POSTING_COMPLIANCE_NOT_CLEARED`.

Publication from `OPTIMIZED` and publication from `SUSPENDED` are the same operation with the same gates, and both set `content_status` to `PUBLISHED`, clear `suspended_reason` back to null, refresh `updated_at` and advance `version` by 1. The suspended path deserves one explicit warning: republishing a posting that was suspended by a compliance flag — which requires a fresh compliance clearance first, since the flag left `compliance_status` at `FLAGGED` and the score gate of at least 70 still applies — does **not** resurrect the flights that the suspension paused. Those flights remain in `SUSPENDED_CONTENT` and each must be resumed explicitly by a buyer, which re-runs the budget waterfall from scratch.

**Archiving.** A lead may archive a posting at any point in its content life: the operation is accepted while `content_status` is `DRAFT`, `OPTIMIZED`, `PUBLISHED` or `SUSPENDED`, and only an already-archived posting is refused, with `ERR_POSTING_ALREADY_ARCHIVED`. Archiving moves `content_status` to the terminal `ARCHIVED` and cancels the posting's advertising outright: every media flight pointing at the posting whose `state` is `RESERVED`, `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED_PACING` or `SUSPENDED_CONTENT` moves to `CANCELLED` with `release_reason` set to `CANCELLED` and `reserved_cents` set to 0, each one writing mirroring `RELEASE` ledger entries that lower the program's `committed_budget_cents` and the families' `reserved_cents`. `active_flight_count` is decremented for each cancelled flight that had been `ACTIVE`, and `IDLE` resolution follows exactly the same precedence rule as the compliance cascade. The posting, every mutated flight, every mutated channel and the program all have `updated_at` refreshed and `version` advanced by 1.

**Conversion measurement.** Alongside the posting record there is a computed read of its application funnel, available to a `BrandProgramLead`, a `ComplianceReviewer`, a `MediaBuyer` or `RecruitingOps`, reporting `apply_start_count`, `submitted_count`, `qualified_count` and `conversion_rate_bp` for a single posting. `apply_start_count` is the number of applications on the posting in any status. `submitted_count` is the number whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `SCREENED`, `QUALIFIED` or `DISQUALIFIED` — that is, every application that got past the start line, including ones later rejected. `qualified_count` is the number whose `status` is `QUALIFIED`. `conversion_rate_bp` is null while `apply_start_count` is 0, and otherwise is ten thousand multiplied by `submitted_count`, divided by `apply_start_count`, and rounded down to a whole basis point. Applications abandoned in `STARTED` therefore raise the denominator without raising the numerator, which is precisely the abandonment signal the team wants.

### 3.4. Buying Paid Media: Pricing a Flight and Funding It

A media flight is one buy on one channel for one job family, driving traffic to one posting. A `MediaBuyer` reserves it with a payload naming the buying channel as `channel_id`, the recruiting family as `job_family_id` and the destination posting as `job_posting_id`, and supplying a `start_date` and `end_date` as calendar days in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, a `daily_cap_cents` of at least 1 cent, and the `expected_program_version` guard described in §2.7. The flight window is **half-open**: `start_date` is the inclusive first day and `end_date` is the exclusive last day, so a flight from the 1st to the 8th occupies seven days and a flight whose `end_date` equals another flight's `start_date` does not overlap it.

Five business gates stand between the request and a reservation, evaluated after the version guard. The channel's `channel_type` must be `JOB_BOARD`, `SOCIAL` or `PROGRAMMATIC`; a career-site, event or email channel cannot carry paid media and the request is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_PAYABLE`. The derived `flight_days` must be at least the channel's `min_flight_days`, or the request is refused with `ERR_FLIGHT_TOO_SHORT`. No other flight on the *same channel and the same job family* whose `state` is `RESERVED`, `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED_PACING` or `SUSPENDED_CONTENT` may have a half-open window intersecting the requested one, or the request is refused with `ERR_FLIGHT_WINDOW_OVERLAP`; because the windows are half-open, back-to-back flights that merely touch are allowed. The requested `daily_cap_cents` added to the sum of `daily_cap_cents` over every flight on the same channel — of any job family — whose `state` is `RESERVED`, `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED_PACING` or `SUSPENDED_CONTENT` and whose window intersects the requested one must be no greater than the channel's `daily_capacity_cents`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_DAILY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. And finally the waterfall must be able to fund the flight's full cost, or the request is refused with `ERR_MEDIA_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` and nothing is persisted.

**Pricing, in a pinned order of six steps.** The order matters more than any individual step, because two plausible-looking rearrangements produce different money.

Step one derives `flight_days` as the number of whole days from `start_date` up to but not including `end_date`, and `base_gross_cents` as `daily_cap_cents` multiplied by `flight_days`. Step two fixes `cost_tier` from `base_gross_cents` alone: the tier is `TIER_1` when `base_gross_cents` is below 500,000 cents (five thousand dollars), `TIER_2` when it is at least 500,000 cents but below 2,000,000 cents (twenty thousand dollars), and `TIER_3` when it is at least 2,000,000 cents. Step three computes `volume_discount_cents` as `base_gross_cents` multiplied by the tier's discount rate and **rounded down** to a whole cent, where the discount rate is 0 percent at `TIER_1`, 5 percent at `TIER_2` and 8 percent at `TIER_3`. Step four takes the net media amount as `base_gross_cents` less `volume_discount_cents`. Step five computes `platform_fee_cents` as that net media amount multiplied by the fee rate for the channel's type at the flight's tier, **rounded half up** to a whole cent. Step six sets `total_cost_cents` to the net media amount plus `platform_fee_cents`, and that total is the amount the waterfall must fund.

The nine fee rates are as follows. On a `JOB_BOARD` channel the rate is 3 percent at `TIER_1`, 3 percent at `TIER_2` and 2 percent at `TIER_3`. On a `SOCIAL` channel it is 6 percent at `TIER_1`, 5 percent at `TIER_2` and 4 percent at `TIER_3`. On a `PROGRAMMATIC` channel it is 10 percent at `TIER_1`, 9 percent at `TIER_2` and 7 percent at `TIER_3`.

Two readings are explicitly wrong and must be avoided. The tier is never re-derived from the post-discount amount: a discount can push a `TIER_2` flight's net below 500,000 cents, and an implementation that re-tiered at that point would apply a `TIER_1` fee rate. And the platform fee is never charged on `base_gross_cents`; its base is always the post-discount net media amount. Once computed, none of these six outputs is ever recomputed — the flight's price is fixed at reservation time, even if the channel's fee schedule or the flight's window were somehow revisited later.

**What a successful reservation writes.** The flight is persisted in state `RESERVED` with `reserved_cents` equal to `total_cost_cents`, `spent_cents` 0, `qualified_credit_bp` 0, and `release_reason` and `activated_at` both null. The waterfall of §2.6 runs against `total_cost_cents`, writing one `RESERVE` ledger entry per drawn bucket in bucket order with ascending `sequence_no` and `reason` `FLIGHT_RESERVATION`, `source_type` `MEDIA_FLIGHT` and `source_id` equal to the new flight's `id`. The program's `committed_budget_cents` rises by `total_cost_cents`, its `available_budget_cents` falls by the same amount, and the program's `version` advances by 1 — which is what makes every other buyer's cached `expected_program_version` stale. The job family's `reserved_cents` rises by whatever was drawn from the `FAMILY_DEDICATED` bucket, and its `dedicated_available_cents` falls by exactly that amount.

### 3.5. Running the Flight: Activation, Delivered Spend and Pacing

**Activation.** A reserved flight starts delivering when a `MediaBuyer` activates it, moving it `RESERVED → ACTIVE`. A flight in any other state is refused with `ERR_FLIGHT_NOT_RESERVED`. Three cross-entity gates apply: the flight's posting must have `content_status` `PUBLISHED`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_POSTING_NOT_PUBLISHED`; the same posting must have `compliance_status` `CLEARED`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_POSTING_COMPLIANCE_NOT_CLEARED`; and the funding program's `brand_health_score` must be at least `55.00`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_BRAND_HEALTH_GATE`. That last gate is the deliberate coupling between reputation management and media spending — a program that has let bad reviews go unanswered cannot buy its way past them.

Activation sets `activated_at` to the logical clock, increments the channel's `active_flight_count` by 1, clears the channel's `idle_since` back to null, and re-runs the channel's blended target and cost-per-qualified-applicant calculations, rewriting `blended_target_cents`, `cpqa_cents` and `pacing_status`. Both the flight and the channel have `updated_at` refreshed and `version` advanced by 1.

**Recording delivered spend.** As the vendor delivers, a buyer records spend against an active flight by supplying an `amount_cents` figure and the `spent_on` calendar day the spend was delivered. Spend is only accepted while the flight's `state` is `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED_PACING`; a `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` flight is refused with `ERR_FLIGHT_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. The flight's `spent_cents` plus the recorded amount must not exceed the flight's `total_cost_cents`, or the record is refused with `ERR_SPEND_EXCEEDS_RESERVATION`. A successful record raises the flight's `spent_cents` and the channel's `spent_cents` by `amount_cents`, then re-runs the channel's blended target and cost-per-qualified-applicant calculations. Both the flight and the channel are versioned; the program is versioned too if the re-pricing released any reservations, as described below.

**How a channel's cost per qualified applicant is measured.** The channel's `credited_qualified_bp` is the sum of `credit_bp` across every touchpoint on that channel whose owning application is currently `QUALIFIED`; touchpoints of applications in any other status contribute nothing. The channel's `spent_cents` is the sum of `spent_cents` across every flight on that channel in *any* state, including `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` ones — historical spend never leaves the denominator of the efficiency picture.

The **blended target** answers "what should a qualified applicant cost on this channel, given which families it actually delivered for". For each job family of the channel's program, the calculation accumulates that family's contributed basis points: the sum of `credit_bp` over touchpoints on this channel whose owning application is `QUALIFIED` and whose application's posting belongs to that family. Let the grand total be the sum of those per-family contributions. When the grand total is zero, `blended_target_cents` is null. Otherwise it is the sum, over all families, of the family's `cpqa_target_cents` multiplied by that family's contributed basis points, divided once by the grand total, and rounded half up to a whole cent. The per-family terms are accumulated as exact integers of cents multiplied by basis points and are never rounded individually — there is a single division and a single rounding at the very end.

The **measured cost** and the pacing verdict follow immediately, on the same channel, in the same request. When the channel's `credited_qualified_bp` is zero, `cpqa_cents` is null, `blended_target_cents` is null, and `pacing_status` becomes `PACING_UNKNOWN` — note that `IDLE` is never produced by this path, only by the flight-departure rule. When `credited_qualified_bp` is positive, `cpqa_cents` is the channel's `spent_cents` multiplied by ten thousand, divided by `credited_qualified_bp`, and rounded half up to a whole cent. The pacing threshold is the channel's `blended_target_cents` multiplied by 12 and divided by 10 — a 20 percent tolerance above target — and rounded half up to a whole cent. `pacing_status` becomes `THROTTLED` when `cpqa_cents` is **strictly greater** than that threshold, and `ON_TARGET` otherwise; a channel sitting exactly on its threshold is `ON_TARGET`.

**The pacing throttle.** When a spend record or an attribution run pushes a channel into `THROTTLED`, every flight on that channel whose `state` is `ACTIVE` is immediately moved to `PAUSED_PACING`, with `release_reason` set to `PACING_THROTTLE` and `reserved_cents` set to 0. Each paused flight then writes one mirroring `RELEASE` ledger entry per original `RESERVE` entry of that flight — same `bucket`, negated `delta_cents`, walked in ascending `sequence_no` of the originals, with `reason` `FLIGHT_PACING_THROTTLE` — which lowers the program's `committed_budget_cents` and the family's `reserved_cents` and correspondingly raises `available_budget_cents` and `dedicated_available_cents`. The channel's `active_flight_count` is decremented once per departing flight, and the channel nonetheless stays `THROTTLED` rather than becoming `IDLE`, because the throttle verdict was computed earlier in the same request and outranks the idle verdict per §2.10. The flight, the channel and the program are each versioned.

**Resumption.** A buyer resumes a flight whose `state` is `PAUSED_PACING` or `SUSPENDED_CONTENT`, moving it back to `ACTIVE`; any other state is refused with `ERR_FLIGHT_NOT_RESUMABLE`. The channel's `pacing_status` must not be `THROTTLED`, or the resumption is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_THROTTLED` — the buyer must first improve the channel's efficiency, typically by qualifying more applicants on it. The posting must still be `PUBLISHED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_POSTING_NOT_PUBLISHED`, and still `CLEARED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_POSTING_COMPLIANCE_NOT_CLEARED`. Crucially, resumption **re-runs the waterfall from scratch** for the flight's full `total_cost_cents`: the money released at pause time is not held for the flight, so if other buys have consumed the buckets in the meantime the resumption is refused with `ERR_MEDIA_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`. A successful resumption writes fresh `RESERVE` ledger entries, sets `reserved_cents` back to `total_cost_cents`, clears `release_reason` to null, increments the channel's `active_flight_count` by 1, clears the channel's `idle_since`, re-runs the channel's pacing calculations, and versions the flight, the channel and the program.

**Completion.** Once the window has closed, a buyer completes a flight whose `state` is `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED_PACING`; any other state is refused with `ERR_FLIGHT_NOT_COMPLETABLE`. The logical clock must have reached the flight's `end_date` at midnight UTC — that is, the instant of `end_date` at `T00:00:00Z` — or the completion is refused with `ERR_FLIGHT_WINDOW_OPEN`. Completion sets `release_reason` to `COMPLETED` and `reserved_cents` to 0 and writes mirroring `RELEASE` ledger entries with `reason` `FLIGHT_COMPLETED` for whatever was still reserved, decrements the channel's `active_flight_count` when the flight had been `ACTIVE`, applies the `THROTTLED`-outranks-`IDLE` precedence rule when that decrement empties the channel, and versions the flight, the channel and the program. `COMPLETED` is terminal.

**Cancellation.** A buyer may cancel a flight whose `state` is `RESERVED`, `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED_PACING` or `SUSPENDED_CONTENT`, optionally attaching a `reason` for the audit trail; an already-terminal flight is refused with `ERR_FLIGHT_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. Cancellation sets `release_reason` to `CANCELLED` and `reserved_cents` to 0, and writes one mirroring `RELEASE` ledger entry per outstanding `RESERVE` entry of the flight — same `bucket`, negated `delta_cents`, ascending `sequence_no` of the originals, `reason` `FLIGHT_CANCELLED` — so that the program's `committed_budget_cents` and the family's `reserved_cents` return to exactly their pre-reservation values while `available_budget_cents` and `dedicated_available_cents` rise by exactly the released amounts. The channel's `active_flight_count` is decremented when the flight had been `ACTIVE`, with the same `IDLE` precedence rule, and the flight, the channel and the program are each versioned. `CANCELLED` is terminal.

### 3.6. The Application Funnel and Source-of-Hire Attribution

Applications are how the marketing investment is judged, so their lifecycle is short and their attribution machinery is long. Starting an application is open to a career-site visitor acting as `Anonymous` or to a signed-in `Candidate`: the request carries a `job_posting_id` naming the posting applied to and a `candidate_email` of 1 to 200 characters in RFC 5322 shape. A new application is in `status` `STARTED` with `submitted_at`, `screened_at` and `decided_at` all null, `attributed_credit_bp_total` 0, `eligible_touch_count` 0 and `attribution_model_applied` null. Starting the application raises the posting's `apply_start_count` by 1 and recomputes the posting's conversion metrics.

Alongside applications, `RecruitingOps` and `MediaBuyer` ingest **touchpoints**, the raw material of attribution. Each touchpoint is ingested with a payload carrying the `application_id` it belongs to and the `channel_id` the interaction happened on, optionally naming the paid flight the interaction is billed to — `media_flight_id` is null for organic touches — and carries a `touch_type` drawn from `IMPRESSION`, `CLICK`, `ORGANIC_VISIT`, `EVENT_SCAN`, `EMAIL_CLICK` and `REFERRAL`, an `occurred_at` instant, and an optional `media_cost_cents` of zero or more defaulting to 0. A touchpoint is written with `credit_bp` 0; credit is assigned only when the owning application reaches `QUALIFIED`, and touchpoints are immutable event rows thereafter.

Submitting a started application — again either `Anonymous` or the `Candidate` themselves — moves it `STARTED → SUBMITTED`. An application in any other status is refused with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_STARTED`; the posting must have `content_status` `PUBLISHED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_POSTING_NOT_PUBLISHED`, and `compliance_status` `CLEARED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_POSTING_COMPLIANCE_NOT_CLEARED` — a posting suspended mid-application stops accepting submissions immediately. Submission sets `submitted_at` to the logical clock, which is the anchor of the attribution lookback window, raises the posting's `submitted_count` by 1, recomputes `conversion_rate_bp`, and versions both the application and the posting. `RecruitingOps` then records screening, moving the application `SUBMITTED → SCREENED` and setting `screened_at`; an application in any other status is refused with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_SUBMITTED`.

**The decision.** `RecruitingOps` decides a screened application by supplying an `outcome` of `QUALIFIED` or `DISQUALIFIED`, optionally with a `reason`. A `QUALIFIED` outcome requires the application to be in `SCREENED`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_SCREENED`, and requires at least one touchpoint inside the attribution lookback window, refusing otherwise with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TOUCHPOINT`. A `DISQUALIFIED` outcome is accepted from `SCREENED` or from `QUALIFIED`, refusing any other status with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_DECIDABLE`. Every decision sets `decided_at` to the logical clock and versions the application; beyond that, the three branches have deliberately disjoint effects.

**How multi-touch credit is computed.** The eligible input set is the touchpoints of the deciding application whose `occurred_at` is strictly later than the application's `submitted_at` minus 30 days and no later than `submitted_at` itself — the lower bound is exclusive, the upper bound inclusive. Touchpoints of every `touch_type` are eligible, and both paid touches and organic ones with a null `media_flight_id` are included; touchpoints outside the window are excluded and keep `credit_bp` 0. The eligible set is ordered by `occurred_at` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending — never by `id`. If the eligible set is empty the decision is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TOUCHPOINT`.

Each eligible touchpoint then receives a weight, selected by the program's `attribution_model`. Under `LINEAR`, every eligible touchpoint carries a weight of 1, so credit spreads evenly and the rounding remainder lands on the earliest touchpoints — with three eligible touches the credits come out as 3,334 then 3,333 then 3,333 basis points. Under `POSITION_BASED`, a single eligible touchpoint carries weight 1, two eligible touchpoints carry weights of 1 and 1, and with three or more the first touchpoint carries 4,000, the last carries 4,000, and each of the middle touchpoints carries 2,000 divided by the number of middle touchpoints, held as an exact value — so the same three touches on the same application become 4,000 then 2,000 then 4,000 basis points. The identical touchpoint set therefore yields a different per-channel credit picture depending on which model the program has chosen.

Exactly ten thousand basis points are then distributed, in the pinned order described in §2.5. Each touchpoint's exact share is ten thousand multiplied by its own weight and divided by the sum of all the weights; each exact share is rounded down to a whole basis point; the shortfall between the sum of those floors and ten thousand is counted; and one extra basis point is awarded to each of that many touchpoints with the largest fractional parts, ties broken by `occurred_at` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. Rounding each exact share half up independently is forbidden: it can total 9,999 or 10,001.

**The five-hop qualification cascade.** Hop one writes the computed `credit_bp` onto every eligible touchpoint and records the outcome on the application: `eligible_touch_count` becomes the number of touchpoints in the input set, `attribution_model_applied` freezes the program's `attribution_model` value as it stood at this moment, and `attributed_credit_bp_total` becomes 10,000. Hop two follows because those credits changed: each channel's `credited_qualified_bp` rises by the `credit_bp` of each of its touchpoints, and each flight's `qualified_credit_bp` rises by the `credit_bp` of the touchpoints whose `media_flight_id` names it. Hop three follows because those channel totals changed: each affected channel's blended target and measured cost are recomputed, rewriting `blended_target_cents`, `cpqa_cents` and `pacing_status`, with `THROTTLED` resulting when the measured cost strictly exceeds the pacing threshold. Hop four follows for any channel that just became `THROTTLED`: every flight on it whose `state` is `ACTIVE` moves to `PAUSED_PACING` with `release_reason` `PACING_THROTTLE` and `reserved_cents` 0. Hop five follows because those flights released their reservations: mirroring `RELEASE` ledger entries are written, lowering the program's `committed_budget_cents` and the families' `reserved_cents` and raising `available_budget_cents`. On top of the cascade, the posting's `qualified_count` rises by 1 and the program's `last_attribution_recompute_at` is set to the logical clock. Every entity the cascade mutated has `updated_at` refreshed and `version` advanced by 1.

**Disqualification from `SCREENED`** is deliberately the empty mirror of that cascade. No attribution credit is assigned at all: every touchpoint of the application keeps `credit_bp` 0, `attributed_credit_bp_total` stays 0, `attribution_model_applied` stays null, no channel counter moves, no flight is paused, and no ledger entry is written. Only `decided_at`, `updated_at` and `version` change on the application itself.

**Disqualification from `QUALIFIED`** is a compensating action that must undo hop one and hop two exactly. Every touchpoint of the application has its `credit_bp` set back to 0, and each affected channel's `credited_qualified_bp` and each affected flight's `qualified_credit_bp` are lowered by precisely the amounts removed — so that the global identity holds: the sum of `credited_qualified_bp` across all channels always equals ten thousand multiplied by the number of applications currently in `QUALIFIED`. The application's `attributed_credit_bp_total` returns to 0 and its `attribution_model_applied` returns to null, and the posting's `qualified_count` falls by 1. Each affected channel's measured cost is then recomputed: a channel whose `credited_qualified_bp` has returned to zero goes back to `pacing_status` `PACING_UNKNOWN` with both `cpqa_cents` and `blended_target_cents` null. What the compensation deliberately does **not** do is restore reservations — flights already moved to `PAUSED_PACING` stay `PAUSED_PACING`, and getting them delivering again requires an explicit resumption that re-runs the waterfall. `decided_at` is set and every mutated entity is versioned.

### 3.7. Talent Community Consent and Nurture Dispatch

Joining the talent community is open to an `Anonymous` visitor or to a `CommunityManager` registering someone on their behalf. The registration names the program whose community is being joined, an `email` of 1 to 200 characters in RFC 5322 shape that must be unique inside that program, and optionally an `interest_job_family_id` naming the family the member cares about — null when they expressed no preference. It may also set `frequency_cap_per_30d`, the maximum number of messages the member will accept inside a trailing 30-day window, an integer of at least 1 that defaults to 4. A new member is in `consent_status` `PENDING_CONFIRMATION` with `sent_count_total` 0 and `last_sent_at`, `consent_confirmed_at` and `consent_changed_at` all null. The server mints a `consent_token` and echoes it on the registration response; this is a double opt-in system and the token is the only way to complete it.

Confirmation is performed by an `Anonymous` visitor following the emailed link or by the `Candidate` themselves, and requires the request's `consent_token` to equal the stored `consent_token`, refusing a mismatch with `ERR_CONSENT_TOKEN_INVALID` (HTTP 422). The member must be in `PENDING_CONFIRMATION`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_RESOLVED`. Confirmation moves the member `PENDING_CONFIRMATION → SUBSCRIBED` and sets both `consent_confirmed_at` and `consent_changed_at` to the logical clock, refreshing `updated_at` and advancing `version` by 1.

Afterwards, consent is changed by supplying an `action` of `PAUSE`, `RESUME` or `UNSUBSCRIBE`. This operation is available to a `Candidate` and to a `CommunityManager`, but a `Candidate` may only act on the member record whose `id` equals their own `X-User-ID`; acting on anyone else's record is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. `PAUSE` requires `SUBSCRIBED` and moves the member `SUBSCRIBED → PAUSED`. `RESUME` requires `PAUSED` and moves the member `PAUSED → SUBSCRIBED`; either action on the wrong starting status is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_TRANSITION_INVALID`. `SUBSCRIBED` and `PAUSED` form an unbounded cycle — a member may pause and resume indefinitely. `UNSUBSCRIBE` is accepted from `PENDING_CONFIRMATION`, `SUBSCRIBED` or `PAUSED`, refusing an already-unsubscribed member with `ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_RESOLVED`, and moves the member to the terminal `UNSUBSCRIBED`, permanently excluding them from every future dispatch audience. Every accepted action sets `consent_changed_at` to the logical clock, refreshes `updated_at` and advances `version` by 1. A `CommunityManager` may unsubscribe a member on their behalf without the ownership restriction.

**Campaigns.** A `CommunityManager` drafts a nurture campaign against a program with a `name` of 1 to 160 characters and a `max_sends` ceiling of at least 1. The campaign may name a `job_family_id` to narrow its audience to members interested in that family; leaving it null targets every member of the program. A new campaign is in `state` `DRAFT` with `scheduled_for` and `dispatched_at` null and `audience_size`, `sent_count`, `suppressed_consent_count` and `suppressed_frequency_count` all 0. Scheduling it supplies a `scheduled_for` instant that must be at or after the logical clock, refusing a past instant with `ERR_SCHEDULE_IN_PAST`; the campaign must be in `DRAFT`, refused otherwise with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_DRAFT`. Scheduling stores `scheduled_for`, moves the campaign `DRAFT → SCHEDULED`, refreshes `updated_at` and advances `version` by 1.

Cancelling a campaign is accepted from `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED`, optionally with a `reason`, and refuses an already-dispatched or already-cancelled campaign with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. The `SCHEDULED → DISPATCHED` and `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` branches are a genuine fork with disjoint effects: cancellation writes **no** send-evaluation row whatsoever and leaves the program's `send_quota_used` completely untouched. It moves the campaign to the terminal `CANCELLED`, refreshes `updated_at` and advances `version` by 1, and nothing else.

**Dispatch.** Dispatching moves the campaign `SCHEDULED → DISPATCHED` and is the heaviest operation in the community workflow. The campaign must be in `SCHEDULED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_SCHEDULED`, and the logical clock must be at or after the stored `scheduled_for`, refused otherwise with `ERR_DISPATCH_TOO_EARLY`. The audience is then assembled and two further gates apply: at least one member of that audience must be in `SUBSCRIBED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_NO_CONSENTED_AUDIENCE`, and the campaign's `max_sends` must be no greater than the program's `send_quota_total` less its `send_quota_used`, refused otherwise with `ERR_SEND_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`. Note carefully that the quota check compares the campaign's *ceiling* against remaining quota, not the number of messages that will actually go out.

The audience is the set of talent-community members belonging to the campaign's program whose `consent_status` is either `SUBSCRIBED` or `PAUSED`, and, when the campaign names a `job_family_id`, whose `interest_job_family_id` equals that family. Members in `PENDING_CONFIRMATION` and members in `UNSUBSCRIBED` are outside the audience entirely and receive no send-evaluation row at all; paused members are *inside* it and are logged as suppressed. The audience is ordered by `created_at` ascending, with `email` ascending as the tie-break should two members share a creation instant — the member's `id` is never used for ordering. The evaluated set is then the first `max_sends` members of that ordered audience, which may be shorter than `max_sends` when the audience is small.

The evaluated members are walked in that strict order, and each one produces exactly one `NurtureSend` row. A running counter assigns `sequence_no` starting at 1 for the first member evaluated. For each member, the service counts that member's existing send-evaluation rows whose `outcome` is `SENT` and whose `sent_at` is **strictly later** than the logical clock minus 30 days — a send that landed exactly 30 days ago is outside the window — and records that count on the new row as `window_send_count_at_eval`, capturing the value as it stood *before* this row was written. The verdict then follows a pinned check order in which **consent is examined before frequency**: if the member's `consent_status` is anything other than `SUBSCRIBED`, the `outcome` is `SUPPRESSED_CONSENT`; otherwise, if the window count is greater than or equal to the member's `frequency_cap_per_30d`, the `outcome` is `SUPPRESSED_FREQUENCY`; otherwise the `outcome` is `SENT`. The comparison against the cap is inclusive — a member whose cap is 4 and who has already received 4 messages in the window is suppressed. Each row records its `campaign_id`, its `member_id`, the `outcome`, the `sequence_no`, an `evaluated_at` equal to the logical clock, and a `sent_at` equal to the logical clock when the outcome is `SENT` and null otherwise. Send-evaluation rows are immutable.

Dispatch then settles the aggregates. For every row whose outcome was `SENT`, the member's `sent_count_total` rises by 1 and their `last_sent_at` becomes the logical clock. The campaign's `audience_size` becomes the number of rows written in any outcome, `sent_count` the number of `SENT` rows, `suppressed_consent_count` the number of `SUPPRESSED_CONSENT` rows and `suppressed_frequency_count` the number of `SUPPRESSED_FREQUENCY` rows. The program's `send_quota_used` rises by the number of `SENT` rows only — suppressed evaluations consume no quota — and `send_quota_used` is by definition the count of `SENT` rows across every campaign of that program. `dispatched_at` is set to the logical clock, and the campaign, every member whose counters moved and the program each have `updated_at` refreshed and `version` advanced by 1. `DISPATCHED` is terminal.

### 3.8. Employer Review Monitoring, Response and Brand Health

Reviews are ingested by a `CommunityManager` or by `RecruitingOps` from the platform monitoring feed. Each `EmployerReview` names the program it is about, the `platform` it was observed on — one of `GLASSDOOR`, `INDEED` or `COMPARABLY` — an `external_ref` of 1 to 80 characters that is the platform-side identifier and must be unique within that platform, a `rating` from 1 through 5 stars as published, and the `posted_at` instant of publication. That instant anchors both the response deadline and the recency weight in the brand health score.

**The response deadline** follows directly from the rating, on the principle that angry reviews need answering fast. A rating of 1 or 2 gives 24 hours; a rating of exactly 3 gives 72 hours — three days; a rating of 4 or 5 gives 168 hours — a full week. `response_due_at` is `posted_at` plus that many hours, recorded as an exact ISO 8601 instant with three fractional digits. A new review is in `state` `NEW` with `sla_status` `PENDING`, `rejection_count` 0, `response_body` null, and `drafted_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id` and `responded_at` all null. Ingesting a review immediately recomputes the program's `brand_health_score`, refreshing that funding program's `updated_at` and advancing its `version` by 1 alongside the newly created review.

`sla_status` is a three-valued verdict stored on the review, and it is rewritten at exactly four moments: ingestion, which always stores `PENDING`; a clock-setting call; triage; and resolution — a legal `APPROVE` or a close without response. It reads `PENDING` while the review is unresolved and the logical clock has not yet moved past `response_due_at`; a clock standing exactly on `response_due_at` still reads `PENDING`. It becomes `MET` when the review reached `RESPONSE_PUBLISHED` or `CLOSED_NO_RESPONSE` at or before `response_due_at`. It becomes `BREACHED` either when the logical clock passes `response_due_at` while the review is still unresolved, or when resolution happens after that deadline. The clock-setting and triage re-evaluations consider unresolved reviews only — a review already in `RESPONSE_PUBLISHED` or `CLOSED_NO_RESPONSE` keeps the verdict resolution wrote for it, whichever way the clock later moves. And because the verdict is a stored value rather than one derived on every read, a review ingested at a moment when `response_due_at` already lies at or before the logical clock reads `PENDING`, and contributes no SLA penalty to the brand health score, until the next clock-setting call or its own triage flips it to `BREACHED`. This is exactly why moving the logical clock is a mutating operation: advancing it flips unresolved overdue reviews to `BREACHED`, and each such flip immediately costs the program brand health.

**The handling workflow** is a five-state pipeline with one rejection loop. A `CommunityManager` triages a review, moving it `NEW → TRIAGED` and optionally attaching a `note`; a review in any other state is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_NEW`. Triage re-evaluates `sla_status` against `response_due_at` and the current logical clock, and it versions the review alone — the program's `updated_at` and `version` stay untouched even when the flip to `BREACHED` moves that program's brand health score with it. The same manager then drafts the public response by supplying a `response_body` of 1 to 2,000 characters; drafting is accepted from `TRIAGED` or from `DRAFTING` — redrafting is normal — and refused from any other state with `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_DRAFTABLE`. Drafting stores the text, moves the review to `DRAFTING`, and sets `drafted_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, which is the value the later two-person rule reads. Submitting to legal moves the review `DRAFTING → LEGAL_REVIEW`, refusing any other state with `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_DRAFTING` and refusing a review with no stored `response_body` with `ERR_RESPONSE_BODY_MISSING`. Each of these three steps refreshes `updated_at` and advances `version` by 1.

A `ComplianceReviewer` then records a legal `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, optionally with a `note`. Both branches require the review to be in `LEGAL_REVIEW`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_IN_LEGAL_REVIEW`, and both require the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from `drafted_by_user_id`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. `APPROVE` moves the review `LEGAL_REVIEW → RESPONSE_PUBLISHED`, sets `approved_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID` and `responded_at` to the logical clock, sets `sla_status` to `MET` when `responded_at` is at or before `response_due_at` and to `BREACHED` otherwise, recomputes the program's `brand_health_score`, and versions both the review and the program. `REJECT` moves the review `LEGAL_REVIEW → DRAFTING`, increments `rejection_count` by 1, retains the existing `response_body` for editing, and versions the review; it does not touch `responded_at`, `sla_status` or the brand health score. The `DRAFTING → LEGAL_REVIEW → DRAFTING` loop may repeat without bound.

Some reviews do not warrant a public reply. A `CommunityManager` may close a triaged review without responding, optionally with a `note`, moving it `TRIAGED → CLOSED_NO_RESPONSE`; a review in any other state is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_CLOSABLE`. This is permitted only for a `rating` of 4 or 5 — a rating of 3 or below must receive a published response, and attempting to close one is refused with `ERR_RESPONSE_REQUIRED`. Closing sets `responded_at` to the logical clock, sets `sla_status` to `MET` when `responded_at` is at or before `response_due_at` and to `BREACHED` otherwise, recomputes the program's `brand_health_score`, and versions both the review and the program. Both `RESPONSE_PUBLISHED` and `CLOSED_NO_RESPONSE` are terminal.

**The brand health score.** `brand_health_score` starts at `100.00` and decays with recent bad reviews and missed deadlines. The window is the reviews of that program whose `posted_at` is strictly later than the logical clock minus 90 days and no later than the logical clock — the lower bound exclusive, the upper inclusive. Reviews outside that window contribute nothing regardless of their state.

For each review in the window, its age in whole days is the elapsed time from `posted_at` to the logical clock divided by 86,400 seconds and rounded down, and its recency weight is `1.00` when the age is 30 days or fewer, `0.50` when the age is 31 through 60 days, and `0.25` when the age is 61 days or more. The rating penalty is the sum over the windowed reviews of the star shortfall — three less the review's `rating`, treated as zero when the rating is 3 or above — multiplied by 2.5 points per missing star and then multiplied by that review's recency weight. So a one-star review posted yesterday costs 5 points, the same review 45 days old costs 2.5 points, and the same review 75 days old costs 1.25 points, while a three-star review costs nothing at all. The SLA penalty is 5 points multiplied by the number of windowed reviews whose `sla_status` is `BREACHED`, with no recency weighting applied to it.

The score is then 100 less the rating penalty less the SLA penalty, held to the inclusive interval from 0 to 100, and rounded half up to exactly two decimal places. All the intermediate arithmetic is exact decimal arithmetic, and the single rounding happens at the very end. The score's operational bite is the activation gate of §3.5: once it drops below `55.00`, no further media flight can be activated on that program until the team works the review backlog down.

### 3.9. Event and Campus Brand Presence

Career fairs and campus events compete with paid media for the same money and, more scarcely, for the same recruiters. A `MediaBuyer` plans a presence by naming the funding program and the job family whose recruiter team will staff it — that family is the contended resource — plus a `venue_code` of 2 to 40 characters identifying the campus or venue, a `start_at` and `end_at` bounding the on-site occupation interval, and a `booth_cost_cents` of at least 1 cent. Unlike a flight window, an event interval is **closed on both ends**: both `start_at` and `end_at` are inside the occupation. A newly planned presence is in `state` `PLANNED` with `reserved_cents` 0 and `confirmed_at` null, and no budget is committed at this stage.

**Confirmation** is where both scarce resources are claimed. The presence must be in `PLANNED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_PRESENCE_NOT_PLANNED`. The recruiter-team buffer is then checked against every other presence of the same job family whose `state` is `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED`: for each such neighbour, either this presence's `start_at` must be at least 48 hours after the neighbour's `end_at`, or this presence's `end_at` plus 48 hours must be at or before the neighbour's `start_at`, with both intervals treated as closed on both ends. A gap of exactly 48 hours satisfies the rule; anything tighter is refused with `ERR_EVENT_BUFFER_CONFLICT`. Note that presences in `PLANNED` or `CANCELLED` are ignored by this check entirely, so two plans may be drawn up against each other and only the first one confirmed will stand.

The money is then drawn through the event variant of the waterfall: the family-dedicated bucket first, then the flex reserve, skipping the channel-type pools altogether because an event is not a channel buy. If those two buckets together cannot fund `booth_cost_cents` in full the confirmation is refused with `ERR_MEDIA_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` and nothing is persisted. On success, one `RESERVE` ledger entry is written per drawn bucket with `source_type` `EVENT_PRESENCE`, `source_id` equal to the presence's `id` and `reason` `EVENT_RESERVATION`; `reserved_cents` becomes `booth_cost_cents`, `confirmed_at` becomes the logical clock, the presence moves `PLANNED → CONFIRMED`, the program's `committed_budget_cents` and the family's `reserved_cents` rise accordingly, and the program's `version` advances by 1 alongside the presence's own.

**Completion** closes the event out. The presence must be in `CONFIRMED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_PRESENCE_NOT_CONFIRMED`, and the logical clock must be **strictly after** `end_at`, refused otherwise with `ERR_EVENT_WINDOW_OPEN` — note the contrast with flight completion, which is permitted at the boundary instant. Completion moves the presence `CONFIRMED → COMPLETED` and deliberately leaves `reserved_cents` at `booth_cost_cents`, writing no ledger entry at all: the reservation has converted into actual spend and the money is genuinely gone. Only `updated_at` and `version` change beyond the state.

**Cancellation** is accepted from `PLANNED` or `CONFIRMED`, optionally with a `reason`, and refuses an already-completed or already-cancelled presence with `ERR_PRESENCE_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. The two starting states behave differently and both behaviours matter. A presence cancelled from `CONFIRMED` writes one mirroring `RELEASE` ledger entry per outstanding `RESERVE` entry of that presence — same `bucket`, negated `delta_cents`, ascending `sequence_no` of the originals, `reason` `EVENT_CANCELLED` — and sets `reserved_cents` to 0, restoring the program's `committed_budget_cents` and the family's `reserved_cents` to exactly their pre-confirmation values; the program is versioned because money moved. A presence cancelled from `PLANNED` writes no ledger entry whatsoever, because it never reserved anything, and the program is not versioned. Either way the presence moves to the terminal `CANCELLED` with `updated_at` refreshed and `version` advanced by 1.
