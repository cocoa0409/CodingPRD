# Arena Changeover Ops API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A modern multipurpose arena earns its keep by being several buildings at once. On a Tuesday the floor is a hockey rink; by Wednesday evening the ice is under insulated panels and a basketball court is down; on Thursday the bowl is curtained to half capacity for a family show, and on Friday the roof grid carries eighteen tonnes of concert rigging. The minutes between those events are the scarcest commodity the building owns. Every one of them is contested by a floor crew striking dashers, a seating crew retracting sections, an infrastructure crew pulling broadcast fibre, truck drivers waiting on a dock, and an ice technician watching a dehumidifier that decides whether the deck under the panels sweats or holds.

This document specifies the Arena Changeover Operations API, the system of record for that contested time. It carries the event calendar and the contractual hold priorities that anchor tenants bargain for; the changeover orders that convert one house configuration into the next, tracked along three independent work dimensions that progress in parallel; the ice deck's installation, covering and melt-out lifecycle with its condensation safety gate; dock windows for load-in and load-out; stagehand labor calls with their guarantees, overtime and cancellation economics; and the equipment pools of stage decks, dasher boards, floor panels and curtains that the crews draw against. Turnaround overruns are not merely reported — they accumulate into a venue-level debt ledger that eventually freezes the opening of new turnarounds until the building has caught its breath.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, safety outranks schedule and schedule outranks money: the building would rather refuse a dangerous turnaround than a merely expensive one, and the validation precedence in §2.10 encodes that literally. Second, duties are segregated — the supervisor who opens a turnaround may never be the director who signs it off, the manager who commits bookings never touches floor execution, and the coordinator who reports labor hours never approves the work those hours paid for.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section applies to every endpoint unless a specific workflow in §3 narrows it. The API is mounted at the server root; there is no common path prefix, and a trailing slash is insignificant — `/venues` and `/venues/` address the same endpoint.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles the target endpoint authorises, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `VenueOperationsDirector`, `BookingManager`, `ChangeoverSupervisor`, `LaborCoordinator` and `AnchorTenantRep`, spelled exactly as shown.

- A `VenueOperationsDirector` owns the physical building: registering arenas, declaring house configurations, contracting anchor tenants, creating and maintaining equipment pools, driving the logical clock, and signing off completed turnarounds. The role is deliberately excluded from opening turnarounds so that sign-off is always a second pair of eyes.
- A `BookingManager` owns the event calendar and conflict resolution: creating, holding, confirming and releasing bookings, sweeping expired holds, and ruling on hold challenges. The role never touches floor execution and never signs a turnaround off.
- A `ChangeoverSupervisor` owns floor execution: ice installation and the covering cycle, opening turnarounds, advancing and reworking the three stage dimensions, dock windows, and equipment issues and returns.
- A `LaborCoordinator` owns labor calls and their money — issuing, closing and cancelling calls — plus the dock windows those calls work.
- An `AnchorTenantRep` acts for exactly one anchor tenant: drafting and holding that tenant's own bookings, spending its challenge credits against incumbent holds, and withdrawing those challenges.

Four of the five roles carry an opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `VenueOperationsDirector`, `BookingManager`, `ChangeoverSupervisor` or `LaborCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example as `opened_by_user_id` when a supervisor opens a turnaround, or as `signed_off_by_user_id` when a director signs one off) but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`.

`AnchorTenantRep` is the one entity-backed role. When `X-User-Role` is `AnchorTenantRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `AnchorTenant`, and ownership checks on any resource that references an anchor tenant compare the resource's tenant reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely: creating a booking that names a `tenant_id`, and placing the hold on such a booking, both require `X-User-ID` to equal that `tenant_id`; opening a hold challenge and withdrawing one both require `X-User-ID` to equal the challenge's `challenger_tenant_id`. Any mismatch is rejected with `ERR_TENANT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

One further identity rule is a hard segregation of duties rather than an ownership check. The `X-User-ID` presented at turnaround sign-off must differ from the `opened_by_user_id` recorded on that turnaround; a director who is the same principal that opened the order is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the decimal digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Failures the transport raises before any domain rule is reached reuse this same error envelope. A request whose path matches no declared endpoint answers `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404, and a request whose method the matched path does not accept answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 405 — so `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` is not exclusively a 400 in practice, even though 400 is the status §5 records for it.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — as is either parameter supplied as anything but the plain decimal spelling of an integer, so `10.5`, `+10` and `010` are all refused rather than coerced, though surrounding whitespace is tolerated.

Results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with insertion order as the tie-break whenever two rows share the same `created_at`, unless the endpoint's own description in §3 pins a different order; `id` is never an ordering key. Every filter named in §3 is an exact-equality match against the stored value, and a filter value that is not a member of the filtered field's enumeration is not a validation error — it simply matches no rows and yields an empty page with a `total_count` of 0. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page). An `offset` past the end of the matching set is not an error: it returns an empty `data` array with `total_count` still reporting the full match count and `has_more` false.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every resource the API persists is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. The identifier carries no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients must treat it as opaque.

Most resources also carry three bookkeeping fields. `created_at` records the instant the row was inserted, and `updated_at` records the instant of the most recent mutation; both are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits. These two stamps are the row's own audit trail rather than domain data: they are read from the server's real wall-clock time at the moment of the insert or of the mutation, **not** from the logical clock of §2.5, so they advance even while the logical clock stands still and they never rewind when the clock is moved backwards. A newly inserted row's `updated_at` equals its `created_at`. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every accepted mutation of that row. Arenas, anchor tenants, event bookings, hold challenges, changeover orders, ice sheets, dock slot reservations, labor calls, equipment pools and equipment issues all carry `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

House configuration profiles are the one exception. A configuration profile is immutable once declared, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all — neither key appears in its response payload.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Every temporal rule in this system — hold expiry, cancellation notice bands, displacement notice bands, cover-hour accumulation, turnaround stamping, the melt-out dependency window and the expiry sweep — reads a server-side **logical clock** rather than real wall-clock time. The clock's startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Until a caller moves it, every rule observes that instant, and the server must never fall back to the host machine's actual time — neither when evaluating those rules nor when stamping any instant onto an entity row, with the single exception of the `created_at` and `updated_at` bookkeeping fields of §2.4, which are real wall-clock stamps.

A `VenueOperationsDirector` moves the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting capability; the response echoes the new position as `current_utc`. Moving the clock mutates no entity row and increments no `version` counter.

Arena-local time is four hours behind UTC. Wherever a rule speaks of a venue-local hour — and only the overnight-shift determination does — the local wall time is obtained by subtracting four hours from the UTC instant, and its hour-of-day component is taken as a truncated whole number between 0 and 23.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on the Arena Record

Two write operations commit against arena-wide scarce capacity, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency control anchored on the arena's `version` counter. In both cases the request body must carry `expected_venue_version` as a required, non-optional integer field holding the `version` the client last observed on the arena. If that value does not equal the arena's current `version` at the moment the write is evaluated, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VENUE_VERSION` and HTTP status 412.

- `POST /event-bookings/{id}/confirm` requires `expected_venue_version`. Confirming a booking claims an exclusive event interval on the calendar and a share of the roof grid's `rigging_capacity_kg`. A confirm whose `expected_venue_version` no longer matches the arena's `version` was computed against a superseded view of committed intervals and committed suspended load, so it is refused rather than allowed to commit against stale arithmetic.
- `POST /changeover-orders` requires `expected_venue_version`. Opening a turnaround claims an exclusive window on the arena floor and a slice of `crew_headcount_cap`, and its frozen plan is derived from `ice_deck_status` and `changeover_debt_minutes`. A create carrying an `expected_venue_version` that no longer matches the arena's `version` derived its plan from a superseded arena snapshot and is refused.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Requests to any endpoint outside these two must not be expected to supply `expected_venue_version`, and the field is not part of their contract.

Because so many operations touch the arena row, it is worth stating plainly which ones advance `version` on the arena. Placing a hold, confirming a booking, releasing a booking, driving a booking into `DISPLACED`, opening a hold challenge, withdrawing one, ruling on one, installing an ice sheet, any accepted ice sheet transition driven through the transition capability, opening a changeover order and signing one off all increment the arena's `version` by exactly 1. The expiry sweep increments it by exactly 1 when it releases at least one booking and leaves it untouched when it releases none. Recording progress on a turnaround, reworking a dimension, reserving or cancelling a dock window, issuing or returning equipment, creating, closing or cancelling a labor call, and equipment maintenance actions do not increment the arena's `version`. Neither does declaring a house configuration profile, registering an anchor tenant nor creating an equipment pool: all three hang off the arena but leave the arena row itself untouched, so an arena registered and then given profiles, tenants and pools is still at `version` 1.

### 2.7. Interval Arithmetic

Three distinct interval conventions coexist in this domain, and mixing them up is the single easiest way to build a subtly wrong calendar. Each is stated here once and referenced by the workflows in §3.

**Unbuffered half-open overlap.** Two intervals collide when the first starts strictly before the second ends *and* the second starts strictly before the first ends. Intervals that merely touch at an endpoint — one ending at exactly the instant the next begins — do not collide. This convention governs event-interval conflicts between confirmed bookings, evaluated on `event_start_utc` and `event_end_utc`, and turnaround window conflicts between changeover orders, evaluated on `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc`.

**Buffered half-open overlap.** Dock reservations on the same dock number must additionally be separated by the arena's `dock_buffer_minutes`. A proposed dock window collides with an existing one when the proposed `window_start_utc` is strictly earlier than the existing `window_end_utc` advanced forward by `dock_buffer_minutes`, and simultaneously the existing `window_start_utc` is strictly earlier than the proposed `window_end_utc` advanced forward by `dock_buffer_minutes`. A gap of exactly `dock_buffer_minutes` between two windows is therefore accepted.

**Closed-interval overlap.** Suspended-load aggregation on the roof grid is deliberately more conservative than calendar conflict detection. Two bookings overlap for rigging purposes when the first starts at or before the second ends and the second starts at or before the first ends — closed on both sides. Two events that merely touch at an endpoint therefore *both* count toward the grid load at that instant, because the outgoing show's rig is still hanging while the incoming show's rig goes up.

### 2.8. Precision, Rounding and Money

Wherever a value is rounded, the method is **round half up** — a value sitting exactly on a half increment rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. Truncation and banker's rounding are never used.

Money is always expressed as a whole number of cents in an integer field; there are no fractional-cent balances anywhere in the system. Labor cost figures are rounded to whole cents once per worker, before the per-worker figure is multiplied by the call's `headcount`; the alternative order, multiplying first and rounding the aggregate, yields a different total whenever the per-worker amount lands on a half cent and is not used. Displacement settlements round to whole cents at two independent points in their waterfall, described in §3.4.

Durations behave differently by kind. Turnaround plan minutes and actual minutes are whole minutes; where a division or a scaling produces a fraction, the specific rounding direction is pinned by the calculation that produces it (§3.6 and §3.8) rather than by a global rule. Panel-set counts round up to whole sets. Notice periods used for cancellation and displacement bands are measured as described in the workflows that use them. `cover_hours_accumulated` is carried to three decimal places. `condensation_risk_score` is carried to two decimal places. Aggregate suspended load in kilograms is carried to two decimal places. Those three quantised figures are emitted as bare JSON numbers rather than as strings, and a result that lands on a whole number is emitted without a fractional part rather than padded out to its decimal places — a zero aggregate load reads as `0`, not `0.00`. Every elapsed span that is converted into hours — a labor call's scheduled length, the notice period before a cancelled call, and an ice sheet's accumulated cover period — is first reduced to a whole number of elapsed seconds, any sub-second remainder being discarded, and only then divided by 3,600.

### 2.9. Reads, Nested Paths and Resolution Order

The resource graph is flat: every entity is its own top-level collection, joined by explicit references carried in payloads and query filters. No write path is nested more than one level below an arena, and no resource is ever addressed through an ancestor chain longer than a single hop.

Four read paths are nested purely as reverse-reachability lookups — the arena's configuration profiles, the arena's anchor tenants, the arena's equipment pools, the arena's ice sheets, and a booking's dock slot reservations. Each supports the pagination parameters of §2.3 plus a small set of filters described alongside its workflow.

Path parameters are validated strictly left to right. When a nested path names an arena that does not exist, the response is `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND` in preference to every *later* rung of the §2.10 ladder — but existence is only rung 7, so the request-shape checks of rung 5 and the pagination checks of §2.3 genuinely do run first: a request that both names a missing arena and carries a malformed body, or a missing arena together with an out-of-range `limit`, reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND`. Only once every path segment resolves does the server begin resolving identifiers carried in the request body, and body identifiers are resolved in the fixed order the endpoint's own contract in §3 declares them — not in whatever order the incoming JSON happens to list its keys, which never affects which 404 a request carrying two unresolvable references receives. The unresolved-identifier vocabulary is specific: `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND` for an arena, `ERR_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND` for a `config_profile_id`, `ERR_TENANT_NOT_FOUND` for a `tenant_id` or a `challenger_tenant_id`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND` for any booking reference in a path or body, `ERR_CHALLENGE_NOT_FOUND` for a challenge in a path, `ERR_CHANGEOVER_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` for a turnaround in a path or body, `ERR_ICE_SHEET_NOT_FOUND` for an ice sheet in a path, `ERR_DOCK_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND` for a dock reservation in a path, `ERR_LABOR_CALL_NOT_FOUND` for a labor call in a path, and `ERR_EQUIPMENT_NOT_FOUND` for either an equipment pool or an equipment issue in a path or body. All ten carry HTTP 404.

### 2.10. Validation Precedence: Identity, Then Shape, Then Safety, Then Schedule, Then Cost

When a single request could fail several checks at once, the response must name the first failure in the ladder below, never a later one. The ladder's rationale is the arena's operating philosophy: it would rather refuse a dangerous turnaround than a merely expensive one, so safety is evaluated before feasibility and feasibility before capacity and money.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` both present and the role one of the five declared names, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorisation.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised list, otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Anchor-tenant ownership scope.** `ERR_TENANT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, on exactly four operations: creating a booking, placing a hold on a booking, opening a hold challenge, and withdrawing a hold challenge. Two of those four read only the request body — creating a booking and opening a challenge compare `X-User-ID` against the `tenant_id` or `challenger_tenant_id` exactly as supplied, so the check genuinely fires at this rung, ahead of the shape checks of rung 5. The other two read a stored row, so the path identifier must resolve first: placing a hold on a booking that does not exist reports `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, and withdrawing a challenge that does not exist reports `ERR_CHALLENGE_NOT_FOUND`, each ahead of `ERR_TENANT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
4. **Segregation of duties.** `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, on turnaround sign-off only. Because the comparison reads `opened_by_user_id` off the stored order, the path identifier must resolve first: signing off a turnaround that does not exist reports `ERR_CHANGEOVER_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` ahead of `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Once the order does resolve, the check genuinely fires at this rung, ahead of the from-state check of rung 9.
5. **Request shape.** Every required input field present, every enum value a member of its declared set, every numeric field inside its declared bounds, `event_end_utc` strictly after `event_start_utc`, `window_end_utc` strictly after `window_start_utc`, `call_end_utc` strictly after `call_start_utc`, `expected_attendance` no greater than the configuration profile's `nominal_seats`, `promoter_name` present whenever `tenant_id` is absent, `damaged_units` no greater than the issue's `units_issued`, and `sheet_thickness_mm` between 18 and 45 inclusive. Any breach is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A request body that is absent, empty or not parseable as JSON is read as an empty JSON object, so such a request fails on its first missing required field rather than on any distinct parse error; a body that parses to a JSON value other than an object is itself `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every instant-valued input is parsed as ISO 8601 and normalised to UTC, so an explicit non-zero offset is honoured and converted, while a value carrying no zone designator at all is read as already being UTC. Applies to every creating or acting endpoint.
6. **Labor call scope exclusivity.** Exactly one of `changeover_order_id` and `booking_id` supplied and non-null on a labor call, otherwise `ERR_LABOR_CALL_SCOPE_INVALID`. Applies to labor call creation only.
7. **Existence.** The ten 404 codes of §2.9, path segments left to right, then body identifiers in declaration order. Applies to every endpoint.
8. **Optimistic concurrency.** `ERR_STALE_VENUE_VERSION`, on booking confirm and changeover-order creation only.
9. **From-state legality.** Whichever of the seven from-state codes belongs to the target entity, all at the same rank because only one operation in the whole surface can trip two of them — closing a labor call scoped to a turnaround, whose internal order §3.9 pins: `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE` on booking hold, confirm and release, on hold-challenge creation, on dock reservation creation and on labor call creation; `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE` on turnaround progress, rework and sign-off, on equipment issue creation, and on labor call creation and close; `ERR_INVALID_ICE_SHEET_STATE` on an ice sheet transition; `ERR_INVALID_CHALLENGE_STATE` on ruling and withdrawal; `ERR_INVALID_LABOR_CALL_STATE` on labor call close and cancel; `ERR_INVALID_ISSUE_STATE` on equipment return and on an equipment maintenance action; `ERR_INVALID_DOCK_RESERVATION_STATE` on dock reservation cancellation.
10. **Incoming booking commitment.** `ERR_INCOMING_BOOKING_NOT_CONFIRMED`, on turnaround progress only.
11. **Pool availability for issue.** `ERR_ASSET_OUT_OF_SERVICE`, on equipment issue creation only.
12. **Single-sheet exclusivity.** `ERR_ICE_SHEET_ALREADY_PRESENT`, on ice sheet installation only.
13. **Safety, in this internal order.** First `ERR_DEHUMIDIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT`, on ice sheet installation and on ice sheet transitions. Then `ERR_CONDENSATION_RISK_TOO_HIGH`, on ice sheet transitions and on turnaround sign-off. Then `ERR_ICE_EVENT_DEPENDENCY`, on ice sheet transitions. Then `ERR_FLOOR_PROTECTION_SHORTFALL`, on turnaround progress.
14. **Booking sequence structure.** `ERR_ORDER_BOOKING_SEQUENCE_INVALID`, on changeover-order creation. This structural check deliberately precedes all window arithmetic so that an inverted pair of bookings reports the sequencing fault rather than a negative-length window.
15. **Temporal feasibility, in this internal order.** `ERR_DOCK_WINDOW_CONFLICT` on dock reservation creation; then `ERR_CHANGEOVER_WINDOW_CONFLICT` on changeover-order creation; then `ERR_TURNAROUND_WINDOW_INSUFFICIENT` on changeover-order creation; then `ERR_BOOKING_TIME_CONFLICT` on booking confirm; then `ERR_HOLD_EXPIRED` on booking confirm.
16. **Constraint satisfaction, in this internal order.** `ERR_CREW_CAP_EXCEEDED` on changeover-order creation; then `ERR_DOCK_NUMBER_INVALID` on dock reservation creation; then `ERR_EQUIPMENT_UNITS_UNAVAILABLE` on equipment issue creation; then `ERR_ANCHOR_HOLD_PROTECTED` on a challenge ruling; then `ERR_HOLD_PRIORITY_INSUFFICIENT` on a challenge ruling; then `ERR_CHALLENGE_CREDITS_EXHAUSTED` on hold-challenge creation; then `ERR_CHANGEOVER_DEBT_FREEZE` on changeover-order creation.
17. **Referential scoping.** `ERR_VENUE_SCOPE_MISMATCH` on booking creation, changeover-order creation, hold-challenge creation, equipment issue creation and ice sheet installation; then `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHALLENGE` on hold-challenge creation.
18. **Fiscal capacity, last.** `ERR_RIGGING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` on booking confirm; then `ERR_TENANT_HOLD_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` on placing a hold.

Two consequences of this ladder are worth spelling out because they are easy to get backwards. Confirming a booking evaluates, in order: the stale-version check, then the from-state check, then the event-interval conflict, then hold expiry, and only last the roof-grid capacity check — so a confirm that is both expired and over capacity reports `ERR_HOLD_EXPIRED`. Opening a turnaround evaluates, in order: the stale-version check, then the booking-sequence check, then the window conflict, then window sufficiency, then the crew cap, then the debt freeze, then venue scoping — so a create that both exceeds the crew cap and hits a frozen debt ledger reports `ERR_CREW_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

### 2.11. Derived Counters and Cascade Propagation

The arena row carries four derived operational fields that are never written directly by a client. `ice_deck_status` mirrors the arena's single ice sheet outside `REMOVED` and reads `NONE` when the arena has no such sheet; its possible values are `NONE`, `BUILDING`, `IN_SERVICE`, `COVERED` and `MELTING`. `open_hold_count` is the number of bookings on the arena whose `status` is either `HELD` or `CHALLENGED`. `rigging_committed_load_kg` is the aggregate suspended load currently hanging, defined in §3.3; because it is evaluated against the logical clock afresh every time the arena is read, moving the clock changes the figure the arena reports without mutating the arena row or advancing its `version`. `changeover_debt_minutes` is a running overrun ledger defined in §3.8. Anchor tenants carry two derived counters in the same spirit: `holds_consumed` is the number of bookings naming that tenant whose `status` is `HELD`, `CHALLENGED` or `CONFIRMED`, and `challenge_credits` is the tenant's remaining challenge budget.

Cascades in this system are transitive, and each hop must be applied in full. Whenever a booking leaves the live calendar — by release from any live state, or by being displaced — two things happen to its dependents. Every dock slot reservation attached to that booking whose `status` is `RESERVED` moves to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to `BOOKING_GONE`. Every changeover order that names the booking as either its outgoing or its incoming event, and whose `status` is neither `SIGNED_OFF` nor `ABANDONED`, moves to `ABANDONED`.

Abandoning a changeover order is itself a cascade origin. Every equipment issue on that order whose `status` is `ISSUED` moves to `RETURNED` with `damaged_units` forced to 0 and `return_reason` set to `ORDER_ABANDONED`, returning its full unit count to the pool. Every labor call on that order whose `status` is `CALLED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_fee_cents` computed from the notice bands of §3.9. A booking release can therefore reach four entity types in three hops: booking, then dock reservations and changeover orders, then equipment issues and labor calls.

Every entity a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented by 1, exactly as if it had been mutated directly. Derived counters on the arena and on affected anchor tenants are recomputed as part of the same atomic operation, so a client reading any of them immediately after a cascading write observes the settled values rather than an intermediate state.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up an Arena and Its House Configurations

A `VenueOperationsDirector` brings a building into the system by registering it with the physical envelope that every later rule reads. The registration supplies a `name` between 1 and 120 characters; a `rigging_capacity_kg` greater than zero, being the total combined suspended load the roof grid may carry across all time-overlapping committed events; a `dock_count` between 1 and 8, which fixes the addressable truck docks; a `max_bowl_seats` greater than zero for the full-bowl capacity of the building; and a `crew_headcount_cap` between 1 and 200, the largest changeover crew the building may staff on a single shift.

Three further registration inputs are optional and carry defaults, and each of them silently governs a temporal rule elsewhere in the system. `min_turnaround_buffer_minutes` is the mandatory idle buffer inserted after an outgoing event's end before a changeover window may open; it accepts 0 through 240 and defaults to three quarters of an hour. `dock_buffer_minutes` is the mandatory separation between two live reservations on the same dock; it accepts 0 through 180 and defaults to half an hour. `overnight_shift_start_local_hour` is the arena-local hour from which a shift counts as overnight; it accepts 18 through 23 and defaults to 22. A newly registered arena starts with `ice_deck_status` reading `NONE`, `rigging_committed_load_kg` at 0, `open_hold_count` at 0, `changeover_debt_minutes` at 0 and `version` at 1.

House configurations are declared next, one **configuration profile** per way the building can be set. Each profile belongs to the arena named in its path and records that arena's `venue_id`. Its `code` names the configuration and is one of `ICE_HOCKEY`, `COURT_BASKETBALL`, `CONCERT_END_STAGE`, `CONCERT_IN_ROUND` or `FAMILY_SHOW`. Two booleans describe the profile's relationship to the ice: `requires_ice` is true when the configuration is played on exposed ice, and `ice_cover_required` is true when the configuration sits on top of a covered ice deck rather than demanding that the ice be melted out first. `seating_mode` describes the bowl treatment and is one of `FULL_BOWL`, `RETRACTABLE_IN` or `CURTAINED_HALF`. `nominal_seats`, greater than zero, is the number of seats sellable in this configuration and is the ceiling every booking's forecast attendance is measured against.

The remaining profile inputs are the raw material of turnaround planning. `base_changeover_minutes`, between 30 and 1440, is the baseline crew-normalised time to convert *into* this configuration. `crew_size_required`, between 1 and 200, is the nominal crew this profile is costed against and serves as the divisor of the crew-productivity factor in §3.7. `floor_protection_panels_required`, between 0 and 2000, counts the individual floor-protection panels laid for this configuration. `dehumidification_required_lph`, at least zero, is the litres-per-hour of moisture removal the configuration demands of the ice deck beneath it, and is zero for configurations with no ice underneath. `rigging_points_required`, between 0 and 400, records how many roof rigging points the configuration hangs from. A configuration profile is immutable once declared; it is only ever created and read. The arena's profile list can be narrowed by `code`.

### 3.2. Anchor Tenants, Hold Entitlements and Challenge Credits

Anchor tenants are the resident teams and promoters whose long-term contracts give them first claim on the calendar. A `VenueOperationsDirector` registers one against an arena, recording that arena as the tenant's `venue_id`, a `name` between 1 and 120 characters, a contractual `hold_priority_tier` of `TIER_1`, `TIER_2` or `TIER_3`, and a `guaranteed_holds_per_season` between 1 and 60 that caps how many concurrent holds the tenant is entitled to. The tenant list for an arena can be filtered by `hold_priority_tier`.

Priority runs strictly downhill: `TIER_1` outranks `TIER_2`, which outranks `TIER_3`, and every one of the three outranks a promoter booking, whose hold sits at the pseudo-tier `OPEN`. That ordering is the whole reason the challenge machinery of §3.4 exists.

Two counters live on the tenant and are maintained by the system rather than by the client. `holds_consumed` counts the tenant's bookings that are currently `HELD`, `CHALLENGED` or `CONFIRMED`, and is the figure measured against `guaranteed_holds_per_season` when a new hold is placed. `challenge_credits` is the tenant's remaining budget of hold challenges; a freshly registered tenant is granted exactly two credits. A credit is spent when the tenant opens a challenge and is restored only when the tenant itself withdraws that challenge — a challenge that is ruled on, in either direction, consumes the credit permanently. Both the spend and the restore rewrite `challenge_credits` on the tenant row in place without refreshing the tenant's `updated_at` or advancing its `version`, so a tenant that has spent credits still reports `version` 1 and an `updated_at` equal to its `created_at`.

The tenant's tier also fixes how long its holds survive without confirmation. A `TIER_1` hold runs for fourteen days from the instant it is placed; a `TIER_2` hold runs for seven days; a `TIER_3` hold runs for three days; and a promoter hold at `OPEN` runs for twenty-four hours. Those windows are contractual, not advisory: §3.5 describes the sweep that harvests holds which outlive them.

### 3.3. The Event Booking Lifecycle: Draft, Hold and Commitment

An **event booking** is one show on one arena in one house configuration, and it moves through six states. It begins as `DRAFT`, becomes `HELD` when the contractual hold is placed, may be pushed into `CHALLENGED` while a rival tenant contests that hold, becomes `CONFIRMED` when the calendar commitment is made, and finishes either as `RELEASED` or as `DISPLACED`. `RELEASED` and `DISPLACED` are terminal; nothing leaves them.

Either a `BookingManager` or an `AnchorTenantRep` drafts the booking. The payload names the arena as `venue_id` and the house configuration as `config_profile_id`, gives the show a `title` of 1 to 160 characters, and pins the show's own footprint on the calendar with `event_start_utc` and `event_end_utc` — the doors-to-curtain start and the end instant, both ISO 8601 UTC, with the end strictly after the start. `expected_attendance` is a forecast of at least zero that must not exceed the `nominal_seats` of the named configuration profile; a forecast above that ceiling is a malformed request. Because that ceiling can only be read once the profile has been resolved, this one shape check runs after the profile's existence check rather than before it: a draft naming a profile that does not exist reports `ERR_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND` however large the forecast, while a forecast over the ceiling of a profile that does exist reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` ahead of any arena-scoping fault. Supplying `tenant_id` makes the booking an anchor booking; omitting it makes it a promoter booking, in which case `promoter_name` becomes mandatory — a string of 1 to 160 characters — and the booking will hold at the pseudo-tier `OPEN`. A caller acting as an `AnchorTenantRep` must name its own tenant: the supplied `tenant_id` has to equal `X-User-ID`, so that role cannot draft a promoter booking at all and a draft from it that omits `tenant_id` is `ERR_TENANT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` rather than a promoter draft. Only a `BookingManager` can create a promoter booking. `rigging_load_kg` declares the suspended load the show hangs from the roof grid; it must be at least zero and defaults to zero when omitted. Both the named configuration profile and, when present, the named anchor tenant must belong to the same arena as the booking, or the draft is rejected with `ERR_VENUE_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. A fresh draft has `status` reading `DRAFT`; `hold_tier`, `hold_placed_at`, `hold_expires_at`, `release_reason` and `displaced_by_booking_id` all unset; and `displacement_penalty_cents` at 0. `displaced_by_booking_id` stays unset unless and until the booking is driven to `DISPLACED` by an upheld challenge.

Placing the hold is the first commitment. It requires the booking to be `DRAFT` — any other state is `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE` — and, for an anchor booking, that the tenant's `holds_consumed` is strictly below its `guaranteed_holds_per_season`, otherwise `ERR_TENANT_HOLD_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`. When the caller acts as an `AnchorTenantRep`, `X-User-ID` must equal the booking's `tenant_id` or the request is `ERR_TENANT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. The operation carries no request body. On acceptance the booking moves `DRAFT → HELD`, `hold_placed_at` is stamped with the logical clock instant, and `hold_tier` is **snapshotted** — taken from the tenant's `hold_priority_tier` for an anchor booking, or set to `OPEN` for a promoter booking. That snapshot is never re-read from the tenant afterwards; if the tenant later renegotiates its contractual tier, holds already placed keep the tier they were placed at. `hold_expires_at` is then computed as `hold_placed_at` advanced by the pinned tier's hold window from §3.2 — fourteen days for `TIER_1`, seven days for `TIER_2`, three days for `TIER_3`, twenty-four hours for `OPEN` — using whole-hour arithmetic and rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. The tenant's `holds_consumed` and the arena's `open_hold_count` are recomputed, each rising by one, and the arena's `version` advances by one.

Confirmation converts a hold into a committed calendar slot and is restricted to a `BookingManager`. It is the more heavily guarded of the two transitions because it commits arena-wide scarce capacity, and it is one of only two operations that carry `expected_venue_version` (§2.6). After the stale-version check and the requirement that the booking be `HELD`, three domain checks run in a fixed order. First, no other booking on the same arena whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` may overlap this booking's event interval under the unbuffered half-open convention of §2.7; an overlap is `ERR_BOOKING_TIME_CONFLICT`. Second, the logical clock must be at or before `hold_expires_at`; confirming at exactly the expiry instant still succeeds, and confirming strictly after it is `ERR_HOLD_EXPIRED`. Third and last, the roof grid must have room, as described in the next paragraph. On success the booking moves `HELD → CONFIRMED`, the arena's `open_hold_count` drops by one, `rigging_committed_load_kg` is recomputed, and the arena's `version` advances by one.

The roof-grid check is an aggregation, not a per-event limit. The system gathers every *other* booking on the same arena whose `status` is `CONFIRMED`, then keeps those whose event interval overlaps the candidate's under the closed-interval convention of §2.7 — that is, the peer starts at or before the candidate ends and the candidate starts at or before the peer ends. The `rigging_load_kg` values of the kept peers are summed and that peer aggregate is quantised to two decimal places, and only then is the candidate's own `rigging_load_kg` added to it and the result quantised to two decimal places again. The two quantisations are deliberately separate rather than collapsed into one rounding of a single exact grand total, so declared loads carrying a third decimal place can settle the comparison either way against what one rounding would have given. The confirm is accepted when the result is less than or equal to the arena's `rigging_capacity_kg`; a load landing exactly on capacity is accepted, and anything above it is `ERR_RIGGING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because the result is a sum, the order in which peers are visited cannot change it. Bookings in `DRAFT`, `HELD`, `CHALLENGED`, `RELEASED` or `DISPLACED` contribute nothing to the figure. The stored arena field `rigging_committed_load_kg` uses exactly the same closed-interval arithmetic with the logical clock instant standing in as a zero-length candidate window: it is the sum of `rigging_load_kg` over confirmed bookings whose closed event interval contains the current instant. Both figures are carried to two decimal places, rounded half up.

Bookings are readable individually — the response carries the pinned `hold_tier`, the computed `hold_expires_at`, the settlement fields and the self-referential `displaced_by_booking_id` that points at whichever booking took this one's slot — and as a filtered list. The list serves reverse reachability for all four of a booking's references and can be narrowed by `venue_id`, `tenant_id`, `config_profile_id`, `displaced_by_booking_id` and `status`.

### 3.4. Contesting an Incumbent Hold: Challenges, Rulings and Displacement

When a resident team wants a date that a weaker-priority hold is already sitting on, it does not negotiate — it spends a credit. A **hold challenge** is opened by an `AnchorTenantRep` and moves through four states: it is created `OPEN` and finishes as `UPHELD`, `DENIED` or `WITHDRAWN`, all three of which are terminal.

Opening a challenge names three things: `booking_id`, the incumbent booking being contested; `challenger_tenant_id`, the tenant spending the credit; and `challenger_booking_id`, the challenger's own draft booking that would take the slot if the challenge succeeds. Seven conditions guard the creation, and they are evaluated in the rung order of §2.10 rather than the order this paragraph happens to list them — in particular the credit budget sits at rung 16 while both scoping faults sit at rung 17, so a request from a tenant that is out of credits *and* attached to the wrong arena reports `ERR_CHALLENGE_CREDITS_EXHAUSTED`. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal `challenger_tenant_id`, otherwise `ERR_TENANT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. The incumbent booking must be `HELD` or `CHALLENGED` — a second anchor tenant is expressly permitted to open its own challenge against a hold that is already contested — and the challenger's own booking must be `DRAFT`; either violation is `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`. The incumbent and challenger bookings must share the same `venue_id`, and the challenging tenant's own `venue_id` must equal the incumbent booking's arena; either mismatch is `ERR_VENUE_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. The challenging tenant must have at least one remaining `challenge_credits`, otherwise `ERR_CHALLENGE_CREDITS_EXHAUSTED`. And no challenge may already exist with the same `booking_id` and the same `challenger_tenant_id` in `OPEN` status, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHALLENGE`.

On acceptance the challenge is created `OPEN`, its `priority_delta` is computed and frozen, the challenging tenant's `challenge_credits` drops by one, and the arena's `version` advances by one. If the incumbent booking is still `HELD`, it moves `HELD → CHALLENGED`; if it is already `CHALLENGED` because another tenant got there first, it stays `CHALLENGED` and its hold fields are left exactly as they are. Being challenged does **not** buy the incumbent any time: `hold_expires_at` is not extended, and the original expiry keeps running while the challenge is pending.

`priority_delta` is the arithmetic heart of the mechanism. Each tier maps to an ordinal in which a *smaller* number is a *stronger* contractual claim: `TIER_1` maps to 1, `TIER_2` to 2, `TIER_3` to 3, and `OPEN` to 4. The delta is the ordinal of the incumbent booking's **pinned** `hold_tier` minus the ordinal of the challenging tenant's current `hold_priority_tier`, so it lands somewhere between negative three and positive three. A strictly positive delta means the challenger outranks the incumbent's pinned tier. Note carefully which side is snapshotted: the incumbent's tier comes from the `hold_tier` frozen on its booking at the moment its hold was placed, never from the incumbent tenant's present-day `hold_priority_tier`, while the challenger's tier is read live from its tenant record. The delta is computed once, at challenge creation, and is re-read as stored when the challenge is later ruled on — it is never recomputed.

A `BookingManager` rules on an open challenge by supplying a `decision` of either `UPHELD` or `DENIED`, optionally with a `ruling_note` of 1 to 1000 characters carrying the free-text rationale. The challenge must be `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CHALLENGE_STATE`. A `DENIED` ruling has no further preconditions: `ruled_at` is stamped with the logical clock instant, the `ruling_note` is stored when supplied, and — provided the incumbent booking is still `CHALLENGED` and no other challenge against it remains `OPEN` — the incumbent moves `CHALLENGED → HELD` with `hold_tier`, `hold_placed_at` and `hold_expires_at` left exactly as they were before the contest. That is the closing half of the `HELD → CHALLENGED → HELD` cycle, and a booking may traverse it repeatedly. A denial does not refund the credit; a spent challenge that was actually adjudicated stays spent.

An `UPHELD` ruling is guarded by two further checks, evaluated in this order. The incumbent booking's pinned `hold_tier` must not be `TIER_1`, because a top-tier anchor hold can never be displaced; an attempt is `ERR_ANCHOR_HOLD_PROTECTED`. Then the stored `priority_delta` must be at least 1; a delta of exactly zero, meaning equal tiers, is insufficient, while a delta of exactly 1 suffices, and anything below 1 is `ERR_HOLD_PRIORITY_INSUFFICIENT`. When both pass, `ruled_at` and `ruling_note` are written, the credit stays spent, and the incumbent booking is driven `CHALLENGED → DISPLACED`.

Displacement is the most far-reaching single event in the system. The displaced booking records `displaced_by_booking_id` pointing at the challenge's `challenger_booking_id`, and its `displacement_penalty_cents` is computed by the settlement waterfall below. Its `release_reason` stays unset, because a displacement is not a release. From there the cascade of §2.11 runs in full: the booking's reserved dock windows move to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` of `BOOKING_GONE`; every non-terminal changeover order referencing the booking on either side moves to `ABANDONED`, which in turn auto-returns that order's issued equipment with `return_reason` of `ORDER_ABANDONED` and cancels its outstanding labor calls with a notice-band fee. The displaced tenant's `holds_consumed` falls by one when the booking named a tenant, the arena's `open_hold_count` falls by one, `rigging_committed_load_kg` is recomputed, and the arena's `version` advances by one. What displacement does *not* reach is its sibling contests: any other challenge against the same booking that is still `OPEN` stays `OPEN`, because only a release drives open challenges to `DENIED`. Ruling on such a survivor is guarded by the challenge's own state alone — the incumbent booking's `status` is never re-checked at rung 9 — so a second `UPHELD` ruling against an already-displaced booking is accepted and simply re-runs the whole displacement: `displaced_by_booking_id` is rewritten to the newer challenger's booking, `displacement_penalty_cents` is recomputed from the notice period measured at the *current* clock instant, the dependent cascade runs again over whatever is still non-terminal, and the booking's and the arena's `version` each advance once more. A `DENIED` ruling on such a survivor leaves the displaced booking untouched, because the restoration step fires only on a booking still sitting at `CHALLENGED`.

The settlement figure is a four-step waterfall, and the step order is pinned because the administrative band looks circular. **Step one** reads a base amount from the displaced booking's pinned `hold_tier`: 650,000 cents for `TIER_2`, 250,000 cents for `TIER_3`, and 120,000 cents for `OPEN`. A `TIER_1` booking never reaches this calculation at all, because the protection check above refuses to displace it. The notice period is then measured as the whole number of days from the logical clock instant to the displaced booking's `event_start_utc`, discarding any partial day. A notice of at least 90 days multiplies the base by 0.25; otherwise a notice of at least 30 days multiplies it by 0.60; otherwise the base is taken in full. That product, rounded half up to whole cents, is the **subtotal**. **Step two** reads an administrative rate off the subtotal alone: 8 percent when the subtotal is at least 400,000 cents, otherwise 5 percent when the subtotal is at least 150,000 cents, otherwise 3 percent. **Step three** multiplies the subtotal by that rate and rounds half up to whole cents to give the administrative fee. **Step four** adds the administrative fee to the subtotal, with no further rounding, to give `displacement_penalty_cents`. The band in step two must be read from the subtotal and never from the sum produced in step four; reading it off the total yields a different figure whenever the subtotal sits just below a band edge. Only the incumbent booking and the logical clock feed the figure — the challenger's booking contributes nothing to it.

Withdrawal is the challenger's own escape hatch and the compensating action of the credit spend. An `AnchorTenantRep` whose `X-User-ID` equals the challenge's `challenger_tenant_id` may withdraw a challenge that is still `OPEN`; a challenge in any other state is `ERR_INVALID_CHALLENGE_STATE`, and a mismatched identity is `ERR_TENANT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. The challenge moves `OPEN → WITHDRAWN` with `ruled_at` and `ruling_note` both left unset, because a withdrawal is not a ruling; the challenging tenant's `challenge_credits` rises by one, and — provided the incumbent is still `CHALLENGED` and no other challenge against it remains `OPEN` — the incumbent returns `CHALLENGED → HELD` with its hold fields untouched. The arena's `version` advances by one. Challenges are readable individually, including the computed `priority_delta`, and as a filtered list narrowed by `booking_id`, `challenger_tenant_id`, `challenger_booking_id` and `status`.

### 3.5. Giving Dates Back: Voluntary Release and the Hold Expiry Sweep

A `BookingManager` may hand a date back from any of the four live states, supplying a `reason` of 1 to 500 characters that is recorded verbatim as the booking's `release_reason`. What else happens depends on where the booking was.

Releasing a `DRAFT` booking is the trivial case: the booking moves `DRAFT → RELEASED`, `release_reason` is written, and the arena's `version` advances by one. Nothing depends on a draft, so nothing cascades.

Releasing a `HELD` booking moves it `HELD → RELEASED` and runs the dependent cascade in full — reserved dock windows to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` of `BOOKING_GONE`, non-terminal changeover orders on either side to `ABANDONED`, and from there the equipment returns and labor cancellations of §2.11. The tenant's `holds_consumed` falls by one when the booking named a tenant, the arena's `open_hold_count` falls by one, and the arena's `version` advances by one.

Releasing a `CHALLENGED` booking does everything the held case does — the same dock reservation cascade, the same changeover-order abandonment, the same `holds_consumed` and `open_hold_count` decrements and the same arena `version` increment — and adds one step in front: every challenge against the booking that is still `OPEN` is driven to `DENIED` with `ruled_at` stamped at the logical clock instant. Those challengers do not get their credits back, because a denial still counts against the grant.

Releasing a `CONFIRMED` booking carries one extra precondition. Every changeover order whose incoming event is this booking must have a `status` other than `SIGNED_OFF`; once a turnaround has been signed off, the building has already been converted for the show and the date can no longer be quietly handed back, so the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`. When that check passes, the booking moves `CONFIRMED → RELEASED`, the same dock and changeover cascades run, the tenant's `holds_consumed` falls by one where applicable, `rigging_committed_load_kg` is recomputed because the grid just gave capacity back, and the arena's `version` advances by one.

Expired holds are harvested in bulk rather than one at a time. A `BookingManager` triggers the sweep against a single arena; it takes no request body. The sweep gathers every booking on that arena whose `status` is `HELD` or `CHALLENGED` and whose `hold_expires_at` is at or before the logical clock instant — a booking whose expiry equals the clock instant exactly *is* swept. Bookings that are `CONFIRMED`, `DRAFT`, `RELEASED` or `DISPLACED` are never swept. The gathered set is then processed in a pinned order: ascending `hold_expires_at` first, ascending `created_at` as the tie-break, and insertion order to break any remaining tie between two bookings that share both. The `id` is never consulted as an ordering key. Processing order matters because each release mutates the arena row and fires its own cascade.

Each swept booking takes the same release transition it would take individually — `HELD → RELEASED` or `CHALLENGED → RELEASED`, including the challenge-denial step for a contested booking — except that its `release_reason` is written as the literal string `HOLD_EXPIRY_SWEEP` rather than a caller-supplied reason. The response reports `swept_count`, the number of bookings released; `released_booking_ids`, their identifiers listed in the same pinned processing order; and `venue_version`, the arena's `version` after the sweep. When nothing matches, the sweep is a no-op that reports a `swept_count` of 0 and an empty `released_booking_ids` list, and the arena's `version` is **not** incremented; a sweep that releases at least one booking increments the arena's `version` by exactly one in total.

### 3.6. The Ice Deck: Flooding, the Covering Cycle and Melt-Out

An arena carries at most one live ice sheet. A `ChangeoverSupervisor` floods a new one against the arena, naming the `config_profile_id` of the configuration the sheet is being built for, the `dehumidification_lph_applied` that will actually run against the deck — a required number of at least zero, carrying no upper bound and permitted to be fractional — and optionally a `sheet_thickness_mm` between 18 and 45, likewise permitted to be fractional, that defaults to 32 millimetres. Three conditions guard the install, and they are evaluated in the rung order of §2.10 rather than the order this paragraph lists them — single-sheet exclusivity at rung 12, then the dehumidification floor at rung 13, then arena scoping at rung 17 — so an install that both under-provisions dehumidification and names a profile belonging to another arena reports `ERR_DEHUMIDIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT`. The arena must have no existing sheet outside `REMOVED`, otherwise `ERR_ICE_SHEET_ALREADY_PRESENT`. The named configuration profile must belong to this arena, otherwise `ERR_VENUE_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. And the applied dehumidification must be at least one quarter of the profile's `dehumidification_required_lph` — evaluated inclusively, so exactly a quarter of the demand passes — otherwise `ERR_DEHUMIDIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT`.

The install snapshots the profile's moisture demand onto the sheet as `required_dehumidification_lph`. That snapshot is the figure every later safety evaluation reads; it is never re-read from the configuration profile, so a profile edited afterwards has no effect on a sheet already in the ground. The sheet's own payload accordingly carries the arena as `venue_id` and that snapshotted rate, but no `config_profile_id`: the installing profile reference is consumed during the install and is not retained on the row. The new sheet starts with `status` reading `BUILDING`, `cover_cycles` at 0, `cover_hours_accumulated` at 0, an initial `condensation_risk_score`, and `cover_installed_at_utc` unset. The arena's `ice_deck_status` becomes `BUILDING` and its `version` advances by one.

A sheet's `status` moves through `BUILDING`, `IN_SERVICE`, `COVERED`, `MELTING` and finally `REMOVED`, which is terminal. The supervisor drives all of those hops through a single transition capability that takes a `to_status` — any one of the five status names — and, optionally, a revised `dehumidification_lph_applied`, bounded only at zero below and applied in the same call. Exactly five hops may be driven by a caller: `BUILDING → IN_SERVICE`, `IN_SERVICE → COVERED`, `IN_SERVICE → MELTING`, `COVERED → IN_SERVICE` and `MELTING → REMOVED`. `COVERED → MELTING` is system-only — it arises solely from the cover-cycle limit described below — and every other ordered pair, including a `to_status` naming the status the sheet already holds and any hop out of terminal `REMOVED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ICE_SHEET_STATE`. Every accepted transition recomputes `condensation_risk_score`, recomputes the arena's `ice_deck_status`, and advances both the sheet's own `version` and the arena's `version` by exactly one; no transition is exempt, and a call that carries the automatic `COVERED → MELTING` hop still increments each of those two counters only once despite changing the sheet's `status` twice. A revised `dehumidification_lph_applied` is written onto the sheet whenever the transition is accepted, whether or not the hop being driven is one the dehumidification floor guards.

`BUILDING → IN_SERVICE` puts the finished sheet into play. The only extra condition is the dehumidification floor again: after any revision supplied in the same request has been applied, `dehumidification_lph_applied` must be at least one quarter of the sheet's snapshotted `required_dehumidification_lph`, otherwise `ERR_DEHUMIDIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT`. This is the only transition the floor guards: a revision that drops below the quarter on any other hop is accepted and stored, its deficit surfacing through `condensation_risk_score` instead. The arena's `ice_deck_status` becomes `IN_SERVICE`.

`IN_SERVICE → COVERED` is the covering half of the cycle, and it is the one transition the condensation gate exists to guard: the freshly recomputed `condensation_risk_score` must be **strictly below** 40.00, so a score of exactly 40.00 is refused with `ERR_CONDENSATION_RISK_TOO_HIGH` while 39.99 is accepted. On acceptance `cover_installed_at_utc` is stamped with the logical clock instant and `cover_cycles` rises by one. The same rule applies whether the covering is requested directly by the supervisor or arrives as the automatic consequence of a covering turnaround being signed off (§3.8).

`COVERED → IN_SERVICE` uncovers the deck. The elapsed cover period — the hours from `cover_installed_at_utc` to the logical clock instant, carried to three decimal places — is added to `cover_hours_accumulated`, and `cover_installed_at_utc` is cleared. The recomputed `condensation_risk_score` will generally be higher than before, because accumulated cover hours push it up. The arena's `ice_deck_status` returns to `IN_SERVICE`. This transition closes the `IN_SERVICE ↔ COVERED` cycle, and a sheet may traverse it repeatedly until the cover-cycle limit forces a melt-out.

That limit is hard equipment policy. A sheet may be covered three times. When the increment on an `IN_SERVICE → COVERED` transition leaves `cover_cycles` strictly greater than 3 — that is, on the fourth covering — the sheet immediately takes a further `COVERED → MELTING` hop in the same operation, without any additional request. The event-dependency guard described next does **not** apply to that automatic hop; the cover-cycle limit overrides calendar convenience.

`IN_SERVICE → MELTING`, requested deliberately, is the guarded melt-out. It is refused with `ERR_ICE_EVENT_DEPENDENCY` when any booking on the arena whose `status` is `HELD`, `CHALLENGED` or `CONFIRMED`, and whose configuration profile has `requires_ice` set, has an `event_start_utc` falling inside the closed window that begins at the logical clock instant and ends twelve hours later — twelve hours being exactly how long a fresh ice build takes, so the guard refuses to melt out ice the building could not rebuild in time. `MELTING → REMOVED` retires the sheet and sets the arena's `ice_deck_status` back to `NONE`.

The condensation score itself is a weighted blend of three deficits, each expressed as a fraction between zero and one. The **dehumidification deficit** is zero outright when `required_dehumidification_lph` is zero; otherwise it is the shortfall of `dehumidification_lph_applied` against `required_dehumidification_lph` divided by `required_dehumidification_lph`, floored at zero so that over-provisioning does not produce a negative contribution. The **cover load** is `cover_hours_accumulated` divided by 72 hours — three full days of accumulated cover — capped at one, so a sheet that has spent 72 hours or more under panels over its lifetime contributes the maximum. The **thickness deficit** is the shortfall of `sheet_thickness_mm` against a nominal 32 millimetres divided by that same 32, floored at zero, so a thicker-than-nominal sheet contributes nothing. Those three are combined with weights of 0.45 for the dehumidification deficit, 0.30 for the cover load and 0.25 for the thickness deficit; the weighted sum is multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to two decimal places to give `condensation_risk_score`. Only the single ice sheet row feeds the figure. It is recomputed at install and on every transition call, so a supervisor can lower it by raising `dehumidification_lph_applied` in the same request that asks to cover the ice. The arena's sheet list includes retired sheets so that cover history is auditable, and can be narrowed by `status`.

### 3.7. Opening a Turnaround and Freezing Its Plan

A **changeover order** is the work order that converts the building from one show's configuration into the next one's. A `ChangeoverSupervisor` opens it by naming the outgoing event as `from_booking_id`, the incoming event as `to_booking_id`, the `crew_headcount` called for the turnaround — a required integer between 1 and 200, a bound independent of the arena's own `crew_headcount_cap`, which is checked separately at rung 16 — and the `expected_venue_version` that guards the write (§2.6). The order's own `venue_id` is copied from the incoming booking's arena rather than supplied by the caller, and every arena-derived quantity the operation reads is read from that same arena: the `version` compared for staleness, `min_turnaround_buffer_minutes`, `overnight_shift_start_local_hour`, `ice_deck_status`, `crew_headcount_cap` and `changeover_debt_minutes`. That choice of arena can only matter for a cross-arena pair of bookings, and such a pair is refused by the scoping check at rung 17.

Checks run in the ladder order of §2.10, and for this operation the sequence is worth restating in full because six domain checks can fire. After the stale-version check, both bookings must be in a live, plannable state — each of `from_booking_id` and `to_booking_id` must have a `status` of either `HELD` or `CONFIRMED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`. Then the structural ordering check: the incoming event's `event_start_utc` must be strictly after the outgoing event's `event_end_utc`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_BOOKING_SEQUENCE_INVALID`. That check deliberately runs before any window arithmetic so that an inverted pair of dates reports the sequencing fault rather than a nonsensical negative window. Then the arena floor must be free: no other changeover order on the same arena whose `status` is neither `SIGNED_OFF` nor `ABANDONED` may overlap this order's window under the unbuffered half-open convention of §2.7, otherwise `ERR_CHANGEOVER_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Then the window must be long enough for the frozen plan, otherwise `ERR_TURNAROUND_WINDOW_INSUFFICIENT`. Then `crew_headcount` must not exceed the arena's `crew_headcount_cap`, otherwise `ERR_CREW_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Then the arena's `changeover_debt_minutes` must be strictly below three hours of accumulated overrun, otherwise `ERR_CHANGEOVER_DEBT_FREEZE` — a building that has already blown its turnaround targets by three hours in aggregate may not open new work until a director clears the backlog. Last, both bookings must share the same arena, otherwise `ERR_VENUE_SCOPE_MISMATCH`.

The window is derived, never supplied. `window_start_utc` is the outgoing event's `event_end_utc` advanced by the arena's `min_turnaround_buffer_minutes`, the mandatory idle period the building insists on before crews go onto the floor. `window_end_utc` is the incoming event's `event_start_utc` exactly. `window_minutes` is the number of minutes between those two instants rounded **down** to a whole minute, any part-minute remainder being discarded exactly as it is for `actual_turnaround_minutes` in §3.8. Sufficiency is then measured inclusively: the order is accepted when `window_minutes` is greater than or equal to `planned_turnaround_minutes`, so a window exactly as long as the plan is accepted, and only a strictly shorter window is refused.

Whether the crew works overnight is decided from `window_start_utc` and recorded on the order as `overnight_shift`. The determination converts the UTC instant to arena-local time by subtracting four hours, takes the truncated whole hour of that local time, and reports true when that hour is greater than or equal to the arena's `overnight_shift_start_local_hour` **or** strictly less than 6. The local hour is truncated, never rounded, and the result is a plain boolean. The identical rule is applied to labor calls in §3.9, where it reads the call's own start instant instead.

`planned_turnaround_minutes` is the forward-looking plan, frozen once at creation and never recomputed for the life of the order. It is assembled from four additive components and then divided by a crew-productivity factor. The starting figure is the incoming configuration profile's `base_changeover_minutes`.

An **ice adder** accounts for what has to happen to the deck, and it has three branches. When the outgoing configuration has `requires_ice` set and the incoming one does not, the turnaround is stripping the building off the ice: the adder is 90 minutes when the incoming configuration has `ice_cover_required` set, because the ice merely gets covered, and 600 minutes — ten hours — when it does not, because the ice has to be melted out entirely. When the outgoing configuration does not require ice and the incoming one does, the turnaround is putting the building back onto ice: the adder is 75 minutes when the arena's `ice_deck_status` is either `IN_SERVICE` or `COVERED`, because there is a deck to uncover, and 720 minutes — twelve hours — otherwise, because a sheet has to be built from scratch. In every other case, including both configurations requiring ice and neither requiring it, the ice adder is zero.

A **seating adder** accounts for the bowl, read from the incoming configuration's `seating_mode`: zero minutes for `FULL_BOWL`, 120 minutes for `RETRACTABLE_IN`, and 180 minutes for `CURTAINED_HALF`. A **panel adder** accounts for floor protection: the incoming configuration's `floor_protection_panels_required` is divided by 40, the number of panels in one handling set, and rounded **up** to a whole number of sets, then multiplied by 15 minutes per set. Those four components — the base, the ice adder, the seating adder and the panel adder — are added together to give the raw minute figure.

The **crew-productivity factor** is the order's `crew_headcount` divided by the incoming configuration's `crew_size_required`, then clamped to lie between 0.5 and 2.0: a factor computed below 0.5 is raised to 0.5, and one computed above 2.0 is lowered to 2.0. That factor is an exact rational value and is never rounded before it is used. The raw minute figure is divided by the factor and the quotient is rounded **up** to a whole minute; this is the first ceiling. When and only when `overnight_shift` is true, that already-ceiled figure is then multiplied by 1.15 to reflect the overnight slowdown and the product is rounded **up** to a whole minute again; this is the second ceiling. The two ceilings are applied in exactly that order — divide and ceil, then scale and ceil — and never collapsed into a single rounding of the raw figure divided by the factor and multiplied by the slowdown. The resulting whole number of minutes is `planned_turnaround_minutes`. Note what the plan reads: two configuration profiles, the arena's `ice_deck_status` and the ordered crew size. It reads no execution history at all and is blind to rework and idle time.

A newly opened order has `status` reading `PLANNED`, all three of `floor_stage`, `seating_stage` and `infra_stage` sitting at `NOT_STARTED`, `actual_turnaround_minutes` unset, `overrun_minutes` at 0, `rework_count` at 0, `started_at_utc` and `completed_at_utc` unset, `signed_off_by_user_id` unset, `abandon_reason` unset, and `opened_by_user_id` recorded as the supervisor's `X-User-ID` — the value that segregation of duties will later compare against at sign-off. The arena's `version` advances by one. Orders are readable individually and as a filtered list narrowed by `venue_id`, `from_booking_id`, `to_booking_id` and `status`.

### 3.8. Floor Execution, Rework, Readiness and Sign-Off

A turnaround is not one job but three, worked in parallel by three crews on the same order row. The **floor dimension** tracks the physical playing surface through `floor_stage`, which runs `NOT_STARTED → STRIKING → LAYING → FLOOR_COMPLETE`. The **seating dimension** tracks the bowl through `seating_stage`, which runs `NOT_STARTED → RETRACTING → CURTAINING → SEATING_COMPLETE`. The **infrastructure dimension** tracks utilities, network and broadcast through `infra_stage`, which runs `NOT_STARTED → UTILITY_SET → NETWORK_SET → BROADCAST_READY`. The three are independent axes: they may sit at any combination of stages at any moment, and the order's compound `status` is derived from that combination rather than stored independently of it.

Each progress call names exactly one `dimension` — `FLOOR`, `SEATING` or `INFRA` — and the `to_stage` it should advance to, and it advances exactly one stage on exactly that dimension, leaving the other two untouched. For shape purposes `to_stage` is validated against the ten distinct stage names of all three dimensions taken together rather than against the named dimension's own four, so a stage name borrowed from another dimension is not a malformed request; only a string outside that union of ten is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A `to_stage` that is not the immediate successor of the named dimension's current stage is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`; crews may not skip stages or reach backwards. Progress is accepted only while the order's `status` is `PLANNED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `REWORK`, and any other status is likewise `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. Independently of stage legality, the incoming booking must already be `CONFIRMED` before any progress at all may be recorded on any dimension — a merely held incoming show is not a commitment worth converting a building for — and a progress call against an unconfirmed incoming booking is refused with `ERR_INCOMING_BOOKING_NOT_CONFIRMED`.

One dimension carries a safety gate. Advancing the floor dimension to `LAYING` means crews are about to put a surface down over the deck, and the floor protection must be physically on site first. The system sums `units_issued` across every equipment issue on this order whose `status` is `ISSUED` and whose pool has an `asset_class` of `FLOOR_PANEL_SET`, and requires that sum to be at least the incoming configuration's `floor_protection_panels_required` divided by 40 panels per set and rounded up to a whole number of sets. The comparison is inclusive, so exactly the required set count passes; anything less is refused with `ERR_FLOOR_PROTECTION_SHORTFALL`.

The first progress call accepted on an order stamps `started_at_utc` with the logical clock instant and moves the compound `status` from `PLANNED` to `IN_PROGRESS`. `started_at_utc` is written exactly once in the order's life and is never rewritten — not by later progress, and emphatically not by rework. Subsequent progress calls that leave at least one dimension short of its terminal stage keep the order in `IN_PROGRESS`. The call after which all three dimensions sit at their terminal stages simultaneously — `floor_stage` at `FLOOR_COMPLETE`, `seating_stage` at `SEATING_COMPLETE` and `infra_stage` at `BROADCAST_READY` — stamps `completed_at_utc` with the logical clock instant and moves the order to `READY_FOR_SIGNOFF`.

Work that fails inspection goes back. A supervisor reworks a dimension by naming the `dimension` and a `reason` of 1 to 500 characters — required, but validated only and persisted nowhere on the order row — and this is permitted while the order is `IN_PROGRESS` or `READY_FOR_SIGNOFF`; the rule and its effects are identical from either state. The named dimension must not already be at `NOT_STARTED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. On acceptance that one dimension resets to `NOT_STARTED` while the other two keep their stages exactly where they were, `rework_count` rises by one, `completed_at_utc` is cleared back to unset, and the compound `status` becomes `REWORK`. From `REWORK`, further progress calls are accepted on the same terms — and any such call that still leaves at least one dimension short of its terminal stage moves the compound `status` straight back to `IN_PROGRESS`, so `REWORK` survives only until the next accepted progress call on any dimension — and the call that once again leaves all three dimensions terminal re-stamps `completed_at_utc` at the current logical clock instant and returns the order to `READY_FOR_SIGNOFF`. Because `started_at_utc` survives the loop untouched while `completed_at_utc` is re-stamped, every minute the order spent in rework is absorbed into the chronological measurement described below. Rework is the order's revision loop, and it may run any number of times.

A read-only readiness projection lets any role see where an order stands. It reports the order's identifier as `order_id` alongside its `status`, `floor_stage`, `seating_stage`, `infra_stage`, `window_minutes` and `planned_turnaround_minutes`, plus `slack_minutes` — the `window_minutes` less the `planned_turnaround_minutes`, which may be negative — and `blocking_reasons`, a list of strings appended in exactly this fixed order: `INCOMING_BOOKING_NOT_CONFIRMED` when the incoming booking's `status` is anything other than `CONFIRMED`; then `FLOOR_PROTECTION_SHORTFALL` when `floor_stage` is still `NOT_STARTED` and the issued floor-panel-set units on this order fall short of the incoming configuration's panel requirement divided by 40 and rounded up; then `FLOOR_INCOMPLETE` when `floor_stage` is anything other than `FLOOR_COMPLETE`; then `SEATING_INCOMPLETE` when `seating_stage` is anything other than `SEATING_COMPLETE`; then `INFRA_INCOMPLETE` when `infra_stage` is anything other than `BROADCAST_READY`; and finally `CHANGEOVER_DEBT_FREEZE` when the arena's `changeover_debt_minutes` has reached or passed three hours. An order with nothing blocking it reports an empty list.

Sign-off belongs to a `VenueOperationsDirector`, carries no request body, and closes the order. The presented `X-User-ID` must differ from `opened_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the supervisor who opened the turnaround may never be the principal who signs it — and because that check sits at rung 4 it is evaluated ahead of the state check, so a same-principal attempt on an order that is not yet ready reports `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` rather than `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. The order must then be `READY_FOR_SIGNOFF`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. One further condition applies to **covering changeovers**, meaning turnarounds where the outgoing configuration has `requires_ice` set, the incoming configuration does not, and the incoming configuration has `ice_cover_required` set: if the arena has an ice sheet sitting `IN_SERVICE`, that sheet's freshly recomputed `condensation_risk_score` must be strictly below 40.00, otherwise the sign-off is refused with `ERR_CONDENSATION_RISK_TOO_HIGH`. A director cannot sign a covering turnaround onto a deck that would sweat.

On acceptance the order moves `READY_FOR_SIGNOFF → SIGNED_OFF` and `signed_off_by_user_id` records the director's `X-User-ID`. The chronological measurement is then written: `actual_turnaround_minutes` is the span from `started_at_utc` to `completed_at_utc`, expressed in whole minutes with any fractional minute discarded — rounded down. Because that span reads only the two logical-clock stamps, it necessarily includes every minute the order spent in `REWORK` and every idle minute between progress calls, which is precisely why it diverges from the frozen `planned_turnaround_minutes` even when the configuration inputs are identical. `overrun_minutes` is then the amount by which the actual measurement exceeds `window_minutes`, floored at zero: an order that finished inside its window records an overrun of 0.

Sign-off cascades in two directions. The arena's `changeover_debt_minutes` rises by exactly this order's `overrun_minutes`. That ledger is monotonic — it is never decremented by any operation in this system — and once it reaches or passes three hours it freezes the opening of new turnarounds, as §3.7 describes and as the readiness projection reports. Separately, on a covering changeover, the arena's `IN_SERVICE` ice sheet is automatically driven `IN_SERVICE → COVERED` with all the consequences of §3.6: `cover_installed_at_utc` stamped, `cover_cycles` incremented, the score recomputed, and the further automatic `COVERED → MELTING` hop when that increment leaves `cover_cycles` above 3. What sign-off does *not* do is settle labor: every labor call on the order whose `status` is `CALLED` is left exactly as it is, to be closed or cancelled on its own terms by a `LaborCoordinator`. The arena's `version` advances by one.

The other way an order can end is `ABANDONED`, and it is never requested directly. Whenever either of the order's bookings enters `RELEASED` or `DISPLACED`, every changeover order referencing that booking whose `status` is neither `SIGNED_OFF` nor `ABANDONED` is driven to `ABANDONED` — the rule and its consequences are identical from `PLANNED`, from `IN_PROGRESS`, from `REWORK` and from `READY_FOR_SIGNOFF`. The order records `abandon_reason` as `FROM_BOOKING_GONE` when the outgoing event went away and as `TO_BOOKING_GONE` when the incoming event did. Abandonment then reaches two child collections: every equipment issue on the order whose `status` is `ISSUED` moves to `RETURNED` with `damaged_units` forced to 0 and `return_reason` set to `ORDER_ABANDONED`, and every labor call on the order whose `status` is `CALLED` moves to `CANCELLED` with a cancellation fee computed from the notice bands of §3.9. Both `SIGNED_OFF` and `ABANDONED` are terminal.

### 3.9. The Logistics Layer: Dock Windows, Labor Calls and Equipment Pools

**Dock windows.** Trucks are the other scarce resource in a turnaround, and dock time is reserved against a booking rather than against a turnaround, because load-in and load-out belong to the show. Either a `ChangeoverSupervisor` or a `LaborCoordinator` reserves a window, supplying the `dock_number` to occupy, a `purpose` of either `LOAD_IN` or `LOAD_OUT`, the occupation's `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc` with the end strictly after the start, and a `truck_count` between 1 and 60 for the trucks expected through in that window. The booking must be `HELD`, `CHALLENGED` or `CONFIRMED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`. The collision test then runs *before* the dock-number range test, so a request that names both an out-of-range dock and a colliding window reports the collision: no other reservation on the same arena and the same `dock_number` whose `status` is `RESERVED` may collide under the buffered half-open convention of §2.7, and a collision is `ERR_DOCK_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Only then is `dock_number` checked to lie between 1 and the arena's `dock_count` inclusive, with `ERR_DOCK_NUMBER_INVALID` otherwise. A reservation is created `RESERVED`; the only other state is `RELEASED`, which is terminal. Its payload names its parent `booking_id` and carries no `venue_id` of its own, the arena being reached through that booking.

Giving a dock window back is available to either of the same two roles and carries no request body. The reservation must be `RESERVED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DOCK_RESERVATION_STATE`, and on acceptance it moves `RESERVED → RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to `OPERATOR_CANCEL`. The same transition also arrives as a cascade whenever the parent booking enters `RELEASED` or `DISPLACED`, in which case `release_reason` reads `BOOKING_GONE` instead. Either way the buffered interval on that dock number is freed, so a window that previously collided becomes bookable. A booking's reservations are listed through a reverse-reachability read that can be narrowed by `purpose` and by `status`.

**Labor calls.** A `LaborCoordinator` owns the calls and their money. A call names a `craft` of `STAGEHAND`, `RIGGER`, `ICE_TECH`, `ELECTRICIAN` or `CONVERSION_CREW`; a `headcount` between 1 and 200; a scheduled `call_start_utc` and `call_end_utc` with the end strictly after the start; and optionally a `guaranteed_hours` between 0 and 12 — a number that need not be whole — defaulting to four, which is the contractual minimum paid per worker regardless of how little work materialises. Scope is exclusive: exactly one of `changeover_order_id` and `booking_id` must be supplied and non-null, so that a call is unambiguously either changeover-crew work or a show's load-in and load-out labor. Supplying both or neither is `ERR_LABOR_CALL_SCOPE_INVALID`. A call scoped to a turnaround requires that order's `status` to be neither `SIGNED_OFF` nor `ABANDONED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`; a call scoped to a booking requires that booking's `status` to be `HELD`, `CHALLENGED` or `CONFIRMED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`. A call is created `CALLED` with `actual_hours` and `settled_cost_cents` unset and `cancellation_fee_cents` at 0, and finishes as either `WORKED` or `CANCELLED`, both terminal. Its `overnight` flag is computed at creation by the same arena-local rule §3.7 applies to turnaround windows, evaluated here on `call_start_utc` and reading `overnight_shift_start_local_hour` from the arena of whichever scope the call names — the turnaround's arena for an order-scoped call and the booking's arena for a booking-scoped one. Calls are readable individually and as a list narrowed by `changeover_order_id`, `booking_id`, `craft` and `status`.

Cost is computed twice from one formula with two inputs for billable hours, and both figures live on the row. The hourly base rate is read from the `craft`: 4,800 cents for `STAGEHAND`, 7,200 cents for `RIGGER`, 5,600 cents for `ICE_TECH`, 6,900 cents for `ELECTRICIAN` and 4,200 cents for `CONVERSION_CREW`. The **estimated** variant, frozen into `estimated_cost_cents` at creation before anyone has worked an hour, takes billable hours as the greater of `guaranteed_hours` and the scheduled span — the possibly fractional number of hours from `call_start_utc` to `call_end_utc`, measured under the whole-second rule of §2.8, so a span carrying a sub-second remainder is billed as though it had ended on the second. The **settled** variant, written into `settled_cost_cents` when the call is closed, takes billable hours as the greater of `guaranteed_hours` and the reported `actual_hours`; overtime that was never scheduled therefore surfaces only in the settled figure. From whichever billable-hours figure applies, straight hours are the lesser of that figure and eight hours, and overtime hours are the amount by which it exceeds eight hours, floored at zero. Overtime hours are weighted at one and a half times straight time. The overnight multiplier is 1.35 when the call's `overnight` flag is true and 1.0 otherwise. The per-worker amount is the base rate multiplied by the sum of straight hours and weighted overtime hours, multiplied by the overnight multiplier, and **then** rounded half up to whole cents. Only after that per-worker rounding is the amount multiplied by `headcount` to give the stored figure. Rounding the aggregate instead of the per-worker amount is explicitly not the rule and produces a different total whenever the per-worker amount lands on a half cent. Neither variant aggregates across sibling calls; each call is costed entirely from its own row.

Closing a call reports the hours actually worked per worker as `actual_hours`, between 0 and 24 and again not necessarily whole. The call must be `CALLED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_LABOR_CALL_STATE`. When the call is scoped to a turnaround, that order's `status` must be either `READY_FOR_SIGNOFF` or `SIGNED_OFF` — hours cannot be settled against work that is still in progress — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. This close is the one operation whose single request can trip two of the rung-9 from-state codes at once, and the call's own state is evaluated first: closing an already-worked call scoped to a turnaround that is still in progress reports `ERR_INVALID_LABOR_CALL_STATE`. On acceptance the call moves `CALLED → WORKED`, `actual_hours` is stored, and `settled_cost_cents` is written from the settled variant.

Cancelling a call charges a notice-band fee against the frozen estimate and carries no request body. The call must be `CALLED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_LABOR_CALL_STATE`. The notice period is the number of hours from the logical clock instant to `call_start_utc`, and it may be negative when the call start has already passed. Notice of at least 48 hours attracts no fee at all; otherwise notice of at least 12 hours attracts a quarter of the estimate; otherwise half the estimate is charged. Both comparisons are inclusive, so exactly 48 hours notice is free and exactly 12 hours notice is the quarter band. `cancellation_fee_cents` is that fraction of `estimated_cost_cents` — read as stored and never recomputed — rounded half up to whole cents. The call moves `CALLED → CANCELLED` and `settled_cost_cents` stays unset, because cancelled work is never settled. The identical fee rule applies whether the cancellation was requested by the coordinator or arrived as the cascade from a turnaround entering `ABANDONED`.

**Equipment pools.** A `VenueOperationsDirector` creates each pool against an arena with an `asset_class` of `STAGE_DECK`, `DASHER_BOARD_SET`, `FLOOR_PANEL_SET`, `CURTAIN_SYSTEM`, `ICE_COVER_PANEL_SET` or `BASKETBALL_FLOOR`; a `units_total` between 1 and 500, which is the conserved quantity of the whole inventory; and optionally a `service_interval_cycles` between 1 and 200, defaulting to twelve, being the number of issue-and-return cycles between mandatory servicing. A new pool has `units_available` equal to `units_total`, `units_out_of_service` at 0, `cycles_since_service` at 0, `service_due` false and `status` reading `IN_SERVICE`, the only alternative being `OUT_OF_SERVICE`. Pools are readable individually and through an arena-scoped list narrowed by `asset_class` and `status`.

Availability is always derived, never written directly: `units_available` is `units_total` less `units_out_of_service` less the sum of `units_issued` across every equipment issue on that pool whose `status` is `ISSUED`. Returned issues contribute nothing. The invariant that must hold after every mutation is that available units plus outstanding issued units plus out-of-service units equal `units_total` exactly; the value is a whole number of units and can never go negative, because the availability check on issue keeps it non-negative. `service_due` is likewise derived, and it is evaluated inclusively: the flag is true as soon as `cycles_since_service` reaches `service_interval_cycles`, so the interval-th cycle already flags the pool rather than the one after it.

Maintenance is driven by an `action` of `START` or `COMPLETE`. A `START` takes the pool `IN_SERVICE → OUT_OF_SERVICE`, and it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ISSUE_STATE` when the pool still has any equipment issue in `ISSUED` status — units cannot be serviced while they are out on a floor — and likewise when the pool is already `OUT_OF_SERVICE`. Starting maintenance moves no units, so `units_available` is recomputed to the same figure it already held. A `COMPLETE` takes the pool `OUT_OF_SERVICE → IN_SERVICE` and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ISSUE_STATE` when the pool is already `IN_SERVICE`. Completing maintenance repairs damaged stock back into the pool: `units_out_of_service` is reset to 0, `cycles_since_service` is reset to 0, `service_due` becomes false, and `units_available` is recomputed as `units_total` less whatever units are still outstanding on issue.

**Equipment issues.** An issue is the many-to-many junction between a pool and a turnaround. A `ChangeoverSupervisor` creates one naming `asset_id`, `changeover_order_id` and a `units_issued` of at least 1. Four conditions guard it, in this order: the order's `status` must be neither `SIGNED_OFF` nor `ABANDONED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`; the pool's `status` must be `IN_SERVICE`, otherwise `ERR_ASSET_OUT_OF_SERVICE`; `units_issued` must not exceed the pool's `units_available`, evaluated inclusively so that taking the last available unit succeeds, otherwise `ERR_EQUIPMENT_UNITS_UNAVAILABLE`; and the pool's arena must be the same arena as the order's, otherwise `ERR_VENUE_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. The issue is created `ISSUED` — the only other state is `RETURNED`, which is terminal — and the pool's `units_available` falls by `units_issued`. Because that figure is derived rather than stored, issuing units writes no field of the pool row itself, so the pool's `version` and `updated_at` are left exactly as they were even though the availability it reports has changed; a return, which does write `units_out_of_service` and `cycles_since_service`, advances the pool's `version` by one. Issues are readable individually and as a list narrowed by `asset_id`, `changeover_order_id` and `status`.

Returning an issue is its compensating action and optionally reports damage. The request may supply `damaged_units`, defaulting to 0 and never negative; a figure above the issue's own `units_issued` is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Because that ceiling can only be read once the issue itself has been resolved, this one shape check runs after the path identifier's existence check: a return naming an issue that does not exist reports `ERR_EQUIPMENT_NOT_FOUND` however large the reported damage, while a negative `damaged_units` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` ahead of that existence check. The issue must be `ISSUED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ISSUE_STATE`. On acceptance the issue moves `ISSUED → RETURNED`, `returned_at_utc` is stamped with the logical clock instant, and `return_reason` is recorded as `OPERATOR_RETURN`. The pool's `units_out_of_service` rises by `damaged_units`, and its `units_available` rises by the undamaged remainder — `units_issued` less `damaged_units` — so that damaged stock is withdrawn from circulation without disturbing the conserved `units_total`. The pool's `cycles_since_service` rises by one and `service_due` is re-evaluated against `service_interval_cycles`. The same transition arrives automatically when the parent turnaround enters `ABANDONED`, except that `damaged_units` is forced to 0 and `return_reason` reads `ORDER_ABANDONED`, so an abandoned order always gives its units back whole.
