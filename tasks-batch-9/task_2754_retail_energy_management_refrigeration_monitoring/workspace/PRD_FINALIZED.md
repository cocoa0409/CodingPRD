# Retail Energy Refrigeration Ops API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A national retail chain spends more on electricity than on any operating line other than labour, and almost every dollar of that spend is decided by equipment that nobody watches in real time: rooftop units chasing a setpoint that was standardised three revisions ago, sales-floor lighting that stays on because a manager extended a schedule and forgot, a refrigerated case drifting warm at two in the morning while the store is enrolled in a demand-response event that is about to ask it to shed load. The Retail Energy Refrigeration Ops API is the system of record that ties those decisions together. It holds the portfolio of stores, the setpoint standards that govern them by climate zone and store format, the utility accounts and metered demand intervals behind every invoice, the refrigeration cases and their temperature excursions, the lighting overrides that defeat automatic control, the fault diagnostics that anticipate equipment failure, and the capital retrofit projects that are supposed to pay for themselves.

The organising idea of the product is that food safety outranks energy savings, always, and that the system must be able to prove it. Every store carries a derived **energy posture** computed from four different families of children — refrigeration alarms, demand-response participation, building-management-system health, and urgent maintenance backlog — and that single posture value gates whether the store may join a curtailment event, whether a manager may extend the lighting schedule, and whether a new setpoint standard may be commissioned. A temperature excursion that escalates does not merely raise an alarm: it locks the store, opts the store's account out of any demand-response event it has been notified of, revokes the schedule-extension overrides that are burning energy, and flags every pending capital proposal for a second look. Conversely, releasing a posture is never automatic; it happens only when the underlying condition has actually been cleared and documented.

The second organising idea is financial defensibility. Utility invoices are not trusted; they are independently recomputed from the account's own rate schedule and the store's own metered intervals, with the demand component selected by one of two peak-detection variants depending on the tariff family, and the result compared against the printed total inside a fixed tolerance. Tariff switches must be justified by a projection, not a hunch. Measured retrofit savings must clear a realization floor before a single kilowatt-hour reaches the savings ledger, and a revoked verification must debit the ledger by exactly what it credited. The same person may not both recompute a bill and authorise its adjustment, nor both propose a capital measure and approve it. This document specifies those rules in full, together with the API contract that exposes them.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the system-wide contract: authentication, response shape, pagination, identity and versioning, the logical clock, rejection precedence, numeric discipline, concurrency protection, and the cross-cutting posture and duty-separation principles that every workflow in Section 3 depends on. All resources are mounted beneath the `/energy-ops` prefix.

### 2.1. Operator Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that operator is acting. A request that omits `X-User-ID`, or supplies it as an empty string, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other consideration — including before the request body is parsed. When the headers are present but the declared role is not permitted to invoke the requested operation, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Seven roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `PlatformAdmin`, `EnergyManager`, `EnergyAnalyst`, `StoreManager`, `FoodSafetyOfficer`, `MaintenanceTech`, and `BmsGateway`.

Five of these roles are **opaque operators**. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example as `published_by_user_id` on a setpoint standard when a publication writes it) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `EnergyManager`, whose identifier is recorded as `published_by_user_id`, `adjusted_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id` and `revoked_by_user_id`; for `EnergyAnalyst`, whose identifier is recorded as `recomputed_by_user_id` and `proposed_by_user_id`; for `FoodSafetyOfficer`, whose identifier is recorded as `documented_by_user_id`; and for `MaintenanceTech`, whose identifier is recorded as `triaged_by_user_id`. None of these five identifiers is resolved against a stored row.

Two roles are **entity-backed on the store**. When `X-User-Role` is `StoreManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Store`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Store` compare the resource's store-referencing field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `BmsGateway`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the `Store` whose building-management-system gateway is calling; ownership checks compare the resource's store-referencing field to `X-User-ID`. For these two roles the comparison follows the reachable path as well as the direct one: a refrigeration case is scoped through its owning store, an excursion through its case's owning store, and a utility account through the store it hangs from. A scope breach is rejected with `ERR_STORE_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

Role authority is summarised in the endpoint table of Section 4. In narrative terms: `PlatformAdmin` onboards stores and owns the logical clock; `EnergyManager` owns setpoint standards, BMS deployment, utility account attachment, tariff strategy, bill adjustment authorisation, demand-response participation, override revocation and capital approvals; `EnergyAnalyst` records and validates bills, settles demand-response performance and verifies measured savings; `StoreManager` requests lighting overrides and acknowledges excursions for its own store; `FoodSafetyOfficer` documents dispositions and closes excursions; `MaintenanceTech` bypasses and rearms cases, triages faults and executes work orders; `BmsGateway` ingests temperature readings and metered demand intervals and reports fault diagnostics for its own store.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds, or one that is not an integer, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless the narrative for a particular collection states otherwise. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page. Single-resource reads omit those four fields from `meta`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Version Counters and Audit Timestamps

Every persisted resource is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix — clients never supply it. A request body that nevertheless carries `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version` or any other server-owned or derived field is not rejected for it: the key is simply ignored, and the server writes its own value. The same holds for the field by which a nested resource points at its parent — `store_id`, `utility_account_id`, `case_id` and `fault_id` are taken from the resolved path, never from the body, so a body `store_id` naming some other store does not move the resource. (Body fields that are genuine inputs referencing another resource, such as `standard_id` when commissioning and `supersedes_standard_id` when drafting a standard, are of course read from the body as their narratives describe.) Alongside it, resources carry `created_at` and `updated_at`, both ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional digits, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock on every mutation.

One entity is deliberately leaner. A metered demand interval is an immutable event: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version`, because nothing ever modifies it after ingestion. Every other entity in the system — stores, setpoint standards, utility accounts, utility bills, demand-response events, refrigeration cases, temperature excursions, lighting overrides, fault diagnostics, maintenance work orders and retrofit projects — carries the full quartet of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

Version increments are load-bearing, not cosmetic. When a cascade touches a parent that the caller did not name in the request — a store degraded because the standard it was bound to was superseded, for instance — that parent's `version` still advances and its `updated_at` still refreshes. This is what makes the stale-view protection described in §2.9 meaningful.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Scheduled Actions

Nothing in this API reads wall-clock time. Every timestamp stamped onto a resource, every comparison against a window boundary, and every trailing-window aggregation reads a single server-wide **logical clock**. At server start the clock stands at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A `PlatformAdmin` may move it forward by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-control capability, which both sets the clock and runs the scheduled-action sweep in a fixed order: first, every demand-response event whose participation dimension is `ACTIVE` and whose curtailment end instant is at or before the new clock value is completed; second, every lighting override sitting in `SCHEDULED` whose window now contains the new clock value is activated; third, every lighting override in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` whose window has ended at or before the new clock value is expired; fourth, every store touched by any of the first three steps recomputes its energy posture. The capability reports what it did as `current_utc`, `dr_events_completed`, `overrides_activated`, `overrides_expired` and `stores_posture_recomputed`. The sweep is idempotent: setting the clock twice to the same instant reports zero for all four counters on the second call, because no row remains eligible.

### 2.6. Path Resolution Order for Nested Energy Resources

Several collections are addressed through their parent — a store's utility accounts, refrigeration cases, lighting overrides, fault diagnostics and retrofit projects; an account's bills, demand intervals and demand-response events; a case's excursions; a fault's work orders. Path segments are always resolved left to right, and the leftmost unresolvable segment determines the error. A request naming an unknown store returns `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND` even if the nested identifier is also nonsense. Once resolution succeeds, the nested identifier must genuinely belong to the named parent; a mismatch is treated as non-existence of the child.

Each entity family has its own not-found code, and when several could apply they are evaluated in this fixed order: store, setpoint standard, utility account, utility bill, demand-response event, refrigeration case, temperature excursion, lighting override, fault diagnostic, maintenance work order, retrofit project — yielding respectively `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BILL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DR_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXCURSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OVERRIDE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FAULT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_RETROFIT_NOT_FOUND`. All eleven are HTTP 404. A setpoint standard named in the body rather than the path — as `standard_id` when commissioning, or as `supersedes_standard_id` when drafting — is subject to the same `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_FOUND` check.

### 2.7. Rejection Precedence: How Competing Failures Are Ranked

Many requests can fail for more than one reason at once, and the response must be deterministic. Checks are therefore evaluated in a single global order, and the **first** violated check determines the response; later checks are never reached and never reported. The order is:

1. Presence of `X-User-ID` — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role permission for the endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. Schema validity of the body and query parameters — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This covers missing required fields, enum members that are not declared, numbers outside their declared range or carrying more decimal places than declared, datetimes that are not ISO 8601 with a `Z` offset, and any window whose end value is not strictly after its start value. The window pairs checked here are `period_start_iso`/`period_end_iso`, `curtailment_start_iso`/`curtailment_end_iso`, `start_iso`/`end_iso` and `scheduled_start_iso`/`scheduled_end_iso`. Two domain limits are also enforced at this level rather than later: a lighting override window longer than the twelve-hour ceiling, and a setpoint standard whose heating setpoint is at or above its cooling setpoint.
4. Existence of every referenced resource, in the family order given in §2.6.
5. Store-scope authority for the two entity-backed roles — `ERR_STORE_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
6. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
7. State admissibility of the target resource — one of `ERR_BMS_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_STANDARD_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_BILL_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_DR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_EXCURSION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_OVERRIDE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_FAULT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_WORK_ORDER_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_RETROFIT_STATE_INVALID`, in that relative order when more than one could apply.
8. Stale-view protection — `ERR_STALE_STORE_VERSION`, then `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION`.
9. Business-safety gates — `ERR_FOOD_SAFETY_HOLD`, then `ERR_DISPOSITION_MANDATE_VIOLATION`, then `ERR_FOOD_SAFETY_DOC_INCOMPLETE`, then `ERR_FAULT_SUPPRESSION_FORBIDDEN`.
10. Referential business gates — `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_PUBLISHED`, then `ERR_STANDARD_SCOPE_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_BMS_NOT_COMMISSIONED`, then `ERR_DR_ENROLLMENT_SUSPENDED`, then `ERR_TARIFF_SWITCH_IN_PROGRESS`.
11. Uniqueness, overlap and data-sufficiency constraints — `ERR_DUPLICATE_STANDARD_SCOPE`, then `ERR_OVERRIDE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, then `ERR_DR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, then `ERR_WORK_ORDER_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, then `ERR_BASELINE_DATA_INSUFFICIENT`, then `ERR_DEMAND_DATA_MISSING`.
12. Temporal gates — `ERR_CURTAILMENT_NOT_ELAPSED`, then `ERR_TARIFF_LOCK_ACTIVE`, then `ERR_MV_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT`.
13. Fiscal and capacity gates — `ERR_OVERRIDE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_WORK_ORDER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_TARIFF_SWITCH_NOT_JUSTIFIED`.

Four consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis because they are easy to get backwards. Duty separation outranks state admissibility: when the operator who recomputed a bill tries to authorise the adjustment of a bill that is not even in a disputed condition, the answer is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, not `ERR_BILL_STATE_INVALID` — and likewise a proposer approving a retrofit that is no longer proposable receives `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` rather than `ERR_RETROFIT_STATE_INVALID`. State admissibility outranks stale-view protection, so a commissioning attempt against a store already mid-commissioning is reported as `ERR_BMS_STATE_INVALID` even when the supplied version is also stale. Stale-view protection in turn outranks the food-safety gate, so a commissioning request that is both stale and aimed at a locked store is reported as `ERR_STALE_STORE_VERSION`. And the food-safety gate outranks every referential check, so a commissioning aimed at a locked store with an unpublished standard is reported as `ERR_FOOD_SAFETY_HOLD`.

### 2.8. Decimal Discipline for Money, Energy and Temperature

All arithmetic in this system is exact decimal arithmetic, and every rounding step rounds **half up**. Where a calculation names a rounding step, the intermediate value is materialised at that precision *before* it is used by the next step; summing unrounded intermediates and rounding once at the end is a different answer and is not what this system specifies.

The default precision for currency amounts is two decimal places. Energy quantities in kilowatt-hours are also carried at two decimal places. Demand values in kilowatts and temperatures in degrees Fahrenheit are carried at one decimal place. Realization and portfolio realization rates are carried at four decimal places, as are per-kilowatt-hour tariff rates and the store's blended electricity rate. Percentages differ by kind: the bill variance percentage and the benchmark percentile carry two and one decimal places respectively, and demand-response performance carries one. Exposure duration is a whole number of minutes obtained by discarding any remainder — the elapsed seconds divided by sixty, truncated toward zero, never rounded up.

### 2.9. Stale-View Protection on Store and Account Writes

Two write operations in this system are legitimate to repeat: a store may be re-commissioned against a different setpoint standard at any time, and a tariff switch may be re-scheduled over a pending one. Because these writes supersede rather than conflict with their predecessors, no state precondition can distinguish an operator working from a current view of the resource from one whose view predates somebody else's write. Both are therefore protected by an explicit expected-version check.

A commissioning request must carry `expected_store_version`, a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target store. If that value does not equal the store's current `version` — because another commissioning, a posture recomputation, or a cascade has advanced it since the client read the store — the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STORE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written. Similarly, a tariff-switch request must carry `expected_utility_account_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the target utility account; a mismatch against the account's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). These are the only two operations in the API that take a version field. No other endpoint accepts one, and none should be given one.

### 2.10. The Energy Posture Ladder and Food-Safety Primacy

Each store carries `energy_posture`, one of `NORMAL`, `CONSTRAINED`, `CURTAILED` or `LOCKED`. It is never supplied by a client and never edited directly. It is recomputed synchronously, at the end of every request that changes one of its inputs, as a strict precedence ladder in which the first matching rule wins:

1. `LOCKED`, when any temperature excursion reachable through this store's refrigeration cases is in `ESCALATED` or `DISPOSITION_REQUIRED`.
2. `CURTAILED`, when any demand-response event of this store's utility account has its participation dimension in `ACTIVE`.
3. `CONSTRAINED`, when at least one of the following holds: some refrigeration case of the store is in `ALARM`; the store's `bms_status` is `DEGRADED`; or the store's `urgent_work_order_count` is 1 or more.
4. `NORMAL`, when none of the above applies. A store with no children at all evaluates to `NORMAL`.

Because the ladder is strict, a food-safety escalation outranks an active curtailment, and an active curtailment outranks a refrigeration alarm or a degraded building-management system. A store with an escalated excursion *and* an active event is `LOCKED`, not `CURTAILED`; a store with an active event *and* a case in `ALARM` is `CURTAILED`, not `CONSTRAINED`.

The posture gates three operations, all of them under `ERR_FOOD_SAFETY_HOLD` (HTTP 409): a demand-response event may not be activated while its store is `LOCKED` or `CURTAILED`; a lighting override may not be created while its store is `LOCKED`; and a BMS commissioning may not be started while its store is `LOCKED`. Note the asymmetry — curtailment blocks only the demand-response activation, whereas the food-safety lock blocks all three.

Leaving `NORMAL` also has consequences beyond gating. Any posture transition out of `NORMAL` revokes every lighting override of that store that is currently `ACTIVE` with an override type of `SCHEDULE_EXTEND`, recording the revocation as a system action rather than a user action. Raising the posture to `CONSTRAINED` or to `LOCKED` additionally marks every retrofit project of that store still in `PROPOSED` as requiring escalated review. Revocation is not reversible: when the posture later falls back to `NORMAL`, previously revoked overrides stay revoked and are not restored.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties and Store-Scoped Authority

Two financial controls in this system require two different human beings. The operator whose `X-User-ID` was recorded as `recomputed_by_user_id` when a bill was recomputed may not be the operator who authorises that bill's adjustment. The operator whose `X-User-ID` was recorded as `proposed_by_user_id` when a retrofit project was proposed may not be the operator who approves or rejects it — the restriction covers rejection as well as approval, so a proposer cannot quietly kill their own proposal either. Both breaches surface as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

Store scoping is the other authority restriction. A `StoreManager` or `BmsGateway` whose `X-User-ID` does not equal the relevant store's `id` is refused with `ERR_STORE_SCOPE_VIOLATION` on four operations: retrieving a single store, creating a lighting override for a store, ingesting a temperature reading against a refrigeration case (scoped through the case's store), and acknowledging a temperature excursion (scoped through the excursion's case's store). Opaque roles are unaffected by store scoping.

### 2.12. Window Arithmetic: Half-Open, Closed and Buffered Comparisons

Time windows in this domain do not all follow the same convention, and the differences are deliberate.

Billing periods and metering are **half-open**: a demand interval belongs to a billing period when its start instant is at or after the period start and strictly before the period end. Curtailment settlement uses the same half-open rule against the curtailment start and end. Lighting override windows are half-open too, both for activation (an override is live when the clock is at or after its start and strictly before its end) and for overlap detection, which means a new override that begins exactly when an existing one ends is admitted rather than refused.

Demand-response event windows, by contrast, are **closed** intervals inclusive of both endpoints, and they carry an additional mandatory recovery separation of four hours measured forward from the earlier event's curtailment end. Maintenance work order windows are also treated as closed intervals for conflict detection.

Alarm thresholds are inclusive on both sides: a reading at exactly the high alarm value breaches, and a reading at exactly the low alarm value breaches. Trailing windows for benchmarking are inclusive at both ends. Where a threshold comparison is stated in Section 3 as "at or above" or "at or below", the boundary value itself satisfies the condition.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Portfolio Onboarding and the Setpoint Standard Library

A `PlatformAdmin` opens the portfolio by registering a store. The registration payload names the store by its `code`, a human-readable identifier of between 1 and 32 characters that must be unique across every store in the portfolio, and describes its physical and commercial character through `climate_zone` — one of `HOT_HUMID`, `HOT_DRY`, `MIXED`, `MARINE` or `COLD` — and `store_format`, one of `SUPERCENTER`, `NEIGHBORHOOD` or `EXPRESS`. Conditioned area is supplied as `floor_area_sqft`, a positive integer that later serves as the denominator of the energy-use-intensity benchmark. Four further inputs are optional and carry declared defaults: `blended_rate_usd_per_kwh`, a positive rate carried to four decimal places and defaulting to 0.11, which is how kilowatt-hour savings become dollars; `override_budget_kwh_month`, a non-negative allowance at two decimal places defaulting to 500.00 kWh; and the lighting schedule pair `lighting_on_time_local` and `lighting_off_time_local`, local twenty-four-hour times of day defaulting to `05:30` and `23:00` respectively. Two boolean control flags, `daylight_harvest_enabled` and `occupancy_control_enabled`, both default to true.

A freshly registered store has nothing deployed. Its `bms_status` is `NOT_DEPLOYED`, its `active_standard_id` is null, its `energy_posture` is `NORMAL` and its `version` is 1. Every derived quantity starts at its zero point: `override_kwh_used_month` and `verified_savings_kwh_ytd` are 0.00, `alarm_case_count` and `urgent_work_order_count` are 0, `eui_kbtu_per_sqft` and `benchmark_percentile` are null, `benchmark_outlier_flag` is false, and `portfolio_realization_rate` sits at its portfolio default of 0.8500 because the store has no measured project yet. Stores can be listed with filters on `climate_zone`, `store_format`, `energy_posture`, `bms_status`, `benchmark_outlier_flag` and `active_standard_id`; the last of these is a reverse-reachability filter that answers "which stores are running this standard?".

Setpoint standards are the chain's engineering doctrine, and only an `EnergyManager` may author them. Drafting a standard fixes its governing scope as a triple of `climate_zone`, `store_format` and `season`, where `season` is either `SUMMER` or `WINTER`, and then states the mandated control values: `cooling_setpoint_f`, which must be at least 60.0 and at most 85.0 degrees; `heating_setpoint_f`, which must be at least 55.0 and at most 78.0 degrees and strictly below the cooling setpoint; `deadband_f`, the mandated separation between heating and cooling action, which must be at least 1.0 degrees; and `lighting_lux_target`, a positive sales-floor illuminance target. A standard is born in `DRAFT` with `published_at_iso` and `published_by_user_id` both null. Its `revision` is 1 when the draft names no predecessor, and otherwise exactly one greater than the `revision` of the standard named in `supersedes_standard_id` — the self-referential pointer that threads a scope's standards into a supersession chain. Supplying that pointer at draft time records the intent only; the predecessor is not touched yet.

Only one standard may hold each scope at a time. Drafting is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STANDARD_SCOPE` (HTTP 409) when a standard in `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED` already governs the same combination of climate zone, store format and season. The single exception is the orderly-succession case: a draft that explicitly names that incumbent `PUBLISHED` standard in `supersedes_standard_id` is admitted, because it is the intended replacement rather than a competitor. A `supersedes_standard_id` that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_FOUND`.

Publication is where the chain of consequences begins. An `EnergyManager` publishing a standard advances it `DRAFT → PUBLISHED`, stamps `published_at_iso` from the logical clock and records the acting operator in `published_by_user_id`. Publishing anything that is already `PUBLISHED` or already `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_STANDARD_STATE_INVALID`, and publishing a successor whose named predecessor is not itself `PUBLISHED` is refused with `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_PUBLISHED`. When the predecessor check passes and a predecessor exists, four things follow in order. First, the predecessor moves `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED` and its `version` increments. Second, every store whose `active_standard_id` points at that now-superseded predecessor and whose `bms_status` is `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → DEGRADED`, on the reasoning that its deployed setpoints no longer match governing doctrine; each such store's `version` increments. Third, each of those stores recomputes its posture, which raises `NORMAL → CONSTRAINED`. Fourth, every retrofit project of each affected store still sitting in `PROPOSED` has `escalated_review` set to true. Standards can be listed with filters on `climate_zone`, `store_format`, `season`, `status` and `supersedes_standard_id`, the last being the reverse-reachability filter over the self-referential chain.

### 3.2. BMS Commissioning and the Degradation Cycle

The building management system of a store moves through a cycle rather than a straight line: `NOT_DEPLOYED → COMMISSIONING → ACTIVE`, then `ACTIVE → DEGRADED` when reality diverges from doctrine, then `DEGRADED → COMMISSIONING → ACTIVE` again when an engineer re-binds a standard and re-activates. A fourth entry into commissioning exists directly from `ACTIVE`, used when the chain simply wants a different standard on a healthy store; this is the re-standardisation path, and it is the write that the store's expected-version check exists to protect.

Commissioning is an `EnergyManager` action carrying two fields and only two: `standard_id`, naming the setpoint standard to bind, and `expected_store_version`, the client's last observed store `version`. The store must not already be in `COMMISSIONING` — attempting it again from there is `ERR_BMS_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The named standard must resolve, must be in `PUBLISHED` status (a `DRAFT` or `SUPERSEDED` standard yields `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_PUBLISHED`), and must govern this store's scope: both its `climate_zone` and its `store_format` must equal the store's, and any mismatch on either yields `ERR_STANDARD_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The store's `energy_posture` must not be `LOCKED`; commissioning a locked store is `ERR_FOOD_SAFETY_HOLD`. When all of that passes, `bms_status` advances to `COMMISSIONING`, `active_standard_id` is set to the supplied `standard_id`, `version` increments by one and `updated_at` is refreshed.

Activation takes no input at all. It requires `bms_status` to be exactly `COMMISSIONING` — anything else is `ERR_BMS_STATE_INVALID` — and it re-validates the binding rather than trusting it: the standard currently named by `active_standard_id` must *still* be in `PUBLISHED` status at the moment of activation. A standard that was published when it was bound but has since been superseded by a successor's publication causes the activation to be refused with `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_PUBLISHED`, which is the system's way of insisting that nobody activate against stale doctrine. A successful activation advances `COMMISSIONING → ACTIVE`, increments `version` and recomputes the store's posture.

Degradation is never a user action. A store leaves `ACTIVE` for `DEGRADED` in exactly two circumstances, and in both the store's `version` increments and the posture is recomputed immediately afterward, raising `NORMAL → CONSTRAINED`. The first circumstance is the maintenance path: triage of a fault produces an urgent work order on refrigeration equipment. The second is the doctrine path described in §3.1: the standard the store is bound to becomes `SUPERSEDED`. Recovery from `DEGRADED` is deliberately manual — completing the offending work order clears the *posture* input but leaves `bms_status` at `DEGRADED` until an engineer explicitly re-commissions and re-activates.

Until a store's building management system is genuinely usable, most of the operational surface is closed. A store whose `bms_status` is `NOT_DEPLOYED` or `COMMISSIONING` cannot have refrigeration cases registered against it, cannot have fault diagnostics reported against it, cannot have lighting overrides created for it, and cannot have retrofit projects approved on it; all four refusals carry `ERR_BMS_NOT_COMMISSIONED` (HTTP 409). Both `ACTIVE` and `DEGRADED` are acceptable for all four — a degraded system is still a deployed one.

### 3.3. Refrigeration Monitoring, Breach Runs and Excursion Escalation

Cases are registered against a commissioned store by a `PlatformAdmin` or an `EnergyManager`. Each carries a `case_code` that must be unique within its owning store, a `case_type` of `MEDIUM_TEMP`, `LOW_TEMP` or `ICE_CREAM`, and a `product_class` of `DAIRY`, `MEAT`, `FROZEN` or `PRODUCE` that is later copied onto food-safety paperwork. Its alarm envelope is `alarm_high_f` and `alarm_low_f`, both at one decimal place with the high bound strictly above the low, and the retail exposure at stake is `product_value_usd`, a non-negative amount at two decimal places. A new case is persisted with `monitoring_state` `ARMED`, `consecutive_breach_count` 0, and `last_reading_temp_f` and `last_reading_iso` both null. Cases of a store can be listed with filters on `monitoring_state`, `case_type` and `product_class`.

Temperature readings arrive only from the store's own gateway. Each ingestion supplies `temp_f`, the observed temperature, and `reading_iso`, the instant of observation, and the response is a consolidated picture of everything the reading changed: `case_id`, `monitoring_state`, `consecutive_breach_count`, `last_reading_temp_f`, `last_reading_iso`, `excursion_id`, `excursion_state`, `exposure_minutes`, `peak_temp_f`, `mandatory_disposition`, `store_energy_posture`, `dr_events_opted_out` and `overrides_revoked`. The four excursion-related fields — `excursion_id`, `excursion_state`, `exposure_minutes`, `peak_temp_f` — together with `mandatory_disposition` are null when no non-closed excursion exists on the case after the reading has been processed. Every reading, breaching or not, updates the case's `last_reading_temp_f` and `last_reading_iso`.

A reading **breaches** when its `temp_f` is at or above the case's `alarm_high_f`, or at or below the case's `alarm_low_f`; both bounds are inclusive, so a reading of exactly 41.0 against a high alarm of 41.0 breaches, and a reading of 27.9 against a low alarm of 28.0 breaches on the cold side. A breaching reading increments `consecutive_breach_count` by one. A non-breaching reading resets `consecutive_breach_count` to zero — but it does *not* close an excursion that is already open; only explicit food-safety closure does that. When `consecutive_breach_count` reaches two and the case has no excursion in a non-closed state, the second consecutive breach opens one: a `TempExcursion` is created in `OPEN` with `case_id` pointing at the case, `first_breach_iso` set to the reading instant of the **first** breach of the current run rather than the reading that crossed the count, `last_breach_iso` set to the current reading instant, and `product_value_usd` copied from the case as it stands at that moment so that the paperwork is not retroactively altered by a later re-pricing. Opening an excursion moves the case `ARMED → ALARM` and increments the store's `alarm_case_count`, and the store immediately recomputes its posture, raising `NORMAL → CONSTRAINED`.

Every further breaching reading against a case with a non-closed excursion extends that excursion rather than creating a second one. It sets `last_breach_iso` to the new reading instant and re-evaluates `peak_temp_f`, which records the actual `temp_f` of whichever breaching reading so far has deviated furthest from the nearer of the two alarm bounds; when two readings tie on deviation, the earlier reading instant wins. From `first_breach_iso` and `last_breach_iso` the system derives `exposure_minutes` as the whole number of minutes between them, obtained by dividing the elapsed seconds by sixty and discarding any remainder.

Exposure drives a second precedence ladder, evaluated after each breaching reading updates the numbers, first match winning. When `exposure_minutes` is 240 or more — four hours of continuous exposure — the excursion moves to `DISPOSITION_REQUIRED` and its `mandatory_disposition` becomes `DISCARD`. Otherwise, when `exposure_minutes` is 120 or more, the excursion moves to `ESCALATED` and `mandatory_disposition` stays `NONE`. Otherwise the excursion is `OPEN`, and an excursion that a store manager had already acknowledged reverts `ACKNOWLEDGED → OPEN`, which is the cyclic behaviour that keeps a re-breaching case from looking handled. Two hours of exposure exactly reaches `ESCALATED` because the threshold is inclusive; one hour and fifty-nine minutes does not.

Escalation is the system's most consequential cascade, and it fires whenever an excursion enters `ESCALATED` or `DISPOSITION_REQUIRED`. The owning store recomputes its posture and, by rule one of the posture ladder, is raised to `LOCKED` from whichever of `NORMAL`, `CONSTRAINED` or `CURTAILED` it held. Because the store is now locked, every demand-response event of the store's utility account whose participation dimension is `NOTIFIED` moves to `OPTED_OUT` with `opt_out_reason` set to `FOOD_SAFETY_POSTURE`, and the response's `dr_events_opted_out` counts them. Every `ACTIVE` lighting override of the store whose `override_type` is `SCHEDULE_EXTEND` is revoked as a system action, and `overrides_revoked` counts those. Each system opt-out increments the account's `dr_optout_count`, and as soon as that count reaches three the account's `dr_enrollment_state` becomes `SUSPENDED` — a store that keeps failing its cases out of curtailment events loses its place in the program. Retrofit projects of the store in `PROPOSED` are marked with `escalated_review` true. An excursion moving from `ESCALATED` on to `DISPOSITION_REQUIRED` keeps the store at `LOCKED`; the lock does not double.

A `MaintenanceTech` who needs to service a case takes it out of monitoring by supplying a `target_state` of `BYPASSED`, and returns it by supplying `ARMED`. Both transitions reset `consecutive_breach_count` to zero. Bypassing is permitted only from `ARMED`: a case in `ALARM` may not be bypassed, because that would be a way of hiding an active food-safety event, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Rearming is permitted only from `BYPASSED`, and a `target_state` equal to the case's current `monitoring_state` is refused with the same code. `target_state` is declared over the case's own `monitoring_state` vocabulary — `ARMED`, `ALARM` and `BYPASSED` — so a value outside those three fails the schema check with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, whereas `ALARM` is a declared member that passes the schema and, on a case that resolves, is refused as a state violation with `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID` whatever the case's current state, because this endpoint never drives a case into alarm. While a case is `BYPASSED`, readings posted against it are still stored — `last_reading_temp_f` and `last_reading_iso` are updated as usual — but they take no part in breach evaluation and can neither open nor extend an excursion.

### 3.4. Food-Safety Documentation, Closure and Posture Release

An excursion passes through as many as three pairs of hands. The store sees it first: a `StoreManager` acknowledging an excursion advances it `OPEN → ACKNOWLEDGED` and records the acting operator in `acknowledged_by_user_id`. Acknowledgement is legal only from `OPEN`; an excursion already `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `ESCALATED`, `DISPOSITION_REQUIRED` or `CLOSED` yields `ERR_EXCURSION_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Because the acting manager is entity-backed on a store, the excursion must belong to a case of that manager's own store, and a cross-store acknowledgement is refused with `ERR_STORE_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Acknowledgement is not a resolution: as §3.3 sets out, a further breaching reading reverts `ACKNOWLEDGED → OPEN`.

Documentation belongs to the `FoodSafetyOfficer` and is a distinct step from closure. Documenting records `disposition`, either `RETAIN` or `DISCARD`, together with a `corrective_action_note` narrating what was done about the underlying failure, and it stamps `documented_by_user_id` from the acting operator and `documented_at_iso` from the logical clock. Documentation is accepted in any state other than `CLOSED`, and it explicitly does **not** change the excursion's state — an excursion that was `DISPOSITION_REQUIRED` before documentation is still `DISPOSITION_REQUIRED` after it. Where the exposure ladder has already set `mandatory_disposition` to `DISCARD`, the documented `disposition` must be `DISCARD` as well; recording `RETAIN` against a mandated discard is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_MANDATE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422). When `mandatory_disposition` is `NONE`, either value is acceptable and the judgement is the officer's.

Closure is also the `FoodSafetyOfficer`'s, and it is gated on the paperwork being complete: both `disposition` and `corrective_action_note` must already be recorded, and closing an excursion missing either is refused with `ERR_FOOD_SAFETY_DOC_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422). Closing anything already `CLOSED` is `ERR_EXCURSION_STATE_INVALID`. A successful closure moves the excursion from whichever non-closed state it held — `OPEN`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `ESCALATED` or `DISPOSITION_REQUIRED` — to `CLOSED`, and stamps `closed_at_iso` from the clock. Two cascades follow. The owning refrigeration case rearms `ALARM → ARMED`, but only when no other non-closed excursion remains on that case, and rearming resets `consecutive_breach_count` to zero and decrements the store's `alarm_case_count`. Then the owning store recomputes its posture.

That recomputation is where a locked store finds its way back. Closing the last escalated excursion clears rule one of the ladder, and the store falls to whatever the remaining rules justify: to `CONSTRAINED` when no event of its account is `ACTIVE` but at least one constraining input still holds — a case still in `ALARM`, a `bms_status` of `DEGRADED`, or an `urgent_work_order_count` of 1 or more — and all the way to `NORMAL` when nothing at all holds. Releases from `CONSTRAINED` to `NORMAL` happen the same way whenever the last constraining input clears, whether that was an excursion closure, a case rearm, a work-order completion or a BMS reactivation. Release from `CURTAILED` to `NORMAL` happens during the clock sweep that completes the last active demand-response event, provided no event of the account remains `ACTIVE` and none of the constraining inputs holds. Whatever route the store takes back, lighting overrides revoked on the way out of `NORMAL` are gone for good and are never restored.

Excursions of a case can be listed with a filter on `state`, and any single excursion can be retrieved to inspect its exposure, mandate and documentation fields.

### 3.5. Lighting Control and the Monthly Override Allowance

Under normal operation a store's sales floor lights follow the schedule expressed by `lighting_on_time_local` and `lighting_off_time_local`, dim against daylight when `daylight_harvest_enabled` is true, and drop out of unoccupied zones when `occupancy_control_enabled` is true. Overrides exist because retail reality intrudes — an inventory count runs late, a display needs full illuminance, a sensor is misbehaving — and each override defeats exactly one of those automatic behaviours. A `StoreManager` requests one for its own store, naming the `zone` affected (`SALES_FLOOR`, `PARKING`, `BACKROOM` or `EXTERIOR_SIGN`) and the `override_type`: `SCHEDULE_EXTEND` to hold lights on past the scheduled off time, `DAYLIGHT_HARVEST_DISABLE` to defeat daylight dimming, or `OCCUPANCY_DISABLE` to defeat occupancy sensing. The window is given as `start_iso` and `end_iso`, the connected load of the zone as `zone_load_kw` — a positive value at one decimal place — and the business justification as `requested_reason`.

Windows are capped at twelve hours. The window duration is the exact elapsed time between `start_iso` and `end_iso`, and a window longer than twelve hours is refused at the schema level with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Within the cap, the energy the override will consume is projected as `projected_kwh`: the exact elapsed minutes of the window divided by sixty, kept exact rather than rounded, multiplied by `zone_load_kw`, with the product rounded half up to two decimal places. A 37.5 kW zone held for eight hours therefore projects 300.00 kWh.

That projection is charged against a monthly allowance. A store's `override_kwh_used_month` is the sum of `projected_kwh` across that store's overrides whose `state` is `SCHEDULED`, `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED` and whose `start_iso` falls inside the calendar month, in UTC, of the current logical clock. Note carefully which rows are counted: a merely scheduled override is charged exactly as a live one is, and an expired override stays charged for the remainder of its month, because the energy was actually spent. Only `REVOKED` rows drop out of the sum, which is precisely how a revocation releases budget. A creation request is admitted only when the store's current `override_kwh_used_month` plus the new `projected_kwh`, rounded half up to two decimal places, is less than or equal to `override_budget_kwh_month`; equality is admitted. Exceeding the allowance is refused with `ERR_OVERRIDE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). A store with a 500.00 kWh allowance that has already committed 300.00 kWh admits a further 200.00 kWh exactly, and then refuses an 80.00 kWh request — until one of the earlier overrides is revoked, at which point the freed kilowatt-hours make room.

Overlap is judged per store and zone, and only against overrides in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE`. Windows are half-open, so a request whose `start_iso` equals an existing override's `end_iso` for the same zone is admitted, while any genuine intersection is refused with `ERR_OVERRIDE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). The same zone may hold overrides of different types at different times, and two different zones of one store never conflict with each other. Creation is additionally gated by store scope, by the food-safety lock (a `LOCKED` store admits no new override, refused with `ERR_FOOD_SAFETY_HOLD`), and by BMS deployment (`ERR_BMS_NOT_COMMISSIONED`).

An admitted override is persisted directly in `ACTIVE` when the logical clock is at or after `start_iso` and strictly before `end_iso`; a window entirely in the future is persisted in `SCHEDULED` instead, and is charged against the monthly allowance immediately regardless. From there the clock sweep drives the rest of the lifecycle: `SCHEDULED → ACTIVE` when the clock enters the window, and `SCHEDULED → EXPIRED` or `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` when the clock reaches or passes `end_iso`, with the projection remaining charged.

Revocation has two sources, distinguished by `revoke_source`. An `EnergyManager` revoking an override moves it from `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` to `REVOKED`, sets `revoke_source` to `USER`, records the acting operator in `revoked_by_user_id`, and releases the override's `projected_kwh` from the store's monthly usage. Revoking an override that is already `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED` is refused with `ERR_OVERRIDE_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The other source is the posture cascade of §2.10: when a store leaves `NORMAL`, each of its `ACTIVE` overrides of type `SCHEDULE_EXTEND` moves `ACTIVE → REVOKED` with `revoke_source` set to `SYSTEM_POSTURE` and `revoked_by_user_id` left null, and that override's projection is released too. Overrides of type `DAYLIGHT_HARVEST_DISABLE` and `OCCUPANCY_DISABLE` are untouched by the posture cascade, as are overrides still merely `SCHEDULED`. Overrides of a store can be listed with filters on `state`, `zone` and `override_type`.

### 3.6. Utility Metering, Bill Validation and Dispute Settlement

Each store's commercial relationship with its utility is held in a utility account, attached by an `EnergyManager`. The account names the serving `utility_name` and the currently effective tariff as `rate_schedule_code`, and classifies that tariff by `rate_family` — either `TOU_DEMAND` for a time-of-use tariff or `FLAT_DEMAND` for a flat one, a choice that decides which peak-detection variant every recompute will use. Its price list is `demand_charge_per_kw`, a positive amount at two decimal places charged per billable kilowatt, together with `rate_on_peak` and `rate_off_peak`, positive per-kilowatt-hour rates at four decimal places. `ratchet_peak_kw` records the highest demand billed over the trailing eleven months, a non-negative value at one decimal place defaulting to 0.0, and feeds the ratchet floor. Five further optional inputs describe an alternate tariff the chain is considering: `candidate_rate_schedule_code`, `candidate_rate_family`, `candidate_demand_charge_per_kw`, `candidate_rate_on_peak` and `candidate_rate_off_peak`, all null by default. A new account is persisted with `tariff_state` `STABLE`, `tariff_switch_effective_from_iso` and `tariff_locked_until_iso` null, `dr_enrollment_state` `ENROLLED`, `dr_optout_count` 0 and `dr_incentive_accrued_usd` 0.00. A store's accounts can be listed with filters on `tariff_state` and `dr_enrollment_state`.

Metered demand arrives in bulk. Either the store's gateway or an `EnergyAnalyst` may submit an `intervals` payload, a non-empty array in which each element carries `interval_start_iso`, the inclusive start of a fifteen-minute metering interval; `kw_demand`, the average demand over that interval as a non-negative value at one decimal place; `is_on_peak`, the utility's own classification of the interval; and optionally `is_dr_event_day`, which defaults to false and marks intervals that fall on a demand-response event day so they can be excluded from baseline candidacy. Ingestion is all-or-nothing: if any element fails validation the entire batch is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and nothing is persisted. Accepted intervals are persisted in the order supplied, each carrying a `utility_account_id` that points at the account they were ingested under and each stamped with `created_at` from the logical clock, and each is immutable thereafter — nothing in the API edits or deletes an interval. Ingestion never triggers a bill recompute; validation is always an explicit act. Intervals of an account can be listed with filters on `is_on_peak` and `is_dr_event_day`.

Bills are recorded by an `EnergyAnalyst` exactly as the utility printed them, with `period_start_iso` and `period_end_iso` bounding the billing period, `on_peak_kwh` and `off_peak_kwh` giving the utility's split of consumption, `billed_kwh` the utility-stated total that later serves as the numerator of the benchmark, `billed_peak_kw` the peak demand as printed, and `billed_amount_usd` the invoice total. All four energy and demand quantities must be non-negative, and `billed_amount_usd` is a non-negative amount at two decimal places. A recorded bill carries a `utility_account_id` pointing at the account it was billed against and lands in `validation_state` `RECEIVED` with every derived field null: `recomputed_energy_cost_usd`, `recomputed_demand_cost_usd`, `recomputed_rider_cost_usd`, `recomputed_surcharge_usd`, `recomputed_amount_usd`, `billable_demand_kw`, `peak_basis` and `variance_pct`. It is worth stating plainly that `billed_peak_kw` is retained as evidence only and is never an input to the recompute — the system determines billable demand from its own metering, never from the utility's assertion. Bills of an account can be listed with a filter on `validation_state`.

Recomputing a bill is the heart of bill validation, and only an `EnergyAnalyst` may do it, and only on a bill in `RECEIVED` (anything else is `ERR_BILL_STATE_INVALID`). It proceeds in a fixed order, and every rounding step materialises its result at the stated precision before the next step consumes it.

The first step prices energy: the on-peak consumption multiplied by the account's on-peak rate, plus the off-peak consumption multiplied by the account's off-peak rate, with the sum rounded half up to two decimal places and stored as `recomputed_energy_cost_usd`.

The second step determines billable demand, and this is where the two tariff families diverge. The candidate interval set is every demand interval of the bill's account whose `interval_start_iso` is at or after `period_start_iso` and strictly before `period_end_iso`; no state filter applies, because intervals are immutable events. On a `TOU_DEMAND` account the system takes the **coincident peak**: it narrows that set to intervals whose `is_on_peak` is true, ranks them by `kw_demand` descending, breaking ties by earlier `interval_start_iso` and then by earlier `created_at`, takes the `kw_demand` of the first ranked interval as the observed peak, and records `peak_basis` as `COINCIDENT_PEAK`. On a `FLAT_DEMAND` account it takes the **anytime peak** instead: the whole candidate set is eligible, on-peak and off-peak intervals alike, ranked by the identical three-key ordering, and `peak_basis` is recorded as `ANYTIME_PEAK`. Whichever variant applied, a ratchet floor is then computed as eighty percent of the account's `ratchet_peak_kw`, rounded half up to one decimal place, and `billable_demand_kw` becomes the greater of the observed peak and that floor, at one decimal place. A time-of-use account whose on-peak intervals top out at 1310.5 kW against a `ratchet_peak_kw` of 1500.0 therefore bills 1310.5 kW, because the 1200.0 kW floor is lower; the same account whose on-peak intervals top out at 900.0 kW bills the 1200.0 kW floor instead. If the eligible set is empty the recompute is refused with `ERR_DEMAND_DATA_MISSING` (HTTP 422), the bill stays in `RECEIVED`, and no derived field is written — and note that emptiness is judged per variant, so an account holding only off-peak intervals fails under the coincident variant while identical data would have succeeded under the anytime variant.

The third step prices demand: `billable_demand_kw` multiplied by the account's `demand_charge_per_kw`, rounded half up to two decimal places, stored as `recomputed_demand_cost_usd`. The fourth step adds the energy and demand components into a base subtotal, both already at two decimal places. The fifth step applies the regulatory rider at a rate of three and three-quarter percent — 0.0375 — of that base subtotal, rounded half up to two decimal places and stored as `recomputed_rider_cost_usd`; the rider is computed on the base subtotal alone and never on the surcharge. The sixth step adds the rider to the base subtotal to form a pre-surcharge total. The seventh step applies the high-usage surcharge: when the pre-surcharge total is below twenty thousand dollars the surcharge is 0.00, and otherwise it is one and a half percent — 0.015 — of the amount by which the pre-surcharge total *exceeds* twenty thousand dollars, rounded half up to two decimal places; the surcharge is never charged on the whole subtotal. That result is stored as `recomputed_surcharge_usd`. The eighth step sets `recomputed_amount_usd` to the pre-surcharge total plus the surcharge.

The ninth step measures the disagreement. `variance_pct` is the utility's `billed_amount_usd` minus the system's `recomputed_amount_usd`, divided by `recomputed_amount_usd`, multiplied by one hundred and rounded half up to two decimal places — a positive value meaning the utility asked for more than the system believes is owed. The tenth step decides the outcome: when the absolute value of `variance_pct` is at most two percent the bill moves `RECEIVED → VALIDATED`, and otherwise it moves `RECEIVED → VARIANCE_FLAGGED`. Either way the acting operator is recorded in `recomputed_by_user_id`, which becomes the duty-separation counterparty for any later adjustment. A validated bill is immediately eligible to contribute to the store's energy-use-intensity benchmark.

Only a flagged bill can be disputed, and again only by an `EnergyAnalyst`. Disputing moves the bill `VARIANCE_FLAGGED → DISPUTED` and stores the `dispute_reason` narrative; disputing a bill in any other state is `ERR_BILL_STATE_INVALID`. Settling that dispute is an `EnergyManager` action that moves the bill `DISPUTED → ADJUSTED`, storing the negotiated `adjusted_amount_usd` and recording the authorising operator in `adjusted_by_user_id`. Three gates guard it, in this order: the authorising operator must differ from the one recorded in `recomputed_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; the bill must actually be `DISPUTED`, or the answer is `ERR_BILL_STATE_INVALID`; and the owning account's `tariff_state` must be `STABLE`, because negotiating a final amount while a tariff switch is pending would settle against a price list that is about to change — a pending switch yields `ERR_TARIFF_SWITCH_IN_PROGRESS` (HTTP 409). An adjusted bill, like a validated one, becomes eligible for the benchmark. `VALIDATED` and `ADJUSTED` are the two terminal outcomes of the bill lifecycle; no further action is accepted on a bill in either.

### 3.7. Rate Schedule Optimization and Tariff Switching

Before the chain moves an account onto a different tariff it asks the system to model the move. The tariff-comparison read, available to `PlatformAdmin`, `EnergyManager` and `EnergyAnalyst`, is a pure projection that writes nothing. It first selects a reference bill: among the account's bills whose `validation_state` is `VALIDATED` or `ADJUSTED`, the one with the latest `period_end_iso`, breaking ties by the later `created_at`. That bill's identifier is reported as `reference_bill_id`. Annualising its `recomputed_amount_usd` over twelve months, rounded half up to two decimal places, gives `current_annual_cost_usd`.

The candidate side re-runs the entire recompute of §3.6 over that same reference bill's consumption figures, substituting `candidate_rate_on_peak`, `candidate_rate_off_peak` and `candidate_demand_charge_per_kw` for the live rates, and re-selecting billable demand under `candidate_rate_family` rather than the live `rate_family`. The substitution of the family is material: a time-of-use account considering a flat candidate has its peak re-selected on the anytime basis, so the candidate figure may rest on an off-peak interval the live tariff ignores. The candidate's own basis and demand are reported as `candidate_peak_basis` and `candidate_billable_demand_kw`. The rider and surcharge steps of the candidate recompute follow exactly the same order and thresholds as the live one. Annualising the candidate total over twelve months, again rounded half up to two decimal places, gives `candidate_annual_cost_usd`, and `annual_delta_usd` is the candidate annual cost minus the current annual cost — negative when the candidate is cheaper. Finally, `switch_recommended` is true when `annual_delta_usd` is at or below negative two thousand five hundred dollars, meaning the switch must save at least 2500.00 dollars a year to be worth doing; a saving of exactly that amount qualifies. When the account has no `VALIDATED` or `ADJUSTED` bill at all, all of the projection's outputs are null.

Scheduling the switch is an `EnergyManager` action carrying `effective_from_iso`, the instant from which the new tariff is to apply, and `expected_utility_account_version` as described in §2.9. Three business gates apply after the stale-view check, in order. First, timing: the request is refused with `ERR_TARIFF_LOCK_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409) when the logical clock is still before the account's `tariff_locked_until_iso`, and with the same code when `effective_from_iso` is not strictly after the clock — a switch cannot be backdated or made instantaneous. Second, justification: the projection above is evaluated, and a request whose `switch_recommended` is false is refused with `ERR_TARIFF_SWITCH_NOT_JUSTIFIED` (HTTP 422). The same refusal applies when the account has no validated or adjusted reference bill, and when any of the five candidate fields is null, because in either case there is nothing to justify the move with.

An accepted switch sets `tariff_state` to `PENDING_SWITCH`, records `tariff_switch_effective_from_iso` from the supplied instant, and increments the account's `version`. It does not stamp `tariff_locked_until_iso`; that field is written only when a scheduled switch is actually applied, at which point it holds the effective instant advanced by the tariff lock period, and while it holds a future instant no further switch may be scheduled. Crucially, scheduling a switch on an account that is *already* `PENDING_SWITCH` is a legitimate operation: it supersedes the pending switch by overwriting the same two fields. There is no state precondition to fail against here, which is exactly why the expected-version check carries the whole burden of distinguishing an operator who has seen the pending switch from one who has not. And as §3.6 notes, while `tariff_state` is `PENDING_SWITCH` no bill on that account may be adjusted.

### 3.8. Demand Response Participation and Settlement

A demand-response event tracks two things that move independently, and the model keeps them in separate dimensions. `event_state` is the participation dimension, running `SCHEDULED → NOTIFIED → ACTIVE → COMPLETED` with `OPTED_OUT` as an alternative terminus. `settlement_state` is the money dimension, running `PENDING → SETTLED` and then possibly on to `REVERSED`. Neither dimension's transitions disturb the other: settling an event leaves its participation at `COMPLETED`, and completing an event leaves its settlement at `PENDING`. Events of an account can be listed with filters on `event_state` and `settlement_state` independently.

An `EnergyManager` enrolls an account in an event by supplying the utility's `program_code`, the curtailment window as `curtailment_start_iso` and `curtailment_end_iso`, and the contracted obligation as `target_reduction_kw`, a positive value at one decimal place. The event is persisted with a `utility_account_id` pointing at the enrolled account, `event_state` `SCHEDULED` and `settlement_state` `PENDING`, with `baseline_kw`, `total_reduction_kwh`, `performance_pct` and `notified_at_iso` all null, `incentive_usd` and `penalty_usd` both 0.00, and `opt_out_reason` and `reversal_reason` null. Creation is refused with `ERR_DR_ENROLLMENT_SUSPENDED` when the account's `dr_enrollment_state` is `SUSPENDED`. It is also refused with `ERR_DR_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409) when it would crowd another event of the same account whose `event_state` is `SCHEDULED`, `NOTIFIED` or `ACTIVE`. Crowding is judged generously in the utility's favour: event windows are treated as closed intervals inclusive of both endpoints, so touching endpoints already collide, and beyond that a mandatory recovery separation of four hours applies, measured forward from the earlier event's `curtailment_end_iso` — a new event whose `curtailment_start_iso` falls less than 240 minutes after an existing event ends is refused even though the windows themselves do not overlap.

Notification is the utility telling the chain that the event is really happening, and an `EnergyManager` records it. Notification requires `event_state` to be `SCHEDULED` (otherwise `ERR_DR_EVENT_STATE_INVALID`) and the account still to be `ENROLLED` (otherwise `ERR_DR_ENROLLMENT_SUSPENDED`), and it advances the event `SCHEDULED → NOTIFIED` while freezing the performance baseline into `baseline_kw` and stamping `notified_at_iso` from the logical clock. The baseline is built from historical metering, selected in two passes. The candidate pool is every demand interval of the event's account whose `is_on_peak` is true, whose `is_dr_event_day` is false, and whose `interval_start_iso` is strictly before the event's `curtailment_start_iso` — event days are excluded precisely because they are already depressed by curtailment. The first pass takes recency: candidates are ordered by `interval_start_iso` descending, breaking ties by the later `created_at`, and the first ten are retained. The second pass takes severity: those retained candidates are ranked by `kw_demand` descending, breaking ties by earlier `interval_start_iso` and then by earlier `created_at`, and the first three are kept — or however many exist, if fewer than three survived the first pass. `baseline_kw` is then the sum of the kept intervals' `kw_demand` divided by the number of kept intervals, with the division performed once on the exact sum and the result rounded half up to two decimal places. Three kept intervals of 1290.5, 1275.0 and 1245.0 kW therefore give a baseline of 1270.17 kW. When the candidate pool is empty the notification is refused with `ERR_BASELINE_DATA_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422), the event stays `SCHEDULED` and `baseline_kw` stays null; one or two candidates is not an error, merely a thinner average.

Activation is where the store actually sheds load, and it is the operation the food-safety posture most jealously guards. An `EnergyManager` activating an event requires `event_state` to be `NOTIFIED`, requires the owning account's store to be in `NORMAL` or `CONSTRAINED` — a store in `CURTAILED` or `LOCKED` is refused with `ERR_FOOD_SAFETY_HOLD` — and requires the logical clock to have reached or passed `curtailment_start_iso`, an earlier attempt being refused with `ERR_CURTAILMENT_NOT_ELAPSED` (HTTP 422). A successful activation advances `NOTIFIED → ACTIVE`, and the owning store immediately recomputes its posture and rises to `CURTAILED`. Because the store has left `NORMAL`, its `ACTIVE` schedule-extension lighting overrides are revoked as a system action and their projections released from the monthly allowance.

Two paths lead out of `ACTIVE` and `NOTIFIED` respectively. The ordinary path is the clock: during the sweep, every event whose `event_state` is `ACTIVE` and whose `curtailment_end_iso` is at or before the new clock value advances `ACTIVE → COMPLETED`, its settlement dimension untouched, after which the store's posture is recomputed and falls out of `CURTAILED` if no other event of the account remains `ACTIVE`. The abandonment path is the food-safety cascade of §3.3: when an excursion escalates and locks the store, every event of that store's account sitting in `NOTIFIED` moves `NOTIFIED → OPTED_OUT` with `opt_out_reason` set to `FOOD_SAFETY_POSTURE`, each such opt-out increments the account's `dr_optout_count`, and reaching three system opt-outs flips `dr_enrollment_state` to `SUSPENDED`.

Settlement is an `EnergyAnalyst` action on the money dimension. It requires `settlement_state` to be `PENDING` and `event_state` to be `COMPLETED` — settling an event whose participation has not completed, or settling one already settled, is `ERR_SETTLEMENT_STATE_INVALID` — and it requires the clock to be at or after `curtailment_end_iso`, an earlier attempt being `ERR_CURTAILMENT_NOT_ELAPSED`. The settlement set is every demand interval of the account whose `interval_start_iso` is at or after `curtailment_start_iso` and strictly before `curtailment_end_iso`. Each of those intervals, taken in order of `interval_start_iso` ascending and then `created_at` ascending, yields a delivered reduction: the frozen `baseline_kw` minus the interval's `kw_demand`, floored at zero so that an interval that consumed more than baseline contributes nothing rather than a negative, multiplied by a quarter of an hour because each interval is fifteen minutes long, and rounded half up to two decimal places. `total_reduction_kwh` is the sum of those already-rounded per-interval figures — the rounding happens per interval and the sum is exact, which is not the same answer as summing the raw products and rounding once at the end. The contracted obligation over the same window is `target_reduction_kw` multiplied by a quarter of an hour and by the number of intervals in the settlement set, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `performance_pct` is the delivered total divided by that obligation, multiplied by one hundred and rounded half up to one decimal place.

Performance then decides the money. When `performance_pct` is at least seventy-five percent the event earns `incentive_usd` equal to `total_reduction_kwh` multiplied by 0.42 dollars per kilowatt-hour, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `penalty_usd` is 0.00. When performance falls below that floor the position reverses: `incentive_usd` is 0.00 and `penalty_usd` is the shortfall — the obligation minus the delivered total — multiplied by 0.18 dollars per kilowatt-hour, rounded half up to two decimal places. On settlement the account's `dr_incentive_accrued_usd` is credited by exactly `incentive_usd`; that ledger must at all times equal the sum of `incentive_usd` across the account's events whose `settlement_state` is `SETTLED`. Where the settlement set is empty the numbers are defined rather than undefined: `total_reduction_kwh` is 0.00, the obligation is 0.00, `performance_pct` is 0.0 without any division being attempted, both money fields are 0.00, and the event settles normally.

Utilities do occasionally recalculate, and an `EnergyManager` may reverse a settlement, moving `settlement_state` `SETTLED → REVERSED` and storing the `reversal_reason`. Reversal requires the settlement to be `SETTLED`; reversing something still `PENDING` or already `REVERSED` is `ERR_SETTLEMENT_STATE_INVALID`. The reversal debits the account's `dr_incentive_accrued_usd` by exactly the amount that event credited, which keeps the ledger identity intact, and it deliberately retains `incentive_usd`, `penalty_usd` and `performance_pct` on the event as evidence of what was originally computed. The participation dimension stays at `COMPLETED` throughout.

### 3.9. Fault Diagnostics, Triage and Maintenance Execution

Automated fault detection and diagnostics are reported against a commissioned store by either the store's gateway or a `MaintenanceTech`. Each report names the `equipment_ref` tag the fault was raised against, classifies it by `equipment_class` — `REFRIGERATION`, `HVAC` or `LIGHTING` — identifies the diagnostic rule by `fault_code`, bands it by `severity` (`INFO`, `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`), states how many times the rule has fired for this equipment as `occurrence_count`, an integer of at least 1, and quantifies the exposure as `affected_load_kw`, a non-negative value at one decimal place. The fault is persisted in `DETECTED` with `detected_at_iso` stamped from the logical clock and `triaged_by_user_id` null. Faults of a store can be listed with filters on `state`, `equipment_class` and `severity`.

Scoring happens at report time and produces `fault_score`, a single number that drives everything downstream. It is built in seven steps. The severity band contributes a base weight: 5 for `INFO`, 20 for `MINOR`, 45 for `MAJOR` and 70 for `CRITICAL`. Persistence contributes a multiplier: one plus one tenth for each recurrence beyond the first, where the number of recurrences counted is the `occurrence_count` minus one but capped at ten, the multiplier being rounded half up to two decimal places — so a fifth occurrence gives 1.40 and an eleventh or beyond gives 2.00. Affected load contributes a second multiplier: one plus the `affected_load_kw` divided by 200.0, rounded half up to four decimal places, and then capped at 1.25, so any fault affecting 50.0 kW or more takes the cap. The base weight multiplied by the persistence multiplier, rounded half up to two decimal places, gives a weighted figure; that weighted figure multiplied by the load multiplier, again rounded half up to two decimal places, gives a loaded figure. Refrigeration equipment then attracts a flat food-safety premium of 15.00 points added to the loaded figure, while `HVAC` and `LIGHTING` faults take no premium. Finally `fault_score` is that result rounded half up to one decimal place and capped at 100.0. A `MAJOR`, sixth-occurrence, 20.0 kW refrigeration fault therefore scores 89.3, while the same severity and load on `HVAC` at its third occurrence scores 62.1.

Two thresholds read that score. A work order raised from the fault is `URGENT` when `fault_score` is at or above 70.0 and `ROUTINE` otherwise, so a score of exactly 70.0 is urgent. A fault may be suppressed only when `fault_score` is strictly below 50.0, so a score of exactly 50.0 may not be suppressed.

Triage is a `MaintenanceTech` decision expressed as an `outcome` of either `WORK_ORDER` or `SUPPRESS`, and it is legal only from `DETECTED` — a fault already `TRIAGED`, `SUPPRESSED` or `RESOLVED` yields `ERR_FAULT_STATE_INVALID`. Suppression moves the fault `DETECTED → SUPPRESSED`, records `triaged_by_user_id`, creates nothing, and is refused with `ERR_FAULT_SUPPRESSION_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 422) when the score is 50.0 or above. A suppressed fault also leaves the refrigeration fault-density population that the risk-adjusted retrofit score counts, since that population comprises only faults in `DETECTED` or `TRIAGED`.

Choosing a work order moves the fault `DETECTED → TRIAGED`, records `triaged_by_user_id`, and sets off a four-link cascade. The first link creates a `MaintenanceWorkOrder` in `OPEN` whose `fault_id` points at the fault, with `priority` `URGENT` or `ROUTINE` per the threshold above, and with `scheduled_start_iso`, `scheduled_end_iso`, `completed_at_iso` and `completion_note` all null. The second link, when that work order is `URGENT`, increments the store's `urgent_work_order_count` — which counts the store's work orders whose `priority` is `URGENT` and whose `state` is `OPEN` or `SCHEDULED`, reached through their faults — and, additionally, when the fault's `equipment_class` is `REFRIGERATION`, moves the store `ACTIVE → DEGRADED`. The third link recomputes the store's posture, raising `NORMAL → CONSTRAINED`, which in turn revokes the store's `ACTIVE` schedule-extension overrides. The fourth link sets `escalated_review` to true on every retrofit project of that store still in `PROPOSED`. Triage reports the whole cascade back as `fault_id`, `fault_state`, `fault_score`, `work_order_id`, `work_order_priority`, `work_order_state`, `store_bms_status`, `store_energy_posture`, `urgent_work_order_count` and `escalated_retrofit_count`; on a suppression the three work-order fields are null.

Scheduling a work order supplies `scheduled_start_iso` and `scheduled_end_iso` and is legal from `OPEN`, which advances the order `OPEN → SCHEDULED`, and equally from `SCHEDULED`, which is a rescheduling that leaves the state at `SCHEDULED` while overwriting both stored instants. A `COMPLETED` work order is locked and yields `ERR_WORK_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`. Two independent capacity rules then apply, in order. The first is per-equipment: no two work orders may occupy intersecting windows on the same `equipment_ref`, reached through each order's fault, where windows are compared as closed intervals against other orders in `SCHEDULED`, and the order being rescheduled is excluded from its own conflict set so that a reschedule onto an overlapping window of its own is not self-defeating. A collision is `ERR_WORK_ORDER_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). The second is per-store crew capacity: at most two work orders of the same store may occupy mutually overlapping windows, so a request is refused with `ERR_WORK_ORDER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) when two or more *other* `SCHEDULED` work orders of that store already overlap the requested window — and this rule ignores `equipment_ref` entirely, because the constraint is technicians, not machines.

Completion carries a `completion_note` and is legal from `OPEN` or `SCHEDULED`, moving the order to `COMPLETED` and stamping `completed_at_iso` from the clock. Three cascades follow. The owning fault moves `TRIAGED → RESOLVED`. Because that fault is no longer triaged, the store's `urgent_work_order_count` decrements when the completed order was `URGENT`. Because that count changed, the store recomputes its posture, falling `CONSTRAINED → NORMAL` when no other constraining input holds. What completion does *not* do is repair the building management system: `bms_status` remains `DEGRADED` and requires an explicit re-commissioning and re-activation, as §3.2 sets out. Work orders raised from a fault can be listed with filters on `state` and `priority`.

### 3.10. Capital Retrofits, Measured Savings and Portfolio Benchmarking

Capital measures are proposed by either an `EnergyAnalyst` or an `EnergyManager`, naming the `measure_type` — one of `LED_RETROFIT`, `CASE_DOORS`, `ECM_MOTORS`, `HVAC_RTU_REPLACEMENT` or `EMS_UPGRADE` — the installed cost as `capex_usd`, a positive amount at two decimal places, and the engineering estimate as `projected_annual_savings_kwh`, a positive quantity at two decimal places. Proposal immediately scores the project on the **simple payback** basis. The projected annual kilowatt-hours multiplied by the owning store's `blended_rate_usd_per_kwh`, rounded half up to two decimal places, gives `annual_savings_usd`. The capital cost divided by those annual savings, rounded half up to two decimal places, gives `payback_years`. And `priority_score` is one hundred less ten points for each year of payback, rounded half up to one decimal place and then clamped into the range from 0.0 to 100.0 inclusive. A project costing 240240.00 dollars against 520000.00 projected kilowatt-hours at the default blended rate therefore shows annual savings of 57200.00 dollars, a payback of 4.20 years and a priority score of 58.0. The project is persisted in `PROPOSED` with `priority_basis` `SIMPLE_PAYBACK`, `escalated_review` false, the proposing operator recorded in `proposed_by_user_id`, and every measurement field — `install_completed_iso`, `baseline_period_kwh`, `post_period_kwh`, `mv_period_days`, `adjusted_baseline_kwh`, `measured_savings_kwh`, `realization_rate`, `approved_by_user_id` and `revocation_reason` — null. Projects of a store can be listed with filters on `state`, `measure_type`, `priority_basis` and `escalated_review`.

The capital decision is an `EnergyManager`'s, expressed as a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, and it is subject in order to duty separation (the operator must differ from `proposed_by_user_id`), to the project being in `PROPOSED`, and to the store having a deployed building management system in `ACTIVE` or `DEGRADED`. Both outcomes record the deciding operator in `approved_by_user_id`. A rejection moves the project `PROPOSED → REJECTED` and leaves `priority_basis` at `SIMPLE_PAYBACK` with the proposal-time numbers intact — `REJECTED` is terminal. An approval moves the project `PROPOSED → APPROVED`, switches `priority_basis` to `RISK_ADJUSTED`, and rewrites all three scoring fields under the risk-adjusted variant. That variant is deliberately more pessimistic about savings and more attentive to equipment risk. `annual_savings_usd` becomes the projected annual kilowatt-hours multiplied by the store's blended rate and rounded half up to two decimal places, and that rounded figure is then multiplied by the portfolio default realization rate of 0.85 and rounded half up to two decimal places again — the two roundings are sequential, not collapsed into one. `payback_years` is recomputed as the capital cost divided by that haircut savings figure, rounded half up to two decimal places. A refrigeration fault-density bonus is then added: the store's fault diagnostics whose `equipment_class` is `REFRIGERATION` and whose `state` is `DETECTED` or `TRIAGED` are counted, that count is capped at three, and eight points are awarded per counted fault. `priority_score` becomes one hundred, less ten points per payback year, plus that bonus, rounded half up to one decimal place and clamped to the range 0.0 through 100.0. The project above, approved with no refrigeration faults outstanding, drops to 48620.00 dollars of annual savings, a 4.94-year payback and a score of 50.6 — a deliberate seven-and-a-half-point haircut against its proposal score; the same project approved with five refrigeration faults outstanding takes the capped bonus of twenty-four points and scores 74.6.

Measurement and verification is an `EnergyAnalyst` submission, legal from `APPROVED` and equally from `SHORTFALL_REVIEW` so that a disappointing measurement can be re-measured later. It supplies `install_completed_iso`, the instant the measure finished installation and the post period began; `baseline_period_kwh`, the metered consumption of the pre-installation baseline; `post_period_kwh`, the metered consumption after installation; and `mv_period_days`, the length of the post period. Two temporal conditions gate it, both refused with `ERR_MV_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 422): `mv_period_days` must be at least 60, and `install_completed_iso` must be at or before the logical clock.

The verification arithmetic normalises for weather before it credits anything. The store's `climate_zone` selects a climate factor — 1.08 for `HOT_HUMID`, 1.05 for `HOT_DRY`, 1.00 for `MIXED`, 0.96 for `MARINE` and 0.93 for `COLD` — and `adjusted_baseline_kwh` is `baseline_period_kwh` multiplied by that factor, rounded half up to two decimal places. `measured_savings_kwh` is the adjusted baseline minus `post_period_kwh`, rounded half up to two decimal places. What the engineering estimate promised over the same span is the `projected_annual_savings_kwh` multiplied by `mv_period_days` and divided by 365 days, rounded half up to two decimal places. `realization_rate` is the measured savings divided by that prorated promise, rounded half up to four decimal places. A `HOT_HUMID` store with a 300000.00 kWh baseline over a ninety-day post period against a 520000.00 kWh annual projection therefore has an adjusted baseline of 324000.00 kWh and a prorated expectation of 128219.18 kWh.

The verification then branches, and the two branches differ in exactly one respect — the ledger. When `realization_rate` is at or above 0.8000 the project moves to `VERIFIED` and the store's `verified_savings_kwh_ytd` is credited by exactly `measured_savings_kwh`; a rate of precisely 0.8000 verifies, because the floor is inclusive. When `realization_rate` falls below 0.8000 the project moves to `SHORTFALL_REVIEW` instead and nothing whatsoever is credited to the ledger. Everything else is symmetric: both branches write `adjusted_baseline_kwh`, `measured_savings_kwh` and `realization_rate`, and both branches recompute the store's `portfolio_realization_rate` as the arithmetic mean of `realization_rate` across that store's projects whose `state` is `VERIFIED` or `SHORTFALL_REVIEW`, rounded half up to four decimal places — falling back to the portfolio default of 0.85 when that set is empty. The store's `verified_savings_kwh_ytd` must at all times equal the sum of `measured_savings_kwh` across that store's projects in `VERIFIED`.

Measurements are sometimes wrong, and an `EnergyManager` may revoke a verification by supplying a `revocation_reason`. Revocation is legal only from `VERIFIED` — anything else is `ERR_RETROFIT_STATE_INVALID` — and moves the project `VERIFIED → SHORTFALL_REVIEW`, debits the store's `verified_savings_kwh_ytd` by exactly that project's `measured_savings_kwh` so the ledger identity holds again, stores the reason, and recomputes `portfolio_realization_rate`. Because `SHORTFALL_REVIEW` is also a legal origin for a fresh measurement, a revoked project can be re-measured and re-verified.

Benchmarking closes the loop between spend and performance, and is available to `PlatformAdmin`, `EnergyManager` and `EnergyAnalyst`. The report is always scoped to one peer group, so it requires both `climate_zone` and `store_format` as query parameters, and accepts an optional `outliers_only` flag that narrows the reported store list to flagged stores only. For each store the system first assembles the qualifying bills: those reachable through the store's utility accounts whose `validation_state` is `VALIDATED` or `ADJUSTED` and whose `period_end_iso` falls inside the trailing 365-day window ending at the logical clock, inclusive at both ends. Bills still `RECEIVED`, `VARIANCE_FLAGGED` or `DISPUTED` are excluded — an unvalidated invoice never influences a benchmark. Summing `billed_kwh` over those bills gives the window's consumption, and `eui_kbtu_per_sqft` is that consumption converted to thousands of British thermal units at 3.412 per kilowatt-hour, divided by the store's `floor_area_sqft`, rounded half up to two decimal places. A store with no qualifying bill has a null intensity.

Peer comparison then proceeds per store. The peer group of a store is every store sharing its `climate_zone` and `store_format` and having a non-null intensity, *including* the subject store itself; a store with a null intensity is in nobody's peer group, not even its own group's count. The comparison set, however, excludes the subject: the peer median reported as `peer_median_eui` is the central value of the other peers sorted by intensity ascending with ties broken by earlier `created_at`, and where the number of other peers is even it is the mean of the two central values rounded half up to two decimal places. `outlier_threshold_eui` is 1.30 times that median, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `benchmark_outlier_flag` is true when the store's own intensity is at or above that threshold — the boundary counts as an outlier. `benchmark_percentile` is one hundred multiplied by the number of peer-group members whose intensity is at or below the subject's own, divided by the size of the peer group, rounded half up to one decimal place; because the subject is in its own peer group, the highest store in any group scores 100.0. When the subject has no other peers at all the threshold is undefined: `benchmark_outlier_flag` stays false and `benchmark_percentile` is 100.0. A store with a null intensity has a null percentile and a false flag. Such a store is still reported: the row list covers every store of the requested `climate_zone` and `store_format`, ordered by `created_at` ascending, whether its intensity is null or not, and a null-intensity store's row carries a null `peer_median_eui` and a null `outlier_threshold_eui` alongside its null intensity and percentile. The `outliers_only` flag narrows that row list alone: `peer_group_count`, `peer_group_median_eui` and `outlier_count` are unchanged by it, and `outlier_count` counts every flagged store of the requested climate zone and format rather than the rows actually returned. Alongside the per-store rows — each carrying `store_id`, `code`, `eui_kbtu_per_sqft`, `benchmark_percentile`, `benchmark_outlier_flag`, `peer_median_eui` and `outlier_threshold_eui` — the report echoes the requested `climate_zone` and `store_format` and adds `peer_group_count`, `outlier_count`, and `peer_group_median_eui`, which is the median over the whole group including every member and is reported for context only rather than used in any threshold.
