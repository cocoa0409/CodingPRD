# Challenge Course Ops API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A challenge course operator runs a physical site of ground initiatives, low elements, high elements, climbing walls and zip lines, and sells structured outdoor-education programs on that site to schools, camps, non-profits and corporate groups. Ten separately regulated concerns decide whether one group may climb on one morning: the annual professional inspection cycle on every element, the facilitator's own pre-use inspection on the day, facilitator certification levels and supervision ratios, participant health screening and weight limits, program sequencing from icebreaker through high elements to debrief, belay-equipment life tracking and rescue readiness, lightning, wind and cold closure thresholds, incident reporting and review, documented emergency-medical access, and the commercial terms of the booking itself. In most operations those ten concerns live in ten binders, and the conflict between them is discovered at the base of a zip line with thirty eleven-year-olds already harnessed.

This document specifies the Challenge Course Operations API: a single server that holds all ten concerns as data and refuses unsafe work at the moment it is attempted rather than in a retrospective audit. The design collapses the site's whole safety condition into one derived value, the course **posture**, and funnels every risk signal — weather, overdue inspections, restricted elements, quarantined belay hardware, open serious incidents, thin rescue staffing — into it. Two operations then carry almost the entire compliance burden: confirming a booking, which is where the commercial commitment is made, and starting a program block, which is where people leave the ground. Everything else in the system exists to make those two decisions correct and explainable after the fact.

Two technical constraints shape the specification. First, every resource is addressed as a top-level collection beneath the `/challenge-course` prefix and linked to its neighbours by identifiers carried in request bodies rather than by nesting; the only exception is the administrative clock endpoint described in §2.4. Second, the server keeps no eventual consistency: every derived counter, forecast date, posture value and roster status is recomputed inside the request that could have changed it, so a read immediately following a write always observes the settled world.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operating Roles and Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that user acts. If either header is absent, or present but empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint (see the table in §4), the rejection is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `ProgramDirector`, `SafetyOfficer`, `Facilitator`, `ProfessionalInspector` and `GroupCoordinator`.

A `ProgramDirector` is operator management. It builds courses, installs and retires elements, hires staff, records training, registers group clients, posts client payments, designs programs, staffs blocks, runs the daily opening checkpoint and may quote a booking. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramDirector`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may accept and log it but never compares it to any row's `id`.

The other four roles are entity-backed, and their identity claim is verified before any business rule runs. When `X-User-Role` is `Facilitator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Facilitator` record, and that record's `staff_type` must be `STAFF_FACILITATOR`. When `X-User-Role` is `ProfessionalInspector`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of a `Facilitator` record, whose `staff_type` must be `CONTRACT_INSPECTOR` and whose `is_operator_employee` must be false. When `X-User-Role` is `SafetyOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of a `Facilitator` record whose `staff_type` is `SAFETY_OFFICER` and whose `certification_level` is `LEVEL_3`. When `X-User-Role` is `GroupCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `GroupClient`; ownership checks on any resource that references a group client compare that resource's client foreign key to `X-User-ID`. If the identifier does not resolve to an instance of the backing entity, or the resolved instance's discriminator does not match the claimed role, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). This identity resolution is applied to every endpoint except course registration and the clock endpoint of §2.4, both of which are reachable before any staff record exists.

Ownership is enforced separately from identity. A `GroupCoordinator` may only confirm or cancel a booking, add a participant to it, or submit a participant's screening packet when `X-User-ID` equals the booking's `group_client_id`. A `Facilitator` recording an operational inspection log must supply its own identifier as the log's `facilitator_id`. A `ProfessionalInspector` filing an inspection outcome must be the same person named as that inspection's `inspector_id`. Any of these six operations performed on somebody else's resource is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

Independence between the person who raises a safety artefact and the person who accepts it is a further, separate rule; it is described in §2.7.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated by two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1 and may not be larger than 100. `offset` skips a number of leading records and defaults to 0; negative offsets are not accepted. A `limit` outside the permitted range, a negative `offset`, or a value for either that is not an integer is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); an omitted or empty parameter simply takes its default.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list is ordered by `created_at` ascending. The list-endpoint envelope reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` counts the records matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` states whether records remain beyond the current window.

Collections also accept optional equality filters, all combinable with each other and with pagination. Facilitators may be filtered by `course_id`, `staff_type`, `cert_status` and `certification_level`; the per-course facilitator roster accepts `cert_status`. Elements accept `course_id`, `status`, `element_type` and `restricting_log_id`. Professional inspections accept `element_id`, `inspector_id`, `status` and `outcome`. Operational logs accept `element_id`, `facilitator_id`, `log_date` and `result`. Training records accept `facilitator_id`, `kind` and `completed_on`; the per-facilitator training list accepts `kind`. Block assignments accept `program_block_id`, `facilitator_id` and `assignment_role`. Bookings accept `course_id`, `group_client_id`, `status`, `roster_status` and `program_date`. Participants accept `booking_id`, `screening_status`, `health_form_status` and `is_chaperone`. Program blocks accept `booking_id`, `element_id`, `phase` and `status`. Belay equipment accepts `course_id`, `assigned_element_id`, `status`, `equipment_kind` and `retirement_basis`. Weather observations accept `course_id` and `resulting_posture`; the per-course weather list is the one exception to the default ordering and is returned newest first, which means `created_at` descending — insertion order reversed — rather than `observed_at` descending. Incidents accept `course_id`, `element_id`, `participant_id`, `reported_by_facilitator_id`, `status` and `severity`. Query parameters an endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected.

### 2.4. The Logical Operating Clock

Every date-sensitive rule in this system — certification expiry, rescue-drill recency, refresher-hour windows, element and equipment retirement forecasts, inspection due dates, cancellation notice tiers and lightning hold windows — reads a server-held logical UTC clock rather than wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, that clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to the host's real time.

Any of the five roles may move the clock by posting `POST /_clock/set_current_utc` with a single field, `current_utc`, carrying the new instant. Setting the clock recomputes nothing on its own: no certification lapses, no forecast shifts and no posture changes until the next write lands or the daily opening checkpoint of §3.1 runs. This is deliberate — the checkpoint is the mechanism by which the passage of time becomes visible in the data, and tests drive it explicitly.

Where a rule speaks of "the clock date", it means the UTC calendar date of the logical clock; where it speaks of "the clock instant", it means the full logical timestamp.

### 2.5. Course Posture: The Site-Wide Operating Gate

Each course carries a derived `posture` of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `CLOSED`, and a companion `posture_reason` drawn from `NONE`, `LIGHTNING_STOP`, `WIND_STOP`, `COLD_STOP`, `INSPECTION_OVERDUE`, `RESCUE_READINESS`, `SERIOUS_INCIDENT`, `ELEMENT_RESTRICTED`, `LIGHTNING_WATCH` and `WIND_WATCH`. A newly registered course starts at `OPEN` with reason `NONE`.

Posture is derived by an ordered first-match walk over ten clauses. The first clause that holds sets both `posture` and `posture_reason`, and no later clause is examined; the ordering is observable, so a site that is simultaneously under a lightning hold and overdue for a professional inspection reports `LIGHTNING_STOP`, never `INSPECTION_OVERDUE`. In order:

1. A lightning hold is active — that is, `lightning_hold_until` is set and lies strictly after the current logical clock instant. Result: `CLOSED`, reason `LIGHTNING_STOP`.
2. `latest_wind_speed_kph` is at least 48.0. Result: `CLOSED`, reason `WIND_STOP`.
3. `latest_air_temp_c` is at most -12.0. Result: `CLOSED`, reason `COLD_STOP`.
4. The course's `next_professional_inspection_due_on` falls strictly before the clock date. Result: `CLOSED`, reason `INSPECTION_OVERDUE`.
5. `rescue_readiness_ok` is false. Result: `CLOSED`, reason `RESCUE_READINESS`.
6. The course has at least one incident of severity `SERIOUS` whose status is `UNDER_REVIEW`. Result: `RESTRICTED`, reason `SERIOUS_INCIDENT`.
7. `restricted_element_count` is greater than zero, or `out_of_service_element_count` is greater than zero. Result: `RESTRICTED`, reason `ELEMENT_RESTRICTED`.
8. `latest_lightning_distance_km` is greater than 8.0 and at most 16.0. Result: `RESTRICTED`, reason `LIGHTNING_WATCH`.
9. `latest_wind_speed_kph` is at least 32.0 and below 48.0. Result: `RESTRICTED`, reason `WIND_WATCH`.
10. Otherwise: `OPEN`, reason `NONE`.

Two inputs to that walk deserve their own definitions. The course-level `next_professional_inspection_due_on` is the earliest `next_professional_inspection_due_on` among the course's elements whose `status` is not `RETIRED`; when the course has no such element the value is null and clause 4 cannot fire. `rescue_readiness_ok` is true only when both halves hold: `rescue_ready_facilitator_count` — the number of the course's facilitators whose `cert_status` is `ACTIVE` — is at least two, and `ems_response_minutes` is at most 30. Either half failing closes the site.

Posture is re-derived at the end of every write that can move one of those signals: recording a weather observation, running the daily opening checkpoint, and any element, belay-equipment or incident transition. Certification-bearing writes are the deliberate exception. Adding a facilitator or recording a training record refreshes `rescue_ready_facilitator_count` and `rescue_readiness_ok` immediately, but does not re-derive `posture`; a brand-new, entirely unstaffed course therefore still reads `OPEN` until its first weather observation, element, equipment or incident transition, or its first daily opening checkpoint. Conversely, a site closed for `RESCUE_READINESS` reopens at the next checkpoint once two facilitators hold `ACTIVE` certification. Whether a re-derivation also increments the course's `version` depends on the write: recording a weather observation, disposing a professional inspection, returning an element to service, retiring an element, closing an incident and the daily opening checkpoint always increment it, whereas installing an element, adding belay equipment, opening an incident review and reporting a non-`SERIOUS` incident increment it only when `posture` or `posture_reason` actually moves. Crediting cycles — at block completion or through a belay usage log — re-derives `belay_ready` and may block out dependent blocks without re-deriving posture or touching the course row at all.

Posture gates work rather than data. Starting any program block while the posture is `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_COURSE_CLOSED` (HTTP 409); starting a block whose phase is `HIGH_ELEMENT` requires the posture to be exactly `OPEN`, and a `RESTRICTED` posture refuses it with `ERR_COURSE_RESTRICTED_HIGH_ELEMENT` (HTTP 409). Reads, roster edits, quoting and inspection work are never gated on posture.

### 2.6. Cascading Consistency Between Site, Program and Roster

Because the site, the program and the roster are separate aggregates, a single write frequently has to settle several of them. The server performs those settlements inside the originating request, in a fixed order, and every entity it touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented exactly as if it had been written directly. Three cascades recur throughout §3 and are named here once.

**Cascade A — a major defect restricts an element.** An operational inspection log whose `result` is `DEFECT_MAJOR` moves the element from `SERVICEABLE` to `RESTRICTED`, records the new log's identifier in the element's `restricting_log_id`, sets the log's `caused_restriction` to true and adds one to the course's `restricted_element_count`. Because that element is now restricted, every program block rigged to it whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` moves to `BLOCKED_UNSAFE`, processed in ascending `sequence_index` order and, for equal indexes, ascending `created_at`. Because those blocks are now blocked, each owning booking recomputes `blocked_block_count` and, if its `status` is `CONFIRMED`, moves to `AT_RISK`. Because those bookings are now at risk, the course's `at_risk_booking_count` rises by one per booking, posture and `posture_reason` are re-derived, and the course's `version` increments.

**Cascade B — a failed professional inspection quarantines belay hardware.** An element driven to `OUT_OF_SERVICE` has its `restricting_log_id` cleared to null and adds one to the course's `out_of_service_element_count`. Because it is out of service, every belay item whose `assigned_element_id` names it and whose `status` is `IN_SERVICE` moves to `QUARANTINED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order. Because that hardware is quarantined, the element's `belay_ready` re-derives to false, the course's `quarantined_equipment_count` rises, posture and `posture_reason` are re-derived and the course's `version` increments. Separately from that three-link chain, the element's `SCHEDULED` blocks fan out to `BLOCKED_UNSAFE` and their `CONFIRMED` bookings to `AT_RISK`.

**Cascade C — weather closes the site mid-delivery.** When a weather observation drives the posture to `CLOSED`, every program block of that course whose `status` is `IN_PROGRESS` moves to `SUSPENDED`, processed in ascending `sequence_index` order and then ascending `created_at`. Because those blocks are suspended, each owning booking whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` moves to `WEATHER_HOLD`, the course's `at_risk_booking_count` rises by one per booking, and that booking's next cancellation will settle under the operator-weather variant rather than the client-notice variant. The course's `version` increments.

Each cascade has a compensating half, and the counters are maintained symmetrically: returning an element to service decrements `restricted_element_count` or `out_of_service_element_count`, releases quarantined hardware rigged to it back to `IN_SERVICE`, walks its `BLOCKED_UNSAFE` blocks back to `SCHEDULED` in ascending `sequence_index` then `created_at` order, and returns any booking whose `blocked_block_count` has fallen back to zero from `AT_RISK` to `CONFIRMED` with a matching decrement of `at_risk_booking_count`. Releasing a weather hold at the daily opening checkpoint moves each `WEATHER_HOLD` booking back to `CONFIRMED` in ascending `created_at` order and decrements `at_risk_booking_count` per booking; blocks that were suspended stay `SUSPENDED` but become startable again.

The server is expected to make these transitions on its own, without a client request naming the affected child or parent. Where an ordering is stated it is observable and must be honoured: the sweeps of the daily opening checkpoint in particular always walk facilitators, elements and equipment in ascending `created_at` order, never in identifier order.

### 2.7. Segregation of Safety Duties

The operator's accreditation requires that nobody accept their own safety artefact. Three acceptances are therefore checked against the acting `X-User-ID`, and each failure is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

Returning an element to service may not be performed by the facilitator who filed the operational log that restricted it: when the element's `restricting_log_id` is set, the acting user must differ from that log's `facilitator_id`. When `restricting_log_id` is null — which is the case for restrictions raised by a conditional professional inspection, by a serious incident or by a corrective incident closure — there is no counterparty and the check is skipped entirely. Disposing a professional inspection may not be performed by the inspector named in the inspection's `inspector_id`. Closing an incident may not be performed by the facilitator named in its `reported_by_facilitator_id`.

### 2.8. Identifier Resolution Order

Every identifier that appears in a URL path is resolved before any business rule is evaluated, strictly left to right, and the first unresolvable identifier decides the error. The mapping from path variable to error code is fixed: `course_id` yields `ERR_COURSE_NOT_FOUND`, `element_id` yields `ERR_ELEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `facilitator_id` yields `ERR_FACILITATOR_NOT_FOUND`, `inspection_id` yields `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `log_id` yields `ERR_OPERATIONAL_LOG_NOT_FOUND`, `group_client_id` yields `ERR_GROUP_CLIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `booking_id` yields `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `participant_id` yields `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`, `program_block_id` yields `ERR_PROGRAM_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND`, `equipment_id` yields `ERR_EQUIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `incident_id` yields `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `training_record_id` yields `ERR_TRAINING_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `block_assignment_id` yields `ERR_BLOCK_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND` and `observation_id` yields `ERR_WEATHER_OBSERVATION_NOT_FOUND`. All fourteen carry HTTP 404.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved next, in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, and produce the same not-found code for the entity that failed to resolve. A creation payload that names both a course and an element, where neither exists, therefore fails on whichever appears first in the endpoint's declared input order.

### 2.9. Record Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID with no type prefix. Every resource also carries a `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped when the row is written. That stamp — like `updated_at` and the envelope's `api_request_timestamp` — is read from the host's real clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.4, and is forced strictly upward from one row to the next, so `created_at` ascending is always exactly insertion order and never ambiguous.

Most resources are mutable and additionally carry an `updated_at` in the same format, refreshed on every write that changes them, and a `version`: an integer that is 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly one on every mutating write to that row, including writes the server performs on its own initiative while settling a cascade. Courses, elements, facilitators, professional inspections, group clients, bookings, participants, program blocks, belay equipment and incidents all behave this way.

Four record types are immutable append-only evidence and are deliberately narrower: training records, operational inspection logs, block assignments and weather observations carry only `id` and `created_at`. They have no `updated_at`, no `version`, and no update or delete endpoint; once written they are never edited, and corrections are made by writing a further record.

Client-supplied values for `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` or `version` are silently ignored rather than rejected.

### 2.10. Counting, Money and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is an integer number of cents; there are no fractional-cent values anywhere in a payload. Three rounding modes are used, and which one applies is never a matter of implementer taste.

Quote lines round half up, once per program-block line, before the lines are summed. Summing unrounded line amounts and rounding the total once is a different number and is wrong. The high-element risk surcharge, the deposit requirement, the chaperone requirement, the supervision ratio and the projected day count of a usage-based retirement forecast all round **up** to the next whole unit — a partial cent of risk loading, a partial chaperone, a partial facilitator or a partial day of remaining element life is never absorbed by the operator. The cancellation fee percentage rounds half up and is then capped, as described in §3.9.

Dates are whole UTC calendar dates and date arithmetic is anniversary arithmetic: adding a number of years to 29 February lands on 28 February in a non-leap target year. Weather readings — distances, wind speeds and temperatures — are carried to one decimal place, and every weather threshold is inclusive on the dangerous side. Life-expiry comparisons on belay hardware are strict: an item whose `retirement_forecast_date` equals the clock date is already unusable, not usable for one last day.

### 2.11. Optimistic Concurrency on Booking Writes

Two writes take a booking's `version` as an explicit input, because both legitimately supersede a value the client may not have observed and neither is refused by the state machine on its own.

Running or re-running a quote requires `expected_booking_version` in the request body as a non-optional field, carrying the `version` of the booking the client last read. If it does not equal the booking's current `version`, the quote is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BOOKING_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Re-quoting a booking that is already `QUOTED` is a legitimate operation that replaces the prior quote in place, so without this check a second writer would silently overwrite a quote computed from a roster it never saw.

Adding a participant to a booking likewise requires `expected_booking_version` in the request body as a non-optional field, matched against the booking's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BOOKING_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A roster addition supersedes the booking's frozen `billable_head_count` and its `roster_status`, and nothing else in the specification would refuse it, so this is the only guard that stops a coordinator from extending a roster whose quote or screening posture it has not seen.

No other endpoint accepts a version field of any kind. Confirming, cancelling, staffing, starting and completing take no expected-version input, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.12. Order of Rejection

When a request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the first failure ends the request. The order matters most on the two heavily gated operations — booking confirmation and block start — where a dozen independent concerns compete to reject the same call.

1. Header presence and role validity — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. Role authorisation for the endpoint — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. Identity resolution for entity-backed roles — `ERR_ROLE_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. Applies to every endpoint except course registration and the clock endpoint.
4. Request shape: the body parses, every required field is present, every enum value belongs to its declared set, every number sits inside its declared range, `limit` is between 1 and 100 with a non-negative `offset`, and a block's `planned_start_at` is strictly before its `planned_end_at` — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Applies to every endpoint. Three shape rules are deliberately late rather than evaluated here, and are reported only once the earlier checks have passed: the non-empty `findings_note` demanded by a conditional or failing inspection filing, the at-least-one-block rule on a quote run, and the `corrective_action`/`element_id` pair demanded by a corrective incident closure — all three sit after the state precondition of step 9, and the closure's also after the segregation check of step 10.
5. Path identifier resolution, left to right — the fourteen not-found codes of §2.8. Applies to every endpoint with path parameters.
6. Body foreign-key resolution, in declared field order — the matching not-found code. Applies to the creation endpoints for elements, facilitators, training records, professional inspections, operational logs, bookings, participants, program blocks, block assignments, belay equipment, weather observations and incidents.
7. Ownership — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Applies to booking confirmation, booking cancellation, participant creation, screening-packet submission, operational-log creation and inspection filing.
8. Booking version match — `ERR_STALE_BOOKING_VERSION`. Applies to the quote run and to participant creation.
9. State precondition for the requested transition — one of `ERR_ELEMENT_NOT_RESTRICTED`, `ERR_ELEMENT_NOT_SERVICEABLE`, `ERR_ELEMENT_RETIRED`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FILED`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_QUOTABLE`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_CONFIRMABLE`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_CONFIRMED`, `ERR_BOOKING_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_SCHEDULED`, `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_REPORTED`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`, `ERR_EQUIPMENT_RETIRED` or `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, all HTTP 409. Applies to every action endpoint.
10. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Applies to element return-to-service, inspection disposal and incident closure.
11. Course posture — `ERR_COURSE_CLOSED`, or `ERR_COURSE_RESTRICTED_HIGH_ELEMENT` for a high-element phase. Applies to block start.
12. The rigged element's daily pre-use inspection — `ERR_DAILY_INSPECTION_MISSING`. Applies to block start.
13. The element's professional inspection currency — `ERR_INSPECTION_OVERDUE`. Applies to booking confirmation and block start.
14. Belay readiness of the rigged element — `ERR_BELAY_NOT_READY`. Applies to block start.
15. Rescue readiness — `ERR_RESCUE_READINESS_UNMET`. Applies to booking confirmation and block start.
16. Emergency-medical access for a high-element phase — `ERR_EMS_ACCESS_UNSUITABLE`. Applies to block start.
17. No open incident on the element — `ERR_OPEN_INCIDENT_BLOCKS_SERVICE`. Applies to element return-to-service.
18. The defect that restricted the element has been cleared — `ERR_DEFECT_NOT_CLEARED`. Applies to element return-to-service.
19. Participant screening completeness — `ERR_PARTICIPANT_SCREENING_INCOMPLETE`. Applies to booking confirmation and block start.
20. Participant weight limits on the rigged element — `ERR_WEIGHT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Applies to block start.
21. Supervision ratio and certification capacity — `ERR_SUPERVISION_RATIO_UNMET`. Applies to block start.
22. Assigned facilitators hold active certification — `ERR_FACILITATOR_CERT_INACTIVE`. Applies to block staffing and block start.
23. Certification level against the element type — `ERR_CERT_LEVEL_INSUFFICIENT`. Applies to block staffing.
24. Facilitator belongs to the same course — `ERR_FACILITATOR_COURSE_MISMATCH`. Applies to block staffing and operational-log creation.
25. Inspector independence — `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_INDEPENDENT`. Applies to professional-inspection creation.
26. Phase and element-type compatibility — `ERR_ELEMENT_PHASE_MISMATCH`. Applies to program-block creation.
27. Element belongs to the same course — `ERR_ELEMENT_COURSE_MISMATCH`. Applies to program-block creation and belay-equipment creation.
28. Program sequencing — one of `ERR_DUPLICATE_BLOCK_SEQUENCE`, `ERR_SEQUENCING_VIOLATION`, `ERR_SEQUENCE_PREREQUISITE_MISSING` or `ERR_SEQUENCE_GAP`. Applies to program-block creation.
29. A debrief block closes the program — `ERR_DEBRIEF_BLOCK_MISSING`. Applies to booking confirmation.
30. The debrief was actually recorded — `ERR_DEBRIEF_NOT_RECORDED`. Applies to block completion.
31. Chaperone coverage — `ERR_CHAPERONE_SHORTFALL`. Applies to booking confirmation.
32. Element occupancy overlap — `ERR_ELEMENT_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. Applies to program-block creation.
33. Facilitator turnaround buffer — `ERR_FACILITATOR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. Applies to block staffing.
34. Program-date lead time and block timing — `ERR_PROGRAM_DATE_IN_PAST`. Applies to booking creation and program-block creation.
35. Sequential start order — `ERR_BLOCK_SEQUENCE_NOT_READY`. Applies to block start.
36. Retirement criteria and active-block interference — `ERR_RETIREMENT_CRITERIA_NOT_MET` or `ERR_ELEMENT_HAS_ACTIVE_BLOCKS`. Applies to element retirement for both clauses, and to element return-to-service for the life-expiry clause only.
37. Client funds — `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CLIENT_CREDIT` (HTTP 402). Applies to booking confirmation.

The practical consequence at block start is that the rigged element's own status precondition is evaluated before posture, posture before the daily inspection, the daily inspection before belay readiness, belay readiness before rescue readiness, and all of those before screening, weight and supervision. A course under a lightning hold whose element also lacks its pre-use log reports `ERR_COURSE_CLOSED`, not `ERR_DAILY_INSPECTION_MISSING`. At booking confirmation, the order is inspection currency, then rescue readiness, then screening completeness, then the debrief block, then chaperone coverage, and finally client credit — so a booking that is both short of chaperones and short of money reports `ERR_CHAPERONE_SHORTFALL`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Commissioning a Site, Installing Elements, and Opening Each Day

A `ProgramDirector` registers a course site by supplying a `course_name`, the documented ground-ambulance response time to the trailhead as `ems_response_minutes` (a whole number of minutes between 1 and 240 inclusive), and an `ems_access_note` describing the emergency medical access plan — staging point, gate code, whatever the responding service needs. The new site starts with `posture` `OPEN`, `posture_reason` `NONE`, `daily_open_index` zero, `last_daily_open_date` null, `lightning_hold_until` null and every derived counter — `restricted_element_count`, `out_of_service_element_count`, `quarantined_equipment_count`, `rescue_ready_facilitator_count`, `at_risk_booking_count` — at zero. `latest_wind_speed_kph` starts at 0, `latest_air_temp_c` at 20, and `latest_lightning_distance_km`, `latest_observation_at` and `next_professional_inspection_due_on` all start null. `rescue_readiness_ok` is derived immediately and is therefore false on a fresh site, since no facilitator holds active certification yet.

Elements are installed onto a site one at a time. The director names the owning site through `course_id`, gives the element an `element_name` that must be unique within that course and is compared case-sensitively (a duplicate is rejected as an invalid request with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), classifies it with an `element_type` of `GROUND_INITIATIVE`, `LOW_ELEMENT`, `HIGH_ELEMENT`, `CLIMBING_WALL` or `ZIP_LINE`, states the commissioning date as `installed_on`, seeds the annual inspection cycle with `next_professional_inspection_due_on`, and declares the inclusive participant weight band as `min_participant_weight_kg` and `max_participant_weight_kg`, which are recorded exactly as supplied with neither an ordering nor a sign constraint enforced between them. An optional `initial_cycle_count` seeds the element's usage history from paper records; when omitted it is zero. The element's `status` begins at `SERVICEABLE` and its `restricting_log_id` at null. Installation re-derives the owning course's posture, but increments the course's `version` only when that posture or its reason actually moves.

Two manufacturer limits are stamped onto the element at installation from its type and are never client-supplied. `design_life_years` is 20 years for a ground initiative, 15 for a low element, 12 for a high element, 15 for a climbing wall and 10 for a zip line. `cycle_life_limit` — the participant-cycle count at which the element must leave service — is 43,800 for a ground initiative, 29,200 for a low element, 21,900 for a high element, 25,550 for a climbing wall and 18,250 for a zip line. `cumulative_cycle_count` starts from `initial_cycle_count` and grows only as blocks complete.

From those figures the server maintains a two-variant retirement forecast, recomputed at installation, whenever a completing block credits cycles, and at every daily opening checkpoint. The **calendar variant** produces `calendar_life_forecast_date` by adding `design_life_years` whole years to `installed_on`. The **usage variant** projects forward from observed wear: take the number of days between the clock date and `installed_on`, treated as at least one day; divide `cumulative_cycle_count` by that day count to obtain an exact average daily cycle rate; subtract `cumulative_cycle_count` from `cycle_life_limit` to obtain the remaining cycles. If the remaining cycles are zero or negative, `usage_life_forecast_date` is the clock date — the element is already exhausted. If the average daily rate is zero there is no history to project from, so `usage_life_forecast_date` is null and the calendar date stands alone. Otherwise divide the remaining cycles by the average daily rate, round the result up to a whole number of days, and add that many days to the clock date. `retirement_forecast_date` is then the earlier of the calendar and usage dates, ignoring a null, and `retirement_basis` records which variant won: `CALENDAR` when the calendar date is earlier than or equal to the usage date, `USAGE` otherwise. Ties resolve to `CALENDAR`, because a fixed manufacturer limit outranks a projection. The difference between the variants is not academic: a high element installed two years ago that has already absorbed 14,600 participant cycles is averaging twenty cycles a day, has 7,300 cycles left, and projects out exactly 365 days — a retirement date one year away on `USAGE` basis where the calendar variant alone would have said ten years.

Permanent retirement is an explicit `ProgramDirector` act carrying a `reason`, and it drives the element to the terminal `RETIRED` state from `SERVICEABLE`, `RESTRICTED` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE` alike; an element already `RETIRED` refuses the call with `ERR_ELEMENT_RETIRED` before any criterion is examined. At least one retirement criterion must hold: `retirement_forecast_date` is on or before the clock date, or `consecutive_conditional_count` has reached two, or `latest_inspection_outcome` is `FAIL`. If none holds, the request is refused with `ERR_RETIREMENT_CRITERIA_NOT_MET`. The element must also carry no program block whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUSPENDED` or `BLOCKED_UNSAFE`; if it does, the refusal is `ERR_ELEMENT_HAS_ACTIVE_BLOCKS`. On success every belay item rigged to the element is unrigged — its `assigned_element_id` set to null — and any such item still `IN_SERVICE` moves to `QUARANTINED`; the course's `out_of_service_element_count`, `restricted_element_count`, `quarantined_equipment_count` and `next_professional_inspection_due_on` are all re-derived, along with posture, and the course's `version` increments. Retiring an element that was `RESTRICTED` additionally decrements `restricted_element_count`.

The passage of time becomes visible through the **daily opening checkpoint**, a `ProgramDirector` operation on one course that performs a single ordered pass. The order is observable, because later steps read what earlier steps wrote; re-deriving posture before the element and equipment sweeps would report a stale posture for the same request. Step one increments `daily_open_index` and sets `last_daily_open_date` to the clock date. Step two clears `lightning_hold_until` when it is at or before the current clock instant. Step three walks the course's facilitators in ascending `created_at` order and re-derives each `cert_status`, which can move a facilitator from `ACTIVE` to `PROBATION` or from either state to `LAPSED`. Step four walks the course's elements whose `status` is not `RETIRED`, in ascending `created_at` order, re-derives the retirement forecast, and — when `retirement_forecast_date` has arrived at or before the clock date and the element is not already `OUT_OF_SERVICE` — moves it to `OUT_OF_SERVICE` by firing Cascade B of §2.6 in full: clearing `restricting_log_id`, adding one to `out_of_service_element_count`, quarantining the `IN_SERVICE` hardware rigged to it, and cascading its `SCHEDULED` blocks to `BLOCKED_UNSAFE` and their `CONFIRMED` bookings to `AT_RISK`. Life-expired elements leave service automatically this way; only the explicit retirement call above reaches `RETIRED`. Step five walks the course's belay equipment whose `status` is not `RETIRED`, in ascending `created_at` order, re-derives its forecast, and retires any item whose `retirement_forecast_date` has arrived, decrementing `quarantined_equipment_count` for an item that was quarantined. Step six re-derives `belay_ready` for every non-retired element in ascending `created_at` order. Step seven re-derives posture from the results of steps two through six. Step eight, when the posture has settled at `OPEN`, releases every `WEATHER_HOLD` booking of the course back to `CONFIRMED` in ascending `created_at` order. Step nine increments the course's `version`. Calling the checkpoint twice without moving the clock is convergent: `daily_open_index` and `version` both increment on the second call, but no entity changes status, because every sweep is idempotent against an unchanged clock date.

### 3.2. Facilitator Rosters, Certification Currency and Training

A `ProgramDirector` adds a person to a course roster by supplying `course_id`, `full_name`, a `staff_type` of `STAFF_FACILITATOR`, `CONTRACT_INSPECTOR` or `SAFETY_OFFICER`, a `certification_level` of `LEVEL_1`, `LEVEL_2` or `LEVEL_3` (an ordered ladder on which `LEVEL_3` is highest), the certification expiry date `cert_expires_on`, and `is_operator_employee`, which is true for the operator's own staff and false only for third-party contractors and which is optional, defaulting to true when omitted. `staff_type` pins which value of `X-User-Role` that record may act as, as described in §2.1. The record's `supervision_capacity` is derived directly from the level: eight participants for `LEVEL_1`, twelve for `LEVEL_2`, sixteen for `LEVEL_3`.

Certification currency is a derived three-value gate, `cert_status`, evaluated as an ordered first-match walk in which the expiry clause outranks the currency clauses. If `cert_expires_on` falls strictly before the clock date, the status is `LAPSED`. Otherwise, if `rescue_drill_last_passed_on` is null, or more than 180 days have elapsed between the drill date and the clock date, or `refresher_hours_24mo` is below eight hours, the status is `PROBATION`. Otherwise the status is `ACTIVE`. The drill-age comparison is strictly greater than 180 days, so a drill passed exactly 180 days ago is still current and a drill passed 181 days ago is not. A rescue drill being current is precisely equivalent to the facilitator being `ACTIVE`, which is why the course's `rescue_ready_facilitator_count` — the count of the course's facilitators at `ACTIVE` — is a sufficient measure of rescue staffing on its own. A newly hired facilitator with no training history therefore starts at `PROBATION` — unless the `cert_expires_on` supplied at hiring already lies before the clock date, in which case the expiry clause wins and the record opens at `LAPSED`.

Two rolling windows feed that walk. `rescue_drill_last_passed_on` is the most recent `completed_on` among the facilitator's training records of kind `RESCUE_DRILL`. `refresher_hours_24mo` is the sum of `hours` across the facilitator's training records of kind `REFRESHER` or `RECERTIFICATION` whose `completed_on` falls within 730 days — two years — of the clock date. That window is inclusive at its far edge: a record completed exactly 730 days before the clock date still counts, one completed 731 days before it does not.

Training is recorded by a `ProgramDirector` as an append-only event naming `facilitator_id`, a `kind` of `INITIAL`, `REFRESHER`, `RESCUE_DRILL` or `RECERTIFICATION`, the `completed_on` date, an optional `hours` figure that may not be negative, and an optional free-text `notes` field for the trainer. An omitted `hours` is recorded as zero and an omitted `notes` as the empty string, not as null. `completed_on` may not be later than the clock date. Recording a drill or refresher can lift a facilitator from `PROBATION` to `ACTIVE`, but only when the certification has not expired — `cert_expires_on` on or after the clock date — and, once the new record has been applied, the drill sits within 180 days and `refresher_hours_24mo` has reached at least eight. That promotion adds one to `rescue_ready_facilitator_count`, re-derives `rescue_readiness_ok`, and increments the facilitator's `version`; a course closed for `RESCUE_READINESS` becomes reopenable once the count reaches two, and it actually reopens at the next daily opening checkpoint.

A `RECERTIFICATION` record is the only route out of `LAPSED`. It resets `cert_expires_on` to its own `completed_on` plus 24 months. If, after that reset, the expiry is on or after the clock date and both currency clauses pass, the facilitator moves `LAPSED → ACTIVE`, adding one to `rescue_ready_facilitator_count` and re-deriving `rescue_readiness_ok`. If the expiry is repaired but the rescue-drill or refresher-hours clause still fails, the facilitator moves `LAPSED → PROBATION` instead; the expiry reset happens either way.

Decay runs in the opposite direction at the daily opening checkpoint. A facilitator at `ACTIVE` whose drill has aged past 180 days or whose `refresher_hours_24mo` has dropped below eight — because older records fell out of the two-year window — moves `ACTIVE → PROBATION`, provided the certification itself has not expired; `rescue_ready_facilitator_count` drops by one. A facilitator at `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION` whose `cert_expires_on` has passed moves to `LAPSED`, and the lapse from `ACTIVE` likewise drops `rescue_ready_facilitator_count` by one. Existing block assignments belonging to a lapsed facilitator are left in place rather than deleted, but any block start that depends on that person is refused with `ERR_FACILITATOR_CERT_INACTIVE`.

### 3.3. Inspection Regimes: Annual Professional Cycles and Daily Pre-Use Logs

Two inspection regimes run in parallel on every element, and they gate different things.

The **professional inspection** is the annual visit by an independent contractor and is recorded as a mutable record moving through a fork-join diamond. Either a `ProgramDirector` or the inspector personally opens it by naming the `element_id` under inspection, the `inspector_id` performing it, the on-site date `performed_on`, and an optional `findings_note` capturing the inspector's narrative, which is recorded as the empty string when omitted. The named inspector must genuinely be independent: their `staff_type` must be `CONTRACT_INSPECTOR`, their `is_operator_employee` must be false, and their `cert_status` must be `ACTIVE`. Any of those failing yields `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_INDEPENDENT`. The record opens with `status` `DRAFT` and `outcome` `NONE`, and its `disposition_note`, `disposed_on` fields stay empty and null respectively until disposal.

Filing is exclusive to the `ProfessionalInspector` who owns the record, and it takes a single input, `outcome`. The record must be in `DRAFT`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_DRAFT`, and the acting user must be the record's `inspector_id`. The outcome forks the record into one of three filed states, and the three branches carry deliberately different element consequences. `DRAFT → FILED_PASS` sets `outcome` to `PASS` and touches the element not at all. `DRAFT → FILED_CONDITIONAL` sets `outcome` to `CONDITIONAL` and moves an element that is `SERVICEABLE` or already `RESTRICTED` to `RESTRICTED`, leaving `restricting_log_id` null because no operational log caused it; the course's `restricted_element_count` rises, the element's `SCHEDULED` blocks move to `BLOCKED_UNSAFE`, their `CONFIRMED` bookings move to `AT_RISK`, and posture is re-derived. `DRAFT → FILED_FAIL` sets `outcome` to `FAIL` and drives an element that is `SERVICEABLE` or `RESTRICTED` to `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, firing Cascade B of §2.6 in full — equipment quarantine, then loss of `belay_ready`, then course posture — with the `SCHEDULED` blocks and their `CONFIRMED` bookings fanning out alongside; a fail on an element that was `RESTRICTED` decrements `restricted_element_count` as it increments `out_of_service_element_count`. Both the conditional and fail branches require the `findings_note` recorded at creation to be non-empty, and a blank narrative on either is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `outcome` is immutable once filed.

Disposal is the join, and it is the `SafetyOfficer`'s acceptance of the filing. It carries a `disposition_note`, requires the record to be in `FILED_PASS`, `FILED_CONDITIONAL` or `FILED_FAIL` — otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FILED` — and is barred to the filing inspector under §2.7. All three join branches move the record to its terminal state `DISPOSED`, and a record already at `DISPOSED` cannot be disposed a second time, which is the other half of `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FILED`. All three stamp `disposed_on` with the clock instant, increment the element's `version` and the course's `version`, and re-derive the course's `next_professional_inspection_due_on` and posture, but the administrative consequences differ per branch. A 12- or 3-month advance is skipped where the element's `next_professional_inspection_due_on` is already null, which stays null. A disposed `PASS` advances the element's `next_professional_inspection_due_on` by 12 months, sets `latest_inspection_outcome` to `PASS`, and resets `consecutive_conditional_count` to zero; this is the disposal that can clear an `INSPECTION_OVERDUE` posture. A disposed `CONDITIONAL` advances `next_professional_inspection_due_on` by only 3 months — an accelerated re-inspection — sets `latest_inspection_outcome` to `CONDITIONAL`, and adds one to `consecutive_conditional_count`; once that count reaches two the element becomes retirement-eligible even though its forecast date is still years away. A disposed `FAIL` sets `latest_inspection_outcome` to `FAIL`, leaves `next_professional_inspection_due_on` untouched, leaves the element `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, and likewise makes it retirement-eligible. `latest_inspection_outcome` always reflects the newest disposed inspection for the element, ordered by `performed_on` descending with `created_at` descending as the tie-break, and reads `NONE` before any inspection has been disposed.

The **operational inspection log** is the second regime: the facilitator's own pre-use walk of an element on the morning it will be used. It is an immutable record naming the `element_id` checked, the acting `facilitator_id`, the `log_date` on which the walk was performed, a `result` of `CLEAR`, `DEFECT_MINOR` or `DEFECT_MAJOR`, and a `defect_note`. The note may be omitted for a clear result — in which case it is recorded as the empty string — but must be present and non-empty for either defect result, or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Either a `Facilitator` or a `SafetyOfficer` may file one, but the `facilitator_id` must be the acting user's own identifier, and that person must belong to the same course as the element, or the log is refused with `ERR_FACILITATOR_COURSE_MISMATCH`. Callers supply the current logical clock date as `log_date`; only a log dated on the clock date satisfies the pre-use gate at block start.

A `CLEAR` result clears that element's daily-inspection gate for its `log_date` and updates `last_clear_log_date`, which tracks the `log_date` of the newest clear log for the element, ordered by `log_date` descending with `created_at` descending as the tie-break. A `DEFECT_MINOR` result records the defect for the audit trail and changes nothing else — the element stays `SERVICEABLE` and remains startable. A `DEFECT_MAJOR` result against a `SERVICEABLE` element fires Cascade A of §2.6 in full and sets the log's `caused_restriction` to true, which is the flag that later identifies this log as the restriction's origin. `caused_restriction` is false on every other log.

Returning an element to service is the `SafetyOfficer`'s countersignature on somebody else's defect, and it carries a `note`. From `RESTRICTED → SERVICEABLE` three preconditions apply: the newest operational log for the element, ordered by `log_date` descending then `created_at` descending, must have `result` `CLEAR`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DEFECT_NOT_CLEARED`; no incident referencing the element may still be `REPORTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`, or the refusal is `ERR_OPEN_INCIDENT_BLOCKS_SERVICE`; and the segregation rule of §2.7 applies whenever `restricting_log_id` is set. From `OUT_OF_SERVICE → SERVICEABLE` the defect-clearance test is different: the element's `latest_inspection_outcome` must be `PASS`, meaning a newer disposed passing inspection exists, otherwise `ERR_DEFECT_NOT_CLEARED`; the element's `retirement_forecast_date` must lie strictly after the clock date, because a life-expired element can never come back, otherwise `ERR_RETIREMENT_CRITERIA_NOT_MET`; and the same open-incident bar applies. Notably, return-to-service never inspects the element's program blocks — restoring blocked blocks is the entire purpose of the call. Attempting it on an element that is `RETIRED` is refused with `ERR_ELEMENT_RETIRED`, and attempting it on an element that is already `SERVICEABLE` is refused with `ERR_ELEMENT_NOT_RESTRICTED`, the retired test being evaluated first of the two. On success the element's `restricting_log_id` is cleared, quarantined hardware rigged to it returns to `IN_SERVICE`, its `BLOCKED_UNSAFE` blocks walk back to `SCHEDULED`, bookings whose `blocked_block_count` has returned to zero move `AT_RISK → CONFIRMED`, and the course's counters, `posture`, `posture_reason` and `version` all settle per §2.6.

### 3.4. Belay Systems, Equipment Life and Element Readiness

Life-safety hardware is inventoried per site by either a `ProgramDirector` or a `SafetyOfficer`. Each item names its owning `course_id`, an `equipment_kind` of `ROPE`, `HARNESS`, `CARABINER` or `LANYARD`, a `serial_number` unique within the course and compared case-sensitively (a duplicate is rejected as an invalid request with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), the `manufactured_on` date, and a hard `max_cycles` cap of at least one cycle after which the item must leave service. Optionally it may be rigged to an element at creation through `assigned_element_id`, carry a `first_use_on` date, and be seeded with `initial_usage_cycles` from paper records; a null `assigned_element_id` means the item is in the store room, a null `first_use_on` means it has never been used, and an omitted `initial_usage_cycles` leaves `usage_cycles` at zero. An `assigned_element_id` naming an element on a different course than the item's `course_id` is refused with `ERR_ELEMENT_COURSE_MISMATCH`. New items open at `status` `IN_SERVICE`, even when the seeded `initial_usage_cycles` already reaches `max_cycles`: that only fixes `retirement_basis` at `CYCLE`, and such an item leaves service on its first usage credit rather than at creation. Adding an item re-derives the owning course's posture but increments the course's `version` only when that posture or its reason actually moves.

Equipment life is forecast from two independent clocks plus a cycle counter. The manufacture clock produces `mfg_life_forecast_date` by adding the shelf life for the kind to `manufactured_on`: ten years for a rope, ten for a harness, fifteen for a carabiner, eight for a lanyard. The service clock produces `service_life_forecast_date` by adding the in-service life for the kind to `first_use_on`: five years for a rope, seven for a harness, ten for a carabiner, five for a lanyard; while `first_use_on` is null this date is null too and the item is scored on shelf life alone. `retirement_forecast_date` is the earlier of the two dates, ignoring a null. `retirement_basis` is decided in a fixed order: `CYCLE` whenever `usage_cycles` has reached `max_cycles`, since an exhausted item is finished today regardless of how much shelf or service life its dates still allow; otherwise `MANUFACTURE` when the service date is null; otherwise `MANUFACTURE` when the manufacture date is earlier than or equal to the service date; otherwise `SERVICE`. A rope manufactured ten years ago and first used four years ago thus retires on its service anniversary with basis `SERVICE`, while a rope manufactured nine years and two months ago but only first used three years ago retires on its shelf anniversary with basis `MANUFACTURE`.

Cycles accrue two ways. Completing a program block credits the block's head count to every in-service item rigged to that block's element, as described in §3.8. Outside a block — rescue drills, staff training, rigging practice — a `Facilitator` or `SafetyOfficer` logs usage directly by supplying a `cycles` figure of at least one, which is added to `usage_cycles`; the call also stamps `first_use_on` if this is the item's first use, re-derives the forecast and basis, and, when `usage_cycles` has reached or passed `max_cycles`, moves the item `IN_SERVICE → RETIRED` with `retirement_basis` `CYCLE`, retaining `assigned_element_id` for the audit trail. Logging usage against an item already in the terminal `RETIRED` state is refused with `ERR_EQUIPMENT_RETIRED`.

Element readiness is the aggregate that stands between hardware condition and permission to climb. Every element carries a `required_belay_set_count` derived from its type: zero for a ground initiative, zero for a low element, two for a high element, two for a climbing wall, three for a zip line. An item counts as usable for an element only when all four conditions hold: its `assigned_element_id` names that element, its `status` is `IN_SERVICE`, its `retirement_forecast_date` lies strictly after the clock date, and its `usage_cycles` is still below `max_cycles`. `belay_ready` is true only when the number of usable items is at least `required_belay_set_count` **and** no item rigged to the element sits at `QUARANTINED`. Unrigged store-room stock never counts toward any element's readiness. The boundary case matters: an element requiring zero sets with no hardware rigged to it at all is ready, because the empty set trivially satisfies a zero requirement, whereas an element requiring two sets with no hardware rigged to it is not ready — a naive "nothing is quarantined, therefore ready" reading gets exactly this case wrong. Readiness is re-derived when equipment is added, when usage is logged, on every element or equipment status change, and at the daily opening checkpoint.

Quarantine is always a consequence of something happening to the element. A rigged element entering `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, being retired, or having an incident closed with a corrective action of `EQUIPMENT_QUARANTINED` moves its `IN_SERVICE` hardware `IN_SERVICE → QUARANTINED`, adds one to the course's `quarantined_equipment_count`, re-derives the element's `belay_ready`, and pushes dependent `SCHEDULED` blocks to `BLOCKED_UNSAFE`. When the element returns to `SERVICEABLE`, quarantined hardware rigged to it moves `QUARANTINED → IN_SERVICE`, but only if it is still viable — `retirement_forecast_date` strictly after the clock date and `usage_cycles` still below `max_cycles` — and `quarantined_equipment_count` drops by one as `belay_ready` is re-derived. The daily opening checkpoint retires life-expired hardware outright: an `IN_SERVICE` item whose forecast date has arrived moves to `RETIRED` with `retirement_basis` set to whichever date won, `MANUFACTURE` or `SERVICE`, and a `QUARANTINED` item whose forecast date has arrived moves to `RETIRED` with `quarantined_equipment_count` decremented. In either case the element's `belay_ready` is re-derived and dependent `SCHEDULED` blocks are pushed to `BLOCKED_UNSAFE`. When a cycle-exhausted retirement turns `belay_ready` false, every `SCHEDULED` block on that element moves to `BLOCKED_UNSAFE` and its owning `CONFIRMED` bookings move to `AT_RISK`; because that retirement is reached through a usage log or a completing block rather than through an element transition, the course's posture is not re-derived and the course's `version` does not increment.

### 3.5. Group Clients, Bookings and the Quote

Paying organisations are registered by a `ProgramDirector` with a `client_name`, an `org_type` of `SCHOOL`, `CORPORATE`, `NONPROFIT` or `YOUTH_GROUP`, and a commercial `tier` of `STANDARD`, `PARTNER` or `ANCHOR`, defaulting to `STANDARD`. A new client opens with `payments_received_cents` and `credit_balance_cents` both zero and `late_cancellation_count` zero.

The client's money is held as a running ledger rather than per-booking invoices. Posting a payment — a `ProgramDirector` operation carrying an `amount_cents` of at least one cent and a free-text `reference` for the bank record — adds the amount to both `payments_received_cents` and `credit_balance_cents` and increments the client's `version`. `credit_balance_cents` is the client's unallocated funds and is always reconcilable as `payments_received_cents` less the sum of `deposit_allocated_cents` and the sum of `retained_cents` across all of that client's bookings. Every movement described below preserves that identity, which is why deposits and refunds always appear in compensating pairs.

A booking is opened as a draft by either a `ProgramDirector` or the group's own `GroupCoordinator`, naming the `course_id` of the site, the `group_client_id` of the payer, the `program_date` of delivery, and an `expected_participant_count` of at least one. That declared headcount is informational only: it is recorded for planning conversations and is never used in pricing, capacity or supervision arithmetic. The `program_date` must fall at least three days after the clock date at the moment of creation, and a shorter lead time is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_DATE_IN_PAST`. A new booking opens with `status` `DRAFT`, `roster_status` `INCOMPLETE`, `cancellation_variant` `NONE`, `notice_days_at_cancel` null, `quote_index` zero, `block_count` and `blocked_block_count` zero, and every monetary field at zero. Over its life the `status` moves through `DRAFT`, `QUOTED`, `CONFIRMED`, `AT_RISK`, `WEATHER_HOLD` and finally one of the two terminal values `DELIVERED` or `CANCELLED`.

Quoting is a `ProgramDirector` operation carrying only `expected_booking_version` (see §2.11). The booking's `status` must be `DRAFT` or `QUOTED`, otherwise the run is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_QUOTABLE`; a quote run from `DRAFT` advances the booking to `QUOTED`, and a re-quote from `QUOTED` supersedes the prior figures in place and leaves the status at `QUOTED`. The booking must already carry at least one program block — `block_count` of one or more — because a program with nothing in it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than quoted at zero. That count is of every block on the booking, `ABANDONED` ones included, and the check is evaluated after the version match of §2.11 and after the `DRAFT`-or-`QUOTED` state precondition. Every successful run increments `quote_index` and the booking's `version`.

The quote is built from three layers. First, `billable_head_count` is the number of participants on the booking whose `is_chaperone` is false. Chaperones ride free. Screening state does **not** filter this count: a child whose health form is still blocked still occupies a place on the course and is still billed. Second, the priced lines are every program block of the booking whose `status` is not `ABANDONED`, taken in ascending `sequence_index` order with ascending `created_at` as the tie-break. Third, the client's tier fixes a discount: no discount for `STANDARD`, 7.5 percent for `PARTNER`, 12.5 percent for `ANCHOR`.

Each line is then priced independently. Multiply the per-phase rate for the block's `phase` by `billable_head_count` — 850 cents per head for an `ICEBREAKER`, 1,250 for a `GROUND_INITIATIVE`, 2,275 for a `LOW_ELEMENT`, 4,650 for a `HIGH_ELEMENT`, 625 for a `DEBRIEF` — then apply the tier discount by multiplying by one minus the discount fraction, then round that single line half up to a whole number of cents. Only after rounding does the line enter the running sum that becomes `raw_subtotal_cents`. Lines whose `phase` is `HIGH_ELEMENT` are additionally accumulated, after their own rounding, into a separate high-element line total that the surcharge below is levied on. The rounding step sits inside the loop, once per line, and summing unrounded line amounts to round the total once at the end is a different and incorrect number: a `PARTNER` client with four billable heads running the canonical five-block program yields lines of 3,145, 4,625, 8,418, 17,205 and 2,313 cents for a `raw_subtotal_cents` of 35,706, where sum-then-round would produce 35,705 — one cent adrift.

The high-element risk surcharge is then levied. Its rate is selected by a band over `raw_subtotal_cents`, and the band must be resolved exactly once, from the pre-surcharge subtotal: a subtotal below 30,000 cents draws 6 percent, a subtotal from 30,000 through 35,999 cents draws 5 percent, and a subtotal of 36,000 cents or more draws 3 percent. `high_element_surcharge_cents` is the high-element line total multiplied by that rate and rounded **up** to a whole cent — the operator never absorbs a partial cent of high-element risk loading. `quoted_total_cents` is `raw_subtotal_cents` plus `high_element_surcharge_cents`. Continuing the example above, a subtotal of 35,706 lands in the 5 percent band, so the surcharge is 17,205 at 5 percent rounded up, which is 861, and `quoted_total_cents` is 36,567. Re-resolving the band against that surcharge-inclusive total would drop into the 3 percent band and silently produce 517 and 36,223 instead; that reading is wrong.

`deposit_required_cents` is 25 percent of `quoted_total_cents`, rounded up to a whole cent — 9,142 on the 36,567 example. The quote run also refreshes `required_chaperone_count`, whose definition belongs with the roster rules of §3.6.

Confirmation belongs exclusively to the booking's own `GroupCoordinator` and takes no request body. The booking must be `QUOTED`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_CONFIRMABLE`. It then passes the gates of §2.12 in their stated order: every element referenced by one of the booking's blocks must have a `next_professional_inspection_due_on` falling on or after the booking's `program_date`, or the refusal is `ERR_INSPECTION_OVERDUE` — and a null due date counts as overdue here; the course's `rescue_readiness_ok` must be true, or `ERR_RESCUE_READINESS_UNMET`; the `roster_status` must be `READY`, or `ERR_PARTICIPANT_SCREENING_INCOMPLETE`; the highest-`sequence_index` block must have `phase` `DEBRIEF`, or `ERR_DEBRIEF_BLOCK_MISSING`; the number of participants whose `is_chaperone` is true must be at least `required_chaperone_count`, or `ERR_CHAPERONE_SHORTFALL`; and finally the client's `credit_balance_cents` must be at least `deposit_required_cents`, or the confirmation is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CLIENT_CREDIT` (HTTP 402). On success the booking moves `QUOTED → CONFIRMED`, its `deposit_allocated_cents` is set to `deposit_required_cents`, the same amount is subtracted from the client's `credit_balance_cents` — the forward half of the client-funds identity — and both the booking's and the client's `version` increment.

### 3.6. Participant Screening and Roster Readiness

Roster management is the `GroupCoordinator`'s work on its own booking. Adding a person requires `booking_id`, the `expected_booking_version` of §2.11, `full_name`, `birth_date`, a `weight_kg` strictly greater than zero, and `is_chaperone`, which defaults to false and marks accompanying adults who supervise and are never billed. A new participant opens with `health_form_status` `MISSING`, `agreement_signed` false, `guardian_signature_provided` false, `medical_clearance_granted` false, `health_flags` an empty list, and `screening_status` `BLOCKED`. Adding a person to a booking that has already reached `DELIVERED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. Every roster write increments the owning booking's `version`.

Two derived attributes fall out of the birth date. `age_years` is the participant's whole-year age **on the booking's `program_date`**, not on the day the record was created, so somebody who turns eighteen between enrolment and delivery counts as an adult. `is_minor` is true exactly when `age_years` is below eighteen.

The screening packet is submitted per participant and carries three inputs: `agreement_signed`, which must be true or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; `guardian_signature_provided`, the guardian counter-signature; and `health_flags`, which must be a subset of `NONE`, `ASTHMA`, `CARDIAC`, `SEIZURE`, `PREGNANCY` and `RECENT_SURGERY` and which is optional, recorded as the empty list when omitted. The owning booking must not already be `DELIVERED` or `CANCELLED`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. Four of those flags are blocking — `CARDIAC`, `SEIZURE`, `PREGNANCY` and `RECENT_SURGERY` — and a packet whose flags intersect that set moves the participant `MISSING → FLAGGED`; a packet with no blocking flag moves it `MISSING → SUBMITTED`. Either way `agreement_signed` is set true and `guardian_signature_provided` takes the submitted value. Packets may be re-submitted: a participant already at `SUBMITTED` who declares a blocking flag on a later packet moves `SUBMITTED → FLAGGED`, and `medical_clearance_granted` is reset to false so that a previously granted clearance cannot be reused against new information. Symmetrically, a later packet carrying no blocking flag moves the participant to `SUBMITTED` from `FLAGGED` or `CLEARED` alike and leaves `medical_clearance_granted` exactly as it stood.

A flagged participant is unblocked only by a `SafetyOfficer` recording medical clearance, which carries a `clearance_note`. The participant's `health_form_status` must be `FLAGGED`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and the booking must not be terminal. The transition `FLAGGED → CLEARED` sets `medical_clearance_granted` true.

`screening_status` is derived after every one of those writes by an ordered first-match walk in which the blocked outcome outranks the conditional one. The participant is `BLOCKED` if `agreement_signed` is false, or `health_form_status` is `MISSING`, or the participant is a minor whose `guardian_signature_provided` is false. Failing that, the participant is `CONDITIONAL` if `health_form_status` is `FLAGGED` and `medical_clearance_granted` is false — the packet arrived but the medical decision is outstanding. Otherwise the participant is `ELIGIBLE`. A cleared flag combined with a signed agreement and, for a minor, a guardian signature is the only route to `ELIGIBLE` for a flagged participant.

`roster_status` is the booking's second, independent state dimension, and it is re-derived from the whole roster after every participant write by another ordered walk. A booking with no participants at all is `INCOMPLETE`. A booking where any participant — chaperones included — is `BLOCKED` or `CONDITIONAL` is `BLOCKED`. A booking whose participants are all chaperones, with no billable participant at all, is `INCOMPLETE` rather than `READY`. Otherwise it is `READY`. Because participants are never deleted, a roster can move `READY → BLOCKED` simply by adding one unscreened person. Participant writes never move the booking's `status`, and status actions never move its `roster_status`; the two dimensions are genuinely independent.

Chaperone coverage is sized off minors only. `required_chaperone_count` is the number of participants whose `is_chaperone` is false and whose `age_years` is below eighteen, divided by ten and rounded **up** to a whole person — a screening-blocked minor still needs supervision on site and is still counted. Four minors need one chaperone, ten minors need one, eleven need two, and a group with no minors needs none.

### 3.7. Program Design, Sequencing and Staffing

A `ProgramDirector` designs the day as an ordered chain of program blocks against a booking. Each block names its `booking_id`, its 1-based `sequence_index`, its `phase` — one of `ICEBREAKER`, `GROUND_INITIATIVE`, `LOW_ELEMENT`, `HIGH_ELEMENT` or `DEBRIEF` — its `planned_start_at` and `planned_end_at` instants, and, for the phases that use hardware, the `element_id` it is rigged to. A block opens with `status` `SCHEDULED`, `started_at` and `completed_at` null, `debrief_completed` false, `assigned_facilitator_count` and `actual_head_count` zero, and `required_facilitator_count` derived from the supervision arithmetic below on every read rather than seeded at one — a freshly created `HIGH_ELEMENT` block therefore already reports two in its own creation response. Creating a block adds one to the booking's `block_count`.

Phase and element must belong to the same family. `ICEBREAKER` and `DEBRIEF` blocks are conversation, not hardware, and must carry a null `element_id`; supplying one is refused. A `GROUND_INITIATIVE` block requires an element of type `GROUND_INITIATIVE`; a `LOW_ELEMENT` block requires an element of type `LOW_ELEMENT`; a `HIGH_ELEMENT` block requires an element of type `HIGH_ELEMENT`, `CLIMBING_WALL` or `ZIP_LINE`. Any other pairing is refused with `ERR_ELEMENT_PHASE_MISMATCH`. The element must also sit on the same course as the booking, or the refusal is `ERR_ELEMENT_COURSE_MISMATCH`.

Sequencing is the pedagogical backbone of the product and is enforced at creation. `sequence_index` must be unique within the booking, or the refusal is `ERR_DUPLICATE_BLOCK_SEQUENCE`. The block at `sequence_index` one must have `phase` `ICEBREAKER`, or the refusal is `ERR_SEQUENCING_VIOLATION` — groups are warmed up before they are put on hardware. A `HIGH_ELEMENT` block may only be added when the booking already contains an earlier `GROUND_INITIATIVE` or `LOW_ELEMENT` block, or the refusal is `ERR_SEQUENCE_PREREQUISITE_MISSING`. And the indexes must form a gap-free run beginning at one, meaning each new `sequence_index` must equal one more than the highest index already present on the booking; a skipped value is refused with `ERR_SEQUENCE_GAP`.

Timing is enforced twice. `planned_start_at` must be strictly earlier than `planned_end_at`, and a non-positive duration is an invalid request. Both instants must fall on the booking's `program_date` as a UTC calendar date, and a block timed onto any other day is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_DATE_IN_PAST`. Elements are also single-occupancy: the new block's interval, treated as half-open so that it includes its start instant and excludes its end instant, may not overlap any non-terminal block already booked on the same element, or the refusal is `ERR_ELEMENT_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. Adjacency is deliberately permitted — one block ending at 10:30 and another starting at 10:30 on the same element is accepted.

Staffing a block is a separate `ProgramDirector` operation naming a `facilitator_id` and an optional `assignment_role` of `LEAD`, `BELAY` or `SPOTTER` recorded as null when omitted, where `BELAY` is meaningful only on a block rigged to a `HIGH_ELEMENT`, `CLIMBING_WALL` or `ZIP_LINE` element. Four gates apply, in the order given in §2.12. The facilitator's `cert_status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the assignment is refused with `ERR_FACILITATOR_CERT_INACTIVE`. The facilitator's `certification_level` must reach the level the rigged element's type demands — `LEVEL_1` for a ground initiative, `LEVEL_1` for a low element, `LEVEL_2` for a high element, `LEVEL_2` for a climbing wall, `LEVEL_3` for a zip line, and `LEVEL_1` for a block with no element at all — compared as an ordinal on the three-rung ladder where `LEVEL_3` outranks `LEVEL_2` outranks `LEVEL_1`. A `LEVEL_2` facilitator put on a zip line is refused with `ERR_CERT_LEVEL_INSUFFICIENT` even when the ratio arithmetic is perfectly satisfied. The facilitator must belong to the same course as the element and booking being staffed, or the refusal is `ERR_FACILITATOR_COURSE_MISMATCH`. And a turnaround buffer of twenty minutes separates a person's consecutive blocks: an assignment is refused with `ERR_FACILITATOR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` when the later block starts before the earlier block's end plus twenty minutes, so a block ending at 15:00 permits a next assignment starting at 15:20 exactly but refuses one starting at 15:10. Assignments the same person already holds on this very block are exempt from that test, so one facilitator may legitimately be assigned to a block more than once, and each such assignment counts separately in both aggregates below.

Each accepted assignment freezes `rescue_current_at_assignment`, a boolean snapshot of whether that facilitator's `rescue_drill_last_passed_on` was within 180 days of the clock date at the moment of assignment. It is never refreshed afterwards; that is the point of a snapshot. The block's `assigned_facilitator_count` — simply the number of assignments on the block — rises by one, and the supervision arithmetic is re-derived.

Supervision sizing is a maximum over three independently computed dimensions, and each one must be evaluated: taking only the obvious ratio understates the requirement on high elements. The **active head count** for a block is the number of participants on its booking whose `is_chaperone` is false and whose `screening_status` is `ELIGIBLE`. The **ratio dimension** is zero for a block with no element; otherwise it is the active head count divided by the per-type ratio and rounded up — one facilitator per twelve participants on a ground initiative, per ten on a low element, per six on a high element, per five on a climbing wall, per four on a zip line. The **phase floor** is one facilitator for an `ICEBREAKER`, `GROUND_INITIATIVE`, `LOW_ELEMENT` or `DEBRIEF` block and two for a `HIGH_ELEMENT` block. The **type floor** is two when the rigged element's type is `HIGH_ELEMENT`, `CLIMBING_WALL` or `ZIP_LINE`, and zero otherwise. `required_facilitator_count` is the largest of the three. A high-element block with four eligible heads therefore needs two facilitators — the ratio alone would have said one — a zip-line block with thirteen heads needs four, and a low-element block with four heads needs one.

A second aggregate runs over the same assignment set: the block's total supervision capacity is the sum of `supervision_capacity` across every assigned facilitator, where capacity is eight for `LEVEL_1`, twelve for `LEVEL_2` and sixteen for `LEVEL_3`, and it must be at least the active head count. Both halves are checked at block start: `assigned_facilitator_count` must be at least `required_facilitator_count` and the summed capacity must cover the active head count, or the start is refused with `ERR_SUPERVISION_RATIO_UNMET`. Every assignment on the block is counted for both aggregates regardless of the assigned facilitator's current `cert_status`; certification currency is a separate gate with its own code. No averaging across blocks is permitted — each block stands alone.

### 3.8. Delivery Day: Starting, Completing and Bubbling Up

Starting a block is the exclusive act of a `Facilitator` and takes no request body, because everything it needs is already in the data. It is also the single most heavily gated operation in the system, and its checks are evaluated in the precedence order of §2.12. The block's `status` must be `SCHEDULED` or `SUSPENDED`, or the refusal is `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_SCHEDULED`. The owning booking's `status` must be exactly `CONFIRMED`; a booking still at `DRAFT` or `QUOTED`, or sitting at `AT_RISK` or `WEATHER_HOLD`, is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_CONFIRMED`. Last of the state preconditions, when the block carries an `element_id`, that element's `status` must be `SERVICEABLE`: an element at `RESTRICTED` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE` is refused with `ERR_ELEMENT_NOT_SERVICEABLE`, and an element at `RETIRED` with the more specific `ERR_ELEMENT_RETIRED` — both ahead of posture, because both belong to step 9. The course posture must not be `CLOSED`, and for a `HIGH_ELEMENT` phase it must be exactly `OPEN`, per §2.5. The sequential-order rule — every block of the same booking with a lower `sequence_index` must already be `COMPLETED` or `ABANDONED`, or the refusal is `ERR_BLOCK_SEQUENCE_NOT_READY`, because the program runs in order and not in parallel — is step 35, and is therefore evaluated last of every gate listed here, after the certification check that closes the people-facing set.

The element-facing gates follow. The element must carry an operational inspection log whose `log_date` equals the clock date and whose `result` is `CLEAR`, with no later log for that element recording anything other than `CLEAR`; otherwise the start is refused with `ERR_DAILY_INSPECTION_MISSING`. The element's `next_professional_inspection_due_on` must not have fallen behind the clock date, or the refusal is `ERR_INSPECTION_OVERDUE`; a null due date passes this gate, unlike at booking confirmation. When the element's `required_belay_set_count` is greater than zero, its `belay_ready` must be true, or the refusal is `ERR_BELAY_NOT_READY`.

Then the people-facing gates. For a `HIGH_ELEMENT` phase, at least one assignment on the block must have `rescue_current_at_assignment` true and belong to a facilitator whose `cert_status` is currently `ACTIVE`; without such a person the start is refused with `ERR_RESCUE_READINESS_UNMET`, the same code the course-wide two-active-facilitator rule uses. Also for a `HIGH_ELEMENT` phase, the course's `ems_response_minutes` must be at most 30, or the refusal is `ERR_EMS_ACCESS_UNSUITABLE` — a site more than half an hour from an ambulance may run ground work but not aerial work. The booking must have at least one participant whose `screening_status` is `ELIGIBLE` and none at `BLOCKED` or `CONDITIONAL`, or the refusal is `ERR_PARTICIPANT_SCREENING_INCOMPLETE`. When the block carries an element, every eligible participant's `weight_kg` must sit inside that element's inclusive band from `min_participant_weight_kg` to `max_participant_weight_kg`, or the refusal is `ERR_WEIGHT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The supervision aggregates of §3.7 must be satisfied, or the refusal is `ERR_SUPERVISION_RATIO_UNMET`. And every facilitator assigned to the block must have `cert_status` `ACTIVE`, or the refusal is `ERR_FACILITATOR_CERT_INACTIVE`.

A successful start moves the block `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS`, stamps `started_at` with the clock instant, freezes `actual_head_count` as the number of participants on the booking whose `screening_status` is `ELIGIBLE`, and increments the block's `version`. Note the deliberate difference between the two head counts in play: `actual_head_count` is the frozen eligible roster including chaperones, since anybody who goes on the element wears the hardware, whereas the supervision ratio of §3.7 is sized on the eligible non-chaperone count. Resuming a block after a weather hold clears uses the same operation and the same complete gate list — the block moves `SUSPENDED → IN_PROGRESS` once the posture has returned to a level its phase permits — and the resume overwrites `started_at` with the resume instant and re-freezes `actual_head_count` against the roster as it now stands.

Completion is also a `Facilitator` act and carries an optional `debrief_completed` flag, which is treated as false when omitted. The block's `status` must be `IN_PROGRESS`, or the refusal is `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`. A block whose `phase` is `DEBRIEF` may only be completed with `debrief_completed` true, since recording that the debrief practice actually happened is the deliverable of that phase; a debrief closed without it is refused with `ERR_DEBRIEF_NOT_RECORDED`. On success the block moves `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`, `completed_at` is stamped with the clock instant, and `debrief_completed` takes the submitted value.

Completion is where wear is booked. When the block carries an element, `actual_head_count` is added to that element's `cumulative_cycle_count`, and `usage_life_forecast_date`, `retirement_forecast_date` and `retirement_basis` are re-derived from it. In the same pass, every belay item whose `assigned_element_id` names that element and whose `status` is `IN_SERVICE` has `actual_head_count` added to its `usage_cycles`, has `first_use_on` stamped with the clock date if it had never been used before, and has its whole forecast — `service_life_forecast_date`, `retirement_forecast_date` and `retirement_basis` — re-derived; any item whose `usage_cycles` has reached `max_cycles` moves `IN_SERVICE → RETIRED`, which re-derives the element's `belay_ready` and can push dependent `SCHEDULED` high-element blocks to `BLOCKED_UNSAFE`. Blocks that end in `ABANDONED` or that sit at `SUSPENDED` never credit cycles to anything.

Finally, completion may finish the whole engagement. When every program block of the booking has reached `COMPLETED` or `ABANDONED`, and the booking has at least one block, the booking bubbles up `CONFIRMED → DELIVERED` on its own. That transition converts the held deposit into earned revenue: `retained_cents` is set to the current `deposit_allocated_cents`, `deposit_allocated_cents` becomes zero, and both the booking's and the group client's `version` increment. The client-funds identity of §3.5 stays balanced, because the money moves from allocated to retained without ever passing back through `credit_balance_cents`.

### 3.9. Weather, Incidents, Closure and Settlement

Weather is recorded as immutable observations by a `Facilitator`, a `SafetyOfficer` or a `ProgramDirector`. Each names the `course_id` observed, the `observed_at` instant, which may not be later than the logical clock, the sustained `wind_speed_kph`, which may not be negative, the `air_temp_c` in Celsius, and an optional `lightning_distance_km` giving the distance to the nearest detected strike, left null when no strike was detected. Recording an observation overwrites the course's `latest_wind_speed_kph`, `latest_air_temp_c`, `latest_lightning_distance_km` and `latest_observation_at` from the newest observation for that site, ordered by `observed_at` descending with `created_at` descending as the tie-break.

Three independent signals are read off each observation. Lightning at 8.0 km or nearer is a stop: it sets the course's `lightning_hold_until` to the later of its existing value and the observation's `observed_at` plus thirty minutes. A strike at exactly 8.0 km is a stop; one at 8.1 km is only a watch, and lightning beyond 8.0 km but at 16.0 km or nearer is a watch. Crucially, a later and farther strike never shortens an existing hold — the window only ever extends, so every close strike restarts the thirty-minute clock. Wind at 48.0 kph or above is a stop, wind from 32.0 kph up to but not including 48.0 kph is a watch, and 48.0 kph exactly is a stop while 47.9 kph is a watch. Air temperature at -12.0 degrees Celsius or below is a stop, with -12.0 exactly counting as a stop. A hold counts as active while `lightning_hold_until` is set and lies strictly after the current clock instant; expired holds are cleared by the daily opening checkpoint before posture is re-derived, which is what allows a stopped site to reopen.

Each observation permanently records what it caused: `resulting_posture` and `resulting_posture_reason` capture the course's `posture` and `posture_reason` immediately after the observation was applied, and `hold_until` captures the resulting `lightning_hold_until`, which is null when the observation triggered no hold. Watch-level signals move a course `OPEN → RESTRICTED` with `posture_reason` `LIGHTNING_WATCH` or `WIND_WATCH`, whichever clause of §2.5 matched first, provided no closing clause matched; while the posture is `RESTRICTED` for any reason, high-element block starts are refused with `ERR_COURSE_RESTRICTED_HIGH_ELEMENT`. Stop-level signals move the course `OPEN → CLOSED` and fire Cascade C of §2.6 — in-progress blocks to `SUSPENDED`, their confirmed bookings to `WEATHER_HOLD`, `at_risk_booking_count` up by one per booking. Cascade C fires on any observation that leaves the posture at `CLOSED`, including one recorded against a site that was already closed, and every observation increments the course's `version` whether or not the posture moved. The course walks `CLOSED → OPEN` only at a daily opening checkpoint, and only once every closing and restricting clause has gone false and any lightning hold has expired or been cleared by the checkpoint; a course sitting at `RESTRICTED` returns to `OPEN` either when its last restricted element comes back to service with no watch-level weather clause matching, or at the next checkpoint. Both restorations set `posture_reason` back to `NONE` and increment the course's `version`.

Incidents are reported by a `Facilitator` or a `SafetyOfficer`, naming the `course_id` where it happened, the `reported_by_facilitator_id` of the reporter — which is by convention the acting user's own facilitator identifier, though the server only resolves it as a foreign key and never compares it to `X-User-ID` — the `occurred_at` instant, which may not be later than the logical clock, a `severity` of `NEAR_MISS`, `MINOR`, `MODERATE` or `SERIOUS`, a non-empty `narrative` describing what happened, and `ems_activated`, true when emergency medical services were summoned. Optionally the report names the `element_id` involved and the `participant_id` affected; both are null for events that involve neither. A report opens at `status` `REPORTED` with `corrective_action` `NONE`, except that a `MODERATE` or `SERIOUS` severity opens a review immediately and lands at `UNDER_REVIEW` on creation. A `SERIOUS` incident naming an element additionally drives that element `SERVICEABLE → RESTRICTED` with `restricting_log_id` left null, adds one to `restricted_element_count`, and drives the course to `RESTRICTED` with `posture_reason` `SERIOUS_INCIDENT` — which outranks `ELEMENT_RESTRICTED` in the clause order precisely because the incident review is the more serious condition — incrementing the course's `version`.

A `SafetyOfficer` opens a review explicitly on a report that arrived at `REPORTED`; an incident already `UNDER_REVIEW` or closed refuses the call with `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_REPORTED`. While a review is open, the element it references cannot be returned to service, which surfaces as `ERR_OPEN_INCIDENT_BLOCKS_SERVICE` on that call.

Closing an incident is likewise a `SafetyOfficer` act and carries a `disposition` of `NO_ACTION` or `CORRECTIVE`, plus an optional `corrective_action`. The incident must be `UNDER_REVIEW`, or the refusal is `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`, and the reporter may not close their own report per §2.7. A `NO_ACTION` disposition moves the incident `UNDER_REVIEW → CLOSED_NO_ACTION`, leaves `corrective_action` at `NONE`, and re-derives the course posture, which is how the `SERIOUS_INCIDENT` clause finally clears. A `CORRECTIVE` disposition moves it `UNDER_REVIEW → CLOSED_CORRECTIVE` and records the supplied `corrective_action`, which must be either `ELEMENT_RESTRICTED` or `EQUIPMENT_QUARANTINED` and must be accompanied by a non-null `element_id` on the incident; a missing or invalid value, or a corrective close on an incident that names no element, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `ELEMENT_RESTRICTED` drives the element `SERVICEABLE → RESTRICTED` with `restricting_log_id` left null, pushes its `SCHEDULED` blocks to `BLOCKED_UNSAFE` and their `CONFIRMED` bookings to `AT_RISK`. `EQUIPMENT_QUARANTINED` instead moves every belay item rigged to that element whose `status` is `IN_SERVICE` to `QUARANTINED`, re-derives the element's `belay_ready`, and pushes dependent `SCHEDULED` blocks to `BLOCKED_UNSAFE`. Either way the course posture is re-derived and the course's `version` increments.

Cancellation closes the commercial side and is available to the booking's `GroupCoordinator` or to a `ProgramDirector`, carrying a `reason`. It is legal from `DRAFT`, `QUOTED`, `CONFIRMED`, `AT_RISK` and `WEATHER_HOLD` alike; a booking already `DELIVERED` or `CANCELLED` refuses the call with `ERR_BOOKING_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. The settlement first records `notice_days_at_cancel` as the number of days from the clock date to the booking's `program_date`, which may be negative when the program date has already passed. It then chooses the variant, which is recorded on `cancellation_variant`: a booking sitting at `WEATHER_HOLD` settles as `OPERATOR_WEATHER_INITIATED`, because the operator, not the group, stopped the work; every other status settles as `CLIENT_INITIATED`.

The two variants price the same cancellation very differently. Under `CLIENT_INITIATED`, a notice of 30 days or more forfeits nothing; a notice from 14 through 29 days forfeits 25 percent; a notice from 7 through 13 days forfeits 50 percent; and a notice of 6 days or fewer, including a negative notice, forfeits the whole deposit. The percentage is applied to `deposit_allocated_cents` and rounded half up. Under `OPERATOR_WEATHER_INITIATED` the notice tier is ignored entirely and a flat administrative fee of 2,500 cents is retained. In both variants the gross figure is then capped: `cancellation_fee_cents` is the smaller of the gross fee and `deposit_allocated_cents`, so the fee can never exceed money actually held. `refund_cents` is `deposit_allocated_cents` less `cancellation_fee_cents`. On a nine-thousand-one-hundred-and-forty-two-cent deposit cancelled at ten days notice, the two variants diverge sharply: 4,571 cents retained under the client variant against 2,500 under the weather variant, a 2,071-cent difference on otherwise identical booking data.

The ledger then moves in one settled step: `retained_cents` takes the `cancellation_fee_cents`, `deposit_allocated_cents` returns to zero, and `refund_cents` is added back to the group client's `credit_balance_cents` — the compensating half of the client-funds identity. Every program block of the booking that has not already reached a terminal status moves to `ABANDONED`, whether it was `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUSPENDED` or `BLOCKED_UNSAFE`; abandoning a `BLOCKED_UNSAFE` block also decrements the booking's `blocked_block_count`, and abandoned blocks no longer count toward it. The course's `at_risk_booking_count` is re-derived, which decrements it for a booking that was at `AT_RISK` or `WEATHER_HOLD`, and both the booking's and the client's `version` increment. A booking cancelled before it ever reached `CONFIRMED` has `deposit_allocated_cents` of zero, so both variants settle to a zero fee and a zero refund.

One reputational counter falls out of all this: a group client's `late_cancellation_count` is the number of its bookings that are `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_variant` `CLIENT_INITIATED` and a `notice_days_at_cancel` below fourteen days. Weather-initiated cancellations never count against a client.
