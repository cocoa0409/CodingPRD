# Vehicle Safety Recall Campaigns API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When an automaker's safety committee concludes that a defect exists in a shipped vehicle population, a regulatory clock starts running and does not stop until the last owner has either been repaired or exhausted every reasonable attempt at contact. The recall campaign office that manages this work sits at the intersection of four pressures that pull in different directions: a statutory filing deadline measured in business days, a remedy that engineering may not have finished designing, a finite pool of physical remedy kits that must be rationed across regions and dealerships, and a completion rate that the regulator will read quarter after quarter. Today that coordination lives in spreadsheets, and the failure modes are expensive — a mailing wave dropped before parts exist produces angry owners and no repairs, a defect notice filed a day late permanently marks the campaign, and a reversed warranty claim silently leaves the reported completion rate overstated.

This document specifies the Vehicle Safety Recall Campaigns API, the system of record for campaign execution from defect determination through campaign closure. The API owns the campaign file and its two independent lifecycles (the regulatory/administrative one and the remedy-engineering one), the derivation of the affected population from build records, the mailing-wave machinery that rations owner notification against parts on hand, the dealer parts allocation and warranty claim pipeline, the reimbursement of owners who paid for the repair before the recall was announced, and the quarterly completion and effectiveness reporting that ultimately permits closure.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **conservation**: physical remedy kits and monetary obligations are conserved quantities, and every forward action has an exact compensating action that restores the prior totals. Second, **segregation of duties**: the operator who records a defect determination may not be the operator who files the defect notice with the regulator, and the operator who releases a remedy may not be the operator who closes the campaign. Both commitments are enforced by the server, not by convention.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they are acting. A request missing either header, or presenting an `X-User-Role` that is not one of the five recognised role names, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A request whose role is recognised but is not listed as authorised for the targeted operation in §4 is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `SafetyEngineer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `RecallOperationsManager`, `DealerServiceManager`, and `ClaimsAuditor`.

A `SafetyEngineer` belongs to recall engineering: it opens campaign files, records the defect determination, defines the affected population, publishes labor operation codes and remedy parts, issues interim advisories, and releases or re-releases the remedy. A `ComplianceOfficer` staffs the regulatory filings office: it files the Part 573 defect notice, quarterly completion reports and amendments, and closes the campaign. A `RecallOperationsManager` runs campaign operations: ingesting build records, planning, releasing and mailing notification waves, allocating parts to dealers, suspending dealers, logging owner outreach, and intaking and re-keying owner reimbursement requests. A `DealerServiceManager` acts on behalf of exactly one franchise dealership, booking recall appointments and submitting repair claims. A `ClaimsAuditor` performs warranty audit: approving, rejecting and reversing dealer repair claims, and adjudicating owner reimbursement requests. All five roles may read every retrieval endpoint and may set the logical clock.

Identity semantics differ by role, and they matter for ownership checks. When `X-User-Role` is `DealerServiceManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Dealer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Dealer` compare that resource's dealer foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `DealerServiceManager` submitting a repair claim or booking an appointment must supply a `dealer_id` equal to its own `X-User-ID`, and when the affected-vehicle row is already scheduled, the row's existing `dealer_id` must also equal `X-User-ID`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_DEALER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `SafetyEngineer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `RecallOperationsManager`, or `ClaimsAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `determined_by_user_id` and `remedy_released_by_user_id` on a campaign, or as `filed_by_user_id` on a regulatory filing — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip; when omitted it defaults to 0, and negative values are not accepted. A `limit` outside 1 through 100 or a negative `offset` is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether records remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every persisted record exposes an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp formatted as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Most records additionally expose `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record — including mutations that only touch derived counters. Outreach attempts are the single exception: an outreach attempt is an immutable audit entry, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`, and neither `updated_at` nor `version` is present on it at all. Both `created_at` and `updated_at`, like the envelope's `api_request_timestamp`, are stamped from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock, and `created_at` is what every `created_at`-ascending ordering in this document sorts on. A record that one request mutates more than once still has its `version` incremented exactly once by that request, however many links of a cascade touch it; only `updated_at` reflects each successive write.

All server-stamped instants — `filed_at`, `remedy_released_at`, `last_notice_date`, `actual_mail_date`, `attempted_at` — and every deadline, mailing-date and eligibility-window comparison read a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. The clock's startup default is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; until an operator moves it, every temporal evaluation in the system uses that default. Any of the five roles may reposition the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock capability, which mutates no entity and answers with the resulting `current_utc`. Dates supplied by clients — `production_date`, `production_start_date`, `production_end_date`, `in_service_date`, `scheduled_mail_date`, `scheduled_repair_date`, `repair_date`, `invoice_date`, `effective_from`, `effective_to` — are calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, while `defect_determination_date` is a full ISO 8601 UTC instant. A value that does not parse in its declared form is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A client-supplied instant is normalised before it is stored or echoed — any offset is folded into UTC and the value is re-rendered with exactly three fractional digits — whereas a client-supplied calendar date is stored and echoed back verbatim as the client wrote it.

### 2.5. Two Independent Campaign Dimensions and Cascade Propagation

A recall campaign carries two lifecycle dimensions on one record, and they advance independently. `campaign_status` is the regulatory and administrative dimension, taking the values `DRAFT`, `DEFECT_DETERMINED`, `FILED`, `INTERIM_NOTIFIED`, `OWNER_NOTIFIED`, `MONITORING`, `CLOSED`, and `SUPERSEDED`; `CLOSED` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal, and any write against a campaign in either of them is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_STATE`. That refusal guards the operations that push the campaign forward or add to it — the determination, the interim advisory, the remedy release, closure, population rules, population runs, remedy parts, labor operations, parts allocations, wave planning, wave release, wave mailing, appointment booking, outreach logging, repair-claim submission, reimbursement intake and amendment filing. The compensating and adjudicating actions that only unwind or judge work already recorded carry no campaign-state gate at all and still succeed against a terminal campaign: allocation revocation, wave cancellation, repair-claim audit and reversal, all three owner-reimbursement steps, and a build-record update. `remedy_status` is the remedy-engineering dimension, taking the values `REMEDY_UNDER_DEVELOPMENT`, `INTERIM_ONLY`, `REMEDY_RELEASED`, and `REMEDY_REVISED`; it has no terminal state, because a released remedy can be forced back into revision and released again without limit. A new campaign begins with `campaign_status` `DRAFT` and `remedy_status` `REMEDY_UNDER_DEVELOPMENT`.

Because the two dimensions live on one record, a single request sometimes advances both. Issuing an interim advisory moves `campaign_status` from `FILED` to `INTERIM_NOTIFIED` and `remedy_status` from `REMEDY_UNDER_DEVELOPMENT` to `INTERIM_ONLY` in the same request, and increments the campaign's `version` once, not twice.

Cascades in this system are multi-link: one operation can mutate several records, and each link is caused by the one before it. When a child record changes in a way that alters a campaign counter, the campaign itself is mutated — its `updated_at` refreshes and its `version` increments by 1 — even though the client addressed the child. The two named cascades are worth calling out because implementers routinely stop at the first link.

**Cascade A** begins when a `ClaimsAuditor` reverses an approved repair claim. Link 1 decrements the affected-vehicle row's `approved_claim_count` and, when that count reaches 0, moves the row's `remedy_status` from `REMEDIED` back to `UNREPAIRED`. Link 2, caused by link 1, recomputes the campaign's `remedied_count` and `completion_rate_pct` and decreases `reimbursement_obligation_cents` by exactly the claim's stored `final_amount_cents`. Link 3, caused by link 2, may synthesize a follow-up mailing wave — the conditions are in §3.8.

**Cascade B** begins inside a quarterly filing when the recomputed effectiveness score falls below its minimum. Link 1 moves `remedy_status` from `REMEDY_RELEASED` to `REMEDY_REVISED` and increments `remedy_revision_count` by 1. Link 2, caused by link 1, moves every affected-vehicle row of the campaign whose `remedy_status` is `REMEDIED` back to `UNREPAIRED` and sets `requires_renotification` to true on each. Link 3, caused by link 2, recomputes `remedied_count`, `recall_population_count` and `completion_rate_pct`, and sets `amendment_required` to true on the quarterly filing that was just created — the snapshot it froze is no longer accurate. In the same cascade, every parts allocation of the campaign still in status `ALLOCATED` is revoked with `revoked_reason` `REMEDY_REVISED`, and each revoked allocation's uncommitted units are returned to its remedy part's pool.

### 2.6. Segregation of Duties

Two operations are guarded by segregation of duties, and both compare `X-User-ID` against a user identifier the campaign recorded earlier. Filing the Part 573 defect notice requires an `X-User-ID` different from the campaign's `determined_by_user_id`. Closing the campaign requires an `X-User-ID` different from the campaign's `remedy_released_by_user_id`, where that field holds the operator who performed the most recent remedy release. In either case, a match is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The role check is separate and comes first: only a `ComplianceOfficer` may attempt either operation at all.

### 2.7. Nested Paths and Validation Precedence

Several paths address a child collection under a campaign — population rules, population runs, notification waves, remedy parts, labor operations, and filings — and others address a sub-collection under an affected-vehicle row. Identifiers in a path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that resolves to nothing determines the response. A request for the outreach attempts of a non-existent affected-vehicle row therefore answers `ERR_AFFECTED_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND` rather than an empty list, and a request for the waves of a non-existent campaign answers `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND` before any wave is examined.

Validation across the whole API is a single ordered chain, and the ordering is normative because many requests violate more than one rule at once. Checks are evaluated in the following bands, and within each band in the order listed:

1. **Authentication**, then **authorization** — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, then `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
2. **Path-identifier existence**, in this precedence: campaign (`ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`), vehicle (`ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`), affected-vehicle row (`ERR_AFFECTED_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`), notification wave (`ERR_WAVE_NOT_FOUND`), parts allocation (`ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`), repair claim (`ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`), owner reimbursement request (`ERR_REIMBURSEMENT_NOT_FOUND`).
3. **Payload shape** — required fields present, enum values drawn from their declared sets, constrained numbers positive, dates and instants parsable, pagination parameters in range — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
4. **Body-referenced existence** — a `dealer_id` that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_DEALER_NOT_FOUND`, a `labor_operation_id` yields `ERR_LABOR_OP_NOT_FOUND`, a `remedy_part_id` yields `ERR_PART_NOT_FOUND`, a `vehicle_id` yields `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`, an `affected_vehicle_id` yields `ERR_AFFECTED_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`, and a body `campaign_id` yields `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`.
5. **Supersession target validity** on campaign creation — `ERR_SUPERSEDED_CAMPAIGN_INVALID`.
6. **Dealer scoping**, then **segregation of duties** — `ERR_DEALER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, then `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
7. **State gates**, in this precedence: campaign state (`ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_STATE`), wave state (`ERR_WAVE_INVALID_STATE`), claim state (`ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`), reimbursement state (`ERR_REIMBURSEMENT_INVALID_STATE`), allocation state (`ERR_ALLOCATION_INVALID_STATE`), affected-vehicle repairability (`ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_REPAIRABLE`).
8. **Version guards** — `ERR_STALE_CAMPAIGN_VERSION` and `ERR_STALE_PART_VERSION`. Version guards are evaluated after the state gates above and before any safety, referential or fiscal rule.
9. **Safety gates**, in this precedence: remedy availability (`ERR_REMEDY_NOT_AVAILABLE`), remedy-already-exists on an interim advisory (`ERR_REMEDY_ALREADY_AVAILABLE`), stop-sale hold (`ERR_STOP_SALE_ACTIVE`).
10. **Referential consistency**, in this precedence: labor-operation effectivity (`ERR_LABOR_OP_NOT_EFFECTIVE`), vehicle-in-population (`ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_IN_POPULATION`), cross-campaign mixing (`ERR_PART_CAMPAIGN_MISMATCH`), dealer participation (`ERR_DEALER_SUSPENDED`).
11. **Temporal rules**, in this precedence: date-range sanity (`ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`), population-rule overlap (`ERR_POPULATION_RULE_OVERLAP`), labor-operation window conflict (`ERR_LABOR_OP_WINDOW_CONFLICT`), wave mailing separation (`ERR_WAVE_MAILING_TOO_CLOSE`), mail-date reached (`ERR_WAVE_MAIL_DATE_NOT_REACHED`), repair date versus remedy release (`ERR_REPAIR_DATE_BEFORE_REMEDY`), reimbursement window (`ERR_REIMBURSEMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED`).
12. **Uniqueness**, in this precedence: `ERR_DUPLICATE_CAMPAIGN_NUMBER` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_VIN`, then `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPAIR_CLAIM`, then `ERR_FILING_PERIOD_DUPLICATE`, then `ERR_NO_QUARTERLY_FILING_TO_AMEND`, then `ERR_MAX_DOCUMENT_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED`.
13. **Quota and fiscal gates**, in this precedence: `ERR_EMPTY_WAVE_POPULATION`, `ERR_CLOSURE_CRITERIA_NOT_MET`, `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PARTS_COVERAGE`, `ERR_NO_PARTS_AVAILABLE`, `ERR_LABOR_HOURS_EXCEED_CAP`, `ERR_REIMBURSEMENT_INVOICE_EXCEEDS_MAX`.

### 2.8. Version Guards on Remedy Releases and Parts Allocations

Exactly two write operations are guarded against writing over data the client never observed, and both guard on the *parent* record's `version`.

A remedy release names the campaign in its path and must carry `expected_campaign_version` as a required, non-optional field holding the `version` the client last read from that campaign. When the supplied value does not equal the campaign's current `version`, the release is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CAMPAIGN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a remedy release legitimately supersedes the previous remedy definition — it re-stamps `remedy_released_at` and `remedy_released_by_user_id`, and it can carry the campaign out of `REMEDY_REVISED` — so without the guard a writer working from an older read would overwrite a remedy decision it never saw.

Creating a parts allocation must carry `expected_part_version` as a required, non-optional field holding the `version` the client last read from the target remedy part. When the supplied value does not equal that remedy part's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PART_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a new allocation for the same remedy part and dealer legitimately supersedes that dealer's prior allocation and returns its uncommitted units to the pool; a writer working from a stale `unallocated_qty` would otherwise supersede a holding it never observed.

No other operation accepts a version field. Endpoints outside these two do not take, and must not require, any expected-version input.

### 2.9. Conservation of Remedy Parts

Physical remedy kits are a conserved quantity. For every remedy part, at every observable point, the following equality holds exactly in whole units: `total_procured_qty` equals `unallocated_qty`, plus the sum over the part's allocations still in status `ALLOCATED` of that allocation's `allocated_qty` minus its `consumed_qty`, plus the part's own `consumed_qty`. Allocations in status `REVOKED` contribute nothing to the sum.

Each forward movement has an exact compensation. Granting an allocation moves units out of `unallocated_qty` into the allocation's `allocated_qty`; revoking an allocation moves that allocation's `allocated_qty` minus `consumed_qty` back into `unallocated_qty`. Approving a repair claim increments both the source allocation's `consumed_qty` and the remedy part's `consumed_qty` by one. Reversing an approved repair claim decrements the remedy part's `consumed_qty` by one and then, when the source allocation is still `ALLOCATED`, decrements that allocation's `consumed_qty` by one; when the source allocation has since been revoked, the freed unit is credited to the remedy part's `unallocated_qty` instead, because there is no live allocation to return it to. Every mutation touching a remedy part, an allocation or a repair claim must leave the equality intact.

### 2.10. Conservation of the Reimbursement Obligation Ledger

Money is likewise conserved, in integer cents. For every campaign, `reimbursement_obligation_cents` equals the sum of `final_amount_cents` over that campaign's repair claims in status `APPROVED`, plus the sum of `approved_amount_cents` over that campaign's owner reimbursement requests in status `APPROVED`. Claims in status `SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED` or `REVERSED` contribute nothing, and reimbursement requests in status `SUBMITTED`, `DOCUMENTS_REQUESTED` or `DENIED` contribute nothing.

Approving a repair claim computes `final_amount_cents` exactly once and adds it to the obligation. Reversing that claim subtracts the **stored** `final_amount_cents` — never a recomputed one — so that a reversal restores the pre-approval obligation to the cent even if the dealer's posted rate, the labor operation's flat hours or the part's unit cost have changed in the interim. Approving an owner reimbursement request adds its `approved_amount_cents`; denying one records `approved_amount_cents` as 0 and moves no money.

### 2.11. Numeric Precision

Currency amounts in request payloads and on dealer and part records — `labor_rate_per_hour`, `unit_cost`, `invoice_amount`, `parts_amount`, `towing_amount` — are decimal numbers with two decimal places. Every derived money amount is computed component by component, with **each component rounded to two decimal places, half up, before the components are summed**; the sum is then itself rounded to two decimal places, half up, and only then converted to integer cents by multiplying by 100 and rounding to the nearest whole cent. Summing unrounded components and rounding once at the end produces a different number and is incorrect.

Hour quantities — `claimed_labor_hours`, `flat_hours`, `labor_hours_claimed` — carry two decimal places. Percentages persisted or reported by the system — `completion_rate_pct`, `remedy_effectiveness_score` — carry two decimal places, rounded half up. Coverage and rate quantities used inside the wave and effectiveness formulas — `provisional_coverage_ratio`, `parts_coverage_ratio`, and the internal repeat and unreachable rates — carry four decimal places, rounded half up. All counters and quantities (`allocated_qty`, `consumed_qty`, `unallocated_qty`, `total_procured_qty`, `requested_qty`, `parts_used_qty`, `notice_count`, `days_late`, and the campaign-level counts) are integers and are never rounded. `coverage_tier_factor` is one of exactly four values: `1.00`, `0.75`, `0.40`, or `0.00`. A currency or hour value that a client supplies with more than two decimal places is not rejected: it is accepted and rounded half up to two decimal places as it is stored on the record, and the stored — rounded — figure is what the money and score formulas subsequently read.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening the Recall File: Determination, Deadline and Supersession

A `SafetyEngineer` opens a campaign file by supplying a `campaign_number` in NHTSA style — two digits, the literal `V`, a hyphen and three digits, as in `26V-104` — together with a `defect_type` of either `SAFETY_DEFECT` or `NONCOMPLIANCE`, and a `defect_description` of between 1 and 2000 characters describing the defect and its safety consequence. A `risk_severity` of `HIGH`, `MODERATE` or `LOW` may be supplied; when it is omitted the campaign is treated as `MODERATE`. Campaign numbers are unique across the whole system, and a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CAMPAIGN_NUMBER`. The new file starts in `campaign_status` `DRAFT` with `remedy_status` `REMEDY_UNDER_DEVELOPMENT`, `stop_sale_flag` and `do_not_drive_flag` both false, `remedy_revision_count` and `quarterly_filing_count` at 0, `reimbursement_obligation_cents` at 0, `completion_rate_pct` at 0.00, and `remedy_effectiveness_score` at 100.00, with `defect_determination_date`, `filing_deadline_utc`, `filed_at`, `determined_by_user_id`, `remedy_released_at`, `remedy_released_by_user_id` and `closure_note` all unset and `days_late` 0 with `late_filing_flag` false. Of the figures a campaign reports, only `remedy_effectiveness_score`, `quarterly_filing_count`, `days_late`, `late_filing_flag`, `remedy_revision_count` and the two advisory flags are stored counters; `affected_vehicle_count`, `recall_population_count`, `remedied_count`, `completion_rate_pct`, `reimbursement_obligation_cents` and `open_followup_wave_count` are derived from the live child records every time the campaign is serialised, so the "recomputations" attributed to individual operations below can never be observed as stale — what those operations really contribute is the accompanying `version` increment.

A campaign may replace an earlier one. Supplying `supersedes_campaign_id` at creation points the new file at the campaign it replaces; when omitted, the field stays null and the campaign stands alone. The target must be a real campaign whose `campaign_status` is not `DRAFT`, `CLOSED` or `SUPERSEDED` — a draft has not yet been determined and so has nothing to supersede, and the two terminal states are already closed out — and any of those three yields `ERR_SUPERSEDED_CAMPAIGN_INVALID`. When the target is valid it moves to `SUPERSEDED` immediately, from whichever of `DEFECT_DETERMINED`, `FILED`, `INTERIM_NOTIFIED`, `OWNER_NOTIFIED` or `MONITORING` it occupied, and its own `version` increments by 1. That supersession carries two cascades on the old campaign: every one of its notification waves in status `PLANNED` or `RELEASE_APPROVED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` recorded as `CAMPAIGN_SUPERSEDED`, and every one of its parts allocations in status `ALLOCATED` moves to `REVOKED` with `revoked_reason` `CAMPAIGN_SUPERSEDED`, each returning its `allocated_qty` minus `consumed_qty` to the owning remedy part's `unallocated_qty` and incrementing that remedy part's `version` by 1. Campaign listings accept a `campaign_status` filter and a `supersedes_campaign_id` filter, the latter being how an operator finds the successor of a superseded file.

The safety committee's decision is recorded next, moving the file from `DRAFT` to `DEFECT_DETERMINED`; attempting it from any other `campaign_status` yields `ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_STATE`. The `SafetyEngineer` supplies `defect_determination_date` as a valid ISO 8601 UTC instant, and may supply `risk_severity` at the same time to overwrite the severity band recorded at creation. The server stamps `determined_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID`, increments `version` by 1, and computes the statutory `filing_deadline_utc`.

That deadline is a **five-business-day** window. Starting from the UTC calendar date of `defect_determination_date`, the server steps forward one calendar day at a time, counting a day only when it falls Monday through Friday and is not a federal holiday, until five such days have been counted; `filing_deadline_utc` is that fifth counted date pinned to 23:59:59Z, rendered to whole seconds exactly as `YYYY-MM-DDT23:59:59Z` with no fractional-second digits. The federal holiday calendar the system honours consists of exactly these dates: 2026-01-01, 2026-07-03, 2026-09-07, 2026-11-26, 2026-12-25, and 2027-01-01. The determination date's own day is never counted, since counting begins after the first step forward.

### 3.2. Building the Affected Population from Build Records

Build records enter the system independently of any campaign. A `RecallOperationsManager` ingests a vehicle with a 17-character `vin` that is unique across all build records (a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_VIN`), the `plant_code` and `model_code` of the assembly, the `model_year`, the `production_date` on which it was built, the `suspect_part_number` sequence number of the installed suspect part, and a `region_code` of `NE`, `SE`, `MW` or `WE` derived from the owner's address. Optional at ingest are `sale_status`, defaulting to `SOLD` and otherwise one of `IN_DEALER_STOCK`, `EXPORTED` or `SCRAPPED`; `in_service_date`, the retail delivery date; `owner_name`, the registered owner of record used for mailings; and `owner_address_valid`, which defaults to true and is set false once mail comes back undeliverable. `stop_sale_hold` is server-managed and starts false. Unlike a population rule, a build record constrains none of its numbers: `model_year` and `suspect_part_number` are accepted as any integers, and `plant_code` and `model_code` as any non-empty strings of any length. A build record's disposition and owner data can later be updated, supplying any of `sale_status`, `owner_name`, `owner_address_valid` and `region_code`; every one of the four is optional, so an update naming none of them is still accepted and still refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version`.

The population itself is described by rules a `SafetyEngineer` attaches to the campaign. Each rule declares an `inclusion_mode` of `INCLUDE` or `EXCLUDE`, an exact `plant_code` and `model_code` of between 1 and 8 characters each, an exact `model_year` between 1980 and 2100, a build window bounded by `production_start_date` and `production_end_date`, and a suspect-part range bounded by `part_number_low` and `part_number_high`. The two bounding conventions differ deliberately and must not be conflated: **the build window is half-open**, so a vehicle matches when its `production_date` is on or after `production_start_date` and strictly before `production_end_date`; **the part range is closed**, so a vehicle matches when its `suspect_part_number` is greater than or equal to `part_number_low` and less than or equal to `part_number_high`. A rule whose `production_start_date` is not strictly earlier than its `production_end_date` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`; the part range gets no equivalent sanity check, so a `part_number_low` above `part_number_high` is accepted and simply matches nothing. Two `INCLUDE` rules with the same `plant_code`, `model_code` and `model_year` may not have intersecting half-open build windows; because the windows are half-open, windows that merely touch — where the existing rule's `production_end_date` equals the new rule's `production_start_date` — do not intersect and are permitted, while any genuine overlap is rejected with `ERR_POPULATION_RULE_OVERLAP`. `EXCLUDE` rules are exempt from the overlap rule entirely. Each rule also reports `matched_vehicle_count`, the number of affected-vehicle rows currently attributed to it in any `remedy_status` — rows since moved to `REMOVED_FROM_POPULATION` keep counting — which starts at 0 because adding a rule performs no matching. Rule listings accept an `inclusion_mode` filter.

Matching is a separate, explicitly triggered pass that either a `SafetyEngineer` or a `RecallOperationsManager` may run, and it is idempotent: running it repeatedly never disturbs rows it already created. The pass considers every build record in the system, walked in order of `production_date` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. The campaign's `INCLUDE` rules are ordered by `production_start_date` ascending then `created_at` ascending, and **the first `INCLUDE` rule that matches a vehicle wins** and is credited as that row's `matched_by_rule_id`; a vehicle matched by no `INCLUDE` rule is passed over silently. A vehicle that did match an `INCLUDE` rule but also matches any of the campaign's `EXCLUDE` rules is carved out, and the run counts it in `excluded_count`; the exclusion test runs before the already-materialised test, so such a vehicle is counted afresh in `excluded_count` on every run, including runs where it already holds a row. A vehicle that already has an affected-vehicle row for this campaign is skipped without change. Otherwise a new affected-vehicle row is created linking the campaign and the vehicle, counted in `matched_count`, and given a starting per-vehicle `remedy_status` of `UNREPAIRED` — unless the build record's `sale_status` is `SCRAPPED` or `EXPORTED`, in which case the row is created directly in `REMOVED_FROM_POPULATION` and also counted in `removed_count`.

After the creation sweep, the run performs a retraction sweep over the campaign's existing rows whose `remedy_status` is not already `REMOVED_FROM_POPULATION`: any row whose vehicle no longer matches any `INCLUDE` rule — because a rule was deleted or edited — is moved to `REMOVED_FROM_POPULATION` and added to `removed_count`. The sweep consults the `INCLUDE` rules alone, so a row is never retracted merely because a newly added `EXCLUDE` rule has come to cover its vehicle: exclusion carves vehicles out only at creation time. The run then refreshes the campaign's `affected_vehicle_count` to the total number of affected-vehicle rows in any status, sets `recall_population_count` to that total minus the number of rows in `REMOVED_FROM_POPULATION`, recomputes `completion_rate_pct`, increments the campaign's `version` by 1, and refreshes every rule's `matched_vehicle_count`. The run answers with `matched_count`, `excluded_count`, `removed_count`, `affected_vehicle_count` and `recall_population_count`.

An affected-vehicle row is the join between a campaign and a build record and is the unit on which all downstream repair work hangs. Beyond its `campaign_id`, `vehicle_id` and `matched_by_rule_id`, it tracks the `notification_wave_id` of the wave that most recently mailed this owner (null before the first mailing), the `dealer_id` holding the current appointment (null while unscheduled), `notice_count` totalling mailings received across all waves, `last_notice_type` and `last_notice_date` describing the most recent mailing, `scheduled_repair_date` for a booked appointment, `approved_claim_count` counting its repair claims currently in status `APPROVED`, `outreach_attempt_count` counting the outreach attempts logged against it, and `requires_renotification`, which the remedy-revision cascade raises. `REMOVED_FROM_POPULATION` is terminal for a row: once entered it is never left, and it is reachable from `UNREPAIRED`, `SCHEDULED`, `REMEDIED` and `UNREACHABLE` alike, which happens when a build record's `sale_status` is updated to `SCRAPPED` or `EXPORTED`. That update leaves the campaign's `affected_vehicle_count` unchanged but decrements `recall_population_count`, recomputes `remedied_count` and `completion_rate_pct`, and increments the campaign's `version` by 1 alongside the vehicle's own. Affected-vehicle listings can be filtered by `campaign_id`, `vehicle_id`, `matched_by_rule_id`, `notification_wave_id`, `dealer_id`, or `remedy_status`.

### 3.3. Regulatory Filing: Defect Notice, Quarterly Reports and Amendments

Only a `ComplianceOfficer` may file with the regulator, and every filing carries a `filing_type` of `DEFECT_NOTICE`, `QUARTERLY` or `AMENDMENT` plus a `narrative` of between 1 and 4000 characters. A `report_period` labelled as four digits, the literal `Q`, and a quarter digit that must itself be 1 through 4 — `2026Q3`, for instance — is required for `QUARTERLY` and `AMENDMENT` filings and is not used by a defect notice, which ignores the field altogether and records `report_period` as null even when a client supplies one. Each accepted filing is created in status `SUBMITTED`, stamped with `filed_at` from the logical clock and `filed_by_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID`, and freezes four snapshot values that must never drift afterwards: `completion_rate_snapshot_pct`, `remedied_count_snapshot`, `recall_population_snapshot` and `effectiveness_score_snapshot`. Filings can be listed and filtered by `filing_type`, `report_period` and `status`.

The **defect notice** carries the campaign from `DEFECT_DETERMINED` to `FILED`. It is refused from any other `campaign_status` with `ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_STATE`, refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the caller's `X-User-ID` equals the campaign's `determined_by_user_id`, and refused with `ERR_FILING_PERIOD_DUPLICATE` when a defect notice already exists for the campaign. On acceptance the campaign's `filed_at` is set to the filing's `filed_at`, and lateness is settled: `late_filing_flag` becomes true when `filed_at` is **strictly after** `filing_deadline_utc`, and false when `filed_at` falls on or before it. When the flag is true, `days_late` is the number of whole days between the deadline and the filing, computed by dividing the elapsed time by 86400 seconds and rounding **up** to the next whole day, so that even a filing one minute past the deadline records one day late; when the flag is false `days_late` stays 0. The filing's own `days_late` is set to the same value the campaign records, and it is 0 on quarterly and amendment filings. The campaign's `version` increments by 1.

A **quarterly completion report** is what moves the campaign from `OWNER_NOTIFIED` into `MONITORING`, and once in `MONITORING` each subsequent quarter's report is accepted as a self-loop that leaves `campaign_status` at `MONITORING`. Either way, a report is refused with `ERR_FILING_PERIOD_DUPLICATE` when another filing for the campaign already claims the same `report_period`, and refused with `ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_STATE` when the campaign is in neither `OWNER_NOTIFIED` nor `MONITORING`. On acceptance the campaign's `quarterly_filing_count` increments by 1, the effectiveness score is recomputed and then frozen onto the filing, and the campaign's `version` increments by 1. A quarterly report is also the only place where a released remedy can be forced back into revision — the threshold and its consequences are in §3.4.

An **amendment** exists to correct a quarterly report whose frozen snapshot went stale. It requires an existing `QUARTERLY` filing for the campaign with the same `report_period` and status `SUBMITTED`; without one, the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_QUARTERLY_FILING_TO_AMEND`. When it is accepted, the prior quarterly filing moves from `SUBMITTED` to `AMENDED`, its `amendment_required` flag is cleared to false, and its own `version` increments by 1; the amendment itself is created in status `SUBMITTED` with freshly computed completion and effectiveness snapshots, and the campaign's own `version` increments by 1 just as it does for the other two filing types. `AMENDED` is terminal for a filing. Beyond needing that prior quarterly, an amendment is gated on nothing else: any `campaign_status` other than the two terminal ones will accept one, and no `ERR_FILING_PERIOD_DUPLICATE` is raised against an amendment whose `report_period` is already claimed.

### 3.4. Remedy Engineering: Interim Advisories, Release and Revision

While engineering is still designing the fix, the campaign can warn owners anyway. Issuing an interim advisory is a `SafetyEngineer` action available only from `campaign_status` `FILED` and only while `remedy_status` is `REMEDY_UNDER_DEVELOPMENT` — an interim notice is lawful only when no remedy exists, so attempting it once any remedy has been released or revised is rejected with `ERR_REMEDY_ALREADY_AVAILABLE`, while attempting it from a `campaign_status` other than `FILED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_STATE`. The request carries two booleans, `stop_sale` and `do_not_drive`, together with a `justification`. Acceptance advances both dimensions at once — `campaign_status` from `FILED` to `INTERIM_NOTIFIED` and `remedy_status` from `REMEDY_UNDER_DEVELOPMENT` to `INTERIM_ONLY` — records the two booleans into `stop_sale_flag` and `do_not_drive_flag`, and increments the campaign's `version` by 1. When `stop_sale` is true, every build record whose `sale_status` is `IN_DEALER_STOCK` and which has an affected-vehicle row for this campaign has its `stop_sale_hold` raised to true. A held vehicle may not be moved to `sale_status` `SOLD` while the hold stands; such an update is rejected with `ERR_STOP_SALE_ACTIVE`. The `do_not_drive_flag` is the flag that unlocks a towing allowance on owner reimbursements, and unlike the stop-sale flag it survives the remedy release and stands until the campaign is closed.

Releasing the remedy is likewise a `SafetyEngineer` action, and it is the one place in the system where three inbound `remedy_status` values converge on `REMEDY_RELEASED`. From `REMEDY_UNDER_DEVELOPMENT` a release is permitted while `campaign_status` is any of `DEFECT_DETERMINED`, `FILED`, `INTERIM_NOTIFIED`, `OWNER_NOTIFIED` or `MONITORING`. From `INTERIM_ONLY` a release is permitted while `campaign_status` is any of `INTERIM_NOTIFIED`, `OWNER_NOTIFIED` or `MONITORING`. From `REMEDY_REVISED` a release is permitted only when the request also carries `revision_reason` explaining what changed; a re-release out of `REMEDY_REVISED` without that field is a malformed request, and that inbound value is otherwise unrestricted by `campaign_status` beyond the two terminal states. A fourth inbound value is not accepted at all: a release attempted while `remedy_status` is already `REMEDY_RELEASED` is rejected with `ERR_REMEDY_ALREADY_AVAILABLE`, and because that is a §2.7 band-9 safety gate it is evaluated *after* the version guard, so a stale `expected_campaign_version` on an already-released campaign answers `ERR_STALE_CAMPAIGN_VERSION` first. Every release carries a `remedy_description` and a `labor_operation_id`, which must name a labor operation belonging to this same campaign — one that resolves but belongs to another campaign is rejected with `ERR_LABOR_OP_NOT_EFFECTIVE`, and the operation's effective window is not consulted at release — and every release carries `expected_campaign_version` per §2.8. A release stamps `remedy_released_at` from the logical clock and `remedy_released_by_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID` — refreshing both on a re-release — and increments the campaign's `version` by 1. It also lifts the sales freeze: `stop_sale_flag` is set to false and every vehicle in the campaign's population carrying `stop_sale_hold` has that hold cleared. `do_not_drive_flag` is deliberately left untouched.

Revision runs in the opposite direction and is never requested by a client. Inside a quarterly filing, once the effectiveness score has been recomputed, the server compares it with the effectiveness floor of 70.00 points. The comparison is **strict**: a score of exactly 70.00 does not revise, and only a score below it does. When the score is below the floor and `remedy_status` is `REMEDY_RELEASED`, the remedy is forced from `REMEDY_RELEASED` to `REMEDY_REVISED` and cascade B of §2.5 runs in full — `remedy_revision_count` increments by 1, every affected-vehicle row of the campaign standing at `REMEDIED` returns to `UNREPAIRED` with `requires_renotification` set true, `remedied_count`, `recall_population_count` and `completion_rate_pct` are recomputed, the just-created quarterly filing has `amendment_required` set true, and every `ALLOCATED` parts allocation of the campaign is revoked with `revoked_reason` `REMEDY_REVISED` with its uncommitted units returned to the pool. The cycle from `REMEDY_RELEASED` to `REMEDY_REVISED` and back to `REMEDY_RELEASED` may repeat any number of times, and `remedy_revision_count` records how many times it has.

Three operations are gated on the remedy actually existing: releasing a `REMEDY` or `FOLLOW_UP` notification wave, booking a repair appointment, and submitting a repair claim. Each requires `remedy_status` to be either `REMEDY_RELEASED` or `REMEDY_REVISED`; attempting any of the three while `remedy_status` is `REMEDY_UNDER_DEVELOPMENT` or `INTERIM_ONLY` is rejected with `ERR_REMEDY_NOT_AVAILABLE`.

### 3.5. Remedy Parts, Labor Operations and Dealer Allocation

Dealers are enrolled by a `RecallOperationsManager` with a unique `dealer_code` of 1 to 16 characters (`name` carries the dealership name), a `region_code` of `NE`, `SE`, `MW` or `WE` naming the region it serves, a `labor_rate_per_hour` posted door rate greater than zero with two decimal places, and a `monthly_repair_capacity` of at least 1 recall repair per month. The `dealer_code` is expected to be unique, but that expectation is not enforced by the server — the error catalogue holds no code for a duplicate dealer code — so a repeat enrollment is accepted. A dealer's `status` starts `ACTIVE` and can be moved to `SUSPENDED` with a `suspension_reason`; suspension carries no state gate of its own, so suspending an already-`SUSPENDED` dealer succeeds again, overwrites `suspension_reason` and increments `version`, and there is no path back to `ACTIVE`. A suspended dealer keeps its existing allocations, but new allocations, new appointments and new repair claims naming it are all refused with `ERR_DEALER_SUSPENDED`.

The remedy part pool is registered against the campaign by a `SafetyEngineer` with a service `part_number`, a `unit_cost` dealer net cost greater than zero in dollars and cents, a `total_procured_qty` of zero or more physical units, and optionally a supplier `lead_time_days` for planning, which must be zero or more and defaults to 30 days when omitted. On registration `unallocated_qty` is initialised to the full `total_procured_qty` and `consumed_qty` starts at 0. Remedy-part listings expose the live pool counters and accept a `part_number` filter.

Labor operation codes are also published by a `SafetyEngineer` against the campaign: a `labor_op_code` for dealers to claim against, a `description` of what the operation covers, `flat_hours` published flat-rate hours greater than zero with two decimal places, a `rate_type` of either `WARRANTY` or `PREVAILING`, and an effective window bounded by `effective_from` and `effective_to`. **The labor-operation window is closed at both ends**: a date qualifies when it is on or after `effective_from` and on or before `effective_to`. A window whose `effective_from` falls after its `effective_to` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. Two labor operations sharing the same campaign and the same `labor_op_code` may not have intersecting closed windows, and because both ends are inclusive, windows that merely touch — where the existing operation's `effective_to` equals the new one's `effective_from` — *do* intersect and are rejected with `ERR_LABOR_OP_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Each labor operation reports `claim_count`, the number of repair claims currently referencing it in status `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`. Labor-operation listings accept a `labor_op_code` filter and an `effective_on` date filter that returns only codes whose closed window contains that date.

Allocating parts to a dealer is a `RecallOperationsManager` action naming a `remedy_part_id`, a `dealer_id`, a `requested_qty` of at least 1, and `expected_part_version`. The allocation names no campaign of its own: it denormalizes `campaign_id` from the remedy part, and dealers are campaign-agnostic, so no cross-campaign mix can arise here and this operation never raises `ERR_PART_CAMPAIGN_MISMATCH`. The grant is computed in a fixed order, and the order matters because an earlier step changes the pool the later steps read.

First, the dealer's prior holding is superseded. At most one allocation exists for a given remedy part and dealer in status `ALLOCATED`; when one does, its `allocated_qty` minus its `consumed_qty` is returned to the remedy part's `unallocated_qty`, and the prior allocation moves to `REVOKED` with `revoked_reason` recorded as `SUPERSEDED`. Second — and only after that return has landed — the regional demand is snapshotted: the server counts the affected-vehicle rows of the remedy part's campaign whose `remedy_status` is `UNREPAIRED` or `SCHEDULED` and whose build record's `region_code` equals the dealer's `region_code`. Third, three caps are applied in a nest. The dealer-side cap is the smallest of the `requested_qty`, the dealer's `monthly_repair_capacity`, and the regional demand count multiplied by the one kit each repair consumes. The granted quantity is then the smaller of that dealer-side cap and the remedy part's `unallocated_qty` as it now stands. When the granted quantity works out to zero — because the dealer is at capacity, because the region has no open demand, or because the pool is empty — the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_PARTS_AVAILABLE` and nothing is written. Otherwise the new allocation records that quantity as `allocated_qty`, records `partially_granted` as true exactly when the granted quantity is less than the `requested_qty` and false when it equals it, starts `consumed_qty` at 0, and denormalizes `campaign_id` from the remedy part. The remedy part's `unallocated_qty` is reduced by the granted quantity and its `version` increments by 1 — by exactly 1 even when a prior holding was superseded in the same request, per the once-per-request rule of §2.4. A new allocation begins in status `ALLOCATED` with `revoked_reason` null.

An allocation can be given back before any repair consumes it, which is the manual compensating action for the grant. Revoking an allocation, optionally carrying a `revoke_note` that is validated as a non-empty string but is neither persisted nor echoed anywhere, requires the allocation to be in status `ALLOCATED` — a second revoke of the same allocation is rejected with `ERR_ALLOCATION_INVALID_STATE` — moves it to `REVOKED` with `revoked_reason` `MANUAL`, adds its `allocated_qty` minus `consumed_qty` back to the remedy part's `unallocated_qty`, and increments both the allocation's `version` and the remedy part's `version` by 1. Across the whole system, `revoked_reason` is one of `SUPERSEDED`, `MANUAL`, `CAMPAIGN_SUPERSEDED` or `REMEDY_REVISED`, according to which of the four paths retired the allocation. Allocation listings accept `remedy_part_id`, `dealer_id`, `campaign_id` and `status` filters.

### 3.6. Owner Notification Waves: Planning, Quota and Mailing

Mailings go out in waves, one region at a time. A `RecallOperationsManager` plans a wave against the campaign with a `wave_type` of `INTERIM`, `REMEDY` or `FOLLOW_UP`, a `target_region_code` of `NE`, `SE`, `MW` or `WE`, and a `scheduled_mail_date` naming the planned drop date. A planned wave starts in status `PLANNED` with `auto_created` false, all its quota fields at their zero defaults, `mailed_vehicle_count` 0, and `actual_mail_date` and `cancel_reason` unset. It receives a `sequence_number` equal to one more than the number of its non-`CANCELLED` sibling waves on the same campaign, where siblings are ordered by `scheduled_mail_date` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. Planning is open from any `campaign_status` other than the two terminal ones, and the plan step makes no attempt to reconcile `wave_type` with the remedy dimension — that agreement is checked only at release. Wave listings accept `wave_type` and `status` filters.

Owners are not to be papered. **A new wave's `scheduled_mail_date` must be at least fourteen whole days away from the `scheduled_mail_date` of every non-`CANCELLED` wave of the same campaign and the same `target_region_code`**, measured as an absolute difference in calendar days in either direction; a separation of exactly fourteen days is acceptable and anything closer is rejected with `ERR_WAVE_MAILING_TOO_CLOSE`. Cancelled waves are ignored by this rule, which is why cancelling a wave frees up the calendar around it.

Approving a wave for mailing is where the parts-coverage arithmetic happens, and it runs in six pinned steps. The campaign's `campaign_status` must be one of `FILED`, `INTERIM_NOTIFIED`, `OWNER_NOTIFIED` or `MONITORING`, and — since campaign state precedes wave state in the §2.7 bands — that check, together with the extra `INTERIM_NOTIFIED` requirement below, is evaluated before the wave's own status, which must be `PLANNED` or the request is `ERR_WAVE_INVALID_STATE`. The remedy dimension must then agree with the `wave_type`: a `REMEDY` or `FOLLOW_UP` wave requires `remedy_status` `REMEDY_RELEASED` or `REMEDY_REVISED` and is otherwise `ERR_REMEDY_NOT_AVAILABLE` per §3.4, while an `INTERIM` wave requires `remedy_status` `INTERIM_ONLY` together with `campaign_status` `INTERIM_NOTIFIED` — an `INTERIM` wave attempted while the remedy is still `REMEDY_UNDER_DEVELOPMENT` is `ERR_REMEDY_NOT_AVAILABLE`, one attempted once the remedy has been released or revised is `ERR_REMEDY_ALREADY_AVAILABLE`, and one whose campaign is not `INTERIM_NOTIFIED` is `ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_STATE`.

*Step 1 — the candidate set.* Candidates are drawn from the campaign's affected-vehicle rows whose build record's `region_code` equals the wave's `target_region_code`, then narrowed by `wave_type`. An `INTERIM` wave takes only rows whose `remedy_status` is `UNREPAIRED` and whose `notice_count` is 0 — nobody who has already received any letter. A `REMEDY` wave takes rows whose `remedy_status` is `UNREPAIRED` or `SCHEDULED` and whose `last_notice_type` is either unset or `INTERIM` — owners who have not yet been told the remedy is available. A `FOLLOW_UP` wave takes rows whose `remedy_status` is `UNREPAIRED`, whose `notice_count` is at least 1, and whose `last_notice_type` is `REMEDY` or `FOLLOW_UP` — owners who were told and did not act. The count of candidates is pinned onto the wave as `eligible_vehicle_count`, and a count of zero is rejected with `ERR_EMPTY_WAVE_POPULATION`.

*Step 2 — the parts capacity snapshot.* For an `INTERIM` wave, `parts_capacity_qty` is set equal to `eligible_vehicle_count`, because an interim letter promises no repair and needs no kit. For a `REMEDY` or `FOLLOW_UP` wave, `parts_capacity_qty` is the sum, over the campaign's parts allocations in status `ALLOCATED` whose holding dealer's `region_code` equals the wave's `target_region_code`, of that allocation's `allocated_qty` minus its `consumed_qty`. Allocations held by dealers in other regions and allocations already revoked contribute nothing.

*Step 3 — the coverage gate.* `provisional_coverage_ratio` is the `parts_capacity_qty` divided by the `eligible_vehicle_count`, rounded half up to four decimal places. The required minimum is selected by the campaign's `risk_severity`: a `HIGH`-severity campaign requires a ratio of at least 1.0000 — one kit reachable per eligible owner — a `MODERATE` campaign at least 0.6000, and a `LOW` campaign at least 0.3000. A ratio exactly equal to the requirement passes; a ratio below it is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PARTS_COVERAGE`.

*Step 4 — the tier factor.* `coverage_tier_factor` is selected from that same provisional ratio, taking 1.00 when the ratio is at least 1.0000, otherwise 0.75 when it is at least 0.6000, otherwise 0.40 when it is at least 0.3000, and otherwise 0.00.

*Step 5 — the mailing size.* `planned_vehicle_count` is the `eligible_vehicle_count` multiplied by the `coverage_tier_factor`, rounded **down** to a whole vehicle. This deliberately lets the mailing run ahead of the kits physically on hand at the higher tiers. A `planned_vehicle_count` of zero is rejected with `ERR_EMPTY_WAVE_POPULATION`.

*Step 6 — the reported coverage.* `parts_coverage_ratio` is the `parts_capacity_qty` divided by the `planned_vehicle_count`, rounded half up to four decimal places. This is a reporting figure only. It is emphatically **not** the number the gate in step 3 compared, and the two must not be swapped: the gate's denominator is the eligible count, the reported ratio's denominator is the planned count.

All six pinned values — `eligible_vehicle_count`, `parts_capacity_qty`, `provisional_coverage_ratio`, `coverage_tier_factor`, `planned_vehicle_count` and `parts_coverage_ratio` — are written onto the wave, its status advances from `PLANNED` to `RELEASE_APPROVED`, and its `version` increments by 1.

Dropping the wave requires status `RELEASE_APPROVED` and requires the logical clock's current date to have reached the `scheduled_mail_date`; an earlier attempt is rejected with `ERR_WAVE_MAIL_DATE_NOT_REACHED`. Those two, plus the campaign not being terminal, are the drop's only gates: it re-checks neither the remedy dimension nor the coverage arithmetic frozen at release. On the drop, the server re-derives the eligible rows and stamps the **first `planned_vehicle_count` of them, ordered by their build record's `production_date` ascending and then by the affected-vehicle row's own `created_at` ascending** — oldest builds first. Each stamped row records the wave in `notification_wave_id`, has `notice_count` incremented by one, has `last_notice_type` set to the wave's `wave_type` and `last_notice_date` set to the clock, and has `requires_renotification` cleared to false. The wave records the number of rows actually stamped as `mailed_vehicle_count` and the clock instant as `actual_mail_date`, and its status advances to `MAILED`, which is terminal. A `REMEDY` or `FOLLOW_UP` drop also carries the campaign from `FILED` or from `INTERIM_NOTIFIED` to `OWNER_NOTIFIED` — the diamond join, since `FILED` reaches `OWNER_NOTIFIED` either directly, when the remedy shipped before any interim notice was needed, or by way of `INTERIM_NOTIFIED` — and leaves `campaign_status` unchanged when the campaign is already `OWNER_NOTIFIED` or `MONITORING`. An `INTERIM` drop never changes `campaign_status`. Either way the campaign's `open_followup_wave_count` is recomputed and the campaign's `version` increments by 1 alongside the wave's own.

A wave can be cancelled from either `PLANNED` or `RELEASE_APPROVED`, recording an optional `cancel_reason`; cancelling a wave already `MAILED` or already `CANCELLED` is rejected with `ERR_WAVE_INVALID_STATE`. Cancellation from `RELEASE_APPROVED` retains the pinned quota fields for audit rather than clearing them. A cancelled wave stops counting toward the fourteen-day mailing-separation rule and stops counting toward `open_followup_wave_count`, which is the number of `FOLLOW_UP` waves of the campaign in status `PLANNED` or `RELEASE_APPROVED`; that counter is recomputed and the campaign's `version` incremented by 1 on every cancellation, as is the wave's own `version`. `CANCELLED` is terminal.

### 3.7. Dealer Repair: Appointments, Claims, Audit and Reversal

A `DealerServiceManager` books an owner's recall appointment by supplying a `dealer_id` equal to its own `X-User-ID` and a `scheduled_repair_date`. The row must be repairable for scheduling, meaning its `remedy_status` is one of the two legitimate predecessors of `SCHEDULED` — either `UNREPAIRED` or `UNREACHABLE`; a row already in `SCHEDULED`, or in `REMEDIED` or `REMOVED_FROM_POPULATION`, is rejected with `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_REPAIRABLE`. The booking dealer must be `ACTIVE`, the campaign's `remedy_status` must be `REMEDY_RELEASED` or `REMEDY_REVISED` per §3.4, and the build record's `sale_status` must be neither `SCRAPPED` nor `EXPORTED` — a scrapped or exported build record is refused with `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_REPAIRABLE`, judged alongside the row's own repairability and therefore ahead of both the remedy gate and the dealer's `ACTIVE` status. Booking stores `dealer_id` and `scheduled_repair_date` on the row, advances its `remedy_status` to `SCHEDULED`, and increments the row's `version` by 1. Booking out of `UNREACHABLE` is how an owner who finally responded rejoins the active population; it leaves `recall_population_count` untouched, but `completion_rate_pct` is still recomputed because the operational denominator changes when a row leaves `UNREACHABLE`. In either case the campaign's `version` increments by 1.

A completed repair is claimed by the same `DealerServiceManager`, naming the `affected_vehicle_id` the repair closes, the `dealer_id` that performed it (again equal to its own `X-User-ID`), the `labor_operation_id` claimed, the `repair_date` on which the work was done, the `claimed_labor_hours` the dealer is billing to two decimal places and greater than zero, and the `parts_used_qty` of kits it reports consuming, at least 1. The claim's `campaign_id` is denormalized from the affected-vehicle row. The labor operation and the affected-vehicle row must belong to the same campaign; a mix across those two is rejected with `ERR_PART_CAMPAIGN_MISMATCH`. A dealer belongs to no campaign, so it is not part of that comparison — its tie to the campaign is enforced instead by the parts reservation below, which only ever finds an allocation the dealer holds against this campaign. The dealer must be `ACTIVE`, the campaign's remedy must be available, and the row must be repairable for a claim, which means its `remedy_status` is `UNREPAIRED`, `SCHEDULED` or `REMEDIED` — a row in `UNREACHABLE` or `REMOVED_FROM_POPULATION` is rejected with `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_REPAIRABLE`. The `repair_date` must fall on or after the calendar date of the campaign's `remedy_released_at`; earlier work is rejected with `ERR_REPAIR_DATE_BEFORE_REMEDY`. The claimed hours are capped for admissibility at **one and a half times** the labor operation's published `flat_hours`, with a value exactly equal to that product still admissible and anything above it rejected with `ERR_LABOR_HOURS_EXCEED_CAP` — a §2.7 band-13 gate, and so the very last check the submission performs, evaluated after the duplicate test and after the parts reservation described in the next paragraph. The referenced labor operation's closed effective window must contain the `repair_date`, or the claim is rejected with `ERR_LABOR_OP_NOT_EFFECTIVE`. Finally, no other claim for the same `affected_vehicle_id` and the same `repair_date` may already stand in status `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`, or the claim is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPAIR_CLAIM`.

Submission also reserves the physical unit. Among the submitting dealer's parts allocations for the claim's campaign that are still in status `ALLOCATED` and still have at least one uncommitted unit — that is, an `allocated_qty` exceeding its `consumed_qty` by at least the one kit a repair consumes — the server ranks candidates by `created_at` ascending so the oldest holding drains first, breaking a tie on identical `created_at` by preferring the larger `allocated_qty`, and records the first candidate as the claim's `parts_allocation_id`. When no candidate qualifies, the submission is rejected with `ERR_NO_PARTS_AVAILABLE`. Submission also sets `is_repeat_repair` to true when the affected-vehicle row already carried at least one `APPROVED` claim at the moment of submission and false otherwise, increments the labor operation's `claim_count`, and leaves the claim in status `SUBMITTED` with `final_amount_cents`, `audit_note` and `reversal_reason` unset.

The amount the dealer expects is pinned at submission as `provisional_amount_cents`, computed from the dealer's own posted numbers with no caps applied. The labor component is the `claimed_labor_hours` multiplied by the dealer's `labor_rate_per_hour`, rounded to two decimal places. The parts component is the dealer's full reported `parts_used_qty` multiplied by the remedy part's `unit_cost`, then increased by a fifteen percent parts-handling markup — that is, multiplied by one plus fifteen hundredths — and rounded to two decimal places. A flat administrative allowance of 12.00 dollars is always added. The two rounded components and the allowance are summed, the sum is rounded to two decimal places, and the result is multiplied by 100 and rounded to whole cents. Where a campaign has more than one remedy part registered, the part used by this arithmetic is the campaign's first remedy part ordered by `created_at` ascending.

A `ClaimsAuditor` then audits the claim with a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and an optional `audit_note`. The claim must be in status `SUBMITTED`; auditing a claim in any other status is rejected with `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`. A **rejection** stores the `audit_note`, which is optional and stays null when the client omits it, leaves `final_amount_cents` null, moves the claim to `REJECTED`, decrements the labor operation's `claim_count` by 1, and increments the claim's `version` by 1. Nothing else moves: no affected-vehicle transition, no part consumption, and no ledger movement whatsoever.

An **approval** does considerably more, and it also requires the campaign's `remedy_status` to be `REMEDY_RELEASED` or `REMEDY_REVISED` and requires the claim's `parts_allocation_id` still to reference an allocation in status `ALLOCATED` with at least one uncommitted unit — an allocation revoked between submission and audit invalidates the approval with `ERR_NO_PARTS_AVAILABLE`. On approval `final_amount_cents` is pinned by the audited arithmetic described below; the affected-vehicle row advances to `REMEDIED` from whichever of `SCHEDULED` or `UNREPAIRED` it occupied — the latter being the owner who walked in without an appointment — and its `approved_claim_count` increments by 1; the source allocation's `consumed_qty` and the remedy part's `consumed_qty` each increment by 1 and both the source allocation's `version` and the remedy part's `version` increment by 1; the campaign's `reimbursement_obligation_cents` increases by the pinned `final_amount_cents`, its `remedied_count`, `recall_population_count` and `completion_rate_pct` are recomputed, its `remedy_effectiveness_score` is recomputed, and its `version` increments by 1. The claim's own `version` and the affected-vehicle row's own `version` each increment by 1.

The audited amount, `final_amount_cents`, differs from the provisional one in four specific ways, and each cap must be applied. The effective hours are the smaller of the `claimed_labor_hours` and the labor operation's published `flat_hours`. The effective rate is the smaller of the dealer's `labor_rate_per_hour` and the regional rate ceiling for the dealer's `region_code`, which is 152.00 dollars per hour in `NE`, 128.50 in `SE`, 121.75 in `MW`, and 165.25 in `WE`. The labor component is the effective hours multiplied by the effective rate, rounded to two decimal places. The parts component pays for **at most one kit** regardless of what the dealer reported, so it is the smaller of `parts_used_qty` and one, multiplied by the remedy part's `unit_cost`, increased by the same fifteen percent handling markup, and rounded to two decimal places. The administrative allowance of 12.00 dollars is added only when the calendar date of the `repair_date` falls on or after the calendar date of the campaign's `remedy_released_at`, and is 0.00 otherwise. The two rounded components and the allowance are summed, the sum is rounded to two decimal places, and the result is multiplied by 100 and rounded to whole cents.

An audit finding can undo an approval. Reversal is a `ClaimsAuditor` action on a claim in status `APPROVED` — any other status is `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE` — and it records a required `reversal_reason` and moves the claim to `REVERSED`, which is terminal. `final_amount_cents` is retained and never recomputed, precisely so that the ledger unwinds exactly: the campaign's `reimbursement_obligation_cents` decreases by that stored value. The part unit comes back per §2.9. The affected-vehicle row's `approved_claim_count` decrements by 1 and, when it reaches 0, the row returns from `REMEDIED` to `UNREPAIRED`; the campaign's `remedied_count` and `completion_rate_pct` are then recomputed. A row can cycle from `REMEDIED` back to `UNREPAIRED` and forward to `REMEDIED` again any number of times, and each pass leaves later claims on that row marked with `is_repeat_repair`. Both the claim's `version` and the campaign's `version` increment by 1. Claim listings accept `campaign_id`, `dealer_id`, `affected_vehicle_id`, `labor_operation_id`, `parts_allocation_id` and `status` filters.

### 3.8. Unresponsive Owners, Outreach and Automatic Follow-Up Waves

Owners who do not respond to a mailing are worked by hand. A `RecallOperationsManager` logs an outreach attempt against an affected-vehicle row with a `channel` of `MAIL`, `PHONE`, `EMAIL` or `CANVASS`, an `outcome` of `NO_RESPONSE`, `BAD_ADDRESS`, `SCHEDULED` or `OWNER_REFUSED`, and an optional free-text `note`. Each attempt records `attempt_number` as one more than the number of prior attempts on the same row, and `attempted_at` from the logical clock; the row's `outreach_attempt_count` increments accordingly. An attempt may be logged against a row in any `remedy_status` at all, `REMEDIED`, `UNREACHABLE` and `REMOVED_FROM_POPULATION` included; only the transitions below consult the row's current status. Attempts are immutable audit entries and can be listed for a row with an `outcome` filter.

Two outcomes move the row. A row in `UNREPAIRED` becomes `UNREACHABLE` either immediately, when the new attempt's `outcome` is `BAD_ADDRESS`, or on accumulation, when the row's attempt count reaches three and every one of those attempts ended in `NO_RESPONSE`, `BAD_ADDRESS` or `OWNER_REFUSED` — an attempt that ended in `SCHEDULED` breaks the accumulation. Whenever the outcome is `BAD_ADDRESS`, the build record's `owner_address_valid` is set to false, independently of the row's current `remedy_status` and so even when no row transition follows. Separately, an `OWNER_REFUSED` outcome logged against a row currently in `SCHEDULED` returns that row to `UNREPAIRED` and clears both its `dealer_id` and its `scheduled_repair_date`. In every case the campaign's `completion_rate_pct` is recomputed and the campaign's `version` increments by 1 alongside the row's own. Moving a row into `UNREACHABLE` shrinks the operational denominator but leaves the regulatory denominator alone — the distinction is spelled out in §3.9 — which is exactly why an unreachable owner still counts against the campaign.

Follow-up mailing can also be scheduled by the system rather than by an operator, and it happens as link 3 of cascade A, inside a repair-claim reversal. The synthesis is attempted only after the reversal has recomputed `completion_rate_pct`, and it stops without effect on any of three conditions: the campaign's `campaign_status` is not `MONITORING`; or the recomputed `completion_rate_pct` is at or above the follow-up trigger of 85.00 percent; or a `FOLLOW_UP` wave already exists for this campaign in the reversed vehicle's `region_code` with status `PLANNED` or `RELEASE_APPROVED`. When none of those stops applies, the server picks a drop date by taking the logical clock's current date and adding a three-week lead of 21 calendar days, then walking that candidate date forward one day at a time for as long as any wave of the same campaign and the same region in status `PLANNED`, `RELEASE_APPROVED` or `MAILED` has a `scheduled_mail_date` fewer than fourteen whole days from the candidate in absolute terms — a separation of exactly fourteen days is acceptable and ends the walk. A `NotificationWave` is then created with `wave_type` `FOLLOW_UP`, `target_region_code` set to the vehicle's region, `scheduled_mail_date` set to the settled date, status `PLANNED`, and `auto_created` true, distinguishing it from an operator-planned wave. The campaign's `open_followup_wave_count` is then recomputed as the number of `FOLLOW_UP` waves in status `PLANNED` or `RELEASE_APPROVED`.

### 3.9. Completion Reporting, Owner Reimbursement and Campaign Closure

Completion is reported two ways from the same population, and the difference is which rows sit in the denominator. Both variants bucket the campaign's affected-vehicle rows by current `remedy_status` — not by historical events — into a total, the number `REMOVED_FROM_POPULATION`, the number `UNREACHABLE`, the number `REMEDIED` and the number `SCHEDULED`.

The **regulatory** variant is the one persisted in `completion_rate_pct` and frozen onto every filing snapshot. Its denominator is the total minus the removed rows — the same figure the campaign carries as `recall_population_count` — and **unreachable rows stay in it**, because the regulator does not forgive an owner the automaker could not find. Its numerator is the `REMEDIED` rows only. The rate is one hundred times the numerator divided by the denominator, rounded half up to two decimal places, and is reported as 0.00 whenever the denominator is zero.

The **operational** variant is a read-only dashboard figure. Its denominator is the total minus the removed rows *and also* minus the unreachable rows, and its numerator adds the `SCHEDULED` rows to the `REMEDIED` ones, so the same population always reports at or above the regulatory rate. It too is one hundred times numerator over denominator, rounded half up to two decimal places, and 0.00 on a zero denominator. A campaign's completion-metrics reading returns both side by side as `regulatory_completion_rate_pct` and `operational_completion_rate_pct`, together with `remedied_count`, `scheduled_count`, `unreachable_count`, `removed_count`, `recall_population_count`, `affected_vehicle_count`, `remedy_effectiveness_score` and `repeat_claim_count`.

Effectiveness is scored out of 100.00 points and penalises the two things that indicate a remedy is not working. `remedy_effectiveness_score` starts from 100.00 points, from which three deductions are taken. The **repeat-repair rate** is the total number of surplus approved repairs divided by the number of `REMEDIED` rows: for each affected-vehicle row, count its repair claims in status `APPROVED`, subtract one, and treat a negative result as zero, then sum those surpluses across the campaign's rows; only `APPROVED` claims count, so `REJECTED` and `REVERSED` claims never contribute. That rate is rounded half up to four decimal places, and is 0.0000 when there are no `REMEDIED` rows. The deduction is 60.00 points multiplied by the repeat-repair rate. The **unreachable rate** is the number of `UNREACHABLE` rows divided by the campaign's `recall_population_count`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and 0.0000 when `recall_population_count` is zero; the deduction is 20.00 points multiplied by that rate. The **late-filing penalty** is a flat 5.00 points whenever the campaign's `late_filing_flag` is true, and 0.00 otherwise. The raw score is 100.00 less those three deductions; the reported score is that raw value clamped into the range 0.00 through 100.00 and then rounded half up to two decimal places. The score is recomputed on every repair-claim approval, on every repair-claim reversal, and on every quarterly or amendment filing — but it is only *compared* with the effectiveness floor of 70.00 points inside a quarterly filing, as described in §3.4. `remedy_effectiveness_score` is a persisted campaign field rather than a figure derived afresh at read time: it is set to 100.00 when the campaign file is opened and thereafter changes only at those recompute events, so a campaign read, the completion-metrics reading, the closure gate and every filing snapshot all report the value left by the most recent recompute. Inside a filing the recompute runs once, immediately before that filing freezes its snapshot — so a defect notice, which is not one of the recompute events, freezes the score as it already stands, and the revision cascade that a quarterly filing may go on to run does not refresh the stored score again even though it moves the counts the score is derived from.

A campaign's obligation is not confined to dealer repairs. Owners who paid out of pocket before the recall was announced are made whole through a separate track. A `RecallOperationsManager` intakes a request naming the `campaign_id` the pre-recall repair relates to and the `vehicle_id` the owner paid to repair, together with the `invoice_date` on the paid invoice, the `invoice_amount` the owner paid in dollars and cents, which must itself be greater than zero, and the `labor_hours_claimed` shown on the invoice, which must be greater than zero. Optionally the intake carries `parts_amount`, the parts portion of the invoice, defaulting to 0.00; `towing_amount`, the towing charge, defaulting to 0.00 — each of those two must be zero or more when supplied; and `documentation_complete`, describing whether the packet arrived complete, defaulting to true. A request starts in status `SUBMITTED` with `document_round` 0, `labor_operation_id`, `approved_amount_cents`, `requested_items` and `denial_reason` all unset.

Two eligibility rules bite at intake. An affected-vehicle row must already link the named `campaign_id` and `vehicle_id` — any such row will do, whatever its `remedy_status`, `REMOVED_FROM_POPULATION` included; without one the request is rejected with `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_IN_POPULATION`. And the `invoice_date` must fall inside the reimbursement window: it must be on or after the calendar date of the campaign's `defect_determination_date` less a **one-year lookback of 365 days**, that boundary being inclusive and simply not applied at all while `defect_determination_date` is still unset, and when the campaign's `remedy_released_at` is set it must also be on or before the calendar date of that release plus a **thirty-day tail**, that boundary also being inclusive. A date outside the window is rejected with `ERR_REIMBURSEMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Both rules are re-checked at adjudication, because the campaign's remedy release date may have moved in between. Separately, an `invoice_amount` above 5000.00 dollars is rejected with `ERR_REIMBURSEMENT_INVOICE_EXCEEDS_MAX`, both at intake and on any re-keying.

Incomplete packets go round-trip, and the round-trip is bounded. A `ClaimsAuditor` may ask the owner for missing paperwork by supplying `requested_items`, which requires the request to be in status `SUBMITTED`, moves it to `DOCUMENTS_REQUESTED`, increments `document_round` by 1, sets `documentation_complete` to false, and increments the request's `version` by 1. A `RecallOperationsManager` then re-keys the request once the owner responds, which requires status `DOCUMENTS_REQUESTED`, overwrites whichever of `invoice_amount`, `parts_amount`, `towing_amount` and `labor_hours_claimed` were supplied, accepts but ignores any `documentation_complete` the client sends and forces that field to true regardless, leaves `requested_items` standing as the document request recorded it, and returns the request to `SUBMITTED`. That `SUBMITTED` to `DOCUMENTS_REQUESTED` and back cycle is capped at **two rounds**: a document request is only permitted while `document_round` is still below 2, and a third attempt is rejected with `ERR_MAX_DOCUMENT_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED`. A step attempted from the wrong status — a document request on a row not `SUBMITTED`, a re-key on a row not `DOCUMENTS_REQUESTED`, or an adjudication on a row that is neither `SUBMITTED` nor `DOCUMENTS_REQUESTED` — is rejected with `ERR_REIMBURSEMENT_INVALID_STATE`.

A `ClaimsAuditor` closes the request with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY`. A **denial** may be taken from either `SUBMITTED` or `DOCUMENTS_REQUESTED`, stores the supplied `denial_reason` — optional, and left null when omitted — records `approved_amount_cents` as 0, moves the request to the terminal `DENIED` state, moves no money, leaves the campaign's `version` alone, and increments the request's `version` by 1. An **approval** may be taken only from `SUBMITTED`, and only when `documentation_complete` is true; an approval attempted from `DOCUMENTS_REQUESTED`, or from `SUBMITTED` while `documentation_complete` is false, is rejected with `ERR_REIMBURSEMENT_INVALID_STATE`.

The approved amount is a cap-and-compare, not a straight payment of the invoice. The server first resolves the campaign labor operation whose closed effective window contains the `invoice_date` — the earliest such operation by `created_at` when more than one qualifies — and records it as the request's `labor_operation_id`; when the campaign has no such labor operation the approval is rejected with `ERR_LABOR_OP_NOT_EFFECTIVE`. The reference part is the campaign's first remedy part ordered by `created_at` ascending. The labor allowance is the smaller of the `labor_hours_claimed` and that labor operation's `flat_hours`, multiplied by a prevailing labor rate of 115.00 dollars per hour, rounded to two decimal places. The parts allowance is the smaller of the owner's `parts_amount` and the retail value of one kit — the part's `unit_cost` multiplied by the one kit a repair consumes and by a customer-price factor of 1.40, that product itself rounded to two decimal places — with the resulting minimum rounded to two decimal places. The towing allowance is paid only when the campaign's `do_not_drive_flag` is true, in which case it is the smaller of the owner's `towing_amount` and a towing cap of 100.00 dollars, rounded to two decimal places; when the flag is false the towing allowance is 0.00 regardless of what the invoice shows. The three rounded allowances are summed and the sum rounded to two decimal places to form the cap. The payable amount is the smaller of the owner's `invoice_amount` and that cap, rounded to two decimal places, and `approved_amount_cents` is that amount multiplied by 100 and rounded to whole cents. Approval moves the request to the terminal `APPROVED` state, increases the campaign's `reimbursement_obligation_cents` by `approved_amount_cents`, and increments both the request's `version` and the campaign's `version` by 1. Reimbursement listings accept `campaign_id`, `vehicle_id`, `labor_operation_id` and `status` filters.

Closing the campaign is the last act, and it is a `ComplianceOfficer` action available only from `campaign_status` `MONITORING`. It records a `closure_note` justifying the closure and moves the campaign to the terminal `CLOSED` state with its `version` incremented by 1. A caller whose `X-User-ID` equals the campaign's `remedy_released_by_user_id` is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and — per the §2.7 bands — that comparison precedes the state check, which rejects closure attempted from any other `campaign_status` with `ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_STATE`. Beyond those, five gates must all hold, and a failure of any of them is reported as `ERR_CLOSURE_CRITERIA_NOT_MET`: `remedy_status` must be `REMEDY_RELEASED`, so a campaign sitting in `REMEDY_REVISED` cannot be closed; `completion_rate_pct` must be at least 95.00 percent; `remedy_effectiveness_score` must be at least 70.00 points; `quarterly_filing_count` must be at least 2, meaning two full quarters of reporting; and no notification wave of the campaign may remain in status `PLANNED` or `RELEASE_APPROVED`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that create a new resource — a campaign, a build record, a population rule, a population run, a notification wave, a dealer, a remedy part, a labor operation, a parts allocation, a repair claim, an outreach attempt, an owner reimbursement request, or a regulatory filing — return `201 Created`. Operations that modify an existing resource, including every state transition, every audit or adjudication decision, and the logical-clock capability, return `200 OK`. Retrievals return `200 OK`.
