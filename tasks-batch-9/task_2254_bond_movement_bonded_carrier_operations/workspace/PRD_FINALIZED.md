# In-Bond Movement & Bonded Carrier Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Merchandise that has entered the United States but has not yet been formally entered for consumption travels under bond. A bonded common carrier accepts custody of that merchandise at one port, moves it under a customs bond to another port, and must account for it there — on time, with its seals unbroken, and with the paperwork that proves either domestic entry or exportation. When any of those obligations fails, the government does not chase the merchandise; it charges the bond. This API is the system of record for that entire chain: the electronic in-bond filing, the bond obligation it consumes, the port-to-port routing, the seals on the container, the examinations that interrupt the movement, the deadlines that expire, and the liquidated damages that follow.

Three operational pressures shape the design. First, a movement has **two independent lifecycles that advance on different triggers** — where the freight is in its transport journey, and whether U.S. Customs and Border Protection still holds it in bonded custody — and conflating them is the most common source of wrong answers in this domain. Second, **every dollar of bond exposure must be reconstructible**: obligated amounts are never written directly, only derived by replaying an append-only ledger, so that a surety can always see exactly which movement or claim consumed its limit of liability. Third, **enforcement must be even-handed and auditable**: the officer who issues a liquidated-damages claim is barred from ever deciding the mitigation petition against it, and the officer who places an examination hold is barred from releasing it.

Because deadlines, decay curves, and general-order timelines are all clock-driven, the system runs on an explicit **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Every derived instant — filing timestamps, transit deadlines, sanction-point decay buckets, sweep outcomes — reads that clock, which makes the whole system deterministically reproducible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold everywhere in the API. Domain behaviour lives in §3; the rules below are invariant across all endpoints.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which it acts. If either header is absent or empty, or if the value of `X-User-Role` is not one of the four recognised role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined — including before the system checks whether the resources named in the path exist. Once authenticated, the caller's role must appear in the target endpoint's authorised-role list; otherwise the response is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Role admission is a pure gate: it says nothing about *which* records the caller may touch, which is settled by the identity bindings below.

The four roles are `BondedCarrier`, `CBPOfficer`, `FPFOfficer`, and `SuretyAgent`.

A `BondedCarrier` is the bonded common carrier and custodian of the merchandise. It files in-bond movements, seals and departs containers, records seal observations, reports arrival and exportation, requests diversions and transit-time extensions, petitions for penalty mitigation, and pays claims. A `CBPOfficer` is a port officer: it registers carriers, bonds, and ports, places and releases examination holds, decides diversion and extension requests, releases cargo from custody, issues liquidated-damages claims, and runs the compliance sweep. An `FPFOfficer` is a Fines, Penalties and Forfeitures officer, who decides mitigation petitions and cancels claims but never issues one. A `SuretyAgent` is the surety that underwrites exactly one bond and may suspend, reinstate, or re-limit that bond and nothing else.

Identity binding differs by role, and the difference is normative:

- When `X-User-Role` is `BondedCarrier`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `BondedCarrier` record. Ownership checks on any resource that references a carrier compare that resource's carrier foreign key to `X-User-ID`. On a filing, `X-User-ID` must equal the `carrier_id` supplied in the request body; on every other carrier-driven write, it must equal the `carrier_id` of the movement being acted on — including when the movement is reached indirectly through a report, an amendment request, a claim, or a petition. A mismatch is `ERR_NOT_MOVEMENT_CARRIER` (HTTP 403). This ownership check applies to exactly ten write operations: filing a movement, departing it, cancelling it, recording a seal event on it, submitting an amendment request against it, filing a movement report, perfecting an export report with proof, filing a mitigation petition, supplementing a petition, and disposing of a claim by payment. It does not apply to any read: a carrier may read another carrier's records.
- When `X-User-Role` is `SuretyAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CustomsBond` — the surety's user identifier *is* the bond identifier it administers. Administering any bond other than the one named in the path yields `ERR_NOT_BOND_SURETY` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `CBPOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it (as `placed_by_user_id` on an `ExaminationHold`, `decided_by_user_id` on an `AmendmentRequest`, `issued_by_officer_id` on a `LiquidatedDamagesClaim`, `executed_by_officer_id` on a `ComplianceSweep`, and `recorded_by_user_id` on a `SealEvent`) but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `FPFOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it as `decided_by_user_id` on a `MitigationPetition` but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

The two opaque roles are subject to a **segregation-of-duties** rule that compares one recorded operator identifier against another. The officer whose `X-User-ID` was stored in `issued_by_officer_id` on a claim may never decide a petition against that claim and may never dispose of that claim, even if that same operator identifier arrives wearing the `FPFOfficer` role. Symmetrically, the officer whose `X-User-ID` was stored in `placed_by_user_id` on an examination hold may not release that hold. All three violations return `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400). That rejection applies to seven top-level collection reads — the bond list, the movement list, the ledger-entry list, the examination-hold list, the report list, the claim list, and the compliance-sweep list — and to the three nested collection reads described in §2.11, which accept and validate `limit` and `offset` identically.

Unless a specific listing states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Four listings deviate for domain reasons and say so where they are described: the seal history for a movement is ordered by `occurred_at` ascending, the amendment requests for a movement by `created_at` ascending, the petitions against a claim by `round` ascending, and the compliance-sweep history by `run_at` descending.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` in the `meta` object of the envelope. `total_count` reflects the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when records beyond the current window remain.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Audit Fields, and the Logical Clock

Every resource is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID with no type prefix or other decoration, and every resource carries a `created_at` timestamp — an ISO 8601 UTC instant rendered with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation of the record, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on each mutation.

Three record types are append-only witnesses rather than living entities, and they carry **only** `id` and `created_at`: `BondObligationLedgerEntry`, `SealEvent`, and `ComplianceSweep`. For those three, both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the representation entirely. Every other resource — `BondedCarrier`, `CustomsBond`, `Port`, `InBondMovement`, `ExaminationHold`, `AmendmentRequest`, `MovementReport`, `LiquidatedDamagesClaim`, and `MitigationPetition` — carries the full set of four common properties.

All time in this system is read from a single **logical clock**. Its value before any caller sets it is `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant; the clock never runs backwards, so a `current_utc` earlier than the clock's current value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422), while a `current_utc` equal to the current value is accepted. Setting the clock is available to all four roles, changes no business state whatsoever, and never itself triggers a sweep, a deadline transition, or a sanction recomputation — those happen only when the corresponding operation is invoked. Every derived instant in the system — `filed_at`, `occurred_at`, `recorded_at`, `reported_at`, `issued_at`, `decided_at`, `released_at`, `run_at`, and every computed deadline — is taken from this clock at the moment of the request. Every instant-valued field a response body carries is rendered in the same form as `created_at`: an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`, so a whole-second instant renders as `2026-09-05T00:00:00.000Z`. That rendering is uniform — it covers clock-derived instants, computed deadlines, `created_at` and `updated_at`, and equally the instants a caller supplied and the server merely stores and echoes back, such as a bond's `effective_date` and `expiry_date` or an examination hold's `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end`, which are normalised to UTC before being rendered; an instant that is not set is null. Wherever this document speaks of "a day" of elapsed time it means exactly 86,400 seconds; no calendar or timezone arithmetic is ever performed.

### 2.5. Money, Days, and Rounding Discipline

Monetary amounts are decimal values with exactly two fractional digits, and every rounding step uses the round-half-up convention unless the rule explicitly calls for rounding down or rounding up. `limit_amount`, `obligated_amount`, `available_amount`, `obligation_amount`, `amount`, `balance_after`, `cargo_value`, `assessed_amount`, `mitigated_amount`, `paid_amount`, `net_before_fee`, `processing_fee`, and `offer_amount` all obey this rule and are returned as JSON numbers rather than strings. `gross_weight_kg` accepts up to two fractional digits.

Two derived quantities round differently and it matters. `sanction_points` is rounded half-up to **one** decimal place and is capped at 12.0. Day counts — `transit_days_allowed`, `overdue_days`, `overdue_days_at_issue`, `extension_days_granted`, `requested_days`, `granted_days` — are whole integers; the sanction throttle rounds a fractional day count **down**, while the overdue measure rounds a fractional day count **up**, so that any fraction of a day past a deadline counts as a whole overdue day.

Where a formula rounds intermediate components before summing them, that per-component rounding is normative and must not be deferred to a single rounding of the total: the two procedures diverge on realistic inputs, and the divergent value is wrong.

### 2.6. The Bond Obligation Ledger

A `CustomsBond` records a `limit_amount` — the surety's limit of liability in U.S. dollars — and exposes two derived figures against it: `obligated_amount`, the exposure currently charged to the bond, and `available_amount`, the headroom that remains. **Neither derived figure is ever supplied by a client.** Both are reconstructed from the bond's append-only stream of `BondObligationLedgerEntry` records.

Each ledger entry names the `bond_id` whose obligation it moves and the `movement_id` the entry is attributed to; entries that arise from a liquidated-damages claim additionally name the `claim_id`, while entries arising purely from a movement leave `claim_id` null. The `entry_type` classifies the movement of money as `OBLIGATE`, `RELEASE`, `INCREASE_LD`, `RELEASE_LD`, or `MITIGATE`. The `amount` is a signed dollar delta — positive for `OBLIGATE` and `INCREASE_LD`, negative for `RELEASE`, `RELEASE_LD`, and `MITIGATE` — and it is never exactly zero. A short machine code in `reason_code` explains why the entry exists; the codes the system writes are `MOVEMENT_FILED`, `MOVEMENT_CANCELLED`, `MOVEMENT_EXPORTED`, `CARGO_RELEASED`, `CARRIER_SUSPENSION_CASCADE`, `CLAIM_ISSUED`, `CLAIM_CANCELLED`, `CLAIM_PAID`, and `PETITION_GRANTED`. Each entry also stamps `recorded_at` from the logical clock and stores `balance_after`, the bond's obligated amount immediately after that entry was applied.

Reconstruction is a chronological replay. Starting from a running balance of 0.00, the bond's entries are processed in `created_at` ascending order — with entries appended within a single request preserving their insertion order — and at each step the running balance becomes the previous balance plus that entry's `amount`, rounded half-up to two decimal places; that rounded running balance is what the entry stores in `balance_after`. When the replay finishes, `obligated_amount` is the final running balance and `available_amount` is `limit_amount` minus `obligated_amount`, rounded half-up to two decimal places. `available_amount` may be negative, but only because a liquidated-damages claim pushed the obligation past the limit — a filing can never do so. Alongside the balances, `open_movement_count` is recomputed as the number of movements charged to that bond whose `transit_status` is `FILED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `OVERDUE`, `ARRIVED`, or `EXPORTED`.

The bond's own `status` is re-derived from `available_amount` at the end of every request that touches the ledger or the limit: a bond that was `ACTIVE` becomes `SATURATED` the moment `available_amount` falls to 0.00 or below, and a bond that was `SATURATED` returns to `ACTIVE` the moment `available_amount` rises strictly above 0.00. `SUSPENDED` and `EXPIRED` are never entered this way.

### 2.7. The Two Status Dimensions of a Movement

An `InBondMovement` carries two independent status fields that advance on different triggers and must never be collapsed into one.

`transit_status` is the transport dimension. It begins at `FILED` and ranges over `FILED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `OVERDUE`, `ARRIVED`, `EXPORTED`, `CLOSED`, and `CANCELLED`, of which `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. It answers "where is the freight in its journey, and is the journey still timely?"

`custody_status` is the customs-custody dimension. It begins at `BONDED` and ranges over `BONDED`, `EXAM_HOLD`, `RELEASED`, and `GENERAL_ORDER`, of which `RELEASED` and `GENERAL_ORDER` are terminal. It answers "does CBP still hold this merchandise, and on what footing?"

The two dimensions interact but do not synchronise. A movement can sit at `ARRIVED` with custody `EXAM_HOLD` and then move to custody `GENERAL_ORDER` while `transit_status` stays `ARRIVED`. A movement can reach `EXPORTED` while an examination hold keeps custody at `EXAM_HOLD`, and only close once the hold clears. Custody reverting out of `EXAM_HOLD` always returns to `BONDED` and never jumps straight to `RELEASED`.

### 2.8. Cascades, Recomputation, and Version Semantics

Many operations in this domain mutate more than the record the caller addressed, and the secondary effects are part of the contract rather than housekeeping. Filing, cancelling, closing, and releasing a movement each append a ledger entry, which recomputes the underwriting bond's `obligated_amount`, `available_amount`, and `open_movement_count`, and re-derives its `status`. Issuing, mitigating, paying, and cancelling a claim do the same. Suspending or revoking a carrier cancels each of that carrier's movements still in `FILED`, and each of those cancellations appends its own ledger entry and re-derives its bond. Placing or releasing an examination hold recomputes the affected port's `open_hold_count`. Every claim issuance, claim disposition, and compliance sweep recomputes the carrier's `sanction_points`, which may in turn cross a threshold and drive the carrier's own state machine.

Where a cascade touches a set of records, the set is processed in `created_at` ascending order so that the resulting ledger sequence is deterministic and reproducible.

Each mutation of a resource refreshes that resource's `updated_at` and increments its `version` by one — including mutations that only change derived fields such as a bond's `obligated_amount` or a carrier's `sanction_points`. Two increments are called out explicitly because clients depend on them: submitting an amendment request against a movement, approving an amendment request, and departing a movement each increment that movement's `version`; filing a petition against a claim increments that claim's `version`. Denying an amendment request, by contrast, changes nothing on the movement and does **not** increment the movement's `version`.

### 2.9. Stale-Version Protection on Amendment and Petition Filing

Two write paths accept a client-observed version and refuse to proceed against a record that has moved on since the client read it. Both exist because the write legitimately supersedes an earlier in-flight request, which means a blind write would silently discard something the client never saw.

Submitting an amendment request requires `expected_movement_version` as a **non-optional** body field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `InBondMovement`. If that value does not equal the movement's current `version`, the submission is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MOVEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Because a new amendment request supersedes any amendment request on that movement still sitting in `SUBMITTED`, accepting a stale write would discard a routing change or a transit-day change the client has not read; the check forces a re-read first.

Filing a mitigation petition requires `expected_claim_version` as a **non-optional** body field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `LiquidatedDamagesClaim`. If that value does not equal the claim's current `version`, the filing is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new petition supersedes any live petition on that claim, so accepting a stale write would petition against an amount or status the client has not re-read.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check.

### 2.10. Ordered Validation Bands

Validation is strictly ordered. The **first** check that fails determines the response; no later check may mask an earlier one, and no check may be evaluated out of band. The bands, in ascending order of evaluation, are:

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` present and equal to one of `BondedCarrier`, `CBPOfficer`, `FPFOfficer`, `SuretyAgent`, and `X-User-ID` present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Evaluated on every request, ahead of path-parameter existence.
2. **Role authorisation.** The caller's role appears in the endpoint's authorised list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Evaluated on every request.
3. **Identity binding.** For a `BondedCarrier`, `ERR_NOT_MOVEMENT_CARRIER` when the acting user is not the carrier behind the record; for a `SuretyAgent`, `ERR_NOT_BOND_SURETY` when the acting user is not the bond named in the path. Deliberately ordered *ahead of* request-shape validation, so that a malformed payload can never leak the existence of another carrier's records.
4. **Segregation of duties.** `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` on a petition decision, a claim disposition, or an examination-hold release performed by the barred operator.
5. **Request shape.** All required body fields present with the declared type, enum values in range, money non-negative with at most two fractional digits, integers non-negative, datetimes valid ISO 8601 UTC — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Unknown body fields are ignored rather than rejected. Pagination parameters on list reads are validated in this same band and yield `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
6. **Existence.** Each referenced record exists, with a distinct error per record type: `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MOVEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HOLD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PETITION_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` for a ledger entry read.
7. **Uniqueness.** `ERR_DUPLICATE_IRS_NUMBER` on carrier registration and `ERR_DUPLICATE_PORT_CODE` on port registration.
8. **Optimistic concurrency.** The two stale-version checks of §2.9, evaluated after existence and before any state check.
9. **The acted-on record's own state.** `ERR_MOVEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_HOLD_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_AMENDMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_REPORT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PETITION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_BOND_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_CARRIER_INVALID_STATE`.
10. **Other records' state.** `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_BOND_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_ARRIVED`, `ERR_MOVEMENT_IN_GENERAL_ORDER`.
11. **Cargo safety and seal integrity.** `ERR_SEAL_NOT_APPLIED`, `ERR_SEAL_INTEGRITY_UNRESOLVED`, `ERR_HOLD_OPEN`, `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_DIVERSION`. This band sits deliberately **above** the temporal band, so that a compromised container is refused even when the paperwork is perfectly timely.
12. **Temporal.** `ERR_TRANSIT_DEADLINE_PASSED`, `ERR_BOND_COVERAGE_GAP`, `ERR_EXAM_WINDOW_INVALID`, `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC`.
13. **Business constraints.** `ERR_ROUTE_TYPE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_STB_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_EXAM_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_GRANTED_DAYS_INVALID`, `ERR_PETITION_ROUND_LIMIT`, `ERR_HOLD_TYPE_NOT_ALLOWED`, `ERR_REPORT_TYPE_NOT_ALLOWED`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM`, `ERR_NO_VIOLATION_TO_CLAIM`, `ERR_MITIGATION_FACTOR_INVALID`.
14. **Fiscal.** `ERR_BOND_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_BOND`, `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`.

Two orderings inside these bands deserve emphasis because they are easy to invert. On a filing, the bond's activity check (`ERR_BOND_NOT_ACTIVE`, band 10) is evaluated strictly **before** the bond's sufficiency check (`ERR_BOND_INSUFFICIENT`, band 14): a `SATURATED`, `SUSPENDED`, or `EXPIRED` bond fails on activity and never reaches the arithmetic. And on any movement report or cargo release, the seal-integrity checks of band 11 are evaluated **before** the routing and timeliness checks of bands 12 and 13.

### 2.11. Path Resolution for Nested Reads

Three read paths reach a child collection through its parent: the seal history and the amendment-request history of a movement, and the petition history of a claim. For these, path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the response reports the **first** identifier that does not resolve. A request for the amendment history of a movement that does not exist therefore returns `ERR_MOVEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, and a request for the petition history of a claim that does not exist returns `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` — the child collection is never consulted. Every other relationship in this system is reached not by nesting but by filtering the child collection: ledger entries by `bond_id`, `movement_id`, `claim_id`, or `entry_type`; examination holds by `movement_id`, `port_id`, `hold_type`, or `status`; movement reports by `movement_id`, `port_id`, `report_type`, or `status`; claims by `movement_id`, `bond_id`, `claim_type`, or `status`; bonds by `carrier_id`, `bond_type`, or `status`; and movements by `carrier_id`, `bond_id`, `origin_port_id`, `destination_port_id`, `arrival_port_id`, `predecessor_movement_id`, `in_bond_type`, `transit_status`, or `custody_status`. The nested seal history additionally filters on `event_type`, the nested amendment history on `amendment_type` and `status`, and the nested petition history on `status`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering the Participants: Carriers, Bonds, and Ports

A `CBPOfficer` registers a bonded carrier by supplying its legal `name` — between 1 and 120 characters — and its `irs_number`, the IRS/EIN identifier written in the pattern of two digits, a hyphen, and seven digits. The `irs_number` must be unique across all carriers; re-registering one already on file is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IRS_NUMBER`. A newly registered carrier opens with `custodial_status` of `ACTIVE`, `sanction_points` of 0.0, `overdue_movement_count` of 0, `open_claim_count` of 0, and `suspension_reason` null. `overdue_movement_count` is always the live count of that carrier's movements whose `transit_status` is `OVERDUE`; `open_claim_count` is the live count of liquidated-damages claims against that carrier's movements whose `status` is `ISSUED`, `PETITIONED`, or `MITIGATED`.

Custodial privileges are `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, or `REVOKED`, with `REVOKED` terminal. An officer may drive the carrier by hand along exactly three edges — `ACTIVE → REVOKED`, `SUSPENDED → REVOKED`, and `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` — by supplying the target `custodial_status` and an optional `reason_code`; any other requested transition, including anything out of `REVOKED`, is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_INVALID_STATE`. Reinstating from `SUSPENDED` to `ACTIVE` additionally requires that the carrier's `sanction_points` be strictly below 10.0, and failing that requirement is likewise `ERR_CARRIER_INVALID_STATE`. Revocation records `suspension_reason` as `PRIVILEGE_REVOKED`; manual reinstatement clears `suspension_reason` back to null.

Bonds are underwritten by a `CBPOfficer`, who names the `carrier_id` of the obligated principal, chooses a `bond_type` of either `SINGLE_TRANSACTION` — which covers one movement window at a time — or `CONTINUOUS`, sets the `limit_amount` (strictly greater than zero, two decimal places), the inclusive `effective_date` from which the bond covers movements and the inclusive `expiry_date` through which it does so, and the `surety_code` of the surety company. The expiry must fall after the effective date. A new bond opens at `status` `ACTIVE`, with `obligated_amount` 0.00, `available_amount` equal to `limit_amount`, and `open_movement_count` 0. The identifier minted for the new bond is also the `X-User-ID` that its `SuretyAgent` will present.

Bond status ranges over `ACTIVE`, `SATURATED`, `SUSPENDED`, and `EXPIRED`, with `EXPIRED` terminal. The `ACTIVE ↔ SATURATED` pair is entirely system-derived from headroom as described in §2.6 and can never be set by hand. A `SuretyAgent` may act on its own bond in exactly two ways, singly or together: it may set `status`, and it may raise or lower `limit_amount`. The only status edges a surety may drive are `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, `SATURATED → SUSPENDED`, and `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`; requesting anything else — touching an `EXPIRED` bond, or setting `SATURATED` by hand — is `ERR_BOND_INVALID_STATE`. Reinstating from `SUSPENDED` to `ACTIVE` is permitted only while the logical clock is at or before the bond's `expiry_date`, and the reinstated bond is immediately re-derived to `SATURATED` if its headroom is not strictly positive. Changing `limit_amount` recomputes `available_amount` as the new limit less the unchanged `obligated_amount` and immediately re-derives the `ACTIVE`/`SATURATED` pair from that new headroom. A `SUSPENDED` bond blocks new filings and departures against it but leaves movements already in flight untouched.

Ports are registered by a `CBPOfficer` with a `port_code` of exactly four digits and unique across all ports — a duplicate is `ERR_DUPLICATE_PORT_CODE` — a `name`, a `region_code` drawn from `NE`, `MW`, `SE`, `PSW`, and `NW`, and an `exam_capacity_slots` count of zero or more concurrent scheduled examination windows the port can host. The optional `go_warehouse_available` flag states whether a general-order warehouse serves the port and defaults to true. A port's `open_hold_count` starts at 0 and is thereafter the live count of examination holds at that port whose `status` is `OPEN`, counting every hold type including those that occupy no scheduled window.

### 3.2. Filing an Electronic In-Bond Movement

A `BondedCarrier` opens a movement by filing electronically. The filing declares the `carrier_id` (which must equal the filer's own `X-User-ID`), the `in_bond_type`, the `origin_port_id` where the movement begins, the `destination_port_id` it routes to, the `manifest_reference` from the carrier's manifest or bill, the declared `cargo_value` in dollars, the `gross_weight_kg`, and the `package_count`. Two body fields are optional: `bond_id`, naming the bond to charge, and `predecessor_movement_id`, naming a prior leg this movement was re-manifested from. `cargo_value` and `gross_weight_kg` must both be strictly greater than zero and `package_count` at least 1, or the filing fails shape validation.

The `in_bond_type` selects among the three statutory forms. `IT` is immediate transportation — merchandise moves from the port of unlading to another port for entry there. `TE` is transportation and exportation — merchandise moves to another port and then leaves the country. `IE` is immediate exportation — merchandise leaves from the port where it currently sits. That last distinction constrains the routing: an `IE` filing requires that `destination_port_id` be the same port as `origin_port_id`, while `IT` and `TE` filings require that the two differ. A filing whose route shape contradicts its declared type is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_TYPE_MISMATCH`.

When `predecessor_movement_id` is supplied, the referenced movement must exist, must belong to the same carrier, and must have reached `transit_status` `ARRIVED`; an onward leg may only be filed against an arrived predecessor, and a predecessor that has not arrived or belongs to another carrier yields `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_ARRIVED`. A movement with no predecessor is an origin leg and leaves `predecessor_movement_id` null.

Before the filing is accepted, the carrier's `custodial_status` must be `ACTIVE` — a `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED` carrier may neither file nor depart, and the refusal is `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_ACTIVE` — and the resolved bond's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, with `SATURATED`, `SUSPENDED`, and `EXPIRED` all refused as `ERR_BOND_NOT_ACTIVE`. The bond must also cover the whole transit: its `expiry_date` must fall at or after the movement's computed transit deadline plus a three-day coverage margin, and its `effective_date` must fall at or before the current logical clock. A bond that lapses mid-transit cannot underwrite the movement even though it is `ACTIVE` and has headroom today, and the refusal is `ERR_BOND_COVERAGE_GAP`.

**How much bond the movement consumes.** The filing computes `obligation_amount` from three independent components, each rounded to cents *before* they are added together. The value component is `cargo_value` multiplied by 0.0325 — that is, three and a quarter cents of bond exposure per dollar of declared value — rounded half-up to two decimal places. The weight component is `gross_weight_kg` multiplied by 0.0125, one and a quarter cents per kilogram, rounded half-up to two decimal places. The package component is `package_count` multiplied by 0.1275, twelve and three-quarter cents per package, rounded half-up to two decimal places. Those three already-rounded figures are summed, and the subtotal is **not** rounded again. Finally, `obligation_amount` is the greater of that subtotal and a floor of 25.00, so that even a trivial shipment carries a minimum obligation of twenty-five dollars. The value stored at filing is never recomputed for the life of the movement.

**How long the movement has.** `transit_days_allowed` and `transit_deadline_utc` are derived together. The starting allowance depends on the in-bond type: an `IT` movement and a `TE` movement each begin with 30 days, while an `IE` movement, which merely leaves from where it already is, begins with 15. If the origin port's `region_code` differs from the destination port's `region_code`, an interport leg surcharge of 5 further days is added. Next comes the **sanction throttle**: if the carrier's `sanction_points`, valued as of this request and after any recomputation performed within it, stands at 6.0 or higher, the running allowance is replaced by the greater of 5 days and the running allowance halved and rounded down to a whole number — a poorly performing carrier gets roughly half the window, but never fewer than five days. The throttled allowance is then capped by taking the lesser of it and 60 days. Only after that cap is applied are granted extension days added: `transit_days_allowed` is the capped allowance plus `extension_days_granted`, which means an approved extension can legitimately push the total beyond the sixty-day ceiling because the ceiling constrains only the earned window. `transit_deadline_utc` is then `deadline_anchor_utc` advanced by `transit_days_allowed` whole days of 86,400 seconds each.

`deadline_anchor_utc` is the instant the transit clock is measured from. At filing it is set to `filed_at`, the logical-clock instant the electronic filing was accepted, and it stays at `filed_at` for the whole life of the movement unless an approved diversion rebases it. Because an approved extension leaves the anchor alone, an extension only lengthens the window while the clock still runs from the original filing; an approved diversion, by contrast, rebases the anchor to the decision instant and re-evaluates the interport surcharge against the *new* destination's region, so the same number of days produces a materially later deadline. That asymmetry is the whole point of separating the two amendment types.

**Which bond underwrites it.** When `bond_id` is omitted, the server selects one deterministically from the filing carrier's bonds. Eligibility is filtered first, and every condition must hold: the bond's `status` is `ACTIVE`; its `effective_date` is at or before the current logical clock; its `expiry_date` is at or after the movement's computed `transit_deadline_utc` advanced by three days of coverage margin; its `available_amount` is at least the computed `obligation_amount`; and, for a `SINGLE_TRANSACTION` bond only, the movement's window does not collide with an existing open movement on that bond under the same window test described below. The surviving candidates are then ranked, and the first row wins: `CONTINUOUS` bonds rank ahead of `SINGLE_TRANSACTION` bonds; among bonds of equal type, larger `available_amount` ranks ahead of smaller; and remaining ties are broken by earlier `created_at`. Identifier ordering is never used as a tiebreak. If no bond survives the filter, the filing fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_BOND` rather than proceeding unbonded.

**The single-transaction window rule.** A `SINGLE_TRANSACTION` bond covers one movement window at a time. Its window is the **closed** interval from `filed_at` through `transit_deadline_utc` inclusive at both ends, and a new filing is refused when that interval collides with the window of another movement on the same bond whose `transit_status` is `FILED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `OVERDUE`, `ARRIVED`, or `EXPORTED`. Because the interval is closed, two windows that merely touch at a single endpoint **do** collide — a distinction that matters, because the examination-window rule of §3.6 uses the opposite convention. The refusal is `ERR_STB_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

**The fiscal gate.** Last of all, the computed `obligation_amount` must be no greater than the bond's `available_amount`. Equality is permitted and is the ordinary way a bond arrives at `SATURATED`. Exceeding the headroom is `ERR_BOND_INSUFFICIENT`.

An accepted filing lands the movement at `transit_status` `FILED` and `custody_status` `BONDED`, with `seal_intact` false because no seal has yet been applied, `seal_number` null, `arrival_port_id` null, `arrival_reported_at` null, `export_reported_at` null, `go_deadline_utc` null, `go_grace_applied` false, `diversion_flag` false, `extension_days_granted` 0, `overdue_days` 0, and `cancellation_reason` null. It appends a ledger entry of type `OBLIGATE` for the full positive `obligation_amount` with `reason_code` `MOVEMENT_FILED`, and that entry recomputes the bond's `obligated_amount`, `available_amount`, and `open_movement_count` and re-derives the bond's `status`, which becomes `SATURATED` when the headroom has fallen to 0.00 or below.

A filed movement that has not yet left can be withdrawn by its carrier with a `reason_code`, which records that code in `cancellation_reason` and drives `transit_status` `FILED → CANCELLED`. The withdrawal appends a `RELEASE` ledger entry for the negative of `obligation_amount` with `reason_code` `MOVEMENT_CANCELLED`, recomputes the bond's `obligated_amount`, `available_amount`, and `open_movement_count`, and returns a `SATURATED` bond to `ACTIVE` when headroom reappears above 0.00. Withdrawal is possible only from `FILED`; attempting it once the movement has departed is `ERR_MOVEMENT_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.3. Sealing, Departure, and the Seal Chain of Custody

Departure is the act that puts the container on the road. Its only body field is the `seal_number` affixed to the container, and it may be performed only by the movement's own carrier, only while `transit_status` is `FILED`, only while `custody_status` is `BONDED` with no examination hold on the movement still `OPEN`, only while the carrier's `custodial_status` is `ACTIVE`, and only while the underwriting bond's `status` is `ACTIVE`. A departure meeting all of those conditions advances `transit_status` `FILED → IN_TRANSIT`, appends a seal event of type `APPLIED` recording the supplied `seal_number` at the origin port's `port_code`, writes that `seal_number` onto the movement, sets `seal_intact` to true, and increments the movement's `version`. Departing a movement that has already departed is `ERR_MOVEMENT_INVALID_STATE`; departing under a suspended carrier is `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_ACTIVE`; departing on a bond that is no longer `ACTIVE` is `ERR_BOND_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Seal observations thereafter form an append-only chain of custody. Either the movement's own carrier or a `CBPOfficer` may record one, naming the `movement_id`, the `event_type`, the `seal_number` observed or applied, and the `port_code` of the location where the observation was made, which must match a registered port. The system stamps `recorded_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and `occurred_at` from the logical clock, and computes `resulting_seal_intact` as the value the movement's `seal_intact` carries after the event.

Five event types exist and their effects differ. `APPLIED` is written only by the system, on the departure path, and leaves `seal_intact` true. `VERIFIED_INTACT` is an inspection that confirms what was already there and leaves the movement's prior `seal_intact` value unchanged. `REPLACED` records a lawfully re-sealed container and sets `seal_intact` true, which is the only way to restore integrity after a break. `BROKEN_AUTHORIZED` records a break performed with permission and sets `seal_intact` false. `BROKEN_UNAUTHORIZED` records an unexplained break: it sets `seal_intact` false **and** triggers a cascade. The system automatically creates an examination hold of type `SEAL_DISCREPANCY` against the movement with `created_by_system` true and `placed_by_user_id` recorded as the literal string `SYSTEM`, which drives `custody_status` to `EXAM_HOLD`, recomputes `open_hold_count` for the port whose `port_code` matches the seal event, and — when the movement is at `ARRIVED` and has not yet consumed its one-time general-order grace — extends the general-order deadline as described in §3.6. This system-created hold is the sole witness a `SEAL_VIOLATION` claim needs.

Because only `APPLIED` may be recorded while `seal_number` is still null, any other seal event on a movement that has not yet departed is refused with `ERR_SEAL_NOT_APPLIED`; the same refusal applies to a movement report filed before any seal exists. Recording a seal event against a movement whose `transit_status` is `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`, or whose `custody_status` has reached `RELEASED` or `GENERAL_ORDER`, is `ERR_MOVEMENT_INVALID_STATE`.

A broken seal is an unresolved integrity problem, and it blocks four things until a `REPLACED` event restores it: releasing cargo from custody, filing a movement report, perfecting an export report with proof, and submitting an amendment request. All four are refused with `ERR_SEAL_INTEGRITY_UNRESOLVED`, and that refusal is evaluated before any timeliness or routing check, so a compromised container is stopped even when the filing is perfectly punctual. The seal history of a movement is readable in `occurred_at` ascending order and may be filtered by `event_type`.

### 3.4. Diversion and Extension Requests

Circumstances change in transit, and two kinds of amendment exist for them. A `DIVERSION` changes where the movement is going and may optionally change what kind of in-bond movement it is; an `EXTENSION` buys more transit days. Both are submitted by the movement's own carrier against a `movement_id`, both carry a `justification_code` drawn from `CONSIGNEE_CHANGE`, `CARRIER_EQUIPMENT_FAILURE`, `PORT_CONGESTION`, and `WEATHER_DELAY`, both carry the mandatory `expected_movement_version` of §2.9, and both open at `status` `SUBMITTED` with `granted_days`, `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at`, `decision_reason_code`, and `resulting_deadline_utc` all null.

The two `amendment_type` values take disjoint payload fields. A `DIVERSION` supplies `requested_destination_port_code`, the four-digit code of the port it wants to be redirected to, which must match a registered port or the submission fails with `ERR_PORT_NOT_FOUND`; it may also supply `requested_in_bond_type` to change the movement's form to `IT`, `TE`, or `IE`; and it must leave `requested_days` null. An `EXTENSION` supplies `requested_days`, an integer of at least 1 and at most 30, and must leave both `requested_destination_port_code` and `requested_in_bond_type` null.

Submission is admitted only while the movement's `transit_status` is `FILED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, or `OVERDUE`; anything else is `ERR_MOVEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. The movement's `seal_intact` must be true, unless the movement has not yet departed and is therefore still at `FILED`, in which case no seal exists to be intact. A movement whose `custody_status` has reached `GENERAL_ORDER` cannot be amended at all and yields `ERR_MOVEMENT_IN_GENERAL_ORDER`. A `DIVERSION` is additionally checked for route shape against the requested destination and, when supplied, the requested type: redirecting an `IE` movement to a port other than its origin, or redirecting an `IT` or `TE` movement to its own origin port, is `ERR_ROUTE_TYPE_MISMATCH`.

Two constraints bind extensions specifically. Timing: an `EXTENSION` may be submitted only while the logical clock stands at or before the movement's `transit_deadline_utc` advanced by a two-day late grace, so the extension window closes two days after the deadline and a later request is `ERR_TRANSIT_DEADLINE_PASSED`. `DIVERSION` requests are not subject to that window at all. Cumulative volume: the movement's existing `extension_days_granted` plus the `requested_days` on this submission must not exceed 30 total granted days, and exceeding it is `ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

An accepted submission has one side effect beyond its own creation: every other amendment request on the same movement still sitting in `SUBMITTED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED` with `decision_reason_code` set to `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEWER_REQUEST`. The submission also increments the movement's `version`, which is precisely why the next client must re-read before submitting again.

A `CBPOfficer` decides a submitted request by supplying a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `DENY`, a `decision_reason_code`, and — for an extension approval — `granted_days`. Only a request in `SUBMITTED` can be decided; a request already `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, or `SUPERSEDED` can never be decided again, and the refusal is `ERR_AMENDMENT_INVALID_STATE`. The underlying movement must still be in `FILED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, or `OVERDUE`, or the decision is `ERR_MOVEMENT_INVALID_STATE`.

A denial records `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at`, and `decision_reason_code`, moves the request `SUBMITTED → DENIED`, and changes **nothing** on the movement: `resulting_deadline_utc` stays null and the movement's `version` is not incremented.

An approval moves the request `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and branches on `amendment_type`. For a **`DIVERSION`**: the movement's `destination_port_id` becomes the identifier of the port whose `port_code` was requested; its `in_bond_type` becomes `requested_in_bond_type` when one was supplied and is left alone otherwise; `deadline_anchor_utc` is rebased to `decided_at`, the decision instant read from the logical clock; and `transit_days_allowed` and `transit_deadline_utc` are recomputed with the interport surcharge re-evaluated against the **new** destination port's `region_code`. For an **`EXTENSION`**: `granted_days` must be present and must be at least 1 and no more than `requested_days` — an officer may grant fewer days than were asked for but never more, and a violation is `ERR_GRANTED_DAYS_INVALID` — and the movement's `extension_days_granted` is increased by `granted_days`, which must keep the cumulative total at 30 or below or the approval fails with `ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. On an extension approval `deadline_anchor_utc` is **not** touched; it remains `filed_at`, and only `transit_deadline_utc` moves out. Both branches then record the recomputed `transit_deadline_utc` onto the request as `resulting_deadline_utc` and increment the movement's `version`.

An approval can also revive a movement that had already gone overdue. When the movement stands at `OVERDUE`, its `overdue_days` is no greater than 2 — the same two-day late grace that governs extension submission — and the freshly recomputed `transit_deadline_utc` now lies strictly after the current logical clock, the movement transitions `OVERDUE → IN_TRANSIT`, its `overdue_days` resets to 0, and the carrier's `overdue_movement_count` is recomputed. If `overdue_days` exceeds 2, or if the recomputed deadline is still in the past, the movement remains `OVERDUE` — but the amendment is still recorded as `APPROVED` and its field effects still apply. The full history of diversion and extension requests against a movement is readable in `created_at` ascending order, filterable by `amendment_type` and `status`.

### 3.5. Arrival, Exportation, and Cargo Release

Accounting for a movement means filing a report. The report names the `movement_id`, the `port_id` where the event occurred, the `report_type` of either `ARRIVAL` or `EXPORT`, and `packages_reported`, the number of packages actually tendered at that port, which may be zero or more. An `EXPORT` report may also carry `proof_of_export_ref`, the reference to the proof-of-exportation document, and `export_conveyance_ref`, the outbound vessel or flight reference. The system stamps `reported_at` from the logical clock and computes `package_discrepancy` as the movement's `package_count` minus `packages_reported` as of filing time.

Which report types are permissible depends on the in-bond type, and this is checked as a business constraint rather than left to the state machine. An `IT` movement admits an `ARRIVAL` report and nothing else. A `TE` movement admits an `ARRIVAL` report and then an `EXPORT` report. An `IE` movement admits an `EXPORT` report and nothing else. An `EXPORT` report on an `IT` movement, or an `ARRIVAL` report on an `IE` movement, is refused with `ERR_REPORT_TYPE_NOT_ALLOWED`. Reporting is restricted to the movement's own carrier, is refused for a movement whose `custody_status` is `GENERAL_ORDER` with `ERR_MOVEMENT_IN_GENERAL_ORDER`, is refused before a seal exists with `ERR_SEAL_NOT_APPLIED`, and is refused while `seal_intact` is false with `ERR_SEAL_INTEGRITY_UNRESOLVED`. A second `ARRIVAL` report on a movement that has already arrived, and any report against a `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` movement, is `ERR_MOVEMENT_INVALID_STATE`.

**Arrival.** An accepted `ARRIVAL` report advances the movement to `ARRIVED` from either `IN_TRANSIT` or `OVERDUE`. Where the reporting port stands relative to the intended destination decides whether the arrival is lawful. Three situations are accepted: the reporting `port_id` is the movement's `destination_port_id`; the reporting port is a different port but shares the destination port's `region_code`, which the system treats as a minor deviation; or an already-approved `DIVERSION` amendment has previously moved `destination_port_id` to the reporting port. Anything else — an arrival at a port outside the destination's region with no approved diversion on the movement — is not a discrepancy but an unauthorized diversion, refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_DIVERSION`.

An accepted arrival sets `arrival_port_id` to the reporting port, sets `arrival_reported_at` to the current logical clock, sets `diversion_flag` to true whenever the reporting port differs from `destination_port_id` (and leaves it false otherwise), computes `go_deadline_utc`, and marks the report `ACCEPTED` with its `package_discrepancy` computed. Arrival from `IN_TRANSIT` and arrival from `OVERDUE` have identical effects with one deliberate exception: an overdue movement **retains** its `overdue_days` rather than resetting it, because that count is the input to a subsequent late-arrival claim.

**Exportation.** An `EXPORT` report perfects the export only when it carries `proof_of_export_ref`. When it does, the movement advances to `EXPORTED` — from `IN_TRANSIT` or `OVERDUE` for an `IE` movement, or from `ARRIVED` for a `TE` movement — `export_reported_at` is set from the logical clock, and the report is marked `ACCEPTED`. Advancing to `EXPORTED` requires `seal_intact` to be true, and for a `TE` movement it additionally requires that `custody_status` not be `GENERAL_ORDER`. An overdue movement likewise retains its `overdue_days` through export, since that count feeds a no-proof-of-export or late-arrival claim.

When an `EXPORT` report arrives **without** `proof_of_export_ref`, it is stored at `status` `PENDING_PROOF` and the export is not perfected: neither `transit_status` nor `export_reported_at` changes. The movement then becomes eligible for a `NO_PROOF_OF_EXPORT` claim as soon as the logical clock passes `transit_deadline_utc`. The carrier can perfect it later by supplying the missing `proof_of_export_ref`, optionally together with an `export_conveyance_ref`; the report moves `PENDING_PROOF → ACCEPTED` and the movement advances to `EXPORTED` exactly as if the proof had arrived with the original report. Perfecting a report requires that it actually be in `PENDING_PROOF` — anything else is `ERR_REPORT_INVALID_STATE` — that the acting user be the movement's carrier, that `seal_intact` be true, and that `custody_status` not be `GENERAL_ORDER`. If a later `EXPORT` report is filed on the same movement while an earlier one still sits in `PENDING_PROOF`, the earlier report moves `PENDING_PROOF → SUPERSEDED` and nothing on the movement changes as a result.

**Closing out.** A movement that has reached `EXPORTED` closes automatically the moment no examination hold on it remains `OPEN` — either immediately, when the export proof was accepted and no hold existed, or later, when the release of the last open hold clears the way. Closing transitions `EXPORTED → CLOSED` and appends a `RELEASE` ledger entry for the negative of `obligation_amount` with `reason_code` `MOVEMENT_EXPORTED`, which recomputes the bond's `obligated_amount`, `available_amount`, and `open_movement_count` and returns a `SATURATED` bond to `ACTIVE` when headroom reappears above 0.00.

**Cargo release.** A `CBPOfficer` releases merchandise from customs custody at the destination port, optionally recording a `reason_code`. Release requires that the movement's `transit_status` be `ARRIVED` or `EXPORTED`, that no examination hold on the movement remain `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_HOLD_OPEN` — that `seal_intact` be true, and that `custody_status` not already be `GENERAL_ORDER`. A release drives `custody_status` `BONDED → RELEASED`. For an `IT` movement standing at `ARRIVED`, the release also completes the journey: `transit_status` advances `ARRIVED → CLOSED` in the same request, appending a `RELEASE` ledger entry for the negative of `obligation_amount` with `reason_code` `CARGO_RELEASED` and re-deriving the bond exactly as above. A `TE` or `IE` movement is not closed by release; its closure comes from perfected exportation.

### 3.6. Examination Holds, General Order, and the Compliance Sweep

A `CBPOfficer` interrupts a movement by placing an examination hold, naming the `movement_id`, the `port_id` whose examination resources the hold consumes, the `hold_type`, and a short `reason_code` explaining why. Five hold types exist and they divide into two families. `EXAM_NII` (non-intrusive inspection) and `EXAM_PHYSICAL` occupy a scheduled examination window and therefore require both a `scheduled_start`, the inclusive start of the window, and a `scheduled_end`, the exclusive end, with the end strictly after the start. `DOCUMENT` and `PGA` holds occupy no bay and require both window fields to be absent. `SEAL_DISCREPANCY` is never placed by a caller — it exists only as the system's automatic response to an unauthorized seal break — and requesting it directly is refused with `ERR_HOLD_TYPE_NOT_ALLOWED`. A missing or inverted window on a scheduled type, or a window supplied on a `DOCUMENT` or `PGA` hold, is `ERR_EXAM_WINDOW_INVALID`.

**Bay capacity.** A port hosts at most `exam_capacity_slots` concurrent scheduled examinations. Occupancy is measured against the candidate window: the system counts the examination holds at the candidate's port whose `status` is `OPEN` and whose `hold_type` is `EXAM_NII` or `EXAM_PHYSICAL`, keeping those whose window overlaps the candidate's. Windows are **half-open** — an existing hold overlaps the candidate exactly when the existing start falls strictly before the candidate's end **and** the candidate's start falls strictly before the existing end — so two windows that merely touch at a single endpoint do **not** conflict and do not consume the same slot. This is the opposite convention from the closed single-transaction bond window of §3.2, and the contrast is deliberate. When the overlapping open count has reached `exam_capacity_slots`, the hold is refused with `ERR_EXAM_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. `DOCUMENT` and `PGA` holds carry no window and are never counted toward occupancy, though they do count toward the port's `open_hold_count`.

A hold may be placed only while the movement's `transit_status` is neither `CLOSED` nor `CANCELLED` and its `custody_status` is neither `RELEASED` nor `GENERAL_ORDER`; otherwise the placement is `ERR_MOVEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. A new hold opens at `status` `OPEN` with `released_at`, `findings_code`, and `discrepancy_pieces` unset — `discrepancy_pieces` reading 0 — and `created_by_system` false, and it records `placed_by_user_id` as the acting officer's `X-User-ID`, the operator who is thereafter barred from releasing it. Placement drives `custody_status` `BONDED → EXAM_HOLD` and recomputes the port's `open_hold_count`. A second and any later concurrent hold leaves custody at `EXAM_HOLD` — a self-loop — and again recomputes `open_hold_count`.

**The general-order timeline.** Arrived merchandise must be entered before it is sent to a general-order warehouse. At the moment an arrival is accepted, `go_deadline_utc` is set to `arrival_reported_at` advanced by 15 days, and `go_grace_applied` is false. The first time an examination hold opens against the movement while its `transit_status` is `ARRIVED` and `go_grace_applied` is still false — whether the hold was placed by an officer or auto-created from an unauthorized seal break — `go_deadline_utc` is pushed out by a further 5 days and `go_grace_applied` becomes true. That grace is one-time only: a second hold on the same movement leaves `go_deadline_utc` exactly where the first one left it.

**Releasing a hold.** A `CBPOfficer` other than the one who placed it releases a hold with a `findings_code` of `CLEAR`, `DISCREPANCY`, or `SEAL_COMPROMISED`, and an optional `discrepancy_pieces` count of pieces found in discrepancy, which defaults to 0 when omitted. Only an `OPEN` hold can be released; releasing one already `RELEASED` is `ERR_HOLD_INVALID_STATE`. Release moves the hold `OPEN → RELEASED`, sets `released_at` from the logical clock, records the supplied `findings_code` and `discrepancy_pieces`, and recomputes the port's `open_hold_count`. When the released hold was the **last** open hold on the movement, custody reverts `EXAM_HOLD → BONDED` — never straight to `RELEASED` — and, if the movement had already reached `EXPORTED`, it now auto-closes `EXPORTED → CLOSED` with its `RELEASE` ledger entry and the bond recomputation that entry entails.

**The compliance sweep.** A `CBPOfficer` runs a deterministic sweep against the current logical clock, optionally recording a free-text `note`. The sweep stamps `run_at` from the clock and `executed_by_officer_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and it performs four passes. Its three integer tallies — `overdue_marked_count`, `bonds_expired_count`, and `general_order_count` — each begin the run at 0, `overdue_movement_ids` begins as an empty array, and `note` is null when omitted. A sweep over a quiet system is perfectly legal and records a run with all three counters at 0 and an empty `overdue_movement_ids`.

First, every movement whose `transit_status` is `IN_TRANSIT` and whose `transit_deadline_utc` now lies strictly before the clock moves `IN_TRANSIT → OVERDUE`. Its `overdue_days` is computed against the current clock, the owning carrier's `overdue_movement_count` is recomputed, the sweep's `overdue_marked_count` is increased by one, and the movement's identifier is appended to the sweep's `overdue_movement_ids` — a list recorded in processing order, which is `created_at` ascending.

Second, every bond whose `expiry_date` now lies strictly before the clock moves to `EXPIRED`, from `ACTIVE`, `SATURATED`, or `SUSPENDED` alike, and the sweep's `bonds_expired_count` is increased by one for each. An expired bond blocks new filings against it.

Third, every movement whose `transit_status` is `ARRIVED`, whose `go_deadline_utc` now lies at or before the clock, and whose destination port has `go_warehouse_available` true has its `custody_status` driven to `GENERAL_ORDER` — from `BONDED` or from `EXAM_HOLD` alike — and the sweep's `general_order_count` is increased by one for each. Going to general order does **not** release the bond obligation: the merchandise remains under bond in the general-order warehouse. Open examination holds on such a movement stay `OPEN` and the port's `open_hold_count` is unchanged. `transit_status` remains `ARRIVED` throughout.

Fourth, every carrier's `sanction_points` is recomputed with the decay buckets valued at the current clock, which can carry a carrier across either of its two thresholds in either direction.

**The overdue-day measure.** Wherever this document speaks of whole days past a deadline, one shared measure applies. If the reference instant falls at or before the deadline, the measure is exactly 0 — including the boundary case where the two instants are identical. Otherwise the measure is the number of seconds from the deadline to the reference instant divided by 86,400 and rounded **up** to the next whole number, so any fraction of a day counts as a full overdue day and an interval of exactly seven days measures 7. This measure produces the movement's `overdue_days` during a sweep, with the sweep's clock as the reference instant, and it produces a claim's `overdue_days_at_issue` at issuance.

### 3.7. Liquidated Damages, Mitigation Petitions, and Sanction Posture

When a bonded carrier fails an obligation, the government charges the bond. A `CBPOfficer` issues a liquidated-damages claim against a `movement_id` with a `claim_type` and an optional `reason_code`. Five violations can be claimed: `LATE_ARRIVAL`, `NON_ARRIVAL`, `SEAL_VIOLATION`, `UNAUTHORIZED_DIVERSION`, and `NO_PROOF_OF_EXPORT`.

**Every claim needs a witness.** Officers cannot manufacture violations; the movement must actually exhibit the condition claimed, or the issuance is refused with `ERR_NO_VIOLATION_TO_CLAIM`. For `LATE_ARRIVAL`, the movement's `arrival_reported_at` must be non-null and must fall strictly after `transit_deadline_utc`. For `NON_ARRIVAL`, `transit_status` must be `OVERDUE` and `arrival_reported_at` must still be null. For `NO_PROOF_OF_EXPORT`, an `EXPORT` report on the movement must be sitting at `PENDING_PROOF` and the logical clock must stand strictly after `transit_deadline_utc`. For `SEAL_VIOLATION`, a seal event of type `BROKEN_UNAUTHORIZED` must exist on the movement. For `UNAUTHORIZED_DIVERSION`, the movement's `diversion_flag` must be true and no `APPROVED` `DIVERSION` amendment may exist on it. Separately, a claim of the same `claim_type` must not already be live on the same movement — live meaning `status` in `ISSUED`, `PETITIONED`, or `MITIGATED` — and a duplicate is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM`. A `PAID` or `CANCELLED` claim of the same type does not block a fresh one.

**How much is assessed.** Issuance computes `overdue_days_at_issue` and `assessed_amount` together. First the reference instant for the overdue measure is chosen by claim type: for `LATE_ARRIVAL` it is the movement's `arrival_reported_at`; for `NON_ARRIVAL`, `NO_PROOF_OF_EXPORT`, `SEAL_VIOLATION`, and `UNAUTHORIZED_DIVERSION` it is the current logical clock. `overdue_days_at_issue` is then the shared overdue-day measure of §3.6 applied to the movement's `transit_deadline_utc` against that reference instant. Next a severity multiplier is selected by claim type: `LATE_ARRIVAL` carries 1.0, `NO_PROOF_OF_EXPORT` carries 2.0, `SEAL_VIOLATION` and `UNAUTHORIZED_DIVERSION` each carry 3.0, and `NON_ARRIVAL`, the gravest, carries 4.0. The chargeable day count is the greater of 1 and `overdue_days_at_issue`, so a violation with no overdue time still charges a single day. The raw assessment is the greater of a statutory minimum of 1,000.00 and the per-diem rate of 250.00 multiplied by the severity multiplied by the chargeable day count, that product rounded half-up to two decimal places. A cap is then taken as the lesser of the movement's `cargo_value` and the underwriting bond's `limit_amount`, and `assessed_amount` is the lesser of the raw assessment and that cap, rounded half-up to two decimal places. Because the cap frequently binds on low-value cargo, the severity multiplier is often invisible in the final figure — that is intended, not a defect.

An issued claim opens at `status` `ISSUED` with `bond_id` copied from the movement, `issued_by_officer_id` set to the issuing officer's `X-User-ID`, `issued_at` stamped from the logical clock, `mitigated_amount` null, `paid_amount` null, and `cancellation_reason` null. It appends an `INCREASE_LD` ledger entry for the positive `assessed_amount` with `reason_code` `CLAIM_ISSUED`, which recomputes the bond's `obligated_amount` and `available_amount` and may drive the bond to `SATURATED`. It also recomputes the carrier's `open_claim_count` and `sanction_points`, and it records on the claim, as `sanction_points_applied`, the undecayed severity points the claim contributes to that score.

**Sanction posture.** A carrier's `sanction_points` is a decayed violation score, recomputed on every claim issuance, every claim disposition, and every compliance sweep. The countable claims are those against the carrier's own movements whose `status` is `ISSUED`, `PETITIONED`, `MITIGATED`, or `PAID`; `CANCELLED` claims are excluded entirely. Each countable claim contributes a type weight multiplied by an age-bucket decay factor. The type weights are 2 for `LATE_ARRIVAL`, 3 for `NO_PROOF_OF_EXPORT`, 4 for `SEAL_VIOLATION`, 4 for `UNAUTHORIZED_DIVERSION`, and 6 for `NON_ARRIVAL`. The age of a claim is the number of seconds from its `issued_at` to the current logical clock divided by 86,400 and rounded **down** to a whole number of days. A claim aged 90 days or fewer decays not at all and counts at full weight; one aged more than 90 but no more than 180 days counts at three quarters of its weight; one aged more than 180 but no more than 365 days counts at half; and one aged more than 365 days counts for nothing at all. The weighted contributions are summed, the sum is rounded half-up to **one** decimal place, and `sanction_points` is the lesser of that rounded figure and a ceiling of 12.0.

Two thresholds read off this one number. At 6.0 or above, the **transit throttle** of §3.2 halves the earned transit window on every new filing. At 10.0 or above, the carrier's `custodial_status` is automatically driven `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` recorded as `SANCTION_POINTS_THRESHOLD`. Suspension cascades: every movement of that carrier whose `transit_status` is `FILED` transitions `FILED → CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` set to `CARRIER_SUSPENSION_CASCADE`, processed in `created_at` ascending order so the resulting ledger sequence is deterministic, and each cancellation appends its own `RELEASE` ledger entry for the negative of that movement's `obligation_amount` with `reason_code` `CARRIER_SUSPENSION_CASCADE`, which in turn recomputes that bond's `obligated_amount`, `available_amount`, and `open_movement_count` and re-derives its `status`. Movements already at `IN_TRANSIT`, `OVERDUE`, `ARRIVED`, or `EXPORTED` are left entirely untouched — the cascade reaches only freight that has not yet moved. The same `FILED → CANCELLED` cascade, with the same ledger releases and bond re-derivation, fires when an officer revokes a carrier's privileges. If a later recomputation brings the score back strictly below 10.0 — which happens when a claim is cancelled and drops out of the countable set — the carrier returns `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` and `suspension_reason` is cleared to null, but movements the cascade already cancelled are **never** reinstated.

**Petitioning for mitigation.** A carrier may petition against a claim that is still `ISSUED`, naming the `claim_id`, asserting between 1 and 5 distinct `mitigation_factors`, supplying a `narrative_reference` pointing at the petition narrative document, and carrying the mandatory `expected_claim_version` of §2.9. The valid factor codes are `FIRST_VIOLATION`, `CARGO_SECURED_INTACT`, `PROMPT_ARRIVAL_REPORT`, `CBP_ADMIN_DELAY`, and `EXTRAORDINARY_CIRCUMSTANCE`. An empty list, or a list containing the same code twice, is rejected with `ERR_MITIGATION_FACTOR_INVALID` rather than silently de-duplicated, so credit can never be double counted. Petitioning a claim that is not `ISSUED` is `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`. A petition's `status` ranges over `SUBMITTED`, `SUPPLEMENT_REQUESTED`, `GRANTED`, `PARTIALLY_GRANTED`, `DENIED`, and `SUPERSEDED`, of which the last four are terminal; a claim's `status` ranges over `ISSUED`, `PETITIONED`, `MITIGATED`, `PAID`, and `CANCELLED`, of which `PAID` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. Filing a petition opens it at `status` `SUBMITTED` and `round` 1, records `filed_by_user_id` as the filing carrier's `X-User-ID`, leaves `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_reason_code` null, drives the claim `ISSUED → PETITIONED`, increments the claim's `version`, and moves any earlier petition on the same claim still in `SUBMITTED` or `SUPPLEMENT_REQUESTED` to `SUPERSEDED` with `decision_reason_code` `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEWER_PETITION`.

**What the petition earns.** The petition's offer is composed in six ordered steps, and the order is normative because a later step never re-runs an earlier one. Step one accumulates credit by walking the factor codes in the fixed order `FIRST_VIOLATION`, `CARGO_SECURED_INTACT`, `PROMPT_ARRIVAL_REPORT`, `CBP_ADMIN_DELAY`, `EXTRAORDINARY_CIRCUMSTANCE` and adding, for each code the petition asserts, its credit: twenty hundredths for `FIRST_VIOLATION`, fifteen hundredths for `CARGO_SECURED_INTACT`, ten hundredths for `PROMPT_ARRIVAL_REPORT`, twenty-five hundredths for `CBP_ADMIN_DELAY`, and thirty hundredths for `EXTRAORDINARY_CIRCUMSTANCE`. Step two debits prior offences: the accumulated credit is reduced by five hundredths for each prior violation counted in `prior_violation_count`, and the result is floored at zero so the debit can never turn negative. `prior_violation_count` is snapshotted onto the petition at filing time as the number of liquidated-damages claims recorded against **other** movements of the same carrier whose `status` is not `CANCELLED` — that is, one of `ISSUED`, `PETITIONED`, `MITIGATED`, or `PAID` — and whose `issued_at` falls within the 365 days before this petition. Step three applies a round-dependent cap: fifty hundredths when the petition's `round` is 1, seventy-five hundredths when it is 2. `mitigation_pct` is the lesser of the debited credit and that cap. Step four computes `net_before_fee` as the claim's `assessed_amount` multiplied by one minus `mitigation_pct`, rounded half-up to two decimal places. Step five selects `processing_fee` from a tier chosen on `net_before_fee` **only**: a net strictly below 2,000.00 draws a flat fee of 100.00; a net of at least 2,000.00 but strictly below 10,000.00 draws a flat fee of 250.00; a net of 10,000.00 or more draws two and a half hundredths of the net, rounded half-up to two decimal places. The tier is never re-selected after the fee has changed the number. Step six sets `offer_amount` to the lesser of the claim's `assessed_amount` and the sum of `net_before_fee` and `processing_fee`, rounded half-up to two decimal places, so an offer can never exceed the gross it mitigates.

**Deciding the petition.** An `FPFOfficer` other than the claim's issuing officer decides a `SUBMITTED` petition with a `decision` and a `decision_reason_code`. Only a `SUBMITTED` petition may be decided — anything else is `ERR_PETITION_INVALID_STATE` — and the underlying claim must still be `PETITIONED`, or the decision is `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`. Four decisions exist and they are symmetric in every respect that matters:

- `GRANT` moves the petition `SUBMITTED → GRANTED` and the claim `PETITIONED → MITIGATED`, setting the claim's `mitigated_amount` to the petition's `offer_amount`.
- `PARTIAL_GRANT` moves the petition `SUBMITTED → PARTIALLY_GRANTED` and the claim `PETITIONED → MITIGATED`, setting `mitigated_amount` to the claim's `assessed_amount` less half of the difference between `assessed_amount` and `offer_amount`, rounded half-up to two decimal places — the officer splits the relief the petitioner asked for down the middle.
- `DENY` moves the petition `SUBMITTED → DENIED` and cycles the claim `PETITIONED → ISSUED` with no ledger movement at all, leaving the full `assessed_amount` obligated. Because the claim is `ISSUED` again, a fresh petition may then be filed against it.
- `REQUEST_SUPPLEMENT` moves the petition `SUBMITTED → SUPPLEMENT_REQUESTED` and leaves the claim at `PETITIONED`. It is permitted only while the petition's `round` is still below 2; once round 2 has been reached, the request is refused with `ERR_PETITION_ROUND_LIMIT`.

Both `GRANT` and `PARTIAL_GRANT` append a `MITIGATE` ledger entry whose `amount` is the negative of the difference between the claim's `assessed_amount` and its newly set `mitigated_amount`, with `reason_code` `PETITION_GRANTED`, and both then recompute the bond's `obligated_amount` and `available_amount` and re-derive its `status`. Mitigation does **not** change the carrier's `sanction_points`: the violation happened, and reducing what is payable does not un-happen it.

**Supplementing a petition.** When an officer has asked for more, the carrier supplements the petition with a fresh set of `mitigation_factors` and a fresh `narrative_reference`. The petition must be sitting at `SUPPLEMENT_REQUESTED` — anything else is `ERR_PETITION_INVALID_STATE` — the acting user must be the claim's carrier, and the petition's `round` must still be below 2, or the supplement is refused with `ERR_PETITION_ROUND_LIMIT`. The supplied `mitigation_factors` are validated exactly as on the original filing — between 1 and 5 codes, all drawn from the five valid codes, all distinct — with the same `ERR_MITIGATION_FACTOR_INVALID` on an empty or repeating list. An accepted supplement increases `round` by one, replaces `mitigation_factors` and `narrative_reference` with the supplied values, moves the petition `SUPPLEMENT_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, and recomputes `mitigation_pct`, `net_before_fee`, `processing_fee`, and `offer_amount` under the round-2 cap of seventy-five hundredths. Because the cap is the only thing that changes, the same factors against the same gross yield a strictly smaller offer in round 2 than in round 1 whenever the accumulated credit already exceeded fifty hundredths — the second bite is genuinely more generous to the petitioner. The cycle from `SUBMITTED` to `SUPPLEMENT_REQUESTED` and back is capped at round 2. A petition sitting in either `SUBMITTED` or `SUPPLEMENT_REQUESTED` moves to `SUPERSEDED` when a newer petition is filed on the same claim, with `decision_reason_code` `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEWER_PETITION`, or when the claim itself is cancelled, with `decision_reason_code` `CLAIM_CANCELLED`.

**Disposing of a claim.** Two dispositions close a claim out, and the `action` field selects between them. `PAY` is the carrier's action and requires the claim to be `ISSUED` or `MITIGATED`; the `paid_amount` supplied must equal exactly what is currently owed — `mitigated_amount` when the claim is `MITIGATED`, otherwise `assessed_amount` — because partial payments are not accepted, and a mismatch is `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. Payment records `paid_amount`, moves the claim to `PAID`, and appends a `RELEASE_LD` ledger entry with `reason_code` `CLAIM_PAID` whose `amount` is the negative of the amount that remained obligated — the full `assessed_amount` when the claim was `ISSUED`, or the `mitigated_amount` when it was `MITIGATED` and a `MITIGATE` entry had already released the difference — so that the claim's entries sum to exactly 0.00. The bond's `obligated_amount`, `available_amount`, and `status` are then re-derived. A paid violation still counts against the carrier: `sanction_points` is unchanged by payment.

`CANCEL` is the `FPFOfficer`'s action, barred to the officer who issued the claim, and requires the claim to be `ISSUED`, `PETITIONED`, or `MITIGATED`; any other status is `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`. Cancellation records the supplied `reason_code` in `cancellation_reason`, moves the claim to `CANCELLED`, and appends a `RELEASE_LD` ledger entry with `reason_code` `CLAIM_CANCELLED` whose `amount` is the negative of whatever remained obligated on the claim — the full `assessed_amount` when no mitigation had been recorded, or the `mitigated_amount` when a `MITIGATE` entry had already released the difference — so that the net of all ledger entries carrying that `claim_id` becomes exactly 0.00. The bond amounts and `status` are re-derived. Cancellation then recomputes the carrier's `sanction_points` with this claim excluded from the countable set, and when the result falls strictly below 10.0 the carrier transitions `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` with `suspension_reason` cleared. Finally, any petition on the cancelled claim still sitting in `SUBMITTED` or `SUPPLEMENT_REQUESTED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`.

Claims are readable individually and as a filtered list, and the petitions filed against a claim are readable in `round` ascending order.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

The API is mounted under `/in-bond`, with the single exception of the logical-clock capability. Operations that bring a new resource into existence — registering a carrier, bond, or port; filing a movement; recording a seal event; placing an examination hold; submitting an amendment request; filing a report, a claim, or a petition; running a compliance sweep — return `201 Created`.
