# Multiemployer Taft-Hartley Fund API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A multiemployer benefit fund is a jointly-trusteed trust: dozens or hundreds of signatory employers remit contributions under a collective bargaining agreement, and a board of trustees — half appointed by the union, half by the employer association — administers the money on behalf of the participants who earned it. The administrative burden is unusual because a single act of bookkeeping propagates in every direction at once. When an employer files one month of hours, those hours price themselves against a bargained contribution rate, buy welfare coverage months for each named worker, accrue pension credit and vesting service in the appropriate plan year, and change what that employer owes the trust. When the same employer files an amendment two months later, the entire chain has to unwind and re-run. Fund offices today reconcile this by hand, in spreadsheets, and the reconciliation is where the errors live.

This document specifies the API for a third-party administrator's fund system. It covers the whole administrative surface: contribution report intake and hours crediting, delinquency identification and the interest and liquidated-damages charges that follow, the payroll audit program that recovers unreported hours, participant eligibility through banked hours and purchased coverage months, pension credit accrual and vesting service, complete-withdrawal liability and its installment schedule, trustee governance under the equal-representation requirement of the Taft-Hartley Act, and reciprocity with sister funds for travelling members. Two design constraints run through all of it. First, money is never approximated: every cent figure is an integer number of cents, every rounding step is pinned to a specific place in a specific order, and an implementation that rounds at a different moment produces a different — and wrong — answer. Second, authority is deliberately fragmented: the auditor who issues findings cannot close them, the actuary who certifies a liability cannot demand payment, the trustee who moves a motion cannot vote on it, and neither bargaining party can outvote the other.

Readers should treat the workflows in section 3 as the specification of record. Section 4 lists the HTTP surface but deliberately omits request and response shapes; the fields that make up each call are introduced in the narrative where the business reason for them appears.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller acts. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five roles below spelled exactly as shown, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well-formed but the named role is not among the roles the target endpoint authorizes, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `FundAdministrator`, `ContributingEmployer`, `Trustee`, `PayrollAuditor`, and `Actuary`.

`FundAdministrator` is third-party administrator staff who run intake, posting, delinquency identification, settlements, demands, tallies and reciprocity decisions on behalf of the joint board. When `X-User-Role` is `FundAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example, comparing it against a stored `certified_by_user_id` when segregation of duties is enforced) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same is true of `PayrollAuditor` — an independent payroll auditor engaged by the board, who opens audits and issues findings but may never finalize them — and of `Actuary`, the plan's enrolled actuary, who certifies withdrawal-liability allocations against the unfunded-vested-benefit pool but may never issue the demand. For both of those roles, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier that is recorded (as `auditor_user_id` on a payroll audit, as `certified_by_user_id` on a withdrawal assessment) but never resolved against an entity's `id`.

Two roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `ContributingEmployer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Employer`; ownership checks on any resource that references an employer compare that resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a contribution report or a payroll audit or a withdrawal assessment is owned by the employer named in its `employer_id`, and a payment installment is owned by the employer named on the withdrawal assessment the installment belongs to. When `X-User-Role` is `Trustee`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Trustee` row; a vote is owned by the trustee whose `trustee_id` it carries, and a motion records its mover in `moved_by_trustee_id` taken from the same header. An entity-backed actor who targets a resource belonging to a different employer or a different trustee is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This ownership check applies to filing a contribution report, remitting against one, responding to payroll-audit findings, requesting review of a withdrawal assessment, and paying an installment.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — and, because the logical clock stands still between calls, rows that share a `created_at` instant are returned in the order they were inserted — unless a workflow in section 3 pins a different order for a particular computation. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` as they were applied, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filter before the window was taken, and `has_more`, which is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps, and the Version Counter

Every resource is created with a server-minted `id`: a UUID string carrying no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recorded from the logical clock at the moment the row was created.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation of the row — and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations that arrive as a side effect of an operation performed on some other resource. Two resource types are the exception: contribution lines and motion votes are immutable ledger rows, and they carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on a contribution line or on a motion vote, and neither should appear in their serialized form.

The `version` counter is not decorative. Several workflows described below explicitly bump the `version` of a parent resource — seating a trustee bumps the fund, filing a contribution report bumps the employer, certifying a withdrawal assessment bumps the fund — and the stale-parent rejection described in section 2.7 is built on exactly those bumps.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The server keeps a logical clock rather than reading wall-clock time. Before any client has set it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. All date arithmetic in this specification — due dates, days late, cure windows, coverage-month comparisons, installment due dates, plan-year elapse — reads the logical clock and nothing else.

An administrator may advance or rewind the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the clock's new value as `current_utc`. This capability exists so that lateness, cure periods and plan-year rollover can be exercised deterministically.

### 2.6. Money, Hours, and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is an integer number of cents. There are no floating-point currency amounts anywhere in the API. Two rates are expressed in finer units so that bargained figures survive intact: an employer's contribution rate is quoted in millicents (thousandths of a cent) per credited hour, and the fund's interest and liquidated-damages rates are quoted in basis points (hundredths of a percent).

All hour figures carry exactly two decimal places. Wherever this document says a value is rounded, the rounding is *round half up* to the stated number of decimal places — a value exactly halfway between two representable results always moves away from zero. Bankers' rounding is never used anywhere in this system.

The *order* in which rounding is applied is part of the contract, not an implementation detail. Where a total is described as "rounded per item and then summed", summing unrounded item values and rounding the total afterwards is wrong and will differ by whole cents on realistic inputs. Where a fraction is described as being rounded to ten decimal places before it multiplies a pool, multiplying by the unrounded fraction is likewise wrong. Each such ordering is stated explicitly at the point in section 3 where the calculation is described, and each is binding.

### 2.7. Stale-Parent Rejection on Intake

Two intake operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the `version` of a parent resource. In both cases the version field is a required input, not an optional one; omitting it is a malformed request under `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A `POST /contribution-reports` request must carry `expected_employer_version`, holding the `version` of the employer as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the employer's current `version`, the filing is rejected with `ERR_STALE_EMPLOYER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The employer row carries the contribution rate and the delinquency ledger from which the new report's line amounts are computed, so a filing built on a stale employer view would post cents derived from a rate or a ledger the fund has since replaced.

A `POST /withdrawal-assessments` request must carry `expected_fund_version`, holding the `version` of the fund as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the fund's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FUND_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Each certification draws a net liability out of the fund's unallocated unfunded-vested-benefit pool, so an allocation computed from a stale pool would double-allocate benefits another assessment has already claimed.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Requests to endpoints not named above must not be rejected for concurrency reasons.

### 2.8. Order of Rejection

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is determined by a fixed evaluation ladder. Checks are evaluated in the order below and the **first** failure wins; later checks are never reached.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the five declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorization.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized list — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
3. **Resource ownership.** For entity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` owns the target — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This step applies to `POST /contribution-reports`, `POST /contribution-reports/{id}/remit`, `POST /payroll-audits/{id}/respond`, `POST /withdrawal-assessments/{id}/review-request`, and `POST /payment-installments/{id}/pay`.
4. **Path existence.** Every identifier appearing in the URL path resolves to an existing row, validated strictly left to right, so that the leftmost unresolvable segment determines the error. The applicable codes are `ERR_FUND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EMPLOYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COVERAGE_MONTH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WITHDRAWAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSTALLMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MOTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VOTE_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND`, each at HTTP 404.
5. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to an existing row — `fund_id`, `employer_id`, `participant_id`, `sister_fund_id`, `motion_id`, `amends_report_id`, `controlled_group_parent_employer_id`, each `participant_id` inside a `findings` entry, and each `participant_id` inside a `lines` entry. A dangling reference yields the matching 404 code from the list above.
6. **Request shape.** Required fields present, no field the endpoint does not itself define, types correct, enum members valid, every month key matching a four-digit year, a hyphen, and a two-digit month between 01 and 12, both prior-year arrays holding exactly five entries, hours values at least 0 with at most two decimal places, and every cents field a non-negative integer — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
7. **Stale parent version.** `expected_employer_version` on report intake and `expected_fund_version` on withdrawal-assessment intake, as described in section 2.7 — otherwise `ERR_STALE_EMPLOYER_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_FUND_VERSION` (412).
8. **From-state.** The target row currently sits in a state from which the requested transition is declared — otherwise `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_POSTED`, `ERR_AUDIT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_WITHDRAWAL_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_MOTION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE`, or `ERR_ASSESSMENT_INVALID_STATE` (409), whichever matches the target resource.
9. **Payment-axis preconditions.** A report whose filing state is `SUPERSEDED` accepts no remittance (`ERR_REPORT_NOT_REMITTABLE`), and an installment that is already `PAID` or `DEFAULTED`, or one that is `MISSED` and whose cure window has closed, accepts no payment (`ERR_INSTALLMENT_NOT_PAYABLE`); both at HTTP 409.
10. **Governance and segregation of duties.** The set of Taft-Hartley safety checks described throughout section 3: the contributing employer is not withdrawn (`ERR_EMPLOYER_WITHDRAWN`), a carried audit-settlement motion exists before an audit is finalized (`ERR_BOARD_APPROVAL_REQUIRED`), the finalizing administrator is not the audit's own auditor and the demanding administrator is not the certifying actuary (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), the moving trustee does not vote on their own motion (`ERR_MOVER_CANNOT_VOTE`), the acting trustee's seat is active (`ERR_TRUSTEE_SEAT_INACTIVE`), the fund's governance posture is open before a motion may be moved (`ERR_FUND_GOVERNANCE_NOT_OPEN`) and not frozen before a demand may issue (`ERR_FUND_GOVERNANCE_FROZEN`), and the sister fund's reciprocity agreement is executed before a transfer may be accepted (`ERR_RECIPROCITY_AGREEMENT_NOT_EXECUTED`); all at HTTP 409. These guard the filing of a contribution report, the finalizing of a payroll audit, the issuing of a withdrawal demand, the moving of a board motion, the casting of a motion vote, and the deciding of a reciprocity transfer.
11. **Temporal geometry.** Audit period overlap (`ERR_AUDIT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`), an assessment demanded on a report that is not yet late (`ERR_REPORT_NOT_DELINQUENT`), and a reciprocity acceptance that would exceed the participant's advance-coverage horizon (`ERR_COVERAGE_HORIZON_EXCEEDED`); all at HTTP 409, guarding audit creation, delinquency assessment creation, and reciprocity decisions respectively.
12. **Cross-entity linkage.** Every reported or audited participant belongs to the employer's fund (`ERR_PARTICIPANT_FUND_MISMATCH`), a voting trustee belongs to the motion's fund (`ERR_TRUSTEE_FUND_MISMATCH`), a reciprocity source is a genuine sister fund and not the participant's own (`ERR_SISTER_FUND_INVALID`), and a motion's subject reference resolves to a row of the type its subject demands (`ERR_MOTION_SUBJECT_INVALID`); all at HTTP 409, guarding contribution report filing, audit findings, motion votes, reciprocity transfer creation, and board motion creation.
13. **Uniqueness and chain rules.** Duplicate work period (`ERR_DUPLICATE_WORK_PERIOD`), invalid amendment target (`ERR_AMENDMENT_TARGET_INVALID`), duplicate vote (`ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE`), empty report (`ERR_EMPTY_REPORT`), and audit round limit reached (`ERR_AUDIT_ROUND_LIMIT_REACHED`); all at HTTP 409, guarding contribution report filing, motion votes, and audit responses.
14. **Quorum.** Each bargaining party has cast enough counting votes for a tally to resolve — otherwise `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET` (409).
15. **Money bounds.** `ERR_REMITTANCE_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_EXCEEDS_ASSESSMENT`, `ERR_INSTALLMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, `ERR_NO_DELINQUENT_PRINCIPAL`, `ERR_UVB_POOL_EXHAUSTED`, and `ERR_NO_PAYMENT_BASE`, all at HTTP 422, guarding remittance, settlement, installment payment, delinquency assessment creation, and withdrawal assessment creation.

The practical consequence worth internalizing: a governance failure surfaces before a money-bound failure, and a from-state failure surfaces before either. An implementation that checks the cheapest condition first will return the wrong code on requests that break several rules at once.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline

Several operations in this system mutate more than one resource. When they do, every downstream figure named in the workflow is recomputed inside the same request, before the response is written, and every mutated row has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented. There is no deferred settlement, no background job, and no eventual consistency: a read issued immediately after a write observes the fully-cascaded state.

Recomputed aggregates are always derived from scratch from their inputs rather than adjusted incrementally, with one deliberate exception described in the withdrawal-review workflow, where the prior allocation is explicitly returned to the pool before the new one is charged against it. Where a workflow below enumerates its cascade as a sequence of hops, the hops describe causation, not scheduling; all of them complete within the request.

### 2.10. Resource Collections and Path Resolution

Every resource in this system is addressed at its own top-level collection. Parent-child relationships are expressed through foreign-key fields carried in request bodies, never through nesting in the URL. Where a path does contain two segments — a participant's accrual rows, a report's lines, an assessment's installments — the first segment identifies the owner and the second names a filtered view of a top-level collection; the owner is still resolved first, left to right, and a missing owner yields the owner's own not-found code rather than the child's.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Chartering the Trust and Registering Its Constituents

Everything in this system hangs off a fund. An administrator charters one by supplying its legal `name` and the trust's `ein`, together with the whole parameter set the trust agreement fixes: `interest_rate_annual_bps` and `ld_rate_bps`, both quoted in basis points and both at least 0; `ld_floor_cents`, the minimum liquidated-damages charge the trust's own damages schedule imposes, at least 0; `grace_days`, the number of days after a report's due date before the fund treats it as delinquent, at least 0; `hours_per_coverage_month`, the credited hours that purchase one month of welfare coverage, at least 1; `bank_cap_hours`, the ceiling on hours a participant may retain in an hour bank, at least 0 and carrying at most two decimals; `max_advance_coverage_months`, the number of granted coverage months a participant may hold beyond the current clock month, at least 1; `eligibility_lag_months`, the number of months between a work period and the first coverage month those hours can buy, at least 0; `accrual_method`, which is either `HOURS_BASED` or `ELAPSED_TIME` and selects the pension accrual variant for every participant of this fund; and `pool_prior_year_contributions_cents`, an array of exactly five non-negative integers, oldest year first, giving all-employer contributions for the five plan years preceding a withdrawal. Three inputs are optional. `fund_role` defaults to `HOME` and may be set to `SISTER`; a `HOME` fund is one this API administers, while `SISTER` rows exist only as reciprocity counterparties. `plan_year_start_month` defaults to 1 and must be a calendar month from 1 through 12. `total_uvb_cents` defaults to 0 and holds the unfunded vested benefit pool available for allocation to withdrawing employers.

A newly chartered fund is not yet governable. Because no trustees have been seated, its `governance_posture` initialises to `FROZEN`, `active_union_trustees` and `active_employer_trustees` are both 0, and `suspended_employer_count`, `reciprocal_receipts_cents`, `allocated_uvb_cents` and `collections_run_index` all start at 0. `unallocated_uvb_cents` starts equal to `total_uvb_cents`, and `reciprocity_agreement_status` starts at `NONE`. Whenever a fund is read, the server recomputes `governance_posture`, `active_union_trustees`, `active_employer_trustees` and `unallocated_uvb_cents` before serialising the row, so a fund read is always current with respect to trustee seats and the pool split. `unallocated_uvb_cents` is always the fund's `total_uvb_cents` less its `allocated_uvb_cents`, and `allocated_uvb_cents` is the sum of the net liability of every withdrawal assessment against this fund's employers whose status is `CERTIFIED`, `DEMANDED`, `REVIEW_REQUESTED`, `PAYMENT_ACTIVE`, `ACCELERATED` or `SATISFIED`. Assessments still in `DRAFT` contribute nothing.

Signatory employers are registered against a fund. An employer registration carries `fund_id`, the employer's `legal_name` and `ein`, its bargained `contribution_rate_millicents_per_hour` — the rate in thousandths of a cent per credited hour, which must be at least 1 — and two five-element arrays used later for withdrawal liability: `prior_year_contributions_cents`, the employer's own contributions for the five plan years preceding a withdrawal, and `prior_year_base_units`, the employer's credited hours for those same five years. Both arrays are oldest-year-first and index-aligned with the fund's pool array, and both must hold exactly five non-negative integers. An employer that is a subsidiary in a controlled group may name its parent through the optional `controlled_group_parent_employer_id`, which is a reference to another employer row and defaults to null. A freshly registered employer opens with `status` `ACTIVE`, `delinquency_strikes` at 0 and `cumulative_delinquent_cents` at 0. Employer listings may be filtered by `fund_id`, by `controlled_group_parent_employer_id`, and by `status`.

Participants are enrolled with `fund_id`, `full_name` and `ssn_last4`, the last four digits of the participant's Social Security number, which must be exactly four digits. Every roll-up on a new participant opens empty: `credited_hours_total`, `reciprocal_hours_total`, `hour_bank_hours`, `forfeited_hours_total`, `vesting_service_years` and `total_pension_credits` are all 0, `consecutive_break_years` is 0, `vested` is false, `coverage_through_month` is null, and `status` is `ACTIVE`. A participant's `eligibility_status` is never stored as a decision made at write time — it is derived every time the participant is read, and reads `ELIGIBLE` exactly when `coverage_through_month` is non-null and is not earlier than the logical clock's own year-and-month key, and `NOT_ELIGIBLE` otherwise. That derivation means a participant who was eligible last month can read as `NOT_ELIGIBLE` today without any write having occurred, simply because the clock moved. Participant listings may be filtered by `fund_id`, by `eligibility_status`, and by `status`.

### 3.2. The Joint Board, Equal Representation, and Motions

A trustee is seated by supplying the `fund_id` of the board they join, their `full_name`, the `appointing_party` that appointed them — either `UNION` or `EMPLOYER` — and `term_expires_on`, the ISO date on which the appointment lapses. An optional `seat_status` defaults to `ACTIVE` and may instead be `RESIGNED`. Seating a trustee immediately recomputes the fund's governance figures and increments the fund's `version` by 1, which matters because that same counter guards withdrawal-assessment intake.

Governance posture is the fund's answer to a single question: can this board act? Count the fund's trustees whose `seat_status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `appointing_party` is `UNION`, and store that count as `active_union_trustees`; count the active `EMPLOYER`-appointed trustees the same way into `active_employer_trustees`. Seats marked `RESIGNED` never count toward either. If either count is 0, `governance_posture` is `FROZEN` — a board missing a bargaining party entirely cannot act at all. Otherwise, if the two counts differ from one another, or if the smaller of the two counts is below two trustees per party, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Only when both counts are equal and each is at least two is the posture `OPEN`. The boundary is worth stating plainly: one active union trustee facing one active employer trustee is `RESTRICTED`, because two per party is the floor; two facing two is `OPEN`. Trustee listings for a fund may be filtered by `seat_status` and by `appointing_party`.

Motions are how the board exercises authority over things this system tracks. A trustee moves a motion by supplying the `fund_id` of the board, a `subject_type` — one of `LD_WAIVER`, `AUDIT_SETTLEMENT`, or `RECIPROCITY_AGREEMENT` — and a `subject_ref_id`, which is a polymorphic reference resolved according to the subject: a delinquency assessment for `LD_WAIVER`, a payroll audit for `AUDIT_SETTLEMENT`, and a fund whose `fund_role` is `SISTER` for `RECIPROCITY_AGREEMENT`. A reference that does not resolve to a row of the type its subject demands is rejected with `ERR_MOTION_SUBJECT_INVALID`, a check that runs after the seat and posture guards described next. The mover's identity is taken from the request headers and recorded as `moved_by_trustee_id` — an `X-User-ID` that resolves to no trustee row at all is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403) before any governance guard is reached, on this endpoint and on vote casting alike; a trustee whose seat is `RESIGNED` may not move a motion and is turned away with `ERR_TRUSTEE_SEAT_INACTIVE`, and no motion at all may be moved unless the fund's posture is `OPEN`, which means a `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` board is refused with `ERR_FUND_GOVERNANCE_NOT_OPEN`. The new motion opens in `OPEN` status with `union_bloc_position`, `employer_bloc_position`, `tallied_on` and `arbitration_award` all null, and the act of moving it bars the mover from voting on it. Motion listings may be filtered by `fund_id`, `status`, `subject_type`, `subject_ref_id`, and `moved_by_trustee_id`.

Voting is one trustee, one vote, one motion. A vote carries the `motion_id` and a `position` of `FOR`, `AGAINST`, or `ABSTAIN`; the `trustee_id` is taken from the acting trustee's identity rather than the payload. The guards are evaluated in the order section 2.8's ladder fixes rather than the order they read most naturally: the motion must still be `OPEN` (`ERR_MOTION_INVALID_STATE`), then the trustee must not be the motion's own mover (`ERR_MOVER_CANNOT_VOTE`), then the trustee's seat must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_TRUSTEE_SEAT_INACTIVE`), then the voting trustee must sit on the same fund's board as the motion (`ERR_TRUSTEE_FUND_MISMATCH`), and last the trustee must not already have voted on this motion (`ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE`). A vote row is an immutable record: like contribution lines, it carries only `id` and `created_at` and has no `updated_at` and no `version`. Vote listings may be filtered by `motion_id`, by `trustee_id`, and by `position`.

Tallying is where Taft-Hartley equal representation becomes arithmetic. Each bargaining party votes as a single bloc, so a three-to-one union majority does not outweigh a two-to-nothing employer bloc. For each party in turn, gather the votes cast on this motion by trustees whose `appointing_party` is that party, count how many are `FOR` and how many are `AGAINST`, and add those two counts together to get the party's counting votes — an `ABSTAIN` never counts toward anything. If either party has contributed fewer than two counting votes, the tally is refused outright with `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET` and the motion stays `OPEN`. Otherwise the party's bloc position is `FOR` when its `FOR` count exceeds its `AGAINST` count, `AGAINST` when its `AGAINST` count exceeds its `FOR` count, and `TIED` when the two counts are equal. Both positions are persisted, into `union_bloc_position` and `employer_bloc_position` respectively, and `tallied_on` records the logical clock's date. If both blocs read `FOR`, the motion becomes `CARRIED`. If both read `AGAINST`, it becomes `FAILED`. In every other case — the two blocs disagreeing, or either bloc landing on `TIED` — the motion becomes `DEADLOCKED`, because equal representation means disagreement is a deadlock rather than a loss. A tally that lands on `FAILED` or `DEADLOCKED` stamps `tallied_on` and the bloc positions but mutates no subject.

A deadlocked motion goes to a neutral arbitrator, whose award is recorded through the same tally capability by supplying an `arbitration_award` of either `CARRIED` or `FAILED`. An `arbitration_award` supplied while the motion is still `OPEN` is ignored and the ordinary bloc tally runs; a `DEADLOCKED` motion tallied without one is refused with `ERR_MOTION_INVALID_STATE`. Either award restamps `tallied_on` with the logical clock's date. An award of `CARRIED` records `arbitration_award` as `CARRIED`, moves the motion from `DEADLOCKED` to `ARBITRATION_CARRIED`, and fires exactly the same subject cascades a motion carried at the vote would have fired. An award of `FAILED` records `arbitration_award` as `FAILED`, moves the motion to `ARBITRATION_FAILED`, and mutates no subject. `CARRIED`, `FAILED`, `ARBITRATION_CARRIED` and `ARBITRATION_FAILED` are all terminal; a motion in any of them accepts no further tally and no further votes.

What a carried motion does depends entirely on its subject, and the `LD_WAIVER` case is the most consequential because it runs four hops deep. First, the referenced delinquency assessment moves to `WAIVED` — out of whatever status it currently holds — and its `waived_cents` is set to that assessment's own `total_cents`. Second, because a waived assessment leaves the employer's ledger sum entirely, the employer's `cumulative_delinquent_cents` is recomputed and falls by exactly the waived amount. Third, if that recomputation brings the ledger to 0 while the employer's `status` is `SUSPENDED`, the employer returns to `ACTIVE` and its `delinquency_strikes` resets to 0. Fourth, because an employer left suspension, the fund's `suspended_employer_count` is recomputed. A carried `RECIPROCITY_AGREEMENT` motion has a single effect: the referenced sister fund's `reciprocity_agreement_status` flips from `NONE` to `EXECUTED`, which is what unlocks the acceptance of transfers from that fund. A carried `AUDIT_SETTLEMENT` motion changes no field on the audit it names at all; its entire effect is to satisfy the board-approval precondition that a payroll audit must clear before it can be finalized.

### 3.3. Filing a Contribution Report and Pricing Its Hours

Each month, a signatory employer reports the hours its bargaining-unit workers performed and remits the contributions those hours generate. A filing carries the `employer_id` of the remitting employer, the `work_period` in which the hours were worked as a year-and-month key, the client's `expected_employer_version` as described in section 2.7, and a `lines` array holding one entry per worker. Every entry of `lines` supplies the `participant_id` of the worker, the `hours` credited to that worker in this work period as a non-negative number with at most two decimals, the `gross_wages_cents` paid to that worker — a non-negative integer that the fund retains purely so a later payroll audit has something to compare against — and a `work_classification` of `JOURNEYMAN`, `APPRENTICE`, or `FOREMAN`. An optional `amends_report_id` names an earlier report this filing is intended to supersede. Both an employer acting on its own behalf and a fund administrator acting on the employer's behalf may file.

Filing is refused in several ways. An employer whose `status` is `WITHDRAWN` may no longer file at all, and the attempt returns `ERR_EMPLOYER_WITHDRAWN`. Any worker named in a line who is enrolled in a different fund than the employer's own is rejected with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_FUND_MISMATCH`. A filing that carries no lines whatsoever is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_REPORT`, which is evaluated ahead of the duplicate-work-period and amendment-target rules that follow. An employer may hold at most one report per work period whose filing state is not `SUPERSEDED`, so filing a second report for a month that already has a live one, without naming an amendment target, is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_WORK_PERIOD`. When `amends_report_id` is supplied it must name a report whose filing state is `POSTED`, whose employer is this same employer, and whose work period is this same work period; any other target is refused with `ERR_AMENDMENT_TARGET_INVALID`.

A report that survives intake is stored with `origin` `EMPLOYER`, `filing_status` `SUBMITTED`, `payment_status` `UNPAID`, `amount_remitted_cents` at 0, and both `posted_at` and `paid_in_full_at` null. Its `due_date` is computed at intake as the twentieth day of the month following its `work_period` — a report for hours worked in a given month is due on the twentieth of the next one. Each entry of `lines` becomes a stored contribution line whose `report_id` is set by the server to the owning report; those line rows are immutable and carry only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version` of their own. Finally, filing recomputes the employer's `cumulative_delinquent_cents` — a recomputation that never lifts a suspension — and increments the employer's `version` by exactly 1, which is why a client that files two reports back to back must re-read the employer between them.

Pricing the hours is where the rounding order becomes load-bearing. Work through the report's contribution lines in ascending `created_at` order. For each line, take the line's `hours`, multiply by the employer's `contribution_rate_millicents_per_hour`, multiply again by the basis-point multiplier that the line's `work_classification` selects, and divide the product by ten million. That divisor is the millicent divisor: it converts thousandths of a cent and a basis-point multiplier back into whole cents in a single step. The multiplier is 10,000 basis points for a `JOURNEYMAN` line, which is to say the full bargained rate; 5,000 basis points for an `APPRENTICE` line, which is half the rate; and 12,500 basis points for a `FOREMAN` line, which is the rate and a quarter. Round that per-line result half up to a whole number of cents and store it as the line's `contribution_cents`. **Only then** sum the rounded line amounts to obtain the report's `total_contributions_due_cents`. Summing the unrounded line values and rounding the total afterwards is not an equivalent computation and will differ by whole cents on realistic filings. Separately, the report's `total_hours_reported` is the sum of the `hours` on its lines, rounded half up to two decimals. Because lines are immutable and belong to exactly one report, this computation reads every line of the report with no status filter of any kind, and it is re-evaluated when the report is posted.

Reports may be listed and filtered by `employer_id`, by `amends_report_id`, by `filing_status`, by `payment_status`, by `work_period`, and by `origin`; the lines of one report may be listed on their own, and a participant's lines across every report may be listed and narrowed with a `report_id` filter.

### 3.4. Posting, Supersession, and the Eligibility Replay

Hours do not count for anything until a fund administrator posts the report that carries them. Posting requires the report's `filing_status` to be `SUBMITTED` — a report already `POSTED` or already `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_POSTED` — requires that the employer has not since withdrawn (`ERR_EMPLOYER_WITHDRAWN`), and requires the report to carry at least one line (`ERR_EMPTY_REPORT`), in that order. If `amends_report_id` is non-null, the amendment target is re-verified at this moment: it must still exist, its filing state must be `POSTED`, its employer must match this report's employer, and its work period must match this report's work period.

The act of posting stamps `posted_at` with the logical clock instant, sets `filing_status` to `POSTED`, and increments the report's `version` by 1. From there the cascade runs four hops. The first hop applies only to amendments: if `amends_report_id` is non-null, the report it names moves from `POSTED` to `SUPERSEDED` and its own `version` increments by 1. Supersession is how an amendment displaces its predecessor — every downstream aggregate in this system reads contribution lines only from reports whose filing state is `POSTED`, so the superseded report's hours silently leave every total, every coverage grant and every accrual the moment its state changes. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal on the filing axis. The second hop replays welfare eligibility for every participant who appears on the newly posted report **or** on the report it just superseded — the union of both line sets, because a worker dropped by the amendment must lose the coverage the original filing bought. The third hop rewrites each replayed participant's `coverage_through_month` to the highest coverage month the replay granted, or to null when the replay granted none. The fourth hop is a consequence rather than a write: because `coverage_through_month` moved, the participant's derived `eligibility_status` may now read `ELIGIBLE` where it previously read `NOT_ELIGIBLE`, or the reverse. Alongside those hops, every touched plan year's pension accrual row is recomputed and the employer's delinquency ledger is recomputed — a recomputation that, if it brings that ledger to 0, lifts a suspension on exactly the terms a remittance does — both as described in the sections that follow.

The eligibility replay is a full rebuild, never an increment, and it proceeds in four steps. **Step zero wipes.** Every coverage month of this participant whose `status` is `GRANTED` is set to `REVOKED`; `hour_bank_hours` is reset to 0, `forfeited_hours_total` is reset to 0, and `coverage_through_month` is set to null. Revoked rows are never deleted — they remain readable as the historical record of grants the fund has withdrawn. **Step one builds the credit-event list.** One event is created for every contribution line naming this participant whose owning report has filing state `POSTED`, carrying the owning report's `work_period` as its month, the line's `hours`, the owning report's identifier, and a kind of contribution. One further event is created for every reciprocity transfer naming this participant whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`, carrying the transfer's own `work_period`, the transfer's `hours`, no report identifier at all, and a kind of reciprocal. Lines on reports that are merely `SUBMITTED`, and lines on reports that are `SUPERSEDED`, contribute nothing. **Step two sorts** those events by month ascending and, within a month, by the underlying row's `created_at` ascending. The row's `id` is never consulted; `created_at` is the tie-break, and rows sharing a `created_at` instant fall in the order they were inserted.

**Step three replays the events in that order**, and each event is processed exactly like this. The hours available to spend are the participant's current `hour_bank_hours` plus the event's own hours. The participant's outstanding advance is the number of their coverage months that are currently `GRANTED` and whose month key is strictly later than the logical clock's own year-and-month key; the allowance is the fund's `max_advance_coverage_months` less that outstanding count. The number of months this event buys is the smaller of two quantities: the available hours divided by the fund's `hours_per_coverage_month` and rounded down to a whole number, and the allowance floored at zero. Multiply the months bought by `hours_per_coverage_month` to get the hours consumed, and subtract that from the available hours to get the leftover. Any leftover in excess of the fund's `bank_cap_hours` is forfeited: that excess, floored at zero, is added to the participant's `forfeited_hours_total`, and the participant's `hour_bank_hours` becomes the smaller of `bank_cap_hours` and the leftover. The first month the grant may occupy is the later of two candidates — the event's own month advanced forward by the fund's `eligibility_lag_months`, and the month immediately after the participant's `coverage_through_month` as it currently stands; when `coverage_through_month` is null, only the first candidate constrains anything. Starting at that month, mint as many consecutive coverage months as the event bought, each with `status` `GRANTED`, each with `hours_applied` equal to the fund's `hours_per_coverage_month`, and each with `granting_report_id` set to the event's report identifier — which is null for a reciprocal event, since no report granted it. The `source` of each minted row is `RECIPROCAL` when the event came from a reciprocity transfer; otherwise it is `CURRENT_HOURS` when the event's own hours were at least `hours_per_coverage_month`, and `BANK_DRAW` when they were not, because in that case the purchase was only possible by drawing on hours banked earlier. If the event bought at least one month, `coverage_through_month` advances to the highest month just minted. **Step four totals**: the participant's `credited_hours_total` is the sum of the hours across contribution events, rounded half up to two decimals, and `reciprocal_hours_total` is the sum of the hours across reciprocal events, rounded half up to two decimals the same way.

One asymmetry inside the replay is deliberate and must not be smoothed over. When an event would buy more months than the allowance permits, the replay **clips** it: only the allowed number of months is minted, and the unspent hours remain in the bank subject to the cap. Clipping is the replay's behaviour. It is emphatically not the behaviour of the reciprocity intake gate, which refuses an over-horizon acceptance outright before any replay runs at all — that distinction is spelled out in section 3.8.

Coverage months are readable individually and as a list, filterable by `participant_id`, by `granting_report_id`, by `status`, by `coverage_month`, and by `source`.

### 3.5. Pension Credit Accrual, Vesting Service, and Breaks in Service

Welfare eligibility is bought month by month; pension credit is earned year by year. The fund keeps one accrual row per participant per plan year, and a row springs into existence the first time a posted contribution line or an accepted reciprocity transfer lands in that plan year. A row once created is never deleted: a plan year whose hours later leave it — because the report carrying them was superseded, say — keeps its row, with every figure on it recomputed down to zero. Each row records the `participant_id`, the `plan_year` it covers, and the figures below. Which plan year a work period falls into depends on the fund's `plan_year_start_month`: when that value is 1 the plan year is simply the calendar year of the work period, and otherwise it is the year of the plan year that contains the work period.

Within a row, `hours_credited` is the sum of the `hours` on contribution lines for this participant that sit on reports whose filing state is `POSTED` and whose work period falls in this plan year, rounded half up to two decimals. `reciprocal_hours_credited` is the sum of the `hours` on this participant's reciprocity transfers whose `status` is `ACCEPTED` **and** whose `transfer_type` is `PRO_RATA`, with a work period in this plan year, likewise rounded half up to two decimals; money-follows-the-man transfers buy welfare coverage but never feed pension accrual. The countable hours for the year are `hours_credited` plus `reciprocal_hours_credited`.

What those countable hours buy depends on the fund's `accrual_method`, and the two variants are genuinely different rules rather than two tunings of one rule. Under `HOURS_BASED`, the year's `pension_credit` is the countable hours divided by 250 and rounded down to a whole number of quarter-credits — each quarter-credit worth 0.25 — with the result capped at a full credit of 1.00; and the year counts as a year of vesting service, setting `vesting_year_earned` to true, when the countable hours are **at least** 870, an inclusive threshold rather than a strict one. Under `ELAPSED_TIME`, the year earns a full `pension_credit` of 1.00 when the countable hours are greater than zero **and** the participant holds at least one `GRANTED` coverage month whose month falls inside this plan year, and 0.00 otherwise; and `vesting_year_earned` is true whenever the countable hours are greater than zero at all, regardless of coverage. The gap between the variants is wide by design: a participant with 151.30 countable hours earns 0.00 credit and no vesting year under `HOURS_BASED`, but a full 1.00 credit and a vesting year under `ELAPSED_TIME`.

Independently of the accrual variant, the row's `break_in_service` flag asks whether the participant worked enough in the plan year to avoid losing ground. It is true when the sum of `hours_credited` and the hours of **every** reciprocity transfer of any `transfer_type` accepted for this participant in this plan year comes to less than 375 hours. Note the deliberate difference from `reciprocal_hours_credited`: the break test counts money-follows-the-man hours as well as pro-rata ones, so a travelling member who worked out of a sister fund's jurisdiction all year does not thereby suffer a break. Each row also carries an `accrual_status` that opens at `OPEN` and flips to `FINALIZED` once the plan year has fully elapsed, which happens only during a collections run.

Three roll-ups on the participant are rewritten whenever any of their accrual rows changes. `vesting_service_years` is the count of the participant's accrual rows whose `vesting_year_earned` is true. `total_pension_credits` is the sum of `pension_credit` across those rows, rounded half up to two decimals. `vested` is true exactly when `vesting_service_years` has reached five years, an inclusive threshold. A fourth roll-up, `consecutive_break_years`, is the length of the current trailing run of the participant's `FINALIZED` accrual rows whose `break_in_service` is true, walking the rows in ascending `plan_year` order — rows still `OPEN` never enter the run, and a single finalized year without a break ends it.

Breaks that persist have teeth. During a collections run, a participant whose `consecutive_break_years` has reached five and whose `vested` is still false moves from `ACTIVE` to `BROKEN_SERVICE`, and the move erases accrued benefit: every one of that participant's accrual rows has its `pension_credit` set to 0.00 and its `vesting_year_earned` set to false, and the participant's own `vesting_service_years` drops to 0 and `total_pension_credits` to 0.00. The reverse move exists too — a participant sitting in `BROKEN_SERVICE` whose most recently finalized accrual row shows `break_in_service` false returns to `ACTIVE` and has `consecutive_break_years` reset to 0 — which is how a member who comes back to covered employment resumes a working career, even though the credit erased by the break does not return: the rows the break zeroed hold a `pension_credit` of 0.00 and a false `vesting_year_earned` under every later recomputation, and only plan years whose rows are opened after the erasure accrue again. A vested participant is never broken by this rule; five years of vesting service is permanent protection. Accrual rows for a participant may be listed and filtered by `plan_year` and by `accrual_status`, and are returned in ascending `plan_year` order rather than by `created_at`.

### 3.6. The Payment Axis: Remittances, Delinquency Charges, and the Employer Ledger

A contribution report carries two entirely independent state axes, and confusing them is the most common way to get this domain wrong. The filing axis — `SUBMITTED`, `POSTED`, `SUPERSEDED` — records whether the fund has credited the hours. The payment axis — `UNPAID`, `PARTIAL`, `PAID_IN_FULL`, `DELINQUENT` — records whether the money arrived. A report may be `POSTED` while still `UNPAID`, and may be `PAID_IN_FULL` while still only `SUBMITTED`; nothing about one axis constrains the other. Every report opens `UNPAID`, and unlike the filing axis the payment axis has no terminal state at all: a report that reached `PAID_IN_FULL` can be driven back into arrears when a later amendment raises the amount due.

Money is applied to a report by remitting against it. A remittance carries a single `amount_cents`, and either the employer itself or a fund administrator acting for it may submit one. A report whose filing state is `SUPERSEDED` accepts no remittance and returns `ERR_REPORT_NOT_REMITTABLE`; an amount larger than the report's outstanding balance — its `total_contributions_due_cents` less its `amount_remitted_cents` — is refused with `ERR_REMITTANCE_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`. A remittance strictly greater than zero and strictly smaller than the outstanding balance adds itself to `amount_remitted_cents` and moves the report to `PARTIAL`, from `UNPAID` and from `DELINQUENT` alike; a remittance of exactly zero against a non-zero balance is accepted, adds nothing, and leaves the payment state exactly where it stood. A remittance exactly equal to the outstanding balance settles the report: `amount_remitted_cents` becomes `total_contributions_due_cents`, `paid_in_full_at` is stamped with the logical clock instant, and the payment state becomes `PAID_IN_FULL` — this same closing move is available from `UNPAID`, from `PARTIAL`, and from `DELINQUENT` alike, on identical terms. Every remittance recomputes the employer's delinquency ledger, and if that recomputation brings the ledger to 0 while the employer's `status` is `SUSPENDED`, the employer returns to `ACTIVE`, its `delinquency_strikes` resets to 0, and the fund's `suspended_employer_count` is recomputed.

The employer ledger itself — `cumulative_delinquent_cents` — is a derived figure, rebuilt from scratch on every mutation of any report's payment state or remitted amount, on every change of a delinquency assessment's status, and at the close of every sweep of a collections run. It is the sum of two quantities. The first is unpaid contributions: across every contribution report of this employer whose filing state is not `SUPERSEDED` and whose payment state is `UNPAID`, `PARTIAL`, or `DELINQUENT`, add up the report's `total_contributions_due_cents` less its `amount_remitted_cents`. The second is open assessments: across every delinquency assessment raised on a report of this employer whose `status` is `ASSESSED`, add up that assessment's `total_cents`. Assessments that have been `SETTLED` or `WAIVED` leave the sum entirely — they do not contribute their settled or waived amount, they contribute nothing. The two quantities added together are the ledger, in whole cents, and because it is a plain sum no ordering or tie-break applies.

When a report is late, the fund assesses interest and liquidated damages against it. Raising an assessment requires a `basis` of either `STATUTORY` or `PLAN_SCHEDULE`, and it is the fund administrator's choice which body of law to charge under. At assessment time the server stamps `assessed_on` from the logical clock date and computes `days_late` as the number of whole days from the report's `due_date` to `assessed_on`, discarding any fractional day and never falling below 0. The report must not be `SUPERSEDED`, an assessment raised on one being refused with `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_POSTED`; `days_late` must be greater than 0, and an assessment demanded on a report that is not yet late is refused with `ERR_REPORT_NOT_DELINQUENT`. `principal_cents` is the report's `total_contributions_due_cents` less its `amount_remitted_cents` as of that moment, and it must be strictly positive — a report with nothing outstanding is refused with `ERR_NO_DELINQUENT_PRINCIPAL`.

Under the `STATUTORY` basis, which mirrors the doubling remedy of the federal statute, interest runs from the first day after the due date with no grace period at all: `interest_cents` is the principal multiplied by the fund's `interest_rate_annual_bps`, divided by 10,000 to turn basis points into a rate, multiplied by `days_late` and divided by 365, then rounded half up to whole cents. `liquidated_damages_cents` is the larger of two figures — the interest just computed, and the principal multiplied by the fund's `ld_rate_bps` and divided by 10,000, rounded half up to whole cents — which is what makes the remedy a doubling in the ordinary case. The fund's `ld_floor_cents` is **not** applied under this basis.

Under the `PLAN_SCHEDULE` basis, which applies the trust agreement's own damages schedule, the fund's grace period shrinks the interest but never the damages tier. Effective days are `days_late` less the fund's `grace_days`, floored at zero, and `interest_cents` is the principal multiplied by `interest_rate_annual_bps`, divided by 10,000, multiplied by those effective days and divided by 365, rounded half up to whole cents. The damages tier is keyed on the raw `days_late` and not on the effective days: 500 basis points when the report is 30 days late or fewer, 1,000 basis points when it is between 31 and 60 days late inclusive, and 2,000 basis points when it is more than 60 days late. `liquidated_damages_cents` is then the larger of two figures — the principal multiplied by that tier and divided by 10,000, rounded half up to whole cents, and the fund's `ld_floor_cents` — so on a small principal the floor can lift the charge above the tiered percentage.

Under both bases, interest and liquidated damages are each rounded half up to whole cents **independently**, and only then added; there is no intermediate unrounded total. `total_cents` is `principal_cents` plus `interest_cents` plus `liquidated_damages_cents`. The new assessment opens with `status` `ASSESSED`, with `settlement_amount_cents` and `waived_cents` both null, and its arrival pushes the employer's ledger upward by exactly its `total_cents`.

An open assessment can leave the ledger in one of two ways. A fund administrator may record a negotiated settlement by supplying `settlement_amount_cents`, which may not exceed the assessment's `total_cents` — an excess is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_EXCEEDS_ASSESSMENT` — and which is only accepted while the assessment's `status` is still `ASSESSED`, an attempt against any other status being refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_INVALID_STATE`. Settlement records the accepted cents in `settlement_amount_cents`, moves the assessment to `SETTLED`, and recomputes the employer ledger, from which the assessment now drops out entirely rather than being reduced to the settled figure; as with a remittance, a ledger that reaches 0 restores a suspended employer to `ACTIVE`. The other exit is the board's: a carried liquidated-damages waiver motion moves the assessment to `WAIVED` and records its full `total_cents` in `waived_cents`, with the four-hop cascade set out in section 3.2. The assessments raised on a report may be listed and filtered by `status` and by `basis`.

### 3.7. The Payroll Audit Program and Underpayment Recovery

Trust funds do not take employer reporting on faith. An independent payroll auditor opens an audit over a closed interval of months for one employer, supplying the `employer_id`, the `period_start_month` that begins the window, and the `period_end_month` that ends it; both are year-and-month keys, both bounds are inclusive, and the end month may not be earlier than the start month — an inverted window is a malformed request under `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The auditor's own opaque identity is captured into `auditor_user_id`, and the audit opens with `status` `IN_FIELDWORK`, `round_count` at 1, an empty `findings` array, `unreported_hours_found` at 0, `underpayment_principal_cents` at 0, and `resulting_report_id` null. An employer may not be audited twice over the same ground: the new window may not intersect the window of any existing audit on that employer whose status is anything other than `ABANDONED`, and an intersection is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Abutting windows are explicitly permitted — an audit that starts the month after another one ends does not overlap it. Because abandoned audits are excluded from the test, an audit that found nothing frees its period for a later look. Audits may be listed and filtered by `employer_id`, by `status`, and by `resulting_report_id`.

When fieldwork is done the auditor issues that round's `findings`, an array whose entries each carry the `participant_id` of the worker whose hours went unreported, the `work_period` in which the unreported work occurred, and `unreported_hours`, the hours the payroll records show but the employer's filings did not. Findings may be issued only while the audit is `IN_FIELDWORK`; issuing from any other status is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_INVALID_STATE`. Only once that status guard has passed is the identity examined: only the auditor who opened the audit — the identity stored in `auditor_user_id` — may issue findings on it, and any other auditor is turned away with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Every participant named in a finding must be enrolled in the same fund as the employer under audit, and a finding naming a participant from another fund is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_FUND_MISMATCH`. Every finding's `work_period` must fall inside the audited window, inclusive of both bounds; one that does not is a malformed request under `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), tested after the status, auditor-identity and fund-mismatch guards above.

A non-empty findings issue moves the audit from `IN_FIELDWORK` to `FINDINGS_ISSUED`, replaces the stored `findings` wholesale with the payload, and prices the underpayment. Pricing follows exactly the same round-per-item-then-sum discipline as ordinary contribution pricing, and it walks the findings in the order the request body supplied them. For each finding, multiply its `unreported_hours` by the employer's `contribution_rate_millicents_per_hour`, multiply again by 10,000 basis points, divide by ten million, and round half up to whole cents. Audit findings are **always** priced at the journeyman multiplier of 10,000 basis points regardless of the `work_classification` the employer originally reported for that worker — the fund does not honour an apprentice discount on hours the employer concealed. Summing those per-finding cents gives `underpayment_principal_cents`. Separately, `unreported_hours_found` is the sum of `unreported_hours` across the findings, rounded half up to two decimals. An issue that supplies an **empty** findings array means the opposite conclusion: the stored `findings` is set to empty, `unreported_hours_found` and `underpayment_principal_cents` both drop to 0, and the audit moves from `IN_FIELDWORK` straight to `ABANDONED`, which is terminal and produces no recovery report at all.

The employer responds to issued findings with a `response` of either `ACCEPT` or `DISPUTE`, and may attach an optional `dispute_note`. Only the audited employer may respond — an employer acting against another employer's audit is turned away with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — and only while the audit sits in `FINDINGS_ISSUED`, any other status yielding `ERR_AUDIT_INVALID_STATE`. `ACCEPT` moves the audit from `FINDINGS_ISSUED` to `EMPLOYER_ACCEPTED` and leaves the priced findings standing. `DISPUTE` sends the audit backwards, from `FINDINGS_ISSUED` to `IN_FIELDWORK`: `round_count` increases by 1, the stored `findings` is cleared to empty, and `unreported_hours_found` and `underpayment_principal_cents` both return to 0, so the auditor must go back into the field and re-issue. Findings from earlier rounds are discarded, not accumulated. The loop is bounded at three fieldwork rounds: a dispute submitted when `round_count` already stands at three is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_ROUND_LIMIT_REACHED`, leaving the audit in `FINDINGS_ISSUED` with the employer's only remaining option being acceptance.

Finalizing an accepted audit is what turns findings into money owed, and it is deliberately the hardest gate in the system. The audit must be in `EMPLOYER_ACCEPTED`, or the attempt fails with `ERR_AUDIT_INVALID_STATE`. The joint board must have blessed the settlement: a motion of this fund whose `subject_type` is `AUDIT_SETTLEMENT` and whose `subject_ref_id` names this very audit must stand in either `CARRIED` or `ARBITRATION_CARRIED`, and finalizing without one is refused with `ERR_BOARD_APPROVAL_REQUIRED`. And the administrator doing the finalizing may not be the auditor who worked the audit — the acting identity must differ from `auditor_user_id`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The auditor who finds the money may never be the officer who books it.

Finalizing moves the audit to the terminal `CLOSED` status and runs a four-hop cascade. The first hop mints a recovery report: a contribution report with `origin` `AUDIT`, the audited employer, a `work_period` equal to the audit's `period_end_month`, `amends_report_id` null, `filing_status` already `POSTED` with `posted_at` stamped from the logical clock, and `payment_status` `UNPAID`, and its `due_date` derived as the twentieth of the month following that work period exactly as for any other report. It carries one contribution line per finding, each with the finding's `participant_id`, `hours` equal to the finding's `unreported_hours`, `gross_wages_cents` of 0, and `work_classification` `JOURNEYMAN`; the audit's `resulting_report_id` is set to that report. The second hop follows from the report being posted: its lines enter the eligibility replay for every participant named in the findings, rewriting `credited_hours_total` and `hour_bank_hours`. The third hop follows from the second: where the replay produced additional whole coverage months, new `GRANTED` coverage months are minted and `coverage_through_month` advances. The fourth hop follows from the third: because `coverage_through_month` has moved past the logical clock's month, a participant's `eligibility_status` now reads `ELIGIBLE` where it previously read `NOT_ELIGIBLE` — recovered hours restore coverage the worker should have had all along. Alongside those hops, the pension accrual rows for the touched plan year are recomputed, and the employer's `cumulative_delinquent_cents` is recomputed to include the newly minted, entirely unpaid audit report.

### 3.8. Travelling Members: Reciprocity with Sister Funds

Members who work out of another local's jurisdiction earn hours in a sister fund, and reciprocity agreements route those hours home. An administrator records an inbound transfer with the `participant_id` of the travelling worker, the `sister_fund_id` of the sending fund, a `transfer_type` of either `MONEY_FOLLOWS_MAN` or `PRO_RATA`, the `work_period` in which the travelling hours were worked, the `hours` being transferred in as a non-negative number with at most two decimals, and the `amount_cents` of money accompanying them, a non-negative integer. The sending fund must be a genuine counterparty: a `sister_fund_id` naming a fund whose `fund_role` is `HOME`, or naming the participant's own fund, is refused with `ERR_SISTER_FUND_INVALID`. A recorded transfer opens with `status` `PENDING` and `applied_coverage_months` at 0, and nothing whatsoever is credited to the participant until the fund decides on it. Transfers may be listed and filtered by `participant_id`, by `sister_fund_id`, by `status`, and by `transfer_type`.

Deciding a transfer takes a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`, and a transfer that is no longer `PENDING` is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE`. `REJECT` is the simple branch: the transfer moves to `REJECTED` and nothing else changes — no participant roll-up, no coverage month, no fund aggregate. `ACCEPT` carries three preconditions. The sending fund's `reciprocity_agreement_status` must read `EXECUTED`, which happens only when the board has carried a reciprocity-agreement motion naming that fund; accepting against a fund still at `NONE` is refused with `ERR_RECIPROCITY_AGREEMENT_NOT_EXECUTED`. The receiving participant's `status` must be `ACTIVE` rather than `BROKEN_SERVICE`; an acceptance for a `BROKEN_SERVICE` participant is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE`. And the acceptance must not push the participant past the fund's advance-coverage horizon: take the participant's current `hour_bank_hours` plus the transfer's `hours`, divide by the fund's `hours_per_coverage_month` and round down to a whole number of months, and that figure may not exceed the fund's `max_advance_coverage_months` less the participant's currently outstanding advance coverage months — the count of that participant's coverage months whose `status` is `GRANTED` and whose month key is strictly later than the logical clock's own year-and-month key. An acceptance that would exceed it is refused outright with `ERR_COVERAGE_HORIZON_EXCEEDED`, **before any replay runs**. This is the counterpart to the clipping rule of section 3.4 and the two must not be conflated: inside the replay, an over-horizon event is silently clipped and its surplus hours stay in the bank; at this intake gate, an over-horizon acceptance is rejected and no state changes at all.

An accepted transfer moves to `ACCEPTED` and pulls the participant's whole eligibility picture forward. The participant's `reciprocal_hours_total` is recomputed, and the eligibility replay of section 3.4 reruns from scratch with this transfer now among the credit events — which means it takes its place in the chronological order by its own `work_period`, not at the end, and may therefore reshuffle which months earlier hours bought. The coverage months this transfer's own replay step grants are minted with `source` `RECIPROCAL` and with `granting_report_id` null, and their count is recorded on the transfer as `applied_coverage_months`. The fund's `reciprocal_receipts_cents` is recomputed as the sum of `amount_cents` across every `ACCEPTED` transfer for participants of this fund. Finally, and only when the `transfer_type` is `PRO_RATA`, the pension accrual row for the transfer's plan year picks up the hours in its `reciprocal_hours_credited` and is re-evaluated end to end — `pension_credit`, `vesting_year_earned` and `break_in_service` all reconsidered, with the participant's `vesting_service_years`, `total_pension_credits` and `vested` rewritten behind them. A `MONEY_FOLLOWS_MAN` transfer buys welfare coverage and moves money, but never touches `reciprocal_hours_credited`; it does, however, still count toward the year's break-in-service test as described in section 3.5.

### 3.9. Withdrawal Liability: Allocation, Certification, Demand, and the Installment Schedule

When a signatory employer withdraws completely from a multiemployer plan, it owes the trust its allocable share of the plan's unfunded vested benefits. A fund administrator opens the assessment with the `employer_id` of the withdrawing employer, the `withdrawal_date` on which the complete withdrawal occurred, a `method` of either `PRESUMPTIVE` or `ROLLING_FIVE`, and the `expected_fund_version` guard described in section 2.7. Opening an assessment moves the employer to `WITHDRAWN` — from `ACTIVE` or from `SUSPENDED` alike, so long as it is not already withdrawn, an assessment opened against an already-`WITHDRAWN` employer being refused with `ERR_EMPLOYER_WITHDRAWN` — and from that moment the employer may file no further contribution reports. An employer that was `SUSPENDED` leaves that state on withdrawal, so the fund's `suspended_employer_count` is recomputed. The fund's `version` increments by 1. The assessment opens in `DRAFT` with `certified_by_user_id`, `demanded_on`, `review_grounds` and `accelerated_balance_cents` all null and `twenty_year_cap_applied` false; crucially, **no unfunded vested benefit is allocated at this point**, because a draft has not been certified. There must, however, be something to allocate: an assessment opened while the fund's `unallocated_uvb_cents` stands at 0 or below is refused with `ERR_UVB_POOL_EXHAUSTED`. Assessments may be listed and filtered by `employer_id`, by `status`, and by `method`.

The allocation itself draws against the pool. Let the pool be the fund's `unallocated_uvb_cents` — its `total_uvb_cents` less whatever is already allocated. The employer's `employer_share_fraction` is computed according to the chosen method and, in both variants, is rounded half up to exactly ten decimal places **before** it is used to multiply anything; multiplying by the unrounded fraction produces different cents on realistic inputs and is not an acceptable substitute. Under `PRESUMPTIVE`, the fraction is the sum of all five entries of the employer's `prior_year_contributions_cents` divided by the sum of all five entries of the fund's `pool_prior_year_contributions_cents`. Under `ROLLING_FIVE`, the fund first finds the three-consecutive-year window that maximises the employer's own contributions: consider the three windows whose start index within the five-element array is 0, 1, or 2, sum the employer's `prior_year_contributions_cents` across each window's three years, and choose the window with the largest sum, breaking any tie in favour of the **lowest** start index, which is to say the earliest window; row identifiers are never consulted in that choice. The fraction is then the employer's sum over that window divided by the fund's `pool_prior_year_contributions_cents` summed over **the same index window** — not over the pool's own best window. In either variant a denominator of zero is not an error: the fraction is simply 0.0000000000.

With the fraction fixed, `raw_allocation_cents` is the pool multiplied by the fraction, rounded half up to whole cents. The statutory de-minimis relief is then computed and phased out. The de-minimis amount is the smaller of 750,000 cents and the fund's `total_uvb_cents` multiplied by 75 basis points, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up to whole cents. The phase-out excess is the raw allocation less 10,000,000 cents, floored at zero. `de_minimis_reduction_cents` is the de-minimis amount less that excess, floored at zero. `net_liability_cents` is `raw_allocation_cents` less `de_minimis_reduction_cents`, and that net figure is the amount ultimately charged against the pool. The phase-out boundary is exact and is worth stating: because the excess is measured against 10,000,000 cents, a raw allocation of exactly 10,000,000 cents keeps the whole de-minimis reduction, while a raw allocation of 10,000,001 cents loses precisely one cent of it.

The payment schedule is computed at the same time as the allocation, and although the twenty-year cap looks circular — the number of years depends on the liability while the capped liability depends on the number of years — the evaluation order below is fixed and must be followed once, never iterated toward a fixed point. First, the net liability is already known from the allocation. Second, and entirely independently of it, the fund picks the three-consecutive-year window that maximises the employer's `prior_year_base_units`: the same three candidate start indices of 0, 1, or 2, the same largest-sum rule, and the same tie-break in favour of the lowest start index. This window is chosen independently of the contribution window used for the allocation fraction, and the two may well land on different indices. The employer's high-three average base units is the sum of `prior_year_base_units` across that window divided by 3 and rounded half up to a whole number. Third, `annual_payment_cents` is that average multiplied by the employer's `contribution_rate_millicents_per_hour` and divided by 1,000 — millicents to cents — rounded half up to whole cents; if the result is 0 there is no finite schedule and the request is refused with `ERR_NO_PAYMENT_BASE`. Fourth, the uncapped number of years is the net liability divided by the annual payment and rounded **up** to a whole number. Fifth, the cap is tested: if the uncapped number of years exceeds 20, then `twenty_year_cap_applied` is true, `schedule_years` is 20, and `capped_liability_cents` is the annual payment multiplied by 20; otherwise `twenty_year_cap_applied` is false, `schedule_years` is the uncapped figure, and `capped_liability_cents` equals `net_liability_cents`. Sixth and last, `de_minimis_reduction_cents` is **not** recomputed after the cap applies — the cap limits collection, it does not reopen the allocation.

Certification is the actuary's act and no one else's. The assessment must be in `DRAFT`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_WITHDRAWAL_INVALID_STATE`. Certifying stamps the acting actuary's opaque identity into `certified_by_user_id`, moves the assessment from `DRAFT` to `CERTIFIED`, adds the assessment's `net_liability_cents` to the fund's `allocated_uvb_cents`, recomputes `unallocated_uvb_cents` from the new allocated figure, and increments the fund's `version` by 1. This is the moment the pool actually shrinks.

Demanding payment is the administrator's act, and it is fenced from the actuary's. The checks run in the ladder's order rather than the order they read most naturally: the assessment must be `CERTIFIED`; the fund's `governance_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, and a demand issued by a board without both bargaining parties represented is refused with `ERR_FUND_GOVERNANCE_FROZEN`; the administrator issuing the demand must not be the same identity recorded in `certified_by_user_id`, an overlap being refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; and last the assessment's `annual_payment_cents` must be greater than 0, a demand against a zero annual payment being refused with `ERR_NO_PAYMENT_BASE`. A demand stamps `demanded_on` with the logical clock date, moves the assessment to `DEMANDED`, and mints exactly `schedule_years` payment installments, each `SCHEDULED`, each carrying its `assessment_id` and a one-based `installment_number`, and each with `paid_on` null. Installment number *k* falls due 60 days after `demanded_on` plus a further 365 days for each year beyond the first — so the first is due 60 days after the demand, the second 425 days after it, and so on. Every installment except the last carries an `amount_cents` equal to `annual_payment_cents`; the final installment carries `capped_liability_cents` less `annual_payment_cents` multiplied by one fewer than `schedule_years`, which is how the schedule lands exactly on the capped total. An assessment's installments may be listed and filtered by `status`, and are returned in ascending `installment_number` order.

A withdrawing employer that disputes the figures may request review while the assessment is `DEMANDED`, supplying `grounds`, which are captured verbatim into `review_grounds`; the assessment moves to `REVIEW_REQUESTED` and the existing installment schedule is left completely intact. Only the assessed employer may request review. The administrator then resolves the review with a `decision` of either `UPHELD` or `REVISED`, and the two branches converge on the payment phase from opposite directions. `UPHELD` moves the assessment from `REVIEW_REQUESTED` straight to `PAYMENT_ACTIVE` and touches the schedule not at all. `REVISED` requires a `revised_total_uvb_cents` value that is present and at least 0 — a requirement tested only once the assessment is confirmed to sit in `REVIEW_REQUESTED` — and rewinds the whole allocation: the assessment's current `net_liability_cents` is first subtracted back out of the fund's `allocated_uvb_cents` as an explicit compensating step, the fund's `total_uvb_cents` is replaced with `revised_total_uvb_cents` and `unallocated_uvb_cents` recomputed, and then the entire allocation and schedule are recomputed against the new pool — `employer_share_fraction`, `raw_allocation_cents`, `de_minimis_reduction_cents`, `net_liability_cents`, `annual_payment_cents`, `schedule_years`, `twenty_year_cap_applied` and `capped_liability_cents` all rewritten by the same rules and in the same order as at intake, with the single departure that a revision yielding an `annual_payment_cents` of 0 is not refused here and simply leaves `schedule_years` and `capped_liability_cents` at 0. The new `net_liability_cents` is then added to the fund's `allocated_uvb_cents`, every installment of this assessment still in `SCHEDULED` is deleted, `demanded_on` returns to null, and the assessment returns to `CERTIFIED` so that a fresh demand must be issued against the revised figures. The fund's `version` increments by 1.

Installments are paid one at a time and in full. A payment carries `amount_cents`, which must equal the installment's own `amount_cents` exactly — anything else, high or low, is refused with `ERR_INSTALLMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. Only the employer that owns the assessment may pay, the installment must be `SCHEDULED` or `MISSED`, and the owning assessment must be `DEMANDED` or `PAYMENT_ACTIVE` — a payment against an assessment in any other status, `ACCELERATED` and `SATISFIED` included, is refused with `ERR_WITHDRAWAL_INVALID_STATE`, and that test runs after the installment's own payability rather than before it. An installment already `PAID` or already `DEFAULTED` accepts nothing further, and a `MISSED` installment accepts payment only while the logical clock date is no later than 60 days past its `due_date` — its cure window — with all three refusals carrying `ERR_INSTALLMENT_NOT_PAYABLE`. A successful payment stamps `paid_on` with the logical clock date and moves the installment to `PAID`. Two knock-on transitions follow. If the owning assessment was still `DEMANDED`, the first payment moves it to `PAYMENT_ACTIVE`. If every installment of the assessment is now `PAID`, the assessment moves to `SATISFIED`, which is terminal.

Default is the other terminal outcome. When a collections run drives one of an assessment's installments from `MISSED` to `DEFAULTED`, the assessment itself moves to `ACCELERATED` — from `DEMANDED` or from `PAYMENT_ACTIVE`, on identical terms — and `accelerated_balance_cents` is set to the sum of `amount_cents` across every installment of that assessment whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `MISSED`, or `DEFAULTED`; installments already `PAID` are excluded. The withdrawing employer's `status` is driven to `SUSPENDED` and the fund's `suspended_employer_count` is recomputed. `ACCELERATED` is terminal: the whole remaining balance is now due at once and the installment schedule no longer governs.

### 3.10. The Collections Run

Everything in this system that happens because time passed rather than because someone acted happens in a single ops-run checkpoint that a fund administrator triggers against a fund. The run is idempotent only in the sense that it acts on whatever the clock now justifies; each invocation increments the fund's `collections_run_index` by 1 and reports what it did, returning `reports_marked_delinquent`, `installments_marked_missed`, `installments_defaulted`, `employers_suspended`, `accruals_finalized`, and the new `collections_run_index`. Three sweeps run in a fixed order, and within any sweep rows are processed by `due_date` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending.

**The first sweep identifies delinquency.** Every contribution report of this fund's employers whose payment state is `UNPAID` or `PARTIAL`, whose filing state is not `SUPERSEDED`, and whose `due_date` plus the fund's `grace_days` now lies strictly in the past relative to the logical clock date, is swept into `DELINQUENT`. That flip cascades four hops. First, `payment_status` becomes `DELINQUENT`. Second, because that report flipped, the owning employer's `delinquency_strikes` increases by 1 and its `cumulative_delinquent_cents` is recomputed. Third, because `delinquency_strikes` has reached three, the employer's `status` becomes `SUSPENDED` — a move made only out of `ACTIVE`, so an employer already `SUSPENDED` or already `WITHDRAWN` keeps the status it has while its strike count still rises. Fourth, because an employer entered suspension, the fund's `suspended_employer_count` is recomputed. Strikes count distinct reports swept into delinquency and are wiped back to 0 only when the employer's ledger returns to 0 — through remittance, through settlement, or through a carried waiver motion — at which point the employer also leaves suspension.

**The second sweep ages the withdrawal installments, in two ordered passes.** Pass A moves every installment still `SCHEDULED` whose `due_date` now lies in the past to `MISSED`. Pass B then moves every installment that is `MISSED` and whose `due_date` plus 60 days now lies in the past to `DEFAULTED` — and pass B explicitly includes the rows pass A has just marked, so a single run can carry one installment all the way from `SCHEDULED` through `MISSED` to `DEFAULTED` when the clock has advanced far enough. Each installment reaching `DEFAULTED` drives its owning assessment to `ACCELERATED` with the consequences set out in section 3.9, including the suspension of the withdrawing employer.

**The third sweep closes out plan years and applies break-in-service consequences.** Pension accrual rows whose plan year has fully elapsed relative to the logical clock — that is, whose plan year's last day now lies strictly in the past — move from `accrual_status` `OPEN` to `FINALIZED`, and those finalizations are what feed the `consecutive_break_years` run described in section 3.5. A participant of this fund whose `consecutive_break_years` has then reached five while `vested` is still false moves from `ACTIVE` to `BROKEN_SERVICE`, with every accrual row's `pension_credit` zeroed and `vesting_year_earned` cleared, and with `vesting_service_years` and `total_pension_credits` reset. A participant already in `BROKEN_SERVICE` whose most recently finalized accrual row shows no break returns to `ACTIVE` with `consecutive_break_years` reset to 0.

At the close of each sweep the employer delinquency ledger is recomputed for the employers the sweep touched, so a run that both marks reports delinquent and defaults installments leaves every affected employer's `cumulative_delinquent_cents` and `status` consistent with the state the run itself produced. That closing recomputation never lifts a suspension: only a remittance, a settlement or a carried waiver motion clears an employer's strikes and returns it to `ACTIVE`.

Sweep 2 runs two passes in order: pass A moves every SCHEDULED installment past its due_date to MISSED, then pass B moves every MISSED installment past due_date + 60 days to DEFAULTED, including rows pass A just marked, so a single run can carry one installment SCHEDULED -> MISSED -> DEFAULTED | `FundAdministrator` |
