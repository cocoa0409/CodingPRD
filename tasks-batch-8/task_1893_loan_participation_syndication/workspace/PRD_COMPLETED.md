# Loan Participation & Syndication Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

This document specifies the product requirements for the Loan Participation & Syndication Administration API, the system of record for an agent bank administering syndicated credit facilities on behalf of a lending group. In a syndicated loan, one lead bank — the agent — structures a facility for a borrower, sells participation interests to participant lenders, collects the participants' funding, receives borrower payments, and distributes those payments pro rata after retaining its servicing fee. The agent also runs the governance machinery of the syndicate: consent solicitations when the borrower asks to modify terms, lender votes when a default is declared, transfers of participation interests between institutions, and repurchase demands when a participant claims the agent breached its obligations.

The API covers the full administrative lifecycle. A borrower is onboarded; a facility is structured with its interest rate, day-count convention, servicing fee, voting thresholds, and transfer minimums; participants subscribe until the facility is fully committed; the borrower draws against the facility and each participant wires its pro-rata share; borrower payments flow through a pinned distribution waterfall of servicing fee, then interest, then principal; modification requests are solicited, voted, tallied, and implemented; defaults are declared, voted on, and resolved by cure, waiver, or acceleration; financial statements and reporting packages are tracked to delivery; and participation interests change hands through agent-approved transfers or are unwound through buyback settlements. All endpoints are mounted under `/api`.

Two design commitments pervade the specification. First, every derived number the system reports — pro-rata shares, accrued interest, servicing fees, distribution pools, tally shares, buyback prices — is pinned to an exact integer formula with an explicit rounding and allocation order, so two independent implementations produce identical ledgers. Second, the three roles that drive the system (the agent bank, the borrower, and the participant lenders) act through a small set of optimistic-concurrency-checked write operations whose cascades are enumerated hop by hop, so the system's behavior under interleaved reads and writes is deterministic.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the system-wide rules, structural contracts, and cross-cutting principles that apply to every endpoint and every resource in the API.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

All interactions with the API are authenticated through two mandatory HTTP headers. Every request must carry `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting party, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that party acts. A request in which either header is missing or empty is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_OR_INVALID_USER_CONTEXT_HEADER` (HTTP 400). The system recognizes exactly four roles, spelled as follows: `AgentBank`, `Borrower`, `ParticipantLender`, and `System`.

`AgentBank` is the syndication agent and loan administrator. It structures facilities, commits and withdraws participants, creates and cancels draws, confirms funding wires, computes and remits distributions, solicits and tallies consents, declares and resolves defaults, receives financial statements, assembles and delivers reporting packages, approves and settles transfers, and acknowledges, disputes, and settles buyback demands. When `X-User-Role` is `AgentBank`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`. `AgentBank` has read access to every resource and may call every endpoint except those reserved below for `Borrower` or `ParticipantLender`.

`Borrower` is the obligor under a facility. When `X-User-Role` is `Borrower`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Borrower entity; ownership checks on any resource that references a Borrower compare the resource's borrower foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. A borrower records payments against its own facilities, requests modifications, submits financial statements, and may withdraw its own modification requests. Recording a payment against a facility whose `borrower_id` does not equal the caller's `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403), as is withdrawing a modification request the caller did not originate.

`ParticipantLender` is a loan participant. When `X-User-Role` is `ParticipantLender`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Participant entity; ownership checks on any resource that references a Participant compare the resource's participant foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. A participant wires its own funding settlements, casts and recasts its own consent votes and default votes, proposes and cancels its own participation transfers, and lodges and rescinds its own buyback demands. Reporting a wire on another participant's settlement, recasting another participant's vote, cancelling another participant's transfer, or rescinding another participant's demand is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

`System` is the automation identity that appears as the actor on bubble-up and cascade transitions only — settlement roll-ups, facility status bubbles, vote voiding on participant exit, borrower status restoration, and similar server-internal reactions. It has no API endpoints; no request may carry `X-User-Role` of `System`. A request carrying `System`, or any role value outside the four recognized spellings, is permitted on no endpoint and fails role authorization with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_FOR_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

Every endpoint declares the roles permitted to call it (§4). A request whose role is valid but not permitted on the endpoint is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_FOR_ROLE` (HTTP 403). All read endpoints are shared by `AgentBank`, `Borrower`, and `ParticipantLender`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

Requests to paths the API does not define, and requests to a defined path with a method that path does not support, are likewise rejected with `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) in the same error envelope.

### 2.3. Pagination

Endpoints that return lists of resources paginate via two query parameters. `limit` specifies the maximum number of items to return; it defaults to 10, and values below 1 or above 100 are clamped to 1 or 100 rather than rejected. `offset` specifies the number of items to skip; it defaults to 0, and negative values clamp to 0. A `limit` or `offset` that does not parse as an integer fails with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Unless otherwise specified, all list endpoints return resources sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, with ties broken by creation (insertion) order. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`; `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of returned items is less than `total_count`.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

The server maintains a logical clock that governs every date- and time-sensitive rule in this document: funding due dates, voting windows, statement and package due dates, transfer blackout windows, interest accrual as-of dates, and all timestamps stamped onto resources. Before any client initializes it, the logical clock reads exactly 2026-08-01T00:00:00Z; it never falls back to the host wall clock. An `AgentBank`-only test-driving capability advances the logical clock to a supplied instant `to_iso8601`; the call returns the resulting `current_utc` and mutates no entity. The supplied value may be a full ISO 8601 datetime with any offset or a bare calendar date (read as midnight UTC); it is stored as supplied, and `current_utc` echoes the supplied string verbatim. The clock may be set to any instant, earlier or later — no monotonicity check applies. The `api_request_timestamp` stamped into every response envelope is read from the same logical clock. Phrases such as "current clock date" throughout this document refer to the calendar date of the logical clock in UTC.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Every resource in the system — Borrower, Facility, Participant, Draw, FundingSettlement, BorrowerPayment, DistributionRun, DistributionLine, ModificationRequest, ConsentVote, DefaultCase, DefaultVote, FinancialStatement, ReportingPackage, ParticipationTransfer, and BuybackDemand — carries a standard set of system-managed fields:

- `id` (string): a unique, server-minted identifier formatted as a UUID, with no type prefix.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the resource was created.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the last modification; refreshed on every mutation.
- `version` (integer): a system-managed integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of the resource, including mutations that only recompute derived fields.

Any server-side recomputation that changes a resource's stored fields — for example, recomputing a facility's aggregates after a participant commits — is itself a mutation of that resource: it refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version`. Where a parent entity's `version` anchors an optimistic-concurrency rule (§2.8), these derived-field recomputations are exactly the increments a client observes. All changes a single API call applies to the same resource — however many cascade hops or recomputations touch it within that call — count as exactly one mutation: one `version` increment and one `updated_at` refresh per resource per request.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points, and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in the system are integer cents carried in fields suffixed `_cents`; no floating-point money appears anywhere in the API. All rates, fees, thresholds, and ownership shares are integer basis points carried in fields suffixed `_bps`, where ten thousand basis points make a whole. Every division in a calculation rounds down — floor — at a granularity pinned per calculation: interest accrues with a floor applied per draw per accrual segment, distribution pools split with a floor applied per participant per pool, and tally shares floor to whole basis points. Wherever flooring leaves a remainder, the remainder is distributed one unit at a time in an order this document pins for each calculation. Threshold comparisons for voting and consent outcomes are inclusive: a share exactly equal to the required threshold passes.

### 2.7. Facility Aggregates, Bubble-Ups, and Cascade Discipline

The Facility is the aggregation root of the syndicate, and the server maintains six derived figures on it continuously. `committed_cents` is the sum of `commitment_cents` over participants in `COMMITTED` or `FUNDED` status. `funded_principal_cents` is the sum of `due_cents` over funding settlements in `CONFIRMED` or `LATE_CONFIRMED` status. `outstanding_principal_cents` is the funded principal not yet distributed, written down, or settled via buyback; it always equals the sum of `outstanding_cents` over the facility's draws. `accrued_interest_cents` is unpaid accrued interest, equal to the sum of `accrued_unpaid_interest_cents` over the facility's settled draws, and is materialized at distribution-run creation, at default declaration, cure, waiver, and acceleration, and at buyback settlement. `undistributed_cash_cents` is borrower cash received but not yet run through a distribution waterfall. `last_distribution_value_date` records the `value_date` of the most recently created distribution run and stays null until the first run exists.

Consistency is maintained by bubble-ups and cascades rather than by client orchestration. When a child's state change satisfies the criteria for a parent state change — the first participant committing, the last sibling settlement confirming, a distribution run remitting, a default resolving — the server transitions the parent automatically, without any client call against the parent. When a parent event invalidates children — a draw cancellation voiding its settlements, a facility entering default withdrawing its open solicitations, a participant exit voiding its votes — the server cascades the transition downward. Every such reconciliation independently mutates the affected resource, refreshing `updated_at` and incrementing `version`. Multi-hop cascades in this document are numbered (hop 1, hop 2, and so on) and every hop is part of the contract, not an implementation detail.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write operations require the caller to prove it has observed the current state of a parent entity before mutating under it, by carrying a non-optional expected-version field. A mismatch between the supplied value and the parent entity's current `version` rejects the request with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). These are stale-view rejections, not race arbitration: they fire whenever the client's last observed version is behind the entity's current version, regardless of timing.

- Creating a draw requires `expected_facility_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target Facility. Because every participant commit and every draw creation advances the facility's version, a stale value means the client's picture of subscription headroom is out of date, and the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- Computing a distribution run for a payment requires `expected_payment_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target BorrowerPayment. A run creation legitimately supersedes the prior computed run, and a stale payment version means the client has not observed the current run lineage; the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- Settling a participation transfer requires `expected_from_participant_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the transferor Participant. A settle debits the transferor's commitment, and a stale value means another settle or a funding confirmation has mutated the transferor since the client last read it; the settle is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint takes a version field.

### 2.9. Validation Priority Order

When several validations could reject the same request, the server evaluates them in the following fixed priority order and returns the error from the first failing check only:

1. Authentication context: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present and non-empty, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_MISSING_OR_INVALID_USER_CONTEXT_HEADER`.
2. Role authorization: the caller's role is permitted on the endpoint — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_FOR_ROLE`.
3. Ownership: on ownership-scoped endpoints (a borrower paying its own facility, a participant wiring its own settlement, a participant casting or recasting its own vote, a participant proposing, cancelling, or rescinding its own transfer or buyback demand), `X-User-ID` equals the acting entity's id — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
4. Existence: every id in the path and every `*_id` reference in the body resolves to an existing resource — otherwise `ERR_NOT_FOUND`.
5. Request shape: the request body a well-formed JSON object, required fields present, enums within their domain, amounts strictly positive, `accrual_end_date` after `accrual_start_date`, `period_end_date` on or after `period_start_date`, the `terms` object containing exactly the key the `modification_type` requires, and exactly one of `transferee_participant_id` or `transferee_name` supplied — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, or `ERR_TRANSFER_TARGET_AMBIGUOUS` for the transferee-pair violation.
6. Target-state legality: the target resource's own state permits the transition; terminal states reject everything — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.
7. Cross-entity facility posture: the facility is not in `IN_DEFAULT`, `ACCELERATED`, `CLOSED`, or `TERMINATED` for solicitation, transfer propose and settle, and prepayment payments; the facility is `COMMITTED` for draw creation; the facility is in `FUNDING`, `ACTIVE`, `IN_DEFAULT`, or `ACCELERATED` for buyback demands — otherwise `ERR_FACILITY_IN_DEFAULT` or `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_COMMITTED` as the rule nominates.
8. Optimistic concurrency: the supplied expected-version equals the parent entity's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.
9. Single active default: at most one `DECLARED` default case exists per facility — otherwise `ERR_DEFAULT_ALREADY_ACTIVE`.
10. Uniqueness and overlap: committed totals within the facility total, half-open non-overlapping draw accrual windows, closed non-overlapping reporting-package periods, the 24-hour solicitation buffer, one vote per participant per solicitation or case, one statement per facility, type, and period end, one open buyback demand per participant per facility, and no self-transfer — otherwise `ERR_COMMITMENT_EXCEEDS_TOTAL`, `ERR_DRAW_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, `ERR_PACKAGE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, `ERR_SOLICITATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_STATEMENT`, `ERR_BUYBACK_ALREADY_PENDING`, or `ERR_SELF_TRANSFER` respectively.
11. Referenced-party eligibility: voters must be `FUNDED` participants of the facility, transferors must be `COMMITTED` or `FUNDED`, wire reporters must be the settlement's own participant, buyback demanders must be `FUNDED`, an attached statement must belong to the facility and be received, and implementation requires a passed solicitation — otherwise `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_RECEIVED`, `ERR_MODIFICATION_NOT_PASSED`, or `ERR_BUYBACK_NOT_FUNDED`.
12. Temporal gates: votes only while a solicitation is open and before its close, tallies only after the close, default-marking only after the funding due date, and transfer settlement dates outside payment blackout windows — otherwise `ERR_VOTING_CLOSED`, `ERR_VOTING_STILL_OPEN`, `ERR_FUNDING_NOT_YET_DUE`, or `ERR_SETTLEMENT_IN_BLACKOUT`.
13. Fiscal caps and thresholds: draw headroom, settlement amount equality and late-penalty equality, the distribution principal pool within outstanding principal, transfer minimums and residual stubs, acceleration and waiver thresholds, and write-downs within outstanding principal — otherwise `ERR_DRAW_EXCEEDS_COMMITMENT`, `ERR_FUNDING_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, `ERR_PENALTY_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`, `ERR_TRANSFER_BELOW_MINIMUM`, `ERR_TRANSFER_EXCEEDS_COMMITMENT`, `ERR_TRANSFER_STUB_BELOW_MINIMUM`, `ERR_ACCELERATION_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`, `ERR_WAIVER_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`, or `ERR_WRITEDOWN_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`.

### 2.10. Nested Resource Retrieval and Reference Resolution

Requests that address resources through nested paths or through foreign-key references in the body are validated left to right and outside in. Each path identifier is resolved in the order it appears in the URL, and the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) at the first identifier that does not resolve, before any later identifier or any body field is examined. The path parameters used across the API are `borrowerId`, `facilityId`, `participantId`, `drawId`, `settlementId`, `paymentId`, `runId`, `lineId`, `modificationId`, `voteId`, `caseId`, `statementId`, `packageId`, `transferId`, and `demandId`, each naming the resource it identifies. The same rule applies to body references: a `facility_id`, `borrower_id`, `financial_statement_id`, `transferee_participant_id`, or any other `*_id` supplied in a request body must resolve to an existing resource, failing with `ERR_NOT_FOUND`. A modification whose terms reference a new borrower therefore fails with `ERR_NOT_FOUND` if `new_borrower_id` does not name an existing Borrower. List endpoints nested under a parent (for example, the draws or payments of a facility) apply the same parent-first resolution before applying filters and pagination; these nested child lists accept only the `status` filter.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

This section specifies the domain behavior of the syndication platform as a set of lifecycle narratives. State names appear as exact uppercase strings; every state change, cascade, tally, and formula stated here is binding.

### 3.1. Borrower Onboarding and Facility Structuring

A syndication begins with the obligor. An `AgentBank` operator registers a Borrower with its `legal_name`, a `sector` classification drawn from `INDUSTRIAL`, `ENERGY`, `REAL_ESTATE`, `HEALTHCARE`, `TECHNOLOGY`, `FINANCIAL`, or `OTHER`, and an internal `risk_rating` of `INVESTMENT_GRADE`, `CROSSOVER`, `SPECULATIVE`, or `DISTRESSED`. A new borrower's `status` starts at `ACTIVE`; the system — never a client — later moves it to `DELINQUENT` or `INSOLVENT` as default cases are declared against its facilities, and restores it to `ACTIVE` when those cases resolve (§3.7).

With a borrower on file, the agent structures a Facility by naming the obligor in `borrower_id` and supplying `facility_name`, the size ceiling `total_commitment_cents`, the `currency` as an ISO 4217 code, the annual `interest_rate_bps`, the `day_count_basis` governing accrual (`ACT_360`, `ACT_365`, or `BASIS_30_360`; §3.4), the annual `servicing_fee_bps` the agent retains off the top of every distribution, the `default_interest_addon_bps` that accrues on top of the base rate while a default is active, and the `maturity_date` as an ISO 8601 date. Four governance knobs carry defaults the caller may override: `majority_threshold_bps` defaults to 5001 and gates majority consents and default waivers; `supermajority_threshold_bps` defaults to 6667 and gates supermajority consents and acceleration; `quorum_bps` defaults to 5000 and sets the minimum cast share for a valid modification tally; `voting_window_days` defaults to 15 and lengths every solicitation window; and `min_transfer_cents` defaults to 1000000, setting both the smallest permissible participation transfer and the smallest residual stub a partial transfer may leave behind. All threshold comparisons are inclusive. Field validation at creation is syntactic only: `total_commitment_cents` must be a strictly positive integer; `interest_rate_bps`, `servicing_fee_bps`, and `default_interest_addon_bps` may be zero but not negative; `currency` is checked only for a non-empty string, with no ISO 4217 format check; and the five governance knobs accept any integer override without range checks.

A facility is born in `STRUCTURING` status with every aggregate at zero. Its lifecycle runs `STRUCTURING` → `SYNDICATING` → `COMMITTED` → `FUNDING` → `ACTIVE`, with `IN_DEFAULT` and `ACCELERATED` as distress branches off `ACTIVE`, and `CLOSED` and `TERMINATED` as terminal states. The early transitions bubble up from participant activity (§3.2): the first committed participant lifts `STRUCTURING` → `SYNDICATING`, and the subscription reaching the full ceiling lifts `SYNDICATING` → `COMMITTED`. If a committed participant withdraws before any draw exists and the committed total falls below the ceiling, the facility bubbles back down from `COMMITTED` to `SYNDICATING`; that demotion is only possible while no draw outside `CANCELLED` exists.

Before any funding has occurred, the agent may abandon the deal: termination moves a facility from `STRUCTURING`, `SYNDICATING`, or `COMMITTED` to `TERMINATED`, and every participant still `PROPOSED` or `COMMITTED` is withdrawn with it, transitioning to `WITHDRAWN`. Termination is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` if any draw with a status other than `CANCELLED` exists, and it is not offered at all from later facility states.

### 3.2. Syndication: Participants, Commitments, and Pro-Rata Shares

The agent builds the lending group one Participant at a time, supplying the `facility_id`, the institution's `participant_name`, and a strictly positive `commitment_cents`. Each participant starts `PROPOSED`. Registration itself is accepted whatever the facility's status; the lifecycle guards fire at commit and withdrawal time, not at registration. When the agent commits a proposed participant, two guards fire in validation order: the facility must still be in `STRUCTURING`, `SYNDICATING`, or `COMMITTED` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — and the resulting committed total may not exceed `total_commitment_cents`, so a commit that would push `committed_cents` above the ceiling is rejected with `ERR_COMMITMENT_EXCEEDS_TOTAL`. A successful commit recomputes the facility's `committed_cents` (a mutation that increments the Facility's `version`), recomputes every participant's `share_bps` as described below, and bubbles the facility upward: `STRUCTURING` → `SYNDICATING` on the first commit, and `SYNDICATING` → `COMMITTED` at the moment the committed total exactly equals the ceiling.

A `PROPOSED` participant may be withdrawn at any time, whatever the facility's status, moving to `WITHDRAWN`. A `COMMITTED` participant may be withdrawn only while the facility remains in `STRUCTURING`, `SYNDICATING`, or `COMMITTED` — once funding has begun, withdrawal is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Withdrawing a committed participant recomputes `committed_cents`, recomputes shares for the remaining participants, and, if the facility was `COMMITTED` and the total has fallen below the ceiling, demotes the facility `COMMITTED` → `SYNDICATING`.

Each participant's `share_bps` is its pro-rata share of the facility in basis points, recomputed on every commit, every committed-participant withdrawal, every transfer settlement, every buyback settlement, and every implemented commitment increase. The computation takes the eligible set — participants in `COMMITTED` or `FUNDED`; `PROPOSED`, `WITHDRAWN`, and `EXITED` participants always carry share zero — and divides each eligible participant's `commitment_cents` by the facility's `committed_cents`, scaling by ten thousand and flooring to a whole basis point. If the eligible base is zero, every eligible share is zero and the computation ends. Flooring almost always leaves a remainder: the shortfall between ten thousand and the sum of the floored shares, which is always smaller than the number of eligible participants, is distributed one basis point at a time in descending order of `commitment_cents`, breaking ties by earlier `created_at` and then by ascending `participant_name`; a single pass always suffices. Ties never fall back to resource ids.

Two ledger fields track each participant's economic history. `funded_cents` accumulates the `due_cents` of the participant's settlements as they confirm (§3.3). `cumulative_interest_cents` and `cumulative_principal_cents` accumulate lifetime interest and principal distributed to the participant, increasing when a distribution line remits and decreasing when one is clawed back (§3.5).

### 3.3. Draws and the Funding Window

Once the subscription exactly fills the ceiling, the borrower can draw. The agent creates a Draw against a facility whose status is `COMMITTED` — any other status rejects with `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_COMMITTED` — carrying `facility_id`, a strictly positive `draw_amount_cents`, and the interest accrual window `accrual_start_date` to `accrual_end_date`. The end date must be strictly after the start date, failing `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Accrual windows of non-cancelled draws on the same facility are half-open intervals — a new window whose start equals a sibling's end is permitted — and any true overlap rejects with `ERR_DRAW_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. The cumulative amount of all non-cancelled draws may not exceed `total_commitment_cents`; a draw that would breach that headroom rejects with `ERR_DRAW_EXCEEDS_COMMITMENT`. Because commitment aggregates shift under the client's feet, draw creation is optimistic-concurrency checked: the request must carry `expected_facility_version` equal to the facility's current `version`, failing with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (§2.8).

Creating a draw is the hinge of the facility lifecycle. The draw is born in `FUNDING` status; the first draw moves the facility `COMMITTED` → `FUNDING` and increments the Facility's `version` by one. The server immediately generates one FundingSettlement per participant in `COMMITTED` status, each settlement's `due_cents` being that participant's pro-rata share of `draw_amount_cents` computed from `share_bps` — floored per participant, with the remainder cents from flooring distributed one cent at a time so the dues sum exactly to the draw amount, in the same remainder order the distribution waterfall uses (§3.5): descending `share_bps`, ties broken by descending `commitment_cents`, then earlier `created_at`, then ascending `participant_name`, cycling through the order again if the remainder reaches the participant count. Every settlement records the `draw_id`, the `participant_id`, and a denormalized `facility_id` for filtered listing, starts in `PENDING` with `received_cents` at zero, and carries a `funding_due_date` set five calendar days — the funding window — after the draw's creation date.

A participant reports its own wire: the caller must be the settlement's participant, failing `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, and supply a strictly positive `amount_cents`, failing `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Reporting moves the settlement `PENDING` → `WIRED`, stamping `received_cents` with the reported amount and `wired_at` with the current clock. A settlement still `PENDING` after its due date may be marked `DEFAULTED` by the agent — but only once the current clock date is strictly after `funding_due_date`, failing `ERR_FUNDING_NOT_YET_DUE` earlier — and a defaulted participant may still wire late, moving `DEFAULTED` → `WIRED` under the same ownership rule.

Confirmation is the agent's act, and it distinguishes timeliness by the wire date. When the wire date is on or before the `funding_due_date`, confirmation moves `WIRED` → `CONFIRMED` and requires only that `received_cents` equals `due_cents` exactly, failing `ERR_FUNDING_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. When the wire date is after the due date, confirmation moves `WIRED` → `LATE_CONFIRMED` and runs two checks in a pinned order: first the same amount equality, failing `ERR_FUNDING_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`; then the caller-supplied `penalty_received_cents` must equal the late-funding penalty exactly, failing `ERR_PENALTY_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. The penalty is the floor of the settlement's `due_cents` multiplied by fifteen basis points per calendar day of lateness — the days between `funding_due_date` and the wire date, always a positive count — divided by ten thousand, and it is recorded on the settlement as `penalty_interest_cents`. Either confirmation adds the settlement's `due_cents` to the participant's `funded_cents` and bubbles the participant `COMMITTED` → `FUNDED` on its first confirmed settlement.

A draw settles when every one of its settlements has reached `CONFIRMED` or `LATE_CONFIRMED`: the draw moves `FUNDING` → `SETTLED`, its `outstanding_cents` is set to `draw_amount_cents`, `settled_at` is stamped, the facility's `funded_principal_cents` and `outstanding_principal_cents` are recomputed, and the first settled draw bubbles the facility `FUNDING` → `ACTIVE`. A draw that will not complete may instead be cancelled by the agent, provided none of its settlements has reached `CONFIRMED` or `LATE_CONFIRMED` — confirmed funds block cancellation with `ERR_DRAW_HAS_CONFIRMED_FUNDS`. Cancellation moves the draw `FUNDING` → `CANCELLED`, voids every settlement still in `PENDING`, `WIRED`, or `DEFAULTED` into `VOID`, and, if no non-cancelled draw then remains on a facility still in `FUNDING`, reverts the facility `FUNDING` → `COMMITTED`. A draw's interest bookkeeping initializes `accrual_as_of_date` to its `accrual_start_date`, `accrued_unpaid_interest_cents` and `outstanding_cents` to zero, and `settled_at` to null; only settled draws accrue (§3.4).

### 3.4. Interest Accrual, Day-Count Conventions, and Default Re-Rating

Interest accrues independently per settled draw, from the draw's `accrual_as_of_date` forward to the as-of date of the materialization event. Accrual is materialized — computed and stored — at five moments: distribution-run creation, default declaration, default cure, default waiver or acceleration, and buyback settlement. Draws in `FUNDING` or `CANCELLED` accrue nothing.

The accrual window is split into segments at every default boundary and at every implemented rate change's effective date that falls inside it. Each segment accrues at the base rate in effect on the segment's first day — the original `interest_rate_bps` up to the first implemented `RATE_CHANGE`, and each new rate from its implementation date forward (§3.6) — and days between a default's declared date and its resolved date add `default_interest_addon_bps` on top of the segment's base rate. Each segment's day count follows the facility's `day_count_basis`. Under `ACT_360` the segment's days are the calendar days between its start and end, over a denominator of 360. Under `ACT_365` the same calendar days sit over a denominator of 365, so identical inputs yield a slightly smaller charge. Under `BASIS_30_360` the day count clamps every month to thirty days: it is 360 times the difference in years, plus 30 times the difference in month numbers, plus the difference between the two day-of-month values with each day first clamped to at most 30, all over a denominator of 360 — so a segment from 2026-08-01 to 2026-08-31 counts 29 days, diverging from both actual-day bases. Each segment's interest is the floor of the draw's `outstanding_cents` times the segment rate in basis points times the segment days, divided by the basis denominator times ten thousand; the per-segment floors are then summed into `accrued_unpaid_interest_cents`, the draw's `accrual_as_of_date` advances to the as-of date, and the facility's `accrued_interest_cents` is the sum over its settled draws.

Default events re-rate accrual by re-segmenting history. At declaration, the stretch from `accrual_as_of_date` to the declared date stays at base rate and the stretch from the declared date onward carries the addon. At cure, the addon applies only from declaration to the cure date, and base rate resumes thereafter — a two- or three-segment history. At waiver, the addon is removed retroactively: the draw's entire accrual history is recomputed at base rate, so post-waiver accrued interest equals the value it would have had if the default had never happened. At acceleration, no new segment end is introduced; the addon simply continues to accrue. Each re-rate reconciles `accrued_interest_cents` on the facility as the sum over draws.

### 3.5. Borrower Payments, the Distribution Waterfall, and Reversal

Repayment begins when the borrower records a BorrowerPayment against its own facility — the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the facility's `borrower_id`, failing `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The request names the `facility_id`, a `payment_type` of `REGULAR` or `PREPAYMENT`, a strictly positive `amount_cents`, and the `value_date` on which the payment is valued for accrual and fee purposes. Payments are accepted only while the facility is `ACTIVE`, `IN_DEFAULT`, or `ACCELERATED`; in any other status the recording is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and a `PREPAYMENT` attempted while the facility is `IN_DEFAULT` or `ACCELERATED` is rejected with `ERR_FACILITY_IN_DEFAULT`. The payment is born `RECEIVED`, its `borrower_id` is copied from the facility, `received_at` is stamped from the clock, and the facility's `undistributed_cash_cents` grows by `amount_cents`.

Cash moves onward when the agent computes a DistributionRun for the payment. Because re-runs are the correction mechanism, the create is optimistic-concurrency checked: the request must carry `expected_payment_version` matching the payment's current `version`, failing `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (§2.8). Each payment's runs carry a 1-based `run_number` that increments with every superseding run, and creating a new run supersedes the previous one: the prior run in `COMPUTED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, its `superseded_by_run_id` points at the new run, and its still-`PENDING` lines collapse to `VOID`. Run creation executes three calculations in a pinned order — servicing fee first, then interest accrual materialized to the payment's `value_date`, then the waterfall — and moves the payment `RECEIVED` → `ALLOCATED` while deducting `amount_cents` from `undistributed_cash_cents` and incrementing the payment's `version`.

The servicing fee is computed before anything else in the run touches principal. Its baseline is the facility's `last_distribution_value_date` if any run has preceded this one, otherwise the `settled_at` date of the facility's first settled draw (earliest `settled_at`, ties broken by earlier `created_at`); the fee accrues for the calendar days between that baseline and the payment's `value_date`, and a `value_date` before the baseline counts zero fee days. The raw fee is the floor of the facility's `outstanding_principal_cents` — as of run creation, deliberately before this run's principal pool is applied — times `servicing_fee_bps` times those fee days, divided by 360 times ten thousand. The retained `servicing_fee_cents` is the smaller of that raw fee and the payment's `amount_cents`.

The waterfall then allocates the payment in strict order. First the servicing fee comes off the top, capped at the payment amount, leaving the remainder. Second, the interest pool `interest_pool_cents` takes the smaller of that remainder and the facility's `accrued_interest_cents` recomputed to the payment's `value_date`. Third, the entire remaining cash becomes the principal pool `principal_pool_cents`; if that pool exceeds the facility's `outstanding_principal_cents`, run creation is rejected outright with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`. Each pool is then split across the participants in `FUNDED` status at run creation: each participant's allocation from a pool is the floor of the pool times the participant's `share_bps` divided by ten thousand, and the leftover cents are distributed one cent at a time in descending order of `share_bps`, breaking ties by descending `commitment_cents`, then earlier `created_at`, then ascending `participant_name`, cycling through the order again if the remainder reaches the participant count. Each participant's DistributionLine records its `run_id` and `participant_id`, its `interest_cents` and `principal_cents`, and their sum `total_cents`, starting `PENDING`. Whatever portion of the two pools is not attributable to any funded participant — interest or principal owed to positions the agent holds on its own book after exits — is retained by the agent as `agent_retained_cents`, computed as the two pools minus the sum of line totals. Two conservation invariants always hold: `servicing_fee_cents` plus the interest and principal pools equals the payment's `amount_cents`, and the line totals plus `agent_retained_cents` equal the two pools.

Remittance is the agent's release of a computed run, and it cascades four hops. Hop 1: the run's `PENDING` lines become `REMITTED` and the run itself moves `COMPUTED` → `REMITTED`, stamping `remitted_at`. Hop 2: each remitted line credits its participant's `cumulative_interest_cents` and `cumulative_principal_cents`, and its `principal_cents` reduces draw principal oldest-first — the principal pool walks the facility's settled draws in ascending `accrual_start_date` order, ties broken by earlier `created_at`, never by id, taking from each draw the smaller of the remaining pool and that draw's `outstanding_cents` until the pool is exhausted. Hop 3: the facility's `outstanding_principal_cents` falls by the principal pool, its `accrued_interest_cents` falls by the interest pool, each draw's `accrued_unpaid_interest_cents` is reduced in the same oldest-first order, every touched draw's `accrual_as_of_date` advances to the payment's `value_date`, and `last_distribution_value_date` is set to that date. Hop 4: when both outstanding principal and accrued interest reach zero and at least one payment of the facility currently sits in `REMITTED` status (a reversed payment no longer counts), the facility bubbles to `CLOSED` — from `ACTIVE` or from `ACCELERATED` alike — and the payment bubbles `ALLOCATED` → `REMITTED`.

Reversal is the compensation path, available to the agent in three strengths. Reversing a `RECEIVED` payment simply deducts `amount_cents` from `undistributed_cash_cents`. Reversing an `ALLOCATED` payment voids its `COMPUTED` runs into `VOIDED` and their `PENDING` lines into `VOID`, leaving `undistributed_cash_cents` untouched because it was already deducted at allocation. Reversing a `REMITTED` payment is a full clawback: hop 1 moves the remitted run's lines from `REMITTED` to `CLAWED_BACK`; hop 2 debits each clawed-back line's amounts from its participant's `cumulative_interest_cents` and `cumulative_principal_cents` and restores draw `outstanding_cents` by walking the same oldest-first allocation in reverse, adding each line's `principal_cents` back; hop 3 restores the facility's `outstanding_principal_cents` and `accrued_interest_cents` to their pre-payment values, and if the facility had closed it reopens `CLOSED` → `ACTIVE`. Every reversal stamps `reversed_at` and leaves the payment `REVERSED`. A remitted payment's reversal also unwinds the reversed run's own accrual materialization: each draw the remittance touched returns its `accrual_as_of_date` and stored accrued interest to the values held immediately before that run was created — not the materialized-to-`value_date` figures the run computed at creation (§3.4) — and the facility's `accrued_interest_cents` is recomputed as the sum over its settled draws. In the canonical flow — a payment recorded, its run created and remitted, then reversed, with the remittance touching every settled draw and no other materialization event interleaved — the facility ledgers equal their pre-payment values exactly.

### 3.6. Borrower Modifications and Consent Solicitation

A borrower negotiates changes to its facility through a ModificationRequest, created with the `facility_id`, a `modification_type`, and a `terms` object. The requesting borrower's identity comes from the caller: `requested_by_borrower_id` is taken from `X-User-ID`. The six types are `RATE_CHANGE`, `MATURITY_EXTENSION`, `COMMITMENT_INCREASE`, `COVENANT_WAIVER`, `PRINCIPAL_WRITE_DOWN`, and `BORROWER_SUBSTITUTION`, and `terms` must contain exactly the key its type demands — `new_interest_rate_bps`, `new_maturity_date`, `commitment_increase_cents`, `waiver_text`, `write_down_cents`, or `new_borrower_id` respectively — any other combination failing `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The value behind that key must likewise be well-formed: a non-negative integer for `new_interest_rate_bps`, a strictly positive integer for `commitment_increase_cents` and `write_down_cents`, a parseable ISO date for `new_maturity_date`, and a non-empty string for `waiver_text` and `new_borrower_id`, failing `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` otherwise. The system derives the consent class from the type and stores it in `required_consent_class`: `COMMITMENT_INCREASE` and `COVENANT_WAIVER` require a `MAJORITY`, `RATE_CHANGE` and `MATURITY_EXTENSION` require a `SUPERMAJORITY`, and `PRINCIPAL_WRITE_DOWN` and `BORROWER_SUBSTITUTION` require a `UNANIMOUS` consent. The required threshold lands in `required_threshold_bps`: the facility's `majority_threshold_bps` for a majority, its `supermajority_threshold_bps` for a supermajority, and ten thousand basis points for unanimity. The request is born in `DRAFT`.

The agent opens the vote by soliciting. Solicitation is permitted from `DRAFT` and again from `FAILED` or `EXPIRED` as a re-solicitation cycle, but never while the facility is in distress or wound down: a facility in `IN_DEFAULT` or `ACCELERATED` rejects with `ERR_FACILITY_IN_DEFAULT`, and one in `CLOSED` or `TERMINATED` rejects with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. A buffer rule keeps votes from colliding: no window of another request currently in `SOLICITING` on the same facility may fall within twenty-four hours of the proposed window — a gap of exactly twenty-four hours passes — failing `ERR_SOLICITATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Soliciting stamps `voting_open_at` from the clock, sets `voting_close_at` that many `voting_window_days` later, snapshots `voting_base_cents` from the facility's current `committed_cents`, and moves the request to `SOLICITING`. On a re-solicitation, every vote from the prior round is voided into `VOIDED` and a fresh window and base snapshot are taken.

Participants vote with `APPROVE`, `REJECT`, or `ABSTAIN`. Casting requires the caller to be a `FUNDED` participant of the facility, failing `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_ELIGIBLE`; the vote's `participant_id` and `modification_id` are taken from the caller and the path, and `voted_share_bps` snapshots the participant's current `share_bps` for information only — the tally works on commitments, not on this snapshot. One participant gets one live vote per solicitation: a second cast while a `CAST` or `RECAST` vote exists fails with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE`, and changing one's mind goes through recasting instead. A vote may be cast only while the solicitation is `SOLICITING` and the clock has not passed `voting_close_at`; otherwise `ERR_VOTING_CLOSED`. Recasting — `CAST` → `RECAST`, and a self-loop from `RECAST` to `RECAST` — carries the same ownership, eligibility, state, and window rules, replaces the `vote`, increments `recast_count`, refreshes `cast_at`, re-snapshots `voted_share_bps` from the participant's current share, and recomputes the tallies.

The tally aggregates over the solicitation's non-voided votes from participants currently `FUNDED`. The adjusted base is the snapshot `voting_base_cents` minus the commitments that have left the facility since solicitation: a participant exited through buyback settlement subtracts its full `commitment_cents`, while a participant exited through a fully-consuming transfer subtracts nothing, because the transferee absorbed its commitment. `approving_commitment_cents` sums the commitments of participants whose current vote is `APPROVE`; `cast_commitment_cents` sums the commitments behind any current vote, including `ABSTAIN`. The two shares — `approving_share_bps` and `cast_share_bps` — are each the floor of ten thousand times the commitment sum divided by the adjusted base, and are zero when the adjusted base is zero. Tallies recompute on every cast, every recast, and every vote voided by participant exit.

After the window closes, the agent closes the tally. Attempting it at or before `voting_close_at` fails with `ERR_VOTING_STILL_OPEN`. Three outcomes are pinned: if `cast_share_bps` is below the facility's `quorum_bps` — including the case of zero votes — the solicitation moves `SOLICITING` → `EXPIRED`; otherwise, if `approving_share_bps` meets or exceeds `required_threshold_bps`, it moves to `PASSED`; otherwise to `FAILED`. The threshold boundary is inclusive.

Implementation applies a passed request to the facility; anything not `PASSED` fails with `ERR_MODIFICATION_NOT_PASSED`. The six types mutate differently, each with equal force. A `RATE_CHANGE` replaces the facility's `interest_rate_bps` with `new_interest_rate_bps`, prospectively only — accrued interest is not restated. A `MATURITY_EXTENSION` replaces `maturity_date` with `new_maturity_date`. A `COMMITMENT_INCREASE` adds `commitment_increase_cents` to `total_commitment_cents`, recomputes every participant's `share_bps`, and, if the facility was `COMMITTED`, demotes it to `SYNDICATING`. A `COVENANT_WAIVER` changes no facility field; the `waiver_text` is simply recorded. A `PRINCIPAL_WRITE_DOWN` requires `write_down_cents` no greater than the facility's `outstanding_principal_cents`, failing `ERR_WRITEDOWN_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`, and reduces draw principal oldest-first through the same allocation order used at remit, after which `outstanding_principal_cents` is recomputed as the sum over draws. A `BORROWER_SUBSTITUTION` requires `new_borrower_id` to name an existing Borrower, failing `ERR_NOT_FOUND`, and re-points the facility's `borrower_id`. Implementation leaves the request `IMPLEMENTED`.

A request in `DRAFT` or `SOLICITING` may be withdrawn by the agent, or by the borrower that originated it — a borrower caller must be the requesting borrower, failing `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Withdrawing a soliciting request voids its `CAST` and `RECAST` votes into `VOIDED`. The same withdrawal happens automatically, without any call, when the facility enters `IN_DEFAULT` (§3.7).

### 3.7. Default Declaration, Lender Voting, and Resolution

When a default occurs, the agent declares it against the facility, naming the `facility_id` and a `default_type` of `PAYMENT_DEFAULT`, `COVENANT_DEFAULT`, `INSOLVENCY`, or `REPRESENTATION_BREACH`. Declaration requires the facility to be `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — and at most one `DECLARED` case may exist per facility at a time, a second attempt failing `ERR_DEFAULT_ALREADY_ACTIVE`. The case is born `DECLARED` with `declared_at` stamped and `voting_base_cents` snapshotted from the facility's `committed_cents`.

Declaration fires a three-hop cascade. Hop 1 moves the facility `ACTIVE` → `IN_DEFAULT`. Hop 2 withdraws every `SOLICITING` modification request on the facility into `WITHDRAWN` and re-rates every settled draw's accrual, adding `default_interest_addon_bps` from the declared date forward (§3.4). Hop 3 reconciles the facility's `accrued_interest_cents` as the sum over draws and marks the borrower: `ACTIVE` → `DELINQUENT` for any non-insolvency type (an already-`INSOLVENT` borrower stays `INSOLVENT`), or moves the borrower to `INSOLVENT` from `ACTIVE` or `DELINQUENT` alike when the type is `INSOLVENCY`.

The syndicate then votes on the remedy. Each `FUNDED` participant may cast one DefaultVote of `ACCELERATE`, `WAIVE`, or `REJECT` on the open case — non-funded callers fail `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, a second live vote fails `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE`, and the vote's `voted_share_bps` snapshots the participant's share. Recasting follows the same self-loop shape as consent votes — `CAST` → `RECAST` → `RECAST` — permitted only while the case remains `DECLARED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and it replaces the `vote`, increments `recast_count`, refreshes `cast_at`, and recomputes the tallies. The default tally uses the case's `voting_base_cents` minus the commitments that have left the facility since declaration as its adjusted base — buyback exits subtract their commitment, transfer exits subtract nothing, exactly as in §3.6, and a zero adjusted base yields zero shares: `accelerate_commitment_cents` and `waive_commitment_cents` sum the commitments behind non-voided `ACCELERATE` and `WAIVE` votes respectively, and `accelerate_share_bps` and `waive_share_bps` floor those sums against the adjusted base. No quorum applies to default votes, and `REJECT` votes count toward neither threshold.

Resolution takes three forms. Curing moves the case `DECLARED` → `CURED` with no vote threshold: the addon stops accruing from the cure date, the facility returns `IN_DEFAULT` → `ACTIVE` — or goes straight to `CLOSED` if outstanding principal and accrued interest are both zero and at least one payment currently sits in `REMITTED` status — and the borrower is restored to `ACTIVE` provided no other `DECLARED` case remains on any of its facilities. Waiving moves `DECLARED` → `WAIVED` and requires the `waive_share_bps` to meet or exceed the facility's `majority_threshold_bps`, failing `ERR_WAIVER_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`; the default addon is then removed retroactively, the full accrual history recomputed at base rate, with the facility returning to `ACTIVE` and the borrower restored on the same condition. Acceleration moves `DECLARED` → `ACCELERATED` and requires `accelerate_share_bps` to meet or exceed the facility's `supermajority_threshold_bps`, failing `ERR_ACCELERATION_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`; the facility moves `IN_DEFAULT` → `ACCELERATED`, all accrued interest becomes due, the addon keeps accruing, and new draws are rejected from then on with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Every resolution stamps `resolved_at`. A borrower leaves `DELINQUENT` or `INSOLVENT` for `ACTIVE` exactly when the last `DECLARED` case across all of its facilities has been cured or waived; the borrower state machine has no other transitions.

### 3.8. Financial Statements and Participant Reporting Packages

Credit surveillance runs on documents. The borrower registers each expected FinancialStatement with the `facility_id`, a `statement_type` of `QUARTERLY` or `ANNUAL_AUDITED`, the `period_end_date` of the reporting period, and a `document_ref` URI; the system copies `borrower_id` from the facility and derives the `due_date` — forty-five days after the period end for a quarterly statement, ninety days for an annual audited one. The statement starts `EXPECTED` with `received_at` null. One non-waived statement may exist per facility, type, and period end: a duplicate triple fails with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STATEMENT`. When the agent marks receipt, the statement moves `EXPECTED` → `RECEIVED` if the current clock date is on or before the `due_date`, and `EXPECTED` → `LATE_RECEIVED` if it is after; either way `received_at` is stamped, and a late receipt is recorded as standing grounds for a `COVENANT_DEFAULT` declaration, which always remains a manual agent action. The agent may instead waive delivery, moving `EXPECTED` → `WAIVED`.

The agent communicates with the syndicate through a ReportingPackage, opened with the `facility_id`, a human `period_label` such as 2026-Q2, a closed period interval from `period_start_date` to `period_end_date` — the end must be on or after the start, failing `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — a `due_date` for delivery, and an optional `financial_statement_id` attaching borrower financials. Period windows on the same facility are closed intervals and must not overlap at all: unlike draws, adjacency collides here, so a package whose start equals a sibling's end is an overlap, rejected with `ERR_PACKAGE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. The package opens in `OPEN`. Assembly moves it `OPEN` → `ASSEMBLED`; if a statement is attached, that statement must belong to the facility and have reached `RECEIVED` or `LATE_RECEIVED`, otherwise `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_RECEIVED`. Delivery moves `ASSEMBLED` → `DELIVERED` when the clock date is on or before the `due_date`, and `ASSEMBLED` → `LATE_DELIVERED` after it; either way `delivered_at` is stamped and `delivered_recipient_count` records the number of participants in `COMMITTED` or `FUNDED` at delivery time.

### 3.9. Participation Transfers and Buyback Demands

A participant exits or reshapes its position through a ParticipationTransfer, proposed by the transferor — `from_participant_id` is taken from the caller's `X-User-ID` — with the `facility_id`, a `transfer_amount_cents`, a `settlement_date`, and exactly one transferee designator: `transferee_participant_id` naming an existing participant to absorb the share, or `transferee_name` naming a new entrant. Supplying both or neither fails with `ERR_TRANSFER_TARGET_AMBIGUOUS`; naming oneself as transferee fails with `ERR_SELF_TRANSFER`. A named transferee participant may be any existing participant of any status on any facility — the system performs no facility-membership or status check on the transferee — while `transferee_name` always creates a new entrant at settle time. Proposal carries a battery of guards, each with its own failure: the facility must not be `IN_DEFAULT` or `ACCELERATED` (`ERR_FACILITY_IN_DEFAULT`) nor `CLOSED` or `TERMINATED` (`ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`); the transferor must be `COMMITTED` or `FUNDED` (`ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_ELIGIBLE`); the amount must be at least the facility's `min_transfer_cents` (`ERR_TRANSFER_BELOW_MINIMUM`) and no greater than the transferor's remaining `commitment_cents` (`ERR_TRANSFER_EXCEEDS_COMMITMENT`); a partial transfer must leave either nothing or a residual stub of at least `min_transfer_cents` (`ERR_TRANSFER_STUB_BELOW_MINIMUM`); and the `settlement_date` must not fall inside the blackout window of any non-reversed payment on the facility — the closed interval from two days before to two days after the payment's `value_date` — failing `ERR_SETTLEMENT_IN_BLACKOUT`. A proposed transfer is born `PROPOSED` with `proposed_at` stamped.

The agent then approves the proposal — `PROPOSED` → `AGENT_APPROVED`, under the same facility-posture rule — or rejects it outright into `REJECTED`. The transferor may cancel its own proposal from `PROPOSED` or `AGENT_APPROVED` into `CANCELLED`, with ownership enforced as `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Settlement moves `AGENT_APPROVED` → `SETTLED` and is optimistic-concurrency checked with `expected_from_participant_version` against the transferor's current `version` (§2.8); at settle time the facility posture is re-validated and the amount is re-checked against the transferor's current commitment, failing `ERR_TRANSFER_EXCEEDS_COMMITMENT` if the transferor's position has shrunk or if the transferor no longer holds `COMMITTED` or `FUNDED` status. Settlement debits `transfer_amount_cents` from the transferor's `commitment_cents`; a transfer that consumes the entire commitment exits the transferor to `EXITED`, voiding its `CAST` and `RECAST` consent votes on soliciting requests and its default votes on declared cases into `VOIDED` with tallies recomputed against the adjusted base, and recomputing shares for the remaining participants. The transferee side credits symmetrically: an existing participant's `commitment_cents` grows by the amount, or a new Participant is created whose status equals the transferor's current status and whose `commitment_cents` equals the amount, with `share_bps` (immediately recomputed), `funded_cents`, and the cumulative ledgers starting at zero; `transferee_resolved_participant_id` records who was credited. The facility's `committed_cents` is unchanged by any transfer — the conservation invariant — while `share_bps` is recomputed for all participants; `settled_at` is stamped, and the transferor Participant's `version` increments by one.

The last remedy is the BuybackDemand: a `FUNDED` participant demands that the agent repurchase its position for breach. The demand names the `facility_id`, a `breach_type` of `MISREPRESENTATION`, `SERVICING_BREACH`, or `DOCUMENTATION_DEFECT`, and an optional `breach_description`; `participant_id` comes from the caller, `demanded_at` from the clock, and the four price fields — `outstanding_share_cents`, `accrued_share_cents`, `penalty_cents`, `repurchase_price_cents` — stay null until settlement. A demander that is not `FUNDED` fails with `ERR_BUYBACK_NOT_FUNDED`; a facility outside `FUNDING`, `ACTIVE`, `IN_DEFAULT`, and `ACCELERATED` fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; and a participant may hold only one open demand per facility — `DEMANDED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, or `DISPUTED` — a second failing `ERR_BUYBACK_ALREADY_PENDING`.

The agent acknowledges a demand — `DEMANDED` → `ACKNOWLEDGED` — or disputes it — `DEMANDED` → `DISPUTED` — and a dispute resolved in the participant's favor cycles back `DISPUTED` → `ACKNOWLEDGED`. The demanding participant may rescind from `DEMANDED` or `DISPUTED` into `RESCINDED`, with ownership enforced. Settlement moves `ACKNOWLEDGED` → `SETTLED`, requires the participant still `FUNDED` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — and prices the repurchase in three hops. Hop 1 materializes interest accrual to the settle date — the current clock date at the settle call — and computes the price. Hop 2 exits the participant `FUNDED` → `EXITED` and subtracts its `commitment_cents` from the facility's `committed_cents`; the position is retained on the agent's own book, so `outstanding_principal_cents` is unchanged. Hop 3 recomputes `share_bps` for the remaining participants and voids the exited participant's live votes on soliciting requests and declared cases, recomputing those tallies.

The repurchase price has two variants selected by `breach_type`. The outstanding share `outstanding_share_cents` is the participant's `funded_cents` minus its `cumulative_principal_cents`. The accrued share `accrued_share_cents` is the floor of the participant's `share_bps` times the facility's `accrued_interest_cents` divided by ten thousand, taken after accrual has been materialized to the settle date. For `MISREPRESENTATION` and `DOCUMENTATION_DEFECT`, the standard variant, the price `repurchase_price_cents` is the outstanding share plus the accrued share. For `SERVICING_BREACH`, the penalty variant adds `penalty_cents` — the floor of the outstanding share times five hundred basis points divided by ten thousand — so the same position prices strictly higher; in the standard variant `penalty_cents` is zero. Settlement stamps `settled_at`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint returns the standard envelope (§2.2). Resource-creating operations respond 201 with the new resource; reads and mutating actions respond 200 with the resource.
