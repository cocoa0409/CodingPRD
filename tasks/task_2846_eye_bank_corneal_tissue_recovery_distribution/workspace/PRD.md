# Eye Bank Corneal Tissue API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A Lions eye bank lives or dies by the clock. From the moment a hospital, hospice, medical examiner or funeral home phones in a referral, the bank has a window measured in hours — not days — to obtain authorization, screen the donor's medical and social history, dispatch a technician into the field, and get the cornea into preservation medium. Everything downstream is a second race against the same physiology: serologic markers must come back from the laboratory, two independent optical examinations must grade the tissue, a physician must classify it for transplant, research or training use, and the resulting graft must reach a credentialed surgeon while it is still viable on the day of the scheduled operation. Most banks still choreograph this with spreadsheets, paper consent forms and telephone calls. The consequence is not merely inefficiency: a bank that cannot reconstruct who authorized what, on which evidence, and at which instant, cannot survive a regulatory audit — and cannot execute a recall when post-donation information arrives weeks after a graft was implanted.

This document specifies the REST API for the platform that replaces that choreography. All business resources are mounted under the `/eye-bank` prefix. The service models the complete custody chain of a corneal graft — donor referral, consent and history screening, recovery and preservation, serology, slit-lamp and specular grading, regulatory classification, allocation against competing surgeon requests, cold-chain shipping, implantation, unused-tissue return and re-issue, disposal, adverse-reaction adjudication and lookback investigation — as a set of explicit state machines with explicit numeric rules. Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **nothing is inferred**: every deadline, every fee, every priority score and every grading verdict is derived from a rule stated here, so that two independent implementations of this document produce byte-identical numbers. Second, **the bank is answerable for the tissue, not merely for the paperwork**: an operation that would move a graft toward a patient is refused whenever the evidence supporting it has gone stale, whether because a serology panel was superseded, a donor fell under investigation, a technician's certification lapsed, or a cold-chain logger recorded an excursion.

Because tissue viability is a function of elapsed time and because the platform must be testable deterministically, the service does not read the host machine's wall clock. A **logical clock** supplies the single notion of "now" for every window, deadline, expiry and calendar-day bucket in the system, and an administrative capability under the `/_clock` namespace advances it. Sections 2 and 3 below are written for engineers: section 2 fixes the wire contract and the cross-cutting invariants, section 3 narrates the business workflows in the order the bank actually performs them, and sections 4 and 5 summarise the HTTP surface and the error vocabulary.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — including the clock capability — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not exactly one of the six role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check precedes every other consideration; an unauthenticated caller never learns whether the resource it named exists.

The six roles are `EyeBankCoordinator`, `LabTechnician`, `RecoveryTechnician`, `EvaluationTechnician`, `MedicalDirector` and `Surgeon`. Each endpoint in section 4 lists the roles authorized to invoke it; a syntactically valid role that does not appear in that list is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). The division of labour is deliberate and non-negotiable. An `EyeBankCoordinator` is operations staff: they intake referrals, record screening answers and consent, register technicians and surgeons, dispatch recoveries, allocate tissue and prepare and dispatch shipments — but they never grade tissue and never adjudicate. A `LabTechnician` draws serology specimens and posts marker results, and has no authority over consent, classification, allocation or shipping. A `RecoveryTechnician` is the field technician on a dispatch: they mark arrival, record preservation, and may abort their own dispatch. An `EvaluationTechnician` performs the slit-lamp and specular examinations. A `MedicalDirector` is the physician with release authority: approving donors for recovery, reinstating deferred donors, classifying tissue, managing quarantine and disposal, setting surgeon credential status, adjudicating adverse-reaction reports and running lookback investigations — never recording screening answers, never allocating and never shipping. A `Surgeon` opens and cancels their own tissue requests, acknowledges delivery, confirms implantation, returns unused tissue and files adverse-reaction reports.

Three of the six roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` is a foreign key into a persisted table rather than an opaque label:

- When `X-User-Role` is `RecoveryTechnician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Technician`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Technician` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `EvaluationTechnician`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Technician`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Technician` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Surgeon`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Surgeon`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Surgeon` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`.

For those three roles, an `X-User-ID` that does not resolve to an existing `Technician` row (for `RecoveryTechnician` and `EvaluationTechnician`) or an existing `Surgeon` row (for `Surgeon`) is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ACTOR` (HTTP 403). The remaining three roles are opaque:

- When `X-User-Role` is `EyeBankCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — it is stored as `recorded_by_user_id` on every `ScreeningRecord` the coordinator writes — but it is never compared to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `LabTechnician`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `MedicalDirector`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`.

Ownership is enforced on exactly ten operations, and a violation is always reported as `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). For a `RecoveryTechnician` acting on a recovery — marking arrival, recording preservation, or aborting — `X-User-ID` must equal that recovery's `technician_id`. For an `EvaluationTechnician` recording a tissue evaluation, `X-User-ID` must equal the `evaluator_technician_id` carried in the request body. For a `Surgeon`, `X-User-ID` must equal the `surgeon_id` reachable from the target resource: on request creation and request cancellation, the request's own `surgeon_id`; on shipment delivery, the `surgeon_id` of the shipment's allocation's request; on implantation and on unused-tissue return, the `surgeon_id` of the request behind the tissue's confirmed allocation; and on filing an adverse-reaction report, the `surgeon_id` carried in the body. Note that both request creation and request cancellation are also open to an `EyeBankCoordinator`, who — being opaque — is not subject to the ownership comparison at all.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Eight collection endpoints accept pagination: the donor list, the recovery list, the tissue list, the surgeon-request list, the allocation list, the shipment list, the adverse-reaction-report list and the lookback-investigation list. Each accepts a `limit` and an `offset` query parameter. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400). The `meta` object of every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of records matching the filter before the window is applied) and `has_more` (true when records remain beyond the current window).

Unless a specific listing states otherwise, records are returned in ascending `created_at` order. The following listings override that default, and their ordering is part of the contract: the donor list, the shipment list and the tissue-evaluation-by-technician list are returned newest first; the tissue list is ordered by descending `preserved_at`; the recovery list by descending `dispatched_at`; the serology-panel list by descending `drawn_at` (invalidated panels included); the tissue-evaluation list for one tissue and the evaluation list for one technician by descending `performed_at`; the allocation list by descending `allocated_at`; the adverse-reaction-report list by descending `reported_at`; the lookback-investigation list by descending `opened_at`; the temperature-reading list by ascending `recorded_at`; and both surgeon-request listings by descending `priority_score` with ascending `created_at` as the tie-break.

The same eight collections accept optional equality filters, all of them expressed as query parameters bearing the name of the field they filter. Donors may be filtered by `status` and by `lookback_flag`. Recoveries may be filtered by `donor_id`, `technician_id` and `status`. Tissues may be filtered by `donor_id`, `recovery_id`, `disposition_status` and `custody_status`. Surgeon requests may be filtered by `surgeon_id` and `status`. Allocations may be filtered by `tissue_id`, `request_id` and `status`. Shipments may be filtered by `allocation_id`, by `replaces_shipment_id` (which finds the re-issue of a given returned parcel) and by `status`. Adverse-reaction reports may be filtered by `tissue_id`, `surgeon_id` and `status`. Lookback investigations may be filtered by `donor_id`, `source_report_id` and `status`. Nested listings — a donor's screening answers, a donor's serology panels, a technician's evaluations, a tissue's evaluations, a surgeon's requests and a shipment's temperature readings — take no query parameters at all and are not subject to the pagination bounds check.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and the Datetime Convention

Every datetime value carried in a request body, in a query filter, or in a response — whether supplied by the client, computed by a rule, or stamped by the server — is an ISO 8601 UTC instant with **exactly three fractional-second digits** and a trailing `Z`, for example `2026-08-03T09:00:00.000Z`. Implementations must emit exactly this width — never zero and never six fractional digits — and must accept exactly this width on input. A datetime that does not conform is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

The server does not consult real wall-clock time. A single **logical clock** is the only source of "now" for every deadline, expiry, monitoring window, viability comparison and calendar-day bucket in this API, and it is also the source of every server-stamped instant: the common `created_at` and `updated_at` columns as well as each computed instant this document describes as being read from "the clock". At startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, and until it is explicitly moved it continues to read that instant; it must never fall back to the host machine's time. An administrative capability, open to an `EyeBankCoordinator` and to a `MedicalDirector`, sets the clock: the request supplies the target instant as `to_iso8601` and the response body carries the resulting `current_utc`. Setting the clock mutates no entity and recomputes no derived field — a donor whose recovery deadline has just passed is not retroactively rewritten; the newly current instant simply changes the outcome of the next comparison. The clock never rewinds: `to_iso8601` must be greater than or equal to the current logical instant, and a strictly earlier target is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 422).

Whole-minute arithmetic — most visibly `death_to_preservation_minutes` on a recovery and `temp_excursion_minutes_over` on a shipment, but also every intermediate minute count in section 3's formulas — truncates toward zero, which for the non-negative differences this system computes means the value is floored. Whole-hour arithmetic behaves the same way. Where a rule adds a whole number of minutes or hours to an instant, the result keeps millisecond resolution and is rendered with the three fractional digits described above.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Every persisted resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID. Identifiers never carry a type prefix; the value is the bare UUID string, and clients must not attempt to parse meaning out of it. Every resource also carries `created_at`, the logical-clock instant at which the row was first written.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — the logical-clock instant of the most recent mutation, initially equal to `created_at` — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Section 3 states, for each operation, which rows are mutated; each such row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` incremented as part of the same request, including rows mutated only as a downstream consequence of the caller's action. Where a single request touches the same row twice — as happens when tissue creation during a recovery completion also drives the donor's status forward — the row's `version` reflects the total number of mutations applied.

Two resources are exceptions. A `ScreeningRecord` and a `TemperatureReading` are append-only observations rather than mutable records: each carries only `id` and `created_at`. Neither has an `updated_at` and neither has a `version`, and neither field appears in their response payloads.

### 2.6. Version Handshakes on Grading and Serology Writes

Two write operations in this system silently invalidate evidence a client may not have seen, and both are therefore guarded by an optimistic-concurrency handshake against the **parent** entity's `version`.

Recording a tissue evaluation requires `expected_tissue_version` as a non-optional field of the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `CorneaTissue`. A new evaluation of a given `method` legitimately supersedes the prior recorded evaluation of that same method, so a write derived from a stale read would discard grading the client never saw. If `expected_tissue_version` does not equal the tissue's current `version`, the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TISSUE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is persisted. On success, the created evaluation's `tissue_version_after` records the tissue's `version` once the write's cascade has settled — that value is the handle the client must echo on its next evaluation write for the same tissue.

Drawing a serology panel requires `expected_donor_version` as a non-optional field of the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Donor`. A newly created panel invalidates every earlier panel of that donor and can reset the donor's `serology_status`, so a write derived from a stale read would discard a laboratory result the client never saw. If `expected_donor_version` does not equal the donor's current `version`, the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DONOR_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is persisted. On success, the created panel's `donor_version_after` records the donor's `version` once the creation cascade has settled, and is the handle to echo on the next serology write for that donor.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. In particular, classification, allocation, confirmation, dispatch, delivery, implantation, return, quarantine and every lookback operation take no expected-version input; their safety comes from the state and business gates described in section 3.

### 2.7. Two Independent State Dimensions and Cascade Discipline

A cornea tissue row carries **two independent state fields**, and understanding that they are independent is the single most important structural fact in this specification. `disposition_status` is the regulatory and inventory fate of the graft, taking the values `UNGRADED`, `ELIGIBLE`, `RESERVED`, `ISSUED`, `IMPLANTED`, `QUARANTINED`, `RELEASED_FOR_RESEARCH`, `RELEASED_FOR_TRAINING` and `DISCARDED`, with `IMPLANTED` and `DISCARDED` terminal. `custody_status` is the physical whereabouts of the graft, taking the values `IN_HOUSE`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `AT_SURGEON`, `RETURNED_TO_BANK` and `DESTROYED`, with `DESTROYED` terminal. Neither dimension implies the other. Cancelling a reservation returns `disposition_status` to `ELIGIBLE` while leaving `custody_status` exactly where it was. Delivering a parcel moves `custody_status` to `AT_SURGEON` while `disposition_status` stays at `ISSUED`. Confirming the graft moves `disposition_status` to `IMPLANTED` while `custody_status` stays at `AT_SURGEON`. Exactly five operations move both dimensions in one request — classification resolving to `UNSUITABLE`, shipment dispatch, delivery resolving to a major cold-chain excursion, unused-tissue return, and disposal — and section 3 says so explicitly at each of them.

Nine other resources carry a lifecycle of their own, and for completeness their value sets, starting values and terminal values are inventoried here; section 3 gives the transitions. A `Donor`'s `status` runs over `REFERRED`, `SCREENING`, `CONSENT_OBTAINED`, `APPROVED_FOR_RECOVERY`, `RECOVERED`, `DEFERRED` and `RULED_OUT`, starting at `REFERRED`, with `RECOVERED` and `RULED_OUT` terminal, and with a `DEFERRED` back to `SCREENING` revision cycle. A `SerologyPanel`'s `status` runs over `DRAWN`, `RESULTED` and `INVALIDATED`, starting at `DRAWN`, with `INVALIDATED` terminal — it is a linear lifecycle with a supersede sink, since any panel may be invalidated from either live state. A `Recovery`'s `status` runs over `DISPATCHED`, `ON_SITE`, `COMPLETED` and `ABORTED`, starting at `DISPATCHED`, with `COMPLETED` and `ABORTED` terminal, and abort is reachable from both live states. A `SurgeonRequest`'s `status` runs over `OPEN`, `ALLOCATED`, `FULFILLED`, `CANCELLED` and `EXPIRED`, starting at `OPEN`, with `FULFILLED`, `CANCELLED` and `EXPIRED` terminal, and the `OPEN` to `ALLOCATED` to `OPEN` cycle may repeat any number of times. An `Allocation`'s `status` runs over `RESERVED`, `CONFIRMED`, `CONSUMED`, `RELEASED` and `CANCELLED`, starting at `RESERVED`, with `CONSUMED`, `RELEASED` and `CANCELLED` terminal: `RESERVED` forks to `CONFIRMED` or `CANCELLED`, and `CONFIRMED` forks to `CONSUMED` or `RELEASED`. A `Shipment`'s `status` runs over `PREPARED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `DELIVERED`, `REJECTED_ON_ARRIVAL` and `RETURNED`, starting at `PREPARED`, with `REJECTED_ON_ARRIVAL` and `RETURNED` terminal, and delivery is the fork point whose two branches carry entirely different side effects. An `AdverseReactionReport`'s `status` runs over `SUBMITTED`, `SUBSTANTIATED` and `UNSUBSTANTIATED`, starting at `SUBMITTED`, with both adjudicated values terminal. A `LookbackInvestigation`'s `status` runs over `OPEN`, `NOTIFIED`, `CLOSED_NO_ACTION` and `CLOSED_WITH_RECALL`, starting at `OPEN`, with both closures terminal and with opposite cascades. Finally a `TissueEvaluation`'s `status` is `RECORDED` or `SUPERSEDED` and a `Technician`'s `status` is `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE`, both toggled as section 3 describes.

A request that attempts a dimension-A transition from an illegal `disposition_status` is rejected with `ERR_TISSUE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 409); a request that attempts a dimension-B transition from an illegal `custody_status` is rejected with `ERR_TISSUE_CUSTODY_INVALID` (HTTP 409). When an operation is gated on both dimensions, the disposition check is evaluated before the custody check.

Cascades in this system are **immediate and complete**: there is no deferred settlement and no background reconciliation. When a single request mutates several rows — and most interesting requests do, because a tissue's disposition change reaches its allocation, the allocation reaches the surgeon request and the surgeon's money ledger, and the surgeon request's reopening triggers a priority recomputation — every one of those mutations is visible in the same response and every affected row's `version` has already been incremented by the time the response is written. Wherever a cascade iterates over a set of sibling rows, the iteration order is **ascending `created_at`** and never identifier order; this matters for the lookback quarantine sweep, for the abort sweep a donor deferral performs over live dispatches, and for the recall sweep at investigation closure. Counters that summarise a set — a donor's `tissues_recovered_count`, a technician's `open_dispatch_count`, a surgeon's `substantiated_reaction_count`, an investigation's `impacted_tissue_count`, `quarantined_count` and `notified_recipient_count` — are recomputed or adjusted inside the same request that changes the underlying set, never afterwards.

### 2.8. Path Resolution and Reference Integrity

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that fails to resolve determines the response. A request naming a donor and, beneath it, a serology panel that belongs to a different donor resolves the donor first: if the donor does not exist the answer is `ERR_DONOR_NOT_FOUND`, and only when the donor resolves is the panel consulted. Every not-found condition carries HTTP 404 and the code named for the missing entity: `ERR_DONOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEROLOGY_PANEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECOVERY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TISSUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SURGEON_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_FOUND`.

Foreign keys supplied in a request **body** are resolved with the same vocabulary, after all path identifiers have resolved: `donor_id`, `technician_id`, `evaluator_technician_id`, `surgeon_id`, `tissue_id`, `request_id`, `allocation_id`, `replaces_shipment_id` and `source_report_id` each yield the corresponding not-found code. Six operations carry body-level foreign keys and are therefore subject to this check: recovery dispatch, tissue evaluation, surgeon-request creation, manual allocation, shipment preparation and adverse-reaction reporting — plus operator-initiated lookback, whose `donor_id` and optional `source_report_id` resolve the same way.

Where a child resource takes its parent's identifier from the URL rather than the body, the stored row still carries that identifier as a field: a `ScreeningRecord` and a `SerologyPanel` each carry the `donor_id` taken from the path, a `TissueEvaluation` carries the `tissue_id` taken from the path, and a `TemperatureReading` carries the `shipment_id` taken from the path. Clients must not send those keys in the body of those three creations; the path is authoritative.

### 2.9. Numeric Precision, Money and Rounding

Money in this system is always an integer count of **whole cents** and never a decimal fraction. The three surgeon ledger fields `gross_billed_cents`, `credited_fee_cents` and `outstanding_fee_cents`, and an allocation's `processing_fee_cents`, are all integers, and all arithmetic over them is exact integer arithmetic with no rounding at all. Rounding enters exactly once, when a processing fee is first composed from percentage surcharges: **each surcharge component is rounded to the whole cent independently, half up, and only the already-rounded components are summed.** Summing raw percentages and rounding once at the end is forbidden and produces a different total; section 3.6 works the difference through in full.

Elsewhere the precision rules are as follows. Risk points, quality points, priority scores and every unit count are plain integers with no rounding. Minute counts and hour counts are floored, as described in section 2.4. A serology panel's `hemodilution_pct` is a decimal percentage compared at two decimal places, and a temperature reading's `temperature_c` is a decimal compared at one decimal place; a shipment's `min_temp_c` and `max_temp_c` likewise carry one decimal place. Comparisons between instants are exact at millisecond resolution, so two instants that differ only below the millisecond cannot arise.

### 2.10. The Order of Rejection

When a request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined: the checks below are evaluated in ascending order and the **first** failing check produces the response. This ordering is part of the contract, because a caller must be able to fix one problem at a time and because a late safety check must never mask an early authorization failure.

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. The acting role appears in the called endpoint's authorized-role list — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. For an entity-backed role, `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing row of the backing table — `Technician` for `RecoveryTechnician` and `EvaluationTechnician`, `Surgeon` for `Surgeon` — otherwise `ERR_UNKNOWN_ACTOR` (403). Applies to every endpoint whose authorized roles include one of those three.
4. Every identifier in the request path resolves, left to right — otherwise the matching 404 code from section 2.8. Applies to every endpoint with a path identifier.
5. Every foreign key in the request body resolves — otherwise the matching 404 code. Applies to recovery dispatch, tissue evaluation, surgeon-request creation, manual allocation, shipment preparation and adverse-reaction reporting.
6. The body conforms to the endpoint's input schema: required fields present, enum values in range, numerics inside their declared bounds, datetimes in the exact ISO-8601 UTC form of section 2.4, and a tissue evaluation's grade fields present or absent exactly as its `method` demands — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
7. Pagination parameters are in range — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400). Applies to the eight paginated collections listed in section 2.3.
8. `expected_tissue_version` equals the tissue's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_TISSUE_VERSION` (412). Applies to tissue-evaluation creation.
9. `expected_donor_version` equals the donor's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_DONOR_VERSION` (412). Applies to serology-panel creation.
10. Resource ownership holds for entity-backed actors — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to the ten operations enumerated in section 2.1.
11. The donor's `status` permits the operation — otherwise `ERR_DONOR_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to consent, approval for recovery, deferral, reinstatement and recovery dispatch.
12. The serology panel's `status` is `DRAWN` — otherwise `ERR_PANEL_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to posting marker results.
13. The recovery's `status` permits the operation — `DISPATCHED` for arrival, `ON_SITE` for completion, either `DISPATCHED` or `ON_SITE` for abort — otherwise `ERR_RECOVERY_STATE_INVALID` (409).
14. The tissue's `disposition_status` is a legal from-state for the requested dimension-A transition — otherwise `ERR_TISSUE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (409). Applies to classification, quarantine, quarantine release, discard, implantation, unused-tissue return, manual allocation and tissue-evaluation creation.
15. The tissue's `custody_status` is a legal from-state for the requested dimension-B transition — otherwise `ERR_TISSUE_CUSTODY_INVALID` (409). Applies to implantation, unused-tissue return, shipment dispatch and manual allocation.
16. The surgeon request's `status` permits the operation — `OPEN` for allocation, `OPEN` or `ALLOCATED` for cancellation — otherwise `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID` (409).
17. The allocation's `status` permits the operation — `RESERVED` for confirmation and cancellation, `CONFIRMED` for shipment preparation and shipment dispatch — otherwise `ERR_ALLOCATION_STATE_INVALID` (409). This check also guards request cancellation, where an allocation that has already left `RESERVED` blocks the cancellation.
18. The shipment's `status` permits the operation — `PREPARED` for dispatch, `PREPARED` or `IN_TRANSIT` for appending a temperature reading, `IN_TRANSIT` for delivery — otherwise `ERR_SHIPMENT_STATE_INVALID` (409).
19. The adverse-reaction report's `status` is `SUBMITTED` — otherwise `ERR_REPORT_STATE_INVALID` (409). Applies to substantiation and dismissal.
20. The lookback investigation's `status` permits the operation — `OPEN` for notification, `NOTIFIED` for closure — otherwise `ERR_INVESTIGATION_STATE_INVALID` (409).
21. The donor's `consent_scope` is not `NONE` and permits the use the operation implies — otherwise `ERR_CONSENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (422). Applies to four operations: recovery completion, tissue classification, manual allocation and the allocation run.
22. The donor's `serology_status` is not `REACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_SEROLOGY_REACTIVE` (422). Applies to four operations: approval for recovery, reinstatement, quarantine release and manual allocation.
23. The donor's `serology_status` is not `PENDING`, meaning a resulted, non-invalidated panel exists — otherwise `ERR_SEROLOGY_PENDING` (422). Applies to tissue classification.
24. The donor's `lookback_flag` is false — otherwise `ERR_DONOR_UNDER_LOOKBACK` (422). Applies to four operations: tissue classification, quarantine release, manual allocation and the allocation run.
25. The receiving surgeon's `credential_status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_SURGEON_NOT_CREDENTIALED` (422). Applies to four operations: surgeon-request creation, manual allocation, allocation confirmation and shipment dispatch.
26. The `evaluator_technician_id` differs from the `technician_id` of the recovery that produced the tissue — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (422). Applies to tissue-evaluation creation.
27. The acting technician has `status` `ACTIVE`, a `certification_expires_at` strictly later than the logical clock, and — when grading tissue — `certification_level` `RECOVERY_AND_EVAL` — otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED` (422). Applies to recovery dispatch and tissue-evaluation creation.
28. Both a recorded `SLIT_LAMP` evaluation and a recorded `SPECULAR` evaluation exist for the tissue — otherwise `ERR_EVALUATION_INCOMPLETE` (422). Applies to tissue classification.
29. When `intended_use_override` is `TRANSPLANT`, the donor's `screening_risk_score` is strictly below 50 — otherwise `ERR_SCREENING_RISK_TOO_HIGH` (422). Applies to tissue classification.
30. The donor's `death_at` is no later than the logical clock — otherwise `ERR_DEATH_TIME_IN_FUTURE` (422). Applies to donor registration.
31. `preservation_at` is no later than the donor's `recovery_deadline_at` — otherwise `ERR_PRESERVATION_WINDOW_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to recovery completion; the same code answers an approval attempted after the deadline has passed.
32. The new dispatch's occupation interval does not overlap any live occupation interval of the same technician — otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_DISPATCH_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to recovery dispatch.
33. `surgery_date` is strictly later than the logical clock — otherwise `ERR_SURGERY_DATE_IN_PAST` (422). Applies to surgeon-request creation.
34. The tissue's `expires_at` is strictly later than the logical clock — otherwise `ERR_TISSUE_EXPIRED` (422). Applies to manual allocation and implantation.
35. The tissue's `expires_at` is at or after the request's `surgery_date` — otherwise `ERR_EXPIRY_BEFORE_SURGERY_DATE` (422). Applies to three operations: manual allocation, allocation confirmation and shipment dispatch.
36. A temperature reading's `recorded_at` lies inside the shipment's monitoring window — otherwise `ERR_READING_OUTSIDE_SHIPMENT_WINDOW` (422). Applies to appending a temperature reading.
37. `replaces_shipment_id`, when supplied, references a `RETURNED` shipment covering the same tissue — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REISSUE_SOURCE` (422). Applies to shipment preparation.
38. The reported tissue is `IMPLANTED` and its consumed allocation belongs to the reporting surgeon — otherwise `ERR_REPORT_TISSUE_NOT_IMPLANTED_BY_SURGEON` (422). Applies to adverse-reaction reporting.
39. The delivering shipment's `integrity_verdict` is `PASS` or `EXCURSION_MINOR` — otherwise `ERR_SHIPMENT_INTEGRITY_FAILED` (422). Applies to implantation.
40. The tissue's `classification` is a transplant classification and permits the request's `procedure_type` — otherwise `ERR_PROCEDURE_CLASSIFICATION_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to manual allocation.
41. The tissue's recorded specular `endothelial_cell_count` is at least the request's `min_ecd` — otherwise `ERR_ECD_BELOW_REQUEST_MINIMUM` (422). Applies to manual allocation.
42. The donor's `age_years` is no greater than the request's `max_donor_age_years` — otherwise `ERR_DONOR_AGE_ABOVE_REQUEST_MAX` (422). Applies to manual allocation.
43. The tissue's `reissue_generation` is at most 1 — otherwise `ERR_REISSUE_LIMIT_REACHED` (409). Applies to manual allocation and shipment dispatch.
44. The submitted `donor_code` is not already used by another donor — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_DONOR_CODE` (409). Applies to donor registration.
45. No screening record already exists for this donor and `question_code` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_SCREENING_ANSWER` (409). Applies to screening-record creation.
46. The surgeon holds at most one other live allocation for the same surgery calendar date — otherwise `ERR_SURGEON_DAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to manual allocation and allocation confirmation.
47. The shipment being dispatched has at least one temperature reading — otherwise `ERR_NO_TEMPERATURE_BASELINE` (422).
48. The allocation run produced at least one match — otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TISSUE` (422).
49. The requested logical instant is at or after the current logical instant — otherwise `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND_FORBIDDEN` (422). Applies to the clock capability.

Two consequences of this ladder deserve emphasis. Because the consent gate at rung 21 precedes the serology gate at rung 22, a donor with no consent at all and a reactive panel surfaces as `ERR_CONSENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. And because every state gate (rungs 11 to 20) precedes every business-safety gate (rungs 21 onward), an operation attempted from the wrong state reports the state conflict even when a safety rule would also have refused it.

### 2.11. Conserved Quantities: the Fee Ledger and the Inventory Ledger

Two aggregates in this system are **conserved**, and their invariants hold at every observable instant rather than merely at end of day.

The first is money. A surgeon's `gross_billed_cents` is the sum of `processing_fee_cents` over every allocation reachable through that surgeon's requests whose `status` is `CONFIRMED`, `CONSUMED` or `RELEASED`. Their `credited_fee_cents` is the sum of `processing_fee_cents` over those allocations whose `status` is `RELEASED`. Their `outstanding_fee_cents` is `gross_billed_cents` less `credited_fee_cents`. At every observable instant, therefore, the outstanding amount plus the credited amount equals the gross billed amount. An allocation that goes from `RESERVED` straight to `CANCELLED` never enters any of the three sums, so a reservation that is abandoned costs the surgeon nothing. A confirmation is a forward action and a release is its compensation: confirming an allocation and then releasing it — because the surgeon returned the tissue unused, or because the cold chain failed on arrival — leaves `outstanding_fee_cents` at exactly its pre-confirmation value and raises `credited_fee_cents` by exactly that allocation's `processing_fee_cents`. `gross_billed_cents` never decreases, for any reason.

The second is unit count. Reading the tissue collection filtered by `disposition_status` and taking `total_count` from the pagination envelope yields the bank's inventory position: the available units are the tissues whose `disposition_status` is `ELIGIBLE` and whose `custody_status` is `IN_HOUSE` or `RETURNED_TO_BANK`; the committed units are those whose `disposition_status` is `RESERVED` or `ISSUED`; the held units are those `QUARANTINED`; the retired units are those `IMPLANTED`, `DISCARDED`, `RELEASED_FOR_RESEARCH` or `RELEASED_FOR_TRAINING`; and the graded units are every tissue whose `disposition_status` is anything other than `UNGRADED`. Available plus committed plus held plus retired always equals graded. A tissue whose `custody_status` is `IN_TRANSIT` or `AT_SURGEON` is always counted as committed and never as available, so the two dimensions cannot double-count the same row. Reservation and its compensating cancellation form a round trip: reserving a tissue and then cancelling that reservation restores the available count to exactly its pre-reservation value and leaves the committed count at exactly its pre-reservation value. No unit is created or destroyed by the round trip.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Referral Intake, the Recovery Window and History Screening

A donor record begins with a referral. The coordinator taking the call supplies a `donor_code` — the bank-assigned identifier printed on every recovery label, between 1 and 32 characters long and globally unique across the bank, so that a code already in use by another donor is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DONOR_CODE` — together with the `referral_source`, which is one of `HOSPITAL`, `HOSPICE`, `MEDICAL_EXAMINER` or `FUNERAL_HOME`. The clinical anchor of the whole record is `death_at`, the instant of death, from which every recovery time limit is measured; a `death_at` later than the current logical instant is nonsense and is refused with `ERR_DEATH_TIME_IN_FUTURE`. Alongside it the coordinator records `death_time_basis`, either `ASYSTOLE` or `PRONOUNCED`, describing how the instant of death was established; `body_cooled_at`, the instant refrigeration began, which defaults to null when the body was never cooled and which — when supplied — must not be earlier than `death_at`, an inversion being an invalid request; `age_years`, the donor's whole-year age at death, between 0 and 110 inclusive; and `cause_of_death_category`, one of `CARDIAC`, `TRAUMA`, `MALIGNANCY`, `NEUROLOGIC`, `INFECTIOUS` or `OTHER`.

Registration is where the bank commits to a deadline, and the arithmetic is worth stating slowly. The base window is **twelve hours — 720 minutes — when `death_time_basis` is `ASYSTOLE`, and eleven hours (660 minutes) when it is `PRONOUNCED`**, reflecting the greater confidence attached to an observed asystole. Refrigeration buys the bank time. When `body_cooled_at` is null there is no cooling delay to measure and the cooling credit is zero. Otherwise the cooling delay is the number of whole minutes from `death_at` to `body_cooled_at`, floored; a delay of at most four hours (240 minutes) earns a credit of four hours, a delay of more than four hours but at most eight hours (480 minutes) earns a credit of two hours (120 minutes), and a delay beyond eight hours earns nothing. Advanced age costs time: a donor aged 71 years or more incurs a penalty of one hour (60 minutes), and every younger donor incurs none. The stored `recovery_window_minutes` is the base window plus the cooling credit minus the age penalty, **but never less than six hours (360 minutes)** — that floor is a hard clamp applied after the addition and subtraction, not a separate branch. Finally `recovery_deadline_at` is `death_at` advanced by `recovery_window_minutes`, and it is the hard cut-off past which the cornea may not be preserved. A newly registered donor is created with `status` `REFERRED`, `consent_type` and `consent_scope` both `NONE`, `authorizing_party_name` and `consent_obtained_at` both null, `serology_status` `PENDING`, `screening_risk_score` 0, `screening_risk_variant` `INTERVIEW_ONLY`, `deferral_reason` null, `lookback_flag` false, `tissues_recovered_count` 0 and `version` 1.

Medical and social history is captured one answer at a time. Each screening record names a `question_code` drawn from `IV_DRUG_USE`, `HIV_RISK`, `HEP_B_C_HISTORY`, `SEPSIS`, `UNEXPLAINED_NEURO`, `CJD_FAMILY`, `TATTOO_12M` and `INCARCERATION_12M`; a `response` of `YES`, `NO` or `UNKNOWN`; and the `historian_relationship` of the person interviewed, one of `SPOUSE`, `CHILD`, `PARENT`, `SIBLING`, `MEDICAL_RECORD` or `OTHER`. The record is append-only and each question may be answered at most once per donor: a second answer to the same `question_code` for the same donor is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SCREENING_ANSWER`. Each stored row also carries `recorded_by_user_id`, copied from the acting coordinator's `X-User-ID`, and `weight_applied`, which starts at 0 and is rewritten on every recomputation described below. The **first** screening record for a donor advances that donor from `REFERRED` to `SCREENING`; second and later records leave `status` untouched but still trigger a full recomputation of the risk score.

Risk scoring is a weighted sum over **all** of the donor's screening rows — there is no status filter, because the rows are append-only and unique per question. The weights encode the bank's clinical priorities: a family history of Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease (`CJD_FAMILY`) is worth 60 points, unexplained neurological death (`UNEXPLAINED_NEURO`) 50, intravenous drug use (`IV_DRUG_USE`) 40, HIV risk behaviour (`HIV_RISK`) 35, a hepatitis B or C history (`HEP_B_C_HISTORY`) 30, sepsis (`SEPSIS`) 25, incarceration within the last twelve months (`INCARCERATION_12M`) 15 and a tattoo within the last twelve months (`TATTOO_12M`) 10. Two running totals are formed: the sum of the weights of every row answered `YES`, and separately the sum of the weights of every row answered `UNKNOWN`. Rows answered `NO` contribute nothing to either total.

Which of those totals reaches `screening_risk_score` depends on the variant recorded in `screening_risk_variant`, and this is the first of two places in the system where the same input data yields two legitimate answers. While the donor's `serology_status` is still `PENDING` — that is, while no resulted, non-invalidated panel exists — the variant is `INTERVIEW_ONLY` and `screening_risk_score` is the `YES` total alone: the historian's uncertainty is deliberately not charged against the donor. Once the donor has a resulted, non-invalidated panel the variant becomes `POST_SEROLOGY_RECONCILED`, and a **reconciliation predicate** is evaluated against that single surviving panel: the predicate holds when the panel's `overall_result` is `INDETERMINATE` **or** its `hemodilution_pct` is strictly greater than 50.00 percent. When the predicate holds, every `UNKNOWN` answer is charged at its full weight and `screening_risk_score` becomes the `YES` total plus the `UNKNOWN` total, which for the same rows is strictly larger than the interview-only figure whenever any `UNKNOWN` answer exists. When the predicate fails, `UNKNOWN` answers still contribute nothing and the two variants agree exactly. Whichever variant applies, `weight_applied` on every one of the donor's rows is rewritten to the weight that row actually contributed — the question's weight for a `YES` row, zero for a `NO` row, and zero for an `UNKNOWN` row whenever the predicate fails. Because only one non-invalidated panel can ever exist for a donor, there is no ordering ambiguity about which panel the predicate reads. A `screening_risk_score` of 50 or more is the bank's **transplant risk ceiling**: at or above that figure the donor can never yield a transplant-grade graft, as section 3.5 makes precise.

### 3.2. Consent, Serology and Donor Release

Authorization is recorded once, and it fixes the ceiling on every downstream use of the donor's tissue. The coordinator supplies `consent_type` — `FIRST_PERSON` when the donor registered their own intent, `NEXT_OF_KIN` when a family member authorized, or `NONE` — together with `consent_scope` and the `authorizing_party_name` printed on the consent form. The permitted scopes are `NONE`, `TRANSPLANT_ONLY`, `TRANSPLANT_AND_RESEARCH`, `RESEARCH_ONLY` and `TRAINING_ONLY`. Recording consent requires the donor to be in `SCREENING`, and a scope of `NONE` is not a legitimate value to submit here — a request that supplies it is rejected as an invalid request. On success the four consent fields are written, `consent_obtained_at` is stamped with the current logical instant, and the donor advances `SCREENING → CONSENT_OBTAINED`. The operation is a modification of an existing donor and returns 200 with the updated donor.

Serologic testing runs in parallel with consent and screening. Drawing a specimen creates a panel carrying `specimen_type`, either `PRE_MORTEM` or `POST_MORTEM`; `drawn_at`, the instant of the draw; and `hemodilution_pct`, the estimated plasma dilution as a percentage between 0.00 and 100.00 at two decimal places. The new panel is created in `DRAWN` with `hiv_result`, `hbv_result`, `hcv_result`, `syphilis_result`, `overall_result`, `resulted_at` and `superseded_at` all null. Creating it is destructive to the donor's serologic history in a controlled way: **every** earlier panel of the same donor, whether it was still `DRAWN` or already `RESULTED`, moves to `INVALIDATED` with `superseded_at` stamped at the current logical instant and its own `version` incremented. Because no resulted, non-invalidated panel then remains, the donor's `serology_status` reverts to `PENDING`, `screening_risk_variant` reverts to `INTERVIEW_ONLY`, and `screening_risk_score` is recomputed under that variant — an `UNKNOWN`-heavy donor's score can therefore fall when a fresh specimen is drawn. The donor's `version` is incremented, and the new panel's `donor_version_after` captures the donor's `version` once that cascade has settled. This is precisely why the draw is guarded by `expected_donor_version` as described in section 2.6.

Posting results is the laboratory's terminal act on a panel. The four marker fields `hiv_result`, `hbv_result`, `hcv_result` and `syphilis_result` are each `NON_REACTIVE`, `REACTIVE` or `INDETERMINATE`, and only a panel still in `DRAWN` accepts them; a panel already `RESULTED` or `INVALIDATED` is refused with `ERR_PANEL_STATE_INVALID`. The panel's `overall_result` is then resolved by an ordered cascade in which **reactivity dominates everything**: if any one of the four markers is `REACTIVE`, `overall_result` is `REACTIVE`; otherwise, if `hemodilution_pct` is strictly greater than 50.00 the specimen is too dilute to trust and `overall_result` is `INDETERMINATE` — a `hemodilution_pct` of exactly 50.00 does **not** trip this gate; otherwise, if any marker is `INDETERMINATE` then `overall_result` is `INDETERMINATE`; and only when none of those conditions holds is `overall_result` `NON_REACTIVE`. The panel moves `DRAWN → RESULTED` with `resulted_at` stamped from the logical clock, and the donor's `serology_status` is republished from the verdict: `NON_REACTIVE` becomes `NEGATIVE`, `REACTIVE` becomes `REACTIVE`, and `INDETERMINATE` becomes `INDETERMINATE`. The donor's risk score is then recomputed under the `POST_SEROLOGY_RECONCILED` variant and `screening_risk_variant` is set to that value. Both the panel's and the donor's `version` increase by 1.

A reactive verdict is the harshest event in the donor lifecycle, and its consequence depends on how far the donor has already travelled. A donor in `SCREENING`, `CONSENT_OBTAINED`, `APPROVED_FOR_RECOVERY` or `DEFERRED` moves to `RULED_OUT`, which is terminal — the bank will recover nothing from that donor. A donor already in `RECOVERED` **stays** in `RECOVERED`, because tissue physically exists and cannot be un-recovered; instead the reactive retest opens a lookback investigation on that donor with `trigger_reason` `POSITIVE_RETEST` and `source_report_id` null, in `status` `OPEN`, which raises the donor's `lookback_flag` to true and runs the full quarantine cascade described in section 3.9. In both cases `serology_status` becomes `REACTIVE` and the donor's `version` is incremented.

Release for recovery is the medical director's decision and the last gate before a technician is sent. It requires the donor to be in `CONSENT_OBTAINED` — any other status is `ERR_DONOR_STATE_INVALID` — and requires `serology_status` to be anything other than `REACTIVE`, a reactive donor being refused with `ERR_SEROLOGY_REACTIVE`. It also requires that the recovery window still be open: the logical clock must read strictly earlier than `recovery_deadline_at`, and an approval attempted at or after that instant is refused with `ERR_PRESERVATION_WINDOW_EXCEEDED`, since no preservation inside the window would then be possible. On success the donor advances `CONSENT_OBTAINED → APPROVED_FOR_RECOVERY` and its `version` is incremented; nothing else changes.

Two revision paths exist in the donor lifecycle. Deferral, available to a coordinator and to a medical director alike, is legal from `SCREENING`, `CONSENT_OBTAINED` or `APPROVED_FOR_RECOVERY`; it records `deferral_reason` and moves the donor to `DEFERRED`. Deferring a donor who was already approved has teeth: **every** recovery of that donor whose `status` is `DISPATCHED` or `ON_SITE` is aborted, processed in ascending `created_at` order, with `abort_reason` set to the literal `DONOR_DEFERRED`; each abort decrements `open_dispatch_count` on that recovery's technician and increments that technician's `version` by 1. Reinstatement is the return edge, reserved to the medical director: it is legal only from `DEFERRED`, requires `serology_status` to be anything other than `REACTIVE`, clears `deferral_reason` back to null and returns the donor to `SCREENING`. Consent already captured is preserved across the round trip — the donor need only be taken through consent again if `consent_scope` is still `NONE`. The `SCREENING → DEFERRED → SCREENING` cycle may repeat any number of times.

### 3.3. The Technician Roster, Dispatch Overlap and Recovery to Preservation

A technician record carries `full_name`, between 1 and 80 characters; `certification_level`, either `RECOVERY_ONLY` for a technician who may only recover tissue or `RECOVERY_AND_EVAL` for one who may also grade it; and `certification_expires_at`, the instant the certification lapses, after which the bank refuses to let the technician work. Every technician is created `ACTIVE` with `open_dispatch_count` 0. The `status` field, either `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE`, is toggled by the coordinator through the roster-availability operation, which sets the supplied value and increments the technician's `version`; an `INACTIVE` technician can neither be dispatched to a recovery nor record an evaluation, and both refusals use `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED`. The `open_dispatch_count` field is a maintained count of that technician's recoveries whose `status` is `DISPATCHED` or `ON_SITE`; it rises by 1 on every dispatch and falls by 1 on every completion and every abort, with the technician's `version` incremented on each such change.

Dispatching a technician to a donor creates a recovery. The coordinator supplies `donor_id`, `technician_id`, `dispatched_at` and `planned_release_at`. The donor must be in `APPROVED_FOR_RECOVERY` — no earlier status is dispatchable and none later is either — and `dispatched_at` must not be earlier than that donor's `death_at`, an earlier instant being an invalid request. `planned_release_at` must be strictly later than `dispatched_at`; equal or inverted instants are an invalid request. The technician must be `ACTIVE` and their certification must still be valid at the moment of dispatch: an `INACTIVE` technician, or one whose `certification_expires_at` is at or before the current logical instant, is refused with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED`, the expiry boundary being inclusive so that a certification expiring exactly now is already lapsed. The new recovery is created in `DISPATCHED` with `arrival_at`, `preservation_at`, `preservation_medium`, `abort_reason`, `death_to_preservation_minutes` and `time_limit_band` all null and `recovered_lateralities` an empty list.

Overlap prevention is the interesting part of dispatch, because a technician standing at one hospital cannot simultaneously be standing at another. Every recovery occupies its technician over an interval that begins at `dispatched_at` and ends at `occupation_end_at`. That end is computed as **the recovery's `preservation_at` once the recovery has reached `COMPLETED`, and otherwise its `planned_release_at`**, in either case advanced by a fixed turnaround allowance of **one hour — 60 minutes** — covering transport and instrument reprocessing. The interval is **half-open on the right**: it includes its start instant and excludes `occupation_end_at`. The candidate set against which a proposed dispatch is checked is every recovery already recorded for that same technician whose `status` is `DISPATCHED`, `ON_SITE` or `COMPLETED`; recoveries in `ABORTED` are excluded entirely, because an aborted dispatch releases the technician. The proposed dispatch conflicts with a candidate when the proposal's `dispatched_at` is strictly earlier than the candidate's `occupation_end_at` **and** the candidate's `dispatched_at` is strictly earlier than the proposal's `occupation_end_at`. If any candidate conflicts, the dispatch is refused with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_DISPATCH_OVERLAP`. Because the interval excludes its right endpoint, **adjacency is legal**: a proposal whose `dispatched_at` equals a candidate's `occupation_end_at` produces no conflict and is admitted. All instant comparisons here are made at millisecond resolution.

The dispatched technician marks arrival by supplying `arrival_at`, which moves the recovery `DISPATCHED → ON_SITE` and increments its `version`; the technician performing this must be the technician named in the recovery's `technician_id`, and any other technician is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

Completion is where corneas come into existence. The recovering technician supplies `preservation_at`, the instant the cornea entered the preservation medium; `preservation_medium`, either `OPTISOL_GS` or `LIFE4C`; and `recovered_lateralities`, an ordered list of one or two members drawn from `OD` and `OS` with no duplicates, an empty list, a list longer than two, or a list containing the same laterality twice all being invalid requests. Only a recovery in `ON_SITE` may complete. `preservation_at` must also not be earlier than the recovery's own `arrival_at`, since the cornea cannot be preserved before the technician arrived, an inversion being an invalid request. **The deadline is enforced here and it is enforced strictly:** a `preservation_at` strictly later than the donor's `recovery_deadline_at` is refused with `ERR_PRESERVATION_WINDOW_EXCEEDED`, while a `preservation_at` exactly equal to the deadline is accepted. The donor's `consent_scope` must also no longer be `NONE` by this point, a consentless donor's recovery completion being refused with `ERR_CONSENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. On success, `death_to_preservation_minutes` is set to the number of whole minutes from the donor's `death_at` to `preservation_at`, floored, and `time_limit_band` is banded from that figure: `WITHIN_STANDARD` when it is at most twelve hours (720 minutes), `WITHIN_EXTENDED` when it is more than 720 but at most fifteen hours (900 minutes), and `EXCEEDED` when it is greater still. Because the deadline check has already passed, the `EXCEEDED` band is only reachable for a donor whose `recovery_window_minutes` itself exceeds 900 — that is, for a promptly cooled asystole donor.

The same request then creates one cornea tissue per member of `recovered_lateralities`, **in the order the lateralities were listed**, so that a list naming `OS` before `OD` produces the `OS` tissue with the earlier `created_at`. Each tissue copies `recovery_id` from the completing recovery, `donor_id` from that recovery's donor, `laterality` from the list member, `preservation_medium` and `preserved_at` from the completion, and is created with `disposition_status` `UNGRADED`, `custody_status` `IN_HOUSE`, `tissue_quality_score` and `classification` null, `issue_count` 0, `reissue_generation` 0, `quarantine_reason` and `disposal_method` null, and `shelf_life_variant` `PROVISIONAL_AT_RECOVERY`. No two tissues of the same recovery may share a laterality, which the no-duplicates rule on the input guarantees. The recovery moves `ON_SITE → COMPLETED`, the donor moves `APPROVED_FOR_RECOVERY → RECOVERED` with `tissues_recovered_count` set to the number of tissues just created, the technician's `open_dispatch_count` falls by 1, and the `version` of the recovery, the donor and the technician each increase by 1.

The provisional shelf life credited at that moment charges only the penalty the bank already knows about. The medium sets the base: **`OPTISOL_GS` gives 336 hours (fourteen days) and `LIFE4C` gives 264 hours (eleven days)**. From that base, 48 hours are deducted when the recovery's `time_limit_band` is `WITHIN_EXTENDED`, and nothing is deducted for the other two bands. The result is then raised to a floor of 24 hours if it fell below, and stored in `shelf_life_hours`, with `expires_at` set to `preserved_at` advanced by that many whole hours. The donor's cooling history and the endothelial cell count are deliberately **not** charged under this variant, because grading has not happened yet; the provisional figure is therefore always greater than or equal to the final figure computed at classification.

A dispatch that produces no tissue is aborted instead, either by the assigned recovery technician or by a coordinator. Abort is legal from `DISPATCHED` or from `ON_SITE`, records the supplied `abort_reason`, moves the recovery to the terminal `ABORTED` status, and releases the technician: `open_dispatch_count` falls by 1 and the recovery drops out of the overlap candidate set, so the freed window becomes immediately dispatchable. **The donor's status is not touched** — the donor remains in `APPROVED_FOR_RECOVERY` and may be dispatched again, possibly to a different technician. The `version` of the recovery and of the technician each increase by 1.

### 3.4. Grading the Tissue

Grading is performed by an evaluation technician and consists of two disjoint examinations of the same cornea. Each evaluation names `evaluator_technician_id`, the technician who performed the exam; `method`, either `SLIT_LAMP` or `SPECULAR`; `performed_at`, the instant of the exam; and `expected_tissue_version`, the optimistic-concurrency handle described in section 2.6. The `tissue_id` is taken from the path, not from the body.

The two methods supply **disjoint** measurement sets, and the request is rejected if the wrong set is supplied. A `SLIT_LAMP` evaluation requires `epithelium_defect_grade`, `stromal_clarity_grade` and `dm_folds_grade`, each a whole number from 0 through 4, and it must omit `guttata_grade` and `endothelial_cell_count`. A `SPECULAR` evaluation requires `guttata_grade`, a whole number from 0 through 2, and `endothelial_cell_count`, the endothelial cell density in cells per square millimetre, a whole number from 500 through 4000, and it must omit the three slit-lamp grades. A missing required grade, a supplied forbidden grade, or a grade outside its range is a schema violation and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Two personnel gates guard the write. The bank enforces **segregation of duties**: the technician who recovered a cornea may not be the technician who grades it. Concretely, if the named `evaluator_technician_id` equals the `technician_id` of the recovery that produced this tissue, the evaluation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Separately, the named evaluator must be fit to grade — `status` `ACTIVE`, a `certification_expires_at` strictly later than the current logical instant, and a `certification_level` of `RECOVERY_AND_EVAL`, because a `RECOVERY_ONLY` technician may recover but never grade. All three of those failures are reported as `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CERTIFIED`, and the segregation-of-duties check is evaluated **before** them, so a recovering technician who is also uncertified is reported as a segregation-of-duties violation. The acting user must be the technician named as evaluator; grading on another technician's behalf is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

Evaluations are append-only and versioned by method. When an evaluation is recorded, it is created with `status` `RECORDED`, and **any earlier evaluation of the same tissue with the same `method`** moves to `SUPERSEDED`; evaluations of the other method are untouched. The consequence is an invariant the classification step relies on: at most one `RECORDED` evaluation exists per method per tissue. Each write increments the tissue's `version` by 1 and stores that post-write value in the new evaluation's `tissue_version_after`, which is the handle the client must echo as `expected_tissue_version` on its next evaluation of the same tissue.

A tissue accepts an evaluation only while it is still gradeable — that is, while its `disposition_status` is neither `IMPLANTED` nor `DISCARDED`; grading either of those is refused with `ERR_TISSUE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Re-grading a tissue that has already been classified is therefore permitted and supersedes the earlier reading of that method, but it does **not** move `expires_at`, because the final shelf life is frozen once classification has applied it.

### 3.5. Classification, the Quality Score and the Final Shelf Life

Classification is the medical director's single act that converts a graded cornea into inventory with a regulatory fate. It is legal only for a tissue whose `disposition_status` is still `UNGRADED`, and it requires that the tissue already carry **one `RECORDED` `SLIT_LAMP` evaluation and one `RECORDED` `SPECULAR` evaluation**; a tissue missing either is refused with `ERR_EVALUATION_INCOMPLETE`. The director may optionally supply `intended_use_override` with the value `TRANSPLANT`, asserting that the tissue is being classified with transplant intent. That assertion is checked against the donor's screening: when `intended_use_override` is `TRANSPLANT` and the donor's `screening_risk_score` is 50 or above, the classification is refused with `ERR_SCREENING_RISK_TOO_HIGH`. When no override is supplied the check does not apply. Beyond that gate, `intended_use_override` does not itself alter the classification the decision table produces. Three further donor conditions bear on classification. The donor's `consent_scope` must not be `NONE`, refused with `ERR_CONSENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. The donor's `serology_status` must not be `PENDING` — a resulted, non-invalidated panel must exist — refused with `ERR_SEROLOGY_PENDING`, because the bank will not assign a regulatory fate to tissue whose serology is unknown. The donor's `lookback_flag` must be false, refused with `ERR_DONOR_UNDER_LOOKBACK`. Note that a **reactive** donor's tissue *is* classifiable: reactive serology is not a gate on this operation, because row 1 of the decision table below routes such tissue straight to `UNSUITABLE` and hence to disposal, which is exactly the outcome the bank wants recorded.

The quality score is a composite of the two examinations and the donor's age, and it is worth naming each component. Let the specular reading be the tissue's `RECORDED` `SPECULAR` evaluation and the slit-lamp reading be its `RECORDED` `SLIT_LAMP` evaluation; exactly one of each exists, because a newer evaluation of the same method supersedes the older. **Endothelial points** reward cell density above a baseline of 1800 cells per square millimetre: subtract 1800 from `endothelial_cell_count`, divide by 50 and take the floor, then raise the result to 0 if it went negative and cap it at 40 — the clamps are applied in exactly that order, so a count of 1799 earns zero rather than a negative contribution and a count of 4000 earns 40 rather than 44. **Clarity points** are 25 minus five times `stromal_clarity_grade`, so a perfectly clear stroma earns 25 and a grade of 4 earns 5. **Epithelium points** are 15 minus four times `epithelium_defect_grade`. **Folds points** are 10 minus three times `dm_folds_grade`. **Guttata points** are 10 minus five times `guttata_grade`, so a guttata grade of 2 earns 0. Finally an **age adjustment** is applied: minus 8 points when the donor's `age_years` is 71 or more, zero when `age_years` is 46 or more but less than 71, and plus 5 points for a donor younger than 46. The five component figures and the age adjustment are added together, and the sum is then raised to at least 0 and lowered to at most 100. The result is stored in `tissue_quality_score`.

The `classification` itself comes from an **ordered decision table in which the first matching row wins and no later row is consulted**. Read in order:

1. When the donor's `serology_status` is `REACTIVE`, the classification is `UNSUITABLE`.
2. Otherwise, when the donor's `consent_scope` is `RESEARCH_ONLY`, the classification is `RESEARCH`.
3. Otherwise, when the donor's `consent_scope` is `TRAINING_ONLY`, the classification is `TRAINING`.
4. Otherwise, when the donor's `screening_risk_score` is 50 or above, the classification is `RESEARCH`.
5. Otherwise, when the donor's `serology_status` is `INDETERMINATE`, the classification is `RESEARCH`.
6. Otherwise, when the recovery's `time_limit_band` is `EXCEEDED`, the classification is `UNSUITABLE`.
7. Otherwise, when `tissue_quality_score` is 70 or above **and** the specular `endothelial_cell_count` is 2000 or above **and** the recovery's `time_limit_band` is `WITHIN_STANDARD`, the classification is `TRANSPLANT_OPTICAL`.
8. Otherwise, when `tissue_quality_score` is 45 or above **and** the specular `endothelial_cell_count` is 1800 or above, the classification is `TRANSPLANT_THERAPEUTIC`.
9. Otherwise, when the donor's `consent_scope` is `TRANSPLANT_AND_RESEARCH`, the classification is `RESEARCH`.
10. Otherwise, the classification is `UNSUITABLE`.

Every threshold in that table is inclusive. The ordering carries real force: rows 2 and 3 fire **even when the quality score would comfortably support a transplant**, because the consent scope caps the permitted use and no amount of tissue quality can widen it; and rows 4 and 5 fire **even when the consent scope permits transplant**, because donor risk and indeterminate serology are absolute bars to grafting. Note also that row 9 is only ever reached by a tissue that failed both transplant tests, so a `TRANSPLANT_AND_RESEARCH` donor's good tissue is classified for transplant and its poor tissue is diverted to research rather than being discarded.

The classification determines the tissue's disposition in the same request. `TRANSPLANT_OPTICAL` and `TRANSPLANT_THERAPEUTIC` both move `disposition_status` from `UNGRADED` to `ELIGIBLE`, making the tissue allocatable. `RESEARCH` moves it to `RELEASED_FOR_RESEARCH` and `TRAINING` to `RELEASED_FOR_TRAINING`; both are non-allocatable resting states from which only disposal is possible. `UNSUITABLE` moves it directly to `DISCARDED`, and because that outcome is physical destruction the same request also sets `disposal_method` to `INCINERATION` and moves `custody_status` from `IN_HOUSE` to `DESTROYED`. The tissue's `version` is incremented by 1, and the inventory ledger of section 2.11 is recomputed.

Classification is also the moment the shelf life is finalised, and the final figure can be materially shorter than the provisional one. The base is again the medium's allowance — 336 hours for `OPTISOL_GS`, 264 hours for `LIFE4C` — and the same 48-hour band penalty applies when the recovery's `time_limit_band` is `WITHIN_EXTENDED`. Two further penalties now attach. A **cooling penalty of 72 hours** is charged when the donor's `body_cooled_at` is null, meaning the body was never refrigerated, **or** when the number of whole floored minutes from `death_at` to `body_cooled_at` is strictly greater than 480 — that is, cooling began more than eight hours after death. A **low-endothelium penalty of 24 hours** is charged when the tissue's `RECORDED` `SPECULAR` evaluation reports an `endothelial_cell_count` below 2200. The base less all three applicable penalties is then raised to a floor of 24 hours, stored in `shelf_life_hours`, and `expires_at` is recomputed as `preserved_at` advanced by that many whole hours. `shelf_life_variant` becomes `FINAL_AT_CLASSIFICATION`. **Once that variant has been applied the figure is frozen**: a later re-evaluation of the tissue, even one reporting a very different endothelial count, does not move `shelf_life_hours` or `expires_at`.

### 3.6. Surgeons, Request Priority and the Allocation Selector

A surgeon is registered with `full_name` (1 to 80 characters), `center_code`, the transplant centre code (1 to 16 characters), and `specialty`, one of `PK`, `DSAEK`, `DMEK` or `THERAPEUTIC`. Registration sets `credential_status` to `ACTIVE` and the four maintained counters `gross_billed_cents`, `credited_fee_cents`, `outstanding_fee_cents` and `substantiated_reaction_count` all to 0. The medical director may later set `credential_status` to `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED`, which increments the surgeon's `version`. **Only an `ACTIVE` surgeon may open a request, hold a new allocation, confirm one or receive a shipment**; a surgeon on `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_SURGEON_NOT_CREDENTIALED`. Because credentialing is re-checked at confirmation, a surgeon suspended between reservation and confirmation loses the ability to take delivery of a tissue already reserved for them.

A request names `surgeon_id`; `procedure_type`, one of `PK`, `DSAEK`, `DMEK` or `THERAPEUTIC`; `surgery_date`, the scheduled surgery instant, which must be strictly later than the current logical instant, an equal or earlier instant being refused with `ERR_SURGERY_DATE_IN_PAST`; `min_ecd`, the minimum acceptable endothelial cell density, a whole number from 500 through 4000; `max_donor_age_years`, the maximum acceptable donor age in whole years, from 0 through 110; `laterality_preference`, one of `OD`, `OS` or `ANY`; and `urgency`, either `ROUTINE` or `URGENT`. A request is created `OPEN` with `cancel_reason` null. Only the requesting surgeon themselves or a coordinator may open a request on that surgeon's behalf, and a surgeon naming a different `surgeon_id` is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

Priority scoring proceeds in **three pinned steps and the order of the steps is part of the contract**. In step one, an urgency weight of 30 is credited for `URGENT` and 10 for `ROUTINE`; separately, the number of whole days from the current logical instant to `surgery_date` is computed by flooring, and this figure may be negative for a request whose surgery date has already passed. Surgery-proximity points are then awarded from that day count: 40 points when it is 2 or fewer, 25 points when it is between 3 and 7 inclusive, 10 points when it is between 8 and 21 inclusive, and 0 points otherwise. `base_score` is the sum of the urgency weight and the proximity points. In step two, `priority_tier` is selected **from `base_score` alone**: `PLATINUM` when `base_score` is 60 or more, `GOLD` when it is at least 40 but less than 60, and `STANDARD` otherwise. In step three a tier bonus of 15 for `PLATINUM`, 8 for `GOLD` and 0 for `STANDARD` is added to `base_score` to produce `priority_score`.

The separation between `base_score` and `priority_score` matters because the bonus can carry a request's total into a higher tier's band, and **re-deriving the tier from `priority_score` is forbidden**. Consider an `URGENT` request whose surgery is four days away: the urgency weight is 30, the proximity points are 25, `base_score` is 55, the tier is therefore `GOLD`, the bonus is 8, and `priority_score` is 63. A system that then re-read 63 against the tier bands would call the request `PLATINUM` and award a bonus of 15 for a `priority_score` of 70. That is wrong: the stored `priority_tier` is `GOLD` and the stored `priority_score` is 63. The three figures are recomputed from scratch — with the clock read afresh, so the day count and hence the score can change — whenever a request is opened, whenever an allocated request is returned to `OPEN`, and at the start of every allocation run.

A request may be cancelled by its surgeon or by a coordinator while it is `OPEN` or `ALLOCATED`. Cancellation records `cancel_reason`, moves the request to the terminal `CANCELLED` status and increments its `version`. If a `RESERVED` allocation exists for that request, that allocation moves to `CANCELLED` with its own `cancel_reason` set to the literal `REQUEST_CANCELLED`, and its tissue returns from `RESERVED` to `ELIGIBLE`. Because a `RESERVED` allocation never entered any money sum, **all three of the surgeon's money fields are unchanged** by a cancellation.

Manual allocation reserves exactly one tissue against exactly one request. The coordinator supplies `tissue_id` and `request_id`. The tissue must be `ELIGIBLE` and the request must be `OPEN`. On success the allocation is created with `allocated_at` set to the current logical instant, `status` `RESERVED` and `source` `MANUAL`; `reserved_ecd_snapshot` is pinned from the `endothelial_cell_count` of the tissue's `RECORDED` `SPECULAR` evaluation and `reserved_classification_snapshot` is pinned from the tissue's `classification`, which at this point is necessarily `TRANSPLANT_OPTICAL` or `TRANSPLANT_THERAPEUTIC`. Those snapshots are deliberately frozen: the fee is keyed on the snapshot, never on any later value the tissue may acquire. `expiry_margin_hours` records the number of whole hours from the request's `surgery_date` to the tissue's `expires_at`, floored, and may legitimately be 0 when the tissue expires within the hour after surgery. `cancel_reason` is null. The tissue moves `ELIGIBLE → RESERVED` while its `issue_count` stays where it was — issuing happens at dispatch, not at reservation — the request moves `OPEN → ALLOCATED`, and the `version` of both the tissue and the request increases by 1. The surgeon's money fields are untouched, because a `RESERVED` allocation has not yet been billed.

Several safety gates guard that reservation, and they are stated in full, in their evaluation order, in section 2.10's ladder. In summary: the donor's `consent_scope` must permit transplantation, meaning it must be `TRANSPLANT_ONLY` or `TRANSPLANT_AND_RESEARCH`, and any other scope — including `NONE`, `RESEARCH_ONLY` and `TRAINING_ONLY` — is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`; the donor's `serology_status` must not be `REACTIVE`, refused with `ERR_SEROLOGY_REACTIVE`; the donor's `lookback_flag` must be false, refused otherwise with `ERR_DONOR_UNDER_LOOKBACK`; the receiving surgeon's `credential_status` must be `ACTIVE`, refused with `ERR_SURGEON_NOT_CREDENTIALED`; the tissue's `expires_at` must be strictly later than the current logical instant, refused with `ERR_TISSUE_EXPIRED`; the tissue's `expires_at` must not be earlier than the request's `surgery_date`, refused with `ERR_EXPIRY_BEFORE_SURGERY_DATE`; the tissue's `classification` must be `TRANSPLANT_OPTICAL` or `TRANSPLANT_THERAPEUTIC` and must permit the request's `procedure_type`, refused with `ERR_PROCEDURE_CLASSIFICATION_MISMATCH`; the specular `endothelial_cell_count` must be at least `min_ecd`, refused with `ERR_ECD_BELOW_REQUEST_MINIMUM`; the donor's `age_years` must not exceed `max_donor_age_years`, refused with `ERR_DONOR_AGE_ABOVE_REQUEST_MAX`; and the tissue's `reissue_generation` must be at most 1, a twice-returned tissue being refused with `ERR_REISSUE_LIMIT_REACHED`. Note that `laterality_preference` is **not** enforced on the manual path — a coordinator reserving by hand may deliberately override the surgeon's stated eye preference — whereas the allocation run does honour it as part of its match filter.

**Procedure compatibility** is asymmetric and it is worth stating the two rows explicitly. A tissue classified `TRANSPLANT_OPTICAL` may be matched to a request whose `procedure_type` is `PK`, `DSAEK`, `DMEK` or `THERAPEUTIC` — optical-grade tissue is acceptable for every procedure. A tissue classified `TRANSPLANT_THERAPEUTIC` may be matched only to a `THERAPEUTIC` or a `PK` request; the lamellar procedures `DSAEK` and `DMEK` demand optical-grade tissue and a therapeutic tissue offered for either is a mismatch.

**Day capacity** limits a surgeon to two grafts per calendar day. The bucket is the UTC calendar date of the request's `surgery_date`. At the moment of reservation, and again at confirmation, the surgeon must hold **at most one other** allocation whose `status` is `RESERVED`, `CONFIRMED` or `CONSUMED` and whose request's `surgery_date` falls on that same UTC date. A third such allocation is refused with `ERR_SURGEON_DAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Allocations in `RELEASED` or `CANCELLED` do not count against the bucket, so a released graft frees a slot for the same day.

The **processing fee** is pinned at reservation and is computed from the snapshot classification. The base is 245000 cents for `TRANSPLANT_OPTICAL` and 189000 cents for `TRANSPLANT_THERAPEUTIC`. Three surcharges may attach: an urgency surcharge of 7.25 percent of the base when the request's `urgency` is `URGENT`; a procedure surcharge of 4.25 percent of the base when the `procedure_type` is `DMEK` or `DSAEK`; and a short-notice surcharge of 1.75 percent of the base when the floored whole days from the current logical instant to `surgery_date` is 2 or fewer. Each applicable surcharge is **rounded half up to the whole cent independently, and only then are the rounded surcharges added to the base**. Summing the raw percentages and rounding once is forbidden and produces a different answer. On a base of 245000 cents with all three surcharges applicable, the raw amounts are 17762.5, 10412.5 and 4287.5, which round to 17763, 10413 and 4288 respectively, summing to 32464 for a `processing_fee_cents` of 277464. Summing first would give 32462.5, rounding to 32463 and a total of 277463 — one cent short, and therefore wrong.

The allocation run is the bank's batch matcher, and it executes as an ordered waterfall. **Step zero is an expiry sweep:** every request whose `status` is `OPEN` and whose `surgery_date` is at or before the current logical instant moves `OPEN → EXPIRED`, and its identifier is reported in `expired_request_ids`. The sweep runs before any matching, so a request whose surgery date has passed can never be matched. **Step one builds the request candidate set** from exactly three predicates: the request's `status` is `OPEN`, its surgeon's `credential_status` is `ACTIVE`, and its `surgery_date` is strictly later than the current logical instant. **Step two orders that set for processing** by `priority_score` descending, then by `surgery_date` ascending, then by the request's `created_at` ascending; `created_at` is the final tie-break and the request's `id` is never consulted. **Step three recomputes the tissue candidate pool before each request is served**, admitting a tissue only when its `disposition_status` is `ELIGIBLE`, its `custody_status` is `IN_HOUSE` or `RETURNED_TO_BANK`, its `classification` is `TRANSPLANT_OPTICAL` or `TRANSPLANT_THERAPEUTIC`, its `reissue_generation` is at most 1, its `expires_at` is strictly later than the current logical instant, its donor's `lookback_flag` is false, and it has not already been reserved earlier in this same run. **Step four applies the per-pair match filter**, which is the same compatibility set the manual path enforces: the specular `endothelial_cell_count` at least `min_ecd`; the donor's `age_years` at most `max_donor_age_years`; the `laterality_preference` either `ANY` or equal to the tissue's `laterality`; the request's `procedure_type` among those permitted for the tissue's `classification`; and the tissue's `expires_at` not earlier than the request's `surgery_date`. **Step five chooses among the matching tissues** by `expires_at` ascending — the soonest-expiring acceptable tissue is spent first, which is the whole point of an expiry-driven selector — then by the specular `endothelial_cell_count` ascending, so that the weakest acceptable tissue goes out and the best tissue is preserved for a later request, then by the tissue's `created_at` ascending. **Step six defines the fallbacks:** a request whose step-four result is empty stays `OPEN` and its identifier joins `unmatched_request_ids`; and when **every** request in the candidate set ends with an empty result — including the degenerate cases where the request set or the tissue pool is itself empty — the entire run is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TISSUE` and **no state change of any kind is persisted, not even the step-zero expiry sweep**.

Each match creates an allocation exactly as the manual path would, but with `source` `RUN`, including the pinned snapshots and the processing fee; each matched tissue moves `ELIGIBLE → RESERVED`, each matched request moves `OPEN → ALLOCATED`, and the `version` of every tissue and request the run touched is incremented by 1. The surgeon money ledger is untouched, because every allocation a run creates is `RESERVED`. The run returns an object rather than a resource, carrying `run_at` set to the current logical instant, `allocations_created` as the number of matches, `allocation_ids` listing the created allocations in the order they were created, `unmatched_request_ids` and `expired_request_ids`.

### 3.7. Confirmation, Billing and Shipping under Cold Chain

Confirmation is the coordinator's commitment to ship, and it is the moment the surgeon is billed. It is legal only for an allocation in `RESERVED`, and it re-checks three things that may have changed since reservation: the surgeon's `credential_status` must still be `ACTIVE`, the tissue's `expires_at` must still be at or after the request's `surgery_date`, and the surgeon's day capacity for that surgery date must still admit the graft. On success the allocation moves `RESERVED → CONFIRMED`, and the money ledger moves with it: the surgeon's `gross_billed_cents` and `outstanding_fee_cents` each increase by exactly that allocation's `processing_fee_cents`, while `credited_fee_cents` is unchanged. The `version` of the allocation and of the surgeon each increase by 1. An allocation may instead be cancelled while `RESERVED`, which records `cancel_reason`, moves it to `CANCELLED`, returns the tissue from `RESERVED` to `ELIGIBLE` **without touching `custody_status`**, and returns the request from `ALLOCATED` to `OPEN` with its priority recomputed against the clock as it now stands. All three of the surgeon's money fields are unchanged by that cancellation, because a `RESERVED` allocation was never billed.

A shipment is prepared against a `CONFIRMED` allocation. The coordinator supplies `allocation_id`; `carrier`, either `COURIER` or `AIR`; and optionally `replaces_shipment_id`, which marks the shipment as a re-issue. When `replaces_shipment_id` is supplied it must reference a shipment whose `status` is `RETURNED` **and** whose allocation covers the same tissue as the new shipment's allocation; anything else — a shipment in another status, or a returned shipment for a different tissue — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REISSUE_SOURCE`. On a first issue the field is null. The shipment is created in `PREPARED` with `shipped_at`, `delivered_at`, `min_temp_c`, `max_temp_c`, `temp_excursion_minutes_over` and `integrity_verdict` all null.

Temperature monitoring runs from preparation through transit. A reading may be appended while the shipment is `PREPARED` or `IN_TRANSIT`, by a coordinator packing the parcel or by the receiving surgeon reading the logger on arrival, and it carries `recorded_at`, the instant the logger sampled, and `temperature_c`, the sampled temperature in degrees Celsius at one decimal place, between minus 40.0 and 60.0 inclusive. The `shipment_id` is taken from the path. The reading must fall inside the shipment's monitoring window: `recorded_at` must be at or after the shipment's own `created_at`, and — once `shipped_at` has been set — at or before `shipped_at` advanced by 168 hours, which is seven days. For a shipment still in `PREPARED` the `shipped_at` bound does not yet exist, so only the lower bound is enforced. A reading outside the window is refused with `ERR_READING_OUTSIDE_SHIPMENT_WINDOW`. Temperature readings carry only `id` and `created_at` beyond their own fields; they have no `updated_at` and no `version`, and they are never modified. Appending a reading refreshes the shipment's `min_temp_c` and `max_temp_c` across all readings recorded so far and increments the shipment's `version` by 1 — note that these running figures are provisional working values; delivery recomputes both over the delivery window only, as section 3.8 describes.

Dispatch hands the parcel to the carrier. The coordinator supplies `shipped_at`. The shipment must be `PREPARED`, its allocation must be `CONFIRMED`, the receiving surgeon must be `ACTIVE`, the tissue's `expires_at` must still be at or after the request's `surgery_date`, the tissue's `reissue_generation` must be at most 1, and — critically — the shipment must already carry **at least one temperature reading**, the pre-dispatch reading being the cold-chain baseline against which the first excursion segment is measured. A shipment with no readings is refused with `ERR_NO_TEMPERATURE_BASELINE`. On success `shipped_at` is stored, the shipment moves `PREPARED → IN_TRANSIT`, and the tissue moves on **both** dimensions in the same request: `disposition_status` goes `RESERVED → ISSUED` and `custody_status` goes `IN_HOUSE → IN_TRANSIT`, or `RETURNED_TO_BANK → IN_TRANSIT` when this is a re-issue of previously returned tissue. The tissue's `issue_count` increases by 1 — this is the only place it ever moves — and the `version` of the shipment and of the tissue each increase by 1.

### 3.8. Delivery, the Integrity Verdict, Implantation and Re-issue

Delivery is performed by the receiving surgeon, who supplies `delivered_at`. It is legal only for a shipment in `IN_TRANSIT` whose tissue's `custody_status` is `IN_TRANSIT`, and only for the surgeon reachable from the shipment's allocation. Delivery is where the cold chain is adjudicated, and the adjudication is worth walking through carefully because it is the most intricate calculation in the system.

First the **included set** is fixed: it is every temperature reading of this shipment whose `recorded_at` is at or after `shipped_at` **and** at or before `delivered_at`, both bounds inclusive. That set is ordered by `recorded_at` ascending, with the reading's own `created_at` ascending as the tie-break when two samples share an instant. Readings outside the window are excluded from the verdict entirely — they were rejected at write time in the case of the upper monitoring bound, and a pre-dispatch reading simply falls before `shipped_at`. The shipment's `min_temp_c` and `max_temp_c` are then recomputed as the smallest and largest `temperature_c` **within that included set**, replacing the provisional running values.

Next, **excursions** are located. A reading is out of range when its `temperature_c` is strictly below 2.0 degrees or strictly above 8.0 degrees; readings at exactly 2.0 or exactly 8.0 are in range. An excursion **segment** is a maximal run of consecutive out-of-range included readings — consecutive in the ordering just fixed, so a single in-range reading between two out-of-range readings splits them into two segments. The duration of a segment runs from the `recorded_at` of the included reading **immediately before the run**, or from `shipped_at` when the run begins at the very first included reading, to the `recorded_at` of the run's **last** reading. That is deliberately conservative in the bank's favour: the exposure is charged from the last known-good sample, not from the first bad one, so a logger sampling every fifteen minutes charges the full fifteen minutes preceding a bad reading. Each segment's duration is floored to whole minutes, and `temp_excursion_minutes_over` is the sum of those floored per-segment figures — the flooring happens per segment, before summation.

The `integrity_verdict` then follows. It is `PASS` when `temp_excursion_minutes_over` is exactly 0. It is `EXCURSION_MINOR` when `temp_excursion_minutes_over` is between 1 and 45 inclusive **and** `max_temp_c` is at most 12.0 degrees **and** `min_temp_c` is at least minus 5.0 degrees — a brief deviation that stayed within survivable bounds. It is `EXCURSION_MAJOR` in every other case, which covers a total above 45 minutes and also covers a short excursion that nonetheless spiked past 12.0 degrees or dropped below minus 5.0 degrees. The boundary is exact: 45 minutes of excursion is minor, 46 minutes is major.

The verdict then branches the whole request. On `PASS` or `EXCURSION_MINOR` the shipment moves `IN_TRANSIT → DELIVERED` and the tissue's `custody_status` moves `IN_TRANSIT → AT_SURGEON`, while `disposition_status` **stays** at `ISSUED`; nothing else changes and the money ledger is untouched. On `EXCURSION_MAJOR` the parcel is refused at the door and the whole commitment unwinds in one request: the shipment moves `IN_TRANSIT → REJECTED_ON_ARRIVAL` with `delivered_at` still recorded, the tissue's `custody_status` moves `IN_TRANSIT → RETURNED_TO_BANK` and its `disposition_status` moves `ISSUED → QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_reason` set to the literal `COLD_CHAIN_EXCURSION_MAJOR`, the allocation moves `CONFIRMED → RELEASED`, and its request moves `ALLOCATED → OPEN` with its priority recomputed. The money follows the release: the surgeon's `credited_fee_cents` increases by that allocation's `processing_fee_cents` and `outstanding_fee_cents` decreases by the same amount, while `gross_billed_cents` does not move — the bank never un-bills, it credits. **`reissue_generation` is not incremented on this path**, because the tissue never reached the surgeon's hands; the count exists to limit how many times a surgeon has physically held and returned a cornea. The `version` of the shipment and the tissue each increase by 1, and on the major-excursion branch the `version` of the allocation, the request and the surgeon each increase by 1 as well.

Implantation is the receiving surgeon's confirmation that the graft was used. It requires the tissue's `disposition_status` to be `ISSUED` and its `custody_status` to be `AT_SURGEON`, requires the tissue's `expires_at` to be strictly later than the current logical instant — a cornea that expired in the surgeon's fridge may not be grafted, refused with `ERR_TISSUE_EXPIRED` — and requires the delivering shipment's `integrity_verdict` to be `PASS` or `EXCURSION_MINOR`, refused otherwise with `ERR_SHIPMENT_INTEGRITY_FAILED`. On success `disposition_status` moves `ISSUED → IMPLANTED`, which is terminal, while `custody_status` **remains** `AT_SURGEON` — the tissue is in the patient and the bank never gets it back, but dimension B does not move. The allocation moves `CONFIRMED → CONSUMED` and its request moves `ALLOCATED → FULFILLED`, which is also terminal. **All three of the surgeon's money fields are unchanged by implantation**: because `CONFIRMED` and `CONSUMED` both count toward `gross_billed_cents` and neither counts toward `credited_fee_cents`, consuming an allocation moves no money. The `version` of the tissue, the allocation, the request and the surgeon each increase by 1.

Unused tissue comes back. The surgeon supplies `return_reason`, and return requires the same starting position as implantation — `disposition_status` `ISSUED`, `custody_status` `AT_SURGEON` — and the same ownership. In every case the `custody_status` moves `AT_SURGEON → RETURNED_TO_BANK`, the delivering shipment moves `DELIVERED → RETURNED`, `reissue_generation` increases by 1, the allocation moves `CONFIRMED → RELEASED` with its `cancel_reason` set to the literal `UNUSED_TISSUE_RETURNED`, and its request moves `ALLOCATED → OPEN` with its priority recomputed. The release credits the surgeon exactly as a cold-chain rejection does: `credited_fee_cents` increases by the allocation's `processing_fee_cents`, `outstanding_fee_cents` decreases by the same amount, and `gross_billed_cents` is unchanged. Where the tissue lands on dimension A depends on its condition. When the delivering shipment's `integrity_verdict` is `PASS` **and** the tissue's `expires_at` is strictly later than the current logical instant, `disposition_status` moves `ISSUED → ELIGIBLE` and the cornea rejoins the allocatable pool. Otherwise — a shipment that arrived with any excursion at all, including a minor one, or a tissue that has since expired — `disposition_status` moves `ISSUED → QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_reason` set to the supplied `return_reason`. Note the asymmetry: an `EXCURSION_MINOR` shipment is good enough to implant but not good enough to put a returned cornea back on the shelf. The `version` of the tissue, the shipment, the allocation, the request and the surgeon each increase by 1.

Re-issue closes the cycle. A tissue in `custody_status` `RETURNED_TO_BANK` and `disposition_status` `ELIGIBLE` may be allocated again, shipped under a new shipment whose `replaces_shipment_id` names the `RETURNED` shipment, and dispatched, which moves custody `RETURNED_TO_BANK → IN_TRANSIT` and disposition `RESERVED → ISSUED` again. The `reissue_generation` counter is the hard limit on this cycle: **a tissue whose `reissue_generation` has reached 2 can never be allocated or dispatched again**, and both attempts are refused with `ERR_REISSUE_LIMIT_REACHED`. Since the counter starts at 0 and rises by 1 on each completed return, a cornea may be sent out at most three times in total — an initial issue and two re-issues — and its `issue_count`, which counts dispatches rather than returns, records how many of those actually happened.

### 3.9. Quarantine, Disposal, Adverse Reactions and Lookback

Quarantine is the bank's pause button, available to the medical director and to a coordinator. It records the supplied `quarantine_reason` and moves the tissue's `disposition_status` to `QUARANTINED` from either `ELIGIBLE` or `RESERVED`; `custody_status` is not touched. When the tissue was `RESERVED`, the reservation is undone in the same request: the tissue's allocation in `RESERVED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` set to the literal `TISSUE_QUARANTINED`, and that allocation's request moves `ALLOCATED → OPEN` with its priority recomputed. Since a `RESERVED` allocation had not been billed, no money moves. Releasing a quarantine is the medical director's alone and returns the tissue from `QUARANTINED` to `ELIGIBLE`, clearing `quarantine_reason` back to null and again leaving `custody_status` untouched. Release is guarded: the tissue's `classification` must be `TRANSPLANT_OPTICAL` or `TRANSPLANT_THERAPEUTIC` — research, training and unsuitable tissue can never be returned to the allocatable pool, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_TISSUE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` — the donor's `lookback_flag` must be false, refused with `ERR_DONOR_UNDER_LOOKBACK`, and the donor's `serology_status` must not be `REACTIVE`, refused with `ERR_SEROLOGY_REACTIVE`. The `ELIGIBLE → QUARANTINED → ELIGIBLE` cycle may repeat any number of times.

Disposal is terminal. The acting director or coordinator supplies `disposal_method`, one of `INCINERATION`, `AUTOCLAVE` or `RESEARCH_TRANSFER`, and the tissue moves to `DISCARDED` on dimension A and to `DESTROYED` on dimension B in the same request. Disposal is legal from `ELIGIBLE`, `QUARANTINED`, `RELEASED_FOR_RESEARCH` and `RELEASED_FOR_TRAINING`, and never from `UNGRADED`, `RESERVED`, `ISSUED`, `IMPLANTED` or `DISCARDED`. **Tissue leaving `RELEASED_FOR_RESEARCH` must be disposed of with `disposal_method` `RESEARCH_TRANSFER`**, since transfer to a research institution is the disposal, and any other method on that path is an invalid request.

Adverse reactions are reported by the implanting surgeon. A report names `tissue_id`, the implanted cornea; `surgeon_id`, the reporting surgeon; `reaction_type`, one of `PRIMARY_GRAFT_FAILURE`, `ENDOPHTHALMITIS`, `INFECTIOUS_KERATITIS` or `REJECTION`; `severity`, either `MINOR` or `SERIOUS`; and `narrative`, a free-text clinical account between 1 and 2000 characters. The report is only accepted for a tissue the reporting surgeon actually grafted: the tissue's `disposition_status` must be `IMPLANTED` **and** the `surgeon_id` of the request behind that tissue's consumed allocation must equal the body's `surgeon_id`, with any other combination refused as `ERR_REPORT_TISSUE_NOT_IMPLANTED_BY_SURGEON`. The report is created with `reported_at` stamped from the logical clock, `status` `SUBMITTED` and `adjudication_note` null.

The medical director adjudicates every submitted report exactly once. Dismissal records `adjudication_note` and moves the report to `UNSUBSTANTIATED`; nothing else in the system changes — no lookback is opened, no tissue is quarantined, and the surgeon's `substantiated_reaction_count` does not move. Substantiation records `adjudication_note`, moves the report to `SUBSTANTIATED` and increments the reporting surgeon's `substantiated_reaction_count` by 1 — **for every substantiated report regardless of severity**. Beyond that, severity decides how far the consequences reach. A `MINOR` substantiated report stops there. A `SERIOUS` substantiated report additionally sets the donor's `lookback_flag` to true and opens a lookback investigation on that tissue's donor with `source_report_id` set to this report, `trigger_reason` `ADVERSE_REACTION` and `status` `OPEN`, running the full open cascade described below. The `version` of the report, the surgeon and the donor each increase by 1.

A lookback investigation may also be opened directly by the medical director, for post-donation information arriving outside the adverse-reaction and retest paths. The director supplies `donor_id` and optionally `source_report_id`; `trigger_reason` is set to `ADVERSE_REACTION` when a `source_report_id` was supplied and to `POSITIVE_RETEST` when it was not. The investigation is created with `opened_at` stamped from the logical clock, `status` `OPEN`, `notified_at`, `closed_at`, `closure_note` and `notified_recipient_count` all null, and the donor's `lookback_flag` set to true with the donor's `version` incremented. The same open cascade runs. A donor already under an open or notified investigation cannot have a second one opened; the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVESTIGATION_STATE_INVALID`.

The **open cascade** is where the investigation reaches back through the donor's whole cohort, and its scope and its ordering are both pinned. The donor's tissues are traversed in ascending `created_at` order, never in identifier order. `impacted_tissue_count` is set to the count of **every** tissue of that donor, with no status filter whatsoever — implanted, discarded, research-released and quarantined tissue all count, because the investigation's job is to state how many corneas came from this donor at all. The **quarantinable** subset is then those impacted tissues whose `disposition_status` is `ELIGIBLE` or `RESERVED`, and every one of them is moved to `QUARANTINED` — a reserved tissue's `RESERVED` allocation being cancelled with `cancel_reason` `TISSUE_QUARANTINED` and its request reopened to `OPEN`, exactly as a manual quarantine would. `quarantined_count` is the size of that subset. **An `ISSUED` tissue is deliberately not quarantined**: it has already left the bank's physical control, so quarantining it in place would be a fiction; it is instead accounted for through the notification step below.

Notification records that every reachable recipient has been contacted. It is legal only from `OPEN`, stamps `notified_at` from the logical clock, moves the investigation `OPEN → NOTIFIED` and sets `notified_recipient_count`. That count is the number of **distinct** surgeons reached by walking from each impacted tissue to its allocations, from each allocation to its surgeon request, and from that request to its surgeon, admitting only allocations whose `status` is `RESERVED`, `CONFIRMED`, `CONSUMED` or `RELEASED`; allocations in `CANCELLED` are excluded, since a cancelled reservation never put a surgeon in possession of anything. A surgeon holding several qualifying allocations for the donor's tissue is counted once. The investigation's `version` increases by 1.

Closure is the medical director's final act and it has two very different resolutions. The director supplies `resolution`, either `NO_ACTION` or `RECALL`, and `closure_note`; `closed_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Closure is legal only from `NOTIFIED` — an investigation may not be closed before its recipients have been notified. On `NO_ACTION` the investigation moves to `CLOSED_NO_ACTION`, the donor's `lookback_flag` is cleared back to false, and **every tissue this investigation quarantined** is returned from `QUARANTINED` to `ELIGIBLE`; the donor's cohort is back in circulation and the donor may yield allocatable tissue again. On `RECALL` the investigation moves to `CLOSED_WITH_RECALL`, the donor's `lookback_flag` **stays true permanently** — a recalled donor is never rehabilitated, so no tissue of that donor can ever be classified, released from quarantine or allocated again — and every tissue this investigation quarantined moves from `QUARANTINED` to `DISCARDED` with `disposal_method` `INCINERATION` and `custody_status` `DESTROYED`. In both cases the sweep visits the affected tissues in ascending `created_at` order, and the `version` of the investigation, the donor and every affected tissue each increase by 1.
