# Bank-Fintech Partnership Oversight API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A sponsor bank that rents its charter to fintech programs inherits every obligation those programs generate: the consumer complaints they attract, the disclosures they publish, the end-user funds they hold in the bank's name, and the regulatory exposure created when any of it goes wrong. The fintech program office inside the bank is the function accountable for that exposure, and today it runs on spreadsheets. Diligence scores live in one workbook, reserve balances in another, complaint counts in a third, and nobody can answer the question a regulator actually asks: *for this partner, right now, what is our supervisory posture and what evidence supports it?*

This document specifies the Bank-Fintech Partnership Oversight API, a single system of record for the full partnership lifecycle — from the first due-diligence file opened on a prospective fintech, through the program agreement that allocates responsibility for customer identification, dispute handling and marketing, through the daily mechanics of transaction monitoring, custody ledger reconciliation, settlement funding and complaint aggregation, to the wind-down plan that returns end-user funds and closes the custody accounts. Every partner carries a derived **oversight posture** that the system recomputes on every material event, and that posture is what gates marketing approvals, end-user inflows and program funding. The bank never has to decide by hand whether a partner has drifted out of tolerance; the ladder is arithmetic, and it is enforced at the point of every write.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and are worth stating up front. First, **all time is logical time**: the system reads a server-held logical clock rather than the host wall clock, so a deadline, a trailing-ninety-day window and a settlement period all evaluate deterministically. Second, **every cascade completes inside the request that triggered it**: booking a ledger entry recomputes the custody balance, the reserve requirement, the shortfall, the posture and — if the posture lands on the most severe rung — freezes the partner's custody accounts, all before the response is returned. The only deferred work in the system is the oversight cycle close, and that is itself an explicit synchronous request.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every collection in this system is mounted as a flat peer under the `/oversight` prefix. There are no nested resource URLs: a diligence review, a settlement batch and a complaint are all top-level collections, and the relationships between them travel in request bodies rather than in path segments. The sole exception to the prefix is the logical-clock control described in §2.4.

### 2.1. Program Actors and Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capability set that principal is exercising. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request is examined. If both are present but `X-User-Role` is not one of the five recognised role names, the rejection is `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401).

The five roles are `ProgramOfficer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `RiskCommittee`, `InternalAudit` and `FintechPartner`, spelled exactly as shown. A recognised role that is not listed among the authorised roles for the endpoint being called is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). The authorised-role list for each endpoint is given in the table in §4.

`ProgramOfficer` is sponsor-bank fintech program office staff and holds the broadest operational surface: it registers partner records, opens diligence files and submits them, drafts program agreements and routes them for approval, files marketing material for compliance review, opens settlement batches and reconciles them, books ledger entries, releases program funds to the partner and claws them back, opens and closes oversight cycles, and drafts, submits and executes wind-down plans. It submits artefacts for decision but never decides them: diligence and marketing outcomes belong to `ComplianceOfficer`, and agreement and wind-down outcomes belong to `RiskCommittee`. It is also the only role permitted to advance the logical clock.

`ComplianceOfficer` owns the compliance management system extended over the partner. It decides diligence files, approves or rejects marketing material and the disclosures inside it, raises transaction monitoring alerts and disposes of them including suspicious-activity filing, and assigns root causes to aggregated complaints and dispositions them. It cannot create the diligence files or marketing filings it decides, cannot move money or touch the custody ledger, and cannot decide program agreements or wind-down plans. `RiskCommittee` is the bank's credit and enterprise-risk approval body, scoped to the contractual layer: it executes or rejects program agreements, terminates an executed agreement on a contracted trigger, and approves or returns wind-down plans; it is read-only everywhere else. `InternalAudit` is third-line assurance exercising the audit-access right reserved in the program agreement. It holds read access across every collection and exactly two settlement-integrity writes: it may reconcile a settlement batch, an ability it shares with `ProgramOfficer`, and it is the only role that may clear a settlement variance once one has been raised. It authors no partner, diligence, agreement, marketing, complaint, alert, cycle or wind-down record and never releases or claws back funds.

The identity semantics differ across the five roles, and the difference matters for every ownership check in the system. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramOfficer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `RiskCommittee` or `InternalAudit`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow transition calls for an attribution — as `created_by_user_id`, `submitted_by_user_id`, `decided_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id` or `disposed_by_user_id` on the artefact being written — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `FintechPartner`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` **is** the `id` of the acting `Partner`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Partner` compare that resource's `partner_id` field to `X-User-ID`.

That partner scoping is enforced as a single rule. A `FintechPartner` request is refused with `ERR_PARTNER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403) whenever the `partner_id` of the target resource differs from its `X-User-ID` — the body `partner_id` on creates, the stored `partner_id` of the resource on action endpoints, and the path id when the partner reports its own account census. The same rule confines partner-visible list and read endpoints to rows bearing its own `partner_id`. A `FintechPartner` may file its own end-user account census, file marketing materials, resubmit a filing whose changes were requested (it is the only role that may resubmit), open its own settlement batches, book its own ledger entries and lodge end-user complaints arriving through its own channels. It may read its own program record, diligence files, program agreements, custody accounts, settlement batches, ledger entries, monitoring alerts, oversight cycles and wind-down plans. It cannot read the bank-internal oversight summary for any partner, cannot decide or approve anything, cannot move program funds, and can never see another partner's data.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds is a schema failure and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422).

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` states whether any matching row remains beyond the current page. Unless a specific endpoint declares otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Two collections declare their own order and are the only exceptions: ledger entries are returned ordered by `custody_account_id` and then by `sequence_no` ascending, and oversight cycles are returned ordered by `cycle_index` ascending.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant rendered with exactly three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation and formatted identically, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. `LedgerEntry` is the one exception in the model: a ledger entry is an immutable historical fact, so it carries only `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from a `LedgerEntry` representation.

All deadline arithmetic, window comparison and trailing-window aggregation in this system reads a server-held **logical clock**, never the host wall clock. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, which is at or before every date the system's fixtures use; an implementation must not fall back to real wall-clock time under any circumstance. A `ProgramOfficer` may advance the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the administrative clock endpoint at `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, which is the only route in the system not mounted under `/oversight`. It responds with the new value of the clock as `current_utc` and performs no recomputation of any entity — advancing the clock changes what future evaluations will see, and nothing more. Note in particular that advancing the clock past a complaint's deadline does not escalate the complaint; only an oversight cycle close does that.

### 2.5. Stale-Version Guards on Partner and Custody Writes

Three write endpoints require the client to prove it has observed the current state of a parent record before it may attach a child to it. Each of the three takes a non-optional version field in its request body, and a mismatch against the parent's stored `version` rejects the write.

Creating a diligence review — `POST /oversight/diligence-reviews` — must carry `expected_partner_version` matching the target `Partner`'s current `version`. A renewal review legitimately supersedes the partner's prior passed review and rewrites `capability_tier` and `latest_composite_score` once decided, so a writer holding a stale view of the partner would silently re-underwrite against superseded scores. If the value supplied does not equal the partner's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARTNER_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Creating a program agreement — `POST /oversight/agreements` — likewise must carry `expected_partner_version` matching the target `Partner`'s current `version`. A new agreement supersedes the partner's earlier draft terms, including the growth cap, the concentration cap and the reserve requirement, so a create carrying an older observed partner version is writing caps derived from a superseded view of the partner's posture and tier. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARTNER_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Booking a ledger entry — `POST /oversight/ledger-entries` — must carry `expected_custody_account_version` matching the current `version` of the `CustodyAccount` named in the body. Ledger movements are additive and carry no state gate of their own, so a repeat booking would otherwise post a second, entirely legitimate movement on top of a balance the client never observed — exactly the condition under which the end-user balance cap and the concentration cap would be evaluated against a stale denominator. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CUSTODY_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint in the system takes a version field. Action endpoints are guarded by their own state preconditions instead, and supplying a version field to them has no meaning.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

Money is held as integer cents throughout the system. No monetary field is ever a decimal, and no monetary computation ever produces a fractional cent. Rates are expressed either in basis points as integers — one basis point being one ten-thousandth — or as percentages with two decimal places.

Three distinct rounding conventions apply, and each calculation states which one it uses; they must not be interchanged.

Where a rate expressed in basis points is applied to an amount in cents, the product is divided by 10000 and then **floored** to a whole cent. Flooring happens independently on each such product, before that product enters any sum, difference, maximum or minimum. Nothing is rounded after a maximum or a minimum has been taken. This governs the reserve requirement, the sponsor-bank fee and the reserve funding leg of a settlement.

Where a value is a percentage or a score, it is rounded **half up** to the stated number of decimal places: two decimal places for `concentration_ratio_pct` and `monthly_growth_pct`, one decimal place for `composite_score` and `latest_composite_score`, and zero decimal places (a whole integer) for `complaint_rate_bps`. Half-up means a value exactly on the midpoint rounds away from zero — a true ratio of 24.995 percent becomes 25.00.

Where a composite is assembled from weighted parts, the rounding order is normative and is spelled out with the formula in §3; rounding a sum of unrounded products is not equivalent to summing rounded products, and the difference is observable in the tier a partner receives.

Finally, threshold comparisons differ deliberately between rules and the direction of each is part of the contract. The oversight posture ladder, the concentration cap and the diligence floors all compare **inclusively**: a value exactly on the threshold satisfies it. The growth caps compare **strictly**: a value exactly on the cap is compliant and only a value above it breaches. Each rule restates its own direction where it appears.

### 2.7. The Oversight Posture Ladder

Every partner carries `oversight_posture`, a derived supervisory rung that is never set by a client and is recomputed by the system whenever any of its inputs change. It takes one of four values, ordered from least to most severe: `OPEN`, `HEIGHTENED`, `RESTRICTED`, `FROZEN`. A partner starts at `OPEN`.

The ladder is evaluated as an ordered sequence of predicates, most severe first, and **the first predicate that matches determines the posture**; later predicates are not consulted. Every input is read as it stands persisted on the partner row at the moment the ladder runs.

The partner's posture becomes `FROZEN` if it has at least one open sanctions alert — that is, `open_sanctions_alert_count` is 1 or more — or if `consecutive_shortfall_cycles` has reached 2. Otherwise the posture becomes `RESTRICTED` if `complaint_rate_bps` has reached 250, or `unreconciled_variance_cents` has reached 5,000,000 cents (fifty thousand dollars of unexplained settlement variance), or `growth_breach_count` has reached 2. Otherwise the posture becomes `HEIGHTENED` if `complaint_rate_bps` has reached 120, or `growth_breach_count` is exactly 1, or `reserve_shortfall_cents` is greater than 0. If none of those hold, the posture is `OPEN`.

Every one of those thresholds is inclusive. A complaint rate of exactly 250 basis points yields `RESTRICTED`; 249 basis points yields `HEIGHTENED`. Note the asymmetry in the growth-breach input: a single cumulative breach lands the partner on `HEIGHTENED`, while two or more land it on `RESTRICTED`.

The ladder only applies to a partner whose program has reached the contractual stage. Partners whose `status` is `PROSPECT`, `IN_DILIGENCE`, `DILIGENCE_PASSED`, `DILIGENCE_FAILED` or `TERMINATED` keep `oversight_posture` at `OPEN` and are skipped by the recomputation entirely.

Posture is not merely a report. `RESTRICTED` and `FROZEN` both block marketing filings, resubmissions and approvals, refused with `ERR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_MARKETING` (HTTP 403). `FROZEN` additionally blocks every end-user credit into custody, refused with `ERR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_INFLOW` (HTTP 403), and freezes both of the partner's custody accounts. Posture also feeds back into the reserve requirement as a multiplier, described next.

### 2.8. Reserve Adequacy and the Recompute Cascade

The reserve floor a partner must maintain is recomputed, together with the resulting shortfall, on every ledger insert, on every settlement funding, clawback and variance clearing, at the moment a program agreement is executed, and during every oversight cycle close. `required_reserve_cents` and `reserve_shortfall_cents` are both written by that single recomputation and are never supplied by a client.

The computation is a **single pass** with a deliberately pinned dependency. The reserve requirement is scaled by a multiplier keyed on the partner's posture, while the posture itself depends on the shortfall, which depends on the requirement. That circularity is broken by reading the posture **as it was persisted before this pass began**. There is no fixed-point iteration and no second pass: the posture value written later in the same pass is not fed back into the reserve formula.

Within one pass, the order of operations is normative. First the reserve requirement and the shortfall are rewritten. Then the posture ladder of §2.7 runs, reading the shortfall value the pass has just written. Then the posture consequences apply: a posture of `FROZEN` moves both of the partner's custody accounts from `ACTIVE` to `FROZEN`, stamping `frozen_at` with the logical clock and incrementing each account's `version` by 1. Unfreezing is never done inside a business write — an account that is `FROZEN` returns to `ACTIVE` only at an oversight cycle close, and only when that close's recomputation leaves the posture at something other than `FROZEN`.

The full chain the system must complete inside a single ledger insert is therefore: the entry is stored, the owning custody account's `balance_cents` is recomputed from its entries, the partner's reserve requirement and shortfall are recomputed from the new balances, the posture ladder is re-evaluated, and a resulting `FROZEN` posture freezes the custody accounts. Every link runs before the response is returned. The same chain, extended with the settlement money fields at the front, runs inside a settlement funding.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Four decision endpoints enforce that the deciding principal is not the principal who authored the artefact under decision. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the id recorded as the originator: `created_by_user_id` on a program agreement risk decision, `submitted_by_user_id` on a diligence decision, `created_by_user_id` on a marketing decision, and `created_by_user_id` on a wind-down plan decision. A match is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), and the check runs ahead of every state, temporal and fiscal check on the same request.

### 2.10. The Order in Which a Request Is Rejected

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is not a matter of implementation taste. Every check in the system sits at a fixed rank; checks are evaluated in ascending rank order, **the first failure wins, and every later check is skipped**. The ranks below are the complete, normative sequence. Where a check applies only to certain endpoints, its scope is stated exhaustively.

**Rank 1 — Authentication headers (all endpoints).** Both `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are present and non-empty, else `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (401).

**Rank 2 — Role recognition (all endpoints).** `X-User-Role` is one of `ProgramOfficer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `RiskCommittee`, `InternalAudit`, `FintechPartner`, else `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (401).

**Rank 5 — Role authorisation (all endpoints).** The role appears in the endpoint's authorised-role list in §4, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).

**Rank 8 — Path existence (every endpoint with a path id).** Path ids are resolved **left to right**, and the first segment that names no existing row determines the error. The not-found error is specific to the collection that segment addresses: `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND` for a diligence review, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MARKETING_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTODY_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND` for a settlement batch, `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALERT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` for a wind-down plan. All are HTTP 404.

**Rank 10 — Body foreign-key existence.** Every foreign-key id supplied in a request body must name an existing row, resolved in this declaration order: `partner_id` first, then `agreement_id`, then `custody_account_id`, then `parent_partner_id`. The error is the collection-specific not-found code listed at rank 8. This applies to the creation endpoints `POST /oversight/partners`, `POST /oversight/diligence-reviews`, `POST /oversight/agreements`, `POST /oversight/marketing-reviews`, `POST /oversight/settlement-batches`, `POST /oversight/ledger-entries`, `POST /oversight/complaints`, `POST /oversight/alerts` and `POST /oversight/wind-down-plans`.

**Rank 12 — Partner scope.** As described in §2.1, a `FintechPartner` may only touch its own rows, else `ERR_PARTNER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). The scope check guards `POST /oversight/partners/{id}/account-census`, `POST /oversight/marketing-reviews`, `POST /oversight/marketing-reviews/{id}/resubmit`, `POST /oversight/settlement-batches`, `POST /oversight/ledger-entries`, `POST /oversight/complaints`, and every GET endpoint that returns partner-scoped rows.

**Rank 14 — Segregation of duties.** As described in §2.9, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). It guards exactly four endpoints: `POST /oversight/agreements/{id}/risk-decision`, `POST /oversight/diligence-reviews/{id}/decide`, `POST /oversight/marketing-reviews/{id}/decide` and `POST /oversight/wind-down-plans/{id}/decide`.

**Rank 18 — Request schema (all endpoints).** All required body fields are present, every enum value is a recognised member, every number lies inside the range declared for that field, every string lies inside its declared length, and `limit` and `offset` lie inside the pagination bounds of §2.3. Any failure is `ERR_VALIDATION` (422).

**Rank 20 — Group acyclicity** on `POST /oversight/partners`: see §3.1, else `ERR_PARTNER_GROUP_CYCLE` (422).

**Rank 22 — Agreement/partner agreement** on `POST /oversight/marketing-reviews`: the `ProgramAgreement` named by the body `agreement_id` must have a `partner_id` equal to the body `partner_id`, else `ERR_AGREEMENT_PARTNER_MISMATCH` (409).

**Rank 24 — Custody/partner agreement** on `POST /oversight/ledger-entries`: the `CustodyAccount` named by the body `custody_account_id` must have a `partner_id` equal to the body `partner_id`, else `ERR_CUSTODY_PARTNER_MISMATCH` (409).

**Rank 28 — Partner stale-version guard** on `POST /oversight/diligence-reviews` and `POST /oversight/agreements`: `expected_partner_version` equals the partner's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_PARTNER_VERSION` (412).

**Rank 29 — Custody stale-version guard** on `POST /oversight/ledger-entries`: `expected_custody_account_version` equals the custody account's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_CUSTODY_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (412).

**Rank 32 — Partner terminality.** No `DiligenceReview`, `ProgramAgreement`, `MarketingReview`, `SettlementBatch`, `LedgerEntry`, `Complaint`, `TransactionMonitoringAlert` or `WindDownPlan` may be created for a partner whose `status` is `DILIGENCE_FAILED` or `TERMINATED`, else `ERR_PARTNER_TERMINAL` (409). This covers every creation endpoint carrying a body `partner_id`, plus `POST /oversight/partners/{id}/account-census` and `POST /oversight/partners/{id}/go-live`.

**Rank 34 — Source-state guard (every action endpoint).** The target resource must currently be in a state from which the requested transition is declared. The code is specific to the machine and the endpoint: `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_SUBMITTED`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_DRAFT_OR_INFO_REQUESTED`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_PENDING_APPROVAL`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_EXECUTED`, `ERR_MARKETING_NOT_DECIDABLE`, `ERR_MARKETING_NOT_CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `ERR_BATCH_NOT_UNRECONCILED`, `ERR_BATCH_NO_OPEN_VARIANCE`, `ERR_BATCH_ALREADY_FUNDED`, `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FUNDED`, `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_DISPOSABLE`, `ERR_ALERT_TERMINAL`, `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_PENDING_APPROVAL` and `ERR_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED`, all HTTP 409. Because this rank sits well above every fiscal rank, a batch that has already been funded reports `ERR_BATCH_ALREADY_FUNDED` even when it also has a reserve shortfall that would fail at rank 86.

**Rank 36 — Reconciliation gate on funding** (`POST /oversight/settlement-batches/{id}/fund`): the reconciliation dimension must have left `UNRECONCILED`, else `ERR_BATCH_NOT_RECONCILED` (409).

**Rank 38 — Operating-program gate.** `POST /oversight/settlement-batches/{id}/fund` and `POST /oversight/ledger-entries` with an `entry_type` of `END_USER_CREDIT` both require the partner's `status` to be `LIVE`, else `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_LIVE` (409).

**Rank 39 — Launch status gate** on `POST /oversight/partners/{id}/go-live`: the partner's own `status` must be exactly `CONTRACTED`, else `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_CONTRACTED` (409). This rank sits deliberately ahead of the readiness sweep, so a partner still in `DILIGENCE_PASSED` with no agreement at all reports the status error rather than the readiness error.

**Rank 40 — Launch readiness sweep** on `POST /oversight/partners/{id}/go-live`: exactly one `EXECUTED` program agreement for the partner and both of its custody accounts in `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_READY_FOR_LAUNCH` (409).

**Rank 42 — Wind-down stage gate.** A wind-down plan may only be created, submitted, decided or executed while the partner's `status` is `WIND_DOWN`, else `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_IN_WIND_DOWN` (409). This guards all four of `POST /oversight/wind-down-plans`, `POST /oversight/wind-down-plans/{id}/submit`, `POST /oversight/wind-down-plans/{id}/decide` and `POST /oversight/wind-down-plans/{id}/execute`.

**Rank 44 — Settlement drain gate** on `POST /oversight/wind-down-plans/{id}/execute`, else `ERR_UNSETTLED_BATCHES_OUTSTANDING` (409).

**Rank 46 — Custody availability.** `POST /oversight/settlement-batches/{id}/fund` requires the partner's `RESERVE` custody account to be `ACTIVE`, and `POST /oversight/ledger-entries` requires the account it moves to be `ACTIVE`; a `FROZEN` or `CLOSED` account yields `ERR_CUSTODY_ACCOUNT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409).

**Rank 50 — Marketing posture gate**, `ERR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_MARKETING` (403), guarding `POST /oversight/marketing-reviews`, `POST /oversight/marketing-reviews/{id}/resubmit` and `POST /oversight/marketing-reviews/{id}/decide` when the decision is an approval.

**Rank 52 — Inflow posture gate** on `POST /oversight/ledger-entries`, `ERR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_INFLOW` (403).

**Rank 54 — Disclosure completeness** on `POST /oversight/marketing-reviews/{id}/decide`, `ERR_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE` (422).

**Rank 56 — Compliance capability floor** on `POST /oversight/diligence-reviews/{id}/decide`, `ERR_COMPLIANCE_CAPABILITY_FLOOR` (422), checked before the composite floor.

**Rank 57 — Composite score floor** on `POST /oversight/diligence-reviews/{id}/decide`, `ERR_DILIGENCE_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR` (422).

**Rank 58 — Root-cause discipline** on `POST /oversight/complaints/{id}/disposition`, `ERR_ROOT_CAUSE_REQUIRED` (409).

**Rank 59 — Termination-trigger discipline**, `ERR_TERMINATION_TRIGGER_NOT_CONTRACTED` (422), guarding both `POST /oversight/agreements/{id}/terminate` and `POST /oversight/wind-down-plans`.

**Rank 60 — Interval sanity**, `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (422), guarding `POST /oversight/settlement-batches`, `POST /oversight/cycles`, `POST /oversight/marketing-reviews` and `POST /oversight/marketing-reviews/{id}/resubmit`.

**Rank 62 — Settlement window overlap** on `POST /oversight/settlement-batches`, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409).

**Rank 63 — Approved-marketing window conflict** on `POST /oversight/marketing-reviews/{id}/decide` when the decision is an approval, `ERR_DISCLOSURE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).

**Rank 64 — Cycle period overlap** on `POST /oversight/cycles`, `ERR_CYCLE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409).

**Rank 66 — Future-timestamp guard** on `POST /oversight/complaints` and `POST /oversight/alerts`, `ERR_TIMESTAMP_IN_FUTURE` (422).

**Rank 67 — Effective-date guard** on `POST /oversight/agreements`, `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_IN_PAST` (422).

### 2.11. Two Conservation Invariants

The system maintains two quantities that must never leak, and they conserve different kinds of thing. Neither is stored as a field; both are properties the implementation is required to preserve, and both are observable through the fields that do exist.

The first conserves **cents**. For any partner, add together the `balance_cents` of its `FBO_END_USER` custody account, the `balance_cents` of its `RESERVE` custody account, its `cumulative_paid_out_cents` and its `cumulative_bank_fee_cents`. Funding a settlement batch only ever *moves* cents between those four terms, so the total is identical immediately before and immediately after a funding — including in the case where the payout was floored at zero and a carry was absorbed by the reserve. A clawback is the exact compensating action, so after a clawback each of the four terms individually returns to the value it held before the funding: the end-user balance, the reserve balance, the cumulative payout and the cumulative fee. Equality here is exact integer equality with no tolerance.

The second conserves **record slots**. For any partner, add together `open_complaint_count`, `escalated_complaint_count`, `resolved_complaint_count` and `withdrawn_complaint_count`. Those four buckets always partition the partner's complaints, so their sum equals `total_complaint_count`, which in turn equals the number of complaint rows bearing that partner's id. Every complaint status change moves exactly one slot from one bucket to another and never creates or destroys a slot. In particular, escalating a complaint and then bringing it back by assigning a root cause leaves the sum unchanged and returns `open_complaint_count` and `escalated_complaint_count` to their pre-escalation values, while the complaint's own `escalation_count` records that the round trip happened. Again the equality is exact.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Partner Intake, Corporate Groups and Diligence Underwriting

A `ProgramOfficer` registers a prospective fintech by supplying its registered `legal_name` — between 1 and 120 characters and not blank — together with the `business_model` under which it intends to operate (`LENDING`, `DEPOSIT_WALLET`, `CARD_ISSUING` or `PAYMENT_FACILITATION`), its `funding_stage` (`SEED`, `SERIES_A`, `SERIES_B_PLUS` or `PUBLIC`), its self-declared `months_runway` (a whole number from 0 through 120 inclusive) and its self-disclosed pre-onboarding consumer complaint history as `disclosed_complaints_per_10k`, a number from 0 through 10000 inclusive carrying two decimal places. An `end_user_account_count` may be supplied at registration and defaults to 0 when it is not; it must not be negative. The new partner is stored with a `status` of `PROSPECT`, an `oversight_posture` of `OPEN`, a `capability_tier` of `UNRATED`, a `version` of 1, and its `created_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`.

Fintechs frequently arrive as corporate groups, and the bank's concentration exposure is a group property rather than a per-entity one. A registration may therefore attach the new partner to an existing one by naming the immediate corporate parent in `parent_partner_id`; leaving it unset (its default is null) declares the partner a group root. The named parent must be an existing partner, and the resulting chain must remain acyclic: walking `parent_partner_id` upward from the supplied parent must never reach the partner being registered. An attempt that would close a loop is refused with `ERR_PARTNER_GROUP_CYCLE`. Registering a partner also recomputes `concentration_ratio_pct` for every member of the resulting group, which is zero everywhere until custody exists.

Partners can be listed with filters on `parent_partner_id`, `status`, `oversight_posture` and `business_model`. The `parent_partner_id` filter is the only channel through which the corporate tree can be traversed downward, since parentage is expressed as a body field rather than as a URL segment. A single partner read returns every derived oversight field on the row. A bank-internal computed read — unavailable to `FintechPartner` — assembles the partner's current supervisory picture into a flat object: `partner_id`, `oversight_posture`, `capability_tier`, `complaint_rate_bps`, `concentration_ratio_pct`, the `group_root_partner_id` reached by walking the parentage chain to its root, `group_balance_cents` and `bank_total_fbo_balance_cents` as the numerator and denominator of the concentration ratio computed live at read time (the ratio reads `0.00` when the bank holds no end-user custody at all), `required_reserve_cents`, `reserve_shortfall_cents`, `consecutive_shortfall_cycles`, `growth_breach_count`, `unreconciled_variance_cents`, `open_sanctions_alert_count`, the `systemic_root_cause_code` determined at the most recently closed oversight cycle, and `last_closed_cycle_index` naming that cycle.

The partner reports its own live end-user population through a census, supplying an `end_user_account_count` and the `as_of_date` the count was taken. A census overwrites `end_user_account_count` outright rather than accumulating, then recomputes the partner's `complaint_rate_bps` and re-evaluates the posture ladder, and increments the partner's `version` by 1. The census matters because `end_user_account_count` is the denominator of the complaint rate: **`complaint_rate_bps` counts the partner's complaints per ten thousand end-user accounts over a trailing ninety days.** Precisely, the numerator is the number of complaint rows for that partner whose `status` is `RECEIVED`, `ROOT_CAUSE_ASSIGNED`, `ESCALATED` or `RESOLVED` — withdrawn complaints are excluded — and whose `received_at` is strictly later than the logical clock less ninety days and no later than the logical clock itself. The denominator is `end_user_account_count`, or 1 if that value is smaller than 1. The rate is the numerator multiplied by 10000, divided by the denominator, rounded half up to a whole number of basis points. A partner that has never filed a census therefore has a denominator floor of 1, so a single complaint alone drives the rate to 10000 basis points.

Diligence is where underwriting happens. A `ProgramOfficer` opens a diligence file against a partner by supplying that partner's id as `partner_id`, a `review_type` of either `INITIAL` or `RENEWAL`, four analyst sub-scores each a whole number from 0 through 100 — `business_model_score`, `funding_score`, `compliance_capability_score` and `complaint_history_score` — and the `expected_partner_version` described in §2.5. The file is stored with a `status` of `DRAFT`, a `revision_round` of 0, a `composite_score` of 0.0, a `tier` of `UNRATED`, an empty `requested_info_items` array, and both `submitted_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` null. Creating the file increments the partner's `version` by 1. At most one diligence file per partner may sit in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `INFO_REQUESTED` at any moment; a second attempt is refused with `ERR_DILIGENCE_ALREADY_OPEN`. Diligence files are listable with filters on `partner_id`, `status` and `review_type`.

Submitting the file moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, optionally carrying a `submission_note`. Submission requires the file to be in `DRAFT` and the owning partner's `status` to be `PROSPECT` or `DILIGENCE_PASSED`; a file in the wrong state reports `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_DRAFT_OR_INFO_REQUESTED`. It sets `revision_round` to 1, records the acting `X-User-ID` as `submitted_by_user_id`, and recomputes `effective_complaint_history_score`, `composite_score` and `tier`. It also drags the partner along: a partner in `PROSPECT` advances `PROSPECT → IN_DILIGENCE`, and a partner in `DILIGENCE_PASSED` advances `DILIGENCE_PASSED → IN_DILIGENCE`, in each case incrementing the partner's `version` by 1 and refreshing its `updated_at`. There is no direct endpoint that moves a partner's status through diligence — submitting the first file is what opens diligence. Re-opening diligence from `DILIGENCE_PASSED` additionally requires that no other diligence file for that partner sits in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `INFO_REQUESTED` apart from the one being submitted.

The composite score is a weighted blend of the four sub-scores, and the rounding order is normative. Before any weighting, the complaint-history input is selected by variant. For an `INITIAL` review, `effective_complaint_history_score` is the analyst's submitted `complaint_history_score` verbatim — the partner's self-disclosed history taken at face value. For a `RENEWAL` review, the submitted `complaint_history_score` is still stored on the row but is **not used**: `effective_complaint_history_score` becomes 100 less one point for every 5 basis points of the partner's persisted `complaint_rate_bps`, with the basis-point quotient taken as a whole number by discarding any fraction, and the result floored at 0 so it never goes negative. The rate read here is whatever is persisted on the partner at submit time, already reflecting any earlier complaint disposition or cycle close; this calculation never recomputes it. The consequence is deliberate: identical submitted sub-scores yield a different `composite_score`, a different `tier` and potentially a different pass-or-fail outcome depending on which variant applies.

Each of the four contributions is then formed **and rounded on its own** to two decimal places, half up: the business-model score weighted at 0.20, the funding score weighted at 0.25, the compliance-capability score weighted at 0.35, and the effective complaint-history score weighted at 0.20. Those four already-rounded contributions are added together, and only that sum is rounded — half up, to one decimal place — to give `composite_score`. Rounding once at the end over unrounded products is not the same computation and is not permitted. The `tier` follows from the composite with inclusive floors: a composite of 85.0 or above is `TIER_1`, 70.0 or above is `TIER_2`, 55.0 or above is `TIER_3`, and anything lower leaves the tier at `UNRATED`.

A `ComplianceOfficer` who did not submit the file then decides it, supplying an `outcome` of `PASSED`, `FAILED` or `INFO_REQUESTED` and optionally a `decision_note`. A decision may only be taken on a file in `SUBMITTED`, else `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_SUBMITTED`. All three outcomes record the acting `X-User-ID` as `decided_by_user_id`, and the three side-effect sets are otherwise disjoint.

An `INFO_REQUESTED` outcome moves the file `SUBMITTED → INFO_REQUESTED` and requires `requested_info_items` to be a non-empty array of strings, which is stored on the row; the partner's `status` remains `IN_DILIGENCE` for the whole duration of the loop and no partner field is touched. From `INFO_REQUESTED` the officer resubmits, moving the file `INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` again; that resubmission requires the partner to be in `IN_DILIGENCE`, increments `revision_round` by 1 rather than setting it, clears `requested_info_items` back to an empty array, records the new `submitted_by_user_id`, and recomputes `effective_complaint_history_score`, `composite_score` and `tier` afresh. The `SUBMITTED → INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` loop may run any number of times.

A `PASSED` outcome moves the file `SUBMITTED → PASSED` and carries two floors, checked in this order. First, `compliance_capability_score` must be at least 60; below that the decision is refused with `ERR_COMPLIANCE_CAPABILITY_FLOOR` and the composite floor is never consulted. Second, `composite_score` must be at least 55.0, else `ERR_DILIGENCE_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR`. A successful pass advances the partner `IN_DILIGENCE → DILIGENCE_PASSED`, copies the file's `tier` onto the partner's `capability_tier` and the file's `composite_score` onto the partner's `latest_composite_score`, and increments the partner's `version` by 1.

A `FAILED` outcome moves the file `SUBMITTED → FAILED` with no floors to satisfy, advances the partner `IN_DILIGENCE → DILIGENCE_FAILED`, sets the partner's `capability_tier` back to `UNRATED`, still copies the file's `composite_score` onto the partner's `latest_composite_score` so the failing number is on the record, and increments the partner's `version` by 1. `DILIGENCE_FAILED` is terminal for the partner: no further diligence file, program agreement, marketing review, settlement batch, ledger entry, complaint, monitoring alert or wind-down plan may ever be created for it, and every such attempt is refused with `ERR_PARTNER_TERMINAL`. `PASSED` and `FAILED` are both terminal for the diligence file itself.

### 3.2. Contracting: Agreement Terms, Risk Approval and Custody Creation

The program agreement is where the bank and the fintech divide the work, and drafting it is a `ProgramOfficer` act. The draft names its `partner_id` and the `expected_partner_version` of §2.5, then allocates responsibility across three dimensions, each taking `BANK`, `PARTNER` or `SHARED`: `kyc_owner` for customer identification obligations, `dispute_owner` for end-user dispute handling, and `marketing_owner` for marketing production. The `marketing_owner` allocation is about who *produces* material — the bank retains approval authority over every filing regardless of its value. The draft also sets the depth of the bank's contractual oversight rights in `oversight_rights_level`, which is `STANDARD`, `ENHANCED` or `FULL_ACCESS`, and the advance notice in days the bank owes the partner before exercising onsite audit access as `audit_access_notice_days`, a whole number from 1 through 30 inclusive.

Alongside the responsibility allocation the draft fixes the program's quantitative limits. `termination_triggers` is a non-empty array drawn from `COMPLAINT_RATE`, `RESERVE_SHORTFALL`, `SANCTIONS_EVENT`, `CONCENTRATION_CAP`, `GROWTH_BREACH` and `VOLUNTARY`, and `VOLUNTARY` is permitted to appear on its own. `monthly_growth_cap_pct` is the maximum permitted cycle-over-cycle growth in new end-user credits as a percentage with two decimals, greater than zero and at most 500.00. `concentration_cap_pct` is the maximum share of the bank's whole end-user custody book this partner's group may hold, again a two-decimal percentage, greater than zero and at most 100.00. `max_end_user_balance_cents` is the program's hard ceiling on the end-user custody balance and simultaneously the ceiling on the computed reserve requirement; it must be greater than zero. `reserve_requirement_bps` is the contractual reserve requirement in basis points, a whole number from 1 through 10000 inclusive, applied both to the end-user balance and to settlement volume. Finally, `effective_date` is the instant from which the agreement governs, and it must not precede the logical clock at the moment of drafting — a backdated agreement is refused with `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_IN_PAST`.

A draft is stored with a `status` of `DRAFT`, a `revision_round` of 1, `executed_at` and `termination_trigger_fired` and `decided_by_user_id` all null, and `created_by_user_id` set to the drafting officer's `X-User-ID`. Creating it increments the partner's `version` by 1. At most one agreement per partner may sit in `DRAFT` or `PENDING_RISK_APPROVAL`, and none may be drafted at all once an agreement has reached `EXECUTED` and has not since been terminated; either situation is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_ALREADY_OPEN`. Agreements can be listed with filters on `partner_id` and `status`.

Routing the draft to the risk committee, optionally with a `submission_note`, moves it `DRAFT → PENDING_RISK_APPROVAL`. It requires the agreement to be in `DRAFT` (else `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_DRAFT`), the partner to be in `DILIGENCE_PASSED`, and the partner's `capability_tier` to be one of `TIER_1`, `TIER_2` or `TIER_3` — an `UNRATED` partner has no underwritten basis for a contract. The routing refreshes the agreement's `updated_at` and increments its `version` by 1.

A `RiskCommittee` member who did not draft the agreement then records a `decision` of either `EXECUTE` or `REJECT`, optionally with a `decision_note`. Either way the agreement must be in `PENDING_RISK_APPROVAL`, else `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_PENDING_APPROVAL`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `created_by_user_id`. The two branches carry deliberately disjoint effects and are specified here at equal depth.

A `REJECT` decision returns the agreement `PENDING_RISK_APPROVAL → DRAFT`, increments `revision_round` by 1, records `decided_by_user_id`, increments the agreement's `version` by 1, and touches nothing else — it creates no custody accounts and does not alter the partner's `status` or `version`. The rejected draft may be routed for approval again, so the `PENDING_RISK_APPROVAL → DRAFT → PENDING_RISK_APPROVAL` loop may run repeatedly, each pass raising `revision_round`.

An `EXECUTE` decision additionally requires the partner to still be in `DILIGENCE_PASSED` and the agreement's `effective_date` to be no earlier than the logical clock. It moves the agreement `PENDING_RISK_APPROVAL → EXECUTED`, stamps `executed_at` with the logical clock, records `decided_by_user_id`, and increments the agreement's `version` by 1. It then cascades. The partner advances `DILIGENCE_PASSED → CONTRACTED`. Exactly two custody accounts are created for that partner, one with an `account_type` of `FBO_END_USER` and one with an `account_type` of `RESERVE`, each with a `status` of `ACTIVE`, a `balance_cents` of 0 and a null `frozen_at`; custody accounts are never created any other way, and the filtered custody list is their only discovery channel. With custody now existing, the partner's `required_reserve_cents` and `reserve_shortfall_cents` become computable and are recomputed, and the recomputed shortfall then re-derives the partner's `oversight_posture`. The partner's `version` increments by 1. The chain is exactly the one described in §2.8: executing the agreement creates custody, custody makes the reserve computable, and the computed shortfall re-derives the posture.

Custody accounts can be listed with filters on `partner_id`, `account_type` and `status`, and read individually — the individual read is how a client obtains the `version` an optimistically-guarded ledger write must quote.

### 3.3. Program Launch and Manual Custody Movements

Contracting does not put a program into production; launching does. A `ProgramOfficer` launches by advancing the partner `CONTRACTED → LIVE`, optionally recording an `attestation_note`. Launch is decided almost entirely by the state of other entities rather than by any field on the partner itself. The partner's own `status` must be exactly `CONTRACTED`, reported as `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_CONTRACTED` when it is not; that guard is deliberately evaluated ahead of the readiness sweep so a partner still sitting in `DILIGENCE_PASSED` with no agreement whatsoever reports its status rather than a confusing readiness failure. The readiness sweep then requires exactly one program agreement for that partner in `EXECUTED` and both of the partner's custody accounts in `ACTIVE`, refused as `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_READY_FOR_LAUNCH` otherwise. Beyond readiness, launch requires `reserve_shortfall_cents` to be exactly 0 and the partner's `oversight_posture` to be `OPEN` or `HEIGHTENED`. A successful launch increments the partner's `version` by 1.

Once custody exists, money moves through ledger entries. A `ProgramOfficer` or the partner itself books a movement by naming the `partner_id`, the `custody_account_id` of the account to move, an `entry_type`, a signed `amount_cents`, the `effective_at` instant at which the movement books, the `expected_custody_account_version` of §2.5, and optionally a free-text `memo` which defaults to an empty string. The account named must belong to the partner named, else `ERR_CUSTODY_PARTNER_MISMATCH`. The entry is assigned a `sequence_no`, a monotonic per-custody-account insertion index starting at 1, which fixes the replay order for balance reconstruction. Manual entries carry a null `settlement_batch_id` and a null `reversal_of_entry_id`; both are set only on entries the settlement machinery synthesises. A ledger entry is immutable once written and, as noted in §2.4, carries neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

Sign discipline is strict and is bound to the movement classification. An `END_USER_CREDIT`, a `RESERVE_FUND` or a `CLAWBACK` must carry a positive `amount_cents`. An `END_USER_DEBIT`, a `RESERVE_RELEASE` or a `FEE` must carry a negative `amount_cents`. A `VARIANCE_ADJUSTMENT` may carry either sign. In no case may `amount_cents` be zero. The classification must also match the account it moves: `END_USER_CREDIT` and `END_USER_DEBIT` are only legal on an `FBO_END_USER` account, and `RESERVE_FUND` and `RESERVE_RELEASE` are only legal on a `RESERVE` account. Any violation of the sign rule, the zero rule or the account-match rule is refused with `ERR_LEDGER_SIGN_MISMATCH`.

Booking is also anchored in time. The `effective_at` of an entry must fall inside the period of an oversight cycle that is currently `OPEN` — at or after that cycle's `period_start` and strictly before its `period_end`. An instant that falls in no open cycle's period, including one that falls inside a cycle which has since begun closing, is refused with `ERR_NO_OPEN_CYCLE`. Two posture-driven gates also apply. An `END_USER_CREDIT` requires the partner to be `LIVE`, refused as `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_LIVE` otherwise, and is refused with `ERR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_INFLOW` while the partner's `oversight_posture` is `FROZEN`. Any entry, of any type, requires the custody account it moves to be `ACTIVE`; a `FROZEN` or `CLOSED` account yields `ERR_CUSTODY_ACCOUNT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Three fiscal limits then guard the movement, and they are evaluated in the order given. No custody account may go negative: after applying `amount_cents`, the account's `balance_cents` must be at least 0, else `ERR_CUSTODY_INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`. After an `END_USER_CREDIT`, the `FBO_END_USER` account's resulting `balance_cents` must not exceed the partner's agreement `max_end_user_balance_cents`; a post-credit balance exactly equal to the cap is accepted, and only a balance above it is refused with `ERR_END_USER_BALANCE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. And after an `END_USER_CREDIT`, the recomputed group concentration ratio must be **strictly below** the agreement's `concentration_cap_pct`; a recomputed ratio exactly equal to the cap counts as a breach and the credit is refused with `ERR_CONCENTRATION_CAP_BREACH`. Note the asymmetry between the last two: the balance cap tolerates equality, the concentration cap does not.

A successful booking recomputes the owning custody account's `balance_cents` as the sum of `amount_cents` over every entry whose `custody_account_id` is that account, increments that account's `version` by 1, then recomputes `required_reserve_cents`, `reserve_shortfall_cents` and `concentration_ratio_pct` for every partner in the group, then re-derives the partner's `oversight_posture`, then — if the posture landed on `FROZEN` — freezes both of that partner's custody accounts as described in §2.8, and finally increments the partner's `version` by 1. Ledger entries are listable with filters on `partner_id`, `custody_account_id`, `settlement_batch_id`, `reversal_of_entry_id` and `entry_type`, and readable individually.

**The reserve floor.** The reserve requirement is a three-way floor scaled by posture and then capped by contract. The computation begins by reading the posture as it stood persisted before the pass began, and by locating the partner's program agreement whose `status` is `EXECUTED` or `TERMINATED` — there is at most one such row. If no such agreement exists, both `required_reserve_cents` and `reserve_shortfall_cents` are 0 and the computation stops there. Otherwise, three candidate floors are formed. The first is the partner's `FBO_END_USER` `balance_cents` multiplied by the agreement's `reserve_requirement_bps`, divided by 10000 and floored to whole cents. The second is the partner's trailing settlement volume multiplied by 150 basis points, divided by 10000 and floored to whole cents, where trailing settlement volume is the sum of `gross_settlement_cents` over the partner's settlement batches whose `reconciliation_status` is `MATCHED` or `VARIANCE_CLEARED` and whose `window_end` is strictly later than the logical clock less ninety days and no later than the logical clock. The third is a flat absolute floor of 250,000 cents — two thousand five hundred dollars — which applies to every contracted partner no matter how small. The largest of the three is taken as the base.

The base is then multiplied by a posture multiplier and the product floored to whole cents: 1.00 at `OPEN`, 1.25 at `HEIGHTENED`, 1.50 at `RESTRICTED` and 2.00 at `FROZEN`. `required_reserve_cents` is the smaller of that scaled figure and the agreement's `max_end_user_balance_cents` — the contractual ceiling wins. Nothing is rounded after that minimum is taken. `reserve_shortfall_cents` is then `required_reserve_cents` less the `RESERVE` account's `balance_cents`, floored at 0 so a surplus reserve reports no shortfall. Both custody accounts are read at whatever `status` they hold, including `FROZEN`; a `CLOSED` account contributes 0.

**The concentration ratio.** Concentration is measured across the corporate group, not the individual partner. The group is resolved by walking `parent_partner_id` upward from the partner until a row with a null `parent_partner_id` is reached — that root, plus every partner transitively reachable back down the parentage edges, is the group. The numerator is the sum of `balance_cents` over every `FBO_END_USER` custody account belonging to a group member whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `FROZEN`; closed accounts are excluded. The denominator is the same sum taken over every `FBO_END_USER` account in the entire bank whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `FROZEN`, regardless of the owning partner's own status — a prospect or a terminated partner still counts toward the bank total so long as such an account exists for it. If the denominator is zero, `concentration_ratio_pct` is defined to be `0.00`; that is a value, not an error. Otherwise the ratio is the numerator multiplied by 100, divided by the denominator, and rounded half up to two decimal places. Every partner in the group is written with the **same** `concentration_ratio_pct`, because the ratio is a property of the group. The cap comparison uses the rounded value, so a true ratio of 24.995 percent rounds to 25.00 and breaches a cap of 25.00.

### 3.4. Marketing Material and Disclosure Approval

Consumer-facing material produced under the program is reviewed by the bank before it runs, and the bank's approval right is unconditional — it holds whatever `marketing_owner` the agreement allocated. Either the partner or a `ProgramOfficer` files material by naming the `partner_id`, the `agreement_id` of the governing agreement, the `material_type` (`AD`, `DISCLOSURE`, `APP_SCREEN` or `EMAIL`), the `placement_channel` on which it will run (`PAID_SOCIAL`, `SEARCH`, `IN_APP`, `DIRECT_MAIL` or `PARTNER_SITE`), the `disclosure_elements` present on the material, the booleans `apr_disclosed` and `fdic_signage_correct`, and the closed window during which the material may run as `effective_from` and `effective_until`. The `disclosure_elements` array is drawn from `APR`, `FEE_SCHEDULE`, `FDIC_SIGNAGE`, `BANK_NAME`, `PASS_THROUGH_INSURANCE` and `DISPUTE_RIGHTS`, and defaults to empty; both booleans default to false. The named agreement must belong to the named partner, else `ERR_AGREEMENT_PARTNER_MISMATCH`, and `effective_until` must be no earlier than `effective_from`, else `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. A filing lands directly in `SUBMITTED` — there is no draft stage — with `review_round` at 1, an empty `change_requests` array, `decided_by_user_id` null, and `created_by_user_id` set to the filer's `X-User-ID`. Marketing reviews are listable with filters on `partner_id`, `agreement_id`, `material_type`, `placement_channel` and `status`; the `agreement_id` filter is the only channel through which the filings under a given agreement can be enumerated.

Every filing carries a computed `risk_flags` array, recomputed at filing, at every resubmission and at every decision attempt. The flags are derived deterministically from the material and the partner's business model. A `MISSING_APR` flag is raised when the partner's `business_model` is `LENDING` and either `apr_disclosed` is false or `APR` is absent from `disclosure_elements`. A `MISSING_FDIC_SIGNAGE` flag is raised when the partner's `business_model` is `DEPOSIT_WALLET` and any of the following holds: `fdic_signage_correct` is false, `FDIC_SIGNAGE` is absent from `disclosure_elements`, or `PASS_THROUGH_INSURANCE` is absent from `disclosure_elements`. A `MISSING_DISPUTE_RIGHTS` flag is raised when the `material_type` is `DISCLOSURE` and `DISPUTE_RIGHTS` is absent from `disclosure_elements`. The emitted array is always ordered `MISSING_APR`, then `MISSING_FDIC_SIGNAGE`, then `MISSING_DISPUTE_RIGHTS` — the fixed declaration order, never the order in which the flags happened to be discovered. An empty array is the only value that permits an approval.

A `ComplianceOfficer` other than the filer records a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `REJECT`, optionally with a `decision_note`. All three require the filing to be in `SUBMITTED`, else `ERR_MARKETING_NOT_DECIDABLE`, and all three recompute `risk_flags` before the decision is evaluated, record `decided_by_user_id`, and increment the filing's `version` by 1.

`CHANGES_REQUESTED` moves the filing `SUBMITTED → CHANGES_REQUESTED` and requires `change_requests` to be a non-empty array of strings, which is stored on the row. `REJECT` moves the filing `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, which is terminal, and imposes no further condition beyond the source state and segregation of duties. `APPROVE` moves the filing `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and is the only branch that carries substantive gates: the partner's `oversight_posture` must be `OPEN` or `HEIGHTENED` (a `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` partner cannot get material approved, refused with `ERR_POSTURE_BLOCKS_MARKETING`), the recomputed `risk_flags` must be empty (else `ERR_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE`), and the window must clear the cooling buffer described next.

The cooling buffer prevents two approved runs of the same material type on the same channel from crowding each other. For a given combination of `partner_id`, `material_type` and `placement_channel`, the closed interval running from the new filing's `effective_from` through its `effective_until` must be separated from the closed interval of every already-`APPROVED` filing on that same combination by **more than three days on both sides**. Concretely, a conflict exists when the new `effective_from` is at or before the existing `effective_until` plus three days *and* the existing `effective_from` is at or before the new `effective_until` plus three days; a conflict is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Unlike the settlement windows of §3.5, touching endpoints conflict here, and so does a gap of exactly three days — only a gap strictly greater than three days clears.

After changes are requested, only the partner itself may resubmit, moving the filing `CHANGES_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`. Resubmission requires the filing to be in `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, else `ERR_MARKETING_NOT_CHANGES_REQUESTED`, requires the resubmitting `X-User-ID` to equal the filing's `partner_id`, and requires the partner's `oversight_posture` to be `OPEN` or `HEIGHTENED`. It overwrites `disclosure_elements`, `apr_disclosed`, `fdic_signage_correct`, `effective_from` and `effective_until` with the values supplied, increments `review_round` by 1, clears `change_requests` back to empty, sets `created_by_user_id` to the resubmitting `X-User-ID` (so the compliance officer who requested the changes may still decide the new round), recomputes `risk_flags`, and increments `version` by 1. The interval sanity check applies to the restated window, but the cooling-buffer check does **not** run at resubmission — it re-runs against the new window at the next approval attempt. The `SUBMITTED → CHANGES_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` loop is unbounded.

An approved filing does not stay approved forever. It moves `APPROVED → EXPIRED`, a terminal state, in two circumstances. At an oversight cycle close, an approved filing expires when its `effective_until` has fallen behind the logical clock, or when the partner's posture recomputed earlier in that same close is `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`; each such expiry increments the closing cycle's `marketing_approvals_expired_count` by 1. Separately, terminating a program agreement expires every filing for that partner sitting in `SUBMITTED`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`, as described in §3.9.

### 3.5. Settlement Batches: Windows, Reconciliation and Variance

A settlement batch is a claim about a window of transaction activity, and the bank treats the partner's number and its own number as two independent assertions that have to be brought together. Either the partner or a `ProgramOfficer` opens a batch by naming the `partner_id`, the window as `window_start` and `window_end`, and four money figures: `gross_settlement_cents` for the gross transaction volume settled in the window, `interchange_cents` for interchange earned, `returns_cents` for ACH returns and chargebacks charged back, and `partner_reported_net_cents` for the net proceeds the partner's own ledger claims. The first three must each be at least 0; `partner_reported_net_cents` may be any whole number of cents including a negative one. The batch is created with `reconciliation_status` at `UNRECONCILED`, `funding_status` at `UNFUNDED`, `variance_cents` at 0, `variance_adjustment_cents` at 0, `bank_reported_net_cents` null, `funded_at` and `funding_reference` and `clawback_reason` all null, and every computed money field at 0.

Settlement windows for one partner are half-open, running from `window_start` inclusive up to `window_end` exclusive, and `window_end` must be strictly later than `window_start`, else `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. Two windows for the same partner may abut but never overlap: a new batch may start at exactly a prior batch's `window_end`, and may end at exactly a prior batch's `window_start`. Intersection — meaning the new `window_start` is strictly before an existing `window_end` *and* that existing `window_start` is strictly before the new `window_end` — is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Batches are listable with filters on `partner_id`, `reconciliation_status` and `funding_status`, each dimension filterable independently of the other.

That independence is the defining structural feature of a batch: **two unrelated state dimensions live on the same row.** The `reconciliation_status` dimension advances `UNRECONCILED → MATCHED` or `UNRECONCILED → VARIANCE_OPEN`, and then `VARIANCE_OPEN → VARIANCE_CLEARED`. The `funding_status` dimension advances `UNFUNDED → FUNDED` and then `FUNDED → CLAWED_BACK`. Neither dimension constrains the other except for exactly one gate: funding requires the reconciliation dimension to have left `UNRECONCILED`. A batch is finished when reconciliation has settled and the money has been reversed, so the terminal combinations are `VARIANCE_CLEARED` with `CLAWED_BACK` and `MATCHED` with `CLAWED_BACK`.

`InternalAudit` — or a `ProgramOfficer` — reconciles a batch by posting the bank's own net figure as `bank_reported_net_cents`. Reconciliation requires the reconciliation dimension to still be `UNRECONCILED`, else `ERR_BATCH_NOT_UNRECONCILED`. The batch's `variance_cents` is set to `bank_reported_net_cents` less `partner_reported_net_cents`. When that difference is exactly zero, the batch advances `UNRECONCILED → MATCHED` and the partner's `unreconciled_variance_cents` is recomputed (it does not change on this branch). When the difference is anything other than zero, the batch advances `UNRECONCILED → VARIANCE_OPEN` and the absolute magnitude of `variance_cents` is added to the partner's `unreconciled_variance_cents`, which then re-derives the partner's `oversight_posture` — this is the pathway by which unexplained variance of 5,000,000 cents or more drives a partner to `RESTRICTED`. Either branch increments the batch's `version` by 1 and the partner's `version` by 1.

Only `InternalAudit` may clear an open variance, and doing so requires it to be explained in full. The clearing supplies a signed `variance_adjustment_cents` correction and a `root_cause_note` recording why the gap arose. The batch's reconciliation dimension must be `VARIANCE_OPEN`, else `ERR_BATCH_NO_OPEN_VARIANCE`. The correction must close the gap exactly: `variance_adjustment_cents` added to `partner_reported_net_cents` must equal `bank_reported_net_cents`, else `ERR_VARIANCE_NOT_EXPLAINED`. On success the correction is stored, `settlement_base_cents` is recomputed as `gross_settlement_cents` plus `interchange_cents`, less `returns_cents`, plus `variance_adjustment_cents`, and the batch advances `VARIANCE_OPEN → VARIANCE_CLEARED`. The absolute magnitude of `variance_cents` is removed from the partner's `unreconciled_variance_cents`.

Clearing a variance is legal on both sides of the funding dimension, and the two cases have deliberately different effects. If the batch is still `UNFUNDED`, nothing further happens to the ledger — the corrected base will simply be used when the batch is eventually funded. If the batch is already `FUNDED`, the money has already moved, so rather than restating `bank_fee_cents` after the fact the system books a compensating `VARIANCE_ADJUSTMENT` ledger entry on the partner's `RESERVE` custody account for exactly `variance_adjustment_cents`, with the entry's `settlement_batch_id` set to the batch. That account's `balance_cents` is then recomputed. Either way, the clearing recomputes the partner's `required_reserve_cents` and `reserve_shortfall_cents`, then `concentration_ratio_pct`, then re-derives `oversight_posture`, and increments both the batch's `version` and the partner's `version` by 1.

### 3.6. Funding a Batch, Fee Tiers, Reserve Routing and Clawback

Funding is the moment cents leave custody, and it is a `ProgramOfficer` act that advances `funding_status` from `UNFUNDED` to `FUNDED` while recording the wire or ACH reference as `funding_reference`. It is the most heavily gated operation in the system. The batch must still be `UNFUNDED`, else `ERR_BATCH_ALREADY_FUNDED`; because the source-state guard outranks every fiscal check, an already-funded batch reports that code even when it would also fail on reserve adequacy. The reconciliation dimension must be `MATCHED` or `VARIANCE_CLEARED`, else `ERR_BATCH_NOT_RECONCILED`. The partner must be `LIVE`, else `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_LIVE`. The partner's `RESERVE` custody account must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_CUSTODY_ACCOUNT_NOT_ACTIVE`. And the partner's `reserve_shortfall_cents` must be exactly 0 — a partner short of reserve does not get paid, and the refusal is `ERR_RESERVE_SHORTFALL_BLOCKS_FUNDING`, which is the one condition in this system reported as HTTP 402.

The money arithmetic runs in six ordered steps, and the order is what makes the outcome well-defined. It reads `reserve_requirement_bps` from the partner's program agreement whose `status` is `EXECUTED` or `TERMINATED`, and aggregates nothing across sibling batches.

First, the sponsor-bank fee tier is selected **on gross volume, before any deduction**, and the selected rate is flat across the whole gross rather than marginal. A `gross_settlement_cents` below 10,000,000 cents — that is, below one hundred thousand dollars — takes a `bank_fee_bps_applied` of 45 basis points. From 10,000,000 cents up to but not including 100,000,000 cents (one million dollars) the rate is 32 basis points. At 100,000,000 cents and above the rate is 21 basis points. Pinning the tier to gross is deliberate: the tier looks as though it ought to key on the amount actually paid out, but that amount is only known after the fee has been deducted, so the order breaks the circularity.

Second, `bank_fee_cents` is a flat base fee of 25,000 cents — two hundred and fifty dollars per batch — plus `gross_settlement_cents` multiplied by `bank_fee_bps_applied`, divided by 10000 and floored to whole cents. The base fee is added **after** that product has been floored, never before.

Third, `settlement_base_cents` is `gross_settlement_cents` plus `interchange_cents`, less `returns_cents`, plus `variance_adjustment_cents` — the last term being 0 unless a variance was cleared before funding.

Fourth, `reserve_funding_cents` is routed into the reserve, and its basis depends on which way reconciliation went. For a batch that is `MATCHED`, the basis is `gross_settlement_cents` alone, ignoring interchange, returns and adjustments: the reserve funding is that gross multiplied by `reserve_requirement_bps`, divided by 10000 and floored to whole cents. For a batch that is `VARIANCE_CLEARED`, the basis is `settlement_base_cents` instead, multiplied by the same `reserve_requirement_bps`, divided by 10000 and floored to whole cents. The consequence is that two batches with identical gross, interchange and returns figures produce a different `reserve_funding_cents` and a different `net_to_partner_cents` purely because one of them passed through a cleared variance.

Fifth, the raw payout is `settlement_base_cents` less `bank_fee_cents` less `reserve_funding_cents`. `net_to_partner_cents` is that raw payout when it is positive, and 0 when it is negative or zero — the payout is floored at zero and never goes negative. Sixth, `shortfall_carry_cents` captures the magnitude the reserve must absorb when the fees and reserve routing outran the base: it is the amount by which the raw payout fell below zero, expressed as a positive number, and 0 whenever the raw payout was at or above zero.

Three ledger legs are then synthesised, each with its `settlement_batch_id` set to the batch. An `END_USER_DEBIT` on the `FBO_END_USER` account carries `amount_cents` equal to the negation of `settlement_base_cents`. A `RESERVE_FUND` on the `RESERVE` account carries `amount_cents` equal to `reserve_funding_cents` plus `shortfall_carry_cents`. A `FEE` on the `RESERVE` account carries `amount_cents` equal to the negation of `bank_fee_cents`, and that third leg is written only when `bank_fee_cents` is greater than 0. The debit leg is itself subject to the no-negative-custody rule, so a funding that would drive the end-user account below zero is refused with `ERR_CUSTODY_INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`.

Funding then closes out the cascade. `net_to_partner_cents` is added to the partner's `cumulative_paid_out_cents` and `bank_fee_cents` to its `cumulative_bank_fee_cents`. Each ledger insert recomputes its account's `balance_cents`; the changed balances recompute the partner's `required_reserve_cents`, `reserve_shortfall_cents` and `concentration_ratio_pct`; the recomputed shortfall re-derives `oversight_posture`; and a posture of `FROZEN` moves both custody accounts `ACTIVE → FROZEN`. Finally `funded_at` is stamped with the logical clock and both the batch's and the partner's `version` increment by 1.

Clawback is the exact compensating action, available to a `ProgramOfficer` while the partner's `status` is `LIVE` or `WIND_DOWN`. It requires the batch to be `FUNDED`, else `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FUNDED`, and takes a `clawback_reason` of `RECONCILIATION_ERROR`, `PARTNER_FRAUD`, `RESERVE_BREACH` or `DUPLICATE_FUNDING`. For every leg the funding wrote, one `CLAWBACK` ledger entry is created whose `amount_cents` is the negation of the entry it reverses and whose `reversal_of_entry_id` names that entry's id. `net_to_partner_cents` is subtracted from the partner's `cumulative_paid_out_cents` and `bank_fee_cents` from its `cumulative_bank_fee_cents`. Both custody balances are recomputed, then the reserve requirement, shortfall and concentration ratio, then the posture. The batch advances `FUNDED → CLAWED_BACK`, and `funded_at`, `bank_fee_cents`, `bank_fee_bps_applied`, `reserve_funding_cents` and `net_to_partner_cents` are all left untouched as the historical record of what was paid. Both `version` counters increment by 1. Because clawback restores each of the four conserved terms of §2.11 individually, the end-user balance, the reserve balance, the cumulative payout and the cumulative fee all return to precisely the values they held before the funding.

### 3.7. Consumer Complaint Aggregation and Root-Cause Analysis

Complaints about a fintech program reach the bank from everywhere — regulators, state attorneys general, social media, the bank's own call centre, the partner's app, plain email — and the point of aggregating them in one place is that the trailing complaint rate is what moves a partner's posture. A `ProgramOfficer`, a `ComplianceOfficer` or the partner itself intakes a complaint by naming the `partner_id`, the `channel` it arrived through (`CFPB_PORTAL`, `STATE_AG`, `SOCIAL`, `BANK_CALL_CENTER`, `PARTNER_APP` or `EMAIL`), the `category` it concerns (`FEES`, `DISCLOSURE`, `ACCOUNT_ACCESS`, `UNAUTHORIZED_TRANSACTION`, `CREDIT_REPORTING` or `SERVICING`), the triage `severity` (`LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH` or `CRITICAL`), the `received_at` instant, the consumer's `narrative` (between 1 and 2000 characters), and `is_reg_e_dispute`, a boolean defaulting to false that marks the complaint as also being an electronic-funds-transfer dispute requiring provisional credit. A `received_at` later than the logical clock is refused with `ERR_TIMESTAMP_IN_FUTURE`.

The complaint is stored in `RECEIVED` with `root_cause_code` null, `escalation_count` at 0, `redress_cents` at 0, and `resolved_at` null. Two computed fields are written at intake. `handling_owner` is copied from the `dispute_owner` of the partner's `EXECUTED` program agreement, so the contractual allocation of dispute handling is frozen onto each complaint as it arrives; when no executed agreement exists for that partner, `handling_owner` defaults to `BANK`. And `response_due_at` is derived from the channel, as described below. Intake increments the partner's `total_complaint_count` and `open_complaint_count` by 1, recomputes `complaint_rate_bps`, re-derives `oversight_posture`, and increments the partner's `version` by 1. Complaints are listable with filters on `partner_id`, `channel`, `status`, `root_cause_code` and `severity`.

**The response deadline.** Each channel carries its own service-level commitment, and — this is the part that trips people up — the channels do not all count days the same way. Four channels count **calendar** days: `CFPB_PORTAL` allows 15 days, while `BANK_CALL_CENTER`, `PARTNER_APP` and `EMAIL` each allow 30 days. For these, `response_due_at` is simply `received_at` advanced by that many days as straight instant arithmetic — weekends are consumed like any other day, and the time of day of `received_at` is preserved exactly.

Two channels count **business** days: `STATE_AG` allows 10 and `SOCIAL` allows 5. For these, the count starts from a normalised midnight anchor. If `received_at` falls on a Saturday or a Sunday in UTC, the anchor is the next Monday strictly after `received_at`, at `00:00:00.000Z`. Otherwise the anchor is the UTC calendar date of `received_at`, at `00:00:00.000Z`. From that anchor the system steps forward one day at a time, counting only Mondays through Fridays and never counting the anchor day itself, until the required number of business days has been consumed. The resulting date is pinned to `23:59:59.999Z`. There is no holiday calendar: Saturday and Sunday are the only non-business days. A visible consequence of the midnight anchor is that a Saturday intake and the following Monday intake on the same channel produce **the same** due date.

Root-cause analysis is a `ComplianceOfficer` act. Assigning a root cause supplies a `root_cause_code` — one of `DISCLOSURE_DEFECT`, `SYSTEM_OUTAGE`, `FEE_CONFIG_ERROR`, `KYC_FRICTION`, `VENDOR_ERROR` or `PARTNER_TRAINING_GAP` — together with an `investigation_note`. It is legal from two source states and is the compensating action that brings an escalated complaint back into the open bucket. From `RECEIVED` it advances `RECEIVED → ROOT_CAUSE_ASSIGNED`, stores the code, and recomputes the partner's `open_complaint_count`, which does not change because `RECEIVED` and `ROOT_CAUSE_ASSIGNED` are both open buckets. From `ESCALATED` it advances `ESCALATED → ROOT_CAUSE_ASSIGNED`, overwrites the code, decrements the partner's `escalated_complaint_count` by 1 and increments `open_complaint_count` by 1, then recomputes `complaint_rate_bps` and re-derives `oversight_posture`. Either way the complaint's `version` increments by 1. From any other status the request is refused with `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_DISPOSABLE`.

Disposition, also a `ComplianceOfficer` act, supplies an `outcome` of `RESOLVED`, `ESCALATED` or `WITHDRAWN`, optionally a `resolution_note`, and optionally `redress_cents` recording consumer redress paid, which must not be negative and is 0 when none was paid. Each outcome is legal from a specific set of source states and moves exactly one slot between the partner's buckets, preserving the conservation property of §2.11. Anything attempted from a status that declares no such transition is refused with `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_DISPOSABLE`.

A `RESOLVED` outcome is legal from `ROOT_CAUSE_ASSIGNED` and from `ESCALATED`, and in both cases requires `root_cause_code` to be non-null — a complaint cannot be closed without a diagnosis, and an attempt to do so is refused with `ERR_ROOT_CAUSE_REQUIRED`. It stamps `resolved_at` with the logical clock and stores `redress_cents`. From `ROOT_CAUSE_ASSIGNED` it decrements `open_complaint_count` by 1 and increments `resolved_complaint_count` by 1; from `ESCALATED` it decrements `escalated_complaint_count` by 1 and increments `resolved_complaint_count` by 1. An `ESCALATED` outcome is legal only from `ROOT_CAUSE_ASSIGNED`: it increments the complaint's own `escalation_count` by 1, decrements the partner's `open_complaint_count` by 1 and increments `escalated_complaint_count` by 1. A `WITHDRAWN` outcome is legal only from `RECEIVED`: it stamps `resolved_at`, decrements `open_complaint_count` by 1 and increments `withdrawn_complaint_count` by 1 — and because withdrawn complaints leave the numerator of the complaint rate entirely, a withdrawal is the one disposition that can lower `complaint_rate_bps`. Every disposition recomputes `complaint_rate_bps`, then re-derives `oversight_posture`, and increments both the complaint's `version` and the partner's `version` by 1. `RESOLVED` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal for the complaint; the `ROOT_CAUSE_ASSIGNED → ESCALATED → ROOT_CAUSE_ASSIGNED` loop may run any number of times, with `escalation_count` recording each round trip.

**Systemic root cause.** Aggregation is not just counting; the bank wants to know what the dominant defect was in a given period. At each oversight cycle close, the system determines a `systemic_root_cause_code` for every partner it recomputes, and that value is what the oversight summary read reports. The pool is the partner's complaints whose `status` is `ROOT_CAUSE_ASSIGNED`, `ESCALATED` or `RESOLVED`, whose `root_cause_code` is non-null, and whose `received_at` falls at or after the closing cycle's `period_start` and strictly before its `period_end`; withdrawn and still-`RECEIVED` complaints never enter the pool. If the pool is empty, `systemic_root_cause_code` is null. Otherwise the pool is grouped by `root_cause_code` and the codes tied for the highest count are the candidates. Ties are broken first by whichever candidate code has the earliest `created_at` among its own qualifying complaints, and then — if two candidates' earliest `created_at` values are identical — by the lexicographically smallest code. The resource `id` is never consulted as a tie-break.

### 3.8. Transaction Monitoring and Sanctions Alerts

A `ComplianceOfficer` raises a monitoring alert against a partner's flow by naming the `partner_id`, the `alert_type` of the rule that fired (`STRUCTURING`, `VELOCITY`, `SANCTIONS_HIT` or `ACH_RETURN_RATE`), the `triggered_at` instant the rule fired, the `transaction_volume_cents` of flow implicated (which must be greater than zero), and a `detection_note` of between 1 and 1000 characters. A `triggered_at` later than the logical clock is refused with `ERR_TIMESTAMP_IN_FUTURE`. The alert is created with a `disposition` of `OPEN`, an `sla_breach_count` of 0, a null `disposed_by_user_id`, and a computed `sla_due_at`.

The response deadline runs on wall-clock hours from `triggered_at`, with no calendar or business-day adjustment — alert SLAs do not stop for weekends. A `SANCTIONS_HIT` allows 24 hours, `STRUCTURING` allows 72 hours (three days), `VELOCITY` allows 120 hours (five days), and `ACH_RETURN_RATE` allows 168 hours (a full week). `sla_due_at` is `triggered_at` advanced by that many hours, retaining the fractional-second part of `triggered_at`.

Raising an alert can move a partner immediately. When the `alert_type` is `SANCTIONS_HIT`, the partner's `open_sanctions_alert_count` is recomputed as the number of that partner's alerts whose `alert_type` is `SANCTIONS_HIT` and whose `disposition` is either `OPEN` or `PARTNER_RESPONSE_PENDING`. A single open sanctions alert satisfies the most severe rung of the posture ladder, so the re-derived `oversight_posture` becomes `FROZEN`, which in turn freezes both of that partner's custody accounts inside the same request. The partner's `version` increments by 1. Alerts are listable with filters on `partner_id`, `alert_type` and `disposition`, and readable individually including the computed deadline and breach count.

Disposing of an alert supplies a `disposition` of `PARTNER_RESPONSE_PENDING`, `CLEARED` or `SAR_FILED` together with a `disposition_note`, and records the acting `X-User-ID` as `disposed_by_user_id`. An alert already in `CLEARED` or `SAR_FILED` is terminal and any further attempt is refused with `ERR_ALERT_TERMINAL`. Moving to `PARTNER_RESPONSE_PENDING` is legal only from `OPEN` and carries no note-length requirement beyond the schema. Moving to `CLEARED` or to `SAR_FILED` is legal from either `OPEN` or `PARTNER_RESPONSE_PENDING`, and both of those closing dispositions require the `disposition_note` to be at least 20 characters long — an alert is not closed on a one-word justification. Closing a `SANCTIONS_HIT` alert to either `CLEARED` or `SAR_FILED` recomputes the partner's `open_sanctions_alert_count` and then re-derives `oversight_posture`. Note carefully that the posture may drop off `FROZEN` at that moment but the custody accounts do **not** thaw inside this request: unfreezing is deferred to the next oversight cycle close. Every disposition increments the alert's `version` by 1.

The `OPEN → PARTNER_RESPONSE_PENDING → OPEN` loop exists because a partner that fails to answer within the SLA loses the benefit of the pending state. At an oversight cycle close, every alert sitting in `PARTNER_RESPONSE_PENDING` whose `sla_due_at` has fallen behind the logical clock is returned to `OPEN`, incrementing that alert's `sla_breach_count` by 1 and the cycle's `alerts_reopened_count` by 1. Since both `OPEN` and `PARTNER_RESPONSE_PENDING` are inside the open set, a reopened `SANCTIONS_HIT` alert leaves `open_sanctions_alert_count` unchanged and the frozen posture continues to hold.

### 3.9. The Oversight Cycle Checkpoint, Termination and Orderly Wind-Down

#### The Cycle Checkpoint

Everything described so far reacts to a single request. The oversight cycle is where the bank steps back and re-underwrites the whole book at once, and it is the only place where the passage of time — overdue complaint responses, breached alert SLAs, month-over-month growth, a reserve shortfall that has persisted — is allowed to change anything.

A `ProgramOfficer` opens the next epoch by supplying a `cycle_label` of between 1 and 40 characters (a human label such as "2026-08"), a `period_start` and a `period_end`. The `cycle_index` is assigned by the system as the current maximum `cycle_index` across all cycles plus one, or 1 when no cycle exists yet; the new cycle is stored with `status` `OPEN`, `closed_at` null, and all six of `partners_frozen_count`, `marketing_approvals_expired_count`, `complaints_escalated_count`, `alerts_reopened_count`, `growth_breach_partner_count` and `plans_completed_count` at 0. The period must be a sane interval, meaning `period_end` strictly after `period_start`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. Cycle periods are half-open — inclusive of `period_start`, exclusive of `period_end` — and no two cycles may overlap: if the new interval intersects the interval of any existing cycle, whatever that cycle's `status`, the request is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Unlike the settlement-window rule, this one is checked against every cycle including already-closed ones. Cycles are listable filtered by `status` and are always presented in ascending `cycle_index` order, and reading one returns every counter its close wrote.

Closing a cycle is the ops-run checkpoint. The acting `ProgramOfficer` may supply an optional `closing_note`. The close is a single request that internally moves the cycle through two hops, and the first hop is a gate. To go from `OPEN` to `CLOSING` the cycle's `status` must be `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_OPEN` — and no other cycle anywhere in the system may already be sitting in `CLOSING`, which also raises `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_OPEN`, since two overlapping checkpoints would read each other's half-finished counters. The cycle's `period_end` must also be at or before the logical clock; a period that has not yet elapsed cannot be closed and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`. Reaching `CLOSING` increments the cycle's `version` by 1 and immediately stops the cycle accepting money: from that moment any ledger entry whose `effective_at` falls inside this cycle's half-open period is refused with `ERR_NO_OPEN_CYCLE`, because the only cycle that covers that instant is no longer `OPEN`.

The second hop, from `CLOSING` to `CLOSED`, then runs six steps inside the same request. **The step order is normative and must be followed exactly**, because each step reads state that an earlier step wrote: step 1 changes the complaint counts that step 3 reads, and the posture step 3 derives is the posture step 4 acts on.

**Step 1 — escalate overdue complaints.** Every complaint whose `status` is `RECEIVED` or `ROOT_CAUSE_ASSIGNED` and whose `response_due_at` falls strictly before the logical clock is moved to `ESCALATED`. They are processed in ascending `received_at` order, with ties broken by ascending `created_at`. Each escalation increments the complaint's own `escalation_count` by 1 and moves one slot in the owning partner's buckets: a complaint escalated out of `RECEIVED` or out of `ROOT_CAUSE_ASSIGNED` decrements that partner's `open_complaint_count` by 1 and increments `escalated_complaint_count` by 1. Each one also increments the closing cycle's `complaints_escalated_count` by 1.

**Step 2 — reopen SLA-breached alerts.** Every monitoring alert whose `disposition` is `PARTNER_RESPONSE_PENDING` and whose `sla_due_at` falls strictly before the logical clock is returned to `OPEN`, processed in ascending `triggered_at` order with ties broken by ascending `created_at`. Each reopen increments that alert's `sla_breach_count` by 1 and the cycle's `alerts_reopened_count` by 1. Because both `OPEN` and `PARTNER_RESPONSE_PENDING` count as open for sanctions purposes, this step does not by itself change any partner's `open_sanctions_alert_count`.

**Step 3 — re-underwrite every live partner.** Every partner whose `status` is `CONTRACTED`, `LIVE` or `WIND_DOWN` is recomputed, in ascending `created_at` order. Partners in `PROSPECT`, `IN_DILIGENCE`, `DILIGENCE_PASSED`, `DILIGENCE_FAILED` or `TERMINATED` are skipped entirely. For each partner the recomputation runs in this fixed inner order: first `complaint_rate_bps`, then `monthly_growth_pct` (which may update `growth_breach_count`), then `concentration_ratio_pct`, then `required_reserve_cents` and `reserve_shortfall_cents`, then `consecutive_shortfall_cycles`, and only then `oversight_posture`. Putting posture last is what makes the checkpoint deterministic: the ladder of §2.7 reads the shortfall and breach counters this same step has just finished writing, and it is evaluated exactly once per partner per close, never iterated to a fixed point.

`consecutive_shortfall_cycles` is a run-length counter, not a cumulative total. At each close, if the partner's freshly computed `reserve_shortfall_cents` is greater than 0, the counter is incremented by 1; if the shortfall is exactly 0, the counter is reset to 0. Two consecutive closes with a shortfall therefore put the partner on the top rung of the posture ladder, while a shortfall in one close, a clean close, and then another shortfall leaves the counter at 1. `growth_breach_count`, by contrast, is cumulative and never resets.

**Growth measurement.** `monthly_growth_pct` compares end-user inflow in the closing cycle's window against inflow in the immediately preceding window. The current figure is the sum of `amount_cents` over the partner's ledger entries whose `entry_type` is `END_USER_CREDIT` and whose `effective_at` falls at or after the closing cycle's `period_start` and strictly before its `period_end`. Settlement debits, reserve movements, fees, clawbacks and variance adjustments are all excluded — only genuine end-user credits count as growth. The prior figure is the same sum evaluated over the period of the cycle whose `cycle_index` is exactly one less than the closing cycle's and whose `status` is `CLOSED`; the prior cycle is selected by index alone, never by `created_at` and never by `id`. If no such cycle exists, the prior figure is 0.

When the prior figure is 0 there is no baseline, so a percentage would be meaningless. In that case `monthly_growth_pct` is recorded as exactly 0.00 and an absolute cap applies instead: the partner is in breach when the current figure is strictly greater than 5,000,000 cents. When the prior figure is non-zero, `monthly_growth_pct` is the current figure minus the prior figure, multiplied by 100, divided by the prior figure, rounded half up to two decimal places; the partner is in breach when that percentage is strictly greater than the `monthly_growth_cap_pct` of the partner's agreement. Both comparisons are strict: growth of exactly the contracted cap is compliant, and a current figure exactly equal to 5,000,000 cents is compliant. Note that this is deliberately the opposite convention from the concentration test of §3.3, which breaches on reaching its cap. Every breach increments the partner's `growth_breach_count` by 1 and the closing cycle's `growth_breach_partner_count` by 1 — the cycle counter counts partners, so one partner contributes at most 1 to it per close.

**Step 4 — apply the posture consequences.** Having settled each partner's posture, the close makes custody and marketing agree with it. A partner whose recomputed `oversight_posture` is `FROZEN` has each of its `ACTIVE` custody accounts moved to `FROZEN`, stamping that account's `frozen_at` with the logical clock and incrementing its `version` by 1; each partner that becomes frozen at this close increments the cycle's `partners_frozen_count` by 1. Symmetrically, a partner whose recomputed posture is no longer `FROZEN` has each of its `FROZEN` custody accounts returned to `ACTIVE`, setting `frozen_at` back to null and incrementing that account's `version` by 1 — this is the deferred thaw promised in §3.8, and it is the only place a frozen account ever reopens. A partner whose posture is `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` additionally has every one of its `APPROVED` marketing reviews moved to `EXPIRED`: a restricted program does not get to keep advertising. Independently of posture, and for every partner the close touches, any `APPROVED` marketing review whose `effective_until` falls strictly before the logical clock is also moved to `EXPIRED`, because an approval cannot outlive its own effective window. Every expiry, whichever of the two reasons caused it, increments the cycle's `marketing_approvals_expired_count` by 1.

**Step 5 — complete drained wind-down plans.** Every wind-down plan whose `status` is `IN_EXECUTION` is checked, its `residual_balance_cents` recomputed, and if that residual is exactly 0 the plan advances `IN_EXECUTION → COMPLETED`: `completed_at` is stamped with the logical clock, every custody account belonging to that partner is moved to `CLOSED`, the partner advances `WIND_DOWN → TERMINATED` with its `version` incremented by 1, and the cycle's `plans_completed_count` is incremented by 1. Because the residual is the sum over the partner's `ACTIVE` and `FROZEN` accounts, a residual of 0 means each of those accounts individually holds a `balance_cents` of 0, which is exactly the condition under which an account may close; an account that was `FROZEN` when it closes also has its `frozen_at` set back to null, and every account that closes increments its own `version` by 1. `CLOSED` is terminal for a custody account: it can never be thawed, reopened, or made to accept another ledger entry. A residual of even 1 cent leaves the plan in `IN_EXECUTION` for the next checkpoint to reconsider.

**Step 6 — write the ledger of the close.** Finally the six counters are persisted onto the cycle along with `closed_at` set to the logical clock, and the cycle's `version` is incremented. `CLOSED` is terminal: a closed cycle can be read but never reopened, closed again, or made to accept ledger entries. The largest `cycle_index` among `CLOSED` cycles is what the oversight summary reports as `last_closed_cycle_index`.

#### Termination and Orderly Wind-Down

Terminating a program is a `RiskCommittee` act performed on the agreement, not on the partner, because the right to terminate is a contractual right and the committee must name which contractual right it is exercising. The request supplies a `termination_trigger` and a `notice_days`. The agreement's `status` must be `EXECUTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_EXECUTED`. The supplied `termination_trigger` must appear in the agreement's own `termination_triggers` list — the bank cannot invoke a ground it never contracted for — and an uncontracted trigger is refused with `ERR_TERMINATION_TRIGGER_NOT_CONTRACTED`. The partner's `status` must be `CONTRACTED` or `LIVE`.

Termination advances the agreement `EXECUTED → TERMINATED`, records the supplied trigger in `termination_trigger_fired`, and advances the partner from `CONTRACTED` or from `LIVE` into `WIND_DOWN`, incrementing the partner's `version` by 1. The two source states differ in exactly one respect, and the difference is worth stating plainly: terminating from `LIVE` also moves every one of that partner's marketing reviews whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` to `EXPIRED`, because a live program's advertising is in market and must be pulled; terminating from `CONTRACTED` declares no such marketing side effect, since a program that never launched has nothing in market. `WIND_DOWN` is not terminal for the partner — the partner remains in the recomputation population of step 3 of every close — but it is a one-way door: the only exit is `TERMINATED`.

A `ProgramOfficer` then drafts the wind-down plan. The plan names the `partner_id`; a `trigger_reason` of `COMPLAINT_RATE`, `RESERVE_SHORTFALL`, `SANCTIONS_EVENT`, `CONCENTRATION_CAP`, `GROWTH_BREACH` or `VOLUNTARY`, which must itself appear in the partner's agreement `termination_triggers` and otherwise draws `ERR_TERMINATION_TRIGGER_NOT_CONTRACTED`; an `end_user_funds_disposition` of `RETURN_TO_END_USERS`, `TRANSFER_TO_SUCCESSOR_BANK` or `ESCHEAT`; a `notice_days` between 1 and 180 inclusive; a `target_completion_date`; and a `milestones` array of labels drawn from `STOP_NEW_ACCOUNTS`, `NOTIFY_END_USERS`, `FINAL_SETTLEMENT`, `RETURN_FUNDS` and `CLOSE_CUSTODY`, which must be non-empty and must contain both `RETURN_FUNDS` and `CLOSE_CUSTODY` — a plan that does not return the money and shut the accounts is not a wind-down plan. The array is stored in the order supplied.

Two further constraints bind at draft time. The partner's `status` must already be `WIND_DOWN`, and it must remain `WIND_DOWN` at every subsequent step — drafting, submitting, deciding and executing all refuse with `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_IN_WIND_DOWN` otherwise. And the notice period must be real: `notice_days` must be at least 30, and `target_completion_date` must fall at or after the plan's `created_at` advanced by `notice_days` days; a violation of either is refused with `ERR_WIND_DOWN_NOTICE_NOT_ELAPSED`. At most one plan per partner may be live at a time, where live means a `status` of `DRAFT`, `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `APPROVED` or `IN_EXECUTION`; a second is refused with `ERR_PLAN_ALREADY_OPEN`. The plan is created in `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 1, `approved_by_user_id` null, `completed_at` null, `created_by_user_id` set to the drafting `X-User-ID`, and `residual_balance_cents` computed. Plans are listable filtered by `partner_id` and `status`, and readable individually including the live residual balance.

`residual_balance_cents` is the sum of `balance_cents` over the partner's custody accounts whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `FROZEN`; accounts already `CLOSED` are excluded. Both account types are included, so the plan cannot complete while either end-user funds or reserve funds remain in custody. It is recomputed on submit, on decide and on execute, and again at step 5 of every close.

Submitting routes the plan to the risk committee. The `ProgramOfficer` may supply an optional `submission_note`; the plan's `status` must be `DRAFT`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_DRAFT`, and the `milestones` must still contain `RETURN_FUNDS` and `CLOSE_CUSTODY`. The plan advances `DRAFT → PENDING_APPROVAL`, `residual_balance_cents` is recomputed and `version` increments by 1.

The `RiskCommittee` decision supplies a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REVISE` plus an optional `decision_note`. Either branch requires the plan's `status` to be `PENDING_APPROVAL`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_PENDING_APPROVAL`, and either branch requires the deciding `X-User-ID` to differ from the plan's `created_by_user_id` per §2.9, or it is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. `APPROVE` additionally requires `notice_days` to be at least 30 and advances `PENDING_APPROVAL → APPROVED`, setting `approved_by_user_id` to the deciding user. `REVISE` returns the plan `PENDING_APPROVAL → DRAFT` and increments `revision_round` by 1, and declares no `approved_by_user_id` write — so a plan that has been revised twice and then approved carries `revision_round` 3 and a non-null approver, while a plan sitting in `DRAFT` after a revision still has a null approver. The `DRAFT → PENDING_APPROVAL → DRAFT` loop may run any number of times. Both branches recompute `residual_balance_cents` and increment `version` by 1.

Execution is the last gate before the money actually leaves. The `ProgramOfficer` may supply an optional `execution_note`. The plan's `status` must be `APPROVED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED`. Both settlement dimensions of §3.5 must be clean across the whole partner: no settlement batch for that partner may have a `funding_status` of `UNFUNDED`, and none may have a `reconciliation_status` of `UNRECONCILED` or `VARIANCE_OPEN`. A single outstanding batch on either dimension refuses the request with `ERR_UNSETTLED_BATCHES_OUTSTANDING` — the bank does not begin returning end-user funds while an unreconciled or unfunded settlement could still move the balances. Finally the notice period must have run: the logical clock must be at or after the plan's `created_at` advanced by `notice_days` days, and a premature attempt is refused with `ERR_WIND_DOWN_NOTICE_NOT_ELAPSED`. The plan then advances `APPROVED → IN_EXECUTION`, `residual_balance_cents` is recomputed, and `version` increments by 1.

From `IN_EXECUTION` there is no manual completion step. The operator drains the accounts with ordinary custody movements — end-user debits returning or transferring the funds, reserve releases emptying the reserve — and the next oversight cycle close notices that the residual has reached exactly 0 and finishes the job: the plan reaches `COMPLETED`, both custody accounts reach `CLOSED`, and the partner reaches `TERMINATED`. `COMPLETED` is terminal for the plan and `TERMINATED` is terminal for the partner, from which no further operation of any kind is accepted.
