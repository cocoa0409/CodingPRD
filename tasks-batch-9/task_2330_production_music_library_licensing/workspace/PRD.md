# Production Music Library API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A production music library sits between two populations that never speak to each other directly. On one side are composers, who hand over recordings under agreements that either buy the work outright or promise a share of everything the work later earns. On the other side are broadcasters, agencies, production companies and game studios, who need a cue for a trailer by Thursday and want to know what it costs before they commit. Between them sits a catalogue that has to be searchable by feel rather than by title, a rate card that has to price the same recording differently for a local corporate video and a worldwide feature film, and a royalty ledger that has to survive the moment a society rejects a cue sheet six weeks after the money was already recognized.

This document specifies the service that runs that business. It covers composer agreements with work-for-hire and royalty-split terms, exclusivity and holdback; catalogue metadata across mood, genre, instrumentation and tempo with a tunable relevance model; a tiered rate card driven by media type, distribution scale and term length; per-use sync requests with quoting, revision rounds, custom edits, stems and alternate lengths; blanket agreements with periodic usage reporting, included-minute caps and overage; cue sheet collection and performance-royalty registration with rights societies; unauthorized-use detection via audio fingerprinting with notice, dispute, settlement and escalation; and finally composer royalty accounting — an append-only ledger that is swept into statements on either an accrual or a cash basis, recoups advances, withholds tax and pays out under a strict separation of duties.

Two constraints shape almost every rule that follows. First, money is never approximated: every monetary figure is an integer number of cents, every rounding point is named explicitly, and the pool-allocation algorithm is required to distribute cents exactly with none created or destroyed. Second, the system is deterministic and testable: it reads no wall clock, and every timestamp it writes comes from a logical clock that only moves when a caller advances it. The whole API is mounted under `/music-library`, with the single exception of the clock-control endpoint described in §2.5.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user acts. If `X-User-Role` is absent, or is present but is not one of the roles listed for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `CatalogAdmin`, `RightsManager`, `LicensingAgent`, `RoyaltyAccountant`, `Composer` and `Licensee`.

The four staff roles are operator identities, not persisted rows. When `X-User-Role` is `CatalogAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `RightsManager`, for `LicensingAgent`, and for `RoyaltyAccountant` — with one qualification for the last of these: a `RoyaltyAccountant`'s `X-User-ID` is recorded on royalty statements as `finalized_by_user_id` and `paid_by_user_id`, and those two recorded values are compared against each other by the separation-of-duties rule below, but neither is ever matched against an entity `id`. Responsibilities divide as follows: `CatalogAdmin` owns catalogue metadata, the search profile, the rate card and creative delivery; `RightsManager` owns composer agreements, society traffic, infringement claims and revocation; `LicensingAgent` owns licensees, quoting, issuance and blanket deals, and may never accept a quote on a licensee's behalf; `RoyaltyAccountant` owns cash collection and composer statements.

The two counterparty roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `Composer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Composer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Composer` compare the resource's composer foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. A `Composer`-role request that addresses or reads another composer's row — signing an agreement, deciding an amendment, requesting a royalty preview, or listing statements and ledger entries — is refused with `ERR_COMPOSER_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `Licensee`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Licensee`; ownership checks compare the licensee foreign-key field of the target row — directly, or indirectly through the blanket agreement or licence the row hangs off — to `X-User-ID`. A `Licensee`-role request touching another licensee's row is refused with `ERR_LICENSEE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). A `Licensee` may never quote, issue, decline, review, accept a usage report, or touch royalty accounting; a `Composer` may never price or issue anything.

Separation of duties applies to one operation. Paying a royalty statement requires that `X-User-ID` differ from the `finalized_by_user_id` already recorded on that statement; if they are equal the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Two distinct `RoyaltyAccountant` user identifiers are therefore needed to move any statement from `DRAFT` all the way to `PAID`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip before collecting the page; it defaults to 0. Both are echoed back in the envelope's `meta` object alongside `total_count`, the number of rows matching the filter before the page window is applied, and `has_more`, which is true when rows remain after the current window.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The only endpoint that departs from that default is the catalogue facet search, whose ordering is defined in §3.3.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every resource the service persists carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix, generated by the server and never accepted from the client. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was written.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. A mutation includes recomputation of a derived field: when a licence expires and a licensee's committed spend falls, the licensee's `version` increments and its `updated_at` is refreshed even though no client touched the licensee directly. Where a cascade affects a given row more than once inside a single request, the row's `version` still advances only once per request, as noted in the specific cascade descriptions in §3.

Two entities are deliberately thinner because they are append-only detection or accounting records rather than living resources. A fingerprint match carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has no `updated_at` and no `version`, and no endpoint updates it. A royalty ledger entry likewise carries only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version`; it is immutable except for two narrowly scoped stamps, `statement_id` when a statement sweeps it and `cash_recognized_at` when the underlying money is collected, and neither stamp produces a version because the entry has none.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

No endpoint reads the host's wall clock. The service maintains a single logical clock that begins at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and changes only when a caller submits a new instant as `to_iso8601` to `POST /_clock/set_current_utc` — the one endpoint mounted outside `/music-library`, available to `CatalogAdmin`, `RightsManager`, `LicensingAgent` and `RoyaltyAccountant`. Time only moves forward: a `to_iso8601` earlier than the current value is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND` (HTTP 422). Every timestamp the service writes — `signed_at`, `terminated_at`, `detected_at`, `noticed_at`, `settled_at`, `submitted_at`, `accepted_at`, `registered_at`, `collected_at`, `finalized_at`, `paid_at`, `delinquent_since`, `accrual_recognized_at`, `cash_recognized_at` — is the value of that clock at the moment of the write.

Advancing the clock is also a sweep. In one request, and in this order, the service expires every licence in `ISSUED` or `ACTIVE` whose term has run out, processing them in `created_at` ascending order and releasing the licensee's committed spend; expires every blanket agreement in `ACTIVE` whose coverage end has been reached; and marks delinquent every blanket agreement in `ACTIVE` that has an unreported cadence period past its grace window, recording `delinquent_since`. The response body reports `current_utc`, the new clock value, plus `expired_license_ids`, `expired_blanket_agreement_ids` and `delinquent_blanket_agreement_ids` — the three arrays of identifiers the sweep touched.

### 2.6. Validation Precedence

Many requests violate more than one rule at once. The service therefore evaluates a single, totally ordered ladder of 51 checks and returns the error of the **first** rule violated; no later rule can mask an earlier one. The ladder, in ascending precedence order, is:

1. **Schema and type validation** — on every write endpoint: a required field is absent, a field has the wrong JSON type, an enum value falls outside its declared set, a numeric value falls outside its declared range, or an array breaches its member bounds → `ERR_VALIDATION` (422).
2. **Path entity exists** — on every endpoint with a path parameter: the identifier in the path matches no row of the addressed entity → the matching not-found error (404).
3. **Body reference exists** — on every write endpoint carrying a foreign key in its body: a body foreign key matches no row of the referenced entity → the matching not-found error (404). Body foreign keys are checked in the order the fields are listed for that entity, and the members of a `cues` array are checked in array order.
4. **Role permitted** — on every endpoint: `X-User-Role` is absent or is not among the endpoint's roles → `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
5. **Licensee ownership** — on sync-request creation, quote acceptance and revision requests, usage-report creation and submission, and cue-sheet creation: role `Licensee` acting where the target row's licensee, reached directly or through the blanket agreement or licence, is not `X-User-ID` → `ERR_LICENSEE_MISMATCH` (403).
6. **Composer ownership** — on agreement signing, amendment decisions, the composer royalty preview, and royalty ledger listing: role `Composer` addressing a composer other than `X-User-ID` → `ERR_COMPOSER_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (403).
7. **Separation of duties on payment** — on `POST /music-library/royalty-statements/{statement_id}/pay`: `X-User-ID` equals the statement's `finalized_by_user_id` → `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
8. **Composition version match** — on `POST /music-library/licenses`: `expected_composition_version` differs from the referenced composition's current `version` → `ERR_COMPOSITION_VERSION_CONFLICT` (412).
9. **Composer version match** — on `POST /music-library/royalty-statements`: `expected_composer_version` differs from the referenced composer's current `version` → `ERR_COMPOSER_VERSION_CONFLICT` (412).

### 2.7. Reference Resolution

Identifiers in a URL path are resolved strictly left to right, and the request fails on the first identifier that matches no row, with the 404 belonging to that entity. Foreign keys carried in a request body are resolved after path identifiers and before any authorization, state or business check, in the field order in which the receiving entity declares them.

Each entity has exactly one not-found error: `ERR_COMPOSER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPOSER_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPOSITION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LICENSE_TIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LICENSEE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SYNC_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BLANKET_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_USAGE_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUE_SHEET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FINGERPRINT_MATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INFRINGEMENT_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROYALTY_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ROYALTY_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`. The singleton search profile has its own: reading the profile, or running a facet search, before the profile has ever been written yields `ERR_SEARCH_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND` (404).

Because this catalogue is a graph rather than a containment tree, almost every collection is flat and every relationship is reachable by filtering. Cue sheets hang off either a licence or a usage report; their cues point at arbitrary works; ledger entries point at licences, cue sheets, usage reports, agreements, statements or claims depending on their origin. Reverse lookup is therefore provided by query filters on the list endpoints rather than by nested paths.

### 2.8. Money, Credits and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary amount in the system is an integer number of cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere, in storage or in a response body. Wherever a monetary result is produced from a multiplication, the result is rounded half up to a whole cent, and each specification in §3 names exactly how many rounding operations occur and where; collapsing two named roundings into one, or inserting an extra one, produces different money and is incorrect.

Performance credits are the one non-monetary quantity carried with decimals: credits are held at exactly 2 decimal places, rounded half up. Percentages that the client supplies or the server derives — share percentages, pool share, discount rate — are carried at 2 decimal places. Fingerprint confidence is carried at 1 decimal place. A relevance score is carried at 2 decimal places. Reported usage is carried in whole minutes.

Three rounding disciplines recur and are easy to get wrong, so they are stated here as well as at their point of use. First, in fee pricing the entire chain of multiplicative factors is evaluated at full precision and rounded exactly once, before any flat add-on is added. Second, in cue-sheet crediting rounding happens once per cue and the total is the sum of the already-rounded per-cue values, never a rounding of the unrounded sum. Third, in pool distribution the proportional share of each work is taken with a floor and the leftover cents are handed out one at a time by largest remainder, so the allocations always sum to the distributable pool exactly.

### 2.9. Status Vocabularies and Parallel Axes

Two entities carry two independent status axes each, and the axes must not be collapsed into one.

A composition has a **catalogue axis**, `catalog_status`, whose values are `INGESTED`, `CATALOGED` and `RETIRED`; a new work starts at `INGESTED` and `RETIRED` is terminal. It also has a **rights axis**, `availability_status`, whose values are `AVAILABLE`, `RESTRICTED` and `WITHDRAWN`; a new work starts at `AVAILABLE` and `WITHDRAWN` is terminal. The two are independent: a `RETIRED` work still shows whatever rights value it last had, and a `CATALOGED` work can perfectly well be `WITHDRAWN`. Retirement is the one operation that writes both axes in the same request.

A blanket agreement has a **coverage axis**, `coverage_status`, whose values are `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `EXPIRED` and `TERMINATED`, starting at `DRAFT` with `EXPIRED` and `TERMINATED` terminal. It also has a **reporting axis**, `reporting_status`, whose values are `COMPLIANT` and `DELINQUENT`, starting at `COMPLIANT`, with neither value terminal — the axis cycles `COMPLIANT → DELINQUENT → COMPLIANT` as reporting lapses and is caught up. An `ACTIVE` agreement can be `DELINQUENT`, and an agreement that reaches `EXPIRED` keeps whatever reporting value it had.

The remaining single-axis vocabularies are: agreement `status` — `DRAFT`, `PENDING_SIGNATURE`, `ACTIVE`, `AMENDMENT_PENDING`, `TERMINATED`; sync-request `status` — `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `QUOTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `EDIT_IN_PROGRESS`, `DELIVERED`, `DECLINED`; licence `status` — `ISSUED`, `ACTIVE`, `EXPIRED`, `REVOKED`; usage-report `status` — `DUE`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED`; cue-sheet `status` — `COLLECTED`, `REGISTERED`, `ACCEPTED_BY_SOCIETY`, `REJECTED_BY_SOCIETY`; claim `status` — `DETECTED`, `NOTICED`, `DISPUTED`, `SETTLED`, `ESCALATED`; statement `status` — `DRAFT`, `FINALIZED`, `PAID`.

### 2.10. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations read a picture of a parent row, make a decision from it, and then write a child row whose correctness depends on that picture. Both are protected by an optimistic version check, and both use the parent's `version` as the token.

Issuing a licence reads the work's rights picture — its `exclusive_block_until`, its `availability_status`, its `active_license_count` — and then writes a new licence against it. A `POST /music-library/licenses` request must therefore carry `expected_composition_version` as a required field, holding the `version` the client last observed on the referenced composition. If that value differs from the composition's current `version`, the request is refused with `ERR_COMPOSITION_VERSION_CONFLICT` (HTTP 412). A caller that read the composition and then had an intervening exclusive issuance land on the same work holds a stale value and is refused; re-reading the composition and retrying with the fresh value succeeds only if the rights picture still permits the licence. Every successful issuance increments the composition's `version`.

Creating a royalty statement sweeps every unswept ledger entry in the period and stamps `statement_id` on each one, so the statement is only correct with respect to the ledger snapshot the caller actually holds. A `POST /music-library/royalty-statements` request must therefore carry `expected_composer_version` holding the `version` the client last observed on the composer. If that value differs from the composer's current `version` — which advances whenever the composer's ledger or advance position moves — the request is refused with `ERR_COMPOSER_VERSION_CONFLICT` (HTTP 412). Every successful statement creation increments the composer's `version`.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two carry no `expected_composition_version` and no `expected_composer_version`, and the server performs no version check on them.

### 2.11. Cascades and Derived-Field Reconciliation

Derived fields are never client-supplied and never drift. Whenever an event changes an input to a derived field, the service recomputes that field in the same request, in a defined order, and every row it touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented. Three cascades run deep enough to be worth naming here, with their full mechanics given in §3.

Terminating a composer agreement runs a four-step chain: the agreement itself terminates, every work it governs is withdrawn, every open sync request on those works is declined, and every licensee whose reservations changed as a result has its credit figures recomputed. Accepting a usage report runs a five-step chain: the report is accepted and its usage frozen, the agreement's year-to-date minutes are recomputed, overage minutes and overage money are recomputed from that, the report's pool figures and per-work allocations are computed including the overage increment just produced, and one ledger entry per allocation is appended for the owning composers. Registering a cue sheet, or having a society reject one, moves performance credits and ledger entries in opposite directions across compositions and composers.

Reversal is always compensating, never destructive. Money that must be undone is undone by appending a new, oppositely-signed ledger entry; an entry that has already been swept into a finalized or paid statement is never mutated or deleted.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Composers, Agreements and the Amendment Cycle

A composer record is the payee identity in this system. Either a `CatalogAdmin` or a `RightsManager` creates one with a `display_name` — the credit name that will appear on cue sheets — and three optional attributes: `pro_affiliation`, naming the performing-rights society the composer is registered with, one of `ASCAP`, `BMI`, `SESAC`, `PRS` or `NONE` and defaulting to `NONE`; `payee_country`, an ISO-3166 alpha-2 code used for the withholding audit trail and defaulting to `US`; and `tax_withholding_pct`, the withholding rate applied to post-recoupment statement amounts, expressed as a fraction between 0.00 and 0.50 with exactly two decimals and defaulting to 0.30. A freshly created composer has `accrued_unpaid_cents`, `outstanding_advance_cents` and `lifetime_paid_cents` all at 0, `latest_statement_period_end` null, and `version` 1.

Those four figures are the composer's money picture and are always derived, never written. `accrued_unpaid_cents` is the sum of the signed amounts of every ledger entry belonging to this composer that has not yet been swept into a statement and whose economic origin is neither an advance nor an advance recoupment. `outstanding_advance_cents` is the negation of the sum of the signed amounts of that composer's advance and advance-recoupment entries — advances are recorded negative and recoupments positive, so the negation yields the unrecouped balance — floored at 0 so it never goes negative. `lifetime_paid_cents` is the sum of `net_payable_cents` over that composer's statements in `PAID`. `latest_statement_period_end` is the `period_end` of that composer's statements ordered by `period_end` descending, taking the first, and is null while the composer has no statement at all.

A `RightsManager` drafts the agreement that governs a composer's works. Creation takes the `composer_id` of the signing composer and an `agreement_type` of either `WORK_FOR_HIRE` — a buyout with no royalty split — or `ROYALTY_SPLIT`. Performance money is divided by `writer_share_pct` and `publisher_share_pct`, the composer's and the library's shares, each between 0.00 and 100.00 with two decimals; the two must add to exactly 100.00 or the request is refused with `ERR_SHARE_SPLIT_NOT_100`. Synchronization money is divided separately by `sync_fee_share_pct`, the composer's share of licence fees and of infringement settlements. `buyout_fee_cents` records a one-time work-for-hire payment and defaults to 0. Those last two fields are cross-checked: a `WORK_FOR_HIRE` agreement must have a `sync_fee_share_pct` of exactly 0 and a `buyout_fee_cents` strictly greater than 0, while a `ROYALTY_SPLIT` agreement must have a `buyout_fee_cents` of exactly 0; any other combination is refused with `ERR_WORK_FOR_HIRE_TERMS_INVALID`. The agreement also declares `exclusivity` as `EXCLUSIVE`, meaning the library is the sole licensor of the works under it, or `NON_EXCLUSIVE`; `holdback_days`, a whole number from 0 through 365 that is appended after an exclusive licence's term to keep the work blocked a while longer, defaulting to 0; `advance_cents`, a recoupable advance of zero or more defaulting to 0; and `blanket_opt_in`, defaulting to true, which when set false bars the works under this agreement from being credited on blanket usage-report cue sheets. A new agreement lands in `DRAFT` with `amendment_round` at 0 and every proposal field null.

Moving the paperwork forward is a two-party affair. A `RightsManager` submits the draft for signature, taking it from `DRAFT` to `PENDING_SIGNATURE` with no ledger or catalogue effect. Only the composer can then sign, supplying a `signature_name`; the acting `X-User-ID` must be the agreement's composer or the request is refused with `ERR_COMPOSER_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. Signature moves `PENDING_SIGNATURE → ACTIVE`, records `signed_at` from the logical clock, and settles the money that signing creates. If `advance_cents` is greater than zero, exactly one ledger entry is appended for the composer with the advance origin, an amount equal to the negation of `advance_cents`, both its accrual and cash recognition instants set to the clock, and the memo `SIGNING_ADVANCE`; the effect is to raise the composer's `outstanding_advance_cents` by `advance_cents`. If the agreement is `WORK_FOR_HIRE` and `buyout_fee_cents` is greater than zero, exactly one entry with the adjustment origin is appended for the positive buyout amount with the memo `WORK_FOR_HIRE_BUYOUT`, raising the composer's `accrued_unpaid_cents`. Both appends increment the composer's `version`.

Terms can be renegotiated up to three times. A `RightsManager` opens an amendment on an `ACTIVE` agreement by supplying `proposed_writer_share_pct`, `proposed_publisher_share_pct`, `proposed_sync_fee_share_pct` and optionally `proposed_holdback_days`. The proposed writer and publisher shares must themselves add to exactly 100.00, again on pain of `ERR_SHARE_SPLIT_NOT_100`, and the amendment is refused with `ERR_AMENDMENT_LIMIT_REACHED` when `amendment_round` has already reached 3. The transition `ACTIVE → AMENDMENT_PENDING` copies the proposal into `pending_writer_share_pct`, `pending_publisher_share_pct`, `pending_sync_fee_share_pct` and `pending_holdback_days` and leaves the effective share fields entirely untouched — royalty maths carried out while an amendment is pending still uses the old, effective values. The composer then decides, supplying a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`, again subject to the identity check. On `ACCEPT`, each pending value is copied into its matching effective field, `amendment_round` increases by 1, and all four pending fields are then cleared to null. On `REJECT`, all four pending fields are cleared to null and both `amendment_round` and every effective field are left exactly as they were. Either decision returns the agreement to `ACTIVE`, so the cycle `ACTIVE → AMENDMENT_PENDING → ACTIVE` can repeat until `amendment_round` reaches 3.

Termination is the exit, and it is the deepest cascade in the system. A `RightsManager` terminates with a `reason` from either `ACTIVE` or `AMENDMENT_PENDING`; from `AMENDMENT_PENDING` the pending fields are cleared to null first, and from then on the two paths behave identically. The agreement moves to `TERMINATED` and records `terminated_at`. Because the agreement is now terminated, every composition it governs whose `availability_status` is `AVAILABLE` or `RESTRICTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. Because those works are now withdrawn, every sync request citing one of them whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `QUOTED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` moves to `DECLINED` with `decline_reason` set to the literal string `CATALOG_WITHDRAWN`, again in `created_at` ascending order. Because those sync requests have left the reserving states, each affected licensee's `reserved_spend_cents` and `credit_available_cents` are recomputed and that licensee's `version` increments exactly once, however many of its requests were declined. Licences already in `ISSUED` or `ACTIVE` are deliberately **not** revoked by termination: they keep their term and their fee, because the licensee bought them in good faith. Any agreement action attempted from a state the transition does not accept — signing a `DRAFT`, amending a `TERMINATED` — is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_STATE_INVALID`.

Agreements are readable individually and as a filtered list, where `composer_id`, `status`, `agreement_type`, `limit` and `offset` narrow the result and a `Composer` sees only rows belonging to that composer.

### 3.2. Ingesting, Cataloguing and Retiring Works

A `CatalogAdmin` ingests a recording by supplying its `composer_id`, the `agreement_id` of the agreement that will govern its splits, exclusivity and holdback, a `title`, a `duration_sec` between 1 and 3600, a `tempo_bpm` between 40 and 240, a `genre` from `CINEMATIC`, `ROCK`, `ELECTRONIC`, `JAZZ`, `FOLK`, `HIPHOP` and `CLASSICAL`, and two facet arrays. `mood_tags` holds from 0 to 5 distinct values drawn from `UPLIFTING`, `HOPEFUL`, `EPIC`, `DARK`, `TENSE`, `CALM` and `QUIRKY`; `instrumentation` holds from 0 to 6 distinct values drawn from `PIANO`, `STRINGS`, `BRASS`, `GUITAR`, `SYNTH`, `PERCUSSION` and `VOCALS`. Both arrays may be empty at ingest — a delivery of raw audio with no tagging is a legitimate intermediate state. The work lands with `catalog_status` `INGESTED`, `availability_status` `AVAILABLE` and `version` 1.

Metadata can be corrected in place: a `CatalogAdmin` may revise `title`, `tempo_bpm`, `genre`, `mood_tags` and `instrumentation`, which refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version`. Neither status axis is ever changed by a metadata revision.

Cataloguing is the gate to commerce, and it takes the work from `INGESTED` to `CATALOGED`. Two conditions guard it. The governing agreement must be in `ACTIVE`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` — a work whose composer has not signed cannot be sold. And the tagging must be complete: `mood_tags` must have at least one member and `instrumentation` must have at least one member, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_METADATA_INCOMPLETE`. Once `CATALOGED`, the work becomes eligible for facet search and for sync requests. Cataloguing a work that is already `CATALOGED`, or already `RETIRED`, is refused with `ERR_COMPOSITION_STATE_INVALID`.

Retirement is available from either `INGESTED` or `CATALOGED` and requires a `reason`. It is refused with `ERR_COMPOSITION_HAS_ACTIVE_LICENSES` while `active_license_count` is greater than 0 — a work under a live licence cannot be pulled. Retirement writes both axes in the same request: `catalog_status` becomes `RETIRED` and `availability_status` becomes `WITHDRAWN`. From that moment the work disappears from facet results and is excluded from sync-request creation and from cue-sheet crediting.

The rights axis moves on its own, driven by infringement work and by agreement termination. A takedown notice on an open claim moves an `AVAILABLE` work to `RESTRICTED`; settlement or escalation of that claim returns it `RESTRICTED → AVAILABLE`, but only once no other claim on the same work remains in `NOTICED` or `DISPUTED`. A `RESTRICTED` work still appears in search results, with its weighted facet score multiplied by the profile's restricted-availability factor, and it may still be licensed non-exclusively; only exclusive issuance is blocked while a claim is open. Withdrawal is reachable from either `AVAILABLE` or `RESTRICTED`, through retirement or through the agreement-termination cascade, and is terminal.

Four figures on a work are derived. `active_license_count` is the number of licences on this work whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `ACTIVE`. `exclusive_block_until` is the greatest `exclusive_block_until` over this work's exclusive licences in `ISSUED` or `ACTIVE`, and is null when that set is empty. `open_claim_count` is the number of claims on this work in `NOTICED` or `DISPUTED`. `lifetime_performance_credits` is the sum, over cue sheets in `REGISTERED` or `ACCEPTED_BY_SOCIETY`, of the credits that sheet attributes to this work, carried at 2 decimals. A fifth figure, `relevance_score`, exists only inside a facet-search response: on a single-work read and in every plain list, it is null.

Works can be read individually or listed with filters on `composer_id`, `agreement_id`, `catalog_status`, `availability_status`, `limit` and `offset`, which is how the catalogue is walked by composer or by agreement.

### 3.3. Facet Search and Relevance Tuning

Discovery in a production library is not title lookup; a music supervisor arrives with a feeling and a tempo. The search endpoint therefore accepts four optional facet parameters — `mood_tags`, `genre`, `instrumentation` and `tempo_bpm` — plus `limit` and `offset`, and returns scored works. The eligible pool is every composition whose `catalog_status` is `CATALOGED` and whose `availability_status` is `AVAILABLE` or `RESTRICTED`; everything else is invisible to search.

Scoring is governed by a single configuration row, the search profile, of which exactly one ever exists. A `CatalogAdmin` writes it with the four facet weights `weight_mood`, `weight_genre`, `weight_instrumentation` and `weight_tempo`, each a whole number from 0 through 100, which must add to exactly 100 or the write is refused with `ERR_WEIGHTS_NOT_100`. Two further knobs are optional: `tempo_tolerance_bpm`, the beat-per-minute distance at which tempo proximity decays to zero, a whole number from 1 through 60 defaulting to 20; and `restricted_availability_factor`, the multiplier applied to the weighted score of a work whose rights axis reads `RESTRICTED`, a value from 0.00 through 1.00 with two decimals defaulting to 0.60. The first write creates the profile and every later write replaces it wholesale. Weights are read from the profile at request time, so changing the profile re-scores every subsequent search. Searching or reading the profile before it has ever been written yields `ERR_SEARCH_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`.

A work's score is built facet by facet. The mood match is the number of values common to the query's `mood_tags` and the work's `mood_tags`, divided by the number of values in the query's `mood_tags`. The genre match is 1 when the query's `genre` equals the work's `genre` and 0 otherwise. The instrumentation match is the number of values common to the query's `instrumentation` and the work's `instrumentation`, divided by the number of values in the query's `instrumentation`. The tempo match is 1 minus the absolute difference between the work's `tempo_bpm` and the query's `tempo_bpm` divided by `tempo_tolerance_bpm`, with any negative result replaced by 0. Only facets the caller actually supplied take part: the weighted figure is 100 multiplied by the sum, over the supplied facets only, of each facet's weight times its match, divided by the sum of the weights of those same supplied facets — so absent facets are dropped and the remaining weights are renormalized rather than diluted.

Two multipliers finish the job. The availability factor is `restricted_availability_factor` when the work's `availability_status` is `RESTRICTED` and 1.00 otherwise. The recency factor is 1.10 when the difference between the current logical clock and the work's `created_at` is at most 90 days, and 1.00 otherwise — new deliveries get a deliberate three-month lift into the top of the results. The `relevance_score` is the weighted figure multiplied by the availability factor and then by the recency factor, rounded half up to 2 decimals exactly once at the very end; because of the recency lift, a score above 100.00 is legitimate and must not be clamped. Results are ordered by `relevance_score` descending, then by `created_at` ascending, then by `id` ascending. When the caller supplies no facet parameter at all, every eligible work scores 0.00 and the ordering degenerates to `created_at` ascending; when the eligible pool is empty, the response is an empty array with a `total_count` of 0.

### 3.4. Rate Cards, Licensee Credit and the Sync-Request Conversation

Pricing starts from a rate card. A `CatalogAdmin` adds a tier with a `code` that must be unique across the rate card — a repeat is refused with `ERR_TIER_CODE_DUPLICATE` — a `media_type` from `BROADCAST_TV`, `ONLINE_AD`, `FILM`, `CORPORATE` and `GAME`, an `allowed_scales` array of 1 to 3 distinct values drawn from `LOCAL`, `NATIONAL` and `WORLDWIDE`, and a `base_rate_cents` strictly greater than 0. The quotable term is bounded by `min_term_months`, defaulting to 1, and `max_term_months`, defaulting to 120, both between 1 and 120 with the maximum at or above the minimum. Two booleans decide which kinds of deal may cite the tier: `per_use_eligible`, defaulting to true, and `blanket_eligible`, defaulting to false.

A `LicensingAgent` opens a licensee account with a `legal_name`, a `licensee_type` from `BROADCASTER`, `PRODUCTION_COMPANY`, `AGENCY` and `GAME_STUDIO`, and a `credit_limit_cents` of zero or more. A new account starts with `committed_spend_cents` at 0, `reserved_spend_cents` at 0 and `credit_available_cents` equal to `credit_limit_cents`. Exposure against that limit is tracked in two derived buckets. `committed_spend_cents` is the sum of `final_fee_cents` over this licensee's licences in `ISSUED` or `ACTIVE`. `reserved_spend_cents` is the sum of `quoted_fee_cents` over this licensee's sync requests in `QUOTED`, `ACCEPTED` or `EDIT_IN_PROGRESS` that have not yet produced a licence. `credit_available_cents` is `credit_limit_cents` less committed less reserved; it can only go negative through a later reduction of the limit itself, never through issuance. Because issuing a licence from a request moves the same money from reserved to committed inside one request, the two buckets never double-count a deal. Both figures are recomputed on every quote, revision, decline, issuance, revocation and expiry, and each recomputation increments the licensee's `version`.

A sync request is the licensee's ask. Either the `Licensee` itself or a `LicensingAgent` creates it with the `composition_id` of the wanted work, the `licensee_id` of the buyer — which, for the `Licensee` role, must be that role's own `X-User-ID` — the `tier_id` of the rate card to price from, a `media_type`, a `distribution_scale`, a `requested_term_start` given as a date at midnight UTC, and a `requested_term_months` between 1 and 120. Five optional flags describe the deliverable and the deal shape: `exclusive_requested`, defaulting to false, asking for media-and-scale exclusivity for the term; `custom_edit_requested`, defaulting to false, requiring an edit pass before delivery; `stems_requested`, defaulting to false, adding the multitrack stems to the deliverable; `alternate_lengths_sec`, an array of 0 to 4 requested cut-down lengths in seconds with each member between 5 and 300, defaulting to empty; and `rush_requested`, defaulting to false, accepting a rush surcharge. A free-text `brief_note` may carry the creative brief and is null when omitted. Two checks guard creation: the work must be `CATALOGED` and not `WITHDRAWN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_COMPOSITION_NOT_LICENSABLE`; and the requested `distribution_scale` must be a member of the tier's `allowed_scales` while the requested `media_type` must equal the tier's own `media_type`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SCALE_NOT_ALLOWED_FOR_TIER`. A new request lands in `SUBMITTED` with `revision_round` 0 and every quote field null.

A `LicensingAgent` picks the request up, moving `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, and then quotes it. Quoting is reachable from `UNDER_REVIEW` and from `REVISION_REQUESTED`, both landing in `QUOTED`. It requires the work to be `CATALOGED` with `availability_status` in `AVAILABLE` or `RESTRICTED`, failing with `ERR_COMPOSITION_NOT_LICENSABLE`; it requires the governing agreement to be `ACTIVE`, failing with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`; and it requires `requested_term_months` to lie within the tier's `min_term_months` and `max_term_months` inclusive, failing with `ERR_TERM_OUTSIDE_TIER_RANGE`. The optional `quote_valid_days` fixes how long the quote stands: `quote_expires_at` is set to the current logical clock plus that many days, defaulting to 14 days.

The fee itself is built in three steps, and the order matters because there is exactly one rounding of the multiplicative part. First, the core is the tier's `base_rate_cents` multiplied by the media factor for the requested `media_type` — 1.30 for `BROADCAST_TV`, 1.75 for `ONLINE_AD`, 2.20 for `FILM`, 0.85 for `CORPORATE`, 1.40 for `GAME` — then by the scale factor for the requested `distribution_scale` — 0.60 for `LOCAL`, 1.00 for `NATIONAL`, 1.90 for `WORLDWIDE` — then by the term factor, which is 0.70 for a term of at most 3 months, 1.00 for a term from 4 through 12 months, 1.45 for a term from 13 through 36 months and 1.90 for a term from 37 through 120 months, then by 1.65 when `exclusive_requested` is true and by nothing otherwise, and finally by 1.20 when `rush_requested` is true and by nothing otherwise. That whole product is rounded half up to a whole number of cents exactly once, before anything is added to it. Second, the flat add-ons are summed as integers and never rounded: 45000 cents when `custom_edit_requested` is true, 30000 cents when `stems_requested` is true, and 12000 cents for each member of `alternate_lengths_sec`. The rounded core plus the add-ons is the pre-discount amount. Third, the volume discount is chosen from a spend basis: the basis is the licensee's `committed_spend_cents` observed at this moment plus this deal's own pre-discount amount, and the rate is 0.00 while the basis is below 500000 cents, 5.00 from 500000 through 1499999 cents, 10.00 from 1500000 through 2999999 cents, and 15.00 at 3000000 cents or above. The fee is the pre-discount amount less the discount, where the discount is the pre-discount amount multiplied by the rate divided by 100 and rounded half up to a whole cent.

That formula is evaluated twice in the life of a deal, in two variants that differ only in the spend snapshot. The provisional variant runs at quote time: the basis adds the `committed_spend_cents` observed at the quote instant and ignores `reserved_spend_cents` entirely. Its outputs are stored on the request as `quoted_fee_cents`, `quoted_spend_basis_cents` — the committed figure observed at quote time plus this quote's pre-discount amount — and `quoted_discount_pct`, the selected rate at 2 decimals. The final variant runs at issuance and is described in §3.5. Quoting also raises the licensee's `reserved_spend_cents` by `quoted_fee_cents` and lowers `credit_available_cents` by the same amount. Before it does so, the credit gate applies: committed plus reserved plus this quote's fee must not exceed `credit_limit_cents`, with equality allowed, or the quote is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

Quotes can be renegotiated three times and no more. A `Licensee` — and only the owning licensee, on pain of `ERR_LICENSEE_MISMATCH` — sends a `revision_note` and optionally `revised_term_months`, `revised_distribution_scale` and `revised_exclusive_requested`, moving the request `QUOTED → REVISION_REQUESTED`. The request is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` once `revision_round` has reached 3. The transition increments `revision_round` by 1, applies whichever revised terms were supplied to the request, clears `quoted_fee_cents`, `quoted_spend_basis_cents`, `quoted_discount_pct` and `quote_expires_at` to null, and releases the licensee's reservation. A revised scale is checked against the tier the same way creation checks it, so a revision to a scale the tier does not allow is refused with `ERR_SCALE_NOT_ALLOWED_FOR_TIER`. Re-quoting from `REVISION_REQUESTED` recomputes the fee from the revised term, scale and exclusivity and replaces the reservation on the licensee — the old quoted amount comes off and the new one goes on.

Acceptance belongs to the licensee alone; a `LicensingAgent` may quote but may never accept on the buyer's behalf. Acceptance moves `QUOTED → ACCEPTED`, requires the acting `X-User-ID` to be the owning licensee, and requires the current logical clock to be at or before `quote_expires_at` — a clock past that instant means `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`. The reservation stays on the licensee until a licence is actually issued from the request.

A `LicensingAgent` may instead decline, from `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `QUOTED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, supplying a `reason` which is recorded as `decline_reason`. Declining releases any reservation the request held: from `QUOTED` this specifically lowers the licensee's `reserved_spend_cents` by `quoted_fee_cents` and raises `credit_available_cents` by the same amount, while from `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` there is no live reservation to release. `DECLINED` and `DELIVERED` are terminal, and any action attempted on a request in a state the transition does not accept is refused with `ERR_SYNC_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`. Requests are readable individually and as a filtered list on `composition_id`, `licensee_id`, `tier_id`, `license_id`, `status`, `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.5. Issuance, Exclusivity, Collection and Delivery

Issuance is where a conversation becomes a contract, and it is the most heavily guarded write in the system. A `LicensingAgent` creates a licence, declaring a `license_mode` of `PER_USE` or `BLANKET` and always supplying `expected_composition_version`. In per-use mode the request carries the `sync_request_id` of an `ACCEPTED` sync request and the service copies `composition_id`, `licensee_id`, `tier_id`, `media_type`, `distribution_scale`, `term_start`, `term_months` and the exclusivity flag from it. In blanket mode the request instead carries `composition_id`, `blanket_agreement_id`, `tier_id`, `media_type`, `distribution_scale`, `term_start` and `term_months` explicitly, and `licensee_id` is copied from the blanket agreement. A per-use request without a sync request, a blanket request missing any of its seven required fields, or a per-use request that also supplies a `blanket_agreement_id`, is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_MODE_FIELDS_INVALID`. The tier must permit the mode: a per-use deal on a tier whose `per_use_eligible` is false, or a blanket deal on a tier whose `blanket_eligible` is false, is refused with `ERR_TIER_INCOMPATIBLE`.

Beyond mode and version, five conditions guard issuance, and their relative order is fixed by §2.6. The work must still be `CATALOGED` and not `WITHDRAWN`, or `ERR_COMPOSITION_NOT_LICENSABLE`. If the source request had `exclusive_requested` true and the work's `open_claim_count` is greater than 0 — that is, some claim on it sits in `NOTICED` or `DISPUTED` — issuance is refused with `ERR_ACTIVE_CLAIM_BLOCKS_LICENSE`; non-exclusive issuance on a `RESTRICTED` work remains perfectly legal. The quote must not have gone stale: a logical clock later than `quote_expires_at` means `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`. The exclusivity window must be free: when the work's `exclusive_block_until` is not null and the new `term_start` falls on or before it, issuance is refused with `ERR_EXCLUSIVE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` — the block day itself is still blocked. For a blanket licence, `term_start` must fall inside the agreement's coverage interval, which is closed at `coverage_start` and open at `coverage_end`, or `ERR_TERM_OUTSIDE_COVERAGE`; and the blanket agreement must be in `ACTIVE` coverage with `reporting_status` `COMPLIANT` at the moment of issuance, or `ERR_BLANKET_NOT_ACTIVE`.

The licence lands in `ISSUED`. Its `term_end` is computed as `term_start` advanced by `term_months` calendar months and then pulled back one day, expressed as a date at midnight UTC; it is the inclusive last day of the term. `is_exclusive` is true only for a per-use licence whose source request had `exclusive_requested` true. `exclusive_block_until` equals `term_end` plus the governing agreement's `holdback_days` days when `is_exclusive` is true, and equals `term_end` exactly when it is false. `final_fee_cents` is the final variant of the fee formula from §3.4, and is 0 for every blanket licence. That final variant differs from the provisional one in exactly one respect: the spend basis adds the `committed_spend_cents` observed at the issuance instant, which includes every licence issued to this licensee after the quote was produced. The recomputed discount tier can therefore differ from `quoted_discount_pct`, and `final_fee_cents` can differ from `quoted_fee_cents`; the quote is never back-patched to match. `final_spend_basis_cents` records the committed figure observed at issuance plus this licence's pre-discount amount, and `final_discount_pct` records the rate selected from it at 2 decimals. The issuance credit gate uses the final figure: committed plus `final_fee_cents` must not exceed `credit_limit_cents`, with equality allowed, or the request is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. It is `final_fee_cents`, never `quoted_fee_cents`, that enters `committed_spend_cents` and that the composer's ledger entry is derived from.

Issuance also fans out. The composition's `version` increments and its `active_license_count` and `exclusive_block_until` are recomputed. For a per-use licence, the source request's `license_id` is set, which moves the amount from the licensee's `reserved_spend_cents` into its `committed_spend_cents` in the same request. The composer's share of the fee, `composer_sync_share_cents`, is computed as `final_fee_cents` multiplied by the governing agreement's `sync_fee_share_pct` divided by 100 and rounded half up to a whole cent, and exactly one ledger entry with the sync-fee origin is appended for that composer with that amount, its accrual instant set to the clock and its cash instant left null — the money has been promised but not yet collected. When `composer_sync_share_cents` works out to 0, as it always does under a work-for-hire agreement, no entry is appended at all. Appending the entry increments the composer's `version`.

Activation is a formality with a date check: `ISSUED → ACTIVE` is allowed only once the current logical clock date has reached `term_start`, and an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_TERM_NOT_STARTED`. It changes no money, since an `ISSUED` licence already counts toward committed spend. Expiry is not requested by anyone; it happens during a clock advance, from `ISSUED` or from `ACTIVE`, once the clock date has passed `term_end`. Expiry lowers the licensee's `committed_spend_cents` by `final_fee_cents` and raises `credit_available_cents` by the same amount, and recomputes the work's `active_license_count` and `exclusive_block_until` — so the work's block lifts as soon as no other exclusive licence in `ISSUED` or `ACTIVE` still covers it.

Revocation is the deliberate unwinding, available to a `RightsManager` from `ISSUED` or from `ACTIVE` with a `reason` recorded as `revoke_reason`. It has four effects, and all four must happen. The licence moves to `REVOKED`. One compensating ledger entry with the sync-fee-reversal origin is appended for the composer, pointing at this licence, with an amount equal to the negation of `composer_sync_share_cents`, its accrual instant set to the clock, and its cash instant set to the clock when the original sync-fee entry had a cash instant and left null when it did not — the effect being that the composer's `accrued_unpaid_cents` returns to its pre-issuance value. The licensee's `committed_spend_cents` falls by `final_fee_cents`. And the work's `active_license_count` and `exclusive_block_until` are recomputed. The reversal entry is appended even when the original sync-fee entry has already been swept into a `FINALIZED` or `PAID` statement: swept entries are never mutated, so the correction lands in the next open period instead. Activating or revoking a licence already in `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED` is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_STATE_INVALID`.

Cash arrives separately from paperwork. A `RoyaltyAccountant` records a payment against a licence by supplying a positive `amount_cents`, which raises `collected_amount_cents`. A payment that is not strictly positive, or that would push `collected_amount_cents` past `final_fee_cents`, is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_FEE`. When `collected_amount_cents` first reaches `final_fee_cents`, the licence's `collected_at` is stamped from the clock and the cash instant is stamped on this licence's sync-fee ledger entry — which is precisely what makes that amount visible to a cash-basis statement. While the licence is short-paid, `collected_at` stays null.

Delivery closes the creative side of a per-use deal, and it is where the branch out of `ACCEPTED` matters. A `CatalogAdmin` may start an edit pass, moving `ACCEPTED → EDIT_IN_PROGRESS` with an optional `edit_note`; this is allowed only when there is edit work to do, meaning `custom_edit_requested` is true, or `stems_requested` is true, or `alternate_lengths_sec` has at least one member. Otherwise it is refused with `ERR_NO_EDIT_WORK_REQUESTED`. Delivery, also by a `CatalogAdmin`, is reachable from either `ACCEPTED` or `EDIT_IN_PROGRESS` and takes a `delivered_asset_count` and an optional `delivery_note`; both paths require the request's `license_id` to be non-null, since nothing may be handed over before a licence exists, and a null one is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_ISSUED`. The two paths then diverge in exactly the way their entry states imply. Arriving straight from `ACCEPTED` is legal only when no edit work was ever asked for — `custom_edit_requested` false, `stems_requested` false, and `alternate_lengths_sec` empty — and an attempt to skip a requested edit pass is refused with `ERR_EDIT_PASS_REQUIRED`; on that path `delivered_asset_count` need only be at least 1, and a value below 1 is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Arriving from `EDIT_IN_PROGRESS`, the count must cover the full deliverable set: at least 1 for the master, plus 1 more when `stems_requested` is true, plus one more for each member of `alternate_lengths_sec`; anything short is refused with `ERR_DELIVERABLE_COUNT_SHORT`. Either way the request reaches the terminal `DELIVERED` and records `delivered_asset_count`. No credit figure moves at delivery, because the reservation was already released when the licence was issued.

Licences are readable individually and as a list filtered by `composition_id`, `licensee_id`, `tier_id`, `sync_request_id`, `blanket_agreement_id`, `status`, `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.6. Blanket Coverage, Usage Reporting and the Pool

Broadcasters do not want to negotiate every cue. A blanket agreement sells them a period of unlimited-within-a-cap access in exchange for an annual fee and a reporting obligation. A `LicensingAgent` drafts one with the `licensee_id` of the broadcaster or production company, a `coverage_start` that is the inclusive first day of coverage and a `coverage_end` that is the **exclusive** end — coverage is the half-open interval running from `coverage_start` up to but not including `coverage_end` — an `annual_fee_cents` strictly greater than 0, a `reporting_cadence` of `MONTHLY` for one-month periods or `QUARTERLY` for three-month periods, a `usage_cap_minutes` of zero or more included minutes per coverage term, and a `pool_share_pct` between 0.00 and 100.00 with two decimals, being the percentage of the fee and of any overage that funds the composer distribution pool. A `coverage_end` at or before `coverage_start` is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_DATES_INVALID`. Coverage may not overlap: if another agreement for the same licensee whose `coverage_status` is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` has a coverage interval overlapping the new one, creation is refused with `ERR_BLANKET_COVERAGE_OVERLAP`; because the intervals are half-open, an agreement ending exactly where the next begins does not overlap it. A new agreement lands with `coverage_status` `DRAFT` and `reporting_status` `COMPLIANT`.

Activation moves `coverage_status` `DRAFT → ACTIVE` and is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_WINDOW_PASSED` when the current logical clock date has already reached `coverage_end`. Once active and compliant, blanket-mode licence issuance against the agreement becomes possible. Expiry happens on a clock advance, `ACTIVE → EXPIRED`, once the clock date reaches `coverage_end` — the interval being half-open means the end day itself is outside coverage. From then on blanket issuance is refused with `ERR_BLANKET_NOT_ACTIVE`, while licences already issued are untouched. Termination by a `LicensingAgent` with a `reason` is reachable from `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`; from `ACTIVE` it also rejects every usage report of the agreement still sitting in `DUE`, setting each one's `rejection_reason` to the literal `BLANKET_TERMINATED` and processing them in `created_at` ascending order, while from `DRAFT` there are no reports to reject because the agreement never carried any. Activating a non-`DRAFT` agreement, or terminating one already `EXPIRED` or `TERMINATED`, is refused with `ERR_BLANKET_STATE_INVALID`.

The reporting obligation is a periodic one. Coverage is divided into cadence periods starting at `coverage_start` — one month each under `MONTHLY`, three months each under `QUARTERLY` — and the licensee owes one usage report per period. A report is created by the `Licensee` or a `LicensingAgent` with the `blanket_agreement_id`, a `period_start` that must be exactly a cadence boundary derived from `coverage_start`, and a `period_end` that must be exactly that boundary advanced by one cadence and pulled back one day; a period that is not a cadence boundary, whose end does not match the cadence, or that falls outside the coverage interval, is refused with `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_INVALID`. A second report for the same period is refused with `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_DUPLICATE` when an existing report for that agreement with the same `period_start` sits in `DUE`, `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` — which is what allows a rejected period to be re-created. New reports land in `DUE` with zero pool figures and an empty `pool_allocations` array.

Submission is the licensee's own act: only the `Licensee` that owns the agreement may submit, on pain of `ERR_LICENSEE_MISMATCH`. Submission is reachable from `DUE` and from `REJECTED`, both landing in `SUBMITTED`, and requires at least one cue sheet to be attached to the report, otherwise `ERR_REPORT_HAS_NO_CUE_SHEETS`. It records `submitted_at` from the clock, and on the path from `REJECTED` it also increments `resubmission_count` by 1 and clears `rejection_reason` to null. Submission additionally re-evaluates the agreement's reporting axis and can move it `DELINQUENT → COMPLIANT`, which clears `delinquent_since` to null; that move is allowed only once every cadence period whose grace window has already closed has a report in `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED`. A `RightsManager` may reject a `SUBMITTED` report with a `reason` recorded as `rejection_reason`; rejection computes no pool and appends no ledger entry, and the `REJECTED → SUBMITTED` loop is unbounded. Submitting an already-`SUBMITTED` report, or accepting one still `DUE`, is refused with `ERR_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`.

Delinquency is assessed on every clock advance, against agreements whose `coverage_status` is `ACTIVE`. A cadence period is overdue at a given instant when that instant is later than the period's `period_end` plus a grace window of 15 days and no report for that agreement with that `period_start` sits in `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED`. One overdue period is enough: the agreement moves `COMPLIANT → DELINQUENT` and records `delinquent_since` from the clock. While `DELINQUENT`, blanket-mode issuance against the agreement is refused with `ERR_BLANKET_NOT_ACTIVE` even though coverage itself is untouched — this is the lever that makes reporting matter.

Acceptance is the money event, and it runs a five-step cascade in this order. **Step one**: a `RightsManager` accepts a `SUBMITTED` report, which requires the agreement's `coverage_status` to be `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED` — an accepted report against a terminated agreement is refused with `ERR_BLANKET_NOT_ACTIVE` — sets the report to the terminal `ACCEPTED`, stamps `accepted_at`, and freezes `reported_minutes_total` and `reported_credits_total` from the attached cue sheets whose `status` is `REGISTERED` or `ACCEPTED_BY_SOCIETY`. `reported_minutes_total` is the sum of those sheets' `total_seconds` divided by 60 and rounded **up** to a whole minute; because the ceiling is applied once per report, partial minutes never carry from one period into the next. `reported_credits_total` is the sum of those sheets' `total_credits` at 2 decimals. **Step two**: because this report is now `ACCEPTED`, the agreement's `reported_minutes_ytd` is recomputed as the sum of `reported_minutes_total` over all of its accepted reports. **Step three**: because year-to-date minutes changed, `overage_minutes` is recomputed as `reported_minutes_ytd` less `usage_cap_minutes`, floored at 0 — usage exactly at the cap is not over it — and `overage_cents` as `overage_minutes` multiplied by the overage rate of 4.50 dollars per minute, that is 450 cents per reported minute. The difference between `overage_cents` after this acceptance and `overage_cents` before it is the overage increment that step four consumes; year-to-date minutes are order-independent, but the increment, and therefore which report's pool absorbs the overage, does depend on the order in which reports are accepted.

**Step four**: because the overage increment is known, this report's pool figures are computed. The cadence fraction is one twelfth for `MONTHLY` and one quarter for `QUARTERLY`, treated as an exact fraction with no intermediate rounding. `pool_cents` is the sum of two separately rounded terms: the agreement's `annual_fee_cents` multiplied by `pool_share_pct`, divided by 100, multiplied by the cadence fraction, rounded half up; plus the overage increment multiplied by `pool_share_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up. `pool_admin_fee_cents` is `pool_cents` multiplied by the administration retention of 15.00 percent divided by 100, rounded half up. `pool_distributable_cents` is `pool_cents` less `pool_admin_fee_cents`. The eligible works are the distinct compositions cited by the cues of this report's attached cue sheets whose `status` is `REGISTERED` or `ACCEPTED_BY_SOCIETY` and whose governing agreement has `blanket_opt_in` true. Each eligible work's credits are the summed per-work credits across those sheets, and the total is the sum of those credit figures. Each work's floor share is the largest whole number not exceeding `pool_distributable_cents` multiplied by that work's credits divided by the total credits. The remainder is `pool_distributable_cents` less the sum of the floor shares, and exactly that many works — taken in order of credits descending, then `created_at` ascending, then `id` ascending — each receive exactly one additional cent. Each work's `allocation_cents` is its floor share plus that one extra cent when it falls in the head slice. The sum of all allocations equals `pool_distributable_cents` exactly, for every possible input; no cent is created and none is destroyed. The results are recorded as `pool_allocations`, an array of objects each carrying `composition_id`, `credits` and `allocation_cents`. When the eligible set is empty, `pool_allocations` is an empty array, `pool_cents` and `pool_admin_fee_cents` are still computed, and `pool_distributable_cents` is simply retained by the library with no ledger entry appended. **Step five**: because allocations exist, one ledger entry with the blanket-pool origin is appended per allocation, for the composer of that allocation's work, whose amount is the allocation multiplied by the governing agreement's `writer_share_pct` divided by 100 and rounded half up, pointing at this report, with both its accrual and cash instants set to the clock; each append raises that composer's `accrued_unpaid_cents`, and the entries are appended in `pool_allocations` order.

The agreement carries two running totals over its accepted reports: `pool_distributed_cents`, the sum of their `pool_distributable_cents`, and `pool_admin_fee_cents`, the sum of their `pool_admin_fee_cents` — the same name appearing on both the report and the agreement, per-period on the one and cumulative on the other. Reports are readable individually and as a list filtered by `blanket_agreement_id`, `status`, `limit` and `offset`; agreements likewise by `licensee_id`, `coverage_status`, `reporting_status`, `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.7. Cue Sheets and Performance Royalty Registration

A cue sheet is the document that tells a rights society which works were heard, for how long, and in what capacity. Either the `Licensee` that owns the parent or a `LicensingAgent` creates one with a `production_title` naming the programme, a `broadcast_date` given as a date at midnight UTC for the first transmission, a `market_tier` of `NETWORK`, `CABLE`, `LOCAL` or `DIGITAL` weighting that transmission, and a `cues` array of 1 to 20 objects, each carrying a `composition_id` that must reference an existing work, a `usage_type` of `THEME`, `FEATURE`, `BACKGROUND` or `LOGO`, and a `duration_sec` between 1 and 3600. Cue array members are validated in array order, so the first bad element is the one reported.

Parentage is exclusive. Exactly one of `license_id` and `usage_report_id` must be supplied: the first for per-use reporting against a single licence, the second for blanket reporting against a period. Supplying both, or neither, is refused with `ERR_CUE_SHEET_PARENT_INVALID`. What a cue may cite then depends on which parent it has. Under a licence parent, every cue must cite the very work that licence covers, and any other work is refused with `ERR_CUE_COMPOSITION_NOT_COVERED`. Under a usage-report parent, a cue may not cite a work whose `availability_status` is `WITHDRAWN`, nor a work whose governing agreement has `blanket_opt_in` false — a composer who opted out of blanket exploitation is not swept into a broadcaster's pool — and either case is refused with the same `ERR_CUE_COMPOSITION_NOT_COVERED`, with array members checked in order.

Credits are computed at creation and never again. For each cue, in array order, the credit figure is the cue's `duration_sec` divided by 30, multiplied by the usage weight for its `usage_type` — 1.00 for `THEME`, 0.65 for `FEATURE`, 0.35 for `BACKGROUND`, 0.10 for `LOGO` — multiplied by the market weight for the sheet's `market_tier` — 1.00 for `NETWORK`, 0.65 for `CABLE`, 0.40 for `LOCAL`, 0.25 for `DIGITAL` — and rounded half up to 2 decimals. That rounding happens once per cue, before any summation. `cue_credit_breakdown` mirrors the `cues` array in the same order, each element carrying `composition_id`, `usage_type`, `duration_sec` and the resulting `credits`. `total_credits` is the sum of those already-rounded per-cue figures — never a rounding of the unrounded sum, which can differ by a cent of credit. `total_seconds` is the plain sum of the cues' `duration_sec`. `credits_by_composition` is a map from each cited `composition_id` to the sum of the rounded per-cue credits for that work on this sheet. A new sheet lands in `COLLECTED`.

Registration is the `RightsManager`'s act of filing the sheet with a society, supplying a `society_reference`. It is reachable from `COLLECTED` and from `REJECTED_BY_SOCIETY`, both landing in `REGISTERED`, and is guarded twice: `total_credits` must be greater than 0.00, otherwise `ERR_CUE_SHEET_HAS_NO_CREDITS`, and every cited work's composer must have a `pro_affiliation` other than `NONE`, otherwise `ERR_COMPOSER_NOT_AFFILIATED` — an unaffiliated writer cannot be registered anywhere. Registration records `society_reference` and `registered_at`, increments `registration_round` by 1, and raises `lifetime_performance_credits` on every work the sheet cites.

Registration also recognizes performance money, in two roundings that must not be collapsed into one. For each composer credited on the sheet, first sum that composer's works' entries in `credits_by_composition` to get the composer's credit figure at 2 decimals. Then multiply that credit figure by the credit value of 1250 cents per credit and round half up to a whole cent — that is the gross performance amount. Then multiply the gross amount by the governing agreement's `writer_share_pct`, divide by 100, and round half up again — that is the writer's amount, and it is the amount of the single ledger entry with the performance origin appended for that composer. The publisher remainder, being the gross amount less the writer's amount, is retained by the library and never appears in the ledger. Only works whose governing agreement is `ACTIVE` and whose composer has a `pro_affiliation` other than `NONE` are credited at all. Composers are processed in ascending order of their `created_at`, so entry order is stable. Every such entry has its accrual instant set to the clock and its cash instant left null, because society money has not been collected yet.

The society then answers, and a `RightsManager` records the answer with a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`. On `ACCEPT` the sheet moves `REGISTERED → ACCEPTED_BY_SOCIETY` and the cash instant is stamped from the clock on every performance ledger entry pointing at this sheet whose cash instant is still null — this is the only mutation ever applied to a ledger entry other than the statement stamp, and it is what makes those amounts visible to a cash-basis statement. On `REJECT` a `rejection_reason` must be supplied, absent which the request is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`; the sheet moves `REGISTERED → REJECTED_BY_SOCIETY`, and one compensating entry with the performance-reversal origin is appended per previously credited composer, each for the negation of the performance amount it compensates. Because the sheet has now left both `REGISTERED` and `ACCEPTED_BY_SOCIETY`, `lifetime_performance_credits` on every cited work returns to its pre-registration value. Re-registration after a society rejection is unbounded and increments `registration_round` each time. Registering an already-`REGISTERED` sheet, or recording a decision on a `COLLECTED` one, is refused with `ERR_CUE_SHEET_STATE_INVALID`. Sheets are readable individually and as a list filtered by `license_id`, `usage_report_id`, `status`, `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.8. Fingerprint Detection, Claims and Settlement

Unauthorized use is discovered by an audio-fingerprinting vendor, whose findings arrive as immutable detection events. A `CatalogAdmin` records one with the `composition_id` of the matched work, a `source_platform` of `UGC_VIDEO`, `BROADCAST`, `THEATRICAL` or `PODCAST`, a `source_reference` locating the detected use on the vendor's side, a `matched_seconds` between 1 and 7200 of the work found in the source, and a `confidence_pct` between 0.0 and 100.0 carried at 1 decimal. The service stamps `detected_at` from the clock. Nothing else happens: no claim is created, and the record is never updated. Matches are readable individually and as a list filtered by `composition_id`, `source_platform`, `limit` and `offset`.

A `RightsManager` turns a detection into a claim by supplying the `fingerprint_match_id`. Two gates apply. The match's `confidence_pct` must be at least 70.0, and a lower confidence is refused with `ERR_CONFIDENCE_BELOW_CLAIM_THRESHOLD`. And no other claim may already cite the same match, on pain of `ERR_MATCH_ALREADY_CLAIMED`. The claim copies `composition_id` from the match, lands in `DETECTED`, and computes its exposure figures immediately. The confidence factor is 1.00 when `confidence_pct` is at least 95.0, 0.75 when it is from 85.0 through 94.9, and 0.50 when it is from 70.0 through 84.9; the bands are closed on their lower bound, so a confidence of exactly 85.0 belongs to the 0.75 band while 84.9 belongs to the 0.50 band. `statutory_amount_cents` is the smaller of the statutory ceiling of 15000000 cents and the value of `matched_seconds` multiplied by the platform rate per second — 1000 cents for `UGC_VIDEO`, 4500 for `BROADCAST`, 9000 for `THEATRICAL`, 1500 for `PODCAST` — multiplied by the confidence factor and rounded half up to a whole cent. The ceiling is applied after that rounding, so a capped claim reports exactly 15000000 cents. `settlement_floor_cents`, the lowest negotiated amount the library will accept, is `statutory_amount_cents` multiplied by 30.00 percent divided by 100 and rounded half up.

Serving notice is the step that touches the catalogue. A `RightsManager` sends a takedown with a `notice_channel`, moving the claim `DETECTED → NOTICED` and recording `noticed_at`. Notice demands more confidence than claim creation did: `confidence_pct` must be at least 85.0, and anything lower is refused with `ERR_CONFIDENCE_BELOW_NOTICE_THRESHOLD`. Notice also moves the cited work's `availability_status` from `AVAILABLE` to `RESTRICTED`, which downweights it in search and blocks exclusive issuance on it while the claim stays open. The counterparty may push back: a `dispute_note` moves the claim `NOTICED → DISPUTED` and the work stays `RESTRICTED`.

Settlement closes the claim with money, and is reachable from either `NOTICED` or `DISPUTED`. The `RightsManager` supplies a `settlement_basis` of `STATUTORY` or `NEGOTIATED` and a `payment_received` flag, plus a `negotiated_amount_cents` when the basis is negotiated. On the statutory basis, `settlement_amount_cents` is `statutory_amount_cents` unchanged. On the negotiated basis it is `negotiated_amount_cents`, which must be at least `settlement_floor_cents` — equality is accepted — and a lower amount, or a negotiated basis with the amount omitted altogether, is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_BELOW_FLOOR`. The claim reaches the terminal `SETTLED`, recording `settlement_amount_cents`, `settlement_basis`, `settlement_collected` from the `payment_received` flag, and `settled_at`. `composer_share_cents` is computed as `settlement_amount_cents` multiplied by the governing agreement's `sync_fee_share_pct` divided by 100 and rounded half up, and exactly one ledger entry with the settlement origin is appended for that amount, with its accrual instant set to the clock and its cash instant set to the clock when `payment_received` is true and left null otherwise. Settlement finally returns the work from `RESTRICTED` to `AVAILABLE`, but only once `open_claim_count` has reached 0 — a second open claim on the same work keeps it restricted.

Escalation is the other exit, reachable only from `DISPUTED`. An `escalation_note` records the litigation referral and the claim reaches the terminal `ESCALATED`. No ledger entry is appended, because no money has been agreed; the work still returns from `RESTRICTED` to `AVAILABLE` once `open_claim_count` reaches 0, on exactly the same condition as settlement. Noticing a claim twice, escalating one still in `NOTICED`, or acting at all on one already `SETTLED` or `ESCALATED`, is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. Claims are readable individually and as a list filtered by `composition_id`, `fingerprint_match_id`, `status`, `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.9. The Royalty Ledger, Statements and Payout

Everything a composer is owed lands in one append-only ledger. Each entry records the `composer_id` that owns it, a `source_type` naming its economic origin, a signed `amount_cents`, a `memo`, and a `source_ref_id` whose meaning is discriminated by the origin. The nine origins are `SYNC_FEE` and `SYNC_FEE_REVERSAL`, whose `source_ref_id` is a licence; `PERFORMANCE` and `PERFORMANCE_REVERSAL`, whose `source_ref_id` is a cue sheet; `BLANKET_POOL`, whose `source_ref_id` is a usage report; `SETTLEMENT`, whose `source_ref_id` is an infringement claim; `ADVANCE`, whose `source_ref_id` is a composer agreement; `ADVANCE_RECOUP`, whose `source_ref_id` is a royalty statement; and `ADJUSTMENT`, whose `source_ref_id` is null. Amounts are negative for `ADVANCE`, `SYNC_FEE_REVERSAL` and `PERFORMANCE_REVERSAL` and positive for every other origin. Every entry also carries `accrual_recognized_at`, the instant the obligation was recognized — at issuance, registration, acceptance or settlement — and `cash_recognized_at`, the instant the underlying money was actually collected, which stays null while the money is outstanding. `statement_id` is null while the entry is unswept and is stamped exactly once, when a statement includes it.

Only one origin may be appended by hand. A `RoyaltyAccountant` may append an entry supplying `composer_id`, `source_type`, `amount_cents` and `memo`, but the origin must be `ADJUSTMENT`; every other origin is produced solely by the workflows above and is refused here with `ERR_LEDGER_SOURCE_NOT_MANUAL`. A manual append sets both `accrual_recognized_at` and `cash_recognized_at` to the clock, leaves `statement_id` null, and increments the composer's `version`. Entries are readable individually and as a list filtered by `composer_id`, `statement_id`, `source_type`, `limit` and `offset`, with a `Composer` restricted to its own rows.

Before committing to a statement, an accountant — or the composer — can dry-run one. The royalty preview takes a `period_start` and `period_end` and an optional `basis`, defaults the basis to `ACCRUAL`, and evaluates the whole statement calculation for that window without creating a statement and without stamping any entry. It returns `basis`, `accrual_gross_cents` and `cash_gross_cents` — always both, so the two bases can be compared on identical data — plus `gross_cents`, `included_entry_count`, `outstanding_advance_cents`, `projected_recoup_cents`, `projected_withholding_cents`, `projected_net_cents` and `projected_payout_status`, all of which follow the selected basis. Nothing is written and the composer's `version` does not change, which is exactly why the preview needs no version token.

Creating the statement is the committing act, and it belongs to a `RoyaltyAccountant`. The request carries the `composer_id`, the inclusive `period_start` and `period_end` as dates at midnight UTC, the `basis`, and `expected_composer_version`. Periods may not overlap: another statement for the same composer covering an overlapping window, both endpoints inclusive, is refused with `ERR_STATEMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. The sweep window runs from `period_start` at the very start of the day to `period_end` at 23:59:59.999, both endpoints inclusive. The sweep set is every ledger entry belonging to this composer whose `statement_id` is still null, whose origin is neither `ADVANCE` nor `ADVANCE_RECOUP`, and whose relevant timestamp falls inside the window; entries are taken in `created_at` ascending order.

Which timestamp is relevant is the only difference between the two bases, and it is a material one. Under `ACCRUAL`, the window is tested against `accrual_recognized_at`, so an obligation recognized in the period is included even when no money has been collected — a performance entry from a cue sheet registered but not yet accepted by the society is swept on this basis. Under `CASH`, the window is tested against `cash_recognized_at` and any entry whose `cash_recognized_at` is null is excluded entirely, deferring uncollected obligations to a later period. Nothing is lost either way: an entry excluded by a cash-basis statement is swept by a later cash-basis statement once its `cash_recognized_at` is stamped, and because `statement_id` is stamped exactly once, no entry can be paid twice under either basis.

From the sweep set the statement derives its figures. `gross_cents` is the sum of the swept `amount_cents`, so reversal entries reduce it. `line_count` is the number of entries in the sweep set, excluding the recoupment entry the statement itself creates. `recouped_cents` is the smaller of the composer's `outstanding_advance_cents` and `gross_cents` treated as zero when negative, so a negative gross never manufactures a recoupment. `withholding_cents` is the post-recoupment base — `gross_cents` less `recouped_cents` — multiplied by the composer's `tax_withholding_pct` and rounded half up exactly once. `net_payable_cents` is `gross_cents` less `recouped_cents` less `withholding_cents`. `payout_status` is `NO_ACTIVITY` when `gross_cents` is 0, `HELD_BELOW_MINIMUM` when `net_payable_cents` is below the minimum payout of 2500 cents, and `PAYABLE` otherwise. Creation stamps `statement_id` on every swept entry, and when `recouped_cents` is greater than 0 appends exactly one entry with the advance-recoupment origin, for the positive recouped amount and already carrying this statement's `statement_id`, so the composer's `outstanding_advance_cents` falls by exactly `recouped_cents`. The statement lands in `DRAFT`, and the composer's `version` increments.

Finalization is the point of no return: `DRAFT → FINALIZED` records `finalized_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and `finalized_at` from the clock, and is refused with `ERR_STATEMENT_NO_ACTIVITY` when `payout_status` reads `NO_ACTIVITY`, since there is nothing to state. The swept entries keep this statement's `statement_id` forever and can never be swept again. Payment then moves `FINALIZED → PAID`, taking a `payment_reference` recorded on the statement alongside `paid_by_user_id` and `paid_at`, and raising the composer's `lifetime_paid_cents` by `net_payable_cents`. Two guards apply, in this order: `payout_status` must read `PAYABLE`, so a statement that is `HELD_BELOW_MINIMUM` or has no activity is refused with `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_PAYABLE`; and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `finalized_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. `PAID` is terminal. Finalizing twice, paying a `DRAFT` statement, or acting at all on a `PAID` one, is refused with `ERR_STATEMENT_STATE_INVALID`. Statements are readable individually and as a list filtered by `composer_id`, `status`, `basis`, `limit` and `offset`, with a `Composer` seeing only its own.
