# False Alarm Reduction Permit Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Police departments spend a disproportionate share of patrol hours answering alarms that turn out to be nothing: a cleaner who forgot the code, a heat detector triggered by a radiator, a panel that dials the central station every time the wind moves a door. The city's false alarm reduction unit exists to push that cost back onto the premises that generate it, and to do so through a defensible administrative process rather than an ad-hoc one. Every alarm system in the city must be registered and permitted; every permit runs an annual term with a renewal deadline and a grace window; every dispatch the police actually roll on is verified against enhanced call-verification requirements before units leave the station; and every alarm that produces no crime is counted against the premises address on an escalating fee ladder that eventually withdraws police response altogether.

This document specifies the service that runs that program end to end. It covers ordinance configuration (fee tiers, thresholds, deadlines and exempt causes held in a versioned fee schedule), premises and system registration, permit issuance and renewal, the dispatch-request pipeline operated jointly by monitoring companies and the police communications centre, false alarm determination and occurrence counting, contested-determination appeals heard on an independent officer's docket, suspension of police response with its reinstatement conditions, and the money trail — assessment, invoicing, payment allocation, dunning and referral of unpaid penalties to an outside collections agency. It also covers the monitoring companies themselves, who owe the unit periodic dispatch-accuracy reports and lose their dispatch-request privilege when their reporting drifts too far from what the system recorded.

Two properties matter more than any individual rule. First, **the program must be reconstructible**: a permit holder who contests a fee is entitled to see exactly which occurrence index produced it, which multipliers applied, and which schedule governed — so every assessment carries an audit of its own inputs. Second, **the program must be reversible without drift**: a granted appeal, a remanded determination, a voided incident or a voided payment has to leave the premises account in precisely the state it would have occupied had the reversed event never happened, including the occurrence numbering of every later incident at the same address. The whole API is mounted under the `/false-alarm-program` prefix, and every deadline it enforces is measured against a server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that the program's behaviour is deterministic and testable.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Program Actors and Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller is acting. If either header is absent or empty the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` before the body is parsed, before any path segment is resolved, and before any role lookup happens. If both headers are present but `X-User-Role` does not match one of the eight declared role names exactly, case included, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`. A recognized role that is not listed among the authorized roles for the endpoint it invoked is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

The eight roles are `ProgramAdministrator`, `PermitClerk`, `AlarmCompanyOperator`, `AlarmUser`, `PoliceDispatchSupervisor`, `FalseAlarmReviewOfficer`, `HearingOfficer` and `BillingClerk`.

The `ProgramAdministrator` is the head of the reduction unit. The role owns program configuration, suspension orders, administrative voids, invoice adjustments and every override, and it is the only role permitted to move the logical clock or trigger the nightly program batch. A `PermitClerk` staffs the permit desk: premises, contacts and system registrations, permit issuance and renewal, and service of suspension orders — never pricing, waiving or adjudication. A `PoliceDispatchSupervisor` runs the communications-centre side: validating, refusing, dispatching and closing dispatch requests. A `FalseAlarmReviewOfficer` rules each incident chargeable or exempt and reviews monitoring-company reports. A `HearingOfficer` presides over the appeal docket and decides contested determinations. A `BillingClerk` posts payments and refers unpaid penalties to collections, but never adjusts, waives or writes off an assessment. An `AlarmCompanyOperator` is a central-station operator lodging dispatch requests for the systems their company monitors and filing that company's compliance reports. An `AlarmUser` is the owner or occupant of the alarmed premises, who registers systems and contacts, renews permits, contests determinations and requests reinstatement of police response.

Six of those roles are **opaque**: when `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdministrator`, `PermitClerk`, `PoliceDispatchSupervisor`, `FalseAlarmReviewOfficer`, `HearingOfficer` or `BillingClerk`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared to any entity's `id`. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `issued_by_user_id` on a permit at issuance, `validated_by_user_id` on a dispatch request, `determined_by_user_id` on an incident, `decided_by_user_id` on an appeal, `reviewed_by_user_id` on a compliance report, `recorded_by_user_id` on a payment, and `hearing_officer_user_id` on a docket — but it carries no ownership semantics of its own.

Two roles are **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `AlarmUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `AlarmSite`; ownership checks on any resource that references a premises compare the resource's premises reference to `X-User-ID`, whether that reference is the site itself or the site reachable from the target system, permit, incident, appeal, invoice, payment or allocation. When `X-User-Role` is `AlarmCompanyOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `AlarmCompany`; ownership checks compare the company reachable from the target resource — the company itself, or the monitoring company of the target system, dispatch request or compliance report — to `X-User-ID`. A mismatch in either direction is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Opaque roles skip the ownership check entirely.

Two further access rules cut across roles. Segregation of duties forbids an operator from performing an administrative step they have already prejudged, and a violation is reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; the specific pairings are set out in §2.11. Docket ownership restricts hearing work to the officer whose docket it is, and a violation is reported as `ERR_NOT_DOCKET_OFFICER`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated with two optional query inputs. `page` is an integer that must be at or above 1 and defaults to 1. `per_page` is an integer that must lie between 1 and 100 inclusive and defaults to 10. A `page` below 1, a `per_page` outside the range 1 to 100, or a sort key that does not name a declared field of the entity being listed is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.

The `meta` block of a list response reports the window in absolute terms: `limit` carries the effective page size (default 10, minimum 1, maximum 100), `offset` carries the number of rows skipped (default 0, being the page size multiplied by one less than the requested page), `total_count` carries the number of rows matching the filter before the window is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window. Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Filters supplied on a list endpoint combine conjunctively — a request naming both a premises and a status returns only rows matching both.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix; clients never supply it. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant recorded at insertion. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row.

Instant-valued fields are rendered in the string form their value arrived in. A field holding an instant a client supplied — echoed from the write's own body, or equal to the instant an administrator last set the logical clock to, which is what every field stamped "at the logical clock" records — is returned in exactly the string the client wrote, fractional digits included or omitted as the client wrote them. An instant the server derives by arithmetic instead, such as a term end, a grace horizon or an invoice due date, is rendered as ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits, which is also the form used before the clock has ever been set. Both forms denote the same instant and every comparison the program makes is on the instant, never on its rendering.

There is one deliberate exception. `PaymentAllocation` rows are immutable ledger lines produced as a by-product of posting a payment; they carry only `id` and `created_at`. An allocation has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all — when an allocation is reversed, its `status` moves from `APPLIED` to `REVERSED` and the reversal is recorded through the parent payment's own mutation, not through a revision counter on the line.

Any operation that recomputes a derived counter on a parent — a premises roll-up, a docket seat count, a company counter — is a mutation of that parent and therefore refreshes its `updated_at` and bumps its `version`. The one documented exception is the read-only false alarm summary on a premises: it recomputes and returns the posture and balance figures at the current logical clock without persisting a version bump.

### 2.5. Path Resolution and Referential Existence

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, so the leftmost unresolvable segment determines the error. The service exposes single-resource reads keyed on `scheduleId`, `companyId`, `siteId`, `contactId`, `systemId`, `permitId`, `requestId`, `incidentId`, `docketId`, `appealId`, `orderId`, `invoiceId`, `paymentId`, `allocationId` and `reportId`, and each unresolvable identifier produces its own code: `ERR_FEE_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALARM_COMPANY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALARM_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SITE_CONTACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALARM_SYSTEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALARM_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPATCH_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FALSE_ALARM_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HEARING_DOCKET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUSPENSION_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PENALTY_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYMENT_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYMENT_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_COMPLIANCE_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`. Where an identifier cannot be attributed to a specific entity type, the umbrella code `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` is returned instead. All fifteen entity-specific codes and the umbrella code carry HTTP 404.

The same codes serve references supplied inside a request body, which are resolved only after every path segment has resolved, and among themselves in the order the fields are declared for that operation. A `monitoring_company_id` that names nothing yields `ERR_ALARM_COMPANY_NOT_FOUND`; an `alarm_site_id` yields `ERR_ALARM_SITE_NOT_FOUND`; an `alarm_system_id` yields `ERR_ALARM_SYSTEM_NOT_FOUND`; an `alarm_permit_id` yields `ERR_ALARM_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`; a `dispatch_request_id` yields `ERR_DISPATCH_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`; a `false_alarm_incident_id` or a `triggering_incident_id` yields `ERR_FALSE_ALARM_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`; a `hearing_docket_id` yields `ERR_HEARING_DOCKET_NOT_FOUND`; a `prior_appeal_id` yields `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND`; a `suspension_order_id` yields `ERR_SUSPENSION_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`; a `site_contact_id` named inside a verification call log yields `ERR_SITE_CONTACT_NOT_FOUND`; a `penalty_invoice_id` named inside an explicit payment split yields `ERR_PENALTY_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`; and a `governing_fee_schedule_id` that cannot be resolved yields `ERR_FEE_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock and the Nightly Program Run

Nothing in this program consults wall-clock time. The server holds a **logical clock** whose startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time; an administrator moves it by supplying a `current_utc` instant, and the clock then reads that value for every subsequent window, deadline, expiry and delinquency computation. Moving the clock recomputes nothing on its own: no permit ages, no invoice escalates and no suspension expires merely because the clock advanced.

Time-driven state changes are instead applied by an administrator-only **nightly program run**, a batch capability that takes no input and, at the current logical clock, walks the whole program in one pass. It moves each permit whose term end is within 30 days into its renewable state, moves an unrenewed permit past its term end into grace, and expires a permit that has outrun its grace window — which in turn drives the unpermitted cascade described in §3.3. It applies the one-time late penalty to each overdue invoice and advances the dunning stage of each invoice that is still owed money, skipping any invoice frozen by a live appeal. It expires each suspension order whose closed interval has run out. It dismisses as untimely each appeal that has sat unscheduled for more than 60 whole days since it was filed. Finally it re-runs the premises roll-up so balances, counters and response posture reflect everything the pass changed, and it bumps the `version` of every entity it mutated by 1.

Because the run is idempotent with respect to money — the late penalty is applied at most once per invoice — running it twice on the same logical instant does not double-charge anyone.

### 2.7. Money, Counts and Rounding Discipline

All money in this program is held and transmitted in whole cents as integers. No field ending in `_cents` ever accepts or returns a floating-point value, and no monetary field is ever negative. Where a computation produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent — a value ending in exactly half a cent rounds away from zero. Percentage-style inputs (`non_ecv_surcharge_pct`, `reinstatement_escalation_pct`, `admin_fee_pct`, `late_penalty_pct`, `company_probation_score_threshold`, `company_suspension_score_threshold`) are decimal fractions confined to the closed range from 0 to 1 inclusive; a value outside that range is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`, as is a non-integer cents amount, a negative count, an unrecognized enum member, a malformed UUID, an empty tier array, or a timestamp that does not parse as an RFC 3339 UTC instant with a trailing `Z`.

Three rounding disciplines are pinned program-wide and must not be substituted for one another.

- **One rounding on a product.** Where a base amount is multiplied by several factors — the false alarm fee is the canonical case — the factors are multiplied together into a single product and that product is rounded once. Rounding each factor's contribution separately is wrong and will produce different money.
- **Round each line, then sum.** Where an invoice carries more than one line, each line's extended amount is rounded to a whole cent first and the rounded line amounts are then added. Summing unrounded lines and rounding the total is wrong. Two lines that each compute to 12345.6 cents therefore yield 12346 plus 12346, that is 24692 cents, and not 24691.
- **Scores carry declared precision.** Per-period reporting metrics are held to four decimal places, and both the per-period score and the company's rolling compliance score are held to two decimal places, all rounded half up.

Day arithmetic is likewise pinned. "Whole days" means integer days measured on UTC seconds, and a difference that does not land on a whole day boundary is truncated toward zero (a floor) unless a rule says otherwise. Delinquency, days late on a renewal, and inspection freshness are all whole-day quantities computed this way, and a negative result is clamped to 0.

Integer counters — occurrence indexes, chargeable counts, seat counts, correction counts, monitored-system counts — are exact and are always recomputed from the underlying rows rather than incremented blindly, so a reversal cannot leave a counter drifting away from the rows it counts.

### 2.8. Stale-Revision Guards on Program Writes

Four write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency against a **parent** entity's `version`. In each case the guard field is a required, non-optional input: omitting it is a missing-field failure, not a bypass. The client reads the parent, notes its `version`, and sends that value back with the write; if the parent's current `version` no longer equals the submitted value, the write is refused with HTTP 412 and the parent is left untouched.

- Opening a suspension order against a premises requires `expected_alarm_site_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `AlarmSite`. If any other writer has advanced that site's version since the client read it, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ALARM_SITE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The request would otherwise look perfectly legitimate — the ordinance permits an additional enforcement order for a premises as long as its interval does not collide with a live one — so nothing in the state machine would catch it; it is refused because an order drafted from a stale view of the premises (`chargeable_count_window`, `response_status`, existing orders) would encode the wrong basis.
- Posting a payment against a premises account requires `expected_alarm_site_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the paying `AlarmSite`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ALARM_SITE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Additional tenders against the same account are legitimate under the product — partial payments accumulate — so no state precondition can catch a stale poster; it is refused because an automatic waterfall allocation derived from a stale invoice set would credit invoices another writer has already settled, voided or written off.
- Posting an adjustment against an invoice requires `expected_penalty_invoice_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `PenaltyInvoice`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PENALTY_INVOICE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Adjustments legitimately accumulate — a reduction may follow a reduction, and a write-off may follow either — so a second adjustment sees an invoice that is still adjustable and would succeed under normal product semantics; it is refused because the amount was sized against an outstanding balance another writer has already reduced.
- Reconfiguring a registered alarm system requires `expected_alarm_system_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `AlarmSystem`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ALARM_SYSTEM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A second reconfiguration is legitimate on its face — the row is still permitted and every configurable field remains editable — so no state precondition can catch it; it is refused because the submitted field set would silently overwrite a change another writer already applied, for example re-enabling enhanced call verification that was just switched off.

Three further operations carry a version guard that is a plain state precondition rather than a concurrency rule, but the mechanics and the status code are identical. A permit renewal requires `expected_alarm_permit_version` to equal the permit's current `version`, failing with `ERR_STALE_ALARM_PERMIT_VERSION`. Filing an appeal requires `expected_false_alarm_incident_version` to equal the contested incident's current `version`, failing with `ERR_STALE_FALSE_ALARM_INCIDENT_VERSION`. Scheduling an appeal onto a docket requires `expected_hearing_docket_version` to equal that docket's current `version`, failing with `ERR_STALE_HEARING_DOCKET_VERSION`. All three carry HTTP 412.

No other operation takes a version field. An operation not named above must not require one, and must not reject a request for omitting one.

### 2.9. The Governing Fee Schedule

Every number the program charges, every threshold it enforces and every deadline it counts comes from a `FeeSchedule` row rather than from code. A schedule is created in `DRAFT`, may be activated to `ACTIVE`, and is pushed to `SUPERSEDED` when a newer schedule is activated in its place; the supersession stamps `superseded_at` on the outgoing row, which is null while a schedule is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`. At most one schedule holds the `ACTIVE` status program-wide at any logical instant, and `schedule_code` is unique across every schedule row regardless of status. Pricing never consults a `DRAFT` schedule.

A schedule is `ACTIVE` **at the logical clock** only when its `status` is `ACTIVE` *and* its `effective_from` is at or before the clock. An activated row whose `effective_from` still lies ahead of the clock therefore holds the status without yet governing anything, and an operation that gates on a schedule being active at the clock behaves as though none were active at all. Two kinds of read deliberately ignore both the clock and `effective_from` and pick the `ACTIVE` row by status alone: the supersession performed by a fresh activation, which is what keeps the status unique; and the program's non-monetary constant lookups — the monitoring-company score thresholds of §3.10, and the recognition of a supplied `exempt_cause_code` when the premises has no pinned schedule to read it from.

A premises is **pinned** to a schedule the first time any of its systems is permitted: at that moment `governing_fee_schedule_id` on the `AlarmSite` is set to the currently `ACTIVE` schedule and becomes immutable, and it stays null until then. Every subsequent assessment for that premises — occurrence counting, fee tiers, occupancy multipliers, caps, thresholds, appeal windows, permit terms, invoice terms, delinquency rules — reads the pinned schedule and not whatever schedule happens to be `ACTIVE` later. A superseded schedule therefore keeps governing the premises already pinned to it, which is exactly the point: activating a new ordinance does not retroactively reprice history.

Where an operation is about to assess money and no schedule is `ACTIVE` at the logical clock, the operation is refused with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_FEE_SCHEDULE`. That clock test guards the two operations that price from whatever ordinance is current: approving a registration and renewing a permit. Ruling an incident chargeable is guarded by the pinning test instead, because it prices from the premises' own pinned schedule — an unpinned premises cannot be priced and yields the same code, while a premises already pinned reads that schedule and does not re-test the clock. Other operations that need the pinned constants, a collections referral among them, likewise answer the same code at a premises that was never pinned.

### 2.10. Order of Rejection

A single request can violate several rules at once, and callers depend on a stable answer. The service therefore evaluates its checks in one fixed sequence and returns the **first** failure it finds; later checks are never reached. The sequence below is that order, from first evaluated to last. Numbered positions are relative priorities, not error codes.

**Positions 1 to 3 — admission.** First, both `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` must be present and non-empty, before any body parsing, path resolution or role lookup, failing with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`. Second, `X-User-Role` must name one of the eight roles with an exact case-sensitive match, failing with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`; an unrecognized role string is rejected here, before per-endpoint permission is consulted. Third, the recognized role must appear in the authorized-role list of the endpoint being called, failing with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. All three apply to every endpoint.

**Positions 4 and 5 — payload shape.** On every write operation, each required input field must be present and non-null; the first missing field in the operation's declared input order is reported with `ERR_MISSING_REQUIRED_FIELD`. Then each supplied field must be well formed: enum fields must hold a declared member, cents and count fields must be non-negative integers with no floats accepted anywhere, timestamps must parse as RFC 3339 UTC with a trailing `Z`, percentage fields must lie between 0 and 1 inclusive, identifiers must be UUID strings, and tier arrays must be non-empty. The first offending field in declared input order is reported with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`.

**Positions 6 and 7 — existence.** Every identifier embedded in the request path must resolve, checked strictly left to right so the leftmost unknown segment wins, and then every foreign reference supplied in the body must resolve, checked in declared input order. Both emit the entity-specific 404 codes catalogued in §2.5.

**Positions 8 to 10 — identity.** An entity-backed caller must be acting inside its own aggregate, failing with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; opaque roles skip this position. Then the segregation-of-duties pairings of §2.11 are enforced with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Then docket ownership: only the officer named on a docket may hold that session or decide an appeal heard on it, failing with `ERR_NOT_DOCKET_OFFICER`.

**Positions 11 to 16 — revision guards.** The concurrency guards come next, in this order: `expected_alarm_site_version` on suspension-order creation and on payment posting (`ERR_STALE_ALARM_SITE_VERSION`); `expected_penalty_invoice_version` on an invoice adjustment (`ERR_STALE_PENALTY_INVOICE_VERSION`); `expected_alarm_system_version` on system reconfiguration (`ERR_STALE_ALARM_SYSTEM_VERSION`); `expected_alarm_permit_version` on permit renewal (`ERR_STALE_ALARM_PERMIT_VERSION`); `expected_false_alarm_incident_version` on appeal filing (`ERR_STALE_FALSE_ALARM_INCIDENT_VERSION`); and `expected_hearing_docket_version` on appeal scheduling (`ERR_STALE_HEARING_DOCKET_VERSION`). Every one of these is checked before any state precondition, so a caller working from a stale read learns that fact rather than receiving a state complaint.

**Position 17 — declared transition.** The target resource must sit in a state from which the requested transition is declared. This covers every state machine in the program — alarm systems, permits, dispatch requests, incidents, hearing dockets, appeals, suspension orders, both invoice dimensions, monitoring companies and compliance reports — and any attempt to act on a resource already in a terminal state fails here with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

**Positions 18 to 21 — occupancy and uniqueness of live work.** A new dispatch request is refused when the named alarm system already has a dispatch request in `SUBMITTED`, `VALIDATED` or `DISPATCHED`, with `ERR_OPEN_DISPATCH_REQUEST_EXISTS`. A new incident is refused when its dispatch request already has an incident that is not `VOIDED` — one live incident per dispatch request — with `ERR_INCIDENT_ALREADY_REPORTED`. Scheduling an appeal requires the target docket to be `OPEN`, failing with `ERR_DOCKET_NOT_OPEN`. And an invoice whose dunning stage has reached `WRITTEN_OFF` can no longer be adjusted, paid or referred, so invoice adjustment, payment posting and collections referral all fail with `ERR_INVOICE_WRITTEN_OFF`.

**Positions 22 to 24 — referential coherence.** The monitoring company named as reporter must be the monitoring company of the referenced alarm system, and the referenced system must belong to the referenced premises; `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_SYSTEM_MONITOR` is the code this position carries, on dispatch-request submission, incident creation and compliance-report filing alike. In practice the position is shadowed on all three. Each of those operations is open to the `AlarmCompanyOperator` role, each reaches its company through exactly one reference, and neither the premises nor the monitor is ever supplied a second time for the two to disagree — so the identity check at position 8 has already refused an operator whose own company is not the one on the far end of that reference, with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The coherence code accordingly keeps its place in the order and its 409 in the catalogue while the incoherence it names is reported one position earlier. A supplied `exempt_cause_code` must be a member of the governing schedule's `exempt_cause_codes` list, on both incident determination and appeal decision, failing with `ERR_EXEMPT_CAUSE_NOT_RECOGNIZED`. Determination coherence then requires that an exempt ruling supplies a recognized exempt cause and a chargeable ruling supplies none, failing with `ERR_EXEMPT_CAUSE_MUST_BE_EXEMPT`.

**Positions 25 to 29 — public-safety gates.** The reporting monitoring company must be `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`; a `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED` company may neither lodge dispatch requests nor open incident reports, and both operations fail with `ERR_COMPANY_DISPATCH_PRIVILEGE_SUSPENDED`. Next, the premises behind the named system must not sit at `RESPONSE_SUSPENDED` posture when a dispatch request is lodged, failing with `ERR_POLICE_RESPONSE_SUSPENDED`. Next, the premises' live contact roster must satisfy the distinct-contact minimum required for that system's posture — a premises that cannot be called back may not have a dispatch requested for it — failing with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ECV_CONTACTS`. Then, at validation time, the request's frozen verification sufficiency must be true, failing with `ERR_ECV_REQUIREMENT_NOT_MET`; and the verified-response proof must match the system's requirement — recorded audio/video asserted when the system demands `AUDIO_VIDEO_REQUIRED`, a guard on scene asserted when it demands `GUARD_REQUIRED` — failing with `ERR_VERIFIED_RESPONSE_NOT_CONFIRMED`.

**Positions 30 to 35 — temporal windows.** An appeal must be filed no later than the incident's determination instant plus the schedule's `appeal_filing_days` (20 days by default, boundary inclusive), failing with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A new hearing docket must have `session_start` strictly after the current logical clock and `session_end` strictly after `session_start`, failing with `ERR_HEARING_SESSION_IN_PAST`. A docket may only be held once the logical clock has reached `session_start`, inclusive, failing with `ERR_HEARING_SESSION_NOT_STARTED`. A reinstatement request's `technician_inspection_date` must be no more than 30 whole days old relative to the logical clock, boundary inclusive, failing with `ERR_INSPECTION_EVIDENCE_STALE`. A new suspension order's `effective_from` must be at or after the logical clock and at or after the triggering incident's occurrence instant, with `effective_through` strictly after `effective_from`, failing with `ERR_SUSPENSION_EFFECTIVE_DATE_INVALID`. And a payment may be voided only within 2 whole days of its tender instant, boundary inclusive, and only while none of its allocations targets an invoice that has since become `WRITTEN_OFF`, failing with `ERR_PAYMENT_VOID_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

**Positions 36 to 53 — program constraints.** In order: the contested incident must have no appeal in `FILED`, `SCHEDULED` or `HEARD`, failing with `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_PENDING`. A rehearing — an appeal that supplies `prior_appeal_id` — is accepted only when the prior appeal reached `DENIED` (not `WITHDRAWN`, not `GRANTED`) and no rehearing has already been filed against it, failing with `ERR_REHEARING_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Scheduling must not push a docket's scheduled appeal count above its `capacity`, the count being evaluated including the appeal being added, failing with `ERR_DOCKET_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A new docket must not overlap a live docket of the same hearing officer under the buffered geometry of §3.6, failing with `ERR_DOCKET_SESSION_OVERLAP`. A new suspension order's interval must not overlap a `PROPOSED`, `EFFECTIVE` or `REINSTATEMENT_PENDING` order for the same premises under the closed-interval geometry of §3.7, failing with `ERR_SUSPENSION_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. A premises' normalized `address_key` must be unique across all non-archived premises, failing with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ADDRESS_KEY`. An alarm system may hold at most one permit in `ACTIVE`, `RENEWAL_DUE` or `GRACE`, so a second issuance fails with `ERR_ACTIVE_PERMIT_EXISTS`. A fee schedule must be available to govern a fee about to be assessed — one `ACTIVE` at the logical clock on registration approval and permit renewal, one pinned on the premises on incident determination — and this position is where either test fails, with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_FEE_SCHEDULE`. A contact's `contact_priority` must be unique among the `ACTIVE` contacts of the same premises, failing with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CONTACT_PRIORITY`. Removing a contact must not drop the premises' live callable roster below 2 while the premises still holds an alarm system in a live registration state — `SUBMITTED` or `PERMITTED` — failing with `ERR_CONTACT_ROSTER_MINIMUM`. A compliance report's closed reporting interval must not overlap an existing report of the same monitoring company in `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `VARIANCE_FLAGGED` or `CORRECTED`, failing with `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. A correction must change at least one reported metric relative to the stored values, failing with `ERR_NO_REPORTABLE_VARIANCE`. A report may be corrected at most twice, with `correction_count` evaluated before the increment, failing with `ERR_CORRECTION_LIMIT_REACHED`. A suspension order's basis must still hold — the premises' chargeable count under its governing window must be at or above `suspension_threshold_count`, which defaults to 6 — both when the order is created and when it is served, failing with `ERR_SUSPENSION_BASIS_NO_LONGER_MET`. A reinstatement request must carry `ecv_attestation` set to true together with a non-empty `corrective_action_summary`, failing with `ERR_ECV_ATTESTATION_REQUIRED`. A company reinstatement action must be earned: two consecutive accepted reporting periods scored at or above the good-standing threshold of 0.70, with those periods ending after the company's `probation_started_at` anchor, failing with `ERR_COMPANY_REINSTATEMENT_NOT_EARNED`. An invoice whose linked incident is `APPEALED` may be neither referred to collections nor escalated in dunning while the appeal is live, failing with `ERR_INVOICE_UNDER_APPEAL`. And a collections referral requires all of its criteria together: dunning stage `FINAL_NOTICE`, days past due at or above `delinquency_days` (90 by default), and outstanding money at or above `collections_referral_threshold_cents` (25000 cents by default), failing with `ERR_REFERRAL_CRITERIA_NOT_MET`.

**Positions 54 to 58 — fiscal gates.** New permit issuance is refused while the premises' delinquent balance is at or above `delinquent_balance_block_cents` (50000 cents by default, boundary inclusive), with `ERR_SITE_BALANCE_DELINQUENT` at HTTP 402. Permit renewal is refused on the same balance condition, with `ERR_PERMIT_RENEWAL_BLOCKED` at HTTP 409. Lifting a suspension requires the premises' outstanding penalty balance, net of any credit held on the account, to be exactly zero, with `ERR_OUTSTANDING_BALANCE_BLOCKS_REINSTATEMENT` at HTTP 402. A waive or reduce adjustment must not exceed the invoice's current `outstanding_cents`, and a write-off is taken at exactly `outstanding_cents`, failing with `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`. And a posted payment must conserve money: the allocation lines must account for exactly the tendered amount net of the residual parked as premises credit, failing with `ERR_ALLOCATION_MISMATCH`.

**Position 59 — paging.** Last of all, on list reads, `page` and `per_page` are range-checked and any sort key is checked against the listed entity's declared fields, failing with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.

Three collisions in this ordering are worth stating outright, because each is a request that genuinely fails two rules in different families and implementers routinely pick the wrong winner.

- **Collision A.** A dispatch request naming a system that already has an open dispatch request, at a premises whose posture is `RESPONSE_SUSPENDED`, fails both the open-request check at position 18 and the suspended-response check at position 26. The open-request check wins, and the caller receives `ERR_OPEN_DISPATCH_REQUEST_EXISTS`.
- **Collision B.** A permit renewal attempted when no fee schedule is `ACTIVE`, for a premises whose delinquent balance is at or above the blocking threshold, fails both the active-schedule check at position 43 and the renewal-block check at position 55. The active-schedule check wins, and the caller receives `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_FEE_SCHEDULE`.
- **Collision C.** An appeal filed on the twenty-first day after determination, against an incident that also already has a live appeal, fails both the filing-window check at position 30 and the pending-appeal check at position 36. The filing-window check wins, and the caller receives `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties

The unit's controls rest on the principle that nobody reviews their own work. Three pairings are enforced, all of them reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` at HTTP 403.

Deciding an appeal is barred to the operator whose identifier is recorded as the incident's `determined_by_user_id`, and equally barred to the operator recorded as the appeal's `filed_by_user_id` — the officer who ruled cannot review the ruling, and the filer cannot decide their own petition. Determining an incident is barred to the operator recorded as `validated_by_user_id` on the dispatch request the incident came from, so the supervisor who released the patrol does not also judge whether the alarm was false. Adjusting an invoice is barred to any operator whose identifier appears as `recorded_by_user_id` on a payment already posted against that invoice, which is why money movement (posting payments, referring to collections) sits with the `BillingClerk` while money reduction (waive, reduce, write off) sits exclusively with the `ProgramAdministrator`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Writing the Ordinance: Fee Schedules

Before the unit can charge anyone, an administrator must publish a fee schedule. A schedule is a single row that carries the entire numeric content of the ordinance, and creating one requires all of it: the `schedule_code` that names it, the `effective_from` instant it governs from, and then every program constant. Its `counting_window_mode` selects which of the two false-alarm counting variants is authoritative — `ROLLING_365` or `PERMIT_YEAR`. Its `free_alarm_allowance` (2 by default) is the number of leading chargeable occurrences per window whose tier base is zero; those occurrences still count toward every threshold, they simply cost nothing. Its `fee_tiers` array holds the marginal ladder as ordered entries of `from_index`, `to_index` (null meaning open-ended) and `base_fee_cents`; the default ladder charges nothing for occurrences 1 and 2, 10000 cents for occurrence 3, 25000 for occurrence 4, 40000 for occurrence 5, and 75000 for occurrence 6 and everything above it. Its `occupancy_multipliers` object maps each premises occupancy class to a multiplier, defaulting to 1.00 for `RESIDENTIAL`, 1.50 for `COMMERCIAL`, 1.25 for `SCHOOL`, 2.00 for `FINANCIAL` and 1.00 for `GOVERNMENT`.

Surcharge and escalation constants come next. `non_ecv_surcharge_pct` (0.20 by default) is the surcharge factor applied when the originating dispatch request declared no verification or only a single call. `unpermitted_multiplier` (2.00 by default) is applied when the system was not permitted at the moment the signal fired. `suspension_period_aggravation_cents` (50000 by default) is a flat amount added after the per-incident cap when the incident happened inside a live suspension interval, and `per_incident_fee_cap_cents` (100000 by default) is the cap itself. `probation_threshold_count` (4) and `suspension_threshold_count` (6) are the window counts at which a premises loses good standing and at which an enforcement order is auto-proposed. `appeal_filing_days` (20) bounds how long a permit holder has to contest a determination.

Permit and money constants finish the row. `permit_term_days` (365) is the length of a permit term and `renewal_grace_days` (30) the curable window after it; `new_installation_grace_days` (30) is the honeymoon after a premises' first permit issuance during which incidents are exempt. `permit_issue_fee_cents` (5000) is billed at first issuance and `permit_renewal_fee_cents` (3000) at each renewal, with `late_renewal_tiers` holding ordered entries of `max_days_late` (null meaning open-ended) and `fee_cents` — 1500 cents up to 15 days late, 3000 up to 30, and 6000 beyond that by default. `unpermitted_operation_penalty_cents` (20000) is billed when a system falls out of permit. `reinstatement_base_fee_cents` (60000) and `reinstatement_escalation_pct` (0.50) price the return of police response. `admin_fee_pct` (0.05), `admin_fee_min_cents` (500) and `admin_fee_max_cents` (5000) govern the administrative charge on every invoice. `invoice_due_days` (30) sets the payment term, `late_penalty_pct` (0.10) the one-time overdue charge, `delinquency_days` (90) the point at which referral becomes possible, and `collections_referral_threshold_cents` (25000) the minimum balance worth referring. `delinquent_balance_block_cents` (50000) is the balance at or above which a premises can no longer obtain or renew a permit. `exempt_cause_codes` lists the causes that force an exempt determination — by default `SEVERE_WEATHER`, `UTILITY_POWER_FAILURE`, `TELEPHONE_LINE_FAILURE`, `AUTHORIZED_TEST_WITH_NOTICE`, `CRIMINAL_OFFENSE_CONFIRMED` and `NEW_INSTALLATION_GRACE`. Finally `company_probation_score_threshold` (0.70) and `company_suspension_score_threshold` (0.50) are the reporting-score levels below which a monitoring company loses standing; both comparisons are strict, so a score exactly equal to a threshold is *not* below it.

A freshly created schedule lands in `DRAFT` and nothing consults it. Activating it moves it to `ACTIVE`, pushes any previously `ACTIVE` schedule to `SUPERSEDED` and stamps that outgoing row's `superseded_at` with the logical clock. Because premises pin their governing schedule at first issuance (§2.9), an activation changes pricing only for premises that have not yet been pinned.

### 3.2. Premises, Responsible Parties and the Callable Roster

A premises record is created with its normalized `address_key`, its `street_address`, `city`, `zip_code`, its `occupancy_class` — one of `RESIDENTIAL`, `COMMERCIAL`, `SCHOOL`, `FINANCIAL` or `GOVERNMENT` — and its `police_beat`. The `address_key` is the counting key for the whole program: false alarms are counted by address, not by system, so two panels in the same building share one ladder. Normalization upper-cases and collapses whitespace across street and unit, and the resulting key must be unique among premises whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; a premises `status` is either `ACTIVE` or `ARCHIVED`. A newly created premises starts with `response_status` at `RESPONSE_ACTIVE`, every counter at zero and `governing_fee_schedule_id` null.

The premises row carries the program's derived picture of the address. `chargeable_count_window` is the authoritative window count under the governing counting variant, while `chargeable_count_rolling_365` and `chargeable_count_permit_year` are both maintained at all times so the two variants can be compared side by side. `lifetime_assessed_cents` totals what has ever been assessed against the address across invoices that are not voided; `outstanding_balance_cents` totals what is still owed on invoices that are unpaid or partially paid and not written off; `credit_balance_cents` holds unallocated payment remainder and is never negative. `unpermitted_system_count` counts the address's systems that have fallen out of permit, `lifted_suspension_count` counts its suspensions that were properly lifted, `ecv_callable_contact_count` counts its live callable contacts, and `renewal_block_active` is true exactly when `outstanding_balance_cents` is at or above the schedule's `delinquent_balance_block_cents`. A read-only summary capability returns all of this recomputed at the current logical clock — both variant counts, the authoritative count, the posture, the outstanding and credit balances and the renewal block flag — without persisting a version bump.

Responsible parties are held as a roster. Adding one supplies the premises reference `alarm_site_id`, the party's `full_name`, a `phone_number` in E.164 form, their `relationship` to the premises (owner, manager, keyholder and the like), a `contact_priority` between 1 and 5 that fixes call order, and `is_ecv_callable`, which defaults to true and decides whether the contact counts toward enhanced call verification. Two `ACTIVE` contacts of the same premises may not hold the same `contact_priority`. A contact's `status` is `ACTIVE` or `REMOVED`; removal is soft, setting `status` to `REMOVED` and stamping `removed_at` (null while active) rather than deleting the row. Both adding and removing a contact recompute `ecv_callable_contact_count` on the premises and bump that premises' `version` by 1 — which is why a client holding an `expected_alarm_site_version` from before a roster edit will be turned away on its next guarded write.

The roster minimum is a safety rule, not an accounting one: an address that cannot be called back should not be sending patrols anywhere. A removal that would leave fewer than 2 live callable contacts is refused with `ERR_CONTACT_ROSTER_MINIMUM` while the premises still holds a system in `SUBMITTED` or `PERMITTED`, and the same minimum of 2 distinct callable contacts is what enhanced call verification is measured against in §3.4.

### 3.3. Registration, Permit Issuance and the Term Cycle

A registration is lodged against a premises naming `alarm_site_id`, the `monitoring_company_id` of the company that will monitor the panel and may request dispatch for it, the `system_type` (`BURGLARY`, `HOLDUP_PANIC` or `COMBINATION`), the `zone_count` of protected zones, which must be at least 1, whether `ecv_enabled` is configured on the panel, the `verified_response_mode` demanded of it (`NONE` by default, or `AUDIO_VIDEO_REQUIRED` or `GUARD_REQUIRED`), and the `installed_at` instant reported on the paperwork. The row is created in `SUBMITTED`, and the new-installation grace anchor is taken from `installed_at`. A registered system may later be reconfigured — its monitoring company, its `ecv_enabled` flag, its `verified_response_mode` or its `zone_count` — by a write that carries `expected_alarm_system_version` and, on success, bumps that system's `version` by 1 and refreshes the enhanced-call-verification requirement derived from it.

Approval by a `PermitClerk` (or an administrator) drives `SUBMITTED → PERMITTED` and is the most consequential single operation in the program. Six preconditions guard it, and all must hold: the premises is `ACTIVE`; the premises has at least 2 live callable contacts, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ECV_CONTACTS`; the premises' outstanding balance is strictly below the schedule's blocking threshold, else `ERR_SITE_BALANCE_DELINQUENT`; the premises has no suspension order in `EFFECTIVE` or `REINSTATEMENT_PENDING`, else `ERR_POLICE_RESPONSE_SUSPENDED`; this system holds no permit in `ACTIVE`, `RENEWAL_DUE`, `RENEWAL_BLOCKED` or `GRACE`, else `ERR_ACTIVE_PERMIT_EXISTS`; and exactly one fee schedule is `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_FEE_SCHEDULE`.

Approval then fires a chain of writes. A permit is created — `PENDING_ISSUANCE` is only the pre-persist state, so the row lands directly in `ACTIVE` — carrying `alarm_system_id`, the denormalized `alarm_site_id` copied from the system, and a `permit_number` assigned as the next zero-padded sequential value in issuance order, formatted `AP-000001`. Its `term_start_at` is the logical clock, `term_end_at` is `term_start_at` plus `permit_term_days` whole days, and `grace_through_at` is `term_end_at` plus `renewal_grace_days` whole days — the last instant at which a grace renewal is still accepted. `renewal_count` starts at 0, `last_renewed_at` is null before the first renewal, `revocation_reason` is null, and `issued_by_user_id` records the acting clerk's `X-User-ID`. On the premises' **first** issuance only, two further things happen: `governing_fee_schedule_id` is pinned to the `ACTIVE` schedule, and `new_installation_grace_through` is set to `term_start_at` plus `new_installation_grace_days`; on every renewed term that field stays null. The system's `permitted_at` is stamped with the logical clock and its `unpermitted_since` is cleared. An invoice of kind `PERMIT_ISSUE_FEE` is assembled and issued. The premises' balances, lifetime assessment and renewal block flag are recomputed, the premises' `version` is bumped by 1, and the monitoring company's `monitored_system_count` is incremented.

Rejection is the sibling branch out of `SUBMITTED`. A clerk supplies `rejection_reason`, the row moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` — a terminal state — the reason is stored on `rejection_reason`, and the monitoring company's `monitored_system_count` is decremented. The system's `version` is bumped by 1. No permit is created, no money is assessed and no premises counter changes.

The term cycle then repeats once per year and is driven entirely by the nightly run on one side and the permit holder on the other. When the logical clock reaches 30 days before `term_end_at`, the run moves `ACTIVE → RENEWAL_DUE`; no money moves, the permit simply becomes renewable. Past `term_end_at` the run moves `RENEWAL_DUE → GRACE`, and past `grace_through_at` it moves `GRACE → EXPIRED`. A fiscal side loop sits alongside: when the premises' `renewal_block_active` becomes true the run moves `RENEWAL_DUE → RENEWAL_BLOCKED`, and renewal attempts are then refused with `ERR_PERMIT_RENEWAL_BLOCKED` until the balance clears; when the balance falls back strictly below the blocking threshold — either on the next nightly run or as part of the recompute cascade of a posted payment — the permit returns `RENEWAL_BLOCKED → RENEWAL_DUE` and the block is cleared. A blocked permit that also outruns its term end moves `RENEWAL_BLOCKED → GRACE`, carrying the fiscal block into grace with it, where renewal is still refused, in that case with `ERR_SITE_BALANCE_DELINQUENT`.

Renewal is available from both `RENEWAL_DUE` and `GRACE`, to an administrator, a clerk or the permit holder, and both branches carry the same requirements and the same effects. The request supplies `expected_alarm_permit_version`, which must equal the permit's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_ALARM_PERMIT_VERSION`. The premises must have at least 2 live callable contacts, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ECV_CONTACTS`, and its `renewal_block_active` must be false, else `ERR_PERMIT_RENEWAL_BLOCKED` — the code §2.10 assigns to this refusal at position 55 — with the single exception that a renewal attempted while the permit sits in `GRACE` reports the very same condition as `ERR_SITE_BALANCE_DELINQUENT`, as the fiscal side loop below describes. Renewing out of `GRACE` additionally requires the logical clock to be at or before `grace_through_at` — past that instant the nightly run has already expired the permit, and the attempt fails on the declared-transition check. On success the permit returns to `ACTIVE` with `term_start_at` set to the logical clock, `term_end_at` recomputed as that instant plus `permit_term_days`, `grace_through_at` recomputed from the new term end, `renewal_count` increased by 1, `last_renewed_at` stamped, and `new_installation_grace_through` left null. A `PERMIT_RENEWAL_FEE` invoice is issued, the premises' `chargeable_count_permit_year` is reset to 0 so the permit-year variant restarts at the new term, and the permit's `version` is bumped by 1. If the system had fallen to `UNPERMITTED`, the renewal returns it `UNPERMITTED → PERMITTED`, stamping `permitted_at`, clearing `unpermitted_since`, decrementing the premises' `unpermitted_system_count` and recomputing its posture; the premises' `version` is bumped by 1.

**Pricing a renewal.** The days late on a renewal are the whole days between the permit's `term_end_at` and the logical clock, computed by dividing the elapsed UTC seconds by 86400 and discarding the remainder, with any negative result clamped to 0. The late tier is the first entry of `late_renewal_tiers`, in declared order, whose `max_days_late` is null or is at least the days late. The late fee is zero when days late is zero, and otherwise the `fee_cents` of the selected tier. A renewal is therefore priced as one `PERMIT_RENEWAL` line at `permit_renewal_fee_cents`, plus — only when the late fee is above zero — one `LATE_RENEWAL` line at that fee. Note the interaction with the grace geometry: renewal is accepted while the clock is at or before `grace_through_at`, which with the default 30-day grace means days late of at most 30, and days late of exactly 30 is still renewable and selects the 3000-cent tier. The open-ended 6000-cent tier is only reachable when the grace window is configured longer than the tier bounds.

**The unpermitted cascade.** Expiry is where the program's cascading side effects are most visible, and every hop must land. Hop one: the nightly run moves `GRACE → EXPIRED` on the permit. Hop two: because the permit expired, the system moves `PERMITTED → UNPERMITTED` with `unpermitted_since` stamped at the logical clock, the premises' `unpermitted_system_count` is incremented, and an `UNPERMITTED_OPERATION_PENALTY` invoice is issued against the premises and that permit at `unpermitted_operation_penalty_cents`. Hop three: because that penalty pushed the premises' `outstanding_balance_cents` to or above `delinquent_balance_block_cents`, `renewal_block_active` flips to true and **every other permit at the same premises** sitting in `RENEWAL_DUE` moves to `RENEWAL_BLOCKED`. Revoking a permit for cause produces the identical downstream chain: an administrator supplies `revocation_reason`, the permit moves from `ACTIVE`, `RENEWAL_DUE`, `RENEWAL_BLOCKED` or `GRACE` to `REVOKED` (terminal), the reason is stored, and the system falls to `UNPERMITTED` with the same penalty and the same fiscal knock-on.

Retirement closes the lifecycle. Decommissioning is available to an administrator, a clerk or the permit holder from either `PERMITTED` or `UNPERMITTED`, and is refused with `ERR_OPEN_DISPATCH_REQUEST_EXISTS` while the system still has a dispatch request in `SUBMITTED`, `VALIDATED` or `DISPATCHED`. On success `decommissioned_at` is stamped and `status` becomes `DECOMMISSIONED`, a terminal state. From `PERMITTED`, any permit of that system in `ACTIVE`, `RENEWAL_DUE`, `RENEWAL_BLOCKED` or `GRACE` is driven to `REVOKED` with a revocation reason of system decommissioning, and the monitoring company's `monitored_system_count` is decremented. From `UNPERMITTED`, the premises' `unpermitted_system_count` and the company's `monitored_system_count` are both decremented. Either way the premises' counters are recomputed and its `version` is bumped by 1.

### 3.4. The Dispatch Pipeline and Enhanced Call Verification

Every police response begins with a monitoring company lodging a dispatch request for one of its own systems. The submission names the `alarm_system_id` that produced the signal, the `alarm_type` (`BURGLARY`, `HOLDUP`, `PANIC` or `DURESS`), the `verification_method` the central station used (`NONE`, `SINGLE_CALL`, `TWO_CALL_ECV`, `AUDIO_VIDEO` or `GUARD_RESPONSE`), the `call_attempts` log, the assertions `audio_video_evidence` and `guard_on_scene` (both defaulting to false), the `occurred_at` instant the signal fired, and an optional company-supplied `idempotency_key` which, when present, must be unique per monitoring company. Each entry in `call_attempts` records a `site_contact_id`, the `attempted_at` instant, and an `outcome` of `NO_ANSWER`, `VOICEMAIL`, `ANSWERED_UNVERIFIED` or `ANSWERED_CANCELLED`. On acceptance the row denormalizes `alarm_site_id` and `monitoring_company_id` from the system and is created in `SUBMITTED`.

**Verification sufficiency is computed once, at submission, and frozen on the row.** The eligible attempts are those whose `site_contact_id` resolves to a contact that is `ACTIVE`, is `is_ecv_callable`, and belongs to the same premises as the system, and whose `outcome` is one of the four declared outcomes; attempts naming a removed contact, a contact of a different premises, or a non-callable contact are dropped before counting. `distinct_ecv_contact_count` is then the number of distinct contact identifiers surviving that filter, defaulting to 0 for an empty log. From that count, `ecv_sufficient` is decided as follows: it is true whenever the `alarm_type` is `DURESS`, because a duress signal bypasses call verification entirely; otherwise it is true when the `verification_method` is `TWO_CALL_ECV` and `distinct_ecv_contact_count` is at least 2; otherwise it is true when the method is `AUDIO_VIDEO` and `audio_video_evidence` is true; otherwise it is true when the method is `GUARD_RESPONSE` and `guard_on_scene` is true; and in every remaining case it is false. Two consequences follow directly and are both intended: a log of two calls placed to the *same* contact yields a distinct count of 1 and is therefore insufficient for a two-call request even though two calls really were made, while a log of exactly two calls to *different* contacts yields 2, meets the inclusive threshold and is sufficient. The same submission also freezes `system_permitted_at_occurrence`, which is true when the system's status was `PERMITTED` at that moment. Both frozen values are what the fee engine later reads; it never re-reads the live system.

Submission itself is gated. It is refused with `ERR_OPEN_DISPATCH_REQUEST_EXISTS` when the system already has a request in `SUBMITTED`, `VALIDATED` or `DISPATCHED`; with the coherence refusal of §2.10 position 22 when the acting company is not the system's monitoring company — which, since the submitter is always an `AlarmCompanyOperator` and the identity check at position 8 comes first, is what the caller actually sees reported as `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; with `ERR_COMPANY_DISPATCH_PRIVILEGE_SUSPENDED` when the company is `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED`; with `ERR_POLICE_RESPONSE_SUSPENDED` when the premises posture is `RESPONSE_SUSPENDED`; and with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ECV_CONTACTS` when the premises' live callable roster is below the distinct-contact minimum its posture requires. The relative order of these gates, and which one wins when several apply, is fixed by §2.10 — in particular an open request outranks a suspended premises.

From `SUBMITTED` the request forks. A `PoliceDispatchSupervisor` may validate it, moving `SUBMITTED → VALIDATED` and stamping `validated_at` with the logical clock and `validated_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`. Validation requires the frozen `ecv_sufficient` to be true, else `ERR_ECV_REQUIREMENT_NOT_MET`; the premises posture not to be `RESPONSE_SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_POLICE_RESPONSE_SUSPENDED`; the monitoring company to be `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`, else `ERR_COMPANY_DISPATCH_PRIVILEGE_SUSPENDED`; and the verified-response proof to match what the system demands — `audio_video_evidence` true when the system's `verified_response_mode` is `AUDIO_VIDEO_REQUIRED`, `guard_on_scene` true when it is `GUARD_REQUIRED` — else `ERR_VERIFIED_RESPONSE_NOT_CONFIRMED`. The sibling branch is refusal: the supervisor supplies a coded `refusal_reason` of `ECV_DEFICIENT`, `RESPONSE_SUSPENDED`, `VERIFIED_RESPONSE_MISSING` or `DUPLICATE_SIGNAL`, the row moves `SUBMITTED → REFUSED` (terminal), the reason is stored, and no incident may ever reference that request. `refusal_reason` is null on any request that is not refused.

From `VALIDATED` the request forks again. Recording the dispatch moves `VALIDATED → DISPATCHED` and stamps `dispatched_at` with the logical clock; it requires that the premises posture is still not `RESPONSE_SUSPENDED` at the moment of dispatch, else `ERR_POLICE_RESPONSE_SUSPENDED`, because a suspension served between validation and dispatch must stop the patrol. Cancellation moves `VALIDATED → CANCELLED` (terminal) with an optional `cancellation_reason`; a company operator cancelling must be the request's own monitoring company, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, and a cancelled request yields no chargeable incident and is excluded from the company's system-side dispatch count for reporting purposes.

From `DISPATCHED` there are two outcomes. Closing the request as a genuine alarm, with optional `closing_notes`, moves `DISPATCHED → CLOSED_VALID` (terminal) and records a verified alarm — no fee, no counting; it is refused with `ERR_INCIDENT_ALREADY_REPORTED` if an incident already references the request. Alternatively an incident is opened against it, which leaves the request in `DISPATCHED` — a deliberate self-loop, since the request is the evidence and does not itself change state — while requiring that no incident already references it and that the submitted `expected_dispatch_request_version` matches the request's current `version`, after which that `version` is bumped by 1. Dispatch requests are listable and readable by the whole program staff, filterable by system, premises, monitoring company and status, and by occurrence window through `occurred_from` and `occurred_to`. The company's `dispatch_request_count_lifetime` counts every request it has ever lodged, in every status.

### 3.5. Determination, Occurrence Counting and the Escalating Fee

When a patrol finds nothing, an incident is opened against the dispatched request. The report supplies the `dispatch_request_id`, the `cause_code`, and an optional `narrative` recording what the officer found. Cause codes span `OPERATOR_ERROR`, `EQUIPMENT_MALFUNCTION`, `UNKNOWN`, `ANIMAL_MOVEMENT`, `OPEN_DOOR_WINDOW`, `SEVERE_WEATHER`, `UTILITY_POWER_FAILURE`, `TELEPHONE_LINE_FAILURE`, `AUTHORIZED_TEST_WITH_NOTICE`, `CRIMINAL_OFFENSE_CONFIRMED` and `NEW_INSTALLATION_GRACE`. The row denormalizes `alarm_site_id`, `alarm_system_id` and `occurred_at` from the request — the occurrence instant is the ordering key for all later numbering — and is created in `REPORTED`, not yet chargeable, with `occurrence_index` and `assessed_fee_cents` both null — not 0, since nothing has been numbered or priced yet — alongside a null `determination`, `exempt_cause_code`, `determined_at`, `determined_by_user_id`, `fee_basis` and `counted_window_mode`, and `appeal_count` at 0.

A `FalseAlarmReviewOfficer` then rules on it, supplying `determination` as either `COUNTED` or `EXEMPT`, an `exempt_cause_code` when exempting, and optional `review_notes`. The row passes through `UNDER_REVIEW` inside the same request — `REPORTED → UNDER_REVIEW` clears any prior determination fields — before the ruling lands. Every check the ruling must pass, the branch-specific ones below included, is evaluated before that hop is taken, so a refused ruling is a pure rejection: the incident keeps its status, its determination fields and its `version` precisely as they were, and no half-finished move to `UNDER_REVIEW` is left behind for the next caller to find. Coherence is required both ways: an `EXEMPT` ruling must supply a recognized exempt cause and a `COUNTED` ruling must supply none, else `ERR_EXEMPT_CAUSE_MUST_BE_EXEMPT`; and any supplied cause must be a member of the governing schedule's `exempt_cause_codes`, else `ERR_EXEMPT_CAUSE_NOT_RECOGNIZED`.

The exempt branch, `UNDER_REVIEW → EXEMPT`, is terminal and deliberately inert. Its precondition is that `exempt_cause_code` is present and listed in the governing schedule's exempt causes, or that it is `NEW_INSTALLATION_GRACE` with the incident's `occurred_at` at or before the premises permit's `new_installation_grace_through`. It sets `determination` to `EXEMPT`, stores the `exempt_cause_code`, stamps `determined_at` and `determined_by_user_id`, leaves `occurrence_index` null and `assessed_fee_cents` at 0, issues no invoice and changes no premises counter. `exempt_cause_code` is null on any incident that is not exempt.

The chargeable branch, `UNDER_REVIEW → COUNTED`, requires three things: the `cause_code` must **not** be one of the governing schedule's `exempt_cause_codes`, else `ERR_EXEMPT_CAUSE_MUST_BE_EXEMPT`; the incident's `occurred_at` must be strictly after the premises permit's `new_installation_grace_through` when that field is set, else the same code, because an incident inside the honeymoon cannot be counted; and the premises must be pinned to a governing schedule, else `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_FEE_SCHEDULE`. It then sets `determination` to `COUNTED`, stamps `determined_at` with the logical clock and `determined_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, assigns the occurrence numbering, prices the incident, and bills it.

**The chargeable set and the two counting variants.** Only incidents in `COUNTED` or `APPEALED` are chargeable. Rows in `REPORTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `EXEMPT`, `EXEMPT_BY_APPEAL` or `VOIDED` never enter the set. An appealed incident deliberately *stays* in the set and keeps its index for as long as its appeal is live — only the dunning on its invoice freezes — and it leaves the set only if the appeal is granted or the determination is remanded.

Which chargeable incidents fall inside the window depends on the governing schedule's `counting_window_mode`. Under `ROLLING_365`, the window is anchored on the incident being numbered: it includes every chargeable incident at the same premises whose occurrence instant is at or after 365 days before this incident's own occurrence instant and at or before that instant, both bounds inclusive. Under `PERMIT_YEAR`, the window is anchored on the permit term that contains the incident: the term start is taken from the premises permit whose closed interval from `term_start_at` through `grace_through_at` contains the incident's occurrence instant, taking the greatest such `term_start_at` when several permits qualify and breaking any remaining tie by earliest `created_at`; the window then includes every chargeable incident at the premises whose occurrence instant is at or after that term start and at or before this incident's occurrence instant, again both bounds inclusive. In either case the incident's `occurrence_index` is the size of the set it belongs to — the incident is already counted at that point, so it counts itself — and `counted_window_mode` records which variant produced the number.

The variants are not interchangeable, which is exactly why both are tracked. Under `ROLLING_365` the window slides with each incident, so occurrences from a previous permit term still count and a renewal has no effect on the index. Under `PERMIT_YEAR` the window restarts at every renewal, so four incidents that would produce an index of 4 — and its 25000-cent tier — under the rolling variant instead produce an index of 1, which is free under the allowance, if a renewal falls between them. Identical data, different fee. The premises' own counters use the same two window starts but evaluated at the logical clock rather than at any one incident's instant, and — unlike the numbering window — they are bounded below only: `chargeable_count_rolling_365` counts every chargeable incident at the premises whose occurrence instant is at or after 365 days before now, `chargeable_count_permit_year` counts those at or after the start of the permit term that contains now, chosen by the same greatest-term-start rule, and `chargeable_count_window` is whichever of the two the governing `counting_window_mode` selects, the rolling one whenever the premises has no pinned schedule to ask. Two consequences of there being no upper bound and no fallback start: a chargeable incident whose occurrence instant lies ahead of the clock is counted all the same, and `chargeable_count_permit_year` counts every chargeable incident at the premises whenever no permit term contains the clock.

**Pricing a counted incident.** The fee is built in four steps from the pinned schedule, the incident's index, and the frozen fields of the originating dispatch request. First, the marginal tier: the tier base is the `base_fee_cents` of the single `fee_tiers` entry whose `from_index` is at or below the index and whose `to_index` is either null or at or above the index — this is a marginal lookup, never a cumulative sum of lower tiers — and the tier base is then forced to zero whenever the index is at or below `free_alarm_allowance`. Second, the three multiplicative factors: the occupancy multiplier is the schedule's `occupancy_multipliers` entry for the premises' `occupancy_class`; the surcharge factor is 1 plus `non_ecv_surcharge_pct` when the originating request's `verification_method` was `NONE` or `SINGLE_CALL`, and 1 otherwise; the unpermitted factor is `unpermitted_multiplier` when the request's frozen `system_permitted_at_occurrence` is false, and 1 otherwise. Third — and this is the only rounding in the whole computation — the tier base is multiplied by all three factors together and that single product is rounded half up to a whole cent. Fourth, the cap and the aggravation: the cap is treated as applied only when the rounded amount is **strictly greater** than `per_incident_fee_cap_cents`, so an amount exactly equal to the cap passes through unchanged and is not flagged as capped; the amount is then clipped to the cap; and finally, if a suspension order for the premises in `EFFECTIVE` or `REINSTATEMENT_PENDING` covers the incident's occurrence instant within its closed interval from `effective_from` through `effective_through`, the flat `suspension_period_aggravation_cents` is added **after** the clip. Because aggravation is added outside the cap, `assessed_fee_cents` can legitimately exceed the cap.

Every input to that computation is preserved on the incident as `fee_basis`, holding `tier_base_cents`, `occupancy_multiplier`, `non_ecv_surcharge_applied` and `unpermitted_multiplier_applied` as booleans recording whether each factor differed from 1, `cap_applied`, and `aggravation_cents`. That audit is what a permit holder is shown when they ask how a number was reached.

**Billing and the knock-on effects.** When the assessed fee is above zero a `FALSE_ALARM_FEE` invoice is issued; when the tier base is zero under the free allowance and no aggravation applies, no invoice row is created at all and no administrative fee is charged. The premises' `chargeable_count_rolling_365`, `chargeable_count_permit_year`, `chargeable_count_window`, `lifetime_assessed_cents`, `outstanding_balance_cents`, `renewal_block_active` and `response_status` are then all recomputed. And when `chargeable_count_window` reaches or exceeds `suspension_threshold_count`, an enforcement order is auto-proposed for the premises: a suspension order in `PROPOSED` with `basis` of `EXCESSIVE_FALSE_ALARMS`, its `triggering_incident_id` set to this incident, `effective_from` at the logical clock plus 10 days and `effective_through` at 30 days after that. The premises' `version` is bumped by 1.

**Response posture.** A premises' `response_status` is derived, never set by hand, and is recomputed at the end of every request that changes a suspension order's status, records a chargeable or exempt determination, renews a permit, or moves the logical clock. The derivation is a strict cascade evaluated in this order. If the premises has a suspension order in `EFFECTIVE` or `REINSTATEMENT_PENDING` whose closed interval contains the current instant, the posture is `RESPONSE_SUSPENDED`. Otherwise, if the premises has a `LIFTED` order and the current instant is at or before that order's `lifted_at` plus its `probation_days` whole days, the posture is `RESPONSE_PROBATION`. Otherwise, if `chargeable_count_window` is at or above `probation_threshold_count`, the posture is `RESPONSE_PROBATION`. Otherwise it is `RESPONSE_ACTIVE`. Orders in `VACATED` or `EXPIRED` are ignored entirely, and a premises with no pinned schedule is `RESPONSE_ACTIVE`. Both thresholds are inclusive: with the defaults, a window count of 4 puts the premises on probation and 3 does not, and a count of 6 proposes a suspension while 5 does not.

**Administrative void.** An administrator may void an incident recorded in error, supplying `void_reason`. The transition is available while the incident is in `REPORTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` and lands in `VOIDED`, a terminal state. Any invoice attached to the incident is voided, any money already settled against it moves to the premises' `credit_balance_cents`, the premises' remaining chargeable incidents are renumbered and re-priced, the premises' counters and posture are recomputed and its `version` is bumped by 1.

**The counting invariant.** After every chargeable determination and after each of its compensating actions — an appeal granted, or a determination remanded — three properties must hold exactly. The premises' `chargeable_count_window` equals the number of chargeable incidents inside the governing window. The set of `occurrence_index` values across those incidents is exactly the contiguous run from 1 to that count, with no gaps and no duplicates, assigned in order of occurrence instant ascending and, for ties, creation instant ascending. And the premises' `lifetime_assessed_cents` equals the sum of the re-derived assessed amounts over every non-voided invoice for the indices currently in force. Renumbering is therefore not optional bookkeeping: when an incident leaves the chargeable set, every remaining chargeable incident is renumbered contiguously in that same order and re-priced at its new tier, until the premises' state is what it would have been had the removed incident never been counted.

### 3.6. Contesting a Determination: Appeals and the Hearing Calendar

**Opening a session.** A hearing docket is one officer's sitting in one room. It is opened with a `hearing_officer_user_id` naming the officer the session belongs to, a `session_start` and `session_end` describing a half-open interval whose end must be strictly after its start, a `location` identifying the hearing room, and an optional `capacity` between 1 and 8 that defaults to 4 and caps how many appeals the session will seat. The session must be in the future: `session_start` must be strictly after the current logical clock, and `session_end` strictly after `session_start`, else `ERR_HEARING_SESSION_IN_PAST`. The docket is created in `OPEN` with `scheduled_appeal_count` at 0 and `held_at` null.

One officer cannot sit twice at once. A proposed session is compared against that officer's existing dockets in `OPEN`, `CLOSED_FOR_SCHEDULING` or `HELD` — a `CANCELLED` docket releases its interval and is ignored — and rejected with `ERR_DOCKET_SESSION_OVERLAP` if a conflict is found. The intervals are half-open, and a 15-minute turnaround buffer is applied on both sides of the existing session: a conflict exists when the new `session_start` is strictly before the existing `session_end` plus 15 minutes **and** the existing `session_start` is strictly before the new `session_end` plus 15 minutes. Both arms are strict and the buffer is symmetric, so back-to-back sessions collide — a new session starting exactly at an existing session's end leaves a zero-minute gap, which is under the buffer — and the earliest legal back-to-back start is exactly the existing `session_end` plus 15 minutes, since strictness makes that value pass. Dockets belonging to different officers never collide however their intervals sit. Seat capacity is a separate gate applied at scheduling time, not at session creation; overlap and capacity never substitute for one another. Comparison is minute-granular in UTC.

**Filing.** A permit holder contests a chargeable determination by supplying the `false_alarm_incident_id`, the `grounds` — one of `WRONG_CAUSE_CODE`, `EXEMPT_CAUSE`, `NOT_MY_SYSTEM`, `VERIFIED_RESPONSE_DEFECT`, `COUNTING_ERROR` or `FEE_CALCULATION_ERROR` — a `narrative` stating the appellant's case, an `expected_false_alarm_incident_version`, and optionally a `prior_appeal_id` to file a rehearing. The incident must sit in `COUNTED` or `APPEALED` for the filing to be entertained at all, and anything else fails position 17 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. `APPEALED` is a declared source on purpose: a second filing against an already-appealed incident has to reach the window rule and the pending-appeal rule below, which is where Collision C is decided, rather than being turned away early on its status. The filing window is measured from the ruling: the appeal must be filed no later than `determined_at` plus the governing schedule's `appeal_filing_days` whole days, the boundary being inclusive, else `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Only one live appeal may exist per incident: if the incident already has an appeal in `FILED`, `SCHEDULED` or `HEARD`, the filing is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_PENDING`. When both faults are present — a late filing against an incident that also already has a live appeal — the window rule is the higher-priority row and `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` is what the caller sees.

An accepted appeal is created in `FILED` with `filed_at` at the logical clock, `filed_by_user_id` set from the acting `X-User-ID`, `hearing_docket_id` null, `decision`, `decided_at`, `decided_by_user_id` and `findings` all null, and `alarm_site_id` denormalized from the incident. The contested incident moves from `COUNTED` to `APPEALED`, its `appeal_count` and `version` each rise by 1, and — this is the point of the state — the fee **stands**. The incident stays in the chargeable set with its `occurrence_index` and `assessed_fee_cents` untouched; what freezes is the dunning: any invoice carrying that `false_alarm_incident_id` keeps its `collections_stage` exactly where it is and is excluded from nightly escalation for as long as the appeal is live. That freeze is also enforced synchronously: a collections referral against an invoice whose linked incident is `APPEALED` is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_UNDER_APPEAL`.

**Rehearings.** A rehearing is a new appeal row, never a reopening of the old one, linked backwards through `prior_appeal_id`. It is accepted only when the prior appeal actually reached `DENIED` — a `WITHDRAWN` or `GRANTED` appeal grants no second bite — and only when no rehearing has already been filed against that prior appeal, else `ERR_REHEARING_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. The new row's `rehearing_depth` is the prior appeal's `rehearing_depth` plus 1, and an original appeal has depth 0. Because depth may never exceed 2, the chain is bounded: an original, a rehearing of it, and one further rehearing, after which no link is eligible.

**Seating and holding.** Placing a filed appeal on a session requires the `hearing_docket_id` and an `expected_hearing_docket_version`. The appeal must be in `FILED`; the docket must be in `OPEN`, else `ERR_DOCKET_NOT_OPEN`; the docket's seat count including the appeal being added must not exceed `capacity`, else `ERR_DOCKET_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; and the session must not already have begun — the logical clock must be strictly before `session_start`, else `ERR_HEARING_SESSION_IN_PAST`. On success the appeal moves to `SCHEDULED` with its `hearing_docket_id` set, and the docket's `scheduled_appeal_count` and `version` each rise by 1. When the count reaches `capacity` the docket closes itself, `OPEN → CLOSED_FOR_SCHEDULING`, and further seating attempts meet `ERR_DOCKET_NOT_OPEN`.

Holding the session is the officer's own act: only the caller whose `X-User-ID` equals the docket's `hearing_officer_user_id` may do it, else `ERR_NOT_DOCKET_OFFICER`; the logical clock must have reached `session_start`, the boundary inclusive, else `ERR_HEARING_SESSION_NOT_STARTED`; and the docket must seat at least one appeal, else `ERR_DOCKET_EMPTY`. A docket may be held from either `OPEN` or `CLOSED_FOR_SCHEDULING` under identical preconditions and with identical effects: `held_at` is stamped with the logical clock, the docket becomes `HELD`, which is terminal, its `version` rises by 1, and every appeal on it in `SCHEDULED` advances to `HEARD`, becoming decidable.

Cancelling a docket is likewise available symmetrically from `OPEN` or `CLOSED_FOR_SCHEDULING`, takes an optional `cancellation_reason`, and lands in the terminal `CANCELLED`. Its consequences cascade downward: every appeal seated on it in `SCHEDULED` reverts to `FILED` with `hearing_docket_id` cleared, returning to the unscheduled pool to await a new session; `scheduled_appeal_count` is reset to 0; the session's interval is released for overlap purposes; and `version` rises by 1. A permit holder may also give up a seat voluntarily: withdrawal is available from `FILED` or `SCHEDULED`, takes an optional `withdrawal_reason`, and may only be exercised by the appellant — the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the appeal's `alarm_site_id`, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Withdrawal from either state returns the incident from `APPEALED` to `COUNTED` and resumes dunning on its invoice; withdrawal from `SCHEDULED` additionally decrements the docket's `scheduled_appeal_count`, and if that docket was `CLOSED_FOR_SCHEDULING` the freed seat reopens it, `CLOSED_FOR_SCHEDULING → OPEN`. What withdrawal does not do is clear the appeal's own `hearing_docket_id`: the withdrawn row keeps pointing at the session whose seat it gave up, and emptying that pointer is a docket cancellation's act alone. A withdrawn appeal is not eligible for rehearing.

An appeal that is filed and then simply forgotten does not sit forever: the nightly program run dismisses any appeal still in `FILED` once the logical clock passes `filed_at` plus 60 days without the appeal ever having been scheduled. The row becomes `DISMISSED_UNTIMELY`, terminal, the incident returns from `APPEALED` to `COUNTED`, and dunning resumes on its invoice.

**Deciding.** A heard appeal is decided by supplying a `decision` of `GRANTED`, `DENIED` or `REMANDED`, the written `findings`, and — only on a grant — an `exempt_cause_code`. Two access rules bind every decision. The decider must be the officer of the docket that heard the appeal, meaning the acting `X-User-ID` equals the `hearing_officer_user_id` of the docket named by the appeal's `hearing_docket_id`, else `ERR_NOT_DOCKET_OFFICER`. And nobody reviews their own work: the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ both from the appealed incident's `determined_by_user_id` and from the appeal's `filed_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. All three branches record `decision`, `decided_at`, `decided_by_user_id` and `findings`, and all three are terminal for the appeal row.

`DENIED` is the conservative outcome: the count stands. The incident returns from `APPEALED` to `COUNTED` with its `determination`, `occurrence_index` and `assessed_fee_cents` exactly as they were before the appeal, its invoice re-enters the nightly escalation set, and the incident becomes eligible for exactly one rehearing.

`REMANDED` sends the determination back for another look. The incident returns from `APPEALED` to `UNDER_REVIEW` with `determination`, `determined_at`, `determined_by_user_id`, `occurrence_index`, `assessed_fee_cents`, `fee_basis` and `counted_window_mode` all cleared to null — `assessed_fee_cents` to null rather than to 0, which is exactly what separates a remand, where the incident is once again unpriced and awaiting a ruling, from a grant, where it has been ruled and priced at zero; any invoice carrying that `false_alarm_incident_id` is voided and its allocations reversed, the reversed amounts landing in the premises' `credit_balance_cents`; the premises' counters are recomputed and its remaining chargeable incidents renumbered contiguously. A different review officer may then re-determine the incident, re-entering the counting cycle. The appeal row is terminal even though the incident is not.

`GRANTED` is the widest-reaching act in the program, and it additionally requires a recognized exempt cause: `exempt_cause_code` must be present and a member of the governing schedule's `exempt_cause_codes`, else `ERR_EXEMPT_CAUSE_NOT_RECOGNIZED`. Its consequences run in four ordered hops. Hop one: the incident moves from `APPEALED` to the terminal `EXEMPT_BY_APPEAL`, with `determination` set to `EXEMPT`, `exempt_cause_code` taken from the ruling, `occurrence_index` set to null and `assessed_fee_cents` set to 0. Hop two: because the incident left the chargeable set, the premises' chargeable counters decrement and every remaining chargeable incident at that premises — those in `COUNTED` or `APPEALED` — is renumbered contiguously in order of occurrence instant ascending then creation instant ascending, and its fee re-derived at its new tier. Hop three: because a renumbered incident's assessed fee changed, its invoice is re-assessed, `assessed_cents` recomputed, and any `paid_cents` above the new `assessed_cents` moved into the premises' `credit_balance_cents`. Hop four: because the premises' `chargeable_count_window` has fallen below `suspension_threshold_count`, every suspension order for that premises in `PROPOSED`, `EFFECTIVE` or `REINSTATEMENT_PENDING` moves to `VACATED` and `response_status` is recomputed. The premises' `version` and the incident's `version` each rise by 1. A single grant can therefore reprice invoices belonging to incidents that were never appealed and restore police response to a premises that had lost it.

**Reading the calendar.** A docket may be read individually — the read is how a client learns the `version` it must send back as `expected_hearing_docket_version` — or listed with filters on `hearing_officer_user_id`, `status`, and a `session_from` / `session_to` window, paged by `page` and `per_page`. Appeals may be read individually, exposing the `decision`, `findings` and `rehearing_depth`, or listed with filters on `false_alarm_incident_id`, `alarm_site_id`, `hearing_docket_id`, `prior_appeal_id` — which is how a rehearing chain is walked — plus `status` and `decision`, again paged by `page` and `per_page`.

### 3.7. Withdrawing Police Response: Suspension Orders and Reinstatement

**Proposing an order.** An administrator proposes withdrawal of police response by supplying the `alarm_site_id`, the `triggering_incident_id` naming the chargeable incident that crossed the threshold, a `basis` of `EXCESSIVE_FALSE_ALARMS`, `UNPERMITTED_OPERATION` or `NONPAYMENT`, the closed-interval bounds `effective_from` and `effective_through`, and an `expected_alarm_site_version`. Four gates apply. The premises revision must still match, else `ERR_STALE_ALARM_SITE_VERSION`. The dates must be coherent: `effective_from` at or after the current logical clock and at or after the triggering incident's `occurred_at`, and `effective_through` strictly after `effective_from`, else `ERR_SUSPENSION_EFFECTIVE_DATE_INVALID`. The premises may not already be under a live order across the same days, else `ERR_SUSPENSION_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. And the basis must still hold: the premises' chargeable count under its governing counting window must be at or above `suspension_threshold_count`, else `ERR_SUSPENSION_BASIS_NO_LONGER_MET` — a count that has since fallen through an appeal grant or a void invalidates the basis, and that same check is re-applied when the order is served, so an order proposed on solid ground may still fail to take effect.

The overlap geometry here is deliberately unlike the hearing calendar's. Candidates are the premises' own orders in `PROPOSED`, `EFFECTIVE` or `REINSTATEMENT_PENDING`; `LIFTED`, `VACATED` and `EXPIRED` orders free their interval. The intervals are **closed**, `effective_from` through `effective_through`, and the buffer is **zero**: a conflict exists when the new `effective_from` is at or before the existing `effective_through` **and** the existing `effective_from` is at or before the new `effective_through`. Touching endpoints therefore collide — a new order starting on the very day an existing order ends is a conflict — and the earliest accepted consecutive start is one day after the existing `effective_through`. There is no capacity notion: a premises may hold at most one live order at a time. Comparison is whole-day in UTC. So a hearing officer's sessions are half-open and cushioned by fifteen minutes while a premises' suspensions are closed and flush; neither rule may be applied to the other's intervals.

The accepted order is created in `PROPOSED` with `served_at`, `reinstatement_requested_at`, `technician_inspection_date`, `ecv_attestation`, `lifted_at` and `vacated_reason` all null. Its `suspension_sequence` is the premises' `lifted_suspension_count` plus 1, and the premises' `version` rises by 1.

**Serving it, and what serving fixes.** Serving is available to an administrator or a permit clerk from `PROPOSED` only, requires no body, and additionally demands that the logical clock has not yet passed `effective_from` — the clock must be at or before it, else `ERR_SUSPENSION_EFFECTIVE_DATE_INVALID` — and that the suspension basis still holds. On success the order becomes `EFFECTIVE`, `served_at` is stamped, and the reinstatement requirements are computed once and frozen on the row from the premises' `lifted_suspension_count` as it stands at serve time. Writing that prior count as the number of previously lifted suspensions: `reinstatement_fee_cents` is `reinstatement_base_fee_cents` multiplied by the quantity 1 plus `reinstatement_escalation_pct` times the prior count, rounded half up to a whole cent; `probation_days` is 90 whole days multiplied by the prior count plus 1; `suspension_sequence` is the prior count plus 1; and `required_conditions` is fixed as an object with `inspection_required` true, `ecv_attestation_required` true, `min_ecv_contacts` equal to 2 plus the prior count, and `invoices_paid_required` true. Only lifted orders feed that ladder — `VACATED` and `EXPIRED` orders never contribute to the prior count, so an order that is vacated leaves the escalation ladder exactly where it was, and the next order costs what this one would have. With the base fee at 60000 cents and the escalation at 0.5, a first suspension carries a 60000-cent reinstatement fee with 90 probation days and 2 callable contacts required, a second carries 90000 cents with 180 days and 3 contacts, a third 120000 cents with 270 days and 4 contacts.

Serving also issues a `REINSTATEMENT_FEE` invoice against the premises with `suspension_order_id` set, recomputes the premises' `response_status` to `RESPONSE_SUSPENDED` along with its `outstanding_balance_cents` and `renewal_block_active`, and bumps the premises' `version` by 1. From that point dispatch requests for the premises' systems are refused for the duration.

**Earning reinstatement.** The permit holder applies by supplying a `technician_inspection_date`, an `ecv_attestation` and a `corrective_action_summary`. The order must be `EFFECTIVE`. The inspection must be fresh and not fictional: `technician_inspection_date` must be at or after the logical clock minus 30 whole days — the boundary inclusive, so an inspection exactly 30 days old still qualifies — and at or before the logical clock, else `ERR_INSPECTION_EVIDENCE_STALE`. The attestation must be affirmative and accompanied by a non-empty `corrective_action_summary`, else `ERR_ECV_ATTESTATION_REQUIRED`. And the premises' `ecv_callable_contact_count` must be at or above the `min_ecv_contacts` frozen in `required_conditions`, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ECV_CONTACTS` — which is why a repeat offender must add contacts before it can apply. On success the order moves to `REINSTATEMENT_PENDING`, storing `reinstatement_requested_at` at the logical clock together with the inspection date and attestation. Police response remains suspended throughout: applying is not being reinstated.

**Lifting.** Only an administrator lifts, only from `REINSTATEMENT_PENDING`, with no body. The money condition is absolute: every invoice for the premises whose `collections_stage` is not `WRITTEN_OFF` must have `payment_status` of `PAID`, so that the premises' outstanding penalty balance net of `credit_balance_cents` is zero, else `ERR_OUTSTANDING_BALANCE_BLOCKS_REINSTATEMENT`. A written-off invoice is excluded from that test — the city has already given up on it — but a merely referred or partially paid one blocks the lift. On success the order becomes `LIFTED`, terminal, with `lifted_at` stamped; the premises' `lifted_suspension_count` rises by 1, raising the next order's fee, probation and contact requirement; `response_status` is recomputed to `RESPONSE_PROBATION` for `probation_days` whole days measured from `lifted_at`, after which it falls back to `RESPONSE_ACTIVE`; and the premises' `version` rises by 1.

**The two ways an order ends without reinstatement.** Vacating is the corrective path, available to an administrator with a `vacated_reason`, or fired by the system as the last hop of a granted appeal. It is reachable from `PROPOSED`, `EFFECTIVE` or `REINSTATEMENT_PENDING` and lands in the terminal `VACATED`. From `PROPOSED` no invoice exists yet, so no money moves and only `vacated_reason` is stored and `response_status` recomputed. From `EFFECTIVE` or `REINSTATEMENT_PENDING` the `REINSTATEMENT_FEE` invoice carrying that `suspension_order_id` is voided and its allocations reversed into the premises' `credit_balance_cents`. In neither case is `lifted_suspension_count` incremented: a vacated order never counted as a suspension at all. The premises' `version` rises by 1.

Expiry is the clock's path. The nightly program run moves an order from `EFFECTIVE` to the terminal `EXPIRED` once the logical clock passes `effective_through`. Police response resumes without a reinstatement, with `response_status` recomputed from the counting variant alone; `lifted_suspension_count` is **not** incremented, so an expired suspension does not raise the next order's fee; and — unlike a vacatur — the `REINSTATEMENT_FEE` invoice stays payable. Sitting out a suspension is therefore cheaper for the next offence than earning reinstatement, but no cheaper for this one.

An order may be read individually, exposing the frozen `required_conditions`, `reinstatement_fee_cents`, `probation_days` and `suspension_sequence`, or listed with filters on `alarm_site_id`, `triggering_incident_id`, `status` and `basis`, paged by `page` and `per_page`.

### 3.8. Assessment, Dunning and Referral

**How an invoice is assembled.** Invoices are never posted by hand; each one is a side effect of exactly one triggering event — a system reaching `PERMITTED`, a permit renewal, a system falling to `UNPERMITTED`, an incident being ruled chargeable, or a suspension order being served — and every rate it uses comes from the schedule pinned in the premises' `governing_fee_schedule_id`, never from a newer active schedule. The row records `alarm_site_id` plus exactly one source link: `false_alarm_incident_id` when `kind` is `FALSE_ALARM_FEE`, `alarm_permit_id` when `kind` is `PERMIT_ISSUE_FEE`, `PERMIT_RENEWAL_FEE` or `UNPERMITTED_OPERATION_PENALTY`, `suspension_order_id` when `kind` is `REINSTATEMENT_FEE`. It receives the next sequential `invoice_number` in issuance order, zero-padded in the form INV-000001.

The `line_items` are constructed per kind, in the declared order, each entry carrying a `code`, a `description`, a `unit_amount_cents`, a `quantity`, a `multiplier` and a computed `extended_cents`. A `FALSE_ALARM_FEE` invoice carries a first line coded FALSE_ALARM_BASE whose `unit_amount_cents` is the incident's tier base, `quantity` 1, and `multiplier` the product of the occupancy multiplier, the surcharge factor and the unpermitted factor — and when the incident's `fee_basis` records `cap_applied`, that line's `extended_cents` is overridden to `per_incident_fee_cap_cents`; it carries a second line coded SUSPENSION_PERIOD_AGGRAVATION at the aggravation amount with `multiplier` 1.00, present only when the aggravation is above zero. A `PERMIT_ISSUE_FEE` invoice carries one PERMIT_ISSUE line at `permit_issue_fee_cents`. A `PERMIT_RENEWAL_FEE` invoice carries a PERMIT_RENEWAL line at `permit_renewal_fee_cents` and, only when the computed late fee is above zero, a second LATE_RENEWAL line at that amount. An `UNPERMITTED_OPERATION_PENALTY` invoice carries one UNPERMITTED_OPERATION line at `unpermitted_operation_penalty_cents`. A `REINSTATEMENT_FEE` invoice carries one REINSTATEMENT line at `reinstatement_base_fee_cents` with `multiplier` equal to 1 plus `reinstatement_escalation_pct` times the premises' prior lifted-suspension count.

The rounding order is pinned and is not a matter of taste: each line's `extended_cents` is `unit_amount_cents` times `quantity` times `multiplier`, rounded half up to a whole cent, and only then are the lines summed into `subtotal_cents`. Rounding per line before summation is mandatory — two lines each computing to 12345.6 cents give 12346 plus 12346, that is 24692, and never the 24691 that summing first would produce. `admin_fee_cents` is then the subtotal times `admin_fee_pct`, rounded half up once on the already-rounded subtotal, and clamped upward to `admin_fee_min_cents` and downward to `admin_fee_max_cents`; the clamp binds before the fee joins the assessment, so a 200-cent subtotal still carries the 500-cent floor and a 200000-cent subtotal carries the 5000-cent ceiling. `late_penalty_cents` is 0 at issuance. `assessed_cents` is `subtotal_cents` plus `admin_fee_cents` plus `late_penalty_cents`; `paid_cents` and `adjusted_cents` start at 0; `outstanding_cents` is `assessed_cents` minus `paid_cents` minus `adjusted_cents`, floored at zero and never negative. `issued_on` is the logical clock and `due_on` is `issued_on` plus `invoice_due_days` whole days. `days_delinquent` starts at 0, and `referral_total_cents`, `referral_agency` and `referred_on` are null. The one case that produces no row at all is a false alarm inside the free allowance with no aggravation: a zero subtotal means no invoice and therefore no administrative fee either.

**Two dimensions on one row.** `payment_status` tracks the money and `collections_stage` tracks the dunning, and they advance independently. An invoice can be `REFERRED` and still reach `PAID`; it can be `PAID` while still sitting at `FINAL_NOTICE` until the next nightly run resettles the stage. Neither dimension is derivable from the other.

The money dimension starts at `UNPAID` and terminates at `PAID` or `VOIDED`. An allocation smaller than `outstanding_cents` moves `UNPAID → PARTIALLY_PAID`, raising `paid_cents` and lowering `outstanding_cents`, with the premises' `outstanding_balance_cents` and `renewal_block_active` recomputed. Settlement — `UNPAID → PAID` or `PARTIALLY_PAID → PAID` — occurs exactly when `paid_cents` plus `adjusted_cents` equals `assessed_cents` after the write, whether that is reached by an allocation covering the remainder or by a `WAIVE` adjustment; `outstanding_cents` becomes 0, the stage resettles to `CURRENT` on the next nightly run, and when every invoice for the premises is `PAID` a `REINSTATEMENT_PENDING` suspension order becomes liftable. Voiding a payment reverses that movement: `PARTIALLY_PAID → UNPAID` when the voided payment's allocations were the only payments against the invoice, and `PAID → PARTIALLY_PAID` when other applied allocations remain, in which case the invoice must not be written off, else `ERR_INVOICE_WRITTEN_OFF`. Both reversals restore `paid_cents` and `outstanding_cents` to their pre-payment values, return the payment's credited residual out of the premises' `credit_balance_cents`, and leave `collections_stage` where it stands until the nightly run. Cascade voids — a remand, an incident void, a vacated order — carry the invoice from `UNPAID` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` to the terminal `VOIDED`: `assessed_cents` is preserved for audit, `outstanding_cents` becomes 0, the invoice leaves every premises rollup and every dunning set, and from `PARTIALLY_PAID` each applied allocation is reversed into the premises' `credit_balance_cents`.

The dunning dimension starts at `CURRENT` and terminates at `WRITTEN_OFF`. `days_delinquent` is the whole days between `due_on` and the logical clock, floored at zero. The nightly run advances `CURRENT → NOTICE` once `days_delinquent` is at least 1, and `NOTICE → FINAL_NOTICE` once it is at least 30; both steps require `outstanding_cents` above zero and require that the linked incident, when there is one, is not in `APPEALED`. The first step is where the late penalty is added, exactly once per invoice; the second adds no further penalty and merely makes the invoice referral-eligible once `days_delinquent` reaches `delinquency_days`. Settlement runs the ladder backwards: the nightly resettlement moves `NOTICE → CURRENT` or `FINAL_NOTICE → CURRENT` once `outstanding_cents` has become 0, dunning stops, and any late penalty already assessed is **not** refunded.

**The delinquency arithmetic.** Both the late penalty and the referral total come out of one five-step computation whose evaluation order is pinned, because the collections tier is keyed on a total that already contains the late penalty while the late penalty is keyed on the pre-penalty principal. Step one: the outstanding principal is `assessed_cents` minus `paid_cents` minus `adjusted_cents`, and must be above zero. Step two: `late_penalty_cents` is that principal times `late_penalty_pct`, rounded half up, and is added into `assessed_cents` — once per invoice, never twice. Step three: an interim total is recomputed as `assessed_cents` minus `paid_cents` minus `adjusted_cents`, **after** step two. Step four: the collections administrative charge is a flat 2000 cents when the interim total is at or below 25000 cents, the interim total times 0.08 rounded half up when it is above 25000 and at or below 100000, and a flat 10000 cents when it is above 100000. Step five: `referral_total_cents` is the interim total plus that charge. Both tier bounds sit with the lower comparison: exactly 25000 takes the 2000-cent flat charge, 25001 takes the 0.08 rate, exactly 100000 still takes the 0.08 rate for 8000 cents, and 100001 takes the 10000-cent flat charge. The order matters concretely — a 24000-cent principal becomes 26400 at step three, which lands in the middle tier for a 2112-cent charge and a 28512-cent referral total, whereas pricing the tier off the pre-penalty 24000 would have yielded the 2000-cent flat charge and 26400. Rounding occurs at steps two and four only. And `referral_total_cents` is what the agency is handed, not what the premises owes the city: it does not feed `outstanding_cents`, so the premises' `outstanding_balance_cents` is untouched by the collections charge. Invoices in `payment_status` of `VOIDED`, invoices already `WRITTEN_OFF`, and invoices whose linked incident is `APPEALED` never escalate at all.

**Referral and adjustment.** A billing clerk refers a delinquent invoice by supplying the `referral_agency`. Every criterion must hold together: `collections_stage` must be `FINAL_NOTICE`, `days_delinquent` at or above `delinquency_days`, and `outstanding_cents` at or above `collections_referral_threshold_cents`, else `ERR_REFERRAL_CRITERIA_NOT_MET`; the linked incident must not be `APPEALED`, else `ERR_INVOICE_UNDER_APPEAL`; and a written-off invoice may not be referred, else `ERR_INVOICE_WRITTEN_OFF`. On success the stage moves `FINAL_NOTICE → REFERRED`, `referred_on` is stamped, `referral_total_cents` and `referral_agency` are stored, the invoice's `version` rises by 1, and — because referred invoices carry the top dunning rank — the invoice moves to the head of the automatic allocation order.

Only the program administrator may move assessed money. An adjustment supplies an `adjustment_kind` of `WAIVE`, `REDUCE` or `WRITE_OFF`, a `reason`, an `expected_penalty_invoice_version`, and an `amount_cents` where the kind takes one. A `WAIVE` or a `REDUCE` takes one and must carry it, its absence being refused with `ERR_MISSING_REQUIRED_FIELD`; a `WRITE_OFF` takes none, is unaffected by one sent anyway, and is always taken at exactly `outstanding_cents`. Because that requirement turns on the kind rather than on the shape of the body alone, it is not tested at the field-validation positions but last of all, after every check below has passed — so an amountless `WAIVE` that also quotes a stale revision, or that comes from the clerk who paid the invoice, is answered by the revision guard or by segregation instead. The invoice revision must still match, else `ERR_STALE_PENALTY_INVOICE_VERSION`; a written-off invoice may not be adjusted again, else `ERR_INVOICE_WRITTEN_OFF`; the adjusting `X-User-ID` must differ from every `recorded_by_user_id` of a payment already posted to that invoice, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; and the amount must fit — a `WAIVE` or `REDUCE` may not exceed the invoice's current `outstanding_cents`, and a `WRITE_OFF` is taken at exactly `outstanding_cents`, else `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`. The adjustment raises `adjusted_cents` and recomputes `outstanding_cents`; a `WAIVE` that closes the gap settles `payment_status` to `PAID`; a `WRITE_OFF` requires the stage to be `REFERRED` and moves it to the terminal `WRITTEN_OFF`, adding the whole outstanding amount to `adjusted_cents`, zeroing `outstanding_cents`, and removing the invoice from the premises' balance and from every allocation candidate set. The invoice's `version` and the premises' `version` each rise by 1, and the premises' `outstanding_balance_cents` and `renewal_block_active` are recomputed.

An invoice may be read individually — exposing its `line_items`, both state dimensions, and the `version` a client must echo as `expected_penalty_invoice_version` — or listed with filters on `alarm_site_id`, `false_alarm_incident_id`, `alarm_permit_id`, `suspension_order_id`, `kind`, `payment_status` and `collections_stage`, paged by `page` and `per_page`.

### 3.9. Taking Money: Payments, Allocation and Reversal

A payment is posted against a premises account, not against an invoice. The tender supplies the `alarm_site_id`, an `amount_cents` of at least 1, the `tendered_on` instant, a `method` of `CARD`, `CHECK`, `CASH` or `ACH`, an `allocation_mode` of `AUTO_WATERFALL` or `EXPLICIT`, an `expected_alarm_site_version`, and — only in explicit mode — an `explicit_allocations` array whose entries each name a `penalty_invoice_id` and an `amount_cents`. The premises revision must still match, else `ERR_STALE_ALARM_SITE_VERSION`, and no allocation may target a written-off invoice, else `ERR_INVOICE_WRITTEN_OFF`. The accepted payment is created in `APPLIED` with the next sequential `receipt_number` in the form RCT-000001 and `recorded_by_user_id` taken from the acting `X-User-ID` — that stamp is what later bars the same person from adjusting the invoice they collected on. `voided_at` and `void_reason` are null.

**Automatic allocation.** In `AUTO_WATERFALL` mode the candidate set is the paying premises' own invoices whose `payment_status` is `UNPAID` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` and whose `collections_stage` is not `WRITTEN_OFF`. Candidates are ordered by dunning stage rank descending, where `REFERRED` ranks 4, `FINAL_NOTICE` ranks 3, `NOTICE` ranks 2 and `CURRENT` ranks 1; ties are broken by `due_on` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending. The row identifier is never an ordering input. The city collects its most desperate debt first: a referred invoice is paid before an older but merely current one, so `due_on` only decides between invoices standing at the same stage.

Walking that order with a running remainder, each invoice takes the lesser of the remainder and its own `outstanding_cents`. Every non-zero application creates a `PaymentAllocation` — which carries only an `id` and a `created_at` beyond its own fields, having no revision counter of its own — recording the `payment_record_id`, the `penalty_invoice_id`, the `amount_cents` applied, a 1-based `sequence_index` giving its position in the order that produced it, and `status` of `APPLIED`. The invoice's `paid_cents` rises by that amount and its `payment_status` becomes `PAID` when `paid_cents` plus `adjusted_cents` equals `assessed_cents`, and `PARTIALLY_PAID` otherwise. Whatever remains after every candidate is exhausted becomes the payment's `credited_cents` and is added to the premises' `credit_balance_cents`; `allocated_cents` is the tendered amount minus that credit. A parked credit is not spent by itself: it is released only by a later payment's allocation or by a void reversal, never auto-consumed by the next invoice the premises incurs.

**Explicit allocation.** In `EXPLICIT` mode the caller dictates the split, and all four conditions must hold or the whole tender is rejected with `ERR_ALLOCATION_MISMATCH`: the sum of the entries' `amount_cents` must equal the payment's `amount_cents` exactly, with no remainder permitted; every entry's amount must be at least 1 and no more than that invoice's `outstanding_cents`; every named invoice must belong to the paying premises, must not be written off, and must be `UNPAID` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`; and no `penalty_invoice_id` may appear twice in the array. `sequence_index` follows the request array order, and `credited_cents` is 0 by construction, since an exact sum leaves nothing to park.

**Voiding a tender.** Only an administrator voids, supplying a `void_reason`, and only inside the window: the logical clock must be at or before `tendered_on` plus 2 whole days, the boundary inclusive, and none of the payment's allocations may target an invoice whose `collections_stage` has since become `WRITTEN_OFF`, else `ERR_PAYMENT_VOID_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The payment moves `APPLIED → VOIDED` with `voided_at` stamped, every one of its allocations flips `APPLIED → REVERSED`, each touched invoice's `paid_cents` drops by the reversed amount and its `payment_status` recomputes downward, the payment's `credited_cents` is subtracted back out of the premises' `credit_balance_cents`, and the premises' `version` rises by 1.

**Cash conservation.** After every posted payment and after every void, one identity must hold exactly for each premises: the sum of `amount_cents` over that premises' payments in `APPLIED` equals the sum of `amount_cents` over allocations in `APPLIED` whose payment is also in `APPLIED` and whose invoice belongs to that premises, plus the premises' `credit_balance_cents`. Cash received is either sitting on an invoice or sitting in credit; it is never anywhere else and never in both places. A void is the exact compensating action: it removes the payment from the left side and its allocations from the right side within the same request, restoring both sides to their pre-payment values with zero drift in `credit_balance_cents`, which may never fall below zero. The identity is exact, never approximate.

**Rolling the premises up.** Every request that creates, adjusts, voids or pays an invoice, changes a contact, changes a system's status, or changes a suspension order's status ends by recomputing the premises in a pinned seven-step order, because each step reads the one before it. First, per invoice, `outstanding_cents` is `assessed_cents` minus `paid_cents` minus `adjusted_cents`, floored at zero — with the single exception of an invoice whose `payment_status` is `VOIDED`, which is held at 0 outright and never recomputed, whatever `assessed_cents` it still preserves for audit, so that no later roll-up can arithmetically resurrect a void. Second, `outstanding_balance_cents` is the sum of `outstanding_cents` over the premises' invoices whose `payment_status` is `UNPAID` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` and whose `collections_stage` is not `WRITTEN_OFF`. Third, `lifetime_assessed_cents` is the sum of `assessed_cents` over every invoice of the premises whose `payment_status` is not `VOIDED` — so a written-off invoice still counts toward lifetime assessment even though it has left the outstanding balance. Fourth, the three derived counts are recomputed: `ecv_callable_contact_count` as the number of contacts that are `ACTIVE` with `is_ecv_callable` true, `unpermitted_system_count` as the number of systems in `UNPERMITTED`, and `lifted_suspension_count` as the number of suspension orders in `LIFTED`. Fifth, `renewal_block_active` becomes true exactly when `outstanding_balance_cents` is at or above `delinquent_balance_block_cents` — the comparison is inclusive, so a balance of exactly 50000 cents blocks renewal. Sixth, the permits follow that flag: every permit of the premises sitting in `RENEWAL_DUE` moves to `RENEWAL_BLOCKED` when the flag has just become true, and every `RENEWAL_BLOCKED` permit moves back to `RENEWAL_DUE` when it has just become false. Seventh, `response_status` is re-derived from the posture cascade, which is why the contact count of step four must already be current.

A payment may be read individually, showing its `allocated_cents` and `credited_cents`, or listed with filters on `alarm_site_id`, `status`, `method` and a `tendered_from` / `tendered_to` window, paged by `page` and `per_page`.

### 3.10. Holding the Monitoring Industry to Account

**Registering a company.** A monitoring company is registered with its `legal_name`, its state `license_number`, a `contact_email`, a `contact_phone` in E.164 form, and a `score_mode` of `VOLUME_WEIGHTED` or `PERIOD_EQUAL` that decides which scoring variant governs it thereafter. The licence number must be unique across companies whose `status` is not `REVOKED` — revocation releases the number for reuse by another company, so a revoked row does not block registration. The company is created in `ACTIVE` with `reporting_compliance_score` at 1.00, `probation_started_at`, `status_reason` and `last_status_action_at` all null, and its derived counters recomputed: `monitored_system_count` as the number of systems it monitors in `SUBMITTED`, `PERMITTED` or `UNPERMITTED`, `dispatch_request_count_lifetime` as the number of dispatch requests it has lodged across every status, and `consecutive_flagged_report_count` as the length of the most-recent unbroken run of its reports ending in `VARIANCE_FLAGGED`, taken in order of `period_start` ascending and settling ties by creation order. That derivation from report history is a registration-time act and happens nowhere else. Thereafter the field is a stateful counter rather than a derived one: only a review outcome moves it — reset to 0 by an `ACCEPT`, raised by 1 by a `FLAG_VARIANCE` — together with the `PROBATION → ACTIVE` hop of the status ladder, which also resets it to 0. Nothing else disturbs it: not a later correction of a flagged report, not an administrative status action, and not any read, roll-up or nightly pass, each of which refreshes the score and the two volume counters but leaves the flagged run precisely where the last review left it. A company may be read individually, showing its recomputed score and counters, or listed with filters on `status` and `license_number`, paged by `page` and `per_page`.

**Filing a period.** A company operator files a report naming its own `monitoring_company_id`, the closed-interval `period_start` and `period_end` where the end must be strictly after the start, and the three claimed figures: `reported_dispatch_count`, `reported_cancellation_count` and `ecv_attempt_count`, each at least 0. Periods may not overlap: the closed interval must not overlap any existing report for the same company in `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `VARIANCE_FLAGGED` or `CORRECTED`, else `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. The filer must be the company itself, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. That identity check is the whole of the coherence requirement here: the report names its own `monitoring_company_id` and no system of its own, so once the filer and the named company match there is nothing left for §2.10 position 22 to catch, and a filing does not return `ERR_COMPANY_NOT_SYSTEM_MONITOR`.

The claim is then checked against the city's own records, and the derived metrics are stored on the row. `system_dispatch_count` is the number of dispatch requests attributed to that company whose `occurred_at` falls inside the closed period interval and whose status is not `CANCELLED` — a `REFUSED` request still counts, because the company did ask for dispatch even though the city declined, and only a cancellation removes the ask. `variance_count` is the absolute difference between `reported_dispatch_count` and `system_dispatch_count`, so over-reporting and under-reporting are penalised identically. `variance_pct` is that count divided by the greater of 1 and `system_dispatch_count`, at four decimal places. `period_accuracy` is 1 minus the lesser of 1 and `variance_pct`, at four decimal places, so accuracy floors at zero however wild the claim. `period_ecv_rate` is the lesser of 1 and `ecv_attempt_count` divided by the greater of 1 and `reported_dispatch_count`, at four decimal places, so extra verification attempts earn no credit above a rate of 1. `period_score` is 0.6 times `period_accuracy` plus 0.4 times `period_ecv_rate`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Every rounding here is half up; the intermediate metrics carry four decimals and only the period score is cut to two. The report is created in `SUBMITTED` with `correction_count` 0 and `reviewed_at`, `reviewed_by_user_id` and `variance_notes` null.

**Review.** Program staff rule on a submitted report with a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `FLAG_VARIANCE` plus optional `variance_notes`. Acceptance moves the report to the terminal `ACCEPTED`, stamping `reviewed_at` and `reviewed_by_user_id`, admitting the `period_score` into the company's score set, and resetting `consecutive_flagged_report_count`. Flagging requires an actual discrepancy — `variance_pct` must be strictly above 0.1000, else `ERR_NO_REPORTABLE_VARIANCE` — and moves the report to `VARIANCE_FLAGGED`, storing the `variance_notes` and incrementing `consecutive_flagged_report_count`. A flagged period still enters the score set: being caught does not erase the period, it prices it.

**Correction, bounded.** The company may correct a flagged report by resupplying `reported_dispatch_count`, `reported_cancellation_count` and `ecv_attempt_count`. Only the company itself may correct, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; at least one of the resupplied metrics must differ from the stored value, else `ERR_NO_REPORTABLE_VARIANCE`; and `correction_count` must be strictly below 2 as evaluated **before** the increment, else `ERR_CORRECTION_LIMIT_REACHED` — so a report may be corrected twice and no more. The correction overwrites the reported counts, increments `correction_count`, and recomputes `variance_count`, `variance_pct`, `period_accuracy`, `period_ecv_rate` and `period_score` from scratch. The row then cycles onward inside the same request: `VARIANCE_FLAGGED → CORRECTED`, and immediately `CORRECTED → SUBMITTED` with `reviewed_at`, `reviewed_by_user_id` and `variance_notes` cleared, so the reviewer must rule again on the corrected figures. Because only `ACCEPTED` and `VARIANCE_FLAGGED` reports enter the score set, a report under correction temporarily leaves the set and re-enters when it is re-reviewed — which can move the company's score in either direction before any new period is ever filed.

**Scoring the company.** The score set is the company's reports in `ACCEPTED` or `VARIANCE_FLAGGED`, ordered by `period_start` ascending and then `created_at` ascending, keeping the last 6 entries of that ordering; `SUBMITTED` and `CORRECTED` rows are excluded. Each entry contributes the `period_score` already stored on it. Under `VOLUME_WEIGHTED`, the weight total is the sum of `system_dispatch_count` across the set, and the score is the sum of each `period_score` times its own `system_dispatch_count` divided by that total — falling back to the plain mean of the period scores when the weight total is zero. Under `PERIOD_EQUAL`, the score is simply the sum of the period scores divided by the number of entries. Either way the result is rounded half up to two decimal places into `reporting_compliance_score`, and an empty score set yields 1.00.

The two modes are not a cosmetic preference; they can invert the regulatory outcome on identical data. Take a period scoring 0.40 on 90 dispatches and a period scoring 1.00 on 10. Under `VOLUME_WEIGHTED` the busy bad period dominates and the score is 0.46, which is below `company_suspension_score_threshold` and costs the company its dispatch privilege. Under `PERIOD_EQUAL` the same two reports average to 0.70, exactly at `company_probation_score_threshold` and therefore — the comparison being inclusive at the top — leaving the company `ACTIVE`. Same reports, opposite consequence.

**Status consequences.** Each review recomputes the score and then drives the company's status. From `ACTIVE`, a recomputed score below `company_probation_score_threshold` but at or above `company_suspension_score_threshold` moves the company to `PROBATION`, stamping `probation_started_at`, `status_reason` and `last_status_action_at`; dispatch requests are still accepted on probation. From `PROBATION`, a score back at or above the probation threshold returns the company to `ACTIVE`, clearing `probation_started_at` and resetting `consecutive_flagged_report_count` to 0 — the ladder is cyclic in both directions. From `PROBATION`, a score below `company_suspension_score_threshold`, or a `consecutive_flagged_report_count` of 3 or more, moves the company to `SUSPENDED`. A score that low can also skip probation entirely and carry an `ACTIVE` company straight to `SUSPENDED`, in which case `probation_started_at` is set to the logical clock so that reinstatement eligibility still has an anchor to measure from.

Suspension has a precise scope. Every subsequent dispatch request for that company's systems is rejected with `ERR_COMPANY_DISPATCH_PRIVILEGE_SUSPENDED`, and so is every incident report it lodges; but requests already in `VALIDATED` may still be dispatched, and requests sitting in `SUBMITTED` may only be refused. Work already in flight is not stranded, and no new work is admitted.

An administrator may also act by hand, supplying an `action` of `SUSPEND`, `REINSTATE` or `REVOKE` with a `status_reason`. `SUSPEND` is available from `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION` and carries the same dispatch consequences as the automatic suspension, stamping `probation_started_at` when the company was still `ACTIVE`. `REINSTATE` returns a `SUSPENDED` company to `PROBATION` — never straight to `ACTIVE` — and must be earned: two consecutive accepted periods scoring at or above the 0.70 good-standing threshold, whose `period_end` falls after the `probation_started_at` anchor, else `ERR_COMPANY_REINSTATEMENT_NOT_EARNED`. `REVOKE` is available from `SUSPENDED` only and is terminal: every system the company monitors keeps its permit, but its dispatch submissions are permanently rejected, and the `license_number` is released for reuse. Every status action stamps `last_status_action_at` at the logical clock and records the `status_reason`.

A report may be read individually, exposing its computed variance and `period_score`, or listed with filters on `monitoring_company_id`, `status` and a `period_from` / `period_to` window, paged by `page` and `per_page`.
