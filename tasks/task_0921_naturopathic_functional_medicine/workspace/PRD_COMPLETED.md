# Naturopathic & Functional-Medicine Practice API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An integrative naturopathic practice does not look like a conventional clinic from the billing desk backwards. Patients arrive with a long history that has to be captured section by section before anyone is willing to book them; laboratory work is ordered from a small catalogue of functional panels whose specimens are only valid inside narrow biological windows; treatment is delivered as a supplement protocol that is titrated up and down against a symptom score rather than prescribed once; the dispensary that fills those protocols is an inventory business with lots, expiry dates and acquisition costs sitting inside a clinical business; and almost none of it is billed to a payer. Members pay a monthly fee that buys a fixed pool of visits and laboratory credits, everything outside that pool is cash, and the only thing an insurer ever sees is a superbill the patient submits themselves.

This document specifies the REST API that runs that practice end to end. The service owns fifteen resource families — practitioners, patients, intake sections, memberships, lab orders and their analyte results, protocols and their supplement lines, dispensary products and lots, dispenses, encounters, message threads, superbills and referrals — and every one of them is addressed at the root of the API; there is no single owning aggregate and no deep nesting of collections under a parent. What binds them together is not a URL hierarchy but a dense web of clinical safety rules: a scope-of-practice review or an unacknowledged red-flag laboratory result freezes an entire family of clinical writes across the whole chart, a supplement the patient is currently taking can invalidate a panel that has not been drawn yet, and stopping a protocol can reach backwards through the dispensary to reverse money and put units back on a shelf.

Two design themes recur and should be read as deliberate rather than accidental. First, **the same question is frequently answered by two different formulas**, selected by a condition the reader has to evaluate: intake completeness is scored one way when it gates a booking and another way when it gates a protocol; an analyte is banded once against the laboratory's reference range and again against the practice's tighter functional range; the next titration review is anchored on the decision instant for some decisions and on the previously scheduled instant for others. Second, **interval geometry is not uniform**. Some windows in this domain are closed and some are half-open, and two of them sit inside a single calculation on a single analyte. Nothing may be assumed by habit; every boundary is stated explicitly where it is used, and an implementation that applies one convention globally will be wrong roughly half the time.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API — without exception, including reads — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` names the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is missing or present but empty, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401 before anything else about the request is examined. If both headers are present but the supplied `X-User-Role` is not among the roles the addressed endpoint declares, the request is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` and HTTP 403.

Seven roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `PracticeAdmin`, `NaturopathicDoctor`, `HealthCoach`, `DispensaryTech`, `ClinicalDirector`, `BillingSpecialist`, and `Patient`.

- A `PracticeAdmin` is the front-office and configuration operator. They register practitioners and patients, maintain the dispensary catalogue, own the entire membership lifecycle, and drive the server's logical clock.
- A `NaturopathicDoctor` is the licensed clinician of record. This is the only role permitted to order laboratory panels, post and interpret results, author and advance protocols, take titration decisions, convert a message thread into an e-visit, and raise a routine referral.
- A `HealthCoach` is a non-prescribing member of the clinical team. Coaches help patients complete intake, collect specimens, run and complete visits, and answer messages, but may never order a panel or touch a protocol.
- A `DispensaryTech` receives lots into stock and fills protocol lines, and may authorise the reversal of a dispense subject to the segregation rule below.
- A `ClinicalDirector` supervises. Directors open and close scope-of-practice reviews and authorise high-value dispensing reversals.
- A `BillingSpecialist` handles the cash-practice billing surface: drafting, issuing, voiding, reissuing, appealing and closing the superbills that patients self-submit to their payers.
- A `Patient` is the member themselves. Patients complete and attest their own intake, book and cancel their own visits, open message threads, report adverse reactions, and report payer denials against their own superbills.

Identity resolution differs by role and this matters for every ownership check in the system. When `X-User-Role` is `NaturopathicDoctor`, `HealthCoach`, `DispensaryTech`, or `ClinicalDirector`, the `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Practitioner`; ownership and segregation checks on any resource that references a practitioner compare that resource's practitioner foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` directly. When `X-User-Role` is `Patient`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Patient`, and ownership checks compare the addressed resource's `patient_id` to it. When `X-User-Role` is `PracticeAdmin` or `BillingSpecialist`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it in an audit field but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Patient ownership is enforced as its own adjudication step. Whenever the acting role is `Patient`, the `X-User-ID` must equal the `patient_id` that owns the addressed resource — the intake section's patient, the message thread's patient, the encounter's patient, the superbill's patient, or, for an adverse-reaction report, the patient behind the protocol that owns the addressed protocol line. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` and HTTP 403. This check runs on every endpoint whose declared role list includes `Patient`, and only on those — and, among those, only where the addressed resource actually has a determinable patient owner. `GET /practitioners/{id}` is the one endpoint that lists `Patient` and owns no patient at all, so the check is a no-op there and any patient may read any practitioner record.

Two operations additionally enforce **segregation of duties**, meaning the actor may not be both parties to the same decision. A `ClinicalDirector` closing a scope-of-practice review must not be the patient's `primary_practitioner_id`. And when a dispense's `total_price_cents` is at or above the reversal authorisation threshold of 20000 cents, the actor reversing it must not be the practitioner recorded in that dispense's `dispensed_by_practitioner_id`. Either violation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403. The identity that authorised a reversal is retained on the dispense as `reversal_authorized_by_user_id`, and the identity that opened a scope review is retained on the patient as `scope_review_opened_by_user_id`; both are audit strings copied from `X-User-ID`, not navigable foreign keys.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field. Every field a resource declares is always present in that projection: a field that has no value is rendered as an explicit `null` rather than omitted, and an array-typed field with nothing in it is rendered as `[]`.
- A request aimed at a path this API does not define, or at a path with a method it does not define, is answered with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` and HTTP 404 — a method mismatch is reported as a 404, never a 405. A request body that is missing, empty, unparseable or not a JSON object is treated as an empty object, so its required fields simply fail at the payload-shape rung of §2.6.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when absent, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 when absent and its minimum accepted value is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` that is not an integer or falls outside the range from 1 to 100 inclusive, or an `offset` that is not an integer or is negative, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` and HTTP 400. "Integer" here means a bare decimal integer string: surrounding whitespace is tolerated, but a fractional part, an explicit plus sign or any other decoration is not an integer for this purpose. An `offset` that lands past the last matching row is not an error at all — the response is a 200 carrying an empty `data` array, with `total_count` still reporting the full match count and `has_more` false.

List results are always ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest row first — regardless of the resource family. This applies even where a newest-first ordering would read more naturally to a clinician, such as a patient's successive membership cycles; those are returned oldest cycle first like everything else. Because the logical clock only moves when something moves it, whole batches of rows routinely share one `created_at`; ties are broken by the order in which the rows were minted — insertion order — and the row `id`, being a random UUID, never participates in the ordering of any list.

Successful list responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the `meta` object, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filter before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

Every documented list filter is applied as a raw, case-sensitive string comparison against the stored field. Filter values are never validated against the enum they belong to: a `?status=BOGUS` or a `?clinical_status=intake` is answered with a perfectly ordinary 200 carrying an empty page and a `total_count` of 0, never with `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` or `ERR_VALIDATION`. A query parameter an endpoint does not document is ignored outright. The single boolean filter in the API, `is_quarantined` on the lot list, reads `true`, `1` or `yes` in any letter case as true and treats every other value — including a misspelling — as false.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource is minted with an `id` that is a server-generated UUID carrying no type prefix of any kind, and a `created_at` timestamp. Almost every resource additionally carries an `updated_at` timestamp and an integer `version` that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row; `updated_at` is refreshed at the same moment. There is exactly one exception: a `LabResult` is an immutable observation and carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all, and neither may be invented for it.

All timestamps this API emits are ISO 8601 UTC strings, and their fractional-second rendering follows one rule with three branches rather than a single fixed width. An instant supplied by the caller — an encounter's `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end`, and the `to_iso8601` handed to the clock — is stored and echoed back **verbatim**: it is never re-formatted, never normalised, and never gains or loses fractional digits. Every instant the server derives from the logical clock — `created_at`, `updated_at`, `submitted_at`, `attested_at`, `expires_at`, `collected_at`, `issued_at`, `activated_at`, `dispensed_at`, `reversed_at`, `completed_at`, `cancelled_at`, `paused_at`, `opened_at`, `sla_due_at`, `first_clinical_reply_at`, `last_decision_at`, `next_review_due_at`, `acknowledged_at`, `report_received_at` and `closed_at` — is rendered in the fractional-second style of the string that most recently *set* the clock. Since the clock starts at `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`, which carries no fractional part, an untouched clock stamps `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z` and never `2026-05-01T00:00:00.000Z`; a clock last set from `...T12:00:00.123456Z` stamps six digits; and a clock last set from an offset-bearing string is converted to UTC and, that string having no fractional part, stamps none. Only `meta.api_request_timestamp` is fixed: it always carries exactly three fractional digits whatever set the clock. Date-only fields — `cycle_start_date`, `cycle_end_date`, `requested_collection_date`, `washout_clearance_date`, `last_dose_date`, `expiry_date` and `due_by_date` — are plain calendar dates in the year-month-day form with no time component, and every rule that compares them compares whole calendar days.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, not the host's wall clock. Before any request sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time under any circumstances. A `PracticeAdmin` moves it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the resulting `current_utc`, which is that supplied string echoed back exactly as it was written — a non-UTC offset included — even though the clock itself advances to the equivalent UTC instant. Every expiry, wash-out, cancellation window, service-level target, lot-expiry and occupancy rule in this specification is evaluated against this clock and nothing else.

Three derived facts are **materialised lazily** — that is, they are brought up to date at the moment something reads them rather than by a background sweep. An `IntakeSection` in `ATTESTED` whose `expires_at` has been reached or passed becomes `EXPIRED` on the next read of that section, of its owning patient, of the patient's intake-section list, or of the patient's gate view, and both of the patient's intake scores are recomputed at that moment. Those four reads are where materialisation is guaranteed, not the only places it happens: the same expiry-then-rescore pass also runs immediately before the intake gate on encounter booking and before the intake gate on protocol issue, and a section's own expiry is materialised before its attestation is state-checked, so neither gate can ever be satisfied by a stale score. Materialisation is bookkeeping rather than a mutation for versioning purposes — a section driven to `EXPIRED` and a patient whose scores moved each have their `updated_at` refreshed while their `version` stays exactly where it was, so a plain `GET` can advance an `updated_at` but never a `version`. A `ProductLot`'s `is_expired` flag is refreshed against the clock whenever the lot is read. A `MessageThread`'s `sla_breached` flag is refreshed whenever the thread is read, becoming true once the clock has strictly passed `sla_due_at` with no clinical reply on record, and settling permanently at false once a first clinical reply landed at or before that instant.

Resource ids embedded in a request path are resolved strictly left to right, so a request naming two ids answers about the leftmost unresolvable one first and never leaks the existence or absence of the second.

### 2.5. The Global Clinical Write Gate

The safety centrepiece of this practice is a single per-patient switch called the **clinical write gate**, exposed on the patient as `clinical_write_gate` with exactly three values: `OPEN`, `FROZEN_SCOPE_REVIEW`, and `FROZEN_RED_FLAG`. A new patient starts `OPEN`. The gate leaves `OPEN` for `FROZEN_SCOPE_REVIEW` when a `ClinicalDirector` opens a scope-of-practice review, and for `FROZEN_RED_FLAG` when a critical analyte result raises an automatic red-flag referral that nobody has acknowledged yet. When both conditions are live at once, `FROZEN_SCOPE_REVIEW` takes precedence and is the value reported.

While the gate is anything other than `OPEN`, exactly eight endpoint families are frozen and answer `ERR_CLINICAL_WRITE_FROZEN` with HTTP 409:

| Frozen while the gate is not `OPEN` |
| --- |
| `POST /lab-orders` |
| `POST /lab-orders/{id}/collect` |
| `POST /protocols` |
| `POST /protocols/{id}/items` |
| `POST /protocols/{id}/issue` |
| `POST /protocols/{id}/activate` |
| `POST /protocol-items/{id}/titration-decisions` |
| `POST /dispenses` |

Everything else remains available, and that availability is deliberate: these are the ways out of a freeze and the safety valves that must never be blocked by it. The following are **never** gated, no matter what `clinical_write_gate` says — every `GET` in the entire API; `POST /patients`; `POST /patients/{id}/intake-sections`; `POST /intake-sections/{id}/attest`; `POST /patients/{id}/scope-reviews` and `POST /patients/{id}/scope-reviews/close`; every `/memberships` endpoint; `POST /lab-orders/{id}/results`; `POST /lab-orders/{id}/interpret`; `POST /lab-orders/{id}/release-washout-hold`; `POST /lab-orders/{id}/cancel`; `POST /protocols/{id}/discontinue`; `POST /protocols/{id}/complete`; `POST /protocol-items/{id}/adverse-reactions`; `POST /dispenses/{id}/reverse`; every `/encounters` endpoint; every `/message-threads` endpoint; every `/superbills` endpoint; every `/referrals` endpoint; every `/dispensary-products` and `/product-lots` endpoint; and `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`.

The gate is checked unusually early — earlier than payload shape and earlier than enum membership. On a gated endpoint, a request that is simultaneously malformed and aimed at a frozen patient is answered with `ERR_CLINICAL_WRITE_FROZEN`, not with a payload complaint: the server declines to tell a caller what is wrong with a payload it is not going to consider. On every non-gated endpoint the ladder behaves in the conventional way, with payload problems surfacing normally. Three of the eight reach the implicated patient through the request body rather than the path — `POST /lab-orders` and `POST /protocols` through `patient_id`, and `POST /dispenses` through the protocol behind `protocol_item_id` — and when that body field is missing altogether the gate simply has no patient to consult, cannot fire, and the request falls through to the payload-shape rung in the ordinary way.

Freezing and unfreezing propagate down the chart. Whenever the gate leaves `OPEN`, every `Protocol` of that patient in status `ACTIVE` has `advancement_blocked` set to true, and every `ProtocolItem` under those protocols in status `ACTIVE` or `HELD` has `titration_locked` set to true. Whenever the gate returns to `OPEN`, the same two flags are cleared back to false across the same rows.

### 2.6. The Order of Refusal

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. The checks form a single ordered ladder; the lowest-numbered violated rung wins and no later rung is consulted. Implementations must reproduce this ordering exactly, because it is frequently the only thing distinguishing two plausible responses to the same malformed request.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are present and non-empty on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role permission.** The supplied `X-User-Role` appears in the addressed endpoint's declared role list, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Exactly one endpoint refines this rung with an action-sensitive clause: on `POST /superbills/{id}/transitions` an acting `Patient` who supplies a declared `action` other than `REPORT_DENIAL` is refused here, at rung 2, before the superbill id in the path is even resolved — so a patient attempting to `VOID` a superbill that does not exist hears about their role, not about the missing row. A missing or undeclared `action` is not judged here and falls through to rungs 11 and 12.
3. **Path existence.** Every id embedded in the request path resolves to a live row, checked strictly left to right, on every endpoint carrying a path parameter. The code is the entity-specific one where the domain declares one: `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRACTITIONER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROTOCOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LAB_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENCOUNTER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_FOUND`; an unresolvable intake-section, protocol-item, dispensary-product, product-lot, dispense, message-thread, superbill or referral id yields `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. All of these carry HTTP 404.
4. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign key that is *present* in the request body resolves to a live row, checked in the order the fields are declared in that endpoint's input list. A foreign key that is absent from the body is not judged here at all — it falls through to the required-field rung at position 11. A body `patient_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, a `practitioner_id` or a `primary_practitioner_id` yields `ERR_PRACTITIONER_NOT_FOUND`, an `encounter_id` yields `ERR_ENCOUNTER_NOT_FOUND`, and a `supersedes_protocol_id` yields `ERR_PROTOCOL_NOT_FOUND`; `product_id`, `product_lot_id` and `protocol_item_id` have no entity-specific code and yield `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`. All carry HTTP 404. This rung applies to the ten body-bearing creates: patient registration, membership opening, lab-order creation, protocol drafting, protocol-item addition, dispense creation, encounter booking, message-thread opening, superbill drafting and referral raising.
5. **Patient ownership.** On every endpoint whose role list includes `Patient`, an acting `Patient` must own the addressed resource — otherwise `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` (403).
6. **Global clinical write gate.** On the eight gated endpoints enumerated in §2.5, the implicated patient's `clinical_write_gate` must be `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_CLINICAL_WRITE_FROZEN` (409). This rung pre-empts every rung below it, payload shape and enum membership included.
7. **Segregation of duties.** On scope-review closure and on dispense reversal, the actor may not be both parties — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
8. **Own-state validity.** The addressed resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, on the relevant axis, on every state-transition endpoint — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409).
9. **Dependent-state validity.** Cross-entity state preconditions hold — otherwise `ERR_DEPENDENT_STATE_INVALID` (409). This rung guards four endpoints: dispense creation (which requires the protocol item's owning protocol to be `ACTIVE` and the item itself to be `ACTIVE` or `HELD`), titration decisions (which require the owning protocol to be `ACTIVE` and the line's `titration_locked` to be false), superbill transitions (where issuing requires the referenced encounter's `scheduling_status` to be `COMPLETED`), and thread-to-e-visit conversion (which requires the thread's `evisit_conversion_eligible` to be true, which is itself false whenever the patient holds an active membership on a tier that includes messaging). Encounter completion carries no cross-entity precondition at all; it is guarded only by its own `SCHEDULED` state at rung 8. A membership pause likewise requires the membership to be `ACTIVE`, but that is an own-state check at rung 8 answering `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; and a wash-out release, which requires either the interfering supplement lines to have moved on or the clock to have reached the clearance date, is judged much lower down at rung 20 with its own code.
10. **Optimistic concurrency.** On the three endpoints that declare an expected-version input, a *supplied* value must equal the parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). A request that omits the field entirely is not judged here; it falls through to the required-field rung below, which is what keeps that rung reachable on these three endpoints. Strictness here is by type as well as by value: a supplied value that is not a JSON integer — a numeric string such as `"3"`, a float, a boolean — is a mismatch, and so is a supplied value whose parent cannot be resolved at all because the body foreign key naming that parent (`patient_id` on a protocol draft, `protocol_item_id` on a dispense) is itself absent. Both answer `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at this rung rather than falling through to a payload complaint.
11. **Payload shape.** Every required input field for the endpoint is present, non-null and of the declared type — and an empty string is not a valid string anywhere in this API, so `""` fails this rung exactly as a missing field does, on optional fields as well as required ones; integers such as `quantity`, `duration_minutes` and `current_symptom_score` are positive where the domain demands it; and `scheduled_end` is strictly after `scheduled_start`. The two symptom figures, `symptom_score_baseline` on a protocol draft and `current_symptom_score` on a titration decision, are additionally bounded to the closed 0-to-40 scale, and a value outside it fails here rather than at the enum rung. Any failure yields `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). This rung applies to all write endpoints.
12. **Enum membership.** Every supplied enum-typed field carries a declared member — `credential`, `section_type`, `tier`, `panel_code`, `funding_source`, `modality`, `outcome`, `action`, `severity` and the pricing hints among them. A value outside the declared set yields `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (422). This rung applies to all write endpoints carrying enum inputs.
13. **Pagination shape.** On every list endpoint, `limit` is an integer from 1 to 100 inclusive and `offset` is an integer of at least 0 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).
14. **Intake completeness.** Booking an encounter and issuing a protocol each require their respective intake score — otherwise `ERR_INTAKE_INCOMPLETE` (422).
15. **Telehealth licensure.** On encounter booking and on thread-to-e-visit conversion, the patient's `state_code` must appear in the rendering practitioner's `licensed_state_codes` — otherwise `ERR_STATE_NOT_LICENSED` (403).
16. **Modality consent.** On the same two endpoints, a telehealth or e-visit modality requires the patient's `telehealth_consent` to be true — otherwise `ERR_MODALITY_CONSENT_MISSING` (422). Because consent is checked strictly after licensure, a patient who is both out of state and unconsented is told about licensure.
17. **Severe contraindication.** On protocol issue, no line's product may carry a `SEVERE` interaction against a medication class the patient reports — otherwise `ERR_CONTRAINDICATION_SEVERE` (422).
18. **Unacknowledged red flag.** On lab-order interpretation, an order in `CRITICAL_ESCALATED` may not be interpreted while its automatic red-flag referral is still `SENT`, `DECLINED` or `ABANDONED` — otherwise `ERR_RED_FLAG_UNACKNOWLEDGED` (409). Interpretation sits deliberately outside the gated family precisely so that this rung is reachable at the moment the gate is frozen.
19. **Collection window.** On specimen collection, the panel's fasting attestation and cycle-day window must be satisfied — otherwise `ERR_COLLECTION_WINDOW_INVALID` (422).
20. **Wash-out elapsed.** On wash-out release, the clearance date must have been reached or every interfering line must have stopped — otherwise `ERR_WASHOUT_NOT_ELAPSED` (409).
21. **Practitioner occupancy.** On encounter booking, the requested window must not collide with another window of the same practitioner — otherwise `ERR_PRACTITIONER_DOUBLE_BOOKED` (409).
22. **Membership cycle overlap.** On membership opening, the requested cycle must not touch or overlap a live cycle of the same patient — otherwise `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_CYCLE_OVERLAP` (409).
23. **Duplicate panel.** On lab-order creation, the patient must not already hold an open order for the same panel — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_PANEL_ORDER` (409).
24. **Named lot usable.** On dispense creation with an explicit lot, that lot must be neither quarantined nor expired — otherwise `ERR_LOT_EXPIRED` (422).
25. **Named lot stocked.** On dispense creation with an explicit lot, that lot's `quantity_on_hand` must be at least the requested `quantity` — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_STOCK` (409).
26. **Selector pool non-empty.** On dispense creation without an explicit lot, the candidate pool must contain at least one usable lot holding the requested quantity — otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_LOT` (409).
27. **Entitlement availability.** On lab-order creation funded by membership credit, the patient must hold an active membership with enough remaining credits — otherwise `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_EXHAUSTED` (402).
28. **Superbill billability.** On superbill issue, the document must actually document cash — otherwise `ERR_SUPERBILL_NOT_BILLABLE` (422).

Note the two consequences of this ordering that are easy to get wrong. Because the gate sits at rung 6 while payload shape sits at rung 11, a frozen patient masks payload errors on gated endpoints. And because optimistic-concurrency checking sits at rung 10, an expected-version value that is present but wrong is reported as a stale version even when the same payload is also missing some other required field.

### 2.7. Interval Geometry: What Touching Means, Case by Case

Interval boundaries in this domain are deliberately **not** uniform, and applying one habit everywhere will produce wrong answers. Each site below states its own geometry, and these statements govern:

- **Practitioner occupancy on an encounter is half-open.** The occupied interval runs from `scheduled_start` inclusive up to `scheduled_end` exclusive. Two encounters that merely touch — one ending at exactly the instant the next begins — do **not** collide.
- **Membership billing cycles on a patient are closed.** The occupied interval runs from `cycle_start_date` through `cycle_end_date`, both endpoints included. Two cycles that merely touch — one ending on the very day the next starts — **do** collide. This is the opposite convention from encounter windows, on purpose.
- **The supplement wash-out bound is closed at the bottom.** A collection dated exactly on the computed `washout_clearance_date` satisfies the wash-out. Only a strictly earlier requested collection date fails it.
- **The menstrual cycle-day window for a hormone panel is closed at both ends.** For a window running from cycle day 19 to cycle day 22, both 19 and 22 qualify, while 18 and 23 do not.
- **The two analyte bands on one and the same result use opposite geometries at the top.** The laboratory's standard reference range is closed at both ends, so a value exactly equal to `standard_high` is `IN_RANGE`. The practice's functional range is closed at the bottom but half-open at the top, so a value exactly equal to `optimal_high` is already `ABOVE_OPTIMAL`. A single number can therefore be conventionally in range and functionally above optimal at the same time, and that is the intended clinical reading.
- **The cancellation refund window is inclusive at its boundary.** A cancellation landing exactly 24 hours before `scheduled_start` still refunds.
- **The extended-visit threshold is inclusive.** A visit lasting exactly the threshold number of minutes buys no extended block; one minute more already buys a whole one.
- **Lot expiry is inclusive of the expiry date.** A lot whose `expiry_date` equals the logical clock's date is already expired and unusable.
- **The e-visit conversion thresholds are inclusive.** Exactly four patient messages, or exactly fifteen cumulative clinical minutes, already qualifies.
- **The reversal authorisation threshold is inclusive.** A dispense whose `total_price_cents` is exactly the threshold value already requires a second pair of hands.
- **The fourteen-day cascade reversal window is inclusive at its far end.** A dispense whose `dispensed_at` sits exactly fourteen days behind the logical clock is still reversed by a protocol-stop or adverse-reaction cascade; only a dispense older than that is left alone. A dispense stamped at an instant the clock has not yet reached is inside the window as well.
- **Membership proration counts elapsed days half-open.** The count runs from `cycle_start_date` inclusive up to the current logical date exclusive, so the day the cycle opened counts as day zero.

### 2.8. Money, Rounding and the Patient Cash Ledger

All monetary amounts in this API are integers denominated in whole cents. There are no decimal currency values anywhere in the request or response surface. Wherever a monetary computation produces a fraction of a cent — a percentage discount, a prorated fraction of a monthly fee — the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent, meaning a residue of exactly half a cent rounds away from zero rather than to the nearest even value.

Where rounding happens relative to multiplication is pinned and is not an implementation detail. For dispensary pricing, the discounted price is rounded to a whole cent **per unit**, and only then multiplied by the dispensed quantity. Multiplying the raw discounted amount by the quantity and rounding the line total once is explicitly not the rule; the two procedures disagree whenever the retail price multiplied by the discount rate leaves a fractional cent.

Non-monetary rounding is stated at each site and differs by quantity. Intake section weights are halved with the fraction discarded — the result is floored. Extended-visit blocks are counted by rounding the excess time up to a whole block. Prorated entitlement grants are rounded up to whole units. Titrated doses are rounded half up to a whole milligram. A paused membership's elapsed days are rounded up to the next whole day whenever any partial day has elapsed.

Every patient carries two lifetime money counters that only ever grow. `cash_charged_total_cents` accumulates everything the practice has charged the patient in cash across encounters, e-visits, cash-funded laboratory panels, dispensed supplements and positive membership proration. `cash_refunded_total_cents` accumulates everything given back — cancelled cash lab orders, reversed dispenses and negative membership proration. Reversals never decrease the charged counter; they increase the refunded counter instead, so both counters are monotonic and the patient's net cash position at any instant is the difference between them.

### 2.9. Conservation Invariants

Four quantities in this system must survive every forward action and its compensating action unchanged. Each is an identity that can be checked from the API surface at any instant, and each is stated here once so that no individual workflow narrative has to restate it.

**Membership visit units.** For any membership, `visits_granted` always equals `visits_consumed` plus `visits_remaining` plus `visits_forfeited`. Booking an encounter reserves a unit by increasing `visits_consumed` by one and decreasing `visits_remaining` by one. Cancelling at least 24 hours ahead returns it, decreasing `visits_consumed` by one and increasing `visits_remaining` by one; cancelling later moves it instead, decreasing `visits_consumed` by one and increasing `visits_forfeited` by one. Rolling the cycle moves whatever is left in `visits_remaining` into `visits_forfeited` while adding a fresh grant. The same unit therefore travels three different routes and the identity holds after every one of them.

**Membership laboratory-credit units.** For any membership, `lab_credits_granted` always equals `lab_credits_consumed` plus `lab_credits_remaining` plus `lab_credits_forfeited`. Ordering a panel funded by membership credit consumes exactly the panel's credit cost; cancelling that order returns exactly the same number of credits, and `lab_credits_granted` is untouched on both legs.

**Dispensary lot units.** For any product lot, `quantity_initial` always equals `quantity_on_hand` plus the total `quantity` across every dispense drawn from that lot whose status is still `DISPENSED`. Filling a line decrements the on-hand figure; reversing a dispense — whether requested directly or driven by a protocol-stop cascade — returns the units and restores the identity. `quantity_initial` is never mutated after the lot is received.

**Patient cash ledger.** For any patient, `cash_charged_total_cents` equals the sum of `cash_charged_cents` across that patient's encounters, plus the sum of `total_price_cents` across that patient's dispenses in status `DISPENSED` or `REVERSED`, plus the sum of `price_cents` across that patient's cash-funded lab orders, plus every positive `last_proration_net_charge_cents` applied to that patient's memberships. Because a reversal leaves the charged figure alone and grows `cash_refunded_total_cents` instead, a reversed dispense still contributes to the charged total — that is what makes the identity hold across reversals.

### 2.10. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Three write endpoints are protected against a client acting on a stale view of a parent record. In each case the request must carry an expected-version field naming the client's last observed `version` of the parent, the field is a required input rather than an optional one, and a value that does not equal the parent's current `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412.

- Drafting a protocol requires `expected_patient_version` matching the target `Patient`'s current `version`. A revision may legitimately supersede whatever protocol the patient currently has, so no state precondition distinguishes a well-informed author from one working off a chart snapshot that has since moved; the patient version is the only thing that does.
- Creating a dispense requires `expected_protocol_version` matching the owning `Protocol`'s current `version`. Repeat fills of the same line are perfectly legitimate while stock lasts, so nothing else catches a dispenser who has not seen the titration decision or the line discontinuation that moved the protocol on.
- Changing a membership tier requires `expected_membership_version` matching that `Membership`'s current `version`. Consecutive tier changes are legitimate and each one re-prorates from the counters it finds, so a stale author would otherwise silently re-prorate against entitlement figures a prior change or an encounter reservation has already rewritten.

No other endpoint in this API accepts or requires a version field, and none may be added. Where an expected-version field is required but entirely absent from the payload, the request is a payload-shape failure rather than a concurrency failure, as §2.6 rung 11 describes.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline

Several operations in this practice mutate far more than the resource they address, and the propagation is specified rather than incidental. Two cascades run five links deep in opposite directions.

The **freeze cascade** fires when a critical analyte is posted: the lab order takes on its critical flag and moves to `CRITICAL_ESCALATED`; an automatic red-flag referral is raised against the patient; the patient's `red_flag_referral_open` becomes true and the gate closes; every `ACTIVE` protocol of that patient has `advancement_blocked` set to true; and every `ACTIVE` or `HELD` line under those protocols has `titration_locked` set to true. The **unfreeze cascade** fires when that referral is acknowledged and runs the same five links in reverse: the referral records its acknowledgement instant, `red_flag_referral_open` clears, the gate is recomputed, protocol advancement unblocks, and the lines unlock. Opening and closing a scope-of-practice review drives the same lower three links.

The **stop cascade** runs when a protocol stops paying attention to money: discontinuing a protocol, or discontinuing its last remaining live line, closes the lines, then reverses recent dispensing against those lines, then restocks the source lots and recomputes product stock, then grows the patient's refunded total. Completing or superseding a protocol closes the same lines but explicitly moves no money at all — that asymmetry between the three terminal outcomes is the whole point of having three of them. A dispense reversed by a cascade is stamped `reversed_at` like any other, but it records `reversal_reason` and `reversal_authorized_by_user_id` as null and keeps its `version` unchanged, because no human authorised it and nobody addressed that row; only the direct `POST /dispenses/{id}/reverse` fills those two audit fields and increments the dispense's `version`.

Whenever a cascade writes to a row, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed. Version increments, however, are stated per operation in the narratives that follow rather than applied blanket-fashion to every touched row: the specification says exactly which entity's `version` an operation increments, and only those are incremented. Stated positively, the rule is this. An operation increments the `version` of the row it addresses — the row named in its path, or for a create the row it mints, which starts at 1 — plus any further row a narrative below explicitly names. Every other row the operation writes keeps its `version` and receives only a fresh `updated_at`: a cascade target, a membership whose entitlement counters moved, a lot restocked, a product whose `stock_on_hand` was recomputed, a patient whose cash ledger or gate moved. Two operations decline to increment even their own addressed row, and each says so where it is specified: posting an analyte result, and converting a message thread to an e-visit. And because §2.4's lazy materialisation is bookkeeping rather than a mutation, it never increments anything either.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Building the Chart: Practitioners, Patients, Intake and Scope of Practice

Before a single patient exists, the practice registers its clinical staff. A `PracticeAdmin` records each practitioner's `full_name` and their `credential`, which is one of `ND`, `HEALTH_COACH`, `DISPENSARY_TECH` or `CLINICAL_DIRECTOR`. Two further attributes drive rules elsewhere in the system: `licensed_state_codes` is the array of two-letter state codes in which this practitioner may deliver telehealth, and `npi` is the National Provider Identifier that gets printed on superbills — it may legitimately be null for staff who never render a billable service, and it defaults to null when omitted. An `is_active` flag defaults to true and is recorded as front-office intent — it is advisory only, and no endpoint consults it, so an inactive practitioner can still be named on a new booking, a thread or a protocol without complaint. Practitioner records are readable by every role in the system, and there is no update endpoint: a practitioner is registered once and thereafter only read.

Registering a patient captures `full_name`, `email`, the two-letter `state_code` of residence that drives telehealth licensure matching, and the `primary_practitioner_id` of the treating naturopathic doctor of record. Two optional fields matter enormously later: `telehealth_consent` defaults to false and gates every remote modality, and `medication_classes` is the array of prescription medication classes the patient reports, which is matched against each dispensary product's interaction profile. Every other patient attribute is server-owned at creation: `clinical_status` starts at `INTAKE`, `scope_review_status` at `NONE` with `scope_review_reason` and `scope_review_opened_by_user_id` both null, `clinical_write_gate` at `OPEN`, `red_flag_referral_open` at false, both `intake_booking_score` and `intake_protocol_score` at 0, and both `cash_charged_total_cents` and `cash_refunded_total_cents` at 0. Either a `PracticeAdmin` or a `NaturopathicDoctor` may register a patient. The patient roster can be listed and narrowed by `primary_practitioner_id` and by `clinical_status`.

A dedicated computed read of the patient's gate exists for clinicians about to attempt clinical work. It answers with the current `clinical_write_gate`, the current `scope_review_status`, the `red_flag_referral_open` flag, the `open_red_flag_referral_id` naming the referral holding the freeze when there is one — resolved as the earliest-created `RED_FLAG_AUTO` referral of that patient still sitting in `SENT`, `DECLINED` or `ABANDONED`, and null when no such referral exists — and both intake scores. It materialises intake-section expiry before answering, so the numbers it returns are never stale. Those six fields are the whole of its `data`: it is a computed view, not a patient projection, and carries no `id`, no timestamps and no `version`.

**Intake is captured section by section.** Each row of the extended history is one `IntakeSection` carrying a `patient_id`, a `section_type` that is one of `CHIEF_COMPLAINT_TIMELINE`, `SYSTEMS_REVIEW`, `DIET_LIFESTYLE`, `MEDICATIONS_SUPPLEMENTS` or `FAMILY_HISTORY`, and a `payload_summary` holding the free-text digest of what the patient supplied. A patient has at most one row per section type. Submission is open to the `Patient` themselves and to a `HealthCoach` assisting them. On submission the section's `status` is `SUBMITTED`, its `submitted_at` is stamped with the logical clock, its `attested_at` and `expires_at` are cleared to null, and its `weight` is copied from the fixed scoring table: `CHIEF_COMPLAINT_TIMELINE` is worth 25, `SYSTEMS_REVIEW` 25, `DIET_LIFESTYLE` 15, `MEDICATIONS_SUPPLEMENTS` 25 and `FAMILY_HISTORY` 10, which is a hundred points across the five. Submitting a section type that already exists for that patient does not create a second row — it overwrites the existing one and drives it back to `SUBMITTED` from wherever it was, whether that was `ATTESTED` or `EXPIRED`, clearing the attestation timestamps in both cases. A first submission mints the section at `version` 1; a re-submission returns that same row — same `id`, same `created_at` — with its `version` incremented by 1. Both of the patient's intake scores are recomputed on every submission, but submission never increments the *patient's* `version`; it refreshes the patient's `updated_at`, and only when a score actually moved.

Only the `Patient` may attest, and only their own sections — the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the section's `patient_id`. Attestation takes a section from `SUBMITTED` to `ATTESTED`, stamps `attested_at` with the logical clock, sets `expires_at` to 180 days after that instant, recomputes both intake scores, and increments the patient's `version` by 1. Attestation is a signature with an expiry date: once the logical clock reaches or passes `expires_at` the section moves `ATTESTED → EXPIRED` and both scores are recomputed with that section contributing nothing. An expired section can be re-submitted and re-attested, so the section lifecycle genuinely cycles rather than terminating. A patient's sections are listable and can be narrowed by `status`.

**The completeness score is computed two different ways from the same five rows**, and which number applies depends on what is being gated. Both variants sum a per-section contribution across the five section types, after expiry has been materialised. In the **booking variant**, an `ATTESTED` section contributes its full weight, a merely `SUBMITTED` section contributes half its weight with the fraction discarded — 25 becomes 12, 15 becomes 7, 10 becomes 5 — and an `EXPIRED` or entirely absent section contributes nothing. That sum is stored as `intake_booking_score`. In the **protocol variant** there is no partial credit whatsoever: only `ATTESTED` sections contribute, at full weight, and everything else including `SUBMITTED` contributes nothing. That sum is stored as `intake_protocol_score`. Whenever any section is merely submitted, the protocol number is strictly the smaller of the two.

Those two numbers gate two different things. Booking an encounter requires `intake_booking_score` to be at least 60, and issuing a protocol requires `intake_protocol_score` to be at least 85; both boundaries are inclusive, so a chart sitting exactly on the number passes. A chart that falls short is refused with `ERR_INTAKE_INCOMPLETE`. The practical effect is that a patient with partially completed paperwork can be seen, but cannot be put on a supplement protocol until they have personally signed off on nearly the whole history.

**The care relationship advances on its own axis.** A patient's `clinical_status` starts at `INTAKE` and moves `INTAKE → ESTABLISHED` automatically the first time one of that patient's encounters is completed, incrementing the patient's `version` by 1. It moves `ESTABLISHED → INACTIVE` when their active membership is rolled without renewal, and back `INACTIVE → ESTABLISHED` when a new membership is opened for them, so a lapsed patient who returns re-enters the roster rather than being stuck. Each of those movements increments the patient's `version` by 1.

**Scope-of-practice review is an entirely independent axis**, and a patient can be `ESTABLISHED` and `UNDER_REVIEW` at the same time. Only a `ClinicalDirector` may open one, supplying a `reason`. The review can be opened from `NONE`, from `CLEARED`, or from `RESTRICTED` — all three lead to `UNDER_REVIEW` — so a cleared patient can be re-reviewed later without any special-casing. Opening a review records the supplied text as `scope_review_reason`, records the acting `X-User-ID` as `scope_review_opened_by_user_id`, sets `clinical_write_gate` to `FROZEN_SCOPE_REVIEW`, sets `advancement_blocked` to true on every `ACTIVE` protocol of that patient, sets `titration_locked` to true on every `ACTIVE` or `HELD` line beneath those protocols, and increments the patient's `version` by 1.

Closing a review is where segregation of duties bites. The acting `ClinicalDirector`'s practitioner id must not be the patient's `primary_practitioner_id`; a director reviewing their own patient is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The closure carries an `outcome` that is either `CLEARED` or `RESTRICTED`, plus optional `notes`. A `UNDER_REVIEW → CLEARED` closure recomputes the gate: it re-opens to `OPEN` when `red_flag_referral_open` is false, but lands on `FROZEN_RED_FLAG` when a red flag is still outstanding, because clearing a scope concern does not clear a critical laboratory result. Only when the recomputed gate is genuinely `OPEN` are `advancement_blocked` cleared on every `ACTIVE` protocol and `titration_locked` cleared on every `ACTIVE` or `HELD` line. A `UNDER_REVIEW → RESTRICTED` closure is the opposite: the gate stays `FROZEN_SCOPE_REVIEW`, and protocol advancement and line titration remain blocked indefinitely. Both closures increment the patient's `version` by 1.

### 3.2. Membership Cycles, Entitlement Pools and Tier Changes

Membership is how this practice funds itself, and the catalogue is fixed at three tiers with no plan resource behind it. A `FOUNDATION` membership costs 9900 cents a month and includes 2 visits, 1 laboratory credit and a 5 percent dispensary discount, and it does **not** include messaging. A `CORE` membership costs 19900 cents a month and includes 4 visits, 2 laboratory credits, a 10 percent dispensary discount, and messaging. A `PREMIER` membership costs 34900 cents a month and includes 8 visits, 4 laboratory credits, a 20 percent dispensary discount, and messaging. Every cycle in this practice is 30 days long. The discount is carried on the membership row as `dispensary_discount_pct`, a plain JSON number rather than a string or a cents figure, so it reads back as `0.05`, `0.1` and `0.2`.

A `PracticeAdmin` opens a membership by naming the `patient_id`, the `tier` and the `cycle_start_date`. The server computes `cycle_end_date` as the start date plus 30 days minus one day, so the cycle covers exactly thirty calendar days and both endpoints belong to it. Because that interval is closed, a new cycle may not touch or overlap the cycle of any existing membership of the same patient whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED`: a cycle ending on a given day collides with one starting on that same day, and the request is refused with `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_CYCLE_OVERLAP`. On success the tier constant block is resolved onto the row — `monthly_fee_cents`, `dispensary_discount_pct` and `includes_messaging` — the `cycle_index` starts at 1, `visits_granted` and `visits_remaining` are both set to the tier's included visits, `lab_credits_granted` and `lab_credits_remaining` are both set to the tier's included laboratory credits, and `visits_consumed`, `visits_forfeited`, `lab_credits_consumed`, `lab_credits_forfeited`, `paused_days_total` and `last_proration_net_charge_cents` all start at 0 with `paused_at` null. If the patient was `INACTIVE`, opening the membership returns them to `ESTABLISHED`. A patient's memberships are listable and can be narrowed by `status`.

Pausing a membership is a `PracticeAdmin` action that takes `ACTIVE → PAUSED`, stamps `paused_at` with the logical clock, and increments the membership's `version` by 1. Entitlement counters are frozen exactly where they stand — nothing is granted, consumed or forfeited by a pause. Resuming takes `PAUSED → ACTIVE` and repays the lost time: the number of whole days from `paused_at` to the current logical clock is computed, rounded **up** to the next whole day whenever any partial day has elapsed, added to `paused_days_total`, and added to `cycle_end_date` so the cycle window slides out by exactly that many days. `paused_at` is cleared to null and the membership's `version` increments by 1.

**Changing tier mid-cycle re-prorates both the money and the entitlement, in a pinned order.** The request names the `new_tier` and carries `expected_membership_version`; a value that does not match the membership's current `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` before any arithmetic runs. There is no state precondition of any kind on a tier change: a `PAUSED` or even an `EXPIRED` membership re-prorates exactly as an `ACTIVE` one does, so long as the expected version matches. The steps then execute in this exact sequence, because the entitlement steps rewrite the very counters the money steps read:

1. Count `elapsed_days` as the whole days from `cycle_start_date` up to the current logical date, counting the start date as day zero.
2. Compute `remaining_days` as 30 minus `elapsed_days`, floored at 0 so it never goes negative. It is not capped above, so a cycle whose `cycle_start_date` is still in the future yields more than 30 remaining days and prorates above a full month's fee and grant.
3. Compute the unused credit on the old tier as the old tier's monthly fee multiplied by `remaining_days`, divided by 30, rounded half up to whole cents.
4. Compute the prorated fee for the new tier the same way: the new tier's monthly fee multiplied by `remaining_days`, divided by 30, rounded half up to whole cents.
5. Set `last_proration_net_charge_cents` to the new prorated fee minus the old unused credit. It is positive on an upgrade and negative — a credit — on a downgrade.
6. Compute the prorated visit grant as the new tier's included visits multiplied by `remaining_days`, divided by 30, rounded **up** to a whole visit.
7. Compute the prorated laboratory-credit grant the same way from the new tier's included laboratory credits, again rounded up to a whole credit.
8. Rewrite `visits_granted` by subtracting the current `visits_remaining` from it and then adding the prorated visit grant, and set `visits_remaining` to the prorated visit grant.
9. Rewrite `lab_credits_granted` by subtracting the current `lab_credits_remaining` from it and then adding the prorated credit grant, and set `lab_credits_remaining` to the prorated credit grant.
10. Re-resolve `tier`, `monthly_fee_cents`, `dispensary_discount_pct` and `includes_messaging` from the new tier's constant block.

Steps 8 and 9 remove the old remaining balance before adding the new grant, which is what leaves `visits_consumed`, `visits_forfeited`, `lab_credits_consumed` and `lab_credits_forfeited` untouched and keeps the conservation identities of §2.9 intact across a tier change. The money then lands on the patient: when `last_proration_net_charge_cents` is positive it is added to `cash_charged_total_cents`, and when it is negative its absolute value is added to `cash_refunded_total_cents`. An upgrade grows the entitlement counters and charges; a downgrade shrinks them and credits, and can leave `visits_remaining` lower than a naive tier swap would have granted. The membership's `version` increments by 1.

**Rolling the cycle is the only operation that can end a membership.** The request carries a `renew` boolean. Either way, whatever sits in `visits_remaining` is added to `visits_forfeited` and whatever sits in `lab_credits_remaining` is added to `lab_credits_forfeited` — unused entitlement never carries forward. When `renew` is true the membership stays `ACTIVE` in a self-loop: a fresh grant equal to the tier's included visits and included laboratory credits is added to `visits_granted` and `lab_credits_granted`, `visits_remaining` and `lab_credits_remaining` are set to those same tier figures, `cycle_index` increments by 1, `cycle_start_date` becomes the day after the previous `cycle_end_date`, and `cycle_end_date` is recomputed as that new start date plus 30 days minus one day. When `renew` is false, `visits_remaining` and `lab_credits_remaining` are instead set to 0, the membership moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED`, and — because the membership expired — the owning patient's `clinical_status` moves to `INACTIVE` if it was `ESTABLISHED`. `EXPIRED` is terminal for a membership; a lapsed patient is served by opening a new one. Either variant increments the membership's `version` by 1.

### 3.3. Booking, Settling and Unwinding a Visit

An `Encounter` runs on **two entirely independent axes**. The `scheduling_status` axis tracks what happened clinically and moves through `SCHEDULED`, then to one of `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` or `NO_SHOW`, all three of which are terminal on that axis. The `billing_status` axis tracks the money and moves through `CASH_PENDING`, `ENTITLEMENT_RESERVED`, `ENTITLEMENT_CONSUMED`, `CASH_CHARGED`, `SUPERBILLED`, `REFUNDED` and `FORFEITED`. The same encounter can be `COMPLETED` on the first axis while still sitting at `ENTITLEMENT_CONSUMED` on the second, waiting for a superbill it may never get. Only `REFUNDED` and `FORFEITED` absorb the billing axis; `SUPERBILLED` deliberately does not, because voiding a superbill has to be able to send the axis back so the encounter can be re-documented.

Booking is open to a `PracticeAdmin`, a `NaturopathicDoctor`, a `HealthCoach`, or the `Patient` themselves, and names the `patient_id`, the `practitioner_id` of the rendering clinician, the `modality` — one of `IN_PERSON`, `TELEHEALTH` or `EVISIT` — and the `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end` of the window. A booking runs a gauntlet of four domain checks in the ladder order of §2.6. First the patient's `intake_booking_score` must be at least 60, or the booking is refused with `ERR_INTAKE_INCOMPLETE`. Then, for a remote modality, the patient's `state_code` must appear in the rendering practitioner's `licensed_state_codes`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_STATE_NOT_LICENSED`. Then, for `TELEHEALTH` or `EVISIT`, the patient's `telehealth_consent` must be true, or the booking is refused with `ERR_MODALITY_CONSENT_MISSING` — and because licensure is checked first, a patient who is both out of state and unconsented hears about licensure, not consent.

The fourth check is **practitioner occupancy**, and its geometry is half-open. The requested window runs from `scheduled_start` inclusive to `scheduled_end` exclusive, and it may not overlap any existing window of the same practitioner whose `scheduling_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`; cancelled and no-show encounters release the slot. Because the interval is half-open, a window that begins at the exact instant another ends does not collide. There is one adjustment: when **both** the existing encounter and the requested one are `TELEHEALTH`, the existing encounter's effective end is pushed out by 10 minutes of turnaround before the comparison is made, so a remote visit needs ten clear minutes after it before the next remote visit may start. That buffer is not applied when both encounters are `IN_PERSON` and it is not applied to a mixed pair — only to telehealth-on-telehealth. An `EVISIT` encounter never occupies the practitioner at all and is invisible to this check in both directions. A collision is refused with `ERR_PRACTITIONER_DOUBLE_BOOKED`.

Entitlement is reserved **at booking, not at completion**, which is what makes cancellation a genuine compensating action rather than a bookkeeping no-op. When the patient holds a membership in status `ACTIVE` with `visits_remaining` of at least 1 and the modality is `IN_PERSON` or `TELEHEALTH`, the encounter's billing axis is created at `ENTITLEMENT_RESERVED` rather than `CASH_PENDING`: the membership's `visits_consumed` increases by 1, its `visits_remaining` decreases by 1, `visits_granted` is untouched, the encounter's `entitlement_consumed` flag is set true, and the membership's `version` increments by 1. An `EVISIT` encounter never reserves a visit unit, and a patient with no available unit books at `CASH_PENDING`. Encounters can be listed and narrowed by `patient_id`, `practitioner_id`, `source_thread_id`, `scheduling_status` and `billing_status`; the `source_thread_id` filter is what exposes the e-visits minted out of message threads, and that field is null on every encounter booked directly. A freshly booked encounter is minted with `duration_minutes`, `service_code`, `completed_at`, `cancelled_at` and `cancel_reason` all null, `diagnosis_codes` an empty array, and `base_fee_cents`, `extended_block_count` and `cash_charged_cents` all 0 — the base fee is resolved at settlement, not at booking — while `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end` are stored and returned exactly as the caller wrote them. Where a patient somehow holds more than one `ACTIVE` membership, the reservation takes the earliest-created one that still has a unit left, and that same membership is the one a later cancellation credits or forfeits against, whatever its status has become by then.

**Completing a visit settles it.** A `NaturopathicDoctor` or a `HealthCoach` records the actual `duration_minutes` of face-to-face time, the professional `service_code`, and the array of `diagnosis_codes`, and the encounter moves `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` with `completed_at` stamped from the logical clock. The settlement then runs in three parts. The `base_fee_cents` is 22500 for an `IN_PERSON` visit, 18000 for a `TELEHEALTH` visit, and 9500 for an `EVISIT`. The `extended_block_count` is zero when `duration_minutes` is at most 45; otherwise it is the number of minutes beyond 45 divided by 15 and rounded **up** to a whole block, so a 46-minute visit already buys one full block and a 60-minute visit buys exactly one. The extended-time charge is that block count multiplied by 4500 cents per block.

How much of that reaches the cash line depends on which bucket the encounter's billing axis is sitting in, and **entitlement covers the base fee only**. When the encounter reserved a visit unit at booking and its billing axis is `ENTITLEMENT_RESERVED`, `cash_charged_cents` is set to the extended-time charge alone — the base fee is absorbed by the visit unit — and the axis advances `ENTITLEMENT_RESERVED → ENTITLEMENT_CONSUMED`. When no unit was available and the axis is `CASH_PENDING`, `cash_charged_cents` is set to the base fee plus the extended-time charge, and the axis advances `CASH_PENDING → CASH_CHARGED`. The clinically identical encounter therefore produces a strictly larger cash figure for a non-member. Whichever branch runs, the resulting `cash_charged_cents` is added to the patient's `cash_charged_total_cents`. If this is the first completed encounter for that patient, their `clinical_status` moves `INTAKE → ESTABLISHED` as described in §3.1.

**Cancelling unwinds the booking, and the refund window is 24 hours.** A `Patient`, a `PracticeAdmin` or a `NaturopathicDoctor` supplies a `reason`; the encounter moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` with `cancelled_at` stamped and the text stored as `cancel_reason`. What happens on the billing axis depends on how far ahead of `scheduled_start` the cancellation lands, measured against the logical clock, and the boundary is inclusive — landing exactly 24 hours ahead still counts as timely. A timely cancellation of a reserved encounter moves the axis `ENTITLEMENT_RESERVED → REFUNDED` and gives the unit back: `visits_consumed` decreases by 1 and `visits_remaining` increases by 1, with `visits_granted` and `visits_forfeited` untouched, and `entitlement_consumed` is set false. A late cancellation of a reserved encounter moves the axis `ENTITLEMENT_RESERVED → FORFEITED` and moves the unit instead of returning it: `visits_consumed` decreases by 1 and `visits_forfeited` increases by 1, with `visits_remaining` and `visits_granted` untouched, and `entitlement_consumed` is again set false — the patient loses the visit but the pool does not pretend it was never spent. A timely cancellation of a cash encounter moves the axis `CASH_PENDING → REFUNDED` and no money moves at all, because nothing had been charged yet. A late cancellation of a cash encounter moves the axis `CASH_PENDING → CASH_CHARGED` and charges the late-cancellation fee of 7500 cents, which is added to the patient's `cash_charged_total_cents`. Whenever a membership counter moved, that membership's `version` increments by 1.

A no-show is harsher and is recorded by a `NaturopathicDoctor`, a `HealthCoach` or a `PracticeAdmin` with no payload at all. The encounter moves `SCHEDULED → NO_SHOW`. A reserved encounter advances `ENTITLEMENT_RESERVED → ENTITLEMENT_CONSUMED` with `cash_charged_cents` left at 0 — the reserved unit is simply consumed and is never returned to the pool and never forfeited. A cash encounter advances `CASH_PENDING → CASH_CHARGED` and is charged the same 7500-cent fee as a late cancellation, added to `cash_charged_total_cents`.

### 3.4. Functional Panels: Ordering, Wash-out Adjudication and Collection

The practice runs four functional laboratory panels and only four. Each `panel_code` resolves a fixed catalogue block that decides specimen handling, biological timing, interference and price:

- `SERUM_METABOLIC` draws a `SERUM` specimen, **requires fasting**, declares no cycle-day window and no interfering supplement class — its wash-out period is 0 days and no wash-out adjudication ever runs for it — and costs 24000 cents in cash or 1 laboratory credit on membership.
- `DUTCH_HORMONE` collects a `DRIED_URINE` specimen, does not require fasting, must be collected between cycle day 19 and cycle day 22 inclusive, is interfered with by the `HORMONE_SUPPORT` supplement class with a 14-day wash-out, and costs 39000 cents in cash or 2 laboratory credits.
- `SALIVA_CORTISOL` collects a `SALIVA` specimen, does not require fasting, declares no cycle-day window, is interfered with by the `ADAPTOGEN` class with a 7-day wash-out, and costs 21000 cents in cash or 1 laboratory credit.
- `STOOL_GI_MAP` collects a `STOOL` specimen, does not require fasting, declares no cycle-day window, is interfered with by the `PROBIOTIC` class with a 10-day wash-out, and costs 45000 cents in cash or 2 laboratory credits.

Only a `NaturopathicDoctor` may order a panel, and ordering is one of the eight families frozen by the clinical write gate. The request names the `patient_id`, the `panel_code`, the `funding_source` — either `MEMBERSHIP_CREDIT` or `CASH` — and the `requested_collection_date`, and may carry `fasting_attested`, which defaults to false, and `cycle_day`, which defaults to null. The `ordering_practitioner_id` is taken from the acting `X-User-ID` and is not supplied by the client. The server resolves `specimen_type`, `washout_class`, `price_cents` and `lab_credit_cost` from the catalogue block; a cash order carries the catalogue price in `price_cents` with `lab_credit_cost` at 0, and a credit-funded order carries the catalogue credit cost in `lab_credit_cost` with `price_cents` at 0.

Two commercial rules run at creation. A patient may not hold two open orders for the same panel: if an order for that same `panel_code` already exists for that patient in status `ORDERED`, `WASHOUT_HOLD` or `COLLECTED`, the request is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PANEL_ORDER`. And a `MEMBERSHIP_CREDIT` order requires a membership in status `ACTIVE` whose `lab_credits_remaining` is at least the panel's credit cost; a patient with no active membership, or one whose credits are short, is refused with `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_EXHAUSTED` and HTTP 402. On success the membership's `lab_credits_consumed` grows by the panel's credit cost and `lab_credits_remaining` shrinks by the same amount, with `lab_credits_granted` untouched. The membership charged is the earliest-created `ACTIVE` membership of that patient holding at least the panel's credit cost, and the order remembers which one it was, so a later cancellation returns the credits to that very membership even if it has since been paused, rolled or expired. The membership's own `version` moves on neither leg. A `CASH` order instead adds the panel's `price_cents` to the patient's `cash_charged_total_cents`.

**Supplement interference is adjudicated at order time, never silently allowed.** When the panel declares no interfering class, `washout_clearance_date` is null and the order is created `ORDERED`. Otherwise the server assembles the interfering set: every `ProtocolItem` belonging to a protocol of that same patient, whose own `status` is `ACTIVE` or `HELD`, and whose product's `supplement_class` equals the panel's interfering class. Lines already `DISCONTINUED` or `COMPLETED` never interfere. For each interfering line the server determines that line's `last_dose_date` — the calendar date of `dispensed_at` on that line's newest dispense still in status `DISPENSED`, taking the newest by `dispensed_at` and breaking a tie by the later `created_at`, which yields the same date either way; when the line has never been dispensed at all, the calendar date of the line's own `created_at` is used. That line's clearance is its `last_dose_date` plus the panel's wash-out days. The order's `washout_clearance_date` is the **latest** of those per-line clearances, and is null when the interfering set is empty.

That bound is closed at the bottom. A `requested_collection_date` equal to `washout_clearance_date` satisfies the wash-out and the order is created `ORDERED`; only a strictly earlier requested date sends the order to `WASHOUT_HOLD` instead, with the computed clearance date recorded on it. A held order is not stuck: a `NaturopathicDoctor` releases it back `WASHOUT_HOLD → ORDERED` once either the logical clock's date has reached the clearance date — again, the clearance date itself is enough — or every interfering line has since moved to `DISCONTINUED` or `COMPLETED`. A release attempted while neither condition holds is refused with `ERR_WASHOUT_NOT_ELAPSED`, and a successful release increments the order's `version` by 1.

Collection is recorded by a `NaturopathicDoctor` or a `HealthCoach` and takes the order `ORDERED → COLLECTED` with `collected_at` stamped from the logical clock and the order's `version` incremented by 1. It is a gated family, and it enforces the panel's biological window: when the catalogue block requires fasting, the order's `fasting_attested` must be true, and when the catalogue block declares a cycle-day window, the order's `cycle_day` must be present and inside that **closed** window — for the hormone panel, cycle days 19 and 22 both qualify while 18 and 23 do not. Either failure is refused with `ERR_COLLECTION_WINDOW_INVALID`.

Cancelling an order is permitted from `ORDERED`, from `WASHOUT_HOLD` and from `COLLECTED`, carries an optional `reason`, and returns exactly what the order consumed. A credit-funded order gives back precisely its `lab_credit_cost`: `lab_credits_consumed` decreases by that amount and `lab_credits_remaining` increases by the same amount, with `lab_credits_granted` deliberately untouched so the conservation identity survives. A cash-funded order instead adds its `price_cents` to the patient's `cash_refunded_total_cents`. Either way the order's `version` increments by 1, and `CANCELLED` is terminal. Lab orders are listable and can be narrowed by `patient_id`, by `ordering_practitioner_id` and by `status`.

### 3.5. Two Ranges on One Number: Results, Red Flags and Referral Loop Closure

Each analyte comes back as its own immutable observation carrying an `analyte_code` such as a thyroid marker, a numeric `value`, the reporting `unit`, and six bounds supplied on the same request: `standard_low` and `standard_high` for the laboratory's conventional reference range, `optimal_low` and `optimal_high` for the practice's tighter functional range, and `critical_low` and `critical_high` for the panic thresholds. Nothing is inferred from other results; each posting stands alone. Only a `NaturopathicDoctor` may post one, and the resulting record carries an `id` and a `created_at` but neither an `updated_at` nor a `version`, because a result is never edited. The analytes recorded against a given order can be listed back in `created_at` order like any other collection.

**The same value is banded twice, and the two bands use different geometry at the top.** The `standard_band` is `LOW` when the value falls below `standard_low`, `HIGH` when it rises above `standard_high`, and `IN_RANGE` in every other case — the conventional range is closed at both ends, so a value sitting exactly on `standard_high` is still `IN_RANGE`. The `optimal_band` is `BELOW_OPTIMAL` when the value falls below `optimal_low`, `ABOVE_OPTIMAL` when the value reaches or exceeds `optimal_high`, and `OPTIMAL` in every other case — the functional range is closed at the bottom but half-open at the top, so a value sitting exactly on `optimal_high` is already `ABOVE_OPTIMAL`. One and the same number can therefore be reported `IN_RANGE` conventionally and `ABOVE_OPTIMAL` functionally, which is precisely the distinction functional medicine trades on. Values and bounds are compared as decimals with up to three fractional digits, and the stored `value` is never rounded. Separately, `is_critical` is true when the value is at or below `critical_low` or at or above `critical_high`.

Those three verdicts route differently. A critical analyte escalates the order and freezes the chart. A non-critical analyte whose `standard_band` is anything other than `IN_RANGE` is a conventional abnormality: the interpretation narrative must carry it, and the doctor may choose to raise a routine referral. A non-critical analyte that is `IN_RANGE` conventionally but not `OPTIMAL` functionally implies no referral at all — it is the protocol-recommendation path, the signal that a supplement plan rather than an outside opinion is warranted.

Posting a non-critical analyte to a `COLLECTED` order moves it `COLLECTED → RESULTED`; posting further non-critical analytes to an order already at `RESULTED` leaves it at `RESULTED` in a self-loop, creating and classifying an additional result each time. Posting a result is one of the two writes in this API that move a row's status without moving its `version`: the order — and, on the escalation path below, the patient — has only its `updated_at` refreshed, and the response body is the newly minted `LabResult`, not the order. Posting a critical analyte moves the order to `CRITICAL_ESCALATED`, from `COLLECTED` or from `RESULTED` alike, and fires the identical five-link escalation cascade in both cases. `COLLECTED` and `RESULTED` are the only two states that accept a result at all: once an order has escalated, a further posting of either kind is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, so the cascade fires at most once per order. The five links run as follows: the result's `is_critical` and the order's `has_critical_result` are set true; a `Referral` is created with `referral_type` of `RED_FLAG_AUTO`, `status` of `SENT`, its `lab_order_id` pointing at this order, its `referring_practitioner_id` copied from the order's `ordering_practitioner_id`, its `attempt_no` at 1, and its `due_by_date` set to 3 days after the logical clock's date — an auto-raised referral has no outside recipient chosen for it yet, so `recipient_name`, `specialty` and `reason` are all null on it, exactly the three fields a routine referral requires; the patient's `red_flag_referral_open` becomes true and their `clinical_write_gate` becomes `FROZEN_RED_FLAG` unless it is already `FROZEN_SCOPE_REVIEW`, in which case the scope freeze stands; every `ACTIVE` protocol of that patient gets `advancement_blocked` set true; and every `ACTIVE` or `HELD` line beneath those protocols gets `titration_locked` set true.

Interpretation closes the order. A `NaturopathicDoctor` records the `interpretation_summary`, moving the order `RESULTED → INTERPRETED` or `CRITICAL_ESCALATED → INTERPRETED`, and the order's `version` increments by 1. Interpretation is deliberately **not** part of the gated family, so it remains available to the very clinician whose other clinical writes are frozen — but an escalated order carries its own guard: it cannot be interpreted while the automatic red-flag referral naming it is still in status `SENT`, `DECLINED` or `ABANDONED`, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_RED_FLAG_UNACKNOWLEDGED`. In other words, somebody outside the practice has to have taken the case before the practice writes its conclusion. `INTERPRETED` is terminal.

Referrals also exist in their own right. A `NaturopathicDoctor` raises a routine one by naming the `patient_id`, the `recipient_name` of the outside clinician or facility, the `specialty` the patient is being sent to, and the clinical `reason`. It is created with `referral_type` of `ROUTINE`, `status` of `SENT`, `attempt_no` of 1, `referring_practitioner_id` taken from the acting `X-User-ID`, and a `due_by_date` 3 days after the logical clock's date. Referrals are listable and can be narrowed by `patient_id`, `lab_order_id`, `referring_practitioner_id`, `referral_type` and `status`, which is how the auto-raised red flags are found.

Loop closure is driven by a single `action` taken by a `NaturopathicDoctor` or a `PracticeAdmin`, and every action increments the referral's `version` by 1. The narrative texts the actions carry — `recipient_name`, `decline_reason`, `report_summary` — are optional inputs stored as null when omitted, and *every* action, not merely `ACKNOWLEDGE`, re-derives the patient's gate afterwards from the referrals then standing, refreshing the patient's `updated_at` only when the gate value actually changed:

- **`ACKNOWLEDGE`** moves `SENT → ACKNOWLEDGED` and stamps `acknowledged_at`. This is the operation that unfreezes a patient, and it runs the five-link unfreeze cascade: once no `RED_FLAG_AUTO` referral of that patient remains in status `SENT`, `DECLINED` or `ABANDONED`, `red_flag_referral_open` is set false; because that flag cleared, `clinical_write_gate` is recomputed to `OPEN` unless `scope_review_status` is `UNDER_REVIEW` or `RESTRICTED`, in which case it remains `FROZEN_SCOPE_REVIEW`; because the gate re-opened, every `ACTIVE` protocol of the patient has `advancement_blocked` set false; and because those protocols unblocked, every `ACTIVE` or `HELD` line beneath them has `titration_locked` set false.
- **`REPORT`** moves `ACKNOWLEDGED → REPORT_RECEIVED`, storing the consult narrative as `report_summary` and stamping `report_received_at`.
- **`CLOSE`** moves `REPORT_RECEIVED → CLOSED` and stamps `closed_at`. `CLOSED` is terminal.
- **`DECLINE`** moves `SENT → DECLINED` and stores the `decline_reason`. Declining never clears a red flag: the patient's gate stays `FROZEN_RED_FLAG`.
- **`RE_REFER`** acts on a `DECLINED` referral and forks on the attempt counter. While `attempt_no` is still below the maximum of 3, the referral moves `DECLINED → SENT`, `attempt_no` increments by 1, the supplied `recipient_name` replaces the previous one — when none is supplied the previous recipient simply stands — and `decline_reason` is cleared: the decline-and-retry cycle. Once `attempt_no` has already reached 3, the same action instead moves the referral `DECLINED → ABANDONED` with `attempt_no` left unchanged and nothing re-sent, leaving `recipient_name` and `decline_reason` exactly as they were even when a new recipient was offered. An abandoned red flag never unfreezes anything; the gate stays `FROZEN_RED_FLAG` and the escalated order stays uninterpretable. `ABANDONED` is terminal.

### 3.6. Authoring a Protocol and the Three Ways It Can End

A protocol is the treatment plan: a titled set of supplement lines with a baseline the patient is measured against. Drafting one is a `NaturopathicDoctor` action, it belongs to the gated family, and it is guarded by optimistic concurrency on the chart. The request names the `patient_id`, a `title`, the `symptom_score_baseline` — a whole number on a scale running from 0 to 40, with anything outside that closed range refused as `ERR_VALIDATION` — and `expected_patient_version`, and may name a `supersedes_protocol_id` when this plan is a revision of an existing one. The `practitioner_id` is taken from the acting `X-User-ID`. A stale `expected_patient_version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`; nothing else would catch an author working from a chart snapshot that has since moved, because superseding the patient's current protocol is itself a legitimate thing to do. On success the protocol is created in `DRAFT` with `advancement_blocked` false, empty `interaction_flags`, and `issued_at` and `activated_at` both null. Its `version_no` is 1 for an original protocol, or one more than the named predecessor's `version_no` when `supersedes_protocol_id` is supplied, so a revision chain numbers itself. Drafting increments the **patient's** `version` by 1. Protocols are listable and can be narrowed by `patient_id`, `practitioner_id`, `supersedes_protocol_id` — which is what makes the revision chain navigable — and `status`.

Supplement lines are normally added to a draft one at a time, and adding a line is also gated. The endpoint itself carries no state precondition, however: a line may be appended to a protocol in any status whatever — `ISSUED`, `ACTIVE`, even one of the three terminal ones — and only the write gate and the payload rules stand in the way. Each line names the `product_id` of the dispensary product being prescribed, the `dose_amount_mg` per administration, the `frequency_per_day`, the planned `duration_days`, and the `titration_interval_days` that governs how often the line is reviewed. A new line starts in `status` `ACTIVE` with `titration_step_no` at 0, `last_decision` and `last_decision_at` null, `next_review_due_at` null until the first decision is taken, `titration_locked` false, and `discontinue_reason` null. Every read of a line additionally carries the computed `last_dose_date` of §3.4 — the calendar date of its newest fill still in status `DISPENSED`, falling back to the date of the line's own `created_at` when it has never been filled. Adding a line increments the **protocol's** `version` by 1. Lines are readable individually and listable, narrowed by `protocol_id`, `product_id` and `status`.

**Issuing is where the safety checks land.** A protocol moves `DRAFT → ISSUED` only when the patient's gate is `OPEN`, the patient's `intake_protocol_score` is at least 85 — failing which the request is refused with `ERR_INTAKE_INCOMPLETE` — and the protocol has at least one line. The line count is an own-state question rather than a payload one: a protocol carrying no lines is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at rung 8, which is to say before the intake score is consulted at all. The contraindication check then runs against the patient's reported `medication_classes`: no line's product may carry `interaction_severity` of `SEVERE` with a medication class that the patient actually reports, and a hit is refused with `ERR_CONTRAINDICATION_SEVERE`. Medication classes are free-form strings on both sides — neither the patient's `medication_classes` nor a product's `interacts_with_med_classes` is validated against any declared enum — and the two lists are intersected as exact, case-sensitive strings, so a differently-cased spelling simply does not hit. A `MODERATE` hit does not block; instead, one entry per offending line is recorded in the protocol's `interaction_flags` at issue time so that the interaction is documented rather than hidden. Each entry is an object carrying exactly four keys — `protocol_item_id`, `product_id`, `interaction_severity` (always `MODERATE`, since a `SEVERE` hit refuses instead) and `medication_classes`, the alphabetically sorted list of the classes the patient reports that this product actually hits. Issuing overwrites `interaction_flags` wholesale with the set computed on that pass, so a protocol with no moderate hits is issued with an empty array. Issuing stamps `issued_at` from the logical clock and increments the protocol's `version` by 1.

Activation moves `ISSUED → ACTIVE`, again requires the gate to be `OPEN`, stamps `activated_at`, and increments the protocol's `version` by 1. When the newly activated protocol names a `supersedes_protocol_id` and that predecessor is currently `ACTIVE`, activation runs a three-link cascade: the predecessor moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`; because it was superseded, every line beneath it in status `ACTIVE` or `HELD` moves to `DISCONTINUED` with `discontinue_reason` recorded as `SUPERSEDED_BY_REVISION`; and because those lines stopped, their `next_review_due_at` is cleared to null and their `titration_locked` is set false. Crucially, **no dispense is reversed on this path** — superseding a protocol never moves money, because the patient really did take the supplements the old plan prescribed.

An active protocol has three terminal outcomes and they differ precisely in what they touch:

- **`ACTIVE → COMPLETED`** closes the plan cleanly. Every line in status `ACTIVE` or `HELD` moves to `COMPLETED` and has its `next_review_due_at` cleared to null. No money moves and no stock moves. The protocol's `version` increments by 1.
- **`ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`** is never requested directly; it happens only as the cascade above, when a successor naming this protocol is activated. Its lines are discontinued rather than completed, and again no money moves.
- **`ACTIVE → DISCONTINUED`** carries a `reason` and is the only outcome that reaches backwards into the dispensary. Every line in status `ACTIVE` or `HELD` moves to `DISCONTINUED`; because those lines stopped, every dispense against exactly those lines — the ones this call stopped, never lines that were already `DISCONTINUED` or `COMPLETED` before it ran — still in status `DISPENSED` and whose `dispensed_at` falls within 14 days of the logical clock moves to `REVERSED`; and because those dispenses reversed, each one returns its `quantity` to its source lot's `quantity_on_hand`, the owning product's `stock_on_hand` is recomputed, and the patient's `cash_refunded_total_cents` grows by each reversed dispense's `total_price_cents`. Dispensing older than that fourteen-day window is left alone. The protocol's `version` increments by 1.

All three of `COMPLETED`, `SUPERSEDED` and `DISCONTINUED` are terminal for a protocol.

### 3.7. Titration Decisions and Adverse Reactions

Every supplement line is reviewed on a rhythm set by its own `titration_interval_days`, and each review is a single decision derived from one reported number. A `NaturopathicDoctor` submits the patient's `current_symptom_score` — itself a whole number inside the closed 0-to-40 range, outside which the request is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` — and the server computes the improvement delta as the owning protocol's `symptom_score_baseline` minus that reported score — a positive delta means the patient got better, since the scale runs from 0 to a maximum of 40 with higher meaning worse. The delta selects one of four decisions on a fixed ladder: an improvement of 8 or more resolves to `ADVANCE`; an improvement of at least 3 but less than 8 resolves to `HOLD`; an improvement of at least 0 but less than 3 resolves to `REDUCE`; and any negative delta — the patient is worse than baseline — resolves to `DISCONTINUE`.

The decision drives the dose. `ADVANCE` replaces `dose_amount_mg` with the current dose multiplied by 1.25, rounded half up to a whole milligram. `REDUCE` replaces it with the current dose multiplied by 0.50, rounded half up to a whole milligram. `HOLD` and `DISCONTINUE` leave the dose exactly as it stands. Every decision increments `titration_step_no` by 1, records `last_decision` as one of `ADVANCE`, `HOLD`, `REDUCE` or `DISCONTINUE`, stamps `last_decision_at` from the logical clock, and increments the line's `version` by 1.

**The next review date is computed by two different formulas, selected by the decision, and the difference is the whole point.** For `ADVANCE` and `REDUCE` the schedule is re-anchored on the decision itself: `next_review_due_at` becomes `last_decision_at` plus `titration_interval_days` days. A review taken late therefore pushes every subsequent review later by exactly that lateness. For `HOLD` the original anchor is preserved: `next_review_due_at` becomes the **previous** `next_review_due_at` plus `titration_interval_days` days, so drift caused by a late review is never absorbed and the line stays on its original rhythm. When a `HOLD` is the first decision ever taken on the line and `next_review_due_at` is still null, the previous anchor is taken to be the line's own `created_at`. For `DISCONTINUE`, `next_review_due_at` is cleared to null. The two anchoring variants diverge by exactly the lateness of the review, which is why the same line reviewed on the same day can end up with two different next-review instants depending only on the band the score fell into.

A decision may only be taken while the line's `titration_locked` is false and the owning protocol is `ACTIVE`; a locked line or a protocol that is not active is refused with `ERR_DEPENDENT_STATE_INVALID`, and taking a decision is itself one of the eight gated families, so a frozen patient is refused with `ERR_CLINICAL_WRITE_FROZEN` first. The line's own status moves according to the decision: an `ADVANCE` or `REDUCE` on an `ACTIVE` line leaves it `ACTIVE` in a self-loop, a `HOLD` moves it `ACTIVE → HELD`, an `ADVANCE` or `REDUCE` on a `HELD` line brings it back `HELD → ACTIVE` — closing the hold-and-resume cycle — and a `HOLD` on an already-`HELD` line leaves it `HELD` with the anchor-preserving arithmetic applied again.

A `DISCONTINUE` decision, from `ACTIVE` or from `HELD`, runs a four-link stop cascade. The line moves to `DISCONTINUED`, its `next_review_due_at` is cleared to null and its `discontinue_reason` is recorded. Because that line stopped, the owning protocol re-evaluates itself: when every one of its lines is now `DISCONTINUED`, the protocol itself moves `ACTIVE → DISCONTINUED`, with its `updated_at` refreshed but its `version` left where it stands, since nobody addressed the protocol. That re-evaluation is a total no-op — nothing moves, no money unwinds and the protocol's status stays exactly where it was — in three cases: when the protocol carries no lines at all, when even one of its lines sits in a status other than `DISCONTINUED` (a single `COMPLETED` or `HELD` sibling is enough to stop it), and when the protocol was not `ACTIVE` to begin with. Because the protocol was discontinued, every dispense drawn against **any** of that protocol's lines — not merely the line that triggered the cascade, and including lines that had stopped earlier — still in status `DISPENSED` and whose `dispensed_at` falls within 14 days of the logical clock moves to `REVERSED`. And because those dispenses reversed, each returns its `quantity` to its source lot's `quantity_on_hand` and adds its `total_price_cents` to the patient's `cash_refunded_total_cents`. `DISCONTINUED` and `COMPLETED` are both terminal for a line.

Adverse-reaction reporting is the safety valve and is therefore **never** blocked by the clinical write gate. A `Patient`, a `NaturopathicDoctor` or a `HealthCoach` reports against a line with a `severity` of `MILD`, `MODERATE` or `SEVERE` and a free-text `description`. No adverse-reaction row is persisted anywhere — the report is an instruction rather than a resource, and the endpoint answers with the protocol line itself. A `MILD` or `MODERATE` report is therefore recorded in the sense of being acted upon and nothing more: the line comes back with its status, its `updated_at` and its `version` all exactly as they were. Because only a `SEVERE` report transitions anything, the line's own state is checked only when `severity` is `SEVERE`, and that check sits at rung 8 ahead of payload validation, so a severe report aimed at an already-stopped line is answered `ERR_INVALID_STATE` even when its `description` is missing. A `SEVERE` report stops the line immediately: it moves to `DISCONTINUED` with `discontinue_reason` recorded as `ADVERSE_REACTION` and the line's `version` incremented by 1, and it runs exactly the same cascade a `DISCONTINUE` decision runs — the owning protocol moves `ACTIVE → DISCONTINUED` when every line is now stopped, dispenses within the same fourteen-day window are reversed, lots are restocked and the patient's refunded total grows. The one difference from a titration decision is the gate: a severe reaction is honoured even on a frozen chart and even on a locked line. When a line is stopped by any route other than an adverse reaction or a superseding revision, its `discontinue_reason` is recorded as `PROTOCOL_DISCONTINUED`.

### 3.8. The Dispensary: Lots, First-Expiry-First-Out, Pricing and Reversal

The dispensary is an inventory business embedded in a clinic. A `PracticeAdmin` creates each product with a `sku` that is expected to be unique across the dispensary — though the server does not police it, so a repeated `sku` is accepted and simply mints a second product — a display `name`, a `supplement_class` drawn from `HORMONE_SUPPORT`, `ADAPTOGEN`, `PROBIOTIC`, `BOTANICAL`, `VITAMIN_MINERAL` and `ENZYME`, a `retail_price_cents` cash price, and a `practitioner_cost_cents` acquisition cost that doubles as the floor under any discounted price; both money figures are non-negative integers and zero is accepted for either. Two optional attributes describe the interaction profile: `interacts_with_med_classes` lists the medication classes this product interacts with, matched against a patient's reported classes, and `interaction_severity` is `NONE`, `MODERATE` or `SEVERE`, defaulting to `NONE`. A product's `stock_on_hand` starts at 0 and is never written directly — it is always the sum of `quantity_on_hand` across that product's lots that are neither quarantined nor expired.

Stock arrives as lots, received by a `PracticeAdmin` or a `DispensaryTech`. A lot carries a `lot_code` that is expected to be unique within its product but, exactly like the product `sku`, is not enforced by the server, the `quantity_initial` units received as a strictly positive integer, and an `expiry_date`; `is_quarantined` defaults to false. On receipt, `quantity_on_hand` is initialised to `quantity_initial`, and the owning product's `stock_on_hand` is recomputed. `quantity_initial` is never mutated afterwards — it is the anchor of the lot conservation identity in §2.9. A lot's `is_expired` flag is true once the logical clock's date has reached or passed its `expiry_date`, so a lot expiring today is already unusable; the flag is materialised whenever the lot is read. Lots are listable and can be narrowed by `product_id` and `is_quarantined`.

Filling a line is a `DispensaryTech` or `NaturopathicDoctor` action, belongs to the gated family, and is protected by optimistic concurrency on the protocol. The request names the `protocol_item_id` being filled, the `quantity` of units, and `expected_protocol_version`, and may name a `product_lot_id` and a `professional_sample` flag that defaults to false. The dispense's `patient_id` is resolved through the line's protocol rather than supplied, and `dispensed_by_practitioner_id` is taken from the acting `X-User-ID`, with `dispensed_at` stamped from the logical clock and `status` starting at `DISPENSED`. The line must be dispensable: its owning protocol must be `ACTIVE` and the line itself must be `ACTIVE` or `HELD`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DEPENDENT_STATE_INVALID`. A mismatched `expected_protocol_version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

**Lot selection follows first-expiry-first-out when the caller does not name a lot.** The candidate pool is every lot of the line's product that is not quarantined, not expired, and holds at least the requested `quantity` on hand. Candidates are ranked by `expiry_date` ascending — the soonest to expire is chosen first — and ties among lots sharing an expiry date are broken by the earlier `created_at`. Row ids never participate in the choice. If the pool is empty, the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_LOT`. When the caller **does** name a `product_lot_id`, the selector does not run at all and that lot is checked directly: a quarantined or expired lot is refused with `ERR_LOT_EXPIRED`, and a lot holding fewer units than requested is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_STOCK`.

**Pricing forks three ways and the variant is chosen by the first matching condition, in this order.** If `professional_sample` is true, the variant is `PRACTITIONER_COST` and the `unit_price_cents` is simply the product's `practitioner_cost_cents`, bypassing retail and discount entirely — this is the practitioner's own sample, not a patient sale. Otherwise, if the patient holds a membership in status `ACTIVE`, the variant is `MEMBER`: the discount rate is that membership's `dispensary_discount_pct`, which is 0.05 on `FOUNDATION`, 0.10 on `CORE` and 0.20 on `PREMIER`. Where more than one `ACTIVE` membership exists the earliest-created one supplies the rate, and its entitlement balances are irrelevant here — a member with no visits and no credits left still prices at their tier's discount. Otherwise the variant is `RETAIL` and the discount rate is zero. For both `MEMBER` and `RETAIL`, the raw unit price is the product's `retail_price_cents` reduced by the discount rate, that raw figure is rounded half up to a whole cent, and the `unit_price_cents` is the larger of that rounded figure and the product's `practitioner_cost_cents` — the practice never sells below acquisition cost, so a deep discount on a thin-margin product silently lands on the floor. The chosen fork is recorded on the dispense as `pricing_variant`. The `total_price_cents` is then `unit_price_cents` multiplied by `quantity`, and the ordering matters: the per-unit price is rounded to a whole cent **before** multiplication, never after. Rounding a single line total instead produces a different number whenever the retail price times the discount rate leaves a fractional cent.

A successful fill decrements the source lot's `quantity_on_hand` by the dispensed `quantity`, recomputes the owning product's `stock_on_hand` across its eligible lots, adds `total_price_cents` to the patient's `cash_charged_total_cents`, and increments the **protocol's** `version` by 1. Dispenses are readable individually and listable, narrowed by `patient_id`, `protocol_item_id`, `product_lot_id`, `dispensed_by_practitioner_id` and `status`.

Reversal is available to a `ClinicalDirector` or a `DispensaryTech`, is never blocked by the clinical write gate, and carries a `reason`. A dispense moves `DISPENSED → REVERSED`, recording `reversed_at`, storing the text as `reversal_reason`, and recording the acting `X-User-ID` as `reversal_authorized_by_user_id`. Where the dispense's `total_price_cents` is at or above 20000 cents, the actor may not be the practitioner named in `dispensed_by_practitioner_id` — a high-value reversal needs a second pair of hands, and a self-authorised one is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Below that threshold the dispenser may reverse their own line. The reversal returns the dispensed `quantity` to the source lot's `quantity_on_hand`, recomputes the product's `stock_on_hand` over its eligible lots, adds `total_price_cents` to the patient's `cash_refunded_total_cents`, restores the lot conservation identity, and increments the dispense's `version` by 1. `REVERSED` is terminal, and the same transition is what the protocol-stop and adverse-reaction cascades drive automatically.

### 3.9. Asynchronous Care: Message Threads, E-visits and the Superbill Loop

Only a `Patient` may open a message thread, naming their own `patient_id`, the `practitioner_id` of the clinician being written to, a `subject` line and the opening message `body`. The thread starts `OPEN` with `patient_message_count` at 1 — the opening message counts — `clinician_message_count` and `clinical_minutes_total` at 0, `opened_at` stamped from the logical clock, `first_clinical_reply_at` null, `sla_breached` false, `evisit_conversion_eligible` false, and `sla_due_at` set to 48 hours after `opened_at`. The opening `body` is a required input, but no message row is stored anywhere in this API: a thread carries counters, states and timestamps, never a transcript, and no endpoint ever reads a message back. Threads are listable and can be narrowed by `patient_id`, `practitioner_id` and `status`.

Posting into a thread is open to the `Patient`, a `NaturopathicDoctor` or a `HealthCoach`, and carries a message `body` plus, on a clinical reply, the `clinical_minutes` spent. A clinical reply moves the thread `OPEN → ANSWERED`, increments `clinician_message_count` by 1, adds the reported minutes to `clinical_minutes_total`, sets `first_clinical_reply_at` when it was still null, and sets `sla_breached` true when that first reply landed after `sla_due_at`. `clinical_minutes` is an optional non-negative integer that defaults to 0 when omitted and is ignored entirely on a patient post. A second and a third clinical reply into an already-`ANSWERED` thread are perfectly legal: the thread stays `ANSWERED` in a self-loop, the counters accumulate, and `first_clinical_reply_at` keeps pointing at the first of them. A patient message increments `patient_message_count` by 1 and moves the thread `ANSWERED → OPEN`; when the thread is already `OPEN` and still awaiting a clinician, a further patient message leaves it `OPEN` in a self-loop while still counting. A long back-and-forth therefore loops between the two states indefinitely until the thread is closed or converted. Every post recomputes conversion eligibility and increments the thread's `version` by 1. A thread can be closed by a `NaturopathicDoctor` or a `HealthCoach` from either `OPEN` or `ANSWERED`, moving it to `CLOSED` with no billing consequence of any kind and incrementing its `version` by 1.

**Conversion eligibility is a two-part test.** The thread crosses the volume threshold when `patient_message_count` reaches 4 or when `clinical_minutes_total` reaches 15; both comparisons are inclusive, so exactly four patient messages or exactly fifteen accumulated clinical minutes already qualifies, and either one alone is enough. But eligibility is then withheld from members who already pay for messaging: `evisit_conversion_eligible` is true only when the volume threshold is crossed **and** the patient does not hold a membership in status `ACTIVE` whose tier includes messaging. `CORE` and `PREMIER` include messaging; `FOUNDATION` does not. A `PREMIER` member can therefore write fifty messages and never become convertible, which is exactly what their fee bought.

Converting is a `NaturopathicDoctor` action available from `OPEN` or `ANSWERED`, and it requires `evisit_conversion_eligible` to be true, the patient to hold no active membership whose tier includes messaging, the thread's practitioner to be licensed in the patient's `state_code`, and the patient's `telehealth_consent` to be true — the same licensure and consent rules that guard a booked telehealth visit apply here, refused with `ERR_STATE_NOT_LICENSED` and `ERR_MODALITY_CONSENT_MISSING` respectively, and an ineligible thread is refused with `ERR_DEPENDENT_STATE_INVALID`. Conversion mints an `Encounter` with `modality` of `EVISIT`, its `source_thread_id` pointing back at this thread, `duration_minutes` of 15, and both axes already resolved: `scheduling_status` is `COMPLETED` with `completed_at` stamped, and `billing_status` is `CASH_CHARGED` with `cash_charged_cents` of 9500 — the e-visit fee. That same 9500 cents is added to the patient's `cash_charged_total_cents`. The minted encounter has no window at all — `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end` are both null — and it carries `base_fee_cents` of 9500, `extended_block_count` of 0, `entitlement_consumed` false, `service_code` null and an empty `diagnosis_codes`; an e-visit never reserves a visit unit however much entitlement the patient has left. The thread itself moves to `CONVERTED_TO_EVISIT`, which is terminal; that move refreshes the thread's `updated_at` but leaves its `version` unchanged, this being the second of the two writes that decline to increment their own addressed row, because what the caller gets back is the encounter and not the thread.

**Superbills are how a cash practice meets insurance.** The practice never bills a payer; it produces a document the patient submits themselves. A `BillingSpecialist` drafts one against a single completed encounter by naming the `patient_id` and the `encounter_id`. Drafting copies the encounter's `diagnosis_codes` and `service_code` onto the superbill and copies the rendering practitioner's `npi` into `rendering_practitioner_npi`, then partitions the value three ways: `billable_total_cents` is the encounter's `cash_charged_cents` and nothing else; `non_billable_membership_cents` is the base fee that membership entitlement absorbed, reported for the patient's information but never billable; and `excluded_supplement_cents` is the value of supplements dispensed to that patient, likewise reported and never billable. An encounter carries no protocol foreign key, so that last figure is resolved without one: it is the sum of `total_price_cents` across every dispense belonging to the *encounter's* patient that is still in status `DISPENSED`, with reversed dispenses excluded and with no narrowing to any single protocol, encounter or date range. The two body inputs are not cross-checked against each other either: `patient_id` is stored exactly as supplied, while the copied codes, the NPI and all three partitions are read from the named `encounter_id`, so a draft naming a patient other than the encounter's own is accepted. Drafting also imposes no state requirement on the encounter — an encounter still `SCHEDULED` can be drafted against, and only `ISSUE` insists it be `COMPLETED`. The superbill starts in `DRAFT` with `revision_no` at 1 and `denial_code`, `denial_reason`, `appeal_narrative`, `void_reason` and `issued_at` all null. Superbills are listable and can be narrowed by `patient_id`, `encounter_id` and `status`.

The lifecycle is driven by a single `action` field. Every action increments the superbill's `version` by 1, and `APPEALED` and `CLOSED` are its terminal states. The narrative fields the actions carry — `void_reason`, `denial_code`, `denial_reason` and `appeal_narrative` — are all optional inputs, recorded as null when omitted; only `action` itself is required.

- **`ISSUE`** moves `DRAFT → ISSUED` and stamps `issued_at`. It requires the referenced encounter's `scheduling_status` to be `COMPLETED`, and it requires the document to actually document cash: `billable_total_cents` must be greater than 0, `diagnosis_codes` must be non-empty, and `rendering_practitioner_npi` must not be null. An encounter fully absorbed by membership entitlement has a billable total of 0 and is refused with `ERR_SUPERBILL_NOT_BILLABLE`. On success the referenced encounter's billing axis advances to `SUPERBILLED`, from `ENTITLEMENT_CONSUMED` when the encounter still charged some cash for extended time, or from `CASH_CHARGED`; from any other billing state the encounter is left exactly where it is while the superbill still issues.
- **`VOID`** moves `ISSUED → VOIDED`, storing the `void_reason`, and sends the encounter's billing axis back the way it came — but only when that axis is still sitting at `SUPERBILLED`: to `ENTITLEMENT_CONSUMED` when the encounter's `entitlement_consumed` is true, and to `CASH_CHARGED` otherwise, while an encounter already moved elsewhere is left alone. This is why `SUPERBILLED` is not an absorbing state.
- **`REISSUE`** moves `VOIDED → DRAFT` and is permitted only while `revision_no` is still below 3. It increments `revision_no` by 1 and re-copies `diagnosis_codes`, `service_code`, `rendering_practitioner_npi` and all three money partitions from the encounter's current state, so a corrected encounter yields a corrected superbill. Once `revision_no` has reached 3 the document can no longer be reissued, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at the own-state rung, exactly as an out-of-order action would be.
- **`REPORT_DENIAL`** moves `ISSUED → DENIED`, recording the payer's `denial_code` and `denial_reason`. This is the only action the `Patient` role may take on a superbill, and only on their own; a patient naming any other declared action is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` at the role rung, before the superbill id is even resolved, while a patient naming no action or an undeclared one falls through to the ordinary payload and enum rungs.
- **`APPEAL`** moves `DENIED → APPEALED`, recording the `appeal_narrative`. The appeal packet is where this system's involvement ends.
- **`CLOSE`** moves `ISSUED → CLOSED` when no further payer activity is expected.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Creating a new resource returns HTTP 201 and the created object; every other successful operation — reads, lists, state transitions and modifications — returns HTTP 200 and the affected object. Because many operations here are posted against an existing resource, it is worth naming the split explicitly.
