# Correctional Food Service Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Feeding a correctional institution is simultaneously a nutrition programme, a security operation and a fixed-price commercial contract, and today those three concerns live in three unconnected places. The dietitian's cycle menu sits in a spreadsheet; the shadow-board count sheet sits on a clipboard by the pot-wash sink; the liquidated-damages worksheet is rebuilt by hand at the end of every contract period from photocopied tray tallies. When an agency auditor asks why 1,180 trays were served against a forecast of 1,240, nobody can answer without three phone calls, and when a knife goes missing at shift-end nobody can prove which cook-chill batches were in the kitchen at the time.

This document specifies the Correctional Food Service Operations API, a single system of record that binds the contracted nutrition standard to the menu that was actually signed, the population count to the trays that were actually forecast, the shadow board to the batches that were actually discarded, and every documented service failure to the cents that are actually withheld from the vendor. All resources are mounted under the `/cfs` prefix. The system is deliberately opinionated: nutrition adequacy is a gate rather than a report, a missing sharp locks the kitchen down before anybody has to decide to lock it down, and the liquidated-damages waterfall is computed in a normative step order so that the agency and the vendor arrive at the same number from the same rows.

Two design constraints shape everything that follows. First, every deadline, expiry, elapsed-minute and window comparison in this domain reads a **logical clock** that operators can reposition, never the host's wall clock — food safety rules are only testable if time is an input. Second, nearly every meaningful field is *derived*: sharps counters, tray forecasts, missed-meal weights, substitution rates and the performance score are all recomputed from source rows rather than typed in by a user, so the specification below spends most of its length on how those derivations must behave.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint, including reads, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the operator's role. A request with `X-User-ID` absent or empty is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_USER_HEADER`; a request with `X-User-Role` absent or empty is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_ROLE_HEADER`. Both carry HTTP 401. A role string that is not one of the eight names below is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`, also HTTP 401 — an unrecognised role is treated as a failure to authenticate, not as a permission problem. Only once the role is recognised does the system ask whether that role is permitted on the endpoint being called; a recognised role that is not listed for the endpoint receives `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` with HTTP 403.

The eight roles are `ContractAdministrator`, `FoodServiceSupervisor`, `RegisteredDietitian`, `CorrectionalOfficer`, `MedicalProvider`, `ChaplainDesignee`, `GrievanceCoordinator` and `ComplianceAuditor`.

- The `ContractAdministrator` is the agency-side contract owner. It alone creates service contracts and facilities, activates menu cycles, and reconciles and closes the damages ledger.
- The `FoodServiceSupervisor` is the vendor-side kitchen manager: it builds menu cycles and their components, forecasts trays, schedules and advances cook-chill production, posts temperature readings, enrols and assigns kitchen workers, signs tools out of the shadow board, opens and closes service records, and documents substitutions.
- The `RegisteredDietitian` is the clinical nutrition authority: it signs menu cycles for adequacy, raises diet orders, requests reverification, revokes orders, decides substitutions, closes the clinical branch of a grievance and may record a QA verdict on a batch.
- The `CorrectionalOfficer` is security staff: it books inmates and records their movements, submits population counts, audits the shadow board, certifies tool returns, declares tools unaccounted, reports contraband and dispositions incidents. It also shares the authority to suspend a diet order with the `FoodServiceSupervisor`.
- The `MedicalProvider` verifies therapeutic diet orders and holds no other write authority anywhere in the system.
- The `ChaplainDesignee` verifies religious diet orders and likewise holds no other write authority.
- The `GrievanceCoordinator` owns the grievance lifecycle: filing, routing into one or both branches, completing the security branch and issuing the response.
- The `ComplianceAuditor` is a read-only oversight role. It may call every read endpoint in the domain, including the contract performance summary, the facility sharps summary and the per-batch temperature log listing, and may call no write endpoint at all.

All eight roles use an **opaque** identity model. When `X-User-Role` is `ContractAdministrator`, `FoodServiceSupervisor`, `RegisteredDietitian`, `CorrectionalOfficer`, `MedicalProvider`, `ChaplainDesignee`, `GrievanceCoordinator` or `ComplianceAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity in this system. The server records it wherever the specification below says an actor is captured — as `nutrition_signed_by_user_id` and `activated_by_user_id` on a menu cycle, as `requested_by_user_id` and `verified_by_user_id` on a diet order, as `submitted_by_user_id` on a population count, as `logged_by_user_id` on a temperature reading, as `issued_by_user_id` and `return_certified_by_user_id` on a tool issuance, as `discovered_by_user_id` and `disposition_by_user_id` on a contraband incident, as `documented_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a menu substitution, and as `responded_by_user_id` on a grievance — but it never compares that identifier against any entity's `id`, and no ownership check anywhere in this domain resolves `X-User-ID` to a row. The only comparisons made against these recorded identifiers are the equality tests of §2.8, which compare one recorded actor to the acting `X-User-ID` on the paired step of the same artifact.

Because identity is opaque, the system also permits any of the eight roles — the `ComplianceAuditor` included — to reposition the logical clock. This is the single write-shaped capability granted to the read-only role, and it exists so that time-dependent behaviour can be driven deterministically.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint in this domain is paginated through two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum is 1 and its maximum is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and its minimum is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422) rather than silently clamped.

Results are ordered by `created_at` ascending on every list endpoint unless that endpoint's own description says otherwise, and this ordering is stable across pages. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` set true when rows remain beyond the current page. Filter parameters on list endpoints are always optional and combine conjunctively: supplying two filters narrows to rows matching both.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Stamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`; a supplied one is ignored. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same format as `created_at` and refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and is incremented by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations that arrive as cascade side effects rather than as direct client requests. Two entity types are deliberate exceptions because they are immutable ledger rows: a population count and a temperature reading carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither has an `updated_at` field and neither has a `version` field, and no operation in this system ever modifies a row of either kind once written.

A distinct **logical clock** governs every temporal comparison. An administrative capability, available to all eight roles, repositions this clock to an arbitrary UTC instant supplied as `current_utc` and returns the instant it now reads. Before that capability is ever invoked the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, which is at or before every default fixture date, so an unset clock behaves as a fixed earliest instant and never as a drifting wall-clock time. Repositioning the clock mutates no entity and triggers no sweep by itself; sweeps happen only when a business operation that declares one is invoked. Every `*_at` field written by the system — `activated_at`, `verified_at`, `verification_expires_at`, `locked_at`, `issued_at`, `returned_at`, `unaccounted_at`, `discovered_at`, `count_at`, `logged_at`, `closed_at`, `reconciled_at`, `last_sharps_audit_at`, `filed_at`, `response_due_at`, `response_at`, `cook_end_at`, `chill_final_at`, `chill_deadline_at` and `buffered_release_at` — takes its value from this clock or from arithmetic over it, never from the host clock and never from the client. That enumeration is closed, and `created_at` and `updated_at` are deliberately outside it: those two audit stamps are taken from the host's wall clock rather than from the logical clock, on every row of every entity type that carries them, and repositioning the logical clock does not change what they read. That is what keeps `created_at` usable as an arrival-order tiebreaker among rows whose logical-clock instants are identical, as §2.3, §3.5 and §3.9 all rely on.

### 2.5. The Rejection Ladder

Because a single request can violate several rules at once, the order in which checks run is part of the contract: a request that fails two checks always reports the earlier one. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the first failure aborts the request with no partial writes.

1. `X-User-ID` present and non-empty, else `ERR_MISSING_USER_HEADER`.
2. `X-User-Role` present and non-empty, else `ERR_MISSING_ROLE_HEADER`.
3. `X-User-Role` names one of the eight declared roles, else `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`.
4. The named role appears in the called endpoint's authorized-role list, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
5. On every write endpoint, every field the endpoint declares as required is present and carries the declared JSON type, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Fields the system computes or stamps itself are silently ignored when a client supplies them — they are neither honoured nor an error.
6. On every write endpoint, every enum-typed field carries a value inside its declared domain, else `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`.
7. On every endpoint, every numeric field satisfies its declared range, and pagination `limit` lies within 1 through 100 with `offset` at least 0, else `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE`.
8. Every resource named by a path segment exists, validated strictly left to right so that the outermost segment's 404 is reported first. The specific code names the entity that was missing: `ERR_SERVICE_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MENU_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MENU_CYCLE_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INMATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DIET_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POPULATION_COUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEAL_FORECAST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCTION_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INMATE_WORKER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TOOL_ISSUANCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRABAND_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEAL_SERVICE_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MENU_SUBSTITUTION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_GRIEVANCE_NOT_FOUND`.
9. Every reference supplied in a request body names an existing row, validated in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, and reported with the same per-entity 404 code as step 8. A `supersedes_diet_order_id` that names nothing yields `ERR_DIET_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`.
10. Cross-parent coherence holds, else `ERR_FACILITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). This check guards seven creations — a grievance, a service record, a production batch, a kitchen worker, a tool issuance, a menu substitution and a diet order — and its content is described where each of those is discussed.
11. A menu component's `cycle_day` lies between 1 and the owning cycle's `cycle_length_days` inclusive, else `ERR_CYCLE_DAY_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422).
12. Every uniqueness key is free, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` (HTTP 409). This check guards contract creation, facility creation, menu cycle creation, menu component addition, inmate booking, worker enrolment and tool issuance.
13. `expected_inmate_version` matches, else `ERR_STALE_INMATE_VERSION`.
14. `expected_facility_version` matches, else `ERR_STALE_FACILITY_VERSION`.
15. `expected_service_contract_version` matches, else `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION`.
16. The target resource is not already sitting in a terminal state for the machine being driven, else `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409). The terminal states are: a contract in `CLOSED`; a menu cycle in `RETIRED`; a diet order in `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED`; a forecast in `LOCKED` or `SUPERSEDED`; a batch whose production axis reads `SERVED` or `DISCARDED`; a batch whose QA axis reads `QA_CONDEMNED`; a tool issuance in `RETURNED`; a contraband incident in `SANCTIONED` or `CLEARED`; a kitchen worker whose clearance reads `BARRED`; a service record in `DISPUTED`; a substitution in `APPROVED` or `REJECTED`; and a grievance in `RESPONDED`, `ESCALATED` or `CLOSED`.
17. The target resource occupies one of the states declared as a legal origin for the requested transition, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).
18. The requested transition target itself is legal for the current state, else `ERR_ILLEGAL_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). This guards the two batch endpoints: a production-axis advance may move only one step forward along `PLANNED` → `COOKED` → `CHILLED` → `STAGED` → `SERVED`, never skipping and never reversing, and a QA verdict may not move a batch out of `QA_CONDEMNED` to anywhere.

Beyond step 18 the ladder continues through the domain's safety, security, temporal and fiscal gates, in this relative order: the kitchen posture gate (`ERR_KITCHEN_POSTURE_BLOCKED`); the open-sharps gate on tool issue (`ERR_SHARPS_DISCREPANCY_OPEN`); sharps eligibility (`ERR_WORKER_NOT_SHARPS_ELIGIBLE`); worker clearance (`ERR_WORKER_NOT_CLEARED`); medical clearance currency (`ERR_MEDICAL_CLEARANCE_EXPIRED`); an open contraband hold on the worker (`ERR_OPEN_CONTRABAND_HOLD`); the clinical-indication requirement (`ERR_CLINICAL_INDICATION_REQUIRED`); verifier-role matching (`ERR_VERIFIER_ROLE_MISMATCH`); segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`); custody status (`ERR_INMATE_NOT_IN_CUSTODY`); nutrition adequacy (`ERR_NUTRITION_BELOW_STANDARD`); the sodium ceiling (`ERR_SODIUM_CAP_EXCEEDED`); cook-chill compliance on a QA pass (`ERR_COOK_CHILL_NONCOMPLIANT`); the per-step production prerequisites (`ERR_COOK_LOG_MISSING`, `ERR_CHILL_LOG_MISSING`, `ERR_QA_NOT_PASSED`, `ERR_REHEAT_LOG_MISSING`); temperature log sequencing (`ERR_LOG_POINT_OUT_OF_SEQUENCE`); the discarded-batch bar on logging (`ERR_BATCH_DISCARDED`); substitution equivalence (`ERR_SUBSTITUTION_NOT_EQUIVALENT`); the sharps over-count bar (`ERR_SHARPS_COUNT_OVER_AUTHORIZED`); the diet window shape (`ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`); the diet window conflict (`ERR_DIET_ORDER_WINDOW_CONFLICT`); the reverification ceiling (`ERR_REVERIFICATION_LIMIT_REACHED`); chiller occupancy (`ERR_CHILLER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`); the forecast date and service-window checks (`ERR_FORECAST_DATE_PAST`, `ERR_SERVICE_WINDOW_MISMATCH`); the active-cycle and locked-forecast prerequisites (`ERR_NO_ACTIVE_MENU_CYCLE`, `ERR_FORECAST_NOT_LOCKED`); the contract-active and cycle-completeness prerequisites (`ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_MENU_CYCLE_INCOMPLETE`); the forecast and service-record duplication bars (`ERR_DUPLICATE_FORECAST`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERVICE_RECORD`); the open-batch bar on closing a service record (`ERR_OPEN_BATCHES_REMAIN`); the grievance join requirement (`ERR_BRANCHES_INCOMPLETE`); remedy admissibility (`ERR_REMEDY_NOT_PERMITTED`, `ERR_REFEED_TARGET_INVALID`, `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_TARGET_INVALID`); the reconcile variant requirement (`ERR_LD_VARIANT_REQUIRED`); the tray budget (`ERR_TRAY_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`); and finally the cost ceiling (`ERR_COST_CEILING_EXCEEDED`). Each of those gates is specified in full in §3, next to the operation it guards.

### 2.6. Stale-Version Guards on Cross-Aggregate Writes

Three write operations reach across an aggregate boundary and therefore require the client to prove it has observed the current state of the parent it is about to affect. Each takes a mandatory version field — never optional, never defaulted — carrying the `version` the client last read from that parent. Every other write endpoint in this domain takes no version field at all, and supplying one there is ignored.

Creating a diet order requires `expected_inmate_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the inmate named by `inmate_id`. If that value does not equal the inmate's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INMATE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A create may legitimately carry `supersedes_diet_order_id` and retire an incumbent order, so a second create issued strictly after the first has completed would otherwise observe a diet picture that still appears to permit another supersede; pinning the inmate's `version` turns that into a deterministic stale-version rejection instead.

Creating a production batch requires `expected_facility_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the facility named by `facility_id`. A mismatch against the facility's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FACILITY_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A sharps audit that finds no shortfall advances the facility's `version` and rewrites `sharps_on_board_count` while leaving `kitchen_posture` at `OPEN`, so a batch create issued strictly after that audit completes sees an identical posture and would otherwise be accepted against a stale view of chiller occupancy and sharps; the guard forces the client to re-read the facility first.

Closing a meal service record requires `expected_service_contract_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the service contract that owns the record's facility. A mismatch against that contract's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Closing a different service record, or a grievance response that disputes an already-closed record, both advance the contract's `version` and mutate `missed_meal_total` without changing the contract's `status`, so a close issued strictly afterwards would see the same contract status and post its damages delta onto a stale ledger snapshot.

### 2.7. Arithmetic, Rounding and Units

Money in this system is always an integer number of cents; there are no fractional cents anywhere and no currency field. Tray counts, head counts, tool counts, kcal figures, sodium figures and elapsed minutes are always whole numbers. Protein figures, percentages, temperature readings, cost-per-meal-day figures and the performance score carry decimals.

The rounding mode is not uniform, and using the wrong one changes results at boundaries, so each rule states its own:

- **Round half up to two decimal places** applies to protein grams per meal-day, every percentage (`substitution_rate_pct`, `kcal_delta_pct`, `protein_delta_pct`), the sodium delta in milligrams, `cost_cents_per_meal_day_actual` and `performance_score`.
- **Round half up to whole units** applies to per-item kcal and per-item sodium figures and to the per-meal-day kcal and sodium quotients derived from them.
- **Ceiling** applies to every tray quantity derived from a rate or a division, to `approved_cost_cents_per_meal_day`, to `substitution_surcharge_cents`, and to the population-weighted damages figure.
- **Floor** applies to `meal_days_served`, to the missed-meal tolerance in trays, to `cost_recovery_credit_cents`, and to every elapsed-minute figure (`chill_minutes` and `minutes_since_cook_end` are whole floored minutes computed from elapsed seconds).

Temperatures are compared at one decimal place. Instants are compared at whole-second resolution. Where a rule states a limit, the limit is inclusive unless the rule explicitly says "strictly"; §3 states inclusivity individually for every gate because the boundary cases are the interesting ones.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties on Signed Artifacts

Five artifacts in this domain carry a two-person rule: the operator who performs the first step may not perform the paired second step on the same row. In every case the check is an inequality between a recorded actor field and the acting `X-User-ID`, and in every case the rejection is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 409.

A diet order's `requested_by_user_id` may not equal the `X-User-ID` that verifies it. A menu cycle's `nutrition_signed_by_user_id` may not equal the `X-User-ID` that activates it. A tool issuance's `issued_by_user_id` may not equal the `X-User-ID` that certifies its return. A contraband incident's `discovered_by_user_id` may not equal the `X-User-ID` that dispositions it. A menu substitution's `documented_by_user_id` may not equal the `X-User-ID` that decides it.

One consequence deserves emphasis: when a suspended diet order re-enters the verification queue, the operator requesting the reverification becomes the new `requested_by_user_id`. The two-person rule therefore re-anchors on the requester of record, and an operator who requested reverification cannot then verify the same order.

### 2.9. Kitchen Posture: the Master Safety Gate

Every facility carries a derived `kitchen_posture` with three possible values — `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` and `LOCKDOWN`. Posture is never set directly by any endpoint. It is recomputed from scratch, not incrementally adjusted, after every tool issue, tool return, unaccounted declaration, sharps audit, contraband report, contraband disposition and contract-level corrective-action cascade. Because it is recomputed from scratch, clearing the last remaining cause returns a facility to `OPEN` with no explicit release operation.

The recomputation walks six clauses in a normative order and the first matching clause wins. A facility satisfying both clause 1 and clause 3 is therefore `LOCKDOWN`, never `RESTRICTED`.

1. If any tool issuance at the facility is in status `UNACCOUNTED` and its `tool_class` is one of `SHARP_KNIFE`, `MEAT_SAW` or `SCISSORS`, the posture is `LOCKDOWN`.
2. Otherwise, if `sharps_audit_shortfall` is at least 1, the posture is `LOCKDOWN`.
3. Otherwise, if `corrective_action_required` is true, the posture is `RESTRICTED`.
4. Otherwise, if `open_contraband_incident_count` is at least 2, the posture is `RESTRICTED`.
5. Otherwise, if any tool issuance at the facility is in status `UNACCOUNTED` with `tool_class` of `NON_SHARP`, the posture is `RESTRICTED`.
6. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`.

Three operations put food or people into the kitchen and are therefore gated on posture: scheduling a production batch, assigning a kitchen worker to a work area, and signing a tool out of the shadow board. Each requires the facility's posture to read `OPEN`, and a facility reading `RESTRICTED` or `LOCKDOWN` rejects the operation with `ERR_KITCHEN_POSTURE_BLOCKED` (HTTP 409).

Tool issuance carries a single deliberate carve-out. On that endpoint alone, the posture gate is evaluated only against clauses 2, 3 and 4 — audit shortfall, corrective action required, and the contraband threshold — and not against the unaccounted-tool clauses 1 and 5, because an unaccounted tool is reported by the immediately following and more specific check as `ERR_SHARPS_DISCREPANCY_OPEN`. The facility's stored `kitchen_posture` is unaffected by this carve-out: it still reads `LOCKDOWN` or `RESTRICTED`. Only which error this one endpoint reports changes.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline

Many operations in this domain mutate more than one row. Where that happens, the specification in §3 names each hop explicitly, and three rules govern all of them.

First, every row a cascade touches has its own `version` incremented by 1 and its own `updated_at` refreshed, exactly as if a client had written it directly. A single service-record close can therefore advance the versions of the record, its contract and every facility under that contract.

Second, cascades are ordered and each hop's precondition is evaluated against the state produced by the previous hop. A hop whose precondition fails is skipped without failing the request — these are **conditional cascades**, and where a hop only fires for some rows, §3 says which rows.

Third, derived counters are reconciled, not blindly adjusted. `Inmate.active_diet_order_count`, `Facility.active_special_diet_count`, `Facility.sharps_issued_count`, `Facility.sharps_unaccounted_count`, `Facility.open_contraband_incident_count`, `InmateWorker.contraband_strike_count`, `MenuCycle.item_count`, `MealServiceRecord.substitution_count` and `ServiceContract.substitution_total` must always agree with a count over their source rows after the operation completes. Where a cascade both adds and removes — a superseding diet verification is the canonical case — the net effect on a counter can legitimately be zero.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Contract and its Kitchens

Everything in this domain hangs off a **service contract**, and a `ContractAdministrator` creates one before anything else exists. The payload states a `contract_code` — a human-readable identifier of 3 to 32 characters that must be unique across every contract in the system, with a collision rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` — together with the vendor's legal name in `vendor_name` and the commercial terms the vendor will be measured against. The nutrition standard is three numbers: `contracted_kcal_per_meal_day`, the minimum energy per inmate per meal-day, which must be at least 1200; `contracted_protein_grams_per_meal_day`, the minimum grams of protein per meal-day, which must be at least 40; and `contracted_sodium_mg_cap_per_meal_day`, the *maximum* milligrams of sodium per meal-day, which must be at least 1000. The commercial terms are four more: `cost_ceiling_cents_per_meal_day`, the raw-food cost ceiling in cents per meal-day, which must be greater than 0; `ld_cents_per_missed_meal`, the liquidated-damages rate charged per weighted missed-meal unit, which must be at least 0; `ld_monthly_cap_cents`, a hard cap on damages for the contract period, also at least 0; and `substitution_allowance_pct`, the fraction of trays that may be served on a substituted component before a surcharge bites, expressed as a percentage between 0 and 100 inclusive with two decimal places.

A new contract lands in `status` `DRAFT`. Its liquidated-damages ledger starts empty in the strictest sense: `total_trays_served`, `meal_days_served`, `missed_meal_total`, `substitution_total`, `grievance_sla_breach_count`, `raw_liquidated_damages_cents`, `substitution_surcharge_cents`, `liquidated_damages_cents`, `gross_food_cost_cents`, `cost_recovery_credit_cents` and `net_liquidated_damages_cents` all read 0, while `substitution_rate_pct`, `cost_cents_per_meal_day_actual` and `performance_score` all read 0.00. The chosen damages algorithm, `ld_variant`, is null until the first reconciliation pins it, and both `reconciled_at` and `closed_at` are null.

Before a contract can be put to work it must have somewhere to work. A `ContractAdministrator` registers each institution as a **facility**, supplying the owning contract through a payload carrying the `contract_id`, a globally unique `facility_code` — a second collision source for `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` — the institution's `name`, and the size of the knife inventory in `sharps_authorized_count`, the number of tools that hang on the shadow board when the kitchen is fully accounted for, which must be at least 0. Two operational parameters are optional. `satellite_site_count`, the number of satellite feeding sites fed from this kitchen, defaults to 0 and must be at least 0; it drives the menu cost overhead tier described in §3.2. `blast_chiller_capacity`, the number of cook-chill batches whose buffered occupancy intervals may overlap, defaults to 2 and must be at least 1.

A newly registered facility is `OPEN` for business: `kitchen_posture` reads `OPEN`, `sharps_on_board_count` is initialised to the same value as `sharps_authorized_count` — every authorised tool is presumed on the board — and every other derived field starts at its zero: `sharps_issued_count`, `sharps_unaccounted_count`, `sharps_audit_shortfall`, `open_sharps_discrepancy_count`, `open_contraband_incident_count` and `active_special_diet_count` all read 0, `corrective_action_required` reads false, and `last_sharps_audit_at` is null because the board has never been counted.

Activation is the moment the contract becomes operable, moving it from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE`, and only a `ContractAdministrator` may do it. Two things must hold: the contract must currently be in `DRAFT`, and at least one facility must already name this contract as its own. A contract with no facilities attached cannot go `ACTIVE` and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Activation itself is minimal — the status flips, `version` is incremented by 1 and `updated_at` is refreshed — but it is the precondition that unlocks menu cycle activation across every facility under the contract.

Facilities and contracts are readable throughout. A facility read returns its sharps counters and its derived posture; the facility listing accepts `contract_id` and `kitchen_posture` as filters; and a dedicated sharps summary returns the whole conservation identity for one kitchen — `sharps_authorized_count`, `sharps_on_board_count`, `sharps_issued_count`, `sharps_unaccounted_count`, `sharps_audit_shortfall`, `open_sharps_discrepancy_count` and `kitchen_posture` — without exposing the underlying issuance rows. Two owned sub-resource listings hang off a facility as well: one enumerates every production batch at that kitchen and accepts a `production_stage` filter, and one enumerates every tool issuance at that kitchen, including recovered and unaccounted rows, and accepts a `status` filter. On the contract side, the listing accepts a `status` filter, and a computed performance summary returns `total_trays_served`, `meal_days_served`, `missed_meal_total`, `substitution_rate_pct`, `grievance_sla_breach_count`, `raw_liquidated_damages_cents`, `substitution_surcharge_cents`, `liquidated_damages_cents`, `cost_recovery_credit_cents`, `net_liquidated_damages_cents`, `performance_score` and `ld_variant` in a single flat object.

### 3.2. Building, Signing and Activating a Menu Cycle

A **menu cycle** is a repeating rotation of components that a facility cooks to, and a `FoodServiceSupervisor` builds it. Creation takes a payload naming the `facility_id` the cycle is built for, a `cycle_code` that must be unique among the cycles at that facility — a duplicate pair is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` — and `cycle_length_days`, the number of distinct cycle days, which must be at least 1 and at most 7. The cycle opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `item_count` at 0 and all four attested figures — `approved_kcal_per_meal_day`, `approved_protein_grams_per_meal_day`, `approved_sodium_mg_per_meal_day` and `approved_cost_cents_per_meal_day` — null, because nothing has been attested yet. `nutrition_signed_by_user_id`, `activated_by_user_id` and `activated_at` are likewise null.

Components are added one at a time to a cycle. Each addition supplies `cycle_day`, the cycle day the component is served on; `meal_slot`, one of `BREAKFAST`, `LUNCH` or `DINNER`; `component_name`, the name printed on the production sheet; the three nutrition densities `kcal_per_100g` (which must be greater than 0), `protein_grams_per_100g` (at least 0) and `sodium_mg_per_100g` (at least 0); `portion_grams`, the served portion weight, which must be greater than 0; and `raw_cost_cents`, the raw-food cost in cents of one served portion, which must be at least 0. Two flags are optional: `therapeutic_safe` declares whether the component may appear on therapeutic medical diet trays and defaults to true, and `religious_tags` is an array of religious diet codes — drawn from the same vocabulary as a diet order's diet code — that this component satisfies, defaulting to an empty array. A cycle day outside the range 1 through the parent cycle's `cycle_length_days` inclusive is rejected with `ERR_CYCLE_DAY_OUT_OF_RANGE`, and the triple of owning cycle, `cycle_day` and `meal_slot` must be unique, so a second lunch on day 3 of the same cycle is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. Every addition increments the cycle's `item_count` by 1 and its `version` by 1.

Adding a component to a cycle that has already been signed has a consequence the supervisor must understand: **the signature is revoked**. A cycle sitting in `NUTRITION_APPROVED` reverts to `DRAFT` on any component addition, all four `approved_*` figures are reset to null, and `nutrition_signed_by_user_id` is cleared to null. Changing what is on the tray invalidates the dietitian's attestation that the tray was adequate, and the cycle must be re-signed from scratch before it can be activated. Cycles themselves are readable individually and through a listing that accepts `facility_id` and `status` filters. Components are readable individually and in bulk as well: the cycle's own component listing accepts a `meal_slot` filter, and a cross-cycle component listing accepts `menu_cycle_id` and `meal_slot` filters.

Signing is the `RegisteredDietitian`'s attestation and moves the cycle from `DRAFT` to `NUTRITION_APPROVED`. It carries no payload. Before anything is attested, the cycle must be **complete**: `item_count` must equal `cycle_length_days` multiplied by the three meal slots served each day, so a 7-day cycle needs exactly 21 components and a 3-day cycle exactly 9. A cycle that is short or over-stuffed is rejected with `ERR_MENU_CYCLE_INCOMPLETE`.

The adequacy figures are then derived, and the step order is normative because per-item rounding happens **before** any summation. For each component of the cycle — every component whose owning cycle is this one, regardless of which meal slot or cycle day it sits on, with nothing ever excluded — three per-item values are computed. The item's energy contribution is its `kcal_per_100g` multiplied by its `portion_grams`, divided by 100, then rounded half up to a whole number of kcal. The item's protein contribution is its `protein_grams_per_100g` multiplied by its `portion_grams`, divided by 100, then rounded half up to two decimal places. The item's sodium contribution is its `sodium_mg_per_100g` multiplied by its `portion_grams`, divided by 100, then rounded half up to a whole number of milligrams. Those already-rounded per-item values are then summed across the cycle to give a cycle energy total, a cycle protein total and a cycle sodium total. Finally each total is divided by `cycle_length_days` and rounded again: `approved_kcal_per_meal_day` is the energy total over the cycle length rounded half up to a whole number, `approved_protein_grams_per_meal_day` is the protein total over the cycle length rounded half up to two decimal places, and `approved_sodium_mg_per_meal_day` is the sodium total over the cycle length rounded half up to a whole number.

Summing the unrounded per-item values and rounding only once at the end is a different and forbidden computation. With portion-scaled values that land on a half, the two orders differ by whole kcal, and that difference can flip the adequacy verdict at the contracted threshold. Implementations must round per item first. A cycle with no components at all is not a special case: its energy total is 0, so `approved_kcal_per_meal_day` is 0 and the first gate fails — though in practice such a cycle has already been stopped by the completeness check.

The three adequacy gates are then evaluated against the contract that serves this cycle's facility, in this order. First, `approved_kcal_per_meal_day` must be at least `contracted_kcal_per_meal_day`; if it is lower, the sign is rejected with `ERR_NUTRITION_BELOW_STANDARD`. Second, `approved_protein_grams_per_meal_day` must be at least `contracted_protein_grams_per_meal_day`; a shortfall here also reports `ERR_NUTRITION_BELOW_STANDARD`, so energy and protein share one code and energy is tested first. Third, `approved_sodium_mg_per_meal_day` must be at or below `contracted_sodium_mg_cap_per_meal_day`; exceeding the cap is the distinct `ERR_SODIUM_CAP_EXCEEDED`. All three comparisons are inclusive at the boundary: a cycle landing exactly on the contracted kcal minimum passes, and a cycle landing exactly on the sodium cap passes.

Signing also freezes the cost figure, which is computed alongside the nutrition figures whenever the cycle's component set changes and re-evaluated at activation. The raw cost total is the sum of `raw_cost_cents` over the cycle's components, in whole cents. That total is divided by `cycle_length_days` to give a base cost per meal-day, and this quotient is deliberately **not** rounded yet. A satellite overhead multiplier is then selected as a whole-tier step from the current `satellite_site_count` of the facility this cycle belongs to, read at calculation time rather than frozen when the cycle was created: a facility with no satellite sites uses a multiplier of 1.00, a facility with 1 or 2 satellite sites uses 1.02, and a facility with 3 or more satellite sites uses 1.05. The tier is never interpolated — one satellite and two satellites both take 1.02. `approved_cost_cents_per_meal_day` is the unrounded base cost per meal-day multiplied by that multiplier, with the ceiling taken exactly once at the very end to reach whole cents. On a successful sign, all four `approved_*` figures are written, `nutrition_signed_by_user_id` records the signing `X-User-ID`, and `version` is incremented by 1.

Activation is a `ContractAdministrator` action moving the cycle from `NUTRITION_APPROVED` to `ACTIVE`, and it carries no payload. Four things must hold. The cycle must be in `NUTRITION_APPROVED`. The service contract of the cycle's facility must itself be in `ACTIVE`, otherwise the attempt is rejected with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `nutrition_signed_by_user_id`, per §2.8, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. And the cost gate must clear: `approved_cost_cents_per_meal_day`, recomputed against the facility's *current* `satellite_site_count`, must be at or below the contract's `cost_ceiling_cents_per_meal_day`, otherwise `ERR_COST_CEILING_EXCEEDED`. A facility that has grown a third satellite site since the cycle was signed can therefore fail activation on a cycle that passed signing.

On success the cycle's status becomes `ACTIVE`, `activated_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, `activated_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `version` is incremented by 1. One cascade hop follows: because this cycle became `ACTIVE`, whichever other cycle at the same facility was previously `ACTIVE` transitions `ACTIVE` → `RETIRED` and has its own `version` incremented by 1. There is at most one such cycle, and this rule is what guarantees it. `RETIRED` is terminal — a retired cycle can never be signed or activated again.

### 3.3. The Roster, Diet Orders and Two-Person Verification

A `CorrectionalOfficer` books each inmate into a facility. The booking payload names the `facility_id` of custody, a `booking_number` that must be unique among inmates at that facility (a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`), and a `housing_unit` of `GENERAL`, `SEGREGATION`, `SATELLITE` or `MEDICAL` — the feeding-location class that decides which forecast bucket this head lands in. `religious_affiliation` is optional free text declaring an affiliation and defaults to the empty string, which means none declared. A booked inmate has `status` `IN_CUSTODY` and `active_diet_order_count` 0. The inmate listing accepts `facility_id`, `housing_unit` and `status` filters.

Roster movement is a single officer-driven operation carrying a `movement_type` of `RELEASE`, `TRANSFER` or `HOUSING_CHANGE`, plus an optional `housing_unit` used only by the last of those. A `RELEASE` sets the inmate's `status` to `RELEASED`; a `TRANSFER` sets it to `TRANSFERRED`; a `HOUSING_CHANGE` leaves `status` untouched and instead replaces `housing_unit` with the supplied value. The inmate's `version` is incremented by 1 in all three cases. Movement then ripples outward in three directions.

On a `RELEASE` or a `TRANSFER`, every diet order for that inmate sitting in `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` transitions to `REVOKED`, each with its own `version` incremented by 1. For each order that had been `ACTIVE`, and only for those, `Inmate.active_diet_order_count` is decremented by 1 and `Facility.active_special_diet_count` is decremented by 1; orders that were merely pending or suspended were never counted, so their revocation moves neither counter. On a `HOUSING_CHANGE` whose destination is `SEGREGATION`, the inmate's kitchen worker record — if one exists — has its `clearance_status` moved from `CLEARED` to `SUSPENDED`, its `assignment_status` set to `UNASSIGNED` and its `work_area` cleared to null, with `version` incremented by 1: an inmate in segregation cannot work the line. And on any movement at all, every meal forecast at that facility still in `DRAFT` whose `forecast_variant` is `ROSTER_DRIVEN` transitions to `SUPERSEDED` with `version` incremented by 1, because the roster it was computed from has just changed. Count-driven drafts are untouched by roster movement; §3.4 explains why.

A `RegisteredDietitian` raises every **diet order**. The payload names the `inmate_id`, an `order_type` of `RELIGIOUS` or `MEDICAL`, a `diet_code`, the window bounds `effective_from` and `effective_to`, and the mandatory `expected_inmate_version` described in §2.6. Two optional fields complete it: `clinical_indication`, free-text clinical justification, and `supersedes_diet_order_id`, naming the incumbent order this one is intended to replace, which is null for a first order. The `diet_code` vocabulary is `KOSHER`, `HALAL`, `VEGETARIAN_RELIGIOUS`, `RENAL`, `DIABETIC_2000`, `GLUTEN_FREE` and `MECHANICAL_SOFT`, and the chosen code must belong to the family implied by `order_type` — the first three are religious accommodations and the last four are clinical therapeutic diets. A code from the wrong family is outside the field's admissible domain for that request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`. `clinical_indication` is where the clinical justification lives for a `MEDICAL` order; it must be omitted or left empty on a `RELIGIOUS` one.

The window is validated before anything else about the diet is considered. `effective_from` must fall strictly before `effective_to`, and `effective_to` must fall strictly after the logical clock's current instant — an order that has already expired cannot be raised. Either failure reports `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. The inmate must be `IN_CUSTODY`; naming a released or transferred inmate reports `ERR_INMATE_NOT_IN_CUSTODY`. When `supersedes_diet_order_id` is supplied, the order it names must belong to the same inmate and carry the same `order_type`; a cross-inmate or cross-family supersede is rejected with `ERR_FACILITY_MISMATCH`, and an id naming nothing at all is `ERR_DIET_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`.

Coverage windows are then tested for collision. A diet order's coverage is the **half-open** interval that starts at `effective_from` inclusive and ends at `effective_to` exclusive. Two orders collide when the new order's `effective_from` falls strictly before the existing order's `effective_to` and the existing order's `effective_from` falls strictly before the new order's `effective_to`. The set of candidates is narrow: only orders for the same inmate, only orders of the same `order_type`, and only orders whose `status` is `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`. Orders in `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED` never collide with anything. The single order named by `supersedes_diet_order_id` is exempt from the test, so a legitimate supersede is always permitted. If any candidate remains after those exclusions, the create is rejected with `ERR_DIET_ORDER_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Half-open semantics matter at the seam: an incumbent running from the first of August to the tenth of August exclusive, and a new order running from the tenth of August to the twentieth, are adjacent rather than overlapping, and both may exist; backing the new order up by a single second creates a conflict.

A created order lands in `PENDING_VERIFICATION` with `requested_by_user_id` recording the raising dietitian's `X-User-ID`, `verified_by_user_id`, `verified_at` and `verification_expires_at` all null, `reverification_count` at 0 and `suspension_reason` null. The create also increments the named inmate's `version` by 1, because the inmate's diet picture has changed even though `active_diet_order_count` does not move until verification. Diet orders are readable individually and in a listing that accepts `inmate_id`, `supersedes_diet_order_id`, `status` and `order_type` filters — the third of these makes it easy to find every order superseded by a given one.

Verification is the two-person step that puts a diet on the tray, moving the order from `PENDING_VERIFICATION` to `ACTIVE`. It accepts an optional `verification_note`. The verifying role must match the order family: a `RELIGIOUS` order is verified by a `ChaplainDesignee` and a `MEDICAL` order by a `MedicalProvider`. Crossing those wires — a chaplain attesting a renal diet, or a provider attesting a kosher one — is rejected with `ERR_VERIFIER_ROLE_MISMATCH` and HTTP 403. A `MEDICAL` order must carry a non-empty `clinical_indication` by the time it is verified; a blank one is `ERR_CLINICAL_INDICATION_REQUIRED` with HTTP 422. The inmate must still be `IN_CUSTODY`, otherwise `ERR_INMATE_NOT_IN_CUSTODY`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `requested_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. And the logical clock must still read strictly before the order's `effective_to`; a window that closed while the order waited in the queue cannot be verified, and the attempt reports `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`.

On success the order becomes `ACTIVE`, `verified_by_user_id` records the verifier, `verified_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `version` is incremented by 1. `verification_expires_at` is `verified_at` plus a validity window that depends on the family: a religious accommodation is good for 180 days, a therapeutic diet for only 30, reflecting how much faster clinical facts go stale. `Inmate.active_diet_order_count` is incremented by 1 and `Facility.active_special_diet_count` is incremented by 1.

When the verified order carries a `supersedes_diet_order_id`, one further cascade hop runs in the same operation: the incumbent order transitions `ACTIVE` → `REVOKED` with its own `version` incremented by 1. Because the incumbent has left `ACTIVE`, a second hop then decrements `Inmate.active_diet_order_count` by 1 and `Facility.active_special_diet_count` by 1. The increment and the decrement cancel exactly, so both counters read the same value after a superseding verification as they did before it — this is the compensating invariant that keeps an inmate's active order count pinned at no more than 1. A verification with no supersede simply raises both counters by exactly 1.

Both census counters are defined as counts over source rows and must always agree with those counts, never with a blindly maintained running total. `Inmate.active_diet_order_count` is the number of diet orders for that inmate whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. `Facility.active_special_diet_count` is the number of `ACTIVE` diet orders whose inmate sits at that facility *and* is still `IN_CUSTODY`. Orders in `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, `SUSPENDED`, `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED` are excluded from both. The custody clause makes the two counters legitimately disagree after a release or transfer: an inmate who leaves custody drops straight out of the facility census, while their own `active_diet_order_count` is unchanged by the departure itself.

Three operations take an order back out of service. Suspension, available to a `FoodServiceSupervisor` or a `CorrectionalOfficer` and typically used after a contraband or refusal finding, moves an `ACTIVE` order to `SUSPENDED`, records the mandatory `suspension_reason` from the payload, increments `version` by 1, and decrements both census counters by 1. Reverification, a `RegisteredDietitian` action taking no payload, moves a `SUSPENDED` order back to `PENDING_VERIFICATION`: `reverification_count` is incremented by 1, `verified_by_user_id`, `verified_at`, `verification_expires_at` and `suspension_reason` are all cleared to null, `requested_by_user_id` is overwritten with the requesting operator's `X-User-ID`, and `version` is incremented by 1. This edge closes a genuine cycle — `PENDING_VERIFICATION` → `ACTIVE` → `SUSPENDED` → `PENDING_VERIFICATION` — but it is bounded: an order whose `reverification_count` has already reached 2 cannot go round again, and the attempt reports `ERR_REVERIFICATION_LIMIT_REACHED`. Revocation, also a `RegisteredDietitian` action, is available from `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, from `ACTIVE` and from `SUSPENDED`; it takes a mandatory `revocation_reason`, which is stored in the order's `suspension_reason` field, sets `status` to `REVOKED` and increments `version` by 1. Only when the revoked order had been `ACTIVE` are the two census counters decremented by 1.

The fourth exit is automatic. A verification does not last forever, and an `ACTIVE` order whose `verification_expires_at` is at or before the logical clock's current instant transitions to `EXPIRED` with `version` incremented by 1, `Inmate.active_diet_order_count` decremented by 1 and `Facility.active_special_diet_count` decremented by 1. This sweep is not a background job: it runs at exactly one point, when a new meal forecast is built, it is scoped to the facility that forecast is for, and it runs **before** that forecast counts special diet trays. §3.4 depends on that ordering.

### 3.4. Counting Heads and Forecasting Trays

Population feeds arrive from security, not from the kitchen. A `CorrectionalOfficer` submits a **population count** for a facility broken out by housing segment: `general_population`, `segregation_population`, `satellite_population` and `medical_population`, each of which must be at least 0, plus a `source` of `FORMAL_COUNT` for a standing count or `EMERGENCY_COUNT` for an unscheduled one. The system computes `total_population` as the plain sum of the four segments, stamps `count_at` from the logical clock — the instant that drives forecast staleness downstream — and records `submitted_by_user_id`. A population count is an immutable ledger row: it has no `updated_at` and no `version`, and no operation ever changes one. Counts are readable individually and through a listing filtered by `facility_id` and `source`.

A **meal forecast** converts one count feed into a tray number for one service. A `FoodServiceSupervisor` builds it, naming the `facility_id` being forecast, the `population_count_id` this forecast is provenance-linked to, the `service_date` as an ISO 8601 calendar date in year-month-day form, the `meal_slot` of `BREAKFAST`, `LUNCH` or `DINNER`, and the `forecast_variant` that decides which algorithm runs. At most one forecast per facility, service date and meal slot may exist in `DRAFT` or `LOCKED` at any time; a second attempt while one of those is live is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FORECAST`. A forecast that has been superseded no longer blocks a replacement.

Before a single tray is counted, the expiry sweep of §3.3 runs across that facility's diet orders. This ordering is deliberate and observable: an order whose verification lapsed yesterday must not be counted as a special diet tray today.

Tray demand is then built segment by segment, and every division or rate multiplication is closed with a ceiling **per segment**, before the segments are summed. The serving-line base is the general population plus the medical population — medical housing eats off the line even though it is fed in place. That base is multiplied by a participation rate of 94 percent, because not every head presents at the servery, and the product is rounded up to a whole tray. Segregation is different: every head in segregation is served a mandatory in-cell tray, so the segregation tray count is exactly the segregation population with no participation discount at all. Satellite sites carry a 3 percent transport loss, so the satellite tray count is the satellite population multiplied by one plus that loss rate, again rounded up to a whole tray. `line_trays` is the sum of those three segment figures.

`waste_allowance_trays` is then added on top, selected from a tier ladder driven by `line_trays` and by `line_trays` alone. A line demand of 500 trays or fewer takes a flat allowance of 8 trays. A line demand above 500 and up to 2000 takes 2 percent of `line_trays`, rounded up to a whole tray. A line demand above 2000 takes 1.5 percent of `line_trays`, rounded up. Because the tier is chosen from `line_trays` and never from the combined figure, the allowance can never feed back into its own tier selection. `total_forecast_trays` is `line_trays` plus `waste_allowance_trays`.

`forecast_special_diet_trays` is counted separately and is not part of the line arithmetic. It is the number of diet orders whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose inmate is `IN_CUSTODY` at this forecast's facility, and whose coverage window contains the service date treated as midnight UTC on that date: the order's `effective_from` must be at or before that instant and its `effective_to` must be strictly after it. The window is half-open here exactly as it is at create time, so an order whose `effective_to` lands precisely on the service date's midnight does not cover that service.

The two variants differ only in where the four segment populations come from, and identical fixtures diverge whenever the roster and the count disagree. A `COUNT_DRIVEN` forecast — the standard path — reads the four population fields off the referenced population count row. Because that row is immutable, the forecast stays pinned to the count feed even if the roster later changes, and this is precisely why roster movement does not supersede a count-driven draft. A `ROSTER_DRIVEN` forecast is used when the count feed has gone stale and the roster of record governs: it ignores the stored numbers entirely and recounts inmates at calculation time, taking every inmate at the facility whose `status` is `IN_CUSTODY` and bucketing them by `housing_unit` — `GENERAL` into the general segment, `SEGREGATION` into the segregation segment, `SATELLITE` into the satellite segment and `MEDICAL` into the medical segment. A population count reference is still mandatory on a roster-driven forecast for provenance, but its numbers are never read. Both variants use the identical diet-order rule for `forecast_special_diet_trays`.

A new forecast opens in `DRAFT` with `locked_at` null. Locking it, a supervisor action with no payload, freezes it for production and moves it to `LOCKED`, stamping `locked_at` from the logical clock and incrementing `version` by 1. Two gates guard the lock. The facility must have a menu cycle in `ACTIVE`, because a locked forecast is a commitment to cook something and there must be a menu of record to cook; a facility with no active cycle reports `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_MENU_CYCLE`. And the forecast's `service_date` must not be earlier than the logical clock's own calendar date at the moment of locking; locking a forecast for a service that has already passed reports `ERR_FORECAST_DATE_PAST`. Locking the forecast for today is permitted. Both `LOCKED` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal for a forecast: neither can be locked again, and a superseded draft can never be rescued. Forecasts are readable individually and through a listing accepting `facility_id`, `population_count_id`, `status` and `service_date` filters.

### 3.5. Cook-Chill Production, HACCP Logging and the Parallel QA Axis

A **production batch** is one recipe component cooked once against one locked forecast, and it carries two independent state machines that interlock only where this specification says so. The production axis, `production_stage`, runs `PLANNED` → `COOKED` → `CHILLED` → `STAGED` → `SERVED` with `DISCARDED` as an alternative terminus. The quality axis, `qa_status`, runs from `QA_PENDING` to `QA_PASSED` or `QA_HELD` and ultimately to the terminal `QA_CONDEMNED`. A batch may sit in `QA_PASSED` while its production axis still reads `COOKED`; the axes evolve independently.

A `FoodServiceSupervisor` schedules a batch, supplying the `facility_id` running it, the `meal_forecast_id` it is produced against, the `recipe_component` being cooked, `planned_trays` (which must be greater than 0), the `scheduled_cook_start_at` instant that anchors chiller occupancy, a `service_site` of `MAIN_KITCHEN`, `SATELLITE_A` or `SEGREGATION_UNIT`, and the mandatory `expected_facility_version` of §2.6. The named forecast must be in `LOCKED` — a draft or superseded forecast reports `ERR_FORECAST_NOT_LOCKED` — and the supplied `facility_id` must equal the forecast's own facility, otherwise `ERR_FACILITY_MISMATCH`. The facility's `kitchen_posture` must read `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_KITCHEN_POSTURE_BLOCKED`.

Chiller occupancy is the physical constraint. Each batch occupies the blast chiller for a fixed six hours from its scheduled cook start — 360 minutes — after which `chill_deadline_at` falls, and then for a further half hour of sanitation before the equipment is genuinely free, giving `buffered_release_at` 30 minutes after `chill_deadline_at`. A batch's occupancy is the **closed** interval running from `scheduled_cook_start_at` through `buffered_release_at` inclusive at both ends. A new batch overlaps an existing one when the new batch's `scheduled_cook_start_at` is at or before that batch's `buffered_release_at` and that batch's `scheduled_cook_start_at` is at or before the new batch's `buffered_release_at`. Only live batches count: existing batches at the same facility whose `production_stage` is `PLANNED`, `COOKED` or `CHILLED` are candidates, while batches in `STAGED`, `SERVED` or `DISCARDED` have released the equipment and are excluded — which means discarding a batch immediately frees its slot. If the number of overlapping live batches is equal to or greater than the facility's `blast_chiller_capacity`, the create is rejected with `ERR_CHILLER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; it succeeds only when that count is strictly less than the capacity. Note the contrast with diet coverage windows, which are half-open: here, endpoints that merely touch **do** overlap, so a batch scheduled to start at the exact instant another is released collides with it.

The tray budget is the fiscal constraint. The sum of `planned_trays` across every batch tied to the same forecast whose `production_stage` is not `DISCARDED`, counting the batch now being created, may not exceed that forecast's `total_forecast_trays`. Exceeding it reports `ERR_TRAY_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`.

A created batch opens with `production_stage` `PLANNED`, `qa_status` `QA_PENDING`, `produced_trays` 0, `cook_end_at`, `chill_final_at`, `chill_minutes` and `qa_reason` all null, `cook_chill_compliant` false, and `chill_deadline_at` and `buffered_release_at` derived as above. It also increments the facility's `version` by 1, because the facility's chiller occupancy has changed. Batches are readable individually and through a listing accepting `facility_id`, `meal_forecast_id`, `production_stage` and `qa_status` filters.

**Temperature readings** are the evidentiary spine of cook-chill. A supervisor posts each one against a batch with a `log_point` naming the HACCP control point, a `temperature_f` reading in degrees Fahrenheit to one decimal place which must lie between -40.0 and 500.0 inclusive, and a `site` of `MAIN_KITCHEN`, `SATELLITE_A` or `SEGREGATION_UNIT` recording where the probe was taken. The six control points are `COOK_END`, `CHILL_INTERMEDIATE`, `CHILL_FINAL`, `TRANSPORT_DEPART`, `TRANSPORT_ARRIVE` and `REHEAT_SERVE`. The system records the `production_batch_id` of the batch the reading belongs to, stamps `logged_at` from the logical clock — never from the client — and records `logged_by_user_id`. Like population counts, temperature readings are immutable: they have no `updated_at` and no `version`, and nothing ever edits one.

Readings must arrive in physical order. Every control point other than `COOK_END` requires an existing `COOK_END` reading on the batch; `TRANSPORT_ARRIVE` additionally requires an existing `TRANSPORT_DEPART` reading; and `REHEAT_SERVE` additionally requires an existing `TRANSPORT_ARRIVE` reading whenever the batch's `service_site` is not `MAIN_KITCHEN`. A reading posted before its prerequisite is rejected with `ERR_LOG_POINT_OUT_OF_SEQUENCE`. No reading of any kind may be posted against a batch whose `production_stage` is `DISCARDED`; that attempt reports `ERR_BATCH_DISCARDED` and is refused before the range evaluation below ever runs.

`minutes_since_cook_end` is null for the `COOK_END` reading itself; for every other reading it is the whole number of minutes from the batch's `cook_end_at` to this reading's `logged_at`, computed as the elapsed seconds divided by 60 and floored. `in_range` is then decided per control point, and every comparison is inclusive at its limit — exactly 80.0 degrees at exactly 120 minutes is in range, while 80.1 degrees or 121 minutes is not:

- A `COOK_END` reading is in range when `temperature_f` is at least 165.0.
- A `CHILL_INTERMEDIATE` reading is in range when `temperature_f` is at or below 80.0 **and** `minutes_since_cook_end` is at most 120.
- A `CHILL_FINAL` reading is in range when `temperature_f` is at or below 40.0 **and** `minutes_since_cook_end` is at most 360.
- A `TRANSPORT_DEPART` reading is in range when `temperature_f` is at or below the cold-chain limit of 41.0.
- A `TRANSPORT_ARRIVE` reading is in range on the same cold-chain limit of 41.0 or below.
- A `REHEAT_SERVE` reading is in range when `temperature_f` is at least 165.0 **and** the whole number of minutes from the batch's most recent `TRANSPORT_ARRIVE` reading to this one is at most 30. "Most recent" means the greatest `logged_at`, and where two arrivals share an instant the one with the earliest `created_at` wins. When the batch has no `TRANSPORT_ARRIVE` reading at all, the elapsed-time clause is treated as satisfied and only the temperature gate applies.

Posting a reading has three further consequences. The batch's `cook_end_at` or `chill_final_at` is set when the corresponding control point is posted. `chill_minutes` and `cook_chill_compliant` are recomputed. And an out-of-range reading drives the QA axis: an out-of-range `CHILL_INTERMEDIATE` or `CHILL_FINAL` reading moves `qa_status` to `QA_HELD` with `qa_reason` set to the literal string `CHILL_GATE_FAILED`, applicable when the batch's `qa_status` is `QA_PENDING` or `QA_PASSED`; an out-of-range `TRANSPORT_ARRIVE` or `REHEAT_SERVE` reading is far more serious and moves `qa_status` straight to `QA_CONDEMNED` with `qa_reason` set to the literal string `TRANSPORT_REHEAT_GATE_FAILED`, applicable from any `qa_status` other than `QA_CONDEMNED` itself. Each such move increments the batch's `version` by 1, and the condemnation carries its own cascade hop: because `qa_status` reached `QA_CONDEMNED`, `production_stage` transitions to `DISCARDED` unless the batch had already reached `SERVED`.

`cook_chill_compliant` is a pure function of the readings posted so far. `chill_minutes` is null while either `cook_end_at` or `chill_final_at` is null, and otherwise is the whole floored number of minutes from `cook_end_at` to `chill_final_at`. The flag is true if and only if all three of these hold: the batch has some `COOK_END` reading with `in_range` true, the batch has some `CHILL_INTERMEDIATE` reading with `in_range` true, and the batch has some `CHILL_FINAL` reading with `in_range` true. This is an existence test rather than a universal one, so a later in-range reading at the same control point rehabilitates an earlier out-of-range one for the purposes of the flag — but the out-of-range reading has already pushed `qa_status` to `QA_HELD`, and only an explicit QA verdict can leave `QA_HELD`. The two anchor instants are chosen deterministically: `cook_end_at` is the `logged_at` of the batch's earliest `COOK_END` reading, and `chill_final_at` is the `logged_at` of the earliest in-range `CHILL_FINAL` reading, or when no `CHILL_FINAL` reading is in range, the `logged_at` of the earliest `CHILL_FINAL` reading of any kind. Ties on `logged_at` are broken by earliest `created_at`. The per-batch reading listing accepts a `log_point` filter.

Advancing the production axis is a one-step-at-a-time supervisor action naming the destination in `to_stage`, with `produced_trays` supplied only when moving to `COOKED`. Skipping a step or reversing one is rejected with `ERR_ILLEGAL_TRANSITION`. Every advance is barred outright while `qa_status` reads `QA_CONDEMNED`. Each step has its own prerequisite:

- Moving `PLANNED` → `COOKED` requires an existing `COOK_END` reading with `in_range` true, otherwise `ERR_COOK_LOG_MISSING`, and requires `produced_trays` to be present in the payload and greater than 0. `produced_trays` is written onto the batch at this step and records the trays actually produced as against the trays planned.
- Moving `COOKED` → `CHILLED` requires an existing `CHILL_FINAL` reading — in range or not — otherwise `ERR_CHILL_LOG_MISSING`. This step also recomputes `cook_chill_compliant`.
- Moving `CHILLED` → `STAGED` requires `qa_status` to read exactly `QA_PASSED`, otherwise `ERR_QA_NOT_PASSED`. This is the one hard cross-dimension guard: the production axis cannot pass `STAGED` until the QA axis has cleared.
- Moving `STAGED` → `SERVED` also requires `qa_status` to read `QA_PASSED`, again reporting `ERR_QA_NOT_PASSED` otherwise, and for a batch whose `service_site` is `SATELLITE_A` or `SEGREGATION_UNIT` it additionally requires an existing `REHEAT_SERVE` reading with `in_range` true, otherwise `ERR_REHEAT_LOG_MISSING`. A `MAIN_KITCHEN` batch needs no reheat evidence because it never left the kitchen.

Every successful advance increments the batch's `version` by 1.

QA verdicts move the other axis and may be recorded by a `FoodServiceSupervisor` or a `RegisteredDietitian`, naming a `qa_decision` and a mandatory `reason` that is stored in `qa_reason`. A `QA_PASSED` verdict may be recorded from `QA_PENDING` or from `QA_HELD`, and only when `cook_chill_compliant` is true; a batch whose three HACCP readings are not all present and in range is rejected with `ERR_COOK_CHILL_NONCOMPLIANT`. A `QA_HELD` verdict may be recorded from `QA_PENDING` or from `QA_PASSED`, pulling a previously passed batch back for review. A `QA_CONDEMNED` verdict may be recorded from `QA_PENDING`, `QA_HELD` or `QA_PASSED`, and cascades: because `qa_status` reached `QA_CONDEMNED`, `production_stage` transitions to `DISCARDED` unless the batch had already reached `SERVED`, with the batch's `version` incremented for the operation. `QA_CONDEMNED` is terminal on the QA axis and no verdict can move a batch out of it.

### 3.6. Kitchen Labour, Tool Control and Contraband

Inmate labour runs the kitchen, and a **kitchen worker** record is the security wrapper around that labour. A `FoodServiceSupervisor` enrols one, naming the `inmate_id` holding the detail, the `facility_id` whose kitchen the detail is at, the `medical_clearance_expires_at` instant at which the food-handler medical clearance lapses, and optionally `sharps_eligible`, which defaults to false and governs whether this worker may ever hold a knife. The named facility must be the inmate's own facility, otherwise `ERR_FACILITY_MISMATCH`. An inmate may hold at most one worker record: a second enrolment for the same `inmate_id` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. A new worker opens with `clearance_status` `CLEARED`, `assignment_status` `UNASSIGNED`, `work_area` null and `contraband_strike_count` 0. Workers are readable individually and through a listing accepting `inmate_id`, `facility_id`, `clearance_status` and `assignment_status` filters.

A worker carries two orthogonal axes. `clearance_status` is the security verdict — `CLEARED`, `SUSPENDED` or `BARRED`, with `BARRED` terminal — and `assignment_status` is the operational one, either `UNASSIGNED` or `ASSIGNED`. `work_area` is null while unassigned and otherwise names one of `BAKERY`, `COOK_CHILL`, `SERVING_LINE` or `SANITATION`.

Assigning a worker to a `work_area` is a supervisor action that moves `assignment_status` to `ASSIGNED`, writes the requested area and increments `version` by 1. Four gates guard it, all evaluated in the ladder order of §2.5: the facility's `kitchen_posture` must read `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_KITCHEN_POSTURE_BLOCKED`; the worker's `clearance_status` must read `CLEARED`, otherwise `ERR_WORKER_NOT_CLEARED`; `medical_clearance_expires_at` must be strictly after the logical clock's current instant, so a clearance expiring at exactly this instant has already lapsed and reports `ERR_MEDICAL_CLEARANCE_EXPIRED`; and the worker must have no contraband incident in `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_OPEN_CONTRABAND_HOLD`. A worker already `ASSIGNED` cannot be assigned again. Workers are never unassigned by a client request — they are pulled off detail only by the system, when a lockdown discards their batches, when a contraband sanction lands, or when the inmate is moved.

**Tool issuance** is the sharps-control ledger, and it is governed by a conservation identity that must hold at every instant: for each facility, `sharps_on_board_count` plus `sharps_issued_count` plus `sharps_unaccounted_count` equals `sharps_authorized_count`. Only the three sharp classes participate in the sharps counters — `SHARP_KNIFE`, `MEAT_SAW` and `SCISSORS` — while a `NON_SHARP` tool is tracked as an issuance but is excluded from every sharps counter. `sharps_issued_count` is the number of issuances at the facility of a sharp class in status `ISSUED`; `sharps_unaccounted_count` is the number of a sharp class in status `UNACCOUNTED`; `open_sharps_discrepancy_count` is `sharps_unaccounted_count` plus `sharps_audit_shortfall`. Rows in `RETURNED` contribute to neither counter, whatever the `recovered` flag says. `sharps_on_board_count` is maintained as the residual: it starts equal to `sharps_authorized_count` when the facility is registered, falls by 1 on each issue, rises by 1 on each return including a recovery return out of `UNACCOUNTED`, and is overwritten wholesale by a physical audit.

A supervisor signs a tool out by naming the `facility_id` whose shadow board it hangs on, the `issued_to_worker_id` receiving it, the `tool_class`, the etched `tool_serial`, and the `shadow_board_slot`, which must be at least 1. The facility must be the worker's own facility, otherwise `ERR_FACILITY_MISMATCH`. Two scoped uniqueness rules apply, both reported as `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`: `tool_serial` must be unique among that facility's issuances in `ISSUED` or `UNACCOUNTED`, and `shadow_board_slot` must be unique among that facility's issuances in `ISSUED`. A returned tool releases both its serial and its slot.

Issuing a tool runs the widest gate set in the system, and the order matters. First the posture gate of §2.9 applies in its carved-out form: on this endpoint alone the posture is evaluated only against the audit-shortfall clause, the corrective-action clause and the contraband-threshold clause, and **not** against the two unaccounted-tool clauses, reporting `ERR_KITCHEN_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. The unaccounted-tool condition is then reported one priority later, and more specifically: if any issuance at the facility is in `UNACCOUNTED` — of any tool class, sharp or not — the issue is rejected with `ERR_SHARPS_DISCREPANCY_OPEN`. The facility's `kitchen_posture` itself is unaffected by this carve-out; it still reads `LOCKDOWN` or `RESTRICTED`, and only the error reported changes. Next, a tool of a sharp class may only go to a worker whose `sharps_eligible` is true, otherwise `ERR_WORKER_NOT_SHARPS_ELIGIBLE`; a `NON_SHARP` tool needs no such eligibility. Then the same three worker gates as assignment: `clearance_status` must be `CLEARED` (`ERR_WORKER_NOT_CLEARED`), `medical_clearance_expires_at` must be strictly after the logical clock's current instant (`ERR_MEDICAL_CLEARANCE_EXPIRED`), and the worker must carry no `OPEN` contraband incident (`ERR_OPEN_CONTRABAND_HOLD`).

A created issuance opens in `ISSUED` with `issued_by_user_id` set to the acting user and `issued_at` stamped from the logical clock, `returned_at`, `return_certified_by_user_id` and `unaccounted_at` all null, and `recovered` false. For a sharp class it increments the facility's `sharps_issued_count` and decrements `sharps_on_board_count`; it then recomputes `kitchen_posture` and increments the facility's `version` by 1. Issuances are readable individually, through a listing accepting `facility_id`, `issued_to_worker_id`, `status` and `tool_class` filters, and through a per-facility listing accepting a `status` filter that includes recovered and unaccounted rows.

**Certifying a return** is a `CorrectionalOfficer` action carrying `returned_count_verified`, which must be present and must equal 1 — the officer is attesting that exactly one tool came back — and which is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` otherwise. The certifying user may not be the issuing user: an attempt by the holder of `issued_by_user_id` is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` per §2.8. A return moves `status` to `RETURNED`, stamps `returned_at`, records `return_certified_by_user_id` and increments `version` by 1. It then restores the facility counters so that the conservation identity holds again — for a sharp class the issued or unaccounted counter falls by 1 and `sharps_on_board_count` rises by 1 — recomputes `kitchen_posture` and increments the facility's `version` by 1. `RETURNED` is terminal: a tool cannot be returned twice.

**Declaring a tool unaccounted** is the emergency path, taken by an officer at a shift-end count that comes up short, and it carries a `narrative` recording the circumstances. It may only be applied to an issuance in `ISSUED`. It moves `status` to `UNACCOUNTED`, stamps `unaccounted_at` and increments `version` by 1, and then cascades four hops deep, with each hop caused by the hop before it:

1. Because the tool left `ISSUED` for `UNACCOUNTED`, for a sharp class the facility's `sharps_unaccounted_count` rises by 1 and `open_sharps_discrepancy_count` rises by 1. For a `NON_SHARP` tool neither sharps counter moves at all. The facility's `version` is incremented by 1.
2. Because a discrepancy is now open, `kitchen_posture` is recomputed. For a sharp class the first waterfall clause fires and the posture becomes `LOCKDOWN`. For a `NON_SHARP` tool the sharp clauses do not fire and the fifth clause does, so the posture becomes `RESTRICTED` instead. This is the point at which the two branches genuinely diverge, and everything below follows from it.
3. Because the posture reached `LOCKDOWN`, every production batch at that facility whose `production_stage` is `PLANNED`, `COOKED` or `CHILLED` becomes `DISCARDED` with `qa_status` set to `QA_HELD`, each with its own `version` incremented by 1. Under a merely `RESTRICTED` posture — the `NON_SHARP` branch — no batch is touched, because a missing spatula does not contaminate food.
4. Because those batches became `DISCARDED`, every worker at that facility whose `assignment_status` is `ASSIGNED` and whose `work_area` is `COOK_CHILL` or `SERVING_LINE` becomes `UNASSIGNED` with `work_area` null and `version` incremented by 1. Workers in `BAKERY` or `SANITATION` keep their details. On the `NON_SHARP` branch, where hop 3 did not fire, no worker is unassigned.

Independently of that hop chain, and on both branches, a contraband incident is automatically raised as a fan-out: at this facility, with `related_tool_issuance_id` naming this issuance and `inmate_worker_id` copied from `issued_to_worker_id`, with `item_class` set to `SHARP` for a sharp class and `FOOD_DIVERSION` for a `NON_SHARP` tool, `severity` set to `CRITICAL` for a sharp class and `MAJOR` otherwise, `status` `OPEN`, and `discovered_by_user_id` set to the literal string `system`. Because that incident is `OPEN`, the facility's `open_contraband_incident_count` rises by 1 as well.

Returning a tool that is in `UNACCOUNTED` is the **compensating action** for a declaration, and it is the same operation as an ordinary return with the same `returned_count_verified` payload and the same segregation-of-duties rule. It additionally sets `recovered` to true, which is how a recovered tool is distinguished from one that never went missing. It decrements `sharps_unaccounted_count` and `open_sharps_discrepancy_count` for a sharp class, increments `sharps_on_board_count`, recomputes `kitchen_posture` — which returns to `OPEN` only when `open_sharps_discrepancy_count` is 0 and no other restricting condition holds — and increments the facility's `version` by 1. It deliberately does **not** undo hops 3 and 4: discarded food stays discarded and cleared work details stay cleared, because neither is recoverable in the physical world.

A **sharps audit** is a `CorrectionalOfficer` recording a physical shadow-board count as `counted_on_board`. Before it is accepted, the count must be reconcilable: `counted_on_board` plus `sharps_issued_count` plus `sharps_unaccounted_count` must not exceed `sharps_authorized_count`, because a kitchen cannot hold more sharps than it is authorized to hold; a count that exceeds the authorization is rejected with `ERR_SHARPS_COUNT_OVER_AUTHORIZED`. The audit then overwrites `sharps_on_board_count` with `counted_on_board` wholesale, sets `sharps_audit_shortfall` to `sharps_authorized_count` minus the sum of `counted_on_board`, `sharps_issued_count` and `sharps_unaccounted_count`, stamps `last_sharps_audit_at` from the logical clock, recomputes `kitchen_posture` and increments `version` by 1. A shortfall of 1 or more drives the posture to `LOCKDOWN` through the second waterfall clause, and it persists on the facility until the next audit overwrites it — clearing a shortfall requires another audit that finds the tools, not the passage of time. A clean audit that finds no shortfall still advances the facility's `version` and still rewrites `sharps_on_board_count`. The sharps conservation identity is also readable directly, as a computed view returning `sharps_authorized_count`, `sharps_on_board_count`, `sharps_issued_count`, `sharps_unaccounted_count`, `sharps_audit_shortfall`, `open_sharps_discrepancy_count` and `kitchen_posture` for one facility.

**Contraband incidents** may also be reported directly by an officer, naming the `facility_id` where the item was found, the `item_class` of `SHARP`, `YEAST_ALCOHOL`, `FOOD_DIVERSION` or `COMMISSARY_ITEM`, the `severity` of `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`, a `narrative`, and optionally an `inmate_worker_id` when the find is attributable to a worker and a `related_tool_issuance_id` when it arose from an issuance. Both optional links default to null; an unattributed find leaves `inmate_worker_id` null. A reported incident opens in `OPEN` with `sanction_type` null, `discovered_by_user_id` set to the acting user and `discovered_at` stamped from the logical clock, then increments the facility's `open_contraband_incident_count`, recomputes `kitchen_posture` — two or more open incidents drive the posture to `RESTRICTED` through the fourth waterfall clause — and increments the facility's `version` by 1. Incidents are readable individually and through a listing accepting `facility_id`, `inmate_worker_id`, `related_tool_issuance_id` and `status` filters.

**Dispositioning an incident** closes it out. The officer supplies a `disposition` of either `SANCTIONED` or `CLEARED`, plus a `sanction_type` that is required on the sanctioned branch and must be absent on the cleared one. The incident must be in `OPEN`; both `SANCTIONED` and `CLEARED` are terminal and a dispositioned incident cannot be dispositioned again. The dispositioning user may not be the reporting user: a match against `discovered_by_user_id` is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Because a system-raised incident carries the literal `system` as its reporter, any human officer may disposition it.

Both branches share a common core: `status` moves to the requested disposition, `disposition_by_user_id` records the acting user, the incident's `version` is incremented by 1, the facility's `open_contraband_incident_count` falls by 1, `kitchen_posture` is recomputed — a `RESTRICTED` posture returns to `OPEN` once the open-incident count falls below 2 and no other condition holds — and the facility's `version` is incremented by 1. From there the two branches diverge sharply.

On the **`SANCTIONED`** branch, `sanction_type` is written from the request as one of `WARNING`, `WORK_SUSPENSION`, `WORK_BAR` or `REFERRAL`, and the incident must be attributed: a sanctioned disposition of an incident whose `inmate_worker_id` is null is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, because there is no one to sanction. A disposition of `SANCTIONED` with no `sanction_type` supplied is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Then the worker cascade fires. Hop 1: because the incident became `SANCTIONED`, the worker's `contraband_strike_count` rises by 1. Hop 2: because that count reached the bar threshold of 2, or because `sanction_type` is `WORK_BAR` regardless of the count, the worker's `clearance_status` becomes `BARRED`; when `sanction_type` is `WORK_SUSPENSION` and the strike count is still below 2, the worker's `clearance_status` becomes `SUSPENDED` instead. In either of those outcomes the worker's `assignment_status` becomes `UNASSIGNED` with `work_area` set to null and `version` incremented by 1. A `SUSPENDED` worker whose second sanction lands moves on to `BARRED`, and `BARRED` is terminal — no operation ever restores clearance. A `WARNING` or `REFERRAL` sanction below the threshold raises the strike count without changing the clearance axis. This cascade deliberately does **not** touch the worker's outstanding tool issuances: a barred worker's tools remain in `ISSUED` and must still be physically certified back onto the board through a return.

On the **`CLEARED`** branch, `sanction_type` stays null, the worker's `contraband_strike_count` is left completely unchanged, and no clearance or assignment field moves. The two branches are otherwise structurally identical, and this divergence — strike and clearance cascade versus no worker effect at all — is the entire difference between them.

### 3.7. Recording Service, Documenting Substitutions and Weighting Missed Meals

A **meal service record** is the settlement document for one service: what was actually put on trays, measured against what the locked forecast committed to. A `FoodServiceSupervisor` opens one, naming the `facility_id`, the `meal_forecast_id` it settles, the `service_date`, the `meal_slot`, and the four served counts — `trays_served_general`, `trays_served_segregation`, `trays_served_satellite` and `special_diet_trays_served`, each of which must be at least 0.

Three gates apply. The named forecast must be in `LOCKED`; a record against a draft or superseded forecast is rejected with `ERR_FORECAST_NOT_LOCKED`. The record's `service_date` and `meal_slot` must both equal the forecast's own, because a service record settles the exact service its forecast committed to; a mismatch on either is rejected with `ERR_SERVICE_WINDOW_MISMATCH`. And at most one record may exist per `meal_forecast_id`, so a second attempt against the same forecast is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERVICE_RECORD`. The record's `facility_id` must also match the forecast's facility, otherwise `ERR_FACILITY_MISMATCH`.

Creating the record freezes `expected_service_trays` from the forecast, computed as the forecast's `total_forecast_trays` minus its `waste_allowance_trays` — the waste allowance is an operational buffer, not a service commitment, so it is never something the vendor is held to having served. That frozen value never moves again even if the forecast is somehow revisited. `total_trays_served` is computed as the sum of the three site tray counts, excluding `special_diet_trays_served`, which is tracked on its own axis. The record opens in `OPEN` with `missed_meal_count` 0, `substitution_count` 0, `dispute_tray_count` 0 and `closed_at` null; missed meals are not computed until close. Records are readable individually and through a listing accepting `facility_id`, `meal_forecast_id`, `status` and `service_date` filters.

While a record is `OPEN`, a supervisor may correct any subset of the four served tray counts. Each supplied count replaces the stored one, `total_trays_served` is recomputed from whatever the three site counts now read, and `version` is incremented by 1. A correction attempted against a record that is not `OPEN` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, because a closed record has already been posted onto the contract ledger and a disputed one is terminal.

**Closing** a record computes the weighted missed-meal count and posts it to the contract, and it carries the mandatory `expected_service_contract_version` of §2.6 because it writes the contract ledger. The record must be in `OPEN`, and no production batch tied to its forecast may still be in flight: if any batch against that forecast is in `PLANNED`, `COOKED`, `CHILLED` or `STAGED`, the close is rejected with `ERR_OPEN_BATCHES_REMAIN`. Only `SERVED` and `DISCARDED` batches are settled — a discarded batch is settled precisely because its trays simply never appear in the served counts, and there is no separate discard adjustment anywhere in the arithmetic.

The **weighted missed-meal count** is then computed. The tray shortfall is the greater of 0 and `expected_service_trays` minus `total_trays_served`. The tolerance is 2 percent of `expected_service_trays`, truncated toward zero to a whole number of trays — this is a floor, not a rounding, so at an expected count of 156 trays the tolerance is exactly 3. The special-diet shortfall is the greater of 0 and the forecast's `forecast_special_diet_trays` minus `special_diet_trays_served`. If the tray shortfall is at or within the tolerance **and** the special-diet shortfall is exactly 0, then `missed_meal_count` is 0 and the service is treated as fully performed. Otherwise `missed_meal_count` is the greater of 0 and the tray shortfall minus the tolerance, plus the special-diet shortfall multiplied by a weight of 3. The tolerance therefore absorbs general line shortfall only; a single unserved religious or therapeutic tray is never absorbed, and contributes 3 weighted units even when the general tray shortfall is comfortably inside tolerance. At an expected count of 156 with no special-diet shortfall, 153 trays served yields 0 while 152 yields 1.

Closing sets `missed_meal_count` from that calculation, moves `status` to `CLOSED`, stamps `closed_at` from the logical clock and increments `version` by 1. It then cascades three hops:

1. Because the record closed, the contract's `total_trays_served` rises by this record's `total_trays_served`, `meal_days_served` is recomputed, and `missed_meal_total` rises by this record's `missed_meal_count`. The whole liquidated-damages waterfall of §3.9 and `performance_score` are recomputed, and the contract's `version` is incremented by 1.
2. Because the contract's `missed_meal_total` reached the escalation threshold of 25 weighted missed meals, every facility under that contract has `corrective_action_required` set to true with `version` incremented by 1.
3. Because `corrective_action_required` became true, every such facility whose `kitchen_posture` reads `OPEN` moves to `RESTRICTED`. This hop is conditional: a facility already `RESTRICTED` or `LOCKDOWN` keeps its stricter posture, because the posture waterfall never relaxes a facility that has a graver condition open.

A closed record is not final. If a grievance is answered with a refeed remedy naming this record, the record moves from `CLOSED` to `DISPUTED`: `dispute_tray_count` is set from the grievance's `refeed_tray_count`, `missed_meal_count` rises by that same figure with no tolerance relief whatsoever — because a refeed is an admitted service failure, not a rounding difference — and `version` is incremented by 1. The contract's `missed_meal_total` rises by the same figure, its damages and `performance_score` are recomputed, and its `version` is incremented by 1. `DISPUTED` is terminal: such a record can neither be closed again nor re-disputed.

A **menu substitution** documents that something other than the planned component actually went on the tray. A supervisor records it against the `menu_cycle_item_id` naming the planned component and the `meal_service_record_id` naming the service it happened at, supplying the `substitute_component_name`, the substitute's `substitute_kcal_per_100g`, `substitute_protein_grams_per_100g` and `substitute_sodium_mg_per_100g` nutrition density figures, the `substitute_portion_grams` actually served, the `substitute_cost_cents`, and a `reason` of `SUPPLY_OUTAGE`, `EQUIPMENT_FAILURE`, `SECURITY_LOCKDOWN` or `QA_CONDEMNATION`. The named menu cycle item must belong to a menu cycle at the service record's own facility, otherwise `ERR_FACILITY_MISMATCH`.

The **nutrition equivalence** verdict is computed at documentation time, and the rounding order is load-bearing: each of the six nutrition figures is portion-scaled and rounded to 2 decimal places with half-up rounding **before** any delta is taken. The planned kilocalories are the item's `kcal_per_100g` multiplied by its `portion_grams` and divided by 100, rounded to 2 decimal places; the planned protein grams and planned sodium milligrams are computed the same way from `protein_grams_per_100g` and `sodium_mg_per_100g`. The substitute's three figures are computed identically from the substitute densities and `substitute_portion_grams`. Then `kcal_delta_pct` is the substitute kilocalories minus the planned kilocalories, multiplied by 100, divided by the planned kilocalories, rounded to 2 decimal places. `protein_delta_pct` is the substitute protein minus the planned protein, multiplied by 100, divided by the greater of 0.01 and the planned protein — the floor on the denominator exists so a zero-protein planned component cannot produce a division by zero — again rounded to 2 decimal places. `sodium_delta_mg` is simply the substitute sodium minus the planned sodium, rounded to 2 decimal places. `cost_variance_cents` is `substitute_cost_cents` minus the item's `raw_cost_cents`, and may be negative when the substitute is cheaper.

`nutrition_equivalent` is true if and only if all three of these hold, and every band is inclusive at its limit: the absolute value of `kcal_delta_pct` is at or below 10.00, `protein_delta_pct` is at or above negative 5.00, and `sodium_delta_mg` is at or below 200.00. Exactly 10.00, exactly negative 5.00 and exactly 200.00 are all equivalent; 10.01, negative 5.01 and 200.01 are not. Note the asymmetry between the bands: kilocalories are bounded in both directions because both underfeeding and overfeeding breach the standard, protein is bounded only from below because more protein is never a nutritional problem, and sodium is bounded only from above for the same reason in reverse. The status of the item's own menu cycle is irrelevant to the equivalence arithmetic. A documented substitution opens in `approval_status` `PENDING` with `documented_by_user_id` set to the acting user, `decided_by_user_id` null, and all four deltas plus the verdict computed as above. Substitutions are readable individually and through a listing accepting `menu_cycle_item_id`, `meal_service_record_id` and `approval_status` filters.

**Deciding** a substitution is a `RegisteredDietitian` action carrying a `decision` of `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` and an optional `decision_note`. The substitution must be in `PENDING`; both `APPROVED` and `REJECTED` are terminal. The deciding user may not be the documenting user, so a match against `documented_by_user_id` is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Approval carries one further gate: a substitution may only be approved when `nutrition_equivalent` is true, **or** when its `reason` is `QA_CONDEMNATION`. That single carve-out exists because condemned food must be replaced with whatever is available and a nutritionally imperfect meal is better than no meal; an out-of-band substitution for any other reason is rejected with `ERR_SUBSTITUTION_NOT_EQUIVALENT`. Rejection has no equivalence gate at all — a nutritionally equivalent substitution may still be rejected on other grounds.

Both branches set `approval_status` to the decision, record `decided_by_user_id` and increment `version` by 1. Beyond that they diverge completely. On the **`APPROVED`** branch the service record's `substitution_count` rises by 1 with its `version` incremented by 1; then cascade hop 1 fires, because an approved substitution changes the served menu of record, so the contract's `substitution_total` rises by 1 and its `substitution_rate_pct` is recomputed as `substitution_total` multiplied by 100 and divided by the greater of 1 and `total_trays_served`, rounded to 2 decimal places, with the contract's `version` incremented by 1; then cascade hop 2 fires, because `substitution_total` changed, so a contract in `RECONCILING` transitions back to `ACTIVE` — the reconciliation it was holding is now stale and must be run again — while a contract already in `ACTIVE` simply stays `ACTIVE`. On the **`REJECTED`** branch the service record's `substitution_count` is unchanged and the contract is not touched at all: no ledger field moves, no version is bumped, and a `RECONCILING` contract stays `RECONCILING`.

### 3.8. Grievances: Fork, Join, Response and Escalation

A **grievance** is an inmate complaint about food service, and its lifecycle is the only fork-join workflow in the system: it splits into as many as two parallel review branches and cannot be answered until every branch it was routed into has reported back.

A `GrievanceCoordinator` files one, naming the `inmate_id` of the grievant, the `facility_id` the complaint concerns, a `category` of `RELIGIOUS_DIET_DENIAL`, `DIET_NOT_HONORED`, `PORTION_SIZE`, `FOOD_TEMPERATURE` or `FOREIGN_OBJECT`, and a `narrative`. Two optional links may be supplied and are what make the later branches and remedies available: `diet_order_id` names the diet order the complaint disputes, and `meal_service_record_id` names the service the complaint arose at. The facility must be the grievant inmate's own facility, otherwise `ERR_FACILITY_MISMATCH`, and the inmate's `status` must be `IN_CUSTODY`, otherwise `ERR_INMATE_NOT_IN_CUSTODY`.

The **response deadline** is computed at filing. `filed_at` is stamped from the logical clock. The window is 5 days when the `category` is `RELIGIOUS_DIET_DENIAL` or `DIET_NOT_HONORED` — a denied religious or therapeutic diet is an ongoing harm and gets the expedited window — and 10 days for every other category. `response_due_at` is `filed_at` plus that many days, each day being exactly 24 hours. Only `filed_at` and the eventual response instant matter; time spent in any particular branch is irrelevant to the deadline. A filed grievance opens in `FILED` with `routed_branch` null, `investigation_complete` false, `diet_review_complete` false, `diet_remedy_required` false, `remedy` null, `refeed_tray_count` 0, `response_at` null, `responded_by_user_id` null and `sla_breached` false. Grievances are readable individually and through a listing accepting `inmate_id`, `facility_id`, `diet_order_id`, `meal_service_record_id` and `status` filters.

**Routing** is the fork, performed by a coordinator naming a `route` of `INVESTIGATION`, `DIET_REVIEW` or `DUAL_TRACK`. It may only be applied from `FILED`, and each choice has its own precondition on the optional links:

- `INVESTIGATION` needs nothing further. It moves `status` to `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` and sets `routed_branch` to `INVESTIGATION`.
- `DIET_REVIEW` requires `diet_order_id` to be non-null, because there is nothing clinical to review otherwise. It moves `status` to `DIET_REVIEW` and sets `routed_branch` to `DIET_REVIEW`.
- `DUAL_TRACK` requires **both** `diet_order_id` and `meal_service_record_id` to be non-null. It moves `status` to `DUAL_TRACK` and sets `routed_branch` to the value `BOTH` — note that the branch marker is `BOTH` while the status is `DUAL_TRACK`; the two are deliberately different strings.

Each successful route increments `version` by 1. A grievance that has already been routed cannot be routed again, since none of the three destinations is reachable from anywhere but `FILED`.

**Completing a branch** carries the `branch` being closed and an optional `finding`. The behaviour depends on which state the grievance is in, and the single-branch and dual-branch cases are deliberately symmetric except at the join:

- From `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, completing the `INVESTIGATION` branch — a coordinator action — sets `investigation_complete` to true and moves `status` straight to `READY_FOR_RESPONSE`, because there is no second branch to wait for.
- From `DIET_REVIEW`, completing the `DIET_REVIEW` branch — a `RegisteredDietitian` action — sets `diet_review_complete` to true and moves `status` straight to `READY_FOR_RESPONSE`. This branch alone has an extra effect: when the supplied `finding` is `KITCHEN_AT_FAULT`, `diet_remedy_required` is also set to true. The investigation branch never sets that flag, whatever finding it reports, and this is the one genuine asymmetry between the two branch completions.
- From `DUAL_TRACK`, completing a branch whose flag is still false while the **other** flag is still false sets the named flag — `investigation_complete` for `INVESTIGATION`, `diet_review_complete` for `DIET_REVIEW` — and leaves `status` at `DUAL_TRACK`, because the join is not yet satisfied. This is a self-loop: the state does not change, but `version` is still incremented by 1 and the flag is still durably recorded. The `KITCHEN_AT_FAULT` rule applies here too, so a dual-track diet review reporting the kitchen at fault sets `diet_remedy_required` even though the status does not move.
- From `DUAL_TRACK`, completing a branch whose flag is still false while the other flag is **already** true satisfies the join: the named flag is set and `status` moves to `READY_FOR_RESPONSE`.

In every case the flag named by `branch` must currently be false — a branch cannot be completed twice — and `version` is incremented by 1.

**Responding** is the coordinator's final act, carrying a `remedy`, an optional `refeed_tray_count` and an optional `response_note`. The grievance must be in `READY_FOR_RESPONSE`; an attempt to answer a grievance still in `FILED`, `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, `DIET_REVIEW` or — most importantly — `DUAL_TRACK` with only one flag set is rejected with `ERR_BRANCHES_INCOMPLETE`. Remedy admissibility is then checked: `NO_ACTION` is rejected with `ERR_REMEDY_NOT_PERMITTED` when `diet_remedy_required` is true, because a diet review that found the kitchen at fault cannot be answered with nothing; `MEAL_REFEED` requires `meal_service_record_id` to be non-null, that record's `status` to be exactly `CLOSED`, and `refeed_tray_count` to be at least 1, with any of those three failures reported as `ERR_REFEED_TARGET_INVALID`; and `DIET_REINSTATEMENT` requires `diet_order_id` to be non-null and that order's `status` to be exactly `SUSPENDED`, otherwise `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_TARGET_INVALID`.

The **SLA verdict** then decides which of three terminal states the grievance lands in, and the timing test dominates the remedy entirely. `sla_breached` is true if and only if the logical clock's current instant is strictly after `response_due_at`. The deadline is inclusive: responding at exactly `response_due_at` is not a breach, and one second later is.

When the response is on time and the `remedy` is `NO_ACTION`, `status` becomes `CLOSED` — the grievance is dismissed on the merits. When the response is on time and the `remedy` is one of the substantive `MENU_CORRECTION`, `MEAL_REFEED` or `DIET_REINSTATEMENT`, `status` becomes `RESPONDED`. In both cases `remedy` is written, `response_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `responded_by_user_id` records the acting user, `sla_breached` is set to false and `version` is incremented by 1. When the response is late, `status` becomes `ESCALATED` regardless of which remedy was chosen — even `NO_ACTION` yields `ESCALATED` rather than `CLOSED` — with `remedy` still written, `response_at` stamped, `responded_by_user_id` recorded, `sla_breached` set to true and `version` incremented by 1. All three of `CLOSED`, `RESPONDED` and `ESCALATED` are terminal.

Two independent sets of consequences hang off the response, and a late substantive response fires both.

A `MEAL_REFEED` remedy disputes the service record, three hops deep. Hop 1: because the remedy admits an unserved meal, the named service record transitions from `CLOSED` to `DISPUTED` with `dispute_tray_count` set to `refeed_tray_count` and `missed_meal_count` increased by that same count with no tolerance relief, and its `version` incremented by 1. Hop 2: because that record's `missed_meal_count` grew, the contract's `missed_meal_total` rises by `refeed_tray_count`, its `liquidated_damages_cents` and `performance_score` are recomputed, and its `version` is incremented by 1. Hop 3: because `missed_meal_total` reached the escalation threshold of 25, every facility under that contract gets `corrective_action_required` set to true, and any facility whose `kitchen_posture` reads `OPEN` moves to `RESTRICTED` while stricter postures are left alone.

A `DIET_REINSTATEMENT` remedy repairs the diet order instead: the named order, which must be `SUSPENDED`, transitions back to `PENDING_VERIFICATION` with `reverification_count` incremented by 1 and both `verified_by_user_id` and `verified_at` cleared to null, so the two-person verification of §3.3 must be performed afresh. `MENU_CORRECTION` carries no downstream cascade at all — it is a promise to fix the menu, recorded and nothing more.

A breach fires its own two hops on top of whatever the remedy did. Hop 1: because `sla_breached` became true, the contract's `grievance_sla_breach_count` rises by 1, its `liquidated_damages_cents` and `performance_score` are recomputed, and its `version` is incremented by 1. Hop 2: because `grievance_sla_breach_count` reached the grievance escalation threshold of 3, every facility under the contract gets `corrective_action_required` set to true and any `OPEN` posture moves to `RESTRICTED`.

### 3.9. Reconciling the Contract Ledger and Closing Out

The contract ledger is maintained incrementally as service records close, substitutions are approved and grievances breach their deadlines, but **reconciliation** is the authoritative recomputation: it rebuilds every computed ledger field from the source rows, in a pinned order, and pins the damages variant the agency has chosen.

A `ContractAdministrator` reconciles a contract in `ACTIVE`, supplying the `ld_variant`. That value is mandatory and must be either `INCIDENT_BASED` or `POPULATION_WEIGHTED`; an omitted or out-of-domain value is rejected with `ERR_LD_VARIANT_REQUIRED`. Two further preconditions apply, both reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`: no service record under the contract may still be in `OPEN`, because an unclosed service would leave the ledger provably incomplete, and at least one record under the contract must be in `CLOSED` or `DISPUTED`, because there is nothing to reconcile against an empty ledger.

The **input set** is precisely defined. Service records contribute when their `status` is `CLOSED` or `DISPUTED` and their facility belongs to this contract; records in `OPEN` are excluded entirely. Grievances are counted across every facility under the contract regardless of grievance status, keyed only on `sla_breached`, so an escalated grievance counts whether or not anyone later revisits it.

The nine steps below are **normative in their order**, and each step may read only the outputs of the steps before it.

**Step 1.** `total_trays_served` is the sum of `total_trays_served` over every service record in the input set.

**Step 2.** `meal_days_served` is `total_trays_served` divided by 3 meals per day, truncated toward zero to a whole number of meal days. A partial day of trays does not earn a meal day.

**Step 3.** `missed_meal_total` is the sum of `missed_meal_count` over every service record in the input set. This overwrites the incrementally maintained running total rather than adding to it, which is the whole point of reconciliation. `grievance_sla_breach_count` is the number of grievances under the contract whose `sla_breached` is true.

**Step 4.** `substitution_total` is the number of substitutions in `approval_status` `APPROVED` whose service record is in the input set. `substitution_rate_pct` is `substitution_total` multiplied by 100, divided by the greater of 1 and `total_trays_served`, rounded to 2 decimal places with half-up rounding. The denominator floor of 1 keeps the rate defined for a contract that has served nothing.

**Step 5.** The reference cost is chosen deterministically. The candidate set is every menu cycle in `ACTIVE` whose facility belongs to this contract, ranked by `approved_cost_cents_per_meal_day` in descending order, with ties broken by earliest `created_at`; the reference cost is the winner's `approved_cost_cents_per_meal_day`. When that candidate set is empty — no facility under the contract has an active cycle — the reference cost falls back to the contract's own `cost_ceiling_cents_per_meal_day`. Then `gross_food_cost_cents` is `meal_days_served` multiplied by the reference cost, and `cost_cents_per_meal_day_actual` is `gross_food_cost_cents` divided by the greater of 1 and `meal_days_served`, rounded to 2 decimal places with half-up rounding.

**Step 6.** The substitution surcharge is charged only on the excess. When `substitution_rate_pct` is strictly greater than the contract's `substitution_allowance_pct`, the excess percentage is `substitution_rate_pct` minus `substitution_allowance_pct` rounded to 2 decimal places, and `substitution_surcharge_cents` is `gross_food_cost_cents` multiplied by that excess percentage, divided by 100, multiplied by the surcharge rate of 0.15, then rounded **up** to the next whole cent. When the rate is at or below the allowance — including exactly equal to it — `substitution_surcharge_cents` is 0.

**Step 7.** `raw_liquidated_damages_cents` is the variant formula below plus `grievance_sla_breach_count` multiplied by 25000 cents per breached grievance. The variant term differs, and identical fixtures diverge between the two:

- Under `INCIDENT_BASED`, the variant term is `missed_meal_total` multiplied by `ld_cents_per_missed_meal`. Each weighted missed meal costs the flat contract rate no matter how concentrated the failures were.
- Under `POPULATION_WEIGHTED`, the variant term is `missed_meal_total` multiplied by `ld_cents_per_missed_meal` multiplied by a severity multiplier, the whole product rounded **up** to the next whole cent. The severity multiplier is 1 plus the quotient of `missed_meal_total` divided by the greater of 1 and `meal_days_served`, that quotient rounded to 2 decimal places with half-up rounding before the multiplication. Failures concentrated into few meal days therefore cost more per failure than the same count spread thin.

Every incremental recompute that fires before the first reconciliation — a service record closing, a substitution being approved, a grievance breaching — happens while `ld_variant` is still null. In that case the `INCIDENT_BASED` formula is the default, so the ledger is never undefined.

**Step 8.** `pre_cap_cents` is `raw_liquidated_damages_cents` plus `substitution_surcharge_cents`, and `liquidated_damages_cents` is the lesser of `pre_cap_cents` and the contract's `ld_monthly_cap_cents`.

**Step 9.** `cost_recovery_credit_cents` is the lesser of `liquidated_damages_cents` and `gross_food_cost_cents`, multiplied by the credit rate of 0.20, then truncated toward zero to a whole cent. `net_liquidated_damages_cents` is the greater of 0 and `liquidated_damages_cents` minus `cost_recovery_credit_cents`. Finally `performance_score` starts at 100 and subtracts `missed_meal_total` multiplied by 2.5, subtracts `grievance_sla_breach_count` multiplied by 5.0, and subtracts the greater of 0 and `cost_cents_per_meal_day_actual` minus `cost_ceiling_cents_per_meal_day` multiplied by 0.1; that result is rounded to 2 decimal places with half-up rounding and then clamped into the closed range from 0.00 to 100.00, so the score can be neither negative nor above 100.

**The cap must be applied before the cost-recovery credit** — step 8 strictly before step 9. Computing the credit from `pre_cap_cents` and capping the net afterwards produces a different and forbidden result: with a pre-cap figure of 29571 cents, a cap of 20000 cents and a gross food cost of 31500 cents, the normative order yields a net of 16000 cents while the forbidden order yields 20000 cents.

Reconciling sets `ld_variant` from the request, overwrites `total_trays_served`, `meal_days_served`, `missed_meal_total`, `substitution_rate_pct`, `grievance_sla_breach_count`, `raw_liquidated_damages_cents`, `substitution_surcharge_cents`, `liquidated_damages_cents`, `gross_food_cost_cents`, `cost_cents_per_meal_day_actual`, `cost_recovery_credit_cents`, `net_liquidated_damages_cents` and `performance_score` from the nine steps, moves `status` to `RECONCILING`, stamps `reconciled_at` from the logical clock and increments `version` by 1.

A reconciliation is fragile by design, because it is a snapshot of a population that can still change. When a substitution is approved while the contract sits in `RECONCILING`, and that substitution's service record belongs to a facility under this contract, the contract cycles back from `RECONCILING` to `ACTIVE`. `ld_variant` is cleared to null and `reconciled_at` is cleared to null; `raw_liquidated_damages_cents`, `substitution_surcharge_cents`, `liquidated_damages_cents`, `cost_recovery_credit_cents`, `net_liquidated_damages_cents` and `gross_food_cost_cents` are all reset to 0; `cost_cents_per_meal_day_actual`, `substitution_rate_pct` and `performance_score` are all reset to 0.00; and `version` is incremented by 1. Crucially, `total_trays_served`, `meal_days_served`, `missed_meal_total` and `grievance_sla_breach_count` are **not** zeroed, because they are incrementally maintained ledgers of things that actually happened rather than reconciliation outputs. This is the one cycle in the contract's state machine: `ACTIVE` to `RECONCILING` and back to `ACTIVE`, and the contract must be reconciled again before it can be closed.

**Closing** the contract is the terminal act and takes no payload. The contract must be in `RECONCILING`; a contract in `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` cannot be closed, and `CLOSED` is terminal so a closed contract cannot be closed again, both reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. No substitution under the contract may still be in `approval_status` `PENDING`, because an undecided substitution could invalidate the very reconciliation being frozen; an outstanding pending substitution is likewise reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Closing moves `status` to `CLOSED`, stamps `closed_at` from the logical clock and increments `version` by 1, then cascades two hops. Hop 1: because the contract reached `CLOSED`, every facility under it has `corrective_action_required` set to the truth of the statement that the contract's `performance_score` is strictly below the corrective-action floor of 70.0 — note that this is an assignment, not a one-way raise, so a facility whose flag was true has it cleared when the final score is at or above 70.0. Hop 2: because `corrective_action_required` flipped to true on those facilities, the posture waterfall is re-evaluated and each such facility whose `kitchen_posture` reads `OPEN` becomes `RESTRICTED`; facilities already `RESTRICTED` or in `LOCKDOWN` keep their stricter posture, and a facility whose flag was just cleared drops back to `OPEN` only when no other waterfall clause holds.

The ledger is also readable without exposing the contract's mutable rows, as a computed performance view returning `total_trays_served`, `meal_days_served`, `missed_meal_total`, `substitution_rate_pct`, `grievance_sla_breach_count`, `raw_liquidated_damages_cents`, `substitution_surcharge_cents`, `liquidated_damages_cents`, `cost_recovery_credit_cents`, `net_liquidated_damages_cents`, `performance_score` and `ld_variant`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is mounted under the `/cfs` prefix and follows one convention for its success status code: an operation that brings a new resource into existence answers with 201, while every read, listing, correction and state transition answers with 200. There is no 204 anywhere in this API — even operations whose only visible effect is a state change return the updated resource in the `data` field, so the caller can observe the derived fields and the new `version` without a follow-up read. Request bodies, response bodies and side effects are deliberately absent from this table; they are specified in the §3 narratives, which remain the authoritative description of what each operation requires and what it changes.
