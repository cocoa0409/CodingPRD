# Lender Appraisal Review Desk API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A residential mortgage lender cannot fund a loan on collateral it has not defended. Between the moment an appraisal management company (AMC) hands over a completed URAR report and the moment an underwriter signs off on the collateral, a lender's appraisal desk must do work that is simultaneously mechanical and adversarial: confirm the report is complete, confirm the comparable sales actually support the value opinion, decide whether the report is trustworthy enough for the loan's risk tier or whether a field review or an outright second appraisal is warranted, entertain a borrower's reconsideration of value on documented evidence rather than sentiment, screen the appraiser's own language and comparable choices for bias indicators, and do all of it without a single lender employee ever leaning on the appraiser to reach a number. Today that work is done in spreadsheets and email threads, which means the audit trail is a reconstruction exercise and the same appraisal can be scored two different ways by two different reviewers on the same afternoon.

This document specifies the **Lender Appraisal Review Desk API**, a backend service mounted under `/appraisal-desk` that turns that desk into a system of record. It holds the loan file and its two independent status dimensions, the appraiser panel and the management companies that staff it, every delivered appraisal report with its comparable grid, the eight automated receipt checks and their persisted outcomes, the bias screen and its adjudications, the desk review with its two scoring variants and its escalation matrix, escalation orders with their priced cost allocation, the borrower reconsideration channel with structured evidence admissibility, disputes filed against management companies under response SLAs, and the appraiser performance record that eventually removes a bad actor from the panel.

Three design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **segregation of duties is structural, not advisory**: the reviewer who submits a desk review can never be the person who decides it, and the roles that talk to borrowers are barred from the message intents that would let them influence a value opinion. Second, **every number is reproducible**: monetary amounts are integer cents, every ratio is integer basis points, rounding is half-up everywhere, and the order in which a formula rounds is pinned by specification rather than left to the implementer. Third, **time is deterministic**: the service reads a logical UTC clock that begins at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and moves only when an operator moves it, so a deadline, a rolling twelve-month performance window, and an inspection-window conflict all evaluate identically on every replay.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Desk Participants and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, empty, or holds nothing but whitespace, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The same `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and the same 401 status are returned when `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the seven roles declared below — an unrecognised role is treated as a failure to authenticate, never as a permission problem. When the role is recognised but is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called (see the authorisation column in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `LoanOfficer`, `AMCCoordinator`, `AppraisalDeskReviewer`, `ChiefAppraiser`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `Appraiser`, and `Borrower`. Their responsibilities partition as follows. A `LoanOfficer` owns the loan file and its underwriting dimension; it reads collateral outcomes but never runs a check, opens a review, or adjudicates anything. An `AMCCoordinator` acts on behalf of exactly one appraisal management company: it delivers reports and their comparables, relays appraiser responses on disputes, and delivers escalation results. An `AppraisalDeskReviewer` runs the automated receipt checks, opens and submits desk reviews, and administers the reconsideration channel — but is structurally incapable of deciding a review. A `ChiefAppraiser` is the panel authority: it decides submitted desk reviews, assigns and cancels escalation orders, resolves disputes, registers appraisers and management companies, and takes panel-review actions. A `ComplianceOfficer` owns the bias and discrimination screen and the adjudication of its flags, and is deliberately excluded from opening, submitting, or deciding desk reviews. An `Appraiser` responds to reconsideration rounds on its own reports. A `Borrower` initiates a reconsideration of value on its own loan and files structured evidence for it.

Four of these roles are opaque and three are entity-backed, and the distinction determines exactly what `X-User-ID` is compared against. When `X-User-Role` is `LoanOfficer`, `AppraisalDeskReviewer`, `ChiefAppraiser`, or `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow calls for it (as `created_by_user_id`, `submitted_by_user_id`, and `decided_by_user_id` on a desk review, as `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a bias flag, and as `sender_user_id` on a recorded communication) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `AMCCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting AMC, and ownership checks compare it to the `amc_id` on the resource being acted upon. When `X-User-Role` is `Appraiser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting appraiser, and ownership checks compare it to the `appraiser_id` of the appraisal behind the resource being acted upon. When `X-User-Role` is `Borrower`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting loan file, and ownership checks compare it to the `loan_file_id` of the resource being acted upon.

Ownership is enforced on six write operations: appraisal delivery, escalation-order completion, an appraiser's reconsideration response, an AMC's dispute response, reconsideration intake, and evidence filing. In each case, an entity-backed role acting on a resource that resolves to a different owner is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403) — an `AMCCoordinator` reaching for another company's report, an `Appraiser` answering another appraiser's reconsideration, a `Borrower` filing against another loan file. Separately, and independently of ownership, the desk-review decision carries a segregation-of-duties check: if the `X-User-ID` deciding a review is equal to the `submitted_by_user_id` recorded when that review was submitted, the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), even though the acting role is the correct `ChiefAppraiser`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request, formed as the literal `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical desk clock of §2.4 rather than from the host's wall clock.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, must be an integer, and must fall in the inclusive range from a minimum of 1 to a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted, must be an integer, and must be zero or greater. A `limit` outside that range, a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

List results are always returned in ascending `created_at` order — oldest first — and where two rows share a `created_at` value — which is the common case, since the logical clock moves only when an operator moves it — the tie is broken by insertion order, that is by the order in which the rows were created inside the process; resource `id` values never participate in ordering, ranking, tie-breaking, or conflict resolution anywhere in this system. Two list endpoints layer an additional guarantee on top of creation order: the rule-check listing and the bias-flag listing return their rows in the fixed emission order the producing run wrote them in, which for rule checks is ascending `sequence_no` and for bias flags is the flag emission order described in §3.5.

For list responses the `meta` object carries `limit` (the effective page size actually applied), `offset` (the effective offset actually applied), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied), and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current window). Single-resource responses and error responses omit all four. Every list filter named in §3 is an exact-match comparison against the raw query-string value applied before the page window is taken — the only exceptions are the three typed filters called out in §3.3 and §3.8, namely the comparable listing's `included_in_grid` and the evidence listing's `admissible` and `round_number`, which are coerced to a boolean or to an integer before the comparison — and a filter value that matches no row yields an empty `data` array with `total_count` 0 rather than an error.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Desk Clock

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix — clients never supply one, and the same UUID appears in URL paths and in cross-entity reference fields. Most resources also carry three further common properties: `created_at` and `updated_at`, both ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z`, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation the specification says mutates it.

Three record types are append-only events and deliberately carry a reduced property set: a rule-check result, a recorded appraiser communication, and a value revision each expose only `id` and `created_at`. For those three, `updated_at` and `version` are absent entirely — not null, not zero, absent. Every other resource in the system (loan files, appraisers, management companies, panel memberships, appraisals, comparable sales, bias indicator flags, desk reviews, escalation orders, reconsideration requests, evidence items, and dispute cases) carries the full `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version` quartet.

All time in this system comes from a logical UTC clock, never from the host's wall clock. Before any operator moves it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must not drift or fall back to real time. An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to any instant supplied as `current_utc`, forward or backward, and is available to all seven roles precisely because every scenario needs it; it answers HTTP 200 with an empty object in `data` rather than any resource. Every `created_at`, `updated_at`, and `sent_at` stamp, every deadline computation (dispute response deadlines, reconsideration response deadlines, escalation turnaround due dates), every inspection-window and review-slot comparison, and every rolling-window performance filter reads that logical clock at the instant the request is processed.

### 2.5. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integer cents. No floating-point money value is ever persisted, and every field whose name ends in `_cents` holds a whole number of cents. All ratio-valued quantities are integer basis points, where 10000 basis points equals 100 percent, and every field whose name ends in `_bps` holds a whole number of basis points.

Rounding is **half-up everywhere**: a value exactly halfway between two representable results rounds away from zero. Unless a specific calculation in §3 pins a different order of operations, a basis-point result is produced by rounding half-up to zero decimal places, and a cent result is produced by rounding half-up to whole cents. Where a formula's rounding order matters — and in this domain it matters in three places, namely the completeness score's per-component rounding, the indicated value's per-comparable rounding, and the escalation cost's surcharge-then-discount sequence — the order is stated explicitly in the relevant workflow narrative and is not open to reinterpretation.

Distances are the one non-integer input: a comparable's `distance_miles` and an evidence item's `distance_miles` are decimal numbers with at most two decimal places, and every distance comparison is performed at two decimal places. Ages and day counts are whole days measured on the logical clock. Interval buffers are whole minutes.

### 2.6. Stale-Read Protection on Parent Resources

Three write operations mutate a child resource while depending on a parent resource whose numbers the caller must already have observed. Each of those three requires an optimistic-concurrency token as a non-optional field in its request body, carrying the caller's last observed `version` of the named parent. If the supplied value does not equal the parent's current `version`, the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is persisted.

- `POST /appraisal-desk/desk-reviews` requires `expected_appraisal_version`, matching the current `version` of the appraisal named in the body. Another reviewer may have re-run the rule checks in the interim, which rewrites `completeness_score_bps` and the rule-check tallies that the preliminary quality score is computed from; a stale token means the review being opened would be scored from superseded numbers, so it is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412.
- `POST /appraisal-desk/appraisals` requires `expected_loan_file_version`, matching the current `version` of the loan file named in the body. A prior delivery may already have landed and superseded the report the AMC believed was current, so a stale token means the caller's supersede pointer and risk-tier assumptions no longer describe the loan it is writing to; the delivery is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412.
- `POST /appraisal-desk/panel-memberships` requires `expected_appraiser_version`, matching the current `version` of the appraiser named in the body. A panel-review action may already have moved that appraiser's `panel_status`, so a stale token means the caller is enrolling an appraiser whose standing it has not observed; the enrolment is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412.

Two further operations take a version token of the same shape and reject the same way, this time guarding the resource named in the path rather than a separate parent. Running the receipt rule checks and running the bias screen both require `expected_appraisal_version` matching the target appraisal's current `version`, and a panel-review action requires `expected_appraiser_version` matching the target appraiser's current `version`; each mismatch is `ERR_STALE_VERSION` with HTTP 412. No other endpoint in this system takes a version field, and supplying one where it is not specified has no effect.

### 2.7. Left-to-Right Path Resolution and Referential Ordering

Several resources are addressed as children of an appraisal or of a reconsideration request. When a request carries more than one path segment that names a resource, those segments are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that does not resolve produces the 404 for its own resource type before any deeper segment, body field, or business rule is examined. The resource-specific 404 codes are `ERR_LOAN_FILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPRAISER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AMC_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PANEL_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPRAISAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BIAS_FLAG_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DESK_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ESCALATION_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROV_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_DISPUTE_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404.

Cross-entity references supplied in a request body follow the same discipline, resolved in the order the fields are declared for that operation. Appraisal delivery resolves `loan_file_id`, then `appraiser_id`, then `amc_id`, then — when present — `supersedes_appraisal_id`, which yields `ERR_APPRAISAL_NOT_FOUND` when it points at nothing. Panel enrolment resolves `appraiser_id` then `amc_id`. Desk-review creation resolves `appraisal_id`. Reconsideration intake resolves `loan_file_id` then `appraisal_id`. Dispute filing resolves `appraisal_id`, then `amc_id`, then `appraiser_id`. Escalation assignment resolves `assigned_appraiser_id`. A dangling reference in any of these positions yields that referenced type's own not-found code with HTTP 404. A body reference position that is absent altogether, or that carries something other than a string, is not a dangling reference at all: it falls through to the body-shape rung and is reported there as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), which means a request that both omits one reference and dangles a later one still answers with the later one's 404.

### 2.8. The Validation Ladder

Because several operations in this system carry a dozen or more independent guards, the order in which guards are evaluated is part of the contract. Validation always proceeds strictly in the following ascending order, and the **first** failing rung is the error returned; a later rung never masks an earlier one.

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. `X-User-Role` names one of the seven declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
3. The acting role is authorised for the endpoint — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).
4. For the entity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` matches the target's own owning identifier — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
5. On a desk-review decision, the acting `X-User-ID` differs from `submitted_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
6. Every resource named in a path position exists, checked left to right — otherwise that type's own not-found code (404).
7. Every foreign key in the body references an existing row, checked in declaration order — otherwise that type's own not-found code (404).
8. The body is well shaped: required fields present, enum values in range, integers non-negative where declared, timestamps ISO 8601 with a `Z` suffix. An integer-valued field must be a JSON integer — a boolean, a number carrying a fractional part, and a numeric string are all malformed in that position — a decimal field may arrive as a JSON number or as a numeric string but never as a boolean, and a timestamp may equivalently carry a `+00:00` offset in place of the `Z` while any other offset or a designator-less local timestamp is malformed. A body that is not a JSON object at all is malformed, whereas an entirely absent body is read as an empty object and fails only on whatever required field it is missing. Unknown fields are silently ignored rather than rejected. The uniqueness checks on `loan_number`, `license_number` and a company `name` are evaluated on this same rung, after every other field in the body has been shape-checked, and report the same `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A malformed body yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
9. On list endpoints, `limit` is an integer in the inclusive range 1 to 100 and `offset` is an integer of zero or more — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).
10. Any declared optimistic-concurrency token equals the guarded parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
11. The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition exists at all — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). This is the catch-all rung: an operation that has its own more specific state error uses that code instead, at the rung listed below.
12. The comparable grid is unlocked — otherwise `ERR_APPRAISAL_GRID_LOCKED` (409).
13. The receipt rule checks have already run on the appraisal — otherwise `ERR_RULE_CHECKS_NOT_RUN` (409).
14. A desk review being decided is in `SUBMITTED` — otherwise `ERR_DESK_REVIEW_NOT_SUBMITTED` (409).
15. An escalation order being assigned is in `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT` or `REASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED` — otherwise `ERR_ESCALATION_NOT_PENDING` (409).
16. A reconsideration receiving an appraiser response is in `AWAITING_APPRAISER` — otherwise `ERR_ROV_NOT_AWAITING_RESPONSE` (409).
17. A dispute receiving a response or a resolution is not already resolved — otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_ALREADY_RESOLVED` (409).
18. The appraisal still accepts communications — otherwise `ERR_COMMUNICATION_LOCKED` (409).
19. The bias screen has been run before a desk review is opened or submitted — otherwise `ERR_BIAS_SCREEN_REQUIRED` (422).
20. The decision action matches the resolved escalation requirement — otherwise `ERR_ESCALATION_REQUIRED` (422).
21. The appraisal is not under a substantiated bias quarantine — otherwise `ERR_BIAS_QUARANTINE_ACTIVE` (422).
22. A communication's sender role is permitted to use the declared `intent` toward the declared `recipient` — otherwise `ERR_INDEPENDENCE_VIOLATION` (422).
23. The named management company is not suspended — otherwise `ERR_AMC_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422).
24. The named appraiser sits on the named company's panel — otherwise `ERR_APPRAISER_NOT_ON_PANEL` (422).
25. The appraiser being assigned sits on the escalated appraisal's company panel — otherwise `ERR_APPRAISER_NOT_ON_PANEL` (422).
26. The appraiser being assigned is otherwise eligible for the order's escalation type — otherwise `ERR_APPRAISER_INELIGIBLE` (422).
27. The loan is eligible for reconsideration — otherwise `ERR_LOAN_NOT_ROV_ELIGIBLE` (422).
28. The dispute's parties match the appraisal's parties — otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_PARTY_MISMATCH` (422).
29. A comparable's `sale_date` is at or before the parent appraisal's `effective_date` — otherwise `ERR_COMP_DATE_AFTER_EFFECTIVE` (422).
30. Every submitted interval is well formed — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (422).
31. The requested inspection window does not conflict under the travel-buffer rule — otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
32. The requested review slot does not exceed the company's concurrent capacity — otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
33. A by-default dispute resolution waits for the response deadline — otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_DEADLINE_NOT_REACHED` (422).
34. A comparable's normalised `street_address` is not already on the appraisal — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_COMPARABLE` (422).
35. The in-grid comparable count stays within its ceiling — otherwise `ERR_COMP_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422).
36. No active panel membership already covers the same appraiser, company and coverage state — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_PANEL_MEMBERSHIP` (422).
37. A reconsideration being routed carries enough admissible evidence in its current round — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ADMISSIBLE_EVIDENCE` (422).
38. A further reconsideration round remains available — otherwise `ERR_ROV_ROUND_LIMIT` (422).
39. The requested panel action is permitted from the appraiser's current standing and score — otherwise `ERR_PANEL_ACTION_NOT_PERMITTED` (422).
40. A value revision stays within the revision cap — otherwise `ERR_REVISION_EXCEEDS_CAP` (422).
41. The escalation charge fits inside the company's budget — otherwise `ERR_ESCALATION_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (402).
42. At most one unresolved dispute exists per appraisal and basis — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_DISPUTE_BASIS` (422).

Note the two deliberate inversions in this ladder that a naive reading would get backwards. The window-conflict and slot-capacity rungs (31 and 32) sit *after* the interval well-formedness rung (30), so a reversed interval always reports `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` rather than a conflict. And the escalation-budget rung (41) sits *after* both the window-conflict rung and the assignee-eligibility rungs, so an assignment that is both over budget and to an ineligible appraiser reports `ERR_APPRAISER_INELIGIBLE`, while an assignment that is over budget and window-conflicting reports `ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Conversely `ERR_DUPLICATE_DISPUTE_BASIS` sits at the very bottom of the ladder (42), so a duplicate-basis dispute that also breaches slot capacity reports the capacity error first. Two endpoints deliberately depart from the ascending order, and in both the bias-quarantine gate (rung 21) is hoisted above the transition-existence gate (rung 11). On an explicit underwriting-status move a quarantined loan answers `ERR_BIAS_QUARANTINE_ACTIVE` even when the requested target status is one that no transition would have reached anyway. And on a reconsideration routing a quarantined appraisal answers `ERR_BIAS_QUARANTINE_ACTIVE` rather than the `ERR_INVALID_STATE` that its non-contestable `review_status` would otherwise have produced.

### 2.9. Two-Dimensional Status Modelling and Cascade Discipline

Two entities in this system carry two independent status dimensions on the same row, and conflating them is the single most common way to get this domain wrong. An appraisal carries `review_status`, its review lifecycle, and `bias_screen_status`, its compliance screening lifecycle. Running the bias screen never touches `review_status`; desk-review transitions never touch `bias_screen_status`. The one place the compliance dimension reaches across into the review dimension is quarantine, and that crossing is reversible — see §3.5. Likewise a loan file carries `underwriting_status`, its lending decision dimension, and `collateral_status`, a compound projection of the value-of-record appraisal's `review_status`; the two move on different triggers and only meet through the decision-impact cascade described in §3.9.

Whenever an operation mutates a resource, that resource's `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock. `version` increments by exactly 1 on each mutation the specification identifies as version-bearing, and the cascades in this system are explicit about which parents get bumped: delivering an appraisal bumps the loan file's `version`; enrolling a panel membership bumps the appraiser's `version`; running the rule checks, running the bias screen, and opening a desk review each bump the appraisal's `version`; a panel-review action and an explicit underwriting-status move bump the appraiser's and the loan file's `version` respectively. Recomputed roll-up counters — an appraiser's tallies, a management company's ledger and breach counts, an appraisal's comparable counts — are refreshed as part of the same request that causes them to change, so a read immediately afterwards observes the new values. Such a refresh, and any automatic cascade that follows from it — an appraiser's panel standing degrading, a loan file's underwriting status suspending or reinstating — refreshes the holder's `updated_at` without incrementing its `version`; only the explicit creations and transitions named in §3 are version-bearing. On the loan file specifically, every cascade that writes a new `collateral_status` does increment the loan file's `version`, whereas a pure value recompute — new `current_appraised_value_cents`, `effective_value_cents`, `ltv_bps` and `decision_impact`, plus any automatic underwriting move that follows — refreshes `updated_at` only. There is no deferred or eventual settlement anywhere in this service: every cascade named in §3 completes inside the request that triggered it, or the whole request fails and nothing is persisted.

### 2.10. Appraiser Independence and the Non-Influence Principle

Appraiser independence is a regulatory obligation, and in this system it is expressed as a hard restriction on who may say what to whom. Every message about an appraisal is persisted as an immutable communication record capturing the `sender_role` and `sender_user_id` taken from the request headers, the `recipient`, the declared `intent`, the verbatim `body`, and a `sent_at` stamp from the logical clock. Nothing is ever redacted or edited: the record exists so that a later examiner can prove that no lender-facing party pressured the appraiser.

The `recipient` is one of `APPRAISER`, `AMC`, or `LENDER_DESK`. The `intent` is one of `ORDER_STATUS`, `DELIVERY_LOGISTICS`, `REPORT_CLARIFICATION`, `COMPARABLE_SUGGESTION`, `VALUE_INFLUENCE`, or `DEADLINE_PRESSURE`. Three prohibitions apply, and any one of them rejects the message with `ERR_INDEPENDENCE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422). A `LoanOfficer` or a `Borrower` may never address the appraiser with any of `VALUE_INFLUENCE`, `COMPARABLE_SUGGESTION`, or `DEADLINE_PRESSURE` — that is, when `recipient` is `APPRAISER` and `intent` is any of those three. An `AppraisalDeskReviewer` may never address the appraiser with `VALUE_INFLUENCE`, though the reviewer's other intents toward the appraiser are permitted. And no role at all, regardless of recipient, may use `VALUE_INFLUENCE` while any reconsideration request on that appraisal sits in `AWAITING_APPRAISER` — the moment the appraiser is on the clock to answer a borrower, the value-influence channel closes for everyone.

The other face of independence is segregation of duties inside the lender. A desk review records three separate operator identities — `created_by_user_id` at creation, `submitted_by_user_id` at submission, and `decided_by_user_id` at decision — and the decision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the deciding identity equals the submitting one. A `ComplianceOfficer` cannot open, submit, or decide a desk review at all, and an `AppraisalDeskReviewer` cannot run the bias screen or adjudicate a bias flag; the compliance opinion and the review opinion are produced by different people by construction.
## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Loan File Intake and the Underwriting Dimension

Everything at the desk hangs off a loan file, and a `LoanOfficer` registers one before any report can be delivered against it. Registration captures the lender's own `loan_number`, which must be between 1 and 32 characters long and unique across all loan files — a duplicate is a malformed body and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than with any dedicated conflict code; a `loan_purpose` of either `PURCHASE` or `REFINANCE`; a `market_type` of `URBAN`, `SUBURBAN`, or `RURAL`, which is what later selects the comparable distance ceiling; a `risk_tier` of `LOW`, `STANDARD`, `ELEVATED`, or `HIGH`, which is what later selects the escalation matrix row; a `loan_amount_cents` that must be greater than zero; and a `borrower_display_name` used on reconsideration correspondence. The purpose field also governs `contract_price_cents`: on a `PURCHASE` loan it is required and must be greater than zero, while on a `REFINANCE` loan it must be null. Supplying a contract price on a refinance is a malformed body and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, as is omitting it on a purchase.

A freshly registered loan file is born with `underwriting_status` `SUBMITTED`, `collateral_status` `PENDING_APPRAISAL`, and `version` 1. Its derived collateral fields all start empty: `value_of_record_appraisal_id`, `current_appraised_value_cents`, `effective_value_cents`, `ltv_bps`, and `decision_impact` are all null until a value becomes of record, and `rov_request_count` starts at 0 and thereafter simply counts the reconsideration requests filed against this loan.

The underwriting dimension moves two ways: by explicit operator action and by the automatic decision-impact cascade. Explicitly, a `LoanOfficer` supplies a `target_status` and an optional `note`, and exactly three explicit moves exist. The loan may advance `SUBMITTED → CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`. It may advance `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED → CLOSED`, but only when the collateral dimension has landed — `collateral_status` must be either `VALUE_ACCEPTED` or `VALUE_REVISED` — and only when no reconsideration request on this loan is still in `INTAKE` or `AWAITING_APPRAISER`; closing freezes the collateral dimension, and reconsideration intake is refused afterwards. And from any of three states the loan may be withdrawn — `SUBMITTED → WITHDRAWN`, `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED → WITHDRAWN`, and `SUSPENDED → WITHDRAWN` all exist, which additionally cancels every escalation order still in `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT` on any of this loan's appraisals — each such order moves to `CANCELLED` with its `cancel_reason` left null, and, exactly as a chief-appraiser cancellation would, reverts its desk review `ESCALATED → SUBMITTED` and that review's appraisal `ESCALATED → IN_DESK_REVIEW`. Any other explicitly requested target — for example `CLOSED` directly from `SUBMITTED`, or anything at all out of `CLOSED` or `WITHDRAWN`, which are both terminal — does not exist as a transition and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Each accepted explicit move increments the loan file's `version` by 1. A loan whose `collateral_status` is `QUARANTINED`, or whose value-of-record appraisal is under a substantiated bias quarantine, cannot have its underwriting dimension moved at all: the request is refused with `ERR_BIAS_QUARANTINE_ACTIVE`, and because that gate is evaluated before the requested transition is looked up at all, it is also what answers a quarantined loan asked for a target no transition would have reached.

The automatic moves are described in full in §3.9, but their shape belongs here: when a value recompute lands the loan in the worst decision-impact tier, the system suspends the loan from either `SUBMITTED` or `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` into `SUSPENDED`, and when a later recompute lifts the loan out of that tier the system reinstates it — always to `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`, regardless of which status it was suspended from. That asymmetry is intentional: a loan that was suspended straight out of `SUBMITTED` comes back as `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`, not as `SUBMITTED`.

Reading a loan file returns the whole derived picture in one shot, and the listing supports filtering by `underwriting_status`, by `collateral_status`, and by `risk_tier`, each of which narrows the result set before pagination is applied.

### 3.2. The Appraiser Panel: Rosters, Management Companies and Memberships

A `ChiefAppraiser` builds and maintains the panel. Adding an appraiser records a `full_name`, a `license_number` that must be unique across all appraisers — a duplicate is refused as a malformed body with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and a `license_state` given as exactly two uppercase letters. The `panel_status` may optionally be set at creation to any of `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION`, `SUSPENDED`, or `REMOVED`, and defaults to `ACTIVE`; after creation it is moved only by the panel state machine — the explicit panel-review actions and the automatic performance cascade of §3.9. A new appraiser starts with both score variants at a perfect 10000 basis points: `performance_score_bps` and `lifetime_performance_score_bps` are each initialised to 10000. Every panel tally starts empty — `revision_rate_bps` at 0, `total_appraisal_count` at 0, `substantiated_bias_count` at 0, `sla_miss_count` at 0, `open_assignment_count` at 0, `completed_assignment_count` at 0, and `panel_membership_count` at 0 — and `probation_started_at` and `removal_reason` are both null. The appraiser resource's `version` starts at 1. Three of those tallies are derived rather than nudged, and are recomputed from the stored rows every time anything touches the appraiser: `open_assignment_count` is the number of escalation orders assigned to that appraiser whose status is `ASSIGNED` or `REASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED`, `completed_assignment_count` the number in `COMPLETED`, and `panel_membership_count` the number of that appraiser's memberships still in `ACTIVE`. Appraisers may be listed filtered by `panel_status` and by `license_state`.

Registering a management company records a `name` that must be unique across all companies — a duplicate is refused as a malformed body with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — plus four operating parameters that all have defaults. `sla_response_hours` is the number of hours the company has to route an appraiser's response to a dispute and defaults to 72, and must be at least 1. `concurrent_dispute_capacity` is the number of mutually overlapping dispute review slots the company can host and defaults to 2, and must be at least 1. `escalation_budget_cents` is the ceiling on escalation spend chargeable to the company and defaults to 500000 cents — five thousand dollars — and must be zero or greater. `panel_status` may be set at creation to `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION`, or `SUSPENDED` and defaults to `ACTIVE`; thereafter it moves only through the SLA-breach cascade of §3.9. The three ledger counters start at zero: `ytd_escalation_spend_cents`, `sla_breach_count`, and `open_dispute_count`, and the company's `version` starts at 1. All three are likewise derived and recomputed from the stored rows rather than nudged: `ytd_escalation_spend_cents` is the sum of `total_cost_cents` over that company's escalation orders whose status is `ASSIGNED`, `REASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED`, or `COMPLETED` — so an order still awaiting assignment contributes nothing and a cancelled order drops straight back out of the ledger — `open_dispute_count` is the number of that company's disputes still in `OPEN`, and `sla_breach_count` is the tally defined in §3.9. Companies may be listed filtered by `panel_status`.

The relationship between appraisers and management companies is genuinely many-to-many, and it is resolved by an explicit membership record. Enrolling one names the `appraiser_id`, the `amc_id`, a `coverage_state` given as exactly two uppercase letters, and a `tier` of `GENERAL`, `COMPLEX`, or `REVIEW_SPECIALIST` describing the assignment tier the appraiser is approved for on that panel. A membership is born with `status` `ACTIVE`. At most one active membership may exist for any one combination of appraiser, company, and coverage state; a second attempt at the same triple is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PANEL_MEMBERSHIP` (HTTP 422). Enrolment is one of the three operations guarded by the stale-read token of §2.6, so `expected_appraiser_version` must carry the appraiser's current `version`; a successful enrolment increments that appraiser's `version` by 1 and increments the appraiser's `panel_membership_count`. An active membership becomes `SUPERSEDED` in exactly one circumstance: when the appraiser is removed from the panel — removal supersedes every active membership that appraiser holds. Enrolment itself never supersedes an earlier membership, since a second enrolment on the same triple is refused outright rather than allowed to replace its predecessor. Memberships can be listed filtered by `appraiser_id`, by `amc_id`, or by `status`.

Panel standing is what gates work. A management company whose `panel_status` is `SUSPENDED` may not receive new report deliveries and may not have new disputes filed against it: both are refused with `ERR_AMC_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). A company in `PROBATION` is not restricted — probation is a warning state that changes nothing operationally. On the appraiser side, panel standing gates escalation assignment with a finer distinction described in §3.7, and an appraiser who holds no active membership with the relevant company is refused with `ERR_APPRAISER_NOT_ON_PANEL` (HTTP 422).
### 3.3. Report Delivery, the Comparable Grid and Supersession

Delivery is the management company's move. An `AMCCoordinator` hands over a completed report against a loan file, naming the `loan_file_id` it values, the `appraiser_id` who authored it, and the `amc_id` that delivered it — and because an `AMCCoordinator` is entity-backed, the `amc_id` in the body must equal the coordinator's own `X-User-ID` or the delivery is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. That ownership comparison is only performed when `amc_id` actually arrives as a non-empty string; anything else skips the ownership rung entirely and leaves the request to be judged on the reference and body-shape rungs, so an absent or non-string `amc_id` is reported there as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) and an empty-string `amc_id` dangles as `ERR_AMC_NOT_FOUND` (404). The report itself is described by a `report_form_type` of `FORM_1004`, `FORM_1073`, or `FORM_2055`; an `effective_date` of value that must be at or before the logical clock at the moment of intake — a future `effective_date` is treated as a malformed body and refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), evaluated on the body-shape rung after every other field in the body has been shape-checked and therefore ahead of the stale-version token; a `reported_value_cents` greater than zero, which is the appraiser's original value opinion; a `sections_present` array listing which report sections the delivery contains; an `exhibit_count` of zero or more photos, sketches, and maps; the free-text `narrative_text` that the bias screen will later read, which is required but may be the empty string; and a `subject_census_tract_band` of `A`, `B`, `C`, or `D` describing the demographic band of the subject property's census tract, used only for comparable-selection skew screening. The `sections_present` array must be a duplicate-free subset of the ten recognised sections `SUBJECT`, `CONTRACT`, `NEIGHBORHOOD`, `SITE`, `IMPROVEMENTS`, `SALES_COMPARISON`, `RECONCILIATION`, `CERTIFICATION`, `ADDENDA`, and `MARKET_CONDITIONS`, and that subset may be empty. Delivery is the second of the three stale-read-guarded writes, so `expected_loan_file_version` must carry the loan file's current `version`.

Two eligibility gates stand in front of a delivery. The named management company must not be in `SUSPENDED` standing, or the delivery is refused with `ERR_AMC_NOT_ELIGIBLE`; and the named appraiser must hold an `ACTIVE` panel membership with that same management company, or the delivery is refused with `ERR_APPRAISER_NOT_ON_PANEL`. A successful delivery is created with `version` 1, with `review_status` `RECEIVED` and `bias_screen_status` `NOT_SCREENED`, and with every derived field empty: `completeness_score_bps`, `indicated_value_cents`, `variance_vs_reported_bps`, `bias_risk_index`, `current_value_cents`, and `quarantine_return_status` are all null, while `eligible_comp_count`, `closed_comp_count`, `max_gross_adjustment_bps`, `max_net_adjustment_bps`, `rule_check_fail_count`, and `rule_check_warn_count` all start at 0. The delivery increments the loan file's `version` by 1, increments the authoring appraiser's `total_appraisal_count` — which, being one of the recomputed roll-ups of §3.9 rather than a nudged counter, simply takes the new report in — and recomputes both of that appraiser's performance-score variants — because a new report changes the denominator of the revision rate, a delivery can move an appraiser's standing all on its own.

Delivery is also how a returned report is replaced. When the optional `supersedes_appraisal_id` is supplied it must name an existing appraisal — a dangling pointer yields `ERR_APPRAISAL_NOT_FOUND` — and that appraisal's `review_status` must be exactly `RETURNED_TO_AMC`. The replacement is created in `RECEIVED` and the superseded report moves `RETURNED_TO_AMC → SUPERSEDED`, which is the only terminal state on the review dimension and a transition that increments the superseded appraisal's own `version` by 1. Supersession has a quiet but consequential second effect: the superseded report is removed from its author's `total_appraisal_count`, and both of that author's performance-score variants are recomputed afterwards, so a report that was thrown back to the AMC no longer counts against — or for — the appraiser who wrote it. Attempting to supersede an appraisal that is in any state other than `RETURNED_TO_AMC` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Comparable sales are added to the appraisal's sales-comparison grid one row at a time by an `AMCCoordinator`, and the parent appraisal is always taken from the path rather than from the body. Grid addition is deliberately not one of the six ownership-checked writes of §2.1, so the acting coordinator's `X-User-ID` is never compared against the appraisal's `amc_id`: any `AMCCoordinator`, not only the delivering one, may add a row. Each row carries a `street_address`; a `sale_price_cents` greater than zero, being the unadjusted transaction price; a `gross_adjustment_cents` of zero or more, being the sum of the absolute values of every line adjustment; a `net_adjustment_cents`, being the signed sum of those same line adjustments and therefore permitted to be negative; a `distance_miles` of zero or more with at most two decimal places, measured from the subject property; a `sale_date` on or before the parent appraisal's `effective_date`; a `sale_status` of `CLOSED`, `PENDING`, or `ACTIVE_LISTING`; a `dwelling_type` of `SFR`, `CONDO`, `TOWNHOUSE`, or `MULTI_2_4`; a `census_tract_band` of `A`, `B`, `C`, or `D`; and an optional `included_in_grid` flag that defaults to true and decides whether this comparable participates in the grid at all.

Three per-row figures are computed the moment the comparable lands, and they are never recomputed afterwards. The `adjusted_price_cents` is the sale price plus the signed net adjustment. The `gross_adjustment_bps` is the gross adjustment expressed as a share of the sale price in basis points: multiply the gross adjustment in cents by 10000, divide by the sale price in cents, and round half-up to a whole number of basis points. The `net_adjustment_bps` is computed the same way but from the **absolute value** of the net adjustment, so a comparable adjusted downward by the same magnitude as one adjusted upward carries the same net-adjustment ratio. Adding a comparable also refreshes the parent appraisal's `eligible_comp_count`, which is the number of its comparables with `included_in_grid` true, and its `closed_comp_count`, which is the number of its comparables that are both in the grid and in `CLOSED` sale status. Those two tallies are the only appraisal-level roll-ups a comparable addition touches: the adjustment maxima `max_gross_adjustment_bps` and `max_net_adjustment_bps` are written by the rule-check run of §3.4 and by nothing else, so on an appraisal whose grid has been filled but whose checks have not yet run they still read 0, and after a run they keep the values that run wrote until the battery is run again.

Four guards protect the grid, and they fire in the ladder order of §2.8. The grid must be unlocked: a comparable may be added only while no desk review on this appraisal sits in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, or `ACCEPTED`, and only while the appraisal's `review_status` is none of `VALUE_ACCEPTED`, `VALUE_REVISED`, `QUARANTINED`, or `SUPERSEDED`. A locked grid rejects the addition with `ERR_APPRAISAL_GRID_LOCKED` (HTTP 409). Next, a `sale_date` later than the parent's `effective_date` is rejected with `ERR_COMP_DATE_AFTER_EFFECTIVE` (HTTP 422) — a comparable cannot have closed after the date as of which value was opined. Next, addresses are deduplicated on a normalised form: the `street_address` is compared case-folded with runs of whitespace collapsed, and a normalised address already present on this appraisal is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_COMPARABLE` (HTTP 422). Finally, the grid holds at most six comparables with `included_in_grid` true; an addition that would make the seventh in-grid comparable is rejected with `ERR_COMP_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), while rows explicitly excluded from the grid do not count toward that ceiling.

Reading the appraisal returns every roll-up in one payload — the rule-check scores, the adjustment maxima, the reconciled value, the bias index, and the current value. The appraisal listing supports filtering by `loan_file_id`, by `appraiser_id`, by `amc_id`, by `supersedes_appraisal_id` (to find the replacement written against a given superseded report), by `review_status`, and by `bias_screen_status`; that listing filtered by loan file is the authoritative reverse lookup from a loan to its reports, since the loan's own `value_of_record_appraisal_id` is a denormalised convenience pointer rather than a declarative relation. The comparable listing may be filtered by `included_in_grid` to see only the rows that actually drive the grid; it is one of the system's two boolean-valued list filters — the other being `admissible` on the reconsideration evidence listing of §3.8, which behaves identically — and it reads `true` and `false` case-insensitively once surrounding whitespace is trimmed, while any other value supplied for it matches no row at all and yields an empty page rather than an error.
### 3.4. The Automated Receipt Rule-Check Battery

Once a report and its grid are in the system, an `AppraisalDeskReviewer` runs the automated receipt checks. The run is guarded by `expected_appraisal_version`, which must equal the appraisal's current `version`, and it is available only from `RECEIVED`, `RULES_CLEARED`, or `RULES_FLAGGED`; asked for from any other review state — from `IN_DESK_REVIEW` onward, for instance — it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Re-running is legitimate and expected after a comparable edit. Availability is keyed on those three review statuses and on nothing else — the grid-lock condition of §3.3 is never consulted here, so a report that has been returned to the AMC, whose grid is unlocked again but whose `review_status` is `RETURNED_TO_AMC`, still cannot have the battery re-run and is answered with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; a corrected grid on a returned report is re-scored only by delivering a superseding report. Each accepted run increments the appraisal's `version` by 1 and answers HTTP 200 with the updated appraisal — not the eight rows it wrote — in `data`; the very first run on an appraisal also seeds `current_value_cents` from `reported_value_cents`, establishing the baseline that every later value revision moves off.

Every run emits **exactly eight** rule-check result rows, in one fixed emission order, and a re-run deletes the previous eight rows and writes a fresh eight — there is never a mixed generation on an appraisal. Each row records the parent `appraisal_id`, its `sequence_no` from 1 through 8, its `check_code`, its `outcome` of `PASS`, `WARN`, or `FAIL`, the `observed_value` as a string capturing the metric that produced the outcome, and a human-readable `detail` explaining it. Rule-check rows are append-only events: they carry `id` and `created_at` only, with no `updated_at` and no `version`. The fixed sequence is `CHK_COMPLETENESS` at position 1, `CHK_COMP_COUNT` at 2, `CHK_COMP_DISTANCE` at 3, `CHK_COMP_AGE` at 4, `CHK_GROSS_ADJUSTMENT` at 5, `CHK_NET_ADJUSTMENT` at 6, `CHK_VALUE_RECONCILIATION` at 7, and `CHK_FORM_TYPE` at 8. The listing endpoint returns them in that order and can be filtered by `outcome`.

Throughout the battery, "eligible" means exactly one thing: the appraisal's comparable rows whose `included_in_grid` is true. Rows excluded from the grid are ignored by all eight checks — they keep their own per-row ratio fields but never move a roll-up.

**Position 1, completeness.** The completeness score is a weighted blend of three components, each rounded to whole basis points *before* it is weighted. The section component looks only at the eight required sections, which are, in order, `SUBJECT`, `CONTRACT`, `NEIGHBORHOOD`, `SITE`, `IMPROVEMENTS`, `SALES_COMPARISON`, `RECONCILIATION`, and `CERTIFICATION`; the two remaining recognised sections, `ADDENDA` and `MARKET_CONDITIONS`, are accepted on input but contribute nothing. Count how many of those eight required sections appear in `sections_present`, multiply that count by 10000, divide by 8, and round half-up: that is the section component in basis points. The comparable component takes the lesser of `closed_comp_count` and the three-closed-comparable target, multiplies by 10000, divides by 3, and rounds half-up — so three or more closed in-grid comparables score a full 10000 and there is no credit beyond three. The exhibit component takes the lesser of `exhibit_count` and the seven-exhibit target, multiplies by 10000, divides by 7, and rounds half-up — again capped, so an eighth photograph earns nothing. The three components are then weighted: half the weight, 0.50, on sections; three-tenths, 0.30, on comparables; and two-tenths, 0.20, on exhibits. Multiply each rounded component by its weight, add the three products, and round the sum half-up once more; the result is `completeness_score_bps`. Weighting the *unrounded* components is forbidden — the per-component rounding happens first, then the weighting, then a single final rounding. The check outcome reads that score: `FAIL` when the score is below 6000 basis points, `WARN` when the score is at least 6000 but still below 8500, and `PASS` when the score is 8500 or higher.

**Position 2, closed-comparable count.** The observed value is `closed_comp_count`. The outcome is `FAIL` when fewer than three in-grid comparables are in `CLOSED` sale status, and `PASS` otherwise. This check never yields `WARN`.

**Position 3, comparable distance.** The distance ceiling is selected by the loan file's `market_type`: 1.00 mile for `URBAN`, 3.00 miles for `SUBURBAN`, and 5.00 miles for `RURAL`. The observed value is the greatest `distance_miles` among eligible comparables, always rendered at exactly two decimal places — `3.00`, never `3` or `3.0` — or the string `0.00` when there are none. The outcome is `FAIL` when any eligible comparable's distance is strictly greater than twice the ceiling; `WARN` when no comparable breaches twice the ceiling but at least one is strictly greater than the ceiling itself; and `PASS` otherwise, including when the eligible set is empty. Both comparisons are strict, so a comparable sitting exactly at the ceiling — 3.00 miles on a suburban loan — passes, and a comparable at exactly twice the ceiling warns rather than fails.

**Position 4, comparable age.** A comparable's age in days is the whole number of days from its `sale_date` to the parent appraisal's `effective_date`. The observed value is the greatest such age among eligible comparables, or the string `0` when there are none. The outcome is `FAIL` when any eligible comparable's age exceeds 365 days — a full year — and `PASS` otherwise, including on the empty set. This check never yields `WARN`.

**Positions 5 and 6, adjustment magnitudes.** The appraisal-level maxima are taken across eligible comparables only: `max_gross_adjustment_bps` is the greatest `gross_adjustment_bps` among them, and `max_net_adjustment_bps` is the greatest `net_adjustment_bps` among them; both are 0 when the eligible set is empty. The gross check is `FAIL` when the gross maximum is strictly greater than 2500 basis points — 25 percent of sale price on a single comparable — `WARN` when the maximum is strictly greater than 1500 basis points but no greater than 2500, and `PASS` otherwise, which includes the empty set where the metric is 0. The net check is `FAIL` when the net maximum is strictly greater than 1500 basis points, `WARN` when it is strictly greater than 1000 but no greater than 1500, and `PASS` otherwise.

**Position 7, value reconciliation.** When there are no eligible comparables, `indicated_value_cents` and `variance_vs_reported_bps` are both set to null and this check passes with its observed value recorded as the string `0` — there is nothing to reconcile. Otherwise the reconciled indicated value is built with a **pinned rounding order**: take the eligible comparables in ascending `created_at` order, divide each one's `adjusted_price_cents` by the number of eligible comparables, round *that per-comparable quotient* half-up to whole cents, and then add the rounded quotients together. Summing the adjusted prices first and dividing once at the end is forbidden and produces a different answer whenever the per-comparable remainders do not cancel. The variance is then the absolute difference between the indicated value and `reported_value_cents`, multiplied by 10000, divided by `reported_value_cents`, and rounded half-up to whole basis points. The outcome is `FAIL` when the variance is strictly greater than 500 basis points, `WARN` when it is strictly greater than 250 but no greater than 500, and `PASS` otherwise.

**Position 8, form type.** The observed value is the `report_form_type`. The outcome is `FAIL` when the form is `FORM_2055` *and* the loan file's `risk_tier` is `ELEVATED` or `HIGH` — an exterior-only form is not acceptable evidence on the lender's riskiest paper — and `PASS` in every other combination.

After all eight rows are written, `rule_check_fail_count` is set to the number of rows whose outcome is `FAIL` and `rule_check_warn_count` to the number whose outcome is `WARN`. The run then lands the review dimension: the appraisal moves to `RULES_CLEARED` when the fail count is zero, and to `RULES_FLAGGED` otherwise. Warnings alone never block — a run with zero failures and any number of warnings still clears, which is why a `RULES_CLEARED` appraisal can still carry a non-zero warn count into the desk-review scoring. In addition to the eight rows and the two tallies, the run writes `completeness_score_bps`, refreshes `eligible_comp_count` and `closed_comp_count`, and writes `max_gross_adjustment_bps`, `max_net_adjustment_bps`, `indicated_value_cents`, and `variance_vs_reported_bps`. Stated as edges, a first run produces either `RECEIVED → RULES_CLEARED` or `RECEIVED → RULES_FLAGGED`; a re-run that comes back with zero failures produces `RULES_CLEARED → RULES_CLEARED` or `RULES_FLAGGED → RULES_CLEARED`, the second of which is how a flagged report is rehabilitated after its grid is corrected.
### 3.5. Bias Screening, Quarantine, and the Appraiser-Communication Channel

The compliance dimension runs on its own track, owned exclusively by a `ComplianceOfficer`. Screening an appraisal is guarded by `expected_appraisal_version` matching the appraisal's current `version`, increments that `version` by 1, and — this is the point of a parallel dimension — leaves `review_status` completely untouched. Indeed the screen never reads `review_status` and imposes no grid-lock condition either, so it is available on a report at any point in its review lifecycle — a superseded or quarantined report included — and it answers HTTP 200 with the updated appraisal in `data`. A screen may be re-run while `bias_screen_status` is `NOT_SCREENED`, `CLEAR`, or `INDICATED`, and a re-run replaces the appraisal's `OPEN` bias flags with a fresh set while leaving already-adjudicated flags exactly where they are; once the status has reached `SUBSTANTIATED` or `CLEARED_ON_REVIEW` the screening channel is closed and a further run is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).

Screening reads two channels. The **narrative channel** performs a case-insensitive substring test of the appraisal's `narrative_text` against an ordered list of eight prohibited terms: position 1 is "desirable neighborhood", position 2 "pride of ownership", position 3 "crime-ridden", position 4 "ethnic", position 5 "student neighborhood", position 6 "up and coming", position 7 "gentrifying", and position 8 "family-oriented". A term that appears several times in the narrative counts exactly once. The **comparable-selection channel** measures band skew: among eligible comparables — those with `included_in_grid` true — count how many carry a `census_tract_band` different from the appraisal's own `subject_census_tract_band`, multiply that off-band count by 10000, divide by the number of eligible comparables, and round half-up to whole basis points. When there are no eligible comparables the skew is 0 basis points rather than undefined.

The `bias_risk_index` is then assembled on a 0-to-100 scale. Each matched narrative term contributes 12 points. The selection channel contributes the skew in basis points divided by 200 and rounded half-up — so a grid where every single comparable is off-band, a skew of 10000 basis points, contributes 50 points, and a grid where exactly half are off-band contributes 25. Add the narrative contribution to the selection contribution and cap the result at 100; that capped value is the index. The screen then lands the compliance dimension: `bias_screen_status` becomes `INDICATED` when the index is 25 or higher, and `CLEAR` when it is below 25. `NOT_SCREENED → CLEAR` and `NOT_SCREENED → INDICATED` are both first-screen outcomes; a re-run can move `CLEAR → INDICATED` when a grid edit pushes the index up to the threshold, and equally `INDICATED → CLEAR` when a grid edit pulls it back below 25 — the re-run restates the status from the freshly computed index alone, taking no account of any flag an earlier generation left behind.

Flags are emitted in a fixed order, and a `CLEAR` outcome does not mean no flags — a single matched term scores only 12 and still writes a flag. First come the narrative flags, one per matched term, ordered by that term's position in the prohibited list: each carries `source` `NARRATIVE`, an `indicator_code` of the form `TERM_` followed by the term's one-based position (so a hit on "crime-ridden" is `TERM_3`), a `matched_text` holding the prohibited term exactly as declared in the list, and a `severity_weight` of 12. Then, and only when the band skew is at least 5000 basis points, comes a single selection flag with `source` `COMP_SELECTION`, `indicator_code` `COMP_BAND_SKEW`, a null `matched_text`, and a `severity_weight` equal to the skew in basis points divided by 200 and rounded half-up. Every flag is born with `status` `OPEN`, with `adjudicated_by_user_id` and `rationale` both null. The flag listing may be filtered by `status` and by `source`.

Adjudication is where the compliance dimension reaches into the review dimension. A `ComplianceOfficer` supplies a `disposition` of either `SUBSTANTIATE` or `CLEAR` together with a mandatory `rationale`; the rationale is recorded on the flag alongside the `adjudicated_by_user_id` taken from `X-User-ID`, and a missing rationale is a malformed body. Either disposition answers HTTP 200 with the updated flag — not the appraisal — in `data`. Substantiating a flag moves the flag itself `OPEN → SUBSTANTIATED` and is available from `OPEN` and nowhere else, so a second `SUBSTANTIATE` against a flag already in `SUBSTANTIATED`, and either disposition against a flag already in the terminal `CLEARED`, are both refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. It is the heavy action, cascading in six directions at once. The appraisal's current `review_status` is first snapshotted into `quarantine_return_status`, and only then is `review_status` set to `QUARANTINED`; `bias_screen_status` moves `INDICATED → SUBSTANTIATED`, and that is the only edge substantiation draws on the compliance dimension, so a flag substantiated on a report whose screen had landed `CLEAR` — or whose earlier appeal had landed `CLEARED_ON_REVIEW` — leaves `bias_screen_status` exactly where it stands while `review_status` still moves to `QUARANTINED`; the loan file's `collateral_status` is set to `QUARANTINED` when it currently reads `VALUE_ACCEPTED` or `VALUE_REVISED`, and in every other collateral state the loan file is not written at all; the authoring appraiser's `substantiated_bias_count` is incremented and both performance-score variants are recomputed; the appraiser's panel standing is re-evaluated against the performance cascade of §3.9; and if the appraiser stands in `PROBATION`, `SUSPENDED`, or `REMOVED` once that recompute has run — whether or not this recompute is what moved it there — every escalation order currently `ASSIGNED` to that appraiser whose escalation type is `SECOND_APPRAISAL` is forced to `REASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED`. Quarantine is reachable from every non-terminal review state — `RULES_CLEARED → QUARANTINED`, `RULES_FLAGGED → QUARANTINED`, `IN_DESK_REVIEW → QUARANTINED`, `VALUE_ACCEPTED → QUARANTINED`, and `VALUE_REVISED → QUARANTINED` all exist and all carry the identical cascade, and `RECEIVED → QUARANTINED`, `ESCALATED → QUARANTINED`, `RETURNED_TO_AMC → QUARANTINED` and `VALUE_CONTESTED → QUARANTINED` stand on exactly the same footing; only the terminal `SUPERSEDED` is closed to it, and a substantiation against a superseded report is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Substantiating a further flag on a report that already reads `QUARANTINED` is permitted and repeats the rest of the cascade, but it leaves the `quarantine_return_status` snapshot the first substantiation wrote exactly as it stands. On the collateral dimension both `VALUE_ACCEPTED → QUARANTINED` and `VALUE_REVISED → QUARANTINED` follow. While the loan's `collateral_status` is `QUARANTINED` the loan cannot close.

Clearing a flag has two quite different meanings depending on where the flag stands, and both must be implemented. Clearing an open flag is the edge `OPEN → CLEARED`, and `CLEARED` is terminal for the flag; if it was the *last* flag on the appraisal still in `OPEN`, the appraisal's `bias_screen_status` moves `INDICATED → CLEAR`, which is what unblocks desk-review acceptance. Clearing a substantiated flag — the edge `SUBSTANTIATED → CLEARED`, which also lands the flag in its terminal `CLEARED` state — is the bias **appeal**, permitted only when no other flag on that appraisal is still `SUBSTANTIATED`, and it reverses the quarantine: `review_status` is restored from `quarantine_return_status` and that snapshot field is cleared back to null, `bias_screen_status` moves `SUBSTANTIATED → CLEARED_ON_REVIEW`, the loan file's `collateral_status`, when and only when it is actually sitting in `QUARANTINED`, is restored to the value implied by the restored review status — so an appraisal whose snapshot was `VALUE_ACCEPTED` returns the loan to `QUARANTINED → VALUE_ACCEPTED` and one whose snapshot was `VALUE_REVISED` returns it to `QUARANTINED → VALUE_REVISED`, while a loan that the substantiation never pulled into quarantine is left unwritten — and the authoring appraiser's `substantiated_bias_count` is decremented with both performance-score variants recomputed afterwards. The snapshot field only ever holds a review status other than `QUARANTINED` or `SUPERSEDED`, or null. This appeal edge is the only inbound path back to a quarantined appraisal's prior status.

A substantiated quarantine is a hard stop on value movement, and it blocks exactly four operations, each with `ERR_BIAS_QUARANTINE_ACTIVE` (HTTP 422): deciding a desk review, routing a reconsideration request to the appraiser, completing an escalation order, and moving a loan's underwriting status. The condition in all four cases is the same — the appraisal's `bias_screen_status` is `SUBSTANTIATED` or its `review_status` is `QUARANTINED`. Note that `CLEARED_ON_REVIEW` is not a blocking value: once the appeal succeeds, all four operations are available again.

Communications live alongside the screen because they are the other half of the independence record. Any of `AppraisalDeskReviewer`, `ChiefAppraiser`, `AMCCoordinator`, `LoanOfficer`, or `Borrower` may record a message against an appraisal, supplying the `recipient`, the `intent`, and the `body` — required, but permitted to be the empty string. Recording a communication is not one of the ownership-checked writes, so a coordinator or borrower with no relationship at all to the report may still record one and the acting `X-User-ID` is never compared with the appraisal's parties. The server stamps `sent_at` from the logical clock and records `sender_role` and `sender_user_id` from the request headers. Communication records are append-only events carrying only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version`. Beyond the intent restrictions of §2.10, one state guard applies: an appraisal stops accepting communications once its `review_status` is `SUPERSEDED` or `QUARANTINED`, and an attempt to record one against such a report is refused with `ERR_COMMUNICATION_LOCKED` (HTTP 409). The audit trail can be listed and filtered by `recipient` and by `intent`.
### 3.6. Desk Review: Quality Scoring, Submission and the Escalation Decision

Opening a desk review is how a human reviewer takes ownership of a delivered report. An `AppraisalDeskReviewer` names the `appraisal_id` in the body, may attach an optional `reviewer_note`, and must supply `expected_appraisal_version` matching the appraisal's current `version`. Three preconditions gate the open. The receipt checks must already have run, which is to say the appraisal's `review_status` must be `RULES_CLEARED` or `RULES_FLAGGED` and its `completeness_score_bps` therefore non-null; an appraisal still in `RECEIVED` is refused with `ERR_RULE_CHECKS_NOT_RUN` (HTTP 409). The bias screen must already have run, which is to say `bias_screen_status` must be anything other than `NOT_SCREENED`; an unscreened appraisal is refused with `ERR_BIAS_SCREEN_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). And no other desk review on that appraisal may currently sit in `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED`; a second concurrent review, or an open attempted from any review state other than `RECEIVED`, `RULES_CLEARED`, or `RULES_FLAGGED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — `RECEIVED` is deliberately let past that rung so that it is answered one rung lower by the more specific `ERR_RULE_CHECKS_NOT_RUN`. Notice the deliberate ordering: because both `RULES_CLEARED → IN_DESK_REVIEW` and `RULES_FLAGGED → IN_DESK_REVIEW` exist, a report that failed its receipt checks is still reviewable — flagged is not the same as rejected.

A successful open does five things. It creates the desk review in `DRAFT`, recording `created_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and leaving `submitted_by_user_id`, `decided_by_user_id`, `final_quality_score_bps`, `required_escalation`, `decision_action`, `findings_note`, and `decision_note` all null. It snapshots the loan file's `risk_tier` into `risk_tier_at_review` — and that snapshot, not the live loan field, is what the escalation matrix will read later, so a risk tier that changes mid-review does not change the matrix row. It writes the preliminary quality score. It moves the appraisal to `IN_DESK_REVIEW` and increments the appraisal's `version` by 1. And it moves the loan file's `collateral_status` to `UNDER_REVIEW` (`PENDING_APPRAISAL → UNDER_REVIEW`) and increments the loan file's `version` by 1 — that write is unconditional, so a loan resting in some other collateral state, `VALUE_ACCEPTED` or `VALUE_CONTESTED` included, is pulled back to `UNDER_REVIEW` just the same — while locking the appraisal's comparable grid against further additions.

The quality score exists in two variants that share a base and differ in which penalties they apply, and the difference is not cosmetic. Both start from the appraisal's persisted `completeness_score_bps`. Both subtract a rule penalty of 600 basis points for every rule-check row with outcome `FAIL` plus 200 basis points for every row with outcome `WARN`. The **preliminary** variant stops there: it is the base minus the rule penalty, floored at zero, and it is computed exactly once at open time, persisted to `preliminary_quality_score_bps`, and never recomputed no matter what happens afterwards. It deliberately excludes the distance and bias penalties entirely, because at open time the reviewer has neither walked the grid nor read the bias screen. The **final** variant, computed at submission and persisted to `final_quality_score_bps`, subtracts all four terms: the same rule penalty, plus a distance penalty of 300 basis points applied when any eligible comparable's `distance_miles` is strictly greater than the ceiling for the loan file's `market_type`, plus a bias penalty of 1500 basis points when `bias_screen_status` is `INDICATED` or 3000 basis points when it is `SUBSTANTIATED` and nothing at all otherwise — again floored at zero. Every term is an integer basis-point quantity, so no rounding is required at any step. For identical appraisal data the final score is therefore strictly lower than the preliminary score whenever a distance breach or a bias indication exists, and the gap is exactly the sum of the distance and bias penalties. The final score is the one the escalation matrix reads.

Submission is the reviewer's handoff, and it may be performed by any `AppraisalDeskReviewer` rather than only by the one who opened the review: `submitted_by_user_id` simply records whoever called it, and it is that recorded identity — never `created_by_user_id` — that the segregation-of-duties check compares against at decision time. The reviewer supplies an optional `findings_note`, the review must be in `DRAFT`, and the appraisal's `bias_screen_status` must still not be `NOT_SCREENED` — the bias-screen requirement is enforced a second time here, with the same `ERR_BIAS_SCREEN_REQUIRED`, because a review could in principle have been opened and the screen results discarded. Submission moves `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, records `submitted_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, writes `final_quality_score_bps`, and resolves `required_escalation`.

Resolving the escalation requirement runs three clauses in a fixed order. **Clause one is a bias override and it short-circuits everything else**: if the appraisal's `bias_screen_status` is `INDICATED` or `SUBSTANTIATED`, `required_escalation` is `SECOND_APPRAISAL` and no further clause is consulted. **Clause two is the risk-tier matrix**, keyed on `risk_tier_at_review` and read with strictly-less-than comparisons throughout, so a score sitting exactly on a cut-off does *not* trigger that row. On a `HIGH` tier loan, a final score below 7000 basis points requires `SECOND_APPRAISAL`; otherwise a score below 8500 requires `FIELD_REVIEW`; otherwise `NONE`. On an `ELEVATED` tier loan, below 6000 requires `SECOND_APPRAISAL`; otherwise below 8000 requires `FIELD_REVIEW`; otherwise `NONE`. On a `STANDARD` tier loan, below 6000 requires `FIELD_REVIEW`, and otherwise `NONE`. On a `LOW` tier loan, below 5000 requires `FIELD_REVIEW`, and otherwise `NONE`. **Clause three is an adjustment-magnitude upgrade applied after the matrix, and it never downgrades**: if the appraisal's `max_gross_adjustment_bps` is strictly greater than 2500 basis points and the matrix produced `NONE`, the requirement is raised to `FIELD_REVIEW`. A matrix result of `FIELD_REVIEW` or `SECOND_APPRAISAL` is left exactly as it is by clause three.

The decision belongs to a `ChiefAppraiser`, who supplies a `decision_action` of `ACCEPT`, `ESCALATE`, or `RETURN` plus an optional `decision_note`. The review must be in `SUBMITTED` or the request is refused with `ERR_DESK_REVIEW_NOT_SUBMITTED` (HTTP 409), and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_user_id` or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The action must agree with the resolved requirement: `ACCEPT` is permitted only when `required_escalation` is `NONE`, and `ESCALATE` only when it is anything other than `NONE`; either mismatch is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). `ACCEPT` additionally requires that the appraisal's `bias_screen_status` is neither `INDICATED` nor `SUBSTANTIATED`: an `INDICATED` screen refuses the acceptance with `ERR_ESCALATION_REQUIRED`, while a `SUBSTANTIATED` one is caught a rung earlier by the quarantine gate and answers `ERR_BIAS_QUARANTINE_ACTIVE` instead. That quarantine gate is not confined to `ACCEPT` — it is evaluated on every decision action, so `ESCALATE` and `RETURN` are refused with `ERR_BIAS_QUARANTINE_ACTIVE` just as readily while the appraisal is quarantined. `RETURN` carries no requirement constraint at all — a review may be returned to the AMC whatever the matrix said. Whichever action is taken, the acting identity is recorded as `decided_by_user_id` and the action itself as `decision_action`.

`ACCEPT` is the value-of-record event. The review moves `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, which is terminal, and the appraisal moves `IN_DESK_REVIEW → VALUE_ACCEPTED`. The loan file's `value_of_record_appraisal_id` is pointed at this appraisal, its `collateral_status` moves `UNDER_REVIEW → VALUE_ACCEPTED`, and `current_appraised_value_cents`, `effective_value_cents`, `ltv_bps`, and `decision_impact` are all recomputed as described in §3.9, with the underwriting cascade following from the resulting impact tier.

`ESCALATE` moves the review `SUBMITTED → ESCALATED` and the appraisal `IN_DESK_REVIEW → ESCALATED`, and it creates an escalation order in `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT` whose `escalation_type` is copied from `required_escalation` and whose `due_at` is stamped as described in §3.7. The loan file's `collateral_status` stays at `UNDER_REVIEW` — a self-transition `UNDER_REVIEW → UNDER_REVIEW`, since escalation is still review in progress, and one the service records by not writing the loan file at all, so neither the loan's `updated_at` nor its `version` moves. Notably, the management company's escalation budget is **not** consulted at escalation time; the budget check happens at assignment, so a company that is already at its ceiling can still have an order created against it and will only be stopped when someone tries to staff it.

`RETURN` moves the review `SUBMITTED → RETURNED`, which is terminal, and the appraisal `IN_DESK_REVIEW → RETURNED_TO_AMC`. The loan file's `collateral_status` again stays at `UNDER_REVIEW` with the loan file likewise left unwritten, and the comparable grid is **unlocked** so that the management company can either correct the grid or deliver a superseding report — though, as §3.4 records, the receipt battery itself cannot be re-run on a report sitting in `RETURNED_TO_AMC`. Desk reviews may be read individually and listed filtered by `appraisal_id` and by `status`.
### 3.7. Escalation Orders: Assignment, Pricing, Delivery and Reversal

An escalation order is created by the desk-review decision, never by a direct client call, and it inherits its context: `desk_review_id` points back at the review whose `ESCALATE` decision produced it, `amc_id` is copied from the escalated appraisal and identifies the party the escalation cost is charged to, and `escalation_type` is copied from the review's `required_escalation` and is therefore either `FIELD_REVIEW` or `SECOND_APPRAISAL`. The order is born in `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT` with `assigned_appraiser_id`, both inspection-window endpoints, all four cost fields, `resulting_value_cents`, `documentation_note`, and `cancel_reason` all null. Its `due_at` is stamped at creation as the order's `created_at` plus the turnaround allowance for its type: 72 hours — three days — for a `FIELD_REVIEW`, and 168 hours — a full week — for a `SECOND_APPRAISAL`.

Staffing the order is a `ChiefAppraiser` action that supplies the `assigned_appraiser_id` together with an `inspection_window_start` and an `inspection_window_end`. It is available from `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT` as a first assignment and from `REASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED` as a re-assignment — both `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT → ASSIGNED` and `REASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED → ASSIGNED` run the same eligibility gauntlet and the same pricing, but they differ in their ledger and counter handling: a first assignment *adds* the computed `total_cost_cents` to the company's `ytd_escalation_spend_cents` and increments the assignee's `open_assignment_count`, whereas a re-assignment recomputes the cost fields from the current clock and the new window, *replaces* the order's previous spend contribution with the recomputed `total_cost_cents`, and decrements the prior appraiser's `open_assignment_count` while incrementing the new appraiser's; from any other status the request is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_NOT_PENDING` (HTTP 409). Four eligibility conditions then apply, in the ladder order of §2.8. The candidate must hold an `ACTIVE` panel membership with the escalated appraisal's management company — that is, with the company named by the order's own `amc_id`, and any `ACTIVE` membership on that company's panel qualifies whatever `coverage_state` it names, since the escalation gate asks only about the appraiser-and-company pair — or the request is refused with `ERR_APPRAISER_NOT_ON_PANEL`. The candidate's panel standing must permit the order's type, and the two types differ: a `SECOND_APPRAISAL` may be assigned only to an appraiser whose `panel_status` is `ACTIVE`, while a `FIELD_REVIEW` may be assigned to an appraiser in `ACTIVE` or in `PROBATION`; an appraiser in `SUSPENDED` or `REMOVED` is eligible for neither. The candidate must also not be the authoring appraiser of the appraisal being escalated — nobody reviews their own report. Both the standing failure and the self-review failure are refused with `ERR_APPRAISER_INELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422).

The submitted window must be well formed: `inspection_window_start` strictly before `inspection_window_end`, and both endpoints present whenever either is, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). Omitting the window altogether is a different failure from submitting a bad one: a body that carries neither endpoint has submitted no interval at all and is rejected on the body-shape rung as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), so `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` is reserved for the cases where exactly one endpoint is supplied or the pair is ordered wrongly. Then the window is checked against every other window the same appraiser already holds. Inspection windows are **half-open** — they run from the start instant inclusive to the end instant exclusive — and the **travel buffer of 90 minutes is applied to the existing window on both sides, while the incoming window is compared bare**. For each escalation order already held by the candidate whose status is `ASSIGNED` or `REASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED` and whose window endpoints are not null, widen that existing window by an hour and a half at each end, and treat any non-empty intersection with the incoming window as a conflict: the incoming window conflicts when its start falls before the widened upper edge and its end falls after the widened lower edge. Because the upper bound is exclusive, a new window that begins exactly at the widened upper edge — exactly 90 minutes after an existing window's end — is accepted. Any conflict is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Note that only appraiser inspection windows use these buffered, half-open, any-overlap-is-fatal semantics; the dispute review slots of §3.8 use entirely different semantics and the two never interact.

Pricing runs at assignment in **five ordered steps**, and reordering the surcharge and discount steps changes the answer. Step one sets `base_fee_cents` from the type: 45000 cents for a `FIELD_REVIEW`, 65000 cents for a `SECOND_APPRAISAL`. Step two decides the rush surcharge from the turnaround still remaining at the instant of assignment: take the number of hours from the logical clock to the order's `due_at`, and treat the order as rush when that remaining figure is **strictly less than** 72 hours — exactly 72.0 hours remaining is not rush, and an order whose `due_at` the clock has already passed carries a negative remaining figure and is therefore always rush. A rush order's `rush_surcharge_cents` is 30 percent of the base fee, rounded half-up to whole cents; a non-rush order's surcharge is 0. Step three picks the volume discount tier from the **post-surcharge subtotal**, being base fee plus surcharge: a subtotal below 50000 cents earns `discount_pct_bps` of 0; a subtotal from 50000 cents up to and including 80000 cents earns 500 basis points; a subtotal above 80000 cents earns 1000 basis points. Step four computes the discount in cents by multiplying the subtotal by the discount rate in basis points, dividing by 10000, and rounding half-up to whole cents. Step five sets `total_cost_cents` to the subtotal minus that discount.

A successful assignment writes all four cost fields, adds `total_cost_cents` to the management company's `ytd_escalation_spend_cents`, increments the assignee's `open_assignment_count`, and reserves the inspection window on that appraiser so it participates in future conflict checks. Before any of that is persisted, however, the fiscal ceiling is enforced: the company's `ytd_escalation_spend_cents` plus this order's freshly computed `total_cost_cents` must not exceed its `escalation_budget_cents`, and an assignment that would breach the ceiling is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402) with no cost fields written and no window reserved.

Re-assignment out of `REASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED` behaves like a fresh assignment on every count and must satisfy every one of the same conditions, but its ledger handling is a replacement rather than an addition: the cost fields are recomputed from the current logical clock and the newly submitted window — which usually changes the rush determination, since the clock has moved on while `due_at` has not — and the company's spend contribution from this order is *replaced* by the recomputed `total_cost_cents` rather than added to. The previous assignee's `open_assignment_count` is decremented and the new assignee's is incremented.

An order can also be pushed into `REASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED` without anyone calling anything, and this is one of the system's most important cascades. Whenever an assigned appraiser's `panel_status` leaves `ACTIVE` — by the automatic performance cascade of §3.9, by a bias substantiation, or by an explicit removal — every order currently `ASSIGNED` to that appraiser whose `escalation_type` is `SECOND_APPRAISAL` moves `ASSIGNED → REASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED`. Such an order retains its `total_cost_cents` and its contribution to the company's spend, and it retains `assigned_appraiser_id` for the audit trail while completion is forbidden until it is re-staffed. Explicit removal of an appraiser is broader still: it moves *every* order for that appraiser that is `ASSIGNED` or already `REASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED` into `REASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED`, regardless of escalation type.

Completion is the delivering `AMCCoordinator`'s move — and because the role is entity-backed, the order's `amc_id` must equal the coordinator's `X-User-ID` or the call is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. It requires the order to be in `ASSIGNED`, so an order sitting in `REASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED` cannot be completed and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The coordinator supplies a `resulting_value_cents` greater than zero, being the value opinion the field review or second appraisal produced, and a mandatory `documentation_note` recording the value revision rationale. The appraisal must not be under a substantiated bias quarantine, and — evaluated after that gate, per the ladder of §2.8 — the resulting value must also respect the revision cap of §3.9, so a completion that is both quarantined and over the cap answers `ERR_BIAS_QUARANTINE_ACTIVE`. Completion moves the order `ASSIGNED → COMPLETED`, moves the desk review `ESCALATED → CLOSED`, and moves the appraisal `ESCALATED → VALUE_REVISED`; it writes a value revision whose `source_type` is `FIELD_REVIEW` or `SECOND_APPRAISAL` to match the order's type; it sets the appraisal's `current_value_cents` to the resulting value; it points the loan file's `value_of_record_appraisal_id` at that appraisal and writes the loan's `collateral_status` to `VALUE_REVISED`, both as plain assignments that do not consult what was there before — so the edge is `UNDER_REVIEW → VALUE_REVISED` in every ordinary run, but a loan resting on some other collateral state is simply overwritten rather than refused — and recomputes the loan's value, `ltv_bps`, and `decision_impact` with the underwriting cascade following; it increments the authoring appraiser's revision tally and recomputes both performance-score variants, cascading panel standing; and it decrements the assignee's `open_assignment_count`, increments the assignee's `completed_assignment_count`, and releases the inspection window. What completion does **not** do is refund anything: a `COMPLETED` order's `total_cost_cents` stays in the company's `ytd_escalation_spend_cents` permanently.

Cancellation is a `ChiefAppraiser` action requiring a `cancel_reason`, and it is available from `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT`, `ASSIGNED`, or `REASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED` — the edges `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT → CANCELLED`, `ASSIGNED → CANCELLED`, and `REASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED → CANCELLED`. Whichever it is, cancellation is a **revert edge**, not a dead end: the desk review moves `ESCALATED → SUBMITTED` and the appraisal moves `ESCALATED → IN_DESK_REVIEW`, so the review returns to the chief appraiser's decision queue for a fresh decision on the same review, while the loan's `collateral_status` stays at `UNDER_REVIEW`. The review keeps its `final_quality_score_bps`, its `required_escalation`, and its `submitted_by_user_id` unchanged — it is not re-scored and the segregation-of-duties constraint still names the same original submitter. The ledger handling depends on where the order was. Cancelling from `ASSIGNED` or `REASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED` writes a compensating ledger entry: `total_cost_cents` is **subtracted** from the company's `ytd_escalation_spend_cents`, while the order itself retains `total_cost_cents` and `assigned_appraiser_id` for audit; the assignee's `open_assignment_count` is decremented and the inspection window is released. Cancelling from `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT` touches no ledger at all, because `total_cost_cents` is still null and nothing was ever charged. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal. One further path reaches cancellation without a chief appraiser: withdrawing a loan file cancels every escalation order still in `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT` on any of that loan's appraisals.

Escalation orders may be read individually — the read exposes the full cost breakdown — and listed filtered by `desk_review_id`, `assigned_appraiser_id`, `amc_id`, and `status`.
### 3.8. Reconsideration of Value: Intake, the Evidence Channel, Rounds and Closure

A borrower who believes the collateral was undervalued has one structured route to say so, and it is deliberately evidence-driven rather than sentiment-driven. Intake may be filed either by the `Borrower` directly or by an `AppraisalDeskReviewer` on the borrower's behalf, naming the `loan_file_id` being contested, the `appraisal_id` whose value is contested, a `borrower_statement` giving the written basis for the reconsideration, and a `requested_value_cents` greater than zero being the value the borrower asks the appraiser to consider. When a `Borrower` files it, `X-User-ID` must equal the `loan_file_id` in the body, or the filing is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. That comparison is made against the raw body value before the reference itself is resolved, so a `Borrower` naming a loan file that is neither its own nor extant still answers `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` rather than a 404; a body that omits `loan_file_id` altogether, or carries something other than a string in that position, offers nothing to compare and falls straight through to the body-shape rung as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The loan must be eligible: its `underwriting_status` must be `SUBMITTED`, `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED`, or `SUSPENDED`, and the named appraisal must actually belong to the named loan; a loan that is `CLOSED` or `WITHDRAWN`, or an appraisal-loan mismatch, is refused with `ERR_LOAN_NOT_ROV_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). The same eligibility rule is enforced a second time when the request is later routed to the appraiser, with the same error code, so a loan that closes between intake and routing blocks the routing.

A new request is created with `status` `INTAKE` and `round_number` 1, with `admissible_evidence_count` at 0, and with `response_deadline_at`, `appraiser_responded_at`, `outcome`, `revised_value_cents`, `intake_note`, `response_note`, `next_round_reason`, and `closure_note` all null and `responded_late` false. Filing also increments the loan file's `rov_request_count`.

Evidence is filed against the request one item at a time, again by either the `Borrower` or an `AppraisalDeskReviewer`, with the parent request taken from the path rather than the body — the item's `rov_request_id` is stamped from that path segment and is never accepted as a body field. Each item declares an `evidence_type` of `CLOSED_SALE`, `ACTIVE_LISTING_WITH_CONTRACT`, `DATA_ERROR_CITATION`, or `OPINION_STATEMENT`, and a `street_address`; and it may carry a `sale_price_cents`, a `sale_date`, a `distance_miles` with at most two decimal places, and a `citation_note`, each of which is optional at the transport level but may be mandatory for admissibility depending on the type. When they are supplied, `sale_price_cents` must be greater than zero and `distance_miles` must be zero or greater, and a value outside those bounds is a malformed body rather than an inadmissible item. Filing is gated on nothing beyond the parent's existence and the caller's ownership of it: no status of the parent request forbids it, so items may be added while the request sits in `AWAITING_APPRAISER` or `RESPONDED` and even after it has closed, and every filing refreshes the parent's `admissible_evidence_count` and `updated_at` without incrementing the parent's `version`. Admissibility is decided **once, at filing**, and is never re-evaluated afterwards: the item's `admissible` flag and, when it fails, its `inadmissible_reason` are written at that moment and frozen. Filing also snapshots the parent's current `round_number` onto the item, so an item always belongs to the round in which it was filed.

The admissibility clauses are evaluated in a pinned order and the **first** clause to fire sets `inadmissible_reason` and stops the evaluation. Clause one: an `OPINION_STATEMENT` is never admissible, and yields `TYPE_NOT_ADMISSIBLE`. Clause two checks required attributes by type — a `CLOSED_SALE` and an `ACTIVE_LISTING_WITH_CONTRACT` each require all three of `sale_price_cents`, `sale_date`, and `distance_miles`, while a `DATA_ERROR_CITATION` requires a `citation_note` — and a missing attribute yields `MISSING_REQUIRED_ATTRIBUTE`. Clause three applies only to the two sale-like types: when the whole number of days from the item's `sale_date` to the contested appraisal's `effective_date` exceeds 365, the reason is `STALE_SALE_DATE`. Clause four also applies only to the two sale-like types: when `distance_miles` is strictly greater than the ceiling selected by the loan file's `market_type` — 1.00 mile urban, 3.00 miles suburban, 5.00 miles rural — the reason is `DISTANCE_EXCEEDED`. Clause five catches redundancy: when the item's `street_address`, compared case-folded with whitespace runs collapsed, already appears on a comparable of the contested appraisal that has `included_in_grid` true, the reason is `DUPLICATE_OF_GRID_COMP`. A `DATA_ERROR_CITATION` skips clauses three, four, and five entirely — it has no sale date, no distance, and no address to collide. An item that survives all applicable clauses is marked admissible with a null `inadmissible_reason`.

Only admissible items filed **in the request's current round** count toward `admissible_evidence_count`, and routing the reconsideration to the appraiser requires at least two of them. An `AppraisalDeskReviewer` performs the routing, optionally attaching an `intake_note`, and its gates are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.8: the request must be in `INTAKE`, or `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the contested appraisal's `review_status` must be one of `RULES_CLEARED`, `VALUE_ACCEPTED`, or `VALUE_REVISED`, and any other review status is likewise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the appraisal must not be under a substantiated bias quarantine, refused with `ERR_BIAS_QUARANTINE_ACTIVE`, and that gate outranks the review-status gate, so a quarantined appraisal reports the quarantine rather than its non-contestable review status; the loan must still be reconsideration-eligible; and only last does the current round have to carry at least two admissible items — fewer is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ADMISSIBLE_EVIDENCE` (HTTP 422). Routing moves the request `INTAKE → AWAITING_APPRAISER`, writes `intake_note` from the call exactly as supplied — so a routing that omits it leaves the field null even when an earlier round recorded one — and stamps `response_deadline_at` as the logical clock plus 120 hours — five days for the appraiser to answer. It also moves the appraisal into `VALUE_CONTESTED`, and that transition is available from three review states: `RULES_CLEARED → VALUE_CONTESTED`, `VALUE_ACCEPTED → VALUE_CONTESTED`, and `VALUE_REVISED → VALUE_CONTESTED`, each with the identical cascade. The loan file's `collateral_status` follows, moving `VALUE_ACCEPTED → VALUE_CONTESTED` or `VALUE_REVISED → VALUE_CONTESTED` as appropriate.

The response belongs to the authoring `Appraiser` and to nobody else: `X-User-ID` must equal the contested appraisal's `appraiser_id`, or the response is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The request must be in `AWAITING_APPRAISER`, or the response is refused with `ERR_ROV_NOT_AWAITING_RESPONSE` (HTTP 409). The appraiser supplies an `outcome` of `VALUE_AFFIRMED` or `VALUE_REVISED`, a mandatory `response_note` giving the narrative, and — when revising — a `revised_value_cents`. A `VALUE_REVISED` outcome carrying no `revised_value_cents` is a malformed body, `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), reported on the body-shape rung and therefore ahead of the `AWAITING_APPRAISER` gate; a `revised_value_cents` supplied alongside `VALUE_AFFIRMED` is ignored and never written. Either way the request moves `AWAITING_APPRAISER → RESPONDED`, `appraiser_responded_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `responded_late` is set true when that instant is past `response_deadline_at` for the current round and false otherwise. Lateness is recorded rather than punished here; the reconsideration channel has no automatic penalty for a late appraiser, unlike the dispute channel of §3.9.

Affirming the value moves the appraisal `VALUE_CONTESTED → VALUE_ACCEPTED` and the loan file's `collateral_status` `VALUE_CONTESTED → VALUE_ACCEPTED`, with the loan's value, `ltv_bps`, and `decision_impact` recomputed. Either disposition also points the loan file's `value_of_record_appraisal_id` at the contested appraisal, but only when the loan carried no pointer at all; a pointer already set is left exactly where it stands. Revising the value is heavier. The revision must stay within the reconsideration revision cap — the movement between the appraisal's `current_value_cents` and the proposed `revised_value_cents` must be no more than 2000 basis points, computed as described in §3.9 — and a larger swing is refused with `ERR_REVISION_EXCEEDS_CAP` (HTTP 422). Within the cap, the appraisal moves `VALUE_CONTESTED → VALUE_REVISED`, a value revision with `source_type` `ROV` is written, the appraisal's `current_value_cents` is set to the revised value, the loan file's `collateral_status` moves `VALUE_CONTESTED → VALUE_REVISED` with its value, `ltv_bps`, and `decision_impact` recomputed and the underwriting cascade following, and the appraiser's revision tally is incremented with both performance-score variants recomputed.

The channel is cyclic, but bounded. From `RESPONDED`, an `AppraisalDeskReviewer` may open a further round by supplying a mandatory `next_round_reason`, which moves the request `RESPONDED → INTAKE` and increments `round_number`. Opening a round clears the whole per-round response record — `response_deadline_at`, `appraiser_responded_at`, `responded_late`, `outcome`, and `revised_value_cents` are all reset — and `admissible_evidence_count` is recomputed over the *new* round only, which means it is 0 the moment the new round opens and the borrower must file at least two fresh admissible items before routing is possible again. What opening a round does *not* touch is anything outside the request itself: the appraisal keeps whatever `review_status` the response left it in — `VALUE_ACCEPTED` after an affirmation or `VALUE_REVISED` after a revision, both of which are contestable again — the loan file keeps its `collateral_status` and every derived value figure, and no cascade runs. At most two rounds exist: a further round may be opened only while `round_number` is strictly less than 2, and an attempt to open a third round when `round_number` already equals 2 is refused with `ERR_ROV_ROUND_LIMIT` (HTTP 422).

Closure is the alternative to another round. From `RESPONDED`, the reviewer supplies a mandatory `closure_note` and the request lands in one of two terminal states determined entirely by the current `outcome`: `RESPONDED → CLOSED_AFFIRMED` when the appraiser affirmed, and `RESPONDED → CLOSED_REVISED` when the appraiser revised. Closure records the note and moves nothing else. Requests may be read individually and listed filtered by `loan_file_id`, `appraisal_id`, and `status`; evidence may be listed filtered by `admissible` and by `round_number`, which is how a reviewer confirms the current round's tally before routing. Both of those are typed rather than raw-string filters: `admissible` is read as a boolean exactly as §3.3's `included_in_grid` is, `round_number` is read as an integer, and a value that parses as neither — `admissible=yes`, `round_number=one` — matches no row at all and yields an empty page rather than an error.
### 3.9. Disputes, the Value of Record, and Panel Performance Consequences

Where a reconsideration is the borrower's channel, a dispute is the lender's. Either an `AppraisalDeskReviewer` or a `ChiefAppraiser` may file one, naming the `appraisal_id` under dispute, the `amc_id` the dispute is filed with, the `appraiser_id` expected to respond, a `dispute_basis` of `COMP_SELECTION`, `ADJUSTMENT_SUPPORT`, `VALUE_OPINION`, or `USPAP_COMPLIANCE`, and a review slot given as `review_slot_start` and `review_slot_end`. The parties must match the report: the dispute's `appraiser_id` must equal the appraisal's own `appraiser_id` and its `amc_id` must equal the appraisal's own `amc_id`, and any mismatch is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_PARTY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The named company must not be in `SUSPENDED` standing, or the filing is refused with `ERR_AMC_NOT_ELIGIBLE`; that standing gate is evaluated *ahead* of the party gate just described, so a filing that both names a suspended company and mismatches the parties answers `ERR_AMC_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. At most one non-resolved dispute may exist for any one combination of appraisal and basis, and a second is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DISPUTE_BASIS` (HTTP 422) — the same appraisal may perfectly well carry simultaneous open disputes on two *different* bases.

Dispute review slots use **closed-interval, unbuffered, aggregate-capacity** semantics, and every one of those three words distinguishes them from the appraiser inspection windows of §3.7. The interval runs from `review_slot_start` to `review_slot_end` inclusive at both ends, so a slot that begins at exactly the instant another slot ends *does* overlap it. No travel buffer is applied to either side. And overlap alone is not an error — what matters is how many overlaps there are. Count the disputes already held by the same management company whose `status` is `OPEN` or `APPRAISER_RESPONDED` and whose slot overlaps the incoming slot under those closed-interval rules, and reject the filing with `ERR_DISPUTE_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409) when that count has already reached the company's `concurrent_dispute_capacity`; the filing succeeds only while the overlap count is strictly less than the capacity. As with inspection windows, the interval must be well formed — start strictly before end, both endpoints present when either is — or the filing is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` first; and again as with inspection windows, a body carrying neither endpoint has submitted no interval at all and is rejected on the body-shape rung as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than as an ill-formed window.

A filed dispute is created in `OPEN` with `appraiser_position`, `response_summary`, `appraiser_responded_at`, `resolution`, `resolved_value_cents`, and `resolution_note` all null and `responded_late` false. Its `response_deadline_at` is snapshotted at creation as the dispute's `created_at` plus the company's `sla_response_hours` — a snapshot, so a later change to the company's SLA parameter does not move an existing dispute's deadline. Filing increments the company's `open_dispute_count`.

The management company answers. An `AMCCoordinator` whose `X-User-ID` equals the dispute's `amc_id` relays the appraiser's position, supplying a mandatory `appraiser_position` of `VALUE_SUPPORTED`, `VALUE_AMENDED`, or `NO_POSITION` together with a `response_summary`. The dispute must not already be resolved, or the relay is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_ALREADY_RESOLVED` (HTTP 409), and it must still be `OPEN`, so a second relay against a dispute that already sits in `APPRAISER_RESPONDED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The relay moves the dispute `OPEN → APPRAISER_RESPONDED`, stamps `appraiser_responded_at` from the logical clock, and sets `responded_late` true when that instant is past `response_deadline_at`. Lateness here has real consequences, unlike in the reconsideration channel: a late relay increments the appraiser's `sla_miss_count` **and** the company's `sla_breach_count`, recomputes both of the appraiser's performance-score variants, and re-evaluates both the appraiser panel cascade and the company panel cascade. Either way the relay decrements the company's `open_dispute_count`.

Resolution belongs to a `ChiefAppraiser`, who supplies a `resolution` of `UPHELD`, `OVERTURNED`, or `BY_DEFAULT` plus an optional `resolution_note` and, where required, a `resolved_value_cents`. A dispute already in one of the three resolved states cannot be resolved again — `ERR_DISPUTE_ALREADY_RESOLVED`. Upholding the appraiser is available from both `OPEN` and `APPRAISER_RESPONDED` — the edges `OPEN → RESOLVED_UPHELD` and `APPRAISER_RESPONDED → RESOLVED_UPHELD` — records the note, and leaves the value of record entirely unchanged; when the dispute was still `OPEN` at that moment, the company's `open_dispute_count` is decremented. Overturning is also available from both states, as `OPEN → RESOLVED_OVERTURNED` and `APPRAISER_RESPONDED → RESOLVED_OVERTURNED`, and requires a `resolved_value_cents` that is present and greater than zero — an `OVERTURNED` resolution carrying none at all is a malformed body, `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), reported on the body-shape rung ahead of every state, deadline and cap gate. Overturning writes a **compensating** value revision — one whose `is_compensating` flag is true and whose `source_type` is `DISPUTE` — sets the appraisal's `current_value_cents` to the resolved value, and recomputes the loan file's `current_appraised_value_cents`, `effective_value_cents`, `ltv_bps`, and `decision_impact`. It points the loan file's `value_of_record_appraisal_id` at the disputed appraisal only when the loan carried no pointer yet, and it leaves the loan's `collateral_status` and the appraisal's `review_status` untouched: the dispute channel moves value figures, never either status dimension. On the review dimension this is the self-transition `VALUE_REVISED → VALUE_REVISED`: the appraisal was already revised and is being revised again, this time to reverse an earlier movement. The resolved value is subject to the same revision cap as every other value movement.

Resolving by default is the SLA remedy and is available only from `OPEN` — a `BY_DEFAULT` resolution attempted on a dispute that has already reached `APPRAISER_RESPONDED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, evaluated before the deadline gate — and only once the logical clock has reached or passed the dispute's `response_deadline_at`; an attempt before the deadline is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_DEADLINE_NOT_REACHED` (HTTP 422). It is the edge `OPEN → RESOLVED_BY_DEFAULT`, and it increments the company's `sla_breach_count`, re-evaluates the company panel cascade, and decrements the company's `open_dispute_count`. All three resolved states are terminal, and disputes may be read individually or listed filtered by `appraisal_id`, `amc_id`, `appraiser_id`, and `status`.

A management company's `sla_breach_count` is the number of its disputes whose `responded_late` is true plus the number of its disputes in `RESOLVED_BY_DEFAULT`, and the company panel cascade is re-evaluated after every dispute breach. When the count reaches 2 while still below 4, the company moves `ACTIVE → PROBATION`; probation changes nothing operationally — existing orders are untouched and new report intake is still permitted, it is purely a recorded warning. When the count reaches 4, the company moves `PROBATION → SUSPENDED`, which blocks new report deliveries naming that company and blocks new disputes being filed against it. Because the cascade is evaluated after every single breach, a company climbing from zero to four breaches necessarily passes through `PROBATION` on the way; there is no direct `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` edge, and a single breach leaves the company in `ACTIVE`.
Every movement of a value of record, whichever channel produced it, writes one row to an append-only revision ledger hanging off the appraisal. A ledger row carries only `id` and `created_at` as common fields — it is an immutable event, so it has no `updated_at` and no `version` — plus the `appraisal_id` whose value moved, a `source_type` of `ROV`, `FIELD_REVIEW`, `SECOND_APPRAISAL`, or `DISPUTE`, and a `source_ref_id`. That reference is polymorphic and is resolved by reading `source_type` first: it holds the reconsideration request's identifier when the source type is `ROV`, the escalation order's identifier when the source type is `FIELD_REVIEW` or `SECOND_APPRAISAL`, and the dispute's identifier when the source type is `DISPUTE`. It is deliberately not a single-target foreign key, and there is no forward listing from a source record to its revisions; the reverse lookup is the value-revisions listing on the parent appraisal, which may be filtered by `source_type`.

Each ledger row also records the arithmetic and the consequences of the movement. `prior_value_cents` is the appraisal's `current_value_cents` immediately before the movement — or, when that field is still null because neither a rule-check run nor an earlier revision has populated it, the appraisal's `reported_value_cents` — and `new_value_cents` is the value immediately after; `delta_cents` is `new_value_cents` minus `prior_value_cents`, and is therefore negative when the value came down; `delta_bps` is 10000 multiplied by the absolute value of `delta_cents`, divided by `prior_value_cents`, rounded half up, save that a `prior_value_cents` of zero records a `delta_bps` of 0 rather than attempting the division — an absolute magnitude, always non-negative, so the direction of the movement lives only in the sign of `delta_cents`. `is_compensating` is false by default and is true only on the row written by a dispute resolved as overturned. `ltv_bps_before` and `ltv_bps_after` capture the loan's `ltv_bps` on either side of the movement, `decision_impact_before` and `decision_impact_after` capture its `decision_impact` on either side, each drawn from `NO_IMPACT`, `PRICING_ADJUSTMENT`, `CONDITION_REQUIRED`, and `DENIAL_RECOMMENDED`, and `documentation_note` is the documentation text copied from the `note` field of the call that triggered the movement, which is null whenever that field was optional and omitted — as a dispute's `resolution_note` may be. Writing a row also sets the appraisal's `current_value_cents` to `new_value_cents` and refreshes the appraisal's `updated_at` without itself incrementing the appraisal's `version`; where the triggering call separately moves the appraisal's `review_status`, that move is what increments the version, which is why a dispute resolved as overturned advances the appraisal's `updated_at` while leaving its `version` untouched.

The loan-level value figures are derived, never entered. `current_appraised_value_cents` is the value-of-record appraisal's original `reported_value_cents` plus the signed sum of `delta_cents` over **every** revision row on that appraisal, compensating rows included — the ledger is a running account, not a set of independent adjustments, so a downward revision followed by a compensating upward revision nets back out. `effective_value_cents` then applies the purchase-money rule: when the loan's `loan_purpose` is `PURCHASE`, it is the lesser of `current_appraised_value_cents` and the loan's `contract_price_cents`; for every other purpose it is simply `current_appraised_value_cents`, and the contract price is ignored even when it is present. `ltv_bps` is 10000 multiplied by `loan_amount_cents`, divided by `effective_value_cents`, rounded half up; should `effective_value_cents` come out as zero or negative, `ltv_bps` and `decision_impact` are both left null. A recompute on a loan that has no `value_of_record_appraisal_id` yet is a no-op that leaves all four derived figures exactly as they stand.

`decision_impact` is then a four-way banding of `ltv_bps` with **inclusive** upper bounds, which is where implementations most often drift by one: the impact is `NO_IMPACT` when `ltv_bps` is at most 8000, `PRICING_ADJUSTMENT` when it is at most 9500, `CONDITION_REQUIRED` when it is at most 9700, and `DENIAL_RECOMMENDED` otherwise. A loan sitting at exactly 8000 basis points is `NO_IMPACT`, not `PRICING_ADJUSTMENT`; one at exactly 9500 is `PRICING_ADJUSTMENT`; one at exactly 9700 is `CONDITION_REQUIRED`. Note the contrast with the escalation matrix of §3.6, whose comparisons are all strictly-less-than — the two threshold ladders in this system deliberately use opposite boundary conventions.

The banding drives the underwriting dimension automatically. When a recompute lands on `DENIAL_RECOMMENDED`, the loan is suspended: `SUBMITTED → SUSPENDED`, which also blocks the loan from closing until the value of record moves again, or `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED → SUSPENDED`. When a recompute lands on any other tier while the loan is suspended, the loan auto-reinstates `SUSPENDED → CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` — and it returns to `CONDITIONALLY_APPROVED` regardless of which status it was suspended from, so a loan suspended out of `SUBMITTED` comes back as conditionally approved rather than as submitted. This whole recompute-and-cascade sequence runs at exactly four moments: a desk-review acceptance, an escalation-order completion, a reconsideration appraiser-response, and a dispute resolved as overturned. Lifting a bias quarantine is deliberately not one of them: the appeal restores the appraisal's review status and the loan's `collateral_status` but leaves `current_appraised_value_cents`, `effective_value_cents`, `ltv_bps`, `decision_impact`, and the underwriting dimension exactly where the quarantine left them.

No channel may move a value of record by an unlimited amount. Whenever a reconsideration appraiser-response, an escalation completion, or a dispute resolution proposes a new value, the system computes 10000 multiplied by the absolute difference between the proposed value and the appraisal's current `current_value_cents`, divided by that current value, rounded half up, and requires the result to be at most 2000 basis points; a larger movement is refused with `ERR_REVISION_EXCEEDS_CAP` (HTTP 422). That current value is read with the same null fallback the ledger uses — the appraisal's `reported_value_cents` when `current_value_cents` has not been populated — and when the base resolves to null or to zero the cap is not enforced at all, there being no meaningful percentage to measure the movement against. The cap is symmetric — a twenty-five percent increase and a twenty-five percent decrease are equally inadmissible — and it is measured against the value *currently* of record, not against the originally reported value, so two successive movements of 2000 basis points each are permitted while one movement of 4000 is not.

The loan's collateral dimension is a compound projection of the value-of-record appraisal's review dimension rather than an independently operated status. It opens at `PENDING_APPRAISAL` and moves `PENDING_APPRAISAL → UNDER_REVIEW` when a desk review is opened on one of the loan's appraisals. From `UNDER_REVIEW` it moves to `VALUE_ACCEPTED` when the chief appraiser accepts, and to `VALUE_REVISED` when an escalation order completes; a return or an escalation decision instead holds the loan at `UNDER_REVIEW` through the self-transition `UNDER_REVIEW → UNDER_REVIEW`, because the appraisal moved but the collateral position did not. Submitting a reconsideration moves either `VALUE_ACCEPTED → VALUE_CONTESTED` or `VALUE_REVISED → VALUE_CONTESTED`; the appraiser's response then resolves the contest to `VALUE_CONTESTED → VALUE_REVISED` on a revision or `VALUE_CONTESTED → VALUE_ACCEPTED` on an affirmation. Substantiating a bias flag pulls the loan into quarantine from either resting state, `VALUE_ACCEPTED → QUARANTINED` or `VALUE_REVISED → QUARANTINED`, and while the loan is quarantined it cannot close. A successful appeal returns it to whichever state the appraisal's `quarantine_return_status` recorded, either `QUARANTINED → VALUE_REVISED` or `QUARANTINED → VALUE_ACCEPTED` — or, when the snapshot recorded some other review status, to that status's own projection: `VALUE_CONTESTED` projects to `VALUE_CONTESTED`, any of `IN_DESK_REVIEW`, `ESCALATED`, and `RETURNED_TO_AMC` projects to `UNDER_REVIEW`, and anything else projects back to `PENDING_APPRAISAL`. No collateral state is terminal, and the dimension is never set by a client — it follows the appraisal identified by the loan's `value_of_record_appraisal_id`, which is repointed whenever a newly delivered report supersedes its predecessor.

An appraiser's standing on the panel is likewise computed rather than declared, and it is computed twice from the same formula over two different windows. Take the appraisals authored by that appraiser whose `review_status` is not `SUPERSEDED` and whose `created_at` is at or after the window start, and take the revision-ledger rows on those appraisals whose `is_compensating` is false and whose `created_at` is at or after the same window start. `revision_rate_bps` is zero when the appraisal count is zero — a brand-new appraiser is not penalised for having no history — and otherwise is 10000 multiplied by the revision count, divided by the appraisal count, rounded half up. The score then starts at 10000 and subtracts three penalties: 0.60 multiplied by `revision_rate_bps` and rounded half up, plus 1200 for each entry in `substantiated_bias_count`, plus 400 for each entry in `sla_miss_count`. The result is clamped into the range 0 through 10000 inclusive.

The two windows differ only in which source records qualify. The rolling variant sets the window start at the logical clock minus 365 days and persists its answer to `performance_score_bps`, alongside `revision_rate_bps` and `total_appraisal_count`; this is the only variant the panel cascade reads. The lifetime variant applies no date filter at all — the window start is the beginning of time — and persists its answer to `lifetime_performance_score_bps`, which is audit-only and never moves anyone's standing. Critically, `substantiated_bias_count` and `sla_miss_count` are lifetime tallies in **both** variants; only the appraisal-and-revision window differs. The practical consequence is that an appraiser whose sole revision predates the rolling window shows `revision_rate_bps` of zero and an otherwise perfect rolling score while still carrying every historical bias and SLA penalty, and the two variants diverge for identical data whenever any qualifying appraisal or revision predates the rolling window.

The panel dimension opens at `ACTIVE` and reacts automatically to each recompute. A rolling score below 7000 but at or above 5500 moves the appraiser `ACTIVE → PROBATION`, stamping `probation_started_at` from the logical clock. A rolling score below 5500 moves the appraiser `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` or `PROBATION → SUSPENDED`. All three of those automatic edges also move the appraiser's `ASSIGNED` second-appraisal orders to `REASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED`, so degraded standing releases the work in flight. The automatic cascade never reaches `REMOVED`, even when the rolling score falls below 4000 — removal is always a deliberate act. Neither does any automatic edge ever improve standing: a rolling score that climbs back above a threshold leaves the appraiser exactly where it stands, and only the deliberate `REINSTATE` action below moves the dimension upward. A move straight into `SUSPENDED` leaves `probation_started_at` untouched, so an appraiser who went `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` without ever passing through probation still shows it as null.

Deliberate acts belong to the `ChiefAppraiser`, who conducts a panel review supplying `expected_appraiser_version`, a `panel_action`, and optionally a `removal_reason` and a `note`. Every accepted panel review increments the appraiser's `version` by 1, and a mismatched version is rejected — after the body's own shape has been checked, per the ladder of §2.8 — before any panel-action rule is examined. A `MONITOR` action is the read-and-recompute action, available in `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION`, and `SUSPENDED`; it recomputes both score variants from the current logical clock and is the only way to observe them side by side. Because that recompute feeds the automatic cascade of the preceding paragraph, `MONITOR` is a self-transition — `ACTIVE → ACTIVE`, `PROBATION → PROBATION`, `SUSPENDED → SUSPENDED` — only while the freshly computed rolling score still supports the present standing; on an appraiser whose score has since fallen, the `MONITOR` is itself what performs `ACTIVE → PROBATION`, `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, or `PROBATION → SUSPENDED` and releases that appraiser's assigned second-appraisal orders. A `REINSTATE` action from probation requires a rolling score of at least 7000 and moves the appraiser `PROBATION → ACTIVE`, clearing `probation_started_at`. A `REINSTATE` action from suspension requires only a rolling score of at least 5500 and moves the appraiser `SUSPENDED → PROBATION`, stamping `probation_started_at`: a suspended appraiser is reinstated to probation and never straight to active, so full rehabilitation always costs two separate reviews. A `REMOVE` action is available only from suspension, requires a rolling score below 4000 and a `removal_reason`, and moves the appraiser `SUSPENDED → REMOVED`; it records the reason, supersedes every `ACTIVE` panel membership held by that appraiser, and moves every `ASSIGNED` or `REASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED` order for that appraiser to `REASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED`. `REMOVED` is terminal. Unlike `MONITOR`, neither `REINSTATE` nor `REMOVE` recomputes anything first: both read `performance_score_bps` exactly as the last recompute left it, so a chief appraiser who wants the threshold tested against a current score issues a `MONITOR` beforehand. A `REMOVE` whose `removal_reason` is absent or empty is refused with `ERR_PANEL_ACTION_NOT_PERMITTED` rather than as a malformed body. Any action that does not match one of those edges from the appraiser's present standing is refused with `ERR_PANEL_ACTION_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 422) — including a reinstatement of an already-active appraiser, a removal from probation, and any action at all on a removed appraiser.
