# Employee Handbook & Policy Acknowledgment API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Corporate HR policy teams operating across many US states carry a burden that has almost nothing to do with writing policy and almost everything to do with proving that policy was properly written, properly localized, properly released, and properly acknowledged. A single handbook chapter may need a Massachusetts addendum reviewed by Massachusetts-licensed counsel, a Spanish rendition that clears an accessibility conformance bar, a release date that respects each state's statutory review lead time, and — years later — a retrievable, signed acknowledgment record for an employee who is now in litigation with the company. Today this evidence lives in email threads, spreadsheets, and a shared drive that nobody trusts.

This service replaces that improvisation with a single system of record for the handbook lifecycle. It covers policy authoring and per-jurisdiction legal review, state-specific addendum management, version release under a controlled effective date, distribution to the employee roster with electronic acknowledgment tracking, escalation of employees who fail to acknowledge, a two-stage policy exception request and approval path, translation and accessibility governance, and the immutable retention of acknowledgment evidence for dispute purposes.

Two design commitments shape almost every rule that follows. First, **segregation of duties is structural, not advisory**: the counsel who certified a version cannot be the counsel who grants an exception against it, and a manager cannot approve their own exception request. Second, **every derived number is auditable**: acknowledgment deadlines, accessibility indices, escalation tiers, risk scores, retention horizons, and coverage percentages are all computed by pinned, ordered formulas whose evaluation order is part of the specification rather than an implementation detail. Where the specification pins an order, an implementation that reorders the steps produces a different — and wrong — answer.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the actor, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capability set the actor is claiming. If either header is absent or empty, the request is refused with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`. If `X-User-Role` carries a value outside the six roles defined below, the request is refused with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`. Both of those refusals are 401 responses. When the header pair is well-formed but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being called, the request is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` at 403.

The six roles are `HRComplianceAdmin`, `PolicyAuthor`, `LegalCounsel`, `LocalizationSpecialist`, `Employee`, and `PeopleManager`.

- `HRComplianceAdmin` is the corporate HR policy operations role. It registers jurisdictions, employees and reviewers, approves and withdraws versions, owns release packages end to end, delivers assignments, revokes acknowledgments, notifies and escalates cases, places retention holds, revokes granted exceptions, and drives the operator sweep and the logical clock.
- `PolicyAuthor` is the handbook author. It creates policy documents and mints versions, drafts jurisdiction addenda, requests and re-requests legal reviews, submits drafts into review, and re-opens approved versions.
- `LegalCounsel` is jurisdiction-licensed counsel. It starts and decides legal reviews, approves state addenda, and renders the legal-stage decision on policy exceptions that fall outside its own review scope.
- `LocalizationSpecialist` owns translation and accessibility. It opens localization tracks, submits per-section accessibility scores, reworks failing renditions, publishes conforming ones, and abandons tracks that are no longer needed.
- `Employee` is the assignee of handbook acknowledgments. An employee signs their own assignments and requests exceptions against them.
- `PeopleManager` is an employee who manages others. It renders the first-stage exception decision for direct reports and monitors their escalations; it may never decide on its own request.

Identity resolution differs by role and matters for every ownership check in the system. When `X-User-Role` is `LegalCounsel`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `LegalReviewer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `LegalReviewer` compare the resource's reviewer foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Employee` or `PeopleManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Employee`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Employee` compare the resource's employee foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. For all three of these entity-backed roles, an `X-User-ID` that does not resolve to an existing row of the backing entity — `Employee` for `Employee` and `PeopleManager`, `LegalReviewer` for `LegalCounsel` — is refused with `ERR_ACTOR_IDENTITY_UNKNOWN` at 403.

When `X-User-Role` is `HRComplianceAdmin`, `PolicyAuthor`, or `LocalizationSpecialist`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example, a new policy document stores the acting author's identifier as `owner_user_id`, and it is never compared to any entity's `id` — but it performs no lookup against any table for these three roles.

Two ownership rules deserve early emphasis because they recur throughout §3. An `Employee` acting on an assignment must be that assignment's own assignee: if the acting employee id differs from the assignment's `employee_id`, the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNMENT_OWNER` (403), and this applies both to recording an acknowledgment and to raising an exception request. A `LegalCounsel` acting on a legal review must be the review's own assigned reviewer: a mismatch against the review's `reviewer_id` yields `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_REVIEWER` (403) on both taking up the review and deciding it.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0; there is no upper bound on it. A `limit` that is not an integer inside the inclusive range from 1 to 100, or an `offset` that is not an integer of at least 0, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` at HTTP 400.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, collections are returned sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, so the oldest row appears first and paging forward walks the history in the order it happened. For list responses the `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows that satisfy the filters before the window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps, and the Version Counter

Every resource in this system is addressed by an `id` that is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix — clients never supply an `id`, and there is no readable encoding of entity type inside it. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the instant the row was written.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, and `version`, a monotonic integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. `updated_at` is refreshed on every mutation alongside the version bump. Three entities are deliberate exceptions because they are append-only evidence rather than mutable records: a **release item**, an **acknowledgment event**, and a **retention record** each carry only `id` and `created_at`. For those three, both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the response payload entirely — even when a compensating action later marks an acknowledgment as revoked, the row's revocation is recorded in its own fields rather than through a version counter.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

All temporal reasoning in this service reads a server-held **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. At startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and until an operator moves it, that is the value every deadline, acknowledgment window, overdue computation, retention horizon and risk expiry is evaluated against; the server never falls back to real wall-clock time. An `HRComplianceAdmin` may advance the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant. The clock is strictly forward-only: a requested `to_iso8601` earlier than the current logical clock is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND` (409), while a value equal to the current reading is accepted as a no-op advance.

Day arithmetic throughout the system is whole-day arithmetic in UTC. Where a difference in days is taken, it is floor-truncated to whole days — a difference of one day and twenty-three hours is one day, not two. Calendar-year arithmetic appears in exactly one place, the retention horizon, and follows the same-month-same-day convention described in §3.7.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Guarded Writes

Three write operations mutate a parent aggregate in a way that would be unsafe if the client were working from a stale read, and each of the three therefore requires an explicit expected-version field. These fields are **not optional**; omitting one is a schema failure.

Minting a new policy version under a policy document requires `expected_policy_document_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `PolicyDocument`. A newly minted version legitimately becomes the document's `latest_version_no` and, once released, supersedes the document's prior published version; a client working from a stale document view would otherwise mint a version whose number and supersede target it never saw. If the supplied value does not equal the document's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POLICY_DOCUMENT_VERSION` at HTTP 412.

Creating a policy exception requires `expected_employee_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the requesting `Employee`. A new exception legitimately supersedes the requester's prior granted exception on the same policy version, and its frozen prior-exception count is read off the employee's current record, so a stale employee view would freeze the wrong risk-score input. If the supplied value does not equal the employee's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_EMPLOYEE_VERSION` at HTTP 412.

Releasing a release package requires `expected_release_package_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `ReleasePackage`. Release legitimately supersedes each document's prior published version and mints the entire assignment roster, so a client working from a stale package view would release an item set or an effective date it never saw. If the supplied value does not equal the package's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RELEASE_PACKAGE_VERSION` at HTTP 412.

No other endpoint in this system accepts an expected-version field, and no other endpoint may reject a request on version-mismatch grounds.

### 2.7. Precision and Rounding

Wherever a monetary-style or percentage-style number is stored or returned, it is carried to **two decimal places and rounded half up**. This governs both coverage percentages, both exception risk scores, the per-section accessibility sub-percentages, and the final accessibility index.

Two computations have an intermediate precision that differs from the final one, and both are specified deliberately. The duration factor inside the exception risk score is rounded half up to **four** decimal places before it enters the weighted blend, and only the resulting scores are rounded to two places. The accessibility index rounds each section's contribution to two decimals **before** summing, and then rounds the quotient to two decimals — this is a two-stage rounding, not a single final rounding, and the two orders give different answers.

Day-valued and tier-valued quantities are integers: acknowledgment deadlines in whole days, escalation tiers in the inclusive range 1 to 3, ledger deltas as signed integers, and denormalized counters as non-negative integers. Retention horizons are instants carried to millisecond precision, preserving the time-of-day of the moment the record was written.

### 2.8. Request Screening Order

Because a single request can violate several rules at once, the order in which checks run is part of the contract — the first failing check determines the response, and no later check may mask an earlier one. Requests are screened in the following order.

First come the authentication and authorization checks: (1) presence and non-emptiness of both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`, yielding `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`; (2) recognition of `X-User-Role` as one of the six declared roles, yielding `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`; (3) for endpoints whose authorized roles include `Employee`, `PeopleManager` or `LegalCounsel`, resolution of `X-User-ID` against the backing entity, yielding `ERR_ACTOR_IDENTITY_UNKNOWN`; and (4) membership of the acting role in the endpoint's authorized role list, yielding `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

Existence checks follow. Every path identifier is resolved **left to right in path order**, so on a nested path the outer resource is looked up before the inner one and the first unresolvable segment is the one reported; failure yields `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Then every foreign-key identifier supplied in the request body is resolved against its referenced entity, with failure yielding `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (404).

Shape checks come next. On every write endpoint the request body must satisfy the endpoint's input schema — required fields present, types correct, enum values within range, arrays non-empty where a non-empty array is declared, and integers inside their declared bounds — with any failure yielding `ERR_VALIDATION` (422). On every list endpoint the pagination window is validated as described in §2.3, yielding `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.

Only after those generic screens do the resource-specific checks run, in this order: fine-grained ownership and jurisdiction checks (assignment ownership, assigned-reviewer identity, reviewer-jurisdiction match), then the two segregation-of-duties checks (manager conflict first, then reviewer conflict), then the three expected-version checks of §2.6, then the state-machine from-state checks for each entity, and finally the business-safety, referential, uniqueness, shape-of-content and temporal checks described throughout §3. The practical consequence worth internalizing: a stale expected-version value is reported **before** any state check, so a request that is both stale and aimed at the wrong state reports the staleness; and a state check is reported **before** any business-safety check, so an escalation case in the wrong status reports its state error rather than a tier error.

### 2.9. Cascades, Denormalized Counters, and Nested Reads

Several operations in this system are deliberately multi-hop: one call mutates a chain of related rows, and the later hops exist precisely because the earlier ones happened. Legal review rejection reverts a version to draft, which withdraws sibling reviews, which releases reviewer capacity, which un-approves addenda. Releasing a package publishes versions, which supersedes prior published versions, which voids their open assignments, which decrements employee counters, which moots open escalation cases. An acknowledgment appends evidence, flips the assignment, writes a retention row, decrements the open-acknowledgment counter, closes any open escalation case, and may close the release package. Each such hop is enumerated in §3 with its own preconditions and effects; none is optional, and an implementation that performs only the first hop is incorrect.

Every mutating hop that touches a versioned row refreshes that row's `updated_at` and increments its `version` by 1. Where a cascade touches several rows, each touched versioned row receives its own increment.

Nested reads and nested writes validate the whole path. A request that names a policy version underneath a policy document, or an item underneath a release package, resolves each segment in order; a well-formed identifier that belongs to a different parent is treated as unresolvable at its own segment.

### 2.10. Two Conserved Quantities

The system maintains two invariants that must hold after every request completes, including after every compensating action.

The **signed acknowledgment ledger** is the running sum of `signed_delta` over all retention rows belonging to an employee, taken across every row with no status filter and no time window. Each acknowledgment capture appends a row with `record_kind` set to `CAPTURE` and `signed_delta` of `+1`; each revocation appends a compensating row with `record_kind` set to `REVOCATION` and `signed_delta` of `-1`. Rows are never deleted or edited. The invariant is that this signed sum always equals the number of that employee's acknowledgment events whose `status` is `EFFECTIVE`. The conserved quantity is the signed record count, not the raw row count.

The **open-acknowledgment counter** on an employee, `open_ack_count`, must always equal the number of that employee's distribution assignments whose `ack_state` is either `UNDELIVERED` or `PENDING`, counted across every release package with no time window. This is recomputed conceptually after every acknowledgment-dimension transition and after every release mint, and it must survive compensating actions — revocation, exception expiry, exception revocation, and voiding all adjust it. The parallel counter `open_escalation_count` must likewise equal the number of that employee's escalation cases whose `status` is `OPEN`, `NOTIFIED` or `ESCALATED_HR`. Neither denormalized counter may ever drift from its recomputed value, and reading a single employee record is the intended way to observe both.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Jurisdictional Ground Rules, Counsel Rosters, and the People Directory

Everything downstream is parameterized by jurisdiction, so an `HRComplianceAdmin` seeds the jurisdiction table first. A jurisdiction is registered with a `state_code`, which is an uppercase two-character US state or territory code and must be globally unique, and a human-readable `display_name`. Its `min_legal_review_days` is the minimum number of whole days that must elapse between the last approved legal-review decision for that jurisdiction and a version's effective date; it is an integer of at least 0 and at most 120. Its `ack_deadline_days` is the number of whole days after the effective date by which an employee working in that jurisdiction must acknowledge, an integer of at least 1 and at most 180. Its `retention_years` is the number of whole years an acknowledgment record must be retained after the acknowledgment date, an integer of at least 1 and at most 30. `required_language_codes` is a non-empty array of BCP-47 primary subtags, each exactly two lowercase characters, and it must contain `en` — a jurisdiction may not be configured to accept a handbook that has never been published in English.

Three jurisdiction settings have defaults and are therefore optional on registration. `esign_permitted` defaults to true and states whether electronic signature is a legally acceptable acknowledgment method there. `proxy_ack_permitted` defaults to false and states whether an `HRComplianceAdmin` may record an attested acknowledgment on an employee's behalf. `ack_window_buffer_days` defaults to 3 and is the mandatory cooling buffer, in whole days, that must separate two acknowledgment windows of the same policy category for the same employee in that jurisdiction; it is an integer of at least 0 and at most 30. Jurisdictions can be listed with an optional `state_code` filter and read individually by any role in the system, because almost every other workflow needs to quote their numbers.

Counsel are registered next. A legal reviewer is created against a `jurisdiction_id` — the single jurisdiction whose law that reviewer is licensed to sign off on — with a `full_name` and a `bar_number` that must be unique within that jurisdiction, so the same bar number may legitimately reappear under a different state. `concurrent_review_limit` defaults to 2 and caps how many of the reviewer's review windows may overlap at once; it is an integer of at least 1 and at most 10. `is_active` defaults to true and controls whether the reviewer may receive new work: requesting a review from a reviewer whose `is_active` is false is refused with `ERR_REVIEWER_INACTIVE`, and the same refusal applies when an author re-raises a review that had asked for changes. `open_review_count` is maintained by the system, starts at 0, and always tracks the number of that reviewer's reviews whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; every transition in §3.3 that opens or closes a review moves it. Reviewer listings can be narrowed by `jurisdiction_id` and by `is_active`, and reading one reviewer surfaces both `open_review_count` and `concurrent_review_limit` so an author can see remaining capacity before assigning work.

The people directory is the third pillar. An employee record is created with a `jurisdiction_id` naming the work-location jurisdiction that governs their deadlines and retention, a globally unique `work_email` that must contain an `@` character, and a `full_name`. `manager_id` is an optional self-referential link to another employee and defaults to null for the top of the reporting chain. `preferred_language_code` defaults to `en` and is exactly two lowercase characters; it names the language the employee must be served the policy body in. `needs_accessible_format` defaults to false; when it is true, an assignment may only be delivered if the matching rendition clears the accessibility floor described in §3.5. `employment_status` defaults to `ACTIVE` and is one of `ACTIVE`, `ON_LEAVE` or `TERMINATED`. A newly created employee starts with `open_ack_count` at 0 and `open_escalation_count` at 0.

An `HRComplianceAdmin` may later update an employee's `employment_status`, `preferred_language_code`, `needs_accessible_format` or `manager_id`, and any such update increments the employee's `version` by 1. Marking an employee `TERMINATED` is the one directory edit with a reporting consequence: it removes that employee's rows from the live denominator of the coverage calculation described in §3.6, without touching the frozen snapshot denominator. Employee listings may be filtered by `jurisdiction_id`, by `manager_id` — which is how a manager finds their direct reports — and by `employment_status`.

Uniqueness across the whole system is enforced with a single error, `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` at 409. It guards: `state_code` globally on jurisdictions; `bar_number` per `jurisdiction_id` on reviewers; `work_email` globally on employees; `title` globally on policy documents; `name` globally on release packages; the pair of `policy_version_id` and `jurisdiction_id` on addenda; the pair of `policy_version_id` and `language_code` on translations; the pair of `release_package_id` and `policy_version_id` on release items; and the pair of `policy_version_id` and `jurisdiction_id` on legal reviews.

### 3.2. Authoring: Policy Documents, Versions, and State Addenda

A `PolicyAuthor` opens a handbook chapter by creating a **policy document**, supplying a `title` that is globally unique and between 3 and 160 characters, and a `category` that is one of `CODE_OF_CONDUCT`, `LEAVE`, `SAFETY`, `COMPENSATION` or `DATA_PRIVACY`. The category is not decorative: it drives the per-employee acknowledgment-window buffer scope, it caps the acknowledgment deadline for safety policies, it lifts escalation tiers for safety policies, and it carries the heaviest weight in the exception risk score. The author may also supply `max_ack_deadline_days`, a category-level cap on the acknowledgment deadline in whole days that is applied as an upper bound over the jurisdiction's own deadline; it defaults to 30 and must be an integer of at least 1 and at most 180. On creation the document's `owner_user_id` is set to the acting author's opaque identifier, `latest_version_no` starts at 0, and `current_published_version_id` starts as null. Documents can be listed with a `category` filter and with a `current_published_version_id` filter, the latter being how an operator walks backwards from a live version to its owning chapter.

Minting a version is the first of the three guarded writes. The author supplies `body_text`, which is the canonical English policy body and must be non-empty; `section_count`, the number of numbered sections in that body, an integer of at least 1 and at most 40, which later fixes exactly how many per-section accessibility scores a rendition must carry; `required_jurisdiction_ids`, a non-empty array of jurisdiction identifiers naming every state that must clear this version before it may be approved; and `expected_policy_document_version` as described in §2.6. The minted version receives `version_no` equal to the document's `latest_version_no` plus 1 and enters status `DRAFT`; the document's `latest_version_no` is incremented by 1 and the document's own `version` is incremented by 1. A version's `effective_date`, `window_end_date` and `approved_at` all start null, `revision_round` starts at 0, and `pinned_addendum_ids` starts as an empty array. Versions may be listed by `policy_document_id` and by `status`, and reading one surfaces its `revision_round`, `effective_date`, `window_end_date` and `pinned_addendum_ids`.

State-specific amending text is carried by **jurisdiction addenda**, each attached to a version with a `jurisdiction_id` and a non-empty `body_text` that is understood to be appended after the canonical body. An author may only attach an addendum while the owning version is in `DRAFT` or `IN_LEGAL_REVIEW`; attempting it against a version in any other status is refused with `ERR_VERSION_INVALID_STATE` (409). Only one addendum may exist per jurisdiction per version. A new addendum begins in `DRAFT` with `approved_by_reviewer_id` null.

Approval of an addendum belongs to jurisdiction-licensed counsel, and it moves the addendum `DRAFT → APPROVED`. Three conditions gate it: the addendum must currently be in `DRAFT`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_ADDENDUM_INVALID_STATE` (409); the acting reviewer's own `jurisdiction_id` must equal the addendum's `jurisdiction_id`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_REVIEWER_JURISDICTION_MISMATCH` (403); and the owning version must be in either `DRAFT` or `IN_LEGAL_REVIEW`. On success the addendum's `approved_by_reviewer_id` records the acting reviewer's identifier and the addendum's `version` increments by 1. Addenda may be listed by `policy_version_id`, by `jurisdiction_id`, by `approved_by_reviewer_id` — which is how an auditor finds everything a given counsel signed — and by `status`.

Two system-driven addendum movements exist and neither is client-callable. When a version is reverted out of legal review, every addendum on it that had reached `APPROVED` is pushed back `APPROVED → DRAFT` and its `approved_by_reviewer_id` is cleared to null; the state addendum must be re-certified against the amended body. When a version still in `DRAFT` is withdrawn, every addendum on it is retired: an addendum sitting in `DRAFT` moves `DRAFT → RETIRED`, and an addendum sitting in `APPROVED` moves `APPROVED → RETIRED`. Both retirement branches are unconditional on the addendum side and both leave `RETIRED` as a terminal state from which no further movement is possible.

### 3.3. The Jurisdiction Legal Review Cycle

Before a draft can go into review, the author raises one **legal review** per required jurisdiction. A review request names a `jurisdiction_id`, the `reviewer_id` of the specific counsel being asked, and a `due_at` instant that is the inclusive upper bound of the reviewer's occupation interval for this piece of work. The system stamps `assigned_at` from the logical clock as the inclusive lower bound of that interval, sets `status` to `REQUESTED`, sets `round_no` to 1, leaves `decided_at` and `comment` null, and increments the chosen reviewer's `open_review_count` by 1. A review may only be raised while the owning version is in `DRAFT` or `IN_LEGAL_REVIEW`; any other status is refused with `ERR_VERSION_INVALID_STATE`. Only one review may exist per jurisdiction per version. Reviews may be listed by `policy_version_id`, `jurisdiction_id`, `reviewer_id` and `status`, and reading one surfaces `round_no`, `assigned_at`, `due_at` and `decided_at`.

Reviewer capacity is measured as interval occupation, and it is measured over **closed** intervals on both sides. Consider the candidate interval running from the logical clock at the moment of the request through the requested `due_at`, inclusive at both ends. Count the reviewer's existing reviews whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `IN_PROGRESS` and whose own closed interval from `assigned_at` through `due_at` intersects that candidate interval. Because both intervals are closed, merely touching endpoints counts as an intersection — a candidate whose start instant equals an existing review's `due_at` overlaps it. That count must be **strictly less than** the reviewer's `concurrent_review_limit`; if it has already reached the limit, the request is refused with `ERR_REVIEWER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). The identical capacity test, with an identical refusal, is applied when an author re-raises a review that had asked for changes, since that also opens a fresh occupation interval.

Submitting a draft into review moves the version `DRAFT → IN_LEGAL_REVIEW` and is the author's own action. It requires the version to be in `DRAFT`, and it requires the required-jurisdiction set to be fully staffed on both dimensions: for every jurisdiction named in `required_jurisdiction_ids` there must be exactly one addendum on this version for that jurisdiction whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `APPROVED`, and exactly one review on this version for that jurisdiction whose `status` is `REQUESTED`. The version's `version` increments by 1.

Counsel then takes up the work, moving their review `REQUESTED → IN_PROGRESS`. This requires the review to be in `REQUESTED`, the acting reviewer to be the review's own `reviewer_id`, and the owning version to be in `IN_LEGAL_REVIEW`. A review whose status is not the from-state a requested movement needs is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_INVALID_STATE` (409).

A decision on an in-progress review carries a `verdict` of either `APPROVE` or `REQUEST_CHANGES`, plus an optional `comment`. The `verdict` is a decision input only: it is never persisted as a field of the review and never appears in a review's response payload, whose attributes remain the ones inventoried above. An `APPROVE` verdict moves the review `IN_PROGRESS → APPROVED`, stamps `decided_at` from the logical clock, and decrements the reviewer's `open_review_count` by 1. A `REQUEST_CHANGES` verdict additionally requires `comment` to be a non-empty string; it moves the review `IN_PROGRESS → CHANGES_REQUESTED`, stamps `decided_at`, decrements the reviewer's `open_review_count` by 1, and then drives the version's revert cascade described immediately below. Either way the review's own `version` increments by 1.

The revert cascade is the most consequential multi-hop effect in the authoring half of the system, and it fires whenever a version leaves `IN_LEGAL_REVIEW` for `DRAFT`. Hop 1: the version moves `IN_LEGAL_REVIEW → DRAFT` and its `revision_round` increases by 1. Hop 2: because the version has left review, every **sibling** review on that version whose `status` is `REQUESTED` or `IN_PROGRESS` is set to `WITHDRAWN` with `decided_at` stamped from the logical clock — a `REQUESTED` sibling moves `REQUESTED → WITHDRAWN` and an `IN_PROGRESS` sibling moves `IN_PROGRESS → WITHDRAWN`, both unconditionally. Hop 3: because those reviews became `WITHDRAWN`, each affected reviewer's `open_review_count` is decremented by 1. Hop 4: because at least one reviewer's open count changed, every addendum on that version whose `status` is `APPROVED` is reset to `DRAFT` with `approved_by_reviewer_id` cleared to null. The version's `version` increments by 1. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal for a review.

The cycle closes when the author re-raises a review that asked for changes, supplying a fresh `due_at`. That moves the review `CHANGES_REQUESTED → REQUESTED`, requires the owning version to be back in `DRAFT`, increases `round_no` by 1, clears `decided_at` to null, restamps `assigned_at` from the logical clock, adopts the supplied `due_at`, and increments the reviewer's `open_review_count` by 1. Because a version can re-enter review after fresh requests are raised, the loop `DRAFT → IN_LEGAL_REVIEW → DRAFT` may repeat any number of times, and `revision_round` counts how many rounds it has been through.

### 3.4. Approving, Re-opening, and Withdrawing a Version

Approval of a version is deliberately not the author's call: it belongs to an `HRComplianceAdmin`, and it moves the version `IN_LEGAL_REVIEW → APPROVED`. Three conditions are evaluated in a fixed order. First the version must currently be in `IN_LEGAL_REVIEW`, otherwise the attempt is refused with `ERR_VERSION_INVALID_STATE`. Then, for every jurisdiction named in `required_jurisdiction_ids`, there must be a review on this version for that jurisdiction whose `status` is `APPROVED`; a gap here is refused with `ERR_LEGAL_REVIEW_INCOMPLETE` (409). Only after the review sweep passes does the addendum sweep run: for every jurisdiction in `required_jurisdiction_ids` there must be an addendum on this version whose `status` is `APPROVED`, and a gap is refused with `ERR_ADDENDUM_NOT_APPROVED` (409). The ordering matters — a version missing both an approved review and an approved addendum for the same state reports the incomplete review, not the unapproved addendum.

On approval the version stamps `approved_at` from the logical clock and **pins its addenda**: `pinned_addendum_ids` is set to the identifiers of every addendum on this version whose `status` is `APPROVED`, ordered by `created_at` ascending. The version's `version` increments by 1. The pin is what makes approval falsifiable later: because any subsequent revert cascade un-approves addenda, a pinned set that no longer matches the live set is evidence that the approval no longer describes the document.

Re-opening is the author's escape hatch, moving the version `APPROVED → DRAFT`. It requires the version to be in `APPROVED`, and it requires that no release item referencing this version belongs to a package whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `RELEASED` or `CLOSED` — a version already committed to a live release cannot be re-opened, and the attempt is refused as a state conflict at HTTP 409. When it succeeds the version clears `approved_at` to null, resets `pinned_addendum_ids` to an empty array, and increases `revision_round` by 1. It also unwinds the review certifications: every review on this version whose `status` is `APPROVED` is moved `APPROVED → REQUESTED` with `round_no` increased by 1 and `decided_at` cleared to null, and each owning reviewer's `open_review_count` is incremented by 1. The version's `version` increments by 1.

Withdrawal is an `HRComplianceAdmin` action available from exactly two states, and the two branches have materially different consequences. From `DRAFT`, withdrawal moves the version `DRAFT → WITHDRAWN` and retires every addendum on it as described in §3.2; nothing else changes because nothing downstream exists yet. From `PUBLISHED`, withdrawal moves the version `PUBLISHED → WITHDRAWN`, stamps `window_end_date` from the logical clock so the effective window closes at the moment of withdrawal, sets the owning document's `current_published_version_id` back to null, and voids the distribution fallout: every assignment on this version whose `ack_state` is `UNDELIVERED` or `PENDING` is moved to `VOIDED`, and for each such assignment the assignee's `open_ack_count` is decremented by 1. In both branches the version's `version` increments by 1, and `WITHDRAWN` is terminal.

Two version movements are system-driven. When a package is released, each of its item versions moves `APPROVED → PUBLISHED`, taking `effective_date` from the releasing package's own effective date; the owning document's `current_published_version_id` is set to that version's identifier while `latest_version_no` is left unchanged, since publishing mints no new version. And when a later version of the same document reaches `PUBLISHED`, the document's prior published version is superseded through a four-hop cascade. Hop 1: that prior version moves `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED` and its `window_end_date` is set to the successor's `effective_date`, closing its window exactly where the successor's opens. Hop 2: because the version became `SUPERSEDED`, every assignment on it whose `ack_state` is `UNDELIVERED` or `PENDING` is set to `VOIDED`. Hop 3: because those assignments became `VOIDED`, each owning employee's `open_ack_count` is decremented by 1. Hop 4: because those assignments became `VOIDED`, every escalation case on them whose `status` is `OPEN`, `NOTIFIED` or `ESCALATED_HR` is closed as `CLOSED_MOOT` with `closed_at` stamped from the logical clock, the assignment's `escalation_state` becomes `MOOT`, and each such employee's `open_escalation_count` is decremented by 1. The superseded version's `version` increments by 1, and `SUPERSEDED` is terminal. A version whose `window_end_date` is null has an open-ended effective window.

### 3.5. Localization Tracks and the Accessibility Index

A `LocalizationSpecialist` opens one **translation** track per target language on a version, supplying a `language_code` that is exactly two lowercase characters and unique within that version. A new track starts with `status` of `PENDING`, an empty `section_scores` array, `a11y_index` null, `a11y_round_no` at 0, and `conformance_level` null. Tracks can be listed by `policy_version_id`, `language_code` and `status`, and reading one surfaces both `a11y_index` and `conformance_level`. A translation whose status is not the from-state a requested movement needs is refused with `ERR_TRANSLATION_INVALID_STATE` (409).

Work begins with the move `PENDING → IN_TRANSLATION`, which additionally requires the owning version to be in `DRAFT`, `IN_LEGAL_REVIEW` or `APPROVED` — localization may run in parallel with legal review, but not against a version that has already been superseded or withdrawn. A track may be abandoned from either of its two early states: `PENDING → ABANDONED` and `IN_TRANSLATION → ABANDONED` are both available and both unconditional beyond the from-state check. `ABANDONED` and `PUBLISHED` are the two terminal states of a localization track.

Submitting for accessibility review moves the track `IN_TRANSLATION → A11Y_REVIEW` and carries the `section_scores` array. That array must have exactly as many entries as the owning version's `section_count`, in document order, and every entry must be a number in the inclusive range from 0 to 1 with up to six decimal digits; a wrong length or an out-of-range element is refused with `ERR_SECTION_SCORE_SHAPE` at HTTP 422. No prior submission's scores are carried forward — each submission fully replaces `section_scores`.

The **accessibility index** is then computed, and its evaluation order is pinned. Step one: for each section in order, multiply that section's score by 100 and round the product half up to two decimal places, producing a per-section percentage. Step two: add those already-rounded per-section percentages together exactly, with no further rounding of the running total. Step three: divide that sum by the section count and round the quotient half up to two decimal places; the result is `a11y_index`, a number in the inclusive range from 0 to 100. The per-section rounding happens strictly before the summation, and this is not interchangeable with rounding only at the end. To make the difference concrete: for a version with four sections and scores of 0.90125, 0.90375, 0.90625 and 0.90875, the pinned order yields per-section percentages of 90.13, 90.38, 90.63 and 90.88, summing to 362.02, which divided by four is 90.505 and rounds to **90.51** — whereas summing the raw scores first and rounding only once yields 362.00 divided by four, or 90.50. Round each section first.

`conformance_level` is derived from the index in the same operation, as a WCAG conformance band: it is `FAIL` when the index is below 90.00, `AA` when the index is at least 90.00 and below 96.00, and `AAA` when the index is at least 96.00. The track's `version` increments by 1.

From accessibility review the specialist has two paths, and both matter. Reworking moves the track `A11Y_REVIEW → IN_TRANSLATION` and increases `a11y_round_no` by 1, closing the localization cycle `IN_TRANSLATION → A11Y_REVIEW → IN_TRANSLATION`, which may repeat as often as needed; `a11y_round_no` counts the completed review attempts. Publishing moves the track `A11Y_REVIEW → PUBLISHED` and is gated on the conformance band: it is permitted only when `conformance_level` is `AA` or `AAA`, and a `FAIL` band is refused with `ERR_A11Y_GATE_NOT_MET` (409). Both movements increment the track's `version` by 1.

Two later gates read this work. Scheduling a release requires, for every jurisdiction in the release scope and for every language in that jurisdiction's `required_language_codes`, a track on each item version with a matching `language_code` and a `status` of `PUBLISHED`; a missing rendition is refused with `ERR_TRANSLATION_NOT_PUBLISHED` (409). Delivery to an individual employee requires a `PUBLISHED` track whose `language_code` matches that employee's `preferred_language_code`, and — when that employee's `needs_accessible_format` is true — that track's `a11y_index` must be at least 96.00, the same threshold that separates the `AAA` band from `AA`. A failure of either condition is refused with `ERR_ACCESSIBLE_RENDITION_UNAVAILABLE` (409).

### 3.6. Release Packages: Assembly, Scheduling, and the Release Event

Approved versions reach employees only through a **release package**, which an `HRComplianceAdmin` creates with a globally unique `name`, an `effective_date` naming the instant every version in the package becomes binding, and a non-empty `scope_jurisdiction_ids` array naming the jurisdictions the release distributes into. Employees outside that scope receive no assignment at all. A new package starts in `status` `DRAFT` with `scheduled_at`, `released_at`, `legal_review_deadline`, `coverage_snapshot_pct` and `coverage_live_pct` all null, and `roster_snapshot_size` at 0. Packages may be listed with a `status` filter, and reading one surfaces its `version`, `legal_review_deadline` and `roster_snapshot_size`.

Contents are added one **release item** at a time, each naming a `policy_version_id`. Items may only be added while the package is in `DRAFT`; attempting it against a package in any other status is refused with `ERR_RELEASE_NOT_DRAFT` (409). The same version may not be added to the same package twice. An item's `supersedes_version_id` stays null until the release actually happens, and items may be listed by `release_package_id`, by `policy_version_id`, and by `supersedes_version_id` — the last being the audit path from a retired version to the item that retired it. Adding an item increments the owning package's `version` by 1.

Scheduling moves the package `DRAFT → SCHEDULED` and is where nearly all of the compliance gating lives. The checks run in a fixed order, and the first failure is the one reported. The package must be in `DRAFT`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_RELEASE_NOT_DRAFT`. Then the translation-coverage rule of §3.5 runs, yielding `ERR_TRANSLATION_NOT_PUBLISHED`. Then the package must contain at least one item, or it is refused with `ERR_RELEASE_EMPTY` (409). Then the effective-window overlap rule described below runs, yielding `ERR_EFFECTIVE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Then the `effective_date` must be strictly later than the logical clock, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_IN_PAST` (409) — a package may not be scheduled to take effect at the current instant or in the past. Finally the legal-review lead-time rule runs, yielding `ERR_LEGAL_REVIEW_LEAD_TIME_SHORT` (409). Independently of the ordering, every item's version must be in `APPROVED`, and a package containing a non-approved item version cannot be scheduled and is refused as a state conflict at HTTP 409.

The **effective-window overlap** rule protects each document's timeline, and its geometry is half-open with adjacency permitted. Two clauses apply against the sibling versions of the same document as each item version. Clause (a): the candidate `effective_date` must not fall inside the half-open occupied window of any sibling whose `status` is `SUPERSEDED` — that window starts at the sibling's `effective_date` inclusive and ends at the sibling's `window_end_date` exclusive. A candidate date exactly equal to such a sibling's `window_end_date` is adjacent rather than overlapping and passes clause (a). Clause (b): when a sibling of the same document has `status` `PUBLISHED`, its window is open-ended, and the candidate `effective_date` must be strictly greater than that sibling's `effective_date`.

The **legal-review lead-time** rule enforces each state's statutory cooling period. For every item version and for every jurisdiction that appears both in the package's `scope_jurisdiction_ids` and in that version's `required_jurisdiction_ids`, take that version's review for that jurisdiction, add that jurisdiction's `min_legal_review_days` whole days to the review's `decided_at`, and require the result to be earlier than or equal to the package's `effective_date`. The bound is inclusive: an exact equality passes. Jurisdictions in the release scope that the version does not list as required are not subject to this rule.

Scheduling also computes and stores `legal_review_deadline`, the latest instant by which all required reviews must have been approved. Its three steps are evaluated in a pinned order and none of them may be re-derived from the result. Step one: the **base days** is the largest `min_legal_review_days` among all jurisdictions named in the package's `scope_jurisdiction_ids` — all of them, regardless of whether any item version lists them as required. Step two: the **lead days** is the whole-day, floor-truncated difference from the logical clock at the instant of scheduling to the package's `effective_date`; the **slack days** is the lead days minus the base days; and the **buffer days** is 7 when the slack days is 0 or negative, 3 when the slack days is between 1 and 9 inclusive, and 1 when the slack days is 10 or more. Step three: `legal_review_deadline` is the package's `effective_date` minus the sum of the base days and the buffer days, in whole days. The buffer is derived from the slack, which is derived from the base days alone; it must never be re-derived from the resulting deadline, which would itself shift the slack. The dependency looks circular and this order is its single well-defined resolution. Scheduling stamps `scheduled_at` from the logical clock and increments the package's `version` by 1.

A scheduled package can be walked back. Un-scheduling moves it `SCHEDULED → DRAFT`, clearing `scheduled_at` and `legal_review_deadline` back to null; it requires the package to be in `SCHEDULED`, and an attempt against any other status is refused with `ERR_RELEASE_NOT_SCHEDULED` (409). Cancelling is available from two states and both branches are permitted: `DRAFT → CANCELLED` and `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`. The cancel from `SCHEDULED` additionally clears `legal_review_deadline` to null, since a scheduled package is the only one that has such a deadline; the cancel from `DRAFT` has no such field to clear. An attempt to cancel a package that is neither `DRAFT` nor `SCHEDULED` is refused with `ERR_RELEASE_NOT_CANCELLABLE` (409). Both branches increment the package's `version` by 1, and `CANCELLED` is terminal.

Releasing is the pivot of the whole system. It moves the package `SCHEDULED → RELEASED`, requires the package to be in `SCHEDULED` (`ERR_RELEASE_NOT_SCHEDULED`), requires `expected_release_package_version` to match the package's current `version` as described in §2.6, requires the logical clock to have reached or passed the package's `effective_date` — a clock strictly earlier than the effective date is refused with `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_NOT_REACHED` (409) — and requires every item's version to still be in `APPROVED`. On success `released_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the following happens in order.

Each item's version moves `APPROVED → PUBLISHED` with its `effective_date` copied from the package. Where the same document already had a published version, that prior version runs the four-hop supersede cascade of §3.4, and the item's `supersedes_version_id` is set to the prior published version's identifier; when the document had no prior published version, `supersedes_version_id` stays null. Then the roster is minted: one **distribution assignment** is created per pairing of a release item with an employee whose `jurisdiction_id` is in the package's `scope_jurisdiction_ids` and whose `employment_status` is `ACTIVE` or `ON_LEAVE`, processed ordered by the employee's `created_at` ascending and then by the item version's `created_at` ascending. Terminated employees are never assigned.

Each minted assignment snapshots the context it will be judged against for the rest of its life: `release_package_id`, `policy_version_id`, `employee_id`, `jurisdiction_id` taken from the employee's current jurisdiction, and `policy_category` taken from the owning document's `category`. It starts with `ack_state` of `UNDELIVERED` and `escalation_state` of `NONE`, with `delivered_at` and `acknowledged_at` null and `days_overdue` at 0. Its `window_start_date` is the package's `effective_date`, and its `due_date` comes from the acknowledgment-deadline calculation described in §3.7. Each minted assignment increments its assignee's `open_ack_count` by 1. The package's `roster_snapshot_size` is set to the number of assignments minted, and this becomes the frozen denominator of the snapshot coverage variant. The package's `version` increments by 1.

Minting also enforces the **acknowledgment-window buffer**, which protects employees from being flooded with same-category obligations. For each candidate assignment about to be minted, look at the employee's existing assignments that share the same `policy_category` and whose `ack_state` is `UNDELIVERED`, `PENDING` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`. Each such assignment occupies the closed window from its `window_start_date` through its `due_date`, and the candidate occupies its own closed window in the same way. The gap between two windows is measured from the earlier window's `due_date` to the later window's `window_start_date`, and it must be at least the employee's jurisdiction's `ack_window_buffer_days` whole days. A gap strictly smaller than that — including adjacency, meaning a gap of zero — is a conflict, and the release is refused with `ERR_ACK_WINDOW_BUFFER_CONFLICT` (409).

Coverage reporting on a package is computed in **two variants from one numerator**, and the difference between them is exactly the difference between the compliance-of-record number quoted in a dispute and the operational number shown on a dashboard. The shared numerator is the count of the package's assignments whose `ack_state` is `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `EXEMPT` — an exempted employee counts as covered. The `snapshot_roster` variant divides that numerator by `roster_snapshot_size`, the mint count frozen at release time, which still includes rows later voided and rows whose assignee has since become `TERMINATED`; the result is written to `coverage_snapshot_pct`. The `live_roster` variant divides the same numerator by the count of the package's assignments whose `ack_state` is anything other than `VOIDED` and whose assignee's `employment_status` is `ACTIVE` or `ON_LEAVE`; the result is written to `coverage_live_pct`. In both variants the quotient is multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to two decimal places, and in both variants a denominator of zero yields exactly 100.00 rather than an error or a null. Because the live denominator excludes voids and terminations, the same numerator generally yields a higher live percentage than snapshot percentage.

Both variants are computed when the package is released, and again when it closes, and again on every coverage read; the coverage read recomputes and returns both numbers **without** incrementing the package's `version`. Closing is system-driven: a package in `RELEASED` moves `RELEASED → CLOSED` at the moment the count of its assignments whose `ack_state` is `UNDELIVERED` or `PENDING` reaches zero, which recomputes and writes both coverage figures and increments the package's `version` by 1. That condition can be satisfied the instant the package is released if zero assignments were minted, in which case the package transitions straight through to `CLOSED` with both coverage figures at 100.00. `CLOSED` is a terminal state with one documented exception: when a revocation returns an assignment to `PENDING`, a package sitting in `CLOSED` returns to `RELEASED`.

### 3.7. Delivery, Acknowledgment, Revocation, and Retention

A distribution assignment carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**, and keeping them independent is essential. `ack_state` tracks the acknowledgment obligation through `UNDELIVERED`, `PENDING`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `EXEMPT` and `VOIDED`. `escalation_state` tracks the chasing process through `NONE`, `ESCALATING`, `RESOLVED` and `MOOT`. A row may legitimately be `PENDING` on one axis and `ESCALATING` on the other, or `ACKNOWLEDGED` and `RESOLVED`; an acknowledgment-dimension move never writes the escalation dimension except through the cross-dimension side effects documented explicitly here and in §3.8. `VOIDED` is the terminal acknowledgment state, reached only by system action when the assignment's policy version enters `SUPERSEDED` or `WITHDRAWN`; both `UNDELIVERED → VOIDED` and `PENDING → VOIDED` are permitted, each decrements the assignee's `open_ack_count` by 1, and an assignment already `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `EXEMPT` or `VOIDED` is left alone. Assignments may be listed by `release_package_id`, `policy_version_id`, `employee_id` and `jurisdiction_id`, and by either dimension via `ack_state` and `escalation_state`; reading one surfaces both dimensions along with `due_date` and `days_overdue`.

The **acknowledgment deadline** for a freshly minted assignment is derived through four clamps applied in a pinned order, because the order changes the answer. Start with the smaller of the assignment jurisdiction's `ack_deadline_days` and the owning document's `max_ack_deadline_days`. Second, if the assignee's `employment_status` is `ON_LEAVE`, add 14 days — two extra weeks for someone who is away. Third, if the assignee's `needs_accessible_format` is true, raise the figure to at least 21 days, which is three weeks and acts as a floor rather than an addition. Fourth, if the owning document's `category` is `SAFETY`, cap the figure at 7 days, a hard one-week ceiling that overrides the accessibility floor because it is applied last. The resulting whole number of days is added to the assignment's `window_start_date` to give `due_date`, and the acknowledgment window is closed on both ends, running from `window_start_date` through `due_date` inclusive. The inputs are the jurisdiction of the assignment, the document of the assignment's version, and the assignee's `employment_status` and `needs_accessible_format` as of the mint instant.

Delivery is an `HRComplianceAdmin` action that moves the assignment `UNDELIVERED → PENDING` on the acknowledgment dimension and stamps `delivered_at` from the logical clock. It requires the assignment to be in `UNDELIVERED` — any other acknowledgment state is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID_ACK_STATE` (409) — the owning package to be in `RELEASED`, the assignee's `employment_status` to be `ACTIVE` or `ON_LEAVE` (a `TERMINATED` assignee is refused with `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_EMPLOYED`, 409), and a published rendition in the employee's language that clears the accessibility floor when required, as covered in §3.5. The assignment's `version` increments by 1.

Recording an acknowledgment appends an immutable evidence row and drives a five-hop cascade. The payload names the `assignment_id`, a `method` that is one of `ESIGN`, `WET_SIGNATURE` or `ATTESTED`, a `language_code` of exactly two lowercase characters naming the rendition the employee actually read, and a non-empty `signature_text` — a typed full name for `ESIGN`, a scan reference for `WET_SIGNATURE`, and an attesting note for `ATTESTED`. The checks run in this order. The acting party must be entitled: an `Employee` actor must be the assignment's own `employee_id`, or the actor must be an `HRComplianceAdmin` recording a proxy acknowledgment with `method` of `ATTESTED`; a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNMENT_OWNER`. The assignment must be in `PENDING`, or `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID_ACK_STATE`. The assignee's `employment_status` must be `ACTIVE` or `ON_LEAVE`, or `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_EMPLOYED`. When `method` is `ESIGN`, the assignment jurisdiction's `esign_permitted` must be true, or `ERR_ESIGN_NOT_PERMITTED` (409). When `method` is `ATTESTED`, the assignment jurisdiction's `proxy_ack_permitted` must be true **and** the actor must be an `HRComplianceAdmin`, or `ERR_PROXY_ACK_NOT_PERMITTED` (409). Finally, the supplied `language_code` must match a rendition on the assignment's version whose `status` is `PUBLISHED`, or `ERR_RENDITION_LANGUAGE_UNKNOWN` (409). A `WET_SIGNATURE` acknowledgment is subject to neither the e-sign nor the proxy gate.

The cascade then runs. Hop 1: an acknowledgment row is appended with `status` of `EFFECTIVE`, `revoked_at` null, `employee_id` copied from the assignment, and `captured_at` stamped from the logical clock. Hop 2: because that row was appended, the assignment moves `PENDING → ACKNOWLEDGED` on the acknowledgment dimension and its `acknowledged_at` is set equal to the acknowledgment's `captured_at`. Hop 3: because the assignment became `ACKNOWLEDGED`, a retention row is appended with `record_kind` of `CAPTURE`, `signed_delta` of `+1`, `retained_from` stamped from the logical clock and `retention_until` from the retention calculation below, and the assignee's `open_ack_count` is decremented by 1. Hop 4: because the employee's open count reached its new value, any escalation case on this assignment whose `status` is `OPEN`, `NOTIFIED` or `ESCALATED_HR` is closed as `CLOSED_ACKNOWLEDGED` with `closed_at` stamped from the logical clock, the assignment's `escalation_state` moves `ESCALATING → RESOLVED`, and the employee's `open_escalation_count` is decremented by 1. Hop 5: if the owning package now has zero assignments whose `ack_state` is `UNDELIVERED` or `PENDING`, the package moves `RELEASED → CLOSED` and both coverage variants are recomputed. The assignment's `version` increments by 1. Acknowledgment events may be listed by `assignment_id`, `employee_id` and `status`.

Revocation is the system's one compensating action on the evidence log, reserved for `HRComplianceAdmin` and optionally carrying a `reason`. It requires the target acknowledgment's `status` to be `EFFECTIVE` and the assignment to be in `ACKNOWLEDGED`. The row itself is **never deleted**: its `status` becomes `REVOKED` and `revoked_at` is stamped from the logical clock. A compensating retention row is appended with `record_kind` of `REVOCATION`, `signed_delta` of `-1`, the same `employee_id`, `jurisdiction_id` and `acknowledgment_id` as the capture row, and a `retention_until` recomputed by the same calculation at the revocation instant. The assignment moves `ACKNOWLEDGED → PENDING`, `acknowledged_at` is cleared to null, and the assignee's `open_ack_count` is incremented by 1. Critically, the assignment's `escalation_state` is left **unchanged** and an escalation case already closed as `CLOSED_ACKNOWLEDGED` is **not** reopened — revoking evidence does not resurrect a chase. If the owning package had reached `CLOSED`, it returns to `RELEASED`. The assignment's `version` increments by 1.

The **retention horizon** on each retention row is the later of two dates. The calendar base is the row's `retained_from` advanced by the governing jurisdiction's `retention_years` whole calendar years, landing on the same month and day, with 29 February mapping to 1 March of the target year. The dispute floor is the row's `retained_from` advanced by 1095 days, which is the three-year minimum the company holds evidence for regardless of state law. `retention_until` is whichever of the two is later. The governing jurisdiction is the one named by the retention row's own `jurisdiction_id`, snapshotted from the assignment — it is deliberately **not** re-read from the employee at revocation time, so an employee who relocates does not retroactively change the retention horizon of evidence already captured. Instant precision is preserved to milliseconds, including the time of day of the original write.

Retention rows are visible only to `HRComplianceAdmin`, may be listed by `acknowledgment_id`, `employee_id`, `jurisdiction_id` and `legal_hold`, and expose `signed_delta`, `retention_until` and `legal_hold` on an individual read. A dispute hold is placed by supplying a `hold_reason` that records the dispute reference; this sets `legal_hold` to true so the row is preserved past its `retention_until`. Both `legal_hold` and `hold_reason` default to their unset values — false and null respectively — on every newly appended row.

### 3.8. Non-Acknowledgment: The Overdue Sweep, Tiers, and Escalation Cases

Overdue-ness is a derived quantity on every assignment. `days_overdue` is 0 whenever the logical clock is at or before the assignment's `due_date`; once the clock passes the due date it is the floor-truncated whole-day difference from `due_date` to the logical clock. Because the acknowledgment window is closed at both ends, an employee acknowledging exactly at the `due_date` instant is on time.

An `HRComplianceAdmin` drives escalation through a batch checkpoint that walks assignments ordered by their `created_at` ascending. For each assignment whose `ack_state` is `PENDING`, whose `escalation_state` is `NONE`, whose `days_overdue` is at least 1, and against which no exception request is currently sitting in `SUBMITTED` or `MANAGER_APPROVED`, the escalation dimension moves `NONE → ESCALATING`, an escalation case is opened, and the assignee's `open_escalation_count` is incremented by 1; the assignment's `version` increments by 1. An employee who has a pending exception request in flight is therefore shielded from escalation while that request is undecided. Assignments whose `ack_state` is anything other than `PENDING` are excluded from the sweep entirely. At most one case per assignment may be in `OPEN`, `NOTIFIED` or `ESCALATED_HR` at any time.

The checkpoint answers with a collection rather than a single resource: once the whole checkpoint has finished — including the re-tiering below and the grant expiry of §3.9 — its response body is the list of every escalation case whose `status` is then `OPEN` or `NOTIFIED`, regardless of which run opened it, so cases carried over from earlier runs appear alongside cases opened by this one while cases in `ESCALATED_HR` or in any closed status are omitted. That list is returned under the same ordering, windowing and `meta` conventions as a list endpoint, as described in §2.3.

The opened case starts with `status` of `OPEN`, `opened_at` stamped from the logical clock, `closed_at` null, `notice_count` at 0, `employee_id` copied from the assignment, and `notified_manager_id` set to the subject's `manager_id` at case-open time — which is null when the subject sits at the top of the reporting chain. Its `tier` comes from the escalation-tier calculation.

The **escalation tier** is a threshold ladder over `days_overdue`, with inclusive lower edges. A base tier of 1 applies when the days overdue is at least 1 and fewer than 7; a base tier of 2 applies when it is at least 7 and fewer than 14; a base tier of 3 applies at 14 or more. So 13 days overdue is base tier 2, exactly 7 days overdue is already base tier 2 rather than 1, and exactly 14 days overdue is already base tier 3 rather than 2. When the assignment's `policy_category` is `SAFETY`, the base tier is raised by one step and then capped at 3 — a safety policy one day overdue is tier 2, and a safety policy 14 days overdue remains tier 3. For every other category the tier is the base tier unchanged. The same checkpoint recomputes `tier` for every existing case whose `status` is `OPEN` or `NOTIFIED`, so tiers climb as the overdue count grows; cases already in `ESCALATED_HR` or closed are not re-tiered.

Chasing then proceeds by hand. Notifying the subject's manager moves the case `OPEN → NOTIFIED` and increases `notice_count` by 1; it requires the case to be in `OPEN` and requires `notified_manager_id` to be non-null, since there is nobody to notify for a subject with no manager. Escalating to HR moves the case `NOTIFIED → ESCALATED_HR` and is gated on severity: it requires the case's `tier` to be exactly 3, and a lower tier is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_TIER_TOO_LOW` (409). A case whose status is not the from-state a requested movement needs is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_INVALID_STATE` (409). Both movements increment the case's `version` by 1.

There are three terminal case outcomes and their side effects diverge sharply. The **disciplinary** close is the only one an operator drives directly: supplying an `outcome` of `DISCIPLINARY` moves the case `ESCALATED_HR → CLOSED_DISCIPLINARY` with `closed_at` stamped from the logical clock, requires the case to be in `ESCALATED_HR` and the linked assignment's `ack_state` to still be `PENDING`, sets the assignment's `escalation_state` to `RESOLVED`, decrements the subject's `open_escalation_count` by 1, and increments the case's `version` by 1. Notably the assignment is **left** at `PENDING` and the subject's `open_ack_count` is untouched: discipline does not discharge the obligation to acknowledge, unlike the other two outcomes.

The **acknowledged** close fires as hop 4 of the acknowledgment cascade and is available from all three open statuses: `OPEN → CLOSED_ACKNOWLEDGED`, `NOTIFIED → CLOSED_ACKNOWLEDGED` and `ESCALATED_HR → CLOSED_ACKNOWLEDGED` are each permitted, each stamps `closed_at` from the logical clock, and each decrements the subject's `open_escalation_count` by 1. The **moot** close fires whenever the linked assignment's `ack_state` becomes `VOIDED` or `EXEMPT`, and it is likewise available from all three open statuses: `OPEN → CLOSED_MOOT`, `NOTIFIED → CLOSED_MOOT` and `ESCALATED_HR → CLOSED_MOOT` each stamp `closed_at` and each decrement `open_escalation_count` by 1. In the moot case the assignment's escalation dimension moves `ESCALATING → MOOT` rather than to `RESOLVED`. Cases may be listed by `assignment_id`, `employee_id`, `notified_manager_id`, `status` and `tier`, and reading one surfaces `tier`, `notice_count` and `status`.

### 3.9. Policy Exceptions: Two-Stage Approval, Risk Scoring, and Expiry

An employee who cannot acknowledge a policy asks for a **policy exception** against their own obligation. The request names the `policy_version_id`, a `reason_code` that is one of `RELIGIOUS`, `MEDICAL`, `COLLECTIVE_BARGAINING` or `ROLE_CONFLICT`, a non-empty `justification` of at most 2000 characters, a `requested_days` count of whole days for which the acknowledgment obligation would be suspended (an integer of at least 1 and at most 365), and `expected_employee_version` as described in §2.6. The requester must have exactly one assignment on that version whose `ack_state` is `PENDING`, and that assignment becomes the request's `assignment_id`; if no such pending assignment exists, the request is refused with `ERR_NO_PENDING_ASSIGNMENT` (409), and where a resolvable assignment exists but its `ack_state` is not `PENDING` the refusal is `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID_ACK_STATE` (409), which guards exception creation just as it guards delivery, acknowledgment and revocation. The requester's `employment_status` must also be `ACTIVE` or `ON_LEAVE`, failing which the refusal is `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_EMPLOYED`. The new request enters `status` `SUBMITTED` with `employee_id` set to the acting employee, `approving_manager_id` and `deciding_reviewer_id` null, and `expires_at` null. Its `prior_exception_count` is frozen at creation to the number of that same employee's exception rows whose `status` is `GRANTED` at that instant, excluding the row being created. Creating a request increments the requesting employee's `version` by 1. Requests may be listed by `employee_id`, `policy_version_id`, `assignment_id`, `approving_manager_id`, `deciding_reviewer_id` and `status`, and reading one surfaces both risk-score variants and `expires_at`.

The **exception risk score** is computed from exactly four factors, read at calculation time, and is produced in two variants. The reason factor is a lookup on `reason_code`: 20 for `RELIGIOUS`, 25 for `MEDICAL`, 45 for `COLLECTIVE_BARGAINING`, and 65 for `ROLE_CONFLICT`. The duration factor is `requested_days` multiplied by 100 and divided by 180 — six months being the reference horizon at which a request is considered maximally long — capped at 100 and rounded half up to four decimal places. The history factor is `prior_exception_count` multiplied by 30, capped at 100, so a fourth prior grant adds nothing beyond the third. The category factor is a lookup on the `category` of the document owning the exception's policy version: 90 for `SAFETY`, 75 for `DATA_PRIVACY`, 55 for `COMPENSATION`, 40 for `CODE_OF_CONDUCT`, and 25 for `LEAVE`.

From those four the system forms a weighted mean: 20 percent of the reason factor plus 15 percent of the duration factor plus 25 percent of the history factor plus 40 percent of the category factor. The `weighted_aggregate` variant is that weighted mean rounded half up to two decimal places, and it is stored as `risk_score_aggregate` for reporting; a single extreme factor is diluted by the weights. The `conservative_ceiling` variant takes the single largest of the four factors — the peak — and blends 60 percent of that peak with 40 percent of the weighted mean, rounding the result half up to two decimal places; it is stored as `risk_score_conservative`. Because the peak dominates it, one extreme factor alone can push the conservative score high even when the weighted mean is moderate, and it is the conservative variant — never the aggregate — that the grant gate reads. Both scores are computed on creation and both are recomputed when the manager records a decision.

The first decision stage belongs to the requester's own manager, who supplies a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `DENY` plus an optional `note`. Two identity conditions apply: the acting manager's identifier must equal the requesting employee's `manager_id`, and it must not equal the request's `employee_id`. A violation of either is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403) — nobody approves their own suspension. An `APPROVE` decision additionally requires the linked assignment's `ack_state` to still be `PENDING`; it moves the request `SUBMITTED → MANAGER_APPROVED` and records `approving_manager_id`. A `DENY` decision moves the request `SUBMITTED → DENIED`, records `approving_manager_id`, and deliberately leaves `deciding_reviewer_id` null and `expires_at` null; the linked assignment is left at `PENDING` and the employee's `open_ack_count` is unchanged. A request whose status is not the from-state a requested movement needs is refused with `ERR_EXCEPTION_INVALID_STATE` (409). Either branch increments the request's `version` by 1.

The second stage belongs to jurisdiction counsel, again with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY` and an optional `note`. Both branches require the acting reviewer's own `jurisdiction_id` to equal the requesting employee's `jurisdiction_id`, failing which the attempt is refused with `ERR_REVIEWER_JURISDICTION_MISMATCH` (403). The `APPROVE` branch adds a segregation-of-duties screen and a risk ceiling. The acting reviewer must not be the `approved_by_reviewer_id` of any addendum on the exception's policy version, and must not be the `reviewer_id` of any review on that version whose `status` is `APPROVED`; a conflict is refused with `ERR_SOD_REVIEWER_CONFLICT` (403), because the counsel who certified a policy may not also excuse someone from it. The request's `risk_score_conservative` must then be **strictly below** 70.00; a score of exactly 70.00 or higher is refused with `ERR_EXCEPTION_RISK_TOO_HIGH` (409).

On a successful legal approval the request moves `MANAGER_APPROVED → GRANTED`, records `deciding_reviewer_id`, and sets `expires_at` to the logical clock advanced by `requested_days` whole days. The linked assignment then moves `PENDING → EXEMPT` on the acknowledgment dimension, the employee's `open_ack_count` is decremented by 1, and any escalation case on that assignment with `status` of `OPEN`, `NOTIFIED` or `ESCALATED_HR` is closed as `CLOSED_MOOT` with the assignment's `escalation_state` becoming `MOOT` and `open_escalation_count` decremented by 1. A legal `DENY` moves the request `MANAGER_APPROVED → DENIED`, records `deciding_reviewer_id`, leaves `expires_at` null, and leaves the linked assignment at `PENDING`. The two denial paths therefore converge on the same status while carrying different field state: a manager denial has `approving_manager_id` set and `deciding_reviewer_id` null, whereas a legal denial has `deciding_reviewer_id` set. Both branches increment the request's `version` by 1.

A grant does not last forever. The same operator checkpoint that opens escalation cases also expires grants: any request whose `status` is `GRANTED` and whose `expires_at` the logical clock has reached or passed moves `GRANTED → EXPIRED`, its linked assignment returns `EXEMPT → PENDING`, the employee's `open_ack_count` is incremented by 1, and the assignment's `due_date` is **re-derived on a different basis** — it becomes the request's `expires_at` advanced by the assignment jurisdiction's `ack_deadline_days` whole days, with none of the four clamps of §3.7 applied. An `HRComplianceAdmin` may also cut a grant short: revoking a request in `GRANTED` moves it `GRANTED → REVOKED`, returns the assignment `EXEMPT → PENDING` and increments `open_ack_count` by 1, but does not touch `due_date`; an optional `reason` may accompany the revocation. `DENIED`, `EXPIRED` and `REVOKED` are all terminal, and each of these movements increments the request's `version` by 1.
