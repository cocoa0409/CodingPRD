# Fare Freeze Guarantee Products API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Airfare is the last big-ticket purchase most consumers still make blind. A traveler shopping a route three weeks out has no way to know whether the fare in front of them is a floor or a spike, and the two available strategies — commit immediately or keep refreshing — both feel like gambling. This document specifies the API behind a product family that removes some of that uncertainty: a forecast layer that reads published demand curves and live fare observations, a watch service that tells a shopper whether to buy now or wait, a price freeze that lets a deposit lock a fare for a fortnight, and cancellation and change protection that converts an itinerary's disruption risk into a priced, disclosed ancillary product.

The counterparty side is just as demanding as the consumer side, and it is the reason this specification is long. Every fare the company guarantees is an exposure it must carry, reserve and hedge; every deposit it collects is restricted money held in custody under a reconciliation that has to balance at expiry; every premium it writes against an ancillary product must be priced actuarially and disclosed before purchase. Routes move through postures — calm, surging, restricted, closed — and the transitions ripple through the region's hedging pool, the exposure books, and every open freeze and protection on the affected itineraries. Finally, because claims are paid out of the company's own book, adjudication is governed by segregation of duties: the identity that files a claim, the identity that approves it, and the identity that settles it may not collapse into one person.

Architecturally, the resources here are flat peers linked by identifier rather than a deep containment tree, and almost every state machine in the model carries at least one sanctioned return arc — a rejected claim sends its freeze back to service, a lapsed rebooking hold returns its change event to option selection, an over-hedged region can un-lapse a position. Implementers should expect cyclic, not one-way, lifecycles.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section is intended to hold for the whole surface of the API, regardless of which resource is being touched. Where a rule here contradicts a workflow narrative later in the document, this section wins.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two mandatory headers. `X-User-ID` names the acting identity and `X-User-Role` names the capacity in which that identity is acting. A request missing either header, carrying a malformed value, or naming a role that the system does not declare is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_OR_INVALID_CONTEXT_HEADER` (HTTP 400). When the role is valid but the role is not entitled to the operation being attempted, or the presented identity is not the entity it claims to be, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_FOR_ACTOR` (HTTP 403).

The platform recognizes six roles, spelled exactly as follows: `Traveler`, `OpsAgent`, `RiskAnalyst`, `ClaimsAdjuster`, `PlatformAdmin` and `SystemOperator`.

Every one of these six roles is opaque — none of them is backed by a persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `Traveler`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the traveler's stake in an itinerary is established by comparing `X-User-ID` against the itinerary's `traveler_user_id`, and never against any entity's `id`. A `Traveler` may only act on itineraries whose `traveler_user_id` equals their presented `X-User-ID`, and may only withdraw claims filed against such an itinerary; a read that names no specific itinerary — the itinerary list, and the watch and claim lists that span itineraries — is not filtered by ownership and returns every matching row to any entitled role. The same opaque-identifier rule holds for `OpsAgent`, whose identifier is recorded nowhere structural and is never compared to an entity `id`; for `RiskAnalyst`, who opens, marks and settles hedging positions and pools without owning any row; for `ClaimsAdjuster`, whose identifier is recorded as `approver_user_id` on a `GuaranteeClaim` when they review, approve or settle it, but is still not an entity `id`; for `PlatformAdmin`, who owns route configuration, postures and the logical clock; and for `SystemOperator`, a batch identity with no commercial authority of its own.

Because the role model is segregation of duties, no identity may hold both sides of a claim. The acting identity for an approval, a review or a settlement must differ from the claim's `filer_user_id`, and must also differ from its `approver_user_id` once one has been recorded. A violation of this rule is rejected with `ERR_SOD_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422). A claim that has no recorded approver does not trip this rule; it is caught instead by the state check described in §2.6, which fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong. Error responses are not confined to the 4xx band: a request path that names no declared endpoint is reported as `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` with an HTTP 404, a method the endpoint does not admit is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with an HTTP 400, and any handler failure that is not an `ApiError` is caught and reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with an HTTP 400.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps how many items are returned in one page; it defaults to 10, and a supplied value outside the permitted band of 1 to 100 is clamped into that band rather than rejected. `offset` skips that many items from the front of the result set, defaults to 0, and a negative value is clamped to 0. Unless a workflow below states otherwise, list results are ordered by the resource's creation timestamp ascending, so `offset` pagination walks the same stable order on every call; an append-only resource (`PriceObservation`) and a republish-in-place resource (`DemandCurve`) are instead listed in their own sequence order — the order the observation was taken and the order the curve was published — while a nested rebooking-option list is ordered by `sequence_index`.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the standard envelope described in §2.2. `total_count` reflects the full size of the filtered result set, not the size of the returned page, which is what makes `has_more` meaningful. The nested lists that a parent-scoped read returns — an itinerary's freezes, protections and destination options, a route's hedging positions and the region's risk pool, a change event's rebooking options, and a freeze's payments — accept no query parameters at all: they are returned in full, reported in `meta` with a `limit` of 100 and an `offset` of 0.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and Money Handling

Every primary resource in this API — `Route`, `Itinerary`, `PriceObservation`, `DemandCurve`, `WatchSubscription`, `PriceFreeze`, `ProtectionProduct`, `GuaranteeClaim`, `ChangeEvent`, `RebookingOption`, `Payment`, `HedgingPosition`, `RiskPool` and `DestinationOption` — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` is a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no embedded meaning: a `PriceFreeze` identifier and a `Route` identifier are indistinguishable in form.
- `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the resource was created, rendered with exactly three fractional digits (for example `2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z`).
- `updated_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the resource's most recent mutation, in the same format. A recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation and refreshes `updated_at`.
- `version` is a system-managed integer that begins at 1 and is incremented by 1 on every mutation of the resource. It is the currency of the optimistic concurrency checks described in §2.12.

All monetary amounts in this API are integer cents; a fare, a deposit, a premium, a payout and a ceiling are all expressed the same way and never as strings. Final values written to a resource are rounded to two decimal places using round-half-up; a value that rounds to a whole number of cents is carried as an integer, while one that does not round to a whole cent (which the round-half-up rule can produce, since a deposit is split across a fourteen-day window) is carried with its fractional cents rather than being truncated to a whole-cent integer range; rates named as constants are treated as exact decimals and are only rounded at the moment they are written into a field. There is one deliberate exception: `deposit_integrity_flag` is a strict comparison of two money amounts rather than a rounding operation, and no rounding is ever applied to it. Configuration rates that a caller supplies, such as `volatility_index` or `hedge_ratio_pct`, keep their input precision; values the server computes are the ones subject to the two-decimal rule.

The resource hierarchy is flat. Child resources reference their parents through an identifier field rather than a nested path, and the API never models containment by URL depth. Timestamps are server-stamped: `booking_date`, `observed_at`, `published_at`, `armed_at`, `freeze_date` and `frozen_effective_at` are all set by the server and are never accepted from a client. One caller-supplied instant is kept as received rather than re-stamped: a demand curve's `price_date`, which is parsed and stored as the supplied instant and reported back in the HTTP-date form (`Tue, 01 Sep 2026 00:00:00 GMT`) rather than the ISO form the rest of the surface uses, and which doubles as the curve's identity for the supersede-in-place rule.

### 2.5. Validation Precedence and Failure Reporting

When a single request offends more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one it is follows a total priority order rather than the order in which an implementation happens to test conditions. The lowest-numbered violated rule wins.

Authentication is resolved first: identity and role validity (which produces `ERR_MISSING_OR_INVALID_CONTEXT_HEADER`), then role entitlement and entity-backed identity (which produces `ERR_FORBIDDEN_FOR_ACTOR`). Existence follows, resolved left-to-right along the path: the route is located first (`ERR_ROUTE_NOT_FOUND`), then the itinerary (`ERR_ITINERARY_NOT_FOUND`), then the freeze (`ERR_FREEZE_NOT_FOUND`), and finally the child resource named in the path or body — a watch subscription, protection product, guarantee claim, change event, rebooking option, payment, hedging position, destination option or risk pool (`ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`). Each is an HTTP 404.

The segregation audit comes next and outranks every state check: a claim approval, review or settlement attempted by the claim's own filer, or by the adjuster who already approved it, fails with `ERR_SOD_VIOLATION` before state is even examined. Only then are state gates consulted: whether the target resource sits in a state the requested transition can leave (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`), whether the itinerary admits the operation at all (`ERR_ITINERARY_NOT_PLANNED`), and whether the notification or hold window admits it (`ERR_NOTIFICATION_SUPPRESSED`).

The fiscal and safety gates occupy the middle of the order. The custody backing gate produces `ERR_CUSTODY_INSUFFICIENT`; the route risk profile gate — a claim whose freeze carries a `capped_impact_cents` larger than the route's `available_exposure_cents` — produces `ERR_ROUTE_EXPOSURE_PROFILE`; the regulatory posture gate, which requires a route to be `ACTIVE` or `SURGE`, produces `ERR_ROUTE_RESTRICTED`; and the per-route exposure cap produces `ERR_GUARANTEE_EXPOSURE_EXCEEDED`. The referential, constraint, disclosure and schema-existence family closes the order: `ERR_PRODUCT_TYPE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_HEDGE_DUPLICATE_COVERAGE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SEQUENCE_INDEX`, `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED` and `ERR_PRICE_FIELD_MISSING`.

Two properties of this design are load-bearing. First, a request that violates a low-priority rule and a high-priority rule reports the high-priority one, so a malformed-body error never masks an authorization failure and an exposure breach never masks a segregation breach. Second, an assertion that a claim's `payout_cents` equals its freeze's `capped_impact_cents` is a conformity gate rather than a numbered validation row, and a failure there surfaces as `ERR_CLAIM_NON_CONFORMANT` (HTTP 422).

Resource resolution follows the path, from left to right. When an action endpoint names more than one resource, the identifiers are resolved in order and the first one that does not correspond to an existing resource terminates the request before any later identifier is examined — a request naming a nonexistent container therefore fails on the container, never on the child. Read this way, a route is resolved through `route_id`, an itinerary through `itinerary_id`, a freeze through `freeze_id`, a watch subscription through `watch_id`, a protection through `protection_id`, a claim through `claim_id`, a change event through `change_event_id`, a rebooking option through `option_id`, a destination option through `destination_option_id`, a payment through `payment_id`, a hedging position through `position_id`, a pool through `pool_id`, a demand curve through `curve_id` and an observation through `observation_id`. Because the hierarchy is flat, these identifiers also appear in request bodies wherever a caller names a parent directly rather than through the path.

### 2.6. State, Temporal and Ownership Gates

A transition may only be attempted from the state set its source state machine declares, and an attempt from anywhere else surfaces as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Two narrower gates sharpen that rule. The itinerary gate accepts an itinerary whose `travel_status` is either `PLANNED` or `WATCHING` when the operation is arming a watch, activating a freeze or activating a protection, but demands `PLANNED` alone when the operation is booking; failing it produces `ERR_ITINERARY_NOT_PLANNED`. The timing gate covers the three windows in the system — a watch that has not passed its `expires_at`, a notification evaluated after `cooldown_until` has elapsed, and a rebooking option whose hold has not lapsed — and failing any of them produces `ERR_NOTIFICATION_SUPPRESSED`.

Ownership is enforced for exactly two parties. A `Traveler` may act on an itinerary only when its `traveler_user_id` equals the presented `X-User-ID`, and may withdraw only claims attached to such an itinerary; anyone else attempting these operations is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_FOR_ACTOR`.

Domain-specific gates fill out the list. A watch may only be armed once at least one demand curve has been published for the route, or `ERR_CURVE_UNPUBLISHED` is returned. A notification may not be evaluated for a watch whose `expires_at` has passed, or for an itinerary that has already departed — `ERR_WATCH_EXPIRED`. A booking must leave at least one seat available on the route, otherwise `ERR_NO_SEATS_LEFT`. A payment that has reached `RETIRED` or `FORFEITED` can be neither allocated nor refunded, and attempting either yields `ERR_PAYMENT_RETIRED`; a refund that would drive a payment's `refundable_remaining_cents` below zero yields `ERR_PAYMENT_OVER_ALLOCATED` instead. A only-one-per-triple rule forbids a second `HedgingPosition` on the same combination of route, `coverage_date` and `hedge_price_cents` (`ERR_HEDGE_DUPLICATE_COVERAGE`). A destination-option bind is refused with `ERR_DESK_ODD_RATIO` when the option's fare exceeds the itinerary's `listed_fare_cents` by more than the desk's odd-ratio tolerance. A freeze may not be converted or expired while its `deposit_integrity_flag` is anything other than `INTEGRITY_CONSISTENT`, which surfaces as `ERR_MANIFEST_UNSETTLED`. A price observation whose itinerary does not belong to the route named in the same request fails with `ERR_ROUTE_ITINERARY_MISMATCH`. A change settlement that would consume more days than the product has remaining fails with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BONUS_DAYS`, and a selection naming an option that is not in `OFFERED` status, or an option belonging to a different change event, fails with `ERR_OPTION_NOT_OFFERED` and `ERR_OPTION_NOT_IN_EVENT` respectively. A second change event on the same protection while one is still `OPEN`, `OPTION_SELECTED` or `HELD` fails with `ERR_CHANGE_ALREADY_OPEN`. Finally, a malformed body — a required field absent, a wrong type, an enum member outside its set, a deposit outside its permitted range, a fare below one cent, a payment amount above its ceiling, or a timestamp that will not parse as ISO 8601 — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) regardless of how far the request otherwise progressed.

### 2.7. Ancillary Product Disclosure and Premium Payability

Ancillary travel products are sold under a disclosure obligation. Each route carries a `disclosure_headline` and a `disclosure_version`. The version begins at 1 and is incremented by 1 whenever the route's `disclosure_headline` changes, or whenever the protection rate constants change. A request to quote a protection product or to open a freeze must cite a `disclosure_version` equal to the route's current value and must carry an acknowledgment at `disclosure_acknowledged`; if the cited version is stale, or the `disclosure_acknowledged` flag is not set, the request is rejected with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED` (HTTP 422). The headline itself is surfaced to the buyer as part of the quote.

A protection product's pricing status (`quoted_status`) runs independently of its lifecycle status (`lifecycle_status`), and the two must never be collapsed into one field. Four pricing statuses exist: `QUOTED`, `QUOTED_FROZEN`, `QUOTED_ACTIVE` and `QUOTED_FROZEN_ON_UNPUBLISHED_CURVE`. Four lifecycle statuses are terminal or near-terminal in the current design: `CANCELLED_PROTECTED`, `REBOOKED`, `TERMINATED` and `EXPIRED`. A product whose pricing status is `QUOTED_FROZEN_ON_UNPUBLISHED_CURVE` has a `quote_probable_loss_cents` of 0 and a `premium_cents` of 0, and its premium is never payable while that value stands — an activation or a premium collection attempted in that condition fails with `ERR_PREMIUM_NOT_PAYABLE` (HTTP 409). A route with no published curve makes that the product's status whether or not a covering freeze exists; the freeze presence is not distinguishable until the curve is there to price against. Re-quoting such a product once the route has a published curve raises both values retroactively and moves the pricing status to `QUOTED`.

### 2.8. Insurance Tier Ceilings

Every money amount in the system can be mapped to an insurance tier ceiling, and that mapping is the backbone of both the disclosed guarantee ceiling and the settlement custody gate. The function takes an amount in cents and returns a ceiling: an amount below 20000 maps to 30000; an amount below 50000 maps to 50000; an amount below 100000 maps to 100000; an amount below 200000 maps to 200000; and any amount at or above 200000 maps to 300000. The comparisons are strict, which matters at the boundaries: an amount of exactly 200000 cents lands in the 200000-to-299999 tier and therefore maps to 200000, not 300000. The function is stateless and order-independent; it is applied at demand-curve publication, at observation append, at freeze activation, and at claim filing and settlement.

### 2.9. Exposure, the War Chest and the Hedging Book

Guarantee liability is tracked per route rather than globally. Each route carries a `war_chest_cents`, which starts at 0 and grows through a hedge-rebalance event, a settled hedged position, or a war-chest-crediting payment such as a captured `MEMBERSHIP_2` — and each such credit re-derives the route's reserved exposure against the enlarged war chest; a `reserved_exposure_cents`, which is the sum, over every freeze not in `EXPIRED`, `CANCELLED` or `CONVERTED`, of the larger of that freeze's `capped_impact_cents` and the largest `payout_cents` among its claims that are not `REJECTED` or `SETTLED_WITH_PURCHASE`; and an `available_exposure_cents` equal to the war chest minus the reserved exposure. That last value may legitimately be negative — when a posture transition freezes the desk after exposure has already been booked, the negative figure is retained and reported rather than clamped, and it is precisely what makes the profile gate in §2.5 fire. Each route also reports a `pooled_exposure_cents`, the sum of `unhedged_exposure_cents` across the region's routes that hold open freezes, reported identically on every route of the region; this is a reporting value and is never itself a posture trigger.

The hedging book has its own vocabulary. A `HedgingPosition` covers one route and one `coverage_date`, locks a `hedge_price_cents`, and records a `projected_day_cost_cents` and a `mark_value_cents` for each mark. A route may hold at most one position per coverage date and fare, and the number of coverage dates already hedged in the route's region is tracked as `pool_binding_count` against a regional cap of four. Each `RiskPool` underwrites a region, carries a `pool_code`, an `absorbed_regression_cents` figure, an `available_capacity_cents` equal to the sum of `war_chest_cents` over the region's routes minus the absorbed regression, and a `bound_position_count` spanning the region's routes in `OPEN` or `HEDGED` status.

### 2.10. Deposit Custody and Integrity Reconciliation

Deposits are restricted custodied funds, not revenue recognized at collection. While a freeze is in flight its deposit is split between an attributed bucket (`deposit_attributed_cents`) and a custody bucket (`custody_held_cents`), with the split advancing as membership days elapse. The frozen entitlement window is treated as half-open — attribution runs from the freeze date up to, but not including, the point fourteen days later — and a postural freeze stops the accrual without rolling any of it back. Money that is released rather than returned is tracked as `custody_return_pending_cents`, and a freeze's payout obligation on conversion is the custody-return figure plus the transaction value of the conversion.

At expiry or conversion the two buckets are compared and a verdict is stamped in `deposit_integrity_flag`. The verdict is `INTEGRITY_UNDER_RECOGNIZED` when the freeze's `pattern_day_count` is greater than zero and its `deposit_attributed_cents` is less than that day count; `INTEGRITY_OVER_RECOGNIZED` when `pattern_day_count` is exactly zero and `deposit_attributed_cents` is greater than zero; and `INTEGRITY_CONSISTENT` in every remaining case. Because the two buckets and the day expectation are drawn from separate models, the verdict compares the attribution against an effective expectation that never exceeds the attributed figure the accrual actually produced, so an attribution that has already reached its own rounded value is not flagged as under-recognised. This field is never rounded and never fuzzy — it is a strict comparison of two amounts. Because the custody and attribution buckets are public fields, a frozen deposit that has been forfeited is likewise public: a forfeiture sets `deposit_forfeited_cents` to twice the deposit, and the corresponding membership payment is driven to `FORFEITED` with its `refundable_remaining_cents` written to zero.

### 2.11. Posture Transitions and Regional Re-Derivation

A route's `posture` moves `ACTIVE → SURGE → RESTRICTED → CLOSED`, with a sanctioned recovery arc from `SURGE` back to `ACTIVE` and a `RESTRICTED → ACTIVE` recovery when the regional pool's absorbed regression falls back to zero. `CLOSED` is terminal. Posture is server-derived and is never accepted from a client; where an operator may request a transition, the request carries an explicit `posture_reason_code`, and where the system drives the transition the reason code is supplied by the system instead. A route also reports a `posture_reason_code`, a machine-readable cause of its current posture, and an `open_freeze_count` covering its freezes in `HELD`, `FROZEN` or `CLAIM_PENDING`.

Any direct posture mutation triggers a full re-derivation of the region's postural aggregate. The re-derivation visits every route sharing the mutated route's `region` in ascending `created_at` order, increments each re-derived route's own version, and clears the reason code of any route that leaves `RESTRICTED` — which increments that route's version a second time. A route already `CLOSED` is skipped by the pass and keeps its version and reason code; only the route a move to `SURGE`, `RESTRICTED` or `ACTIVE` was requested on is spared the version bump, since it was already stamped by the request itself. The exposure engine then recomputes the hedged, unhedged, reserved and available exposure figures for every freeze on every re-derived route. The `peak_day_demand_multiplier`, a live demand uplift applied to the route's `base_fare_cents`, is exactly 1.0 in every posture other than `SURGE`, where it is 1.5. Because that multiplier is read off the live posture every time the active base fare is taken, a re-derivation performed while the route is in `SURGE` measures the freeze's exposure against the uplifted base.

### 2.12. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Five writes in this API are create-with-supersede shapes, and each is protected by an optimistic concurrency check on its parent's version. Opening a price freeze against an itinerary requires the request to carry `expected_itinerary_version` matching the itinerary's current `version`. Arming a watch subscription requires the same `expected_itinerary_version` field against the same parent. Opening a hedging position requires `expected_route_version` matching the route's current `version`. Requesting a route posture transition requires `expected_route_version`. Settling a guarantee claim requires `expected_claim_version` matching the claim's current `version`. In every one of these five cases the field is mandatory rather than optional, and a value that no longer matches the parent's current version is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Endpoints that are not among these five do not consult a version field on this basis at all, and the presence of a matching field on an unrelated endpoint carries no meaning. Within the pass that validates a request's body, a field the endpoint does declare but the request leaves absent is a malformed body (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`) rather than a skipped check, so an absent `expected_freeze_version` on an activation, conversion, reinstatement or expiry attempt is refused on the schema before any state or exposure gate is reached. The derived recomputation of a route's reserved and available exposure, which happens as a side effect of a freeze or hedge mutation, does not by itself advance the route's version; only the write endpoints named here do.

Beyond those five mandatory checks, several endpoints additionally accept a version field for the resource they act on: freeze activation, freeze conversion and freeze reinstatement each carry `expected_freeze_version`, protection activation and protection reinstatement take `expected_protection_version`, and a change-event settlement takes `expected_change_event_version`. Freeze expiry accepts the same freeze version field as an optional convenience. Where such a field is optional it may be omitted, and the version check is simply not performed on that call; freeze cancellation, by contrast, carries no version field on its parent at all.

### 2.13. The Logical Clock and Scheduled Sweeps

Every deadline, expiry, cooldown and window in this system is evaluated against a logical clock rather than wall-clock time. Before any call is made the clock reads exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to system time. A platform operator holds two capabilities over it: setting the clock to a supplied `to_iso8601`, and advancing it to a supplied `to_iso8601`, which additionally runs the scheduled sweep; an advance whose instant does not lie after the current reading is a rewind rather than a no-op, moving the clock back to that instant and still running the sweep. Advancing the clock is what expires freezes past their `freeze_expires_at`, lapsing watches past their `expires_at`, lapses held rebooking options past their hold, lapses open hedging positions past their coverage date, and re-derives each route's postural aggregate, each itinerary's curve-derived fields and each freeze's exposure chain in one pass. The same advance also drives the delayed halves of the freeze machine — a `HELD` freeze past its window becomes `FROZEN` and a `FROZEN` freeze past it becomes `EXPIRED` — re-splits each in-flight freeze's deposit buckets against the new instant, revives a `LAPSED` hedging position whose region's resolved pool has fallen back below the revival threshold, retires an authorization the clock has left uncaptured for more than seven days, and rebuilds every exposed product's pricing status.

### 2.14. Fares, Forecasts and the Demand-Curve Baseline

The route is the anchor resource for everything measurable. A route is registered with an `origin_airport` and a `destination_airport` (three uppercase IATA letters each), a `flight_number`, a `travel_class` drawn from `ECO`, `PREMIUM` or `BUSINESS` and defaulting to `ECO`, its `region`, its live `base_fare_cents`, a five-point `fare_band_cents` shock distribution and a `peak_price_day_index` defaulting to 0. Its risk configuration is carried on the same resource: a `volatility_index` defaulting to 0.20 that scales shock distances when the route is stressed, a `hedge_ratio_pct` defaulting to 0.60 and confined between 0 and 0.90 exclusive at the top, a `max_guarantee_exposure_cents` defaulting to 50000 as the per-route ceiling on aggregate guarantee exposure, and the protection-pricing inputs `default_disruption_probability` (0.25), `disruption_severity_index` (0.1429), `protection_base_rate` (0.25), `protection_floor_rate` (0.20) and `cancel_minimum_disruption` (0.30). A newly registered route starts `ACTIVE`, with an empty `posture_reason_code`, a `peak_day_demand_multiplier` of 1.0, `war_chest_cents`, `reserved_exposure_cents` and `available_exposure_cents` all at 0, `open_freeze_count` at 0, `curve_published` false and `highest_curve_price_cents` at 0. Registration also carries the mandatory `disclosure_headline`.

The forecast half of the system is the `DemandCurve`. A curve is published for a route and a `price_date`, with a five-element `each_day_prices_cents` forecast indexed by demand day 0 through 4 and a matching five-element `seats_left_at_each_day`. Its `published_at` instant is server-stamped, and publishing a curve for a route and date that already has one supersedes the earlier curve in place — the superseded row keeps its own `id` and `created_at`, with only its prices, seats, `published_at` and `price_date` replaced, and the republished row re-takes the head of the sequence so that a supersession again becomes the route's most recently published forecast even over a curve published for a different date in the interim. Publication flips the route's `curve_published` flag to true and re-derives the route's `highest_curve_price_cents` and each itinerary's forecast-derived fields: `demand_curve_price_cents` is the curve's day-indexed price for the current demand day — the day index climbing with the logical clock and clamped to the 0-to-4 range the forecast is indexed by — `each_day_disruption_fare_cents` is that day's curve price scaled by the route's `disruption_severity_index` and rounded half-up to two decimal places, and `fare_tier_ceiling_cents` is the insurance tier ceiling of the same day's curve price. Until a curve exists, an itinerary's `demand_curve_price_cents`, `each_day_disruption_fare_cents` and `fare_tier_ceiling_cents` all read 0, and an itinerary's `listed_fare_cents` and `last_observed_fare_cents` read 0 until the first live observation arrives.

Live reality arrives as a `PriceObservation`. Each observation names a route and an itinerary, carries a strictly positive `fare_cents` and a `seats_left` snapshot, and is stamped with an `observed_at` on the logical clock. An observation is born in `OBSERVED` and never leaves it; the feed is append-only, there is no update and no delete for an observation, and there is likewise no way to move one out of that state. An itinerary keeps the ladder of everything it has seen in `observed_fares_cents`, in the ascending order the observations were taken; an observation is listed in the order it was appended. Both the append and the re-derivation of an itinerary's fare fields leave an empty ladder alone: `listed_fare_cents` and `last_observed_fare_cents` stay at their creation value of 0 until an observation actually arrives, and `demand_curve_price_cents` reads 0 for an itinerary whose route has no published curve. A demand curve behaves the same way: it is born in `PUBLISHED` and remains there, its superseding being expressed by replacing the row in place rather than by a state change.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering a Route and Opening the Forecast Book

A `PlatformAdmin` registers a route by supplying the commercial parameters described in §2.14 — the two airport codes, the `flight_number`, the `travel_class`, the `region`, the live `base_fare_cents`, the five-point `fare_band_cents`, the `disclosure_headline` and whichever of the optional risk parameters the desk wishes to override. Only the mandatory keys must be present; every parameter not supplied takes its declared default. Registration leaves the route `ACTIVE` and its exposure books empty, ready for the demand-curve feed.

Live commercial parameters are adjustable in flight. A `PlatformAdmin` may replace the `base_fare_cents`, the `fare_band_cents`, the `hedge_ratio_pct`, the `max_guarantee_exposure_cents` or the `disclosure_headline` on an existing route. Registration and every in-flight replacement are equivalently constrained: the `base_fare_cents` must be at least one cent, the `fare_band_cents` must carry exactly five entries, and the `hedge_ratio_pct` must satisfy `0 < value <= 0.90` — the top of that band is included, so 0.90 itself is accepted. A change to the `disclosure_headline` increments the route's disclosure version, which means a buyer who has been staring at a cached quote must fetch the new version before a freeze or a protection quote will be accepted; a replacement that supplies the value the route already carries is not a change, and leaves the disclosure version, the route's version and its derived exposure books untouched. Any such update re-derives the hedged and unhedged exposure figures for every open freeze on the route and increments the route's version.

The forecast feed and the observation feed are separate responsibilities and are driven by the operations desk. Publishing a demand curve requires exactly five strictly positive entries in `each_day_prices_cents`, and exactly five entries in `seats_left_at_each_day` whose first element is at least as large as its last — that is, availability is forecast to fall, not rise, across the five demand days. An existing curve for the same route and date is superseded in place rather than versioned, with `price_date`, `each_day_prices_cents` and `seats_left_at_each_day` all replaced and `published_at` re-stamped. Publication flips the route's curve flag, recomputes its `highest_curve_price_cents` as the maximum single-day price across every curve it has ever published, and re-derives the forecast fields of every itinerary on the route, incrementing each of those itineraries' version by 1. A `price_date` is not private to a route: republishing a curve for the same route and `price_date` binds to the stored curve by that date alone, and the `seats_left_at_each_day` series is only ever required to fall across its five entries — the invariant binds the first element against the last, with the interior entries unconstrained, and it is enforced on publication only, not on route registration's `fare_band_cents`.

An observation, by contrast, is a single point of truth about what a fare actually is right now. Appending one requires the itinerary to belong to the route named in the same request, and requires a `fare_cents` of at least one cent. The append sets the itinerary's `listed_fare_cents` and its `last_observed_fare_cents` to the observed fare, re-derives `demand_curve_price_cents`, `each_day_disruption_fare_cents` and `fare_tier_ceiling_cents` from the route's most recently published curve, and recomputes the entire exposure chain — `overshoot_cents`, `capped_impact_cents`, `exposure_cents`, `hedged_exposure_cents` and `unhedged_exposure_cents` — on every freeze on that itinerary that is `FROZEN` or `CLAIM_PENDING`. The itinerary's version advances by 1 with each append, which is exactly why a client that armed a freeze or a watch against an older reading has to re-read before its next write.

Nothing in this API is deleted. Every lifecycle is expressed as status movement, and the status field each resource carries is the one that governs whether an operation is admissible: a `Route` reports its posture, an `Itinerary` its `travel_status`, a `DemandCurve`, a `PriceObservation` and a `WatchSubscription` each a `notification_status`, a `PriceFreeze`, a `ProtectionProduct`, a `GuaranteeClaim`, a `ChangeEvent`, a `RebookingOption`, a `Payment`, a `HedgingPosition`, a `RiskPool` and a `DestinationOption` its own `status`, and a protection product separately its `lifecycle_status` and its `quoted_status`. A read of any resource therefore always reflects the latest transition rather than a snapshot, because a status change is a mutation like any other and advances `updated_at` and `version`.

### 3.2. Arming, Notifying and Resolving a Fare Watch

A `Traveler` creates a `WatchSubscription` on their own itinerary to convert monitoring into a decision. The request carries an `expected_itinerary_version`, and a stale value is refused. Arming requires the itinerary to be `PLANNED`, requires that at least one demand curve has been published for the route, and requires the acting traveler to own the itinerary. Success creates the watch in `ARMED` with an `armed_at` of the current clock instant and an `expires_at` equal to the watched itinerary's `departure_date` — the watch must resolve before the flight, so its window is the itinerary's own. The itinerary moves to `WATCHING` with `watch_armed` true, and its version increments.

The decision itself comes from the cost-of-waiting evaluation. If the itinerary is already `BOOKED` it returns `WATCH`; if the current clock instant has reached a non-null `booking_hold_expires_at` it returns `NOTIFIED`; otherwise it compares prices. The declared `WATCHING` guard, which would otherwise answer `NO_ACTION`, is not applied as a short-circuit: arming a watch is exactly what moves an itinerary to `WATCHING`, so applying the guard literally would make the watch machine's non-terminal arcs unreachable, and a `WATCHING` itinerary therefore falls through to the price comparison below. The two prices that matter are the itinerary's `fare_tier_ceiling_cents` — deliberately not `last_observed_fare_cents` — against the latest observed fare: when the tier ceiling is below the latest fare the recommendation is `BUY_NOW`; when the tier ceiling is above the latest fare the recommendation is `WAIT`; when the two are equal it falls through to a secondary comparison of the latest fare against the demand-curve expectation, yielding `BUY_NOW` if the latest fare is the higher of the two and `HOLD` otherwise. The consequence of keying on the tier ceiling rather than the fare is that a fare which has risen but stayed inside its own tier still reads `WAIT`, which is the intended conservatism: the company would rather the traveler wait than commit on a movement that has not crossed a real boundary.

Notifications are rate-limited by a six-hour cooldown. A watch in `NOTIFIED` or `WAIT` evaluates its cooldown before delivering: if the current instant is at or before the watch's `cooldown_until` the notification is suppressed, `cooldown_suppressed` is set true, and the recommendation is computed but not delivered. The window is half-open — a clock reading exactly equal to `cooldown_until` is suppressed, which is why the field can be read as strictly private to the watcher until one instant past itself. A watch in any other status has no cooldown at all, so the first notification of a fresh watch is never suppressed. Every evaluation that is not an expiry increments `notification_attempt_count` by 1, and a delivered `BUY_NOW` records the fare it carried in `last_notified_fare_cents` and the instant in `last_notified_at`. An evaluation that fires a `BUY_NOW` also sets the watch's `buy_now_warranty_applied` flag to true and leaves it false on a pass that delivers nothing, so the flag is the durable record of whether this watch has ever engaged the warranty.

The watch machine is cyclic rather than a pipeline, and every arc out of its non-terminal states is reachable. From `ARMED` a watch may move to `NOTIFIED` on any delivered decision, to `BUY_NOW` on the system-driven escape clause of §3.2, or to `EXPIRED` when the clock passes its window. From `NOTIFIED` it may move to `WAIT` when the applied recommendation is a wait, or to `CONFIRMED` when a satisfying `BUY_NOW` notification has been delivered and the traveler confirms it. From `BUY_NOW` it moves to `CONFIRMED` on confirmation. A `WAIT` watch that receives any delivered recommendation returns to `NOTIFIED`, which is the return path that lets a watch that has been told to wait be told something else later. Every delivered receipt that advances the machine through the recommendation path stamps a fresh six-hour `cooldown_until` and records `last_notified_at`; the system-fired `BUY_NOW` escape clause records its own notification instant and opens the twenty-four-hour hold instead, without a cooldown of its own. A cooldown-suppressed pass delivers nothing at all — it sets `cooldown_suppressed` true and increments the attempt count, but leaves the watch in the status it already held, so a suppressed pass on a `NOTIFIED` watch does not re-enter that state and a suppressed pass on a `WAIT` watcher stays `WAIT`. An evaluation may be requested against a watch in any of `ARMED`, `NOTIFIED` or `WAIT`; the terminal states admit no evaluation. A watch leaves its machine for good when the clock runs past its `expires_at`: the watch goes to `EXPIRED`, and its itinerary's `travel_status` is returned to `PLANNED` with `watch_armed` false. The two terminal states of the machine are `CONFIRMED` and `EXPIRED`; a `CONFIRMED` watch books its itinerary, and `watch.locked_fare_cents` is the minimum of the fare observed when `BUY_NOW` fired and the fare observed at the end of the twenty-four-hour holding window.

A `BUY_NOW` is a promise, and the promise is honoured by a 24-hour warranty. When `BUY_NOW` fires, the itinerary's `booking_hold_expires_at` is set to the current instant plus 24 hours, and the observed fare is recorded twice — once when the notification fires and once again at the end of the holding window — with the lock value being the minimum of the two. A `Traveler` confirms the notification, which is the only way to close the warranty — confirmation is the `Traveler`'s alone, and the `acknowledge_warranty` acknowledgment it may carry is optional, so a confirmation presented without it still closes the warranty — and the confirmation sets `locked_fare_cents` to that minimum, books the itinerary, and converts every freeze on it that is `FROZEN` to `CONVERTED` — the confirmation books the itinerary through the same ordinary booking path, so a freeze still in `DRAFT` or `HELD` at that instant is not carried into the conversion. The confirmation request carries an acknowledgment at `acknowledge_warranty`, and it is admitted only while the itinerary still admits a booking. A confirmation is admitted while the clock is at or before the cooldown instant plus 24 hours for a `BUY_NOW` fired by a notification, or at or before the itinerary's `booking_hold_expires_at` for a system-fired one; for a system-fired `BUY_NOW`, which sets no cooldown of its own, the hold is the whole window. The system fires its own `BUY_NOW` in one narrow circumstance: when the route has moved to `RESTRICTED` or `CLOSED` while the itinerary is still `PLANNED` or `WATCHING`, and the watch is still inside its window. That escape clause exists because a route that is closing admits no new freezes, so a watcher who has not yet bought is about to lose the option entirely and is pushed to buy at the last observed fare. An evaluation carries an optional `request_source` describing what prompted it, which the server records for audit; it never changes the outcome. The `request_source` is inert in a stronger sense than that: the system-fired escape clause is entered on the route's posture alone, and supplying a `request_source` — of any value, including a system-flavoured one — does not itself invoke it, so a copied evaluation payload never reaches the escape clause ahead of the ordinary recommendation path or sets `buy_now_warranty_applied`.

A `Schedule`-driven lapse sweeps the watch machine on the clock advance. A held rebooking option is not part of this machine; it belongs to §3.7.

### 3.3. Opening and Activating a Price Freeze

A price freeze is the product that lets a traveler lock a fare today and settle later. A `Traveler` — or an `OpsAgent` acting for them — opens a freeze manifest against an itinerary by supplying a `deposit_cents` between 100 and 5000 inclusive, the route's current `disclosure_version`, an acknowledgment at `disclosure_acknowledged`, and the `expected_itinerary_version` describing the itinerary read the client is acting on. The manifest pins `manifest_fare_cents` from the route's `base_fare_cents` at that instant — the bare route fare, so a draft manifest is pinned from `base_fare_cents` while a later live re-derivation may measure the same freeze against the uplifted base. and sets `cap_cents` to the manifest fare multiplied by 1.4 and rounded half-up to two decimal places — the company's absorption ceiling for this freeze. It also fills the three tier ceilings described in §2.8: `firm_tier_ceiling_cents`, the ceiling of the most recent observed fare at or before filing; `cap_tier_ceiling_cents`, the ceiling of `cap_cents`; and `guaranteed_ceiling_cents`, the larger of the two. The itinerary's `guarantee_locked` flag turns on and the itinerary's version increments. A freeze created in this way sits in `DRAFT` until it is activated. Because a fresh `DRAFT` freeze on an itinerary with no observation history carries a `capped_impact_cents` of 0, the per-route exposure cap binds later — on activation and on claim filing — rather than on first creation.

Activation is the step that puts the freeze in flight, and it re-pins the manifest rather than reusing the draft-time value. A `Traveler` or `OpsAgent` activates a freeze by presenting `expected_freeze_version`; the manifest fare is re-pinned from the live route base fare at the activation instant — the `base_fare_cents` scaled by the route's live `peak_day_demand_multiplier` — with `cap_cents` recomputed from the new manifest, so a freeze created against an older fare is re-based at the moment it becomes real and a freeze activated while the route is in `SURGE` re-pins against the uplifted base. Activation requires the itinerary to be `PLANNED` or `WATCHING`, requires the route to be `ACTIVE` or `SURGE`, and requires the freeze to be in `DRAFT`; a `RESTRICTED` or `CLOSED` route admits no new freeze, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_RESTRICTED`. A freeze whose capped impact would push the route's `reserved_exposure_cents` above the route's `max_guarantee_exposure_cents` is refused with `ERR_GUARANTEE_EXPOSURE_EXCEEDED`. The projection replaces the freeze's own existing contribution to the reservation with its re-pinned one and is tested as an upper bound on the result: only a re-pinning that both raises the freeze's own contribution and carries the resulting reservation past the ceiling is refused, so a freeze that is not increasing its slice of the book — its contribution already booked and merely superseded by an equal or smaller one — does not trip the gate. Success collects the deposit: `custody_held_cents` is set to the deposit, a payment of kind `DEPOSIT` is auto-recorded as captured against the freeze with `refundable_remaining_cents` equal to the deposit, and `freeze_date` and `freeze_expires_at` are stamped from the current instant and the instant fourteen days later. The freeze moves to `HELD`. A second activation against a fully-custodied freeze drives it to `FROZEN`, and a freeze still `HELD` when its expiry passes is driven to `FROZEN` by the scheduled sweep instead, which also increments the route's `open_freeze_count`. Once a freeze has entered `FROZEN`, `frozen_effective_at` records the instant it last did so, and it stays null while the freeze is `DRAFT` or `HELD`. Any change event belonging to a protection attached to this freeze carries `itinerary_id`, the itinerary being changed, alongside its own `protection_id`.

Two return-and-exit arcs keep this machine from being a pipeline. A cancelled freeze may be reinstated by an `OpsAgent` when the itinerary is still `PLANNED` or `WATCHING`, which returns it to `DRAFT` with `deposit_forfeited_cents`, `custody_held_cents`, `custody_return_pending_cents`, `overshoot_cents`, `capped_impact_cents` and `exposure_cents` all reset to 0 and both `manifest_fare_cents` and `cap_cents` cleared. And a freeze may be cancelled from `DRAFT`, from `HELD` or from `FROZEN`; the forfeiture rule described in §3.6 decides what the traveler loses, and the credited custody portion is released to the return-pending bucket.

### 3.4. The Guarantee Exposure Chain

Every freeze carries an exposure chain, and the chain is what the risk desk actually reserves against. The chain is re-sized by the hedge-rebalance rule, which runs when a manifest is opened, when a freeze is activated, on every price observation, and on every postural re-derivation of a route.

The base fare the chain is measured against depends on whether the freeze is a legacy manifest. A freeze whose `migrated_manifest` flag is true — one whose fare was pinned before the re-pinning rule was introduced — measures against its own `manifest_fare_cents` and nothing else and uses the legacy branch of the rebalance. Every other freeze measures against the live base fare at the evaluation instant, which is the route's `base_fare_cents` multiplied by its `peak_day_demand_multiplier` and rounded half-up to two decimal places. The arrival fare is the itinerary's most recent observation at or before the evaluation instant; when the itinerary has no observation at all the arrival is undefined and the freeze's `capped_impact_cents` is 0.

With those two prices fixed, the overshoot is the smaller of two quantities: the amount by which the arrival fare exceeds the active base fare, floored at zero, and an absorption allowance. That allowance is the larger of the freeze's `cap_cents` and the fare relief the manifest already asserts — twice its manifest fare less the active base fare — which keeps the absorption ceiling continuous at the cap: a cap set below the freeze's own asserted free allowance cannot raise the exposure above the allowance the manifest was disclosed with, and where the cap is the binding ceiling the allowance is exactly `cap_cents`. The capped impact is that overshoot multiplied by 1.2 and rounded half-up to two decimal places — the uplift is the company's margin on absorbing the increase, and it is the figure the desk actually reserves. The exposure figure is the smaller of the overshoot and the capped impact; it is evaluated with the freeze-time base fare and the freeze-time arrival observation, and once computed it is not re-derived. Hedged exposure is then the smaller of the exposure figure and the exposure figure multiplied by the route's `hedge_ratio_pct`, rounded half-up to two decimal places. The hedged share rate is hedged exposure divided by exposure, and is defined as 0 when exposure is 0.

Three derived quantities fall out of that chain. The product's `suspended_under_cap` flag turns true when a covering freeze exists and the hedged share rate is below 0.50. The `guaranteed_ceiling_cents` is the larger of the insurance tier ceiling of the manifest fare and the insurance tier ceiling of the cap. And the unhedged exposure — the quantity that is aggregated into the region's pool — is the smaller of the exposure figure and the guaranteed ceiling, minus the hedged exposure.

The chain behaves differently for a pending freeze than for an active one. A freeze in `DRAFT` or `HELD` sizes its hedged and unhedged exposure from its own `capped_impact_cents`, which is 0 for a freeze on an itinerary with no observation and is the freeze-time overshoot for one that already has observation history. A pending freeze therefore reserves nothing until the itinerary's feed reaches it, and its reservation only moves upward as later observations arrive. A freeze in `FROZEN`, `CLAIM_PENDING` or `CLAIMED` re-derives the whole chain against the live arrival observation, and that re-derivation is monotone for the reserved amount — it can push the reservation up but not down. An observation that drives reserved exposure past the route's ceiling is reported as a negative `available_exposure_cents`, never clamped. Freezes in `EXPIRED`, `CANCELLED` or `CONVERTED` release their reservation and are excluded from the calculation entirely, as are claims in `REJECTED` or `SETTLED_WITH_PURCHASE`.

### 3.5. Claim Adjudication under Segregation of Duties

A guarantee claim is how a frozen fare that rose is made good, and the whole adjudication pipeline is built to keep one pair of hands from both asserting and paying a loss. A `Traveler` files a claim against their own `FROZEN` freeze by supplying the `market_price_cents` they observed. Filing requires the freeze to actually have something to claim: if the freeze's `overshoot_cents` is zero the filing is refused with `ERR_NO_OVERSHOOT` (HTTP 422), because a frozen fare that did not rise above the manifest has nothing to settle. A claim or a reconsideration must also be raised within fourteen days after the freeze's own window closed; past that instant the attempt fails with `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A filing creates the claim in `FILED` with `filer_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, `approver_user_id` left null, and `audit_ready` false. The freeze moves to `CLAIM_PENDING`, and the claim-settlement calculation described below computes the claim's overshoot, capped impact, payout and both transaction values on the spot.

The settlement calculation reads the same active base fare the exposure chain uses — the route's `base_fare_cents` multiplied by its `peak_day_demand_multiplier`, rounded half-up to two decimal places — and derives the claim's overshoot as the smaller of the amount by which the market price exceeds that base fare (floored at zero) and the freeze's `cap_cents`. The capped impact is the overshoot multiplied by 1.2, rounded half-up. The payout is the smaller of the capped impact and the `guaranteed_ceiling_test_cents`, the latter being a re-evaluation of the guarantee ceiling against the active route base fare. The product absorbs the uplifted cap rather than re-paying the traveler's own overshoot, so the payout tracks the capped impact until the guarantee ceiling binds. Two transaction values are maintained in parallel: the pre value is twice the manifest fare, minus the amount by which the market price exceeds the manifest (floored at zero) multiplied by a discount share, and the final value is the same expression with the arrival fare in place of the market price. Both are rounded half-up to two decimal places, and the discount share is a rate computed as the route's `disruption_severity_index` multiplied by its `default_disruption_probability`, rounded half-up to three decimal places before it is scaled. Because both expressions floor the excess at zero, the transaction value is exactly twice the manifest fare for every price at or below the manifest. `transaction_value_cents` reports the pre value while the claim is not settled, and switches to the final value once the claim reaches `SETTLED_WITH_PURCHASE`. The two components are retained side by side on the claim as `transaction_value_pre_cents` and `transaction_value_final_cents`, so a reader can inspect either without re-deriving it, and the claim's `claim_status` field is what determines which of the two `transaction_value_cents` currently mirrors. The claim's `status` dimension is exactly this `claim_status`; the two names refer to the same field and both are used interchangeably in this document. The claim's `payout_cents` is expected to equal its freeze's `capped_impact_cents` whenever the exposure chain was built inside a single insurance tier, and that equality is the conformity test enforced at approval: a claim whose payout does not equal its freeze's capped impact is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NON_CONFORMANT` (HTTP 422).

The claim machine cycles through review rather than running straight to payment. A claims adjuster moves a `FILED` claim into `UNDER_REVIEW`, which stamps `approver_user_id` with the acting identity; a `FILED` claim may also be approved directly, which records the approver on the same pass. Both routes enforce the segregation gate, and an adjuster who filed the claim itself is refused with `ERR_SOD_VIOLATION`. A review presented against a claim already sitting in `UNDER_REVIEW` is treated as the approval step the endpoint's table names, so it carries the claim through to `APPROVED` under the same conformity test rather than stamping a second reviewer in place. An `UNDER_REVIEW` claim that passes the conformity test is approved, which fences the payout as reserved exposure; the approval is also reachable directly from `FILED`, so the claim may reach `APPROVED` from either branch or from a `FILED` state that has never been reviewed. A rejection can be issued from `FILED` or from `UNDER_REVIEW` and returns the freeze to `FROZEN` so a corrected claim may be filed; it carries a `rejection_reason` recording the desk's ground. A `Traveler` may withdraw a claim in the same two states, but only for an itinerary they own, and supplies a `withdrawal_reason`; a withdrawal moves the claim to `WITHDRAWN`. Review and approval carry human-readable notes of their own — a `review_note` on the review step and an `approval_note` on the approval step — and a reconsideration carries a `reconsideration_reason`. Reconsideration is a genuine arc rather than a restart: an approved claim can be reopened within fourteen days after the freeze's expiry, and a rejected claim can be reopened as well; it returns the claim to `UNDER_REVIEW` without re-stamping the recorded approver, so the review that follows is the approving pass. Reopening releases any fenced payout back to reserved exposure and re-runs the schema-existence check on the fully formed data.

Settlement is the point of no return, and it is gated by the full ordered validation cascade described in §2.5 rather than by a single check. Three gates in that cascade are worth naming here because they are specific to this operation. The audit gate, which resolves before any state check, requires the claim's recorded `approver_user_id` to be set and to differ both from the `filer_user_id` and from the identity attempting the settlement — the adjuster who approved a claim may not also pay it. The profile gate requires the freeze's `capped_impact_cents` to be no larger than the route's `available_exposure_cents`. And the custody gate requires either that the claim was built inside a single insurance tier — the guarantee ceiling re-evaluated against the active route base fare equals the freeze's own `guaranteed_ceiling_cents` — or that the freeze's `custody_return_pending_cents` is at least the claim's payout; failing both produces `ERR_CUSTODY_INSUFFICIENT`. Within the ordered cascade the custody gate outranks the profile gate, so a claim that offends both is reported against its custody backing. The settlement request also carries `expected_claim_version` and is refused if it no longer matches. On success the claim reaches `SETTLED_WITH_PURCHASE`, the freeze reaches `CLAIMED`, the payout is disbursed, the freeze's reserved exposure is released, and the custodian credits the freeze's `custody_held_cents` with the disbursed payout, which is then folded into the freeze's ordinary attribution split against the freeze-time window.

Two derived diagnostics summarize a claim's position in the book. The `depth_ratio` is the claimed freeze's `unhedged_exposure_cents` divided by its `capped_impact_cents`, rounded half-up to four decimal places and defined as 0 when the denominator is 0; it measures how far the hedge scale has exceeded the safe cap. The `continuum_absorbed_cents` is the share of the overshoot borne by a loss continuum rather than by a bounded product: it is computed by taking the overshoot multiplied by 1.2 and rounded half-up, dividing that by 1.25 and rounding half-up to two decimal places. For a product whose `depth_ratio` is at most 1 the continuum term is lossless — the guaranteed ceiling covers the capped impact, so the continuum absorbs exactly the capped impact divided by 1.25 and the two models agree. Above that ratio the term is floored at the capped impact divided by 1.25 and grows with the unhedged share, so an identical overshoot produces a strictly larger absorption than the bounded case.

### 3.6. Deposit Custody, Attribution and Forfeiture

The deposit a traveler places is not revenue. It is restricted money held in custody and released against a reconciliation that has to balance. The attribution rule advances a slice of the deposit into the attributed bucket as the freeze's window elapses. It measures `membership_days_elapsed` as the whole days between the freeze date and the current clock instant, truncated rather than rounded — so a span of ten days and twenty-three hours credits ten days, not eleven — and sets `deposit_attributed_cents` to the deposit multiplied by that day count and divided by 14, rounded half-up to two decimal places. Each accrual moves the newly credited delta out of `custody_held_cents` and into the attributed bucket, which is what keeps the custody bucket equal to the unattributed remainder of the deposit at all times. Attribution is also reconciled against a pattern expectation: `pattern_day_count` is computed from a freeze's own fourteen model days — the days of its window the clock has consumed, capped at the full window — scaled by a `freeze_share` derived from the window length and the number of migrated membership heads, and rounded half-up. A freeze whose window has not yet begun therefore carries 0 model days, and one that has run its full window carries the window's full fourteen. At expiry or conversion the two are compared and the verdict described in §2.10 is stamped into `deposit_integrity_flag`.

Two operations would otherwise leave that reconciliation ambiguous, and both are blocked. Converting or expiring a freeze whose `deposit_integrity_flag` is not `INTEGRITY_CONSISTENT` is refused with `ERR_MANIFEST_UNSETTLED` (HTTP 409), so a freeze cannot be closed while its custody balance and its attribution disagree. An expiry additionally requires custody backing: when the freeze has no custody balance to return and the settlement does not sit inside a matching insurance tier, the expiry is refused with `ERR_CUSTODY_INSUFFICIENT`.

One caveat on timing. The guarantee claim window closes fourteen days after the freeze's own window has ended, but a late claim should not be reported as a malformed body simply because the clock has moved: the claim-window rule is a temporal gate that is evaluated after the request's own shape has been accepted, and it may present itself either as a window failure at the point the claim would otherwise be created or at the end of the field checks, depending on how far the request had progressed. Implementations should evaluate it in both places so that a timestamp-carrying filing is never silently admitted past its window.

Forfeiture is the one case where the deposit is not returned. When a freeze in `HELD` or `FROZEN` is cancelled, the forfeiture rule looks for a payment on the same itinerary whose kind is `MEMBERSHIP_2` and whose status is `CAPTURED` or `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`. If one exists, `deposit_forfeited_cents` is set to twice the freeze's deposit and that membership payment is driven to `FORFEITED` with its `refundable_remaining_cents` written to zero — the multiplier is applied to the whole deposit and the result is not rounded again. If no such payment exists, `deposit_forfeited_cents` is 0. There is one exemption: a carrier-driven cancellation that carries the request's exempt flag forfeits nothing even when the membership payment is present, and leaves that payment untouched. A `DRAFT` freeze, which by definition has collected nothing, always forfeits 0.

Conversion is the happy ending. A freeze in `DRAFT`, `HELD` or `FROZEN` converts into a purchase when the traveler books, when a watch confirmation books the itinerary, or on a direct conversion request — a direct conversion request being admitted against an itinerary that is still `PLANNED` or `WATCHING`. Conversion orders the freeze's deposits by their creation instant, applies them against the attributed and custody buckets in that order — treating the attributed figure as credited and the remainder as custody — moves the unattributed remainder into `custody_return_pending_cents`, zeroes `custody_held_cents`, and stamps the integrity verdict. The realized value of the conversion is the claim transaction value computed with the arrival fare rather than the market price. The freeze reaches `CONVERTED`, the itinerary reaches `BOOKED`, any live watch on the itinerary is carried to `CONFIRMED`, a seat is consumed from the route, and any protection product attached to the freeze with lifecycle status `QUOTED` becomes `ACTIVE` with its premium payable while one already `ACTIVE` is left as it is. The freeze's reserved exposure is released.

Expiry is the more prosaic ending, and it is driven either by the explicit expiry operation or by the clock sweep once the freeze's window has passed. The explicit operation closes a `FROZEN` freeze whose window has elapsed; a `HELD` freeze that is still in flight is instead driven to `FROZEN` by the sweep once its window passes, and only then becomes eligible for expiry. Expiry moves the freeze to `EXPIRED`, adds the held custody to `custody_return_pending_cents` and zeroes `custody_held_cents`, clears the itinerary's `guarantee_locked` flag, decrements the route's `open_freeze_count`, retires the freeze's outstanding deposit payments, and runs the attribution reconciliation so the integrity verdict is stamped at the same moment. Any protection product whose covering freeze this was has its `suspended_under_cap` flag cleared and its pricing status carried from `QUOTED_ACTIVE` back to `QUOTED`, with its `bonus_days_total` set from the effective disruption band. The freeze's reserved exposure is released and the route's pooled exposure and posture are re-derived.

### 3.7. Change Protection: Rebooking Options and Their Holds

The change product exists for the traveler whose plans move, and its mechanics are the most deceptive in the system because the price a traveler sees is not the price a traveler pays. A `Traveler` or `OpsAgent` files a change event against a `CHANGE`-typed protection by naming a `new_departure_date` that differs from the itinerary's current departure date and optionally a `gap_direction` of `ORIGIN_DIRECTION` or `DESTINATION_DIRECTION`, which defaults to `ORIGIN_DIRECTION`. Filing requires the product's lifecycle status to be `ACTIVE`, requires the product to actually be of type `CHANGE` (otherwise `ERR_PRODUCT_TYPE_MISMATCH`), and refuses when another change event for the same protection is already `OPEN`, `OPTION_SELECTED` or `HELD` (which yields `ERR_CHANGE_ALREADY_OPEN`). The product moves to `CHANGE_IN_PROGRESS` and a change event is created in `OPEN`, capturing the itinerary's departure date as `old_departure_date` and computing `day_shift` as the absolute day distance between the old and new dates.

Rebooking options are the alternatives offered against that event. Each option names a `departure_date` and an `option_fare_cents`, and is sequenced by a total sort key of ascending departure date, ties broken by ascending creation instant, and remaining ties broken by ascending option fare — the server-minted identifier is never consulted, which is what makes the ordering reproducible. That position is recorded as `sequence_index`, and a collision within the same change event is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SEQUENCE_INDEX`: a proposal whose departure date, option fare and creation instant all match an existing option is refused outright, while an equal date and fare raised at a later instant is admitted and takes the next sequence position.

Presentation and billing diverge, and this is the trap. What the traveler is shown is a display schedule computed from a lead-time-scaled discount rate: the rate is the product's `disruption_probability` multiplied by the itinerary's `disruption_severity_index` and by the days remaining until departure, divided by a lead-time normalizer of 30, and rounded half-up to three decimal places before it is applied. The `display_price_cents` is the option fare multiplied by that rate, rounded half-up to two decimal places, and the display order sorts by that display price ascending with creation instant as the tie-break. Because the rate collapses as departure approaches, a near-term option displays an almost-zero price and sorts ahead of a cheaper but distant one. What is actually billed is the raw option fare, with a change fee computed as that raw fare multiplied by a rate of 0.10 and rounded half-up to two decimal places. The discount is never applied to the bill.

Selection pins one option and supersedes its siblings. A selection names the chosen option in `option_id` and, from `OPEN` or `OPTION_SELECTED`, requires the named option to belong to the event (otherwise `ERR_OPTION_NOT_IN_EVENT`) and to be in `OFFERED` status (otherwise `ERR_OPTION_NOT_OFFERED`); the named option becomes `SELECTED`, every other option in the event becomes `SUPERSEDED`, and the event moves to `OPTION_SELECTED` with `selected_option_id` recording the choice. Accepting the selected option opens a 24-hour hold: the option becomes `HELD` with its `hold_expires_at` set to the current instant plus 24 hours and the event moves to `HELD` with `option_hold_expires_at` stamped to the same instant. Acceptance requires the option's `departure_date` to be strictly in the future, and a departure date that is not a legal replacement is refused at the referential gate described in §2.6.

The hold lapses on its own if nothing closes it. Once the current instant passes the event's `option_hold_expires_at`, the event returns to `OPTION_SELECTED`, the option's hold stops being live and the option becomes `EXPIRED`, and the event's `unrecovered_travel_fare_cents` is stamped with the itinerary's own departure-side fare — its recorded listing, falling back to its curve price and then to the route's base fare — re-rated by the route's `disruption_severity_index` and rounded half-up to two decimal places — the written record of a change that was started but never completed. The stamp is a lapse-only field: the scheduled sweep writes it on a first lapse, and an explicit reversion of a held option leaves it at 0. A second lapse for the same event is not treated as a repeat: it fires a terminal override that settles the event with no rebooking, recording `disruption_days` of 0 and leaving `remaining_days` equal to the product's `bonus_days_remaining`. The product itself reaches `REBOOKED` on the settlement path once a rebooking has actually been completed. A separate terminal override catches the traveler whose option was never in the offered set at all: an event that has been open longer than 45 days with no selection settles the same way.

Settlement is where days are consumed. A settlement requires a selected option that is held and not lapsed and a day balance that can absorb the consumption; an option whose hold has lapsed produces `ERR_OPTION_HOLD_LAPSED` and a settlement that would overrun the balance produces `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BONUS_DAYS`. On success the event reaches `SETTLED`, the option reaches `ACCEPTED`, the option's departure date replaces the itinerary's, and the owning protection moves to `REBOOKED` with its consumption recorded as described in §3.8. Cancelling a change event from `OPEN`, `OPTION_SELECTED` or `HELD` moves it to `CANCELLED` and returns the protection from `CHANGE_IN_PROGRESS` to `ACTIVE` with no day consumed and no premium re-rated; when the cancellation happens from `OPTION_SELECTED` every option in the event is superseded as well, while one taken from `OPEN` or from `HELD` sends the event's held option back through the `EXPIRED` path instead. Returning a held option before its hold lapses is recorded by the caller through an optional `reversion_reason` and sends the option back to the selected pool as `EXPIRED`, with the event returning to `OPTION_SELECTED`.

Directional recovered-travel options run alongside rebooking options and answer a different question: where the traveler can be recovered to rather than when. A `DestinationOption` names a `departure_date`, an `option_fare_cents`, and a `direction` of `ORIGIN_DIRECTION` or `DESTINATION_DIRECTION`, defaulting to `ORIGIN_DIRECTION`. Its `eligible` flag is derived rather than supplied: an option is eligible when it lies beyond the gap in the direction named by the change event's `gap_direction` and its departure date is in the future. Binding an `eligible` option requires the itinerary to be `PLANNED` or `WATCHING` and requires a change event on that itinerary to be `OPTION_SELECTED` with an eligible option pending acceptance; the bind moves the option to `BOUND`, leaves the event's own status untouched because a bind does not arm a hold, and the fare it fences as recovered-travel exposure is the raw `option_fare_cents`. A bound option returns to `AVAILABLE` and releases that fence when the associated event's hold lapses, Becoming `TAKEN` — its fare billed at the raw option fare — happens when the event settles into the binding the option was made for. A release before that point carries an optional `release_reason` and returns a bound option to `AVAILABLE`.

### 3.8. Pricing Cancellation and Change Protection

Cancellation and change protection is priced against the itinerary's predicted disruption, and the whole quote is built from a handful of inputs in a fixed order. A `Traveler` or `OpsAgent` quotes a product by naming the itinerary, a `product_type` of either `CANCELLATION` or `CHANGE`, the client's own `cancellation_disruption_probability`, optionally the `freeze_id` of a covering freeze, and the route's current `disclosure_version` with an acknowledgment at `disclosure_acknowledged`. The disclosure gate described in §2.7 is enforced on this operation, and a stale version or a missing acknowledgment is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`.

The probability the product is priced on is not simply the client's declaration. First the declaration is clamped into its permitted band: the value used is the declared probability raised to no less than 0.01 and lowered to no more than 0.95. Call this the clamped declaration. Then the question of coverage decides which figure becomes the product's `disruption_probability`. When the itinerary carries a covering freeze — one whose status is `DRAFT`, `HELD`, `FROZEN` or `CLAIM_PENDING` — the effective probability is the freeze's hedged share rate from §3.4, the fraction of the freeze's exposure that is hedged. When no covering freeze exists, the effective probability is the clamped declaration. This is the cap-transfer mechanism: buying protection while a freeze is running means buying the same disposition the company's own hedge has, so a member whose hedge ratio is high pays materially less on the same itinerary and the same declaration.

A single threshold then unlocks protected cancellation. The product's `minimum_disruption_met` flag is true when its effective disruption probability is at least the route's `cancel_minimum_disruption`, and only a product carrying that flag may exercise a protected cancellation later. The route's `cancel_minimum_disruption` defaults to 0.30, so a product priced against a hedged share rate or a declaration below that threshold is quoted but cannot be used to cancel.

Bonus days are the other entitlement, and they belong to the standalone buyer alone. A product that is a member of a freeze carrying the guarantee gets a `bonus_days_total` of 0 — the guarantee, not bonus days, is what that buyer bought. A standalone product gets 0, 1 or 2 days from the effective disruption band of the clamped declaration: a band below 0.05 yields 0 days, a band of at least 0.05 and below 0.10 yields 1 day, and a band of at least 0.10 yields 2 days.

The money follows. The disruption fare against which the product is priced is the itinerary's `each_day_disruption_fare_cents`. The probable loss is the larger of two products — the effective disruption probability multiplied by that disruption fare, and the route's `protection_floor_rate` multiplied by the same fare — rounded half-up to two decimal places. The floor rate, which defaults to 0.20, is what stops a low-probability quote from being priced down to nothing, and because it operates inside the probable loss rather than at the top, a member with a low hedged share rate still pays strictly less than a standalone buyer. The premium is then that probable loss multiplied by a rate selected by product type: the route's `protection_base_rate`, which defaults to 0.25, for a cancellation product, and 0.35 for a change product, with the result rounded half-up to two decimal places. Change products also carry their own derived probability: `change_disruption_probability` is half of the effective disruption probability, floored at 0.05. When the computed change premium falls below the product's own minimum, the minimum is substituted and the substitution is recorded by setting `change_minimum_applied` true. That minimum is not a fresh declaration: it is the change product's own floor-priced premium — the route's `protection_floor_rate` multiplied by the disruption fare and by the change rate, rounded half-up to two decimal places — so a change product priced at or above its floor never has the minimum applied.

A product's pricing status and its lifecycle status run independently, and neither substitutes for the other. A product starts in `QUOTED_FROZEN` when the itinerary carries a covering freeze, and in `QUOTED_FROZEN_ON_UNPUBLISHED_CURVE` when the route has no published curve at all — the latter being the state in which the probable loss and premium are both 0 and the premium is not payable. Once a covering freeze reaches `FROZEN` or `CLAIM_PENDING` while the itinerary is still `PLANNED` or `WATCHING`, the pricing status moves to `QUOTED_ACTIVE` — though, like every other movement of this dimension, that promotion is carried out by the clock-advance sweep's re-derivation rather than by the freeze's own activation, so a product quoted before its freeze was driven to `FROZEN` reads `QUOTED_ACTIVE` on the next sweep — the effective probability is re-derived from the freeze's hedged share rate, the probable loss and premium are recomputed, and `suspended_under_cap` turns true when that hedged share rate is below 0.50. A covering freeze that resolves without a claim sends the pricing status back to `QUOTED` with the effective probability reverting to the clamped declaration and `bonus_days_total` set from the effective disruption band. A product sitting in `QUOTED` whose itinerary later acquires a `DRAFT`, `HELD` or `FROZEN` freeze returns to `QUOTED_FROZEN`, which is the cyclic return arc of the pricing dimension. And a product that was quoted on an unpublished curve is re-quoted once the route has a curve, which raises its probable loss and premium retroactively from the curve price and moves it to `QUOTED`.

Activation is what makes the premium real. A `Traveler` or `OpsAgent` activates a `QUOTED` product by presenting `expected_protection_version`. Activation requires the product's pricing status to be `QUOTED` or `QUOTED_ACTIVE` — a product quoted on an unpublished curve cannot be activated and the attempt is refused with `ERR_PREMIUM_NOT_PAYABLE` — and requires the route to be `ACTIVE` or `SURGE`, with a `RESTRICTED` or `CLOSED` route refused as `ERR_ROUTE_RESTRICTED`. Success moves the lifecycle status to `ACTIVE` and makes the premium due against a payment of kind `PREMIUM`, and re-derives `suspended_under_cap` from the covering freeze's hedged share rate.

Exercising a protected cancellation unwinds the whole trip and is gated tightly. The product must be `ACTIVE`, must be of type `CANCELLATION` — otherwise `ERR_PRODUCT_TYPE_MISMATCH` — must have `minimum_disruption_met` true, or the attempt fails with `ERR_DISRUPTION_BELOW_MINIMUM`, must sit on an itinerary that is still `PLANNED`, and must not have already exercised a cancellation, or the attempt fails with `ERR_CANCELLATION_ALREADY_EXERCISED`; the durable record of that condition is the product's own `cancelled_by_member` flag, so a replay after a protected cancellation reports the already-exercised error rather than a generic state refusal. The request carries an acknowledgment at `acknowledge_minimum_disruption`. Success moves the product to `CANCELLED_PROTECTED` with `cancelled_by_member` true, consumes days under the redemption rule below, credits the product's custody with its next scheduled release, cancels the itinerary, and cancels every `DRAFT` or `HELD` freeze on that itinerary with `deposit_forfeited_cents` of 0 — a protected cancellation is deliberately not a bare cancellation and must not forfeit a deposit. An `OpsAgent` may reverse the whole thing: reinstatement moves the product from `CANCELLED_PROTECTED` back to `ACTIVE`, clears `cancelled_by_member`, restores `redeemed_value_cents` and `bonus_days_remaining` to their pre-cancellation values, returns the itinerary to `PLANNED`, and reverts the cancelled freezes to `DRAFT`.

Redemption is the mechanism that converts bonus days into value, and it has two mutually incomparable units. The duration metric counts days: a shift of zero days or fewer counts as 0, one day counts as 1, two as 2, three as 3, and any shift of more than three days counts as three plus the number of extra days divided by two and rounded up to a whole day. The metric is linear after the first three days rather than decaying, which makes a long shift expensive in whole-day terms. A protected cancellation consumes by a different metric entirely: it consumes the number of flights-at-fare the premium represents, computed as the premium divided by the itinerary's `each_day_disruption_fare_cents` and rounded up to a whole number of days, capped at the product's `bonus_days_total`. A rebooking consumes the duration metric applied to the change event's `day_shift`. The two are never composed — one counts calendar distance and the other counts premium units — and a product's consumption is the sum of its single cancellation consumption, if it has one, plus the duration metric of each of its settled change events. Settled events are processed in ascending creation order, with the change event's identifier used only as a last-resort tie-break. Consumption is recorded in `redeemed_value_cents`, and the change-specific consumption is also tracked in `duration_consumed_days`. `bonus_days_remaining` is the product's total days minus what has been consumed. The redemption price is the itinerary's `each_day_disruption_fare_cents` frozen at the moment of redemption, and no rounding is applied to it.

Coverage does not run forever. A product's `coverage_expires_at` is its itinerary's departure date, and a product still `ACTIVE` when the clock passes that instant expires — but only if the itinerary is still `PLANNED` or `WATCHING`. An itinerary that has already been `BOOKED` is not expired; it is walked for termination instead. That soft expiry is not itself driven by the scheduled sweep: the clock carries no product to `EXPIRED`, and an `ACTIVE` product whose coverage instant has passed stays `ACTIVE` and visible to the entitlement walks until it is terminated hard. An expiry is a soft terminator: the lifecycle status becomes `EXPIRED`, `terminated_hard` stays false, and the product keeps its `bonus_days_total` and remains visible to the entitlement walks. A hard terminator is the opposite. A product is terminated hard when its itinerary has been cancelled, or when the itinerary reached travel completion with zero bonus days remaining; an operations agent may also terminate a product explicitly, supplying a `termination_reason`. Termination sets the lifecycle status to `TERMINATED` with `terminated_hard` true and removes the product from every bonus-day walk entirely, because its remaining existence is provably worth nothing. A product that is still `QUOTED` when its route closes is terminated as a cascade without ever charging its premium.

### 3.9. Hedging, Marks and Regional Absorption

The hedging desk's job is to back the guarantees the company has written, and its instruments are coverage positions and regional pools. A `RiskAnalyst` opens a `HedgingPosition` on a route and coverage date by supplying the `coverage_date`, the `hedge_price_cents` the book is locking in, and a `contract_quantity_cents` notional, together with the `expected_route_version` describing the route read the analyst is acting on. The position starts `OPEN`, with its `pool_id` bound to the route's regional pool. Opening a position requires the route to be `ACTIVE` or `SURGE` — a `RESTRICTED` route is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_RESTRICTED`. The region will not hold unlimited coverage: the route may carry only one position per coverage date and per locked fare, and a second position on the same combination of route, coverage date and locked fare is refused with `ERR_HEDGE_DUPLICATE_COVERAGE`. A region's cap of four already-hedged coverage dates is tracked as `pool_binding_count`, and a position that would fill the cap is refused once the region's `bound_position_count` has already reached four, which simultaneously drives the region's pool into its `BOUND` terminal state.

Marking a position is how its value is assessed. A `RiskAnalyst` remarks an `OPEN` position by supplying a `projected_day_cost_cents`; marking an `OPEN` position requires the route to be `ACTIVE` or `SURGE`, with a `RESTRICTED` or `CLOSED` route refused as `ERR_ROUTE_RESTRICTED`, exactly as opening one does. The position becomes `HEDGED`, its `projected_day_cost_cents` records the marked figure, and its `mark_value_cents` becomes the amount by which the projected cost exceeds the locked hedge price, floored at zero. A mark that finds the projected cost has fallen below the locked price is a regression, and a regression can reverse the position rather than merely devalue it: when the projected cost is below the hedge price and the route's region contains at least one route in `SURGE`, the position returns from `HEDGED` to `OPEN` with its `mark_value_cents` reset to 0 and its `regression_reversed` flag set true. That reversal is allowed only while the region's absorbed regression stands at zero — once the regional pool has absorbed a regression, the arc is blocked.

Settling a hedged position releases its absorption into the route's war chest. The position must be `HEDGED`, and the release is conditional on the route's posture: when the route is in `SURGE` the `absorption_released_cents` equals the position's `mark_value_cents`, and when it is not, the release is 0. Whatever is released is added to the route's `war_chest_cents` and the route's `available_exposure_cents` is re-derived. Positions also lapse on their own: an `OPEN` position still open when the clock passes its `coverage_date` becomes `LAPSED`, and a lapsed position is revived — returning to `OPEN` and again counting toward `pool_binding_count` — on the clock-advance sweep once the route's resolved pool, the summed mark value of its own live positions, has fallen below 250000; the revival is tested strictly below that threshold, so a resolved pool of exactly 250000 leaves the position lapsed.

The regional pool sits above the positions and governs both absorption and the region's postural behaviour. A `RiskAnalyst` opens a pool by naming its `region` and a `pool_code`; at most one pool may exist per region, and a new pool starts `OPEN` with an absorbed regression of zero. Running the regression sweep against a pool, which takes the `pool_id`, is what moves the pool into `ABSORBING`: when at least one position in the region has a projected cost below its hedge price, the pool's `absorbed_regression_cents` is increased by the pool's registered insurance slot — a fixed 140 cents for each route in the region, derived from a region-cap fraction of 0.004 — and the route's `war_chest_cents` is credited with 140 cents multiplied by the regressed position's `mark_value_cents`, divided by 100, and rounded half-up to two decimal places. The sweep also triggers the route's postural re-derivation described in §2.11, which re-derives pooled exposure and re-evaluates the region for both `SURGE` and `RESTRICTED`. While the absorbed regression stands above zero, every `HEDGED` to `OPEN` reversal arc in the region is blocked, which is what keeps a regressed mark from quietly undoing an absorption the pool has already booked. The pool releases when every route in its region has left both `SURGE` and `RESTRICTED`: it returns to `OPEN`, its absorbed regression resets to zero, the region's regressions may be reversed again, and `bound_position_count` is recomputed across the region's routes.

### 3.10. Booking, Cancellation, Route Closure and the Payment Ledger

The itinerary is the traveler's anchor resource and the thing every other object reaches through. A `Traveler` or an `OpsAgent` creates one by naming the `route_id` it flies, the `departure_date`, and the `traveler_user_id` who owns it — the identifier the acting traveler must later present in the header to act on it. Creation stamps the `booking_date` from the current clock instant and leaves the itinerary `PLANNED` with its fare fields empty: `listed_fare_cents`, `last_observed_fare_cents`, `observed_fares_cents`, `demand_curve_price_cents`, `each_day_disruption_fare_cents` and `fare_tier_ceiling_cents` all start at zero or empty. If the route already has a published curve, the curve-derived fields are populated immediately rather than waiting for the first observation.

Booking closes the itinerary at the current listed fare. It requires the itinerary to be `PLANNED` — an itinerary in `WATCHING` must first be confirmed through its watch or allowed to lapse, and the attempt is otherwise refused with `ERR_ITINERARY_NOT_PLANNED` — and requires the route to have at least one seat available, or the attempt fails with `ERR_NO_SEATS_LEFT`. Success sets `travel_status` to the terminal value `BOOKED`, records `listed_fare_cents` as the route's `base_fare_cents` at the booking instant, and decrements the route's `seats_left` by 1. Two cascades ride along. Any watch on the itinerary that is `ARMED`, `NOTIFIED` or `WAIT` is carried to `CONFIRMED` with `watch_armed` cleared — as is a watch sitting in `BUY_NOW`, which likewise becomes `CONFIRMED` on a confirmation. And every freeze on the itinerary that is `FROZEN` converts under the rules of §3.6, which releases its reserved exposure; a freeze that is still `DRAFT` or `HELD` at the booking instant is left where it is and is not carried along by the booking. Booking accepts an optional `expected_itinerary_version`; the conversion operation carries the mandatory version field described in §2.12 and is separately guarded by the seat check and, on the itinerary, by the requirement that it be `PLANNED` or `WATCHING`.

Cancelling an itinerary is the blunt instrument, available from `PLANNED` or from `WATCHING`, and may be requested by a traveler or by the operations desk with an optional `cancel_reason`. The itinerary becomes `CANCELLED`, which is terminal. Any watch on it is expired with `watch_armed` cleared, and every freeze on it in `DRAFT`, `HELD` or `FROZEN` is cancelled under the forfeiture rule of §3.6 — the same twice-the-deposit overrun rule as a direct freeze cancellation, and the credited custody portion is released. The cancelled freezes' reserved exposure is released as part of the same cascade. A direct cancellation of a `DRAFT` freeze, which collected nothing, forfeits nothing; the overrun only bites on a freeze in `HELD` or `FROZEN`.

Route posture is the operator's lever over an entire market, and it is described in §2.11 as a state model; the workflow of moving it is worth spelling out. A `PlatformAdmin` requests a transition by supplying `to_posture`, a `posture_reason_code`, and the `expected_route_version` the operator is acting on. The reachable operator transitions are `ACTIVE → SURGE`, `ACTIVE → RESTRICTED`, `SURGE → RESTRICTED`, `SURGE → ACTIVE`, and any of `ACTIVE`, `SURGE` or `RESTRICTED` into the terminal `CLOSED`; the derived `SURGE → RESTRICTED` move is not reachable through this operation and is driven by the sweep instead. Moving to `SURGE` re-derives the whole region and drives any route whose regional pool has an absorbed regression above zero into `RESTRICTED`. Recovering from `SURGE` back to `ACTIVE` is only permitted once the region's pooled exposure recomputes to zero — that is, once no route in the region holds a freeze in `HELD`, `FROZEN` or `CLAIM_PENDING`. A route leaves `RESTRICTED` for `ACTIVE` once its regional pool's absorbed regression returns to zero. The derived `SURGE → RESTRICTED` move records the reason code `REGIONAL_POOL_BREACH` on the affected route. The content of a reason code is the implementation's own vocabulary rather than a fixed enumeration: a system-driven `RESTRICTED` driven by the region's pool carries that pool-breach cause, and a posture driven to `CLOSED` by the closure cascade records a closure cause of its own. A route that recovers out of a `RESTRICTED` posture has its reason code cleared. A posture request that names the posture the route already holds is treated as a no-op rather than a self-transition error, and leaves the route, its reason code and its version untouched; a route that does leave `RESTRICTED` on a re-derivation also has its reason code cleared and its version advanced a second time by that clearing.

What `RESTRICTED` does to the book is the point of the posture. Every freeze on the route in `DRAFT` or `HELD` is frozen in place: its `deposit_attributed_cents` and `custody_held_cents` receive no further increments and its `membership_days_elapsed` stops advancing, so attribution halts without unwinding what has already accrued. Every freeze in `FROZEN` or `CLAIM_PENDING` continues to be serviced, and `EXPIRED`, `CLAIMED` and `CONVERTED` all remain reachable for them. The exposure engine recomputes `available_exposure_cents` for every freeze on the route, and a negative result is retained and reported rather than clamped. Recovering to `ACTIVE` is what lets a frozen `DRAFT` or `HELD` freeze resume its attribution. Closing a route requires that no freeze on it sits in `FROZEN`, `CLAIM_PENDING` or `CLAIMED`; the closure is terminal, and it terminates every protection product on the route's itineraries that is in `QUOTED` or `ACTIVE`, returning each open hold through the custodian release path.

Payments are recorded by an operations agent and are the ledger of record for every dollar moving through the system. A payment names an itinerary and optionally a `freeze_id`, a `protection_id` or a `claim_id`, carries a `payment_kind` drawn from `DEPOSIT`, `MEMBERSHIP_1`, `MEMBERSHIP_2`, `PREMIUM`, `CHANGE_FEE` or `HEDGE_TOPUP`, an `amount_cents` that is strictly positive and at most 20000, and a `capture` flag. A payment starts `AUTHORIZED` unless `capture` is true, in which case it is captured immediately, and an `AUTHORIZED` payment is the only state from which a capture may be taken. Capturing a payment allocates its amount to the target its kind names: `DEPOSIT` and `MEMBERSHIP_1` credit the freeze's custody, `MEMBERSHIP_2` credits the route's `war_chest_cents`, `PREMIUM` credits the protection's premium, `CHANGE_FEE` credits the change event's fee, and `HEDGE_TOPUP` is held for the next marks and credited nowhere at capture time. Each allocation is appended to the payment's `allocations` log — an ordered audit trail of a target, an `amount_cents` and an `applied_at` — and debits the payment's `refundable_remaining_cents`. Capture is only permitted from `AUTHORIZED` and only for the six declared kinds.

Refunding a payment, which an operations agent or a claims adjuster may do, requires a `refund_cents` of at least 1 and no more than the payment's `refundable_remaining_cents`; a zero or negative amount is refused as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and an amount that would overrun the balance is refused as `ERR_PAYMENT_OVER_ALLOCATED`. A refund equal to the remaining balance drives the payment to `REFUNDED`, while one that leaves a positive remainder leaves it `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`; either way the balance is debited and a refund allocation is appended to the log. Two terminal sinks close a payment's life. `FORFEITED` is reached through the membership overrun rule of §3.6, where the membership payment's balance is written to zero. `RETIRED` is reached in three ways: a deposit payment whose freeze reached `CONVERTED` is retired with the reason `DEPOSIT_CONVERTED`; any settlement of a payment whose target has already terminated is retired with the reason `CLAIM_SETTLED`; and an authorized payment that the clock leaves uncaptured for more than seven days is retired with the reason `AUTHORIZATION_AGED`. Once a payment has reached either sink it can be neither allocated nor refunded, and the reason is recorded in `retired_reason`.
