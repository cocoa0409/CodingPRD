# Resort Spa Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A destination resort spa sells minutes of therapist time inside a fixed inventory of treatment rooms, and every one of those minutes is simultaneously a clinical act, a licensed act and a revenue event. The operational pain the spa lives with today is that these three concerns are tracked in unrelated systems: the front desk books a hydrotherapy suite without knowing that the assigned therapist's state licence lapses the day before the appointment, a guest declares a high-risk medical condition on a paper questionnaire that never reaches the therapist, and the gratuity envelope collected at the desk is reconciled against the treatment revenue days later, if at all. The Resort Spa Operations API replaces all three with one authoritative record of the treatment menu, the therapist roster and its credentials, the guest's clinical intake, the appointment itself, and the money that flows out of it.

This document specifies that API. The system governs treatment menu design (duration, room-class requirement, skill and scope-of-practice mapping, contraindication registry, consent version), scheduling optimisation across therapist certification, shift availability and room turnover, guest intake with health questionnaire screening and consent documentation, therapist licensing verification with continuing-education compliance, the commission, service-charge and gratuity distribution that follows a delivered treatment, the retail attachment programme, the cancellation and no-show charge policy on high-demand slots, and the utilisation, revenue-per-room-hour and labour-cost reporting the spa director runs against all of it.

Two structural properties shape almost every rule below. First, the system is **temporally driven**: expiries, continuing-education windows, booking lead time, no-show grace and cancellation bands are all evaluated against a server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that operations are reproducible. Second, the system is **compliance-gated with segregation of duties**: a clinical or licensing decision recorded by one operator cannot be reviewed or overridden by that same operator. All collections are mounted at the root of the URL space — `/treatment-types`, `/treatment-rooms`, `/therapists`, `/therapist-licenses`, `/certifications`, `/guests`, `/intake-forms`, `/appointments`, `/retail-attachments`, `/reports` and `/compliance-sweeps` — and are linked to one another by explicit identifier fields carried in request payloads rather than by deep URL nesting.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold for every endpoint in the system: authentication, response shape, pagination, the logical clock, common resource properties, monetary precision, the validation precedence ladder, path resolution, optimistic concurrency, cascade discipline and the two running ledgers.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs. Emptiness is literal and nothing is trimmed first: only the empty string counts as empty, so a header carrying nothing but whitespace is a present, non-empty value that clears this check and is then matched as-is against the role list. Five roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `SpaDirector`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `SpaCoordinator`, `Therapist` and `Guest`. A recognised role that is not listed as authorised for the requested operation — and likewise an unrecognised role string — is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The roles divide the work as follows. A `SpaDirector` is spa leadership: it owns the treatment menu, the room inventory, the therapist roster, clinical overrides, final settlements and the revenue and utilisation reports. A `ComplianceOfficer` is the licensing, continuing-education and clinical-screening authority: it submits and reviews licences (never the same licence), records continuing-education courses, grants and revokes skill certifications, and screens intake forms. A `SpaCoordinator` runs front-desk operations: booking, confirming, checking in, cancelling and no-showing appointments, publishing shift blocks, registering guests, and resolving retail recommendations on a guest's behalf. A `Therapist` is a licensed service provider who starts and completes only their own appointments and recommends retail products on them. A `Guest` is a resort guest who files and amends their own intake forms, signs consent, books and cancels their own appointments, and resolves retail recommendations on their own appointments.

Identity binding differs by role and is not negotiable. When `X-User-Role` is `Therapist`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Therapist` entity; ownership checks on any resource that references a therapist compare that resource's therapist reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Therapist` caller may act on an appointment only when `X-User-ID` equals that appointment's `therapist_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Guest`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Guest` entity; a `Guest` caller may act only on resources that hang off that guest — their own intake forms, their own appointments, the retail attachments and folio postings of those appointments, and their own guest record. When `X-User-Role` is `SpaDirector`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `SpaCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition says so — as `submitted_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` on a licence, `recorded_by_user_id` on a continuing-education record, `revoked_by_user_id` on a certification grant, `screened_by_user_id` and `overridden_by_user_id` on an intake form, and `resolved_by_user_id` on a retail attachment — but it is never compared against any entity's `id`. Where two such recorded identifiers are compared with each other, that comparison is a segregation-of-duties rule, not an ownership rule.

An ownership violation by a `Guest` or `Therapist` caller is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Two segregation-of-duties rules run on top of role authorisation. The `ComplianceOfficer` who submitted or last resubmitted a licence may not be the officer who reviews it, compared against `submitted_by_user_id`; and the `SpaDirector` who overrides a flagged intake form may not be the user recorded as `screened_by_user_id` on that form. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A` through `Z` and the digits `0` through `9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits. It reads the logical clock of §2.4 and never the host machine's wall clock, so every response returned while the clock stands still carries the same `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx and 5xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Transport-level failures use this same envelope but carry the HTTP status of the transport failure rather than the status the catalogue of §5 lists for the `error_id`. A request to a path no endpoint serves answers `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 404; a request to a path that exists only under a different HTTP method answers `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 405; and a request the framework itself rejects as unintelligible before any handler runs answers `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 422. A trailing slash is insignificant in routing, so `/guests` and `/guests/` address the same endpoint. An unexpected server fault answers `error_id` `ERR_INTERNAL` with HTTP 500; that identifier is deliberately absent from the catalogue of §5 because no documented rule produces it.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` skips that many records before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range of 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a shape violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422), and so is a value that is not an integer at all, such as `?limit=abc` or `?limit=10.5`. A query parameter supplied with an empty value — `?limit=` or `?offset=` — is treated exactly as an omitted parameter and takes its default; this holds for every optional query parameter in the system, not just these two, so an empty filter value applies no filter. An `offset` at or beyond `total_count` is not an error: `data` is simply the empty array, with `total_count` still reporting the full match count.

Unless a specific list endpoint states otherwise, records are returned sorted by their creation timestamp `created_at` in ascending order. Because the logical clock stands still between the requests that drive it, many records legitimately share one `created_at`, so that ordering is completed by a tiebreaker rather than left ambiguous: records bearing the same `created_at` are returned in ascending order of creation — the order in which the server minted them — and a list documented as newest first is the exact reverse of that same total order, so its ties come out in descending order of creation. The same `created_at`-then-creation-order tiebreaker settles equal `posted_at` values on a folio listing and equal `start_at` values wherever a cascade orders appointments. Three list endpoints deviate and say so explicitly in their own narrative: a guest's intake forms are returned newest first, a guest's folio postings are returned in ascending `posted_at` order, and an appointment's settlement rows are returned with the `PROVISIONAL` row first and the `FINAL` row second.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the envelope's `meta` object, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the request's filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` states whether any matching record remains beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads the host machine's wall clock. The server holds a single logical UTC instant, and every expiry comparison, continuing-education window, booking lead-time check, no-show grace period, cancellation band and report boundary is evaluated against it. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it never falls back to real time.

An administrative test-driving capability moves the clock forward. It requires a single field, `current_utc`, carrying the new instant — omitting it, or sending it as `null`, is a shape violation rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — returns an object carrying exactly that one key, `{ current_utc }`, spelled with the uniform three-fractional-digit instant format of §2.5 whatever spelling the client submitted, with HTTP 200, and mutates no entity — subsequent requests simply evaluate every temporal rule against the new instant. `SpaDirector`, `ComplianceOfficer` and `SpaCoordinator` callers may all drive it. The clock never rewinds: a requested instant earlier than the current logical instant is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND` (HTTP 422), while an instant equal to the current one is accepted.

Where a rule compares a date-only value such as `expires_on` against "the current date", the comparison uses the UTC calendar date of the logical clock. Where a rule compares an instant, it uses the logical instant itself.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and Immutable Records

Every persisted resource exposes an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp, which is an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T09:15:00.000Z`). That spelling is uniform across the whole API: every instant the server emits anywhere in a response — `created_at`, `updated_at`, `posted_at`, `verified_at`, `started_at`, `completed_at`, `swept_at`, `consent_signed_at`, `start_at`, `end_at` and the envelope's own `api_request_timestamp` — carries exactly three fractional digits and the trailing `Z`, whatever the client's own submitted spelling was, and every date-only field the server emits is spelled `YYYY-MM-DD`.

Most resources additionally expose `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant refreshed on every mutation of that resource, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource. This applies to treatment types, treatment rooms, therapists, therapist licences, certification grants, shift blocks, guests, intake forms, appointments, retail attachments and settlement records. A mutation includes a change to a computed or derived field: when the room minute ledger is adjusted, when a guest's folio balance is recomputed, or when an appointment's realised price is written, the affected resource's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments even though no client sent a payload naming that resource.

Two record types are write-once audit rows and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from them. The first is the continuing-education record, which is immutable after it is filed and has no update capability at all. The second is the folio posting: postings are never edited or deleted, and a correction is expressed as an additional posting whose amount may be negative.

Every datetime the client submits — `start_at`, `end_at`, `current_utc`, `from_at`, `to_at`, whether it arrives in the request body or as a query parameter — must be a valid ISO 8601 UTC instant, and one single spelling of an instant is accepted: a four-digit year, two-digit month and two-digit day, the literal `T` separator, two-digit hours, minutes and whole seconds, then optionally a `.` followed by one to nine fractional digits, and finally the literal `Z` UTC designator (for example `2026-08-01T09:15:00.000Z`, and `2026-08-01T09:15:00Z` equally). Surrounding whitespace is trimmed before the value is matched, and any other spelling is malformed rather than interpreted: a space in place of the `T`, an omitted seconds component, an absent trailing `Z`, and a numeric UTC offset such as `+02:00` or `+00:00` are all rejected outright, and an offset-bearing value is never converted to UTC. Every date-only field the client submits — `issued_on`, `expires_on`, `current_period_start_on`, `granted_on`, `completed_on`, `as_of` — must be a valid `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date, again with surrounding whitespace trimmed before matching and with no other spelling accepted — a date carrying a time component, a two-digit year or slash separators is malformed, and so is a syntactically well-formed but non-existent date such as `2026-02-30`. A malformed value is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in cents; no money field is ever a decimal or a string. Wherever a rate is applied, the rate is expressed in **basis points** — one basis point is one ten-thousandth — so applying a rate means multiplying the base amount by the basis-point figure and dividing the product by ten thousand. A rate of 1,800 basis points is therefore eighteen percent, and a rate of 10,000 basis points is the whole amount.

Rounding is always **half up**, and it happens exactly at the named steps of each calculation and nowhere else. No intermediate monetary value is ever carried at sub-cent precision into a later step: when a step says the result is rounded to a whole number of cents, the following step consumes the rounded integer. Where a calculation rounds several sibling buckets and then derives a final bucket as the remainder, that ordering is load-bearing and is spelled out where the calculation is described.

Two report figures are percentages rather than money. Both `utilization_pct` and `attach_rate_pct` are decimals carrying exactly two decimal places, rounded half up, and a zero denominator yields exactly `0.00` rather than an error. Continuing-education hours are the one non-money quantity carried with fractional precision: hours are decimals with at most one decimal place, rounded half up to one place, and hour totals are never converted to integers. A submitted hours value with more than one decimal place is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

### 2.7. Validation Precedence: The Thirty-Four-Rung Ladder

Requests are validated against one global, strictly ordered ladder. The server walks the ladder from the top and returns the error of the **first** rung the request violates; lower rungs are not evaluated once a rung has failed. This ordering is a contract, not an implementation detail: clinical safety outranks licensing, licensing outranks resource availability, and fiscal capacity is checked last. The rungs, in order, are:

1. **Authentication** — both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty. Applies to every endpoint. Violation: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorisation** — `X-User-Role` is one of the five roles and that role is authorised for the endpoint. Applies to every endpoint. Violation: `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
3. **Request shape** — required fields present, enum values legal, integers within their declared ranges, datetimes valid ISO 8601 UTC instants, dates valid calendar dates, `limit` within 1 through 100 and `offset` not negative. Types are checked strictly rather than coerced: a string field must be a JSON string and, unless a wider range is stated for it, at least one character long, so `""` fails; an integer field must be a JSON integer, so a boolean, a string of digits and any number written with a decimal point — `30.5` and `30.0` alike — all fail; an enum field must be a JSON string listed among that field's values; and a code-list field such as `condition_codes`, `medications` or `contraindication_codes` must be a JSON array every element of which is a string. A field sent explicitly as `null` counts as absent — a required field sent as `null` fails this rung exactly as an omitted one does, and an optional field sent as `null` silently takes its documented default. A request body that is absent altogether, or that is not parseable JSON, is read as the empty object and therefore fails only on whichever required fields it lacks; a body that parses to something other than a JSON object, such as an array or a bare string, is itself a shape violation. Applies to every endpoint. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422).
4. **Path existence** — every identifier in the URL path resolves, checked strictly left to right, so the guest, therapist, licence or appointment segment is resolved before any segment nested to its right. Applies to every endpoint carrying a path parameter. Violation is the 404 matching the first unresolved segment: `ERR_GUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_THERAPIST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INTAKE_FORM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RETAIL_ATTACHMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND`.
5. **Body reference existence** — every identifier supplied in a request body resolves, checked in the body-field order `guest_id`, then `treatment_type_id`, then `room_id`, then `therapist_id`. Applies to appointment booking. Violation: `ERR_GUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TREATMENT_TYPE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_THERAPIST_NOT_FOUND` (404).
6. **Code uniqueness** — a treatment type's `code` is unique across all treatment types and a room's `code` is unique across all rooms. Applies to treatment-type registration and room registration. Violation: `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (409).
7. **Resource ownership** — a `Guest` caller's `X-User-ID` equals the guest the resource hangs off, and a `Therapist` caller's `X-User-ID` equals the identifier of the therapist the resource hangs off: the therapist named in the path on a therapist self-read or a shift-block listing, and the appointment's `therapist_id` on an appointment-scoped endpoint. A caller in any other role is never subject to this rung. Applies to every endpoint whose authorised-role list includes `Guest` or `Therapist`. Violation: `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
8. **Optimistic concurrency** — the submitted expected-version field equals the parent resource's current `version`. Applies to intake-form creation and final-settlement creation. Violation: `ERR_STALE_GUEST_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_APPOINTMENT_VERSION` (412).
9. **State legality** — the target resource currently sits in a state from which the requested transition departs. This rung also rejects repeating a trigger that has already been applied and acting on a resource in a terminal state. Applies to every action endpoint. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_APPOINTMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_INTAKE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_LICENSE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_THERAPIST_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ATTACHMENT_STATE` or `ERR_SETTLEMENT_ALREADY_FINAL` (409).
10. **Clearance state** — the appointment's `clearance_status` is `CLEARED` for check-in and for service start, and is `BLOCKED` for the checked-in abort path of cancellation. Applies to those three operations. Violation: `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_CLEARED` (409).
11. **Segregation of duties** — the licence reviewer differs from `submitted_by_user_id`, and the intake overrider differs from `screened_by_user_id`. Applies to licence review and intake override. Violation: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409).
12. **Intake cleared** — the guest has an authoritative intake form whose `status` is `SCREENED_CLEAR` or `OVERRIDDEN`. Applies to appointment booking and appointment confirmation. Violation: `ERR_INTAKE_NOT_CLEARED` (409).
13. **Consent on file** — that same form's `consent_signed_at` is set and its `consent_version` equals the treatment type's `required_consent_version`. Applies to appointment booking and appointment confirmation. Violation: `ERR_CONSENT_MISSING` (409).
14. **Contraindication screening** — the guest's `screened_condition_codes` shares no code with the treatment type's `contraindication_codes`. Applies to appointment booking and appointment confirmation. Violation: `ERR_CONTRAINDICATION_PRESENT` (409).
15. **Licence verified** — the therapist holds a licence whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` and whose `state_code` equals the therapist's `practice_state_code`. Applies to appointment booking and appointment confirmation. Violation: `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_VERIFIED` (409).
16. **Licence unexpired** — that licence's `expires_on` falls on or after the current logical-clock date. Booking and confirmation check *current* validity only; a licence that lapses between booking and service is caught later by the compliance sweep, which blocks the appointment's clearance dimension rather than retroactively invalidating the booking. Applies to appointment booking and appointment confirmation. Violation: `ERR_LICENSE_EXPIRED` (409).
17. **Continuing-education shortfall** — the calendar-window continuing-education evaluation reports a shortfall of zero hours for the licence under review. Applies to a licence review carrying the `VERIFIED` decision. Violation: `ERR_CE_HOURS_INSUFFICIENT` (409).
18. **Ethics minimum** — when the licence's `ce_hours_required` is greater than zero, that same evaluation's credited ethics hours are at least the ethics minimum. Applies to a licence review carrying the `VERIFIED` decision. Violation: `ERR_CE_ETHICS_REQUIREMENT_UNMET` (409).
19. **Scope of practice** — the therapist holds an `ACTIVE` certification grant for the treatment type's `required_skill_code` whose `expires_on` falls on or after the current logical-clock date and whose scope rank is at least the treatment type's required scope rank. Applies to appointment booking and appointment confirmation. Violation: `ERR_SCOPE_OF_PRACTICE_VIOLATION` (409).
20. **Therapist active** — the therapist's `status` is `ACTIVE`. Applies to appointment booking, appointment confirmation and service start. Violation: `ERR_THERAPIST_NOT_ACTIVE` (409).
21. **Room class** — the room's `room_class` equals the treatment type's `room_class_required` and the room's `capacity_guests` is at least 1. Applies to appointment booking. Violation: `ERR_ROOM_CLASS_MISMATCH` (409).
22. **Room available** — the room's `status` is `ACTIVE`. Applies to appointment booking and appointment confirmation. Violation: `ERR_ROOM_UNAVAILABLE` (409).
23. **Shift containment** — an `AVAILABLE` shift block of the assigned therapist contains the appointment's interval with both bounds inclusive. Applies to appointment booking. Violation: `ERR_OUTSIDE_SHIFT_WINDOW` (409).
24. **Therapist conflict** — the assigned therapist has no `BREAK` or `ADMIN` shift block and no non-terminal appointment whose closed interval intersects the appointment's closed interval. Applies to appointment booking. Violation: `ERR_THERAPIST_DOUBLE_BOOKED` (409).
25. **Room turnover** — the room's occupancy interval does not overlap the occupancy interval of any other appointment in that room. Applies to appointment booking. Violation: `ERR_ROOM_TURNOVER_CONFLICT` (409).
26. **Booking lead time** — `start_at` is at least the minimum lead time after the logical clock. Applies to appointment booking. Violation: `ERR_BOOKING_LEAD_TIME` (409).
27. **Booking privilege** — the guest's `booking_privilege` is `OPEN`. Applies to appointment booking. Violation: `ERR_GUEST_BOOKING_RESTRICTED` (409).
28. **Retail product line** — the attachment's `product_line` equals the appointment's treatment type's `retail_product_line`. Applies to retail-attachment creation. Violation: `ERR_PRODUCT_LINE_MISMATCH` (409).
29. **Gratuity cap** — the submitted `gratuity_cents` does not exceed the gratuity cap applied to the appointment's `quoted_price_cents`. Applies to appointment completion. Violation: `ERR_GRATUITY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422).
30. **Folio capacity** — the guest's `folio_balance_cents` plus the amount about to post does not exceed the guest's `folio_limit_cents`. Applies to resolving a retail attachment as purchased and to appointment check-in. Violation: `ERR_FOLIO_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (402).
31. **Eligible therapist exists** — when `therapist_id` was omitted from a booking, the automatic-assignment candidate pool is not empty. Applies to appointment booking without an explicit therapist. Violation: `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_THERAPIST` (409).
32. **Report window** — a report's `from_at` is strictly earlier than its `to_at` and the window spans no more than the maximum reporting span. Both bounds are required, but neither their presence nor their spelling is judged on this rung: an omitted, empty or malformed `from_at` or `to_at` fails the shape rung with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` long before this rung is reached, so this rung only ever fires on a well-formed pair that is ordered wrongly or spans too far. A malformed continuing-education `as_of` value is not judged on this rung at all: like any other calendar-date field it is a rung-3 shape violation and is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, never as the report-window error. Applies to the room-utilisation report and the retail-attachment report. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_WINDOW` (422).
33. **Clock monotonicity** — the requested clock instant is greater than or equal to the current logical clock. Applies to the clock-setting capability. Violation: `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND` (422).
34. **Amendment budget** — the intake form's `amendment_round` is below the amendment cap. Applies to intake amendment. Violation: `ERR_AMENDMENT_LIMIT` (409).

Two consequences of this ordering deserve to be stated outright, because they are easy to get backwards. First, on a booking a clinical rejection always wins over a scheduling rejection: a guest with an unscreened intake form receives `ERR_INTAKE_NOT_CLEARED` even when the requested slot is also outside the therapist's shift window and the lead time is also too short. Second, `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_THERAPIST` sits at the very bottom of the booking ladder, so a booking with no explicit therapist that also violates the lead-time rung fails with `ERR_BOOKING_LEAD_TIME`, not with the empty-pool error.

### 2.8. Path Resolution and Reverse Reachability

Identifiers in a URL path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the 404 returned. A request naming a licence beneath a therapist that does not exist therefore fails with `ERR_THERAPIST_NOT_FOUND` and never reaches the licence lookup, and a request naming a continuing-education collection beneath a non-existent licence fails with `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_FOUND`. Left-to-right resolution precedes every business rule, including state checks and ownership checks.

Because the entity graph is flat and linked by identifier fields rather than by nesting, each such link is navigable in reverse through a dedicated list capability: a therapist's licences, a therapist's certification grants, a therapist's shift blocks, a licence's continuing-education records, a guest's intake forms, a guest's folio postings, an appointment's retail attachments, an appointment's settlement rows and an appointment's folio postings are each reachable as a filtered, paginated list. The appointment list itself is the reverse channel for an appointment's `guest_id`, `treatment_type_id`, `room_id` and `therapist_id`, each of which is available there as an optional filter alongside `schedule_status`, `clearance_status`, `from_at` and `to_at`.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Exactly two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency, and on both of them the version field is a required input rather than an optional one. The guard is always on the **parent** resource's `version`, never on the created resource's own version. Endpoints not named here accept no expected-version field at all.

Creating an intake form for a guest requires `expected_guest_version` carrying the `version` of that `Guest` as the client last observed it. If the guest's current `version` differs, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_GUEST_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The rule exists because a newly screened form legitimately supersedes the guest's previous authoritative form and rewrites both `authoritative_intake_form_id` and `screened_condition_codes` on the guest; a writer holding a stale view of the guest would otherwise silently discard a screening decision it never saw.

Cutting the final settlement for an appointment requires `expected_appointment_version` carrying the `version` of that `Appointment` as the client last observed it. If the appointment's current `version` differs, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_APPOINTMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The final settlement supersedes the provisional row and writes the appointment's `realized_price_cents`, so a stale writer would distribute a pool computed from duration, gratuity or retail figures that have since changed.

### 2.10. The Clearance Dimension and Cascade Discipline

An appointment carries two independent state dimensions on the same row. `schedule_status` tracks where the booking sits in the front-desk workflow; `clearance_status` tracks whether the appointment is currently clinically and operationally cleared to proceed. They advance independently: an appointment may legitimately sit at `CONFIRMED` with `BLOCKED` clearance, at `CONFIRMED` with `CLEARED` clearance, or at `CHECKED_IN` with `BLOCKED` clearance, and it is the **pair** that decides which transitions are legal.

The clearance predicate has exactly four clauses, evaluated in a fixed order. The first clause is the clinical one: the guest's authoritative intake form exists and its `status` is `SCREENED_CLEAR` or `OVERRIDDEN`. The second is licensing: the assigned therapist holds a licence whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED`, whose `state_code` equals the therapist's `practice_state_code`, and whose `expires_on` falls on or after the current logical-clock date. The third is roster status: the therapist's `status` is `ACTIVE`. The fourth is the room: the room's `status` is `ACTIVE`. When all four clauses hold, clearance is `CLEARED`. When one or more fail, clearance is `BLOCKED` and `clearance_block_reason` is set from the **first** failing clause in that order — `INTAKE_FLAGGED`, then `LICENSE_INVALID`, then `THERAPIST_SUSPENDED`, then `ROOM_UNAVAILABLE`. `clearance_block_reason` is null whenever `clearance_status` is `PENDING` or `CLEARED`. If a later event repairs only some of the failing clauses, the appointment stays `BLOCKED` and `clearance_block_reason` is rewritten to the new first failing clause.

Only appointments whose `schedule_status` is `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `CHECKED_IN` participate in clearance re-evaluation; terminal appointments are never re-blocked or re-cleared. Four kinds of event drive the clearance dimension to `BLOCKED`: an intake screening that flags the guest, a therapist suspension, a room moved out of `ACTIVE`, and a compliance sweep that expires a licence. Four kinds of event can drive it back to `CLEARED`: a director override of the flagged form, a fresh screening that comes back clear, a therapist reinstatement, and a room moved back to `ACTIVE` — in each case only when all four clauses hold again.

Cascades in this system are deterministic in both their reach and their order. Whenever a cascade re-evaluates a set of appointments, that set is processed in ascending `start_at` order, ties broken by ascending `created_at`; whenever a cascade cancels a guest's other appointments, that set is processed in ascending `created_at` order. Every appointment driven to `BLOCKED` also expires that appointment's retail recommendations: each retail attachment still in `RECOMMENDED` becomes `EXPIRED`. This is one-directional — a retail attachment that has already reached `EXPIRED` is **never** revived when the appointment is later re-cleared. A cascade that re-evaluates an appointment already sitting at `BLOCKED` rewrites its `clearance_block_reason` to the new first failing clause but does not treat it as a fresh block: it is not reported among the newly blocked appointment identifiers a cascading operation returns, and its retail recommendations are not expired a second time, so a recommendation created while the appointment was already blocked survives that later cascade. A re-clearing cascade is symmetrically narrow: it considers only appointments whose `clearance_status` is `BLOCKED`, so one still sitting at `PENDING` is left exactly where it is. Each entity a cascade actually changes is mutated in its own right, so each one's `updated_at` is refreshed and each one's `version` increments by 1; an appointment a cascade re-evaluates without changing either its `clearance_status` or its `clearance_block_reason` is not mutated at all, and neither its `updated_at` nor its `version` moves.

### 2.11. The Guest Folio

Every guest carries a folio: an append-only ledger of postings against the guest's resort room. A posting records the `guest_id` whose folio it lands on, the `appointment_id` it originates from (never null — every posting in this system originates from an appointment), a `kind` discriminator, a signed `amount_cents`, a human-readable `memo` and the logical instant `posted_at` at which it was recorded. Six posting kinds exist: `TREATMENT`, `RETAIL`, `SETTLEMENT_ADJUSTMENT`, `REVERSAL`, `LATE_CANCEL` and `NO_SHOW`. Postings are immutable; `SETTLEMENT_ADJUSTMENT` and `REVERSAL` rows may carry negative amounts, and that is the only mechanism by which the folio moves downward.

The guest's `folio_balance_cents` is not an independently maintained counter but a derived total: after every posting it is recomputed as the exact sum of `amount_cents` over every posting whose `guest_id` is that guest. Because recomputation mutates the guest row, each posting also refreshes the guest's `updated_at` and increments the guest's `version` by 1 — which is precisely why intake-form creation is guarded by the guest's version.

Exposure is capped. A guest's `folio_limit_cents` is the maximum folio exposure permitted, and any operation about to post a positive amount first checks that the guest's current `folio_balance_cents` plus that amount does not exceed the limit — the comparison is strict, so a projected total exactly equal to `folio_limit_cents` is permitted and only a projected total strictly greater than it fails; when it would, the operation is rejected with `ERR_FOLIO_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402) and no posting is written. Two operations are subject to this check: resolving a retail attachment as purchased, and checking a guest in.

### 2.12. Room and Therapist Minute Ledgers

Two running counters give the spa a live picture of committed capacity, and both are maintained as strict double-entry-style increments and decrements rather than being recomputed from scratch.

A room's `booked_minutes_total` is the running total of committed occupancy minutes for that room, where one appointment's committed occupancy is its scheduled duration plus that room's `turnover_buffer_minutes`. Booking an appointment adds those minutes to the room; any transition that releases the slot — an ordinary cancellation, a late cancellation, a no-show, the checked-in abort, a sweep-driven automatic cancellation or a restriction-driven cancellation — subtracts exactly the same quantity. A therapist's `assigned_minutes_total` is the running total of scheduled treatment minutes committed to that therapist, excluding turnover: booking adds the appointment's scheduled duration and every releasing transition subtracts it. Completed appointments are never released, so the minutes of a `COMPLETED` appointment remain in both ledgers permanently.

Each adjustment is a mutation of the room or therapist row in its own right, refreshing that row's `updated_at` and incrementing its `version` by 1. Both counters start at 0 when the room or therapist is registered.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Designing the Treatment Menu and the Room Inventory

Everything the spa sells begins as a menu item authored by a `SpaDirector`. Registering one requires a `code` that is unique across the whole menu and is between 1 and 24 characters of uppercase alphanumerics plus the hyphen character, a guest-facing `name` of 1 to 200 characters, and a `duration_minutes` between 15 and 240 that is an exact multiple of 15 — the spa books in quarter-hour grains and will not accept a 50-minute service. The item declares the class of room it must be delivered in through `room_class_required`, which is one of `DRY`, `WET`, `HYDRO` or `COUPLES`, and the credential it demands through `required_skill_code` (1 to 32 uppercase characters) together with `required_scope_level`, which is either `BASIC` or `ADVANCED`. Scope levels are ranked, with `BASIC` ranked 1 and `ADVANCED` ranked 2, and a therapist satisfies the requirement when their granted rank is at least the required rank — so an `ADVANCED` grant satisfies a `BASIC` requirement but never the reverse. A second registration reusing an existing `code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.

The commercial and clinical attributes travel on the same record. `base_price_cents` is the list price before any room-class, surcharge or loyalty adjustment and must be at least 1. `contraindication_codes` is an array of uppercase condition codes that bar the treatment outright; it is optional on registration and defaults to an empty array, and its entries are stored exactly as submitted — the only rule enforced on them is that each is a string, so no case, length or registry-membership check applies and a lowercase or unrecognised code is accepted verbatim (it simply never matches a screened code). `retail_product_line`, of 1 to 200 characters, names the product line every retail recommendation made on a booking of this treatment must belong to. `required_consent_version`, of 1 to 64 characters, names the consent document version the guest must have signed, written in the style `2026.1`. `high_demand` is a boolean that defaults to false; marking an item as high-demand feeds both the price surcharge and the late-cancellation uplift described later.

Rooms are registered by a `SpaDirector` too, with a `code` unique across all rooms and between 1 and 24 characters — unlike a menu code it carries no character-class restriction, so lowercase letters and punctuation are accepted, and the uniqueness comparison is exact string equality — a physical `room_class` drawn from the same four values, a `capacity_guests` of 1 or 2, a `turnover_buffer_minutes` between 0 and 60 that represents the sanitation and reset time appended to every appointment's occupancy in that room, and an `hourly_fixed_cost_cents` of at least 0 that the margin calculations charge against delivered minutes. A room's `status` may be supplied at registration and otherwise defaults to `ACTIVE`; the legal values are `ACTIVE`, `MAINTENANCE` and `OUT_OF_SERVICE`. A freshly registered room starts with `booked_minutes_total` at 0, and reading a room back exposes that ledger so the operator can reconcile committed occupancy against the schedule.

Taking a room out of service is a director-only status change, and it is one of the four events that move appointments on the clearance dimension. The patch payload carries `status` and nothing else, and `status` is required on it: every other room attribute — `code`, `room_class`, `capacity_guests`, `turnover_buffer_minutes` and `hourly_fixed_cost_cents` — is immutable once the room is registered, which is precisely what makes the minute-ledger arithmetic of §2.12 exact, since a slot is always released with the same buffer figure it was committed with. There is no state gate on the change: a patch naming the status the room already holds is accepted rather than refused, the room is mutated in its own right so its `updated_at` and `version` move, and the cascade belonging to the resulting status runs again — re-patching an already-inactive room to another inactive value runs the block cascade a second time, and re-patching an `ACTIVE` room to `ACTIVE` runs the re-clearing cascade. When a room's `status` moves to any value other than `ACTIVE`, every appointment in that room whose `schedule_status` is `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `CHECKED_IN` has its `clearance_status` driven to `BLOCKED`; the reason recorded is `ROOM_UNAVAILABLE`, subject to the fixed reason precedence, so an appointment that is simultaneously failing the intake clause records `INTAKE_FLAGGED` instead. Each blocked appointment's `RECOMMENDED` retail attachments become `EXPIRED` in the same operation. When the room's `status` returns to `ACTIVE`, those appointments are re-evaluated against all four clearance clauses and become `CLEARED` only when every clause holds; the retail attachments already expired stay expired.

### 3.2. The Therapist Roster: Registration, Suspension and Availability

A `SpaDirector` registers a therapist with a `full_name` of 1 to 200 characters, an `employment_type` of either `EMPLOYEE` or `CONTRACTOR` for payroll classification, a `commission_tier` of `JUNIOR`, `SENIOR` or `MASTER` that decides their treatment commission rate, a `base_hourly_cost_cents` of at least 0 that drives their hourly labour cost, and a `practice_state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters naming the state they practise in. A new therapist begins with `status` `ACTIVE`, `suspension_reason` null and `assigned_minutes_total` at 0. The lifecycle values for `status` are `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` and `INACTIVE`, and `suspension_reason` is null while the therapist is `ACTIVE` and otherwise one of `LICENSE_EXPIRED` or `DIRECTOR_ACTION`.

Suspension is a director action carrying two optional fields: a `permanent` flag defaulting to false and a `reason_note` of at most 500 characters. The note is validated for shape and then discarded — the therapist row carries no free-text note field, so a suspension records only the coded `suspension_reason`. With `permanent` absent or false, an `ACTIVE` therapist moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`; with `permanent` true, a therapist in either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `INACTIVE`. In both cases `suspension_reason` is written as `DIRECTOR_ACTION`, and in both cases the same cascade runs: every appointment of that therapist whose `schedule_status` is `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `CHECKED_IN` has its `clearance_status` driven to `BLOCKED` with `clearance_block_reason` `THERAPIST_SUSPENDED`, and each such block expires that appointment's `RECOMMENDED` retail attachments. `INACTIVE` is the only terminal roster state — no transition leads out of it, and an attempt to suspend a therapist who is not `ACTIVE` (other than the permanent path), to reinstate one who is not `SUSPENDED`, or to move a therapist out of `INACTIVE` at all is refused with `ERR_INVALID_THERAPIST_STATE`.

Reinstatement runs the other way, `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, and it is credential-gated: the therapist must hold at least one licence whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` and whose `expires_on` falls on or after the current logical-clock date. A therapist with no verified licence at all is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_VERIFIED`, and a therapist whose only verified licence has already lapsed is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_EXPIRED`. A successful reinstatement clears `suspension_reason` back to null and re-evaluates every `BLOCKED` appointment of that therapist whose `schedule_status` is `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `CHECKED_IN`, promoting each to `CLEARED` when all four clearance clauses hold and leaving it `BLOCKED` with a rewritten reason when they do not.

Availability is published as shift blocks by a `SpaCoordinator` or a `SpaDirector`. A block carries a `block_type` of `AVAILABLE`, `BREAK` or `ADMIN`, an inclusive `start_at`, an inclusive `end_at` that must be strictly after `start_at`, and an optional scheduling `note` of at most 500 characters that defaults to null; a block whose `end_at` is not strictly after its `start_at` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and because that is a shape rule it is judged before the therapist in the path is resolved, so a badly ordered interval aimed at a therapist who does not exist yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_THERAPIST_NOT_FOUND`. Blocks are never validated against one another: overlapping, duplicated or contradictory blocks of any type may be published freely, and nothing is refused as a double-published block. `AVAILABLE` blocks are the only windows an appointment may sit inside, while `BREAK` and `ADMIN` blocks are exclusion windows that make the therapist unbookable for any interval they touch. Publishing a block has no retroactive effect whatsoever: existing appointments are never re-validated against a newly published block, so a `BREAK` block laid over an already-booked slot does not disturb that booking. A therapist's blocks are listable with an optional `block_type` filter and an optional `from_at` and `to_at` window; both temporal bounds are compared against the block's `start_at` alone and both are inclusive, so `from_at` keeps blocks starting at or after it and `to_at` keeps blocks starting at or before it — a long block that begins before `from_at` and runs deep into the window is excluded, because no interval-overlap test is applied. A `Therapist` caller may list only their own blocks, and the same holds for the therapist self-read.

### 3.3. Licensing Verification, Continuing Education and Scope of Practice

A therapist may not deliver a treatment on the strength of the roster alone; the credential chain is what the state regulator audits. A `ComplianceOfficer` files a licence against a therapist with a `license_number` of 1 to 64 characters, a `license_type` of `LMT`, `ESTHETICIAN` or `NAIL_TECH`, a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters, an `issued_on` date, an `expires_on` date and a `current_period_start_on` date that marks the first day of the licence's current continuing-education period. An optional `ce_hours_required` states how many continuing-education hours this renewal period demands; it defaults to 12, must be at least 0 and carries at most one decimal place. Three consistency conditions are enforced on filing, each rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and they fire in this order: `expires_on` must be strictly after `issued_on`, then `current_period_start_on` must be on or before `expires_on`, and then — since it can only be judged once the therapist in the path has been resolved, so it sits behind the path-existence rung rather than in front of it — the `state_code` must equal the therapist's `practice_state_code`. Nothing else is cross-checked: `current_period_start_on` need not fall on or after `issued_on`, and neither date is compared against the logical clock, so a licence may be filed already expired. The filed licence lands in `verification_status` `PENDING_REVIEW` with `credited_ce_hours` at 0, `reviewed_by_user_id`, `review_note` and `verified_at` all null, and `submitted_by_user_id` set to the filing officer's `X-User-ID` — which is exactly what bars that officer from reviewing it.

Review is the segregation-of-duties gate. A different `ComplianceOfficer` submits a `decision` together with an optional `review_note` of at most 1000 characters. Four spellings of `decision` are accepted and any other value is a shape violation: `VERIFY` and `VERIFIED` both mean verification, and `REJECT` and `REJECTED` both mean rejection, so the imperative and the past-participle spelling are interchangeable and the stored `verification_status` is always the past-participle form. Reviewing a licence that is not `PENDING_REVIEW` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_LICENSE_STATE`, and a reviewer whose `X-User-ID` equals `submitted_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A `REJECTED` decision moves the licence `PENDING_REVIEW → REJECTED`, records `reviewed_by_user_id` and the supplied `review_note` (null when omitted), leaves `verified_at` null and leaves `credited_ce_hours` untouched. A `VERIFIED` decision passes three further gates before it may proceed: the licence's `expires_on` must fall on or after the current logical-clock date, or the review is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_EXPIRED`; the calendar-window continuing-education evaluation must report a shortfall of exactly zero hours, or the review is refused with `ERR_CE_HOURS_INSUFFICIENT`; and when `ce_hours_required` is greater than zero, that evaluation's credited ethics hours must be at least the two-hour ethics minimum, or the review is refused with `ERR_CE_ETHICS_REQUIREMENT_UNMET`. A licence that clears all three moves `PENDING_REVIEW → VERIFIED`, stamps `verified_at` with the logical clock, records `reviewed_by_user_id` and `review_note`, and writes `credited_ce_hours` with the credited hours figure that the calendar-window evaluation produced at that moment.

The licence machine is cyclic and has no terminal state. `PENDING_REVIEW → REJECTED → PENDING_REVIEW` is the revision loop and `VERIFIED → EXPIRED → PENDING_REVIEW → VERIFIED` is the renewal loop; the hop into `EXPIRED` is driven only by the compliance sweep. Resubmission is what closes both loops: a `ComplianceOfficer` may return a licence that is either `REJECTED` or `EXPIRED` to `PENDING_REVIEW`, optionally replacing `license_number`, `expires_on`, `current_period_start_on` and `ce_hours_required` — each replaced only when supplied and otherwise left as it was. Resubmission writes `submitted_by_user_id` with the resubmitter's `X-User-ID`, which re-arms the segregation-of-duties check for the next review, and resets `reviewed_by_user_id`, `review_note` and `verified_at` all back to null. `credited_ce_hours` is not reset: it keeps whatever figure the last verification wrote, and only a fresh `VERIFIED` decision rewrites it. `issued_on`, `license_type` and `state_code` are not replaceable on a resubmission, and a resubmission never re-checks the state code or the date ordering of the fields it does replace. Resubmitting a licence that is neither `REJECTED` nor `EXPIRED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_LICENSE_STATE`. A therapist's licences are listable with an optional `verification_status` filter, and a single licence read exposes `verification_status`, `credited_ce_hours` and both segregation-of-duties user identifiers.

Continuing-education courses are filed against a specific licence by a `ComplianceOfficer` with a `course_code` of 1 to 64 characters, a `category` of `ETHICS`, `CLINICAL` or `SAFETY`, a `ce_hours` figure that must be greater than 0, no greater than 8 and carry at most one decimal place, and a `completed_on` date that decides which evaluation window the record falls into. The filing officer is recorded as `recorded_by_user_id`. These records are audit rows: they are immutable, there is no capability to update one, and the `license_id` they hang off is taken from the creation path rather than from the body. Filing carries no state gate and no date gate: a record may be filed against a licence in any `verification_status`, including one already `VERIFIED` or `EXPIRED`, and `completed_on` is never compared against the licence's own dates or against the logical clock — a record dated outside every window is stored faithfully and simply credits nothing. A licence's records are listable with an optional `category` filter.

The compliance evaluation itself is a computed read and can be requested at any time. It takes only the continuing-education records whose `license_id` is the licence in question — records belonging to a different licence of the same therapist are excluded outright — and keeps those whose `completed_on` falls inside the variant's window, reporting the window it used as `window_start_on` and `window_end_on`. For each of the three categories separately, the raw category total is the sum of `ce_hours` over the in-window records in that category, and the credited category figure is the smaller of that raw total and the per-category cap of 8 hours; a record outside the window contributes nothing to any total, including its own category's raw sum. `credited_hours` is the sum of the three credited category figures, and `ethics_hours_credited` is the credited figure for the `ETHICS` category alone. `shortfall_hours` is the licence's `ce_hours_required` less `credited_hours` when that difference is positive and exactly 0 otherwise. `ethics_requirement_met` is true whenever `ce_hours_required` is 0, and otherwise true only when `ethics_hours_credited` is at least the two-hour ethics minimum. `compliant` is true only when `shortfall_hours` is 0 and `ethics_requirement_met` is true. The response also echoes the `variant` used and the licence's `ce_hours_required`.

Two variants of that evaluation exist and they can disagree on the same set of records. `CALENDAR_WINDOW` is the default, and it is always the variant the review gate uses: its window runs from the licence's `current_period_start_on` to the licence's `expires_on` with **both** bounds inclusive, and it ignores the `as_of` input entirely. `ROLLING_24M` is requested explicitly through the `variant` query parameter: its window opens strictly after the date 24 months before `as_of` — that date is the same day of the month two calendar years earlier, clamped back to the last day of that month whenever the day does not exist there, as it does not for an `as_of` of 29 February, and the lower bound is exclusive — and closes on `as_of` inclusive, where `as_of` defaults to the logical clock's UTC date. Because the rolling window can exclude older courses that the calendar window credits, the same records can credit fewer hours under `ROLLING_24M`, drop a whole category below the ethics minimum, and flip `compliant` to false. This read never writes: `credited_ce_hours` on the licence is updated only by a `VERIFIED` review.

Skill certification is granted separately from licensing. A `ComplianceOfficer` grants a therapist a `skill_code` of 1 to 32 uppercase characters at a `scope_level` of `BASIC` or `ADVANCED`, with a `granted_on` date and an `expires_on` date that must be strictly after `granted_on`, or the grant is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — again a shape rule, judged before the therapist in the path is resolved. Neither date is compared against the logical clock, so a grant may be issued already expired, and nothing prevents two live grants for the same `skill_code` at different scope levels: when several match, the scope check reads the highest granted rank among them. A new grant starts with `status` `ACTIVE`; the legal values are `ACTIVE`, `LAPSED` and `REVOKED`, and `revoked_by_user_id` is null unless the grant has been revoked. Revocation is the one transition the API exposes: it sets `status` to `REVOKED`, records the revoking officer as `revoked_by_user_id`, accepts an optional `reason_note` of at most 500 characters that is validated and then discarded rather than stored, and immediately removes that therapist from the eligible pool for every treatment requiring that skill. Revocation carries no state gate of its own — there is no invalid-certification-state error in the catalogue — so revoking a grant that is already `REVOKED`, or one whose `expires_on` has long passed, is accepted rather than refused: it simply rewrites `status` and `revoked_by_user_id` and mutates the row, moving its `updated_at` and incrementing its `version` by 1. Expiry is not a status rewrite — a grant is treated as valid only while its `expires_on` falls on or after the current logical-clock date, so an unrevoked grant simply stops satisfying scope checks once that date has passed. A therapist's grants are listable with an optional `status` filter.

### 3.4. Guest Registration, Health Intake and Clinical Screening

A `SpaCoordinator` or a `SpaDirector` registers a resort guest with a `full_name` of 1 to 200 characters, the `resort_room_number` the folio posts against, of 1 to 32 characters and uniqueness-free so two guests may legitimately share a resort room, an optional `loyalty_tier` of `NONE`, `SILVER`, `GOLD` or `PLATINUM` that defaults to `NONE`, and a `folio_limit_cents` of at least 0. Registration initialises the whole derived block: `folio_balance_cents` at 0, `late_cancel_count` at 0, `no_show_strikes` at 0, `booking_privilege` at `OPEN`, `authoritative_intake_form_id` at null and `screened_condition_codes` at an empty array. Reading a guest back exposes all of them together with the guest's `version`, which is the concurrency token intake-form creation must quote.

Clinical intake is a small cyclic workflow with a hard budget on how many times it may loop. A `Guest` or a `SpaCoordinator` opens a form against a guest, quoting `expected_guest_version`, which is required and must be a non-negative integer — a negative or non-integer value is a shape violation rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the version is ever compared — and optionally supplying `condition_codes` — the health-questionnaire condition codes the guest declares, defaulting to an empty array — and `medications`, a declared medication list that is recorded for the therapist's benefit but never screened and defaulting to an empty array too. The new form lands in `status` `DRAFT` with `flagged_condition_codes` an empty array, `amendment_round` at 0 and `consent_version`, `consent_signed_at`, `screened_by_user_id`, `screened_at`, `overridden_by_user_id` and `override_reason` all null, and opening it increments the guest's `version` by 1. Nothing caps how many forms a guest may hold: opening a second form leaves any existing `DRAFT` exactly where it is, and only screening ever supersedes an earlier form. From `DRAFT`, submitting the form advances it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. Consent is orthogonal to that flow but not to the state machine: recording a signature is legal from `DRAFT` and from `SUBMITTED` and from no other state, so a signature on a form that has already been screened, overridden or superseded is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTAKE_STATE` and consent must therefore be captured before screening (or after an amendment has returned the form to `DRAFT`). Signing writes the supplied `consent_version`, of 1 to 64 characters, onto the form and stamps `consent_signed_at` with the logical clock; both fields are null until a signature is recorded, and a second signature on a form still in `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` is accepted and simply overwrites both. Acting on a form from a state that has no such transition — submitting a form that is not `DRAFT`, or touching a form that has already reached `SUPERSEDED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTAKE_STATE`.

Screening is a `ComplianceOfficer` act on a `SUBMITTED` form, and it is where the clinical registry bites. The registry of high-risk conditions is a closed literal set of five codes — `ACUTE_HYPERTENSION`, `PREGNANCY_FIRST_TRIMESTER`, `DEEP_VEIN_THROMBOSIS`, `RECENT_SURGERY_90D` and `ACTIVE_INFECTION` — and it is not configurable. Comparison is exact string equality, so a declared code that is not one of those five is recorded faithfully and never flags. When the form's `condition_codes` share no code with the registry the form moves `SUBMITTED → SCREENED_CLEAR` and `flagged_condition_codes` is written as an empty array; when they share at least one code the form moves `SUBMITTED → SCREENED_FLAGGED` and `flagged_condition_codes` is written as exactly that shared set, listed in the order those codes appear in the form's own `condition_codes` and with any duplicate collapsed to a single entry. Either outcome records `screened_by_user_id` with the screening officer's `X-User-ID` and stamps `screened_at` with the logical clock, and the screening officer thereby becomes the segregation-of-duties counterparty for any later override.

Screening is also what makes a form authoritative. A guest's **authoritative intake form** is the guest's form with the greatest `created_at` among those whose `status` is `SCREENED_CLEAR`, `SCREENED_FLAGGED` or `OVERRIDDEN`, with ties broken in favour of the greater `version` and any remaining tie broken in favour of the form the server minted later, and `authoritative_intake_form_id` on the guest is the computed pointer to that form's `id` — null when the guest has no screened form at all. It is a computed pointer rather than a foreign key, and the way to enumerate a guest's forms is the guest's own intake-form list, which returns them newest first and accepts an optional `status` filter. Both screening outcomes rewrite `authoritative_intake_form_id` to the freshly screened form and copy that form's full `condition_codes` — not merely the flagged subset — into the guest's `screened_condition_codes`, which is the input set every contraindication check reads. The guest's previous authoritative form, when there was one, transitions to `SUPERSEDED`, a terminal state with no way out. Each screening increments the guest's `version` by 1.

The two screening outcomes then diverge sharply in their cascades. A `SCREENED_FLAGGED` result runs the block cascade: because the guest's authoritative form is now flagged, every appointment of that guest whose `schedule_status` is `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `CHECKED_IN` has its `clearance_status` driven to `BLOCKED` with `clearance_block_reason` `INTAKE_FLAGGED`, processed in ascending `start_at` order with ties broken by ascending `created_at`, and because those appointments became blocked, every retail attachment on them still in `RECOMMENDED` becomes `EXPIRED`. A `SCREENED_CLEAR` result runs the reverse: every appointment of that guest in the same three schedule states whose `clearance_status` is `BLOCKED` is re-evaluated and becomes `CLEARED` when all four clearance clauses hold.

A flagged form has two exits. The first is a `SpaDirector` override, which moves the form `SCREENED_FLAGGED → OVERRIDDEN`, requires an `override_reason` of 1 to 1000 characters in the payload, records `overridden_by_user_id`, and is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the overriding user is the same user recorded as `screened_by_user_id`. An override rewrites the form alone: the guest row is not touched, so `authoritative_intake_form_id` continues to point at this very form — `OVERRIDDEN` is one of the three statuses that keep a form authoritative — `screened_condition_codes` keeps the value screening copied there, and the guest's `version` does not move. An override re-evaluates the guest's `BLOCKED` non-terminal appointments and promotes each to `CLEARED` when all four clauses hold — but retail attachments that already reached `EXPIRED` are not revived. The second exit is the guest amendment loop, available to a `Guest` or a `SpaCoordinator`, which returns the form `SCREENED_FLAGGED → DRAFT` with corrected answers: `condition_codes` and `medications` are replaced wholesale by the submitted values, each defaulting to an empty array when omitted, so leaving a list out of an amendment clears it rather than preserving the previous answers; `flagged_condition_codes` is reset to an empty array, and `screened_by_user_id` and `screened_at` are reset to null. The consent fields survive an amendment untouched — `consent_version` and `consent_signed_at` are not cleared — so a form already signed need not be signed again after amending. Each amendment increments `amendment_round` by 1, and the loop is hard-capped at two completed rounds: a third amendment attempt on a form whose `amendment_round` has already reached 2 is refused with `ERR_AMENDMENT_LIMIT`. Because an amended form is no longer screened, the guest's `authoritative_intake_form_id` is recomputed and becomes null when no other screened form exists, with `screened_condition_codes` following it, and the guest's `version` increments by 1. Appointments already `BLOCKED` for `INTAKE_FLAGGED` stay blocked through an amendment — there is no cleared authoritative form that could release them.

### 3.5. Booking a Treatment: The Scheduling Gate, Automatic Assignment and the Price Quote

A booking is created by a `SpaCoordinator` or by the `Guest` themselves, with a payload naming the `guest_id` receiving the treatment, the `treatment_type_id` being booked, the `room_id` the treatment will occupy, an optional `therapist_id`, and the inclusive `start_at` instant. A successful booking lands with `schedule_status` `REQUESTED` and `clearance_status` `PENDING` with `clearance_block_reason` null: the appointment exists but is neither confirmed nor clinically cleared. Several fields are derived at that moment and never recomputed afterwards. `scheduled_duration_minutes` is a snapshot of the treatment type's `duration_minutes` taken at creation, so a later edit to the menu cannot move an existing booking. `end_at` is `start_at` advanced by `scheduled_duration_minutes`. `high_demand_slot` is true when either the treatment type's `high_demand` flag is true or the UTC hour of `start_at` is at least 16 and less than 20 — the four-hour late-afternoon peak — and false otherwise. `assignment_mode` is `EXPLICIT` when `therapist_id` was supplied and `AUTO` when the server chose the therapist; either way `therapist_id` is never null on a successfully created appointment. `actual_duration_minutes`, `realized_price_cents`, `started_at` and `completed_at` are all present on the created row and all null until later stages write them, `gratuity_cents` starts at 0 and `cancellation_charge_cents` starts at 0.

Before any of that is written the booking must clear the clinical block, then the credential block, then the resource block, in that order. Clinically, the guest must have an authoritative intake form in `SCREENED_CLEAR` or `OVERRIDDEN` (`ERR_INTAKE_NOT_CLEARED`), that form must carry a consent signature whose `consent_version` matches the treatment type's `required_consent_version` (`ERR_CONSENT_MISSING`), and the guest's `screened_condition_codes` must share no code with the treatment type's `contraindication_codes` (`ERR_CONTRAINDICATION_PRESENT`). On credentials, the assigned therapist must hold a `VERIFIED` licence for their `practice_state_code` (`ERR_LICENSE_NOT_VERIFIED`) whose `expires_on` is on or after the current logical-clock date (`ERR_LICENSE_EXPIRED`), must hold an `ACTIVE`, unexpired certification grant for the treatment's `required_skill_code` at or above the required scope rank (`ERR_SCOPE_OF_PRACTICE_VIOLATION`), and must themselves be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_THERAPIST_NOT_ACTIVE`). Credential validity is judged against the clock at booking time, not against the appointment's `start_at`; a credential that lapses in between is caught later by the compliance sweep.

On resources, the room's `room_class` must equal the treatment's `room_class_required` and its `capacity_guests` must be at least 1 (`ERR_ROOM_CLASS_MISMATCH`), and the room's `status` must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_ROOM_UNAVAILABLE`). The therapist's calendar must contain the work: some `AVAILABLE` shift block of theirs must contain the appointment's interval with both bounds inclusive, meaning the block's `start_at` is at or before the appointment's `start_at` and the block's `end_at` is at or after the appointment's `end_at` (`ERR_OUTSIDE_SHIFT_WINDOW`). The therapist must also be free: no `BREAK` or `ADMIN` block of theirs and no other appointment of theirs in `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `IN_SERVICE` may intersect the appointment's interval, where intersection is judged with both intervals closed at both ends, so merely touching at an endpoint counts as a conflict (`ERR_THERAPIST_DOUBLE_BOOKED`). The room has its own, deliberately different geometry: a room's occupancy interval begins at the appointment's `start_at` and runs up to but not including its `end_at` advanced by that room's `turnover_buffer_minutes`, and the new occupancy may not overlap the occupancy interval of any appointment in that room that is non-terminal or `COMPLETED`. Because the upper bound is excluded, a booking that starts at exactly the instant the previous appointment's turnover ends is accepted (`ERR_ROOM_TURNOVER_CONFLICT` otherwise). Finally the slot must be far enough out — `start_at` must be at least two hours, that is 120 minutes, after the logical clock (`ERR_BOOKING_LEAD_TIME`) — and the guest's `booking_privilege` must be `OPEN` (`ERR_GUEST_BOOKING_RESTRICTED`).

When `therapist_id` is omitted, the server picks the therapist itself and records `assignment_mode` as `AUTO`. Every therapist row is a candidate before filtering, and the filter admits a therapist only when every one of these clauses holds: their `status` is `ACTIVE`; they hold a licence whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED`, whose `state_code` equals their own `practice_state_code` and whose `expires_on` is on or after the current logical-clock date; they hold a certification grant whose `skill_code` equals the treatment's `required_skill_code`, whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `expires_on` is on or after the current logical-clock date and whose scope rank is at least the treatment's `required_scope_level` rank; some `AVAILABLE` shift block of theirs contains the appointment's interval with both bounds inclusive; no `BREAK` or `ADMIN` block of theirs intersects that interval with both intervals closed; and no appointment of theirs in `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `IN_SERVICE` intersects it with both intervals closed. Note that licence and grant validity are again judged at the current clock date rather than at `start_at`.

Survivors are then ranked, and the ranking is applied in a fixed sequence of four keys. The first key is the therapist's committed minutes on the requested calendar date, taken in ascending order so that the least-loaded therapist wins: that figure is the sum of `scheduled_duration_minutes` over that therapist's appointments whose `schedule_status` is `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `IN_SERVICE` or `COMPLETED` and whose `start_at` falls on the same UTC calendar date as the requested `start_at` — a per-date figure that is deliberately not the same thing as the therapist's lifetime `assigned_minutes_total`. The second key is the scope rank of the matching certification grant, taken in descending order, so an `ADVANCED` holder outranks a `BASIC` holder at equal load; when a therapist holds more than one qualifying grant for the required skill, the rank that enters this key is the highest scope rank among them. The third key is `base_hourly_cost_cents` in ascending order, so the cheaper therapist wins at equal load and equal scope. The fourth and final tie-break is the therapist's `created_at` in ascending order, so the longest-tenured record wins — and because the still logical clock lets two therapists share one `created_at`, that key is itself completed by creation order, the therapist the server minted first winning. If the filter admits nobody at all, the booking is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_THERAPIST`. Because the eligibility filter already embeds the licensing, scope, roster-status, shift-containment and conflict clauses, a booking with no explicit therapist reaches that rejection only after the guest-side clinical checks, the room checks, the lead-time check and the booking-privilege check have all passed.

The price is quoted once, at creation, and frozen into `quoted_price_cents`; nothing later re-quotes it. The quote reads exactly three records — the booked treatment type, the booked room (its `room_class` is read even though that class must already equal `room_class_required`) and the guest (its `loyalty_tier` only) — and no other appointment participates. It proceeds in a fixed order, and each step consumes the rounded output of the steps above it. First, the room-adjusted amount is the treatment's `base_price_cents` multiplied by the room-class multiplier and divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to a whole cent; the multipliers are 10,000 basis points for a `DRY` room (no adjustment at all), 11,500 for `WET`, 13,000 for `HYDRO` and 12,500 for `COUPLES`. Second, a surcharge rate is assembled: a peak component of 1,500 basis points applies when the UTC hour of the submitted `start_at` is at least 16 and less than 20 (this is the UTC hour of the submitted instant and never a local-time conversion) and is otherwise zero, a high-demand component of 1,000 basis points applies when the treatment's `high_demand` is true and is otherwise zero, and the effective surcharge rate is the smaller of their sum and the surcharge cap of 2,000 basis points — so a high-demand treatment booked in the peak window is surcharged at 20 percent rather than 25. Third, the surcharge amount is the room-adjusted amount multiplied by that rate and divided by ten thousand, rounded half up, and the subtotal is the room-adjusted amount plus that surcharge.

The loyalty discount and the price floor close the quote. The raw discount is the subtotal multiplied by the loyalty rate and divided by ten thousand, rounded half up, where the loyalty rate is 0 basis points for `NONE`, 500 for `SILVER`, 1,000 for `GOLD` and 1,500 for `PLATINUM`; the applied discount is the smaller of that raw figure and the absolute discount cap of 4,000 cents, so no single treatment may be discounted by more than 40 units of currency however generous the tier. The price floor is the treatment's `base_price_cents` multiplied by 8,000 basis points and divided by ten thousand, rounded half up — eighty percent of list. `quoted_price_cents` is the larger of the discounted subtotal and that floor, which means a deep loyalty discount on a `DRY`-room treatment with no surcharge can be clipped by the floor while the same discount on a `HYDRO` booking is not. Booking an appointment finally adds `scheduled_duration_minutes` plus the room's `turnover_buffer_minutes` to that room's `booked_minutes_total` and adds `scheduled_duration_minutes` to the therapist's `assigned_minutes_total`, each increment bumping that row's own `version` by 1.

### 3.6. Service Delivery: Confirmation, Check-In, Start and Completion

Confirmation is a `SpaCoordinator` act and the only way an appointment leaves `REQUESTED` on the happy path, advancing `REQUESTED → CONFIRMED`. Confirming an appointment whose `schedule_status` is anything other than `REQUESTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPOINTMENT_STATE`. Confirmation deliberately re-runs the clinical and credential gates rather than trusting the booking-time verdict, because hours or days may have passed and the logical clock may have moved: the authoritative intake form must still be `SCREENED_CLEAR` or `OVERRIDDEN` (`ERR_INTAKE_NOT_CLEARED`), consent must still match `required_consent_version` (`ERR_CONSENT_MISSING`), the contraindication check must still pass (`ERR_CONTRAINDICATION_PRESENT`), the licence must still be `VERIFIED` for the practice state (`ERR_LICENSE_NOT_VERIFIED`) and unexpired at the clock date (`ERR_LICENSE_EXPIRED`), the certification must still satisfy the required skill and scope rank (`ERR_SCOPE_OF_PRACTICE_VIOLATION`), the therapist must still be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_THERAPIST_NOT_ACTIVE`) and the room must still be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_ROOM_UNAVAILABLE`). Confirmation is also the transition that advances the second dimension: `clearance_status` is written as `CLEARED` and `clearance_block_reason` as null whatever the dimension held beforehand. Normally that is `PENDING → CLEARED`, but a `REQUESTED` appointment left sitting at `BLOCKED` whose blocking clauses have since been repaired without a re-clearing cascade having visited it is driven to `CLEARED` all the same, which is safe precisely because every clearance clause is among the gates confirmation has just re-run. The room-class, shift-window, turnover, lead-time and booking-privilege rules are booking-time rules and are not re-evaluated at confirmation.

Check-in is where money first touches the guest's folio. A `SpaCoordinator` checks a guest in only from `CONFIRMED`, and only while `clearance_status` is `CLEARED`; a blocked appointment is refused with `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_CLEARED` even though its `schedule_status` is perfectly valid, which is the whole point of the parallel dimension. Check-in advances `CONFIRMED → CHECKED_IN` and then does two things in one operation. It cuts a settlement row using the provisional variant of the distribution calculation, with `variant` `PROVISIONAL` and `status` `PROVISIONAL`, filling every computed money field on that row; the appointment must not already have a settlement row of either variant for this to happen, and one that does is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPOINTMENT_STATE` on the state rung — that is, ahead of the clearance rung, so an already-settled appointment that has since lost its clearance reports the state error rather than `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_CLEARED`. It then posts a folio charge of `kind` `TREATMENT` whose `amount_cents` is that row's `folio_total_cents`, recomputes the guest's `folio_balance_cents` as the exact sum of the guest's postings and increments the guest's `version` by 1. Because a positive amount is about to post, the folio capacity rule applies: if the guest's current `folio_balance_cents` plus that `folio_total_cents` would exceed `folio_limit_cents`, the whole check-in is rejected with `ERR_FOLIO_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and neither the settlement row nor the posting is written. A successful check-in answers with the updated appointment, not with the settlement row it cut, and with HTTP 200 rather than 201 even though a settlement row came into existence.

Only the assigned therapist may begin the service. Starting advances `CHECKED_IN → IN_SERVICE` and requires three things beyond the schedule state: `clearance_status` must be `CLEARED` (`ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_CLEARED`), the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the appointment's `therapist_id` (`ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`), and the therapist's own `status` must still be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_THERAPIST_NOT_ACTIVE`). Starting is otherwise inert: beyond `schedule_status`, `started_at` — stamped with the logical clock — and the appointment's own `updated_at` and `version`, no field changes, no money moves and no other row is touched.

Completion advances `IN_SERVICE → COMPLETED` and is likewise restricted to the assigned therapist. The therapist reports `actual_duration_minutes`, an integer between 1 and 480 that records the minutes actually delivered and is null on every appointment that has not reached `COMPLETED`, and may pass a `gratuity_cents` figure of at least 0 that defaults to 0 when omitted. The gratuity is capped at 5,000 basis points of the frozen `quoted_price_cents` — half the quoted price, rounded half up — and a larger figure is rejected with `ERR_GRATUITY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Completion writes both submitted values onto the appointment, stamps `completed_at` with the logical clock, and stops there: **no settlement is cut at completion**. The final settlement is a separate, director-driven act, which is why `realized_price_cents` remains null on a freshly completed appointment.

### 3.7. The Retail Attachment Programme

Retail is a therapist-driven upsell recorded against the appointment it was recommended on. Only the assigned therapist may create a recommendation, and only while the appointment's `schedule_status` is `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `IN_SERVICE` or `COMPLETED`; a recommendation against an appointment in any other schedule state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ATTACHMENT_STATE`. The payload carries the `product_sku` being recommended and the `product_line` it belongs to, each of 1 to 64 characters, together with its `amount_cents`, which must be at least 1 and carries no upper bound. Because the attachment's own `product_line` is capped at 64 characters while a treatment type's `retail_product_line` may run to 200, a recommendation quoting a line longer than 64 characters is a rung-3 shape violation rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than with the product-line mismatch error. The `product_line` must equal the appointment's treatment type's `retail_product_line`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_LINE_MISMATCH` — a body-lotion recommendation cannot ride along on a treatment whose retail line is aromatherapy. A new recommendation lands in `status` `RECOMMENDED` with `resolved_by_user_id` and `resolved_at` both null, and it posts nothing to the folio; it is a tracked recommendation, not a sale.

The recommendation state machine branches from `RECOMMENDED` into three terminal outcomes with materially different effects. A `Guest` or a `SpaCoordinator` resolves one by submitting an `outcome`. With `outcome` `PURCHASED`, the attachment moves `RECOMMENDED → PURCHASED`, records the resolving user as `resolved_by_user_id` and stamps `resolved_at` with the logical clock, and posts a folio charge of `kind` `RETAIL` whose `amount_cents` is the attachment's `amount_cents`, after which the guest's `folio_balance_cents` is recomputed as the exact sum of the guest's postings and the guest's `version` increments by 1. That purchase is subject to the folio capacity rule: when the guest's current `folio_balance_cents` plus the attachment's `amount_cents` would exceed `folio_limit_cents`, the resolution is rejected with `ERR_FOLIO_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402) and nothing is written. A purchase also requires the parent appointment to be in one of the same four schedule states. With `outcome` `DECLINED`, the attachment moves `RECOMMENDED → DECLINED`, records `resolved_by_user_id` and `resolved_at` in exactly the same way, and posts nothing at all. Resolving an attachment that is not `RECOMMENDED` — including one that has already been purchased, declined or expired — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ATTACHMENT_STATE`.

The third branch is not a client action. When the parent appointment's `clearance_status` becomes `BLOCKED` for any reason, every attachment on that appointment still in `RECOMMENDED` moves `RECOMMENDED → EXPIRED` as a system act; `resolved_by_user_id` and `resolved_at` stay null precisely because no user resolved it, and no folio posting is made. Expiry is final: re-clearing the appointment does not revive an expired recommendation, and the guest must be re-offered the product as a new recommendation. An appointment's attachments are listable with an optional `status` filter, and only attachments in `PURCHASED` ever feed the settlement and reporting figures.

### 3.8. Cancellations, No-Shows and the Loss of Booking Privilege

Cancellation is available to a `SpaCoordinator` or to the `Guest` who owns the appointment, and it accepts an optional `cancel_reason` of at most 500 characters that is validated for shape and then discarded — the appointment row carries no cancellation-reason field, so nothing written there is persisted or echoed back. From `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED` the outcome is not chosen by the caller: the charge calculation decides it. That calculation reads exactly one appointment and the logical clock at the instant of the request, and it works from the exact number of hours between the clock and the appointment's `start_at`, a figure that may be negative when the request arrives after the slot has begun. When that figure is 24 hours or more — the boundary is inclusive — the appointment moves `REQUESTED → CANCELLED` or `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED`, `cancellation_charge_cents` is 0 and **no folio posting is made at all**. Otherwise the appointment moves to `LATE_CANCELLED` and a charge is assessed. The band rate is 5,000 basis points when the figure is 4 hours or more (again inclusive) and 10,000 basis points when it is under 4 hours, which includes every negative figure. When the appointment's `high_demand_slot` is true, an uplift of 2,000 basis points is added to whichever band was selected, and the resulting rate is then capped at 10,000 basis points — so a short-notice cancellation of a high-demand slot is charged the whole quote rather than 120 percent of it, while a late cancellation of a high-demand slot is charged 70 percent. The charge is the appointment's frozen `quoted_price_cents` multiplied by the final rate and divided by ten thousand, rounded half up; it is never a re-quote at cancellation time.

Both cancellation outcomes release capacity: the room's `booked_minutes_total` is reduced by `scheduled_duration_minutes` plus that room's `turnover_buffer_minutes`, and the therapist's `assigned_minutes_total` is reduced by `scheduled_duration_minutes`. On the free path the clearance dimension is left exactly as it stood — `clearance_status` and `clearance_block_reason` freeze at their current values rather than being reset — and nothing else happens. On the charged path a five-hop cascade runs. Because the charge exists, a folio posting of `kind` `LATE_CANCEL` is written for `cancellation_charge_cents`; because that posting exists, the guest's `folio_balance_cents` is recomputed as the exact sum of the guest's postings and the guest's `late_cancel_count` increases by 1, incrementing the guest's `version`. Because `late_cancel_count` has now reached the restriction threshold of 2, the guest's `booking_privilege` flips from `OPEN` to `RESTRICTED`. Because the guest became restricted, every **other** appointment of that guest whose `schedule_status` is `REQUESTED` is cancelled by the system, processed in ascending `created_at` order, each landing in `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_charge_cents` 0 and no folio posting of its own. And because those appointments were cancelled, each one's room `booked_minutes_total` and therapist `assigned_minutes_total` are decremented by that appointment's own committed minutes. A guest below the threshold simply keeps `booking_privilege` at `OPEN` and none of the last three hops run.

A no-show is the coordinator's tool for a guest who never arrives, and it is legal only from `CONFIRMED` and only once the grace period has run: the logical clock must be at or past the appointment's `start_at` plus 15 minutes, and an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPOINTMENT_STATE`. The charge uses the same calculation with the no-show band of 10,000 basis points, again taking the 2,000-basis-point high-demand uplift and again capped at 10,000 basis points, which makes a no-show cost the full quoted price whether or not the slot was in demand. The appointment moves `CONFIRMED → NO_SHOW`, the room and therapist minute ledgers are released exactly as on a cancellation, and the same shape of cascade runs with two differences: the posting carries `kind` `NO_SHOW`, and the counter that moves is `no_show_strikes`, whose restriction threshold is 1. A single no-show therefore restricts the guest immediately, cancelling every other `REQUESTED` appointment of theirs in ascending `created_at` order and releasing each one's minutes. Once `booking_privilege` is `RESTRICTED`, further booking attempts for that guest fail with `ERR_GUEST_BOOKING_RESTRICTED`.

One further cancellation path exists and it is a compensating abort rather than a policy charge. When an appointment has already reached `CHECKED_IN` but its `clearance_status` has since been driven to `BLOCKED` — the guest is checked in and the therapist has just been suspended, say — a `SpaCoordinator`, and only a `SpaCoordinator`, may cancel it; the `Guest` role is not permitted on this path and is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). That refusal can only be judged once the appointment has been read and found to be `CHECKED_IN`, so it lands after the state rung rather than at the role rung — a `Guest` cancelling their own `CONFIRMED` appointment is perfectly legal — and it precedes the clearance requirement, so a `Guest` aiming at a checked-in appointment receives the role error whether that appointment is `BLOCKED` or `CLEARED`. The abort requires `clearance_status` to be exactly `BLOCKED`, and a checked-in appointment that is still `CLEARED` is refused with `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_CLEARED`. It unwinds the check-in in full: the appointment's settlement row in `status` `PROVISIONAL` moves `PROVISIONAL → REVERSED` with its computed money fields frozen where they stood, and a compensating folio posting of `kind` `REVERSAL` is written whose `amount_cents` is the negative of that row's `folio_total_cents`, returning the guest's recomputed `folio_balance_cents` to its exact pre-check-in value. No penalty attaches to the guest for an abort the spa caused: `cancellation_charge_cents` stays 0 and `late_cancel_count` is **not** incremented. The room and therapist minute ledgers are released as on any other cancellation. The clearance dimension is left exactly as it stood here too, so an aborted appointment comes to rest at `schedule_status` `CANCELLED` with `clearance_status` still `BLOCKED` and its `clearance_block_reason` intact. Cancelling from any state other than `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `CHECKED_IN` — including a second cancellation of an already-terminal appointment — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPOINTMENT_STATE`.

### 3.9. Settlement: Service Charge, Gratuity Distribution, Commission and Margin

Each appointment can carry at most two settlement rows, and both are produced by one waterfall calculation run in one of two variants. Every row records the `appointment_id` it belongs to, the `variant` that produced it (`PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`) and a `status` drawn from `PROVISIONAL`, `SUPERSEDED`, `FINALIZED` and `REVERSED`. A row cut at check-in is created with `variant` `PROVISIONAL` and `status` `PROVISIONAL`; it later becomes `SUPERSEDED` when a final row is cut for the same appointment, or `REVERSED` when the checked-in appointment is aborted, and in both cases its computed money fields freeze at the values they held. A row cut at settlement time is created with `variant` `FINAL` and `status` `FINALIZED`. An appointment's rows are listable with an optional `variant` filter and are returned with the provisional row first.

Cutting the final row is a `SpaDirector` act on a `COMPLETED` appointment, and it is the second of the two concurrency-guarded writes: the payload carries `expected_appointment_version` and nothing else. Attempting it on an appointment that has not reached `COMPLETED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPOINTMENT_STATE`; attempting it when a row with `variant` `FINAL` already exists is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_ALREADY_FINAL`; a version that does not match the appointment's current `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_APPOINTMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412), and an `expected_appointment_version` that is not a non-negative integer at all is a rung-3 shape violation judged before any comparison is made. Those three refusals are catalogued here in narrative order rather than in precedence order: the ladder of §2.7 places the concurrency rung above the state rung, so a stale version aimed at an appointment that has also not reached `COMPLETED`, or that already carries a `FINAL` row, reports `ERR_STALE_APPOINTMENT_VERSION` and not the state error. A successful cut does four things beyond creating the row. It writes the row's `realized_price_cents` onto the appointment's own `realized_price_cents` and increments the appointment's `version` by 1. It transitions the appointment's provisional row `PROVISIONAL → SUPERSEDED`. It posts a folio charge of `kind` `SETTLEMENT_ADJUSTMENT` whose `amount_cents` is the final row's `folio_total_cents` less the sum of `amount_cents` over every folio posting already recorded against that appointment — a figure that is negative whenever the delivered service came in cheaper than the provisional estimate, and one that is written unconditionally, including when the difference works out to exactly 0, so every finally settled appointment carries exactly one `SETTLEMENT_ADJUSTMENT` posting whatever its amount. And it recomputes the guest's `folio_balance_cents` as the exact sum of the guest's postings, incrementing the guest's `version` by 1.

The calculation reads one appointment together with its guest, therapist, room and treatment type, plus that appointment's retail attachments in `status` `PURCHASED`; attachments in `RECOMMENDED`, `DECLINED` or `EXPIRED` are excluded from both variants, and no other appointment's rows ever participate. It runs in six steps whose order is load-bearing.

The first step fixes the basis and the treatment revenue, and it is the only step that differs between variants. Under the provisional variant, `basis_duration_minutes` is the appointment's `scheduled_duration_minutes` and `realized_price_cents` is exactly the frozen `quoted_price_cents` — there is no overtime charge, no shortfall credit, and `gratuity_cents` on the row is 0 because a gratuity is only captured at completion. Under the final variant, `basis_duration_minutes` is the reported `actual_duration_minutes`, and the realised revenue is adjusted in both directions. Overtime is granted a five-minute grace: take the delivered minutes less the scheduled minutes less 5, treat any non-positive result as zero, divide by 15 and round **up** to a whole number of blocks, then charge 2,500 cents per block. A shortfall has its own five-minute grace: when the delivered minutes are at least the scheduled minutes less 5, the shortfall credit is zero; otherwise the credit is the frozen `quoted_price_cents` multiplied by the missing minutes and divided by the scheduled minutes, rounded half up, where the missing minutes are the whole difference between the scheduled and the delivered minutes — the five-minute grace decides only whether a credit arises at all and is never deducted from the shortfall once it does. `realized_price_cents` is then the frozen `quoted_price_cents` plus the overtime charge less the shortfall credit.

The second step totals retail: `retail_revenue_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the appointment's `PURCHASED` attachments, and both variants apply that same filter — a purchase resolved between check-in and settlement therefore appears on the final row but not the provisional one. The third step selects the service-charge rate, and the basis for that selection is pinned deliberately: the tier basis is `realized_price_cents` plus `retail_revenue_cents`, evaluated **before** any service charge exists, never `folio_total_cents` (which already contains the service charge and would make the rule circular). When that tier basis is 50,000 cents or more, `service_charge_bp` is the reduced rate of 1,500 basis points; below that threshold it is the standard rate of 1,800 basis points. `service_charge_pool_cents` is then `realized_price_cents` — the treatment revenue alone, not the tier basis — multiplied by `service_charge_bp` and divided by ten thousand, rounded half up.

The fourth step distributes. `gratuity_cents` on the row is a snapshot of the appointment's `gratuity_cents` at evaluation time, and `distributable_pool_cents` is `service_charge_pool_cents` plus that gratuity; this pool is the quantity the three shares conserve exactly. The buckets are rounded in a declared order and the last one takes the residual: `therapist_share_cents` is the pool multiplied by 6,000 basis points and divided by ten thousand, rounded half up; `support_pool_cents` is the pool multiplied by 2,500 basis points and divided by ten thousand, rounded half up; and `house_share_cents` is whatever remains of the pool after both of those are removed. The house's nominal rate is 1,500 basis points, but the house share is **never** computed by applying that rate — it is always the remainder, so that the three buckets sum to `distributable_pool_cents` to the cent regardless of how the two roundings fell.

The fifth step pays commission. `therapist_commission_cents` is `realized_price_cents` multiplied by the therapist's tier rate and divided by ten thousand, rounded half up, where the rate is 3,000 basis points for a `JUNIOR` therapist, 3,800 for `SENIOR` and 4,500 for `MASTER`. `retail_commission_cents` is computed per line and then summed: for each `PURCHASED` attachment, multiply its `amount_cents` by 1,200 basis points, divide by ten thousand and round half up, then add those per-line figures together. It is **not** one rounding applied to the summed retail revenue, and on a multi-line attachment set the two approaches differ by cents.

The sixth step produces the costs, the guest-facing total and the margin. `labor_cost_cents` is the therapist's `base_hourly_cost_cents` multiplied by `basis_duration_minutes` and divided by 60, rounded half up, and then increased by `therapist_commission_cents`, `therapist_share_cents` and `retail_commission_cents`. `room_fixed_cost_cents` is the room's `hourly_fixed_cost_cents` multiplied by `basis_duration_minutes` and divided by 60, rounded half up. `folio_total_cents` is `realized_price_cents` plus `retail_revenue_cents` plus `service_charge_pool_cents` plus `gratuity_cents`, and it is the guest-facing total for the appointment — the figure the check-in posting charges and the figure the settlement adjustment reconciles against. `net_margin_cents` is `realized_price_cents` plus `retail_revenue_cents` less `labor_cost_cents` less `room_fixed_cost_cents`. Because the final variant changes the basis and the realised revenue, the pool, all three buckets, both commissions, the labour cost, the room cost, the folio total and the margin all differ from the provisional row cut for the same appointment.

### 3.10. Compliance Sweeps and Management Reporting

The compliance sweep is the operator batch step that keeps the credential chain honest as the logical clock advances, and either a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `SpaDirector` may run it. It takes no input, evaluates everything at the current logical instant, and answers with HTTP 200 rather than 201 because it mints no resource of its own, reporting what it did as `swept_at` — which is exactly the logical instant it ran at — together with five arrays: `expired_license_ids`, `suspended_therapist_ids`, `blocked_appointment_ids`, `auto_cancelled_appointment_ids` and `expired_attachment_ids`. All five are always present, each an empty array rather than an omitted key when its hop fired for nothing. Their orderings follow the order the sweep did the work in: `expired_license_ids` in the licences' own creation order, `suspended_therapist_ids` in the order the sweep first met each of those therapists' expired licences, `blocked_appointment_ids` and `auto_cancelled_appointment_ids` in the third hop's cascade order, and `expired_attachment_ids` in that same appointment order with each appointment's own attachments in their creation order. Its work is a five-hop cascade in which each hop exists only because the previous one fired.

The first hop expires credentials: every licence whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` and whose `expires_on` falls strictly before the sweep instant's UTC date moves `VERIFIED → EXPIRED`. The second hop suspends the therapist: only a therapist one of whose licences the first hop has just expired is examined at all, and such a therapist who is `ACTIVE` and now holds no `VERIFIED` unexpired licence moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `LICENSE_EXPIRED` — a therapist who still holds a second valid licence is left alone. That survival test ignores `state_code` altogether, unlike the licensing clause of the clearance predicate: a therapist whose only surviving `VERIFIED` unexpired licence is for some state other than their own `practice_state_code` escapes suspension, and because the later hops visit only the therapists this hop suspended, that therapist's appointments are not touched by the sweep at all even though the clearance predicate would now fail them. The third hop blocks the work: because the therapist became suspended, every appointment of that therapist whose `schedule_status` is `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `CHECKED_IN` has its `clearance_status` driven to `BLOCKED` with `clearance_block_reason` `LICENSE_INVALID` — subject to the fixed reason precedence of §2.10, so an appointment that is simultaneously failing the intake clause records `INTAKE_FLAGGED` instead — processed in ascending `start_at` order with ties broken by ascending `created_at`. The fourth hop expires the upsells: because those appointments became blocked, every retail attachment on them in `RECOMMENDED` becomes `EXPIRED`. The fifth hop protects the near-term schedule: among those newly blocked appointments, each one whose `schedule_status` is `CONFIRMED` and whose `start_at` is earlier than the sweep instant advanced by 48 hours moves `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED` as a system act, with `cancellation_charge_cents` 0 and no folio posting — the spa, not the guest, caused this — and each such cancellation decrements that appointment's room `booked_minutes_total` and therapist `assigned_minutes_total` by the appointment's committed minutes. The cancellation does not rewind the clearance dimension: such an appointment comes to rest at `CANCELLED` while still carrying `clearance_status` `BLOCKED` and its `clearance_block_reason`. Appointments in `REQUESTED` or `CHECKED_IN`, and confirmed appointments starting beyond the 48-hour horizon, stay blocked but are not cancelled.

The room-utilisation report answers the spa director's capacity question over a half-open window: it requires `from_at` and `to_at`, accepts an optional `room_id` to scope it to a single room — a query filter matched by exact equality and not a path lookup, so an unrecognised `room_id` yields an empty `rooms` array rather than `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND` — and treats `to_at` as **exclusive**. `window_minutes` is the number of minutes between `from_at` and `to_at`, truncated toward zero to a whole minute, so a window shorter than one minute reports `window_minutes` 0 and, on the strength of the zero-denominator rule, `utilization_pct` exactly `0.00` and `revenue_per_room_hour_cents` 0 for every room. The response echoes `from_at`, `to_at` and `window_minutes` alongside a `rooms` array and carries nothing else — the `room_id` filter is not echoed back. Every room appears in that array whether or not it saw any business, ordered by the room's own `created_at`, each element carrying `room_id`, `room_code`, `occupied_minutes`, `utilization_pct`, `revenue_cents`, `labor_cost_cents`, `room_fixed_cost_cents`, `net_margin_cents` and `revenue_per_room_hour_cents`. For each room the report takes only that room's appointments whose `schedule_status` is `COMPLETED` and whose `start_at` is at or after `from_at` and strictly before `to_at`; appointments in `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `IN_SERVICE`, `CANCELLED`, `LATE_CANCELLED` or `NO_SHOW` are all excluded. Because selection is on `start_at` alone, an appointment that starts inside the window and finishes after `to_at` contributes its full occupied minutes.

`occupied_minutes` is the sum, over those appointments, of each one's `actual_duration_minutes` plus that room's `turnover_buffer_minutes`; because the occupancy intervals of completed appointments in a single room never overlap, merging the intervals gives exactly the same total as summing their lengths. `utilization_pct` is `occupied_minutes` multiplied by 100 and divided by `window_minutes`, rounded half up to exactly two decimal places. The money figures come only from the `FINALIZED` settlement rows of those appointments — provisional, superseded and reversed rows are excluded — so `revenue_cents` is the sum over those rows of `realized_price_cents` plus `retail_revenue_cents`, and `labor_cost_cents` is the sum of their `labor_cost_cents`. `room_fixed_cost_cents` is the room's `hourly_fixed_cost_cents` multiplied by `occupied_minutes` and divided by 60, rounded half up. `net_margin_cents` is `revenue_cents` less `labor_cost_cents` less `room_fixed_cost_cents`. `revenue_per_room_hour_cents` is `revenue_cents` multiplied by 60 and divided by `window_minutes`, rounded half up. A room with no completed appointments in the window reports `occupied_minutes` 0, `utilization_pct` exactly `0.00` and every money field 0 rather than being omitted.

The retail-attachment report measures the upsell programme over the same kind of half-open window, requiring `from_at` and `to_at` and accepting an optional `therapist_id` to scope it to one provider — again a query filter matched by exact equality rather than a path lookup, so an unrecognised `therapist_id` simply matches nothing and yields `eligible_appointment_count` 0 with `attach_rate_pct` `0.00`, never `ERR_THERAPIST_NOT_FOUND`. The eligible set is the appointments whose `schedule_status` is `COMPLETED`, whose `start_at` is at or after `from_at` and strictly before `to_at`, and whose `therapist_id` matches the filter when one was supplied; `eligible_appointment_count` is the size of that set. From those appointments the report takes the retail attachments in `status` `PURCHASED`: `purchased_count` is how many there are, `purchased_amount_cents` is the sum of their `amount_cents`, and `commission_cents` is computed per line and then summed — each attachment's `amount_cents` multiplied by 1,200 basis points, divided by ten thousand and rounded half up, with those per-line figures added together. Attachments in `RECOMMENDED`, `DECLINED` or `EXPIRED` contribute to none of those three figures, yet the appointment they hang off still counts toward `eligible_appointment_count`; attachments on appointments that are not `COMPLETED` are excluded from the report entirely. `attach_rate_pct` is exactly `0.00` when `eligible_appointment_count` is 0 — never an error — and is otherwise `purchased_count` multiplied by 100 and divided by `eligible_appointment_count`, rounded half up to two decimal places. It is a per-appointment attachment ratio and is deliberately **not** capped at 100.00: two purchased products on a single completed appointment yield `200.00`. The response echoes `from_at` and `to_at` alongside the five figures.

Both reports reject a window whose `from_at` is not strictly earlier than its `to_at`, and both reject a window spanning more than 92 days, with `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). That error is reserved for those two conditions alone: both bounds are required, but an omitted bound, an empty one and a malformed one are all rung-3 shape violations judged before any window arithmetic runs, so a request that leaves `from_at` out altogether answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422) and never the report-window error. Neither report is a list capability: both accept `limit` and `offset` not at all, apply no pagination and answer inside the object envelope of §2.2.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

All list endpoints accept `limit` and `offset` as described in §2.3 and return their page inside the array envelope. An endpoint that answers with a computed object rather than a page — the two management reports and the compliance sweep of §3.10 among them — is not a list endpoint: it accepts neither `limit` nor `offset` and answers inside the object envelope instead.
