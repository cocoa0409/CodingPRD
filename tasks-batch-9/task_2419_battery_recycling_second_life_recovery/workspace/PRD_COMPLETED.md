# Battery Recycling & Second-Life Recovery API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

End-of-life lithium-ion batteries arrive at a recycling campus from three very different worlds. An automaker ships warranty returns and line scrap on a contract, with paperwork that is usually accurate. A dismantler ships salvage packs pulled from crashed vehicles, where the chemistry stamped on the housing and the chemistry actually inside can disagree. A consumer collection programme ships drums of mixed cells whose declared assay is little more than an educated guess. All three streams have to be weighed, identified, discharged to a transport-safe state of charge, packaged under dangerous-goods rules, documented on a hazardous-material manifest, and then split three ways — whole modules good enough to refurbish and resell, modules good enough for stationary second-life storage, and everything else into mechanical shredding and hydrometallurgical recovery. The recycler pays for the incoming feedstock, and it pays twice: once provisionally against the supplier's declared assay when custody is released, and once finally against the metal actually recovered when the processing lot closes its assay ledger. The gap between those two numbers is where the commercial argument lives.

This document specifies the service that runs that business. It is, at its heart, a **reconciliation ledger with a pricing engine bolted to it**: feedstock mass flows from a shipment into modules, from modules into a recovery lot, and from a lot into per-metal recovery outputs, while money flows in the opposite direction from those recovery outputs into a supplier settlement and out to the counterparty's payable balance. Every mass and every cent must be traceable, reversible, and reconcilable — a voided lot must give its modules and its mass back, a voided settlement must give the supplier's payable balance back exactly, and a voided discharge event must give the permit allowance back exactly. Alongside the ledger sit the regulatory surfaces the plant cannot operate without: air and water discharge permits with daily caps, residue accounting, and recycled-content battery passports issued to downstream cathode and cell customers.

The system is deliberately built for ten narrow operational roles rather than a handful of broad ones, because the audit story matters as much as the arithmetic. The technician who books recovery yields must never be the accountant who posts the settlement those yields price, and the compliance officer who submits a hazardous-material manifest must never be the one who counter-signs it. Time in this service is a logical clock that an administrator moves explicitly, never the host's wall clock, so that permit expiry, campaign windows and the forward-looking capacity projection are all deterministic and reproducible. The entire API is mounted under the `/recycling` prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operating Roles and Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the actor, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role the actor is acting as. If either header is absent, or if the value of `X-User-Role` is not one of the ten declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. If both headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` and HTTP 403. The authorised role list for each endpoint is given in §4.

The ten roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `MarketAnalyst`, `IntakeClerk`, `PlantOperator`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `TriageEngineer`, `AssayTechnician`, `SettlementAccountant`, `CertificationOfficer`, and `SupplierRep`. Their responsibilities divide as follows. A `PlatformAdmin` configures campuses, counterparties and processing lines, re-plumbs a line to another chemistry family, and drives the logical clock; it may also read every resource in the system. A `MarketAnalyst` publishes the metal index quotes that price feedstock and reads them back. An `IntakeClerk` books arriving shipments, records the chemistry confirmed at the identification bench, and books dismantled modules against a shipment. A `PlantOperator` runs the plant floor: discharge measurements, packaging, custody release, module shipping, campaign scheduling and start and cancellation, lot opening, lot charging and lot voiding. A `ComplianceOfficer` owns hazardous-material documentation (submit, close, reject), permit allotments and their suspension, discharge-event booking and voiding, and the supplier quality register. A `TriageEngineer` grades modules and routes them between refurbishment, second life and material recovery. An `AssayTechnician` books recovery outputs, finalizes lots and records umpire assay results. A `SettlementAccountant` owns the payable ledger — posting, applying an umpire result, cash settlement and voiding. A `CertificationOfficer` issues and revokes recycled-content battery passports. A `SupplierRep` is the counterparty's own representative: it reads its shipments, settlements, supplier record and passports, and it may dispute a posted final settlement.

Nine of the ten roles carry an **opaque identity**. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `MarketAnalyst`, `IntakeClerk`, `PlantOperator`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `TriageEngineer`, `AssayTechnician`, `SettlementAccountant`, or `CertificationOfficer`, the value of `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — it is stored as `submitted_by_user_id` and `closed_by_user_id` on a hazardous-material manifest, as `decided_by_user_id` on a triage decision, and as `recorded_by_user_id` on a recovery output — but it never compares that value against any entity's `id`.

`SupplierRep` is the single exception and is **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `SupplierRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Supplier`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Supplier` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, when a `SupplierRep` disputes a settlement, the settlement's `supplier_id` must equal `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_OWNER` and HTTP 403.

Beyond role separation there is one **same-actor** restriction. Closing a hazardous-material manifest requires that the `X-User-ID` performing the close differs from the `submitted_by_user_id` recorded when the manifest was submitted; if they are equal the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403, even though both actors hold the `ComplianceOfficer` role. The equivalent separation between assay work and money work is enforced structurally rather than by comparing identifiers: `AssayTechnician` is simply not authorised on any settlement posting, cash-settlement or voiding endpoint, and `SettlementAccountant` is not authorised on recovery-output booking or lot finalization.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A value outside those bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400. The four pagination fields reported in `meta` are `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective starting position), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current window). Every other query parameter a list endpoint accepts is a filter matched by exact equality against the stored field and is not itself checked against any enumeration, so a filter value naming no declared state, status or code simply matches nothing and yields an empty page rather than an error.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, oldest row first; because the logical clock only moves when an administrator moves it, rows created in the same clock position routinely share a `created_at`, and such ties are broken by insertion sequence, earliest-inserted row first. Two list surfaces make that ordering explicit in domain terms and behave identically to the default: the manifest revisions of a shipment come back oldest revision first, and the assay ledger of a lot comes back oldest line first, as do the triage decisions of a module. The single exception to the default is the metal index quote list, which is ordered by `effective_date` descending — newest effective quote first, with ties broken by `created_at` descending and then by insertion sequence, latest-inserted row first.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Version Counter

Every resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits, stamped from the logical clock at the moment the row is created.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, of the same format, refreshed on every accepted mutation of that row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every accepted mutation of that row. The resources carrying the full set — `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version` — are `Facility`, `Supplier`, `Intake`, `HazmatManifest`, `Module`, `TriageDecision`, `ProcessingLine`, `ProcessingCampaign`, `RecoveryLot`, `SupplierSettlement`, `PermitAllotment`, `DischargeEvent` and `BatteryPassport`.

Two resources are immutable ledger rows and carry **only** `id` and `created_at`: `MetalIndexPrice`, a published market quote that is never edited, and `RecoveryOutput`, an append-only assay line. For those two, `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the response body entirely, and there is no update endpoint and no delete endpoint for either.

An important consequence of the version rule is that when one request mutates several rows — and in this domain a single request routinely mutates five or six — the `version` of *every* row it touches advances by one, and each of those rows also gets a fresh `updated_at`. The cascades in §3 name the rows they touch precisely for this reason.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The server keeps a logical clock whose startup default is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. Before any request has moved it, every timestamp the server stamps and every window, expiry or lookback comparison it performs uses that default instant — never the host's real wall-clock time. A `PlatformAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a target instant as `to_iso8601`, forwards or backwards with no ordering constraint against the clock's current position; the response carries the single field `current_utc` reporting the new position. The clock governs `received_at` on a shipment, `released_at` at custody release, `finalized_at` at lot finalization, `issued_at` on a battery passport, lazy permit expiry, the campaign-window checks at campaign start, and the lookback window of the capacity projection.

### 2.6. Nested Path Resolution and Referential Existence

Many resources are addressed through a nested path — a manifest under a shipment, a lot under a campaign, a recovery output or a discharge event or a passport under a lot. Path parameters are resolved strictly **left to right**, and the first segment that names a row which does not exist determines the error. The path parameter names are `facility_id`, `supplier_id`, `intake_id`, `manifest_id`, `module_id`, `line_id`, `campaign_id`, `lot_id`, `settlement_id`, `allotment_id`, `event_id` and `passport_id`; a handful of read-only list routes spell the parent segment simply as `id`. The corresponding 404 responses are `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INTAKE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MANIFEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MODULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISCHARGE_EVENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_PASSPORT_NOT_FOUND`. Left-to-right resolution means that a request naming a shipment that does not exist and a manifest that does not exist reports the shipment, not the manifest. It also means that a manifest identifier which exists but belongs to a *different* shipment than the one in the path is reported as `ERR_MANIFEST_NOT_FOUND`, because a manifest is only addressable underneath its own shipment.

Foreign keys arriving in a **request body** are checked the same way and produce the same 404 codes. The bodies that carry them are shipment creation (`supplier_id` and `facility_id`), processing-line commissioning (`facility_id`), campaign scheduling (`line_id`), permit-allotment registration (`facility_id`), lot charging (every identifier in `module_ids`) and discharge-event booking (`allotment_id`).

### 2.7. Validation Precedence

Because a single request can violate several rules at once, the order in which checks run is part of the contract. Checks are evaluated in the following thirteen tiers, and the **first** tier that fails determines the response; no later tier can mask an earlier one.

1. **Header authentication** — both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present and the role declared. `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorisation** — the acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised list. `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, 403. Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Actor-identity checks** — two rules share this tier. On settlement dispute, a `SupplierRep` whose `X-User-ID` differs from the settlement's `supplier_id` is rejected with `ERR_NOT_OWNER`, 403. On manifest close, an `X-User-ID` equal to the manifest's `submitted_by_user_id` is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403. Both rules have to read the addressed row before they can compare anything, so a path naming a settlement or a manifest that does not exist reports its tier-5 404 ahead of either check.
4. **Request shape** — required fields present, enum values within range, numbers inside their declared constraints, arrays non-empty, and the three uniqueness constraints satisfied: a module's `serial_code` is unique within its shipment, a recovery output is unique per lot-and-metal pair, and a passport's `passport_code` is globally unique. `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400. Applies to every endpoint.
5. **Path existence**, left to right, as described in §2.6. The applicable 404 code. Applies to every endpoint with path parameters.
6. **Body foreign-key existence**, as described in §2.6. The applicable 404 code. Applies to shipment creation, processing-line commissioning, campaign scheduling, permit-allotment registration, lot charging and discharge-event booking.
7. **Optimistic-concurrency version match** — `expected_intake_version` on module creation and `expected_lot_version` on lot charging. `ERR_STALE_INTAKE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION`, 412. Applies to exactly those two endpoints.
8. **State legality** — the target row is in a state from which the requested transition is declared. HTTP 409, with the code chosen from `ERR_INVALID_CUSTODY_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_COMPLIANCE_STATE`, `ERR_MANIFEST_NOT_SUBMITTED`, `ERR_MODULE_NOT_TRIAGEABLE`, `ERR_MODULE_NOT_IN_RECOVERY_QUEUE`, `ERR_MODULE_NOT_SHIPPABLE`, `ERR_LOT_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_LOT_NOT_CHARGED`, `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_RUNNING`, `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_SCHEDULED`, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_POSTED`, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_DISPUTED`, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_IN_UMPIRE_ROUND`, `ERR_DISCHARGE_EVENT_NOT_POSTED`, `ERR_PASSPORT_NOT_ISSUED`, `ERR_PASSPORT_ALREADY_ISSUED`, `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` and `ERR_LINE_HAS_RUNNING_CAMPAIGN`. Applies to every state-changing endpoint.
9. **Regulatory and physical-safety gates**, HTTP 422 — a campus must be `ACTIVE` to accept a new shipment (`ERR_FACILITY_SUSPENDED`), a shipment's state of charge must be at or below the packaging limit before packaging (`ERR_SOC_ABOVE_PACKAGING_LIMIT`), a permit allotment must be `ACTIVE` after lazy expiry has been evaluated before a discharge is booked (`ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE`), and a processing line must be `AVAILABLE` before a campaign starts (`ERR_LINE_NOT_AVAILABLE`). Applies to shipment creation, packaging, discharge-event booking and campaign start.
10. **Interval geometry** — campaign window overlap on a line (`ERR_CAMPAIGN_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, 409), changeover buffer against a different-family neighbour (`ERR_CHANGEOVER_BUFFER_VIOLATION`, 422), a discharge instant outside the permit's closed validity interval (`ERR_PERMIT_WINDOW_EXCLUDED`, 422), and a campaign start attempted after its window has closed (`ERR_CAMPAIGN_WINDOW_CLOSED`, 422). Applies to campaign scheduling, campaign start and discharge-event booking.
11. **Quantitative ceilings and floors**, HTTP 422 except where noted — planned campaign input against line capacity (`ERR_CAMPAIGN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`), charge mass against the campaign plan (`ERR_LOT_CHARGE_EXCEEDS_PLAN`), the daily discharge bucket against the permit cap (`ERR_DISCHARGE_DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED`), the mass-balance floor at finalization (`ERR_MASS_BALANCE_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE`), the requirement that a lot have at least one assay line before it finalizes (`ERR_NO_RECOVERY_OUTPUTS`), the recycled-content floor for a passport (`ERR_RECYCLED_CONTENT_BELOW_MINIMUM`), a duplicate index quote (`ERR_DUPLICATE_INDEX_PRICE`, 409), an operator override that the module's grade does not permit (`ERR_DISPOSITION_OVERRIDE_NOT_PERMITTED`), and a dispute raised on a variance that is within tolerance (`ERR_VARIANCE_WITHIN_TOLERANCE`). Applies to campaign scheduling, lot charging, discharge-event booking, lot finalization, passport issuance, index-quote publication, triage decisions and settlement disputes.
12. **Cross-entity link integrity**, HTTP 422 except where noted — a charged module's chemistry family not matching its campaign (`ERR_MODULE_CHEMISTRY_MISMATCH`), a metal stream that the campaign's line type cannot recover (`ERR_METAL_NOT_RECOVERABLE_ON_LINE`), a module whose shipment has not been released (`ERR_INTAKE_NOT_RELEASED`), a manifest rejection attempted while a module of the shipment is already committed to a lot (`ERR_MODULES_ALREADY_COMMITTED`, 409), and a campaign whose chemistry family differs from its line's (`ERR_CAMPAIGN_FAMILY_MISMATCH`). Applies to lot charging, recovery-output booking, module shipping, manifest rejection and campaign scheduling.
13. **Money gates, evaluated last** — the supplier must not be on credit hold (`ERR_SUPPLIER_CREDIT_HOLD`, 402) and an index quote must exist on or before the price basis date for every metal being priced with a non-zero contained mass (`ERR_INDEX_PRICE_UNAVAILABLE`, 422). Applies to shipment creation, custody release and lot finalization.

The practical significance of putting money last is that a shipment whose paperwork or state is wrong reports the paperwork problem, not the credit hold; and a lot whose mass balance is out of tolerance reports the tolerance failure even when an index quote is also missing.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Shipments and Lots

Two write endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent row's `version`, and only those two accept a version field. Every other endpoint in this API takes no version field at all.

The first is module creation, `POST /recycling/intakes/{intake_id}/modules`. That request must carry `expected_intake_version` as a required, non-optional field, holding the `version` of the target `Intake` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the shipment's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INTAKE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Module creation is legitimately repeatable — a dismantler books one module row per module pulled from the pack, and each insertion recomputes the shipment's total and unassigned module mass — so a second insertion carrying a fresh version succeeds on its own merits. The stale version is the only thing that distinguishes the rejected write.

The second is lot charging, `POST /recycling/lots/{lot_id}/charge`. That request must carry `expected_lot_version` as a required, non-optional field, holding the `version` of the target `RecoveryLot` as the client last observed it. A mismatch with the lot's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The charge sheet is built from a read of the lot, and any accepted mutation of that lot in the meantime — a prior charge, a void, an assay line booked against it — invalidates the mass arithmetic on which the client based its module list.

### 2.9. Mass and Money Precision Discipline

Rounding in this service is always **half up**, and it is always applied at the step where it is specified, never deferred to the end of a calculation.

Masses in kilograms are carried to **three decimal places**. This applies to a shipment's net mass, its total, assigned and unassigned module mass, a lot's charged, residue, recovered, accounted and unaccounted mass, a permit allotment's consumed mass, and every projected daily mass in the capacity projection.

Percentages are carried to **two decimal places** — a triage score, a lot's mass-balance and recycled-content percentages, and each per-metal assay variance in percentage points. One percentage is the exception and is carried to **four decimal places**: the quality deduction rate applied to a settlement, which is expressed as a fraction of one (so a four-percent deduction is stored as 0.04).

All money is held in **whole cents** as integers. Where a monetary figure is derived by multiplication, it is rounded half up to a whole cent at that step. The most consequential instance of this rule is the per-metal payable line: each metal's line amount is rounded to whole cents **first**, and the settlement's gross amount is the sum of those already-rounded lines. Summing unrounded line values and rounding once at the end is incorrect and can differ from the required result by one or more cents.

### 2.10. Ledger Invariants and Compensating Reversals

Three invariants hold at all times, and each has a forward action and an exact compensating action. They are the reason every void endpoint in this API exists.

The **supplier obligation invariant** holds that a supplier's `payable_balance_cents` equals the sum of `posted_amount_cents` across that supplier's settlements whose `state` is `POSTED`, `DISPUTED`, `UMPIRE_ROUND` or `SETTLED`, less that supplier's `paid_to_date_cents`. The forward action is posting a settlement; the compensating actions are voiding a posted settlement and applying an umpire result, each of which removes the previously posted amount from the balance and resets `posted_amount_cents` to 0.

The **shipment mass conservation invariant** holds that for every shipment, `assigned_mass_kg` plus `unassigned_mass_kg` equals `module_mass_total_kg`. The forward action is charging modules into a lot, which moves mass from unassigned to assigned; the compensating action is voiding that lot, which moves it back.

The **permit allowance invariant** holds that a permit allotment's `consumed_kg` equals the sum of `mass_kg` across that allotment's discharge events whose `status` is `POSTED`. The forward action is booking a discharge event; the compensating action is voiding it, which subtracts that event's mass again.

### 2.11. Cascade Semantics and Version Propagation

Several operations in this domain are **cascades**: one accepted request changes state on rows the caller did not name. Three properties of cascades are contractual. First, cascades are applied immediately and synchronously within the triggering request — there is no deferred or eventual settling. Second, every row a cascade touches has its `version` incremented by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed, exactly as if it had been mutated directly. Third, a cascade-created child does **not** appear in the triggering response body; the response body of a mutation always contains the resource the endpoint nominally addresses. The provisional settlement cut when custody is released is the canonical case: it is created by the release request, but it is discovered only by listing settlements filtered on the shipment.

Two families of cascade recur. **Reverting cascades** unwind derived work when an upstream document fails — a rejected hazardous-material manifest quarantines the shipment and voids the triage decisions taken on its modules, returning those modules to the pending-triage queue. **Bubble-up cascades** derive a parent's state from its children — a running processing campaign closes itself the moment its last non-terminal lot becomes terminal, without any request naming the campaign.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Configuring the Recycling Network and the Market Price Feed

Before a single battery can be booked, a `PlatformAdmin` must stand up the physical and commercial network. A recycling campus is registered with a `name`, a `state_code` consisting of exactly two uppercase letters, a mechanical shredding ceiling given as `mech_capacity_kg_per_day` and a hydrometallurgical ceiling given as `hydro_capacity_kg_per_day`; both ceilings must be greater than zero. The campus also carries a `status` which the caller may supply and which otherwise defaults to `ACTIVE`; the only other permitted value is `SUSPENDED`. A `SUSPENDED` campus is closed to new business: any attempt to book an arriving shipment into it is rejected with `ERR_FACILITY_SUSPENDED`.

Counterparties are registered next. A supplier carries a `legal_name`, a `channel` describing how its feedstock reaches the plant — `AUTOMAKER`, `DISMANTLER` or `CONSUMER_COLLECTION` — a `credit_limit_cents` figure of zero or more expressing the largest unpaid obligation the recycler is willing to carry for that counterparty, and an optional accounts-payable `contact_email` which defaults to null when omitted. Four further supplier attributes are maintained entirely by the system and are never supplied by a caller: `payable_balance_cents` and `paid_to_date_cents` both start at 0 and track the running unpaid obligation and the cumulative cash paid out; `credit_hold` starts false and is true exactly while `payable_balance_cents` is greater than `credit_limit_cents`; and `quality_strike_count` starts at 0 and counts logged feedstock quality incidents.

That quality register is a `ComplianceOfficer` capability, and it is the only way a strike is recorded manually. Logging an incident against a counterparty takes an `incident_code` naming the incident, increases the counterparty's `quality_strike_count` by exactly 1, advances the supplier's `version` by 1, and returns the updated supplier record. Strikes matter commercially rather than administratively: once a counterparty has accumulated at least three of them, its quality deduction on every subsequent settlement is escalated, as §3.5 explains.

Feedstock pricing is anchored to published market quotes, which a `MarketAnalyst` maintains. A quote names its `metal` — one of `NI`, `CO`, `LI`, `CU`, `MN` or `GRAPHITE` — an `effective_date` given as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits from which the quote applies, a `price_cents_per_kg` greater than zero expressing the index price per contained kilogram, and an optional `source_index` naming the publishing index, which defaults to `LME`. Quotes are immutable once published and there is no edit or delete path for them. A second quote for the same combination of `metal` and `effective_date` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_INDEX_PRICE`. Quotes can be listed and filtered by `metal` and by `effective_on_or_before`, which restricts results to quotes whose `effective_date` falls at or before the supplied instant.

Processing capacity is organised as lines within a campus. A line records the `facility_id` of the campus it sits in, a `line_code` identifying it on the plant floor, a `line_type` of either `MECHANICAL` (which shreds) or `HYDROMET` (which leaches and precipitates), a `chemistry_family` of either `NICKEL_RICH` or `IRON_PHOSPHATE` describing what the line is currently plumbed for, a nameplate `throughput_kg_per_hour` greater than zero, a `changeover_buffer_hours` figure of zero or more which defaults to 6 and expresses the idle hours the line needs between campaigns of different chemistry families, and a `status` of `AVAILABLE` (the default) or `MAINTENANCE`. Chemistry families are a fixed mapping from cell chemistry: `NMC622`, `NCA` and `LMO` all belong to the `NICKEL_RICH` family, while `LFP` belongs to the `IRON_PHOSPHATE` family.

Re-plumbing a line to the other family is also a `PlatformAdmin` capability and takes the new `chemistry_family` as its only input. It is refused with `ERR_LINE_HAS_RUNNING_CAMPAIGN` while any campaign on that line has status `RUNNING`, and it advances the line's `version` by 1 on success. Re-plumbing deliberately does **not** rewrite the family recorded on campaigns that are already scheduled — those campaigns keep the family they were scheduled with, which is precisely what makes the changeover buffer between neighbouring campaigns observable in practice. Lines can be listed and filtered by `facility_id` and `line_type`.

### 3.2. Taking Custody: Booking, Identification, Discharge and Packaging

An `IntakeClerk` books an arriving shipment into custody. The booking payload names the counterparty through `supplier_id` and the receiving campus through `facility_id`, states the `declared_chemistry` from the shipping paperwork as one of `NMC622`, `NCA`, `LMO` or `LFP`, and reports the weighbridge readings: `gross_mass_kg` greater than zero including packaging, and `packaging_tare_kg` which must be at least zero and strictly less than `gross_mass_kg`. Six further measurements are optional and each defaults to 0.0 when omitted: `declared_ni_pct`, `declared_co_pct`, `declared_li_pct` and `declared_cu_pct` state the supplier's declared nickel, cobalt, lithium and copper assays as mass percentages of net mass, while `moisture_pct` reports measured free moisture and `foreign_material_pct` reports measured non-battery foreign material, again as mass percentages. All six must fall between 0.0 and 100.0 inclusive. The server stamps `received_at` from the logical clock — this instant is later the provisional price basis date — and computes `net_mass_kg` as the gross mass less the packaging tare, rounded half up to three decimals.

A newly booked shipment starts on **two independent state dimensions carried on the same row**. Its `custody_state` — the physical dimension — starts at `RECEIVED` and may take the values `RECEIVED`, `IDENTIFIED`, `DISCHARGED`, `PACKAGED`, `RELEASED` and `QUARANTINED`, with `RELEASED` terminal. Its `compliance_state` — the paperwork dimension — starts at `MANIFEST_DRAFT` and may take the values `MANIFEST_DRAFT`, `MANIFEST_SUBMITTED`, `MANIFEST_CLOSED` and `MANIFEST_REJECTED`, with `MANIFEST_CLOSED` terminal. Neither dimension is a function of the other; they advance on their own triggers and they are guarded against each other only at the two points §3.3 and §3.5 name. Everything else on a fresh shipment is empty: `identified_chemistry`, `soc_pct`, `packaging_type`, `hold_reason` and `released_at` are all null, and `module_mass_total_kg`, `assigned_mass_kg` and `unassigned_mass_kg` are all 0.0. Booking is refused with `ERR_FACILITY_SUSPENDED` when the receiving campus is `SUSPENDED`, and with `ERR_SUPPLIER_CREDIT_HOLD` when the counterparty's `credit_hold` is true — the recycler will not take more material from a supplier it already owes beyond the agreed limit.

At the identification bench the clerk confirms what the pack actually is. Recording the confirmed chemistry supplies `identified_chemistry` as one of the same four values and advances `custody_state` from `RECEIVED` to `IDENTIFIED`, incrementing the shipment's `version` by 1 and refreshing its `updated_at`. Discharge follows, and is a `PlantOperator` responsibility: supplying the measured `soc_pct` reading — a required number carried with no range constraint of its own — advances `custody_state` from `IDENTIFIED` to `DISCHARGED`, and the transition additionally requires that `identified_chemistry` is no longer null, a shipment failing that second requirement being refused with `ERR_INVALID_CUSTODY_STATE` just as a wrong custody state is. **Discharge is the only repeatable custody step.** A shipment already in `DISCHARGED` accepts a further measurement, staying in `DISCHARGED` while overwriting `soc_pct` with the new reading and incrementing `version` by 1 again — the operating reality is that a pack whose first reading came in too high is discharged further and re-measured until it is safe to pack.

Packaging closes the physical sequence. Supplying the chosen `packaging_type` — the UN-approved packaging selected on the floor — advances `custody_state` from `DISCHARGED` to `PACKAGED`. Packaging is refused with `ERR_SOC_ABOVE_PACKAGING_LIMIT` whenever `soc_pct` is still null or is greater than 30.0 percent; a reading of exactly 30.0 passes. Any custody step requested from a state for which it is not declared — packaging a shipment that is still `RECEIVED`, identifying one that is already `IDENTIFIED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CUSTODY_STATE`. Shipments can be listed and filtered by `supplier_id`, `facility_id`, `custody_state` and `compliance_state`, and a single shipment read back with both dimensions and its whole mass ledger.

Capacity planning reads the same shipment ledger forward. The campus projection takes a required integer `horizon_days` of at least 1 and reports, without persisting anything, a `baseline_kg_per_day`, the `horizon_days` it was asked for, a `projected_input_kg` total, the campus `mech_capacity_kg_per_day` and `hydro_capacity_kg_per_day` for reference, a `first_deficit_day`, a `first_deficit_date` and a `deficit_kg`. The baseline is built over a **thirty-day lookback** ending at the current clock position: the server sums `net_mass_kg` across the shipments of that campus whose `received_at` falls at or after the instant thirty days before the current clock position and at or before the current clock position — both ends inclusive — counting only shipments whose `custody_state` is `RECEIVED`, `IDENTIFIED`, `DISCHARGED`, `PACKAGED` or `RELEASED`, so that quarantined mass is excluded from the return stream. That sum is divided by thirty and rounded half up to three decimals to give `baseline_kg_per_day`. For each day numbered 1 through `horizon_days`, the projected mass for that day is the baseline multiplied by one plus four thousandths of the day number — a growth rate of 0.4 percent per day compounding linearly on the day index — rounded half up to three decimals **for that day, before any summing**. `projected_input_kg` is the sum of those already-rounded daily figures, itself rounded half up to three decimals. `first_deficit_day` is the smallest day number in that range whose projected daily mass is strictly greater than the campus `mech_capacity_kg_per_day`, and is null when no day qualifies. `first_deficit_date` is the current clock position advanced by that many days, and is null when there is no deficit day. `deficit_kg` is the projected mass of the first deficit day less the mechanical ceiling, rounded half up to three decimals, and is 0.0 when there is no deficit day. Because shipment rows are never deleted, the projection is a pure function of the clock position and the shipment ledger.

### 3.3. Hazardous-Material Documentation and the Quarantine Cascade

The compliance dimension is owned end to end by `ComplianceOfficer` actors. Submitting the hazardous-material manifest for a shipment supplies the `un_number` for the packaged cells — one of `UN3480`, `UN3481` or `UN3536` — the dangerous-goods `packing_group` as `I`, `II` or `III`, and the `transporter_name` of the licensed hazmat carrier. A first submission is only accepted while `compliance_state` is `MANIFEST_DRAFT` and `custody_state` is either `DISCHARGED` or `PACKAGED`; a submission attempted from any other compliance state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COMPLIANCE_STATE`, while one attempted from `MANIFEST_DRAFT` with the custody dimension in any state other than `DISCHARGED` or `PACKAGED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CUSTODY_STATE` — the compliance dimension is read first, and only once it admits the submission is the custody dimension checked. On success a manifest document is created with `status` `SUBMITTED`, `revision` 1, `submitted_by_user_id` recorded from the acting `X-User-ID`, and `closed_by_user_id` and `rejection_reason` both null; the shipment's `compliance_state` advances from `MANIFEST_DRAFT` to `MANIFEST_SUBMITTED` and its `version` increments by 1. Every revision ever submitted for a shipment remains readable, oldest revision first, and the list can be filtered by manifest `status`.

Closing a manifest is the counter-signature, and it takes no payload at all. It requires the manifest's `status` to be `SUBMITTED` — otherwise `ERR_MANIFEST_NOT_SUBMITTED` — and it requires the shipment's `custody_state` to be `PACKAGED` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CUSTODY_STATE`, checked after the manifest status — because there is nothing to sign off until the cells are actually in their drums. It also requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the manifest's `submitted_by_user_id`, failing with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` if the submitter tries to close their own document. On success the manifest moves `SUBMITTED → CLOSED`, its `closed_by_user_id` is recorded from `X-User-ID`, the shipment's `compliance_state` moves `MANIFEST_SUBMITTED → MANIFEST_CLOSED`, and both the manifest's `version` and the shipment's `version` increment by 1 each.

Rejection is the interesting path, because it is a **four-link cascade** that reaches across three entity types. It takes a `rejection_reason` as free text, requires the manifest's `status` to be `SUBMITTED`, and is blocked with `ERR_MODULES_ALREADY_COMMITTED` when any module of that shipment already has status `CHARGED` or `CONSUMED` — once feedstock has entered a recovery lot the paperwork cannot be unwound. When it proceeds: the first link sets the manifest's `status` to `REJECTED`, records the `rejection_reason`, and moves the shipment's `compliance_state` `MANIFEST_SUBMITTED → MANIFEST_REJECTED`. The second link fires *because* the compliance dimension changed: the shipment's `custody_state`, which at this point is `IDENTIFIED`, `DISCHARGED` or `PACKAGED`, moves to `QUARANTINED` and its `hold_reason` is set to `MANIFEST_REJECTED`. The third link fires *because* custody became `QUARANTINED`: every triage decision on that shipment's modules whose `status` is `ACTIVE` becomes `VOIDED`, and each module so affected reverts to `PENDING_TRIAGE` with its `second_life_score` and `grade` reset to null — a shipment under a paperwork hold has no valid dispositions. The fourth link is bookkeeping: the manifest's `version` and the shipment's `version` each increment by 1, and so does the `version` of every module and every triage decision row the cascade touched. Note that `hold_reason` is null whenever `custody_state` is anything other than `QUARANTINED`; its only two possible non-null values are `MANIFEST_REJECTED` and `SUPPLIER_CREDIT_HOLD`.

Recovery from a rejection is a resubmission, and it closes **two cycles at once**. Submitting again is accepted while `compliance_state` is `MANIFEST_REJECTED`, and it additionally requires that `custody_state` is `QUARANTINED` with `hold_reason` equal to `MANIFEST_REJECTED`, a failure of either part of that requirement being refused with `ERR_INVALID_CUSTODY_STATE`. It creates a fresh manifest document whose `revision` is the highest existing revision for that shipment plus 1, with `status` `SUBMITTED`, and moves `compliance_state` `MANIFEST_REJECTED → MANIFEST_SUBMITTED`. Simultaneously the custody dimension moves `QUARANTINED → IDENTIFIED` and `hold_reason` is cleared to null, and the shipment's `version` increments by 1. The physical consequence is deliberate: the pack must be **re-discharged and re-packaged** before it can be released, so custody travels the full cycle `PACKAGED → QUARANTINED → IDENTIFIED → DISCHARGED → PACKAGED`, while compliance travels `MANIFEST_SUBMITTED → MANIFEST_REJECTED → MANIFEST_SUBMITTED`.

### 3.4. Dismantling, Triage Scoring and Module Dispositions

Dismantling turns one shipment into many modules, one row per module pulled from the pack, and it is an `IntakeClerk` responsibility. Each module booking carries the manufacturer `serial_code`, which must be unique among the modules of that shipment — a duplicate is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — a `mass_kg` greater than zero, a `min_cell_capacity_pct` between 0.0 and 100.0 inclusive giving the worst cell's remaining capacity as a percentage of nameplate, a `mean_cell_capacity_pct` which must be at least the minimum and at most 100.0, an optional `cycle_count` of lifetime equivalent full cycles which defaults to 0 and must be zero or more, and the mandatory `expected_intake_version` described in §2.8. The module's `chemistry` is not supplied by the caller: it is copied from the shipment's `identified_chemistry` at creation. A new module has `lot_id` null, `second_life_score` and `grade` null, `status` `PENDING_TRIAGE`, and `shipped_to` null. Each accepted insertion recomputes the shipment's `module_mass_total_kg` as the sum of module masses, rounded half up to three decimals, and recomputes `unassigned_mass_kg` accordingly, and it increments the shipment's `version` by 1.

Triage is where the three-way commercial decision is made, and it belongs to the `TriageEngineer`. The decision payload is deliberately small: a `scoring_variant` of either `CONSERVATIVE` or `FLEET_AVERAGE`, and an optional `override_disposition` which is `REFURBISH`, `SECOND_LIFE`, `MATERIAL_RECOVERY`, or null to accept whatever the score implies; both inputs are echoed verbatim on the decision row that results, as `scoring_variant` and `override_disposition`. A decision may only be recorded while the module's `status` is `PENDING_TRIAGE` and its shipment's `custody_state` is either `PACKAGED` or `RELEASED`; any other combination is refused with `ERR_MODULE_NOT_TRIAGEABLE`.

The score itself runs in a fixed order over exactly one module row, read at request time; no other module and no lot participates. First, a state-of-health input is chosen according to the variant: the `CONSERVATIVE` variant — the warranty-grade assessment used when a module might be resold — takes the module's `min_cell_capacity_pct`, the worst cell in the pack, while the `FLEET_AVERAGE` variant — fleet-level screening — takes the module's `mean_cell_capacity_pct`. Second, a cycle penalty is computed as the module's `cycle_count` divided by 200, giving one penalty point for every two hundred lifetime equivalent full cycles, and then capped at 20.0 points, so a very heavily cycled module never loses more than twenty points to age. Third, an imbalance penalty is computed as the spread between `mean_cell_capacity_pct` and `min_cell_capacity_pct` multiplied by 0.80, charging eight tenths of a point for every percentage point of cell-to-cell divergence. Fourth, the `score` is the chosen state-of-health input less the cycle penalty and less the imbalance penalty, rounded half up to two decimal places. Fifth, the `grade` band is read off the **rounded** score against inclusive lower bounds: a score of at least 80.0 is grade `A`, otherwise at least 70.0 is grade `B`, otherwise at least 60.0 is grade `C`, and anything below that is grade `D`. Sixth, a `derived_disposition` follows from the grade alone: grade `A` derives `REFURBISH`, grades `B` and `C` both derive `SECOND_LIFE`, and grade `D` derives `MATERIAL_RECOVERY`. Seventh, the effective `disposition` is the supplied `override_disposition` when one was supplied, and the derived disposition otherwise.

Because the two variants read different capacity figures, the same module data can grade differently under each. Whenever the mean cell capacity exceeds the minimum, `FLEET_AVERAGE` produces a strictly higher score than `CONSERVATIVE` — enough, in the common case, for a module to come out grade `A` and headed for refurbishment under `FLEET_AVERAGE` while coming out grade `B` and headed for second life under `CONSERVATIVE`.

Overrides are constrained by the grade they are overriding. An `override_disposition` of `REFURBISH` is permitted only when the computed grade is `A` or `B`; an override of `SECOND_LIFE` is permitted when the grade is `A`, `B` or `C`, and therefore refused when the grade is `D`; an override of `MATERIAL_RECOVERY` is always permitted, since routing better material into shredding is a commercial choice rather than a safety risk. A disallowed override is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_OVERRIDE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

The resolved disposition forks the module's lifecycle into three queues, and the three branches carry genuinely different downstream consequences. When the resolved `disposition` is `REFURBISH`, the module moves `PENDING_TRIAGE → REFURB_QUEUE`; a decision row is created with `status` `ACTIVE`, its `score`, `grade`, `derived_disposition` and `disposition` recorded, and `decided_by_user_id` taken from the acting `X-User-ID`; the score and grade are copied onto the module as `second_life_score` and `grade`; the module's `version` increments by 1; and the module is reserved for whole-module refurbishment and is **not** chargeable into a recovery lot. When the resolved `disposition` is `SECOND_LIFE`, the module moves `PENDING_TRIAGE → SECOND_LIFE_QUEUE`; a decision row is created with `status` `ACTIVE`, its `score`, `grade`, `derived_disposition` and `disposition` recorded, and `decided_by_user_id` taken from the acting `X-User-ID`; the score and grade are copied onto the module as `second_life_score` and `grade`; the module's `version` increments by 1; and the module is reserved for stationary storage assembly and is likewise **not** chargeable into a recovery lot. When the resolved `disposition` is `MATERIAL_RECOVERY`, the module moves `PENDING_TRIAGE → RECOVERY_QUEUE`; a decision row is created with `status` `ACTIVE`, its `score`, `grade`, `derived_disposition` and `disposition` recorded, and `decided_by_user_id` taken from the acting `X-User-ID`; the score and grade are copied onto the module as `second_life_score` and `grade`; the module's `version` increments by 1; and the module becomes chargeable into a recovery lot, provided the campaign's chemistry family matches the family its `chemistry` maps to. Only this third branch feeds mass into the recovery ledger. A triage decision's `status` is `ACTIVE` until a manifest-rejection cascade voids it, at which point it becomes `VOIDED`.

Modules that took either of the first two branches leave the plant whole. Shipping such a module is a `PlantOperator` action carrying the `customer_name` of the receiving downstream customer, which is recorded on the module as `shipped_to`. It requires the module's `status` to be `REFURB_QUEUE` or `SECOND_LIFE_QUEUE` — anything else is refused with `ERR_MODULE_NOT_SHIPPABLE` — and it requires the module's shipment to have `custody_state` `RELEASED`, failing with `ERR_INTAKE_NOT_RELEASED` otherwise. On success the module moves to `SHIPPED`, which together with `CONSUMED` is a terminal module state, and its `version` increments by 1. Modules can be listed and filtered by `intake_id`, `lot_id` and `status`, and each module's decision history can be listed and filtered by decision `status`.

### 3.5. Release and the Provisional Feedstock Payable

Release is the hinge of the whole system: it is the moment the recycler accepts the shipment as its own and simultaneously incurs an obligation to pay for it. A `PlantOperator` releases a shipment with no payload at all, and four preconditions must hold. The shipment's `custody_state` must be `PACKAGED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CUSTODY_STATE`. Its `compliance_state` must be `MANIFEST_CLOSED` — this is the one cross-dimension guard in the custody machine, and violating it yields `ERR_INVALID_COMPLIANCE_STATE`. The counterparty's `credit_hold` must be false, otherwise `ERR_SUPPLIER_CREDIT_HOLD`. And a published index quote must exist with an `effective_date` at or before the shipment's `received_at` for every payable metal whose priced contained mass would be greater than zero, otherwise `ERR_INDEX_PRICE_UNAVAILABLE`. On success `custody_state` moves `PACKAGED → RELEASED`, `released_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the shipment's `version` increments by 1.

Release also **cuts a settlement**, as a cascade-created child that never appears in the release response. The new settlement records the `intake_id` of the shipment it pays for and a `supplier_id` copied from the shipment's own `supplier_id`; it starts with `variant` `PROVISIONAL` and `state` `DRAFT`, and its `price_basis_date` is set to the shipment's `received_at`. Its `posted_amount_cents` is 0, its `dispute_round` is 0, and `max_variance_pp`, `dispute_reason`, `umpire_metal`, `umpire_contained_kg`, `payment_reference` and `void_reason` are all null. The only way to find it is to list settlements filtered by `intake_id`; the same list also accepts `supplier_id`, `variant` and `state` filters. A settlement's `state` moves through `DRAFT`, `POSTED`, `DISPUTED`, `UMPIRE_ROUND`, and terminally `SETTLED` or `VOIDED`.

Pricing the provisional settlement walks the four **payable metals in the fixed order `NI`, `CO`, `LI`, `CU`**. The `MN` and `GRAPHITE` streams are recovered by the plant but are never payable to the supplier, and they take no part in this calculation. For each payable metal in turn under the `PROVISIONAL` variant, the contained mass is the shipment's `net_mass_kg` multiplied by that metal's declared percentage — `declared_ni_pct`, `declared_co_pct`, `declared_li_pct` or `declared_cu_pct` respectively — divided by one hundred, rounded half up to three decimals. A recovery factor is then looked up from the nominal recovery table keyed on the shipment's `identified_chemistry`: an `NMC622` shipment recovers 95 percent of its nickel, 95 percent of its cobalt, 80 percent of its lithium and 90 percent of its copper; an `NCA` shipment recovers 95 percent of its nickel, 90 percent of its cobalt, 80 percent of its lithium and 90 percent of its copper; an `LMO` shipment recovers no nickel and no cobalt at all, 75 percent of its lithium and 90 percent of its copper; and an `LFP` shipment likewise recovers no nickel and no cobalt, 75 percent of its lithium and 90 percent of its copper. The price date under this variant is the shipment's `received_at`.

The per-metal line then resolves as follows. If the contained mass multiplied by the recovery factor comes to zero, the line amount is zero cents, its `unit_price_cents_per_kg` is reported as **null** rather than 0, and **no index lookup is performed at all** — this is why a chemistry with no nickel content never triggers a missing-quote failure for nickel. Otherwise the server selects the applicable `unit_price_cents_per_kg` by taking the published quotes for that metal whose `effective_date` is at or before the price date, ordering them by `effective_date` descending and then by `created_at` descending, and taking the first; if no such quote exists the whole request fails with `ERR_INDEX_PRICE_UNAVAILABLE`. The line amount is then the contained mass multiplied by the recovery factor, multiplied by the unit price in cents per kilogram, multiplied by that metal's payable share, rounded half up to a whole number of cents. The payable shares are fixed by contract: 70 percent for `NI`, 70 percent for `CO`, 55 percent for `LI` and 40 percent for `CU`. Each metal contributes one entry to the settlement's `line_breakdown` array, and each entry carries `metal`, `contained_kg`, `unit_price_cents_per_kg`, `payable_share` and `line_cents`. The settlement's `gross_cents` is the sum of the `line_cents` values, each already rounded to whole cents at its own line before the sum is taken.

From the gross figure, a strict seven-step waterfall produces the amount actually payable, and the order is mandatory. **Step one** takes `gross_cents` from the pricing above. **Step two** selects `processing_fee_pct` from a three-band tier table read on `gross_cents`: a gross below 500,000 cents attracts 12 percent, a gross of at least 500,000 cents and below 2,000,000 cents attracts 9 percent, and a gross of 2,000,000 cents or more attracts 6 percent. **Step three** computes `processing_fee_cents` as `gross_cents` multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to a whole cent. **Step four** computes `quality_deduction_pct` as described below and then `quality_deduction_cents` as `gross_cents` multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to a whole cent. **Step five** computes `pre_surcharge_net_cents` as `gross_cents` less `processing_fee_cents` less `quality_deduction_cents`. **Step six** computes `hazmat_surcharge_cents` as the smaller of two candidates: a fixed 2,500 cents plus the shipment's `net_mass_kg` multiplied by 6 cents per kilogram and rounded half up to a whole cent, or `pre_surcharge_net_cents` multiplied by 5 percent and rounded half up to a whole cent. Taking the cap on the pre-surcharge figure rather than on the final net is what resolves what would otherwise be a circular definition. **Step seven** computes `net_payable_cents` as `pre_surcharge_net_cents` less `hazmat_surcharge_cents`. The whole waterfall reads exactly one settlement row plus its shipment and its supplier; nothing is aggregated across settlements.

The quality deduction is the non-linear part of the waterfall: three additive components, each individually capped, then a multiplicative escalation gated on the supplier's history, then a global cap. It begins at zero. If the shipment's `identified_chemistry` differs from its `declared_chemistry`, a flat mislabelling penalty of four percent — 0.04 as a fraction — is added. Then the moisture component: the excess moisture is the shipment's `moisture_pct` less a free allowance of 2.0 percentage points, floored at zero, and the component is that excess multiplied by one percent per percentage point, capped at six percent, and added. Then the foreign-material component: the excess is the shipment's `foreign_material_pct` less a free allowance of 1.5 percentage points, floored at zero, and the component is that excess multiplied by two percent per percentage point, capped at five percent, and added. If the counterparty's `quality_strike_count` is at least 3, the accumulated total is then multiplied by 1.5 as a repeat-offender escalation. Finally the result is capped at twenty percent overall and rounded half up to four decimal places. The supplier's strike count is read at the **start** of the request, before any strike the same request might add later, so the calculation never observes its own side effect.

### 3.6. Campaign Scheduling and Line Occupation

Material recovery runs in campaigns, and a campaign is fundamentally a **booking of a line for a window of time**. A `PlantOperator` schedules one by naming the `line_id` of the line to occupy, the `chemistry_family` the campaign will run, a `window_start` which is the inclusive start of the occupation interval, a `window_end` which is the exclusive end and must be strictly after the start, and a `planned_input_kg` greater than zero stating the feedstock mass the campaign intends to charge. The campaign's `chemistry_family` must equal the line's current `chemistry_family`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_FAMILY_MISMATCH` — a tier-12 link-integrity check, so it is evaluated **last**, only once the three geometric checks described next have all passed.

Three geometric checks then run against the line's existing bookings. The candidate interval is half-open, covering its start instant and excluding its end instant. The incumbents are every campaign on the **same** line whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING`, considered in order of `window_start` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; campaigns that are `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` have released their windows and are ignored entirely. An incumbent conflicts when the candidate's start falls strictly before the incumbent's end **and** the incumbent's start falls strictly before the candidate's end, which is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_WINDOW_CONFLICT`; exact adjacency, where the candidate starts at precisely the instant an incumbent ends, touches without overlapping and is accepted. Next, for every incumbent whose `chemistry_family` differs from the candidate's, the gap between them is measured in hours — the candidate's start less the incumbent's end when the candidate comes after, or the incumbent's start less the candidate's end when the candidate comes before — and a gap of fewer hours than the line's `changeover_buffer_hours` is refused with `ERR_CHANGEOVER_BUFFER_VIOLATION`. Neighbours of the **same** family need no gap at all: zero hours between them is accepted. Instants are compared at millisecond resolution and the gap is treated as an exact rational number of hours, not rounded to whole hours. Finally, capacity: the window length in hours multiplied by the line's `throughput_kg_per_hour` is the most the line can physically process, and a `planned_input_kg` strictly greater than that product is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; a planned input exactly equal to the product is accepted.

A campaign that survives all three checks is created with `status` `SCHEDULED`, `committed_input_kg` 0.0 and `cancel_reason` null. Its `status` may take the values `SCHEDULED`, `RUNNING`, `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED`, with `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` terminal. `committed_input_kg` is maintained by the system as the sum of `charged_mass_kg` over the campaign's lots whose `status` is `OPEN`, `CHARGED` or `FINALIZED`, rounded half up to three decimals.

Starting a campaign advances it `SCHEDULED → RUNNING` and takes no payload. It requires `status` to be `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_SCHEDULED`; it requires the line's `status` to be `AVAILABLE`, since a line in `MAINTENANCE` accepts no start and yields `ERR_LINE_NOT_AVAILABLE`; and it requires the current clock position to be strictly before the campaign's `window_end`, otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_WINDOW_CLOSED`. On success the campaign's `version` increments by 1.

Cancellation is the alternative exit from `SCHEDULED`. It carries a `cancel_reason`, requires `status` to be `SCHEDULED`, and additionally requires that no lot of the campaign has `status` `CHARGED` or `FINALIZED`; both failures report `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_SCHEDULED`. On success the campaign moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, the `cancel_reason` is recorded, every lot of the campaign still in `OPEN` becomes `VOIDED` with its `void_reason` set to `CAMPAIGN_CANCELLED`, the line's occupation window is released so a later campaign may reuse it, and the `version` of the campaign and of every voided lot increments by 1.

Lots are opened under a campaign by a `PlantOperator` supplying only a `lot_code`, the identifier printed on the charge sheet; opening is not gated on the campaign's `status` at all, so a lot may be opened under a campaign that is `SCHEDULED`, `RUNNING`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` — only the subsequent charge insists on a `RUNNING` campaign. A new lot records the `campaign_id` of its campaign and starts with `status` `OPEN`, `charged_mass_kg` 0.0 and `recovered_mass_kg` 0.0, while `residue_mass_kg`, `accounted_mass_kg`, `unaccounted_mass_kg`, `mass_balance_pct`, `recycled_content_pct`, `finalized_at` and `void_reason` are all null. Opening a lot increments the campaign's `version` by 1. A lot's `status` may be `OPEN`, `CHARGED`, `FINALIZED` or `VOIDED`, with the last two terminal.

Campaign state is partly **derived from its children**. When at least one lot exists under a `RUNNING` campaign and every one of that campaign's lots has reached `FINALIZED` or `VOIDED`, the campaign itself moves `RUNNING → CLOSED` and its `version` increments by 1. No request names the campaign to make this happen; it bubbles up from whichever lot transition made the last non-terminal lot terminal. Campaigns can be listed and filtered by `line_id` and `status`, and lots by `campaign_id` and `status`.

### 3.7. Charging, Assay Reconciliation and Lot Finalization

Charging is the transfer of physical custody of feedstock from the shipment ledger into the processing ledger. A `PlantOperator` charges a lot by supplying `module_ids`, a non-empty array of module identifiers — a repeated identifier is collapsed to a single occurrence before any check runs, so no module is ever counted or charged twice — together with the `expected_lot_version` described in §2.8. The checks run in the global precedence order of §2.7, which means the stale-version check is evaluated before any state check: a mismatch between `expected_lot_version` and the lot's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION`. Then the state checks: the lot's `status` must be `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_LOT_NOT_OPEN`; the campaign's `status` must be `RUNNING`, otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_RUNNING`; and every module named must have `status` `RECOVERY_QUEUE`, otherwise `ERR_MODULE_NOT_IN_RECOVERY_QUEUE`. Then the quantitative check, which as a tier-11 ceiling precedes the link-integrity checks: the campaign's current `committed_input_kg` plus the total mass of the modules being charged must be at most the campaign's `planned_input_kg`, and exceeding it is refused with `ERR_LOT_CHARGE_EXCEEDS_PLAN`. Finally the referential checks: every named module's shipment must have `custody_state` `RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_INTAKE_NOT_RELEASED`; and every named module's `chemistry` must map to the campaign's `chemistry_family` under the fixed family mapping — `NMC622`, `NCA` and `LMO` to `NICKEL_RICH`, `LFP` to `IRON_PHOSPHATE` — otherwise `ERR_MODULE_CHEMISTRY_MISMATCH`.

A successful charge moves the lot `OPEN → CHARGED` and moves every named module `RECOVERY_QUEUE → CHARGED` with its `lot_id` set to that lot. Four aggregates are then recomputed: the lot's `charged_mass_kg` as the sum of `mass_kg` over modules whose `lot_id` is this lot and whose `status` is `CHARGED` or `CONSUMED`, rounded half up to three decimals; each contributing shipment's `assigned_mass_kg` as the sum of `mass_kg` over its modules in `CHARGED` or `CONSUMED`, likewise to three decimals; each contributing shipment's `unassigned_mass_kg` as its `module_mass_total_kg` less its `assigned_mass_kg`; and the campaign's `committed_input_kg`. The `version` of the lot, of every module charged, of every shipment touched and of the campaign each increments by 1. A lot already in `CHARGED` accepts no further charge call — additional mass requires opening a new lot.

Assay lines are booked by an `AssayTechnician` against a charged lot, and the ledger is strictly append-only: there is no way to edit or delete a line once booked. Each line names its `metal` from the six recoverable streams, a `recovered_mass_kg` greater than zero giving the contained mass recovered in that stream, and an `assay_purity_pct` greater than 0.0 and at most 100.0. The acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `recorded_by_user_id`. Only one line may exist per combination of lot and `metal`; a second line for the same metal on the same lot is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The lot's `status` must be `CHARGED`, otherwise `ERR_LOT_NOT_CHARGED`. There is also a metallurgical restriction: nickel, cobalt and lithium can only be won by leaching, so a line booked for `NI`, `CO` or `LI` against a lot whose campaign runs on a `MECHANICAL` line is refused with `ERR_METAL_NOT_RECOVERABLE_ON_LINE`; `MN` and `GRAPHITE` streams carry no such restriction. Each accepted line recomputes the lot's `recovered_mass_kg` and increments the lot's `version` by 1. The assay ledger of a lot can be listed and filtered by `metal`.

Finalization closes the lot, reconciles its mass and re-prices everything downstream of it. The `AssayTechnician` supplies the weighed `residue_mass_kg` — the non-recovered residue coming off the process, a required figure of zero or more — and five preconditions must hold. The lot's `status` must be `CHARGED`, otherwise `ERR_LOT_NOT_CHARGED`. The lot must have at least one assay line, otherwise `ERR_NO_RECOVERY_OUTPUTS`. The computed mass balance must be at or above the tolerance floor, otherwise `ERR_MASS_BALANCE_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE`. Every contributing shipment's settlement must be in `state` `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_DRAFT` — the plant cannot re-price a settlement the accountants have already posted. And a published index quote with an `effective_date` at or before the current clock position must exist for every payable metal that has an assay line on this lot, otherwise `ERR_INDEX_PRICE_UNAVAILABLE`.

The mass reconciliation itself aggregates the whole assay ledger with no status filter, since the ledger is append-only and immutable, and it counts all six metal streams including the non-payable `MN` and `GRAPHITE`. The lot's `recovered_mass_kg` is the sum of `recovered_mass_kg` over every assay line of the lot, rounded half up to three decimals. Its `accounted_mass_kg` is that recovered figure plus the supplied `residue_mass_kg`, rounded half up to three decimals. Its `unaccounted_mass_kg` is the lot's `charged_mass_kg` less the accounted figure, rounded half up to three decimals. Its `mass_balance_pct` is the accounted figure divided by `charged_mass_kg`, multiplied by one hundred and rounded half up to two decimals. Its `recycled_content_pct` is the recovered figure divided by `charged_mass_kg`, multiplied by one hundred and rounded half up to two decimals. The gate is on `mass_balance_pct`, which must be at least 97.00 — a value of exactly 97.00 passes, and anything below it fails with `ERR_MASS_BALANCE_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE`.

Finalization is a four-link cascade with a conditional bubble-up. The first link writes the reconciliation onto the lot — `residue_mass_kg`, `recovered_mass_kg`, `accounted_mass_kg`, `unaccounted_mass_kg`, `mass_balance_pct`, `recycled_content_pct` — and stamps `finalized_at` from the logical clock, which becomes the final price basis date. The second link fires because the lot became `FINALIZED`: every module whose `lot_id` is this lot and whose `status` is `CHARGED` moves `CHARGED → CONSUMED`. The third link fires because those modules became `CONSUMED`: each contributing shipment's `DRAFT` settlement flips its `variant` `PROVISIONAL → FINAL`, is re-priced under the `FINAL` pricing rules and then through the seven-step waterfall of §3.5, has its `price_basis_date` reset to the lot's `finalized_at`, and has its `max_variance_pp` computed. The fourth link fires conditionally: for any settlement that lands in `FINAL` with a `max_variance_pp` strictly greater than 2.00 percentage points, that supplier's `quality_strike_count` increases by 1 — the same counter that escalates the quality deduction on the counterparty's future shipments. Then the bubble-up: if every lot of the campaign is now terminal and the campaign is `RUNNING`, the campaign moves to `CLOSED`. The `version` of the lot, and of every module, settlement, supplier and campaign row the cascade touched, each increments by 1.

The `FINAL` variant prices the same four payable metals in the same fixed order `NI`, `CO`, `LI`, `CU`, but it prices what was actually recovered rather than what was declared. For each payable metal, the lot's recovered figure for that metal is the `recovered_mass_kg` of the lot's assay line for that metal. Because a lot's charged mass can come from several shipments, that recovered figure is apportioned to each contributing shipment by that shipment's share of the lot: the sum of `mass_kg` over that shipment's modules whose `lot_id` is this lot and whose `status` is `CHARGED` or `CONSUMED`, divided by the lot's `charged_mass_kg`. The shipment's `contained_kg` for that metal is the lot's recovered figure multiplied by that share, rounded half up to three decimals. Where the pro-rata split leaves a rounding remainder, the shipment with the **latest** `created_at` absorbs it, so that the apportioned masses sum exactly to the lot's recovered figure for that metal; `created_at` is the tie-break input and the randomly minted `id` is never consulted. Under this variant the recovery factor is exactly 1.0, because actual yields are already net of recovery, and the price date is the lot's `finalized_at`. The per-metal line is then formed exactly as in §3.5 — a zero contained mass produces a zero line with no index lookup, otherwise the newest quote effective at or before the price date supplies `unit_price_cents_per_kg`, and the line amount is contained mass times the recovery factor times the unit price times the metal's payable share, rounded half up to whole cents before the sum. Identical feedstock therefore yields a different `gross_cents` under `FINAL` than under `PROVISIONAL` through three independent channels: the assay truth, the recovery factor, and the quote date.

The assay variance that gates disputes is computed at the same moment. For each of the four payable metals in the fixed order `NI`, `CO`, `LI`, `CU`, the final percentage is the shipment's `contained_kg` for that metal from the `FINAL` line breakdown, divided by the shipment's `net_mass_kg` and multiplied by one hundred; the per-metal variance in percentage points is the absolute difference between the shipment's declared percentage for that metal and that final percentage, rounded half up to two decimals. The settlement's `max_variance_pp` is the largest of those four per-metal variances, rounded to two decimals per metal before the maximum is taken; `MN` and `GRAPHITE` never participate. Two thresholds read that figure and both are **strict**: a dispute may be raised only when `max_variance_pp` is greater than 0.50, so exactly 0.50 is not disputable, and a quality strike is added only when `max_variance_pp` is greater than 2.00, so exactly 2.00 adds no strike. While a settlement is still `PROVISIONAL`, `max_variance_pp` is null.

Voiding a lot is the compensating action for charging, and it accepts a `void_reason` while the lot's `status` is either `OPEN` or `CHARGED`; any other status is refused with `ERR_LOT_NOT_OPEN`. Voiding an `OPEN` lot records the `void_reason`, subtracts the lot's `charged_mass_kg` from the campaign's `committed_input_kg`, resets `charged_mass_kg` to 0.0, and increments the lot's `version` by 1. Voiding a `CHARGED` lot does all of that and unwinds the charge as well: every module whose `lot_id` is this lot and whose `status` is `CHARGED` reverts to `RECOVERY_QUEUE` with its `lot_id` reset to null; for each affected shipment, `assigned_mass_kg` decreases and `unassigned_mass_kg` increases by that module's `mass_kg`, keeping the sum of assigned and unassigned equal to `module_mass_total_kg` at all times; the released mass is subtracted from the campaign's `committed_input_kg` and the lot's `charged_mass_kg` is reset to 0.0; and the `version` of the lot, of every module, of every shipment and of the campaign touched each increments by 1. A lot that is already `FINALIZED` cannot be voided, and modules already `CONSUMED` are never returned.

### 3.8. Downstream Certification and Environmental Discharge Compliance

Downstream cathode and cell customers buy recycled content, and they buy it on paper as much as in a drum. A `CertificationOfficer` issues a battery passport against a finalized lot, supplying a `passport_code` — the certificate code handed to the customer, which must be globally unique, a duplicate being refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and the `customer_name` of the downstream buyer. Issuance is refused with `ERR_PASSPORT_ALREADY_ISSUED` when the lot already has a passport whose `status` is `ISSUED`, and then — the ceiling check being a later tier — with `ERR_RECYCLED_CONTENT_BELOW_MINIMUM` when the lot's `recycled_content_pct` is below 35.00. Issuance is not otherwise gated on the lot's `status`: because `recycled_content_pct` stays null until finalization, a lot that has not been finalized is itself refused with `ERR_RECYCLED_CONTENT_BELOW_MINIMUM`. On success the certificate copies the lot's `recycled_content_pct` verbatim and builds a `metal_content` array with one object per assay line of that lot in assay-ledger order, oldest line first, each carrying `metal`, `recovered_mass_kg` and `assay_purity_pct`; its `status` is `ISSUED`, its `issued_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and its `revoked_reason` is null. Revoking a certificate carries a `revoked_reason`, requires `status` to be `ISSUED` — otherwise `ERR_PASSPORT_NOT_ISSUED` — moves it `ISSUED → REVOKED`, and increments its `version` by 1. Because a revoked certificate no longer counts as the lot's issued passport, a replacement may then be issued for the same lot under a new `passport_code`. Passports can be listed and filtered by `lot_id` and `status`.

Air and water discharges are governed by permits, which a `ComplianceOfficer` records against a campus. An allotment names the `facility_id` of the permitted campus, the `medium` it governs as either `AIR` or `WATER`, the regulator-issued `permit_no`, a `valid_from` giving the inclusive first instant covered, a `valid_to` giving the inclusive last instant covered which must be strictly after `valid_from`, and a `daily_cap_kg` greater than zero expressing the maximum discharge mass permitted per UTC calendar day. A new allotment starts with `status` `ACTIVE`, `consumed_kg` 0.0 and `suspend_reason` null. Its `status` may be `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `EXPIRED`, with `EXPIRED` terminal, and `consumed_kg` is maintained as the sum of `mass_kg` over that allotment's discharge events whose `status` is `POSTED`, rounded half up to three decimals. Suspending an allotment after a regulator finding carries a `suspend_reason`, requires `status` to be `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE`, the same code a booking against a non-active allotment reports — moves it `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and increments its `version` by 1. Allotments can be listed and filtered by `facility_id`, `medium` and `status`.

Expiry is **lazy and clock-driven**, never scheduled. At the very start of a discharge-event booking, the server evaluates the target allotment: if its `status` is `ACTIVE` and the current clock position is strictly later than its `valid_to`, the allotment moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` and its `version` increments by 1, and the triggering booking is then rejected with `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The same rejection code covers a booking against an allotment that is already `SUSPENDED` or already `EXPIRED`. Since the clock only ever moves because an administrator moved it, this behaviour is fully deterministic.

A discharge event is booked by a `ComplianceOfficer` against a lot, naming the `allotment_id` of the permit it is charged to, the `lot_id` of the lot whose residue or effluent it came from, an `event_date` giving the instant of discharge, a `mass_kg` greater than zero, and the regulated `analyte` as one of `NICKEL_DISSOLVED`, `FLUORIDE` or `TSS`. Two gates run before the row is created. The window gate is a **closed** interval with both ends inclusive: the `event_date` must fall at or after `valid_from` and at or before `valid_to`, and falling outside is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_WINDOW_EXCLUDED`. The daily gate then buckets by the **UTC calendar date** of the `event_date`: the server sums `mass_kg` over the discharge events of that same allotment whose `status` is `POSTED` and whose `event_date` falls on the same UTC calendar date, rounded half up to three decimals, adds the request's `mass_kg`, rounds the total half up to three decimals, and refuses with `ERR_DISCHARGE_DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED` when that total is **strictly greater** than the allotment's `daily_cap_kg` — a total exactly equal to the cap is accepted. Events belonging to other allotments never participate, and `VOIDED` events are excluded from the bucket. Because the bucket key is the UTC date alone, a discharge at the last millisecond of one day and one at the first millisecond of the next fall into different buckets. On success the event is created with `status` `POSTED` and `void_reason` null, the allotment's `consumed_kg` increases by the event's `mass_kg` rounded half up to three decimals, and the allotment's `version` increments by 1.

Voiding a mis-booked event returns its allowance exactly. It carries a `void_reason`, requires the event's `status` to be `POSTED` — otherwise `ERR_DISCHARGE_EVENT_NOT_POSTED` — moves the event `POSTED → VOIDED`, subtracts its `mass_kg` from the allotment's `consumed_kg`, and increments both the event's `version` and the allotment's `version` by 1. Discharge events can be listed and filtered by `allotment_id`, `lot_id` and `status`.

### 3.9. The Payable Ledger: Posting, Disputes, Umpire Rounds and Cash

Everything upstream converges on a settlement, and a `SettlementAccountant` owns its money side. Posting a settlement takes no payload and requires its `state` to be `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_DRAFT`. Posting is a four-link cascade. The first link freezes `posted_amount_cents` at the current `net_payable_cents` — from this point on, the payable ledger is driven by the frozen figure, not by the live calculation. The second link fires because the settlement became `POSTED`: the supplier's `payable_balance_cents` increases by `posted_amount_cents`. The third link fires when that increase pushes `payable_balance_cents` above the supplier's `credit_limit_cents`: `credit_hold` flips from false to true. The fourth link fires because `credit_hold` became true: every shipment of that supplier whose `custody_state` is `RECEIVED` moves to `QUARANTINED` with `hold_reason` set to `SUPPLIER_CREDIT_HOLD`. Only shipments still sitting in `RECEIVED` are caught — material already past the identification bench keeps moving. The `version` of the settlement, of the supplier, and of every shipment quarantined each increments by 1.

Disputes belong to the counterparty. A `SupplierRep` disputes a posted settlement by supplying a `dispute_reason`, and five conditions must hold: the settlement's `state` must be `POSTED`, otherwise `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_POSTED`; the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the settlement's `supplier_id`, otherwise `ERR_NOT_OWNER`; the settlement's `dispute_round` must be below the limit of 3, otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, a state-legality check that therefore runs ahead of the tolerance test; and finally the settlement's `variant` must be `FINAL`, since there is nothing to argue about before actual yields exist, and its `max_variance_pp` must be strictly greater than 0.50 percentage points — a `PROVISIONAL` variant, a null variance and a within-tolerance variance are all refused with the same `ERR_VARIANCE_WITHIN_TOLERANCE`. On success the `dispute_reason` is recorded, `dispute_round` increases by 1, the settlement moves `POSTED → DISPUTED`, and its `version` increments by 1. Critically, the supplier's `payable_balance_cents` is left **untouched**: a disputed amount is still owed until it is re-priced or paid.

Resolving a dispute needs an independent laboratory. An `AssayTechnician` records the umpire result by supplying the `metal` re-assayed — one of `NI`, `CO`, `LI` or `CU` — and the `umpire_contained_kg` the umpire found, a figure of zero or more. The settlement's `state` must be `DISPUTED`, otherwise `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_DISPUTED`, and an assay line must then already exist for the requested metal on the contributing lot — the lot whose finalization re-priced this settlement — a metal with no such line being refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, checked after the state gate rather than before it. On success `umpire_metal` and `umpire_contained_kg` are recorded, the settlement moves `DISPUTED → UMPIRE_ROUND`, and its `version` increments by 1. The technician who records the umpire figure cannot act on the money it changes: applying the result is a `SettlementAccountant` capability.

Applying the umpire result closes the settlement cycle by returning it to the beginning. It takes no payload and requires the settlement's `state` to be `UMPIRE_ROUND`, otherwise `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_IN_UMPIRE_ROUND`. The settlement is re-priced under the `FINAL` rules with one substitution: for the metal named in `umpire_metal`, the lot's recovered figure is replaced by `umpire_contained_kg` before apportionment, while the other three payable metals are priced from the assay ledger as usual. The seven-step waterfall then reruns, and the quality deduction is recomputed from the shipment and supplier as they stand at the start of that request. On the ledger side, the supplier's `payable_balance_cents` decreases by the **old** `posted_amount_cents` and `posted_amount_cents` resets to 0. If that leaves `payable_balance_cents` at or below `credit_limit_cents`, `credit_hold` flips from true to false, and every shipment of that supplier whose `custody_state` is `QUARANTINED` with `hold_reason` `SUPPLIER_CREDIT_HOLD` returns to `RECEIVED` with `hold_reason` cleared to null — note that a credit-hold quarantine releases back to `RECEIVED`, whereas a manifest-rejection quarantine releases to `IDENTIFIED`. The settlement itself moves `UMPIRE_ROUND → DRAFT`, ready to be posted again at the new figure. The `version` of the settlement, of the supplier, and of every shipment released each increments by 1. The full commercial cycle is therefore `DRAFT → POSTED → DISPUTED → UMPIRE_ROUND → DRAFT`.

Cash settlement is the happy ending. It carries a `payment_reference` recording the cash payment, requires the settlement's `state` to be `POSTED` — otherwise `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_POSTED` — and moves it `POSTED → SETTLED`. The supplier's `paid_to_date_cents` increases by `posted_amount_cents` and its `payable_balance_cents` decreases by the same amount. If that brings `payable_balance_cents` to or below `credit_limit_cents`, `credit_hold` flips from true to false and every shipment of that supplier quarantined with `hold_reason` `SUPPLIER_CREDIT_HOLD` returns to `RECEIVED` with `hold_reason` cleared. The `version` of the settlement, of the supplier, and of every shipment released each increments by 1.

Voiding is the exact reversal of posting, and it exists so that the payable ledger can be corrected without inventing a compensating credit. It carries a `void_reason`, requires the settlement's `state` to be `POSTED` — otherwise `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_POSTED` — and moves it `POSTED → VOIDED`, which is terminal. The supplier's `payable_balance_cents` decreases by `posted_amount_cents` and `posted_amount_cents` resets to 0, which restores the supplier obligation invariant of §2.10 precisely. As with the other two reversals, if `payable_balance_cents` falls to or below `credit_limit_cents` then `credit_hold` flips from true to false and the credit-hold quarantines of that supplier are released back to `RECEIVED` with `hold_reason` cleared. The `version` of the settlement, of the supplier, and of every shipment released each increments by 1. Settlements are read individually with their full per-metal `line_breakdown`, and every derived money field — `gross_cents`, `processing_fee_pct`, `processing_fee_cents`, `quality_deduction_pct`, `quality_deduction_cents`, `pre_surcharge_net_cents`, `hazmat_surcharge_cents`, `net_payable_cents` and `posted_amount_cents` — is exposed on the settlement resource so that a counterparty can reproduce the arithmetic line by line.
