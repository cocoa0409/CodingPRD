# Public Records Redaction & Body-Worn Camera Release API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A municipal police records unit sits at the intersection of two obligations that pull in opposite directions. Statute requires that body-worn camera footage be released to the public on a fixed timetable, and statute equally requires that faces, licence plates, spoken identifiers, juvenile contacts, victim identities and confidential-informant material never leave the building unobscured. Between those two commands lies weeks of human labour: scoping a vague request down to an answerable set of recordings, viewing every minute of that footage, arguing exemption by exemption over what may be withheld, obscuring what must be obscured, proving the obscuration was done correctly, telling depicted people that their footage is about to be published, collecting the statutory cost-recovery fee without over-charging, and — when the answer is no — writing a denial letter that cites the correct statute and explains where to appeal.

This document specifies the API that runs that unit end to end. The system is deliberately built as an audit-grade record rather than a convenience tool: every fee movement is an append-only ledger line, every exemption decision names the attorney who made it and the attorney who did not, every redaction passes through a reviewer who did not perform it, and every deadline is computed from a stored anchor rather than from the moment someone happened to press a button. The two things the unit is most often accused of — missing a statutory deadline and releasing something that should have been withheld — are both modelled as first-class, machine-checked conditions.

Two structural properties of the design deserve early emphasis, because they shape almost every rule that follows. First, a request's records-processing lifecycle and its cost-recovery lifecycle are **independent dimensions** that advance on their own schedules and touch each other at exactly two pinned junctions. Second, the same recording can be responsive to many different requests at the same time, each with its own exemption posture, its own redaction work and its own release date; responsiveness is therefore a per-request relationship carrying its own state, never a property of the recording itself.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every endpoint in this specification is mounted beneath the `/records` prefix, with the sole exception of the clock-control surface described in §2.4.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — including the clock-control surface — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID` identifying the actor, and `X-User-Role` naming the capacity in which the actor is operating. If either header is absent or empty, if `X-User-Role` does not name one of the six roles declared below, or if the role is one of the entity-backed roles and `X-User-ID` resolves to no persisted row of its backing entity, the call is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When the headers are well-formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the addressed endpoint, the call is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `RecordsClerk`, `RecordsSupervisor`, `LegalCounsel`, `RedactionTechnician`, `QualityReviewer` and `Requester`. They are peers with deliberately disjoint exclusive surfaces rather than a privilege ladder: `RecordsSupervisor` is the unit's fiscal and destruction authority but does **not** inherit `LegalCounsel`'s adjudication surface, and `LegalCounsel` cannot invoice, reverse a ledger entry or destroy a recording.

- `RecordsClerk` is the intake and production desk. It registers filers and recordings, drives scope clarification, responsiveness determination, notification and release production. When `X-User-Role` is `RecordsClerk`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may accept and carry it but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- `RecordsSupervisor` is the unit supervisor. When `X-User-Role` is `RecordsSupervisor`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and is never compared to any entity's `id`.
- `LegalCounsel` is the attorney who asserts and adjudicates statutory exemptions, issues denial letters, decides appeals and manages litigation holds. When `X-User-Role` is `LegalCounsel`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `StaffMember`; ownership and segregation-of-duties checks on any resource that references a `StaffMember` compare the resource's staff reference field to `X-User-ID`. In particular the value is persisted as `asserted_by_staff_id` when a claim is asserted and as `adjudicated_by_staff_id` when a claim is adjudicated.
- `RedactionTechnician` is the redaction operator who opens tasks and submits finished work, and who may never approve it. When `X-User-Role` is `RedactionTechnician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `StaffMember`, and is compared against a task's `assigned_technician_id`.
- `QualityReviewer` passes or fails submitted redaction work and may never have performed the work under review. When `X-User-Role` is `QualityReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `StaffMember`, and is compared against a task's `qa_reviewer_id`.
- `Requester` is the filer. When `X-User-Role` is `Requester`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Requester` row; ownership checks compare a public records request's `requester_id` to `X-User-ID`.

The `Requester` ownership rule is enforced narrowly and specifically. On `GET /records/requests/{id}`, `GET /records/requests`, `POST /records/requests/{id}/payments` and `POST /records/denial-letters/{id}/appeal`, an actor presenting the `Requester` role may only reach a public records request whose `requester_id` equals the presented `X-User-ID`; any attempt to read or act on another filer's request is rejected with `ERR_REQUESTER_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). Note that this is a distinct failure from `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`: the role is permitted at the endpoint, but the addressed resource belongs to someone else.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query-string parameters. `limit` controls page size; it defaults to 10 when omitted, must be at least 1, and must be at most 100. `offset` controls how many rows are skipped before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and must not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range of 1 to 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema failure and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a particular endpoint's description states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest row first. When a list endpoint responds, its `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether further rows exist beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and Statutory Day Arithmetic

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. The server maintains a single logical clock whose value at startup is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and every deadline computation, objection-window comparison, purge eligibility test and audit timestamp reads that clock. A test-driving capability accepts a `current_utc` instant and moves the logical clock to it; the call mutates no entity, and subsequent reads of the clock return the supplied value. Because the clock is authoritative, an operation that "happens later" in a scenario happens later only because the clock was advanced between the two calls.

All statutory windows in this specification are expressed in **whole days added to a stored instant**, and the time-of-day component of the anchor instant is preserved exactly. A notice issued at `2026-08-01T14:22:00.000Z` with a ten-day window closes at `2026-08-11T14:22:00.000Z`, not at midnight. Where a rule says a window is still open at its deadline, the comparison is inclusive of the deadline instant itself; where a rule says a waiting period has elapsed at its deadline, the comparison is likewise inclusive. Each such boundary is stated explicitly at the workflow that owns it, because the two conventions deliberately meet at the same instant in the release path.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and Left-to-Right Path Resolution

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` in canonical UUID form with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp recorded as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Almost every resource additionally carries an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of that resource, and a `version` integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on each mutation. The single exception is the cost-recovery ledger entry: because the ledger is append-only and a posted line is never edited, a ledger entry carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and neither field appears in its representation.

Endpoint paths nest resources, and the identifiers embedded in a path are resolved strictly left to right; the first identifier that resolves to nothing determines the reported error, and no later identifier or body field is examined. The mapping from path segment to error is fixed: a missing `/requests/{id}` yields `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `/video-assets/{id}` yields `ERR_VIDEO_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `/responsive-records/{id}` yields `ERR_RESPONSIVE_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `/exemption-claims/{id}` yields `ERR_EXEMPTION_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `/redaction-tasks/{id}` yields `ERR_REDACTION_TASK_NOT_FOUND`, `/depicted-individuals/{id}` yields `ERR_DEPICTED_INDIVIDUAL_NOT_FOUND`, `/denial-letters/{id}` yields `ERR_DENIAL_LETTER_NOT_FOUND`, `/litigation-holds/{id}` yields `ERR_LITIGATION_HOLD_NOT_FOUND` and `/fee-ledger-entries/{id}` yields `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`. All nine carry HTTP 404. A staff member addressed by path identifier that resolves to no row yields `ERR_STAFF_NOT_FOUND`, also HTTP 404.

Foreign keys supplied inside a request body are resolved after the path, and each has its own code: an unknown `requester_id` yields `ERR_REQUESTER_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `video_asset_id` yields `ERR_VIDEO_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `exemption_claim_id` yields `ERR_EXEMPTION_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `depicted_individual_id` yields `ERR_DEPICTED_INDIVIDUAL_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `responsive_record_id` yields `ERR_RESPONSIVE_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`; and an unknown `assigned_technician_id`, `qa_reviewer_id` or `asserted_by_staff_id` yields `ERR_STAFF_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on the Public Records Request

Two operations mutate a public records request in ways that cannot be guarded by a state precondition, because both are legal from the state the request is already in and both overwrite information the caller believes it has read. Both therefore require the caller to declare the `version` it last observed, in a non-optional input field named `expected_request_version`.

The first is the scope-clarification write, `POST /records/requests/{id}/scope-clarification`. A clarification replaces the stored scope narrative and requested date window wholesale and is legal from both `INTAKE` and `SCOPING`, so a caller holding a stale view would silently overwrite a newer clarification with the scope it read earlier. The request must therefore carry `expected_request_version` matching the addressed request's current `version`; if the two differ the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REQUEST_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

The second is the fee-estimate write, `POST /records/requests/{id}/fee-estimates`. A new estimate legitimately supersedes the prior active one, so the operation cannot be blocked by a state check; a clerk holding a stale view of the request would price it against superseded scope, burden or waiver inputs. This call likewise requires `expected_request_version` to match the addressed request's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REQUEST_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint takes a version field. Every other write is guarded by its own state preconditions, and supplying a version to those endpoints is neither required nor meaningful.

### 2.7. Parallel Request Dimensions, Cascades and Derived-Field Reconciliation

A public records request advances along two independent dimensions. The first, `processing_status`, is the records-processing lifecycle and takes one of `INTAKE`, `SCOPING`, `EXEMPTION_REVIEW`, `REDACTION`, `RELEASE_READY`, `PARTIALLY_RELEASED`, `RELEASED`, `DENIED` or `CLOSED`; it begins at `INTAKE` and `CLOSED` is its only terminal value. The second, `fee_status`, is the cost-recovery lifecycle and takes one of `NOT_ASSESSED`, `ESTIMATED`, `DEPOSIT_DUE`, `DEPOSIT_PAID`, `INVOICED`, `SETTLED` or `WAIVED`; it begins at `NOT_ASSESSED` and `WAIVED` is its only terminal value. Neither dimension constrains the other except at two pinned junctions, both described in §3.8: a request that has reached `RELEASED` advances to `CLOSED` once its fee dimension reaches `SETTLED` or `WAIVED`, and a reversal that undoes a settled charge drops the fee dimension from `SETTLED` back to `INVOICED` without touching `processing_status` at all.

Cascades are immediate and complete within the call that triggers them. When an operation mutates several entities, every one of those mutations is visible to the very next read, and every derived field on every touched entity is brought into agreement before the response is written. There is no deferred settlement and no eventual consistency anywhere in this system.

Derived fields are **reconciled, not accumulated**. Every count, sum and posture field described in §3 is defined as a query over the current rows that satisfy its stated filter, and the implementation must produce the same answer that re-running that query would produce. This matters most where work is undone: a redaction task that is submitted for review and then rejected must leave the enclosing request's `billable_labor_minutes` at exactly the value it held before the submission, and a task that passes review and is later cancelled must restore the pre-approval value exactly. Incremental bookkeeping that adds on the way in and forgets to subtract on the way out is detectable as drift and is incorrect.

Mutation bookkeeping follows one rule with no exceptions: whenever a resource's own fields change — including when they change only because a cascade reached it — that resource's `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock and its `version` increases by 1. Where a transition description below says a cascade "bumps the request's version", it means exactly this single increment for that call.

### 2.8. Cost-Recovery Arithmetic: Cents, Minutes and Rounding

Every monetary field in this system is an integer count of cents. Fractional cents are never stored, never returned and never carried between steps of a calculation. Where a formula divides, the division is performed in exact arithmetic and the result is rounded only at the point the specification names.

Two rounding operations appear throughout. Rounding **half up** yields the nearest integer, with a value exactly halfway between two integers going to the one further from zero. Rounding **up** — the ceiling — yields the smallest integer that is not less than the value, and is used wherever a partial minute of footage, a partial minute of labour or a partial unit of media must be paid for in full. Percentages such as the statutory waiver fraction are the only non-integer quantities the system stores, and they are carried to two decimal places.

Where the specification says a total is rounded once, rounding once is part of the contract. In particular, the final invoice rounds the money for **each** approved redaction task individually and only then adds the results; rounding the aggregate instead produces a different figure, and the difference is observable. Two five-minute licence-plate tasks are 298 cents plus 298 cents, which is 596 cents, not the 595 cents that an aggregate rounding would yield.

The cost-recovery ledger obeys a conservation invariant at every observable instant. Reading the request's `deposit_balance_cents` must always give the same number as taking `deposit_paid_cents`, adding `waiver_credit_total_cents`, and subtracting `fee_recognized_cents`. A charge followed by its reversal returns `deposit_balance_cents` to the exact pre-charge value with zero drift, while the number of ledger rows grows by two.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties and Redaction Certification

The unit is organised around the principle that no person signs off on their own work. Four separations are enforced, and all four report `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). An attorney may not adjudicate an exemption claim they themselves asserted, so the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the claim's `asserted_by_staff_id` — a condition that is vacuously satisfied when `asserted_by_staff_id` is null, as it is for claims that materialised from a depicted individual's objection. A quality reviewer may not pass or fail a task whose `assigned_technician_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`. A technician submitting work may not nominate themselves, so the `qa_reviewer_id` they supply must differ from the task's `assigned_technician_id`. And an attorney may not decide the appeal of a denial letter they themselves issued.

Certification is the second gate on redaction work. Each `StaffMember` carries a `redaction_certification_level` between 0 and 3 inclusive, where 0 means uncertified, 1 clears faces and licence plates, 2 clears audio identifiers and protected content, and 3 additionally clears juvenile material. The requirement for a given task is derived from what the task obscures and from the exemption it implements: a task whose `target_type` is `FACE` or `LICENSE_PLATE` requires level 1 as its base, any other `target_type` requires level 2 as its base, and if the task names an `exemption_claim_id` whose claim carries `exemption_code` `JUVENILE` the requirement is raised to 3 regardless of target. The technician nominated on the task must hold at least the derived requirement. The quality reviewer must hold at least the greater of the derived requirement and 2, so that even a plain face-blur task is reviewed by someone cleared for audio and protected content. Failing either comparison is rejected with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422); the comparison is "at least", so a technician whose level exactly equals the requirement is acceptable.

### 2.10. Uniqueness and Cross-Parent Coherence

Three uniqueness rules protect the case file from duplicate rows. A recording may be attached to a given request only once, so a second attempt to create a responsive record for the same combination of `request_id` and `video_asset_id` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESPONSIVE_RECORD` (HTTP 409). A `matter_number` is globally unique across every litigation hold ever placed, and a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_MATTER_NUMBER` (HTTP 409). A `display_label` is unique within one recording, so two depicted individuals on the same `video_asset_id` may not share a label; a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DEPICTED_LABEL` (HTTP 409). No separate duplicate-objection code exists, because a depicted individual can only leave `NOTIFIED` for `OBJECTED` once and the state check already forbids a second attempt.

Four write operations accept a reference that must belong to the same parent as the resource being addressed, and all four report a coherence failure as `ERR_CLAIM_RECORD_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). When a redaction task names an `exemption_claim_id`, that claim's `responsive_record_id` must equal the record named in the path. When an exemption claim is asserted, it must be asserted against the record named in the path. When an objection is recorded, the supplied `responsive_record_id` must reference a responsive record whose `video_asset_id` equals the objecting individual's own `video_asset_id` — an individual depicted in one recording cannot object to the release of a different recording. And when a ledger entry is reversed, the target entry named by `reverses_entry_id` must belong to the same request.

### 2.11. The Order in Which Rejections Are Reported

Several conditions can fail on the same call. The system evaluates them as a single ordered ladder and stops at the first violation, so the reported `error_id` is fully determined by the ladder position rather than by which check happens to be cheapest. The order is authoritative and is as follows.

1. Authentication, as described in §2.1 — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. This applies to every endpoint, including the clock-control surface.
2. Role authorisation against the endpoint's role list — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. Schema validation: the body parses, every required input field is present and non-null, every enumeration field holds a declared value, every integer field is an integer inside its declared range, and pagination parameters are in range — `ERR_VALIDATION`. Applies to every endpoint.
4. Existence of every identifier embedded in the path, resolved left to right, with the per-segment mapping of §2.5 — `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VIDEO_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESPONSIVE_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXEMPTION_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REDACTION_TASK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEPICTED_INDIVIDUAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DENIAL_LETTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LITIGATION_HOLD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_STAFF_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint carrying a path identifier.
5. Existence of every foreign key supplied in the body, with the per-field mapping of §2.5 — `ERR_REQUESTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VIDEO_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXEMPTION_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEPICTED_INDIVIDUAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESPONSIVE_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_STAFF_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint whose input includes a foreign key.
6. Requester ownership on the four `Requester`-reachable request surfaces — `ERR_REQUESTER_MISMATCH`.
7. Version agreement on the two optimistic-concurrency writes of §2.6 — `ERR_STALE_REQUEST_VERSION`.
8. State admissibility: the addressed entity is in a state from which the requested transition exists. The code depends on which dimension refused — a `processing_status` refusal is `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`, a `fee_status` refusal is `ERR_FEE_STATE_INVALID`, an `inclusion_status` refusal is `ERR_RECORD_STATE_INVALID`, adjudicating a claim whose `status` is no longer `ASSERTED` is `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`, a redaction-task `status` refusal is `ERR_TASK_STATE_INVALID`, a `notification_status` refusal is `ERR_NOTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID`, a denial-letter `status` refusal is `ERR_LETTER_STATE_INVALID`, a `disposition_state` refusal is `ERR_ASSET_STATE_INVALID`, and a litigation-hold `status` refusal is `ERR_HOLD_STATE_INVALID`. All nine carry HTTP 409. This step applies to every write endpoint that drives a transition.
9. Release and invoice readiness — `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_RELEASE_READY`, on `POST /records/requests/{id}/release-packages` and `POST /records/requests/{id}/invoice`. Producing a package requires `processing_status` to be `RELEASE_READY` or `PARTIALLY_RELEASED`; invoicing requires it to be `RELEASED`, `PARTIALLY_RELEASED` or `DENIED`. **This step sits deliberately above every fiscal check**, so a release attempt on a request that is both not release-ready and carrying an unpaid deposit reports `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_RELEASE_READY` and never `ERR_DEPOSIT_UNPAID`.
10. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, on the adjudication, submit-for-review, quality-verdict and appeal-decision writes described in §2.9.
11. Certification sufficiency — `ERR_CERTIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT`, on redaction-task creation and on submission for quality review.
12. Release posture — `ERR_RECORD_RELEASE_BLOCKED`, on redaction-task creation and on release-package production. **This step sits deliberately above the clip-overlap check**, so an attempt to open overlapping redaction work on a record whose `release_posture` is `BLOCKED` reports `ERR_RECORD_RELEASE_BLOCKED` and never `ERR_CLIP_OVERLAP`.
13. Unadjudicated exemption claims — `ERR_UNADJUDICATED_CLAIM`, on release-package production, denial-letter issuance and the quality verdict.
14. Unresolved notifications and objections — `ERR_OBJECTION_UNRESOLVED`, on release-package production.
15. Retention holds — `ERR_RETENTION_HOLD_ACTIVE`, on recording destruction and on release-package production.
16. Cross-parent coherence — `ERR_CLAIM_RECORD_MISMATCH`, on the four writes named in §2.10.
17. Uniqueness, as named in §2.10 — `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESPONSIVE_RECORD` on responsive-record creation, `ERR_DUPLICATE_MATTER_NUMBER` on litigation-hold placement and `ERR_DUPLICATE_DEPICTED_LABEL` on depicted-individual creation; objection recording carries no duplicate code of its own and is refused by the state check instead.
18. Statutory citation agreement — `ERR_CITATION_MISMATCH`, on exemption-claim assertion.
19. Estimate basis permission — `ERR_ESTIMATE_BASIS_NOT_PERMITTED`, on fee-estimate creation.
20. Clip range sanity — `ERR_INVALID_CLIP_RANGE`, on redaction-task creation.
21. Clip overlap — `ERR_CLIP_OVERLAP`, on redaction-task creation.
22. Open-request limit for commercial filers — `ERR_OPEN_REQUEST_LIMIT`, on request intake.
23. Scope-clarification limit — `ERR_SCOPE_CLARIFICATION_LIMIT`, on scope clarification.
24. Non-empty releasable set — `ERR_NO_RELEASABLE_RECORDS`, on release-package production.
25. Ordered instants — `ERR_INVALID_TIME_RANGE`, on request intake, scope clarification and submission for quality review.
26. Reviewer window conflict — `ERR_QA_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, on submission for quality review.
27. Reviewer concurrent load — `ERR_QA_LOAD_EXCEEDED`, on submission for quality review. It is evaluated **after** the window conflict, so a reviewer who is both double-booked in time and at capacity reports the window conflict.
28. Open windows — `ERR_WINDOW_CLOSED`, on appeal filing and objection recording.
29. Purge due date — `ERR_PURGE_NOT_DUE`, on recording destruction.
30. Deposit satisfaction — `ERR_DEPOSIT_UNPAID`, on release-package production and redaction-task creation.
31. Positive payment amount — `ERR_INVALID_AMOUNT`, on payment posting.
32. Payment does not exceed what is owed — `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`, on payment posting.
33. Reversal target eligibility — `ERR_ENTRY_ALREADY_REVERSED`, on ledger reversal.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering the Records Unit and Its Filers

Before any request can be processed, the unit's own people must exist as records. Registering a staff member is reserved to a `RecordsSupervisor` and captures a `full_name`, a `role_type` drawn from `CLERK`, `TECHNICIAN`, `REVIEWER`, `COUNSEL` and `SUPERVISOR`, and the `redaction_certification_level` described in §2.9, which defaults to 0 when omitted and must be an integer from 0 through 3. A newly registered staff member begins with `open_qa_assignment_count` at 0; that counter tracks how many redaction tasks currently name this person as their booked quality reviewer and sit awaiting a verdict, and it is maintained by the review workflow of §3.6.

Filers are registered by either a `RecordsClerk` or a `RecordsSupervisor` and carry a `full_name`, an `email` used for estimates, notices and denial letters, a `requester_type` drawn from `MEDIA`, `INVOLVED_PARTY`, `ATTORNEY`, `GENERAL_PUBLIC` and `COMMERCIAL`, and an optional `indigency_certified` flag that defaults to false and records whether a certified indigency affidavit was filed. Registration freezes the filer's statutory waiver fraction once and for all: `fee_waiver_pct` is set to 1.00 when `indigency_certified` is true, otherwise to 0.25 when `requester_type` is `MEDIA`, otherwise to 0.00. It is carried to two decimal places and is never recomputed afterwards, even if the filer's circumstances change — a later affidavit means a new filer record, not an amended one. `open_request_count` starts at 0 and thereafter reflects the number of that filer's requests whose `processing_status` is none of `RELEASED`, `DENIED` and `CLOSED`.

### 3.2. Cataloguing Recordings, Retention Schedules and Litigation Holds

Cataloguing a body-worn camera recording is the clerk's or supervisor's first act on the evidence side. The payload names the `incident_number` the recording belongs to, the `device_serial` of the camera, the `officer_badge` of the officer wearing it, the `recorded_start_at` wall-clock instant at which recording began, the `duration_ms` length in milliseconds, which must be strictly greater than zero, the `classification` drawn from `ROUTINE`, `USE_OF_FORCE`, `JUVENILE_CONTACT`, `OFFICER_INVOLVED_SHOOTING` and `IN_CUSTODY_DEATH`, the `retention_category` drawn from `EVIDENTIARY_7Y`, `INVESTIGATIVE_3Y` and `NON_EVIDENTIARY_180D`, the `scheduled_purge_at` instant that is the earliest moment the retention schedule permits destruction, and an optional `is_critical_incident` flag defaulting to false that marks the recording as depicting a statutorily defined critical incident. A new recording opens with `disposition_state` at `SCHEDULED` and `depicted_individual_count` at 0.

Cataloguing immediately consults the outstanding legal holds. `retention_locked` on a recording is true exactly when at least one litigation hold with `status` `ACTIVE` carries a `scope_incident_number` equal to the recording's `incident_number`, or at least one responsive record references the recording with `inclusion_status` among `CANDIDATE`, `RESPONSIVE` and `WITHHELD_IN_FULL` on a request whose `processing_status` is none of `RELEASED`, `DENIED` and `CLOSED`. If an `ACTIVE` hold already covers the incident at the moment of cataloguing, the recording does not merely become locked: its `disposition_state` is set to `SUSPENDED`, its `suspended_by_hold_id` is pointed at that hold — the matching `ACTIVE` hold with the smallest `created_at` when more than one matches — and that hold's `suspended_asset_count` increases by 1.

People appearing in a recording are logged individually by a `RecordsClerk` or `LegalCounsel` against that recording, each with a non-identifying `display_label` such as SUBJECT-1 and a `relationship` drawn from `VICTIM`, `JUVENILE`, `WITNESS`, `OFFICER`, `BYSTANDER` and `SUSPECT`. Whether the law requires telling this person before their footage is published is derived, never supplied: `notification_required` is true exactly when `relationship` is `VICTIM`, `JUVENILE` or `WITNESS`. That derivation picks the individual's initial position directly — a row that requires notification is created at `notification_status` `PENDING`, and a row that does not is created at `notification_status` `NOT_REQUIRED`, which is terminal and never gates a release. Either way the recording's `depicted_individual_count` increases by 1. The `notice_method` field is null until a notice actually issues.

Placing a litigation hold is `LegalCounsel`'s exclusive authority. The hold names a `matter_number` — the docket identifier, globally unique — the `scope_incident_number` it covers, and a `hold_type` drawn from `LITIGATION`, `SUBPOENA` and `INTERNAL_AFFAIRS`. It is created with `status` `ACTIVE` and `placed_at` set to the logical clock, with `released_at` null. Placement then fans out over every catalogued recording whose `incident_number` exactly equals the hold's `scope_incident_number`, in a three-link chain: because the hold became `ACTIVE`, each matching recording's `retention_locked` becomes true; because it became locked, the recording moves `SCHEDULED → SUSPENDED` and its `suspended_by_hold_id` is set to this hold's identifier; and because assets were suspended, the hold's `suspended_asset_count` is incremented once per suspended recording. Recordings already at `PURGED` are skipped entirely and are not counted.

Lifting a hold reverses that chain, but only where nothing else still justifies suspension. The release accepts an optional `release_note`, moves the hold `ACTIVE → RELEASED`, stamps `released_at` with the logical clock, and resets the hold's `suspended_asset_count` to 0. Every recording whose `suspended_by_hold_id` pointed at the lifted hold is then re-examined: if another hold with `status` `ACTIVE` still carries a matching `scope_incident_number`, `suspended_by_hold_id` is repointed to that hold — again the matching `ACTIVE` hold with the smallest `created_at` — and `disposition_state` stays `SUSPENDED`; only when no `ACTIVE` hold matches does the recording move `SUSPENDED → SCHEDULED` with `suspended_by_hold_id` cleared to null. In either branch `retention_locked` is recomputed from its full definition, so a recording still referenced by an open request remains locked even with every hold gone. Lifting a hold that already carries `status` `RELEASED` is rejected with `ERR_HOLD_STATE_INVALID`.

Destruction closes the retention story and is a `RecordsSupervisor` authority. It accepts an optional `destruction_note` and demands three things simultaneously: the recording's `disposition_state` must be `SCHEDULED`, its `retention_locked` must be false, and the logical clock must have reached or passed `scheduled_purge_at`. A recording that is `SUSPENDED` or already `PURGED` is refused with `ERR_ASSET_STATE_INVALID`; one that is still locked — whether by an `ACTIVE` matching hold or by an open request — is refused with `ERR_RETENTION_HOLD_ACTIVE`; and one whose purge date has not yet arrived is refused with `ERR_PURGE_NOT_DUE`. Since the posture check sits above the due-date check in the ladder of §2.11, a locked recording whose date has also not arrived reports the hold, not the date. A successful destruction moves the recording `SCHEDULED → PURGED`, which is terminal. Existing responsive records that reference the destroyed recording keep their rows for audit, but the recording can never be attached to a new request afterwards, and an attempt to do so is refused with `ERR_ASSET_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.3. Intake, Scope Clarification and the Statutory Response Clock

Intake is performed by a `RecordsClerk` or `RecordsSupervisor` and opens the file. The payload names the `requester_id` of the filer, the `received_at` instant at which the request arrived at the counter — normally the current logical clock value, and the statutory anchor for ordinary requests — the `subject_incident_number` the request describes as free text rather than a foreign key, the inclusive `requested_date_from` and `requested_date_to` bounds of the recording window sought, and the `scope_narrative` describing in words what is wanted. An optional `extension_days_granted` may be supplied at intake, defaults to 0, and must be an integer from 0 through 10 inclusive. `requested_date_from` must not fall after `requested_date_to`; a reversed pair is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_RANGE`.

Commercial filers are rate-limited. A filer whose `requester_type` is `COMMERCIAL` may hold at most three requests whose `processing_status` is none of `RELEASED`, `DENIED` and `CLOSED`, and the count is taken **before** the new row would be inserted: a commercial filer already holding three open requests is refused with `ERR_OPEN_REQUEST_LIMIT`, while one holding two is accepted and ends up with three. No other `requester_type` is limited.

A successful intake creates the request at `processing_status` `INTAKE`, `fee_status` `NOT_ASSESSED` and `version` 1, increments the filer's `open_request_count` by 1, and strikes the first `statutory_deadline_at`. At this moment the request has no responsive records at all, so its `is_critical_incident` is false and `tolled_days` is 0. Thereafter `is_critical_incident` on the request is derived: it is true exactly when at least one of the request's responsive records whose `inclusion_status` is `RESPONSIVE`, `WITHHELD_IN_FULL` or `RELEASED` points at a recording whose own `is_critical_incident` is true.

The deadline itself is computed in three steps and is recomputed at intake, at every scope clarification, at every inclusion ruling, and once more when a required deposit is satisfied. **Step one selects the anchor.** Gather the request's responsive records whose `inclusion_status` is `RESPONSIVE`, `WITHHELD_IN_FULL` or `RELEASED` and whose recording carries `is_critical_incident` true. If the request's `is_critical_incident` is true and that gathered set is not empty, the anchor is the earliest `recorded_start_at` among those recordings; otherwise the anchor is `received_at`. **Step two builds the day budget** from three additive terms: a base of forty-five days when the request's `is_critical_incident` is true and twenty days otherwise, plus the lesser of `extension_days_granted` and ten, plus `tolled_days`. **Step three applies a floor guard**: `statutory_deadline_at` is the later of the anchor advanced by the budget in whole days, and `received_at` advanced by twenty whole days. The guard exists because a critical-incident recording may be months older than the request, and a stale incident date must never shorten the response clock below the ordinary twenty-day floor.

`tolled_days` accounts for time the unit waited on the filer's money, and it is written exactly once. When the qualifying deposit posts, `tolled_days` is frozen at the number of whole days from `deposit_due_at` to the payment instant, computed by taking the elapsed duration between the two, dividing by one day, discarding any fractional remainder, and then flooring the result at zero so that an early payment tolls nothing. Once frozen it is never recomputed, and `statutory_deadline_at` is immediately re-struck with the frozen value. `deposit_due_at` itself is set when the fee dimension first enters `DEPOSIT_DUE`, at the logical clock of that moment advanced by thirty whole days, and is null until then.

Clarifying scope is how a vague request becomes an answerable one, and it is legal from both `INTAKE` and `SCOPING`. From `INTAKE` it advances the request `INTAKE → SCOPING`; from `SCOPING` it is a legal self-loop, and each loop re-runs every side effect below, which is precisely how a second clarification supersedes the first. The payload carries a replacement `scope_narrative`, a replacement `requested_date_from` and `requested_date_to`, the `expected_request_version` of §2.6, and optionally `extension_days_granted`. Every one of the first three replaces the stored value wholesale rather than merging with it. When `extension_days_granted` is supplied it is stored as the lesser of the supplied value and ten; when it is omitted the stored value is left exactly as it was. The call then increments `scope_clarification_count` by 1, re-strikes `statutory_deadline_at` with the new extension, increments the request's `version` by 1, and marks any fee estimate on this request whose `status` is `ACTIVE` as `STALE`, because the scope it priced no longer exists. The date-ordering rule of intake applies here too, again reporting `ERR_INVALID_TIME_RANGE`.

Two limits bound clarification. A request accepts at most two clarifications in total: a further clarification attempted once `scope_clarification_count` has already reached 2 is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_CLARIFICATION_LIMIT`. And clarification is a scoping-phase act only — attempted once the request has moved past `SCOPING` into `EXEMPTION_REVIEW` or beyond, it is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.4. Responsiveness Determination and Exemption Assertion

Once scope is settled, the clerk attaches candidate recordings to the request one at a time, each attachment naming a single `video_asset_id`. The resulting row is the **responsive record** — the per-request view of one recording — and it is the object almost every later rule operates on. It records the `request_id` it answers and the `video_asset_id` it covers, opens at `inclusion_status` `CANDIDATE` and `release_posture` `OPEN`, and starts with `approved_labor_minutes` at 0, `unadjudicated_claim_count` at 0, `redacted_segment_ms` at 0 and `released_at` null. Attaching the same recording to the same request twice is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESPONSIVE_RECORD`, and attaching to a request that has already reached `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`. Because an open request locks the evidence, the attachment recomputes the recording's `retention_locked`, and it recomputes the request's `responsive_record_count`, which counts the request's records whose `inclusion_status` is anything other than `EXCLUDED`.

Attachment also freezes the projected viewing labour. `estimated_review_minutes` is derived from the recording alone, in two steps: first the recording's `duration_ms` is divided by sixty thousand and rounded up to a whole number of minutes, then that minute count is multiplied by a review multiplier chosen by the recording's `classification` and the product is again rounded up to a whole minute. The multiplier is 1.0 for `ROUTINE`, 2.0 for `USE_OF_FORCE`, 2.5 for `JUVENILE_CONTACT`, 3.0 for `OFFICER_INVOLVED_SHOOTING` and 3.0 for `IN_CUSTODY_DEATH` — an in-custody death and an officer-involved shooting cost the same three passes over the footage. Both roundings up are real and both matter: a 90-second `USE_OF_FORCE` recording is two whole minutes of footage and therefore four review minutes. This value is frozen at attachment and is never recomputed, however the request's scope or the recording's classification is later described.

Every recording also carries an unavoidable first pass — faces and licence plates must be obscured whether or not anyone claims an exemption. That floor is the **baseline obscuration**: the recording's whole-minute length, multiplied by one quarter, rounded up to a whole minute. A freshly attached record therefore opens with both `presumptive_burden_minutes` and `adjudicated_burden_minutes` equal to that baseline figure, not at zero.

Ruling on responsiveness is open to a `RecordsClerk`, `LegalCounsel` or `RecordsSupervisor`, and the payload carries a single `inclusion_status` that must be either `RESPONSIVE` or `EXCLUDED`. The record must currently be at `CANDIDATE`; ruling twice is refused with `ERR_RECORD_STATE_INVALID`. The parent request must be at `SCOPING` or `EXEMPTION_REVIEW`; a request still at `INTAKE` is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`, because responsiveness cannot be judged against a scope nobody has clarified. A ruling of `EXCLUDED` moves the record `CANDIDATE → EXCLUDED`, which is terminal, recomputes the request's `responsive_record_count`, and recomputes the recording's `retention_locked`, since the excluded record no longer justifies locking it. A ruling of `RESPONSIVE` moves the record `CANDIDATE → RESPONSIVE` and does considerably more: the request's `responsive_record_count`, `is_critical_incident`, `statutory_deadline_at` and `open_notification_count` are all recomputed — and note that the deadline may jump backwards or forwards here, because the anchor can move from `received_at` to the earliest critical-incident `recorded_start_at` — and the first such ruling bubbles up to advance the request `SCOPING → EXEMPTION_REVIEW`. The request's `open_notification_count` counts the depicted individuals on the recordings behind this request's `RESPONSIVE` records whose `notification_status` is `PENDING`, `NOTIFIED` or `OBJECTED`.

Exemption analysis is `LegalCounsel`'s exclusive province. An assertion against a responsive record names the `exemption_code` claimed, the `statutory_citation` relied on, and the `asserted_scope` — `FULL_WITHHOLD` to withhold the entire recording, or `PARTIAL_REDACT` to require targeted obscuration. The claim is created with `status` `ASSERTED`, `claim_source` `STAFF_ANALYSIS`, `asserted_by_staff_id` set to the asserting attorney's `X-User-ID`, `depicted_individual_id` null, and `adjudicated_by_staff_id`, `adjudicated_at` and `adjudication_note` all null. Asserting an exemption against a record that is `EXCLUDED` or already `RELEASED` is refused with `ERR_RECORD_STATE_INVALID`.

The citation is not free text in practice; it must match the code exactly. `ONGOING_INVESTIGATION` requires the citation `119.071(2)(c)`, `PERSONAL_PRIVACY` requires `119.071(4)(d)`, `JUVENILE` requires `985.04(1)`, `VICTIM_PROTECTION` requires `119.071(2)(j)`, `MEDICAL` requires `456.057(7)` and `CONFIDENTIAL_INFORMANT` requires `119.071(2)(f)`. Any other string, including a correct citation paired with the wrong code, is refused with `ERR_CITATION_MISMATCH`.

Assertion freezes the claim's own labour projection in `estimated_redaction_minutes`. Take the whole-minute length of the recording behind the parent record — the recording's `duration_ms` divided by sixty thousand and rounded up — and multiply it by a weight, then round the product up to a whole minute. The weight is 1.00 whenever `asserted_scope` is `FULL_WITHHOLD`, regardless of code, because withholding the whole recording means reviewing the whole recording. Otherwise the weight comes from the code: 0.30 for `ONGOING_INVESTIGATION`, 0.40 for `PERSONAL_PRIVACY`, 0.50 for `MEDICAL`, 0.60 for `VICTIM_PROTECTION`, 0.80 for `JUVENILE` and 0.90 for `CONFIDENTIAL_INFORMANT`. Scope dominates code: a `FULL_WITHHOLD` `ONGOING_INVESTIGATION` claim scores the whole recording, not thirty percent of it. The value is frozen at creation.

Creating a claim then reconciles four derived quantities. The record's `unadjudicated_claim_count` becomes the number of claims on it whose `status` is `ASSERTED`, and the request's `unadjudicated_claim_count` becomes the number of `ASSERTED` claims across all of that request's records. The record's two burden figures are re-derived from the baseline obscuration plus a sum over claims: `presumptive_burden_minutes` is the baseline plus the total `estimated_redaction_minutes` of every claim on the record whose `status` is `ASSERTED` or `SUSTAINED`, while `adjudicated_burden_minutes` is the baseline plus the total over claims whose `status` is `SUSTAINED` only. The two differ in nothing but that status filter, so the presumptive figure is the worst-case exposure and is always at least the adjudicated figure for the same data; the baseline is rounded up before the sum is taken, never afterwards. Finally the record's `release_posture` is re-derived, and any fee estimate on the request whose `status` is `ACTIVE` becomes `STALE`.

`release_posture` is the gate that decides whether redaction work may be opened on a record and whether the record may be included in a release, and it is evaluated in a pinned order. If any claim on the record has `status` `SUSTAINED` **and** `asserted_scope` `FULL_WITHHOLD`, the posture is `BLOCKED`. Otherwise, if any claim on the record has `status` `ASSERTED` or `SUSTAINED`, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise it is `OPEN`. Claims that are `OVERRULED` or `WITHDRAWN` are ignored entirely, and `claim_source` is irrelevant to the computation. Because the full-withhold test runs first, a record carrying both a sustained full-withhold claim and a separate asserted partial-redaction claim is `BLOCKED`, not `RESTRICTED`.

### 3.5. Adjudication and the Four-Level Withholding Cascade

Adjudication is the single most consequential act in the system, and it is reserved to `LegalCounsel`. The payload names a `disposition` — one of `SUSTAINED`, `OVERRULED` or `WITHDRAWN`, which is also the `status` the claim will carry afterwards — and optionally an `adjudication_note` recording the rationale. The claim must still be at `ASSERTED`; a second attempt on an already-decided claim is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`. The acting attorney's `X-User-ID` must differ from the claim's `asserted_by_staff_id`, or the call is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; where `asserted_by_staff_id` is null — which is the case for every claim that materialised from a depicted individual's objection — the condition is satisfied trivially and any attorney may decide it. All three dispositions share the same bookkeeping on the claim itself: `adjudicated_by_staff_id` is set to the deciding attorney's `X-User-ID`, `adjudicated_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and `adjudication_note` is stored.

Sustaining a claim fires a four-link chain in which each link exists only because the previous one did. **Link one**, caused by the status flip itself, re-derives the parent record's `release_posture` and `adjudicated_burden_minutes` — the sustained claim's `estimated_redaction_minutes` now counts toward the adjudicated figure — and decrements `unadjudicated_claim_count` on both the record and the request. **Link two** fires only when the sustained claim's `asserted_scope` is `FULL_WITHHOLD`: the record moves `RESPONSIVE → WITHHELD_IN_FULL` and its `release_posture` becomes `BLOCKED`. **Link three**, caused by link two, cancels the redaction work that has just become pointless: every redaction task on that record whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `QA_PENDING` or `QA_REJECTED` moves to `CANCELLED` with its `approved_labor_minutes` left at 0. Tasks already at `APPROVED` are terminal and are not touched, which is why this link provably leaves the request's `billable_labor_minutes` unchanged. **Link four**, caused by link three, releases the reviewers' calendars and may finish the request: for every task link three cancelled out of `QA_PENDING`, the booked reviewer's `open_qa_assignment_count` is decremented while `qa_reviewer_id` is retained on the task for audit; and because link three left every redaction task on the request terminal, the request auto-advances `REDACTION → RELEASE_READY` provided at least one of its records is still `RESPONSIVE`.

Alongside that chain, and independent of it, sustaining a claim marks any fee estimate on the request whose `status` is `ACTIVE` as `STALE`, because the adjudicated burden the estimate projected has changed. And when the sustained claim's `claim_source` is `DEPICTED_OBJECTION`, the linked depicted individual moves `OBJECTED → OBJECTION_SUSTAINED`, which is terminal and removes them from the request's `open_notification_count`.

Overruling a claim is a deliberately narrower act, and the difference is the point of the fork. The parent record re-derives `release_posture` and `adjudicated_burden_minutes`, and `unadjudicated_claim_count` decrements on both the record and the request, exactly as with a sustain. But an overruled claim cancels only the redaction work that implemented **that specific claim**: every redaction task on the record whose `exemption_claim_id` names this claim and whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `QA_PENDING` or `QA_REJECTED` moves to `CANCELLED`, because the obscuration it performed is no longer authorised, while tasks on the same record tied to other claims or to the baseline pass are untouched. The record's `inclusion_status` does not move. The request's `ACTIVE` fee estimate becomes `STALE`, and when `claim_source` is `DEPICTED_OBJECTION` the linked individual moves `OBJECTED → OBJECTION_OVERRULED`.

Withdrawing a claim is the only disposition that lowers the worst-case figure. The parent record re-derives `release_posture`, `presumptive_burden_minutes` **and** `adjudicated_burden_minutes`, and both the record's and the request's `unadjudicated_claim_count` decrement. Because a withdrawn claim leaves the presumptive set as well as the adjudicated one, the record's `presumptive_burden_minutes` actually falls — something neither of the other two dispositions does, since a sustained claim was already in the presumptive set and an overruled one leaves both sets by the same amount the adjudicated figure never gained. The request's `ACTIVE` fee estimate becomes `STALE`, and a `DEPICTED_OBJECTION` claim moves its individual `OBJECTED → OBJECTION_OVERRULED`, the same destination an overrule produces.

Withdrawal is also the door out of full withholding. Whenever a claim on a record moves to `WITHDRAWN` — whether decided directly here or swept there by the appeal reversal of §3.9 — and no claim remaining on that record is both `SUSTAINED` and `FULL_WITHHOLD`, a record sitting at `WITHHELD_IN_FULL` returns `WITHHELD_IN_FULL → RESPONSIVE` and re-derives `release_posture` and `adjudicated_burden_minutes`. Redaction tasks that were cancelled on the way in are **not** resurrected; the work must be opened again as new tasks.

After the cascade settles, the request's processing dimension is re-evaluated, and the two outcomes are mutually exclusive. If no claim on the request remains `ASSERTED` and at least one `RESPONSIVE` record still has `release_posture` `RESTRICTED`, the request advances `EXEMPTION_REVIEW → REDACTION`, because targeted obscuration is now owed. If instead no claim remains `ASSERTED`, no `RESPONSIVE` record has `release_posture` `RESTRICTED`, no redaction task on the request sits at `DRAFT`, `QA_PENDING` or `QA_REJECTED`, and at least one record is `RESPONSIVE`, the request skips straight to `EXEMPTION_REVIEW → RELEASE_READY`. A request whose every claim was overruled or withdrawn therefore never enters the redaction phase at all.

### 3.6. Redaction Production and Quality Review

Opening a redaction task is available to a `RedactionTechnician` and to a `RecordsSupervisor`, and it describes one obscuration over one interval of one recording. The payload names the `target_type` — `FACE`, `LICENSE_PLATE`, `AUDIO_IDENTIFIER` or `PROTECTED_CONTENT` — the inclusive `clip_start_ms` offset at which the obscured interval begins, the exclusive `clip_end_ms` offset at which it ends, the `assigned_technician_id` of the staff member who must perform and submit the work, and optionally an `exemption_claim_id` when the redaction implements a specific claim. The field is left null for the baseline face-and-plate pass that every recording needs regardless of any claim. The task is created at `status` `DRAFT` with `rework_count` 0, `logged_labor_minutes` 0, `approved_labor_minutes` 0 and `defect_codes` as an empty array, and with `qa_reviewer_id`, `qa_window_start_at` and `qa_window_end_at` all null until the first submission books a reviewer.

The first live task on a request moves it into the production phase: when the created task is the first on the request whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `QA_PENDING` or `QA_REJECTED`, the request advances `EXEMPTION_REVIEW → REDACTION`. No claim state is required for this — a request carrying no exemption claim at all still reaches `REDACTION` on its baseline pass and from there `RELEASE_READY`.

Four conditions guard task creation beyond the certification rule of §2.9. The parent record's `release_posture` must not be `BLOCKED`, since no work may be opened on a recording the unit has agreed to withhold in full; a blocked record is refused with `ERR_RECORD_RELEASE_BLOCKED`, and that refusal outranks the overlap check below, so an overlapping clip on a blocked record reports the block. The clip interval must satisfy `clip_start_ms` at least zero, `clip_end_ms` strictly greater than `clip_start_ms`, and `clip_end_ms` no greater than the `duration_ms` of the recording behind the parent record; any violation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLIP_RANGE`. A supplied `exemption_claim_id` must name a claim whose `responsive_record_id` is the record in the path, or the call is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_RECORD_MISMATCH`. And the request's cost-recovery obligation must be current: a request whose `fee_status` is `DEPOSIT_DUE` with `deposit_paid_cents` still below `deposit_required_cents` is refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_UNPAID`, so the unit does not spend redaction labour on a request nobody has funded.

Overlap between tasks is judged on half-open intervals with **no buffer whatsoever**. Two tasks conflict only when they sit on the same responsive record, carry the same `target_type`, the existing task's `status` is `DRAFT`, `QA_PENDING`, `QA_REJECTED` or `APPROVED`, and their intervals genuinely intersect — that is, the new `clip_start_ms` is strictly less than the existing `clip_end_ms` and the existing `clip_start_ms` is strictly less than the new `clip_end_ms`. A conflict is refused with `ERR_CLIP_OVERLAP`. Because the intervals are half-open, a task ending at 30000 milliseconds and a task starting at 30000 milliseconds are adjacent rather than overlapping and both are accepted, while a single millisecond of penetration — a new task starting at 29999 — is a conflict. Tasks at `CANCELLED` are invisible to the check, so the interval a cancelled task occupied is immediately reusable. And a different `target_type` over the identical range is always accepted: a face blur and a plate blur legitimately cover the same seconds.

Submitting finished work is the `RedactionTechnician`'s exclusive act, and it does double duty as a booking. The payload carries the `logged_labor_minutes` the technician claims for the work, the `qa_reviewer_id` nominated to review it, and the `qa_window_start_at` and `qa_window_end_at` bounding the window in which that review will happen. The task must be at `DRAFT` or `QA_REJECTED`, or the call is refused with `ERR_TASK_STATE_INVALID` — a task already at `QA_PENDING` or one that has been `CANCELLED` cannot be resubmitted. The submission must come from the technician the work was assigned to, so the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the task's `assigned_technician_id`. The nominated `qa_reviewer_id` must differ from `assigned_technician_id`, and self-nomination is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The reviewer's `redaction_certification_level` must meet the reviewer requirement of §2.9, failing which the call is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT`. And `qa_window_start_at` must fall strictly before `qa_window_end_at`, or the call is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_RANGE`.

Reviewer calendars use deliberately stricter interval semantics than clip intervals do: **closed** endpoints inflated by a symmetric thirty-minute buffer. A booking conflicts with an existing one when both name the same `qa_reviewer_id`, the existing task's `status` is `QA_PENDING`, and the buffered intervals meet — that is, the new `qa_window_start_at` moved thirty minutes earlier is at or before the existing `qa_window_end_at`, and the existing `qa_window_start_at` moved thirty minutes earlier is at or before the new `qa_window_end_at`. A conflict is refused with `ERR_QA_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Two windows that merely touch — one ending at 14:00 and the next starting at 14:00 — do conflict here, and so do windows exactly thirty minutes apart; a gap of thirty-one minutes is clear. Tasks that have already passed or failed review free the reviewer's calendar entirely and are ignored. Separately, and regardless of where the windows sit, a reviewer may hold at most three tasks at `QA_PENDING` at once; a fourth booking is refused with `ERR_QA_LOAD_EXCEEDED`. That capacity check is evaluated **after** the window check, so a reviewer who is both double-booked and at capacity reports the window conflict.

A successful submission moves the task `DRAFT → QA_PENDING`, or `QA_REJECTED → QA_PENDING` when the work is being resubmitted after a failure, in which case `rework_count` also increases by 1. Whichever edge is taken, `qa_reviewer_id`, `qa_window_start_at`, `qa_window_end_at` and `logged_labor_minutes` are all overwritten by this call — the claimed minutes are replaced, never accumulated across attempts — and the nominated reviewer's `open_qa_assignment_count` increases by 1. Nothing fiscal moves: the request's `billable_labor_minutes` is untouched, because the task has not been approved. A task may loop between `QA_PENDING` and `QA_REJECTED` indefinitely, and `rework_count` is the loop counter.

The verdict belongs exclusively to a `QualityReviewer`, and it must be the task's own booked reviewer: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the task's `qa_reviewer_id`, and a verdict attempted by the assigned technician is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The task must be at `QA_PENDING`, so a verdict on a `DRAFT` task is refused with `ERR_TASK_STATE_INVALID`. The payload names the `outcome`, either `PASS` or `FAIL`, and optionally the `defect_codes` observed. A pass additionally requires that the claim being implemented has actually been decided: if the task's `exemption_claim_id` is non-null and the referenced claim is still at `ASSERTED`, the pass is refused with `ERR_UNADJUDICATED_CLAIM`; a task whose `exemption_claim_id` is null carries no such dependency.

A pass moves the task `QA_PENDING → APPROVED`, freezes `approved_labor_minutes` at the current `logged_labor_minutes`, and sets `defect_codes` to the empty array. A failure moves the task `QA_PENDING → QA_REJECTED`, stores the supplied `defect_codes`, and leaves `approved_labor_minutes` at 0 — which is why a submission followed by a failure leaves the request's `billable_labor_minutes` exactly where it started. Either verdict decrements the reviewer's `open_qa_assignment_count`, releasing the booked window. Both then reconcile the labour rollup: a record's `approved_labor_minutes` is the total `approved_labor_minutes` of the tasks on it whose `status` is `APPROVED`, its `redacted_segment_ms` is the total of `clip_end_ms` minus `clip_start_ms` over those same approved tasks, and the request's `billable_labor_minutes` is the total of `approved_labor_minutes` across every one of its records — including `EXCLUDED` ones, whose tasks are cancelled and therefore contribute nothing anyway. A task's `approved_labor_minutes` is zero in every status other than `APPROVED`, `QA_REJECTED` and `CANCELLED` included. Finally, once every redaction task on the request sits at `APPROVED` or `CANCELLED` and at least one record is `RESPONSIVE`, a passing verdict advances the request `REDACTION → RELEASE_READY` and re-derives `billable_labor_minutes` in the process.

### 3.7. Notification and the Objection Rights of Depicted Individuals

Some people have a statutory right to be told before footage of them is published, and to say no. Issuing that notice is the `RecordsClerk`'s exclusive act. The payload names only the `notice_method` used — `FIRST_CLASS_MAIL`, `EMAIL` or `PERSONAL_SERVICE`. The individual must be at `notification_status` `PENDING` with `notification_required` true; anyone at `NOT_REQUIRED`, or already notified, is refused with `ERR_NOTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID`. Issuance moves the individual `PENDING → NOTIFIED`, stores the `notice_method`, stamps `notified_at` with the logical clock, and computes `objection_deadline_at` as `notified_at` advanced by fifteen whole days when the individual's `relationship` is `JUVENILE` and by ten whole days for every other relationship. That deadline is frozen at notification and never recomputed. Because the same recording can be responsive to several requests at once, issuance then recomputes `open_notification_count` on every request whose `RESPONSIVE` records reference this recording.

Recording an objection is open to a `RecordsClerk` or `LegalCounsel` and is the mechanism by which a private person's protest enters the adjudication machinery as a first-class claim. The payload names the `objection_basis` asserted — `PRIVACY`, `VICTIM_STATUS`, `JUVENILE_STATUS` or `MEDICAL_STATUS` — and the `responsive_record_id` of the request-scoped record the objection is aimed at. The individual must be at `NOTIFIED`, or the call is refused with `ERR_NOTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID`; since the move out of `NOTIFIED` is one-way, this also means nobody can object twice. The logical clock must be at or before `objection_deadline_at` — the deadline instant itself is still open — and a later attempt is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The named record must sit on the same recording the individual appears in, so its `video_asset_id` must equal the individual's own `video_asset_id`, and a cross-recording objection is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_RECORD_MISMATCH`.

A recorded objection moves the individual `NOTIFIED → OBJECTED`, stores the `objection_basis`, and materialises a new exemption claim on the named record. That claim is created with `claim_source` `DEPICTED_OBJECTION`, `depicted_individual_id` pointing back at the objector, `asserted_by_staff_id` null — no staff member asserted it, which is why any attorney may later decide it — `status` `ASSERTED` and `asserted_scope` `PARTIAL_REDACT`. Its `exemption_code` and `statutory_citation` are **derived, never supplied**: `PRIVACY` maps to `PERSONAL_PRIVACY`, `VICTIM_STATUS` to `VICTIM_PROTECTION`, `JUVENILE_STATUS` to `JUVENILE` and `MEDICAL_STATUS` to `MEDICAL`, and the citation is then taken from the code-to-citation mapping of §3.4. The claim's `estimated_redaction_minutes` is frozen by the same weighting rule as a staff-asserted claim, using the derived code's partial-redaction weight. The record's `presumptive_burden_minutes`, `unadjudicated_claim_count` and `release_posture` are re-derived, the request's `unadjudicated_claim_count` increases, and any fee estimate on that request whose `status` is `ACTIVE` becomes `STALE`.

From `OBJECTED` there are exactly two exits, and both run through the adjudication of the materialised claim described in §3.5: sustaining it moves the individual `OBJECTED → OBJECTION_SUSTAINED`, while overruling or withdrawing it moves them `OBJECTED → OBJECTION_OVERRULED`. Both are terminal and both remove the individual from the request's `open_notification_count`. A third exit exists from `NOTIFIED` rather than from `OBJECTED`: an individual who was notified and simply never objected is swept to `WAIVED` by the release sweep of §3.9 once their window has run.

### 3.8. Cost Recovery: Estimates, Deposits, Invoices and the Ledger

The fee dimension runs on its own schedule. A `RecordsClerk` or `RecordsSupervisor` issues an estimate against a request whose `fee_status` is `NOT_ASSESSED`, `ESTIMATED` or `DEPOSIT_DUE`; from any other fee state the call is refused with `ERR_FEE_STATE_INVALID`. The payload names the `basis` on which the projection rests, the `media_type` the production is expected to use, and the `expected_request_version` of §2.6. Which bases are permitted depends on how far records processing has come: `basis` `PRESUMPTIVE` is mandatory while `processing_status` is `INTAKE` or `SCOPING`, and `basis` `ADJUDICATED` is permitted only once `processing_status` is `EXEMPTION_REVIEW`, `REDACTION`, `RELEASE_READY` or `PARTIALLY_RELEASED` — that is, only once at least one record has been ruled responsive. An `ADJUDICATED` estimate attempted too early is refused with `ERR_ESTIMATE_BASIS_NOT_PERMITTED`.

Issuing an estimate supersedes whatever came before. Every estimate on the request whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `STALE` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, the new estimate is created at `status` `ACTIVE`, and its `sequence_number` is the count of prior estimates on the request plus one, so the first estimate is number 1. The request's `estimated_total_cents` and `deposit_required_cents` mirror the new estimate's corresponding figures — and are 0 whenever no `ACTIVE` estimate exists — and the request's `version` increases by 1.

Every monetary field of the new estimate is pinned by the following eleven steps, in this order. **First**, gather the labour set: the request's records whose `inclusion_status` is `RESPONSIVE` or `WITHHELD_IN_FULL`. Records at `CANDIDATE` and `EXCLUDED` are in no set at all; a withheld record stays in the labour set because the review labour was genuinely spent on it. **Second**, `projected_review_minutes` is the total of `estimated_review_minutes` over the labour set, taken from the frozen per-record values rather than re-derived from the recordings. **Third**, `projected_burden_minutes` is the total over the labour set of `presumptive_burden_minutes` when `basis` is `PRESUMPTIVE`, or of `adjudicated_burden_minutes` when `basis` is `ADJUDICATED`. **Fourth**, `billable_minutes` is the greater of zero and the sum of `projected_review_minutes` and `projected_burden_minutes` less a statutory free allowance of one hour — sixty minutes — applied exactly once to the combined total and never per record. **Fifth**, `estimated_labor_cents` is `billable_minutes` divided by sixty and multiplied by the hourly rate of 4200 cents, the result rounded half up to a whole cent.

**Sixth**, gather the narrower media set — the request's records whose `inclusion_status` is `RESPONSIVE` only, since a record withheld in full is never pressed onto media — and compute `media_production_cents` by the media rule below. **Seventh**, `subtotal_cents` is `estimated_labor_cents` plus `media_production_cents`. **Eighth**, the public-interest tier is chosen from `subtotal_cents`: the tier fraction is 0.00 when `subtotal_cents` is at most 5000, 0.10 when `subtotal_cents` is from 5001 through 25000 inclusive, and 0.20 when `subtotal_cents` exceeds 25000. **Ninth**, `effective_waiver_pct` is the lesser of 1.00 and the sum of the filer's frozen `fee_waiver_pct` and the tier fraction, so a media outlet in the top tier reaches 0.45 while an indigency-certified filer is already at 1.00 and stays there. **Tenth**, `waiver_credit_cents` is `subtotal_cents` multiplied by `effective_waiver_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent, and `estimated_total_cents` is the lesser of `subtotal_cents` less `waiver_credit_cents` and the statutory cost-recovery ceiling of 50000 cents. **Eleventh**, `deposit_required_cents` is `estimated_total_cents` multiplied by one half and rounded half up when `estimated_total_cents` is **strictly greater** than 3500 cents, and is 0 otherwise — a total of exactly 3500 cents demands no deposit.

One reading of the tier ladder must be ruled out explicitly, because it looks plausible and produces different money. The tier is **always** keyed on `subtotal_cents`, the pre-waiver figure computed in step seven. It is never keyed on `estimated_total_cents`, the amount the filer actually owes; re-keying it there would make the tier depend on the waiver which depends on the tier, and it yields a different tier, a different `effective_waiver_pct` and a different total whenever the subtotal sits within one tier width of a boundary. Monetary rounding happens at step five and step ten, and the deposit in step eleven is rounded the same way; no intermediate value is rounded.

Media production is priced from minutes of footage and the medium chosen. Sum the `duration_ms` of the recordings behind the included records, divide by sixty thousand, and round up to a whole number of `produced_minutes`. For `media_type` `DIGITAL_DOWNLOAD` the `disc_count` is 0 and the cost is 0 cents. For `DVD` the `disc_count` is `produced_minutes` divided by 120 and rounded up, and the cost is 500 cents per disc. For `USB` the `disc_count` is `produced_minutes` divided by 240 and rounded up, and the cost is 1500 cents per unit. The boundaries are exact: 120 produced minutes on DVD is precisely one disc and 121 minutes is two. An empty included set yields `produced_minutes` 0, `disc_count` 0 and a cost of 0 cents for every medium.

Where the estimate lands decides the fee dimension. When `estimated_total_cents` comes out as 0, the request moves to `fee_status` `WAIVED` — terminal — and a waiver credit is written to the ledger for the full `waiver_credit_cents` with `entry_type` `WAIVER_CREDIT` and `source_kind` `WAIVER`; this is the path an indigency-certified filer takes, and also the path a request whose labour falls entirely under the free allowance takes. When `deposit_required_cents` comes out greater than 0, the request moves to `fee_status` `DEPOSIT_DUE` and `deposit_due_at` is set to the logical clock advanced by thirty whole days. Otherwise — a positive total at or under the deposit threshold — the request moves to `fee_status` `ESTIMATED`. Because a superseding estimate can cross the deposit threshold in either direction, both `ESTIMATED → DEPOSIT_DUE` and `DEPOSIT_DUE → ESTIMATED` are legal moves.

Money moves only through the append-only ledger, and each ledger line records the `request_id` whose account it moves, the `entry_type` classifying it, the signed `amount_cents`, an operator `memo`, a `source_kind` naming the workflow that emitted it, and — on reversals only — the `reverses_entry_id` of the line being undone. `entry_type` is one of `DEPOSIT_PAYMENT`, `LABOR_CHARGE`, `MEDIA_CHARGE`, `WAIVER_CREDIT`, `REVERSAL` and `REFUND`; `source_kind` is one of `DEPOSIT`, `INVOICE`, `ADJUSTMENT` and `WAIVER`. The sign convention is fixed: `DEPOSIT_PAYMENT` and `WAIVER_CREDIT` are positive, `LABOR_CHARGE`, `MEDIA_CHARGE` and `REFUND` are negative, and a `REVERSAL` carries exactly the negation of its target's `amount_cents`. `amount_cents` is never zero, which is why a zero-valued invoice component is skipped rather than written as a zero line. A posted line is never edited; a correction is always a new reversal line, so the ledger grows monotonically and lists in `created_at` ascending order.

Four figures on the request are read straight off that ledger. `deposit_balance_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over every entry of the request, reversals included. `deposit_paid_cents` is the sum over the request's `DEPOSIT_PAYMENT` entries together with any reversal entries whose target was a `DEPOSIT_PAYMENT`. `fee_recognized_cents` is the absolute value of the sum over the request's `LABOR_CHARGE` and `MEDIA_CHARGE` entries together with any reversal entries whose target was one of those two types. `waiver_credit_total_cents` is the sum over the request's `WAIVER_CREDIT` entries together with any reversal entries whose target was a `WAIVER_CREDIT`. The conservation identity of §2.8 ties the four together at every observable instant.

A payment may be posted by the filer themselves or by a `RecordsClerk`, and it carries an `amount_cents` and an optional `memo`. The amount must be strictly greater than zero, or the call is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AMOUNT`. It must also not exceed what is currently owed: the ceiling is the greater of `deposit_required_cents` less `deposit_paid_cents` and `invoiced_total_cents` less `deposit_paid_cents`, evaluated against the request's current `fee_status`, and an overpayment is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`. Paying a request whose `fee_status` is `WAIVED` is refused with `ERR_FEE_STATE_INVALID`, and a filer paying someone else's request is refused with `ERR_REQUESTER_MISMATCH`. A successful payment appends a ledger line with `entry_type` `DEPOSIT_PAYMENT` and `source_kind` `DEPOSIT` carrying the positive amount, then re-derives `deposit_paid_cents` and `deposit_balance_cents`.

The payment may then move the fee dimension. When `fee_status` is `DEPOSIT_DUE` and `deposit_balance_cents` after the payment has reached `deposit_required_cents`, the request moves `DEPOSIT_DUE → DEPOSIT_PAID`; on that specific edge, and only on it, `tolled_days` is frozen and `statutory_deadline_at` is re-struck as described in §3.3. When `fee_status` is `INVOICED` and `deposit_paid_cents` has reached `invoiced_total_cents` with `deposit_balance_cents` at zero or above, the request moves `INVOICED → SETTLED`.

Invoicing is the `RecordsSupervisor`'s exclusive authority and accepts only an optional `memo`. It requires `processing_status` to be `PARTIALLY_RELEASED`, `RELEASED` or `DENIED`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_RELEASE_READY`, and `fee_status` to be `ESTIMATED`, `DEPOSIT_DUE` or `DEPOSIT_PAID`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_FEE_STATE_INVALID` — invoicing a request whose fee was never assessed is exactly that failure. The invoice deliberately disagrees with the estimate on identical work: an estimate prices **projected** minutes at the flat hourly rate, while an invoice prices **measured** minutes at a rate scaled by the difficulty of each obscuration. A request may therefore be invoiced above or below its `ACTIVE` estimate, and that divergence is never an error.

`invoiced_total_cents` is pinned in five steps. **First**, take the request's redaction tasks whose `status` is `APPROVED`, ordered by `created_at` ascending, and price each one on its own: that task's `approved_labor_minutes` divided by sixty, multiplied by the hourly rate of 4200 cents, multiplied by the complexity factor of its `target_type` — 1.00 for `FACE`, 0.85 for `LICENSE_PLATE`, 1.15 for `AUDIO_IDENTIFIER` and 1.30 for `PROTECTED_CONTENT` — and the result rounded half up to a whole cent. The labour component is the total of those per-task amounts; the rounding happens once per task and only then are the results added, exactly as §2.8 requires. Tasks at `DRAFT`, `QA_PENDING`, `QA_REJECTED` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing even where their `logged_labor_minutes` is non-zero. **Second**, the review component is measured on what actually went out the door: total the `estimated_review_minutes` of the request's records whose `inclusion_status` is `RELEASED`, subtract the sixty-minute free allowance once, take the greater of that and zero, divide by sixty, multiply by the hourly rate and round half up. **Third**, the media component is the total of `production_cost_cents` over every release package produced for the request. **Fourth**, the pre-waiver subtotal is the sum of the labour, review and media components; `effective_waiver_pct` is the lesser of 1.00 and the sum of the filer's `fee_waiver_pct` and the public-interest tier fraction keyed on that subtotal by the same ladder as the estimate; and the waiver credit is the subtotal multiplied by that fraction, rounded half up. **Fifth**, `invoiced_total_cents` is the lesser of the subtotal less the waiver credit and the statutory cost-recovery ceiling of 50000 cents — the ceiling binds the invoice, not the subtotal.

Issuing the invoice appends up to three ledger lines, all with `source_kind` `INVOICE`, in a fixed order: a negative `LABOR_CHARGE` covering the labour and review components together, then a negative `MEDIA_CHARGE`, then a positive `WAIVER_CREDIT`. Any component that computes to zero is skipped entirely rather than written as a zero line. `fee_recognized_cents`, `waiver_credit_total_cents` and `deposit_balance_cents` are then re-derived. The request moves to `fee_status` `SETTLED` when `deposit_paid_cents` has already reached `invoiced_total_cents`, and to `INVOICED` otherwise.

Reversal is the only correction mechanism and is likewise reserved to a `RecordsSupervisor`, taking a mandatory `memo`. The targeted ledger line must not itself be a `REVERSAL` and must not already be named by an existing reversal's `reverses_entry_id`; either condition is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_ALREADY_REVERSED`. A successful reversal appends a line with `entry_type` `REVERSAL`, `source_kind` `ADJUSTMENT`, `reverses_entry_id` set to the targeted line, and `amount_cents` set to exactly the negation of the target's `amount_cents`. Every derived money figure on the request is then re-derived, and `deposit_balance_cents` returns to its exact pre-target value with no drift while the ledger row count grows by two. When the reversal undoes a settled charge and leaves `deposit_balance_cents` below zero — equivalently, leaves `deposit_paid_cents` short of `invoiced_total_cents` — the fee dimension moves back `SETTLED → INVOICED`. This is the reverse cross-dimension junction, and it is one-directional: a request that already reached `processing_status` `CLOSED` is **not** reopened by a reversal.

### 3.9. Endgame: Release Production, Denial, Appeal and Closure

Producing a release package is open to a `RecordsClerk` or `RecordsSupervisor` and takes a single `media_type` naming the medium actually produced. The request must be at `processing_status` `RELEASE_READY` or `PARTIALLY_RELEASED`, or the call is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_RELEASE_READY` — and per §2.11 that refusal precedes the deposit check, so an unfunded request that is also not ready reports the readiness failure.

**The notification sweep runs first, before anything else in the call.** Every depicted individual with `notification_required` true, on a recording behind one of this request's `RESPONSIVE` records, whose `notification_status` is `NOTIFIED` and whose `objection_deadline_at` the logical clock has reached or passed, moves `NOTIFIED → WAIVED` and is counted in the new package's `waived_notification_count`. The request's `open_notification_count` is then re-derived. This ordering is load-bearing: an individual swept in this call counts as satisfied within the same call, so a release attempted at exactly the deadline instant succeeds. It is also the point where the two boundary conventions of §2.4 meet — an individual notified at some instant with a ten-day window is still able to object at exactly ten days later, and is swept to `WAIVED` by a release attempted at exactly that same instant.

With the sweep settled, eligibility is frozen. A record is releasable when **all seven** of the following hold: it belongs to this request and its `inclusion_status` is `RESPONSIVE`; its `release_posture` is not `BLOCKED`; its `unadjudicated_claim_count` is zero; every redaction task on it is at `APPROVED` or `CANCELLED` **and** at least one is at `APPROVED`; the recording behind it is not at `disposition_state` `PURGED`; every depicted individual on that recording with `notification_required` true is at `notification_status` `WAIVED`, `NOT_REQUIRED` or `OBJECTION_OVERRULED`, or else is at `NOTIFIED` with the clock at or past their `objection_deadline_at`; and the recording has no litigation hold with `status` `ACTIVE` matching its `incident_number`. The eligible set is taken in `created_at` ascending order. The fourth condition deserves emphasis: a record whose every task was cancelled by a full-withhold cascade is **not** releasable even after a withdrawal returned its posture to `RESTRICTED`, because no task on it ever reached `APPROVED`.

Four rejections guard the production, each keyed to one of those conditions failing in a way the ladder of §2.11 orders. A record whose `release_posture` is `BLOCKED` yields `ERR_RECORD_RELEASE_BLOCKED`. An exemption claim still at `ASSERTED` on a record the package would release yields `ERR_UNADJUDICATED_CLAIM`. A notification-required individual sitting at `OBJECTED`, or at `NOTIFIED` with a window that has not yet run, yields `ERR_OBJECTION_UNRESOLVED`. An `ACTIVE` litigation hold over an included recording, or a recording that is otherwise `retention_locked` against destruction, yields `ERR_RETENTION_HOLD_ACTIVE`. Beyond those, an eligible set that comes out empty after the sweep yields `ERR_NO_RELEASABLE_RECORDS`, and a request whose `fee_status` is `DEPOSIT_DUE` with `deposit_paid_cents` below `deposit_required_cents` yields `ERR_DEPOSIT_UNPAID`.

A successful production moves every member of the eligible set `RESPONSIVE → RELEASED` with `released_at` stamped from the logical clock, and re-derives each one's `redacted_segment_ms`. The package itself records the `request_id` it answers, the `media_type` produced, the `included_record_count` of records it moved to `RELEASED`, the `produced_minutes`, `disc_count` and `production_cost_cents` computed by the media rule of §3.8 over exactly those records, and `released_at` from the clock. Its `package_kind` is **derived and never supplied**: it is `FINAL` when the eligible set covered every `RESPONSIVE` record of the request, and `INTERIM` when `RESPONSIVE` records remain. Each released recording's `retention_locked` is re-derived. The request then advances `RELEASE_READY → PARTIALLY_RELEASED` on an interim package, or `RELEASE_READY → RELEASED` or `PARTIALLY_RELEASED → RELEASED` on a final one; on the final edge the filer's `open_request_count` decreases by 1. A final package additionally requires that no record on the request is still at `inclusion_status` `CANDIDATE` — undecided records must be ruled on before the file can be declared complete.

Denial is `LegalCounsel`'s act and comes in two flavours that differ sharply. The letter names a `denial_type` — `FULL` to deny the entire request, or `PARTIAL` to accompany a release that leaves some records withheld — and an `appeal_pathway_note` of instructional text appended to the statutory appeal paragraph. Issuance is permitted while `processing_status` is `EXEMPTION_REVIEW`, `REDACTION` or `RELEASE_READY`, and requires that the request's `unadjudicated_claim_count` be zero, failing which it is refused with `ERR_UNADJUDICATED_CLAIM`. A `FULL` denial further requires that every record on the request whose `inclusion_status` is not `EXCLUDED` be at `WITHHELD_IN_FULL` — the unit cannot deny a request outright while releasable material remains.

The letter is created at `status` `ISSUED` with `issued_at` from the logical clock and `appeal_deadline_at` thirty whole days later, that final instant inclusive, and with `appeal_grounds`, `appeal_filed_at` and `appeal_decided_at` all null. Its `statutory_citations` array is assembled from the distinct `statutory_citation` values of the claims with `status` `SUSTAINED` on this request's records, deduplicated and sorted ascending as strings. Its `withheld_record_count` is the number of the request's records at `inclusion_status` `WITHHELD_IN_FULL` at the moment of issuance. Its `appeal_authority` is derived from the type: `SUPERIOR_COURT` when `denial_type` is `FULL`, `DISTRICT_ATTORNEY` when it is `PARTIAL`. A `FULL` denial then moves the request `EXEMPTION_REVIEW → DENIED` and decrements the filer's `open_request_count` by 1; a `PARTIAL` denial moves `processing_status` not at all and only records the citations alongside the release it accompanies.

Filing the appeal belongs exclusively to the `Requester`, who supplies the `appeal_grounds`. The letter must be at `status` `ISSUED`, or the call is refused with `ERR_LETTER_STATE_INVALID`; the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `requester_id` of the letter's request, or it is refused with `ERR_REQUESTER_MISMATCH`; and the logical clock must be at or before `appeal_deadline_at`, or it is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Filing moves the letter `ISSUED → APPEALED`, stores the `appeal_grounds` and stamps `appeal_filed_at` from the clock.

Deciding the appeal returns to `LegalCounsel`, who supplies a `decision` of either `UPHELD` or `REVERSED`, and who may not be the attorney who issued the letter — a self-review is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The letter must be at `APPEALED`, or the call is refused with `ERR_LETTER_STATE_INVALID`. Both decisions stamp `appeal_decided_at` from the logical clock, and the two branches then diverge as far as any fork in this system.

Upholding the denial is the inert branch. The letter moves `APPEALED → UPHELD`, and **no** exemption claim, responsive record or redaction task is touched. Where the letter's `denial_type` is `FULL` and the request sits at `DENIED`, the request advances `DENIED → CLOSED`. Because the request is then no longer open, `retention_locked` is re-derived for every recording referenced by its records, so a recording with no `ACTIVE` matching hold and no other open request becomes unlocked and therefore destructible.

Reversing the denial reopens three levels at once. The letter moves `APPEALED → REVERSED`. Every exemption claim on the request's records whose `status` is `SUSTAINED` moves `SUSTAINED → WITHDRAWN`. Each affected record then re-derives its `release_posture` and both burden figures, and any record sitting at `WITHHELD_IN_FULL` with no remaining sustained full-withhold claim returns `WITHHELD_IN_FULL → RESPONSIVE`. Where the letter's `denial_type` is `FULL`, the request returns `DENIED → EXEMPTION_REVIEW` and the filer's `open_request_count` increases by 1 again. `DENIED` is therefore not terminal while an appeal can still reverse it. Redaction tasks cancelled during the original withholding cascade stay `CANCELLED`; the obscuration work must be opened afresh.

Closure is reached from two directions. A fulfilled request advances `RELEASED → CLOSED` as soon as its fee dimension finishes — that is, as soon as `fee_status` is `SETTLED` or `WAIVED` — which in practice happens inside the invoicing call or inside the payment that settles the invoice. An exhausted denial advances `DENIED → CLOSED` on an upheld appeal. Either way, closure re-derives `retention_locked` for every recording the request referenced, releasing evidence that nothing else still holds.
