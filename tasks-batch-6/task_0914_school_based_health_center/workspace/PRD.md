# School-Based Health Center API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A school-based health center (SBHC) is a clinic that lives inside a school building. It is staffed by a sponsoring hospital, it treats children who are legally minors, and it therefore sits on top of two rulebooks at once: the clinical rulebook that governs consent, immunization and referral follow-up, and the revenue-cycle rulebook that governs how a sponsor hospital bills a Medicaid program, a commercial payer, or a family on a sliding fee scale. The staff who run these sites today reconcile the two rulebooks by hand — a nurse checks a paper consent folder before an encounter, a registrar retypes a household income figure into a discount worksheet, a care coordinator keeps a spreadsheet of which specialist referrals ever came back. The result is predictable: encounters delivered without a usable consent on file, sports clearances issued to students whose immunization record has quietly lapsed, and referral loops that are never closed because nobody owns the last phone call.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces those spreadsheets. The service is mounted under the `/sbhc` path prefix and is responsible for the full arc of a school health program: registering sites and clinicians, enrolling students for a school year, capturing guardian-granted and minor-granted consent, scheduling and closing encounters delivered either in person or by telehealth into a classroom, recording immunization doses and screening results, raising and chasing external referrals, issuing sports-physical clearances, minting and adjudicating billable charges under a sliding fee scale, notifying parents, and producing the aggregate snapshots that funders demand at the end of a reporting period.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **posture gates action**: a site has a program posture, a student has an immunization compliance posture, an enrollment has a lifecycle posture and a consent has an expiry, and each of these is re-read at the moment an operation is attempted rather than trusted from when the record was created. Second, **every derived number is derived by the server**: fee tiers, patient responsibility, validity windows, dose validity dates, booked-minute totals and closure rates are all computed from stored inputs by the rules in this document, never accepted from a client. Where two defensible ways to compute the same quantity exist, this document names both and states which one is operative.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service — without exception, including list and read requests — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. The same refusal, with the same code and status, is returned when `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the seven roles defined below. Only once both headers are well formed does the service ask the second question — whether that role is permitted to invoke this particular operation. A recognised role attempting an operation outside its permission set is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` and HTTP 403. Section 4 lists the authorized roles for each endpoint; that table is the authority.

The seven roles are `SbhcAdmin`, `Registrar`, `Clinician`, `CareCoordinator`, `BillingSpecialist`, `Guardian` and `Student`.

- **`SbhcAdmin`** is the health-center operations administrator: site configuration, the logical clock, the daily sweep, notification delivery and reporting. `X-User-ID` for an `SbhcAdmin` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- **`Registrar`** is front-office staff: the student roster, guardians, the enrollment lifecycle, guardian-granted consent and the sliding-fee financial profile. `X-User-ID` for a `Registrar` is likewise an opaque operator identifier, not tied to any persisted entity and never compared to an entity's `id`.
- **`Clinician`** is a licensed provider delivering encounters. When `X-User-Role` is `Clinician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Clinician` row; ownership and segregation-of-duties checks on any resource that references a clinician compare that resource's clinician-valued field to `X-User-ID`. In particular, a referral records the requesting clinician's `X-User-ID` and later refuses closure by that same identifier.
- **`CareCoordinator`** is care-coordination staff who work the referral outreach loop and close the loop once the external report arrives. `X-User-ID` for a `CareCoordinator` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it on a referral it closes but does not compare it to any entity's `id`.
- **`BillingSpecialist`** is sponsor-hospital revenue-cycle staff owning the entire charge lifecycle. `X-User-ID` for a `BillingSpecialist` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared to an entity's `id`.
- **`Guardian`** is a parent or legal guardian. When `X-User-Role` is `Guardian`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Guardian` row; ownership checks compare it to the guardian's own `id` and to the guardian-valued field on a parent notification.
- **`Student`** is the minor themself, acting under minor-consent-by-law for the self-consentable categories. When `X-User-Role` is `Student`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Student` row; ownership checks compare it to the student `id` in the path and to the granting-actor field on a consent.

Because three of the seven roles are entity-backed, a second class of refusal exists above and beyond role permission: a principal who holds the right role but is acting on somebody else's row is refused with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` and HTTP 403. Concretely, that covers four situations — a `Student` recording a self-consent whose path student id is not their own; a `Guardian` acknowledging a notification whose target guardian is not them; a `Guardian` changing the notification preference of a guardian row that is not their own; and a `Guardian` or `Student` revoking a consent they did not themselves grant. Ownership is evaluated after role permission and after the identifiers in the path have been resolved, but before any domain rule.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint in this service is paginated by the same two query parameters. `limit` controls how many rows come back and defaults to 10 when omitted; it must be an integer of at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` controls how many rows are skipped and defaults to 0 when omitted; it must be an integer of at least 0 and has no upper bound. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or an `offset` below 0, or either parameter supplied as something other than an integer, is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` and HTTP 400.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest first. The one documented exception is the guardian list for a student, which is deliberately returned in the same order the notification-target selector uses, described in §3.2. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`: `total_count` is the number of rows that match the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Version Counter

Every stored resource is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and no embedded meaning; a client must never construct one. Alongside `id`, every resource carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recorded when the row was first written.

All resources except one additionally carry `updated_at` — same format as `created_at`, refreshed on every mutation of the row — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. The single exception is the aggregate report snapshot, which is immutable by construction: a report carries `id` and `created_at` only, and has neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

The `version` counter is not decorative. Three write operations use the student's `version` as an optimistic-concurrency token (§2.7), and several apparently unrelated operations advance it — recording a dose, voiding a dose, activating or withdrawing or reinstating an enrollment, writing a financial profile, setting or clearing an immunization exemption, granting a consent, creating an enrollment, and recording a sports-physical determination all bump `Student.version` by 1. Likewise `School.version` advances on every program-posture change and on every telehealth booking or release. Wherever this document says a version "increments", it means increments by exactly 1 and refreshes that row's `updated_at`.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and the Academic Calendar

The service does not read the host's wall clock. It maintains a **logical clock** whose value before any client sets it is exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00.000Z`, and every temporal rule in this document — consent expiry, dose intervals, no-show grace, acknowledgement windows, deferral windows, reporting periods — is evaluated against that logical instant rather than real time. An `SbhcAdmin` may move the clock to an arbitrary instant by supplying a `to_iso8601` value; doing so mutates no entity, and the response reports the new `current_utc`. Moving the clock does not retroactively rewrite stored timestamps; it only changes the answer to every subsequent temporal question.

Two academic years are supported: `2025-2026`, which runs from 2025-08-01 through 2026-07-31, and `2026-2027`, which runs from 2026-08-01 through 2027-07-31. Any request that supplies a `school_year` outside this pair is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. The **current school year** is derived from the logical clock date: when the month is August or later, the current school year is the label formed from the clock's calendar year and the following year; otherwise it is the label formed from the previous calendar year and the clock's calendar year. At the default clock instant of 2026-05-01 the month is May, so the current school year is `2025-2026`.

Where this document refers to a "school-year end instant", it means the last second of the final day of that year expressed in UTC: `2026-07-31T23:59:59.000Z` for `2025-2026` and `2027-07-31T23:59:59.000Z` for `2026-2027`. Where it refers to a "school-year end date" it means the bare date — 2026-07-31 or 2027-07-31 respectively.

### 2.6. The Order of Refusal — a Single Validation Ladder

When more than one rule would refuse a request, the response is decided by a fixed ladder rather than by whichever check the implementation happens to run first. The ladder is evaluated strictly in the order below and the **first** failing rung decides the error identifier and HTTP status; no later rung can mask or override an earlier one. The scopes given for each rung are exhaustive — a rung is applied on exactly the operations named and nowhere else.

1. **Headers present.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and non-empty, on every endpoint. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role recognised.** `X-User-Role` is one of the seven declared roles, on every endpoint. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
3. **Role permitted.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list in §4, on every endpoint. Otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
4. **Path identifiers resolve.** Every identifier embedded in the URL path resolves to a stored row, validated left to right, on every endpoint that has a path identifier. Otherwise HTTP 404, carrying `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLINICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VISIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CHARGE_NOT_FOUND` for those six resource types, and `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` for an enrollment, consent, guardian, clearance, dose, screening, notification, telehealth session or report identifier that does not resolve.
5. **Body identifiers resolve.** Every identifier supplied inside the request body — `school_id`, `clinician_id`, `student_id`, `visit_id` — resolves to a stored row. This rung applies to `POST /sbhc/visits`, `POST /sbhc/students/{id}/enrollments`, `POST /sbhc/telehealth-sessions`, `POST /sbhc/students/{id}/immunization-doses` and `POST /sbhc/reports`. Otherwise HTTP 404 with `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLINICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_VISIT_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate.
6. **Body well formed.** Every required body field is present, and every enum, integer, boolean, date and datetime field is well formed and inside its declared range, on every endpoint that takes a request body. Otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (400).
7. **Pagination well formed.** `limit` is an integer between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` is an integer of at least 0, on every list endpoint. Otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (400).
8. **Reporting period ordered.** `period_end_date` is the same as or later than `period_start_date` — equal dates describe a legitimate single-day period — on `POST /sbhc/reports`. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_PERIOD` (400).
9. **Session window well formed.** `end_at` is strictly after `start_at` and the resulting duration is at least 15 minutes and at most 60 minutes, both bounds inclusive, on `POST /sbhc/telehealth-sessions` and `POST /sbhc/telehealth-sessions/{id}/reschedule`. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SESSION_WINDOW` (400).
10. **Optimistic concurrency.** `expected_student_version` equals the student's current `version`, on `POST /sbhc/students/{id}/consents`, `POST /sbhc/students/{id}/enrollments` and `POST /sbhc/visits/{id}/sports-clearances`. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
11. **Ownership.** An entity-backed actor acts only on its own row, on `POST /sbhc/students/{id}/consents`, `POST /sbhc/consents/{id}/revoke`, `POST /sbhc/guardians/{id}/notification-preference` and `POST /sbhc/notifications/{id}/acknowledge`. Otherwise `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` (403).
12. **Self-consent permitted.** A consent granted by the student themself is accepted only for the self-consentable categories and only at or above the age threshold, on `POST /sbhc/students/{id}/consents`. Otherwise `ERR_SELF_CONSENT_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
13. **Segregation of duties.** The actor closing a referral loop is not the actor who requested it, on `POST /sbhc/referrals/{id}/close`. Otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
14. **Site posture.** The school where the operation happens has an `ACTIVE` program posture, on `POST /sbhc/visits`, `POST /sbhc/enrollments/{id}/activate`, `POST /sbhc/enrollments/{id}/reinstate`, `POST /sbhc/students/{id}/enrollments` and `POST /sbhc/telehealth-sessions`. Otherwise `ERR_SCHOOL_SUSPENDED` (409).
15. **Enrollment posture.** The student holds an enrollment in `ACTIVE` status for the current school year at the operation's site, on `POST /sbhc/visits`, `POST /sbhc/students/{id}/referrals` and `POST /sbhc/students/{id}/immunization-doses`. Otherwise `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409).
16. **Exhausted-attempt guards.** These sit deliberately *above* the generic state check, because the very attempt that exhausts a cap also drives the row into a terminal state; were the cap checked later, its error could never be observed again. Two guards live here: an outreach attempt when the referral's outreach counter has already reached 3 yields `ERR_OUTREACH_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, and a delivery attempt when the notification's delivery counter has already reached 3 yields `ERR_DELIVERY_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`. Both are HTTP 409 and apply on `POST /sbhc/referrals/{id}/outreach-attempts` and `POST /sbhc/notifications/{id}/deliver` respectively.
17. **State legality.** The target row is in a state from which the requested transition exists, on every action endpoint. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409).
18. **Consent on file.** A consent of the category the visit type requires exists for the student with `ACTIVE` status and a current logical instant at or before its expiry, on `POST /sbhc/visits` and `POST /sbhc/visits/{id}/start`. Otherwise `ERR_CONSENT_REQUIRED` (409).
19. **Telehealth consent on file.** For telehealth delivery, a consent in the `TELEHEALTH` category exists with `ACTIVE` status and a current logical instant at or before its expiry, on `POST /sbhc/visits` and `POST /sbhc/telehealth-sessions`. Otherwise `ERR_TELEHEALTH_CONSENT_REQUIRED` (409).
20. **Discipline match.** The clinician's discipline equals the discipline the visit type requires, on `POST /sbhc/visits`. Otherwise `ERR_DISCIPLINE_MISMATCH` (409).
21. **Immunization posture.** The student's immunization compliance posture is anything other than `NON_COMPLIANT`, on `POST /sbhc/visits/{id}/sports-clearances` and `POST /sbhc/sports-clearances/{id}/re-evaluate`. Otherwise `ERR_IMMUNIZATION_NON_COMPLIANT` (409).
22. **Dose sequence.** `dose_number` equals one more than the highest dose number among the student's `VALID` doses of that series, on `POST /sbhc/students/{id}/immunization-doses`. Otherwise `ERR_DOSE_SEQUENCE` (409).
23. **Dose uniqueness.** No `VALID` dose already exists for the same student, `series_code` and `dose_number`, on `POST /sbhc/students/{id}/immunization-doses`. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_DOSE` (409).
24. **Dates not in the future.** `administered_date` and `report_received_date` are not after the current logical clock date, on `POST /sbhc/students/{id}/immunization-doses` and `POST /sbhc/referrals/{id}/report-back`. Otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (400).
25. **No-show grace elapsed.** A no-show close is accepted only once the current logical instant has reached the scheduled start plus 10 minutes, on `POST /sbhc/visits/{id}/close-unfulfilled` and `POST /sbhc/telehealth-sessions/{id}/close`. Otherwise `ERR_NO_SHOW_TOO_EARLY` (409).
26. **Clinician slot free.** No buffered-overlap conflict exists on the clinician axis, on `POST /sbhc/telehealth-sessions` and `POST /sbhc/telehealth-sessions/{id}/reschedule`. Otherwise `ERR_CLINICIAN_SLOT_CONFLICT` (409).
27. **Site capacity free.** The count of school-overlapping sessions is strictly below the site's concurrent capacity, on `POST /sbhc/telehealth-sessions` and `POST /sbhc/telehealth-sessions/{id}/reschedule`. Otherwise `ERR_SCHOOL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Note the ordering against rung 26: when a requested window fails both axes, the clinician conflict is the reported one.
28. **Reinstatement cap.** The enrollment's reinstatement counter is strictly below 2, on `POST /sbhc/enrollments/{id}/reinstate`. Otherwise `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409).
29. **Re-evaluation cap.** The clearance's re-evaluation counter is strictly below 2, on `POST /sbhc/sports-clearances/{id}/re-evaluate`. Otherwise `ERR_REEVALUATION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409).
30. **Reschedule cap.** The session's reschedule counter is strictly below 2, on `POST /sbhc/telehealth-sessions/{id}/reschedule`. Otherwise `ERR_RESCHEDULE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409).
31. **Acknowledgement window open.** Acknowledgement happens while the current logical instant is at or before the sent instant plus 14 days, on `POST /sbhc/notifications/{id}/acknowledge`. Otherwise `ERR_ACK_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (409).
32. **Claim routing and money rules.** On `POST /sbhc/charges/{id}/submit-claim` the charge's payer route must be Medicaid or private insurance, and on `POST /sbhc/charges/{id}/record-patient-payment` it must be self-pay — either violation is `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_APPLICABLE`; on `POST /sbhc/charges/{id}/appeal` the appeal counter must be strictly below 2, otherwise `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`; on `POST /sbhc/charges/{id}/write-off` the appeal counter must equal exactly 2, otherwise `ERR_WRITE_OFF_NOT_PERMITTED`; and on `POST /sbhc/charges/{id}/record-patient-payment` the supplied `amount_cents` must equal the charge's operative patient responsibility exactly, otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. All four are HTTP 409.

Five consequences of this ordering are worth stating outright, because they are the cases where two plausible errors compete. A student with no active enrollment *and* no consent on file produces `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, not `ERR_CONSENT_REQUIRED` (rung 15 precedes rung 18). A telehealth window that collides with the same clinician's other session *and* exhausts the site's capacity produces `ERR_CLINICIAN_SLOT_CONFLICT` (rung 26 precedes rung 27). A fourth outreach attempt on a referral already lost to follow-up produces `ERR_OUTREACH_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, not `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (rung 16 precedes rung 17), and identically a fourth delivery attempt on an undeliverable notification produces `ERR_DELIVERY_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`. An appeal attempted on a charge that is not in a denied state produces `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, not `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (rung 17 precedes rung 32). And a referral closure attempted by the original requester on a referral that is in the wrong state still produces `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (rung 13 precedes rung 17).

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on the Student Record

Three write operations in this service are guarded by optimistic concurrency, and all three guard the **same** parent: the `Student` row. Each of the three requires a non-optional body field named `expected_student_version` carrying the `version` the client last observed on that student. If the value does not equal the student's current `version` at the moment the write is processed, the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and nothing is created or mutated. Endpoints not named here take no version field at all, and supplying one to them has no effect.

- **Granting a consent** (`POST /sbhc/students/{id}/consents`) requires `expected_student_version` to match the student's current `version`. This matters because a new consent in the same category and school year legitimately supersedes the prior one; a writer holding a stale view of the student would silently displace a consent it never saw. Mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
- **Creating an enrollment** (`POST /sbhc/students/{id}/enrollments`) requires `expected_student_version` to match the student's current `version`. A new enrollment for the same school year legitimately transfers the prior one away, so a writer working from an out-of-date view of the student would otherwise move the child to a different site on top of a record it has not observed. Mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
- **Recording a sports-physical determination** (`POST /sbhc/visits/{id}/sports-clearances`) requires `expected_student_version` to match the student's current `version`. A new clearance supersedes the student's prior clearance for the same school year, and the student's `version` also advances whenever the immunization compliance posture that gates clearance changes — so a stale view may no longer justify the determination being recorded at all. Mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).

Because rung 10 of the ladder sits above every domain rung, a stale version is reported even when the request would also have failed a later domain check.

### 2.8. Money, Rates and Rounding

All monetary amounts in this service are integers of whole cents. There are no fractional cents and no floating-point money fields; wherever a computation produces a fraction of a cent, the result is rounded half-up to a whole number of cents — that is, 0 decimal places. Percentages of the federal poverty level are carried to exactly 2 decimal places, rounded half-up. Referral closure rates in aggregate reports are carried to exactly 4 decimal places, rounded half-up, and an empty denominator yields exactly 0.0000 rather than an error or a null.

The published fee schedule is fixed and is the sole source of a charge's gross amount: an acute office visit is 12,000 cents, a behavioral therapy encounter is 15,000 cents, a dental preventive encounter is 9,000 cents, a sports-physical exam is 5,000 cents, an immunization administration is 3,000 cents, and a telehealth encounter is 11,000 cents. `IMMUNIZATION_ADMIN` is the one and only no-charge service category: its gross amount is still 3,000 cents and still counts toward the encounter's gross bucket, but the family is never asked to pay any part of it, regardless of discount tier or payer route.

### 2.9. Nested Paths, Left-to-Right Resolution and Site Posture

Many resources in this service are addressed through a parent: guardians, enrollments, consents, immunization doses and referrals hang off a student; screenings and sports clearances hang off a visit. When a path carries more than one identifier, the identifiers are resolved **left to right** and the first one that fails to resolve decides the 404 — a screening recorded against a non-existent visit reports `ERR_VISIT_NOT_FOUND` without ever considering the body. A child identifier that resolves but belongs to a different parent than the one named in the path is treated as unresolvable for that request.

Site posture is the outermost domain gate. A school carries `program_status`, which is `ACTIVE` when the site is registered and can be moved to `SUSPENDED` and back an unlimited number of times. While a site is `SUSPENDED`, no enrollment may be created, activated or reinstated there, no encounter may be scheduled there, and no telehealth session may be booked there — every such attempt is refused with `ERR_SCHOOL_SUSPENDED`. Suspension is deliberately non-destructive: rows already stored at that site keep their state, encounters already in progress are untouched, and reinstating the site restores normal operation without any replay.

### 2.10. Cascades, Ordering and Derived Totals

Several operations in this service mutate more than one entity. Where that happens, this document enumerates every link of the cascade, and the implementation must apply all of them within the single request that triggered them — the consistency model is immediate, and there is no deferred or eventual reconciliation anywhere. Where two notifications arise from one cascade, their relative creation order is pinned and stated in §3.

Three quantities in the system are **conserved** and are recomputed from their children rather than incremented ad hoc. A school's `telehealth_booked_minutes_total` is always the sum of the durations of that school's telehealth sessions whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`; booking a session and then cancelling it must return the total to exactly its pre-booking value. A visit's five charge buckets are always recomputed from that visit's charge rows. And the visit-level residual — the gross bucket minus the sum of the payer-billed, patient-responsibility, write-off and no-charge buckets — must be exactly 0 both before and after every forward action and its compensating action.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Site and its Clinical Roster

Before a single child can be seen, an `SbhcAdmin` registers the site. A school site is described by its `name`, which must be between 1 and 120 characters; its `site_code`, a short identifier between 1 and 16 characters that must be unique across all school rows, with uniqueness compared case-sensitively so that `NORTH1` and `north1` are two different sites; and `sponsor_hospital_name`, the hospital that owns the billing relationship for everything delivered at that site. Registration may optionally supply `telehealth_concurrent_capacity`, the number of telehealth sessions the site's classroom and presenter staff can host at overlapping times; when it is omitted the value defaults to 1, and any supplied value must be at least 1 and at most 4. A newly registered site starts with `program_status` of `ACTIVE`, a `telehealth_booked_minutes_total` of 0 and a `suspended_count` of 0.

The program posture switch is the blunt instrument an administrator uses when a site closes for a strike, an outbreak or a building failure. Supplying a `program_status` of `SUSPENDED` moves the site `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, which is permitted only when the site is currently `ACTIVE`; the transition increments `suspended_count` by 1 and increments the school's `version` by 1. Supplying `ACTIVE` moves it back `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, permitted only when the site is currently `SUSPENDED`, and increments the school's `version` by 1 without touching `suspended_count`. The cycle may repeat without limit — there is no cap on how many times a site is suspended and reinstated. Attempting to suspend an already-suspended site, or reinstate an already-active one, is a state error and returns `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Reading a site back exposes both its posture and its conserved `telehealth_booked_minutes_total`.

Clinicians are registered separately, each with a `full_name`, a `license_number` and a `discipline` that is one of `MEDICAL`, `BEHAVIORAL` or `DENTAL`. Discipline is a hard scope-of-practice boundary rather than a label: an acute encounter and a sports-physical encounter both require a `MEDICAL` clinician, a behavioral encounter requires a `BEHAVIORAL` clinician, and a dental encounter requires a `DENTAL` clinician. Scheduling an encounter with a clinician whose discipline does not match the mapping for the requested visit type is refused with `ERR_DISCIPLINE_MISMATCH`.

### 3.2. The Student Record, the Household and the Family Contacts

A `Registrar` opens a student record with `first_name`, `last_name` and `date_of_birth`. The birth date is an ISO calendar date and must be strictly before the current logical clock date — a birth date equal to today's logical date is rejected as invalid. A freshly created student is deliberately pessimistic on every derived field: `fpl_percent` is 0.00 and `discount_tier` is `TIER_A` (an income of 0 in a household of 1 is 0.00% of the poverty level, which lands in the deepest-discount tier), `payer_route` is `SELF_PAY`, `insurance_member_id` is null, `household_income_annual_cents` is 0, `household_size` is 1, `immunization_exemption_type` is `NONE`, `immunization_compliance_status` is `NON_COMPLIANT`, all four dose counters — `valid_dose_count`, `invalid_dose_count`, `voided_dose_count` and `total_dose_count` — are 0, and `version` is 1. Reading a student back exposes all of these, including the `version` that every optimistic-concurrency write compares against.

The **sliding fee position** is written by the financial-profile operation, which a `Registrar` uses to record `household_income_annual_cents`, `household_size`, `payer_route` and optionally `insurance_member_id`. Income must be at least 0 and household size at least 1; the payer route is one of `MEDICAID`, `PRIVATE_INSURANCE` or `SELF_PAY`, and `insurance_member_id` is null for self-pay families. From these the server derives the household's position on the federal poverty level in two pinned steps. **First**, it computes the poverty threshold for that household: 1,560,000 cents for a household of one, plus 550,000 cents for each additional person — so a household of four has a threshold of 3,210,000 cents. It then divides the household's annual income by that threshold, multiplies by one hundred, keeps the quotient exact with no truncation along the way, and rounds the result half-up to 2 decimal places to produce `fpl_percent`. **Second**, and this order is deliberate, it selects `discount_tier` using the *rounded* percentage, not the raw one: at or below 100.00% the tier is `TIER_A`; above 100.00% and at or below 138.00% it is `TIER_B`; above 138.00% and at or below 175.00% it is `TIER_C`; above 175.00% and at or below 200.00% it is `TIER_D`; and strictly above 200.00% it is `TIER_E`. Rounding before banding is what decides the edges — a raw percentage of 100.0000641 rounds to 100.00 and therefore lands in `TIER_A`, not `TIER_B`. Each tier carries a discount fraction used later by the billing rules: 1.00 for `TIER_A`, 0.80 for `TIER_B`, 0.60 for `TIER_C`, 0.40 for `TIER_D` and 0.00 for `TIER_E`. Writing a financial profile increments the student's `version` by 1; charges already minted keep the tier and payer route snapshotted onto them and are never retroactively repriced.

Guardians are added one at a time against a student and carry `full_name`, `relationship` — one of `MOTHER`, `FATHER`, `LEGAL_GUARDIAN` or `OTHER` — a `contact_email` that serves as the delivery address for parent notifications, and an optional `is_primary` flag that defaults to false. Every guardian also carries `notification_opt_out`, which defaults to false and can be flipped later by the guardian themself or by a `Registrar`; flipping it affects only notifications created afterwards, and notifications already created keep whatever status they were given. Listing a student's guardians returns them in the selector's own order: primary contacts first, and within each group oldest `created_at` first.

That ordering is not cosmetic — it *is* the **notification-target selector**. Whenever any event in this system raises a parent notification, the server takes every guardian row belonging to the event's student as the candidate set (there is no status filter on guardians, so every row of that student is a candidate), ranks them with `is_primary` true before `is_primary` false and, within a group, earlier `created_at` first, breaks any remaining tie by `created_at` ascending and never by identifier, and selects the first row. Four outcomes follow. If the candidate set is empty the notification is created with a null `guardian_id`, a status of `UNDELIVERABLE` and a `suppression_reason` of `NO_GUARDIAN_ON_FILE`. Otherwise, if the encounter that raised the event is confidential, the notification is created against the selected guardian with status `SUPPRESSED_CONFIDENTIAL` and a `suppression_reason` of `CONFIDENTIAL_SERVICE`. Otherwise, if the selected guardian has opted out, it is created against them with status `SUPPRESSED_OPT_OUT` and a `suppression_reason` of `GUARDIAN_OPT_OUT`. Otherwise it is created against them in `QUEUED` with a null `suppression_reason` and a `delivery_attempt_count` of 0. These branches are evaluated in exactly that order, so a confidential encounter whose selected guardian has also opted out produces `SUPPRESSED_CONFIDENTIAL`, not `SUPPRESSED_OPT_OUT`.

### 3.3. Enrolling a Student for a School Year

Enrollment ties a student to a site for one academic year and is the first of the two gates that stand between a child and an encounter. A `Registrar` creates one by naming the `school_id` and the `school_year`, together with the `expected_student_version` that §2.7 requires. The site must currently be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SCHOOL_SUSPENDED`, and the year label must be one of the two supported years. A new enrollment is born in `PENDING`, with `activated_at` and `withdrawn_at` both null, `withdrawal_reason` null and `reinstated_count` at 0.

Creating an enrollment is a supersede-on-create operation, and that is precisely why it is version-guarded. Any enrollment already on file for the same student and the same school year whose status is `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` is moved to `TRANSFERRED` and stamped with a `withdrawn_at` of the current logical instant — both the `PENDING → TRANSFERRED` and the `ACTIVE → TRANSFERRED` legs behave identically in this respect. A transfer is *not* a withdrawal: encounters already anchored to the displaced enrollment keep pointing at it and are **not** cancelled. `TRANSFERRED` is terminal; nothing leaves it. The create also increments the student's `version` by 1.

Activation is what starts the clock on immunization grace. Moving a `PENDING` enrollment to `ACTIVE` is permitted only from `PENDING` and only while the site is `ACTIVE`; it stamps `activated_at` with the current logical instant, recomputes the student's immunization compliance posture — which now has an anchor for its provisional window — and increments the student's `version` by 1.

Withdrawal is the destructive leg. Moving an `ACTIVE` enrollment to `WITHDRAWN` requires a `reason`, which is stored as `withdrawal_reason`, and stamps `withdrawn_at` with the current logical instant. It then cascades: every encounter anchored to that enrollment whose clinical state is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` moves to `CANCELLED`; every telehealth session belonging to one of those just-cancelled encounters whose own status is `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED`, and each such cancellation decrements the site's `telehealth_booked_minutes_total` by that session's `duration_minutes`; and finally the student's immunization compliance posture is recomputed and the student's `version` increments by 1.

Reinstatement closes the cycle `ACTIVE → WITHDRAWN → ACTIVE` on the same row, and it is capped: a row may be reinstated at most 2 times, so the third attempt on the same enrollment is refused with `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Reinstating requires the row to be `WITHDRAWN`, the site to be `ACTIVE`, and `reinstated_count` to be strictly below 2. It increments `reinstated_count` by 1, resets `activated_at` to the current logical instant so that the immunization provisional window restarts from the reinstatement rather than from the original activation, clears `withdrawn_at` back to null, recomputes the compliance posture and increments the student's `version` by 1. Cancelled encounters are not resurrected by a reinstatement.

Enrollments can be read individually or listed with filters on `student_id`, `school_id`, `school_year` and `status`.

### 3.4. Consent: Who Grants It, What It Covers, How Long It Lasts

Consent is the second gate, and it is the one with the sharpest legal edges. A consent record names its `category`, its `school_year`, who granted it through `granted_by`, and the identity of that grantor through `granted_by_user_id`. Five categories exist: `GENERAL_TREATMENT`, `BEHAVIORAL_HEALTH`, `CONFIDENTIAL_SERVICES`, `DENTAL` and `TELEHEALTH`. Consent creation is optimistic-concurrency guarded on the student, so `expected_student_version` travels with the request.

There are exactly two grant paths. When `granted_by` is `GUARDIAN`, `granted_by_user_id` must be the identifier of a guardian row belonging to the student named in the path; a guardian identifier belonging to another child is not a valid grantor. When `granted_by` is `STUDENT_SELF`, `granted_by_user_id` is the student's own identifier, and the minor-consent-by-law rules apply: the category must be `BEHAVIORAL_HEALTH` or `CONFIDENTIAL_SERVICES`, and the student's age in whole completed years at the current logical clock date must be at least 14. A student who turns 14 exactly today qualifies; a student one day short does not. Either failure is refused with `ERR_SELF_CONSENT_NOT_PERMITTED`. Separately, a principal acting in the `Student` role may only self-consent on their own record — a mismatch between `X-User-ID` and the student identifier in the path is an `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION`.

Every consent is born `ACTIVE` with `effective_at` set to the current logical instant, `revoked_at` and `revocation_reason` null, and `expires_at` set to the school-year end instant of its own `school_year` — `2026-07-31T23:59:59.000Z` for `2025-2026`, `2027-07-31T23:59:59.000Z` for `2026-2027`. A consent authorizes an operation while its status is `ACTIVE` **and** the current logical instant is at or before `expires_at`; at exactly `expires_at` the consent is still usable. Creating a consent also supersedes: any consent already on file for the same student, the same `category` and the same `school_year` whose status is `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`. Encounters already anchored to the superseded consent keep their consent link and keep their confidentiality flag — supersession never rewrites history. The create increments the student's `version` by 1.

Revocation moves a consent `ACTIVE → REVOKED`, is permitted only from `ACTIVE`, records `revoked_at` as the current logical instant and stores the supplied `reason` as `revocation_reason`. It may be requested by a `Registrar`, or by the `Guardian` or `Student` who granted it — an entity-backed actor whose `X-User-ID` does not equal the consent's `granted_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION`. Revoking a `TELEHEALTH` consent triggers a four-link cascade, and each link must fire: the consent itself becomes `REVOKED`; every telehealth session whose encounter belongs to that student and whose own status is `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED`; each such cancellation decrements the hosting site's `telehealth_booked_minutes_total` by that session's `duration_minutes`; and the encounter behind each cancelled session moves its clinical state to `CANCELLED` when it is still `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. Revoking a consent in any other category cascades to nothing.

A consent can also leave `ACTIVE` without anyone asking. The daily epoch advance moves any `ACTIVE` consent whose `expires_at` is strictly before the current logical instant to `EXPIRED`; at exactly `expires_at` the consent survives the sweep. Expiry cascades to nothing — the consent simply stops authorizing new encounters. `REVOKED`, `EXPIRED` and `SUPERSEDED` are all terminal. Re-consenting after a revocation is not a transition out of `REVOKED`; it is simply a fresh consent record created by the same grant path, and the revoked row stays revoked forever.

Which category a given encounter needs is fixed by the service line. An acute encounter needs `GENERAL_TREATMENT` when it is not flagged as a confidential service, and `CONFIDENTIAL_SERVICES` when it is; a behavioral encounter needs `BEHAVIORAL_HEALTH`; a dental encounter needs `DENTAL`; and a sports-physical encounter needs `GENERAL_TREATMENT`. On top of whichever of those applies, an encounter delivered by telehealth additionally needs an `ACTIVE`, unexpired consent in the `TELEHEALTH` category. Consents may be read as a list for one student with filters on `category`, `status` and `school_year`.

### 3.5. The Encounter — Scheduling, Delivering and Closing

An encounter is the unit of clinical work, and it carries **two independent state axes on the same row**. The clinical axis, `clinical_status`, tracks what happened to the child: `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` or `NO_SHOW`. The billing axis, `billing_status`, tracks what happened to the money: `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `PENDING_CHARGES`, `CHARGES_POSTED` or `REVERSED`. The two evolve independently — the billing axis is a pure function of the encounter's charge rows and moves without any clinical-axis change, and the clinical axis never waits for money to settle.

A `Clinician` schedules an encounter by supplying `student_id`, `school_id`, `clinician_id`, `visit_type` and `scheduled_start_at`, plus optionally `delivery_mode` (which defaults to `IN_PERSON` and may be set to `TELEHEALTH`) and `confidential_service_requested` (which defaults to false). `visit_type` is one of `ACUTE`, `BEHAVIORAL`, `DENTAL` or `SPORTS_PHYSICAL`. Five gates then run in the ladder order of §2.6: the site must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_SCHOOL_SUSPENDED`); the student must hold an enrollment at that site for the current school year whose status is `ACTIVE` (`ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`); a consent of the category §3.4 maps this visit type to must exist with `ACTIVE` status and an expiry at or after the current logical instant (`ERR_CONSENT_REQUIRED`); a telehealth delivery additionally needs an `ACTIVE` unexpired `TELEHEALTH` consent (`ERR_TELEHEALTH_CONSENT_REQUIRED`); and the named clinician's discipline must match the mapping in §3.1 (`ERR_DISCIPLINE_MISMATCH`).

What the server stores on a successful schedule is more than what the client sent. It resolves and stores `enrollment_id`, the identifier of the `ACTIVE` enrollment it matched; it resolves and stores `consent_id`, the identifier of the governing consent; and it computes `confidential`, which is true exactly when that governing consent was granted by the student themself and false otherwise. Confidentiality is decided once, at scheduling, from the consent that governs this encounter — later supersession of that consent does not change the flag. The encounter opens with `clinical_status` of `SCHEDULED` and `billing_status` of `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `completed_at` null and `disposition` null.

Beginning the encounter moves it `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS`. It is permitted only from `SCHEDULED`, only while the site's program posture is still `ACTIVE`, and only while the governing consent is still `ACTIVE` with an expiry at or after the current logical instant — a consent revoked or expired between scheduling and the child walking in stops the encounter with `ERR_CONSENT_REQUIRED`, which is the whole point of re-checking. No other side effect attaches to this transition.

Completing the encounter moves it `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` and requires a `disposition`, one of `TREATED_RETURNED_TO_CLASS`, `TREATED_SENT_HOME`, `REFERRED_OUT` or `NO_TREATMENT_NEEDED`. If the encounter is delivered by telehealth, completion additionally demands that a telehealth session belonging to this encounter has itself reached `COMPLETED`; attempting to complete a telehealth encounter with no completed session behind it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Completion stamps `completed_at` with the current logical instant, stores the `disposition`, and then fires a five-link cascade that must be applied in order: **one** billable charge is minted for the encounter's own service category, plus **one** additional immunization-administration charge for every immunization dose linked to this encounter whose status is `VALID`; the provisional sliding-fee calculation of §3.10 runs on each new charge; the encounter nominal-fee floor of §3.10 runs across the whole new charge set and may write a top-up onto exactly one of them; the encounter's five bucket totals are recomputed from the charge set and the billing axis moves accordingly; and one parent notification of event type `VISIT_COMPLETED` is created through the selector of §3.2, created directly in `SUPPRESSED_CONFIDENTIAL` when the encounter's `confidential` flag is true.

An encounter that never happened is closed as unfulfilled by supplying an `outcome` of either `CANCELLED` or `NO_SHOW`. `CANCELLED` is accepted from `SCHEDULED` and from `IN_PROGRESS`, and in both cases every telehealth session on this encounter whose status is `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED`, decrementing the site's `telehealth_booked_minutes_total` by each such session's `duration_minutes`. `NO_SHOW` is accepted only from `SCHEDULED`, and only once the current logical instant has reached `scheduled_start_at` plus 10 minutes — the boundary is inclusive, so exactly 10 minutes past the scheduled start is permitted while 9 minutes is refused with `ERR_NO_SHOW_TOO_EARLY`. A no-show close moves every `SCHEDULED` telehealth session on that encounter to `NO_SHOW`, likewise decrementing the site's booked-minutes total. `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` and `NO_SHOW` are all terminal on the clinical axis.

Encounters can be read individually — the read exposes both axes and all five charge buckets — or listed with filters on `student_id`, `school_id`, `clinician_id`, `enrollment_id`, `consent_id`, `clinical_status` and `billing_status`.

### 3.6. Telehealth into the Classroom

A telehealth encounter is delivered by a clinician who is somewhere else, into a classroom where a presenter and a device are physically with the child. That means every booking consumes **two** distinct resources, and the service checks both, in a fixed order.

A `Clinician` books a session by naming the `visit_id` it delivers and the window `start_at` to `end_at`. The encounter must have `delivery_mode` of `TELEHEALTH` and a clinical state of `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the site must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_SCHOOL_SUSPENDED`; and an `ACTIVE`, unexpired `TELEHEALTH` consent must exist for that student, otherwise `ERR_TELEHEALTH_CONSENT_REQUIRED`. The window itself must have `end_at` strictly after `start_at`, and the resulting `duration_minutes` — the whole number of minutes between the two instants — must be at least 15 and at most 60, both bounds inclusive; a 15-minute or 60-minute session is fine, a 14-minute or 61-minute one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SESSION_WINDOW`.

**The clinician axis uses buffered intervals.** A clinician needs turnaround time between classrooms, and that turnaround is 15 minutes. The proposed window conflicts with an existing session of the same clinician — considering only sessions whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`, and excluding the very row being rescheduled from its own check — when the proposed start is earlier than that session's end extended forward by 15 minutes **and** that session's start is earlier than the proposed end extended forward by 15 minutes. A gap of exactly 15 minutes on either side therefore does **not** conflict: an existing session running 09:00 to 09:30 leaves a proposed 09:45 to 10:15 session free, while a proposed 09:44 to 10:14 session collides. A collision is refused with `ERR_CLINICIAN_SLOT_CONFLICT`.

**The site axis uses plain half-open intervals with an aggregate capacity and no buffer.** The server counts the sessions at the same site, again only those whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`, whose window overlaps the proposed one — meaning the proposed start is strictly earlier than the existing end and the existing start is strictly earlier than the proposed end. Adjacency is therefore fine on this axis: an existing 09:00 to 09:30 session and a proposed 09:30 to 10:00 session do not overlap at all. If that count has reached the site's `telehealth_concurrent_capacity`, the booking is refused with `ERR_SCHOOL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; the booking is permitted only while the count is strictly below capacity. Sessions in `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED` never block a new booking on either axis. The clinician check runs before the site check, so a window that fails both reports the clinician conflict.

A successful booking copies `clinician_id` and `school_id` from the parent encounter onto the session, computes `duration_minutes`, opens the row in `SCHEDULED`, adds `duration_minutes` to the site's `telehealth_booked_minutes_total` and increments the site's `version` by 1.

Sessions then move in four ways. Completing one moves it `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` and leaves the booked-minutes total untouched — a completed session keeps its booking — and it is completion of the session that unlocks completion of the parent encounter. Closing one with an `outcome` of `CANCELLED` is accepted from `SCHEDULED`, where it releases the booking by decrementing `telehealth_booked_minutes_total` by `duration_minutes` and incrementing the site's `version` by 1, and is also accepted from `NO_SHOW`, where the booking was already released by the no-show and so the total is left unchanged. Closing one with an `outcome` of `NO_SHOW` is accepted only from `SCHEDULED` and only once the current logical instant has reached `start_at` plus 10 minutes inclusive, otherwise `ERR_NO_SHOW_TOO_EARLY`; it releases the booking and increments the site's `version` by 1. Either close may carry an optional `closure_reason`, stored as free text.

Rescheduling closes the loop `SCHEDULED → NO_SHOW → SCHEDULED`. It is accepted only from `NO_SHOW`, is capped at 2 reschedules per row so that a third attempt is refused with `ERR_RESCHEDULE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, and re-runs every window check that creation ran — the same 15-to-60-minute duration bounds, the same 15-minute buffered clinician check and the same half-open site-capacity check. On success it replaces `start_at`, `end_at` and `duration_minutes` with the new window, increments `reschedule_count` by 1, adds the **new** duration to the site's `telehealth_booked_minutes_total` and increments the site's `version` by 1. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. Sessions can be read individually or listed with filters on `visit_id`, `clinician_id`, `school_id` and `status`.

### 3.7. Immunization Records and the Compliance Posture

Two series are required of every student: hepatitis B, recorded under the `series_code` `HEPB` and complete at 3 doses, and measles-mumps-rubella, recorded under `MMR` and complete at 2 doses. A `Clinician` records an administered dose against a student by supplying `series_code`, `dose_number`, `administered_date` and optionally the `visit_id` of the encounter the dose was given at — that link is null when the dose is being transcribed from outside records rather than given on site. `dose_number` must be between 1 and 3 for `HEPB` and between 1 and 2 for `MMR`. Recording a dose requires the student to hold an `ACTIVE` enrollment for the current school year, otherwise `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Three checks then run in ladder order. `dose_number` must equal exactly one more than the highest dose number among that student's `VALID` doses of that series — so the first dose of an empty series must be 1, and skipping straight to 3 is refused with `ERR_DOSE_SEQUENCE`. No `VALID` dose may already exist for the same student, `series_code` and `dose_number`, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_DOSE`. And `administered_date` must not be after the current logical clock date, otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION`.

**A dose given too early is recorded, not rejected.** This is the single most important thing to understand about this part of the system: the record of a premature dose is what makes the two compliance countings observable, so the server always stores the row and marks it. Two dates are computed for every dose. The **strict earliest valid date** depends on the position in the series. For a first dose it is the student's `date_of_birth` advanced by the series minimum age: 0 days for `HEPB`, so any date on or after the birth date qualifies, and 365 days for `MMR`. For a later dose the server looks for the **predecessor** — the dose of the same student and same series whose `dose_number` is exactly one less and whose status is `VALID`; doses that are themselves premature or voided are never usable as predecessors. If such a predecessor exists, the strict earliest valid date is that predecessor's `administered_date` advanced by the series interval: 28 days for the second `HEPB` dose, 56 days for the third `HEPB` dose, and 28 days for the second `MMR` dose. The **earliest valid date** is then the strict date pulled 4 days earlier — a grace of 4 calendar days. Both dates are stored, as `strict_earliest_valid_date` and `earliest_valid_date` respectively.

The dose's `status` follows directly: it is `VALID` when `administered_date` falls on or after `earliest_valid_date` — the boundary is inclusive, so a dose given exactly on the earliest valid date counts — and `INVALID_TOO_EARLY` otherwise. A dose that fails carries an `invalid_reason` of `MIN_AGE_NOT_MET` when it is a first dose and `MIN_INTERVAL_NOT_MET` when it is a later one. A later dose whose predecessor cannot be found at all is stored as `INVALID_TOO_EARLY` with `invalid_reason` of `PREDECESSOR_VOIDED`, and in that case both computed dates keep whatever values they last held rather than being recomputed. A `VALID` dose has a null `invalid_reason`. Recording any dose recomputes the student's four counters — `valid_dose_count` counts doses in `VALID`, `invalid_dose_count` counts doses in `INVALID_TOO_EARLY`, `voided_dose_count` counts doses in `VOIDED`, and `total_dose_count` is every dose row for that student and equals the sum of the other three — recomputes the compliance posture, and increments the student's `version` by 1.

The **compliance posture** on the student is what gates athletic clearance, and it is computed the *grace-counting* way. Counting only doses whose status is `VALID`, and evaluating the branches in this order: a student with any `immunization_exemption_type` other than `NONE` is `COMPLIANT`; otherwise a student with at least 3 valid `HEPB` doses and at least 2 valid `MMR` doses is `COMPLIANT`; otherwise a student whose `ACTIVE` enrollment has a non-null `activated_at`, whose current logical clock date is at or before that activation date advanced by 30 days, and who has at least 1 valid `HEPB` dose and at least 1 valid `MMR` dose, is `PROVISIONAL`; otherwise the student is `NON_COMPLIANT`. The 30-day provisional window is inclusive on its far end — on day 30 the student is still provisional, on day 31 they are not — and it restarts from scratch on every reinstatement because reinstatement resets `activated_at`.

A second, *strict* counting of the very same stored doses exists purely for reporting. Under strict counting a dose counts only when its status is `VALID` **and** its `administered_date` is on or after its `strict_earliest_valid_date`, meaning the 4-day grace is withdrawn, and exemptions are ignored entirely. A student is strictly compliant only when they have at least 3 strictly-counted `HEPB` doses and at least 2 strictly-counted `MMR` doses. The same record can therefore be `COMPLIANT` on the student row and not compliant in the report, which is exactly the gap the funders want to see. Only the grace counting is ever written to the student.

Exemptions are set through a `Registrar` operation carrying `exemption_type`, one of `NONE`, `MEDICAL` or `RELIGIOUS`, which is stored as the student's `immunization_exemption_type`; sending `NONE` clears an exemption already on file. Either direction recomputes the compliance posture and increments the student's `version` by 1. Clearing an exemption that leaves the student `NON_COMPLIANT` is not a quiet bookkeeping change: it fires the compliance cascade described next, and raises a parent notification of event type `IMMUNIZATION_NON_COMPLIANT`.

Voiding a dose is the cascade that hurts, and all five of its links must fire. A `Clinician` voids a dose with a free-text `reason`, stored as `void_reason`; a dose may be voided from `VALID` or from `INVALID_TOO_EARLY`, and `VOIDED` is terminal. **Link one:** the dose itself becomes `VOIDED` and stops counting anywhere. **Link two:** because that dose is no longer valid, every later dose of the same student and series is re-evaluated, and any dose that has lost its valid predecessor moves `VALID → INVALID_TOO_EARLY` with `invalid_reason` set to `PREDECESSOR_VOIDED`. **Link three:** the student's counters and compliance posture are recomputed from the new dose statuses — the posture may fall to `NON_COMPLIANT` — and the student's `version` increments by 1. **Link four**, which fires only when link three actually produced `NON_COMPLIANT`: the student's sports-physical clearance whose status is `ACTIVE` or `PENDING_REEVALUATION` moves to `VOIDED` with a `void_reason` of `IMMUNIZATION_COMPLIANCE_LOST`. **Link five:** that clearance void creates a parent notification of event type `CLEARANCE_VOIDED`. When both notifications arise from one void, their order is pinned: the `IMMUNIZATION_NON_COMPLIANT` notification raised by link three is created **before** the `CLEARANCE_VOIDED` notification raised by link five. Voiding a dose that was already `INVALID_TOO_EARLY` still recomputes the counters and the posture and still increments the student's `version` by 1.

Doses can be read individually or listed with filters on `student_id`, `visit_id`, `series_code` and `status`.

### 3.8. Screening, Referral and Closing the Loop

Screenings are recorded against an encounter that is either `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED`; a screening against a `SCHEDULED`, `CANCELLED` or `NO_SHOW` encounter is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A `Clinician` supplies an `instrument` — one of `VISION`, `HEARING`, `DENTAL`, `BMI` or `BEHAVIORAL` — and a `numeric_result`, which must be at least 0 and is interpreted per instrument as a Snellen denominator, a decibel hearing level, a count of untreated caries, a BMI percentile, or a depression-screen total. The `student_id` is copied from the parent encounter. No rounding is applied to `numeric_result` before it is banded. Every screening is created in `RECORDED` and leaves that state within the same request, driven by the band the server computes.

The band is decided server-side and each instrument has its own boundary, some inclusive and some not. A `VISION` result refers at 40 or more — the boundary is inclusive. A `HEARING` result refers only when it is strictly greater than 25, so exactly 25 passes. A `DENTAL` result refers at 1 or more, inclusive, so a single untreated carie refers. A `BMI` result refers when it is strictly below 5 **or** at or above 95 — the low boundary excludes 5 itself while the high boundary includes 95. A `BEHAVIORAL` result refers at 10 or more, inclusive. Anything else passes. The screening's `outcome` is then `PASS` or `REFER`, and `band_label` mirrors it: `NORMAL` on a pass, `ABNORMAL` on a refer.

A passing screening moves `RECORDED → CLOSED_PASS`, leaves `generated_referral_id` null and cascades to nothing. A referring screening moves `RECORDED → REFERRED` and fires a four-link cascade. **Link one** writes the `REFER` outcome and the `ABNORMAL` band. **Link two** auto-creates a referral in `OPEN` whose `source_screening_id` is this screening, whose `source_visit_id` is the encounter, whose `requested_by_user_id` is the acting clinician's `X-User-ID`, and whose `specialty` comes from the instrument: `VISION` refers to `OPHTHALMOLOGY`, `HEARING` to `AUDIOLOGY`, `DENTAL` to `DENTISTRY`, `BMI` to `NUTRITION` and `BEHAVIORAL` to `BEHAVIORAL_HEALTH`; the screening's `generated_referral_id` is set to that new referral, and the referral itself raises a parent notification of event type `REFERRAL_CREATED`. **Link three** fires because a referral with a non-null `source_screening_id` now exists for this student: the student's sports-physical clearance whose status is `ACTIVE` or `PENDING_REEVALUATION` moves to `VOIDED` with a `void_reason` of `SCREENING_REFERRAL_RAISED`. **Link four** creates the `CLEARANCE_VOIDED` parent notification for that void. The two notifications are ordered: `REFERRAL_CREATED` is created **before** `CLEARANCE_VOIDED`. Both `CLOSED_PASS` and `REFERRED` are terminal for a screening. Screenings can be read individually or listed with filters on `student_id`, `visit_id`, `instrument`, `outcome` and `generated_referral_id`.

A clinician may also raise a referral directly, without a screening behind it, by supplying a `specialty` — one of `OPHTHALMOLOGY`, `AUDIOLOGY`, `DENTISTRY`, `BEHAVIORAL_HEALTH`, `PRIMARY_CARE` or `NUTRITION` — a `reason`, and optionally the `source_visit_id` of the encounter it was raised at. Direct referrals require the student to hold an `ACTIVE` enrollment, otherwise `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and they leave `source_screening_id` null, which means they do **not** void a clearance. Every referral, however it is raised, opens in `OPEN` with `outreach_attempt_count` at 0, records `requested_by_user_id` from the acting clinician's `X-User-ID`, and raises a `REFERRAL_CREATED` parent notification subject to the confidentiality and opt-out suppression rules.

Working the loop belongs to a `CareCoordinator`, who logs each attempt with an `outcome` of either `REACHED` or `NO_ANSWER`. Every logged attempt increments `outreach_attempt_count` by 1, and the cap is 3 attempts. A `NO_ANSWER` from `OPEN` moves the referral `OPEN → OUTREACH_IN_PROGRESS`. A further `NO_ANSWER` while the counter is still strictly below 2 keeps it looping `OUTREACH_IN_PROGRESS → OUTREACH_IN_PROGRESS`. The `NO_ANSWER` logged when the counter stands at exactly 2 is the third attempt: it takes the counter to 3 and moves the referral `OUTREACH_IN_PROGRESS → LOST_TO_FOLLOW_UP`, which is terminal. A `REACHED` outcome, from either `OPEN` or `OUTREACH_IN_PROGRESS` and while the counter is strictly below 3, increments the counter and moves the referral to `SCHEDULED`. Once the counter has reached 3, any further attempt is refused with `ERR_OUTREACH_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` — and per §2.6 that is the answer even on a referral already sitting in `LOST_TO_FOLLOW_UP`, where a naive reading would have produced a state error instead.

Once the external provider reports back, the coordinator records `report_summary` and `report_received_date`, moving the referral `SCHEDULED → REPORT_RECEIVED`. The received date must not be after the current logical clock date, otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION`. Only from `REPORT_RECEIVED` may the loop be closed, and closure is where segregation of duties bites: the closer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the referral's `requested_by_user_id`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A permitted closure stores the acting identifier as `closed_by_user_id` along with the supplied `closure_note` and moves the referral `REPORT_RECEIVED → CLOSED`, which is terminal. Referrals can be read individually — the read exposes the outreach counter and the closer's identity — or listed with filters on `student_id`, `source_screening_id`, `source_visit_id`, `requested_by_user_id` and `status`.

### 3.9. The Sports-Physical Clearance

An athletic participation determination is recorded against the sports-physical encounter that produced it. The encounter must have a `visit_type` of `SPORTS_PHYSICAL` and a clinical state of `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The student's `immunization_compliance_status` must be anything other than `NON_COMPLIANT` — both `COMPLIANT` and `PROVISIONAL` are acceptable — otherwise `ERR_IMMUNIZATION_NON_COMPLIANT`. The determination is optimistic-concurrency guarded on the student, so `expected_student_version` travels with it. The clearance copies `student_id` from the parent encounter and copies `school_year` from that encounter's enrollment; the copied year is what scopes both supersession and the validity cap.

`outcome` is one of four values, and they split the row across two starting states. `CLEARED`, `CLEARED_WITH_RESTRICTIONS` and `NOT_CLEARED` all open the clearance directly in `ACTIVE`. `DEFERRED` opens it in `PENDING_REEVALUATION` instead. `CLEARED_WITH_RESTRICTIONS` additionally requires a non-null `restrictions` text describing what the student may not do; omitting it is `ERR_VALIDATION`. All four paths stamp `determination_date` with the current logical clock date, start `reevaluation_count` at 0 and leave `void_reason` null. All four also supersede: any clearance already on file for the same student and same `school_year` whose status is `ACTIVE` or `PENDING_REEVALUATION` moves to `SUPERSEDED` with no further side effect. All four increment the student's `version` by 1, and all four raise one parent notification of event type `CLEARANCE_DETERMINED`, created directly in `SUPPRESSED_CONFIDENTIAL` when the parent encounter's `confidential` flag is true.

Two validity windows are computed and **both are stored**, because the calendar answer and the academic answer differ. The validity length is 365 days for `CLEARED` and 180 days for `CLEARED_WITH_RESTRICTIONS`; for `DEFERRED` and `NOT_CLEARED` there is no validity length at all, and both windows are stored as null. `unrestricted_valid_until` is the advisory, pure-calendar answer: `determination_date` advanced by the validity length. `valid_until` is the operative answer: the earlier of `unrestricted_valid_until` and the end date of the clearance's own school year — 2026-07-31 for `2025-2026` and 2027-07-31 for `2026-2027`. A `CLEARED` determination made on 2026-05-10 therefore stores an `unrestricted_valid_until` of 2027-05-10 but a `valid_until` of 2026-07-31, because a school-year clearance cannot outlive the school year. Every gate that reads a clearance reads `valid_until`, and the clearance is usable while the current logical clock date is at or before it — the boundary is inclusive.

A deferred clearance is re-evaluated rather than replaced. Re-evaluation is accepted only from `PENDING_REEVALUATION`, only while `reevaluation_count` is strictly below 2 — a third attempt on the same row is `ERR_REEVALUATION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` — and only while the current logical clock date is at or before `determination_date` advanced by 30 days; outside that window the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A re-evaluation carrying `DEFERRED` again keeps the row in `PENDING_REEVALUATION`, increments `reevaluation_count` by 1 and resets `determination_date` to the current clock date, which restarts the 30-day window from the new determination. A re-evaluation carrying `CLEARED`, `CLEARED_WITH_RESTRICTIONS` or `NOT_CLEARED` additionally re-checks that the student is not `NON_COMPLIANT` (`ERR_IMMUNIZATION_NON_COMPLIANT`), moves the row `PENDING_REEVALUATION → ACTIVE`, increments `reevaluation_count` by 1, updates `outcome` and `determination_date`, and recomputes both validity windows from the new determination date. Re-evaluation raises no parent notification.

Three ways out of the live states exist beyond re-evaluation, and each is available from both `ACTIVE` and `PENDING_REEVALUATION`. **Voiding** is driven by the system, never by a request: it fires when a student's compliance posture becomes `NON_COMPLIANT`, in which case `void_reason` is `IMMUNIZATION_COMPLIANCE_LOST`, and it also fires when a referral with a non-null originating screening is created for that student, in which case `void_reason` is `SCREENING_REFERRAL_RAISED`. Either way the void creates one `CLEARANCE_VOIDED` parent notification. **Expiry** happens during the daily epoch advance: an `ACTIVE` clearance with a non-null `valid_until` expires once the current clock date is strictly after `valid_until`, so on `valid_until` itself the clearance is still active; a `PENDING_REEVALUATION` clearance expires once the current clock date is strictly after `determination_date` advanced by 30 days, abandoning the deferral loop so that a fresh determination from a fresh encounter is the only way forward. Expiry raises no notification. **Supersession** happens when a newer clearance is created for the same student and school year, as described above. `VOIDED`, `EXPIRED` and `SUPERSEDED` are all terminal. Clearances can be read individually — the read exposes both validity variants — or listed with filters on `student_id`, `visit_id` and `status`.

### 3.10. Charges, the Sliding Fee and the Sponsor-Hospital Claim Cycle

Charges are never created by a client. They are minted by the server at the moment an encounter completes, and the set that gets minted follows a fixed rule. The encounter itself yields exactly one charge whose `service_category` is derived from how the encounter was delivered: any encounter with a `delivery_mode` of `TELEHEALTH` yields `TELEHEALTH_ENCOUNTER`, and that override beats the visit type; otherwise an `ACUTE` encounter yields `ACUTE_OFFICE_VISIT`, a `BEHAVIORAL` one yields `BEHAVIORAL_THERAPY`, a `DENTAL` one yields `DENTAL_PREVENTIVE`, and a `SPORTS_PHYSICAL` one yields `SPORTS_PHYSICAL_EXAM`. On top of that, one further charge with `service_category` of `IMMUNIZATION_ADMIN` is minted for **each** immunization dose linked to that encounter whose status is `VALID`. A charge copies `visit_id` and `student_id` from the encounter, takes its `gross_amount_cents` from the published fee schedule in §2.8, and snapshots `payer_route` and `discount_tier_snapshot` from the student as they stand at that instant. Those snapshots are frozen — a later financial-profile rewrite never reprices an existing charge, and adjudication deliberately re-reads the snapshot rather than the student's live tier.

`IMMUNIZATION_ADMIN` charges are born terminal, in `NO_CHARGE`, with a provisional responsibility of 0 and an operative `patient_responsibility_cents` of 0, and they are excluded from the encounter nominal-fee floor entirely. Their 3,000-cent gross still counts toward the encounter's gross bucket and its no-charge bucket. Every other charge is born in `PENDING`. Whichever state a charge opens in, it opens with `payer_paid_amount_cents` at 0, `patient_paid_amount_cents` at 0, `write_off_amount_cents` at 0, `floor_adjustment_cents` at 0, `appeal_count` at 0, `denial_reason` null and `final_patient_responsibility_cents` null.

The family's share is computed twice from the same stored data, under two named variants, and which one is operative depends on how far the charge has travelled. Both start from the discount fraction of the charge's own `discount_tier_snapshot` — 1.00 for `TIER_A`, 0.80 for `TIER_B`, 0.60 for `TIER_C`, 0.40 for `TIER_D`, 0.00 for `TIER_E` — and both finish by multiplying a base amount by one minus that fraction and rounding half-up to whole cents.

The **provisional** variant runs at charge creation, before any payer has been heard from, and it takes its base gross-first: the base is 0 for a no-charge service category, 0 when the snapshotted payer route is `MEDICAID`, a flat 2,500 cents when it is `PRIVATE_INSURANCE`, and the full `gross_amount_cents` when it is `SELF_PAY`. The result is stored as `provisional_patient_responsibility_cents`. The **final** variant runs only at adjudication with a paid outcome, and it takes its base net-of-payer: the residual is the gross amount less what the payer actually allowed, clamped at 0 so it never goes negative, and the base is 0 for a no-charge category, 0 for `MEDICAID`, the residual for `PRIVATE_INSURANCE`, and the full gross for `SELF_PAY`. The result is stored as `final_patient_responsibility_cents`, which is null until adjudication. Identical stored inputs produce different numbers under the two variants — a 12,000-cent gross on a `TIER_C` private-insurance charge where the payer allowed 8,000 gives a provisional 1,000 cents but a final 1,600 cents. The **operative** amount owed, `patient_responsibility_cents`, is the final value when it is non-null and the provisional value otherwise, plus whatever `floor_adjustment_cents` the encounter floor has assigned.

The **encounter nominal-fee floor** exists because a sponsor hospital will not open a chart for pennies, and it is computed across the whole encounter in four pinned steps. First, the eligible set is every charge of that encounter whose status is neither `NO_CHARGE` nor `REVERSED`; each eligible charge has its `floor_adjustment_cents` cleared to 0, and its unfloored amount is taken as its `final_patient_responsibility_cents` when that is non-null and its `provisional_patient_responsibility_cents` otherwise. Second, those unfloored amounts are summed into an encounter total. Third, the floor is applied only when all three of the following hold: the eligible set is non-empty, the student's `payer_route` is not `MEDICAID` — Medicaid families are never charged a nominal fee — and the encounter total is strictly below 500 cents, so an encounter that already totals exactly 500 is left alone. When it applies, the target is the eligible charge with the greatest `gross_amount_cents`, ties broken by earliest `created_at` and never by identifier, and that charge's `floor_adjustment_cents` is set to 500 cents minus the encounter total. Fourth, the encounter's `patient_responsibility_total_cents` becomes the sum over the eligible set of each unfloored amount plus its floor adjustment — which comes to exactly 500 when the floor fired and to the encounter total when it did not. The floor is re-derived from scratch whenever an encounter completes, whenever a charge is adjudicated, and whenever a charge is reversed.

The encounter's five buckets are likewise recomputed from the charge rows after every charge creation and every charge transition. `gross_charge_total_cents` sums `gross_amount_cents` over charges that are not `REVERSED`. `no_charge_total_cents` sums `gross_amount_cents` over charges in `NO_CHARGE`. `patient_responsibility_total_cents` sums `patient_responsibility_cents` over charges that are neither `REVERSED` nor `NO_CHARGE`. `write_off_total_cents` sums `write_off_amount_cents` over charges in `WRITTEN_OFF`. And `payer_billed_total_cents` sums, over charges that are neither `REVERSED` nor `NO_CHARGE`, the gross amount less the operative patient responsibility less the written-off amount. Every charge lands in exactly one of the four right-hand buckets, which is why the residual described in §2.10 is always 0.

A `BillingSpecialist` owns everything that happens next. **Submitting a claim** moves a charge `PENDING → CLAIM_SUBMITTED` and is permitted only when the snapshotted payer route is `MEDICAID` or `PRIVATE_INSURANCE`; a self-pay charge submitted as a claim is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_APPLICABLE`. **Recording a family payment** moves a charge `PENDING → PAID`, is permitted only when the route is `SELF_PAY` — a Medicaid or private-insurance charge is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_APPLICABLE` — and requires the supplied `amount_cents` to equal the charge's operative `patient_responsibility_cents` exactly, otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`; on success it stores `patient_paid_amount_cents`, recomputes the encounter buckets and may bubble the encounter's billing axis to `CHARGES_POSTED`.

**Adjudication** carries an `outcome` of `PAID` or `DENIED` together with `payer_paid_amount_cents` and, on a denial, a `denial_reason`. It is accepted from `CLAIM_SUBMITTED` and from `CLAIM_APPEALED`. A `PAID` adjudication requires `payer_paid_amount_cents` to be at least 0 and at most `gross_amount_cents`, otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION`; it stores that amount, computes `final_patient_responsibility_cents` under the net-of-payer variant, switches the operative `patient_responsibility_cents` to the final value, re-runs the encounter floor across the whole encounter and recomputes the buckets, moving the charge to `PAID` from either origin state. A `DENIED` adjudication requires a non-null `denial_reason`, otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION`; it stores the reason, forces `payer_paid_amount_cents` to 0, and moves the charge `CLAIM_SUBMITTED → CLAIM_DENIED` or `CLAIM_APPEALED → CLAIM_DENIED`.

**Appealing** moves a charge `CLAIM_DENIED → CLAIM_APPEALED`, carries an `appeal_note`, increments `appeal_count` by 1 and is capped at 2 appeals — a third is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The pair `CLAIM_DENIED → CLAIM_APPEALED → CLAIM_DENIED` is a genuine cycle and may run twice. **Writing off** moves a charge `CLAIM_DENIED → WRITTEN_OFF` and is permitted only once both appeals have been used, that is only when `appeal_count` equals exactly 2; attempting it earlier is refused with `ERR_WRITE_OFF_NOT_PERMITTED`. A write-off carries a `reason` and sets `write_off_amount_cents` to the gross amount less `payer_paid_amount_cents` less the operative `patient_responsibility_cents`, then recomputes the buckets.

**Reversal** is the compensating action for a charge posted in error. It carries a `reason` and is accepted from `PENDING`, `CLAIM_SUBMITTED`, `CLAIM_DENIED` and `CLAIM_APPEALED`, moving the charge to `REVERSED` in every case with identical side effects: the charge leaves every encounter bucket, its `floor_adjustment_cents` is cleared and the encounter nominal-fee floor is re-derived across the surviving charges, the buckets are recomputed, and the conserved residual stays 0. `NO_CHARGE`, `PAID`, `WRITTEN_OFF` and `REVERSED` are the four terminal charge states.

The encounter's **billing axis** follows the charge rows without anybody driving it. An encounter that completes and mints at least one charge that is not already terminal moves `NOT_APPLICABLE → PENDING_CHARGES`. An encounter that completes and mints only terminal charges — the classic case being an encounter whose entire charge set is immunization administration — moves `NOT_APPLICABLE → CHARGES_POSTED` directly. Thereafter, any charge transition that leaves every charge of the encounter terminal moves the axis `PENDING_CHARGES → CHARGES_POSTED`. And any charge transition that leaves *every* charge of the encounter in `REVERSED` moves the axis to `REVERSED`, from either `PENDING_CHARGES` or `CHARGES_POSTED`, and recomputes all five buckets to zero. The all-reversed test is evaluated **first**: when every charge is reversed, the axis lands on `REVERSED` rather than `CHARGES_POSTED`, even though every reversed charge is also technically terminal. `REVERSED` is terminal on the billing axis. Charges can be read individually — the read exposes both sliding-fee variants and the floor adjustment — or listed with filters on `student_id`, `visit_id`, `status` and `service_category`.

### 3.11. Telling the Family — Notification Delivery, Retry and Acknowledgement

Parent notifications are never created directly by anyone; they are raised as a by-product of five domain events, recorded in `event_type`. `VISIT_COMPLETED` is raised when an encounter completes. `CLEARANCE_DETERMINED` is raised when an athletic determination is recorded, whether that determination opened the clearance in `ACTIVE` or in `PENDING_REEVALUATION`. `CLEARANCE_VOIDED` is raised when a clearance is voided, by either void reason. `REFERRAL_CREATED` is raised when a referral is raised, whether by a clinician directly or automatically by a referring screening. And `IMMUNIZATION_NON_COMPLIANT` is raised when a student's compliance posture falls to `NON_COMPLIANT`. Each notification copies `student_id` from the event, copies `visit_id` from the encounter that raised it when there is one and leaves it null when the event is not anchored to an encounter, and takes its `guardian_id` and its opening status from the selector in §3.2.

Delivery is an `SbhcAdmin` operation carrying an `outcome` of `DELIVERED` or `FAILED`, and it is capped at 3 attempts. From `QUEUED`, a `DELIVERED` outcome increments `delivery_attempt_count` by 1, stamps `sent_at` with the current logical instant and moves the row `QUEUED → SENT`; a `FAILED` outcome increments the counter by 1 and moves it `QUEUED → FAILED`. From `FAILED`, a further `FAILED` while the counter is still strictly below 2 keeps it looping `FAILED → FAILED` with the counter incremented; the `FAILED` logged when the counter stands at exactly 2 is the third attempt, takes the counter to 3, moves the row `FAILED → UNDELIVERABLE` and leaves `suppression_reason` null — an exhausted notification is not a suppressed one. A `DELIVERED` outcome from `FAILED`, while the counter is strictly below 3, rescues the notification: the counter increments, `sent_at` is stamped and the row moves `FAILED → SENT`. Once the counter has reached 3, any further attempt is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED` — including on a row already sitting in `UNDELIVERABLE`, where §2.6 puts the cap check above the state check precisely so that this error remains observable.

Acknowledgement belongs to the guardian and to nobody else. It is accepted only from `SENT`; the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the notification's `guardian_id`, otherwise `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION`; and it must happen while the current logical instant is at or before `sent_at` advanced by 14 days — the boundary is inclusive, so an acknowledgement at exactly 14 days lands, and one at 14 days and a minute is refused with `ERR_ACK_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. A successful acknowledgement stamps `acknowledged_at` with the current logical instant and moves the row `SENT → ACKNOWLEDGED`.

Four of the seven notification states are terminal: `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `UNDELIVERABLE`, `SUPPRESSED_CONFIDENTIAL` and `SUPPRESSED_OPT_OUT`. The two suppressed states deserve emphasis because their asymmetry is deliberate: an encounter governed by a consent the student granted themself is `confidential`, and every notification that encounter would otherwise raise is created directly in `SUPPRESSED_CONFIDENTIAL` with a `suppression_reason` of `CONFIDENTIAL_SERVICE` — the record exists for audit but is never delivered and can never be delivered. The identical event on a guardian-consented encounter produces a `QUEUED` notification that will be delivered normally. Notifications can be read individually or listed with filters on `student_id`, `guardian_id`, `visit_id`, `status` and `event_type`.

### 3.12. The Daily Epoch Advance and Aggregate Reporting

Two administrator capabilities close out the system. The first is the **daily epoch advance**, a sweep that applies every time-driven consequence that has accumulated since the clock last moved. It expires every `ACTIVE` consent whose `expires_at` is strictly before the current logical instant; it expires clearances, moving `ACTIVE` rows whose `valid_until` has passed and `PENDING_REEVALUATION` rows whose 30-day deferral window has closed, both strictly; and it recomputes every student's `immunization_compliance_status`, which is where a `PROVISIONAL` posture lapses to `NON_COMPLIANT` once the 30-day provisional window closes. Each student whose posture changes has their `version` incremented by 1 and may therefore trigger the clearance-void cascade and its `CLEARANCE_VOIDED` notification. The sweep reports what it did: `consents_expired`, `clearances_expired`, `students_recomputed` and the `current_utc` it ran at.

The second is the **aggregate report**, an immutable snapshot taken over an inclusive date period. An `SbhcAdmin` supplies `period_start_date` and `period_end_date`, optionally a `school_id` to scope the snapshot to one site and optionally a `visit_type_filter` to scope it to one service line; a null site filter means all sites and a null visit-type filter means all visit types. The end date must be the same as or later than the start date, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_PERIOD` — equal dates describe a legitimate one-day period. The snapshot records `generated_at` as the logical instant it was taken, and it mutates nothing outside itself.

The encounter partition counts every encounter whose `created_at` date falls inside the period, both ends inclusive, after the site and visit-type filters are applied and with no status filter applied before partitioning. `total_visits` is the size of that set, and `scheduled_visit_count`, `in_progress_visit_count`, `completed_visit_count`, `cancelled_visit_count` and `no_show_visit_count` count the members in each clinical state. The five counts sum to `total_visits` exactly. The money buckets are summed straight across the same in-period encounters: `gross_charge_total_cents`, `no_charge_total_cents`, `patient_responsibility_total_cents`, `write_off_total_cents` and `payer_billed_total_cents` on the report are the sums of the identically-named buckets on those encounters, and because every encounter bucket is already a whole number of cents there is no per-visit rounding to argue about.

Referral follow-through is reported two ways, because the two ways disagree and funders ask for both. The referral set is every referral whose `created_at` date falls inside the period, both ends inclusive; when the report is scoped to a site, the set is further narrowed to referrals whose student holds an enrollment at that site for the current school year with a status of `ACTIVE`, `WITHDRAWN` or `TRANSFERRED`. Referral statuses are read at report-generation time, not as they stood at creation. `referral_total_count` is the size of that set, `referral_closed_count` counts those in `CLOSED` and `referral_lost_count` counts those in `LOST_TO_FOLLOW_UP`. The first rate, `referral_closure_rate_all`, divides the closed count by the total count and keeps lost-to-follow-up referrals in the denominator. The second, `referral_closure_rate_excluding_lost`, divides the closed count by the total count less the lost count. Both are rounded half-up to 4 decimal places, and both yield exactly 0.0000 when their denominator is 0 rather than raising an error. Two closed referrals out of four created, one of which was lost, therefore reports 0.5000 on the first rate and 0.6667 on the second.

Immunization compliance is likewise reported two ways over the students in scope. `grace_compliance_count` counts the students whose stored `immunization_compliance_status` is `COMPLIANT` — the grace-counting answer, with the 4-day grace applied and exemptions honoured. `strict_compliance_count` counts the students who *would* be compliant under the strict counting of §3.7, with the 4-day grace withdrawn and exemptions ignored. Reports can be read back individually or listed with an optional `school_id` filter, and because a report is immutable it carries only `id` and `created_at` from the common properties — no `updated_at` and no `version`.
