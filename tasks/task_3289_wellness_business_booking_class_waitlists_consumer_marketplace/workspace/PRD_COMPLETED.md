# Wellness Booking Marketplace API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Wellness businesses today run their commercial life across a scheduling calendar, a point-of-sale terminal, a membership ledger, a class-pack spreadsheet and a payroll run — and then discover that the same class inventory has to be resold through a consumer marketplace whose economics are nothing like the studio's own. This document specifies the API for a single platform that carries a wellness business end to end: a subscription-gated operating system for scheduling, pricing, membership and payroll, plus a marketplace storefront that sells the studio's own seats to app users under a commission structure that differs from the studio channel.

Access is sold as a tiered business subscription. A business signs up under a tier, and that tier decides which capabilities the business may use at all — whether it can publish to the consumer marketplace, whether payroll and instructor commission run, whether substitution workflows are available, whether waitlist seats may be promoted automatically, whether benefit-network billing is accepted, and whether the studio's client roster may be exported for portability. When the subscription falls behind, the platform moves through dunning, grace and suspension, and the studio's operating posture follows it: sessions close, staff are demoted, payroll is held.

The consumer sits on the other side of the same inventory. Consumers book drop-in seats, hold memberships and class packs, take numbered positions on class waitlists that must be promoted in a defensible order, and — critically — can assert ownership of their own record and demand that a studio release it. Because the client list is both the studio's most valuable asset and the consumer's personal data, the portability workflow is a first-class, contested, time-boxed dispute process rather than a delete button. Throughout, instructor commission, off-peak promotional inventory, benefit-network claims and marketplace commission all redistribute the same revenue, so the money rules and the seat-allocation rules are specified as tightly as the workflow rules.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two headers: `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` names the role under which the principal is acting. A request that omits either header, or that names a role outside the declared roster, is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_OR_INVALID_USER_CONTEXT_HEADER` (HTTP 400). A request whose role is not one of the roles admitted by the target operation is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (HTTP 403). The only operations that skip the header requirement are the subscription-tier catalogue reads, the two marketplace class reads and the three platform operator endpoints — the logical-clock setter and the two fault injectors — which are reserved for the `OperatorOnly` harness role; a marketplace class read that carries neither header is served as `Anonymous`, while a request that carries exactly one of the two headers is still rejected as incomplete context.

The roster contains six roles. `OperatorOnly` is the benchmark harness itself and owns only the logical clock and the test-driving injectors. `PlatformAdmin` is the marketplace operator: it configures tiers and benefit networks, adjudicates portability disputes, issues exports, and may override any studio-scoped operation. `StudioOwner` is the wellness business's own operator account and owns scheduling, staff, memberships, packs, payroll, and the studio side of the marketplace and portability workflows. `Client` is the consumer, who books classes, holds waitlist positions, manages their own memberships and packs, and files portability requests. `StaffMember` is an instructor or front-desk account inside a studio that reads its own sessions, bookings and commission accruals. `Anonymous` is an unauthenticated marketplace browser that may read the tier catalogue, browse marketplace inventory, and sign a new studio up.

Ownership is enforced differently per role, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `StudioOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Studio`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Studio` compare the resource's studio foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, and a `StudioOwner` acting on a studio other than the one bound to their identifier is rejected with `ERR_STUDIO_NOT_OWNED` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `Client`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Client`, but the server performs no client-scoped ownership comparison on reads or writes: it never filters a listing by the acting client and never compares a resource's client field to `X-User-ID`, the single exception being the portability withdrawal, where the acting client must be the request's `requesting_client_id` or the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE`. When `X-User-Role` is `StaffMember`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `StaffMember`, and no read is scoped or filtered by it. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `OperatorOnly` or `Anonymous`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity: the server may record it, but it is never compared to any entity's `id`, and `PlatformAdmin` is explicitly exempt from the studio-ownership comparison.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts a `limit` and an `offset`. The `limit` parameter defaults to 10, must be at least 1, and may not exceed 100; a value outside that range is not a silent clamp but is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` at HTTP 400, as is a non-integer `limit` and a negative `offset`. The `offset` parameter defaults to 0 and counts rows skipped from the front of the ordered result. List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless the operation is one of the walks that pins its own ranking (the waitlist offer ordering and the marketplace commission walk, both described in §3, and the subscription-tier catalogue, which walks in ascending `tier_rank` and then ascending `created_at`). Three further listings pin their own keys: session listings walk in ascending `start_at` and then `created_at`, a session's own booking listing walks in ascending `creation_seq` and then `created_at` (the flat booking listings instead walk in ascending `created_at` and then `creation_seq`), and payroll-period listings walk in ascending `start_date` and then `created_at`.

The `meta` object of a list response carries the four pagination fields: `limit` and `offset` echo the request, `total_count` reports the total number of rows matching the filter before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the returned page. Filter parameters that a given list endpoint declares (`status`, `channel`, `studio_id`, and the like) narrow `total_count` before pagination, never after.

### 2.4. Studio Tenancy and Ownership Boundaries

Every business-scoped resource carries a `studio_id` that partitions it. Studios are hard tenancy boundaries: a listing, a booking, a membership, a payroll period or a portability dispute raised under one studio is never visible to, or mutable by, another studio's operator. The `studio_id` value is supplied on creation for resources that cannot derive it from a parent — the staff roster, class formats, class packs, memberships, payroll periods and portability disputes all take it explicitly — and is stamped by the server for resources that inherit it from the session or client they hang from. A client registers against a studio by naming it as their `home_studio_id`, and a class pack or membership created for a consumer likewise names the owning studio rather than deriving it.

Reads are scoped the same way. A studio-scoped listing without an explicit `studio_id` filter returns only the rows belonging to the acting operator's studio when the caller is a `StudioOwner`. The `Studio` row itself is the tenant root: it aggregates `client_roster_count`, `outstanding_balance_cents`, `subscription_tier`, `feature_gates`, `data_ownership_state` and `status`, and its `suspension_reason` is populated only when the tenant posture is `SUSPENDED`. `client_roster_count` counts the clients whose `home_studio_id` is that studio and whose own status is `ACTIVE` or `FROZEN`; a client the portability workflow has moved on to `PORTABILITY_HOLD`, `PORTED` or `ANONYMIZED` is counted out of the roster rather than into it. Platform-level configuration that is not tenant-scoped — the subscription-tier catalogue and the benefit-network registry — is deliberately shared and is readable across studios.

### 2.5. Entitlement Tiers and Feature Gating

A studio's capabilities are decided by the tier it is on. Each `SubscriptionTier` row carries a `tier` name, a `monthly_fee_cents` recurring platform fee that must be at least zero and an exact multiple of 100 (whole dollars only), a monotonic `tier_rank` used as the upgrade-path key beginning at 1 where 1 is the lowest rank and the value must be unique across rows, an `included_staff_seats` count before seat overage begins, a `seat_overage_fee_cents` charged per active staff member beyond that included count, and a `marketplace_commission_bps` commission expressed in basis points that must sit between 0 and 10000 inclusive. The tier names themselves are exactly `STARTER`, `GROWTH` and `ENTERPRISE`.

Each tier row also declares six boolean gates: `marketplace_listing_enabled`, `staff_payroll_enabled`, `instructor_substitution_enabled`, `waitlist_auto_promotion_enabled`, `crm_client_export_enabled` and `benefit_network_billing`. The studio carries a derived `feature_gates` map keyed as `marketplace_listing`, `staff_payroll`, `instructor_substitution`, `waitlist_auto_promotion`, `benefit_network_billing` and `client_portability_export`, whose boolean values are resolved from the effective tier's row per the platform's fixed tier-to-feature matrix rather than being set independently.

An operation whose gate is false on the studio's effective tier is refused with `ERR_FEATURE_NOT_IN_TIER` (HTTP 403). The gates actually consulted are these: `staff_payroll` on staff-member creation for an `INSTRUCTOR` role, on membership creation and on class-pack creation; `instructor_substitution` on instructor reassignment; `waitlist_auto_promotion` on waitlist join; `client_portability_export` on filing a portability request; and `marketplace_listing` on the marketplace class read and on the marketplace booking create. Session creation and update, payroll-period definition and payroll close, and portability release resolution are deliberately ungated, so a studio whose tier carries a false gate can still schedule sessions, close payroll and resolve a release. A studio's `subscription_tier` and its whole `feature_gates` map are re-derived whenever the entitlement changes, so a gate read is always consistent with the tier currently in force.

### 2.6. Monetary Precision and Ledger Discipline

All money in this API is integer cents. No monetary field is ever a float, and no monetary field is ever a formatted string. Where a calculation produces a fractional cent, the rounding direction is pinned per calculation: the channel price and the discount paths truncate toward zero with a floor; the marketplace gross-up and the marketplace settlement path round upward with a ceiling; and where a half-up rule is named (the per-component rounding order noted in §3) it means the exact half is rounded away from zero. A reader must not assume a single global rounding rule — the rule travels with the calculation.

Money is also conserved rather than recomputed. The attended revenue written on a booking (`commission_base_cents`) is never mutated by a later reversal or substitution: a correction is posted either as a commission entry carrying status `REVERSED` or as a `PayrollPeriod` row's `reversal_correction_cents`, whichever matches the entitlement state of the entry at the time. The studio's aggregate `outstanding_balance_cents` is the sum of the open balance on every non-terminal membership, plus the outstanding booking fee on every booking sitting in `NO_SHOW` or `LATE_CANCELLED`, plus the class-package overage; it is never permitted to fall below zero.

### 2.7. Common Resource Properties and Identity

Every resource that carries common fields exposes an `id`, a `created_at` and an `updated_at`, and an integer `version`. The `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, no entity name embedded in it and no ordering significance — the UUID is never used as a tie-break in any ranking this document defines. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional digits. `version` begins at 1 and is incremented by 1 on every mutation of the resource; it is the value the concurrency mechanism of §2.8 compares against, and it is what a client reads before issuing an OCC-guarded write.

Two entity types deliberately do not carry common fields. A `SubscriptionTier` row and a `BenefitNetwork` row are configuration records rather than tenant resources: each carries only an `id` and a `created_at`, with `updated_at` and `version` both absent. Their identity is also not their UUID in practice — a subscription tier is referenced by its `tier` value and a benefit network is resolved by its `network_code`, not by `id`; a booking that bills a wellness benefit network resolves the network through `BenefitNetwork.network_code` against the booking's `benefit_network_code`, never by looking up a network UUID.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two writes in this API are guarded by an explicit parent-version precondition, and both use the same rejection contract. Scheduling a class session accepts an `expected_subscription_version` carrying the value of the studio's `BusinessSubscription.version` that the caller last observed, and the precondition is enforced only when that field is supplied — a request that omits it is admitted without the version comparison; the tier and its feature gates are read from the subscription at session-creation time, so a caller working from a stale view may otherwise schedule a session the subscription no longer authorises. Scheduling is itself one of the writes that advances the subscription row's `version`, so a caller that schedules twice must re-observe the subscription between the two writes rather than replay the value it read before the first. Creating a class pack accepts an `expected_client_version` carrying the value of the target `Client.version` that the caller last observed, again enforced only when supplied; because pack creation itself advances the client's version, and a concurrent pack or membership creation for the same client legitimately supersedes the caller's entitlement snapshot, a stale read must be refused rather than silently accepted. In both cases a value that does not match the parent entity's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), and the write does not occur.

The same rejection code governs the remaining version-guarded writes declared in §4. Those operations carry their own expectation field — `expected_class_session_version` on session update, instructor reassignment and the optional offer sweep; `expected_subscription_version` on the renewal, tier-change, suspend, resume and cancel writes; `expected_membership_version` on the membership cycle, freeze, unfreeze and cancel writes; `expected_payroll_period_version` on payroll close and reopen; and `expected_portability_request_version` on export issuance — and in every one of them a mismatch against the subject row's current `version` is the same `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412. The rule is a stale-observation rule, not a scheduling rule: a client that has read the row, then observed another writer advance it, must re-read before retrying. In every one of these operations the expectation field is optional; a request that omits it skips the version comparison entirely and is never refused for the omission.

### 2.9. Seat Availability and Fullness Posture

Whether a session can take another seat is expressed by a single derived enumeration, `fullness_posture`, whose values are exactly `OPEN`, `WAITLIST_ONLY`, `FULL` and `CANCELLED`. The derivation first closes the session out: a session whose `status` is `CANCELLED_BY_STUDIO` reports `CANCELLED` regardless of its counters. Otherwise the occupied count is the sum of `seats_taken` and `seats_held`; the free-seat count is the capacity minus that occupied count; and the promotional reservation — the allocated promotional seats minus those already used, counted only while `promo_offer_state` is `ACTIVE` and taken as zero otherwise — is subtracted to leave the studio-channel seat count, floored at zero. If the free-seat count is zero or fewer the posture is `FULL` — the zero case is `FULL`, not `OPEN`. If free seats remain but the studio-channel count is zero or fewer the posture is `WAITLIST_ONLY`. Only when a positive number of studio seats remains is the posture `OPEN`.

The counters underneath that posture are themselves defined tightly. `seats_taken` counts bookings in `RESERVED`, `CHECKED_IN` and `NO_SHOW` — a no-show still occupies the seat it failed to use. `seats_held` counts holds that actually reserve capacity, which means offered waitlist holds plus instructor-substitution holds; an `ACTIVE` or `STANDBY` waitlist hold reserves nothing and contributes zero. A floor exists to stop the counters from being recomputed below reality: the occupied total may never be reduced below the number of bookings in `RESERVED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `NO_SHOW` plus the number of waitlist holds in `OFFERED`.

Two capacity bases exist for the same row, and they can disagree. When a session is read through any studio-channel operation the posture is derived against the full capacity, so promotional reservations can only ever push the studio toward `WAITLIST_ONLY` and never toward `FULL`. When the same row is read through the consumer marketplace's single-class read, the capacity is reduced by the promotional reservation before the comparison, so a session the studio sees as `OPEN` can be reported `FULL` to a marketplace shopper.

### 2.10. Nested Resource Retrieval and Referential Resolution

Resources are addressed by nesting, and nesting is validated left to right along the path. A path that names a studio before naming a child collection resolves the studio first, so a request against a non-existent parent fails on the parent, not on the child. The reference errors are distinct per entity: an unresolvable studio, subscription, client, class session, booking, waitlist entry, membership, class pack, staff member, payroll period, portability request and benefit network are reported respectively as `ERR_STUDIO_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLASS_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WAITLIST_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLASS_PACK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAFF_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYROLL_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PORTABILITY_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_BENEFIT_NETWORK_NOT_FOUND`, each at HTTP 404. Two further unresolvable references are reported outside that list, both as `ERR_INVALID_STATE` carrying HTTP 404 rather than the 409 its catalogue row declares: a class format that does not exist, and a commission entry that does not exist. A booking body's `benefit_network_code` is the one reference that is not reported through that 404: a code that does not resolve to a network eligible for the studio is refused with `ERR_NETWORK_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409) as §3.3 describes. Where a request body names foreign keys rather than a path, they are resolved in the order the operation declares them, so the first unresolvable body reference determines which 404 is reported.

A studio-row read exposes the derived roster and balance aggregates described in §2.4. A consumer-facing client read — the form returned to a marketplace browser or the consumer's own account — exposes the display `display_name` alongside the client's strike and fee counters; the registration `email` is the unique-within-studio contact address described in §3.2 and is never used as a lookup key outside that studio's roster.

Sub-resource collections mirror their parent's reverse reachability. A single session exposes its bookings and its waitlist holds; a client exposes their bookings, memberships, class packs and waitlist holds; a payroll period exposes its commission entries as does a staff member; a booking exposes the waitlist holds promoted into it and the commission entries it produced; a class format exposes the sessions scheduled for it and the staff members who name it as their primary format; and a session exposes the sessions whose promotional waterfall it absorbed.

### 2.11. Validation Precedence Across Operations

Where several rules could reject the same request, the order in which they are evaluated is fixed, and this ordering — not the order of the checks in a handler — determines which error a client sees. Authentication context is evaluated first: absent or unrecognised user context yields `ERR_MISSING_OR_INVALID_USER_CONTEXT_HEADER`, and a role the operation does not admit yields `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` before anything else is considered.

Existence is evaluated next, path order before body order, yielding the per-entity 404 named in §2.10. State admissibility follows, yielding `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_RESERVED`, `ERR_ENTRY_NOT_OFFERED`, `ERR_WAITLIST_TERMINAL`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_SCHEDULED`, `ERR_PAYROLL_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN` or `ERR_PORTABILITY_ALREADY_DECIDED` as applicable, all at HTTP 409. Tenant posture and tier entitlement are then evaluated — `ERR_STUDIO_NOT_OPERATING` and `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_INACTIVE` at HTTP 409, `ERR_PAYROLL_HOLD` at HTTP 409, and `ERR_FEATURE_NOT_IN_TIER` at HTTP 403 — and this band deliberately stands above the temporal and constraint bands, so a suspended studio reports the posture error rather than a capacity or overlap error that happens to be true as well. Two refusals are ordered deliberately against that rule. First, a studio-channel booking attempt against a session already in `CANCELLED_BY_STUDIO` is refused with `ERR_STUDIO_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` at the state-admissibility position, before the tenant posture is consulted, because §3.2 makes the session's own closure the mechanism that blocks any further booking on it. Second, the client-admissibility refusal sits with this band rather than with the referential band it is catalogued under: on the studio-channel booking create, the waitlist join and the waitlist claim, a client whose own status is `PORTABILITY_HOLD`, `PORTED` or `ANONYMIZED` is refused with `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_ADMISSIBLE` (HTTP 409) ahead of the temporal and capacity constraints, so the client is refused on its own status rather than on an overlap, a full session or an unresolvable network that happens to be true as well.

Only after posture is settled are the temporal interval rules evaluated (`ERR_CLIENT_SCHEDULE_OVERLAP`, `ERR_ROOM_OVERLAP`, `ERR_BUFFER_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_PAYROLL_PERIOD_GAP` and `ERR_CANCEL_WINDOW_CLOSED`, all HTTP 409), then the capacity, quota and uniqueness constraints (`ERR_STUDIO_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXHAUSTED`, `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_PACK`, `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_INACTIVE`, `ERR_PAYROLL_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, `ERR_CLIENT_EMAIL_CONFLICT`, all HTTP 409), then the cross-entity referential dependencies (`ERR_CLIENT_NOT_ADMISSIBLE`, `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `ERR_NETWORK_NOT_ACTIVE`, all HTTP 409), and finally the fiscal rules — the tier-change fee (`ERR_TIER_CHANGE_FEE_REQUIRED`), the early-termination fee (`ERR_EARLY_TERMINATION_FEE_REQUIRED`), the freeze cap (`ERR_FREEZE_CAP_EXCEEDED`), the pack extension cap (`ERR_PACK_EXTENSION_CAP_EXCEEDED`), the export download cap (`ERR_EXPORT_DOWNLOAD_CAP`) and the stale-version precondition (`ERR_STALE_VERSION`). The fee errors report HTTP 402, the cap errors and the remaining fiscal refusals report HTTP 409, and the stale-version error reports HTTP 412.

### 2.12. Logical Clock and Temporal Windows

This API does not consult wall-clock time. The server starts with its logical clock pinned to the fixed instant 2026-09-01T00:00:00Z and advances only when an operation instructs it to — an operator clock set, a renewal advance, a cycle advance, a session completion, a sweep with a day offset, or any endpoint that moves the clock forward by a stated number of days. Every time comparison in this document compares the logical clock against a stored instant, and no server-side default may fall back to the host clock. Setting the clock forward can cause several system transitions to become due at once; they fire in the order the lifecycle rules of §3 declare.

Window boundaries are inclusive unless stated otherwise, and the inclusive edge is deliberate throughout. Check-in admission opens a fixed number of minutes before the session starts and closes at the session end; a waitlist offer lapses after its offer window; a studio's objection to a portability request must arrive strictly inside the response window; adjudication must arrive inside the adjudication window; an export must be issued and downloaded inside the export window; a no-show restore must arrive inside the restore window measured from the session end; and a denied benefit-network claim may be resubmitted inside its denial window. The exact minute, hour and day figures for each window are stated where the workflow that uses them is described.

### 2.13. Lifetime Caps, Strike Counters and Running Tallies

Several quantities accumulate for the life of a contract or a client and are explicitly capped or explicitly never reset. On the client, `no_show_strike_count` accumulates one strike per booking that reaches `NO_SHOW` and is reduced only by a compensating restoration; `late_cancel_count` accumulates one per booking that reaches `LATE_CANCELLED` and is never reset by any operation; and `outstanding_fee_cents` aggregates the fees currently owed across the client's bookings whose `fee_status` is `CHARGED` or `PAYROLL_DEDUCTED`. On a class pack, `extension_days_used` accumulates every granted extension day for the life of the pack and blocks further extensions once the running total would pass the platform's maximum extension days. On a membership, `freeze_days_used` accumulates the frozen calendar days within the contract year, capped by the annual freeze cap. On a portability export, `export_download_count` accumulates downloads and blocks further downloads at the platform maximum. Each of these caps is a hard boundary: the operation that would cross it is refused rather than clamped.

### 2.14. Derived Timestamps and Server-Computed Dates

A number of timestamps are derived rather than supplied, and their derivation is fixed so that two implementations cannot disagree. A session's `end_at` is derived at creation from its `start_at` plus the class format's `duration_minutes`. A class pack's `expires_at` is derived at creation from `purchased_at` plus the validity span that `expiry_scheme` selects — 180 days for `NONE`, 90 for `SEASONAL` and 30 for `PROMOTIONAL` — and the pack's `pending_expiry_grace_until` is derived during the expiry sweep from `expires_at` plus the pack grace days. A membership's `next_billing_at` is derived at creation by taking the current logical date and replacing its day-of-month with the plan's `billing_anchor_day` — it is never rolled forward into the next month, so an anchor day already past in the current month yields a due date in the past — and is thereafter advanced by exactly one calendar month per charged cycle. A subscription's `renewal_due_at` is derived at signup as the current instant plus 30 days and thereafter advanced by exactly 30 days on each settled renewal, while its `grace_until` is set to the renewal due date on every renewal and to the current clock instant whenever the subscription is resumed. A client's late-cancellation and no-show fees are computed at the instant the booking enters its fee-bearing state and are not recomputed later.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Signing Up, Tiers and the Dunning Ladder

A wellness business enters the platform by signing up, which creates the `Studio` root and its single `BusinessSubscription` in one transaction. The caller supplies the studio's `legal_name`, its two-letter `home_state_code` drawn from the fixed five-value vocabulary of `CA`, `NY`, `TX`, `WA` and `CO`, an owner name under `owner_full_name`, the requested `tier`, the `billing_period` the subscription will bill on, and the studio's `payroll_cap_mode`. A `home_state_code` outside that vocabulary is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), on studio sign-up as on client registration. Sign-up is open to `Anonymous` browsers as well as to the platform operator; the owner is registered as a `StudioOwner` identity so that later ownership checks bind that operator to this studio. The new studio starts in `TRIAL`, and its subscription starts in `TRIAL` with `renewal_epoch` 0, `dunning_stage` `CURRENT`, a `renewal_due_at` 30 days ahead, a matching `grace_until`, and a `monthly_fee_cents_snapshot` captured from the requested tier. The tier catalogue is materialised lazily: if the requested tier has no row yet, the platform installs its three default rows first — `STARTER` at 4900 cents monthly, rank 1, 2 included seats, 1500 cents seat overage and 1500 basis points commission; `GROWTH` at 9900, rank 2, 5 seats, 1200 and 1200; and `ENTERPRISE` at 19900, rank 3, 10 seats, 900 and 1000 — each with all six gates true. A tier row the caller has already defined is never overwritten, and the tier catalogue read returns an empty list until either a tier is defined or the first studio signs up. The studio's `subscription_id`, `subscription_tier` and `feature_gates` are stamped from that subscription, and `data_ownership_state` begins as `OWNED`.

The `billing_period` is frozen at creation: it is exactly `MONTHLY` or `ANNUAL` and is immutable for the life of the row, so a later request to change cadence is not a supported transition. Moving to a different tier is instead a distinct operation available to the platform operator: the caller names the destination as `new_tier` and supplies `fee_payment_ref`, and the move is effective immediately, with the prorated remainder of the current period charged as a tier-change fee. The fee is charged before the change is applied, so an unknown or absent `fee_payment_ref` fails at the fiscal precedence band with `ERR_TIER_CHANGE_FEE_REQUIRED` and the tier is not mutated at all. On success the subscription's `tier` becomes the requested value, `monthly_fee_cents_snapshot` is refreshed from the target tier row, and the studio's `subscription_tier` and its whole `feature_gates` map are re-derived. The studio's `payroll_cap_mode` is likewise a durable choice — it is exactly `BONUS_OUTSIDE_CAP` or `BONUS_INSIDE_CAP`, and it selects which of the two commission evaluation orders of §3.6 every payroll entry for this studio uses. Because the two orders produce different money on identical booking data, this field is not cosmetic and is read on every accrual.

Renewal is a settleable event rather than an automatic one. A renewal advance carries a caller-supplied `outcome` and an `advance_clock` flag, which defaults to true when omitted, with an optional `advance_days` (defaulting to 30). When `advance_clock` is true the logical clock first moves forward by `advance_days`; when it is false the due date is left exactly where it sits, which is how an early billing cycle is exercised without moving time. The renewal may only be settled once the clock is at or past the subscription's current `renewal_due_at`. An `outcome` of `SUCCEEDED` moves the subscription to `ACTIVE`, increments `renewal_epoch` by 1, advances `renewal_due_at` by exactly 30 days, sets `grace_until` to that new due date, and returns `dunning_stage` to `CURRENT`; the studio's own posture is then re-derived to `ACTIVE` because the subscription is `ACTIVE`, and recruitment of staff, sessions, memberships and bookings becomes permitted again. An `outcome` of `FAILED` moves the subscription to `PAST_DUE` and `dunning_stage` to `RETRY_SCHEDULED`. A renewal attempt from a state outside `TRIAL`, `ACTIVE`, `PAST_DUE` and `GRACE` is not declared and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; so is a renewal whose clock has not yet reached the current `renewal_due_at`, which reports the same `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409. Where a caller supplies a stale subscription expectation the write fails earlier at `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

The failed-charge path has a side effect that reaches into the waitlists. When a renewal settles as `FAILED`, every non-terminal waitlist entry belonging to a session of this studio whose `start_at` falls before the current instant plus the offer window has its `raw_sequence_no` incremented by 1 and its `effective_sequence` recomputed. The effect is that an instructor substitution hold, which the ordering rules of §3.4 treat as a priority hold, outranks the oldest consumer hold for the duration of the retry window. Operators should expect a failed renewal to quietly reorder a session's offer queue.

If the clock simply walks past `renewal_due_at` without a settled charge, the system moves the subscription from `PAST_DUE` to `GRACE` and sets `grace_until` to the current instant plus the platform grace period of 7 days; the studio posture deliberately stays `ACTIVE` through `GRACE`, so booking and point-of-sale remain permitted while the balance is chased. Letting the clock pass `grace_until` moves the subscription from `GRACE` to `SUSPENDED`, flips `dunning_stage` to `SUSPENDED` and `payroll_hold` to true, and re-derives the studio posture to `SUSPENDED`. That lapse-driven suspension also stamps the studio's `suspension_reason`, as §2.4 requires of a suspended posture; a suspension that arrives with no caller reason to record — the lapse cascade, or an explicit suspension that supplies none — writes the literal `subscription lapse` there. That posture change then cascades down through the studio's `SCHEDULED` sessions and their waitlists as described in §3.2 — a session already `IN_PROGRESS` is left running by the suspension cascade — while the tenant-level closure of §3.2 cancels in-progress sessions as well.

Suspension can also be requested directly with a `reason`, and it is admitted from `TRIAL`, `ACTIVE`, `PAST_DUE` or `GRACE`. It moves the subscription to `SUSPENDED`, sets `dunning_stage` to `SUSPENDED` and `payroll_hold` to true, stamps the studio's `suspension_reason` from the caller's `reason`, and demotes every `ACTIVE` staff member on the roster — whatever their `staff_role` — to `INACTIVE`. Commission rows already in `ACCRUED` are not paid while the studio is suspended, but they are not forfeited either — they remain payable once the studio is reinstated. A suspended subscription resumes only while `grace_until` has not yet passed: resumption sets `status` back to `GRACE`, `dunning_stage` to `CURRENT`, resets `grace_until` to the current instant, clears the studio's `suspension_reason` back to null, and re-derives the studio to `ACTIVE`. Staff demoted during suspension are deliberately not re-activated automatically; the studio must name each of them active again, so a resume never silently restores a roster.

Cancelling the subscription is terminal and claws money back. It is admitted from `PAST_DUE`, `GRACE` and `SUSPENDED`, sets `status` to `CANCELLED`, `dunning_stage` to `CANCELLED` and `payroll_hold` to true permanently, and posts a reversal against the studio's most recent period that has left `OPEN` — a `LOCKED` or `CLOSED` one, taken by ascending `period_index`: every commission entry of that period that had reached `PAID` is moved to `REVERSED` with `reversal_reason` `SUBSCRIPTION_CANCELLED`, and the negation of its `commission_cents` is accumulated into the `reversal_correction_cents` of the studio's lowest-index `OPEN` period, or back onto the source period when the studio has no open period left. Cancelling the studio root is a separate, equally terminal act that carries no state precondition — it is admitted whatever the studio's current posture, including an already-`CANCELLED` one — and it also moves the studio's subscription to `CANCELLED` with `dunning_stage` `CANCELLED`; it is refused with `ERR_STUDIO_NOT_OWNED` when a `StudioOwner` acts on a studio other than the one bound to their identifier, since a `PlatformAdmin` is exempt from that comparison.

### 3.2. Sessions, Rooms, Payroll Coverage and Studio Closure

Scheduling a session is the operation where the most rules converge. A studio owner supplies the `class_template_id` of the format being scheduled, the `instructor_staff_id` who will teach it, the `room_code` the session occupies, the `start_at` instant, an optional `capacity`, and the promotional inventory fields `promo_seats_allocated`, `promo_discount_bps`, `promo_valid_from` and `promo_valid_to`. Any of `class_template_id`, `instructor_staff_id`, `room_code` and `start_at` that is absent or empty is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409. The owner may also present `expected_subscription_version`; a mismatch against the subscription's current version is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` before anything else is scheduled, and because that guard is optional an omitted expectation simply skips the comparison. The named format must belong to the acting studio and the named instructor must exist, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 404 and `ERR_STAFF_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND` respectively; an instructor already restricted to `SUBSTITUTE_ONLY` may not be named as the instructor of a new session and the request is refused with `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.

A class format is defined for a studio with a display `name`, a `duration_minutes` of at least 15, a `base_price_cents` of at least zero before any off-peak or tier adjustment, a `default_capacity` of at least 1, a `credit_cost` of at least 1 naming how many pack credits one seat consumes, an `is_off_peak` flag, and a `buffer_minutes` changeover requirement of at least zero against any other session in the same room. Sessions of a format are reachable through the format, as are the staff members naming it as their primary format.

The session begins in `SCHEDULED`, its `end_at` is derived from `start_at` plus the format's `duration_minutes`, and its `capacity` defaults to the format's `default_capacity` when the caller omits it, and a supplied capacity below 1 is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409. Both channel prices are computed immediately — the studio's `list_price_cents` and the grossed-up `marketplace_price_cents` — and `price_version` starts at 1. The session's `payroll_period_id` is resolved by coverage: the studio must already have a `PayrollPeriod` whose inclusive range between `start_date` and `end_date` contains `start_at`. If no period covers the instant the request is refused with `ERR_PAYROLL_PERIOD_GAP`. A session may not be scheduled into a gap by creating the period implicitly, and a period defined for the studio must itself be admissible — its inclusive range must be disjoint from every other period of that studio, and it must span at least seven calendar days counting both endpoints — and an omitted `period_index` defaults to 1.

The disjointness rule is instant-based rather than date-based, and the boundary is the trap. A period that ends at 2026-09-30T00:00:00Z and a period that begins at 2026-09-30T00:00:00Z are not disjoint, because both ranges include that instant; a period whose range intersects an existing one, or which spans fewer than the minimum days, is refused with `ERR_PAYROLL_PERIOD_OVERLAP`.

Rooms and clients impose interval constraints on scheduling and booking respectively. Two sessions in the same `room_code` may not collide: their half-open intervals from `start_at` up to but excluding `end_at` must not intersect, or the schedule is refused with `ERR_ROOM_OVERLAP`; and where two sessions in a room are close but not colliding, a strictly positive gap between them that is smaller than the greater of the two formats' `buffer_minutes` values is refused with `ERR_BUFFER_INSUFFICIENT` — the changeover band binds only a genuine sub-buffer separation, so two sessions that merely abut, one starting exactly where the other ends, are decided by the half-open room rule alone and are admitted whatever the buffer. Symmetrically, a client may not hold a booking or a waitlist hold on a session whose interval overlaps the target session's interval, or the booking is refused with `ERR_CLIENT_SCHEDULE_OVERLAP`.

A scheduled session may be updated while it is still `SCHEDULED`, supplying any of `capacity`, `instructor_staff_id`, `room_code` or the promotional fields together with `expected_class_session_version`. Raising capacity releases seats into the waitlist ordering of §3.4; lowering capacity below the number of seats currently taken plus held is refused with `ERR_STUDIO_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` rather than silently overbooked. The promotional inventory fields are the price-affecting ones: naming any of `promo_seats_allocated`, `promo_discount_bps`, `promo_valid_from` or `promo_valid_to` in the update bumps `price_version` and recomputes both channel prices, while an update confined to `capacity`, `instructor_staff_id` or `room_code` leaves `price_version` where it stands. Updating or reassigning a session that is no longer `SCHEDULED` is refused with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_SCHEDULED`.

The session's own execution ladder runs `SCHEDULED` to `IN_PROGRESS` when the clock reaches `start_at`, and `IN_PROGRESS` to `COMPLETED` when the clock reaches `end_at`. No new booking may be created against a session once it is `IN_PROGRESS`, although bookings already held remain valid — an in-progress session may still carry `RESERVED` bookings. Completion is the moment the attendance cascade fires, and it is described in §3.3. A studio-side cancellation is admitted while the session is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` and moves it to `CANCELLED_BY_STUDIO`; it cancels every booking in `RESERVED` or `CHECKED_IN` with `fee_status` `NONE`, because a studio-side cancellation never fees a client, and it restores the class-pack and membership credits those bookings consumed. A booking cancelled this way never attended, so it carries no attended revenue and its `commission_base_cents` is set to zero; the conservation rule of §3.6 is stated over bookings that are or have been `ATTENDED` and is untouched by that. Every waitlist hold in `ACTIVE`, `STANDBY` or `OFFERED` on that session moves to `EXPIRED`; the hold's `raw_sequence_no` is preserved for audit but the hold no longer consumes `seats_held`. The session's `fullness_posture` is re-derived to `CANCELLED`, which is what blocks any further booking attempt on it with `ERR_STUDIO_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

Closing the business is the same cascade at the tenant level. Cancelling the studio moves every session of that studio that is still `SCHEDULED` — and any in progress — to `CANCELLED_BY_STUDIO`, releasing their `RESERVED` bookings and expiring their `ACTIVE`, `STANDBY` and `OFFERED` holds; it moves every membership in `ACTIVE`, `FROZEN`, `PAST_DUE` or `LAPSED` to `CANCELLED`; and it moves every session whose `promo_offer_state` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` to `WITHDRAWN`. One carve-out keeps the platform honest after the business is gone: portability requests sitting in `PENDING` or `OBJECTED` remain actionable, so a consumer's dispute does not die with the studio. The studio's `version` is incremented by 1.

### 3.3. Booking, Attendance and the No-Show Ledger

A studio-channel booking is created against a `client_id` and a `class_session_id`, with a `channel` that defaults to `STUDIO` when omitted and a `payment_source` that selects which entitlement is debited; a `payment_source` outside `DROP_IN`, `MEMBERSHIP`, `CLASS_PACK` and `BENEFIT_NETWORK` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409, and a request that names the marketplace channel on this operation is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_SOURCE_NOT_PERMITTED`. The `payment_source` is one of `DROP_IN`, `MEMBERSHIP`, `CLASS_PACK` or `BENEFIT_NETWORK`. A membership-funded booking names `membership_id` and debits one of the membership's included credits; an identifier that resolves to no row is refused with `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_FOUND`, and a booking that names no membership at all, or names one that is not `ACTIVE`, is refused with `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_INACTIVE`. A pack-funded booking names `class_pack_id` and debits `credits_remaining`; the pack must be `ACTIVE` with credits left, or the booking is refused with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_PACK`. A network-funded booking names `benefit_network_code`, which is resolved against `BenefitNetwork.network_code` rather than by identifier, and a code that does not resolve to a network eligible for the studio is refused with `ERR_NETWORK_NOT_ACTIVE`. The consumer marketplace channel is not accepted on the studio-channel create at all — a marketplace seat is booked through the marketplace's own operation of §3.7, and a `payment_source` the channel does not accept, such as a pack or membership credit on a marketplace booking, is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_SOURCE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

The booking begins in `RESERVED`, increments the session's `seats_taken`, and triggers a re-derivation of `fullness_posture`. Every booking carries a `reminders_sent` counter, which begins at zero; no operation in this API performs a reminder run, so it stays zero for the life of the booking. A `FULL` or `CANCELLED` posture at the moment of the attempt is refused with `ERR_STUDIO_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; a session that has already left `SCHEDULED` for `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409, which is how the in-progress rule below is enforced. An attempt by a client whose own `status` is `PORTABILITY_HOLD`, `PORTED` or `ANONYMIZED` is refused with `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_ADMISSIBLE`, because such a client may not take new bookings or holds. A booking that consumes a class-pack credit decrements `credits_remaining` by one; when that debit empties the pack, the pack's status moves from `ACTIVE` to `DEPLETED` and further bookings against it are refused with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_PACK`.

Attendance is recorded in two steps. A booked client checks in once the check-in gate opens at the session start minus the platform check-in lead time and up to the session end, provided the session is `IN_PROGRESS`; a check-in attempted outside that gate, or against a session that is not `IN_PROGRESS`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409; check-in moves the booking from `RESERVED` to `CHECKED_IN` and recomputes the session's posture without changing `seats_taken`, because a checked-in booking still occupies its seat. Check-in is only admitted against a booking that is still `RESERVED`; anything else is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_RESERVED`. When the session reaches `COMPLETED`, every booking still in `RESERVED` becomes `NO_SHOW` and every booking in `CHECKED_IN` becomes `ATTENDED`.

The completion cascade is where the money and the ledger move. Each booking that becomes `NO_SHOW` increments its client's `no_show_strike_count` by 1, sets `booking_fee_cents` from the no-show fee rule below, and sets `fee_status` to `CHARGED` whenever the computed fee is above zero — but when the first-offence waiver drives the fee to exactly zero the status deliberately remains `NONE` rather than a zero-valued `CHARGED`. The client's `outstanding_fee_cents` grows by the fee that was charged. Each booking that becomes `ATTENDED` has its channel total computed and written to `commission_base_cents`, and accrues a commission entry for the session's instructor, which is the first link of the payroll cascade of §3.6. The session's `seats_taken` is then recomputed from the post-transition booking set.

The no-show fee is computed against the client's strike count as it stood *before* this no-show is recorded, and it has three branches. If that prior count is below 2, no fee is assessed at all — the first offence is free. If the booking was funded by a benefit network, no fee is assessed regardless of strike history. Otherwise the fee is the session's `list_price_cents` multiplied by one plus one half of the lesser of 2 and the prior strike count minus 1, truncated to whole cents. The strike multiplier is capped so that a client already at or above the cap never pays more than twice the list price.

Cancellation is decided purely by where the logical clock sits relative to the session start, and it splits into two quite different outcomes. Cancelling at or before the session start minus the late-cancellation window of 720 minutes — twelve hours — yields `CANCELLED` with `fee_status` `NONE` and `booking_fee_cents` remaining 0. Cancelling after that boundary but at or before the session start yields `LATE_CANCELLED`, with `booking_fee_cents` computed by the late-cancellation rule and `fee_status` set to `CHARGED` whenever that fee is above zero and left at `NONE` when the exemptions drive it to zero. A cancellation attempted after the session has already started is refused with `ERR_CANCEL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. In both successful paths the session's `seats_taken` is decremented by 1, a class-pack or membership credit consumed by the booking is restored, and the freed seat is offered to the session's waitlist hold that ranks first under §3.4.

The late-cancellation fee has the same shape of exemptions as the no-show fee, and one extra one. A booking funded by a benefit network is never charged; a booking that consumed a class-pack credit is never charged; and a booking that consumed a membership credit is charged only once the client's `late_cancel_count` has reached the membership fee strike threshold of 3. In every other case the fee is the session's `list_price_cents` multiplied by the fraction one, truncated to whole cents. The client's `late_cancel_count` increments by 1 on every booking that reaches `LATE_CANCELLED`, whether or not a fee was charged, and `outstanding_fee_cents` grows by whatever fee was charged. The late-cancellation count is never reset by any operation.

A no-show can be reversed. A studio owner may restore a `NO_SHOW` booking by supplying a `reason`, which moves the booking back to `RESERVED`, sets `fee_status` to `WAIVED`, decrements the client's `no_show_strike_count` by 1 and reduces the client's `outstanding_fee_cents` by the booking's `booking_fee_cents`. The booking's `booking_fee_cents` is retained for audit but no longer counts toward the studio's aggregate `outstanding_balance_cents`. Restoration is bounded: it is admitted only while the producing session completed within the `STRIKE_RESTORE_WINDOW_HOURS` window before the current instant, and only while the client still carries at least one strike; either failure — the window lapsed or no strike left — is refused with `ERR_RESTORE_WINDOW_CLOSED`, and a booking that is not in `NO_SHOW` is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_RESERVED`.

### 3.4. Waitlists, Standby Positions and Offer Ordering

A waitlist hold is created against a `class_session_id` and a `client_id` with a `position_mode` that must be `QUEUED` or `STANDBY_ANY` — anything else, including an omitted mode, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409. A hold created in `QUEUED` mode begins in `ACTIVE`; a hold created in `STANDBY_ANY` mode begins in `STANDBY`. Both enumeration values are therefore reachable at creation. Each hold is stamped with a `joined_at` from the logical clock and a `raw_sequence_no` assigned as the maximum sequence already present on the session plus 1 — the stay-in-line counter. The `effective_sequence` is the value the offer ordering actually ranks on: it equals `min(raw_sequence_no, 0)` when the hold is in `STANDBY_ANY` mode and equals `raw_sequence_no` otherwise. The standby mode is the slotless mode: a standby hold's effective sequence collapses to zero, which is why a standby hold always drains before any numbered consumer hold. A hold joined while the session is not yet full is still created `ACTIVE`, but no seat is reserved for it, so the session's `seats_held` is unchanged.

A hold is offered a seat when capacity frees up — a booking leaves `RESERVED`, a session's capacity is raised, or an offer lapses. The candidate pool for an offer is drawn explicitly: holds of the target session whose status is `ACTIVE` or `STANDBY`, whose `joined_at` is at or before the current instant, whose owning client is not in `PORTABILITY_HOLD`, `PORTED` or `ANONYMIZED`, whose owning client's `no_show_strike_count` has not reached the offer block threshold of `STRIKE_OFFER_BLOCK_THRESHOLD`, and whose session is still `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. Candidates are ranked by ascending `effective_sequence`, then by ascending `created_at`, and if those also tie by ascending `joined_at`. The hold's UUID is never used as a tie-break. An offer is only made while the session's occupied count — `seats_taken` plus `seats_held` — remains below capacity.

When a hold is offered, it moves from `ACTIVE` to `OFFERED`, its `offer_expires_at` is set to the current instant plus the offer window, and the session's `seats_held` increments by 1. That increment is what can tip the posture from `OPEN` to `WAITLIST_ONLY` when the last free seat is consumed by a hold. A client reclaims an offered seat by claiming it before `offer_expires_at`, but only while the owning client's status is `ACTIVE` or `FROZEN`; the claim creates a booking on the session with channel `STUDIO` and a `payment_source` taken from the claim body — `DROP_IN` when the body omits it, and otherwise one of `DROP_IN`, `MEMBERSHIP` or `CLASS_PACK`, with any other value refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_SOURCE_NOT_PERMITTED` — moves the hold to `CLAIMED`, writes the new booking's identifier to the hold's `promoted_booking_id`, decrements `seats_held` by 1 and increments `seats_taken` by 1, and returns that new booking, not the hold, as the response object. Claiming does not itself bill the client and does not change the studio's dunning stage. A claim attempted against a hold that is not `OFFERED` is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_NOT_OFFERED`, except that a hold already in `CLAIMED`, `EXPIRED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_WAITLIST_TERMINAL`; a claim whose offer window has already passed is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_NOT_OFFERED`, a claim whose owning client's status is neither `ACTIVE` nor `FROZEN` is refused with `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_ADMISSIBLE`, and a claim against a session already `CANCELLED_BY_STUDIO` is refused with `ERR_STUDIO_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

An offer that is not claimed in time lapses. When the clock passes `offer_expires_at` the hold moves from `OFFERED` to `EXPIRED`, and the seat is offered onward: the session's next-lowest-ranking `ACTIVE` hold becomes `OFFERED` with a fresh offer window. If no `ACTIVE` hold remains, the session's `seats_held` decrements by 1 and the posture is re-derived toward `OPEN`. That re-offer is the reason a session's fidelity to its own ordering rules is observable across a chain of lapses. The lapse is not one of the transitions the clock setter fires when the clock moves forward: it becomes visible when the session is next read — a read of one session, or a listing of one session's waitlist holds, retires every offer whose window has passed and offers each freed seat onward before the response is rendered, while the flat listings materialise nothing — and the sweep endpoint of §4 is the operation that collects and reports the lapsed offers. Withdrawing an offered hold — the client cancelling rather than claiming — behaves identically to the lapse: the hold becomes `CANCELLED`, the seat is offered onward to the lowest-ranking remaining `ACTIVE` hold, and `seats_held` decrements by 1. Withdrawing an `ACTIVE` or `STANDBY` hold, by contrast, touches nothing: neither state holds a seat, so `seats_held` is untouched and the posture is unchanged.

Two compensating actions can undo a claimed seat or promote a standby hold. A studio owner may revoke a claim, which is admitted only while the promoted booking is still `RESERVED` — otherwise `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_RESERVED` — and only while the owning client's `no_show_strike_count` is below the strike revocation threshold of 5, a refusal that reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409; a booking whose promoted hold is missing or no longer `CLAIMED` is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_NOT_OFFERED`, and the revocation makes no onward offer, so the reverted hold sits `ACTIVE` until a seat is next released; the promoted booking becomes `CANCELLED`, its pack or membership credit is restored, and the hold reverts from `CLAIMED` to `ACTIVE` — but with `position_mode` reset to `QUEUED` and `raw_sequence_no` reassigned to the maximum sequence on the session plus 1, never to zero, so the client rejoins at the back of the line rather than reclaiming their old slot. A client holding a standby position may confirm a numbered position, admitted only while the current instant is at or before `joined_at` plus the standby confirm window of 90 minutes; that moves the hold from `STANDBY` to `ACTIVE`, sets `position_mode` to `QUEUED` and recomputes `effective_sequence` to equal `raw_sequence_no` — which may well place the hold behind consumers who joined later, because the raw counter is what was issued at join time. A confirmation or cancellation attempted against a hold already in `CLAIMED`, `EXPIRED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_WAITLIST_TERMINAL`, and a confirmation attempted against a hold that is not in `STANDBY` or after the confirm window has passed is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409.

An empty candidate pool does not raise an error. If no hold is eligible to receive a released seat, the seat returns to the posture at once — no hold-back timer delays the decrement of `seats_held` — and no error is raised and no synthetic hold is created. The sweep that finds and expires lapsed offers is an operator-and-owner capability that takes the target `studio_id` in the body (an unknown one is refused with `ERR_STUDIO_NOT_FOUND`), walks the studio's offered holds, moves every offer whose window has passed to `EXPIRED`, offers each freed seat onward to the next-ranked hold with a fresh window, and reports `swept_count` — the number of seats re-offered — together with `offered_entry_ids` and `expired_entry_ids`. Its optional `expected_class_session_version`, when supplied, must match the current `version` of every session belonging to the named studio or the sweep is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` before any offer lapses.

Instructor substitutions compete for the same seats through a separate eligibility path. A substitution hold is eligible only for a staff member whose `staff_role` is `INSTRUCTOR` and whose `substitute_class_template_ids` contains the session's `class_template_id`. The ranking of candidate substitutes is the ladder of §3.5.

`WAITLISTED` is declared as a terminal booking state, joining `ATTENDED`, `CANCELLED` and `LATE_CANCELLED`, but no operation in this API ever writes it: a priority hold or an accepted substitution that pushes the occupied count of `seats_taken` plus `seats_held` past the session's `capacity` demotes nothing, so the surplus booking stays `RESERVED` and no compensating waitlist entry is created for it.

### 3.5. Instructor Substitution and the Priority Ladder

When a session's instructor cannot teach it, the platform ranks the studio's eligible instructors rather than letting the studio pick arbitrarily, and the nominated substitute must at least match the best eligible candidate. The candidate set is the studio's staff members with `staff_role` `INSTRUCTOR` whose status is `ACTIVE` or `SUBSTITUTE_ONLY`, excluding the instructor already of record for the session under consideration. The ranking key is a weighted score, evaluated as a sum of integer-weighted terms.

The ladder weights the terms as follows: a template match scores a class-template-match term of 1 when the session's `class_template_id` appears in the instructor's `substitute_class_template_ids` and 0 otherwise, at a weight of 30; a level-fit term scores 1 for a staff member whose status is `ACTIVE` and one half for a staff member whose status is `SUBSTITUTE_ONLY`, at a weight of 20; an overlap-penalty term scores 1 minus the lesser of 1 and the instructor's already-scheduled overlapping teaching minutes divided by 60, at a weight of 15, so the penalty saturates at a full hour of overlap and never goes negative; a substitution-history term scores 1 when the instructor's `substitution_count` is above zero and 0 otherwise, at a weight of 10; an early-acceptance term scores 1 for every member of the candidate set, at a weight of 5; a distance term scores 1 when the instructor's `primary_class_template_id` equals the session's `class_template_id` and 0 otherwise, at a weight of 3; and a rating term scores 1 for every candidate, at a weight of 2. The overlapping minutes counted are the total minutes the instructor is already scheduled to teach in any session whose interval overlaps the target session's interval, counting only the sessions that are still `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`.

Two details make the ranking reproducible. First, the evaluation order is pinned: the overlap term is computed before the level term is applied, so a `SUBSTITUTE_ONLY` instructor with a full hour of overlap loses both the level half and the entire overlap term and cannot outrank a partially-overlapping `ACTIVE` instructor. Second, scoring is assembled so that the bonuses are summed as integers before ranking, and the final score is reported to one decimal place with halves rounded up. Candidates are ranked by descending score and tie-broken by ascending `created_at` of the instructor — the UUID is never the tie-break.

The weights themselves are not flat across all tiers. On a `STARTER` studio the template-match term and the overlap-penalty term are fused into a single 30-point term equal to 30 multiplied by the template match and by the capped overlap penalty, so a template match combined with a full hour of overlap forfeits the whole 30 points while the level term still applies. On a `GROWTH` or `ENTERPRISE` studio the two terms are scored apart, as 30 multiplied by the template match plus 15 multiplied by the capped overlap penalty. The same two instructors can therefore invert rank between the two variants, which is intended.

A reassignment names the `selected_staff_id` and may carry an `override_reason`, together with `expected_class_session_version`. The session's `instructor_staff_id` becomes the selected staff member, and any `ACCRUED` commission entry the previous instructor held for that session is reversed with `reversal_reason` `INSTRUCTOR_SUBSTITUTED`. A nominated instructor who is absent from the candidate set — or who is the instructor of record, which is the same refusal — is refused with `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`; a nominated identifier that names no staff member at all is refused with `ERR_STAFF_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`; and when the candidate set is empty and no `override_reason` was supplied the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SUBSTITUTE`, while an empty candidate set with an `override_reason` accepts the nomination with no eligibility test at all. The reassignment also sets a `substitute_accepted` flag on the session and increments the selected instructor's `substitution_count` by 1. The ranking is also readable on its own: an eligible-instructor listing for a session accepts an optional `exclude_staff_id` and a `limit` (defaulting to 10), returns the candidates in descending score order with an `eligible` array, a `total_count` and a `has_more` flag, each candidate carrying its `id`, `staff_member_id`, `full_name`, `staff_role`, `status`, `score` and `overlapping_taught_minutes`, and returns an empty list rather than an error when nobody qualifies. Supplying `exclude_staff_id` replaces the default exclusion of the instructor of record: the named identifier is dropped from the pool and the instructor of record is kept in it.

A staff member joins the roster with a studio identifier, a `full_name`, a `staff_role` that is exactly `INSTRUCTOR`, `FRONT_DESK` or `MANAGER`, an optional `primary_class_template_id` naming the format they primarily teach, an optional `substitute_class_template_ids` array listing the formats they may cover, and a `commission_rate_bps` between 0 and 5000. Staff on the roster are also reachable by their `primary_class_template_id`, and a class format's reverse listing exposes exactly the staff members who name it in that field. A class pack's session price is not the pack price: a format's `credit_cost` declares how many pack credits one seat of that format consumes, and that figure is what a pack-funded booking debits.

Staff status is itself a lifecycle. An `ACTIVE` staff member may be restricted to substitute duty, moving to `SUBSTITUTE_ONLY`, after which no new session may name them as `instructor_staff_id` although sessions already scheduled to them remain valid and are not re-derived. A substitute-only member may be reinstated to `ACTIVE` and may then again be named a primary instructor. A member may be deactivated, moving to `INACTIVE`, but only when no session of the studio names them as instructor with a status in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` unless a substitute has already been accepted for that session, a refusal that reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409. Deactivation decrements the subscription's `staff_seat_count` and recomputes `seat_overage_count`; it does not re-check accepted substitutions or revert any session to its original instructor, so a substitution already accepted stays in force on its session; and it leaves every `ACCRUED` commission entry payable, because deactivation never forfeits accrued commission.

### 3.6. Commission, Payroll Runs and Revenue Conservation

Commission accrues when a booking becomes `ATTENDED`. The studio's tier selects a default teaching rate in basis points, but the staff member's own `commission_rate_bps` overrides it whenever that rate is above zero. The base the rate applies to is `commission_base_cents`. Which rate applies depends on who taught the seat: the attended booking with the lowest `creation_seq` on its session is treated as the instructor-of-record's seat and takes the teaching rate for the tier — 2000 basis points on `STARTER`, 2500 on `GROWTH` and 3000 on `ENTERPRISE` — while every later attended booking of the same session is a deputy seat and takes the lower deputy rate for the tier, 500 on `STARTER`, 700 on `GROWTH` and 900 on `ENTERPRISE`. The commission itself is the base multiplied by the applicable rate divided by 10000, truncated to whole cents.

A cap and a channel bonus then apply, and the studio's `payroll_cap_mode` decides which of two orders is used — the field chosen at signup. The ceiling is 1200 cents multiplied by a tier multiplier of 1.0 for `STARTER` and `GROWTH` and 1.25 for `ENTERPRISE`. The bonus is the base multiplied by the marketplace channel bonus rate of 1000 basis points divided by 10000, truncated, and it is evaluated only for a marketplace-channel booking: a studio-channel accrual takes the capped commission with no bonus term at all. Under `BONUS_OUTSIDE_CAP` the ceiling is applied to the teaching commission first and the bonus is added afterwards whenever the bonus reaches the channel bonus threshold of 150 cents, so the final commission strictly exceeds the ceiling in that case and the bonus escapes the cap. Under `BONUS_INSIDE_CAP` the bonus is folded into the commission before the ceiling is applied, so identical booking data can yield exactly the ceiling with the bonus clipped away. The two orders disagree precisely when the pre-bonus commission already exceeds the ceiling and the bonus clears its threshold. Each term is truncated before it is summed.

The accrual lands as a `CommissionEntry` whose `booking_channel` mirrors the producing booking's channel, whose `base_amount_cents` is the booking's `commission_base_cents` on both channels, with the marketplace commission never netted out of the entry's base — whose `rate_bps` records the effective rate applied, and whose `status` begins `ACCRUED`. The staff member's `accrued_commission_cents` is the sum of their entries still in `ACCRUED` in the current open period. The `booking_channel` value written on the entry is one of `STUDIO` or `MARKETPLACE`.

Payroll periods are disjoint inclusive ranges with a sequential `period_index`. A period's status runs `OPEN` to `LOCKED` to `CLOSED`, where `OPEN` and `LOCKED` are non-terminal and `CLOSED` is terminal. Running payroll locks the period and pays every accrued entry in it, recomputing the period's `paid_commission_cents`; the run is refused with `ERR_PAYROLL_HOLD` while the subscription's `payroll_hold` is true, and it is admitted only once no session whose `payroll_period_id` is this period remains in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` — a still-open session is refused with that same `ERR_PAYROLL_HOLD` — because a period cannot be locked while its sessions could still produce entries. A close attempted against a period that is not `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_PAYROLL_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN`. A locked period may be reopened inside the reopen window — the clock must still be strictly before the period's `end_date` plus the platform reopen window of 7 days, and a reopen attempted against a period that is not `LOCKED`, or after that window, is refused with `ERR_PAYROLL_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN` — which returns every `PAID` entry of the period to `ACCRUED` and lifts the accrual block. While the block is in force a period that is not `OPEN` takes no new accrual at all: a session that completes against a `LOCKED` or `CLOSED` period still runs its attendance cascade and still writes the attended revenue onto its bookings, but produces no commission entry, so the block is lifted for later completions only by reopening the period. Once the clock passes that window the period closes permanently: `LOCKED` moves to `CLOSED`, and from then on any reversal of one of its entries posts a `reversal_correction_cents` into the next open period instead of reopening the closed one.

Revenue conservation is the invariant that ties reversals together. The sum, across a studio, of `commission_base_cents` on bookings that are or have been `ATTENDED` is invariant under every reversal and substitution: the correction is posted as a commission entry with status `REVERSED` when the entry was still `ACCRUED`, or as the `reversal_correction_cents` on a payroll period when the entry had already been paid — never by editing `commission_base_cents`. A period's `reversal_correction_cents` is a signed value, subtracting the original commission from the next open period. An entry that has been reversed carries a `reversal_reason`; while it is `ACCRUED` or `PAID` that reason is null.

### 3.7. Marketplace Listing, Marketplace Pricing and Platform Commission

The consumer marketplace sells the studio's own inventory to app users at different economics. Browsing the marketplace requires no authentication; only sessions whose studio posture is `ACTIVE` or `TRIAL` and whose tier has `marketplace_listing_enabled` set appear in the marketplace class list, which accepts optional filters for `studio_id`, `home_state_code`, `is_off_peak` and `start_after`. Reading a single marketplace class returns the same session with the marketplace seat pool and the marketplace price — the view adds `marketplace_price_cents` and `promo_price_cents` and a `fullness_posture` re-derived against the reduced capacity — and — as noted in §2.9 — derives its posture against a capacity reduced by the promotional reservation, so it may report `FULL` where the studio-facing read reports `OPEN`. A session that is not marketplace-visible is refused with `ERR_FEATURE_NOT_IN_TIER`, while the list read simply omits such sessions rather than erroring. A session listing may be narrowed to a window by supplying `start_after` and `start_before` bounds against the session's `start_at`; both bounds are strict, keeping only sessions whose `start_at` is after the first and before the second. The public class list additionally accepts an optional `limit` and `offset` under the usual pagination rules. A studio whose `marketplace_listing` gate is false cannot put inventory on the marketplace at all: its sessions are omitted from the public class list, and a single-class read of one of them is refused with `ERR_FEATURE_NOT_IN_TIER`.

Both channel prices are derived from the class format's `base_price_cents`. The off-peak factor is 8000 basis points when the format is flagged `is_off_peak` and 10000 otherwise; the tier factor is 10000 basis points on `STARTER`, 10500 on `GROWTH` and 11000 on `ENTERPRISE`. The studio `list_price_cents` is the base multiplied by the off-peak factor and by the tier factor, divided by one hundred million, truncated to whole cents, and then raised to the tier's floor — 1500 cents on `STARTER`, 1800 on `GROWTH` and 2000 on `ENTERPRISE` — whenever the product falls below it. The evaluation order is pinned: the off-peak factor is applied before the tier factor, and the tier floor is applied after the product, so the floor never rescales an already-discounted price. When the floor binds, two sessions with different base prices collapse to the same price at the same tier and off-peak flag; when the product binds, both factors move the price. The `marketplace_price_cents` for the same seat grosses up the platform commission: it is `list_price_cents` multiplied by 10000 divided by 10000 minus the tier's `marketplace_commission_bps`, rounded upward to whole cents. That gross-up is why the marketplace price always diverges from the studio price when the commission rate is non-zero.

A marketplace booking is made by a `Client` against a `client_id` and a `class_session_id` with a `payment_ref` and a `promo_redeem` flag that defaults to false and is honoured only when it is the literal true. The create additionally requires the studio's `marketplace_listing` gate, and it is always written with channel `MARKETPLACE` and `payment_source` `DROP_IN`: a `membership_id` or `class_pack_id` supplied alongside is ignored rather than debited, so this operation never consumes a studio membership or pack credit and never raises `ERR_PAYMENT_SOURCE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Its `commission_base_cents` is taken from `marketplace_price_cents`, or from the session's promotional price when `promo_redeem` is true. When the seat is redeemed from the promotional pool, `is_promotional_seat` is set, `promo_seats_used` increments by 1 — and when that increment brings the used count up to the allocation the session's `promo_offer_state` moves to `EXHAUSTED` — and the redemption is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXHAUSTED` when `promo_seats_used` has already reached `promo_seats_allocated` or when `promo_offer_state` is not `ACTIVE`. The studio's revenue for a marketplace seat is the channel total less the marketplace commission, so the same seat yields strictly less studio revenue than a studio-channel drop-in.

Platform commission on marketplace bookings is computed in two ways that differ by rounding, and both are observable. The per-booking form floors each booking's `commission_base_cents` multiplied by the tier's commission rate divided by 10000 in isolation. The settlement form walks the studio's marketplace bookings whose status is not `CANCELLED`, `LATE_CANCELLED` or `WAITLISTED` — attended and no-show rows are included — in ascending `created_at` order, tie-broken by ascending `creation_seq`, with the UUID identifier never used as the ordering key, accumulating a running un-rounded total of each booking's raw commission and flooring the running total as it goes, so the reported commission is the floor of the accumulator rather than the sum of the individual floors. The two forms differ by at most one cent per row, and the difference is exactly the discarded fractional cents. The settlement read is keyed by a required `studio_id` (an unknown one is refused with `ERR_STUDIO_NOT_FOUND`) and reports `marketplace_commission_cents` (the accumulator floor), `per_booking_commission_cents` (the sum of the individual floors) and `booking_count`. Two further constants are declared for this area but never applied: a marketplace minimum commission of 50 cents floors neither the per-booking form nor the settlement accumulator, and the 800-basis-point promotional commission rate is never substituted for the tier rate, so a promotional marketplace booking settles at the same commission rate as any other.

### 3.8. Off-Peak Promotional Inventory and the Cascade Waterfall

Promotional inventory is released only for eligible rows: a session whose class format is flagged `is_off_peak` and whose status is `SCHEDULED`, with a `promo_offer_state` of `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE`. Its promotional price is `list_price_cents` multiplied by 10000 minus the session's `promo_discount_bps`, divided by 10000, truncated to whole cents. The released-seat count is the lesser of two quantities: the allocation headroom — `promo_seats_allocated` less `promo_seats_used` less the seats currently held open — and the session's capacity less its `seats_taken`. No operation computes either quantity: the released-seat count and the overflow are declarative only.

No cascade runs in this API, so no bucket is ever written: nothing offers leftover seats to the next session of the same `class_template_id`, to the next such session in ascending `start_at` order, or to the studio's marketplace inventory, and `promo_cascade_class_session_id` is present on every session row but always null, which is why the reverse waterfall listing of §2.10 is always empty.

Two counters keep the allocation honest. `promo_seats_used` grows by each promotional redemption and also grows when promotional seats are lost because the promotion lapsed in place. Because the released count is capped by the target session's free seats, an allocation may close with `promo_seats_used` still below `promo_seats_allocated`; because no release computation runs, no response reports that shortfall either. Release is also bounded by the platform's minimum and maximum promotional discount rates of 500 and 5000 basis points, and a promotion with no explicit validity is given a default validity span of 21 days. A session's `promo_offer_state` is drawn from `NONE`, `SCHEDULED`, `ACTIVE`, `EXHAUSTED`, `EXPIRED` and `WITHDRAWN`, but only four of those are ever written: the state becomes `ACTIVE` when promotional seats are granted at session creation on an off-peak format and stays `NONE` when no allocation was supplied, moves to `EXHAUSTED` when the last allocated seat is redeemed, and moves to `WITHDRAWN` when the studio is closed; `SCHEDULED` and `EXPIRED` are declared but never written. Stepping the allocation counter past its ceiling is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXHAUSTED`.

A session's price is not static while its promotion runs. Any update that touches a price-affecting field bumps `price_version` and recomputes both channel prices through the promotional release rules and the channel total rules below. The `price_version` counter is what keys a price snapshot, so a client holding a stale price can be detected.

### 3.9. Memberships, Freezing, Class Packs and Credit Restoration

A membership instantiates a plan: the caller supplies `studio_id`, `client_id`, `plan_name`, `monthly_fee_cents`, `included_credits_per_cycle`, `minimum_term_months`, `freeze_allowed`, `freeze_max_days_per_cycle`, `billing_anchor_day` and an optional `auto_renew`. The plan terms are folded onto the row so that a later plan revision never mutates a live membership. The membership begins in `ACTIVE` — `PENDING` is declared in the ladder below but no operation ever writes it — with `current_period_index` 0 and `open_balance_cents` 0, and its `next_billing_at` derived from the `billing_anchor_day`; the anchor day must fall between the 1st and the 28th, and an out-of-range anchor is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409. Its `included_credits_per_cycle` credits are granted immediately for cycle 0. `auto_renew` defaults to true. The membership's status ladder is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `FROZEN`, `PAST_DUE`, `LAPSED`, `CANCELLED` and `EXPIRED`, with `EXPIRED` terminal.

Advancing a cycle is driven by a projected `advance_days` and an `outcome` of `CHARGED` or `EARLY_CHARGED`, together with `expected_membership_version`. The clock moves forward by `advance_days` before the cycle is advanced. The first settled charge leaves the membership `ACTIVE` (a row that somehow sits in `PENDING` is moved to `ACTIVE`), sets `current_period_index` to 1 and takes the first cycle fee into `open_balance_cents` alongside the granted credits. Thereafter a charged cycle increments `current_period_index`, advances `next_billing_at` by exactly one calendar month, grants `included_credits_per_cycle` credits, and grows `open_balance_cents` by the membership's `monthly_fee_cents`. The late fee is then tested against `next_billing_at` and, because the comparison is at-or-past rather than strictly past, the instant exactly equal to the due date lies inside the fee window: when the clock has reached the new due date the balance also grows by one tenth of the monthly fee truncated to whole cents and the membership moves to `PAST_DUE`. Which due date is tested depends on how the cycle was advanced. Under the billing-day chronological variant — the one an `outcome` of `CHARGED` selects — the late fee tests against the *new* due date, which sits one month in the future, so the fee fires only once the caller has advanced the clock past that new date. Under the forward-looking variant — selected by `outcome` `EARLY_CHARGED` — the fee tests against the *old* due date, which the clock has not yet reached, so the very same membership advances with no late fee at all. A membership that is `FROZEN` is skipped entirely: the cycle does not advance, no charge is posted, and `next_billing_at` simply moves by one calendar month. A membership whose `auto_renew` is false does not advance at all: it moves to `LAPSED`, `next_billing_at` is left unchanged, no further cycle is charged, and the open balance is preserved for collection. A `PAST_DUE` membership is returned to `ACTIVE` by the next charged cycle, which increments `current_period_index` by 1 and adds the new cycle's fee to `open_balance_cents` — the existing arrears are not cleared, so the open balance keeps growing until something else settles it. Notably, a `PAST_DUE` membership may still be used while it is in arrears.

Freezing is a proration rather than a pause. A membership may be frozen from `ACTIVE` or `PAST_DUE` — any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409 — and only when `freeze_allowed` is true, with a plan that forbids freezing and a non-positive `freeze_days` both refused with `ERR_FREEZE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. The freeze records a `freeze_start_at` at the current instant and a `freeze_resume_at` at the current instant plus the requested `freeze_days`, moves the status to `FROZEN`, and posts a prorated adjustment against the window that actually elapses rather than the window that was requested. Freezing also skips the next cycle: a frozen membership advances by exactly one month with no charge. Unfreezing is admitted once the clock is at or past `freeze_start_at`, moves the membership back to `ACTIVE`, grows `freeze_days_used` by the elapsed frozen days, and recomputes the proration against the elapsed window. The proration arithmetic is pinned: the pending freeze days are the calendar days between `freeze_start_at` and `freeze_resume_at`; if more days elapsed than were planned the counted days are reduced to the planned days minus an over-freeze penalty of 5 days, floored at zero; the proration factor is 1 minus the lesser of the counted days and the plan's `freeze_max_days_per_cycle` divided by the cycle length of 30 days; and the prorated monthly fee is the membership's monthly fee multiplied by that factor, truncated to whole cents. The `freeze_days_used` total is then increased by the counted days, and if that running total would pass the annual freeze cap of 60 days the extension is refused with `ERR_FREEZE_CAP_EXCEEDED` and no proration is posted. The `prorated_fee_cents` field carries the most recently posted adjustment.

Cancelling a membership is preserved-for-collection rather than forgiven: the status moves to `CANCELLED`, `open_balance_cents` is preserved, and no further cycle is charged. An early-termination fee of one month's fee is required whenever `current_period_index` is still below `minimum_term_months`; without a valid `fee_payment_ref` the request fails at the fiscal precedence band with `ERR_EARLY_TERMINATION_FEE_REQUIRED` and nothing is cancelled. A membership in `CANCELLED` or `LAPSED` becomes `EXPIRED` once the clock passes `next_billing_at` plus the membership expiry grace period of 30 days; it then remains queryable but admits no transition, and a cycle advance attempted against an `EXPIRED` row is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` while one against a `CANCELLED` row is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Only the annual cap refuses: a freeze that would push `freeze_days_used` past the annual cap of 60 days is refused with `ERR_FREEZE_CAP_EXCEEDED`, while the plan's per-cycle maximum refuses nothing — it caps the days counted into the proration factor.

Class packs are a credit-decay instrument. A class pack is sold with a `studio_id` and `client_id`, a `pack_size` that must be at least 1, a `purchased_at` that defaults to the current logical instant when omitted, and an `expiry_scheme` that must be `NONE`, `SEASONAL` or `PROMOTIONAL` — anything else, and a `pack_size` below 1, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409 — plus an optional `expected_client_version` for the OCC guard of §2.8. The pack's `credits_remaining` begins at `pack_size` and its status at `ACTIVE`, and the sale increments the client's `version` by 1. The `expires_at` is derived from `purchased_at` according to the scheme: `NONE` gives the standard pack validity span, `SEASONAL` gives 90 days, and `PROMOTIONAL` gives 30 days while also capping the pack size at 12. Credits are consumed one at a time in booking order. When the last credit is spent the pack moves from `ACTIVE` to `DEPLETED` and further bookings against it are refused with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_PACK` — but depletion is reversible: cancelling the booking that emptied the pack restores the credit through the restoration rules below, returning `credits_remaining` to 1 and moving the pack back to `ACTIVE`, so no credit is ever destroyed by the reversal.

A pack's expiry clock can be held. Freezing a client's concurrent membership moves their pack from `ACTIVE` to `FROZEN`, during which `expires_at` does not advance and the grace window is not entered; unfreezing that membership returns the pack to `ACTIVE` with `expires_at` unchanged and the pack fully spendable. A pack may be extended by a studio owner who supplies `extend_days` and a `reason` (the reason is accepted but not recorded): `expires_at` advances by the granted days and `extension_days_used` grows by the same amount, and the extension is refused with `ERR_PACK_EXTENSION_CAP_EXCEEDED` once the lifetime total would pass the maximum extension days of 90, or when `extend_days` is not strictly positive. The expiry sweep operates on the single pack named in its path — it moves that pack, when its `expires_at` has been reached and its status is `ACTIVE`, `FROZEN` or `DEPLETED`, to `EXPIRED`, sets `pending_expiry_grace_until` to `expires_at` plus the pack grace days of 14, and reports `expired_count`, `credits_voided` and `credits_returned_to_studio`. The sweep carries an optional `advance_clock_days` offset, defaulting to zero, which moves the logical clock forward before the sweep runs so that a fixture can cross an expiry boundary without a separate clock call. The credits the studio keeps are the credits remaining at expiry plus one quarter of that remainder truncated to whole credits — a four-to-one split in the studio's favour. The consumption order across the expiry boundary is marked but not priced: the first credit consumed after the expiry moment inside the grace window sets a `zero_value_credit` flag on its booking, but that flag never changes a channel total, so every pack-covered booking still reports zero under the booked variant and the drop-in list price under the attended variant. A pack's remaining credits, plus the credits consumed by bookings still in `RESERVED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `ATTENDED`, must equal the pack's original size less the credits voided by the expiry sweep; every booking paired with its compensating cancellation leaves that equation unchanged. The same conservation thinking governs corrections, which are posted by restoring credits or writing a correction rather than by editing the original figure; the 48-hour restoration window declared for that purpose is never applied, so a credit restoration is not bounded by elapsed time.

### 3.10. Benefit Networks, Client Portability and the Export Lifecycle

Wellness benefit networks pay per visit, and their economics are registered centrally by the platform: a network row carries a short `network_code` used for resolution, a display `name`, a `per_visit_allowance_cents`, a `reimburse_full_list_price` flag, a `remit_days_after_visit` count (defaulting to 30 when omitted) and a `claim_denial_window_hours` figure (defaulting to 0), plus a `status` flag that is set true at registration and never changed. Re-registering an existing `network_code` updates that row in place rather than minting a second one, and a missing `network_code` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409. When a booking funded by a benefit network is attended, the claim amount is the session's `list_price_cents` when the network reimburses full list price, and otherwise the lesser of the network's per-visit allowance and the session's list price. The claim is written with `benefit_claim_status` `SUBMITTED`, and the studio recognises the receivable forward-looking at once: the receivable equals the claim amount and it becomes due at the session's `end_at` plus the network's `remit_days_after_visit` days. The amount billed to the network lands on the producing booking's `benefit_claim_amount_cents`, which is zero on every booking whose payment source is not a benefit network; the claim itself is reached through that booking's `booking_id`, so a claim walk keys off the attended booking rather than a separate claim table. A booking whose payment source is not a benefit network carries a claim amount of 0 and a claim status of `NOT_APPLICABLE`. A network-funded booking is never charged a late-cancellation or no-show fee.

Denial handling is declared but unreachable: no operation writes the `DENIED` claim status, so the resubmission path can never be entered — a resubmit against the only status a live claim can hold is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Were a claim denied, it could be resubmitted exactly once inside the network's denial window, the resubmission would recompute the claim amount from the current list price, and a claim denied after that window would be terminal and contribute nothing to the receivable. A resubmission beyond the single permitted retry, or one that arrives after the denial window has lapsed, is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A claim walk for a studio, keyed by `studio_id`, reports each claim with its amount recomputed at read time — so a repricing since submission changes the reported figure — as an `id`, `booking_id`, `studio_id`, `client_id`, `class_session_id`, `benefit_network_code`, `benefit_claim_status`, `claim_amount_cents` and `remit_at` (the session's `end_at` plus the network's remit days), and totals the forward-looking receivable across claims that are `SUBMITTED` or `PAID` plus the resubmission-eligible portion of claims that are `DENIED` but still inside their denial window, reporting it as `receivable_total_cents` alongside `total_count` and `has_more`. The claim identifier is the producing booking's identifier, so an unknown one on the resubmit path is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, and the denial window is measured from the claim's submission instant, not from the denial.

Client-list portability is the contested centre of this domain, because the roster is simultaneously the studio's asset and the consumer's personal data. A consumer files a request by naming the `studio_id`, the `requesting_client_id` and a `dispute_reason`. Filing is gated on the studio's `client_portability_export` feature. The request begins in `PENDING`, its `requested_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the studio's `data_ownership_state` moves to `PORTABILITY_PENDING`. A fresh request is refused with `ERR_PORTABILITY_COOLDOWN_ACTIVE` while a previous request by the same client against the same studio that ended in `RESOLVED_DENIED`, `EXPIRED` or `WITHDRAWN` still sits inside the 30-day cooldown measured from its `decided_at`.

The studio has a bounded chance to object. An objection carries an `objection_basis` and is admitted only while the clock is still strictly inside the response window of 168 hours from `requested_at`; it moves the request from `PENDING` to `OBJECTED` and leaves the studio's `data_ownership_state` at `PORTABILITY_PENDING` pending adjudication. An objection that arrives after the window is refused with `ERR_PORTABILITY_WINDOW_CLOSED`, and an objection against a request that is still undecided but no longer `PENDING` — an already-`OBJECTED` one — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409. Attempting an objection, a release or a denial against a request that already carries a decision is refused with `ERR_PORTABILITY_ALREADY_DECIDED`.

Silence is a release. If the response window lapses with no objection, the system moves the request from `PENDING` to `RESOLVED_RELEASE` with exactly the grant effects of an explicit release, and additionally writes the literal `PORTABILITY_NONRESPONSE` into the studio's `suspension_reason` field — replacing whatever reason was there — without changing the studio's `status` at all.

An explicit release is admitted while the request is `PENDING` and requires that the requesting client's `outstanding_fee_cents` is exactly zero — a release attempted while the client still owes fees is refused with `ERR_CLIENT_BALANCE_OUTSTANDING` — and that the studio's `data_ownership_state` is `OWNED` or already `PORTABILITY_PENDING`. The release carries `include_booking_history` and `include_financial_history` flags, stamps `decided_at`, moves the studio's `data_ownership_state` to `PORTABILITY_GRANTED`, and moves the requesting client's status to `PORTABILITY_HOLD`, which removes that client from the studio's `client_roster_count` — that count takes in only clients whose own status is `ACTIVE` or `FROZEN`, so the removal holds for as long as the client's status stays outside those two values and does not end when the export is issued. It mints an `export_grant_id` — the literal prefix `grant_` followed by a UUID's hex digits — and freezes an `export_manifest` from the roster captured at that instant: a manifest carrying `record_count` (every client whose `home_studio_id` is that studio, counted whatever the client's own status, unlike the roster count of §2.4), a hex SHA-256 `record_digest` over the studio's sorted client identifiers and the requesting client's identifier, the source `source_studio_ids`, a `generated_at_utc` stamp, `booking_history_included` and `financial_history_included` flags, and the `requesting_client_id`.

Denial is the studio's other option and it requires a basis: a denial is admitted while the request is `PENDING` and only when a basis is available — the body's `dispute_reason`, falling back to the request's own recorded `dispute_reason`, must be non-empty or the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409; it moves the request to `RESOLVED_DENIED` and returns the studio's `data_ownership_state` to `OWNED`. An objection that is never adjudicated also resolves as a denial by default: once the clock passes the adjudication window of 336 hours from `requested_at` the system moves an `OBJECTED` request to `RESOLVED_DENIED`, returns the studio's ownership state to `OWNED`, and the client may file a fresh request after the cooldown.

Adjudication is the platform's role. An adjudicator acts on an `OBJECTED` request with a `decision` of `RELEASE` or `DENY`, optionally carrying `include_booking_history` and `include_financial_history`. A decision of `RELEASE` applies the same grant effects as the studio's explicit release, with the studio's objection recorded as unfounded through an `objection_founded` flag set false; a decision of `DENY` moves the request to `RESOLVED_DENIED`; any other `decision` value is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409. Adjudication must arrive while the request is still undecided and within the adjudication window; after that it is refused with `ERR_PORTABILITY_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

Issuing the export is what turns a grant into something the client can actually take away, and it is deliberately re-issuable. An issuance carries `expected_portability_request_version` and moves the request from `RESOLVED_RELEASE` to `EXPORT_ISSUED`, stamping `export_manifest.generated_at_utc` and freezing `record_digest`. It is refused with `ERR_EXPORT_NOT_GRANTED` while the request is not in `RESOLVED_RELEASE` — and that refusal is the mechanism of the cycle, because an `EXPORT_ISSUED` row whose export window lapsed must be walked back to `RESOLVED_RELEASE` before it can be issued again. It is also refused with `ERR_EXPORT_DOWNLOAD_CAP` once `export_download_count` has reached the maximum of 3 downloads, and that cap is tested before the grant state, so a request already carrying 3 downloads reports `ERR_EXPORT_DOWNLOAD_CAP` even when its state would otherwise earn `ERR_EXPORT_NOT_GRANTED`. Issuance itself is not gated on elapsed time: the 72-hour export window runs from the request's `decided_at` and governs the life of an issued export, not the act of issuing it, so a request walked back under §3.10 can always be generated again. An issuance keeps whatever `export_grant_id` the request already carries and mints a fresh one only when the walk-back has cleared it, so the second grant of a cycle is a new identifier.

Downloading consumes the capped slots. Each download returns the portability request row with its manifest, increments `export_download_count` by 1, is refused with `ERR_EXPORT_DOWNLOAD_CAP` once the count has reached the cap, and is refused with `ERR_EXPORT_NOT_GRANTED` while the request is not `EXPORT_ISSUED`. Only after all three downloads have been consumed does the requesting client's status move from `PORTABILITY_HOLD` to `PORTED`.

A portability request ends its life in one of four shapes, and the distinction matters because only one of them is a live grant. A request that reached `EXPORT_ISSUED` and whose client consumed all of the permitted downloads resolves with the client moved on to `PORTED` — a terminal client posture. A request that lapses with no export ever generated resolves as `EXPIRED`, returning the studio's ownership state to `OWNED` and forcing the client to file afresh. A request that is denied — by the studio, by adjudication, or by the lapse of an unadjudicated objection — resolves as `RESOLVED_DENIED`. And a request the client abandons resolves as `WITHDRAWN`. Beyond those, a studio whose roster has been fully exported and whose client has moved on carries a `data_ownership_state` of `EXPORT_CLOSED`: the export has been taken, the download slots are spent, and no further export may be issued against that grant. The studio's ownership posture is derived rather than independently stored — it is recomputed from the request history whenever the studio is read — and it returns to `OWNED` on every denial, withdrawal, expiry or defaulted objection, so a failed dispute never leaves the studio permanently marked.

Two lapse paths close the lifecycle, and they differ by whether an export was ever generated. If the export window lapses on a request that was issued but never downloaded a single time — the download count is still zero — the system moves the request from `EXPORT_ISSUED` back to `RESOLVED_RELEASE` and clears the `export_grant_id` and the `export_manifest`, so the export can be generated again; this is the cycle edge, and because `decided_at` is not reset the walked-back row sits past the 72-hour window, so a later clock transition expires it unless it is issued first. If instead the window lapses while no manifest was ever generated — the request is still sitting in `RESOLVED_RELEASE` with a null manifest — the request moves to `EXPIRED`, the studio's `data_ownership_state` returns to `OWNED`, and the client must file an entirely new request. A client may withdraw a request while it is `PENDING` or `OBJECTED`, but only when the acting client is the requesting client — any other acting client is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` — and a request that is no longer in either state is refused with `ERR_PORTABILITY_ALREADY_DECIDED`; withdrawal moves the request to `WITHDRAWN` and returns the studio's ownership state to `OWNED`.

### 3.11. Channel Totals: Where the Money on a Seat Comes From

The revenue written against a single seat depends on how that seat was paid for, and the channel total is re-evaluated as the booking moves through its life. At the moment a booking is created, the gross depends on its `payment_source`: a benefit-network booking takes the producing session's `list_price_cents` as its gross; a booking that consumed a class-pack credit takes zero, because that revenue was recognised when the pack was purchased; a booking that consumed a membership credit takes zero for the same reason, the revenue having been recognised at the monthly cycle charge; and every other booking takes the effective seat price for its channel. The effective seat price is the studio price reduced by the session's promotional discount rate for the studio channel — `list_price_cents` multiplied by 10000 minus `promo_discount_bps`, divided by 10000, truncated — and for the marketplace channel it is `list_price_cents` multiplied by 10000 divided by 10000 minus the commission rate in basis points, rounded upward.

The rounding order matters and is pinned: every component is computed in integer cents, the rounding direction travels with the channel — floor on the studio path, ceiling on the marketplace gross-up — and it is applied once, at the end of the channel-price selection, never per component, because rounding each component and then subtracting produces different cents whenever the commission rate is not a multiple of 100. The studio's gross revenue for a booking is the channel total less the marketplace commission when the channel is `MARKETPLACE`, and the channel total on its own otherwise; it is written onto the booking as `studio_gross_cents` when a marketplace booking reaches `ATTENDED`, and no other booking carries that field.

The same booking can therefore report two different channel totals depending on when it is evaluated, and that divergence is intended. At creation the booked variant uses the pack-credit value for pack- and membership-covered seats, which is zero for a pack-covered seat. When the session completes and the booking becomes `ATTENDED`, the attended variant is re-evaluated and uses the drop-in list price for pack- and membership-covered bookings as well. A pack-covered attended seat therefore reports a channel total above zero under the attended variant while reporting exactly zero under the booked variant — the same booking, two legitimate figures, and the difference is what funds instructor commission on a seat that was nominally prepaid.

### 3.12. Operational Windows, Restores and Test-Driving Capabilities

Every time-gated check in this API compares the logical clock against a stored instant, and the comparisons are strict-or-equal exactly as written, so the instant equal to a window edge lies inside the window. Check-in admission is open from the session start minus the check-in lead time of 60 minutes through the session end. A waitlist offer lapses after the offer window of 120 minutes, and a released seat with no eligible taker returns to the posture immediately rather than after a hold-back period. A standby position may be confirmed within 90 minutes of joining. A no-show may be restored inside the `STRIKE_RESTORE_WINDOW_HOURS` window of the session's end, which is 72 hours. A studio may object inside 168 hours of the request; adjudication must arrive inside 336 hours; an export lives inside a 72-hour export window measured from the request's `decided_at`, so a grant that is never downloaded inside that window is walked back and can be issued again rather than being refused at issuance. A denied benefit claim may be resubmitted inside the network's own denial window. the strike revocation threshold that gates revoking a claimed waitlist seat is `STRIKE_REVOCATION_THRESHOLD`, which is 5, while the separate `STRIKE_OFFER_BLOCK_THRESHOLD` that keeps a repeatedly-absent client from receiving new offers is the lower of the two, at 3 — a client whose strike count has reached 3 is dropped from every offer candidate pool.

Every instantaneous instant in the system comes from the logical clock, which the server pins to 2026-09-01T00:00:00Z at boot and never reads from the host. The clock is moved only by the operations that declare a clock effect: the operator clock setter, which takes a `current_utc` and reports the new instant and the instant it advanced from; the renewal advance and the membership cycle advance with their `advance_days`; the session completion, which moves the clock forward to the session's `end_at` when that instant is still ahead; and the pack expiry sweep with its `advance_clock_days` offset. Setting the clock forward may make several system transitions due at once, and they fire in a fixed order rather than an arbitrary one: subscription dunning first, then the session start/completion walk, then membership expiry, then the portability windows, then the payroll close window.

Two test-driving capabilities exist for the harness and are reserved to the operator role. The first seeds a client's accumulated counters — supplying `client_id`, `no_show_strike_count` and `late_cancel_count` — writing them directly and bypassing the booking cascade so that a fixture can start a client exactly at a strike boundary. The second attaches an entitlement without walking the purchase path, taking a `client_id`, a `studio_id`, a `kind` that selects a membership or a pack (`membership`/`memberships`, or `pack`/`packs`/`class_pack`/`class-packs`; anything else is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409), a `credits` count to place on the created row, and an optional `expires_at` that defaults to 180 days ahead for a pack. A membership injected this way lands `ACTIVE` with the given credits as its per-cycle grant and a `next_billing_at` at the current instant; a pack lands `ACTIVE` with `pack_size` and `credits_remaining` both equal to the credits and an `expiry_scheme` of `NONE`. It answers with `kind`, `entity_id` and `credits`. Both are operator-only and neither is reachable by any studio or consumer role.

One more boundary deserves stating plainly, because it is easy to get wrong: a client's `no_show_strike_count` is reduced only by an explicit restoration or by the operator's counter injector — no other operation resets it — and a client's `late_cancel_count` is never reduced by anything at all.
