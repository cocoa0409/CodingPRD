# Personal Sales Engineer Support API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Sweetwater's competitive advantage has never been the gear on the shelf; it is the sales engineer who remembers which amplifier a customer already owns, which pedalboard the new guitar has to sit on, and which pickup swap will finally make the room sound right. Today that relationship lives in inboxes, sticky notes, and the memory of whoever answered the phone. This document specifies the Personal Sales Engineer Support API, the service that turns that relationship into a durable record: a customer-to-sales-engineer assignment that persists for the life of the account, carries commission attribution through every quote the engineer authors, and survives reassignment, temporary coverage, and account suspension without losing its history.

The platform also has to survive the less glamorous half of a musician's retail life. New gear arrives needing a bench setup and a documented inspection before it leaves the building. A discontinued cable is backordered for six weeks while three engineers' customers wait on the same shipment. An extended warranty is offered free, then quietly accumulates repair rounds against a finite allowance. A customer who bought a tube amp in a neighbouring state needs a service center that is authorized to touch that product family. And the person on the phone asking why the amp hums is entitled to something — but only because of what they have already bought, and only for as long as the relationship holds. Backorders and preorders must be allocated across engineers' books without over-committing a single received unit, tone-sensitive returns must be trialled, restocked, and refunded under a credit balance that never drifts, and engineers themselves must be certified in the families they sell before they are allowed to sell them.

Architecturally the service is a flat collection of fifteen resource types. Two of them — `Engineer` and `Customer` — are the people on either side of the relationship. `Assignment` is the durable link between them. `Quote`, `QuoteLine`, and `Product` describe what the engineer is proposing to sell. `InstrumentUnit` documents the serialized instrument that actually ships. `WarrantyPlan`, `WarrantyClaim`, and `ServiceCenter` administer post-sale repair, while `SupportTicket` and `EscalationLedger` handle the conversations that are not repair at all. `Backorder`, `GearExchange`, and `CertificationAttempt` round out the operational surface: allocation, tone-trial returns, and engineer product knowledge. Every path below is mounted at the service root; the base URL carries no additional prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every authenticated request carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, which identifies the caller, and `X-User-Role`, which names the role the caller claims. Both are mandatory. A request missing either header — or carrying one that is empty or malformed — is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401 before any resource is touched. A request that presents a perfectly well-formed pair of headers but claims a role the target operation does not admit is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403. A health probe at `GET /health`, together with self-service account and catalogue creation, is exempt from the header requirement entirely — the probe answers in the standard envelope with its `data` carrying a status string and the service's current logical instant: `POST /products`, `POST /service-centers`, `POST /engineers`, and `POST /customers` do not require identity headers, and neither does the health probe. A caller that presents any identity header on one of those four creation routes is still checked against it rather than waved through: the pair must name a declared role the route admits — `POST /products` and `POST /service-centers` admit `PlatformAdmin`, `POST /engineers` admits `PlatformAdmin` and `SalesEngineerOps`, and `POST /customers` admits `Anonymous` and `SalesEngineerOps` — so a caller supplying only one of the two headers, or a malformed pair, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and a declared-but-inadmissible role with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

The service recognizes seven roles, spelled exactly as follows: `PlatformAdmin`, `SalesEngineerOps`, `Engineer`, `SetupTechnician`, `SupportAgent`, `ServiceDepartment`, and `Anonymous`.

Five of them are operators rather than persisted people, and the header is not compared against anything the service stores. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `SalesEngineerOps`, `SetupTechnician`, `SupportAgent`, and `ServiceDepartment`: in all five cases the identifier is opaque, untethered from the persisted graph. The service may still record it — for example as `escalated_by` on a `SupportTicket`, or as `setup_technician_id` on an `InstrumentUnit` — but it never resolves it to a row. A sixth role, `Anonymous`, is likewise opaque and is the only role that may create a customer account unaccompanied by a credential; it represents a first-contact caller who has not been authenticated to anything.

The seventh role is the only one backed by a persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `Engineer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Engineer`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Engineer` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. This is what makes assignment acceptance and decline personal: the acting `Engineer` may accept or decline only an `Assignment` whose `engineer_id` is their own, may amend or reopen only a `Quote` whose `engineer_id` is their own, and may place or cancel only those `Backorder` rows whose `engineer_id` is their own. The comparison is exact: an `Engineer` caller whose `X-User-ID` does not equal the resource's `engineer_id` is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` at HTTP 403.

Role authority is deliberately narrow at the edges. `PlatformAdmin` is the only role that may advance the logical clock, register catalogue products and service centers, void a warranty plan, suspend or terminally close a customer account, or expire a certification attempt. `SalesEngineerOps` owns the assignment flow it shares with `PlatformAdmin`, and holds exclusive control of quotation rejection, warranty plan registration and activation, every step of the backorder allocation sweep, and the entire certification administration surface. `Engineer` exclusively owns the personal actions — accepting, declining, amending, reopening — while sharing quote authoring, line authoring, submission, reservation, cancellation, and backorder claiming with `SalesEngineerOps`. `SetupTechnician` exclusively owns the four bench operations and shares instrument registration and shipment. `SupportAgent` exclusively owns warranty claim handling, ticket triage and escalation, and the entire gear-exchange case lifecycle. `ServiceDepartment` exclusively owns ticket resolution and de-escalation, and shares nothing else but reads.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts `limit` and `offset` as optional query parameters. When `limit` is omitted it defaults to 10; when `offset` is omitted it defaults to 0. `limit` must be an integer of at least 1 and no greater than 100 — a request below the minimum or above the maximum is out of contract and must not be silently clamped. `offset` is a zero-based count of records to skip and must not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer at all, a `limit` below the minimum or above the maximum, and a negative `offset` are each refused with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE` at HTTP 400 rather than being clamped, rounded, or ignored. The `meta` object of a list response carries all four pagination fields: `limit`, `offset`, the total number of records matching the query before pagination as `total_count`, and a boolean `has_more` that is true exactly when more records remain beyond the returned window. `response_type` is `"array"` for these responses and the `data` field holds the page of resource objects.

Lists are ordered by `created_at` ascending. This is the default and the only ordering a caller may rely on for list endpoints; the ordering is stable across pages so that a caller walking `offset` forward observes each record exactly once. Filtering query parameters narrow the candidate set before pagination is applied, and `total_count` reflects the narrowed set rather than the unfiltered table. Every filter an endpoint declares is applied before the ordering, and the ordering is applied to the whole filtered set before the page is cut, so a page is a contiguous window of that one ordered set rather than a separately ordered sample; records that tie on `created_at` are broken by a second key so that the order is total and reproducible across pages.

### 2.4. The Customer Book: Exclusivity, Coverage, and Book Capacity

At the centre of the domain is the idea that a customer has one engineer. `Assignment` rows record who holds a book, why they came to hold it, and for how long; the `status` of an assignment moves through `PENDING_ACCEPT`, `ACTIVE`, and `ENDED`. Book exclusivity is the invariant that makes the relationship meaningful: at most one `Assignment` for a given customer may be `ACTIVE` at a time. An attempt to activate a second concurrent holder is refused. `Customer` mirrors the outcome of that rule in `assigned_engineer_id`, which holds the `engineer_id` of the customer's `ACTIVE` assignment and is null when no assignment is `ACTIVE`. The mirror is written by the platform, never supplied by a client, and is the field downstream features read when they need to know who owns an account. The four assignment routes that move the book — creation, acceptance, ending and reopening — advance the Customer's `version` by one as part of that write, which is what makes the version guard of §2.5 a reliable detector of a racing book write. The customer-lifecycle routes that empty a book wholesale — activate, suspend and close — refresh the customer's `updated_at` but leave `version` where it stood, and a decline likewise touches only the assignment row.

Two constraints guard the engineer's side of the relationship. Every engineer carries a `max_book_size`, the ceiling on how many book rows they may hold, and a derived `active_book_size` that counts their `Assignment` rows currently in `ACTIVE` status. A newly registered engineer starts with `max_book_size` defaulting to 120 when none is supplied, an `active_book_size` of zero, an empty certification record, the `TIER_1` tier and its 5000-basis-point cap, a `lifetime_commission_cents` of zero, and `ACTIVE` status. Accepting a pending assignment or reopening an ended one is admitted only while `active_book_size` is still strictly below `max_book_size`; a transition that would reach the ceiling or pass it is rejected with `ERR_BOOK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` at HTTP 422.

The other guard is temporal. Each engineer row also records a `region` drawn from `MIDWEST`, `SOUTH`, `WEST`, or `EAST`, and a `status` drawn from `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, or `OFFBOARDED`; only an `ACTIVE` engineer may take on new book rows. An assignment's coverage window is the half-open interval that begins at the instant the assignment became `ACTIVE` and ends at the instant it was ended, tracked as `assigned_at` and the instant the row left `ACTIVE`. A requested coverage window may not overlap the half-open interval of any already-`ENDED` `Assignment` for the same customer. The guard is evaluated both when an `Assignment` is created and when an `ENDED` one is reopened: the proposed window begins at the logical clock at that moment — creation is itself a write against the book, so the window it introduces opens now, before the row has been accepted — and it is measured against the half-open intervals `[assigned_at, ended-at)` of the already-`ENDED` rows on the same book. Adjacency is permitted: an interval that ends at exactly the instant the next one begins is not an overlap, so the comparison is strict and a proposed window opening exactly at a prior window's `assigned_at` still overlaps it while one opening exactly at a prior window's ended instant does not. Reopening measures the re-proposed window the same way, excluding the row being reopened so that it does not match itself. A genuine overlap surfaces as `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_COVERAGE_OVERLAP` at HTTP 409. The `sequence` field itself is a monotone reassignment index within the customer's book, starting at 1 for the first assignment an account ever receives and incrementing each time the book changes hands. Creation reserves the next index — one more than the highest presently on the book, ignoring the row being created — while acceptance re-derives the index afresh from the book as it then stands, adopting that value on the accepted row and moving every other row of the book that holds that index or a higher one to one past it, so an account's very first accepted assignment carries sequence 1.

Assignments are also classified by `reason_code`, one of `NEW_ACCOUNT`, `PERMANENT_TRANSFER`, `REGION_CHANGE`, or `TEMPORARY_COVERAGE`. Temporary coverage is a different animal from the other three: it is the only reason that must be bounded in time. An assignment whose `reason_code` is `TEMPORARY_COVERAGE` must carry `effective_until`, the instant at which the coverage lapses; an assignment with any of the other three reasons must not carry one. Either violation — a temporary coverage with no end instant, or a permanent reason carrying a stray one — is rejected with `ERR_TEMPORARY_COVERAGE_UNTIL_REQUIRED` at HTTP 422. `assigned_at` is never accepted from a client; it is stamped from the service's logical clock at the moment the row first transitions into `ACTIVE`.

The commission split is agreed at assignment time. `commission_share_bps` is the requested engineer share of a quote's commission pool, expressed in basis points and defaulting to 5000. It is bounded above by the engineer's current `commission_cap_bps`: a share equal to the cap is admitted, and a share one basis point above the cap is rejected with `ERR_COMMISSION_CAP_EXCEEDED` at HTTP 422. The cap is not a static constant — it moves with the engineer's certification record, and §3 describes how. Of the assignment routes, only creation carries a body and only creation and reopen carry a version field: accept and decline take no body at all, `expected_customer_version` is mandatory on creation, and on reopen it is optional and is compared only when the caller chooses to supply it.

### 2.5. Optimistic Concurrency on the Book-Writing Operations

Two write operations in this service are guarded by an optimistic concurrency check rather than by a lock. Both guards take the same shape: the caller must present, as a non-optional request field, the version number it last observed for a parent resource, and the write is refused if the parent has moved on since.

Reserving a quote is the first. A reservation request against a `Quote` must carry `expected_quote_version` in its body. The value is compared against the quote's current `version`. If the two disagree — which is what happens when a legitimate amend has already returned the quote to `SUBMITTED` and bumped its `version`, leaving the current caller's view stale — the reservation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412. Note that `expected_quote_version` is mandatory here even though the amend operation accepts an optional `expected_quote_version` of its own as a defensive extra; the reservation is the operation where the field is non-optional and the mismatch is an error.

Creating an assignment is the second. A request to create an `Assignment` must carry `expected_customer_version` in its body, non-optionally, and it must match the target `Customer`'s current `version`. A caller holding a stale view of the customer's book — typically because another writer has already ended the customer's prior `ACTIVE` assignment, which updates `assigned_engineer_id` and advances `version` — is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412. The version comparison is not evaluated first on this route: the existence of the named customer and engineer, the shape of the body, the commission-cap ceiling, and the temporary-coverage pairing are all settled before `expected_customer_version` is compared, so a creation request that fails any of those is refused with that error rather than `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field. Adding quote lines, accepting or ending an assignment, submitting, cancelling, reopening, amending outside the reservation path, and every instrument, warranty, ticket, backorder, exchange, and certification operation are unguarded by this mechanism and must not require the caller to supply a version.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock and Half-Open Time Windows

The service does not read wall-clock time. It maintains a logical clock whose default instant before any configuration call is `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time once that clock has been set. A privileged administrative capability, restricted to `PlatformAdmin`, sets the clock by supplying `current_utc`; every timestamp the service stamps — `assigned_at`, `reserved_at`, `shipped_at`, `routed_at`, `closed_at`, `escalated_at`, `assessed_at`, `posted_at`, `trial_start_date` — is drawn from that clock rather than from the host. Deadlines are therefore deterministic and testable, and an operation that is "too late" is too late relative to the logical clock, not to the machine's clock.

Every deadline in this domain is half-open with respect to its own boundary, which is to say that landing exactly on the boundary is still in time. A preorder sweep must begin no later than thirty days after the instant the backorder's preorders first attached; at exactly that deadline the sweep is still admitted, and only past it does `ERR_PREORDER_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409) apply. A gear exchange may complete or be withdrawn for return no later than the instant its trial window closes; at exactly that instant the exchange is still honoured, and past it the operation fails with `ERR_TRIAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409). A warranty claim may be closed only while the logical clock is not past the plan's expiry, and a claim reopen carries the same ceiling. A warranty plan's coverage term is anchored at activation by a supplied `starts_on` instant which may not be later than the logical clock by more than zero days.

Two windows in the service are expressed as fixed offsets from an event rather than as absolute instants. A backorder's `eta_deadline` is its `preorder_open_at` plus thirty days, the last instant at which a preorder may still be honoured. A gear exchange's `trial_end_date` is its `trial_start_date` plus fourteen days. A warranty plan's `expires_on` is its `starts_on` plus its `term_months`; a certification attempt's `expires_at` is its `assessed_at` plus twelve months. `days_remaining` on a gear exchange is derived rather than stored: it is the number of whole days from the current logical clock to the trial's end, rounded up, and floored at zero so that an expired window reports no negative days. It is refreshed whenever the case moves — when the trial starts, when a return is requested, and when a return is denied — so the figure a caller reads always reflects the clock at the last write.

### 2.7. Money, Rates, and Rounding Discipline

All monetary values in this API are integer cents, and all rate parameters are integer basis points, where 10000 basis points represents the whole. Every rate multiplication that produces a cent value is rounded half-up to 2 decimal places, and although the result is mathematically a whole number of cents once rounded, the rule is stated in decimal places because it is applied identically to intermediate values. Rounding is never banker's rounding and is never truncation. Basis-point shares themselves are integers and are never floored per line or per round; a share is applied to the whole it belongs to and only the resulting cent value is rounded.

The rounding is applied at the points named in the domain rules, not indiscriminately. Quote line pricing rounds once per unit before the units are multiplied, so a line's total is the quantity times an already-rounded per-unit price rather than a rounding of quantity times an unrounded price. Package pricing rounds the per-unit line price, sums, selects a tier, and then rounds once more when the tier discount is applied. Restocking fees are rounded once against the line total. Service fees are rounded once against the constant. Where more than one rounding could be imagined, §3 names the step order explicitly, and that order is normative.

### 2.8. Derived Fields, Cascades, and Reconciliation

A large share of the state in this system is derived rather than stored as an independent fact, and the service is responsible for keeping the derived copy reconciled with the rows it summarizes. `active_book_size` counts an engineer's `ACTIVE` assignments. `certified_family_count` counts the distinct product families among an engineer's certification attempts that are currently certified. `purchased_instrument_count` on a customer counts the sold lines tied to that customer's quotes — a line being sold once a serialized instrument registered against it has shipped, so the count is over shipped units rather than over quote status. `open_ticket_count` on a service center counts the open tickets currently consuming its capacity, whether they are warranty claims routed to it or tickets escalated to it. `repair_rounds_used` and `remaining_repair_rounds` on a plan count and bound its completed claims. `escalation_count` on a ticket counts the escalation ledger rows appended against it — both the escalations and the withdrawals, since the ledger is append-only. `gross_subtotal_cents`, `dependency_closure_size`, and `bundle_ready` on a quote summarize its lines. `lifetime_commission_cents` summarizes finalized commission across an engineer's shipped quotes. None of these is client-settable; each is recomputed by the operation that would change its inputs.

Derived state propagates. When one operation changes a fact that another resource summarizes, the summarizer is updated in the same mutation rather than being left stale for a later reader to fix. The deepest chain in this domain runs from ending an assignment: it clears the customer's assigned engineer, which can drop the customer's support entitlement to nothing, which can escalate every open extended-entitlement ticket that customer holds, which in turn consumes a slot at each ticket's named service center. Each hop of that cascade recomputes the summarizer it moves, so ending a book re-derives the customer's entitlement, escalates the affected tickets, appends their ledger rows, and adjusts each desk's `open_ticket_count` in the one mutation. A second chain runs the other way, from a quote's reservation creating a backorder per line, through allocation and receiving, to shipment, which finalizes the engineer's commission and updates both the customer's purchase count and the engineer's lifetime commission. A third chain runs from a warranty claim closing: it consumes one of the plan's repair rounds, recomputes the remaining allowance, and returns the plan either to service or to expiry, releasing the service center slot it had been holding. §3 traces each chain in the workflow that owns it.

Common derived fields have fixed shapes. Every entity in this API declares both `updated_at` and `version`: `updated_at` is refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version` is an integer that starts at 1 and is incremented by each mutation that the domain rules assign to it. There are no entities in this service that carry only `id` and `created_at`; the universal shape applies throughout. A service center's `open_ticket_count` is a good illustration of why the reconciliation matters: it is what the capacity check reads, so a slot that is not released and a slot that is double-released are both correctness failures, not merely cosmetic drift.

### 2.9. Catalogue Compatibility and the Dependency Closure

A quote in this domain is not a shopping basket; it is a proposed signal chain. `Product` rows describe the catalogue: each carries a `product_code`, a `display_name`, a `product_family` from `GUITAR`, `AMPLIFIER`, `INTERFACE`, `MICROPHONE`, `EFFECT_PEDAL`, or `ACCESSORY`, a `unit_price_cents`, an optional `required_family` naming a family that must also be present for the product to be usable, a `requires_certification` flag, and an `in_catalog` flag that is false for discontinued items. Catalogue codes are unique, prices are non-negative integers, and a product that is not in the catalogue cannot be newly quoted.

Compatibility is computed rather than asserted by the client. A quote line may declare that it depends on another line in the same quote, and the quote as a whole must present exactly one anchor line from which the dependency graph is explored. The set of lines reachable from that anchor is the quote's dependency closure, and its size is reported as `dependency_closure_size`. A line that depends on nothing and that nothing depends on is, by construction, outside the closure and does not contribute to the size even though it is quoted. The quote is `bundle_ready` only when two conditions hold together: every dependency declared by a line in the closure resolves to a line of the same quote, and every product in the closure whose `required_family` is set finds at least one other member of the closure in that family. A dependency that points outside the quote leaves the quote unready; a dependency graph that doubles back on itself is refused outright rather than being reported with a size. Because the closure is explored from the anchor, a line that lies on a cycle nowhere reachable from the anchor is not itself an error at authoring time — the cycle check examines the whole quote's edges rather than only the closure — and the closure size simply reports the reachable set.

Certification gates the sale. When a product's `requires_certification` flag is true, the engineer authoring the line must hold a current certification in that product's `product_family`. Certifications are earned through graded assessments, they carry a twelve-month validity, and a lapsed certification stops counting — the service excludes any attempt whose `expires_at` is at or before the logical clock from the count that determines the engineer's tier.

### 2.10. Resource Identity, Timestamps, and Nested Path Resolution

Every resource in this API is identified by an `id` that is a server-minted UUID rendered as a bare identifier with no type prefix, no entity name embedded in it, and no decorating characters. Clients never mint identifiers and never send one in a creation body. Alongside the identifier, each resource carries the universal pair described above — `created_at`, set once at creation and never changed, and `updated_at`, refreshed by every mutation — plus the integer `version`. All timestamps in request and response bodies are ISO 8601 UTC instants rendered with three fractional digits. Creation of a new resource answers with HTTP 201 unless §4 says otherwise for a particular route; modification of an existing resource, including every state transition, answers with HTTP 200.

Where an endpoint names a parent in its path and a child after it, the parent segment is validated before the child is resolved, left to right. A path that names a customer that does not exist fails on the customer before the service ever considers the child; a path that names a quote that does not exist fails on the quote. Each resource type has its own not-found code so that a caller learns which segment broke: an unknown `Customer` yields `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown `Engineer` yields `ERR_ENGINEER_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown `Quote` yields `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown `InstrumentUnit` yields `ERR_INSTRUMENT_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown `WarrantyPlan` yields `ERR_WARRANTY_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown `Backorder` yields `ERR_BACKORDER_NOT_FOUND`, and an unknown `GearExchange` yields `ERR_GEAR_EXCHANGE_NOT_FOUND`. All of these carry HTTP 404. The remaining resource types do not mint a code of their own for an unresolved path segment: an unknown `QuoteLine`, `WarrantyClaim`, `SupportTicket`, `ServiceCenter`, `CertificationAttempt`, or `EscalationLedger` named in a path resolves to `ERR_REFERENCED_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` at HTTP 404, and an unknown `Assignment` is reported the same way the surface already reports its two neighbors — as `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND` on the assignment reads and as `ERR_ENGINEER_NOT_FOUND` on its accept, decline, end and reopen transitions. The same discipline applies to resources named in a request body rather than in the path: a `product_code` that resolves to no product, a `service_center_id` that names no center, or a `requires_line_id` that names no line of the same quote is rejected with `ERR_REFERENCED_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` at HTTP 404 before any state check runs.

### 2.11. Validation Precedence

When several of the checks in this document could apply to one request, they are evaluated in a fixed order and the first failure wins. That ordering is part of the contract, because a caller probing an endpoint must be able to predict which code comes back.

Authentication comes first: missing or malformed identity headers produce `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401) before anything else is examined, and a well-formed caller whose role the endpoint does not admit produces `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403) next. Only then is existence examined — path-segment resources left to right, then resources named in the body — so a 404 is always decided before a state or business check. Structural body problems are the exception that proves the rule: a required body field that is simply absent raises `ERR_MISSING_REQUIRED_FIELD` at HTTP 400 rather than a state error, and a field whose value falls outside its declared enum or numeric range raises `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE` at HTTP 400. Both are shape validations on the request itself, evaluated alongside header validation in the earliest group rather than deferred.

Once identity, existence, and request shape have passed, the state checks run. A resource that is not in a state the requested transition admits fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409, and a resource already sitting in a terminal state — one of `SHIPPED`, `CANCELLED`, `EXCHANGED`, `DENIED`, `RESOLVED`, `VOIDED`, or `CLOSED` — fails with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, also 409. Only after the resource is confirmed to be in an admissible state do the business rules of the domain apply: the safety, referential, fiscal, and temporal checks described throughout §2 and §3, each of which names its own error code. Among the business rules, the ordering that matters most is called out where it arises — for example, checking a service center's suspension before its capacity, and checking a warranty plan's remaining repair rounds before admitting a new claim. Where a general rule and a specific rule could both describe the same request, the specific one is evaluated first: a commission share above the engineer's current cap is reported as `ERR_COMMISSION_CAP_EXCEEDED` rather than as a malformed field, and the temporary-coverage pairing is settled before the customer's version is compared, so a creation request that breaks both reports the coverage pairing. Every business rule is evaluated before the write it guards, so a refused transition leaves the resource, its parents, and every derived counter exactly as they were.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Building the Book: How a Customer Comes to Have an Engineer

A customer's relationship with the company begins anonymously. A self-registration creates a `Customer` in `PENDING` status, carrying a `full_name`, an `email` that must be unique among customers, a two-letter `home_state`, and an `account_tier` drawn from `STANDARD`, `PREMIUM`, or `STUDIO` and defaulting to `STANDARD`. The new account begins with `assigned_engineer_id` null, `purchased_instrument_count` of zero, `lifetime_gear_credit_cents` of zero, and `tech_support_entitlement` of `NONE`. Registering a second `Customer` whose `email` is already in use is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE` at HTTP 400. Registration does not create a relationship and does not assign anybody: the customer sits unowned until an operations desk activates the account, and activation alone still leaves the customer unowned, because a `Customer`'s `assigned_engineer_id` stays null until an assignment actually becomes `ACTIVE`.

Ownership arrives through an `Assignment`, created by an operations desk in `PENDING_ACCEPT` status and naming a `customer_id`, an `engineer_id`, a `reason_code`, an optional `commission_share_bps`, an optional `effective_until`, and a mandatory `expected_customer_version`. Creation is itself a write against the customer's book, so the version guard of §2.5 applies at this moment rather than at acceptance: a caller whose view of the customer predates someone else's change to that customer's assignment history is turned away with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` before the assignment row exists at all. Creation also reserves the customer's next `sequence` value — one more than the highest `sequence` presently in that customer's book — and recomputes the target engineer's `active_book_size`.

A `PENDING_ACCEPT` assignment is a proposal, and the engineer decides. Accepting it is the engineer's own action: the acting `Engineer` must be the holder named by the assignment's `engineer_id`. Acceptance succeeds only when the engineer is in `ACTIVE` status, when their `active_book_size` is still strictly below their `max_book_size`, and when no other assignment for the same customer is already `ACTIVE` — the book-exclusivity invariant of §2.4. The engineer's status and the book's exclusivity are settled before the capacity ceiling is measured, so a request that both names an engineer who may not take on book rows and would overflow the book reports the state failure rather than `ERR_BOOK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Neither acceptance nor decline carries a request body, and each answers with the updated assignment. Acceptance stamps `assigned_at` from the logical clock, advances the customer's `sequence` bookkeeping by assigning the next index, increments the holding engineer's `active_book_size`, and mirrors the holder into the customer's `assigned_engineer_id`. The engineer's book capacity check is the last thing that can stop a customer from being taken on, and it produces `ERR_BOOK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` at HTTP 422; a request that would land exactly on `max_book_size` is admitted, and only one that would reach the ceiling from above is refused.

Declining is the other exit from `PENDING_ACCEPT`, and it is quieter. A declined assignment moves to `ENDED`, and nothing else changes — the customer's `assigned_engineer_id` is left exactly as it was, still null, because there was never anything to clear. This asymmetry is deliberate: a proposal that was never accepted must not perturb the account.

Ending an accepted assignment is an operations decision rather than an engineer's, and it is the most consequential write in the domain. When an `ACTIVE` assignment is ended, the customer's `assigned_engineer_id` is cleared to null, the holding engineer's `active_book_size` drops by one, and a four-hop cascade unwinds the customer's post-sale support. The first hop recomputes `tech_support_entitlement` on the customer. The second hop acts on the outcome: when no warranty is in force and the customer's purchase history can no longer support a basic entitlement, the entitlement drops to `NONE`. The third hop escalates every one of that customer's open support tickets that is in `OPEN` or `IN_TRIAGE` status and whose `required_entitlement` is `EXTENDED`, moving each to `ESCALATED` and stamping its `escalated_at` from the logical clock and its `escalated_by` from the acting operator's identifier, assigning the ticket's `escalation_seq` and appending the matching escalation ledger row exactly as an operator-triggered escalation would. The fourth hop increments `open_ticket_count` on each such ticket's named service center, because an escalated ticket now occupies a desk slot. Ending touches the customer's `updated_at` and `version` as well, which is exactly why an assignment creation that raced against it is caught by the version guard.

Reopening an ended assignment is available to an operations desk or an administrator, and it puts a customer's book back under an engineer after a gap. It requires the assignment to be `ENDED`, the engineer to be `ACTIVE`, the engineer to have room under `max_book_size`, and — the temporal rule of §2.4 — the re-proposed half-open interval to be disjoint from every later-`sequence` ended interval in the same customer's history, with exact adjacency permitted. The engineer's status and the book-exclusivity invariant are settled before the coverage rule is measured, and the coverage rule is measured before the capacity ceiling, so a reopen that breaks several of the preconditions reports the earliest one in that order. Reopening restamps `assigned_at` from the logical clock, increments the assignment's `sequence` index by one, sets `assigned_engineer_id` again, and re-increments the engineer's `active_book_size`. A taken-over interval fails with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_COVERAGE_OVERLAP` at HTTP 409.

Account-level status changes act on the book wholesale. Activating a `PENDING` customer makes it `ACTIVE` and leaves its assignment mirror alone. Suspending an `ACTIVE` customer returns it to `PENDING` and ends every assignment on that book that is in either `PENDING_ACCEPT` or `ACTIVE`, decrementing `active_book_size` by one for each affected engineer and clearing `assigned_engineer_id`. Closing an account — permitted from either `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` — moves it terminally to `CLOSED` and empties the book the same way. A customer that is already `CLOSED` is terminal, so an attempt to suspend or close it is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` at HTTP 409 rather than `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and suspending a customer that is still `PENDING` fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` because `PENDING` is not a state suspension is declared from. Both are administrator-only operations, and both leave the engineer's book strictly smaller than they found it.

### 3.2. Consultative Quoting: Assembling a Compatible Signal Chain

An engineer earns commission by authoring a quote, not by selling a line item, and the two central objects reflect that split: a `Quote` is the proposal, and its `QuoteLine` rows are the gear. A quote opens in `DRAFT` status against a `customer_id` and an `engineer_id`, and declares a `package_type` of `SYSTEM_PACKAGE`, `INSTRUMENT_ONLY`, or `ACCESSORY_ONLY`. Those three fields and nothing else are required to open one. That single field controls the discount economics for everything that follows, and the package price calculation in §3.3 reads it directly. The operation records `engineer_id` exactly as supplied and does not require the named engineer to be the one holding the customer's book at that moment, because that agreement is asserted at submission rather than at authoring. Opening a quote immediately computes `bundle_ready` and `dependency_closure_size`, both of which are false and zero until lines arrive.

Adding a line is where compatibility is actually established. A line names a `product_code`, an optional `quantity` defaulting to 1 and required to be at least 1, an optional `is_anchor` flag defaulting to false, and an optional `requires_line_id` naming a prerequisite line in the same quote. The service resolves the `product_id` and copies `unit_price_cents` from the catalogue at creation time, derives `product_family` from the product, and assigns `bundle_discount_bps` — the per-line discount that a system package enjoys. Because prices are pinned at line creation rather than at submission, a catalogue price change does not retroactively reprice an existing quote. A line may be added only while the quote is `DRAFT`, a `requires_line_id` on another quote's line is not a valid prerequisite and is refused as an unresolvable reference, and a product that is flagged as requiring certification may not be lined by an engineer who holds no current certification in its family — that last check refuses with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409 rather than a business code of its own. Each line records the family its product belongs to alongside the product reference, so the closure test of §3.2 reads the line rather than resolving the catalogue afresh.

The dependency closure is computed, not stored by the client. Exactly one line in the quote is the anchor, identified by `is_anchor` being true, and the anchor is mandatory: a quote with no anchor line has nothing to explore from, so its closure is empty, its `dependency_closure_size` is zero, and it can never be `bundle_ready`. From that anchor the closure is the set of lines reachable by following `requires_line_id` edges outward, including the anchor itself. Its size is reported as `dependency_closure_size`. Lines that neither declare a prerequisite nor are required by anything else fall outside the closure and do not contribute to its size even though they appear on the quote. A dependency edge that names a line belonging to a different quote is not a closure member at all — it makes the quote unready. A dependency graph that loops back on itself cannot be walked and is rejected outright with `ERR_DEPENDENCY_CYCLE` at HTTP 422 rather than being reported with a size. A dangling edge, by contrast, is not a structural error but a completeness failure: it leaves `bundle_ready` false and surfaces at submission time as `ERR_DEPENDENCY_MISSING` at HTTP 422.

`bundle_ready` is the conjunction of two conditions over that closure. Every line in the closure must either declare no prerequisite or declare one that resolves to a line of the same quote; and every product in the closure whose `required_family` is set must find at least one other member of the closure occupying that family. An amp that requires a guitar is only ready when a guitar is in the closure with it; a cable with no requirements is ready on its own. The service recomputes `dependency_closure_size`, `bundle_ready`, and the quote's `gross_subtotal_cents` every time a line is added and again at submission.

Submission is the engineer's assertion that the chain is right and the commercial terms are acceptable. It requires the quote to be in `DRAFT`, to be `bundle_ready`, and — the referential rule that ties quoting back to assignment — the customer's assignment in that customer's book to be `ACTIVE` and held by the very engineer named as the quote's author. An unassigned account, or one that has been reassigned to a different engineer, cannot have a quote submitted or reserved against it, whether the acting role is the engineer or the operations desk: both are refused with `ERR_CUSTOMER_ASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED` at HTTP 422. Submission moves the quote to `SUBMITTED`, recomputes the package price described in §3.3, and stores the engineer's commission in its provisional form.

A submitted quote is no longer the engineer's to change. An operations desk may reject it, returning it to `REJECTED` and recomputing `bundle_ready` to false so that the engineer must re-establish readiness before resubmitting. The engineer may reopen a rejected quote, which returns it to `DRAFT` and recomputes `bundle_ready` from the lines as they stand. Cancellation is available to both the authoring engineer and the operations desk, but only from `DRAFT` or `REJECTED` — a quote that has been reserved has to be unwound through the amendment path described in §3.3 instead. Cancellation advances the quote's `version` by one and is terminal.

### 3.3. Reserving Gear, Amending, and the Economics of a Package Price

Reserving is the point at which a proposal becomes a commitment to deliver. A reservation requires the quote to be `SUBMITTED`, to still be `bundle_ready`, to have an `ACTIVE` assignment on the customer's book, and to have no backorder on any of its lines already sitting in `BOARD_FILLED` or `SHIPPED` status. It also carries the mandatory `expected_quote_version` guard described in §2.5: a caller whose view of the quote predates a legitimate amendment is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412 rather than being allowed to reserve terms that have since changed. On this route the assignment rule is evaluated before the version comparison, so a reservation against an unowned or reassigned account reports `ERR_CUSTOMER_ASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED` even when the caller's version view is also stale.

A successful reservation stamps `reserved_at` from the logical clock, advances the quote's `version` by one, moves the quote to `RESERVED`, and — the cross-entity effect that drives the whole fulfilment side of the domain — creates exactly one `Backorder` per `QuoteLine` on that quote, each in `PREORDER_PENDING` status with an `alloc_sequence` one greater than the highest already present. A backorder the reservation creates is stamped `preorder_open_at` from the logical clock, given a `variant_bps` of 6000, a `units_requested` equal to the line's `quantity`, an `units_filled` and `overcommit_estimate_units` of zero, an `engineer_id` copied from the quote, and an `eta_deadline` computed as §2.6 describes. The quote's `bundle_ready` is recomputed as part of the same mutation. From this point the quote's fate is coupled to its backorders: allocation outcomes in §3.5 feed back into the quote's status. The owning customer's `purchased_instrument_count` is recomputed as part of the same mutation whenever a line is added to one of their quotes or one of their quotes is reserved, as well as at shipment, so the derived count is re-derived at each write against the quote rather than carried forward between them.

Amendment unwinds a reservation that has not yet been fulfilled. It is available to the authoring engineer, requires the quote to be `RESERVED`, and requires that no backorder on any of its lines has reached `BOARD_FILLED`, `SHIPPED`, or `RECEIVING` — in other words, nothing may already be on its way. A reservation is refused outright while any backorder on the quote's lines is already `BOARD_FILLED` or `SHIPPED`, but a backorder merely in `RECEIVING` does not block it. Amendment clears `reserved_at` to null, advances `version` by one, returns the quote to `SUBMITTED`, and cancels the backorders that the prior reservation created to the extent they are still sitting in `ALLOCATION_SWEEP`. Because amendment advances the version, it is precisely the event that makes a later reservation attempt stale, and §2.5's rejection rule exists to catch that case. An amendment request may optionally carry an `expected_quote_version` of its own as a defensive check, but unlike the reservation the field is not mandatory there.

Two computed prices sit on the quote and are refreshed by these transitions. The first is `gross_subtotal_cents`, the plain sum of every line's `line_total_cents` after per-line discounts. The second is the package price, produced by the package-bundle-price calculation, which is recomputed at submission.

The package price has a pinned evaluation order that must be honoured step for step, because the tier is keyed on a subtotal that the line discounts have already reduced — reordering the steps changes which tier a quote lands in, and therefore changes the price. First, the per-unit price for a line is the catalogue `unit_price_cents` reduced by the system line discount of 6667 basis points and rounded half-up to 2 decimal places; this first step applies only to quotes whose `package_type` is `SYSTEM_PACKAGE`, while `INSTRUMENT_ONLY` and `ACCESSORY_ONLY` quotes use the raw catalogue price unconverted. Second, those per-unit prices are multiplied by their line `quantity` values and summed into a subtotal. Third, a tier rate is chosen from that already-discounted subtotal: a tier of 1000 basis points applies when the subtotal is at least 500000 cents, a tier of 500 basis points applies when the subtotal is at least 200000 cents but below that higher threshold, and no tier applies below 200000 cents. Fourth, the chosen tier rate is applied once to the subtotal and the result is rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. A package whose raw prices would have crossed the 200000-cent threshold but whose discounted subtotal falls below it therefore stays in the lower tier — that is the intended consequence of the pinned order, not an artefact.

Commission is computed in two forms against the same formula, and both are stored on the engineer's behalf at different moments. Neither figure is a field of the `Quote` resource itself: the provisional value is recorded when the quote is submitted and is replaced by the final value once every line of the quote has shipped, and the pair is surfaced through the quote's summary read — which names which variant is currently stored — rather than as part of the quote body. The pool is 800 basis points of the quote's `gross_subtotal_cents`, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. The engineer's share is the assignment's `commission_share_bps`, capped at the engineer's `commission_cap_bps` — the lower of the two wins. In the provisional variant, applied when the quote is submitted, the engineer's credit is the pool multiplied by that share in basis points and divided by 10000, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. In the final variant, applied once every line on the quote has a shipped instrument, the pool is prorated by the fraction of line value that actually shipped: the shipped line totals are summed, multiplied through the same pool and share, divided by `gross_subtotal_cents`, and rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. Because the provisional variant pays the full share before anything ships, it always exceeds the final variant whenever any line remains unshipped; when everything ships, the two agree. The finalized amount rolls into the engineer's `lifetime_commission_cents`, which sums finalized engineer commission across the quotes that engineer authored, limited to quotes that are `RESERVED` and fully shipped.

### 3.4. Bench Setup, Inspection, and the Per-Instrument Documentation Trail

Not every product ships as a sealed box. A serialized instrument — a guitar, most often — is registered as an `InstrumentUnit` against the `quote_line_id` that sold it, carrying a `serial_number` and a `model_code`. A serial number may back at most one instrument; registering a second unit against a serial already in use is refused with `ERR_SERIAL_ALREADY_REGISTERED` at HTTP 409. The unit begins life in `PENDING_SETUP` with no `setup_technician_id`, no measurements, `inspection_overall` at `PASS` as a placeholder, `modified_since_setup` false, and `shipped_at` null; registration itself does not require the owning quote to be reserved, and it is starting setup that does. It may only enter setup when the `QuoteLine` it was registered against belongs to a quote that is currently `RESERVED` — an instrument attached to a draft or amended quote has nothing to be set up for.

The bench workflow has four operator steps and they are strictly ordered. Starting setup is available only from `PENDING_SETUP`; it records the acting technician's identifier as `setup_technician_id`, seeds `inspection_overall` to `PASS` as a baseline, and clears `modified_since_setup` to false before any measurement exists. Completing setup is available only from `IN_SETUP` and is the step that requires the measurements: the request must supply `action_height_mm`, `relief_mm`, and `intonation_offset_cents`, which are stored on the unit, and the service then evaluates the preliminary inspection verdict and stores it on `inspection_overall`. Recording the inspection is available only from `SETUP_COMPLETE` and is the step that re-evaluates the verdict in its final form, storing that and clearing `modified_since_setup` to false.

The verdict is the fold of three independent measurement checks, and the two variants differ in exactly one respect. Each measurement is first rounded half-up to 2 decimal places, and only then compared — the rounding happens before the comparison, not after. The string action at the twelfth fret passes when the rounded value is at least 0.50 millimetres and at most 2.00 millimetres inclusive at both ends. Neck relief passes when the rounded value is at least 0.05 millimetres; there is no upper bound. Intonation passes when the absolute deviation is at most 5 cents. Those three pass-or-fail results are counted, and the preliminary variant used at setup completion assigns `PASS` when all three pass, `CONDITIONAL` when exactly two pass, and `FAIL` when one or none pass. The final variant used at recorded inspection differs only in the two-check case: three passes still yield `PASS`, but of the three possible two-of-three combinations, only the ones in which intonation passed yield `CONDITIONAL` — a two-of-three unit whose failing check is intonation is graded `FAIL`. This is the single place where the two variants diverge, and it means an instrument can read `CONDITIONAL` at the bench and `FAIL` after the inspection is recorded.

Shipping is gated on that documentation and on the quote behind it. A unit may ship only from `INSPECTED`, only when its `inspection_overall` is `PASS` or `CONDITIONAL`, and only when `modified_since_setup` is false — a `FAIL` verdict, or a measurement changed after inspection, blocks shipment with `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_PASSED` at HTTP 422. Shipping additionally requires the unit's quote to still be `RESERVED`, which is the instrument-side consequence of §3.3's amendment path: an instrument whose quote has been amended back to `SUBMITTED` is held at the bench and refused with `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_RESERVED` at HTTP 409. Both checks guard the same operation, and both name their own code so that the bench knows whether the problem is the instrument or the quote.

A successful shipment stamps `shipped_at` from the logical clock, marks the unit `SHIPPED`, recomputes the customer's `purchased_instrument_count` for the quote's customer, recomputes the engineer commission in its final variant against the quote, and rolls the result into the engineer's `lifetime_commission_cents`. Shipment is terminal — an action attempted against a `SHIPPED` unit raises `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` at HTTP 409. Rework is the escape hatch before that point: returning a unit to the bench is available from `SETUP_COMPLETE` or from `INSPECTED`, resets the unit to `IN_SETUP` and `inspection_overall` to `PASS`, and clears `modified_since_setup` to false so that the subsequent measurement pass starts clean. An attempt to restart the bench against a unit that has already shipped is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` at HTTP 409, and one against a unit that is still in `PENDING_SETUP` or `IN_SETUP` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.5. Backorder and Preorder Allocation Across Engineers' Books

When a line is reserved but the gear is not in stock, the reservation creates a preorder claim rather than a shipment. Each `Backorder` names the `quote_line_id` it fulfils, the `engineer_id` whose book owns the claim, a `units_requested` that must be at least one, a `variant_bps` defaulting to 6000 that is used by the provisional estimate, and a `preorder_open_at` instant marking when preorders first attached to the claim. Creation assigns the claim its `alloc_sequence` — one more than the highest already issued — and computes `eta_deadline` as `preorder_open_at` plus thirty days, which is the last instant at which the claim's preorder may still be honoured. A claim an operator places directly in this way exists alongside the ones a reservation creates automatically, and the two share the same single pool of sequence values.

A claim asking for zero units is refused rather than being quietly satisfied with nothing: `units_requested` of exactly 0 — or of anything below it, since a negative request is meaningless in the same way — defines an empty candidate set and raises `ERR_ZERO_ALLOCATION_REQUEST` at HTTP 422. This is the single-line guard that keeps an empty request from turning into an empty allocation further down the pipeline.

A claim enters the sweep from `PREORDER_PENDING` only while it is unfilled and still inside its preorder window — the logical clock must not be past `preorder_open_at` plus thirty days, and landing exactly on that deadline is still in time per §2.6. A sweep attempt past the window fails with `ERR_PREORDER_WINDOW_EXPIRED` at HTTP 409. The window is measured against the claim's own `eta_deadline` rather than recomputed from `preorder_open_at`, so the two never drift apart, and a sweep that starts a claim already in `PARTIALLY_FILLED` remeasures the same window and zeroes the claim's `units_filled` before it re-enters. Starting a sweep moves the claim to `ALLOCATION_SWEEP` and stores a provisional estimate in `overcommit_estimate_units`. A partially filled claim may also re-enter the sweep, which resets `units_filled` to zero and recomputes the provisional estimate afresh. Both the sweep and the finalization carry a mandatory `pool_units` input naming how many units have arrived into the shared pool for this backorder group, and a walk cannot be run without it. Either sweep path, and the finalization path described next, refuses a `pool_units` below zero with `ERR_ALLOCATION_POOL_NEGATIVE` at HTTP 422 before any waterfall walk begins, because a negative pool would make the arithmetic meaningless.

The allocation itself is a two-variant calculation over a shared pool, and the distinction between the variants is the whole point of the design. The eligible set is the backorders for the same product whose status is `ALLOCATION_SWEEP`, `PARTIALLY_FILLED`, or `BOARD_FILLED`; `CANCELLED` and `SHIPPED` rows are excluded from the walk entirely. The walk visits claims in a fixed order: ascending `alloc_sequence` first, then ascending `preorder_open_at`, then ascending row creation time when the first two tie. The provisional variant, stored in `overcommit_estimate_units` when the sweep starts, measures each claim against the full pool independently, giving each claim the smaller of its own requested units and the whole pool. Over-commitment of the provisional estimate is explicitly permitted — it is the designed behaviour of the estimate rather than a defect to be corrected — and because each claim is measured on its own, those estimates may sum to far more than the pool actually holds. This is the intended reading of `overcommit_estimate_units`, not an error. The final variant, stored in `units_filled` when the allocation is finalized, is a single pass that depletes the pool: it starts with the full pool as remaining capacity, grants each claim in walk order the smaller of its requested units and what remains, and subtracts the grant before moving on, so the pool is conserved exactly and no fraction of a unit is ever allocated. A consequence worth stating plainly: because the final walk stops once the pool is exhausted, later claims in `alloc_sequence` order receive strictly less than their own preliminary estimate whenever the pool cannot cover everyone.

Finalizing reads that outcome and routes the claim down one of two branches. When the final allocation grants more than zero but fewer than `units_requested`, the claim moves to `PARTIALLY_FILLED`, and this is the branch with consequences beyond the claim itself: the containing quote loses its full reservation, returning to `SUBMITTED` with `reserved_at` cleared, and every other backorder on the same quote that is still in `PREORDER_PENDING` or `ALLOCATION_SWEEP` is cancelled. When the final allocation grants at least `units_requested` and at least one unit was requested, the claim moves to `BOARD_FILLED` and the quote is left alone. Both branches recompute `overcommit_estimate_units` in its provisional form as part of the same mutation, and both store the final `units_filled`.

Downstream of allocation the path is linear and unremarkable. A `PARTIALLY_FILLED` or `BOARD_FILLED` claim may be released into `RECEIVING`, and a `RECEIVING` claim may be shipped, which is terminal. Cancellation is available to the operations desk and to the owning engineer from any of `PREORDER_PENDING`, `ALLOCATION_SWEEP`, `PARTIALLY_FILLED`, or `BOARD_FILLED`, and it too is terminal; the engineer's ownership check compares the claim's `engineer_id` to `X-User-ID`, as §2.1 describes. A `RECEIVING` or already-`SHIPPED` claim is past the point where cancellation is declared and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A swept allocation can also be abandoned without cancelling the claim: resetting the sweep returns the claim to `PREORDER_PENDING`, zeros `units_filled`, and recomputes the provisional estimate — measured against the largest pool this claim's group has been swept with so far rather than against nothing, so a reset preserves the group's allocation context instead of erasing it. `SHIPPED` and `CANCELLED` are the two terminal states for a claim, and both refuse further action with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

### 3.6. Free Extended Warranty: Coverage, Repair Rounds, and Service Routing

Every serialized instrument that ships is eligible for a free extended warranty. A `WarrantyPlan` is registered against the `customer_id` that owns it and the `instrument_unit_id` it covers, with a `term_months` drawn from exactly `12`, `24`, or `36` and defaulting to 24; a term outside that set is a shape violation and raises `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE` at HTTP 400. A plan begins in `PENDING` with `starts_on` and `expires_on` both null, `repair_rounds_used` of zero, and `remaining_repair_rounds` at the full allowance of three. It holds no coverage until it is activated, which the operations desk does by supplying `starts_on`. Activation is admitted only from `PENDING`, only when the covered instrument has actually reached `SHIPPED` status, and only when `starts_on` is not later than the logical clock by more than zero days — a plan cannot be backdated into a gap it never had. Either violation — a `starts_on` later than the logical clock, or a covered instrument that has not yet reached `SHIPPED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` at HTTP 409. Activation sets `expires_on` to `starts_on` plus `term_months` and recomputes `remaining_repair_rounds`; a term expressed in calendar months may not land beyond the twenty-eighth of a month when the anchor day is later than that, so `starts_on` on the thirty-first of January with a twelve-month term expires on the twenty-eighth of the following January.

The repair allowance is finite and is the plan's real constraint. `repair_rounds_used` counts the claims on the plan that have been closed, and `remaining_repair_rounds` is three minus that count, floored at zero so that it never goes negative. Opening a claim consumes nothing yet, but it requires a strictly positive remaining allowance: a plan whose `remaining_repair_rounds` is exactly zero admits no further claim, and the attempt fails with `ERR_REPAIR_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` at HTTP 422. That check is evaluated before the claim row is created.

Opening a claim is a support-desk action against an `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED` plan, and the two paths differ slightly. From `ACTIVE`, the claim is admitted when a positive repair allowance remains and the claim is opened no later than the plan's expiry. From `EXPIRED`, the claim is admitted only when fewer than three rounds have already been used, which is the narrow reopening the allowance permits. Either way the plan moves to `CLAIM_OPEN` and the new `WarrantyClaim` begins in `PENDING_ROUTING` with `routed_at` and `closed_at` null, carrying a `reported_fault` in the customer's own words and a `service_center_id` naming the intended repair desk. An `EXPIRED` plan may be reopened only in the narrow sense described — the allowance check is measured against `repair_rounds_used` directly, so an exhausted plan refuses the claim with the same `ERR_REPAIR_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` on either path.

Routing is where the post-sale logistics tighten. A claim in `PENDING_ROUTING` may be routed to its named center only if that center is `OPEN` — a suspended center refuses the route with `ERR_SERVICE_CENTER_SUSPENDED` at HTTP 409 — and only if the center's `open_ticket_count` has not yet reached its `capacity_slots`, because the center's capacity is a count of concurrently open tickets rather than a pairwise time check; a full desk refuses with `ERR_SERVICE_CENTER_CAPACITY_FULL` at HTTP 422. The claim's product family must also appear in the center's `accepted_families` list, so that a microphone is not routed to a desk authorized only for amplifiers and guitars; a family mismatch raises `ERR_SERVICE_CENTER_FAMILY_NOT_ACCEPTED` at HTTP 422. The three checks stand in that order — suspension, then capacity, then family — so a suspended desk that is also full reports the suspension. The family is read through the covered instrument's line, and where that cannot be resolved the family check is passed over rather than failing. Routing stamps `routed_at`, increments the center's `open_ticket_count`, moves the claim to `ROUTED`, moves the plan to `REPAIR_IN_PROGRESS`, and stores the service fee in its provisional form.

The service fee is dual-variant and the two variants disagree about what drives the number. Like the commission figure, the fee is internal bookkeeping on the plan rather than a field of the plan's resource body. Provisional is a per-round bench fee: the base round fee of 2500 cents multiplied by the plan's `repair_rounds_used` plus one, charged when a claim routes, so it grows with each successive repair round and ignores the plan's term entirely. Final is a term-scaled flat fee: 2500 cents multiplied by the plan's `term_months` and divided by 24, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places, charged when the claim closes, and completely independent of how many rounds have been used. On a 36-month plan the final fee exceeds a first-round provisional fee by exactly 1250 cents. Note that the final variant reads `repair_rounds_used` before the closing transition increments it.

Closing is the transition that realizes the allowance arithmetic and the cascade that follows from it. A claim may close only while it is `ROUTED`, and closing stamps `closed_at`, increments the plan's `repair_rounds_used`, and recomputes `remaining_repair_rounds` as three minus that value floored at zero. The plan then either returns to `ACTIVE` when the allowance is still positive or moves to `EXPIRED` when it has reached zero. The routed center's `open_ticket_count` is decremented, releasing the slot the claim was holding, and the service fee is stored in its final form. Because closing is what exhausts the allowance, the `EXPIRED` plan that results is the same state that §3.7's entitlement rules treat differently from an `ACTIVE` one.

A routed claim can also be pulled back for re-diagnosis rather than closed. Reopening is admitted only from `ROUTED` and only while the logical clock is not past the plan's expiry; it returns the claim to `PENDING_ROUTING`, clears `routed_at` to null, decrements the center's `open_ticket_count` to release the slot, and returns the plan to `CLAIM_OPEN`. This is the path that lets a bad diagnosis be corrected without spending a repair round.

Voiding is the administrator's terminal override, available from `PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, or `EXPIRED`, and it is deliberately blunt: the plan moves to `VOIDED`, every claim on it that is still `PENDING_ROUTING` or `ROUTED` is closed as part of the same mutation, and the owning customer's `tech_support_entitlement` is recomputed because a plan that no longer stands may have been the only warranty holding the entitlement at `EXTENDED`. A claim already `CLOSED` is left untouched. `VOIDED` is terminal and refuses further action with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

### 3.7. Tech Support Entitlement and Escalation to the Service Department

Technical support in this business is not a subscription; it is a consequence of what the customer has bought and of whether they still have an engineer. `tech_support_entitlement` on a `Customer` is one of `NONE`, `BASIC`, or `EXTENDED`, and it is recomputed rather than set. The rule reads three facts: whether the customer has an assignment currently `ACTIVE`, whether the customer has at least one warranty plan whose status is `ACTIVE`, `CLAIM_OPEN`, or `REPAIR_IN_PROGRESS`, and how many instruments the customer has purchased. A warranty in force alone earns `EXTENDED`. With no warranty in force, the entitlement is `BASIC` only when the customer has both an `ACTIVE` assignment and at least one purchased instrument, and it is `NONE` otherwise. The practical reading is that an active warranty outranks everything, a purchase history plus an engineer is enough for basic help, and losing the engineer drops the customer to `NONE` the moment nothing else supports them — which is exactly the cascade described in §3.1. The same recomputation runs when an assignment becomes `ACTIVE`, so taking on a customer can raise their entitlement as well as lower it.

Everything the service stores is process-local and starts empty on every boot; no row is seeded at start-up, and every entity a caller reads comes into existence through the API surface itself. `SupportTicket` is the record of a conversation. It is opened against a `customer_id` with a `subject` and a `channel` of `PHONE`, `EMAIL`, or `CHAT`, plus a mandatory `service_center_id` naming the desk the ticket expects to reach. A ticket opens in `OPEN` status with `escalated_by`, `escalated_at`, and `prior_service_center_id` all null, and `escalation_seq` and `escalation_count` both at zero; `service_center_id` is recorded as supplied and is the desk the ticket will escalate to, but it consumes no slot until an escalation actually reaches it. The `required_entitlement` field is derived rather than supplied and starts at `BASIC`.

Triaging an open ticket is the support desk's first move, and it is where entitlement is actually tested. A ticket in `OPEN` status may be triaged only when the customer's `tech_support_entitlement` is at least `BASIC` — a customer at `NONE` is refused with `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_REQUIRED` at HTTP 403, which is a different class of refusal from the business-constraint violations elsewhere in this document and carries its own status. A successful triage moves the ticket to `IN_TRIAGE` and recomputes `required_entitlement`: it becomes `EXTENDED` when the customer holds at least one warranty plan whose status is `ACTIVE`, `CLAIM_OPEN`, or `REPAIR_IN_PROGRESS`, and `BASIC` otherwise. That recomputation is the hinge the escalation path turns on, because escalation is available only to extended-entitlement work.

Three checks stand between a triaged ticket and the service department, and they are evaluated in order. First, the ticket's `required_entitlement` must be `EXTENDED`; a ticket computed as `BASIC` cannot be escalated and is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_NOT_PERMITTED` at HTTP 422. Second, the ticket must name a service center to escalate to; a ticket with no `service_center_id` fails with `ERR_ESCALATION_DESK_REQUIRED` at HTTP 422. Third, the named desk must have a free slot; a desk already at `capacity_slots` fails with `ERR_SERVICE_CENTER_CAPACITY_FULL_AT_ESCALATION` at HTTP 422. Note that the desk is checked for capacity here even though it is the same count that routing a warranty claim consumes — the two features share the service center's slot ledger.

Escalation itself is a five-part mutation. It stamps `escalated_at` from the logical clock and `escalated_by` from the acting operator's identifier, both of which had been null. It assigns `escalation_seq` on the ticket as one more than the highest `escalation_seq` currently recorded in the escalation ledger for that same ticket, and appends exactly one `EscalationLedger` row with an `event_type` of `SERVICE_DEPARTMENT_ESCALATION`, the ticket's `service_center_id`, and that sequence value, stamped at `posted_at` from the logical clock. It increments the service center's `open_ticket_count`, and it recomputes the ticket's `escalation_count` as the number of escalation ledger rows appended against that ticket. The ledger's vocabulary is fixed: the only two event types it carries are `SERVICE_DEPARTMENT_ESCALATION` and `ESCALATION_WITHDRAWN`, and a ledger row also records the sequence value it was stamped with and the service center it names. The ticket moves to `ESCALATED`.

De-escalation is the service department's own move and is available from `ESCALATED` only. It clears `escalated_at` to null, appends a second `EscalationLedger` row with an `event_type` of `ESCALATION_WITHDRAWN`, decrements the center's `open_ticket_count`, and returns the ticket to `IN_TRIAGE`. The ledger is append-only in spirit: a withdrawal does not remove the escalation row, it adds its own, which is why `escalation_count` and the ledger's row count can exceed the number of escalations currently outstanding. `prior_service_center_id` records the center a ticket was last routed to before escalation and stays null when there was none; in this API it is never written, so it remains null on every ticket.

Resolution is the terminal move and is available from `IN_TRIAGE` to the support desk and from either `IN_TRIAGE` or `ESCALATED` to the service department. Resolving from `ESCALATED` also decrements the named center's `open_ticket_count`, releasing the slot the escalation was occupying; resolving from `IN_TRIAGE` has no slot to release. `RESOLVED` is terminal, and any further action raises `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

### 3.8. Tone Trials, Gear Exchange, and the Conserved Credit Balance

Tone is not a specification, which is why this business offers a trial. A `GearExchange` is opened by the support desk against a `quote_line_id` and the `customer_id` that bought it, declaring a `reason` of `TONE_MISMATCH`, `DEFECT_DOA`, `WRONG_MODEL`, or `CHANGED_MIND`. The case begins in `DRAFT` with no trial window, `days_remaining` at zero, and both fee fields at zero.

Starting the trial anchors the clock on the case. It is admitted only from `DRAFT`, and only when the customer's assignment in their book is currently `ACTIVE` — an unowned account has no engineer to stand behind the exchange, and the attempt fails with `ERR_CUSTOMER_ASSIGNMENT_REQUIRED` at HTTP 422. Starting the trial stamps `trial_start_date` from the logical clock truncated to that calendar day, derives `trial_end_date` as fourteen days later, computes `days_remaining` from the clock, and computes the restocking fee in its provisional form.

Return requests are accepted only for a genuine tone mismatch. A case in `TRIAL_ACTIVE` whose `reason` is `TONE_MISMATCH` enters the return path, moving to `RETURN_REQUESTED`, with `days_remaining` recomputed from the logical clock and the restocking fee recomputed in its final form. A request on any other reason does not enter that path: it is refused rather than silently accepted, carrying `ERR_RETURN_REASON_NOT_ELIGIBLE` at HTTP 422, and the case is recorded as `DENIED` — a terminal outcome — with `days_remaining` recomputed so the record shows how much trial time remained when the request was made. The denial is written before the refusal is raised, so the refused request leaves the case in `DENIED` rather than in its prior trial state; the fee fields are left as the provisional computation set them, since the return never proceeds to the final variant. `DEFECT_DOA`, `WRONG_MODEL`, and `CHANGED_MIND` all take this branch.

The restocking fee is composed from three additive rate components, and the two variants decide how many of them are in play. Two stored fields carry the outcome: `restocking_fee_bps` holds the composed rate in basis points, defaulting to zero until a rate is computed, and `restocking_fee_cents` holds the charge that results from applying that rate to the line total. The reason rate is fixed per reason: `TONE_MISMATCH` carries 1500 basis points, `DEFECT_DOA` carries 0, `WRONG_MODEL` carries 800, and `CHANGED_MIND` carries 2500. The tier adjustment is fixed per account tier: `STANDARD` adjusts the rate by 0 basis points, `PREMIUM` adjusts it by -500 basis points, and `STUDIO` adjusts it by -1000 basis points. The near-expiry component contributes 500 basis points only when the case's `days_remaining` is strictly less than 3, and 0 otherwise. These three are summed and floored at zero, so the effective rate is never negative; a `DEFECT_DOA` exchange always yields an effective rate of 0 no matter what the tier and the clock contribute. In the provisional variant, stored when the trial starts, only the reason rate is applied — the tier adjustment and the near-expiry component are not yet known and are ignored, so the fee is the line total multiplied by the reason rate and divided by 10000, rounded half-up to 2 decimal places. In the final variant, stored when the return is requested, the composed effective rate is applied in place of the bare reason rate, with the same single rounding against the line total. The gap between the two variants can be wide: a `STUDIO` customer returning for `TONE_MISMATCH` pays 500 basis points effective against the 1500 that the provisional form had suggested.

Completing the exchange is the money-moving transition, and it is bounded by the trial window. It is admitted only from `RETURN_REQUESTED`, and only while the logical clock is not past `trial_end_date` — the boundary instant itself still counts as in time, and only a later instant fails with `ERR_TRIAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED` at HTTP 409. The same window governs withdrawal, which returns a `RETURN_REQUESTED` case to `TRIAL_ACTIVE` without spending the exchange. Completion recomputes `refund_credit_cents` as the line total minus the final restocking fee, moves the case to `EXCHANGED`, and adjusts the customer's `lifetime_gear_credit_cents` — the conserved money balance that accumulates the fees credited by completed exchanges and is drawn down as credits are consumed by restocking — in the same mutation with two increments: the refund credit is added, and the restocking fee is then credited back as a fee offset. The second increment is what makes the conservation invariant exact rather than approximate; the net effect of a completed exchange on the balance is the full line total, and the fee and the credit can never drift apart because they are written together.

Cancellation is available to the support desk from `DRAFT`, `TRIAL_ACTIVE`, or `RETURN_REQUESTED`, and it moves the case to `CANCELLED`. `EXCHANGED`, `DENIED`, and `CANCELLED` are all terminal, and each refuses further action with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

### 3.9. Certification, Product Knowledge, and the Seniority Ladder

An engineer may only sell what they are certified to sell, and the certification record is built from graded assessments. A `CertificationAttempt` is scheduled by the operations desk against an `engineer_id`, a `product_family` from the same six-family set used by the catalogue, and a `score_pct` expressed as a whole percentage between 0 and 100 inclusive; a score outside that range is a shape violation and raises `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE` at HTTP 400. A scheduled attempt starts in `SCHEDULED` status with `attempt_index` at 1 and `eligible_to_retake` true, and `certified` false until it is graded; `assessed_at` and `expires_at` are null until a passing grade derives them. The index a newly scheduled attempt carries is the count of that engineer's prior attempts in the same family plus one, so a first attempt for a family still lands at 1 while an attempt scheduled against a family that already carries two attempts arrives at 3 and is immediately ineligible to retake. Scheduling is not restricted to an engineer in any particular state, and the index is recounted at grading so that the pairing's history is always what it reflects.

Grading divides cleanly at 80 percent. A `SCHEDULED` attempt scoring at least 80 is graded `PASSED`: `certified` is set to true as a derived flag that a client may never set directly, `assessed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `expires_at` is derived as twelve months after that instant, and the engineer's `certified_family_count` is recomputed across their whole record. A `SCHEDULED` attempt scoring below 80 is graded `FAILED`, with `certified` set to false, `attempt_index` recomputed as one more than the number of prior attempts already recorded for that same engineer and family pairing, and `eligible_to_retake` set to false once `attempt_index` reaches 3. Three attempts is the floor of the ladder: a failed third attempt is the end of the road for that family until the record is revisited.

A failed attempt that still has eligibility may be rescheduled by the operations desk, returning it to `SCHEDULED` and clearing `score_pct` to null so the record shows no score for an assessment that has not happened yet; the index is left untouched, so the retake carries the number its failure earned. A failed attempt that has already reached `attempt_index` 3 cannot be rescheduled and fails with `ERR_RETAKE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` at HTTP 422, and rescheduling is admitted only from `FAILED` — a passed or expired attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` and an already-scheduled one is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` on the same footing as any other repeated transition. An attempt whose certification window has lapsed is marked `EXPIRED`, an action admitted from `SCHEDULED`, `PASSED`, or `FAILED` which clears `certified` to false and recomputes the engineer's `certified_family_count` and seniority tier; `EXPIRED` is the terminal state for this entity, so a second attempt to expire it is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` at HTTP 409.

The seniority ladder is a threshold function over current certifications, and it is deliberately re-evaluated rather than frozen. The count that feeds it is the number of distinct product families among the engineer's certification attempts that are marked `certified` and whose `expires_at` is after the logical clock — an expired certification drops out of the count entirely, which means an engineer's tier can fall back down as well as climb. Four or more current certified families place the engineer at `TIER_3` with a commission cap of 7500 basis points. Two or three place them at `TIER_2` with a cap of 6500 basis points. Anything below that leaves them at `TIER_1` with a cap of 5000 basis points, which is also the default for a newly registered engineer with no assessed attempts at all. The ladder's two outputs are written back onto the engineer as the enum `seniority_tier` and the integer `commission_cap_bps`, and both are recomputed whenever a certification attempt is graded or expired.

Two things follow from the ladder. First, the cap is not a cosmetic label — it is the ceiling that §2.4's assignment-time check reads, so an engineer who loses a certification can find a previously acceptable `commission_share_bps` now exceeding their reduced cap, which surfaces as `ERR_COMMISSION_CAP_EXCEEDED` at HTTP 422 on the next assignment. Second, the total engineer share is always the smaller of the assignment's requested share and the cap, so an engineer negotiating at the tier ceiling is paid the ceiling rather than the request. Beyond the ladder, the certification flag itself gates selling: a catalogue product whose `requires_certification` flag is true may only be quoted by an engineer currently certified in that product's family.

### 3.10. The Operator Surface: Clock, Catalogue, and Operational Reads

Three capabilities sit outside the customer-facing workflows and exist so that the rest of the service can be driven deterministically and configured at all. The logical clock is advanced by a capability restricted to `PlatformAdmin`, which accepts `current_utc` and moves the service's notion of now to that instant. The catalogue is populated by an administrator registering `Product` rows with their `product_code`, `display_name`, `product_family`, `unit_price_cents`, and the three optional flags `required_family`, `requires_certification`, and `in_catalog`; catalogue codes must be unique across products and prices must be non-negative. Registering a second `Product` whose `product_code` already exists, or a second `Engineer` whose `email` already exists, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE` at HTTP 400. Service centers are registered with a `name`, a `region` from `MIDWEST`, `SOUTH`, `WEST`, or `EAST`, a `capacity_slots` ceiling on concurrently open repair tickets, and an `accepted_families` list of the product families the center is authorized to repair. A registered center begins in `OPEN` status, and an administrator may suspend it — moving it to `SUSPENDED` so that it accepts no further claim routes, per §3.6 — or return a suspended center to `OPEN`.

The service also exposes a broad read surface. Engineers and customers can each be fetched individually or listed, with engineer listings filterable by `region` and `status` and customer listings by `status`. Catalogue products can be listed with filters on `product_family` and `in_catalog`, the latter taking a textual boolean so that `true`, `1`, and `yes` all select the catalogue and anything else selects the remainder; service centers with filters on `region` and `status`. Assignments can be listed by `engineer_id` or by `customer_id`, which is how a caller reconstructs an engineer's book or an account's assignment history. Quotes can be listed by `customer_id`, `engineer_id`, or `status`, a single quote can be fetched, and a quote's lines can be listed directly. A standalone list of quote lines is also available, filterable by `quote_id` and by the `requires_line_id` they point at, and a single line can be fetched by its own `id`. A dedicated computed read on a quote returns the derived figures together: `gross_subtotal_cents`, `package_bundle_price_cents`, `dependency_closure_size`, `bundle_ready`, `engineer_commission_cents`, and `commission_variant`, the last of which names whether the commission figure currently stored is the provisional or the final variant of §3.3. Quote lines can be listed by their owning `quote_id` or by the `requires_line_id` they point at, which is the query that reconstructs a dependency graph in reverse.

The post-sale side has its own reads. Instrument units can be listed by `quote_line_id` or `setup_status`. Warranty plans can be listed by `customer_id`, `instrument_unit_id`, or `status`; warranty claims by `warranty_plan_id`, `service_center_id`, or `status`. Support tickets can be listed by `customer_id`, by the `service_center_id` that names their escalation desk, or by `status`. The `EscalationLedger` is itself readable: rows can be listed filtered by `support_ticket_id` or `service_center_id`, or fetched individually, which is how a caller audits the escalation and withdrawal history that §3.7 appends. Backorders can be listed by `engineer_id`, `quote_line_id`, or `status`; gear exchanges by `customer_id`, `quote_line_id`, or `status`; and certification attempts by `engineer_id`, `product_family`, or `status`. Every one of these list operations honours the pagination contract of §2.3.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Two status-code rules govern the surface below. An operation that brings a new resource into existence — registering an engineer, a customer, a product, a service center, a quote, a quote line, an instrument unit, a warranty plan, a warranty claim, a support ticket, a backorder, a gear exchange, or a certification attempt — answers with HTTP 201 Created. Every operation that modifies a resource already in existence, including each state transition on a lifecycle, answers with HTTP 200 OK, and the `data` field of a 2xx response carries the created or updated resource unless the envelope section says otherwise.
