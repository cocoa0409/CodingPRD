# Mobile Driver License Issuance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A state motor vehicle agency is standing up a mobile driver license (mDL) program: a digital credential, provisioned onto a resident's own handset, that a police officer, a bartender, a bank teller or an airline gate agent can read in seconds without the resident surrendering the phone. The agency already issues plastic credentials, and that plastic remains the root of trust — nobody receives an mDL who does not already hold a valid physical license and who has not been identity-proofed to the assurance level the jurisdiction's national interoperability profile demands. What the program adds on top of the plastic is everything the plastic cannot do: revocation that propagates to readers in near real time, an address change that reaches the handset instead of waiting for a renewal cycle, and a presentation model where a retailer asking whether the holder is over twenty-one learns nothing except that.

Two obligations pull against each other throughout this specification, and the API exists to hold both simultaneously. The first is **credential integrity** — an mDL must never outlive the physical credential behind it, never sit on an unattested or lost handset, never be presentable while its on-device payload disagrees with agency records, and never be issued or reissued on a stale view of the handset it binds to. The second is **holder privacy** — a verifier receives the minimum element set its declared purpose entitles it to, cannot retain that data longer than its contract permits, cannot be handed the physical handset under any circumstance including a traffic stop, and cannot correlate its presentation log with any other verifier's log to follow a person around. Every rule in §2 and §3 traces back to one of these two obligations, and where they collide the specification names the winner explicitly.

The service is mounted under the `/mdl` path prefix. It is deliberately deterministic and single-tenant in behaviour: all time-dependent logic reads a logical clock rather than wall-clock time, all derived counters are recomputed from their source rows rather than trusted, and all monetary and score arithmetic follows a pinned evaluation order so that two conforming implementations produce byte-identical numbers. Readers of this document should expect the operational rules to be scattered across §2 and §3 in the order a program administrator would encounter them, not grouped by entity; §4 and §5 are reference tables only.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operators, Roles and Identity Binding

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the actor, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the actor is operating. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names declared below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other consideration. If both headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorized for the endpoint being called (see the Authorized Role(s) column in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `SystemAdmin`, `ProofingAgent`, `CredentialAdjudicator`, `Holder`, `VerifierOperator`, and `PrivacyAuditor`. The role model is one of segregation of duties: the agency deliberately splits identity proofing from adjudication, and issuance authority from privacy oversight, so that no single operator can both manufacture the evidence for a credential and bless it, and no operator who profits from a disclosure can also erase the record of it.

- A `SystemAdmin` is the state agency's program administrator. It owns the interoperability profile, trust-list publication, holder records, handset attestation and remote wipe, credential revocation, update dispatch, verifier trust decisions and reader-app listing.
- A `ProofingAgent` is a field-office identity-proofing clerk who opens proofing sessions, captures evidence and submits sessions for adjudication.
- A `CredentialAdjudicator` is a central issuance adjudicator who decides proofing outcomes and provisions and renews digital credentials.
- A `Holder` is the resident the credential belongs to, acting from the handset.
- A `VerifierOperator` is a relying-party operator who registers reader applications, attests retention practice and drives presentation requests through to disclosure.
- A `PrivacyAuditor` is the independent privacy oversight office. It reads the pseudonymised presentation log and the exposure indices and voids the billing of presentations found to breach purpose scope. It holds no issuance authority whatsoever.

Identity binding differs by role, and it is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Holder`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Holder record; ownership checks on any resource that references a Holder compare the resource's `holder_id` to `X-User-ID`. A `Holder` may act only within its own `holder_id` chain — its own handsets, its own credentials, its own propagation orders, and the presentation sessions raised against its own credentials.
- When `X-User-Role` is `VerifierOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Verifier record; ownership checks compare the `verifier_id` of the reader application involved (directly, or reached through the presentation session's `reader_app_id`) to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `SystemAdmin`, `ProofingAgent`, `CredentialAdjudicator`, or `PrivacyAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — a proofing session stores the opening clerk's identifier as `proofing_agent_user_id` and the deciding adjudicator's identifier as `adjudicator_user_id` — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`.

An ownership violation by either entity-backed role is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, with a minimum accepted value of 1 and a maximum accepted value of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A value outside these bounds is a request-schema failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint description in §4 pins a different ordering, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Where a different key is pinned — revocation ledgers replay in `sequence` ascending order, trust-list snapshots list in `snapshot_sequence` ascending order, and the pseudonymised presentation log lists in `recorded_at` ascending order — that ordering is stated with the endpoint and takes precedence.

The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filters before the page window was applied, and `has_more` indicating whether any matching row remains beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` in canonical UUID form with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp recorded as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed to the current logical instant whenever any field of the row changes, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Three resources are append-only by design and therefore carry **only** `id` and `created_at` — they have no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, because nothing ever mutates them after creation: proofing evidence items, revocation ledger entries, and pseudonymised presentation log entries. (The `superseded` label on an evidence item and the `published` flag on a revocation entry are derived read-time labels recomputed by the projections that own them, not client-visible mutations of an immutable row; they are still returned on the row.)

All time-dependent behaviour in this specification reads a single **logical clock**, referred to throughout as the current instant. On a freshly started server the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Only the administrative clock-setting capability moves it: that capability accepts a `to_iso8601` instant, returns the new `current_utc`, mutates no entity, and applies no lazily-evaluated transition by itself. Wall-clock time is never consulted. Two lazy transitions are evaluated on read — a verified proofing session lapses when the clock has reached its expiry instant, and an active credential expires when the clock has reached the end of its validity window — and both are described with the workflows that own them in §3.

### 2.5. Left-to-Right Resolution of Nested Resources

Many endpoints address a child through its parent, and several accept foreign keys in the request body. Resolution is strictly positional. Path-templated identifiers are resolved left to right, and the first identifier that resolves to nothing decides the response: a request naming a holder that does not exist fails with `ERR_HOLDER_NOT_FOUND` regardless of whether the nested identifier further right would also have failed. The per-entity not-found codes are `ERR_INTEROP_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HOLDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROOFING_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UPDATE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UPDATE_DELIVERY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VERIFIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_READER_APP_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_PRESENTATION_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, each returning HTTP 404.

Body-supplied foreign keys are resolved after every path identifier has resolved, and among themselves they are resolved in the order the endpoint's input list declares them. So a provisioning request that names a non-existent holder and a non-existent handset reports the holder failure, because `holder_id` precedes `device_id` in that endpoint's input list. Body-level foreign-key resolution applies to the creation of proofing sessions, handsets, credentials, reader applications, trust-list snapshots and presentation sessions.

### 2.6. The Rejection Ladder

When more than one rule would reject a request, the outcome is never ambiguous: the checks below are evaluated **strictly in this order**, and the **first** one that fails decides the response. Every later check is skipped. Implementations that reorder this ladder will return the wrong error code for perfectly ordinary requests, so the ordering is part of the contract, not an implementation detail.

1. Header presence and role validity — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. The acting role is authorized for the endpoint — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. Request-body schema conformance: every required field present, every enum value a member of its declared set, every integer inside its declared range, every array an array of strings, every datetime parseable as ISO 8601, and every interval's start strictly before its end — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
4. Every path-templated resource resolves, left to right — the per-entity 404 codes.
5. Every body-supplied foreign key resolves, in the endpoint's declared field order — the per-entity 404 codes.
6. Ownership for the entity-backed roles — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. This applies to credential activation, loss reporting, handset recovery, reader-app registration, presentation-session creation, presentation consent, disclosure, and retention attestation.
7. The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, except where one of the three more specific state checks below applies.
8. An update delivery is still awaiting a result before it can be confirmed or failed — `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_PENDING`.
9. A presentation session is awaiting the holder's decision before consent can be recorded — `ERR_SESSION_NOT_CONSENT_PENDING`.
10. A completed presentation's billing has not already been voided before a void is applied — `ERR_BILLING_ALREADY_VOIDED`.
11. The optimistic-concurrency guard — `ERR_STALE_DEVICE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_INTEROP_VERSION` (see §2.7).
12. Segregation of duties at adjudication — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
13. Handset-handling policy — `ERR_HANDSET_SURRENDER_PROHIBITED`.
14. Law-enforcement context entitlement — `ERR_LAW_ENFORCEMENT_CONTEXT_NOT_PERMITTED`.
15. Reader trust listing and trust-list coverage — `ERR_READER_NOT_TRUSTED`.
16. Verifier trust status — `ERR_VERIFIER_NOT_TRUSTED`.
17. Device binding integrity — `ERR_DEVICE_BINDING_MISMATCH` or `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_BOUND`.
18. Physical-credential eligibility — `ERR_PHYSICAL_CREDENTIAL_INELIGIBLE`.
19. Proofing assurance still within its validity period — `ERR_PROOFING_ASSURANCE_EXPIRED`.
20. Credential validity-window overlap on the same handset — `ERR_CREDENTIAL_VALIDITY_OVERLAP`.
21. Trust-list snapshot tiling — `ERR_TRUST_LIST_WINDOW_OVERLAP` or `ERR_TRUST_LIST_WINDOW_GAP`.
22. Reader-certificate renewal buffer — `ERR_CERT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.
23. Reader-certificate serial uniqueness — `ERR_DUPLICATE_READER_APP`.
24. Assurance level sufficiency — `ERR_ASSURANCE_LEVEL_INSUFFICIENT`.
25. Multi-device policy cap — `ERR_DEVICE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.
26. Active-credential slot cap — `ERR_ACTIVE_CREDENTIAL_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.
27. Data minimisation in the age-only context — `ERR_DATA_MINIMIZATION_VIOLATION`.
28. Unlock-proof match — `ERR_PROTECTION_METHOD_MISMATCH`.
29. Revocation-status freshness on the delivery path — `ERR_REVOCATION_STATUS_STALE`.
30. Minimum evidence count at submission — `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`.
31. Declared retention within the verifier's contractual ceiling — `ERR_RETENTION_CEILING_EXCEEDED`.
32. Verifier account standing — `ERR_VERIFIER_ACCOUNT_DELINQUENT`.

The practical consequences of this ladder are worth stating plainly. A forbidden role always beats a malformed body. A missing resource always beats an ownership failure. An ownership failure always beats a wrong state. A stale parent version always beats every business rule below it — so a provisioning request that is simultaneously built on a stale handset view and names an ineligible physical credential reports the stale version, not the ineligibility. And within the business tier, a handset-surrender demand is reported before any trust-listing problem, which is intentional: the agency wants the prohibited demand named as the reason, not masked by an unrelated trust failure.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Two write operations in this system are built on a client's earlier read of a **parent** record, and both require the client to echo the `version` it observed so the server can refuse to act on a stale view.

Provisioning a digital credential requires `expected_device_version` as a non-optional field of the request. Its value must equal the current `version` of the handset named by `device_id`. If the handset's `version` has advanced since the client last read it — because it was attested, bound to another credential, reported lost, recovered, or wiped in the interim — the provisioning is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEVICE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This matters most in the reissuance case: a provisioning that names `supersedes_credential_id` legitimately drives the prior credential on that handset to a terminal state, so a provisioning built on a stale view of the handset would silently replace a credential the client never observed.

Publishing a trust-list snapshot requires `expected_interop_profile_version` as a non-optional field of the request. Its value must equal the current `version` of the interoperability profile named by `interop_profile_id`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INTEROP_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A published snapshot supersedes its predecessor for every reader in the ecosystem, so a publication derived from a stale profile view would distribute an outdated `standard_version` and an outdated reader set.

No other endpoint accepts a version field of any kind. Concurrency control is the parent-`version` echo described here and nothing else.

### 2.8. Two Independent State Axes on a Digital Credential

A digital credential is unusual in this specification: it carries **two state fields that evolve independently of one another** on the same row. `lifecycle_state` tracks issuance and revocation and takes the values `PENDING_PROVISION`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `REPLACED`, `REVOKED` and `EXPIRED`, of which `REPLACED`, `REVOKED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal. `sync_state` tracks whether the payload sitting on the handset still matches agency records and takes the values `IN_SYNC`, `UPDATE_PENDING`, `UPDATE_FAILED` and `STALE`, none of which is terminal.

The two axes are genuinely orthogonal. A credential may be `ACTIVE` and `UPDATE_PENDING` at the same time; it may be `SUSPENDED` and `IN_SYNC` at the same time. Reading one axis tells you nothing about the other. There is exactly **one** coupling between them, and it runs in one direction only: when a second delivery failure drives `sync_state` to `STALE`, that event also forces `lifecycle_state` from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`. No other sync event touches the lifecycle axis, and no lifecycle event ever changes the sync axis.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline, Compensating Actions and Conserved Counters

Several operations in this system fan out across multiple entities, and the fan-out is specified as an ordered sequence of hops in which each hop is caused by the one before it — not as a flat list of things that happen to occur. Where §3 numbers the hops of a cascade, an implementation must produce every hop, including the ones that are two or three steps removed from the endpoint the client called. A missing third hop is not a cosmetic omission: the trust-list republication counters that readers rely on to detect missed revocation deltas live there.

Every cascade in this system either has an exact compensating counterpart or is deliberately terminal. Reporting a handset lost suspends its credentials; recovering the handset reinstates exactly those credentials and returns the holder's active-credential counter to its pre-loss value with no drift. Suspending a holder's physical credential suspends the digital credentials it backs; restoring the physical credential to valid reinstates exactly those. Billing a disclosure raises the verifier's outstanding balance; voiding that billing removes exactly the same amount, so the balance returns to its exact pre-disclosure value. `credited_amount_cents` on a verifier accumulates every amount so removed as the audit counterpart of the conserved balance, and is never subtracted from the balance a second time.

Whenever a row changes, its `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical instant and its `version` increments by 1 — including when the row changed only because it was the target of another operation's cascade. Derived counters (`active_credential_count`, `bound_device_count`, `revoked_credential_count`, `active_binding_count`, `pending_update_count`, `failed_delivery_count`, `delivery_count`, `delivered_count`, `failed_count`, `open_session_count`, `query_count`, `evidence_count`, `submission_count`, `reprovision_count`, `pending_delta_count`, `pending_republish_count`, `probation_strike_count`, `listed_reader_app_count`) are maintained to agree at all times with the rows they count.

### 2.10. Fees, Scores, Precision and Rounding

Money in this system is expressed in **whole cents as integers** — there are no fractional-currency fields anywhere. Where a fee, a settlement or an invoice line involves multiplication by a percentage or a factor, the intermediate value is rounded to a whole number of cents using **round half up** (a value exactly halfway between two integers rounds away from zero, upward). Each calculation in §3 pins the exact point at which rounding is applied, and that point is part of the contract: rounding earlier or later than specified produces different, wrong totals.

Assurance scores and exposure indices are integer point totals with no rounding at all, because every weight and every point value that feeds them is itself an integer. Staleness measurements are integer counts of whole seconds, obtained by discarding any fractional second (that is, taking the floor). Element ordering in the minimisation projection is exact set algebra rendered in a fixed canonical order, never in the order the client happened to submit.

### 2.11. Privacy Invariants

Four privacy invariants hold system-wide and are not waivable by any role, any verifier category, or any presentation context.

**No handset surrender.** A presentation request declares its `handset_handling` as one of `NO_SURRENDER`, `TAP_TO_READ` or `SURRENDER_REQUESTED`. The third value is permitted by the schema so that the demand can be named and refused, but it is **never** accepted: a request declaring `SURRENDER_REQUESTED` is rejected with `ERR_HANDSET_SURRENDER_PROHIBITED` (HTTP 422). This holds for law-enforcement verifiers exactly as it holds for a supermarket. No verifier may require the holder to hand over the physical handset.

**Non-linkable logs.** Every presentation produces a log entry whose subject is recorded as `credential_pseudonym`, a per-verifier salted pseudonym of the credential — deliberately **not** the credential's `id`. Two different verifiers logging presentations of the same credential receive different pseudonyms, so their logs cannot be joined to reconstruct a holder's movements. Within a single verifier the pseudonym is stable, which is what makes repeat-visit detection possible for that verifier alone and what the `pseudonym_reuse_index` measures.

**Data minimisation.** A reader receives the intersection of what it asked for with what its context and declared purpose entitle it to, and nothing else. The withheld remainder is recorded against the verifier as exposure even when the disclosure never happens.

**Retention ceilings are contractual.** A verifier's `retention_ceiling_days` is a hard contractual maximum between 0 and 90 days inclusive. A presentation declaring `retention_days` above that ceiling is refused; declaring exactly the ceiling is accepted.

### 2.12. Three Flavours of Interval Semantics

This system compares time windows in three places, and the three use **deliberately different** rules. Confusing them is the most common source of wrong answers, so each is named explicitly here and reinforced in §3.

**Credential validity windows are half-open, and adjacency is allowed.** A credential's window runs from `valid_from` inclusive to `valid_until` exclusive. Two windows on the same handset conflict only if they strictly overlap — that is, when the new window's start is earlier than the other's end **and** the other's start is earlier than the new window's end. Windows that merely touch at an endpoint, where the new window's start equals the other's end, are accepted.

**Trust-list snapshot windows must tile exactly — no gap and no overlap.** A snapshot's window runs from `effective_from` inclusive to `effective_until` exclusive, and for the second and every later snapshot of a profile, `effective_from` must **equal** the newest existing snapshot's `effective_until` exactly. Starting earlier is an overlap; starting later is a gap; both are rejected, with distinct error codes.

**Reader-certificate windows are closed, and adjacency is forbidden.** A reader certificate is valid from `cert_not_before` through `cert_not_after`, both inclusive. When a reader application is registered whose `app_identifier` matches an existing application of the same verifier, the new certificate must start **at least 24 hours after** the incumbent's `cert_not_after`. Exact adjacency is rejected along with anything earlier — the buffer is mandatory, not advisory.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Governing the Jurisdiction: the Interoperability Profile and the Trust List

Before a single credential can be issued, a `SystemAdmin` establishes the jurisdiction's **interoperability profile** — the single record that encodes how this state participates in the national mDL ecosystem and what limits it places on its own issuance. Creating a profile requires a `profile_code`, unique across all profiles and between 3 and 24 characters long, and a `standard_version` naming the national standard revision the trust list and credential payloads conform to: one of `ISO_18013_5_2021`, `ISO_18013_5_2024` or `ISO_18013_7_2024`. Everything else may be omitted and takes a jurisdiction default. `accepted_issuer_states` carries the two-letter codes of the issuing jurisdictions whose mDLs this state will accept for cross-state presentation and defaults to an empty array. `required_assurance_level` names the minimum identity assurance a proofing session must reach before a credential may be provisioned and defaults to `IAL2`, the strongest of the three levels `IAL1`, `IAL1_5` and `IAL2`. `max_devices_per_holder` is the multi-device policy cap and defaults to 2, and `max_active_credentials_per_holder` caps simultaneous live credentials per resident and also defaults to 2; both must be at least 1. `proofing_validity_days` — the shelf life of a verified proofing result — defaults to 180 days and must be at least 1. `revocation_freshness_seconds` defaults to 300 seconds, that is five minutes, and may be as low as zero. `age_only_allowed_elements` is the exact element allow-list for an age-only presentation and defaults to the three elements `age_over_18`, `age_over_21` and `portrait`. A newly created profile is marked `is_active` as true — it is the profile currently governing issuance — starts with `pending_republish_count` at zero, and starts at `version` 1.

Readers in the field do not query the agency for trust decisions one at a time; they consume **trust-list snapshots**, immutable publications that name the reader applications the state currently trusts. Publishing a snapshot is a `SystemAdmin` action that names the `interop_profile_id` it distributes for, the coverage window `effective_from` and `effective_until`, and the `expected_interop_profile_version` described in §2.7. The first snapshot of a profile takes `snapshot_sequence` 1 and each later publication takes one more than the highest sequence already published under that profile. At publication the snapshot freezes `listed_reader_app_count` to the number of reader applications currently listed, copies the profile's `standard_version` into its own `standard_version`, records `published_at` as the current logical instant, and sets `revocation_pointer_sequence` to the total number of revocation ledger entries in existence across the whole system at that moment — the pointer readers use to detect deltas they have missed. A fresh snapshot starts with `revocation_pointer_stale` false and `pending_delta_count` at zero. Publication also flips the `published` flag to true on every revocation entry appended before this moment, resets the profile's `pending_republish_count` to zero, and increments the profile's `version` by 1.

Because snapshots are what readers rely on, they must tile the timeline exactly. For the second and every later snapshot of the same profile, `effective_from` must equal the newest existing snapshot's `effective_until` precisely. A start strictly earlier than that boundary is an overlap and is rejected with `ERR_TRUST_LIST_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409); a start strictly later leaves readers with an uncovered stretch of time and is rejected with `ERR_TRUST_LIST_WINDOW_GAP` (HTTP 422). Snapshots of a profile are listed back in `snapshot_sequence` ascending order and may be filtered on `revocation_pointer_stale` to find publications whose revocation pointer has since fallen behind.

That staleness flag is the ecosystem's early-warning signal, and it is written by cascades that originate far away from the trust-list machinery. Every time a revocation entry is appended anywhere in the system — by a loss report, a physical-credential change, a delivery failure, an explicit revocation, a reinstatement or a lazy expiry — the newest snapshot of the active profile has its `revocation_pointer_stale` set to true and its `pending_delta_count` incremented by 1, and the active profile's `pending_republish_count` is incremented by 1. Listing or delisting a reader application likewise increments the newest snapshot's `pending_delta_count` by 1 and the profile's `pending_republish_count` by 1, because the composition of the trusted reader set has changed since the last publication. When a `pending_republish_count` grows, the administrator knows a republication is overdue.

### 3.2. Enrolment and Identity Proofing

Enrolment begins with the plastic. A `SystemAdmin` creates the holder record with the resident's `legal_name`, `date_of_birth` as a calendar date in year-month-day form, the address of record as `address_line1`, `city`, `state_code` (exactly two uppercase letters) and `postal_code`, and the two facts that establish eligibility: `physical_credential_number`, which must be unique across all holders, and `physical_credential_expires_on`, the calendar date the plastic lapses. Two further inputs are optional: `physical_credential_status`, which defaults to `VALID` and may otherwise be `EXPIRED`, `SUSPENDED`, `REVOKED` or `CANCELLED`, and `restriction_codes`, the driving restrictions carried on the credential such as corrective lenses or daylight-only operation, defaulting to an empty array. A new holder starts with `active_credential_count`, `bound_device_count` and `revoked_credential_count` all at zero and `address_version` at 1.

**Only `VALID` confers eligibility, and eligibility is re-checked at four separate points.** The physical-credential gate requires both that `physical_credential_status` reads `VALID` and that `physical_credential_expires_on` falls strictly later than the current logical date, and it is evaluated when a proofing session is opened, when a proofing session is submitted for adjudication, when a credential is provisioned, and when a credential is activated. In all four the rejection is `ERR_PHYSICAL_CREDENTIAL_INELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). A plastic credential expiring today is not eligible; the comparison is strictly later, not later-or-equal.

A `ProofingAgent` opens an identity-proofing session naming the `holder_id` of the person being proofed and the `requested_assurance_level` the agent is attempting to establish, which must be either `IAL1_5` or `IAL2`. The session opens in `COLLECTING` with `submission_count` and `evidence_count` at zero, and the agent's own `X-User-ID` is stored as `proofing_agent_user_id`. That stored identifier is the segregation-of-duties comparand and the reason the field exists: the clerk who collected the evidence may never be the operator who blesses it.

Evidence is captured one item at a time and is **append-only** — an evidence row is never mutated after creation. Each item declares an `evidence_type` from `DOCUMENT_SCAN`, `BIOMETRIC_MATCH`, `KNOWLEDGE_CHECK` and `ADDRESS_CORROBORATION`, a `quality` band of `STRONG`, `FAIR` or `WEAK` as adjudicated at capture, a `captured_at` instant, and a `source_reference` — the opaque handle of the capture artefact, whether that is a scan identifier, a biometric probe identifier or a quiz identifier. On creation the item's `strength_points` are resolved from its type and quality band and its `superseded` label is written as false. Appending an item increments the owning session's `evidence_count` by 1. The strength table is fixed: a document scan is worth 250 points when strong, 150 when fair and 60 when weak; a biometric match 200, 120 and 40; a knowledge check 90, 50 and 20; and an address corroboration 80, 40 and 10. Evidence items of a session are listed back in `created_at` ascending order, optionally filtered by `evidence_type`, with their resolved `strength_points` and `superseded` labels.

The **assurance score** is computed in two different ways over the very same evidence set, and the difference between them is deliberate. The authoritative variant, used at adjudication, is strict about recency. For each evidence type that appears at least once in the session, only one item counts: the one with the greatest `captured_at`; if two items of the same type share the greatest `captured_at`, the tie is broken in favour of the item with the earlier `created_at`, and the item's `id` is never consulted for ordering. The strength points of that single chosen item per type are summed. From that raw total a repeat-attempt penalty is subtracted: 25 points for each submission after the first, that is, 25 points multiplied by the session's `submission_count` less one, and never a negative multiplier — a session on its first submission incurs no penalty at all. The strict score is the raw total less the penalty, and it is never allowed below zero. The advisory variant is optimistic instead: for each evidence type present it takes the **strongest** item of that type regardless of when it was captured, sums those, and applies no repeat-attempt penalty whatsoever. For an identical evidence set the optimistic score is therefore never below the strict score, and is strictly greater whenever a weaker item of some type was captured later than a stronger one, or whenever the session has been submitted more than once. No status filter is applied on either pass — evidence is immutable and is never withdrawn — and the `superseded` label is a derived output of the strict pass, not a filter on the optimistic one.

Either score maps onto the assurance ladder identically: a score of at least 420 points is `IAL2`, a score of at least 260 points but below 420 is `IAL1_5`, and anything below 260 is `IAL1`. Both boundaries are inclusive, so exactly 420 reaches `IAL2` and exactly 260 reaches `IAL1_5`. A read-only preview capability returns both computations side by side so the agent can see the gap before submitting: `preview_score` with its mapped `preview_assurance_level` from the optimistic variant, `adjudication_score` with its mapped `adjudication_assurance_level` from the strict variant, plus the session's current `submission_count` and the `evidence_type_count` — the number of distinct evidence types present. The preview mutates nothing.

Submitting a session for adjudication moves it `COLLECTING → SUBMITTED` and increments `submission_count` by 1. Submission requires at least two evidence items on the session; a session with fewer is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` (HTTP 422). Submission also re-runs the physical-credential eligibility gate described above.

Adjudication is a `CredentialAdjudicator` action on a `SUBMITTED` session, carrying a `decision` of `VERIFY`, `RETURN` or `DENY`. Before anything else the acting `X-User-ID` is compared against the session's `proofing_agent_user_id`; if they are equal the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). Every adjudication, whatever the decision, recomputes the `superseded` label on every evidence row of the session — an item is superseded exactly when another item of the same `evidence_type` in the same session has a strictly later `captured_at` — and records the strict score into the session's `adjudication_score`. Beyond that the three branches diverge, and each carries a full specification of its own:

- **`VERIFY`** moves the session `SUBMITTED → VERIFIED`. It requires the strict score to be at least 260 points; a lower score is rejected with `ERR_ASSURANCE_LEVEL_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). On success `achieved_assurance_level` is written from the recorded score — `IAL2` at 420 points or above, `IAL1_5` at 260 points or above, otherwise `IAL1` — `verified_at` is set to the current logical instant, `assurance_expires_at` is set to that instant advanced by the profile's `proofing_validity_days` (180 days by default), `adjudicator_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and any `return_reason` left over from an earlier round is cleared to null.
- **`RETURN`** moves the session `SUBMITTED → RETURNED` and requires a `return_reason` in the body; without one the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The supplied text is stored as `return_reason` and `adjudicator_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`. The strict score is still recorded into `adjudication_score`, but it is not mapped to a level.
- **`DENY`** moves the session `SUBMITTED → DENIED`, a terminal state. `adjudicator_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID` and `achieved_assurance_level` is set to null.

The proofing lifecycle is a genuine cycle, and appending evidence is what closes it. Appending an evidence item to a session sitting in `RETURNED` reopens the session: its state returns to `COLLECTING`, its `return_reason` is cleared to null, and its `evidence_count` increments by 1 as it would for any append — completing the loop `COLLECTING → SUBMITTED → RETURNED → COLLECTING`. The second and every subsequent trip through that loop is what the 25-point-per-resubmission penalty in the strict score is charging for.

A verified session is a perishable asset with two possible ends. Reading a verified session lazily applies its lapse: when the logical clock has reached or passed `assurance_expires_at`, the session moves `VERIFIED → EXPIRED` and its `achieved_assurance_level` is retained for audit rather than cleared. The same lapse is applied when a provisioning attempt names an expired session. Otherwise, a verified session backs exactly one provisioning: when a credential is provisioned naming this session, the session moves `VERIFIED → CONSUMED`, which is terminal — one session, one credential, no reuse. `DENIED`, `EXPIRED` and `CONSUMED` are the three terminal states of a proofing session; the full state set is `COLLECTING`, `SUBMITTED`, `VERIFIED`, `RETURNED`, `DENIED`, `EXPIRED` and `CONSUMED`. Sessions can be listed per holder, filtered by `state`.

### 3.3. Binding the Handset: Registration, Attestation and the Multi-Device Cap

A handset enters the system as a registration, which either the resident from the phone itself or a `SystemAdmin` at a counter may perform. Registration names the `holder_id` that owns the handset, the `platform` — `IOS` or `ANDROID` — a human-readable `model_name`, the opaque platform `hardware_attestation_token` the operating system produced, and the `protection_method` the resident will use to unlock the credential: `BIOMETRIC` or `PASSCODE`. That choice is binding in the literal sense; it is the proof the resident must reproduce at activation and again at every consent. A newly registered handset sits in `REGISTERED` with `active_binding_count` and `reprovision_count` at zero. The full handset state set is `REGISTERED`, `ATTESTED`, `BOUND`, `LOST_REPORTED`, `WIPED` and `RETIRED`, and of those `WIPED` and `RETIRED` are terminal.

Attestation is where the handset's hardware is judged, and it is the clearest **divergent branch** in the specification. A `SystemAdmin` records the outcome by supplying an `attestation_result` of `PASS` or `FAIL` together with the `secure_element_grade` the platform reported — `HARDWARE_STRONGBOX`, `HARDWARE_TEE` or `SOFTWARE`, where a grade of `SOFTWARE` denotes key storage that the national standard does not regard as adequate for an `IAL2` credential. The handset must be in `REGISTERED`; anything else is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Both branches record `attestation_result` and `secure_element_grade` and both increment the handset's `version` by 1, but there they part company. The `PASS` branch drives `REGISTERED → ATTESTED` and increments the owning holder's `bound_device_count` by 1. The `FAIL` branch drives `REGISTERED → RETIRED` and leaves `bound_device_count` completely untouched — a retired handset never counted against the cap in the first place, so nothing has to be undone later.

The **multi-device policy** is enforced on the passing branch only, and it is enforced before the counter moves: the holder's `bound_device_count` must be strictly below the profile's `max_devices_per_holder` for a passing attestation to be accepted, and a holder already at the cap is rejected with `ERR_DEVICE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). With the default cap of 2 this means a resident may carry an mDL on a phone and a tablet but not on a third handset until one is retired or wiped. What `bound_device_count` counts is precisely the holder's handsets in `ATTESTED`, `BOUND` or `LOST_REPORTED` — a handset reported lost still occupies a slot, because the resident has not yet given it up. A holder's handsets can be listed and filtered by `state`.

### 3.4. Provisioning and Activating a Digital Credential

Provisioning is a `CredentialAdjudicator` action and is the point at which a proofing result becomes a credential. The request names the subject `holder_id`, the target `device_id`, the `proofing_session_id` whose adjudication supplies the assurance level, the validity window as `valid_from` and `valid_until`, and the `expected_device_version` guard of §2.7. Two optional inputs shape a reissuance rather than a first issuance: `supersedes_credential_id`, a self-referential pointer to the credential this one replaces, and the `expedite` flag. Both are absent for a first issuance, in which case `supersedes_credential_id` is null and `reissuance_settlement_cents` is zero.

Several gates guard the provisioning, and they are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.6. The named handset must be in `ATTESTED` or `BOUND` and its `holder_id` must match the credential's subject; otherwise `ERR_DEVICE_BINDING_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). The physical credential must be eligible, or `ERR_PHYSICAL_CREDENTIAL_INELIGIBLE`. The named proofing session's `assurance_expires_at` must fall strictly later than the current logical instant — and a session that has already lapsed into `EXPIRED` fails the same way — with `ERR_PROOFING_ASSURANCE_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409). The session's `achieved_assurance_level` must sit at or above the profile's `required_assurance_level` on the ordered ladder where `IAL1` is weakest, `IAL1_5` sits in the middle and `IAL2` is strongest; a shortfall is `ERR_ASSURANCE_LEVEL_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422).

The validity window is checked against the other credentials living on the same handset, using the half-open, adjacency-permitting rule of §2.12. The comparison set is every other credential on that same `device_id` whose `lifecycle_state` is `PENDING_PROVISION`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, **excluding** the credential named by `supersedes_credential_id`, which is expected to be displaced. A strict overlap — the new window starting before the other's end while the other starts before the new window's end — is rejected with `ERR_CREDENTIAL_VALIDITY_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). A new window whose `valid_from` equals an existing window's `valid_until` is accepted, which is what makes back-to-back renewal on one handset possible.

A successful provisioning writes the credential in `PENDING_PROVISION` on the lifecycle axis and `IN_SYNC` on the sync axis, with `doc_version` — the payload revision pushed to the handset, distinct from the concurrency `version` — at 1. `assurance_level` is copied from the proofing session's `achieved_assurance_level`, `address_version_synced` is set from the holder's current `address_version`, `restriction_codes` is snapshotted from the holder's `restriction_codes`, and `age_only_capable` is set true, marking that the credential can answer an age-only presentation without releasing identifiers. Three cascading effects follow: the proofing session moves `VERIFIED → CONSUMED`; the handset moves `ATTESTED → BOUND` (a handset already in `BOUND` simply stays there, which is what happens on a renewal or a second validity window — the state edge fires only on the first provisioning) while its `active_binding_count` increments by 1; and the handset's `version` increments by 1. The holder's `bound_device_count` is deliberately **not** touched here — it was already incremented at attestation, and double-counting it would silently consume a device slot. What `active_binding_count` counts is the credentials on that handset in `PENDING_PROVISION`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`.

**Reissuance settlement.** When `supersedes_credential_id` is non-null, the credential's `reissuance_settlement_cents` is computed at provisioning time from the request and from the predecessor credential, which may be in any lifecycle state other than `REPLACED`. The evaluation order below is pinned, and it is pinned precisely because the rule looks circular — the waiver percentage is keyed on a fee total that the waiver itself reduces. No fixed-point iteration is performed.

1. A rebinding applies when the `device_id` in the request differs from the predecessor credential's `device_id`.
2. The pre-waiver total is the base reissuance fee of 3000 cents, plus 1500 cents when `expedite` is set, plus 800 cents when a rebinding applies.
3. The waiver tier is selected **from the pre-waiver total, never from the settlement**: 20 percent when the pre-waiver total is at least 5300 cents, otherwise 10 percent when it is at least 3800 cents, otherwise nothing.
4. An additional 5 percent is added to the waiver percentage when the predecessor credential is `SUSPENDED` with a `suspend_reason` of `DEVICE_LOST` — the resident whose phone was stolen is not charged full price for the replacement.
5. The waiver credit is the pre-waiver total multiplied by the combined waiver percentage, rounded half up to whole cents. This is the one and only rounding step.
6. The settlement is the pre-waiver total less the waiver credit.

Re-deriving the tier from the step-6 result would produce a different and wrong number and is explicitly forbidden.

**Activation** is the resident's own act, performed from the handset, and it carries a single input: `device_unlock_proof`, the unlock method the resident actually proved. Activation drives `PENDING_PROVISION → ACTIVE` and is guarded by a stack of conditions. The credential must be in `PENDING_PROVISION`; the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the credential's `holder_id` or the request fails with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; the handset must be in `ATTESTED` or `BOUND` and must still belong to the same holder, or the request fails with `ERR_DEVICE_BINDING_MISMATCH`; the originating proofing session must already be `CONSUMED`; the physical credential must still be eligible; and `device_unlock_proof` must match the handset's `protection_method` exactly, or the request fails with `ERR_PROTECTION_METHOD_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Finally the **active-credential slot cap** applies: the holder's `active_credential_count` must be strictly below the profile's `max_active_credentials_per_holder`, and a holder already at the cap is rejected with `ERR_ACTIVE_CREDENTIAL_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). A credential that supersedes an `ACTIVE` predecessor is exempt from that cap, because the predecessor is displaced inside the same request and the net slot count does not rise.

A successful activation records `activated_at` as the current logical instant, copies the handset's `protection_method` into `protection_method_at_activation` — the value every later consent must reproduce — increments the holder's `active_credential_count` by 1, and increments the credential's `version` by 1. Notably, activation appends **no** revocation ledger entry: a first valid status is implicit, and the credential's `revocation_sequence` stays at zero until something actually happens to it. When the credential names a predecessor and that predecessor is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, activation also displaces it in the same request: the predecessor moves to `REPLACED`, a revocation entry is appended against the predecessor with status `REVOKED` and reason `RENEWAL_SUPERSEDE`, the handset's `reprovision_count` increments by 1, and the holder's `active_credential_count` is decremented by 1 if — and only if — the predecessor had been `ACTIVE` rather than `SUSPENDED`.

Reading a credential exposes both state axes and lazily applies one transition: when the logical clock has reached or passed `valid_until`, an `ACTIVE` credential moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED`, which appends a revocation entry with status `EXPIRED` and reason `VALIDITY_LAPSED`, decrements the holder's `active_credential_count` by 1 and decrements the handset's `active_binding_count` by 1. The same lazy expiry is applied when a presentation is opened against the credential. Credentials can be listed and filtered by `holder_id`, `device_id`, `proofing_session_id`, `supersedes_credential_id`, `lifecycle_state` or `sync_state`.

### 3.5. Propagating Change to the Handset

Agency data changes; the payload on the phone must follow. Three distinct events open a **propagation order**, and all three run the same machinery. A `SystemAdmin` recording a new address of record supplies `address_line1`, `city`, `state_code` and `postal_code`; the holder's address fields are overwritten, the holder's `address_version` increments by 1, the holder's own `version` increments by 1, and an order is opened with `reason` `ADDRESS_CHANGE`. A `CredentialAdjudicator` renewing a credential — which requires the credential to be `ACTIVE`, failing with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` otherwise — supplies `new_valid_until`, which overwrites the credential's `valid_until` and increments the credential's `version` by 1, and opens an order with `reason` `RENEWAL`. A `SystemAdmin` recording new `restriction_codes` on the physical credential opens an order with `reason` `RESTRICTION_CHANGE`. The `reason` field takes its value from the set `ADDRESS_CHANGE`, `RENEWAL`, `RESTRICTION_CHANGE` and `REVOCATION`. Every order carries a `payload_summary`, a human-readable description of the changed fields it is carrying, and an `opened_at` recording the logical instant it was raised. Address-change requests return the created order rather than the holder.

`target_address_version` is what each order pins so that a delivery landing later cannot claim to have synced data it never carried. For an address change it is the holder's `address_version` **after** the increment; for a renewal and for a restriction change it is the holder's `address_version` unchanged, because neither of those events alters the address.

Opening an order runs a **five-hop cascade**, and every hop must fire:

1. The order itself is created in `OPEN` with `opened_at` and `target_address_version` set, and — for an address change only — the holder's `address_version` has already been bumped.
2. Caused by hop 1: one delivery is created in `PENDING` for **every** digital credential of that holder whose `lifecycle_state` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, in ascending `created_at` order of the credentials. Each delivery names its `update_order_id` and the `credential_id` it refreshes.
3. Caused by hop 2: every created delivery drives its target credential's `sync_state` to `UPDATE_PENDING` and increments that credential's `pending_update_count` by 1. A credential in `IN_SYNC` or in `UPDATE_FAILED` moves to `UPDATE_PENDING`; a credential in `STALE` moves to `UPDATE_PENDING` as well, which is how a stalled credential gets another chance.
4. Caused by hop 3: a credential entering `UPDATE_PENDING` aborts every presentation session against that credential whose state is `REQUESTED`, `CONSENT_PENDING` or `CONSENT_GRANTED` — each moves to `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` `CREDENTIAL_UPDATE_PENDING`. A credential whose on-device payload is known to be out of date must not be disclosed mid-flight.
5. Caused by hop 4: every aborted session appends a pseudonymised log entry with `outcome` `ABORTED`, `billed_amount_cents` zero and `billing_state` `UNBILLED`, and decrements the owning verifier's `open_session_count` by 1.

Alongside the cascade, any delivery still sitting in `PENDING` against the same credential from an **older** order moves `PENDING → SUPERSEDED`, because the newer order carries strictly fresher data. A superseded delivery is excluded from its own order's counters, and the older order's status is re-derived immediately.

The order's `status` is never set by a client. It is **always** derived from its children by the rollup rule, which runs at order creation and again after every child delivery leaves `PENDING`. The rollup counts `delivery_count` as the children whose `status` is anything other than `SUPERSEDED`, `delivered_count` as the children in `DELIVERED`, and `failed_count` as the children in `FAILED`. From those three counts the status follows: when `delivery_count` is zero, the status is `COMPLETED`; otherwise when `delivered_count` equals `delivery_count`, the status is `COMPLETED`; otherwise when `delivered_count` plus `failed_count` equals `delivery_count` — meaning nothing is outstanding and at least one child failed — the status is `FAILED_CLOSED`; otherwise when `delivered_count` plus `failed_count` is greater than zero, the status is `PARTIALLY_DELIVERED`; otherwise the status remains `OPEN`. When the resulting status is `COMPLETED` or `FAILED_CLOSED` and `closed_at` was still null, `closed_at` is set to the current logical instant; in every other case `closed_at` is left as it was. `COMPLETED` and `FAILED_CLOSED` are the terminal statuses of an order.

The zero-children case deserves emphasis because it is a first-class outcome and not an error: an address change for a resident who holds no credential in `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` still produces a real order, which the rollup immediately closes as `COMPLETED` inside the very same request, with `closed_at` set. The order transitions `OPEN → COMPLETED` before the client ever sees it. Orders can be listed and filtered by `holder_id`, `reason` or `status`, and an order's children can be listed in `created_at` ascending order and filtered by `status`; a credential's own deliveries can likewise be listed and filtered by `status`.

Confirming a delivery is a `SystemAdmin` action carrying the handset-signed `device_receipt_token`. It requires the delivery to still be in `PENDING` — otherwise `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_PENDING` (HTTP 409) — requires the handset behind the credential to be in `BOUND`, otherwise `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_BOUND` (HTTP 409), requires the credential's `lifecycle_state` to be `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and requires the credential's replayed revocation status to read valid and to be fresh within the profile's `revocation_freshness_seconds`, otherwise `ERR_REVOCATION_STATUS_STALE` (HTTP 409). On success the delivery moves `PENDING → DELIVERED`, its `attempt_count` increments by 1, its `delivered_at` is set to the current logical instant and the supplied `device_receipt_token` is recorded. The credential's `pending_update_count` decrements by 1, and when that leaves no outstanding delivery — that is, when the count had been exactly 1 — the credential's sync axis moves `UPDATE_PENDING → IN_SYNC`, its `doc_version` increments by 1, its `address_version_synced` takes the owning order's `target_address_version`, and its `restriction_codes` are refreshed from the holder's current `restriction_codes`. The delivery's `resulting_doc_version` records the credential's `doc_version` after that increment, and remains null on any delivery that never reached `DELIVERED`. Finally the owning order's counters are refreshed and its status and `closed_at` are re-derived by the rollup.

Recording a failed push is the same actor's other option and carries a `failure_code` of `DEVICE_UNREACHABLE`, `ATTESTATION_STALE` or `USER_DECLINED`. It too requires the delivery to be in `PENDING`, failing with `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_PENDING` otherwise. The delivery moves `PENDING → FAILED`, its `attempt_count` increments by 1 and its `failure_code` is recorded; the credential's `pending_update_count` decrements by 1 and its `failed_delivery_count` increments by 1. What happens next depends on **which** failure this is for that credential, and the two branches are specified separately:

- On the **first** failure for that credential — the case where `failed_delivery_count` had been zero before this failure — the sync axis moves `UPDATE_PENDING → UPDATE_FAILED` and the lifecycle axis is left completely untouched. The resident's credential still works; only its payload is behind.
- On the **second** failure for that credential — the case where `failed_delivery_count` had been exactly 1 before this failure — the sync axis moves `UPDATE_PENDING → STALE`, and the single cross-axis coupling of §2.8 fires: the lifecycle axis moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspend_reason` `UPDATE_FAILED`, which appends a revocation entry with status `SUSPENDED` and reason `UPDATE_FAILED` and decrements the holder's `active_credential_count` by 1. Two consecutive failures to reach the handset mean the agency can no longer vouch for what is on it.

Either way the owning order's counters are refreshed and the rollup re-derives its status and `closed_at`. A credential in `UPDATE_FAILED` or in `STALE` returns to `UPDATE_PENDING` as soon as a later order opens a new delivery against it, which closes the `UPDATE_PENDING → UPDATE_FAILED → UPDATE_PENDING` cycle on the sync axis.

### 3.6. Loss, Suspension, Revocation and the Near-Real-Time Ledger

A credential's standing at any instant is not stored as a single flag; it is **replayed** from an append-only ledger. Every ledger entry names its `credential_id`, carries a per-credential `sequence` — a monotonically increasing counter that starts at 1 for the first entry against a credential and is the replay order key — a `status` of `SUSPENDED`, `REVOKED`, `REINSTATED` or `EXPIRED`, a `reason` drawn from `DEVICE_LOST`, `HOLDER_REQUEST`, `FRAUD`, `PHYSICAL_CREDENTIAL_REVOKED`, `RENEWAL_SUPERSEDE`, `ADMIN_HOLD`, `RECOVERY`, `VALIDITY_LAPSED` and `UPDATE_FAILED`, an `effective_at` set to the logical instant of the append, and a `published` flag that turns true once a trust-list snapshot published after the entry covers it. Entries are never modified or deleted. The credential's own `revocation_sequence` mirrors the `sequence` of the newest entry against it, and reads zero while no entry exists.

The replay itself is exact and order-sensitive. Entries are read in ascending `sequence` order — never in insertion order and never keyed on `id` — starting from an effective status of valid. An entry with status `SUSPENDED` sets the effective status to suspended. An entry with status `REVOKED` sets it to revoked and is **absorbing**: no later entry can lift it. An entry with status `EXPIRED` is likewise absorbing. An entry with status `REINSTATED` returns the effective status to valid **only** when the effective status at that point in the replay is suspended; in any other case the entry is retained in the ledger and ignored by the replay. The credential's revocation-status read reports the replayed `status`, the `entry_sequence` of the newest entry (zero when there are none), that entry's `effective_at`, a `staleness_seconds` measuring the whole seconds elapsed from that entry's `effective_at` to the current logical instant with any fraction discarded (zero when there are no entries), and an `is_fresh` verdict that is true when `staleness_seconds` is at most the profile's `revocation_freshness_seconds` — the comparison is inclusive, so a staleness of exactly 300 seconds against the default profile is still fresh. The ledger of one credential can also be read directly in ascending `sequence` order.

**Reporting a handset lost** is the resident's emergency stop, and a `SystemAdmin` may do it on their behalf. It drives the handset `BOUND → LOST_REPORTED`, records `lost_reported_at` as the current logical instant, and increments the handset's `version` by 1. Three hops follow, and the second and third are consequences of the first rather than of the call:

1. Every credential on that handset whose `lifecycle_state` is `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspend_reason` `DEVICE_LOST`.
2. Caused by hop 1: each such suspension appends a ledger entry with status `SUSPENDED` and reason `DEVICE_LOST`, and decrements the holder's `active_credential_count` by 1.
3. Caused by hop 2: each appended entry sets the newest trust-list snapshot of the active profile to `revocation_pointer_stale` true, increments that snapshot's `pending_delta_count` by 1, and increments the profile's `pending_republish_count` by 1.

**Recovering a handset** is the exact compensating action, and it must restore the counters with no drift. It carries a `reattestation_token`; without one the request fails with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The handset moves `LOST_REPORTED → BOUND`, `lost_reported_at` is cleared to null, and the handset's `version` increments by 1. Its own three hops mirror the loss cascade: every credential on that handset that is `SUSPENDED` **with `suspend_reason` exactly `DEVICE_LOST`** moves `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` with `suspend_reason` cleared to null; each such reinstatement appends a ledger entry with status `REINSTATED` and reason `RECOVERY` and increments the holder's `active_credential_count` by 1; and each appended entry again marks the newest snapshot `revocation_pointer_stale`, increments its `pending_delta_count` by 1 and increments the profile's `pending_republish_count` by 1. Credentials suspended for any other reason are left alone — recovery of a phone does not undo an administrative hold. A reinstatement additionally requires the current logical instant to still be before the credential's `valid_until`. This closes the `BOUND → LOST_REPORTED → BOUND` cycle on the handset and the `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` cycle on the credential's lifecycle axis.

**Wiping a handset** is the `SystemAdmin`'s terminal option for a phone that is not coming back, available only from `LOST_REPORTED`. The handset moves `LOST_REPORTED → WIPED`, its `active_binding_count` is set to zero, the holder's `bound_device_count` decrements by 1 — freeing the device slot at last — and the handset's `version` increments by 1. Every credential on that handset in `SUSPENDED` moves `SUSPENDED → REVOKED`, each appending a ledger entry with status `REVOKED` and reason `DEVICE_LOST` and incrementing the holder's `revoked_credential_count` by 1, and each decrementing the handset's `active_binding_count`. Because those credentials had already been decremented out of `active_credential_count` at suspension, that counter is not touched a second time.

**Changing the physical credential** is the other major source of cascades, and its three status branches are specified in equal detail. The `SystemAdmin` may supply any combination of `physical_credential_status`, `physical_credential_expires_on` and `restriction_codes`; the holder's `version` increments by 1 in every case.

- Setting `physical_credential_status` to `SUSPENDED` drives every credential of that holder in `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` with `suspend_reason` `PHYSICAL_CREDENTIAL_SUSPENDED`, each appending a ledger entry with status `SUSPENDED` and reason `ADMIN_HOLD` and decrementing the holder's `active_credential_count` by 1.
- Returning `physical_credential_status` to `VALID` drives every credential of that holder in `SUSPENDED` **whose `suspend_reason` is exactly `PHYSICAL_CREDENTIAL_SUSPENDED`** back to `ACTIVE` with `suspend_reason` cleared to null, each appending a ledger entry with status `REINSTATED` and reason `RECOVERY` and incrementing the holder's `active_credential_count` by 1. Credentials suspended because the phone was lost stay suspended.
- Setting `physical_credential_status` to `REVOKED` drives every credential of that holder in `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` to `REVOKED`, each appending a ledger entry with status `REVOKED` and reason `PHYSICAL_CREDENTIAL_REVOKED` and incrementing the holder's `revoked_credential_count` by 1.
- Supplying `restriction_codes` opens a propagation order with `reason` `RESTRICTION_CHANGE` whose `target_address_version` is the holder's unchanged `address_version`, running the identical five-hop cascade described in §3.5.

Every ledger entry appended by any of those branches also marks the newest trust-list snapshot `revocation_pointer_stale` true, increments its `pending_delta_count` by 1 and increments the profile's `pending_republish_count` by 1.

**Explicit revocation** is a `SystemAdmin` action that supplies a `reason` and is accepted from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`; from any other lifecycle state it fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The credential moves to `REVOKED`, terminal on the lifecycle axis. A ledger entry is appended with status `REVOKED` and the supplied `reason`, and the credential's `revocation_sequence` takes that entry's `sequence`. The holder's `revoked_credential_count` increments by 1; the holder's `active_credential_count` decrements by 1 only when the credential had been `ACTIVE`, since a suspended credential was already decremented; and the handset's `active_binding_count` decrements by 1. Every presentation session against that credential in `REQUESTED`, `CONSENT_PENDING` or `CONSENT_GRANTED` moves to `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` `CREDENTIAL_REVOKED`, each appending a log entry with `outcome` `ABORTED` and decrementing the owning verifier's `open_session_count` by 1. And the newest trust-list snapshot is marked `revocation_pointer_stale` true with `pending_delta_count` incremented by 1 and the profile's `pending_republish_count` incremented by 1. The credential's `version` increments by 1.

Read together, the `suspend_reason` values tell the whole suspension story: `DEVICE_LOST` for a reported-lost handset, `PHYSICAL_CREDENTIAL_SUSPENDED` for a plastic on hold, `UPDATE_FAILED` for the second consecutive delivery failure, and `ADMIN_HOLD` for a discretionary hold. The field is null in every lifecycle state other than `SUSPENDED`.

### 3.7. Admitting Verifiers and Reader Applications to the Ecosystem

A relying party enters the ecosystem as a **verifier**: a `SystemAdmin` registers the `organization_name`, a `category` of `COMMERCIAL`, `LAW_ENFORCEMENT`, `GOVERNMENT_SERVICE` or `AGE_RESTRICTED_RETAIL`, a `purpose_scope` listing the exact element names the organisation's declared purpose entitles it to receive in an ordinary presentation, and a `retention_ceiling_days` between 0 and 90 inclusive. A new verifier begins in `trust_status` `APPLIED` with `exposure_index`, `open_session_count`, `billable_query_balance_cents`, `credited_amount_cents` and `probation_strike_count` all at zero, and it cannot register reader applications or open presentations until it has been approved. The trust lifecycle is genuinely cyclic: the full set is `APPLIED`, `APPROVED`, `PROBATION`, `SUSPENDED` and `TERMINATED`, with only `TERMINATED` terminal.

A `SystemAdmin` drives that lifecycle with a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `PROBATION`, `SUSPEND`, `REINSTATE` or `TERMINATE`, and every accepted decision increments the verifier's `version` by 1.

- `APPROVE` moves `APPLIED → APPROVED`, and is accepted only from `APPLIED`; from anywhere else it fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.
- `PROBATION` moves `APPROVED → PROBATION`. Reader applications stay `LISTED` throughout probation — the organisation keeps operating — but every invoice line acquires a 15 percent surcharge for as long as the verifier remains on probation.
- `SUSPEND` moves the verifier to `SUSPENDED` and is accepted from `APPROVED` or from `PROBATION`. Presentation sessions already in flight are deliberately left alone; what suspension does is bar **new** presentations, which are then rejected with `ERR_VERIFIER_NOT_TRUSTED`.
- `REINSTATE` returns a verifier to `APPROVED` from either `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED`, closing the `APPROVED → PROBATION → APPROVED` cycle. It carries two hard conditions and one more that applies only on the probation path: the verifier's `exposure_index` must be strictly below 60, and — when reinstating from `PROBATION` — its `last_attestation_at` must not be null, meaning the organisation has actually filed a retention attestation. A reinstatement attempted from any other status, or with an `exposure_index` of 60 or more, or from `PROBATION` without a prior attestation, fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.
- `TERMINATE` moves the verifier to `TERMINATED` and is accepted from any status other than `TERMINATED` itself. Every reader application of that verifier whose `trust_state` is `LISTED` becomes `DELISTED`, and every presentation session of those applications in `REQUESTED`, `CONSENT_PENDING` or `CONSENT_GRANTED` moves to `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` `VERIFIER_TERMINATED`, each appending a log entry with `outcome` `ABORTED` and decrementing `open_session_count` by 1.

Two of those transitions can also fire without any administrator involvement, driven by the exposure index described in §3.9: an index landing at 60 or above drives an `APPROVED` verifier to `PROBATION`, and an index landing at 90 or above drives an `APPROVED` or `PROBATION` verifier to `SUSPENDED`. When a threshold breach is the cause, `probation_strike_count` increments by 1; an administrator's discretionary decision does not move that counter.

An approved verifier registers **reader applications** itself, acting as `VerifierOperator`, and may only register them under its own `verifier_id` — a mismatch against `X-User-ID` is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Registration is refused with `ERR_VERIFIER_NOT_TRUSTED` (HTTP 403) unless the owning verifier's `trust_status` is `APPROVED` or `PROBATION`; that same trust gate also guards presentation-session creation, so `APPLIED`, `SUSPENDED` and `TERMINATED` verifiers are shut out of both operations. A registration supplies an `app_identifier` between 1 and 64 characters, the `certificate_serial` of the reader certificate presented for trust listing, the certificate window `cert_not_before` and `cert_not_after`, the `requested_elements` the application intends to ask for, and optionally a `base_rate_cents` per-query base rate that defaults to 25 cents and may not be negative. The application starts in `trust_state` `PENDING` with `query_count` at zero and `volume_discount_pct` at zero.

`app_identifier` is deliberately **not** unique: a certificate renewal registers a brand-new application row sharing the identifier with its predecessor, and that is exactly the case the renewal-buffer rule polices. When a new registration shares its `app_identifier` with an existing application of the same verifier whose `trust_state` is `PENDING` or `LISTED`, the new `cert_not_before` must fall at least 24 hours after that incumbent's `cert_not_after`. Anything earlier — including exact adjacency, where the new certificate begins at the very instant the old one ends — is rejected with `ERR_CERT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 422). Separately, the pair of owning verifier and `certificate_serial` must not already be registered; a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_READER_APP` (HTTP 409).

Listing decisions belong to the agency, not the relying party. A `SystemAdmin` supplies a `decision` of `LIST` or `DELIST`: listing sets `trust_state` to `LISTED` and records `listed_at` as the current logical instant, delisting sets `trust_state` to `DELISTED`, and either way the newest trust-list snapshot of the active profile has its `pending_delta_count` incremented by 1, the profile's `pending_republish_count` is incremented by 1, and the application's `version` increments by 1. A verifier's applications can be listed and filtered by `trust_state` or `app_identifier`, and each carries its live `query_count` — the number of that application's presentation sessions that reached `COMPLETED` — and its `volume_discount_pct`, which is 10 percent once `query_count` has reached 250 and nothing at all below that.

### 3.8. Presentation, Consent and Data Minimisation

A presentation begins when a `VerifierOperator` opens a session naming the `credential_id` being presented and the `reader_app_id` doing the asking, along with the `context`, a human-readable `purpose` string shown to the resident, the `requested_elements` the reader wants, the declared `handset_handling`, and the `retention_days` the organisation commits to. The `context` is `ROUTINE`, `AGE_ONLY` or `LAW_ENFORCEMENT`, and it determines both which allow-list governs minimisation and whether the law-enforcement entitlement check applies.

The gates run in the ladder order of §2.6, and the ordering has real consequences. First, `handset_handling` is checked: a declared value of `SURRENDER_REQUESTED` is rejected outright with `ERR_HANDSET_SURRENDER_PROHIBITED` (HTTP 422), and only `NO_SURRENDER` and `TAP_TO_READ` proceed — this holds at a roadside stop exactly as it holds at a liquor store, and it is checked before any trust or listing problem so that the prohibited demand is named as the reason. Second, a `context` of `LAW_ENFORCEMENT` is permitted only when the owning verifier's `category` is `LAW_ENFORCEMENT`; otherwise `ERR_LAW_ENFORCEMENT_CONTEXT_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). Third, the reader application's `trust_state` must be `LISTED` **and** the newest trust-list snapshot of the active profile must actually cover the current logical instant, meaning the instant falls at or after `effective_from` and strictly before `effective_until`; either failure is `ERR_READER_NOT_TRUSTED` (HTTP 403). Fourth, the owning verifier's `trust_status` must be `APPROVED` or `PROBATION`, or `ERR_VERIFIER_NOT_TRUSTED`. Then the minimisation projection runs, and finally `retention_days` must be no greater than the verifier's `retention_ceiling_days` — exactly the ceiling is accepted — or the request is rejected with `ERR_RETENTION_CEILING_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). That retention check is enforced at **both** ends of the presentation: at session creation and again at disclosure.

**Minimisation** is exact set algebra over a fixed canonical element ordering. The eleven core elements, in canonical order, are `family_name`, `given_name`, `birth_date`, `portrait`, `document_number`, `expiry_date`, `issuing_authority`, `driving_privileges`, `address`, `age_over_18` and `age_over_21`. The allow-list depends entirely on the context: in the `AGE_ONLY` context it is the profile's `age_only_allowed_elements`; in the `LAW_ENFORCEMENT` context it is the full set of core elements; in the `ROUTINE` context it is the intersection of the reader application's own `requested_elements` with the owning verifier's `purpose_scope`. From that allow-list the projection writes two arrays. `minimized_elements` contains every core element that appears both in the session's `requested_elements` and in the allow-list, emitted in canonical core order. `withheld_elements` contains every requested element that is **not** in the allow-list, emitted with the core elements first in canonical order and then any requested element that is not a core element at all, in the order the request itself listed them. `excess_element_count` is the number of withheld elements. `age_attestation_only` is true when the context is `AGE_ONLY` and every element in `minimized_elements` is one of `age_over_18` and `age_over_21`.

The two enforcement postures differ sharply and are the reason `AGE_ONLY` exists. **`AGE_ONLY` is strict**: if anything at all would be withheld — that is, if the excess count would be greater than zero because some requested element is not a member of `age_only_allowed_elements` — the request is rejected with `ERR_DATA_MINIMIZATION_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422) and **no session row is created at all**. A retailer asking for an age attestation plus a document number does not get a partial answer; it gets a refusal. **`ROUTINE` and `LAW_ENFORCEMENT` are soft**: the surplus elements are simply withheld, the session proceeds, and the excess count is carried forward into the verifier's exposure index as a permanent record of the over-asking.

A session that clears every gate then **forks**, and the branch depends on the credential's presentability. The credential's `lifecycle_state` must be `ACTIVE`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and this is where the lazy expiry of §3.4 is applied. Given an active credential, the session moves `REQUESTED → CONSENT_PENDING` when the credential's `sync_state` is `IN_SYNC` **and** its replayed revocation staleness is at most the profile's `revocation_freshness_seconds`; the owning verifier's `open_session_count` increments by 1 and `opened_at` is set to the current logical instant. If instead the `sync_state` is anything other than `IN_SYNC`, or the staleness exceeds the freshness window, the session instead moves `REQUESTED → BLOCKED_BY_POLICY`: a log entry is appended with `outcome` `BLOCKED_BY_POLICY`, `disclosed_element_count` zero, `billed_amount_cents` zero and `billing_state` `UNBILLED`; `open_session_count` is **not** incremented because the session never opened; nothing is billed; and the reader application's `query_count` does not move. Note the deliberate asymmetry with §3.5: the same stale-revocation condition that merely blocks a presentation is a hard `ERR_REVOCATION_STATUS_STALE` rejection on the delivery-confirmation path. Whichever branch is taken, a freshly created session records `opened_at`, `disclosed_element_count` zero, `billed_amount_cents` zero and `billing_state` `UNBILLED`. The full session state set is `REQUESTED`, `CONSENT_PENDING`, `CONSENT_GRANTED`, `COMPLETED`, `DENIED_BY_HOLDER`, `BLOCKED_BY_POLICY` and `ABORTED`, of which the last four are terminal.

**Consent** belongs to the resident and to nobody else. The session must be in `CONSENT_PENDING`, or the request fails with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_CONSENT_PENDING` (HTTP 409), and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the presented credential's `holder_id`, or `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The request carries a `consent_decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY` and, when approving, an `unlock_method`; an `APPROVE` arriving without `unlock_method` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The supplied `unlock_method` must equal the credential's `protection_method_at_activation` exactly, or the request is rejected with `ERR_PROTECTION_METHOD_MISMATCH` — the same code and the same kind of check as the activation-time `device_unlock_proof` comparison, applied at the second of the two points where an unlock proof is demanded. The two branches carry different consequences:

- `APPROVE` moves `CONSENT_PENDING → CONSENT_GRANTED` and records `consent_unlock_method` from the supplied value. Nothing else moves — no log entry, no billing, no counter.
- `DENY` moves `CONSENT_PENDING → DENIED_BY_HOLDER` and appends a log entry with `outcome` `DENIED_BY_HOLDER`, `disclosed_element_count` zero, `excess_element_count` copied from the session's own excess count, `billed_amount_cents` zero and `billing_state` `UNBILLED`. The verifier's `open_session_count` decrements by 1. Nothing is billed and the reader application's `query_count` does not move. Carrying the excess count into the log on a refusal is deliberate: a reader that over-asks accrues exposure even when the resident says no.

Either branch increments the session's `version` by 1.

**Disclosure** is the `VerifierOperator`'s final act and the only branch of the fork that has financial consequences. The session must be in `CONSENT_GRANTED`, or `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the reader application's `verifier_id`, or `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; `retention_days` must still be within the verifier's `retention_ceiling_days`, or `ERR_RETENTION_CEILING_EXCEEDED`; and the verifier's `billable_query_balance_cents` must be no greater than 50000 cents — that is, 500 dollars of unsettled query charges — or the disclosure is refused with `ERR_VERIFIER_ACCOUNT_DELINQUENT` (HTTP 402). On success the session moves `CONSENT_GRANTED → COMPLETED`; `disclosed_element_count` is set to the number of entries in `minimized_elements`, which the projection wrote at creation and which is re-read unchanged here; `billed_amount_cents` is set to the reader application's `base_rate_cents`; and `billing_state` becomes `BILLED`. The reader application's `query_count` increments by 1 and its `volume_discount_pct` is recomputed against the 250-query threshold. The verifier's `billable_query_balance_cents` rises by the billed amount and its `open_session_count` decrements by 1. A log entry is appended with `outcome` `DISCLOSED` and `billing_state` `BILLED`, carrying the per-verifier salted `credential_pseudonym`, the session's `retention_days` copied into `retention_days_declared`, the billed amount, a computed `pseudonym_reuse_index`, and `recorded_at` set to the current logical instant. Finally the verifier's `exposure_index` is recomputed by the rolling-window variant and its thresholds are applied. The session's `version` increments by 1.

Every log entry names the `presentation_session_id` it records and the `verifier_id` that made the request, and copies `disclosed_element_count` and `excess_element_count` from the session at the moment of logging. Its `pseudonym_reuse_index` is one more than the number of existing log entries that share both the same `verifier_id` and the same `credential_pseudonym` and whose `recorded_at` is strictly earlier — so a verifier's first sighting of a credential scores 1, its second scores 2, and at 3 or above the entry begins to count as a tracking signal. Log entries are immutable apart from the billing posture, and a session's own log entries can be read back directly. A verifier's whole log can be read in `recorded_at` ascending order by the `PrivacyAuditor` or a `SystemAdmin`, filtered by `outcome` or `billing_state`. Presentation sessions themselves can be listed and filtered by `credential_id`, `reader_app_id` or `state`.

### 3.9. Privacy Oversight: the Exposure Index, Invoicing and Compensation

The agency does not police verifier behaviour by inspecting individual disclosures; it scores the whole log. A verifier's **exposure index** is a weighted count of three privacy-adverse signals found in its pseudonymised log entries, and the entries counted are **every** entry of that verifier inside the window with no filtering on `outcome` — a denied or aborted presentation still records the reader's over-asking, and that is the whole point of writing the excess count onto refusals.

Three signals are counted. **Over-retention**: the number of entries whose `retention_days_declared` is strictly greater than the applicable ceiling, weighted 12 points each. The applicable ceiling is the verifier's `attested_max_retention_days` when it has ever filed an attestation, and otherwise its contractual `retention_ceiling_days` — except during a retention attestation itself, where the ceiling used is the `attested_max_retention_days` arriving in that very request, because the purpose of the attestation is to score the historical log against the newly declared, usually lower, ceiling. **Excess elements**: the sum of `excess_element_count` across all entries in the window, weighted 6 points each. **Pseudonym reuse**: the number of entries whose `pseudonym_reuse_index` is 3 or more, weighted 20 points each — a verifier that keeps seeing the same pseudonym is, whatever its intent, in a position to track a person. The index is the sum of those three weighted terms, capped at 100 points; a raw sum above 100 is reported as 100.

The two variants differ **only** in which log entries fall inside the window, and for an identical log they diverge whenever an entry sits inside one window and outside the other. The **rolling-window** variant counts entries whose `recorded_at` falls at or after the current logical instant less 30 days and at or before the current logical instant, with the lower bound inclusive. The **since-last-attestation** variant counts entries whose `recorded_at` is at or after the verifier's `last_attestation_at` as it stood **before** the attestation being processed; when `last_attestation_at` is still null, that window is every entry ever recorded for the verifier. A read-only capability returns both side by side — `rolling_window_index` with its `rolling_window_entry_count`, `since_last_attestation_index` with its `since_attestation_entry_count`, and the `computed_at` instant of the computation — without writing anything.

Both variants write `exposure_index` when they run for real, and both apply the same **threshold triggers immediately after the index is written**. An index of 90 or more drives a verifier whose `trust_status` is `APPROVED` or `PROBATION` to `SUSPENDED` and increments `probation_strike_count` by 1. Otherwise an index of 60 or more drives an `APPROVED` verifier to `PROBATION` and increments `probation_strike_count` by 1. Both comparisons are inclusive at the boundary: an index of exactly 60 trips probation while 59 does not, and an index of exactly 90 trips suspension. The rolling-window variant runs on every disclosure, as described in §3.8. The since-last-attestation variant runs when a `VerifierOperator` files a **retention attestation**, declaring the `attested_max_retention_days` the organisation actually applies in practice; that call scores the historical log against the newly declared ceiling, records the declared value as `attested_max_retention_days`, applies the thresholds, sets `last_attestation_at` to the current logical instant — restarting the window — and increments the verifier's `version` by 1. An operator may only attest for its own verifier record; otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

**Invoicing** is a read-only computation over the verifier's reader applications, and its evaluation order is pinned because the ordering changes the answer. A surcharge percentage of 15 percent applies when the verifier's `trust_status` is `PROBATION`, and nothing otherwise. The applications considered are those of the verifier whose `trust_state` is `LISTED` or `DELISTED`, iterated in ascending `created_at` order; applications still in `PENDING` are excluded entirely, because they have never been in a position to serve a query. For each application in turn:

1. A per-query rate is chosen from the application's whole volume, not marginally per tier. When `query_count` is at most 100, the rate is the application's `base_rate_cents` unchanged. When `query_count` is greater than 100 and at most 500, the rate is `base_rate_cents` multiplied by 0.80, rounded half up to whole cents. When `query_count` is greater than 500, the rate is `base_rate_cents` multiplied by 0.60, rounded half up to whole cents. The chosen rate applies to **every** query the application served, not only to the ones above a boundary.
2. The gross line value is `query_count` multiplied by that rate.
3. The line amount is the gross value multiplied by one less the application's `volume_discount_pct`, then multiplied by one plus the surcharge percentage, and that product is rounded half up to whole cents.

The invoice total is the **sum of the already-rounded line amounts**. Rounding is applied once per line, before summation — it is emphatically not applied once to the sum of the unrounded line values. Two lines whose unrounded values both end exactly halfway between two cents yield two upward roundings under the pinned order and only one under the sum-then-round reading, so the two orders give different totals and only the pinned one is correct. The computation returns `invoice_amount_cents`, the `line_count` of applications that contributed a line, the `surcharge_pct` in force, and the verifier's current `billable_query_balance_cents`.

**Voiding a billing** is the `PrivacyAuditor`'s only write authority, and it is the exact compensating action for the billing side effects of a disclosure. It is available only when the session's `billing_state` is `BILLED`; a session that was never billed or whose billing has already been voided is rejected with `ERR_BILLING_ALREADY_VOIDED` (HTTP 409). The session's `billing_state` moves `BILLED → VOIDED` while `billed_amount_cents` is left intact as the audit record of what was voided; every log entry of that session has its `billing_state` set to `VOIDED`; the verifier's `credited_amount_cents` rises by the voided amount and its `billable_query_balance_cents` falls by exactly the same amount, returning the balance to its precise pre-disclosure value with no drift. The verifier's `exposure_index` is deliberately **left unchanged** — the exposure formula has no billing filter at all, so a billing compensation must never move the privacy score. The session's `version` increments by 1.

The segregation of duties that governs proofing governs money too: the `VerifierOperator` that was billed for a disclosure may never void that billing, and the `PrivacyAuditor` that can void it holds no issuance authority and cannot open a presentation in the first place. What `billable_query_balance_cents` represents at any moment is precisely the sum of `billed_amount_cents` across that verifier's log entries whose `billing_state` is still `BILLED`, and that identity must hold after every billing and every void.
