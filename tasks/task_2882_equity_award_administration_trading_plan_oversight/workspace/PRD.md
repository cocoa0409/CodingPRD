# Equity Award Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Public-company stock plan administration is where three normally separate disciplines collide: corporate governance (who may authorise an award, and under what delegated budget), payroll taxation (which country and which state may tax income earned by an employee who moved twice during the vesting period), and securities compliance (when an insider may trade, how long a pre-established plan must sit in a cooling-off period, and by when a transaction must be reported). Each discipline has its own calendar, its own arithmetic, and its own idea of what "final" means. Most issuers glue them together with spreadsheets, and the glue fails in exactly the places regulators look first — a grant approved by the same person who drafted it, a share reserve that quietly went negative, a Section 16 report filed a day late because nobody counted business days.

This document specifies the Equity Award Administration API: a single system of record for share plans, awards, vesting, tax withholding, and insider-trading oversight. It is deliberately narrow in one respect and unforgiving in another. Narrow, because it does not attempt to be a general ledger or a brokerage — it models commitments against an authorised share pool, not cash accounts. Unforgiving, because every fiscal number it reports must be reconstructible from the operations that produced it: the share reserve is an accumulator pair, not a re-derived scan; the burn rate is reported in two variants that are permitted to disagree; the per-jurisdiction tax total is a round-then-sum figure whose evaluation order is pinned so that two implementations cannot differ by a cent.

The intended readers are the engineers who will build this service. Wherever this document names a threshold, a boundary, or an ordering, that value is the contract — not a suggestion to be normalised later. The system is exercised by six operator populations (stock plan administration, the compensation committee, delegated officers, insider-trading compliance, award recipients themselves, and read-only audit), and every operation in it is either a state transition on a named entity or a computed read over entities that already exist.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API carries its identity in two HTTP headers. `X-User-ID` names the acting identity, and `X-Role` names the operational capacity in which that identity is acting. Both are mandatory on every endpoint, including reads and including the logical-clock endpoint. Some clients and gateways spell the role header `X-User-Role`; an implementation must accept either spelling for the role header and treat whichever one is present as authoritative, but the canonical name used throughout this document is `X-Role`.

Six roles exist, and their names are matched exactly and case-sensitively: `PlanAdministrator`, `BoardMember`, `DelegatedOfficer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `Participant`, and `Auditor`. A request whose `X-User-ID` header is absent, empty, or consists only of whitespace is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A request whose role header is absent, or whose value is not one of those six exact strings, is likewise rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and not with a permission error — no identity can be established, so the failure is authentication rather than authorisation. When the headers are well formed but the named role is not permitted to invoke the endpoint, the answer is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

Five of the six roles are **opaque operator identities**. When `X-Role` is `PlanAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition says so (for example as `submitted_by_user_id` on a grant, or as `created_by_user_id` on a performance cycle) but never resolves it to a row. The same holds for `BoardMember` (recorded as `approved_by_user_id` on the board approval branch and as `certified_by_user_id` on a certified performance cycle), for `DelegatedOfficer` (recorded as `approved_by_user_id`, and additionally compared for equality against the `delegate_user_id` of the delegation being exercised), for `ComplianceOfficer` (recorded as `decided_by_user_id` on a preclearance decision), and for `Auditor` (never recorded anywhere, because the role writes nothing).

The sixth role is entity-backed. When `X-Role` is `Participant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Participant` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Participant` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. A `Participant` may therefore reach their own participant record, their own grants, tranches, settlements, tax allocations, mobility assignments, preclearance requests, trading plans, and Section 16 reports, and nothing belonging to a colleague. An attempt to reach a sibling participant's row returns `ERR_PARTICIPANT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403) and never a 404, so the mere existence of the other row is not disclosed. This row-level scoping check applies to every read a `Participant` may perform — the single-participant read, the single-grant read, the single-tranche read, the single-settlement read, the settlement's tax-allocation list, the single tax-allocation read, the participant's mobility-assignment list, the participant's Section 16 report list, the single preclearance-request read, and the single trading-plan read — and to every write a `Participant` may perform: creating a preclearance request, resubmitting one, executing one, adopting a trading plan, activating a trading plan, modifying one, and terminating one. Roles other than `Participant` skip the scoping rule entirely.

The `Auditor` role is read-only in the strongest sense: it appears in the authorised-role list of every read endpoint and of no write endpoint, so every write attempt by an `Auditor` — including an attempt to move the logical clock — is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

One endpoint carries a second layer of role gating. Grant approval is open to both `BoardMember` and `DelegatedOfficer`, but the two roles own different branches of the same operation: the `BOARD` approval path is the `BoardMember` branch and the `DELEGATED` approval path is the `DelegatedOfficer` branch. A `DelegatedOfficer` selecting the board path, or a `BoardMember` selecting the delegated path, is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` before any fiscal or delegation check runs.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0; it has no upper bound. A `limit` of 0, a `limit` of 101, or a negative `offset` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400), while a `limit` of 1, a `limit` of 100, and an `offset` of 0 are all accepted. Pagination bounds are validated separately from ordinary request-shape validation so that a caller can distinguish a bad page window from a bad filter.

Unless a list endpoint's description states another ordering, results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. Three list endpoints state a domain ordering instead: a participant's mobility assignments are returned in `start_date` order, a plan's trading windows in `open_at` order, a settlement's tax allocations in `jurisdiction_code` order, and an insider's Section 16 reports in `transaction_at` order.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside `meta`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every stored row is addressed by an `id` that is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix; clients never supply it. Every row also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits.

Most entities additionally carry `updated_at` — refreshed on every mutation of that row, in the same format — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations caused by a cascade the client did not directly request. `EquityPlan`, `ApprovalAuthority`, `Participant`, `Grant`, `VestingTranche`, `PerformanceCycle`, `VestingEvent`, `TradingWindow`, `PreclearanceRequest`, `TradingPlan`, and `TransactionReport` all behave this way.

Two entities are immutable by construction and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at`: a `MobilityAssignment`, once recorded, is never updated, and a `TaxAllocation` row is created by an allocation run and deleted by a reversal but never edited in place. Neither of them has an `updated_at` field and neither of them has a `version` field; there is no update endpoint for either.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads the wall clock. Every temporal comparison — a scheduled vest date against today, a cooling-off deadline, a filing deadline, whether a trading window is currently open — is evaluated against a deterministic **logical clock** whose value at boot is `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and which never advances on its own. An administration or compliance operator moves it by supplying a `current_utc` instant; the response echoes that instant back. Moving the clock mutates no entity, and moving it backwards is permitted and does not retroactively re-evaluate any stored state. Where this document speaks of "the logical clock date", it means the calendar date of that instant in UTC.

### 2.6. Rejection Order

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined by a fixed ladder. Checks are evaluated in this order, and the first violation short-circuits the request so that nothing is written:

1. Presence of a non-empty `X-User-ID`, then presence of a recognised role — both yielding `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. The role gate, yielding `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. This runs **before** any path parameter is resolved, so a role that may not call an endpoint receives 403 even when the addressed row does not exist.
3. The `Participant` row-scoping gate, yielding `ERR_PARTICIPANT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
4. Existence of every `{id}` path parameter, yielding the entity-specific not-found error described in §2.7.
5. Existence of every foreign key supplied in the request body, yielding the same family of not-found errors. Body-level references are checked in the order the endpoint declares them: creating a grant checks `plan_id` and then `participant_id`; appending a tranche checks `performance_cycle_id`; creating a preclearance request checks `participant_id` and then `window_id`. Creating a delegation checks `plan_id`; creating a mobility assignment checks `participant_id`; creating a performance cycle checks `plan_id`; creating a trading window checks `plan_id`; adopting a trading plan checks `participant_id`.
6. Request shape — every required field present, of the declared JSON type, inside its declared numeric or length range, and a member of its enumeration when the field is an enum — yielding `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). A missing required field, a wrong JSON type, a zero or negative share count where a positive integer is declared, a string outside its length bounds, an unknown enum member, a malformed ISO-8601 date or instant, and a start-after-end date pair inside one payload all land here. Unknown extra fields are ignored rather than rejected.
7. Pagination bounds, yielding `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
8. The strike-price immutability check on grant revision, yielding `ERR_EXERCISE_PRICE_MISMATCH`.
9. The state gate — the addressed row must be in a state from which the requested transition exists — yielding the entity-specific invalid-state error described in §2.7. Replaying a transition that already succeeded lands here, which is how the system refuses idempotent repeats.
10. The plan-frozen gate, yielding `ERR_PLAN_FROZEN`.
11. Optimistic concurrency on the parent row, yielding the stale-version errors of §2.9. Because this sits below existence and state, a stale version quoted against a row that does not exist still returns 404.
12. Business-safety, temporal, and fiscal rules, in the order set out where each workflow is described in §3. The segregation-of-duties control sits at the top of this group; the uniqueness constraints sit at the bottom, so a payload that is both malformed and duplicated reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` first.

### 2.7. Not-Found and Invalid-State Error Families

Path and body existence failures resolve to an entity-specific identifier rather than a single generic code: `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUTHORITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GRANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANCHE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VESTING_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TAX_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRECLEARANCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRADING_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`. All of them carry HTTP 404.

Paths are resolved outermost segment first. A reserve adjustment addressed at a plan resolves the plan before anything else; a request for a settlement's tax allocations resolves the settlement before listing its rows; a request appending a tranche to a grant resolves the grant before reading the body.

State failures resolve the same way, each carrying HTTP 409: `ERR_PLAN_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_AUTHORITY_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PARTICIPANT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_GRANT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_TRANCHE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_VESTING_EVENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PRECLEARANCE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_TRADING_PLAN_INVALID_STATE`, and `ERR_REPORT_INVALID_STATE`.

### 2.8. Basis Points, Whole Shares and Rounding Discipline

Rates, ratios, and proportions in this system are expressed in **basis points** — hundredths of a percent — as integers, so a full 100 percent is 10000 basis points and a quarter of one percent is 25. Share counts are always whole integers; no fractional share is ever created or stored. Money is always a whole number of cents.

Two rounding modes appear, and they are never interchangeable. The default is **round half up**: the value is rounded to the stated number of decimal places (usually zero, meaning whole shares, whole cents, or whole basis points) and a value sitting exactly halfway rounds away from zero. The withholding mechanics of §3.7 instead round **up** (ceiling) when converting a tax bill into a share count, because a share count that merely rounds to nearest could leave the tax under-covered.

Where a comparison decides whether a limit has been breached, the comparison is performed on exact integers by cross-multiplication rather than on a rounded rate, so that rounding can never mask a breach. The burn-rate approval gate of §3.4 and the reserve-posture test of §3.1 both work this way.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Rows

Two write endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency against the version of a **parent** row, because in both cases a second write would legitimately succeed on its own merits and therefore cannot be distinguished by a state precondition alone.

Adopting a trading plan — `POST /trading-plans` — requires a non-optional `expected_participant_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Participant` named by `participant_id`. If that value does not equal the participant's current `version`, the adoption is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARTICIPANT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard matters precisely because a second adoption is legitimate product behaviour: a replacement plan supersedes and expires the participant's prior effective plan, so without the version check a client could silently blow away a plan it never observed.

Posting a share-reserve adjustment — `POST /equity-plans/{id}/share-reserve-adjustments` — requires a non-optional `expected_plan_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the addressed `EquityPlan`. If that value does not equal the plan's current `version`, the adjustment is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Reserve adjustments are cumulative, so a second adjustment would itself legitimately apply; the guard exists so that an adjustment sized against a plan snapshot cannot land on a plan that has since moved.

No other endpoint takes a version field, and no other endpoint returns a 412.

### 2.10. Plan Status as a Gate on the Grant Lifecycle

An `EquityPlan` is `ACTIVE`, `FROZEN`, or `TERMINATED`. A plan that is `FROZEN` or `TERMINATED` accepts no new commitment, no new calendar entry, and no grant-lifecycle write at all: drafting a grant, appending a tranche, submitting, approving, issuing, cancelling, opening a performance cycle, certifying one, scheduling a trading window, adjusting the share reserve, and running the annual rollover are all refused with `ERR_PLAN_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). That list is exhaustive and includes the compensating actions — a frozen plan cannot even have a grant cancelled, because cancellation moves the reserve ledger.

Operations that deliver shares already committed remain available while a plan is `FROZEN`, because freezing is about new commitments rather than about stranding participants: vesting a tranche, allocating taxes, settling, and reversing a settlement are gated only on the plan not being `TERMINATED`.

### 2.11. Cascades, Propagation and Version Bumps

Several operations in this system mutate rows the client did not name. When they do, every touched row has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1, and the whole cascade either commits or is refused as a unit — a request that fails a precondition writes nothing, not even the part of the cascade that would have succeeded.

The deepest such chain is the employment-termination cascade, which walks four hops from tranches to grants to the plan's share ledger to the plan's reserve posture, plus an independent fan-out over the participant's trading plans and preclearance requests; §3.8 sets it out hop by hop. Performance certification runs a three-hop chain from tranches to grants to the plan ledger. Suspending or closing a trading window cascades over that window's preclearance requests. Terminating a plan revokes its delegations and closes its windows, which in turn runs the window-close cascade. In every case the cascade is described where the workflow lives, and in every case the plan-level accumulators are advanced by explicit addition and subtraction rather than re-derived by scanning children.
## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Establishing a Plan, Keeping Its Reserve, and Closing Its Fiscal Year

Everything begins with a shareholder-approved share plan. A `PlanAdministrator` registers one by supplying its `name` (a human-readable label such as "2026 Omnibus Incentive Plan", between 1 and 120 characters inclusive), the `total_authorized_shares` the shareholders authorised (at least 1), the `common_shares_outstanding` figure that will serve as the denominator for both burn-rate variants and for the evergreen provision (also at least 1), and the `fiscal_year_start_date` as a calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form marking the first day of the current fiscal year. Seven further inputs are optional and each has a defined default: `current_fiscal_year_label` labels the fiscal year the accumulators belong to and defaults to `FY2026`; `evergreen_pct_bps` expresses the evergreen provision in basis points of common shares outstanding and defaults to 0, with an accepted range of 0 through 10000 inclusive; `evergreen_cap_shares` is a hard ceiling in shares on any single evergreen top-up, defaults to 0, and may not be negative; `burn_rate_limit_bps` is the maximum permitted fiscal-year gross burn rate in basis points, defaults to 10000 (which is the value that disables the gate entirely, since no issuer can grant more than its whole share count), and must lie between 1 and 10000 inclusive; `fungible_ratio_full_value_bps` is the fungible share ratio charged against the reserve for full-value awards, defaults to 10000 — one share charged per share — and may never be set below 10000; `low_reserve_threshold_bps` is the reserve floor expressed in basis points of the authorised pool, defaults to 0, and ranges from 0 through 10000 inclusive; and `next_earnings_release_at` records the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the next scheduled earnings release, defaulting to null, and is read later by the cooling-off calculation of §3.9.

A freshly registered plan is `ACTIVE` at `version` 1 with `freeze_reason` null, and all five of its accumulators start at zero: `shares_committed_cumulative`, `shares_returned_cumulative`, `fiscal_year_gross_granted_shares`, `fiscal_year_returned_shares`, and `evergreen_topup_last_shares`. Both reported rates, `gross_burn_rate_bps` and `net_burn_rate_bps`, likewise start at 0.

**The reserve ledger.** The plan's headroom is not stored as a hand-maintained number; it is derived. `available_reserve_shares` equals the `total_authorized_shares` minus `shares_committed_cumulative` plus `shares_returned_cumulative`. Those two accumulators are monotonic: commitments only ever add to the first, and releases only ever add to the second, so a cancellation does not "un-commit" — it credits back. From the same three integers the system derives `reserve_posture`, which is `OPEN` when the available reserve multiplied by 10000 is greater than or equal to `low_reserve_threshold_bps` multiplied by `total_authorized_shares`, and `RESTRICTED` otherwise. That comparison is an exact integer cross-multiplication, so a reserve sitting precisely on the threshold is `OPEN`. Because the default threshold is 0 basis points, a plan registered without a floor is `OPEN` until its reserve would go negative. A conservation identity must hold after every request and after every individual cascade hop: the available reserve, plus cumulative commitments, minus cumulative returns, always equals the authorised total. A compensating action must therefore credit back exactly the amount the forward action debited — never a re-derived figure.

The ledger is recomputed at the end of every operation that touches either accumulator: grant approval, grant cancellation, the third hop of performance certification, the third hop of the termination cascade, a share-reserve adjustment, and the annual rollover.

**Adjusting the authorised pool.** A `PlanAdministrator` may apply a signed adjustment to the pool — a shareholder-approved increase, or a reduction — by supplying `delta_shares` together with the `expected_plan_version` described in §2.9 and an optional free-text `adjustment_reason`. The adjustment adds `delta_shares` to `total_authorized_shares`, reruns the reserve ledger so that `available_reserve_shares` and `reserve_posture` are rewritten, and increments the plan's `version` by 1. A negative adjustment that would drive `available_reserve_shares` below zero is refused with `ERR_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT`; an adjustment that lands the available reserve on exactly zero is permitted.

**The plan lifecycle.** Either a `PlanAdministrator` or a `BoardMember` may drive the plan's status by naming an `action` of `FREEZE`, `UNFREEZE`, or `TERMINATE`, with an optional `freeze_reason` string. Freezing moves an `ACTIVE` plan to `FROZEN` and stores the supplied `freeze_reason`; no child row is mutated at all, because the freeze acts purely as the precondition gate described in §2.10. Unfreezing moves a `FROZEN` plan back to `ACTIVE` and clears `freeze_reason` to null. Terminating is reachable from both `ACTIVE` and `FROZEN`, sets the status to `TERMINATED`, stores the supplied `freeze_reason`, and then cascades: every delegation of the plan that is still `ACTIVE` moves to `REVOKED` with its `revoked_at` stamped from the logical clock, and every trading window of the plan whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `OPEN`, or `SUSPENDED` moves to `CLOSED` — which in turn runs the window-close cascade over that window's preclearance requests described in §3.9. The plan's own `version` advances by 1, as does the `version` of every delegation, window, and preclearance request the cascade touched. Each of the three actions is a single ordinary write, so none of them advances `version` by more than 1 on the plan itself. `TERMINATED` is terminal: no plan action has an outgoing transition from it, and attempting `UNFREEZE` on an `ACTIVE` plan or any action on a `TERMINATED` plan yields `ERR_PLAN_INVALID_STATE`. Note that vesting, settlement, and reversal of awards already issued remain permitted after termination in the sense that they are gated on the plan not being `TERMINATED` — which means, concretely, that terminating a plan does stop them, while merely freezing it does not.

**The two burn-rate variants.** Both are recomputed whenever either fiscal-year accumulator moves. The `GROSS` variant writes `gross_burn_rate_bps` and equals the `fiscal_year_gross_granted_shares` multiplied by 10000, divided by `common_shares_outstanding`, rounded half up to whole basis points. The `NET` variant writes `net_burn_rate_bps` and equals `fiscal_year_gross_granted_shares` minus `fiscal_year_returned_shares`, multiplied by 10000, divided by `common_shares_outstanding`, rounded half up to whole basis points, with the result floored at zero so that a fiscal year in which more shares came back than went out reports 0 rather than a negative rate. The two variants read only those two fiscal-year accumulators, never the grant table, so an award approved in an earlier fiscal year contributes nothing after a rollover has reset them. The variants are deliberately allowed to disagree: `GROSS` ignores forfeitures and cancellations entirely and therefore never decreases inside a fiscal year, while `NET` falls as shares come back. A grant can consequently be refused on the gross projection while the net rate sits comfortably below the same limit.

A computed read exposes both variants side by side for a plan, together with `plan_id`, `fiscal_year_gross_granted_shares`, `fiscal_year_returned_shares`, `burn_rate_limit_bps`, `available_reserve_shares`, `reserve_posture`, and a `headroom_shares` figure. `headroom_shares` is the `burn_rate_limit_bps` multiplied by `common_shares_outstanding`, divided by 10000 and rounded **down** to a whole number of shares, minus `fiscal_year_gross_granted_shares`, and the result is floored at 0 so that an over-budget plan reports zero headroom rather than a negative one.

**Closing the fiscal year.** A `PlanAdministrator` runs the annual rollover by supplying the new `fiscal_year_label`. The rollover first computes the evergreen top-up: the `common_shares_outstanding` multiplied by `evergreen_pct_bps`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up to whole shares, and then the smaller of that figure and `evergreen_cap_shares` is taken. Rounding happens before the cap is applied. When the cap is the binding term the top-up equals the cap exactly, and when the cap happens to equal the uncapped amount the top-up equals that amount — the comparison is a genuine minimum with no off-by-one. The resulting top-up is added to `total_authorized_shares` and recorded in `evergreen_topup_last_shares`. The rollover then overwrites `current_fiscal_year_label` with the supplied label, advances `fiscal_year_start_date` by exactly one calendar year from its prior value, and zeroes all four fiscal-year figures — `fiscal_year_gross_granted_shares`, `fiscal_year_returned_shares`, `gross_burn_rate_bps`, and `net_burn_rate_bps`. The reserve ledger is then rerun so that `available_reserve_shares` and `reserve_posture` reflect the larger pool. Finally, every delegation of the plan that is still `ACTIVE` and whose `effective_to` falls before the new fiscal-year start date moves to `EXPIRED`. Grants are untouched by a rollover; only the plan row and its delegations change.

Plans may be listed with optional `status` and `reserve_posture` filters, and read individually with their live reserve and burn-rate fields; the single-plan read is what a client uses to obtain the `version` it will later quote in `expected_plan_version`.
### 3.2. Delegated Approval Authority and Its Budgets

The compensation committee cannot meet for every award, so a `BoardMember` may delegate grant-approval authority to a named officer. Creating a delegation names the `plan_id` whose grants it may approve, an `authority_type` of either `DELEGATED_OFFICER` or `COMMITTEE`, the `delegate_user_id` — the opaque operator identifier of the `DelegatedOfficer` permitted to act under it — a `per_grant_share_cap` of at least 1 share, an `aggregate_share_cap` of at least 1 share, and the inclusive term bounds `effective_from` and `effective_to` as calendar dates. Only a `BoardMember` may create a delegation, and only a `BoardMember` may revoke one.

A new delegation is `ACTIVE` with `consumed_shares` at 0 and `remaining_shares` equal to the full `aggregate_share_cap`; `revoked_at` is null. `consumed_shares` accumulates the fungible-weighted shares that grants approved under this authority have charged to it, and `remaining_shares` is always the `aggregate_share_cap` minus `consumed_shares`. A delegation's status is one of `ACTIVE`, `EXHAUSTED`, `REVOKED`, or `EXPIRED`: it becomes `EXHAUSTED` the moment `remaining_shares` reaches exactly 0 through an approval, returns to `ACTIVE` if a cancellation credits capacity back, becomes `EXPIRED` when the annual rollover finds its term has lapsed, and becomes `REVOKED` either when the board revokes it explicitly or when the plan it belongs to is terminated.

Revocation accepts an optional `revocation_reason`. It moves an `ACTIVE` or `EXHAUSTED` delegation to `REVOKED` and stamps `revoked_at` from the logical clock; `consumed_shares` and `remaining_shares` are frozen at whatever they were, and grants already approved under the delegation are entirely unaffected. Revoking a delegation that is already `REVOKED` or already `EXPIRED` is refused with `ERR_AUTHORITY_INVALID_STATE`.

Delegations are readable individually — with their live consumption — and listable per plan with an optional `status` filter.

### 3.3. Enrolling Recipients, Classifying Insiders, and Recording Workdays

A `PlanAdministrator` enrols an award recipient by supplying their `full_name`, an issuer-wide unique `employee_number`, the `home_country_code` of their home payroll as an ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code, the `hire_date` that anchors service, and the `birth_date` that anchors age. Two inputs are optional: `home_state_code` names the home state or province and is null outside the United States, and `insider_status` classifies the recipient as `NON_INSIDER`, `DESIGNATED_INSIDER`, or `SECTION_16_OFFICER`, defaulting to `NON_INSIDER`. A new participant is `ACTIVE` at `version` 1 with `termination_date`, `termination_reason`, and `ptep_expiry_date` all null. If another participant already carries the same `employee_number` — compared exactly and case-sensitively — the enrolment is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMPLOYEE_NUMBER`. Uniqueness is checked after request-shape validation, so a payload that is both malformed and duplicated reports the malformation.

The row's `id` is the identity a recipient authenticates as: a request carrying the `Participant` role must send that `id` in `X-User-ID`.

**Age, service, and retirement eligibility** are recomputed rather than stored statically. Against a reference date — the logical clock date while the participant is `ACTIVE`, or the frozen `termination_date` once they are `TERMINATED` — the system derives `age_years` as the number of whole completed years from `birth_date` to that reference date, and `service_years` as the number of whole completed years from `hire_date` to it. "Whole completed years" means the count of anniversaries falling on or before the reference date, and an anniversary landing exactly on the reference date counts. From those two integers, `retirement_eligible` is true when `age_years` is at least 55 **and** the sum of `age_years` and `service_years` is at least 65. Both comparisons are inclusive, so a participant who is exactly 55 years old with exactly 65 combined points is retirement eligible. No grant or tranche data participates in this calculation. Reading a single participant reruns it, so eligibility tracks the logical clock while employment is `ACTIVE`; terminating pins it.

**Reclassification.** A `ComplianceOfficer` — and only a `ComplianceOfficer` — may overwrite a participant's `insider_status`, which increments the participant's `version` by 1. Reclassification is forward-looking only: a trading plan already adopted keeps the `insider_status_at_adoption` and the `cooling_off_days` it was born with, and only future adoptions and modifications see the new classification. A participant whose `employment_status` is `TERMINATED` may not be reclassified; the attempt is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

That same active-employment gate guards four other creations: a terminated participant receives no new grant, no new mobility period, no new preclearance request, and no new trading plan. In each of those cases the code is `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). Their existing tranches, settlements, and Section 16 reports remain fully processable, so a post-termination exercise period can run to completion.

**Workday sourcing records.** Because award income earned by a mobile employee is taxable where the work was performed, a `PlanAdministrator` records one assignment period per jurisdiction. Each carries the `participant_id` it belongs to, a `jurisdiction_code` between 2 and 8 characters — keys such as `US-CA`, `US-NY`, `GB`, or `IE` — a `tax_rate_bps` giving that jurisdiction's combined supplemental withholding rate in basis points between 0 and 10000 inclusive, a `start_date` that is the first day of the period, a `workdays` count of days physically worked in that jurisdiction during the period which may be zero but never negative, and an optional `end_date` which is the last day of the period and may not precede `start_date`.

Assignment periods are closed intervals, inclusive at both ends, and no two periods of the same participant may intersect. The comparison is inclusive on both endpoints, which means a new period beginning on the exact `end_date` of an existing one overlaps and is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, while a period beginning one day later is accepted. An existing assignment whose `end_date` is null is treated as running to the end of time for overlap purposes. Assignment rows are immutable once written — there is no update endpoint, no `updated_at`, and no `version`. They can be read individually and listed per participant with an optional `jurisdiction_code` filter.

Participants may be listed with optional `employment_status` and `insider_status` filters.
### 3.4. Drafting an Award, the Revision Cycle, and the Two Approval Paths

A grant is drafted long before it becomes real. A `PlanAdministrator` drafts one against a `plan_id` and for a `participant_id`, declaring the `award_type` — `RSU`, `PSU`, `ISO`, or `NSO` — and the `target_shares` at target performance, which must be at least 1. The optional `exercise_price_cents` gives the per-share strike price in cents. It defaults to 0 and must be either omitted or supplied as 0 for the two full-value instruments, `RSU` and `PSU`; the two option instruments, `ISO` and `NSO`, require a positive value. A drafted grant begins at status `DRAFT` with `changes_requested_count` at 0, `grant_date` null, `approval_path` null, `approved_under_authority_id` null, `approved_by_user_id` null, `submitted_by_user_id` null, `approved_at` null, `cancel_reason` null, and both `weighted_reserve_shares` and `outstanding_shares` at 0. Nothing is committed against the plan reserve at draft time.

The vesting design is then built up one tranche at a time, and only while the grant is in `DRAFT` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED` — appending a tranche to a grant in any other status is refused with `ERR_GRANT_INVALID_STATE`. Each tranche declares a `tranche_index`, a one-based ordinal that must be at least 1 and unique inside its grant; a `vest_type` of either `TIME` or `PERFORMANCE`; a `scheduled_vest_date` as the calendar date on which it becomes vestable; a `target_shares` count of at least 1, which is the share figure at target performance and is immutable once the grant exists; and an optional `performance_cycle_id`. A `TIME` tranche must omit `performance_cycle_id` and has its effective `shares` set equal to `target_shares` immediately. A `PERFORMANCE` tranche must supply `performance_cycle_id`, and its `shares` sit at 0 until the cycle is certified — which is why an uncertified performance tranche commits nothing against the reserve. Every new tranche is `UNVESTED` with `acceleration_reason` null and `vested_at` null. The grant's own `target_shares` is expected to equal the sum of its tranches' `target_shares`.

**What a grant costs the reserve.** The fungible-weighted charge is recomputed from scratch whenever it could have changed — at approval, at the second hop of performance certification, at the second hop of the termination cascade, and at cancellation — and written to `weighted_reserve_shares`. It is derived in two steps. First, the committed share count is the sum of `shares` over the grant's tranches whose status is `UNVESTED`, `VESTED`, or `SETTLED`; a `FORFEITED` tranche never contributes, and the sum is 0 when no tranche qualifies. Second, that committed count is weighted by instrument family: for the full-value types `RSU` and `PSU`, the count is multiplied by the plan's `fungible_ratio_full_value_bps` and divided by 10000, then rounded half up to whole shares; for the option types `ISO` and `NSO`, the count is charged one share for one share with no weighting at all. The rounding is applied exactly once, to the aggregate — never per tranche. Separately, `outstanding_shares` is the sum of `shares` over the grant's tranches whose status is `UNVESTED` or `VESTED`, which is a narrower set than the reserve charge because a `SETTLED` tranche has left the outstanding pool while remaining committed against the reserve.

**Submission and the revision cycle.** Submitting moves a grant from `DRAFT` — or back from `CHANGES_REQUESTED` — to `PENDING_APPROVAL` and records the acting `X-User-ID` as `submitted_by_user_id`, overwriting any previous submitter. A grant with no tranche at all cannot be submitted; the attempt is refused with `ERR_GRANT_INVALID_STATE`. Submission additionally requires the plan to be `ACTIVE` and the recipient's employment to be `ACTIVE`; a submission for a recipient whose employment has ended is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

An approver — either a `BoardMember` or a `DelegatedOfficer` — may send a pending grant back with a mandatory `change_note`, moving it from `PENDING_APPROVAL` to `CHANGES_REQUESTED` and incrementing `changes_requested_count` by 1. The counter is compared against a cap of 2 **before** the increment, so the first and second send-backs are allowed and the third is refused with `ERR_MAX_GRANT_REVISIONS_EXCEEDED`. Sending a grant back touches no reserve figure, no delegation, and no burn-rate accumulator.

While the grant sits in `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, the administrator revises it by supplying a new `target_shares` and, optionally, `exercise_price_cents`. A revision may change the share figure and the vesting design but never the strike price, because the strike is pinned to the fair market value captured at draft time: when `exercise_price_cents` is supplied and differs from the value stored on an `ISO` or `NSO` grant, the revision is refused with `ERR_EXERCISE_PRICE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 400). The rule does not apply to `RSU` or `PSU` grants, whose strike is always 0. A revision does not touch `changes_requested_count`, which the send-back already advanced, and it leaves the grant in `CHANGES_REQUESTED` until it is submitted again. That closes the cycle `PENDING_APPROVAL → CHANGES_REQUESTED → PENDING_APPROVAL`.

**Approval, and the fiscal ladder.** Approval takes an `approval_path` of `BOARD` or `DELEGATED` and, on the delegated path only, an `authority_id`. Both paths move the grant from `PENDING_APPROVAL` to `APPROVED`, record `approved_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, stamp `approved_at` from the logical clock, store the chosen `approval_path`, write `weighted_reserve_shares` from the calculation above, add that figure to the plan's `shares_committed_cumulative` and to its `fiscal_year_gross_granted_shares`, and then rerun the reserve ledger and both burn-rate variants. Both paths require the plan to be `ACTIVE`, the recipient's employment to be `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_ACTIVE` — and the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from `submitted_by_user_id`. That last control is the four-eyes rule: whoever submitted a grant may not approve it, and the refusal is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). Role membership alone never satisfies it.

The two paths then diverge. On the `BOARD` branch, `approved_under_authority_id` stays null and no delegation row is touched at all; the grant's `version` and the plan's `version` each advance by 1. On the `DELEGATED` branch, `approved_under_authority_id` is set to the cited `authority_id`, and that delegation's `consumed_shares` is increased by the grant's `weighted_reserve_shares`, `remaining_shares` is recomputed, and the delegation moves to `EXHAUSTED` if `remaining_shares` reaches exactly 0; the grant's `version`, the delegation's `version`, and the plan's `version` each advance by 1. Only the delegated branch consumes a delegated budget.

The delegated branch also carries checks the board branch does not, and the whole set is evaluated in a fixed order so that a grant busting several limits reports the most informative one:

1. **Path eligibility.** A `DELEGATED` approval is impossible for a `PSU` award, and impossible for any award to a participant whose `insider_status` is `SECTION_16_OFFICER`; both require the full board. The refusal is `ERR_DELEGATED_APPROVAL_BLOCKED`, and it is evaluated before any delegation cap so the caller learns the path itself is unavailable rather than merely exhausted.
2. **Delegation effectiveness.** The cited delegation must be `ACTIVE`, must belong to the grant's own plan, must name the acting `X-User-ID` as its `delegate_user_id`, and the logical clock date at approval must fall inside `effective_from` through `effective_to` with both endpoints inclusive. A `REVOKED`, `EXHAUSTED`, or `EXPIRED` delegation, one belonging to another plan, one delegated to a different officer, or an approval dated outside the term all yield `ERR_AUTHORITY_NOT_EFFECTIVE`.
3. **Per-grant ceiling.** The grant's `weighted_reserve_shares` may not exceed the delegation's `per_grant_share_cap`; exceeding it yields `ERR_AUTHORITY_PER_GRANT_CAP_EXCEEDED`.
4. **Aggregate ceiling.** The delegation's `consumed_shares` plus this grant's `weighted_reserve_shares` may not exceed its `aggregate_share_cap`. Landing exactly on the cap is allowed and drives the delegation to `EXHAUSTED` as a side effect; exceeding it by a single share is refused with `ERR_AUTHORITY_CAP_EXCEEDED` and leaves the delegation `ACTIVE`.

Two plan-level fiscal gates then apply to **both** paths, in this order. First the burn-rate gate: the projected figure is `fiscal_year_gross_granted_shares` plus the `weighted_reserve_shares` just computed, and the approval is refused with `ERR_BURN_RATE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` when that projection multiplied by 10000 is strictly greater than `burn_rate_limit_bps` multiplied by `common_shares_outstanding`. The comparison is exact integer cross-multiplication and never rounds, so a projection landing exactly on the limit is approved. Only the gross variant gates approval; the net variant never does. Second the reserve gate: `available_reserve_shares` must be at least the grant's `weighted_reserve_shares`, and a shortfall is refused with `ERR_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT`. Consuming the reserve down to exactly zero is permitted. Because the burn-rate gate sits above the reserve gate, an approval that busts both reports the burn rate.

**Cancellation.** Cancelling is the compensating action for both approval paths and takes a mandatory `cancel_reason`. It is reachable from `DRAFT`, `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `GRANTED`, and `PARTIALLY_FORFEITED`, requires the plan to be `ACTIVE`, and is impossible once any tranche of the grant has reached `SETTLED`. Cancelling sets the status to `CANCELLED`, stores the reason, and moves every tranche of the grant whose status is `UNVESTED` or `VESTED` to `FORFEITED`, which for a `VESTED` tranche also reverses any settlement of that tranche still awaiting tax treatment (see §3.7). When the grant had a non-zero `weighted_reserve_shares`, that exact figure is added to the plan's `shares_returned_cumulative` and to its `fiscal_year_returned_shares`, after which the reserve ledger, `reserve_posture`, and the net burn rate are recomputed — the gross burn rate is deliberately **not** unwound. When the grant had been approved under a delegation, that delegation's `consumed_shares` is reduced by the released figure, `remaining_shares` is recomputed, and an `EXHAUSTED` delegation returns to `ACTIVE` once `remaining_shares` is greater than 0. Finally the grant's own `weighted_reserve_shares` and `outstanding_shares` are set to 0. The grant's `version` advances by 1, and the plan's `version` advances by 1 whenever the reserve was credited.

Grants are listable with optional `plan_id`, `participant_id`, `approved_under_authority_id`, and `status` filters, and readable individually. Tranches are listable with optional `grant_id`, `performance_cycle_id`, and `status` filters, and readable individually. `FORFEITED` and `CANCELLED` are the terminal statuses of a grant.
### 3.5. Issuance and the Grant-Date Determination Rule

An approved award is not yet a legal grant: it needs a grant date, and the issuer's own policy is that a grant date must anchor to the trading calendar. Issuance is a `PlanAdministrator` operation that advances a grant from `APPROVED` to `GRANTED`, requires the plan to be `ACTIVE`, and writes `grant_date`. Every tranche keeps its `UNVESTED` status, and `outstanding_shares` is recomputed as the sum of `shares` over the tranches that are `UNVESTED` or `VESTED`.

The date itself is determined by scanning the trading windows of the grant's own plan and nothing else. If any window of that plan is `OPEN` and the logical clock lies at or after its `open_at` and strictly before its `close_at`, the `grant_date` is the logical clock's own calendar date. The interval test is half-open, so a clock sitting exactly on `close_at` is not inside the window. If no window satisfies that test, the system looks instead at windows of the plan whose status is `SCHEDULED` and whose `open_at` is strictly after the logical clock; among those it chooses the one with the smallest `open_at`, breaking a tie in favour of the earlier `created_at`, and sets `grant_date` to that window's `open_at` calendar date. Windows whose status is `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED` are excluded from both searches.

When neither an eligible open window nor a future scheduled window exists, issuance is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_GRANT_DATE` (HTTP 409), nothing is written, and the grant stays in `APPROVED` until compliance schedules a new window.

### 3.6. Performance Cycles and Certification

A performance award is sized by a metric the board must certify. A `PlanAdministrator` opens a cycle by naming the `plan_id` under which the metric is measured, a `metric_code` such as `REV_2026` or `TSR_2026`, a `target_value` greater than zero that represents the level of the metric producing a 100 percent payout, and a `certification_due_date` by which the board is expected to certify. The `metric_code` must be unique among the plan's cycles that are not `CANCELLED`; a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_METRIC_CODE`. A new cycle is `OPEN` with `actual_value`, `payout_multiplier_bps`, `certified_at`, and `certified_by_user_id` all null, and `created_by_user_id` recorded from the acting `X-User-ID` so that the four-eyes control has something to compare against later. Cycles are readable individually and listable per plan with optional `status` and `metric_code` filters.

Certification is performed by a `BoardMember` or a `ComplianceOfficer` and takes a single input, the achieved `actual_value`. The certifier's `X-User-ID` must differ from `created_by_user_id`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The plan must be `ACTIVE`, and a cycle already `CERTIFIED` or `CANCELLED` cannot be certified again — that attempt yields `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE`.

**The payout multiplier.** The achievement ratio is the `actual_value` divided by the `target_value`, rounded half up to six decimal places before anything else happens. That ratio is then looked up against a tier schedule whose boundaries are inclusive on the lower edge: a ratio below 0.800000 pays 0 basis points; a ratio from 0.800000 up to but not including 0.900000 pays 5000; from 0.900000 up to but not including 1.000000 pays 7500; from 1.000000 up to but not including 1.100000 pays 10000; from 1.100000 up to but not including 1.200000 pays 15000; and a ratio of 1.200000 or more pays the maximum of 20000. Because the lower edges are inclusive, a ratio of exactly 1.000000 pays 10000 basis points rather than 7500. The resulting figure is stored as `payout_multiplier_bps`.

**The certified branch.** When `payout_multiplier_bps` is greater than 0, the cycle moves from `OPEN` to `CERTIFIED`, stores `actual_value` and the multiplier, and stamps `certified_at` from the logical clock and `certified_by_user_id` from the acting identity. Three hops then run. In the first, every tranche bound to the cycle whose status is `UNVESTED` has its `shares` rewritten as its own `target_shares` multiplied by `payout_multiplier_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded half up to whole shares; a tranche already `VESTED`, `SETTLED`, or `FORFEITED` keeps whatever share count it had. In the second, every grant owning a tranche the first hop touched recomputes `weighted_reserve_shares` and `outstanding_shares` by the rule of §3.4. In the third, each grant's signed weight delta is applied to the plan: a positive delta is added to `shares_committed_cumulative` and also to `fiscal_year_gross_granted_shares`, while a negative delta is added to `shares_returned_cumulative` and also to `fiscal_year_returned_shares`. The reserve ledger, `reserve_posture`, `gross_burn_rate_bps`, and `net_burn_rate_bps` are then all recomputed. The cycle's `version` advances by 1, as does the `version` of every tranche, grant, and plan the cascade mutated. A cycle whose upward revision would drive `available_reserve_shares` below zero is refused in its entirety with `ERR_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT`, and nothing is written — the reserve headroom check is a precondition of certification, not an afterthought.

**The cancelled branch.** The same operation takes a completely different shape when the achievement ratio falls below the zero-payout floor of 0.800000, which is exactly the condition that makes `payout_multiplier_bps` equal 0. In that case the cycle moves from `OPEN` to `CANCELLED` rather than to `CERTIFIED`, stores `actual_value` and a multiplier of 0, and stamps `certified_at`; every tranche bound to the cycle whose status is `UNVESTED` moves to `FORFEITED` with its `shares` set to 0. The second hop recomputes each owning grant's `outstanding_shares` and `weighted_reserve_shares` and moves the grant to `PARTIALLY_FORFEITED` or `FORFEITED` exactly as the termination cascade of §3.8 does. The third hop adds the weight decrease to `shares_returned_cumulative` and `fiscal_year_returned_shares` and recomputes `available_reserve_shares` and `reserve_posture`. The cycle's `version` and the `version` of every mutated row each advance by 1. A below-floor certification therefore forfeits rather than revalues, and the two branches of the same certification operation carry genuinely different side-effect sets. Both `CERTIFIED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal for a cycle.
### 3.7. Vesting, Mobile-Employee Tax Sourcing, and Settlement Mechanics

**Vesting a tranche.** A `PlanAdministrator` vests a tranche when its schedule has arrived. The tranche must be `UNVESTED`, its owning grant must be `GRANTED` or `PARTIALLY_FORFEITED`, the plan must not be `TERMINATED`, and the logical clock date must be at or after the tranche's `scheduled_vest_date`. A tranche that is already `VESTED`, `SETTLED`, or `FORFEITED` is refused with `ERR_TRANCHE_INVALID_STATE`. When the tranche's `vest_type` is `PERFORMANCE`, its bound cycle must be `CERTIFIED`; a cycle still `OPEN` or already `CANCELLED` yields `ERR_PERFORMANCE_NOT_CERTIFIED`, because a performance tranche's share count is provisional until the multiplier is fixed. `TIME` tranches skip that check entirely.

The request supplies the `fmv_per_share_cents` — the fair market value per share in cents on the vest date, at least 1 — and may optionally elect a `withholding_method` of `SHARE_WITHHOLD`, `SELL_TO_COVER`, or `CASH_REMITTANCE`; when omitted the method defaults to `SHARE_WITHHOLD`. Vesting sets the tranche to `VESTED`, stamps `vested_at` from the logical clock, leaves `acceleration_reason` null (a tranche that vests on schedule was not accelerated), and creates exactly one settlement record. That record starts with `tax_status` at `PENDING_ALLOCATION` and `settlement_status` at `PENDING_TAX`; its `tranche_id` points back at the tranche, its `gross_shares` is a snapshot of the tranche's `shares` at that moment, its `vest_date` is the logical clock's calendar date, its `sourcing_start_date` is copied from the owning grant's `grant_date`, and its `taxable_income_cents` is `gross_shares` multiplied by `fmv_per_share_cents`. The grant's `outstanding_shares` is unchanged, because a `VESTED` tranche is still outstanding. The tranche's `version` advances by 1, and the newly created settlement is what the operation returns.

**Two independent status dimensions.** A settlement carries two statuses that advance independently. `tax_status` moves along `PENDING_ALLOCATION → ALLOCATED`, and `settlement_status` moves along `PENDING_TAX → SETTLED → REVERSED`. Allocating taxes moves the first dimension and leaves the second alone; settling moves the second and leaves the first alone. They are independent but ordered: shares cannot be delivered before the jurisdictional withholding is known, because the withheld share count is derived from the allocated tax total.

**Sourcing income across jurisdictions.** Either a `PlanAdministrator` or a `ComplianceOfficer` runs the allocation. The settlement must have `tax_status` `PENDING_ALLOCATION` and `settlement_status` `PENDING_TAX`; an allocation attempted on an already-`ALLOCATED` settlement is refused with `ERR_VESTING_EVENT_INVALID_STATE`.

The sourcing period runs from the settlement's `sourcing_start_date` to its `vest_date`, inclusive at both ends. Contributing assignment records are those of the vesting participant whose `start_date` is at or after the beginning of that period **and** whose `end_date` is at or before its end — full containment. An assignment that begins before the grant date, or ends after the vest date, contributes nothing at all, not even partially. The contributing rows are then grouped by `jurisdiction_code`. Inside each group, the sourced workday figure is the sum of `workdays` across the group's rows, and the group's rate is the largest `tax_rate_bps` among them, which keeps the result deterministic when a jurisdiction appears more than once. The settlement's `total_workdays` is the sum of the group figures. If that total is zero — whether because no assignment is contained in the period at all or because every contained assignment records zero workdays — the request is refused with `ERR_WORKDAY_COVERAGE_GAP` and no row is written. A contained group whose own workdays sum to zero is still a contributing group when other groups carry workdays: it produces a tax row with an `allocation_bps` of 0 and a `tax_amount_cents` of 0.

The proportional split is a largest-remainder waterfall in basis points, and its steps are pinned. First, each group receives a floor allocation: its workday figure multiplied by 10000, divided by `total_workdays`, rounded **down** to a whole number of basis points. The integer remainder retained for the tie-break is that group's workday figure multiplied by 10000, minus its floor allocation multiplied by `total_workdays`. Second, the leftover basis points — 10000 minus the sum of all floor allocations — are distributed one basis point at a time to the groups ordered by descending remainder, with ties broken by ascending `jurisdiction_code`, until the leftover is exhausted. Each group's `allocation_bps` is its floor allocation plus one if it received a leftover basis point. The allocations therefore sum to exactly 10000, always. Third, each group's `tax_amount_cents` is the settlement's `taxable_income_cents` multiplied by that group's `allocation_bps` multiplied by that group's rate, divided by 100000000, rounded half up to whole cents; the division is evaluated once over the exact product, with no intermediate rounding after the allocation step. Fourth, the settlement's `total_tax_cents` is the sum of those already-rounded per-jurisdiction amounts — round first, then sum. Summing the exact per-jurisdiction values and rounding once at the end is explicitly not the pinned order and can differ by a cent. Finally, `blended_rate_bps` is `total_tax_cents` multiplied by 10000, divided by `taxable_income_cents`, rounded half up to whole basis points.

The run materialises one immutable tax row per contributing jurisdiction, each carrying its `vesting_event_id`, its `jurisdiction_code`, the `workdays_in_period` sourced to it, its `allocation_bps`, the `tax_rate_bps` copied from the contributing assignment records, and its `tax_amount_cents`. The settlement's `total_workdays`, `total_tax_cents`, and `blended_rate_bps` are written, `tax_status` becomes `ALLOCATED`, `settlement_status` stays at `PENDING_TAX`, and the settlement's `version` advances by 1. Tax rows are listable per settlement and readable individually.

**Settling.** A `PlanAdministrator` settles a settlement whose `settlement_status` is `PENDING_TAX`, whose `tax_status` is `ALLOCATED`, and whose owning tranche is `VESTED`. Settling while `tax_status` is still `PENDING_ALLOCATION` is refused with `ERR_TAX_ALLOCATION_MISSING`; settling something already `SETTLED` or `REVERSED` is refused with `ERR_VESTING_EVENT_INVALID_STATE`. The tax figure is never recomputed at settlement time — the round-then-summed `total_tax_cents` is taken as given.

Three withholding mechanics exist, all of them rounding share counts **up** rather than to nearest, because a share count that merely rounded to nearest could leave the tax bill uncovered. Under `SHARE_WITHHOLD`, `shares_withheld` is the total tax divided by the fair market value per share, rounded up to the next whole share; `shares_sold` is 0; `net_shares_delivered` is `gross_shares` minus `shares_withheld`; `residual_cash_cents` is `shares_withheld` multiplied by the fair market value per share, minus the total tax — the over-withholding refunded to the participant; and `cash_due_cents` is 0. No commission is charged.

Under `SELL_TO_COVER`, a broker commission of 25 cents is charged on every share sold, which makes the share count and the cost it must cover look mutually dependent; the evaluation order is therefore pinned to a closed form. The net proceeds per share are computed first, as the fair market value per share minus the 25-cent commission. The share count is computed second: `shares_sold` is the total tax divided by those net proceeds per share, rounded up to the next whole share. The realised proceeds are computed third, as `shares_sold` multiplied by the net proceeds per share. Then `shares_withheld` is 0, `net_shares_delivered` is `gross_shares` minus `shares_sold`, `residual_cash_cents` is the realised proceeds minus the total tax, and `cash_due_cents` is 0. Because it rounds up against a smaller per-share figure, this method always sells at least as many shares as `SHARE_WITHHOLD` would have withheld and usually strictly more, so for identical gross shares, fair market value, and tax the two methods deliver a different `net_shares_delivered` **and** a different `residual_cash_cents`.

Under `CASH_REMITTANCE`, no share is disposed of at all: `shares_withheld` and `shares_sold` are both 0, `net_shares_delivered` equals `gross_shares`, `residual_cash_cents` is 0, and the entire tax becomes `cash_due_cents`, which the participant must remit. On every one of the three branches the same identity must hold: `gross_shares` equals `net_shares_delivered` plus `shares_withheld` plus `shares_sold`.

Settling records the `withholding_method` in force, writes the five derived figures, moves `settlement_status` to `SETTLED`, and moves the owning tranche from `VESTED` to `SETTLED`, which causes the grant's `outstanding_shares` to be recomputed — a `SETTLED` tranche leaves the outstanding pool while remaining committed against the reserve. Both the settlement's `version` and the tranche's `version` advance by 1. When the participant's `insider_status` is `SECTION_16_OFFICER` or `DESIGNATED_INSIDER`, settling also creates one Section 16 report, described in §3.9.

**Reversing a settlement.** Reversal is the compensating action and takes a mandatory `reversal_reason`. It applies to a settlement whose `settlement_status` is `SETTLED` and whose owning tranche is `SETTLED`; anything else is refused with `ERR_VESTING_EVENT_INVALID_STATE`. Reversal sets `settlement_status` to `REVERSED`, stores the reason, zeroes `shares_withheld`, `shares_sold`, `residual_cash_cents`, and `cash_due_cents`, and restores `net_shares_delivered` to `gross_shares`. It also drives `tax_status` back to `PENDING_ALLOCATION`, zeroes `total_tax_cents`, `total_workdays`, and `blended_rate_bps`, and deletes every tax row of the settlement, so the allocation can be redone from current workday records. The owning tranche moves from `SETTLED` back to `VESTED` with its `shares` untouched, and the grant's `outstanding_shares` returns to its pre-settlement value. Any Section 16 report whose source is this settlement and whose status is still `PENDING` is deleted, while a report already `FILED` or `LATE_FILED` is left in place — history that has been filed with the regulator is not rewritten. Both the settlement's `version` and the tranche's `version` advance by 1. The conserved quantities across the settle-and-reverse loop are the tranche's share count and the settlement's share-split identity; `REVERSED` is terminal for the settlement dimension.

Settlements are listable with optional `tranche_id`, `tax_status`, and `settlement_status` filters, and readable individually with their withholding split.
### 3.8. Employment Termination: Acceleration, Forfeiture, and the Four-Hop Cascade

Termination is the single most consequential write in the system, and it is a `PlanAdministrator` operation. It takes the `termination_date` on which employment ends, a `termination_reason` of `VOLUNTARY`, `INVOLUNTARY`, `FOR_CAUSE`, `RETIREMENT`, or `DEATH`, and an optional `acceleration_fmv_per_share_cents` giving the fair market value per share used for any tranche that accelerates. The participant's `employment_status` must be `ACTIVE` — terminating someone already `TERMINATED` is refused with `ERR_PARTICIPANT_INVALID_STATE` — and the `termination_date` may not precede their `hire_date`.

Before any hop runs, one guard is evaluated: if the resolution below would accelerate at least one `UNVESTED` tranche and `acceleration_fmv_per_share_cents` was omitted, the entire request is refused with `ERR_ACCELERATION_FMV_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409) and nothing whatsoever is written. Rather than invent a valuation, the system declines. A termination in which every tranche forfeits does not need the field, so omitting it is fine there and supplying it anyway is harmless.

The request first sets `employment_status` to `TERMINATED`, stores `termination_date` and `termination_reason`, and freezes `age_years`, `service_years`, and `retirement_eligible` as they stand at the termination date. It also writes `ptep_expiry_date`, the date on which the post-termination exercise period ends: the `termination_date` advanced by 0 days for `FOR_CAUSE`, 90 days for `VOLUNTARY`, 180 days for `INVOLUNTARY`, 1095 days for `RETIREMENT`, and 365 days for `DEATH`. A `FOR_CAUSE` termination therefore expires the exercise period on the termination date itself.

**Hop 1 — resolving tranches.** The resolution walks the participant's grants in ascending `created_at` order and, inside each grant, its tranches in ascending `tranche_index` order, so that the reserve ledger comes out identical on every replay. Only tranches belonging to grants that are `GRANTED` or `PARTIALLY_FORFEITED` participate; tranches of grants still in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, or `APPROVED` are left completely untouched, because those grants have no grant date yet. Those untouched grants can nevertheless no longer be submitted or approved, since both require an actively employed recipient.

For each `UNVESTED` tranche, the reference date is the `termination_date` and eligibility is `retirement_eligible` as evaluated at that date. If the reason is `DEATH`, the tranche moves to `VESTED` with `acceleration_reason` set to `DEATH`. Otherwise, if the reason is `RETIREMENT`, the participant is retirement eligible, and the tranche's `scheduled_vest_date` falls at or before the termination date advanced by 365 days, the tranche moves to `VESTED` with `acceleration_reason` set to `RETIREMENT_ELIGIBILITY`; the window comparison is inclusive, so a tranche scheduled exactly 365 days out accelerates while one scheduled on day 366 forfeits. In every other case the tranche moves to `FORFEITED`. A `RETIREMENT` termination of a participant who is not retirement eligible accelerates nothing and behaves exactly like `VOLUNTARY` for vesting purposes — although the post-termination exercise period still uses the 1095-day `RETIREMENT` entry.

For each tranche already `VESTED`, the reason decides: under `FOR_CAUSE` the tranche is clawed back to `FORFEITED`, and under every other reason it is left alone. The clawback only applies while no settlement of that tranche has reached `SETTLED`; delivered shares are not retracted. When a `VESTED` tranche is clawed back, every settlement of that tranche still sitting at `PENDING_TAX` moves to `REVERSED` with `reversal_reason` set to `FOR_CAUSE_CLAWBACK`, and any tax rows of those settlements are deleted. Tranches already `SETTLED` or `FORFEITED` are never touched by any branch.

Each accelerated tranche gets one settlement record of its own, created exactly as a scheduled vesting would create it — `tax_status` `PENDING_ALLOCATION`, `settlement_status` `PENDING_TAX`, `gross_shares` equal to the tranche's `shares`, `vest_date` equal to the termination request's calendar date, `sourcing_start_date` copied from the grant's `grant_date` — except that its `fmv_per_share_cents` comes from the `acceleration_fmv_per_share_cents` supplied on the termination request, and its `vested_at` is the termination request instant. Because an accelerated tranche is already `VESTED` when the cascade finishes, a later attempt to vest it on schedule is refused with `ERR_TRANCHE_INVALID_STATE`.

**Hop 2 — resolving grants.** Every grant whose tranches changed in hop 1 recomputes `outstanding_shares` and `weighted_reserve_shares` by the rule of §3.4. A grant whose recomputed `outstanding_shares` is greater than zero moves to `PARTIALLY_FORFEITED`; a grant whose `outstanding_shares` recomputes to exactly 0 and none of whose tranches is `SETTLED` moves to `FORFEITED` with both `outstanding_shares` and `weighted_reserve_shares` set to 0. Both destinations are reachable from `GRANTED` and from `PARTIALLY_FORFEITED`, so a second termination-style cascade can move an already partially forfeited grant the rest of the way. `FORFEITED` is terminal.

**Hop 3 — the plan ledger.** Each grant's weight decrease from hop 2 is added to the plan's `shares_returned_cumulative` and to its `fiscal_year_returned_shares`. A fully forfeited grant returns its entire prior `weighted_reserve_shares`. The reserve ledger is then rerun, rewriting `available_reserve_shares`, and the net burn rate is recomputed. The gross burn rate is not unwound.

**Hop 4 — the reserve posture.** The increase in `available_reserve_shares` from hop 3 is fed back through the posture rule, which can flip `reserve_posture` from `RESTRICTED` to `OPEN` and thereby restore headroom that a later delegated approval depends on. This is the fourth and final hop of the chain.

**The independent fan-out.** Separately from those four hops, and not part of the chain, termination sweeps the participant's trading activity: every trading plan of theirs whose status is `PENDING_COOLING_OFF` or `EFFECTIVE` moves to `TERMINATED` with its `termination_reason` recorded as `EMPLOYMENT_TERMINATED`, and every preclearance request of theirs whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, or `APPROVED` moves to `VOID`.

The participant's own `version` advances by 1, as does the `version` of every grant, tranche, trading plan, preclearance request, and plan that the cascade mutated. `TERMINATED` is terminal for a participant.
### 3.9. The Trading Calendar, Preclearance, Pre-established Plans, and Section 16 Filings

**Scheduling windows.** The trading calendar belongs to compliance: only a `ComplianceOfficer` may schedule a window or drive its status. A window names the `plan_id` whose calendar it belongs to, a `quarter_label` identifying the fiscal quarter it follows — such as `Q3-FY2026`, and unique among the windows of that plan — an `open_at` instant at which it opens and a `close_at` instant strictly after it at which it closes, and an optional `aggregate_share_cap` limiting the cumulative precleared shares compliance may approve inside it, which must be at least 1 when supplied and means "uncapped" when omitted. A new window is `SCHEDULED` with `approved_shares_cumulative` at 0 and `suspension_reason` null.

Two calendar rules guard creation, and their order matters. First, the new interval may not overlap any window of the same plan that is not `CLOSED`. Window intervals are half-open — inclusive of `open_at`, exclusive of `close_at` — and are compared half-open, so a new window whose `open_at` equals an existing window's `close_at` does not overlap. An overlap is refused with `ERR_TRADING_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Windows already `CLOSED` are ignored, so a replacement window may reuse a retired slot. Second, even non-overlapping windows must be separated by a 48-hour blackout buffer measured from the preceding non-`CLOSED` window's `close_at` to the new window's `open_at`, so that the market can absorb the prior window's trades; a gap of exactly 48 hours is allowed, while 47 hours and 59 minutes is refused with `ERR_TRADING_WINDOW_BUFFER_VIOLATION`. Because the buffer is checked immediately after the overlap rule, a window that both overlaps and violates the buffer reports the overlap.

**Driving a window.** The status endpoint takes an `action` of `OPEN`, `SUSPEND`, `RESUME`, or `CLOSE`, plus an optional `suspension_reason`. `OPEN` moves a `SCHEDULED` window to `OPEN` and requires the plan not to be `TERMINATED`. Being `OPEN` is necessary but not sufficient for trading: a window is genuinely tradeable only when its status is `OPEN` **and** the logical clock lies at or after `open_at` and strictly before `close_at`. `SUSPEND` moves an `OPEN` window to `SUSPENDED`, stores the `suspension_reason`, and voids every preclearance request of that window whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, or `APPROVED`, after which `approved_shares_cumulative` is recomputed over the survivors; the window's `version` and the `version` of every voided request each advance by 1. `RESUME` moves a `SUSPENDED` window back to `OPEN` and clears `suspension_reason` to null, but deliberately does **not** restore the requests the suspension voided — a resumed window restarts from an `approved_shares_cumulative` recomputed over whatever survived. `CLOSE` is reachable from `SCHEDULED`, `OPEN`, and `SUSPENDED`, moves the window to `CLOSED`, expires every `APPROVED` request of the window and voids every request that was `SUBMITTED` or `INFO_REQUESTED`, then recomputes `approved_shares_cumulative` over the surviving requests that are `APPROVED` or `EXECUTED`; the window's `version` and the `version` of every mutated request each advance by 1. `CLOSED` is terminal, and an action with no outgoing transition from the window's current status — `RESUME` on an `OPEN` window, or anything at all on a `CLOSED` window — is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID_STATE`. Windows are readable individually and listable per plan with an optional `status` filter.

**Requesting preclearance.** A `Participant` acting for themselves, or a `ComplianceOfficer` acting on an insider's behalf, files a preclearance request naming the `participant_id` seeking clearance, the `window_id` the trade would settle inside, a `transaction_type` of `SELL`, `BUY`, or `GIFT`, a `requested_shares` count of at least 1, and the `intended_trade_date` on which the participant intends to trade. The new request is `SUBMITTED` with `revision_count` at 0, `valid_until` null, `executed_shares` at 0, `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at`, `decision_note`, `executed_at`, and `price_per_share_cents` all null, and `requested_by_user_id` recorded from the acting `X-User-ID` for the four-eyes control at decision time.

Three checks guard creation, in this order. The named window must be genuinely tradeable in the sense above — a `SUSPENDED` window, a `SCHEDULED` window whose `open_at` is still in the future, and an `OPEN` window whose `close_at` has already passed on the logical clock all yield `ERR_TRADING_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The `intended_trade_date` must fall inside the window's date span, from the calendar date of `open_at` through the calendar date of `close_at` inclusive, and must not be earlier than the logical clock date; a violation is `ERR_TRADE_DATE_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`. And when the window carries a non-null `aggregate_share_cap`, its `approved_shares_cumulative` plus the `requested_shares` may not exceed that cap, otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_SHARE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. That last rule is an aggregate check across sibling requests: each individual request may be modest while the collective busts the ceiling. Only requests in `APPROVED` and `EXECUTED` status count toward `approved_shares_cumulative`; a `DENIED`, `EXPIRED`, or `VOID` request releases its reservation. The window-status rule sits above the trade-date rule, so a request that violates both reports `ERR_TRADING_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

**Deciding.** Only a `ComplianceOfficer` decides, by supplying a `decision` of `REQUEST_INFO`, `APPROVE`, or `DENY` and an optional `decision_note`. In all three cases the request must currently be `SUBMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_PRECLEARANCE_INVALID_STATE`, and in all three cases the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from `requested_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

`REQUEST_INFO` moves the request from `SUBMITTED` to `INFO_REQUESTED`, stores the `decision_note`, stamps `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at`, leaves `valid_until` null, and takes no share reservation. Its own guard is the revision cap: `revision_count` is compared against 2 before the loop is opened, so the first and second information requests are allowed and the third is refused with `ERR_MAX_PRECLEARANCE_REVISIONS_EXCEEDED`.

`APPROVE` carries the full set of preconditions. The window must be genuinely tradeable, otherwise `ERR_TRADING_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The participant's employment must be `ACTIVE`. The `intended_trade_date` is re-checked against the window's date span and against the logical clock date, because the clock has moved on since the request was filed, and a violation is `ERR_TRADE_DATE_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`; when both the window-status rule and the trade-date rule are violated, the window-status rule wins. The participant must have no trading plan in `EFFECTIVE` status, because an insider trading under an effective pre-established plan may not also obtain individual clearance; that refusal is `ERR_TRADING_PLAN_CONFLICT`. And when the window has a non-null `aggregate_share_cap`, `approved_shares_cumulative` plus `requested_shares` must still fit inside it, otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_SHARE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. On success the request becomes `APPROVED`, `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at`, and `decision_note` are stored, `valid_until` is computed as described below, the window's `approved_shares_cumulative` is increased by `requested_shares`, and both the request's `version` and the window's `version` advance by 1.

`DENY` is the divergent sibling: it requires only that the request be `SUBMITTED` and that the four-eyes control be satisfied, and it deliberately skips every window, trade-date, trading-plan, and cap precondition. It moves the request to `DENIED`, stamps `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at`, and `decision_note`, leaves the window's `approved_shares_cumulative` completely unchanged, and advances only the request's `version` by 1. `DENIED` is terminal.

**Validity of a clearance.** On approval, `valid_until` is the earlier of the `decided_at` instant advanced by 5 days and the window's own `close_at` — a clearance is good for five days or until the window closes, whichever comes first. The arithmetic is exact instant arithmetic.

**Answering an information request.** A `Participant` acting for themselves, or a `ComplianceOfficer`, resubmits a request that is `INFO_REQUESTED` — anything else yields `ERR_PRECLEARANCE_INVALID_STATE` — by supplying a fresh `requested_shares` and `intended_trade_date`. When the actor is a `Participant`, the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the request's `participant_id`, and the participant's employment must be `ACTIVE`. Resubmission moves the request back to `SUBMITTED`, overwrites both fields, clears `decision_note` to null, and increments `revision_count` by 1; the cap itself is enforced on the decision that opens each loop, not here. The `intended_trade_date` is re-validated against the window's date span, and the window share cap is re-checked, so a revised request that now busts the ceiling is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_SHARE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. That closes the cycle `SUBMITTED → INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, and the request's `version` advances by 1.

**Executing.** A `Participant` acting for themselves — with `X-User-ID` equal to the request's `participant_id` — or a `ComplianceOfficer` reports the executed trade against a clearance that is `APPROVED`, supplying `executed_shares` and `price_per_share_cents`. The logical clock must be at or before `valid_until`; executing at exactly `valid_until` succeeds, one millisecond later is refused with `ERR_PRECLEARANCE_EXPIRED` and the request is left in `APPROVED` so an operator can see the miss. `executed_shares` must be positive and no greater than `requested_shares`. The window must be genuinely tradeable and the execution instant must fall at or after `open_at` and strictly before `close_at`, otherwise `ERR_TRADING_WINDOW_CLOSED`. On success the request becomes `EXECUTED`, storing `executed_shares`, `price_per_share_cents`, and an `executed_at` stamped from the logical clock, and its `version` advances by 1. The window's `approved_shares_cumulative` is deliberately **not** reduced when the insider executes fewer shares than were cleared: the cap reserves the requested amount for the life of the window. When the participant's `insider_status` is `SECTION_16_OFFICER` or `DESIGNATED_INSIDER`, execution also creates a Section 16 report. `EXECUTED`, `EXPIRED`, and `VOID` are all terminal, and `VOID` is reachable from `SUBMITTED`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, and `APPROVED` through the window-suspension cascade, the window-close cascade, and the employment-termination fan-out; a request voided that way has its `version` advanced by 1 and causes the window's `approved_shares_cumulative` to be recomputed. `EXPIRED` is reached only from `APPROVED`, and only by the window-close cascade. Requests are listable with optional `participant_id`, `window_id`, and `status` filters, and readable individually.

**Adopting a pre-established plan.** A `Participant` acting for themselves, or a `ComplianceOfficer` acting on the insider's behalf, adopts a Rule 10b5-1 pre-established trading plan by naming the `participant_id` adopting it, a `plan_document_ref` pointing at the executed plan document, the `first_scheduled_trade_date` as an ISO date of the form `YYYY-MM-DD`, a `total_shares` count of at least 1 that the plan may transact over its life, and `expected_participant_version`. Adoption is the write guarded by optimistic concurrency on the participant, because it both mutates the participant and retires the participant's prior plan; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_PARTICIPANT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The participant's employment must be `ACTIVE`. The participant must not already hold a plan in `PENDING_COOLING_OFF` status — one plan may be waiting out its cooling-off period at a time — and a second concurrent adoption is refused with `ERR_TRADING_PLAN_CONFLICT`.

A successful adoption creates the plan in `PENDING_COOLING_OFF` with `modification_count` at 0, `termination_reason` and `superseded_plan_id` null, `adopted_at` stamped from the logical clock, and `insider_status_at_adoption` frozen from the participant's `insider_status` at that instant. Freezing matters: a participant later promoted to `SECTION_16_OFFICER` keeps the shorter cooling-off period that their status at adoption earned them, and a participant later demoted keeps the longer one. Only future adoptions and modifications observe the new classification. `Participant.version` advances by 1.

**The cooling-off period.** `cooling_off_days` and `first_trade_allowed_at` are derived from `insider_status_at_adoption` and the equity plan's earnings calendar, anchored at `adopted_at`. A `NON_INSIDER` adoption has a cooling-off period of 0 days and may be activated immediately. A `DESIGNATED_INSIDER` adoption has a fixed cooling-off period of 30 days. A `SECTION_16_OFFICER` adoption is the interesting branch: it is bounded below by 90 days and above by 120 days, and inside that corridor it tracks the earnings calendar. The candidate length is the whole number of days from the calendar date of `adopted_at` to the calendar date obtained by advancing the calendar date of the equity plan's `next_earnings_release_at` forward by two business days, where business days are Monday through Friday, Saturdays and Sundays are skipped, and no holiday calendar applies. That candidate is then raised to 90 if it is smaller and lowered to 120 if it is larger, so an earnings release two weeks away still yields 90 days and one six months away yields 120. When the equity plan's `next_earnings_release_at` is null, the officer branch uses 90 days directly. The equity plan consulted is the single plan referenced by the adopting participant's grants; when the participant holds no grant, the sole equity plan row is used. `first_trade_allowed_at` is then `adopted_at` advanced by `cooling_off_days` whole days using exact instant arithmetic, so the time of day of `adopted_at` is preserved rather than being truncated to midnight.

**Activating, modifying, terminating.** Only a `ComplianceOfficer` activates a plan, and only from `PENDING_COOLING_OFF`; any other status is `ERR_TRADING_PLAN_INVALID_STATE`. The logical clock must be at or after `first_trade_allowed_at` — activating at exactly that instant is permitted, one millisecond earlier is refused with `ERR_COOLING_OFF_NOT_ELAPSED` — and the participant's employment must be `ACTIVE`. The plan becomes `EFFECTIVE` and its `version` advances by 1.

A `Participant` acting for themselves, or a `ComplianceOfficer`, may modify a plan that is `EFFECTIVE`, supplying a fresh `first_scheduled_trade_date` and `total_shares`. `modification_count` is compared against 2 before the loop is opened, so the first and second modifications are allowed and the third is refused with `ERR_MAX_MODIFICATIONS_EXCEEDED`. A modification is not a light edit: it returns the plan to `PENDING_COOLING_OFF`, overwrites both supplied fields, increments `modification_count` by 1, restamps `adopted_at` from the current logical clock, refreezes `insider_status_at_adoption` from the participant's **current** `insider_status`, and recomputes `cooling_off_days` and `first_trade_allowed_at` from scratch against that refreshed status. A participant promoted to `SECTION_16_OFFICER` between adoption and modification therefore inherits the officer cooling-off corridor on the modified plan even though the original adoption did not use it. That closes the cycle `PENDING_COOLING_OFF → EFFECTIVE → PENDING_COOLING_OFF`, and the plan's `version` advances by 1.

A `Participant` acting for themselves, or a `ComplianceOfficer`, may terminate a plan that is `PENDING_COOLING_OFF` or `EFFECTIVE`, supplying a `termination_reason` that is stored on the row; the plan becomes `TERMINATED` and its `version` advances by 1. The same status is reached by the employment-termination fan-out with a `termination_reason` of `EMPLOYMENT_TERMINATED`.

The fourth destination is reached only by the system. When a replacement plan is adopted while the participant already holds an `EFFECTIVE` plan, the prior plan moves to `EXPIRED` with a `termination_reason` of `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_ADOPTION`, its `version` advances by 1, and its id is written onto the new row as `superseded_plan_id`, preserving the chain of successive adoptions. `TERMINATED` and `EXPIRED` are both terminal. Plans are readable individually and listable with optional `participant_id`, `superseded_plan_id`, and `status` filters, so an auditor can walk a supersede chain from either end.

**Section 16 reports.** Reports are never created by a client. Two side effects create them, and both fire only when the participant's `insider_status` is `SECTION_16_OFFICER` or `DESIGNATED_INSIDER`. A vesting settlement creates a report with `source_type` `VESTING_SETTLEMENT` and `source_id` equal to the vesting event's id, `transaction_at` stamped from the logical clock, and `shares` equal to `shares_withheld` plus `shares_sold` for a share-disposing method and to `gross_shares` for `CASH_REMITTANCE`. A preclearance execution creates a report with `source_type` `PRECLEARANCE_EXECUTION` and `source_id` equal to the request's id, `transaction_at` equal to `executed_at`, and `shares` equal to `executed_shares`. That pair of discriminator values is why `source_id` cannot be interpreted without reading `source_type` first. In both cases `form_type` is `FORM_4` for a `SECTION_16_OFFICER` and `FORM_5` for a `DESIGNATED_INSIDER`, `status` is `PENDING`, `filed_at` is null, and `participant_id` names the reporting insider.

`due_at` is the end of the second business day after `transaction_at`. Take the calendar date of `transaction_at`; if that date is itself a Saturday or Sunday, begin from the next business day; then advance forward two business days, counting Monday through Friday only with no holiday calendar; `due_at` is that resulting date at `23:59:59.999Z`. A Wednesday transaction is therefore due at the end of Friday, and a Thursday transaction is due at the end of the following Monday.

Only a `ComplianceOfficer` files, and only a report that is `PENDING`; a second filing attempt on a report already `FILED` or `LATE_FILED` is refused with `ERR_REPORT_INVALID_STATE`. The filer must not be the reporting insider — when `X-User-ID` equals the report's `participant_id` the filing is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, so an insider cannot certify their own filing. Filing stamps `filed_at` from the logical clock and then branches on a single millisecond-precision comparison from the same trigger: at or before `due_at` the report becomes `FILED`, and strictly after `due_at` it becomes `LATE_FILED`. Filing at exactly `23:59:59.999Z` on the due date is timely; one millisecond later is late. Nothing rejects a late filing — lateness is recorded, not prevented, because the regulatory record must show it. Both statuses are terminal, and the report's `version` advances by 1. Reports are readable individually and listable per participant in `transaction_at` order with optional `status` and `form_type` filters. A settlement that is reversed deletes any report it created that is still `PENDING`, but a report already `FILED` or `LATE_FILED` is left standing, because a filing made with the regulator cannot be unmade by an internal reversal.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

All 63 endpoints of the service are listed below. Every path is rooted directly at the service root — the topology is flat, and no resource is nested under another in its own canonical path, even where a collection is scoped by a parent through a path parameter. Every request carries the two identity headers described in §2.1 and, where a request body is defined, a JSON body whose fields are the ones named in §2 and §3. Every response, success or failure, uses the envelope of §2.2.

Status codes follow one rule. A `POST` that brings a brand-new resource into existence returns HTTP 201 with the created resource in `data`: registering an equity plan, delegating an approval authority, enrolling a participant, recording a mobility assignment, drafting a grant, appending a vesting tranche, opening a performance cycle, vesting a tranche, scheduling a trading window, filing a preclearance request, and adopting a trading plan. Every other `POST` — every lifecycle transition, every decision, every recomputation, every compensating action — returns HTTP 200 with the updated resource in `data`. Every `GET` returns HTTP 200, with `response_type` of `object` for a single resource and `array` for a list. Note that vesting a tranche returns 201 even though its path reads as an action, because its purpose is to bring a settlement row into existence and that new row is what it returns.
