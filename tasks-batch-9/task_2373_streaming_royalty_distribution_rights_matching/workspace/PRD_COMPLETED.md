# Streaming Royalty Distribution API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A music streaming service collects money from subscribers and advertisers in one shape and must pay it out in an entirely different one. Revenue arrives as a monthly figure per territory and per subscription tier; obligations arise per play, per recording, per composition, and per contractual share held by a label, a distributor, a publisher or a collecting society. Between those two shapes sits the hardest part of the business: reported usage does not identify its owners. A play aggregate arrives with a track title, sometimes an artist name, sometimes a recording identifier that is wrong, and the service must decide which catalogued work it belongs to, who owns that work in the territory where the play happened, and how much of the money that ownership is worth to the cent.

This document specifies the Streaming Royalty Distribution API, the system of record for that translation. It governs the metadata catalogue of recordings and compositions, the ingestion and sealing of usage batches, the confidence-scored matching of reported plays to catalogued works, the adjudication of competing ownership claims, the sizing and attribution of royalty pools, the assembly of line-item statements to payees, the audit rights that rights holders may exercise against those statements, and the retroactive re-computation forced on the whole chain when a regulatory rate proceeding changes the rates after the fact. Every operation is mounted under the `/royalties` path prefix.

Two properties dominate the design and should be read as constraints on every requirement that follows. First, money is conserved: the value the service commits to a pool is either attributed to a matched owner, held in that pool's holding account awaiting a claim, or booked as a rounding residual — never created, never destroyed, and never silently drifted by a cascade or its compensating reversal. Second, arithmetic is reproducible: rounding direction, rounding order, and the exact moment at which each derived figure is frozen are all pinned by this specification, because a rights holder auditing a statement is entitled to reconstruct every cent from the same inputs the service used.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that apply across the whole API: authentication, response shape, pagination, identifier and versioning semantics, the logical clock, monetary discipline, territory and interval geometry, concurrency protection, cascade propagation, and the order in which requests are rejected.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user acts. Four roles exist, and their spellings are exact: `RoyaltyAdmin`, `RightsOpsAnalyst`, `RightsHolderUser`, and `RegulatoryOfficer`. A request missing either header, or presenting an `X-User-Role` that is not one of those four strings, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A well-formed request whose role is not listed among the authorized roles of the target endpoint is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The four roles embody a segregation-of-duties model. A `RoyaltyAdmin` is the royalty operations controller: it registers rights holders, opens accounting periods and royalty pools, calculates and finalizes distributions, builds and issues statements, produces audit data and settles audit findings. A `RightsOpsAnalyst` maintains the metadata catalogue, ingests and matches usage, and adjudicates ownership claims, but may never size a pool or move money. A `RightsHolderUser` acts for a single label, distributor, publisher or collective: it files claims, supplies evidence, disputes rival claims, exercises audit rights and asserts findings. A `RegulatoryOfficer` records proposed rate schedules and brings them into force, and may touch nothing else. The separations are deliberate and enforced by the endpoint authorization lists in §4: a `RegulatoryOfficer` may promulgate rates but may not finalize pools, and a `RoyaltyAdmin` may finalize pools but may neither promulgate rates nor adjudicate registrations.

Identity semantics differ by role, and they determine what `X-User-ID` means on the wire. When `X-User-Role` is `RightsHolderUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `RightsHolder`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `RightsHolder` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `RightsHolderUser` may only act on an `AuditEngagement` whose `rights_holder_id` equals `X-User-ID`, may only read a `RoyaltyStatement` whose `rights_holder_id` equals `X-User-ID`, may only file a `RightsRegistration` whose `claimant_id` equals `X-User-ID`, may only supply evidence on a registration whose `claimant_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and may only raise a dispute whose `disputing_holder_id` equals `X-User-ID`. Violations of any of these are rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). The gate bites on single-resource reads and on writes only; the list endpoints of §4 are never narrowed by caller identity, so a `RightsHolderUser` listing statements, registrations or audit engagements sees every record matching the supplied filters regardless of who owns it.

When `X-User-Role` is `RoyaltyAdmin`, `RightsOpsAnalyst` or `RegulatoryOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition demands an audit trail — as `provisional_match_by_user_id` on a `Work` when a `RightsOpsAnalyst` records a below-certainty match, as `reviewed_by_user_id` on a `RightsRegistration` when a `RightsOpsAnalyst` approves or rejects it — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`. The `findings_recorded_by_user_id` stamped on an `AuditEngagement` is likewise a recorded caller identifier, in that case the identifier of the `RightsHolderUser` who asserted the finding, and it is compared only against the identifier of the operator who later settles the engagement.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of records returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. The `offset` parameter skips a number of records from the front of the result set and defaults to 0. A `limit` outside its range, a negative `offset`, or either parameter supplied as something other than an integer is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; either parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as if it had been omitted. Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, and because the logical clock can leave many records sharing a `created_at`, records that tie are ordered by the sequence in which the server created them. Every list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope described in §2.2.

Beyond pagination, list endpoints accept optional equality filters, and the filter parameter names are the field names of the resource being listed. Works may be filtered by `work_type`, by `linked_work_id`, or by `rights_state`. Usage batches may be filtered by `period_id` or by `status`. Usage lines may be filtered by `batch_id`, `work_id`, `match_status`, `territory_code` or `subscription_tier`. Rights registrations may be filtered by `work_id`, `claimant_id`, `collective_id`, `payee_holder_id`, `disputing_holder_id`, `status` or `right_type`. Royalty pools may be filtered by `period_id`, `applied_rate_schedule_id`, `residual_payee_holder_id`, `status`, `territory_code` or `subscription_tier`. Statements may be filtered by `rights_holder_id`, `period_id` or `status`. Statement lines may be filtered by `statement_id`, `pool_id`, `work_id`, `payee_holder_id`, `adjustment_of_line_id`, `line_type`, `right_type`, `territory_code` or `subscription_tier`. Audit engagements may be filtered by `rights_holder_id`, `statement_id`, `production_status` or `finding_status`. Filters combine conjunctively; omitting a filter means the field is not constrained. A supplied value is compared as a string against the stored field and is never validated against that field's domain, so an unrecognised value is not an error and simply matches nothing, and a record whose filtered field is null never matches a supplied value.

### 2.4. Identifiers, Timestamps and Record Versioning

Every resource the API mints carries an `id` that is a server-generated UUID. Identifiers never carry a type prefix; a statement line's `id` and a pool's `id` are indistinguishable in shape, and callers must not parse them. Every resource also carries a `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recording the moment the record came into existence.

Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp, in the same format, refreshed on every mutation, and a `version`, an integer that is set to 1 at creation and incremented by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record. This applies to `RightsHolder`, `Work`, `AccountingPeriod`, `UsageBatch`, `UsageLine`, `RightsRegistration`, `RoyaltyPool`, `RoyaltyStatement`, `AuditEngagement` and `RateSchedule`. Recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation: when a cascade rewrites a pool's holding account or a batch's play-count aggregates, that pool's and that batch's `updated_at` and `version` move too. A recomputation that leaves every stored value exactly where it was is not a mutation and moves neither field: a review transition that leaves a work's `rights_state` at the value it already held, or a matching run that re-stamps a work's `provisional_match_by_user_id` with the identifier already recorded there, leaves that work's `updated_at` and `version` alone.

`StatementLine` is the one exception. A statement line is immutable evidence of a payment obligation, written once when its parent statement is built and never edited afterwards. It therefore carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all. A correction to a statement line is expressed by issuing a new line of type `ADJUSTMENT` that points at the original through `adjustment_of_line_id`, never by editing the original.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Time-Boxed Windows

Several rules in this specification depend on the current instant: claim windows, audit windows, settlement interest, and the choice of attribution denominator. To make those rules deterministic and testable, the service reads time from a logical clock rather than from the host wall clock. Before any caller has set it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real wall-clock time. Any of the four roles may move the clock to an arbitrary instant by supplying `to_iso8601` to `POST /royalties/_clock/set_current_utc`; a missing or unparseable `to_iso8601` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and a successful set answers `200 OK` with an empty `data` object rather than any representation of the clock. Everything the specification calls the current instant is read from this clock, including the `issued_at` stamped on a statement and the `promulgated_at` stamped on a rate schedule.

Three windows are derived from the clock and from stored dates. An `AccountingPeriod` computes `claim_window_closes_at` as its `usage_end` taken at midnight UTC — that is, at `00:00:00.000Z` on that date — advanced by 60 days; the bound is inclusive, so the current instant exactly equal to `claim_window_closes_at` still counts as open. An `AuditEngagement` may be opened only while the current instant is at or before 180 days after the audited statement's `issued_at`. An engagement computes `production_deadline_at` as its own `created_at` advanced by 30 days, and a data production is accepted only while the current instant is at or before `production_deadline_at`.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in whole cents. There are no fractional-cent balances, no decimal money fields, and no currency conversion. Rates and shares are expressed in basis points, where 10,000 basis points is the whole; a share of 2,500 basis points is one quarter. One rate is finer grained still: `statutory_floor_per_play_millicents` is expressed in thousandths of a cent, because a per-play floor is a fraction of a cent in every realistic proceeding.

The default rounding method throughout the system is round half up to a whole cent: a computed amount whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero. Four places deliberately depart from that default, and each departure is load-bearing rather than incidental. The statutory floor of a pool is rounded **up** to the next whole cent, never down, because it is a floor. The per-usage-line attribution of a pool's distributable amount is rounded **down** to a whole cent, so that attribution drift is always non-negative and lands in a single explicitly tracked residual instead of over-committing the pool. Statement line gross amounts are rounded **per contributing usage line first and summed afterwards**; summing unrounded bases across a group and rounding once at the end is incorrect and can differ by up to one cent for every usage line in the group. Audit settlement interest is rounded **exactly once**, at the very end, on the fully assembled quotient; rounding an annual figure first and prorating it by days afterwards is incorrect and can differ by one cent.

Where a derived integer is a sum of already-rounded integers — a statement's `gross_cents`, `admin_fee_cents` and `net_payable_cents`, a holder's `receivable_balance_cents`, a period's `total_holding_cents` — the sum is taken as-is and never re-rounded.

### 2.7. Territory Coverage and Interval Geometry

Territories are ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 codes written as exactly two uppercase letters, plus one reserved literal, `WORLD`. The literal is accepted in three places and forbidden in two. It is accepted as a `RightsHolder`'s `home_territory_code`, meaning the holder is not tied to a single jurisdiction; as the sole member of a `RightsRegistration`'s `territory_set`, meaning a worldwide claim; and as a `RateSchedule`'s `territory_code`, meaning a fallback rate layer. It is rejected on a `UsageLine`'s `territory_code` and on a `RoyaltyPool`'s `territory_code`, because plays and pools always happen somewhere concrete.

A `territory_set` is either exactly the single-element list containing `WORLD`, or a non-empty list of between 1 and 250 distinct two-letter codes. Mixing `WORLD` with concrete codes in the same list is invalid. When two registrations are compared for territorial overlap, `WORLD` behaves as a wildcard that intersects every territory, including another `WORLD`.

The system uses two different interval geometries, and confusing them changes results. A `RightsRegistration`'s ownership interval runs from `effective_from` inclusive to `effective_to` **exclusive**; equal endpoints would describe an empty interval and are therefore invalid, so `effective_to` must be strictly after `effective_from`. A `RateSchedule`'s applicability window runs from `effective_from` to `effective_to` **inclusive at both ends**; equal endpoints describe a legal one-day window, so `effective_to` need only be on or after `effective_from`. The two geometries also differ in how `WORLD` behaves for conflict detection: a worldwide registration collides with a concrete-territory registration of the same work, right and claimant, whereas a `WORLD` rate schedule does **not** collide with a concrete-territory rate schedule for the same tier, because those are separate specificity layers resolved at selection time rather than competing occupants of the same slot.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations require the client to prove it is acting on a current view of a parent record, and both reject a stale view outright rather than merging it.

A `POST /royalties/rights-registrations` request must carry `expected_work_version`, a non-optional input field holding the client's last observed `version` of the `Work` named by `work_id`. If that value does not equal the `Work`'s current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_WORK_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The protection matters because a claimed share is sized against the work's currently approved registration set, and that set legitimately changes when another registration is approved with `supersede_overlapping` set to true, or when a fuzzy match stamps `provisional_match_by_user_id`. A registration built against the earlier view would be admitted even though the share space it targeted has already been redistributed.

A `POST /royalties/statements` request must carry `expected_period_version`, a non-optional input field holding the client's last observed `version` of the `AccountingPeriod` named by `period_id`. If that value does not equal the period's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PERIOD_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Without it, a new statement for a `(rights_holder_id, period_id)` pair would silently snapshot a set of pools that a cascade has since reopened, re-finalized or re-attributed.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Callers must not send one, and the server must not require one, on any operation other than the two named above.

### 2.9. Derived Aggregates, Cascades and Conserved Quantities

A large share of this system's state is derived rather than supplied. Play-count aggregates on a batch, money aggregates on a pool and on a statement, a work's rights state, a holder's receivable balance, a period's holding total and its status — all of these are functions of their children, recomputed by the server whenever a child changes, without the client ever asking for that specific record to be updated. Two of the entity statuses are compound in exactly this sense: an `AccountingPeriod`'s `status` and a `UsageBatch`'s `status` bubble up from the state of their children rather than being set by a dedicated call.

Several operations therefore reach well beyond the record named in the request. Approving an ownership claim can re-attach usage lines, promote batches, and re-attribute pools. Promulgating a retroactive rate schedule can reopen finalized pools, supersede issued statements, reduce payee balances, and reopen periods. Each such reach is specified with its causing operation in §3, and each record actually touched has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented as described in §2.4.

Three quantities are conserved across every cascade and every compensating reversal, and no operation in this system may leave them broken:

- Within a `RoyaltyPool`, `matched_attributed_cents` plus `holding_cents` plus `rounding_residual_cents` equals `distributable_cents` at all times. Approving a claim moves cents out of `holding_cents` and into `matched_attributed_cents`; disputing that same claim moves exactly those cents back. Once a pool is finalized, `distributable_cents` and `rounding_residual_cents` are frozen and must not drift by either action.
- Within a `UsageBatch`, `matched_play_count` plus `unmatched_play_count` plus `disputed_play_count` equals `total_play_count` at all times. Approving a claim moves plays from `unmatched_play_count` into `matched_play_count`; disputing it moves exactly those plays from `matched_play_count` into `disputed_play_count`. `total_play_count` is a pure function of the immutable reported lines and must never change once the lines exist.
- For a `RightsHolder`, `receivable_balance_cents` equals the sum of `net_cents` over every `StatementLine` whose `payee_holder_id` is that holder and whose parent statement's `status` is `ISSUED`, plus the sum of `settled_amount_cents` and `interest_cents` over every `AuditEngagement` of that holder whose `finding_status` is `SETTLED`. Statements in `DRAFT` or `SUPERSEDED` contribute zero, with no time window; engagements in `NONE`, `ASSERTED` or `WITHDRAWN` contribute zero. The companion counter `issued_statement_count` equals the number of statements addressed to that holder whose `status` is `ISSUED`. Issuing a statement raises the balance by exactly that statement's `net_payable_cents`; superseding it lowers the balance by exactly the same amount, returning it to its pre-issue value.

### 2.10. Rejection Order

Many endpoints carry several independent checks, and a request that fails more than one must report a single, predictable `error_id`. Checks are therefore evaluated in the following fixed order, and the first failure decides the response. Text order elsewhere in this document does not imply evaluation order; this list does.

1. Authentication: `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are present and the role is one of the four declared in §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. Role authorization: the supplied role appears in the target endpoint's authorized list — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. Existence of every referenced identifier, validated left to right: first the path parameters in the order they appear in the path, then the body foreign-key fields in the order their entity declares them. The first missing identifier decides which not-found error is returned, drawn from `ERR_RIGHTS_HOLDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_USAGE_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_USAGE_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATEMENT_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_RATE_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint carrying a path identifier or a body foreign key.
4. Structural validity of the request body — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Applies to every write endpoint.
5. Uniqueness of natural keys — `ERR_DUPLICATE_WORK_IDENTIFIER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_PERIOD_LABEL`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_POOL` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_STATEMENT`. Applies to `POST /royalties/works`, `POST /royalties/periods`, `POST /royalties/pools` and `POST /royalties/statements`.
6. Resource ownership for the entity-backed role — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Applies to every endpoint whose authorized list includes `RightsHolderUser`.
7. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Applies to `POST /royalties/rights-registrations/{id}/approve` and `POST /royalties/audit-engagements/{id}/settle`.
8. Usage batch state — `ERR_INVALID_USAGE_BATCH_STATE`. Applies to `POST /royalties/usage-batches/{id}/lines`, `POST /royalties/usage-batches/{id}/seal` and `POST /royalties/usage-batches/{id}/match`.
9. Registration state — `ERR_INVALID_REGISTRATION_STATE`. Applies to `POST /royalties/rights-registrations/{id}/review`, `/request-evidence`, `/evidence`, `/approve`, `/reject` and `/dispute`.
10. Pool state — `ERR_INVALID_POOL_STATE`. Applies to `POST /royalties/pools/{id}/calculate` and `POST /royalties/pools/{id}/finalize`.
11. Statement state — `ERR_INVALID_STATEMENT_STATE`. Applies to `POST /royalties/statements/{id}/issue` and `POST /royalties/audit-engagements`.
12. Engagement finding state — `ERR_INVALID_ENGAGEMENT_STATE`. Applies to `POST /royalties/audit-engagements/{id}/data-productions`, `/findings` and `/settle`.
13. Rate schedule state — `ERR_INVALID_RATE_SCHEDULE_STATE`. Applies to `POST /royalties/rate-schedules/{id}/promulgate`.
14. Accounting period state — `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_STATE`. Applies to `POST /royalties/usage-batches`, `POST /royalties/pools/{id}/calculate`, `POST /royalties/statements` and `POST /royalties/periods/{id}/close`.
15. Optimistic concurrency, as specified in §2.8 — `ERR_STALE_WORK_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_PERIOD_VERSION`. Applies to `POST /royalties/rights-registrations` and `POST /royalties/statements`. Because this check sits after every state check above, a stale version supplied against an already-illegal target still yields the state error rather than the 412.
16. Statutory rate floor ratchet — `ERR_STATUTORY_RATE_FLOOR_BREACH`. Applies to `POST /royalties/rate-schedules/{id}/promulgate`.
17. Claim window — `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Applies to `POST /royalties/rights-registrations`.
18. Audit and production windows — `ERR_AUDIT_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Applies to `POST /royalties/audit-engagements` and `POST /royalties/audit-engagements/{id}/data-productions`.
19. Interval well-formedness — `ERR_INVALID_EFFECTIVE_PERIOD`. Applies to `POST /royalties/rights-registrations`, `POST /royalties/rate-schedules`, `POST /royalties/periods` and `POST /royalties/audit-engagements`.
20. Collective and direct-deal eligibility — `ERR_COLLECTIVE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Applies to `POST /royalties/rights-registrations`.
21. Agreement between the claimed right and the work, and the claimant's eligibility to claim it — `ERR_RIGHT_TYPE_WORK_MISMATCH`. Applies to `POST /royalties/rights-registrations`.
22. Aggregate share oversubscription per concrete territory — `ERR_SHARE_OVERSUBSCRIBED`. Applies to `POST /royalties/rights-registrations/{id}/approve`.
23. Rate schedule exclusive occupancy — `ERR_RATE_SCHEDULE_OVERLAP`. Applies to `POST /royalties/rate-schedules/{id}/promulgate`.
24. Structural coherence of the publishing split — `ERR_RATE_SHARE_SPLIT_INVALID`. Applies to `POST /royalties/rate-schedules`, and is re-verified at `POST /royalties/rate-schedules/{id}/promulgate`.
25. Non-degenerate usage — `ERR_EMPTY_USAGE_BATCH` on `POST /royalties/usage-batches/{id}/seal` and `ERR_ZERO_PLAY_COUNT` on `POST /royalties/usage-batches/{id}/lines`.
26. Evidence round cap — `ERR_EVIDENCE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. Applies to `POST /royalties/rights-registrations/{id}/request-evidence`.
27. Dispute standing — `ERR_DISPUTE_BY_CLAIMANT`. Applies to `POST /royalties/rights-registrations/{id}/dispute`.
28. Availability of a governing rate — `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_RATE_SCHEDULE`. Applies to `POST /royalties/pools/{id}/calculate`.
29. Data production completeness — `ERR_DATA_PRODUCTION_INCOMPLETE`. Applies to `POST /royalties/audit-engagements/{id}/findings`.
30. Settlement amount against the assertion — `ERR_SETTLEMENT_EXCEEDS_ASSERTION`. Applies to `POST /royalties/audit-engagements/{id}/settle`.
31. Presence of statement lines — `ERR_NO_STATEMENT_LINES`. Applies to `POST /royalties/statements/{id}/issue`.
32. Emptiness of the holding account — `ERR_HOLDING_UNRESOLVED`. Applies to `POST /royalties/periods/{id}/close`.
33. Absence of open ownership claims — `ERR_UNRESOLVED_REGISTRATIONS`. Applies to `POST /royalties/periods/{id}/close`.

The practical consequence worth internalizing: authorization precedes existence, existence precedes shape, shape precedes uniqueness, ownership and duty separation precede every state check, all state checks precede the concurrency check, and every business rule about money, time and territory comes last.

### 2.11. Resolving Referenced Records

Identifier resolution is strictly ordered, as item 3 of §2.10 states, and the ordering is observable. Path parameters resolve first, in the order they appear in the path, so a request naming a non-existent parent never reports a missing child. Body foreign keys resolve next, in the order the target entity declares its fields. A registration create, for example, resolves `work_id` before `claimant_id` and `claimant_id` before `collective_id`, so a request naming both a non-existent work and a non-existent claimant reports `ERR_WORK_NOT_FOUND`. A registration's `linked_work_id` reference and a work's own `linked_work_id`, when supplied, resolve as `Work` references and report `ERR_WORK_NOT_FOUND` when absent. Identifiers supplied inside `statement_line_ids` on a data production resolve as `StatementLine` references and report `ERR_STATEMENT_LINE_NOT_FOUND` when absent.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Rights Holders and the Metadata Catalogue

Everything the service pays out is addressed to a **rights holder**. A `RoyaltyAdmin` registers one by supplying its `legal_name` — non-empty, at most 200 characters — its `holder_type`, which is one of `LABEL`, `DISTRIBUTOR`, `PUBLISHER` or `COLLECTIVE`, and its `home_territory_code`. A holder that intends to be paid under a direct deal must also supply `direct_deal_rate_bps`, the administration rate in basis points that the service withholds from every line it pays that holder directly; the field defaults to null and, when supplied, must lie between 0 and 5,000 inclusive. A holder is born with `receivable_balance_cents` and `issued_statement_count` both at zero, and both are derived thereafter exactly as §2.9 specifies. Reading a holder is the way to observe the running receivable balance.

The catalogue itself is a set of **works**, and a work is either one side of the copyright or the other. Its `work_type` is `RECORDING` for a sound recording — the master side — or `COMPOSITION` for a musical composition — the publishing side. Its `identifier` carries the ISRC for a recording and the ISWC for a composition; it is non-empty, at most 32 characters, and unique in combination with `work_type`, so a recording and a composition may share a string but two recordings may not. A second recording claiming an existing `(work_type, identifier)` pair is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_WORK_IDENTIFIER`. Every work carries a `title` of at most 300 characters, and a recording additionally carries `artist_credit`; the credit is required on a recording and must be null on a composition, and either mistake is a structural failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A recording may name the composition it embodies through `linked_work_id`, which points at another work; that link is null on compositions and on recordings whose composition has not been identified, and it is the bridge the publishing side of a statement walks. The server does not police that convention structurally: the only requirement on a supplied `linked_work_id` is that it resolve to an existing `Work`, so one offered on a `COMPOSITION`, or one pointing at another recording, is stored as given rather than refused.

Two normalized forms are computed at creation and are what the matcher actually compares. `normalized_title` is derived from `title` by lowercasing it, removing every character that is not a lowercase letter, a digit or a space, collapsing every run of consecutive spaces into a single space, and trimming leading and trailing spaces. `normalized_artist` is the same transformation applied to `artist_credit`, and is null whenever `artist_credit` is null. Neither is supplied by the caller.

A work also exposes its ownership posture. `rights_state` is derived from the work's own registrations in a pinned precedence order and defaults to `UNMATCHED` before any claim exists: it is `DISPUTED` if any registration of the work is in `DISPUTED` status; otherwise it is `REGISTERED` if any registration of the work is `APPROVED` and claims the right that matches the work's side of the copyright — a `MASTER` right on a `RECORDING`, or a `MECHANICAL` or `PERFORMANCE` right on a `COMPOSITION`; otherwise it is `PROVISIONAL` if any registration of the work is in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `NEEDS_EVIDENCE` status; otherwise it is `UNMATCHED`. Alongside it, `approved_master_share_bps` reports the sum of `share_bps` over the work's `APPROVED` registrations whose `right_type` is `MASTER` and whose `territory_set` contains the literal `WORLD`; it starts at 0 and exists for observability only. The oversubscription rule described in §3.4 is evaluated per concrete territory and never against this field. Finally, `provisional_match_by_user_id` records the `X-User-ID` of the `RightsOpsAnalyst` whose matching run last attached a usage line to this work at a confidence strictly below 10,000 basis points; it is null until such a fuzzy match happens, and it is the input to the segregation-of-duties rule that later blocks that same analyst from approving the claim that monetises the guess.

### 3.2. Accounting Periods, Usage Batches and Reported Plays

Royalties are computed a period at a time. A `RoyaltyAdmin` opens an **accounting period** with a `label` — a human name such as `2026-06`, unique across all periods, with a collision reported as `ERR_DUPLICATE_PERIOD_LABEL` — and the inclusive date range it covers, `usage_start` and `usage_end`, both ISO 8601 calendar dates. `usage_end` must be on or after `usage_start`; an inverted range is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EFFECTIVE_PERIOD`. On creation the service computes `claim_window_closes_at` as described in §2.5 and sets `status` to `OPEN`. A period also reports `total_holding_cents`, the sum of `holding_cents` across its pools, which is the number the closing rule in §3.8 cares about.

Inside an `OPEN` period, a `RightsOpsAnalyst` or a `RoyaltyAdmin` opens a **usage batch** — one reporting source's submission — by naming the owning period through `period_id` and giving the source a `source_label` of at most 120 characters. A batch that would be opened inside a period whose `status` is anything other than `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_STATE`. A new batch starts at `status` `OPEN` with `line_count`, `total_play_count`, `matched_play_count`, `unmatched_play_count` and `disputed_play_count` all at zero.

A `RightsOpsAnalyst` then appends **usage lines** to the open batch, one per reported cell of play activity. A line names its owning batch through `batch_id` and carries what the source actually reported: `reported_title`, non-empty and at most 300 characters; `reported_isrc`, which may be absent, may be wrong, and is capped at 32 characters; `reported_artist`, which may be absent and is length-unbounded; the `territory_code` in which the plays happened, which must be exactly two uppercase letters, with the literal `WORLD` refused on a usage line; the `subscription_tier` of the subscribers who generated them, one of `FREE_AD_SUPPORTED`, `INDIVIDUAL`, `FAMILY` or `STUDENT`; and `play_count`, the number of qualifying plays, which must be at least 1. A line offered with a `play_count` below 1 is refused with `ERR_ZERO_PLAY_COUNT`. A newly appended line starts with `work_id` null, `match_status` `UNMATCHED`, `match_confidence_bps` 0 and `attributed_revenue_cents` 0, and its append recomputes the owning batch's `line_count`, `total_play_count` and `unmatched_play_count`. Appending to a batch that is no longer `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_USAGE_BATCH_STATE`.

Sealing closes the batch to further reporting. A `RightsOpsAnalyst` seals an `OPEN` batch whose `line_count` is at least 1 while its parent period is still `OPEN`, moving the batch `OPEN → SEALED`; after that no further line may be appended. A seal attempted on a batch with no lines at all is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_USAGE_BATCH`, because an empty batch would otherwise let a period be sealed with no usage in it.

### 3.3. Resolving Reported Plays to Works

Matching is the operation that turns anonymous play aggregates into ownership-bearing ones. A `RightsOpsAnalyst` — and only that role, as the endpoint table of §4 records — runs it over a batch whose `status` is `SEALED`; a batch in any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_USAGE_BATCH_STATE`. The run scores every line of the batch against the catalogue, then forks the batch into one of two outcomes.

Scoring a single line proceeds in four steps. First, the candidate set is built: it contains every work whose `work_type` is `RECORDING` and which satisfies at least one of two conditions — the line's `reported_isrc` is present and the work's `identifier` equals it, or the work's `normalized_title` equals the normalization of the line's `reported_title` under the rules of §3.1. Compositions are never candidates; usage is always reported against recordings.

Second, every candidate is scored in basis points, and the branches are tried in this exact order. A candidate scores 10,000 when the line's `reported_isrc` is present and the candidate's `identifier` equals it — an exact identifier hit. Otherwise it scores 7,500 when the line's `reported_artist` is present, the candidate's `normalized_artist` is not null, the candidate's `normalized_title` equals the normalized `reported_title`, and the candidate's `normalized_artist` equals the normalized `reported_artist` — a title-and-artist agreement. Otherwise it scores 5,000 when the candidate's `normalized_title` equals the normalized `reported_title` — a title-only agreement. Otherwise it scores 0.

Third, the winner is the candidate with the highest score; ties are broken by the earlier `created_at`, and a remaining tie by the lexicographically smaller `identifier`.

Fourth, the outcome is written. If the candidate set is empty, the line keeps `work_id` null, records `match_confidence_bps` 0 and keeps `match_status` `UNMATCHED`. Otherwise `match_confidence_bps` is set to the winner's score, and acceptance depends on a threshold. When the score is at least 7,500, the line takes `work_id` from the winner and moves to `match_status` `AUTO_MATCHED`; and when that accepted score is strictly below 10,000 — that is, when the service accepted a metadata inference rather than an identifier hit — the winning work's `provisional_match_by_user_id` is stamped with the `X-User-ID` of the calling analyst. When the score is below 7,500 — in practice a title-only agreement scoring 5,000 — the line is **not** attached: `work_id` stays null and `match_status` stays `UNMATCHED`, but the line still records the best score it achieved, so a later claim can see how close the catalogue came. The value of such a line will fall into a pool's holding account rather than being paid to anyone.

Once every line has been scored, the batch forks. When `unmatched_play_count` has fallen to 0, the batch moves `SEALED → MATCHED` and no holding account will ever be opened on its behalf. When `unmatched_play_count` is 1 or more, the batch moves `SEALED → PARTIALLY_MATCHED` instead; the divergence is not cosmetic, because those still-`UNMATCHED` lines are precisely what will fund pool holding accounts at calculation time. Either way the run rewrites `work_id`, `match_status` and `match_confidence_bps` on every line, recomputes the batch's `matched_play_count`, `unmatched_play_count` and `disputed_play_count`, re-derives `rights_state` on every work it touched, and then asks the parent period to re-evaluate its own bubble-up.

That bubble-up is the period's `OPEN → USAGE_SEALED` transition, and it is driven by the system rather than by any caller. It fires when the period owns at least one usage batch and every batch of the period has a `status` of either `MATCHED` or `PARTIALLY_MATCHED`; the last batch to finish matching is the one that flips the period. The period's `version` increments when it does.

### 3.4. Ownership Claims: Filing, Review and Adjudication

A **rights registration** is one holder's assertion that it owns a share of one right in one work over one territory footprint and one time interval. Either a `RightsHolderUser` acting for itself or a `RightsOpsAnalyst` acting on a holder's behalf files one, naming the `work_id` whose rights are claimed, the `claimant_id` asserting the share, the `right_type` being claimed — `MASTER`, `MECHANICAL` or `PERFORMANCE` — the `routing`, which is `DIRECT` when the money goes straight to the claimant and `COLLECTIVE` when it flows through a society, the `share_bps` claimed, between 1 and 10,000, the `territory_set`, the `effective_from` and `effective_to` bounding the ownership interval, and the `expected_work_version` described in §2.8. A `COLLECTIVE` routing additionally requires `collective_id`, the holder that administers the share; a `DIRECT` routing requires `collective_id` to be null. Supplying the wrong one of those two is a structural failure and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The optional `supersede_overlapping` flag defaults to false and is discussed in §3.5.

Filing is gated by four business rules beyond the structural ones. The claimant must be entitled to the right it claims: a `MASTER` claim requires the work's `work_type` to be `RECORDING` and the claimant's `holder_type` to be `LABEL` or `DISTRIBUTOR`, while a `MECHANICAL` or `PERFORMANCE` claim requires the work's `work_type` to be `COMPOSITION` and the claimant's `holder_type` to be `PUBLISHER` or `COLLECTIVE`; any other combination is refused with `ERR_RIGHT_TYPE_WORK_MISMATCH`. Routing must be viable: when `routing` is `COLLECTIVE`, the `collective_id` must reference a holder whose `holder_type` is `COLLECTIVE` and whose `home_territory_code` is either `WORLD` or a member of the registration's `territory_set` — and a registration whose `territory_set` is exactly the single literal `WORLD` accepts any collective home, concrete or not — and when `routing` is `DIRECT`, the claimant's `direct_deal_rate_bps` must not be null — otherwise `ERR_COLLECTIVE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. The interval must be non-degenerate under the half-open geometry of §2.7, or `ERR_INVALID_EFFECTIVE_PERIOD` follows. And the claim window must still be open.

The claim window rule deserves care because its scope is narrow. A registration may be filed only while the current instant is at or before `claim_window_closes_at` for **every** accounting period that already carries at least one usage line touching the claimed work — for a `MASTER` claim, a line whose `work_id` is the claimed work; for a `MECHANICAL` or `PERFORMANCE` claim, a line whose matched work's `linked_work_id` is the claimed work. The bound is inclusive: the current instant exactly equal to `claim_window_closes_at` is still open. A claim on a work that carries no matched usage anywhere is never blocked by this rule at all; for such works the closing of the window instead redirects money through the attribution variant of §3.6. A late claim against a period that does carry the work's usage is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

A successful filing lands the registration at `status` `SUBMITTED` with `evidence_round` at 0. The service computes `payee_holder_id` from the routing — it equals `collective_id` when `routing` is `COLLECTIVE` and `claimant_id` when `routing` is `DIRECT` — re-derives the work's `rights_state`, which becomes `PROVISIONAL` if no stronger state applies, and increments the work's `version`. Only one `DRAFT`-or-`ISSUED` statement may exist per holder and period, but registrations carry no such uniqueness constraint; competing claims are resolved by adjudication, not by rejection at the door.

Adjudication is a cyclic review loop run by a `RightsOpsAnalyst`. Taking a `SUBMITTED` claim into review moves it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and re-derives the work's `rights_state`, which stays `PROVISIONAL`. From `UNDER_REVIEW` the analyst may bounce the claim back for documentary evidence, moving it `UNDER_REVIEW → NEEDS_EVIDENCE`, but only while `evidence_round` is strictly below 2; the third such attempt is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, and that cap is what guarantees the loop terminates. The claimant — a `RightsHolderUser` whose `X-User-ID` equals the registration's `claimant_id`, or a `RightsOpsAnalyst` acting for it — then supplies the evidence, which increments `evidence_round` by 1 and moves the claim `NEEDS_EVIDENCE → UNDER_REVIEW`; that step, too, requires `evidence_round` to be below 2 at the time it runs. Rejection is the terminal alternative: a `RightsOpsAnalyst` may move a claim `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED` by supplying a `rejection_reason`, which is stored alongside `reviewed_by_user_id` — the analyst's own `X-User-ID` — after which the work's `rights_state` is re-derived. A rejecting analyst is subject to the same duty separation as an approving one: the `X-User-ID` performing the rejection must differ from the claimed work's `provisional_match_by_user_id`. `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` accept no transition whatsoever, and any transition requested against a registration that is not in the state the transition requires is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REGISTRATION_STATE`.

Approval carries three additional gates on top of the state check. The approving `X-User-ID` must differ from the work's `provisional_match_by_user_id`, so that the analyst who guessed the match cannot be the analyst who monetises it — a violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The claimed right must still agree with the work and the claimant's `holder_type` must still permit it, on the same terms as at filing. And the aggregate share must fit. For the registration being approved, and for each concrete territory in its `territory_set` — or, when the set is exactly the single literal `WORLD`, for every territory already claimed on this work, meaning every concrete code appearing in the `territory_set` of any registration of this work whatever that registration's status, falling back to the single pseudo-territory `WORLD` when no concrete code has ever been claimed there, in which case only registrations that are themselves worldwide enter the sum — the service sums `share_bps` over all `APPROVED` registrations of the same work and the same `right_type` whose `territory_set` contains that territory or the literal `WORLD` and whose half-open interval overlaps this registration's interval, then adds this registration's own `share_bps`. If any territory's total exceeds 10,000 basis points, the approval is refused with `ERR_SHARE_OVERSUBSCRIBED`; a total of exactly 10,000 is accepted and a total of 10,001 is not. Rows that the supersede step has just moved to `SUPERSEDED` are excluded from the sum, because that step runs before the ceiling is tested.

A holder that believes an approved claim is wrong raises a **dispute**. Either the disputing `RightsHolderUser` itself or a `RightsOpsAnalyst` submits the `disputing_holder_id`, which is the request's one required field and is stored on the registration; a reason supplied alongside it is accepted but neither validated nor persisted, and the registration exposes no field for it. The registration must be `APPROVED`, the `disputing_holder_id` must differ from both the registration's `claimant_id` and its `collective_id` — a self-dispute or a dispute by the administering society is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_BY_CLAIMANT` — and when the caller is a `RightsHolderUser`, its `X-User-ID` must equal the `disputing_holder_id` it submits. The registration moves `APPROVED → DISPUTED` and the work's `rights_state` is re-derived to `DISPUTED`. From there the analyst may take it back into review, moving it `DISPUTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, which re-derives the work's `rights_state` and clears `DISPUTED` if no other registration of that work is still disputed; approving it again closes the `APPROVED → DISPUTED → UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED` cycle.

### 3.5. The Approval Cascade and Its Compensation

Approving a claim is the most far-reaching operation in the system, and it is best understood as three links, each caused by the previous one rather than by the approving call itself.

**Link one** settles the registration. Its `status` becomes `APPROVED` and `reviewed_by_user_id` records the approving `X-User-ID`. If the registration was filed with `supersede_overlapping` set to true, then before the share ceiling of §3.4 is tested, every `APPROVED` registration sharing the same work, the same `right_type` and the same `claimant_id`, whose `territory_set` intersects this one's and whose half-open interval overlaps this one's, is moved `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED`. That ordering is what allows a holder to replace its own incumbent share without tripping the oversubscription check. The supersession is nevertheless only committed once the ceiling has passed: an approval refused with `ERR_SHARE_OVERSUBSCRIBED` leaves every would-be victim `APPROVED`, at its previous `version`, exactly as the request found it.

**Link two** follows because link one changed the work's approved registration set. The work's `rights_state` is re-derived, moving from `UNMATCHED` or `PROVISIONAL` to `REGISTERED`, and `approved_master_share_bps` is recomputed. Then the service re-runs the matcher in claim mode over unattached usage: every usage line whose `work_id` is still null, whose batch belongs to a period where the current instant is at or before `claim_window_closes_at`, and whose best score against **this newly approved work alone** is at least 5,000, takes `work_id` from that work, records that score in `match_confidence_bps`, and moves to `match_status` `CLAIM_MATCHED`. The threshold is the whole point of the variant: the same title-only agreement scoring 5,000 that the batch-level run refused is now accepted, because an adjudicated owner has come forward to claim it. The candidate set is restricted to the just-approved work, and the resulting status is `CLAIM_MATCHED` rather than `AUTO_MATCHED`, which keeps released-from-holding money distinguishable from ordinary usage on the statement.

**Link three** follows because link two re-attached usage lines. Every affected usage batch recomputes `matched_play_count` and `unmatched_play_count`, and any batch whose `unmatched_play_count` has reached 0 moves `PARTIALLY_MATCHED → MATCHED`, which in turn lets the parent period re-evaluate its `OPEN → USAGE_SEALED` bubble-up. Every affected royalty pool is reconciled, and the treatment depends on whether the pool has been frozen. A pool whose `status` is `CALCULATED` re-runs attribution in full, so `holding_cents` falls and `matched_attributed_cents` rises by exactly the same number of cents. A pool whose `status` is `FINALIZED` keeps its frozen amounts and simply moves the affected value from `holding_cents` into `matched_attributed_cents` without re-attributing anything. In both cases `distributable_cents` and `rounding_residual_cents` are untouched, which is what keeps the pool conservation identity of §2.9 intact. The work's `version` increments, and so does the `version` of every usage line, usage batch and royalty pool the cascade touched.

Disputing an approved registration is the exact compensating action for that cascade, and it unwinds each link. Every usage line whose `match_status` is `CLAIM_MATCHED` and whose `work_id` is the disputed registration's work moves to `match_status` `DISPUTED`. Because those lines have left the matched set, every affected batch recomputes `matched_play_count` and `disputed_play_count` while `total_play_count` stays exactly where it was, and every affected pool moves the same cents from `matched_attributed_cents` back into `holding_cents`, again leaving `distributable_cents` and `rounding_residual_cents` untouched. Finally, every statement whose `status` is `ISSUED` and which carries at least one line of `line_type` `HOLDING_RELEASE` for the disputed work moves `ISSUED → SUPERSEDED`, so the payee's `receivable_balance_cents` drops back by exactly that statement's `net_payable_cents`. The registration's own `version` increments. Both conservation identities named in §2.9 hold before and after the pair, with no drift in either direction.

### 3.6. Pool Sizing and Attribution

A **royalty pool** is the monetised cell of one period, one territory and one subscription tier. A `RoyaltyAdmin` creates it by naming the owning period through `period_id`, the `territory_code` it covers — exactly two uppercase letters, never `WORLD` — the `subscription_tier` it covers, the `subscriber_count`, the average paying subscribers in that cell during the period, which must be at least 0, and `gross_revenue_cents`, the gross subscription and advertising revenue booked for the cell, which must also be at least 0. The triple of period, territory and tier is unique; a second pool for the same cell is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_POOL`. A new pool starts at `status` `DRAFT` with every computed money field at zero, `total_play_count` and `denominator_play_count` at zero as well, and `applied_rate_schedule_id`, `denominator_basis`, `allocation_basis` and `residual_payee_holder_id` all null. Creation is not gated on the parent period's `status`; a pool may be opened against a period in any state.

Calculation is what turns a `DRAFT` pool into a priced one, and it has three legal entry states: `DRAFT → CALCULATED` for a first run, `CALCULATED → CALCULATED` for a deliberate self-loop that recomputes in place, and `REOPENED_FOR_ADJUSTMENT → CALCULATED` for a run after a retroactive rate change. A `RoyaltyAdmin` triggers all three with the same operation. In the first two cases the parent period's `status` must be `USAGE_SEALED` or `REOPENED`; in the third it must be `REOPENED`. A pool that is `FINALIZED` cannot be calculated, and a pool in any state the requested transition does not allow is refused with `ERR_INVALID_POOL_STATE`, while a wrongly-stated parent period yields `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_STATE`.

Every calculation begins by choosing the governing rate. The candidate set contains every rate schedule whose `status` is `EFFECTIVE`, whose `subscription_tier` equals the pool's `subscription_tier`, whose `territory_code` either equals the pool's `territory_code` or is the literal `WORLD`, and whose inclusive applicability window contains the period's `usage_end` — that is, `effective_from` is on or before `usage_end` and `effective_to` is on or after `usage_end`. Schedules in `PROPOSED` or `SUPERSEDED` status are excluded entirely. Among the candidates, a schedule naming the pool's concrete territory outranks a `WORLD` fallback; among schedules of equal specificity, the one with the later `effective_from` wins; a remaining tie is broken by the earlier `created_at`, and then by the lexicographically smaller `proceeding_ref`. The winner's `id` is recorded in `applied_rate_schedule_id`. If the candidate set is empty, the calculation is refused with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_RATE_SCHEDULE`.

Sizing the pool then produces three competing figures from that schedule and picks among them. The pool's `total_play_count` is the sum of `play_count` over its in-scope usage lines, where a line is in scope when its batch belongs to the pool's period, its `territory_code` equals the pool's `territory_code` and its `subscription_tier` equals the pool's `subscription_tier` — and crucially, in scope regardless of `match_status`, so that the statutory floor is computed on gross reported usage and does not shrink merely because some lines are unmatched. From there:

- `revenue_share_pool_cents` is `gross_revenue_cents` multiplied by the schedule's `revenue_share_bps`, divided by 10,000, rounded half up to a whole cent.
- `minimum_pool_cents` is `subscriber_count` multiplied by the schedule's `per_subscriber_minimum_cents`.
- `statutory_floor_cents` is `total_play_count` multiplied by the schedule's `statutory_floor_per_play_millicents`, divided by 1,000 to convert thousandths of a cent into cents, and then rounded **up** to the next whole cent.

`distributable_cents` is the largest of three quantities: the lesser of `revenue_share_pool_cents` and three times `minimum_pool_cents`; `minimum_pool_cents` itself; and `statutory_floor_cents`. The cap multiple of three is a deliberate brake on a revenue share that would otherwise run far ahead of the per-subscriber commitment.

`allocation_basis` records which of the three decided the outcome, and the comparisons are evaluated in this pinned order. It is `REVENUE_SHARE` when `revenue_share_pool_cents` is greater than or equal to `minimum_pool_cents`, and greater than or equal to `statutory_floor_cents`, and less than or equal to three times `minimum_pool_cents`. Otherwise it is `PER_SUBSCRIBER_MINIMUM` when `minimum_pool_cents` is greater than or equal to `statutory_floor_cents`. Otherwise it is `STATUTORY_FLOOR`.

Attribution runs immediately after sizing and spreads `distributable_cents` across the in-scope usage lines. Its denominator is chosen by a clock-dependent selector evaluated once per calculation run. While the current instant is at or before the period's `claim_window_closes_at`, `denominator_basis` is `ALL_USAGE` and `denominator_play_count` is the pool's `total_play_count`; once the current instant has passed `claim_window_closes_at`, `denominator_basis` is `MATCHED_ONLY` and `denominator_play_count` is the sum of `play_count` over in-scope lines whose `match_status` is `AUTO_MATCHED` or `CLAIM_MATCHED`. This is why a second run over identical stored data can legitimately produce different numbers: the variant is a function of the clock, not of the data.

If `denominator_play_count` is zero, every in-scope line's `attributed_revenue_cents` is set to 0. Otherwise each in-scope line is attributed individually. Under `MATCHED_ONLY`, a line whose `match_status` is `UNMATCHED` or `DISPUTED` receives exactly 0. Every other line receives `distributable_cents` multiplied by its own `play_count`, divided by `denominator_play_count`, rounded **down** to a whole cent. Rounding down rather than half up is intentional: the drift is then always non-negative and lands in a single tracked residual instead of over-committing the pool.

Three aggregates close the run. `matched_attributed_cents` is the sum of `attributed_revenue_cents` over in-scope lines whose `match_status` is `AUTO_MATCHED` or `CLAIM_MATCHED`. `holding_cents` is the sum of `attributed_revenue_cents` over in-scope lines whose `match_status` is `UNMATCHED` or `DISPUTED` — this is the pool's holding account, money the service owes to somebody it cannot yet name. `rounding_residual_cents` is `distributable_cents` minus the sum of `attributed_revenue_cents` over **all** in-scope lines. The consequence of the two denominator variants is worth stating plainly: under `ALL_USAGE` the unmatched and disputed lines receive a positive attribution that funds the holding account, whereas under `MATCHED_ONLY` they receive exactly 0, `holding_cents` becomes 0, and their value is redistributed across the matched lines by the smaller denominator, so identical stored usage yields a strictly larger `attributed_revenue_cents` on every matched line. A successful run also recomputes the parent period's `total_holding_cents` and increments the pool's `version`. The pool ends the run at `status` `CALCULATED` whether it entered as `DRAFT`, as `CALCULATED`, or as `REOPENED_FOR_ADJUSTMENT`.

Finalizing freezes the result. A `RoyaltyAdmin` may finalize a pool whose `status` is `CALCULATED`, whose parent period's `status` is `USAGE_SEALED` or `REOPENED`, and where every usage batch of that period has a `status` of `MATCHED`, `PARTIALLY_MATCHED` or `RECONCILED`. Finalizing freezes `distributable_cents`, `denominator_basis`, `denominator_play_count`, `rounding_residual_cents` and every in-scope line's `attributed_revenue_cents`, and moves the pool `CALCULATED → FINALIZED`.

Finalizing also picks the holder that will receive the residual. `residual_payee_holder_id` is chosen by totalling, for every holder that would receive at least one non-residual line from this pool under the statement-assembly rules of §3.7, the gross it would receive; the holder with the largest total wins, with ties broken by the earlier `RightsHolder.created_at` and then by the lexicographically smaller `legal_name`. Only `APPROVED` registrations produce payees for this purpose; claims that are `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `NEEDS_EVIDENCE` or `DISPUTED` produce none. When no holder qualifies at all, `residual_payee_holder_id` stays null, the residual is simply not paid out, `rounding_residual_cents` keeps its value, and the pool finalizes anyway.

Two bubble-ups follow a finalize. When the period owns at least one pool and every pool of the period has `status` `FINALIZED`, the period moves `USAGE_SEALED → DISTRIBUTED`, or `REOPENED → DISTRIBUTED` when it was reopened by a rate cascade; the second form closes the reopen cycle. On the `USAGE_SEALED → DISTRIBUTED` transition every usage batch of the period is reconciled, by way of `MATCHED → RECONCILED` for a fully matched batch and `PARTIALLY_MATCHED → RECONCILED` for one that still carries unmatched plays. Note that a `PARTIALLY_MATCHED` batch may reconcile while still carrying unmatched plays; that is legitimate, and their value simply stays in the pool holding account. The period's `version` increments on either bubble-up.

### 3.7. Statement Assembly, Administration Fees and Issuance

A **royalty statement** is the line-item bill the service sends one payee for one period. A `RoyaltyAdmin` builds one by naming the addressed holder through `rights_holder_id`, the period through `period_id`, and the `expected_period_version` of §2.8. The period's `status` must be `DISTRIBUTED` or `REOPENED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_STATE`. At most one statement whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `ISSUED` may exist for a given holder and period; a second is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STATEMENT`. The statement is created at `status` `DRAFT` with `issued_at` null, and building it materialises its immutable statement lines in the same operation. Building also increments the parent period's `version`, which is precisely why the next statement built against that period needs a fresh `expected_period_version`.

Assembly walks the period's `FINALIZED` pools in ascending `created_at` order; pools in any other state contribute nothing. For each such pool, the schedule recorded in its `applied_rate_schedule_id` supplies the publishing split. For each in-scope usage line of that pool, taken in ascending `created_at` order, whose `match_status` is `AUTO_MATCHED` or `CLAIM_MATCHED` — unmatched and disputed lines contribute nothing, and their value stays in `holding_cents` — the line's `attributed_revenue_cents` is cut into four slices in this order:

- The master slice is `attributed_revenue_cents` multiplied by 7,500 basis points and divided by 10,000 — that is, three quarters of the attributed value — rounded half up to a whole cent.
- The publishing slice is `attributed_revenue_cents` minus the master slice, so the two together always account for the whole attributed amount with no drift.
- The mechanical slice is the publishing slice multiplied by the schedule's `mechanical_share_bps`, divided by 10,000, rounded half up to a whole cent.
- The performance slice is the publishing slice minus the mechanical slice, again leaving no drift.

Each slice is then offered to the registrations that can claim it, taken in ascending `created_at` order. A registration participates when its `status` is `APPROVED`, when its `territory_set` intersects the usage line's `territory_code` — with `WORLD` acting as the wildcard of §2.7 — when its `payee_holder_id` equals the statement's `rights_holder_id`, when the period's `usage_end` falls inside its half-open interval, meaning `effective_from` is on or before `usage_end` and `effective_to` is strictly after `usage_end`, and when it points at the right work for the right it claims. A `MASTER` registration must name the same work as the usage line's `work_id`; a `MECHANICAL` or `PERFORMANCE` registration must name the composition reached through that recording's `linked_work_id`. This is the only place the self-referential catalogue link is walked, and it is what routes publishing money to publishers while master money goes to labels.

The base for a participating registration is the master slice for a `MASTER` claim, the mechanical slice for a `MECHANICAL` claim, and the performance slice for a `PERFORMANCE` claim. Its contribution is that base multiplied by the registration's `share_bps`, divided by 10,000, rounded half up to a whole cent. Contributions are then accumulated into groups, and the group key has seven parts: the pool, the registration's claimed work, the `right_type`, the usage line's `territory_code`, its `subscription_tier`, the registration's `routing`, and finally a line type that is `HOLDING_RELEASE` when the contributing usage line's `match_status` is `CLAIM_MATCHED` and `USAGE` otherwise. Each group becomes exactly one statement line.

The rounding order here is pinned and non-negotiable, and it is the single most common way an implementation diverges. Each contributing usage line's contribution is rounded to a whole cent **first**, and the rounded contributions are then summed into the line's `gross_cents`. Summing attributed values across the group and rounding once at the end is incorrect and can differ from the correct answer by as much as one cent for every usage line in the group.

Each materialised line records where its money came from and who it is for. `statement_id` points at the owning statement and `pool_id` at the pool whose distributable amount funded it. `work_id` carries the recording for a `MASTER` line and the composition for a `MECHANICAL` or `PERFORMANCE` line. `payee_holder_id` repeats the parent statement's `rights_holder_id`, denormalised so that a holder's receivable balance can be summed without joining back through the statement. `line_type` is one of `USAGE`, `HOLDING_RELEASE`, `RESIDUAL` or `ADJUSTMENT`. `right_type` and `routing` echo the producing registration. `territory_code` and `subscription_tier` echo the funding pool. `play_count` is the sum of `play_count` over the usage lines that contributed to the group. `share_bps` is the producing registration's `share_bps`. `gross_cents` is the sum just described, `admin_fee_cents` the fee resolved below, and `net_cents` is `gross_cents` minus `admin_fee_cents`. `adjustment_of_line_id` is null on everything but an `ADJUSTMENT` line.

One further line may appear. When the pool's `residual_payee_holder_id` equals this statement's `rights_holder_id` and the pool's `rounding_residual_cents` is not zero, the statement also carries a residual line: `line_type` `RESIDUAL`, `work_id` null, `right_type` null, `routing` null, `play_count` 0, `share_bps` 10,000, `gross_cents` equal to the pool's `rounding_residual_cents`, `admin_fee_cents` 0, and `net_cents` equal to that same residual.

Administration fees are resolved once per line, for lines of type `USAGE`, `HOLDING_RELEASE` and `ADJUSTMENT`. The rule looks circular — the fee band depends on the amount payable, and the amount payable is gross less the fee — and the resolution of that apparent circularity is that the band is keyed on `gross_cents` and never on `net_cents`, in three strictly ordered steps. **First**, the rate is chosen. On a line whose `routing` is `COLLECTIVE`, the rate is 1,200 basis points while `gross_cents` is below 100,000 cents, 900 basis points while `gross_cents` is below 1,000,000 cents, and 600 basis points otherwise. Both ceilings are compared strictly, so a `gross_cents` exactly equal to a ceiling falls into the higher band and therefore gets the cheaper rate. On a line whose `routing` is `DIRECT`, the rate is instead the flat `direct_deal_rate_bps` of the payee holder, regardless of volume. **Second**, `admin_fee_cents` is `gross_cents` multiplied by that rate, divided by 10,000, rounded half up to a whole cent. **Third**, `net_cents` is `gross_cents` minus `admin_fee_cents`, a plain subtraction that is never rounded again. Solving for a fixed point over `net_cents` is incorrect. A `RESIDUAL` line bypasses all three steps: its `admin_fee_cents` is 0 and its `net_cents` equals its `gross_cents`. The two routings therefore differ in both the amount and the recipient of the money: a collective-administered share is paid to the registration's `collective_id` at a volume-tiered rate, while a direct-deal share is paid to the `claimant_id` at a flat rate, and on identical gross amounts the nets diverge whenever `direct_deal_rate_bps` differs from the applicable band.

The statement header aggregates its lines: `gross_cents` is the sum of the lines' `gross_cents`, `admin_fee_cents` the sum of their `admin_fee_cents`, `net_payable_cents` the sum of their `net_cents` — which is necessarily `gross_cents` minus `admin_fee_cents` — and `line_count` the number of lines.

Issuance moves the money onto the books. A `RoyaltyAdmin` may issue a statement whose `status` is `DRAFT`, whose `line_count` is at least 1, and where every pool referenced by one of its lines has `status` `FINALIZED`. A statement that assembled to no lines at all cannot be issued and is refused with `ERR_NO_STATEMENT_LINES`; a statement that is not `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATEMENT_STATE`. Issuing moves the statement `DRAFT → ISSUED`, stamps `issued_at` from the logical clock, raises the payee's `receivable_balance_cents` by exactly `net_payable_cents`, increments the payee's `issued_statement_count` by 1, and increments the statement's `version`.

### 3.8. Rate Proceedings, Retroactive Adjustment and Period Closure

Rates come from regulatory proceedings, not from operations. A `RegulatoryOfficer` records a **rate schedule** by supplying the `proceeding_ref` citing the proceeding that produced it, at most 120 characters; the `territory_code` it governs, which may be the literal `WORLD` to create a fallback layer; the `subscription_tier` it governs; `revenue_share_bps`, between 0 and 10,000, the share of gross revenue that forms the revenue-share pool; `per_subscriber_minimum_cents`, at least 0, the per-subscriber-per-period floor; `mechanical_share_bps` and `performance_share_bps`, each between 0 and 10,000, dividing the publishing slice; `statutory_floor_per_play_millicents`, at least 0, the absolute per-play floor in thousandths of a cent; and the inclusive window `effective_from` to `effective_to`. The optional `retroactive` flag defaults to false. The publishing split must be exhaustive: `mechanical_share_bps` and `performance_share_bps` must sum to exactly 10,000, and a proposal that fails this is refused with `ERR_RATE_SHARE_SPLIT_INVALID`. An inverted window — `effective_to` earlier than `effective_from`, remembering that the schedule window is closed at both ends so equal endpoints are legal — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EFFECTIVE_PERIOD`. A new schedule is born at `status` `PROPOSED` with `promulgated_at` null.

Promulgation brings a proposal into force. Only a `RegulatoryOfficer` may do it, only against a schedule whose `status` is `PROPOSED` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_RATE_SCHEDULE_STATE` — and it accepts a single optional input, `supersede_incumbent`. Four gates apply. The schedule's `revenue_share_bps` must be at least 5,000 basis points, the statutory minimum revenue share below which no proceeding may set a rate, and its `statutory_floor_per_play_millicents` must not be strictly below the value carried by the incumbent `EFFECTIVE` schedule for the same territory and tier — regulatory floors ratchet upward only. A breach of either is refused with `ERR_STATUTORY_RATE_FLOOR_BREACH`. The publishing split is re-verified, and a schedule whose `mechanical_share_bps` and `performance_share_bps` no longer sum to 10,000 is refused with `ERR_RATE_SHARE_SPLIT_INVALID`. And occupancy must be exclusive: promulgation is refused with `ERR_RATE_SCHEDULE_OVERLAP` when another `EFFECTIVE` schedule exists for the same `territory_code` and `subscription_tier` whose closed window overlaps this one's — and touching endpoints do count as an overlap — unless `supersede_incumbent` is true, in which case the incumbent is displaced first and no conflict remains. As §2.7 notes, a `WORLD` schedule does not collide with a concrete-territory schedule for the same tier; they are separate specificity layers.

A successful promulgation drives a four-link cascade, each link caused by the previous one. **Link one** moves the schedule `PROPOSED → EFFECTIVE` and stamps `promulgated_at` from the logical clock; when `supersede_incumbent` is true, every intersecting `EFFECTIVE` schedule sharing the `territory_code` and `subscription_tier` moves `EFFECTIVE → SUPERSEDED`. **Link two** fires only when the newly effective schedule has `retroactive` set to true: every royalty pool whose `status` is `FINALIZED`, whose `subscription_tier` equals the schedule's, whose `territory_code` equals the schedule's or whose schedule is the literal `WORLD`, and whose parent period's `usage_end` falls inside the schedule's inclusive window, moves `FINALIZED → REOPENED_FOR_ADJUSTMENT`. **Link three** follows because those pools reopened: every statement whose `status` is `ISSUED` and which owns at least one line pointing at a reopened pool moves `ISSUED → SUPERSEDED`, and each such statement's payee has its `receivable_balance_cents` reduced by exactly that statement's `net_payable_cents` and its `issued_statement_count` reduced by one. **Link four** follows because those pools reopened and those statements were voided: every accounting period that owns a reopened pool moves to `REOPENED` — the `CLOSED → REOPENED` transition for a period that had already been closed, and the same move for a period still sitting in `DISTRIBUTED` when the cascade lands. The schedule's `version` increments, and so does the `version` of every pool, statement, holder and period the cascade touched.

Recovery from a retroactive reopen runs forward through the same machinery. Each `REOPENED_FOR_ADJUSTMENT` pool is recalculated, which now selects the newly `EFFECTIVE` schedule and moves the pool `REOPENED_FOR_ADJUSTMENT → CALCULATED`, and is then finalized again, which bubbles the period `REOPENED → DISTRIBUTED`. Only then is a corrected statement built.

A corrected statement is not a restatement. When a statement is built for a holder and period that already has at least one `SUPERSEDED` statement, the service switches from ordinary assembly to delta assembly. Note carefully that the discriminator is the existence of a `SUPERSEDED` predecessor for that same holder and period, not the period's `status`, because re-finalizing every pool has already bubbled the period from `REOPENED` back to `DISTRIBUTED` before the corrected statement is built. Delta assembly walks the `SUPERSEDED` statements of that holder and period in ascending `created_at` order — `DRAFT` and `ISSUED` statements are excluded — and within each, walks its lines of `line_type` `USAGE`, `HOLDING_RELEASE` or `RESIDUAL` in ascending `created_at` order. For each old line, the service determines what ordinary assembly would now produce for the same group key, treating a group that no longer exists as a line of zero. The gross delta is the new line's `gross_cents` minus the old line's `gross_cents`, and the fee delta is the new line's `admin_fee_cents` minus the old line's `admin_fee_cents`. When either delta is non-zero, one line of `line_type` `ADJUSTMENT` is emitted: its `adjustment_of_line_id` points at the old line, its `pool_id`, `work_id`, `right_type`, `routing`, `territory_code`, `subscription_tier` and `share_bps` are copied from the old line, its `play_count` is 0, its `gross_cents` is the gross delta, its `admin_fee_cents` is the fee delta, and its `net_cents` is the gross delta minus the fee delta. Deltas may be negative, so an adjustment line's `gross_cents` may be negative. The corrected statement carries **only** adjustment lines; it never restates the original lines.

Closing a period is the last act, and it is deliberately hard. A `RoyaltyAdmin` may close a period whose `status` is `DISTRIBUTED` — any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_STATE` — and only when three further conditions hold. The current instant must be strictly after the period's `claim_window_closes_at`, so no claimant is shut out mid-window. The period's `total_holding_cents` must be exactly 0, because money sitting in a holding account may not be locked away by a close; a non-zero holding total is refused with `ERR_HOLDING_UNRESOLVED`. And no registration touching a work that carries usage in this period may still be in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `NEEDS_EVIDENCE` or `DISPUTED` status; an open claim is refused with `ERR_UNRESOLVED_REGISTRATIONS`. A successful close moves the period `DISTRIBUTED → CLOSED` and increments its `version`. `CLOSED` accepts nothing further except the promulgation cascade described above, which is the only path back out through `CLOSED → REOPENED → DISTRIBUTED → CLOSED`.

### 3.9. Audit Rights, Data Production and Settlement

A rights holder that doubts a statement may audit it. A `RightsHolderUser` opens an **audit engagement** by naming its own `rights_holder_id`, the `statement_id` under audit, and the inclusive usage date range it wants data for, `requested_scope_start` and `requested_scope_end`. The ownership gate of item 6 of §2.10 bites here on the supplied `rights_holder_id` alone: it must equal `X-User-ID`, or the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The audited statement's own `rights_holder_id` is not part of that gate, so a holder acting in its own name may open an engagement against a statement addressed to a different holder; the later gates on the statement, and every subsequent step of the engagement, then run exactly as they would on the holder's own statement. The audited statement's `status` must be `ISSUED`; an engagement opened against a `DRAFT` or `SUPERSEDED` statement is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATEMENT_STATE`. The requested scope must be well formed and contained: `requested_scope_end` must be on or after `requested_scope_start`, and the whole scope must lie inside the audited statement's period, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EFFECTIVE_PERIOD`. The engagement must also be timely: it may be opened only while the current instant is at or before 180 days after the statement's `issued_at`, and a late request is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. On creation the service freezes `required_line_count` from the number of statement lines the audited statement carries at that moment — frozen thereafter, so lines added later do not move the target — computes `production_deadline_at` as `created_at` advanced by 30 days, and sets `produced_line_count` to 0, `production_status` to `PENDING` and `finding_status` to `NONE`.

An engagement carries two status dimensions that advance independently, and keeping them independent is a requirement rather than an accident. `production_status` tracks whether the service has handed over the data: it is `PENDING` while `produced_line_count` is 0, `PARTIAL` while `produced_line_count` is above 0 but below `required_line_count`, and `COMPLETE` when `produced_line_count` equals `required_line_count`. `finding_status` tracks the dispute: it is `NONE` until a finding is asserted, `ASSERTED` thereafter, and then either `SETTLED` or `WITHDRAWN`. Advancing one dimension never touches the other.

A `RoyaltyAdmin` produces data by submitting `statement_line_ids`, the set of lines being handed over. Every identifier supplied must belong to the audited statement: an identifier that does not exist at all is refused with `ERR_STATEMENT_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, and an identifier that resolves to a line of some other statement is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Production is accepted only while `finding_status` is `NONE` or `ASSERTED`; an engagement already `SETTLED` or `WITHDRAWN` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ENGAGEMENT_STATE`. It is also accepted only while the current instant is at or before `production_deadline_at`, with a late production refused with `ERR_AUDIT_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. `produced_line_count` is then recomputed as the count of **distinct** line identifiers accepted across every production call on this engagement, so re-submitting a line already produced does not advance the count. When the recomputed count is still strictly below `required_line_count`, the engagement moves `PENDING → PARTIAL`; when it equals `required_line_count`, the engagement moves `PENDING → COMPLETE` if the whole set arrived in one call, or `PARTIAL → COMPLETE` if it accumulated over several. Either way `finding_status` is untouched and the engagement's `version` increments.

Only the auditing holder may assert a finding, and only once the data is fully in hand. A `RightsHolderUser` whose `X-User-ID` equals the engagement's `rights_holder_id` submits `asserted_underpayment_cents`, which must be at least 1, and optionally a free-text `finding_note`. The engagement's `finding_status` must be `NONE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ENGAGEMENT_STATE`, and its `production_status` must be `COMPLETE` — a `PENDING` or `PARTIAL` production is not enough, and asserting against one is refused with `ERR_DATA_PRODUCTION_INCOMPLETE`. A successful assertion stores the amount and the note, stamps `findings_recorded_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID`, and moves `finding_status` `NONE → ASSERTED` while leaving `production_status` exactly where it was.

Settlement forks. A `RoyaltyAdmin` settles an engagement whose `finding_status` is `ASSERTED` and whose `production_status` is `COMPLETE`, supplying a `resolution` and, on the upholding branch, a `settled_amount_cents`. The settling operator's `X-User-ID` must differ from the engagement's `findings_recorded_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On the branch where `resolution` is `UPHOLD`, `settled_amount_cents` must be at least 1 and must not exceed `asserted_underpayment_cents`; a larger amount is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_EXCEEDS_ASSERTION`. The amount is stored, interest is computed, `finding_status` moves `ASSERTED → SETTLED`, and the audited holder's `receivable_balance_cents` rises by exactly `settled_amount_cents` plus `interest_cents`. On the branch where `resolution` is `WITHDRAW`, `settled_amount_cents` must be exactly 0 — a non-zero amount on a withdrawal is likewise refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_EXCEEDS_ASSERTION` — both `settled_amount_cents` and `interest_cents` are set to 0, `finding_status` moves `ASSERTED → WITHDRAWN`, and the holder's `receivable_balance_cents` is left completely unchanged. The two branches have deliberately disjoint effects, and the withdrawal branch is the compensating action that returns the receivable quantity of §2.9 to where the assertion found it. Either branch increments the engagement's `version`.

Interest on an upheld settlement compensates the holder for the delay. First the elapsed time is measured: `days_elapsed` is the number of whole days between the audited statement's `issued_at` and the current instant, obtained by dividing the elapsed seconds by 86,400 and discarding any remainder. Then `interest_cents` is computed as `settled_amount_cents` multiplied by 900 basis points, multiplied by `days_elapsed`, divided by the product of 10,000 and 365 days, with the result rounded half up to a whole cent. That rounding happens exactly once, on the fully assembled quotient; rounding an annual interest figure first and prorating it by days afterwards is incorrect and can differ by one cent. A withdrawn engagement never reaches this calculation and keeps `interest_cents` at 0.
