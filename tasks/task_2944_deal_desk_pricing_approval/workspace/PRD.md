# Deal Desk & Pricing Approval API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Enterprise software deals rarely close at list price. A sales representative assembles a bundle of subscription licences, metered usage, professional services and support, asks for a discount, stretches the payment terms, promises an uptime number the standard contract does not promise, and then hands the whole package to a deal desk that has minutes rather than days to decide whether the company can live with it. The people who must sign off — a deal desk analyst, a sales director, a VP of sales, a CFO, legal counsel, a revenue accountant — each own one narrow question, and each of them needs the same deal to be described in numbers they can trust. Today those numbers are recomputed by hand in spreadsheets, the approval thresholds are remembered rather than enforced, and the margin floor is a guideline that quietly moves at quarter end.

This document specifies the Deal Desk & Pricing Approval service: a single system of record for quote configuration, the discount approval matrix, non-standard term review, competitive pricing exceptions, revenue recognition impact, order form generation and booking. The service is opinionated about arithmetic. Every price, discount, margin, risk score and booking value is derived by a named calculation with a fixed rounding discipline, and the same quote is priced twice under two deliberately different band-treatment conventions so that the deal desk can see both the pessimistic and the contractual view of the same configuration. It is equally opinionated about authority: the approval level a quote requires is read off a configured matrix rather than negotiated, authority may be lent to another user only inside an explicit window and only within an explicit discount ceiling, and nobody may approve a quote they own or accept an exception they wrote.

Two operational realities shape the design. The first is quarter end: approval turnaround service levels tighten, escalation jumps further up the chain, and a quote approved inside the surge window cannot be given a validity period that runs past the quarter boundary. The second is that deals move backwards as often as forwards — approvers request changes, reps reopen and re-submit, exceptions are revoked, bookings are reversed. Every backward move in this specification is a compensating move: consumed allowance is returned to the account pool, booked recurring revenue is unwound from the account ledger, and stale revenue assessments are marked for redoing rather than silently trusted.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section applies to every endpoint unless a later section states a narrower rule. The service is mounted at the root: there is no common URL prefix, and every business collection (quotes, order forms, approval steps, delegation grants and so on) is a top-level path. Child collections are discovered through sub-resource reads and filtered lists rather than through nested write paths.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting as. If either header is absent, or present but an empty string, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined — including before the path is resolved. If both headers are present but `X-User-Role` is not one of the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

There are exactly eight roles, spelled exactly as follows: `SalesRep`, `DealDeskAnalyst`, `SalesDirector`, `VPSales`, `CFO`, `LegalCounsel`, `RevenueAccountant`, and `PlatformAdmin`. A `SalesRep` owns quote construction — building the configuration, documenting exceptions, submitting for approval, reopening, renegotiating and papering the deal. A `DealDeskAnalyst` is the level-1 approver and additionally owns signature recording and booking, and may open revenue assessments. A `SalesDirector` is the level-2 approver and may delegate its tier. A `VPSales` is the level-3 approver and may likewise delegate its tier. The `CFO` is the level-4 backstop approver, the co-signer of the dominant non-standard term, the only role that may revoke an accepted exception or reverse a booking. `LegalCounsel` reviews non-standard terms and owns the terms track of the approval fork. A `RevenueAccountant` clears or blocks the revenue recognition assessment. A `PlatformAdmin` operates the configuration surface, the sweeps and the logical clock. Every role may call every read endpoint.

None of the eight roles is backed by a persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `SalesRep`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any stored record; the server records it (as `owner_rep_user_id` on the quote the rep creates, and as `documented_by_user_id` on a competitive exception the rep files) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for the other seven roles: when `X-User-Role` is `DealDeskAnalyst`, `SalesDirector`, `VPSales` or `CFO`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque identifier that the server may record as `acting_user_id` on an approval step, as `decided_by_user_id` on a competitive exception, as `grantor_user_id` on a delegation grant, or — for the `CFO` alone — as `cfo_cosigned_by_user_id` on a co-signed term; when `X-User-Role` is `LegalCounsel`, `X-User-ID` may be recorded as `reviewed_by_user_id` on the reviewed term; when `X-User-Role` is `RevenueAccountant`, `X-User-ID` may be recorded as `assessed_by_user_id` on the assessment; and when `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is recorded nowhere at all. In no case is `X-User-ID` resolved against a user table, and in no case does an unknown `X-User-ID` produce a not-found error.

Because identity is opaque, ownership is expressed as string equality against a recorded identifier rather than as a lookup. Three comparisons matter, and each has its own rejection code. Quote-owner operations require that `X-User-ID` equal the quote's `owner_rep_user_id`, and reject a mismatch with `ERR_NOT_QUOTE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Revoking a delegation requires that `X-User-ID` equal the grant's `grantor_user_id`, and rejects a mismatch with `ERR_NOT_GRANT_GRANTOR` (HTTP 403). Segregation of duties requires the opposite — that `X-User-ID` differ from the recorded owner or author — and rejects a match with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). A delegation names its beneficiary by the opaque `grantee_user_id` it was created with, which is compared against the acting `X-User-ID` when a step is decided under that grant.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside those bounds is a malformed request rather than a silently clamped one.

Unless an endpoint's description in section 4 names a different ordering, list results are returned sorted by `created_at` ascending. Four endpoints do name a different ordering, and those orderings are part of their contract: a policy's approval matrix rows come back sorted by `tier_level` ascending, a product's quantity bands by `min_quantity` ascending, a product's price holds by `hold_start_date` ascending, and delegation grants by `starts_at` ascending.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`. `total_count` is the number of records matching the request's filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when records matching the filters remain beyond the current window. Single-resource responses carry neither of those four fields.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every stored record carries a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`. Every record also carries `created_at`, the instant the record was written, formatted as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). Every datetime field in this specification — `activated_at`, `superseded_at`, `submitted_at`, `approved_at`, `expires_at`, `booked_at`, `decided_at`, `reviewed_at`, `cfo_cosigned_at`, `released_at`, `assessed_at`, `sla_due_at`, `sent_at`, `signed_at`, `declined_at`, `voided_at`, `revoked_at`, `booking_reversed_at` and the rest — uses that identical format.

Twelve of the fifteen record types additionally carry `updated_at` and `version`. `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock on every mutation of the record, and equals `created_at` on a freshly created record. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the record is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record. Those twelve types are the desk policy, the product, the account, the quote, the quote line, the non-standard term, the competitive exception, the revenue recognition assessment, the approval request, the approval step, the delegation grant and the order form.

Three record types are pure configuration rows and are never edited after creation: the approval matrix row, the product quantity band and the bundle rule. Those three carry only `id` and `created_at`. They have no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all — the keys are absent from their representations rather than present and null.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and the Quarter-End Surge Window

The service never reads wall-clock time. It maintains a logical clock which, before any client has set it, reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and which is advanced only by an administrator-only test-driving capability that accepts the target instant as `to_iso8601` and answers with the new `current_utc`. Every timestamp the service writes, every expiry comparison and every approval turnaround comparison reads that logical clock, so a test that never advances the clock sees a completely still world.

Quarter end is a first-class concept because quarter end is when deal volume surges. For any instant, the **quarter-end instant** is the first instant of the calendar quarter that follows it — that is, the 1st of January, the 1st of April, the 1st of July or the 1st of October at 00:00:00 UTC, whichever comes next. The calendar quarters therefore begin in months 1, 4, 7 and 10. The **surge window** is the stretch of time that ends at that quarter-end instant and begins the policy's `surge_window_days` days earlier. An instant is inside the surge window when it is at or after the surge window's start and strictly before the quarter-end instant; the start boundary is inside the window and the quarter-end instant itself is not.

Being inside the surge window has two effects, described in detail later: an approval step created inside the window gets a stretched turnaround allowance and escalates two levels rather than one when it breaches, and a quote approved inside the window has its validity truncated so that it cannot survive past the quarter boundary.

### 2.6. Money, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integer counts of cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere in the system, and every field whose name ends in `_cents` is an integer. Wherever a monetary result is produced by multiplication or division, it is rounded half up to a whole cent — a value exactly on the half-cent boundary rounds away from zero — and it is rounded exactly once, at the point the specification says so, never twice.

Percentages and rates are stored as decimal fractions rather than as percentage points: a rate of one quarter is `0.25`, not `25`. Three different precisions are in play, and each is part of the contract. Discount and margin ratios computed by the pricing engine — a line's `effective_stack_discount_pct`, a quote's `provisional_blended_discount_pct` and `final_blended_discount_pct`, its `provisional_margin_pct` and `final_margin_pct`, and a term's `severity_factor` — are rounded half up to six decimal places. Risk figures and elapsed-time figures — a term's `risk_contribution`, a quote's `terms_risk_score_aggregate` and `terms_risk_score_dominant`, a step's `sla_hours_effective` and `turnaround_hours`, and a request's `total_turnaround_hours` — are rounded half up to two decimal places. The desk policy's `margin_floor_pct` is configured to four decimal places.

One place in the system deliberately does not round half up: the revenue recognition allocation waterfall floors each non-final share so that the rounding remainder is non-negative and lands entirely on one line. That exception is spelled out where the waterfall is described.

### 2.7. Desk Policy Pinning and Policy Revalidation

Commercial rules live in a **desk policy** revision, and at most one revision is in `ACTIVE` status at any moment. A quote captures the identifier of the revision that was `ACTIVE` when it was created into `desk_policy_id`, and from then on every calculation that touches that quote reads that pinned revision — including after the revision has been superseded. Pinning is what makes a quote's arithmetic reproducible: activating a new policy never silently reprices an existing quote.

Pinning is not, however, a licence to book against stale rules. At submit time the quote records the pinned revision's `version` into `pinned_policy_version`, giving the desk a way to detect drift. When a newer revision is activated while a quote sits in `PENDING_APPROVAL`, that quote's `policy_revalidation_required` flag is raised, and the flag blocks booking until the quote has been reopened and re-submitted under the current rules. Submitting a quote whose pinned revision has already reached `SUPERSEDED` is refused outright.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline: Parent-Child Propagation and Compensating Moves

Almost every write in this system moves more than one record. The rules governing those movements are uniform. A cascade that runs downwards from a parent to its children is applied in full within the same request that triggered it — there is no deferred queue and no eventual consistency; a client that reads the children immediately after the parent write sees the children already moved. A cascade that runs upwards, from a child's decision to its parent's state, is likewise applied inside the same request, and may chain further upwards: a step decision can clear a track, a cleared track can approve a request, and an approved request can approve the quote, all before the response is written.

Every mutation of a record refreshes that record's `updated_at` and increments its `version`, and this applies to records touched by a cascade exactly as it applies to the record the client addressed. Several cascades are explicitly called out in this document as incrementing `Quote.version` because that counter is what the stale-version protections and the order-form booking gate compare against.

Backward movements are compensating rather than destructive. When accepted allowance is unwound the exact number of cents that was consumed is returned to the account's pool, and the consuming record keeps its recorded amount for audit rather than zeroing it. When a booking is unwound the exact recurring amount that was added to the account is subtracted again, and the order form keeps its recorded booking figures. Nothing is ever recomputed from scratch during a compensation, because recomputation against changed inputs would leave the ledger unbalanced.

### 2.9. Stale-Version Protection on Quote Writes

Two write endpoints are protected by optimistic concurrency control against the quote they act on, because both of them legitimately destroy work that a client may not have observed.

A `POST /quotes/{id}/renegotiate` request must carry `expected_quote_version` as a required, non-optional body field holding the client's last observed `version` of the source quote. If that value does not equal the source quote's current `version`, the clone is refused with `ERR_STALE_QUOTE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no clone is created. A renegotiation clone legitimately supersedes any earlier `DRAFT` clone of the same source quote, so a second create issued against an unchanged view of the source is a well-formed request under the product semantics; the version check is what makes the outcome deterministic instead of letting the later caller silently discard a clone it never saw.

A `POST /order-forms` request must likewise carry `expected_quote_version` as a required, non-optional body field holding the client's last observed `version` of the quote named by `quote_id`. The value is compared against the quote's `version` as observed before this request's own version bump; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_QUOTE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no form is generated. Generating a form legitimately voids any prior form of the same quote that is still `GENERATED` or `OUT_FOR_SIGNATURE`, so an unconditional second generate would silently discard signature routing the caller never observed.

No other endpoint in this specification takes a version field of any kind. In particular, editing a quote header, writing or deleting a line, attaching a term, filing or deciding an exception, deciding an approval step, reviewing a term, sending or signing or booking an order form, and every read endpoint all take no expected-version input, and a client that supplies one is sending an unknown field.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties and Delegated Authority

Two independence rules run through the whole approval surface, and both are enforced as hard rejections rather than warnings. First, the user who owns a quote may not decide any approval step on it: if the acting `X-User-ID` equals the quote's `owner_rep_user_id`, the step decision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Second, the user who documented a competitive exception may not decide it: if the acting `X-User-ID` equals the exception's `documented_by_user_id`, the accept-or-reject decision is refused with the same `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Those are the only two operations the rule guards, and it guards both of them identically regardless of which authorised role the acting user is presenting.

Authority may be lent. A `SalesDirector`, a `VPSales` or the `CFO` may issue a **delegation grant** naming a `grantee_user_id`, the `tier_level` being lent, a `max_discount_pct` ceiling, and a half-open window running from an inclusive `starts_at` to an exclusive `ends_at`. A grant is created in `ACTIVE` status and leaves that status in exactly two ways: the grantor revokes it, moving it to `REVOKED` and stamping `revoked_at`; or the expiry sweep observes that the logical clock has reached `ends_at` and moves it to `EXPIRED`. A grant records how many step decisions have been made under it in `used_step_count`, and neither revocation nor expiry rewinds that counter or detaches the grant from steps already decided under it.

Delegated authority is narrower than owned authority in four independent ways, and each narrowing has its own rejection. The approval matrix row for the step's level must permit delegation at all, which it signals through `max_delegation_levels`: the value 1 permits a grant to cover that level and the value 0 forbids it, and relying on a grant for a level configured with 0 is refused with `ERR_DELEGATION_LEVEL_NOT_DELEGABLE` (HTTP 422). The grant must be live: it must still be `ACTIVE`, and the logical clock must be at or after `starts_at` and strictly before `ends_at`, otherwise the decision is refused with `ERR_DELEGATION_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). The deal must be inside the ceiling the grantor set: the quote's `final_blended_discount_pct` must not exceed the grant's `max_discount_pct`, and a deal above that ceiling is refused with `ERR_DELEGATION_SCOPE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). And the grant must match the step: its `tier_level` must equal the step's `level` and its `grantee_user_id` must equal the acting `X-User-ID`. A user who neither holds the step's own `assigned_role` nor holds a matching grant is refused with `ERR_APPROVER_ROLE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

Grants issued by one grantor for one tier may not overlap in time. Creating a grant whose half-open window from inclusive `starts_at` to exclusive `ends_at` intersects the window of an existing `ACTIVE` grant with the same `grantor_user_id` and the same `tier_level` is refused with `ERR_DELEGATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Two grants that merely touch — where one's `ends_at` equals the other's `starts_at` — do not overlap, because the interval is half-open at its upper end.

### 2.11. Path Resolution and Validation Precedence

Identifiers embedded in a URL are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that resolves to nothing decides the response. A read of a nested collection such as a product's quantity bands therefore answers `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND` when the product identifier is unknown, without considering anything further to the right. Each record type has its own not-found code: `ERR_DESK_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUOTE_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TERM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXCEPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVREC_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPROVAL_STEP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DELEGATION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ORDER_FORM_NOT_FOUND`, all returned with HTTP 404.

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined by the following order. Checks are evaluated from the top of the list downwards, the first violated check decides the response, and no side effect of the request — not a state change, not a recomputation, not a ledger move — is applied when any check fails.

1. **Authentication.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty strings, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorisation.** `X-User-Role` is one of the roles listed for the endpoint in section 4, otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path existence.** Every identifier embedded in the path resolves, left to right, otherwise the matching 404 code above. Applies to every path containing an identifier segment.
4. **Request shape.** The body carries every required field for the endpoint, every value matches its declared type, every enumerated value is a declared member, every numeric value lies inside its declared range, a price-hold window has its `hold_start_date` at or before its `hold_end_date`, a delegation window has its `starts_at` strictly before its `ends_at`, and no unknown field is present; any violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every create, patch and delete endpoint.
5. **Body referential integrity.** Every foreign key supplied in the body resolves — `account_id`, `product_id`, `quote_id`, `approval_request_id`, `hold_product_id`, `desk_policy_id`, `anchor_product_id` and `attached_product_id` — otherwise `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_DESK_POLICY_NOT_FOUND` (404) as appropriate. Applies to `POST /quotes`, `POST /quote-lines`, `POST /nonstandard-terms`, `POST /competitive-exceptions`, `POST /revrec-assessments`, `POST /order-forms`, `POST /approval-tiers`, `POST /volume-tiers` and `POST /bundle-rules`.
6. **Configuration uniqueness and band integrity.** A product's `sku` is unique; a policy's approval matrix bands neither overlap nor repeat a `tier_level`; a product's quantity bands neither overlap nor repeat a `tier_level`, and at most one of them is unbounded; a bundle rule is unique per anchor-and-attached pair and never names the same product twice; and on activation a policy's bands cover the whole discount range contiguously. The rejections are `ERR_DUPLICATE_PRODUCT_SKU`, `ERR_APPROVAL_TIER_OVERLAP`, `ERR_VOLUME_TIER_OVERLAP` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_BUNDLE_RULE` with HTTP 409, and `ERR_APPROVAL_TIER_COVERAGE_INVALID` with HTTP 422. Applies to `POST /products`, `POST /approval-tiers`, `POST /volume-tiers`, `POST /bundle-rules` and `POST /desk-policies/{id}/activate`.
7. **Resource ownership.** `X-User-ID` equals `Quote.owner_rep_user_id` for quote-owner operations, otherwise `ERR_NOT_QUOTE_OWNER` (403); and `X-User-ID` equals `DelegationGrant.grantor_user_id` for grant revocation, otherwise `ERR_NOT_GRANT_GRANTOR` (403). The quote-owner operations are exactly `PATCH /quotes/{id}`, `POST /quote-lines`, `PATCH /quote-lines/{id}`, `DELETE /quote-lines/{id}`, `POST /quotes/{id}/submit`, `POST /quotes/{id}/reopen`, `POST /quotes/{id}/renegotiate`, `POST /nonstandard-terms`, `POST /competitive-exceptions`, `POST /order-forms` and `POST /order-forms/{id}/send`; the grantor check applies to `POST /delegation-grants/{id}/revoke`.
8. **State precondition.** The target record is in a status from which the requested transition is declared. The rejections are `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_PENDING_APPROVAL`, `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `ERR_QUOTE_STATE_INVALID_FOR_REVOKE`, `ERR_STEP_ALREADY_DECIDED`, `ERR_TERM_ALREADY_REVIEWED`, `ERR_TERM_NOT_LEGAL_APPROVED`, `ERR_EXCEPTION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_REVREC_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ORDER_FORM_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_DELEGATION_STATE_INVALID` and `ERR_DESK_POLICY_NOT_DRAFT`, all with HTTP 409. Applies to every write endpoint that drives a state transition.
9. **Optimistic concurrency.** `expected_quote_version` equals the current `Quote.version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_QUOTE_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /quotes/{id}/renegotiate` and `POST /order-forms`.
10. **Segregation of duties.** `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403) on `POST /approval-steps/{id}/decide` and `POST /competitive-exceptions/{id}/decide`, as described above.
11. **Legal-review gating.** A term review is accepted only while the quote's approval request has its terms track in `PENDING_LEGAL`, otherwise `ERR_LEGAL_REVIEW_NOT_REQUIRED`; a co-signature additionally requires the term's `requires_cfo_cosign` to be true, otherwise `ERR_TERM_COSIGN_NOT_REQUIRED`, and requires `cfo_cosigned_at` to still be unset, otherwise `ERR_TERM_ALREADY_COSIGNED`. All three carry HTTP 409 and apply to `POST /nonstandard-terms/{id}/review`.
12. **Approver authority.** The four delegation and role checks described in section 2.10: `ERR_APPROVER_ROLE_MISMATCH` (403), `ERR_DELEGATION_NOT_ACTIVE` (409), `ERR_DELEGATION_LEVEL_NOT_DELEGABLE` (422) and `ERR_DELEGATION_SCOPE_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to `POST /approval-steps/{id}/decide`.
13. **Temporal conflicts.** The logical clock is at or before the quote's `expires_at`, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`; a new delegation window does not overlap an existing `ACTIVE` grant of the same grantor and tier, otherwise `ERR_DELEGATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`; and a price-hold window respects the configured cooldown against other live holds on the same account-and-product pair, otherwise `ERR_PRICE_HOLD_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. All three carry HTTP 409. Applies to `POST /quotes/{id}/submit`, `POST /quotes/{id}/reopen`, `POST /approval-steps/{id}/decide`, `POST /order-forms`, `POST /order-forms/{id}/send`, `POST /order-forms/{id}/signature`, `POST /order-forms/{id}/book`, `POST /delegation-grants` and `POST /nonstandard-terms`.
14. **Structural submit gates.** `ERR_QUOTE_HAS_NO_LINES`, `ERR_DESK_POLICY_SUPERSEDED`, `ERR_TERM_BELOW_PRODUCT_MINIMUM`, `ERR_NONSTANDARD_TERM_REQUIRED` and `ERR_REVREC_NOT_CLEARED`, all with HTTP 422, in the order just listed. Applies to `POST /quotes/{id}/submit` only.
15. **Fiscal floors and budgets.** `ERR_DISCOUNT_STACK_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_ABSOLUTE_MARGIN_FLOOR_BREACH`, `ERR_MARGIN_FLOOR_BREACH`, `ERR_EXCEPTION_NOT_NEEDED`, `ERR_EXCEPTION_ALLOWANCE_EXHAUSTED` and `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_DESK_POLICY`, all with HTTP 422. Applies to `POST /quotes`, `POST /quote-lines`, `PATCH /quote-lines/{id}`, `POST /quotes/{id}/submit` and `POST /competitive-exceptions/{id}/decide`.
16. **Order acceptance criteria.** `ERR_ACCOUNT_CREDIT_HOLD` (409) is evaluated first, and only then the four criteria that share `ERR_BOOKING_CRITERIA_UNMET` (422). Applies to `POST /order-forms/{id}/book` only.

Two consequences deserve emphasis because they are easy to get backwards. A request with a bad role and a bad path identifier answers the role error, because role authorisation precedes path existence. And a request that is both stale on `expected_quote_version` and aimed at a record in the wrong status answers the state error, because the state precondition precedes the concurrency check.

### 2.12. Conserved Ledgers: Booked Revenue and the Exception Allowance Pool

Two account-level counters are **conserved**: their stored value must at all times equal the sum of the contributions currently recognised as live, and every write that changes a contribution must move the counter in the same request.

`Account.booked_arr_cents` equals the sum of `booked_arr_cents` over that account's order forms whose status is `BOOKED`. Booking an order form increases it by that form's recorded amount; reversing a booking decreases it by the same amount. No other operation touches it. An account's pre-existing revenue outside this system is held separately in `existing_arr_cents`, which the service records at account creation and never modifies afterwards.

`Account.exception_allowance_used_cents` equals the sum of `allowance_consumed_cents` over that account's competitive exceptions whose status is `ACCEPTED`. Exceptions in `SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED`, `REVOKED` and `RELEASED` contribute nothing. Accepting an exception increases the counter by the amount that exception consumed; revoking it, or releasing it automatically when the quote it defends is rejected or expires, decreases the counter by exactly that same recorded amount. The pool's ceiling is the policy's `exception_allowance_cents`, so an exception that would carry the counter past the ceiling cannot be accepted.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Configuring the Desk: Policy Revisions, the Approval Matrix and the Catalogue

Before a single quote can exist, a `PlatformAdmin` must stand up a desk policy revision. A revision is created in `DRAFT` status with a human label in `name` and seventeen configured numbers that between them define the whole commercial rulebook. Two of them are margin floors: `margin_floor_pct` is the soft floor, below which a quote needs an accepted competitive exception to proceed, and `absolute_margin_floor_pct` is the hard floor, below which no exception can rescue the deal. Both are decimal fractions strictly between zero and one, and the hard floor must be configured strictly below the soft floor. `quote_validity_days`, at least 1, is how long a quote stays live. `standard_payment_terms_days`, at least 1, is the payment term considered standard; anything longer is a deviation and attracts a financing charge at the rate `uplift_rate_per_30_days`, which is at least zero and strictly below one. `max_stack_discount_pct`, strictly between zero and one, caps how much per-line discount may accumulate.

Four numbers configure the order-level volume rebate as a two-step ladder. `volume_rebate_tier1_min_cents`, at least zero, is the net-subtotal threshold at which the first rebate step begins and `volume_rebate_tier1_pct` is its rate; `volume_rebate_tier2_min_cents`, which must be strictly greater than the tier-1 threshold, begins the second step at `volume_rebate_tier2_pct`, which must be strictly greater than the tier-1 rate. Both thresholds are inclusive, and both rates are at least zero and strictly below one. Three more numbers describe quarter end and price holds: `surge_window_days`, at least 1, is how many days before the quarter boundary the surge window opens; `surge_sla_multiplier`, strictly greater than zero, stretches approval turnaround allowances inside that window; and `price_hold_cooldown_days`, at least zero, is the mandatory gap between two price-hold windows on the same customer-and-product pair. `exception_allowance_cents`, at least zero, is the per-account margin give-up budget. Finally, `terms_review_risk_threshold` and `terms_cfo_risk_threshold` are both scores between 0 and 100 inclusive, and they gate mandatory legal review and mandatory CFO co-signature respectively. A freshly created revision has `activated_at` and `superseded_at` both unset.

A revision only becomes law when a `PlatformAdmin` activates it, driving `status` from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE`. Activation is refused with `ERR_DESK_POLICY_NOT_DRAFT` unless the revision is still `DRAFT`, and it is refused with `ERR_APPROVAL_TIER_COVERAGE_INVALID` unless the revision's approval matrix rows, sorted by `tier_level` ascending, cover the entire discount range from zero to one with no gap and no overlap: the row at `tier_level` 1 must start with `min_discount_pct` exactly 0.0, every subsequent row's `min_discount_pct` must equal the previous row's `max_discount_pct`, and the last row's `max_discount_pct` must be exactly 1.0. A successful activation stamps `activated_at` with the logical clock. It then cascades downward: whichever revision currently holds `ACTIVE` — and at most one ever does — moves `ACTIVE` to `SUPERSEDED` and has its `superseded_at` stamped with the same instant. That supersession in turn cascades conditionally: every quote whose `desk_policy_id` names the newly superseded revision **and** whose `status` is `PENDING_APPROVAL` has `policy_revalidation_required` set to true and its `version` incremented by one. Quotes pinned to that revision in any other status — `DRAFT`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `REJECTED`, `EXPIRED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `BOOKED` — are left completely untouched, keeping their flag and their version. A superseded revision keeps serving the quotes that pinned it, which retain their `pinned_policy_version`, but no newly created quote may reference it.

The **approval matrix** is a set of discount bands attached to a revision, each added by a `PlatformAdmin` in a payload naming the `desk_policy_id` it belongs to. A band declares its `tier_level`, which must be 1, 2, 3 or 4 and must be unique within the revision, and it owns the discount range from an inclusive `min_discount_pct` to an exclusive `max_discount_pct` — the lower bound is at least zero and strictly below one, and the upper bound is strictly above the lower bound and at most one. It names the `required_role` that owns decisions at that level, which is one of `DealDeskAnalyst`, `SalesDirector`, `VPSales` or `CFO`. It sets `sla_hours`, at least 1, as the approval turnaround service level in hours before any surge stretching. And it sets `max_delegation_levels` to either 0, forbidding delegation of that level entirely, or 1, permitting a grant to cover it. A band whose range overlaps an existing band of the same revision, or whose `tier_level` is already taken, is refused with `ERR_APPROVAL_TIER_OVERLAP`.

Catalogue setup is likewise administrator-only. A product carries a `sku`, which must be unique across the whole catalogue and whose duplication is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PRODUCT_SKU`; a display `name`; a `product_type` drawn from `SUBSCRIPTION_LICENSE`, `USAGE_METERED`, `PROFESSIONAL_SERVICES` and `SUPPORT_PLAN`; a `rev_rec_category` drawn from `RATABLE`, `POINT_IN_TIME` and `USAGE_AS_INCURRED`, which is what decides how the item is recognised; and a `min_term_months` of at least 1, the shortest quote term the item may appear on. Its two money fields deserve care: `list_unit_price_cents`, strictly greater than zero, and `unit_cost_cents`, at least zero and strictly below the list price, are both per-unit amounts for the **whole contract term**, quoted at a twelve-month reference term. A three-year commitment is therefore not priced by tripling the term — it is rewarded through the term multiplier described in the next subsection.

Quantity bands hang off a product, one row per band, each naming its `product_id`. A band declares a `tier_level` of at least 1, unique within the product, and covers quantities from an inclusive `min_quantity` of at least 1 to an inclusive `max_quantity`; leaving `max_quantity` unset makes the band unbounded upwards, and at most one band per product may be unbounded. `tier_discount_pct`, at least zero and strictly below one, is the discount that band contributes. Overlapping bands, a repeated `tier_level`, and a second unbounded band are all refused with `ERR_VOLUME_TIER_OVERLAP`. Bundle rules are the cross-sell mechanism: each declares an `anchor_product_id` that must be present on the quote and an `attached_product_id` that receives the discount, a `bundle_discount_pct` strictly between zero and one, and a `min_attach_quantity_ratio` strictly greater than zero. The attached product may not be the anchor, and a rule is unique for each anchor-and-attached pairing; both violations are refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BUNDLE_RULE`. A single read lists every rule in which a given product appears on either side of the pairing, and separate reads list every quote line referencing a product and every price hold registered against it.

Customer accounts may be created either by a `PlatformAdmin` or by a `DealDeskAnalyst`, with a legal `name`, a `segment` of `ENTERPRISE`, `MID_MARKET` or `SMB`, a `region` code, and an optional `existing_arr_cents` recording revenue already on the books outside this system, which defaults to zero when omitted and must not be negative. A new account starts with `credit_status` of `CLEAR`, an unset `credit_note`, and both conserved counters — `booked_arr_cents` and `exception_allowance_used_cents` — at zero. Credit posture is changed afterwards by a `PlatformAdmin` or the `CFO` recording a decision that sets `credit_status` to `CLEAR`, `WATCH` or `HOLD` together with an optional `credit_note`. `CLEAR` and `WATCH` both permit booking; `HOLD` blocks it, and an attempt to book against an account on hold is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_CREDIT_HOLD`.

### 3.2. Building a Quote: Lines, Bands, Bundles and the Term Multiplier

A `SalesRep` opens a deal by creating a quote against an account, supplying `account_id`, a deal name in `title`, and a `term_months` between 1 and 60 inclusive. Two further inputs are optional: `payment_terms_days`, between 1 and 180 inclusive, which defaults to 30 when omitted; and `billing_frequency`, one of `ANNUAL`, `SEMI_ANNUAL`, `QUARTERLY` or `MONTHLY`, which defaults to `ANNUAL`. The service stamps `owner_rep_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, pins `desk_policy_id` to whichever revision currently holds `ACTIVE`, and refuses the whole request with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_DESK_POLICY` when no revision is active. The quote is born in `DRAFT`, with `renegotiated_from_quote_id` unset, `revision_count` and `renegotiation_count` both zero, every computed money and percentage field zero, `required_approval_level` zero, `arrangement_type` provisionally `SINGLE_ELEMENT`, `policy_revalidation_required` false, `pinned_policy_version` unset, and `submitted_at`, `approved_at` and `booked_at` all unset. Its `expires_at` is set to its `created_at` plus the pinned revision's `quote_validity_days` days.

The commercial header stays editable while the quote is `DRAFT`, and only by its owner. A header edit may carry any subset of `title`, `term_months`, `payment_terms_days` and `billing_frequency`; it triggers a full repricing of every line and of the quote header, increments `Quote.version` by one, and marks every revenue recognition assessment on the quote as stale. Attempting the edit while the quote is in any status other than `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_DRAFT`.

Lines are added one at a time, each naming the `quote_id` it belongs to, the `product_id` being sold, a `quantity` between 1 and 1,000,000 inclusive, and an optional `requested_discount_pct` — the rep's own ask, at least zero and strictly below one, defaulting to zero. A rep-requested discount above the pinned revision's `max_stack_discount_pct` is refused outright with `ERR_DISCOUNT_STACK_EXCEEDED`, and the same check applies when a line's discount is edited. Writing a line snapshots three values off the product onto the line and never reads them from the product again: `unit_list_price_cents`, `unit_cost_cents` and `rev_rec_category`. That snapshot is what makes a quote immune to later catalogue edits. Existing lines may be edited in place, carrying any subset of `quantity` and `requested_discount_pct`, or removed entirely; a removal answers with no body at all. Every line write — create, edit or delete — reprices **every** line on the quote rather than just the touched one, because a sibling line's presence and quantity can change another line's bundle eligibility; it then rolls the quote-level totals, increments `Quote.version` by one, and marks every revenue recognition assessment on the quote as stale. Once the quote leaves `DRAFT`, its lines are frozen: creating, editing or deleting a line is refused with `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_DRAFT`.

Line pricing begins with two raw extensions. `extended_list_cents` is the `quantity` multiplied by the line's snapshotted `unit_list_price_cents`, and `extended_cost_cents` is the `quantity` multiplied by the snapshotted `unit_cost_cents`. Both are whole-term amounts and neither carries any discount or any term factor. Term length then enters through a single **term multiplier** read off the quote's `term_months`: a term shorter than 12 months is penalised at 1.10, a term of at least 12 but fewer than 24 months is neutral at 1.00, a term of at least 24 but fewer than 36 months is rewarded at 0.95, and a term of 36 months or longer is rewarded at 0.90. The multiplier is applied once, at the end of the line calculation, and it is never applied to the cost side.

Two discount ingredients are read from configuration before the discount stack is assembled. The **quantity band** is found among the line's product's bands, considered in ascending `min_quantity` order: the applicable band is the single row whose `min_quantity` is at or below the line's `quantity` and whose `max_quantity` is either unset or at or above that `quantity`. That row's `tier_level` is recorded on the line as `volume_tier_level_applied`, and when the product has no bands at all the field records zero. The **bundle discount** is found among the bundle rules whose `attached_product_id` is this line's product, considered in ascending `created_at` order: the first such rule for which some sibling line on the same quote carries the rule's `anchor_product_id` is selected, and the rule's `bundle_discount_pct` is recorded on the line as `bundle_discount_applied_pct` provided that this line's `quantity` divided by the anchor line's `quantity` is at least the rule's `min_attach_quantity_ratio`. If the ratio falls short, or if no rule with a present anchor exists, `bundle_discount_applied_pct` records zero.

Every line is then priced **twice**, under two deliberately different treatments of the quantity bands, and both results are stored. Under the **whole-quantity-average** treatment the entire quantity receives the discount of the single band that contains it: the line's `effective_stack_discount_pct` is the applicable band's `tier_discount_pct` — zero when no band applies — plus `bundle_discount_applied_pct` plus `requested_discount_pct`, with the sum capped at the pinned revision's `max_stack_discount_pct` and rounded half up to six decimal places, and `provisional_net_cents` is `extended_list_cents` multiplied by one minus that stacked discount, multiplied by the term multiplier, and rounded half up once to a whole cent. Under the **marginal-progressive** treatment the units are split across the bands: for each band, the number of units falling inside it is the smaller of the line's `quantity` and the band's `max_quantity` (or the `quantity` itself where the band is unbounded), less the band's `min_quantity`, plus one, and never less than zero. Each band's units carry that band's own `tier_discount_pct` plus the same `bundle_discount_applied_pct` and `requested_discount_pct`, capped at the same `max_stack_discount_pct`. The bands are walked in ascending `min_quantity` order, each band contributing its unit count multiplied by `unit_list_price_cents` multiplied by one minus that band's own capped stack, the contributions are summed, the sum is multiplied by the term multiplier, and the result is rounded half up once to a whole cent and stored as `final_net_cents`. A product with no quantity bands collapses the two treatments onto the same number, because the average band discount is zero and there is a single implicit band running from unit 1 to the full quantity at a band discount of zero. Whenever more than one band is straddled, the marginal-progressive figure is strictly the larger of the two.

### 3.3. Rolling Up the Order: Rebates, Financing Uplift, Blended Discount and Margin

Once the lines are priced, the quote header is rolled up. `list_total_cents` is the sum of `extended_list_cents` across the quote's lines and `cost_total_cents` is the sum of `extended_cost_cents`; neither depends on which band treatment is in play. The **net subtotal** does depend on it: for each of the two passes, the net subtotal is the sum of the matching line figure — `provisional_net_cents` for the provisional pass, `final_net_cents` for the final pass.

Two order-level adjustments then apply, in a fixed order, and the order matters because each is rounded separately. First comes the **volume rebate**, keyed on the net subtotal, meaning after the per-line discounts and before any financing charge. The rebate rate is the pinned revision's `volume_rebate_tier2_pct` when the net subtotal is at or above `volume_rebate_tier2_min_cents`; otherwise it is `volume_rebate_tier1_pct` when the net subtotal is at or above `volume_rebate_tier1_min_cents`; otherwise it is zero. `volume_rebate_cents` is the net subtotal multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to a whole cent. Second comes the **financing uplift** for extended payment terms, charged on the post-rebate amount. The number of extra days is the quote's `payment_terms_days` less the pinned revision's `standard_payment_terms_days`, treated as zero whenever the quote's terms are standard or shorter. Those extra days are converted into whole 30-day blocks rounded **up**, so a single extra day still buys a full block and 31 extra days buy two. `payment_terms_uplift_cents` is the net subtotal less `volume_rebate_cents`, multiplied by the pinned revision's `uplift_rate_per_30_days`, multiplied by that block count, rounded half up to a whole cent. The order total is then the net subtotal, less `volume_rebate_cents`, plus `payment_terms_uplift_cents`. The rebate is never computed on an uplifted amount and the uplift is never computed on a pre-rebate amount.

Both passes run on every repricing, and their results land in different places. The provisional pass writes its order total to `provisional_net_total_cents`. The final pass writes its order total to `final_net_total_cents`, and it is the final pass alone whose intermediate adjustments are recorded on the quote: `volume_rebate_cents` and `payment_terms_uplift_cents` always hold the final pass's figures. Because the marginal-progressive line nets are the larger ones, the final pass sees a larger net subtotal and therefore a larger base for both the rebate and the uplift — and it can cross a rebate threshold that the provisional pass falls short of, so the same quote may legitimately carry two different rebate rates across the two passes.

The **blended discount** measures how far the whole order has fallen from list. It is one minus the ratio of the post-rebate, pre-uplift amount to `list_total_cents`, rounded half up to six decimal places. The financing uplift is deliberately excluded from that base, because an uplift is a charge for late payment rather than a discount; the rebate is deliberately included, because a rebate is a discount. As with everything else, both passes are stored: the provisional pass writes `provisional_blended_discount_pct`, which is informational only, and the final pass writes `final_blended_discount_pct`, which is the single value the approval matrix and every delegation ceiling compare against. Reading the provisional figure where the final one belongs selects a higher approval level on any multi-band quote. A quote at submit always has at least one line and every list price is strictly positive, so `list_total_cents` is never zero at the moment the ratio is taken.

The approval matrix is read from `final_blended_discount_pct`: the **matrix level** is the `tier_level` of the single band of the pinned revision whose `min_discount_pct` is at or below that figure and whose `max_discount_pct` is strictly above it. Band membership is tested on the already-rounded six-decimal value, with the lower bound inclusive and the upper bound exclusive. `required_approval_level` is then derived in three cases: it is 0 when `final_blended_discount_pct` is exactly zero to six decimal places **and** no competitive exception on the quote is in `ACCEPTED` status; it is the greater of the matrix level and 3 whenever some competitive exception on the quote is in `ACCEPTED` status, because an accepted exception always drags the deal up to at least the third level of authority; and it is simply the matrix level in every other case.

**Margin** is computed from the order total rather than from the net subtotal, so the financing uplift raises it. The margin ratio is the order total less `cost_total_cents`, divided by the order total, rounded half up to six decimal places. A longer payment term therefore improves margin while leaving the blended discount untouched — a quirk of the definition that the desk relies on rather than works around. Once again both passes are recorded: `provisional_margin_pct` from the provisional order total and `final_margin_pct` from the final one. The provisional figure reports the lower margin on multi-band quotes and can show a floor breach that the final pass does not have; `final_margin_pct` is the only figure the margin floors and the competitive-exception preconditions read.

One further derived money figure belongs here, because it is the price of an exception. The **margin give-up** is the pinned revision's `margin_floor_pct` less the quote's `final_margin_pct`, multiplied by `final_net_total_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent. It is only ever charged when `final_margin_pct` sits strictly below `margin_floor_pct`; a quote at or above the soft floor has no give-up to pay for.

All of the above is recomputed on every line create, line edit, line delete and header patch, and again at submit. The blended discount and approval level are additionally recomputed when a competitive exception is accepted and when an accepted exception is revoked, and margin is additionally recomputed when an exception is accepted. Any reader may also ask for a **pricing preview** on a quote, a purely computed read that mutates nothing and returns both passes side by side: `list_total_cents`, `cost_total_cents`, `provisional_net_total_cents`, `final_net_total_cents`, `provisional_blended_discount_pct`, `final_blended_discount_pct`, `provisional_margin_pct`, `final_margin_pct`, `volume_rebate_cents`, `payment_terms_uplift_cents`, `required_approval_level`, both risk scores in `terms_risk_score_aggregate` and `terms_risk_score_dominant`, the `arrangement_type`, the revenue-allocation figures `usage_excluded_cents`, `allocable_amount_cents` and `allocation_residual_cents`, the `margin_give_up_cents` the deal would cost in allowance, and a `lines` array carrying the per-line figures.

### 3.4. Non-Standard Terms and the Two Risk Scores

A deal rarely deviates from the standard contract in only one way, so deviations are recorded individually. A `SalesRep` attaches a **non-standard term** to a quote that is `DRAFT` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, naming the `quote_id`, a `term_category`, and a `requested_numeric_value` of at least zero whose meaning depends entirely on the category. The five categories are `PAYMENT_TERMS`, where the number is a count of days; `SLA_COMMITMENT`, where it is an uptime percentage; `INDEMNITY_CAP`, where it is a multiple of annual contract value; `TERMINATION_FOR_CONVENIENCE`, where it is a notice period in months; and `PRICE_HOLD_RENEWAL`, where it is a renewal uplift cap expressed as a percentage. Proposed rider language may be supplied as optional `requested_text`. Three further inputs are optional in the request but conditionally mandatory in the domain: `hold_product_id`, `hold_start_date` and `hold_end_date` are required together when and only when `term_category` is `PRICE_HOLD_RENEWAL`, and are left unset for every other category. The hold window is inclusive at both ends, and `hold_end_date` must be on or after `hold_start_date`. Every term is created with `review_state` of `PENDING`, with `reviewed_by_user_id`, `reviewed_at`, `reviewer_note`, `cfo_cosigned_at` and `cfo_cosigned_by_user_id` all unset, and with `requires_cfo_cosign` false until the scoring says otherwise.

Price holds are rationed in time. Consider a customer who already holds a renewal price cap on a particular product running through the end of March, and whose rep now proposes a second cap on the same product starting in early April. The proposal is refused with `ERR_PRICE_HOLD_WINDOW_CONFLICT` unless the two inclusive windows are separated by at least the pinned revision's `price_hold_cooldown_days` days. The comparison considers only other `PRICE_HOLD_RENEWAL` terms whose `review_state` is `PENDING` or `APPROVED`, and only those registered against the same customer — reached through the quote's `account_id` — and the same `hold_product_id`. Terms already `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN` impose no cooldown at all.

Each term carries a **category weight** that reflects how much the desk cares about that kind of deviation. A payment-terms deviation is weighted at 20; the other four categories — service level commitments, indemnity caps, termination for convenience and price-hold renewals — are each weighted at 30. Each term also carries a **severity factor**, a figure clamped into the range zero to one inclusive and derived from `requested_numeric_value` by a category-specific ramp, stored on the term as `severity_factor` and rounded half up to six decimal places. For `PAYMENT_TERMS`, severity ramps linearly from zero at 30 days to one at 150 days, that is, the requested day count less 30, divided by 120. For `SLA_COMMITMENT`, severity ramps from zero at 99.9 percent uptime to one at 100 percent, that is, the requested percentage less 99.9, divided by 0.1. For `INDEMNITY_CAP`, severity ramps from zero at a multiple of zero to one at a multiple of 6, that is, the requested multiple divided by 6. For `TERMINATION_FOR_CONVENIENCE`, severity runs the other way — shorter notice is worse — ramping from zero at 12 months of notice to one at zero notice, that is, 12 less the requested months, divided by 12. For `PRICE_HOLD_RENEWAL`, severity ramps from zero at a cap of zero percent to one at a cap of 36 percent, that is, the requested cap divided by 36. In every case the ratio is first floored at zero and then capped at one, so a value beyond either end of the ramp simply pins to that end. The term's `risk_contribution` is its category weight multiplied by its `severity_factor`, rounded half up to two decimal places.

The quote-level risk is then measured twice from the identical input set — every non-standard term on the quote whose `review_state` is `PENDING`, `APPROVED` or `REJECTED`, with `WITHDRAWN` terms excluded entirely. The **aggregate** measure, stored as `terms_risk_score_aggregate`, sums the already-rounded `risk_contribution` values across that input set, rounds the sum half up to two decimal places, and caps the result at 100. Because it sums, many mild deviations can trip a threshold that no single term reaches. The **dominant** measure, stored as `terms_risk_score_dominant`, ignores the count entirely: it takes the single term with the highest `risk_contribution`, breaking ties by earlier `created_at`, multiplies that one contribution by 1.25, rounds half up to two decimal places, and caps the result at 100. Because it amplifies, it can exceed the aggregate score when exactly one severe term exists, and it is insensitive to how many other terms sit alongside it.

The two scores gate two different escalations. Legal review is mandatory exactly when `terms_risk_score_aggregate` is at or above the pinned revision's `terms_review_risk_threshold`; below that threshold the terms are handled automatically, as described under submission. A CFO co-signature is mandatory exactly when `terms_risk_score_dominant` is at or above the pinned revision's `terms_cfo_risk_threshold`, and the obligation attaches to a single term: `requires_cfo_cosign` is set true on the dominant term only, and stays false on every other term of the quote. Attaching a term recomputes the severity and contribution of the new row, recomputes both quote-level scores and the `requires_cfo_cosign` placement across all of the quote's terms, and increments `Quote.version` by one. The scores are recomputed again at submit, on every legal review decision, and whenever a pricing preview is taken.

### 3.5. Margin Floors and Competitive Pricing Exceptions

The desk defends two floors, and it defends them differently. The hard floor, `absolute_margin_floor_pct`, is absolute: a quote whose `final_margin_pct` sits below it cannot be submitted under any circumstances, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_ABSOLUTE_MARGIN_FLOOR_BREACH`. The soft floor, `margin_floor_pct`, is a floor with a documented escape hatch: a quote whose `final_margin_pct` sits below it may still be submitted, but only if some competitive exception on that quote has reached `ACCEPTED` status, and otherwise the submission is refused with `ERR_MARGIN_FLOOR_BREACH`. At submit the hard floor is checked before the soft floor, so a deal that breaches both reports the absolute breach.

A `SalesRep` documents an exception against a quote by supplying the `quote_id`, the `competitor_name` whose pricing justifies it, that competitor's `competitor_quoted_total_cents` — strictly greater than zero, and recorded purely as documentation rather than fed into any calculation — an `evidence_reference` pointing at the filed artefact, and a narrative `justification` retained for audit. The service stamps `documented_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and creates the row in `SUBMITTED` status with `allowance_consumed_cents` at zero and `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at`, `decision_note` and `released_at` all unset. Filing an exception consumes no allowance whatsoever; only a decision does.

A documented exception is decided by a `DealDeskAnalyst` or the `CFO`, who supplies a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` together with an optional `decision_note`. Acceptance is the most heavily guarded operation in the system, and its six preconditions are evaluated in this order. The exception must still be in `SUBMITTED` status, otherwise `ERR_EXCEPTION_STATE_INVALID`. The parent quote must be in either `DRAFT` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_DRAFT`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `documented_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The quote's `final_margin_pct` must actually sit strictly below the pinned revision's `margin_floor_pct` — an exception on a healthy deal is refused with `ERR_EXCEPTION_NOT_NEEDED`. The quote's `final_margin_pct` must sit at or above the pinned revision's `absolute_margin_floor_pct`, because no exception can rescue a deal below the hard floor, otherwise `ERR_ABSOLUTE_MARGIN_FLOOR_BREACH`. And the account must still have budget: the account's `exception_allowance_used_cents` plus this deal's margin give-up must not exceed the pinned revision's `exception_allowance_cents`, otherwise `ERR_EXCEPTION_ALLOWANCE_EXHAUSTED`.

An acceptance drives the exception from `SUBMITTED` to `ACCEPTED`, stamping `decided_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, `decided_at` with the logical clock, and the supplied `decision_note`. It records the margin give-up figure into `allowance_consumed_cents`, and it moves the account's conserved pool upwards by exactly that amount. It then reaches back into the quote: `required_approval_level` is recomputed as the greater of the matrix level and 3, and `Quote.version` is incremented by one. That version bump is consequential — it marks every revenue recognition assessment on the quote stale, and it invalidates any previously generated order form for booking.

A rejection is the quiet branch. It requires the same first three preconditions — `SUBMITTED` status, a parent quote in `DRAFT` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, and an acting user who is not the author — and it drives the exception from `SUBMITTED` to `REJECTED`, stamping `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at` and `decision_note`. `allowance_consumed_cents` remains zero, no account counter moves, and the quote's approval level is untouched.

An accepted exception leaves `ACCEPTED` in exactly two ways. The `CFO`, and only the `CFO`, may revoke it manually with an optional `decision_note`, which requires the exception to be in `ACCEPTED` status — otherwise `ERR_EXCEPTION_STATE_INVALID` — and requires the parent quote to be in `DRAFT`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `PENDING_APPROVAL` — otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_STATE_INVALID_FOR_REVOKE`. Revocation moves `ACCEPTED` to `REVOKED`, stamps `released_at` with the logical clock and records the note, returns exactly `allowance_consumed_cents` to the account's pool while retaining that recorded figure on the exception for audit, recomputes the quote's `required_approval_level` from the matrix without the accepted-exception floor, and increments `Quote.version` by one. Alternatively, the exception is released automatically: whenever the parent quote reaches `REJECTED` or `EXPIRED`, every exception of that quote sitting in `ACCEPTED` moves `ACCEPTED` to `RELEASED`, has `released_at` stamped, and returns its `allowance_consumed_cents` to the account's pool. `REJECTED`, `REVOKED` and `RELEASED` are all final for an exception; `SUBMITTED` and `ACCEPTED` are the only statuses from which it can still move.

### 3.6. Revenue Recognition: Arrangement Classification and the Allocation Waterfall

A quote that sells only one kind of performance obligation raises no accounting question. A quote that mixes a ratable licence with a one-off services engagement, or that mixes either of those with metered usage, does — and the desk will not let such a deal through without an accountant's signature. The classification is derived from the distinct `rev_rec_category` values snapshotted on the quote's lines. When exactly one distinct category appears, the quote's `arrangement_type` is `SINGLE_ELEMENT`. When at least two distinct categories appear and `USAGE_AS_INCURRED` is among them, it is `USAGE_BASED_HYBRID`. In every other case — two or more distinct categories with no usage-metered line — it is `MULTI_ELEMENT`.

An assessment row is opened by a `RevenueAccountant` or a `DealDeskAnalyst` against a `quote_id`. Creating it snapshots the classification into the row's own `arrangement_type`, computes the allocation described below, records `snapshot_quote_version` as the quote's current `version`, and sets `status` to `ASSESSED` with `assessed_by_user_id`, `assessed_at` and `assessment_note` all unset.

The allocation itself is a waterfall over standalone selling prices. Usage-metered revenue is recognised as it is incurred rather than allocated, so it is carved out first: `usage_excluded_cents` is the sum of `final_net_cents` over the quote's lines whose `rev_rec_category` is `USAGE_AS_INCURRED`, and `allocable_amount_cents` is the quote's `final_net_total_cents` less that carve-out. The **allocable lines** are the quote's lines whose `rev_rec_category` is anything other than `USAGE_AS_INCURRED`, ordered by `created_at` ascending. Each allocable line's standalone selling price, reported as `ssp_cents`, is that line's `extended_list_cents`, and the standalone-price total is the sum of those figures across the allocable lines. Every allocable line except the last then takes its proportional share **floored** to a whole cent — the allocable amount multiplied by that line's standalone price, divided by the standalone-price total, with any fraction of a cent discarded rather than rounded. The last allocable line takes whatever remains: the allocable amount less the sum of the shares already allocated. `allocation_residual_cents` records how much extra the last line absorbed, namely its allocated amount less the floored share it would have received under the proportional rule. Flooring rather than rounding half up is what guarantees the residual is never negative and always lands on exactly one line — the newest allocable line, since the ordering is by ascending `created_at`.

The result is published as `allocation_lines`, an ordered array with one object per line of the quote — not merely per allocable line — where each object carries the `quote_line_id` it describes, that line's `ssp_cents`, and its `allocated_cents`. Usage-metered lines appear in that array with `allocated_cents` of zero, since they are recognised as incurred rather than allocated.

A `RevenueAccountant`, and only a `RevenueAccountant`, then clears or blocks the assessment with a `decision` of `CLEAR` or `BLOCK` and an optional `assessment_note`. Either decision requires the assessment to be in `ASSESSED` or `STALE` status, otherwise `ERR_REVREC_STATE_INVALID`, and requires the parent quote to be in `DRAFT` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_DRAFT`. A `CLEAR` decision — whether it arrives from `ASSESSED` or from `STALE` — recomputes the whole classification and allocation against the quote's current lines before committing, rewriting `arrangement_type`, `allocable_amount_cents`, `usage_excluded_cents`, `allocation_lines` and `allocation_residual_cents`, then moves `status` to `CLEARED`, re-snapshots `snapshot_quote_version` to the quote's current `version`, and stamps `assessed_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, `assessed_at` with the logical clock and the supplied `assessment_note`. A `BLOCK` decision — again identically from either `ASSESSED` or `STALE` — moves `status` to `BLOCKED` and stamps the same three audit fields; a blocked assessment means the quote can no longer satisfy the revenue gate at submission.

No assessment status is final, because an assessment is only as good as the quote it was taken against. Whenever the quote's `version` advances past the assessment's `snapshot_quote_version`, an assessment in `ASSESSED`, `CLEARED` or `BLOCKED` moves to `STALE`, and its `assessed_by_user_id`, `assessed_at` and `assessment_note` are cleared while `allocation_lines` are retained until the next clear-or-block recomputes them. The producers of that version advance are the operations that change what is being sold or what it costs: adding a line, editing a line, deleting a line, patching the quote header, reopening the quote, and deciding a competitive exception. The gate itself is stated in two places. Submission requires either that the quote's `arrangement_type` be `SINGLE_ELEMENT` or that some assessment on the quote be in `CLEARED` status, refusing otherwise with `ERR_REVREC_NOT_CLEARED`. Booking requires exactly the same condition, and refuses with `ERR_BOOKING_CRITERIA_UNMET`.

### 3.7. Submission, the Forked Approval Request, and the Two Tracks

Submission is the busiest operation in the system. Only the owning `SalesRep` may submit, and only from `DRAFT` — a submission attempted from any other status is refused with `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_DRAFT`, and a submission attempted after the logical clock has passed the quote's `expires_at` is refused with `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`. Beyond that, five structural gates are evaluated in a fixed sequence and the first one violated decides the answer. The quote must carry at least one line, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_HAS_NO_LINES`. Its pinned desk policy revision must still be in `ACTIVE` status, otherwise `ERR_DESK_POLICY_SUPERSEDED`. Its `term_months` must be at least as large as the greatest `min_term_months` among the products on its lines, otherwise `ERR_TERM_BELOW_PRODUCT_MINIMUM`. If its `payment_terms_days` exceeds the pinned revision's `standard_payment_terms_days`, there must exist a non-standard term on this quote whose `term_category` is `PAYMENT_TERMS`, whose `requested_numeric_value` equals that same `payment_terms_days`, and whose `review_state` is either `PENDING` or `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_NONSTANDARD_TERM_REQUIRED`. And the revenue gate must be satisfied — either the `arrangement_type` is `SINGLE_ELEMENT` or some assessment on the quote is `CLEARED` — otherwise `ERR_REVREC_NOT_CLEARED`. Only after all five pass are the two margin floors tested, the hard floor before the soft one.

A successful submission first reprices everything: the line-level and order-level figures, the blended discount and approval level, both margin figures, both risk scores, and the arrangement classification are all recomputed and written, so a quote's stored numbers are guaranteed fresh at the moment it enters approval. It stamps `submitted_at` with the logical clock and captures the pinned revision's `version` into `pinned_policy_version`. It drives the quote from `DRAFT` to `PENDING_APPROVAL` and increments `Quote.version` by one.

It then cascades downward and creates exactly one **approval request** for this submit cycle, in `status` `OPEN`, with its own `submitted_at` at the same logical instant, `quote_id` naming the quote, `required_level` copied from the quote's freshly computed `required_approval_level`, `escalation_count` at zero, `at_backstop` false, `sla_state` `ON_TRACK`, `decided_at` and `total_turnaround_hours` unset, and `requires_cfo_cosign` set to whether the quote's `terms_risk_score_dominant` is at or above the pinned revision's `terms_cfo_risk_threshold`.

The request carries **two independent state axes** that fork here and must join before the request can be approved. The first axis, `discount_track_state`, takes the values `NOT_REQUIRED`, `PENDING`, `CLEARED` and `BLOCKED`; it is set to `NOT_REQUIRED` when the request's `required_level` is 0, and to `PENDING` otherwise. The second axis, `terms_track_state`, takes the values `NOT_REQUIRED`, `PENDING_LEGAL`, `CLEARED` and `BLOCKED`; it is set to `NOT_REQUIRED` when the quote has no non-standard term whose `review_state` is `PENDING`, otherwise to `PENDING_LEGAL` when the quote's `terms_risk_score_aggregate` is at or above the pinned revision's `terms_review_risk_threshold`, and otherwise to `NOT_REQUIRED`. That last branch is the auto-approval path: when there are pending terms but the aggregate score falls short of the review threshold, every non-standard term on the quote whose `review_state` is `PENDING` is moved from `PENDING` to `APPROVED` in the same request, with `reviewed_at` stamped at the logical clock, `reviewed_by_user_id` left unset because no human decided it, and `reviewer_note` set to the fixed string `AUTO_APPROVED_BELOW_RISK_THRESHOLD`. No legal counsel is called and the terms axis ends up `NOT_REQUIRED`.

When the discount axis lands on `PENDING`, the submission also creates the first **approval step** — one row, at `level` 1, with `decision` `PENDING`, `assigned_role` copied from the `required_role` of the pinned revision's approval matrix band at `tier_level` 1, `approval_request_id` naming the request it belongs to, `delegation_grant_id` and `acting_user_id` and `decided_at` and `turnaround_hours` and `decision_note` all unset, `escalated_from_step_level` unset because it came from normal progression rather than an escalation, and its turnaround fields `in_surge_window`, `sla_hours_effective` and `sla_due_at` computed as described in the next subsection — and sets the request's `current_level` to 1. When the discount axis lands on `NOT_REQUIRED`, no step is created and `current_level` stays 0.

The join is evaluated immediately, in the same request. When the discount axis is either `NOT_REQUIRED` or `CLEARED` **and** the terms axis is either `NOT_REQUIRED` or `CLEARED`, the request moves `OPEN` to `APPROVED` right there, which in turn moves the quote `PENDING_APPROVAL` to `APPROVED` — so a clean, undiscounted, low-risk quote can be created, submitted and approved without any human approver ever touching it.

The **discount axis** advances through step decisions. An approval step is decided by a `DealDeskAnalyst`, a `SalesDirector`, a `VPSales` or the `CFO` supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `REJECT` or `REQUEST_CHANGES` together with an optional `decision_note`. All three decisions share an identical set of preconditions. The step's `decision` must still be `PENDING`, otherwise `ERR_STEP_ALREADY_DECIDED`. The parent quote must be in `PENDING_APPROVAL`, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_PENDING_APPROVAL`. The logical clock must be at or before the quote's `expires_at`, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the quote's `owner_rep_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. And the acting user must hold authority for the step, either by presenting an `X-User-Role` equal to the step's `assigned_role` or by relying on a matching delegation grant under the four narrowing conditions of section 2.10.

Every decision records `acting_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, `decided_at` from the logical clock, the elapsed `turnaround_hours`, and the supplied `decision_note`; and when the decision was made under a delegation it additionally records that grant's identifier in the step's `delegation_grant_id` and increments the grant's `used_step_count` by one. From there the three decisions diverge sharply. An `APPROVE` on a step whose `level` is below the request's `required_level` moves the step `PENDING` to `APPROVED` and creates the next step at `level` one higher, with `assigned_role` taken from the matrix band at that next `tier_level` and fresh turnaround fields, and advances the request's `current_level` to that new level. An `APPROVE` on a step whose `level` equals the request's `required_level` moves the step `PENDING` to `APPROVED` and clears the discount axis, moving `discount_track_state` from `PENDING` to `CLEARED`, then re-evaluates the join. A `REJECT` moves the step `PENDING` to `REJECTED` and blocks the discount axis, moving `discount_track_state` from `PENDING` to `BLOCKED`; that forces the request `OPEN` to `REJECTED` regardless of where the terms axis stands, which forces the quote `PENDING_APPROVAL` to `REJECTED` with all of the quote's own rejection cascades. A `REQUEST_CHANGES` moves the step `PENDING` to `CHANGES_REQUESTED` and moves the request `OPEN` to `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, leaving `discount_track_state` at `PENDING` and `terms_track_state` exactly as it was; the quote follows from `PENDING_APPROVAL` to `CHANGES_REQUESTED`. Approval steps may also be listed and read individually, filtered by `approval_request_id`, by `delegation_grant_id` or by `decision`.

The **terms axis** advances through legal review. `LegalCounsel` owns the `APPROVE` and `REJECT` decisions on a single term and the `CFO` owns the `COSIGN` decision, all three expressed as a `decision` value with an optional `reviewer_note`. An `APPROVE` or `REJECT` requires the term's `review_state` to be `PENDING`, otherwise `ERR_TERM_ALREADY_REVIEWED`; requires the parent quote to be in `PENDING_APPROVAL`, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_PENDING_APPROVAL`; and requires the request's `terms_track_state` to be exactly `PENDING_LEGAL`, otherwise `ERR_LEGAL_REVIEW_NOT_REQUIRED`. An `APPROVE` moves the term `PENDING` to `APPROVED`, stamping `review_state`, `reviewed_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, `reviewed_at` from the logical clock and the `reviewer_note`. A `REJECT` moves the term `PENDING` to `REJECTED`, stamping the same three audit fields, and blocks the terms axis: `terms_track_state` moves from `PENDING_LEGAL` to `BLOCKED`, which forces the request `OPEN` to `REJECTED` regardless of where the discount axis stands, which forces the quote `PENDING_APPROVAL` to `REJECTED` with its cascades.

A `COSIGN` is the `CFO`'s separate act and its preconditions are its own. The term's `review_state` must already be `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_TERM_NOT_LEGAL_APPROVED`; the term's `requires_cfo_cosign` must be true, otherwise `ERR_TERM_COSIGN_NOT_REQUIRED`; the term's `cfo_cosigned_at` must still be unset, otherwise `ERR_TERM_ALREADY_COSIGNED`; and the parent quote must be in `PENDING_APPROVAL`, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_PENDING_APPROVAL`. A co-signature stamps `cfo_cosigned_at` with the logical clock and `cfo_cosigned_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and deliberately leaves `review_state` at `APPROVED` — a co-signature is an addition to a legal approval, not a replacement for one.

The terms axis clears only when three things hold together: `terms_track_state` is `PENDING_LEGAL`, no non-standard term on the quote is left in `review_state` `PENDING`, and either the request's `requires_cfo_cosign` is false or the one term carrying `requires_cfo_cosign` has its `cfo_cosigned_at` set. When all three hold — whether the triggering act was the last legal approval or the awaited co-signature — `terms_track_state` moves from `PENDING_LEGAL` to `CLEARED` and the join is re-evaluated.

The request's own `status` is the joined outcome of the two axes and takes the values `OPEN`, `APPROVED`, `REJECTED`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `CANCELLED` and `EXPIRED`, of which the last four other than `CHANGES_REQUESTED` — that is, `APPROVED`, `REJECTED`, `CANCELLED` and `EXPIRED` — are final. Moving `OPEN` to `APPROVED` requires the request to still be `OPEN` with the discount axis in `NOT_REQUIRED` or `CLEARED` and the terms axis in `NOT_REQUIRED` or `CLEARED`; it stamps `decided_at`, computes `total_turnaround_hours`, and sets `sla_state` to `MET` when `escalation_count` is zero and to `BREACHED` otherwise, then moves the quote `PENDING_APPROVAL` to `APPROVED`. Moving `OPEN` to `REJECTED` requires the request to still be `OPEN` with either axis in `BLOCKED`; it stamps `decided_at` and `total_turnaround_hours`, and sets `sla_state` to `BREACHED` when `escalation_count` is greater than zero and to `MET` otherwise, then moves the quote to `REJECTED`.

Two quote-level cascades hang off those outcomes. Reaching `APPROVED` stamps the quote's `approved_at`, re-bases its `expires_at` as described in the next subsection, and freezes its lines. Reaching `REJECTED` withdraws and releases: every non-standard term on the quote whose `review_state` is `PENDING` moves `PENDING` to `WITHDRAWN`, after which it no longer contributes to any later risk recomputation; and every competitive exception on the quote in `ACCEPTED` moves to `RELEASED`, stamps `released_at`, and returns its `allowance_consumed_cents` to the account's pool. Reaching `CHANGES_REQUESTED` moves no money and changes no monetary field at all; the quote is not yet editable, and stays that way until the owner reopens it.

Reopening is the owner's move and it is what makes this workflow cyclic. It requires the quote to be in `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_CHANGES_REQUESTED`; requires the acting `X-User-ID` to be the `owner_rep_user_id`; and requires the logical clock to be at or before `expires_at`, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`. It moves the quote `CHANGES_REQUESTED` to `DRAFT`, increments `revision_count` by one, clears `submitted_at`, `approved_at` and `policy_revalidation_required`, and increments `Quote.version` by one. It then cascades downward in four levels. The quote's approval request moves `CHANGES_REQUESTED` to `CANCELLED` and stamps its `decided_at`. Every approval step on that request whose `decision` is still `PENDING` moves `PENDING` to `VOIDED`, with `decided_at` stamped and `turnaround_hours` deliberately left unset. Every non-standard term on the quote whose `review_state` is `APPROVED` moves back from `APPROVED` to `PENDING`, clearing `reviewed_by_user_id`, `reviewed_at`, `reviewer_note`, `cfo_cosigned_at` and `cfo_cosigned_by_user_id` — so a term can be reviewed more than once across a deal's life. And every revenue recognition assessment on the quote in `ASSESSED`, `CLEARED` or `BLOCKED` moves to `STALE`. The quote may then be edited and submitted again, incrementing `revision_count` on each further round.

### 3.8. Turnaround Service Levels, Escalation, Expiry and Renegotiation

Approvers are held to a clock. Every approval step, at the moment it is created, records whether that moment fell inside the quarter-end surge window into `in_surge_window`, and computes the allowance it is given. `sla_hours_effective` is the `sla_hours` configured on the pinned revision's matrix band for the step's `level`, multiplied by the pinned revision's `surge_sla_multiplier` when `in_surge_window` is true and multiplied by one otherwise, rounded half up to two decimal places. `sla_due_at` is the step's own `created_at` advanced by that many hours. A quarter-end surge with a multiplier of, say, one and a half turns a configured eight-hour allowance into twelve hours — the desk buys itself slack precisely when the queue is longest. Breach is strict: a step is late only when the logical clock is strictly past its `sla_due_at`, and a clock reading exactly equal to `sla_due_at` is on time.

When a step is decided, `turnaround_hours` records the elapsed time between the step's `created_at` and its `decided_at`, expressed in hours — that is, the elapsed seconds divided by 3,600 — rounded half up to two decimal places. When the whole request reaches a terminal status, `total_turnaround_hours` records the elapsed time between the request's `submitted_at` and its `decided_at` on the same basis, likewise in hours rounded half up to two decimal places. A step voided by a cancellation or an expiry has its `decided_at` stamped but leaves `turnaround_hours` unset, because nobody took a turn on it.

A `PlatformAdmin` drives lateness with a service-level escalation sweep, which walks every approval step whose `decision` is still `PENDING` in ascending `created_at` order and escalates each one whose deadline has passed. A step is escalated when its `decision` is `PENDING`, the logical clock is strictly past its `sla_due_at`, and its parent quote is still in `PENDING_APPROVAL`. Escalation moves the step's `decision` from `PENDING` to `ESCALATED`, increments the parent request's `escalation_count` by one, and sets the request's `sla_state` to `BREACHED`. It then creates a replacement step whose `escalated_from_step_level` records the breached step's `level`, and whose own `level` depends on when the breached step was created: a step created inside the surge window jumps **two** levels rather than one, so the replacement level is the breached level plus two, and otherwise it is the breached level plus one — with either result capped at 4, the highest level the matrix defines. The replacement's `assigned_role` comes from the matrix band at its own `tier_level`, and its `in_surge_window`, `sla_hours_effective` and `sla_due_at` are computed fresh from the sweep instant rather than inherited. The request's `current_level` is set to the replacement's level, and its `required_level` is raised to the greater of its existing `required_level` and that replacement level — an escalated deal never falls back to needing less authority than it has already been pushed to. When the replacement lands at level 4, the request's `at_backstop` is set true, marking that the CFO backstop has been reached and no further escalation is possible. The sweep answers with the instant it ran as `swept_at`, the identifiers of the steps it escalated as `escalated_step_ids`, the identifiers of the replacements it created as `created_step_ids`, and the identifiers of the requests it marked breached as `breached_request_ids`.

Quote validity is set twice in a quote's life. At creation, `expires_at` is the quote's `created_at` advanced by the pinned revision's `quote_validity_days` days. At approval it is re-based: `expires_at` becomes `approved_at` advanced by the same `quote_validity_days` days, except that when `approved_at` itself falls inside the surge window the result is truncated to the quarter-end instant — a deal approved in the closing days of a quarter cannot carry a price into the next one. Expiry, like breach, is strict: a quote expires only when the logical clock is strictly past its `expires_at`.

The same `PlatformAdmin` drives expiry with an expiry sweep, which processes candidate quotes in ascending `created_at` order and also clears out lapsed delegations. A quote in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `APPROVED` whose `expires_at` the clock has strictly passed moves to `EXPIRED`, and `EXPIRED` is final. Each origin status carries its own cascade, and all three share one compensation. From `DRAFT`, every non-standard term on the quote with `review_state` `PENDING` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, and every competitive exception in `ACCEPTED` moves to `RELEASED` and returns its `allowance_consumed_cents` to the account's pool. From `PENDING_APPROVAL`, the quote's approval request moves `OPEN` to `EXPIRED` with its `decided_at` stamped — setting `sla_state` to `BREACHED` when `escalation_count` is greater than zero and otherwise retaining `ON_TRACK` — every approval step on it whose `decision` is `PENDING` moves to `VOIDED`, every `PENDING` term moves to `WITHDRAWN`, and every `ACCEPTED` exception is released with the same allowance return. From `APPROVED` there is an extra precondition: the quote is expired only when no order form on it is in `BOOKED` status, since a booked deal is done and cannot lapse. When it does expire from `APPROVED`, every order form on the quote whose `status` is `GENERATED` or `OUT_FOR_SIGNATURE` moves to `VOID` with `voided_at` stamped and `void_reason` set to the fixed string `QUOTE_EXPIRED`, and every `ACCEPTED` exception is released with the same allowance return. Separately, every delegation grant still in `ACTIVE` whose `ends_at` the logical clock has reached — at or past, since the window's upper bound is exclusive — moves `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED`; nothing else about that grant or its already-decided steps changes, and any later attempt to decide a step under it is refused with `ERR_DELEGATION_NOT_ACTIVE`. The sweep answers with `swept_at`, `expired_quote_ids`, `expired_delegation_grant_ids`, `voided_order_form_ids` and `released_exception_ids`.

Renegotiation is the forward-looking counterpart to reopening: rather than editing a quote in place, the owning `SalesRep` clones it into a fresh negotiation. The request carries the mandatory `expected_quote_version` described in section 2.9 and may optionally override `term_months`, `payment_terms_days` and `billing_frequency` on the clone. A successful clone is created as a brand-new quote in `DRAFT` with `renegotiated_from_quote_id` naming the source quote, with a copy of every one of the source's lines, and with its own `expires_at` computed from the clone's own `created_at` and the pinned validity period. The source quote has its `renegotiation_count` incremented by one and its `version` incremented by one. And because a source quote may be renegotiated repeatedly, only the newest clone is live: any earlier clone of the same source quote that is still in `DRAFT` — that is, any quote whose `renegotiated_from_quote_id` names the same source and whose `status` is `DRAFT` — moves from `DRAFT` to `SUPERSEDED`. A superseded clone stops being editable and `SUPERSEDED` is final for it, but its lines are retained for audit rather than deleted. Quotes may be listed with filters on `account_id`, `desk_policy_id`, `renegotiated_from_quote_id` and `status`, which is how the renegotiation chain of a deal is reconstructed.

### 3.9. Order Forms, Signature Routing, Booking and Reversal

An approved quote still has to be papered. The owning `SalesRep` generates an **order form** naming the `quote_id`, the customer signatory's `signer_name` and `signer_email` — the address signature routing targets — and the mandatory `expected_quote_version`. The form is created in `GENERATED` status with `sent_at`, `signed_at`, `declined_at`, `booked_at`, `voided_at`, `signed_by_name`, `void_reason`, `reversal_reason` and `booking_reversed_at` all unset, with `booked_arr_cents` and `booked_tcv_cents` at zero, and with `booking_reversal_count` at zero. Generation increments `Quote.version` by one and then records `generated_from_quote_version` as the **incremented** value, so a freshly generated form always matches its quote exactly while any later write to the quote leaves the form behind. Generation also voids the previous paperwork: every existing order form of the same quote whose `status` is `GENERATED` or `OUT_FOR_SIGNATURE` moves to `VOID`, with `voided_at` stamped and `void_reason` set to the fixed string `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_ORDER_FORM`.

Routing and signature are two separate acts by two different roles. The owning `SalesRep` sends the form, moving it `GENERATED` to `OUT_FOR_SIGNATURE` and stamping `sent_at`; this requires the form to be in `GENERATED`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_FORM_STATE_INVALID`, requires the parent quote to be in `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_APPROVED`, and requires the logical clock to be at or before the quote's `expires_at`, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`. A `DealDeskAnalyst` then records what the customer did, supplying an `outcome` of either `SIGNED` or `DECLINED` and, optionally, the `signed_by_name` recorded on the signature event. Both outcomes require the form to be in `OUT_FOR_SIGNATURE`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_FORM_STATE_INVALID`, and both require the parent quote to be in `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_APPROVED`. The `SIGNED` outcome additionally requires the logical clock to be at or before the quote's `expires_at`, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`, and moves the form `OUT_FOR_SIGNATURE` to `SIGNED`, stamping `signed_at` and `signed_by_name`. The `DECLINED` outcome carries no expiry requirement — a customer may decline a lapsed form — and moves `OUT_FOR_SIGNATURE` to `DECLINED`, stamping `declined_at`; the parent quote keeps its `APPROVED` status, no ledger counter moves, and `DECLINED` is final for that form.

Booking is where the deal becomes revenue, and it is gated by the order acceptance criteria. A `DealDeskAnalyst` books a signed form, which requires the form to be in `SIGNED`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_FORM_STATE_INVALID`; requires the parent quote to be in `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_APPROVED`; and requires the logical clock to be at or before the quote's `expires_at`, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`. Then come the acceptance criteria themselves, in a fixed order. The account's `credit_status` must be `CLEAR` or `WATCH`; an account on `HOLD` is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_CREDIT_HOLD`. After that, four criteria share the single code `ERR_BOOKING_CRITERIA_UNMET`: the form's `generated_from_quote_version` must still equal the quote's current `version`, so any write to the quote since the form was generated forces a regeneration; the quote's `policy_revalidation_required` must be false, so a quote whose pinned policy was superseded mid-approval must be reopened and re-submitted first; the quote's `arrangement_type` must be `SINGLE_ELEMENT` or else some assessment on it must be `CLEARED`; and the computed total contract value must be strictly greater than zero.

The two booking figures are computed at the moment of booking from the quote's lines as they then stand. **Recurring** revenue counts only the lines whose `rev_rec_category` is `RATABLE`: their `final_net_cents` are summed, and `booked_arr_cents` is that sum annualised — divided by the quote's `term_months` expressed in years, that is, by the term in months divided by twelve — and rounded half up to a whole cent. A two-year ratable commitment therefore books half of its whole-term net as annual recurring revenue. `booked_tcv_cents` is simply the quote's `final_net_total_cents`; lines whose category is `POINT_IN_TIME` or `USAGE_AS_INCURRED` contribute to that total contract value but never to the annual recurring figure. Booking moves the form `SIGNED` to `BOOKED`, stamps `booked_at`, writes both figures onto the form, increases the account's conserved `booked_arr_cents` by the form's `booked_arr_cents`, and bubbles up: the quote moves `APPROVED` to `BOOKED`, stamps its own `booked_at`, and copies both booking figures onto itself.

`BOOKED` is not the end of the road, because bookings are occasionally wrong. The `CFO`, and only the `CFO`, may reverse one, supplying a mandatory `reversal_reason`. Reversal requires the form to be in `BOOKED`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_FORM_STATE_INVALID`, and requires the parent quote to be in `BOOKED`. It moves the form `BOOKED` to `VOID`, stamping `booking_reversed_at` and `voided_at` with the logical clock, recording the supplied `reversal_reason`, setting `void_reason` to the fixed string `BOOKING_REVERSED`, and incrementing `booking_reversal_count` by one. It decreases the account's `booked_arr_cents` by exactly the amount that form had contributed, while the form retains its own `booked_arr_cents` and `booked_tcv_cents` for audit. And it bubbles up: the quote moves `BOOKED` back to `APPROVED`, has its `booked_at` cleared, and has its own `booked_arr_cents` and `booked_tcv_cents` reset to zero. The quote is then approved once more and may be papered again — which is precisely why `BOOKED` is not a terminal quote status, while `REJECTED`, `EXPIRED` and `SUPERSEDED` are.
