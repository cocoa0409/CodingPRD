# Surgical Robot Placement API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Placing a surgical robotics platform is never a single transaction. It is a multi-year relationship between a manufacturer and a hospital system, stitched together from commercial commitments, physical construction work, surgeon credentialing, instrument consumption, and field-service guarantees. This document specifies the API that models that relationship end to end: how a hospital system is opened and progressed from a prospect into a live customer, how a placement agreement is drafted, submitted, accepted and later suspended or terminated, how the physical robot systems that an agreement mints are installed, commissioned and eventually decommissioned, and how every procedure performed on those systems is billed, counted against a utilization commitment, and rolled up into the reporting the manufacturer uses to decide where the next systems go.

The business stakes pull in two directions at once. The manufacturer needs committed procedure volume to justify the capital tied up in every placed platform, and it therefore tracks per-interval utilization commitments, shortfall exposure, and expansion eligibility with some rigor. The hospital, for its part, needs assurance that the physical site actually satisfies the dimensional and integration requirements of the configuration it is buying, that its surgeons are genuinely credentialed before they operate, and that a service incident will be answered within a contractual response window. Both sides are served by a single authoritative record of readiness, entitlement, instrument life, and coverage.

Scope is deliberately broad. The API covers the operational-and-ops-run surfaces (month-close reporting, scheduled reclamation waves, credential and contract expiry sweeps) alongside the ordinary create-and-transition surfaces. A single logical clock governs every time-sensitive rule, so expiry, acceptance windows, coverage windows and reporting periods are all evaluated against a value the caller can drive deterministically — never against real wall-clock time. Where a rule has a boundary, the boundary is stated exactly, because the difference between an inclusive and an exclusive edge is the difference between a system that meets its commitment and one that is charged for a shortfall.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section collects the rules that apply across the whole surface: who may call what, what every response looks like, how lists are paged, and the cross-cutting invariants — posture gating, dimensional qualification, credentialing, coverage, instrument life, commitment accounting, concurrency and precision — that individual workflows in §3 then depend on.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry `X-User-ID` to identify the acting party and `X-User-Role` to declare its capacity. A request that omits either header, or supplies a blank value, is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_OR_INVALID_USER_CONTEXT_HEADER` (HTTP 400). This check runs before anything else. Immediately after it, the server verifies that the declared role is permitted to invoke the operation at all; a role that is not among an endpoint's authorized roles is rejected with `ERR_AGENT_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). One further scope check follows: some roles are region-scoped, and a region-scoped role may only act on a `HospitalAccount` whose `region_code` equals the acting identity's own region; crossing that line yields `ERR_REGION_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

The system recognizes ten roles. They are `PlatformAdmin`, `RegionalSalesDirector`, `ClinicalOperationsLead`, `HospitalProcurementOfficer`, `ServiceEngineer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `IDNCoordinator`, `AccountOwner`, `Surgeon`, and `Anonymous`.

`PlatformAdmin` is an opaque operator identity: `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it where a transition writes the acting id, but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. `RegionalSalesDirector` is likewise opaque. `ClinicalOperationsLead` is opaque. `HospitalProcurementOfficer` is opaque. `ServiceEngineer` is opaque. `ComplianceOfficer` is opaque. `IDNCoordinator` is opaque. `Anonymous` is opaque as well — it exists only so that fixtures can drive the logical clock from an empty database.

The remaining two roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `AccountOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `HospitalAccount`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `HospitalAccount` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Surgeon`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `SurgeonTrainingRecord`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `SurgeonTrainingRecord` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. An entity-backed role that acts on a resource whose owning foreign key is not the acting identity is rejected with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

One further segregation-of-duties check is layered on top of the ordinary role checks. The identity that approved a proctor case at credentialing time, and the identity that decides an expansion review, must differ from the `hospital_owner_user_id` recorded on the owning account — that is, the user id stamped from `X-User-ID` when the account was created. An approver whose `X-User-ID` equals `hospital_owner_user_id` is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This applies both when a `ComplianceOfficer` approves a proctor case and when a `ComplianceOfficer` approves an expansion review.

Two further rules apply to the procedure-case surfaces specifically. A procedure case that names a training record failing the credential-validity step of the training-path evaluation is rejected with `ERR_SURGEON_NOT_CREDENTIALED` (HTTP 409); the same rule guards every procedure-case mutation. And the bulk completion operation rejects any request naming a system outside the acting account identity's scope with `ERR_BULK_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

For a complete listing of which roles may invoke which endpoint, see §4.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paged. The `limit` parameter caps the number of items returned; when it is absent it defaults to 10, and it is bounded below by 1 and above by 100. The `offset` parameter skips that many items from the front of the result and defaults to 0. A list response echoes `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope; `total_count` reflects the full size of the filtered result set rather than the size of the returned page, and `has_more` is true when further items remain beyond the returned page. Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

The server never reads real wall-clock time. Before any call it reads exactly the fixed startup default `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`. Every time-sensitive rule in this document — acceptance windows, coverage windows, credential expiry, evidence grace, month-close reporting periods, loaner windows, deployment windows, incident due instants, the monthly decay — is evaluated against this logical clock. The clock is advanced only by the admin test-driving capability that sets it to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant; advancing it changes no entity's `version`. `PlatformAdmin` may drive it, and the `Anonymous` role is admitted here as well so that fixtures can be established from an empty database.

### 2.5. Posture Gating and the Three-Dimensional Account Bottleneck

Not every status change on a hospital system is requested by a client. Three separate state dimensions meet on `HospitalAccount`: the commercial `account_status`, the derived `account_posture`, and the collection of readiness verdicts. `account_status` runs `PROSPECT → READINESS → CONTRACTED → ACTIVE → DORMANT → CLOSED`. `account_posture` is a computed gate value drawn from the closed set `{ACTIVE, ONBOARDING_GATE, EXPANSION_BLOCKED}` and is re-evaluated after essentially every mutation in this document.

The posture is evaluated in a pinned order that matters more than it looks. `EXPANSION_BLOCKED` is tested first: the posture is `EXPANSION_BLOCKED` whenever any of the account's systems carries `service_status == SERVICE_ACTION_REQUIRED`. Only if that fails is `ONBOARDING_GATE` tested: the posture is `ONBOARDING_GATE` whenever any `SiteReadinessReview` on the account has `readiness_status` in `PENDING_REVIEW`, `CONDITIONAL` or `REJECTED`, or when the account has no review at all. Otherwise the posture is `ACTIVE`. The consequence is that a site in service-action-required blocks expansion even if its paperwork is completely clean.

A large family of operations requires an ungated account and is rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_POSTURE_GATE` (HTTP 409) when `account_posture` is not `ACTIVE`. Those operations are: submitting a site-readiness verdict; accepting a placement agreement; installing a robot system; and drafting, submitting, suspending, reinstating, rejecting or terminating an agreement where the corresponding transition declares the requirement.

`PROSPECT` to `READINESS` is driven by the account readiness activation, which requires the `account_status` to still be `PROSPECT` (`ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_IN_ELIGIBLE_STATE`, HTTP 409, checked before the rest), at least one readiness review on file (`ERR_READINESS_REVIEW_REQUIRED`, HTTP 409), and at least one surgeon training record on file (`ERR_TRAINING_PATH_UNINITIALIZED`, HTTP 409). Activation recomputes the posture and opens a fresh utilization cycle for every robot system of the account — none exist on this path, so the effect is a no-op in practice but must still be performed.

`READINESS` to `CONTRACTED` is not client-driven: it is evaluated after a placement agreement is drafted for the account, and requires the account to still be `READINESS` (`ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_IN_ELIGIBLE_STATE`, checked first) together with the freshly drafted agreement row. An advance from `READINESS` to `CONTRACTED` with no agreement row on file at all is reported as `ERR_NO_PLACEMENT_AGREEMENT` (HTTP 409).

Reference resolution across nested paths is sequential and left-to-right. Every endpoint whose path carries one or more resource identifiers resolves each of them in order and rejects the request with HTTP 404 at the first identifier that does not correspond to an existing resource, before any business rule for the operation is evaluated. The identifier-to-code mapping is fixed: a path `accountId` yields `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`; an `agreementId` yields `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`; a `systemId` yields `ERR_SYSTEM_NOT_FOUND`; a `reviewId` on the site-readiness collection yields `ERR_READINESS_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, while a `reviewId` on the expansion-review collection yields `ERR_EXPANSION_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`; a `caseId` yields `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`; a `recordId` yields `ERR_TRAINING_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`; an `evidenceId` yields `ERR_EVIDENCE_NOT_FOUND`; a `contractId` yields `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`; an `incidentId` yields `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`; a `transactionId` yields `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND`; a `ledgerId` yields `ERR_LEDGER_NOT_FOUND`; a `configId` yields `ERR_CONFIGURATION_NOT_FOUND`; a `deploymentId` yields `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_NOT_FOUND`; a `proctorCaseId` yields `ERR_PROCTOR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`; and a `dispatchId` yields `ERR_LOANER_DISPATCH_NOT_FOUND`. `CONTRACTED` to `ACTIVE` happens automatically when a placement agreement reaches `ACTIVE`, and requires that the account carry exactly zero non-terminal readiness reviews (`ERR_READINESS_REVIEW_PENDING`, HTTP 409) and at least one agreement in `ACTIVE` status (`ERR_NO_ACTIVE_AGREEMENT`, HTTP 409). That transition stamps the newly active agreement's `cycle_start_utc` from the logical clock and recomputes its commitment state.

An `ACTIVE` account turns `DORMANT` automatically at a month-close run when no procedure case on any of the account's systems was scheduled inside the just-closed cycle window; the dormancy instant is stamped onto `dormant_since_utc`. It is revived by the dormant-account reactivation, which requires `account_status == DORMANT` (`ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_IN_ELIGIBLE_STATE`, checked first) and clears `dormant_since_utc` back to null.

Closing an account is a heavier operation. It is reachable from either `ACTIVE` or `DORMANT`; any other status yields `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_CLOSABLE` (HTTP 409, checked first), and any agreement still in `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` yields `ERR_ACTIVE_AGREEMENT_EXISTS` (HTTP 409). A successful close moves `account_status` to `CLOSED`, cancels every non-terminal procedure case on the account's systems, terminates every `ACTIVE` service contract on those systems, recomputes the posture, and increments the account's `version`.

### 2.6. Site Readiness, Dimensional Qualification and Uplift

A site-readiness review is the physical gate between a configuration specification and a real operating room. It is opened against an account and an evaluated configuration and is initialized to `readiness_status` `PENDING_REVIEW` and `review_round` 1, with `conditional_clearance_count` computed against the evaluated configuration at creation. `readiness_status` runs `PENDING_REVIEW → PASSED / CONDITIONAL / REJECTED`, with a `REJECTED → PENDING_REVIEW` revision loop; `PASSED` is terminal, which is why an account carrying any non-`PASSED` review is never gated `ACTIVE`.

Four dimensional checks are evaluated on every review, against the minimums of the configuration named by `evaluated_configuration_id`: the operating-room length (`operating_room_length_cm`), the operating-room width (`operating_room_width_cm`), the table throw radius (`table_throw_radius_cm`), and the ceiling clearance (`ceiling_clearance_cm`). Two integration checks ride alongside them: whether the site provides a dedicated power circuit (`has_dedicated_power_circuit`) when the configuration requires one, and whether it provides a dark fiber run (`has_dark_fiber_run`) when the configuration's `integration_tier` calls for one.

`conditional_clearance_count` counts the dimensional or integration checks that miss an exact match but fall inside the conditional tolerance band. The band is asymmetric by design: a measurement exactly at the configuration minimum is a clean match and contributes nothing to the conditional count, while a measurement strictly above the minimum but below the minimum plus the near-miss tolerance of 5 cm lands inside the conditional band and increments the count by one.

The tolerance band's width is driven by the configuration's `integration_tier`, which is an integer depth from 1 to 3.

The construction uplift quoted at review creation is computed in a pinned order. First count the dimensional checks whose submitted value falls below the evaluated configuration's minimum; call that the unmet count. Then count those same checks whose submitted value is at or above the minimum but below the minimum plus 5 cm; call that the near-miss count. The uplift unit count is the sum of the two. When the uplift unit count is zero the `construction_uplift_fee_cents` is zero. Otherwise it is 304800 cents multiplied by the uplift unit count, plus a further 76200 cents multiplied by however many uplift units there are beyond the first — so the second and each later uplift unit costs strictly more than the first. One uplift unit therefore quotes 304800 cents, and two quote 685800 cents rather than 609600.

Submitting a verdict is where qualification is enforced. The review must be in `PENDING_REVIEW` (or, when the verdict is a pass, in `CONDITIONAL`) — otherwise `ERR_READINESS_REVIEW_NOT_PENDING` (HTTP 409, checked first). Every operating-room dimension and the table throw radius must meet or exceed the evaluated configuration's minimums, failing which the verdict is rejected with `ERR_ROOM_REQUIREMENTS_UNMET` (HTTP 422). A `PASSED` verdict additionally requires `conditional_clearance_count` to be zero, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_CONDITIONAL_CLEARANCE_REQUIRES_REVIEW` (HTTP 422) — the reason being that a review with unresolved conditional clearances has not achieved exact match on everything. A verdict of `CONDITIONAL` instead requires at least one dimensional or integration check to have missed exact match while staying inside the conditional tolerance band; a review with no such clearances cannot be marked `CONDITIONAL`. Both paths require the site's dedicated power circuit whenever the configuration demands one (`ERR_POWER_CIRCUIT_MISSING`, HTTP 422) and the dark fiber run whenever the configuration demands one (`ERR_NETWORK_INTEGRATION_MISSING`, HTTP 422), and both require `account_posture` to be something other than `EXPANSION_BLOCKED` (`ERR_ACCOUNT_POSTURE_GATE`).

Rejection of a review requires `PENDING_REVIEW` (checked first) and a non-empty `remediation_note` string, without which the request fails `ERR_REMEDIATION_NOTE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). Returning a rejected review to review requires the review to be `REJECTED` (`ERR_READINESS_REVIEW_NOT_REJECTED`, HTTP 409, checked first) and increments `review_round` by one, recomputes `conditional_clearance_count` against the evaluated configuration, and recomputes the posture. There is also an automatic rejection path with no client trigger: when the review's evaluated configuration is `RETIRED`, a `PENDING_REVIEW` review is moved to `REJECTED` and the posture is recomputed. Re-measuring a single dimensional check against a review leaves its `readiness_status` untouched but recomputes the conditional clearance count. That re-measurement names the check being re-taken as `check_type` and supplies its new figure as `measured_value_cm`.

Separately, both the verdict path and the configuration catalog enforce power and network requirements at the configuration level: creating or updating a system configuration that demands a power circuit or dark fiber against a site that cannot supply it fails with the same two 422 codes.

### 2.7. Common Resource Properties

Every top-level resource in this API — `HospitalAccount`, `SiteReadinessReview`, `SystemConfiguration`, `PlacementAgreement`, `RobotSystem`, `SurgeonTrainingRecord`, `CredentialingEvidence`, `UtilizationCycle`, `ProcedureCase`, `LoanerSystemDispatch`, `ServiceContract`, `IncidentServiceRequest`, `SoftwareRelease`, `SoftwareReleaseDeployment`, `ExpansionReview`, `UtilisationConsumptionLedger`, and `UtilisationTransferTransaction` — carries the standard system-managed fields.

- `id` is a unique, server-minted identifier formatted as a UUID with no type prefix. Where a child is reached as a sub-resource, its own `id` is what a client passes back as that child's identifier; a `CredentialingEvidence` row, for instance, is addressed by its own `id` while its `surgeon_training_record_id` records the training record it substantiates.
- `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording first creation.
- `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on any change to the resource's properties.
- `version` is a system-managed integer that increments by one on every mutation, and is the value clients read to construct concurrency checks.

Because every entity in this scaffold sets `common_fields: true`, all seventeen carry the full quartet; none is limited to `id` and `created_at` alone.

Derived fields are mutations for these purposes. When a computed value on a parent is reconciled — the account's posture, an agreement's commitment state, a system's remaining instrument lives, a cycle's band, a contract's response commitment — the parent's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` incremented, even though no client asked for that specific change. Actions that touch two parents increment both; accepting an agreement, for instance, increments the agreement's version and the account's.

### 2.8. Coverage Contracts and Response-Time Commitments

A service contract attaches coverage to one robot system. `coverage_tier` is drawn from the closed set `COMPREHENSIVE`, `STANDARD`, `LIMITED`, `PARTS_ONLY`. It resolves to a `service_tier_index`: `COMPREHENSIVE` is 3, `STANDARD` is 2, `LIMITED` is 1, `PARTS_ONLY` is 0. A coverage tier that does not map into that closed set is rejected at the first step of the computation with `ERR_CONTRACT_TIER_UNRECOGNIZED` (HTTP 422).

The response-time commitment is computed in a pinned order whose nesting is load-bearing. First form a base of 48 hours minus 12 hours multiplied by the service tier index. Then clamp that base from below at 4 hours, and clamp the result of that from above at 24 hours. The inner floor is applied first: a tier that mapped below `PARTS_ONLY` lands on 4 hours. The outer ceiling then pulls the `COMPREHENSIVE` branch down from 36 hours to 24. An implementation that applies the ceiling before the floor would report 36 hours for `COMPREHENSIVE` instead of the correct 24. The resulting commitment is 24 hours for `COMPREHENSIVE`, 12 for `STANDARD`, 18 for `LIMITED` and 24 for `PARTS_ONLY` once the tier index is applied directly.

Contract coverage must be temporally valid: `contract_start_utc` must fall on or after the system's installation instant, and the end instant — `contract_start_utc` plus `contract_months` multiplied by 30 days — must be strictly later than the start. Violations are rejected with `ERR_CONTRACT_TEMPORALLY_INVALID` (HTTP 422), both when a contract is created and when a suspended agreement is reinstated. `contract_status` runs from `ACTIVE` to either `EXPIRED` or `TERMINATED`. Terminating a contract requires it to be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_SERVICE_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`, HTTP 409, checked first), resolves every incident on the system that is `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`, and recomputes the posture.

A service contract is also required infrastructure for two other operations: commissioning an installed system and restoring a system from service-action-required both demand an `ACTIVE` in-window contract on the system, failing which they are rejected with `ERR_SERVICE_CONTRACT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409).

### 2.9. Incident Response and Penalty Accounting

A service incident is filed with the instant the failure interrupted serviceable operation. Filing initializes `request_status` to `OPEN` and derives `incident_severity`, which is drawn from the closed set `MINOR`, `MAJOR`, `CRITICAL`. The severity is read from the system's posture and coverage tier at the filing instant: `CRITICAL` when the system's `service_status` is `SERVICE_ACTION_REQUIRED` at filing; `MAJOR` when the system is `IN_SERVICE` but its owning account's posture is not `ACTIVE`; `MINOR` otherwise. The `response_due_at_utc` is the incident start instant advanced by the `response_time_commitment_hours` of the active service contract. The breach instant is exclusive: an acknowledgement stamped exactly at `response_due_at_utc` is not a breach.

`request_status` advances through `OPEN`, then `ACKNOWLEDGED`, then `LOANER_PENDING`, then `LOANER_DISPATCHED`, then `SERVICE_ACTION_REQUIRED`, and finally `RESOLVED`. Acknowledging stops the response-time clock.

Resolving an incident scores a penalty by a decay function. Let `t` be the service tier index plus one. A resolution at or before `response_due_at_utc` scores zero. A resolution strictly after the due instant but at or before the due instant plus 6 hours scores 2 points. A resolution strictly after the due instant plus 6 hours but at or before the due instant plus 24 hours scores 2 points multiplied by `t`. A resolution strictly after the due instant plus 24 hours scores 4 points multiplied by `t`. The raw penalty is added to the account's penalty balance and the total is then clamped to a maximum of 15 points.

The penalty balance itself is an aggregate over the account. It is the opening balance after decay, plus the sum of the resolution penalties of every incident resolved in the interval, plus the existing penalty points carried by transferred loaner units, clamped to the range from 0 to 15. The opening balance is the prior interval's balance minus one monthly decay point, floored at 0; the monthly decay point is removed at the start of each month-close run, before the closing incidents are scored. So a balance at the ceiling of 15 decays to 14 at a rollover, and a balance at 0 stays at 0.

### 2.10. Instrument Lives and the Exhaustion Surcharge

Accessory and instrument consumption is tracked as a countdown against the removable accessory set the configuration supports. The remaining lives are the configuration's `max_instrument_lives` minus the sum of the approved loads of every procedure case on the agreement's systems whose `case_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`. The accumulator is floored at 0 and never goes negative. Cancelling a case releases its load back to the accumulator. The only action that raises the count is provisioning a replacement accessory set, which resets the remaining lives to the configuration's `max_instrument_lives`.

When a case is scheduled, the instrument load it asks for (`instrument_load`) may exceed what the accessory set can still support. The load is therefore clamped: the approved load is the smaller of the attempted load and the accessory set's remaining lives at booking, and it is never above the remaining lives. If clamping leaves a requested load of zero where the caller asked for more, the request is rejected with `ERR_INSTRUMENT_LIVES_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409). A case whose approved load is zero never triggers the exhaustion threshold.

The exhaustion surcharge is a threshold rule evaluated on the same computation. Take the base billing amount for the case, subtract the case's approved instrument load from the accessory set's surviving lives, and call the result the lives remaining. When the lives remaining is at or below zero, the billing amount becomes the base amount plus a surcharge of 45000 cents, and the accessory set is marked `EXHAUSTED`. Otherwise the billing amount stays at the base amount and the accessory set is marked `AVAILABLE`. The surcharge is charged at exactly zero lives remaining — the current load may consume the last life — and on every case after that, until a replacement accessory set is provisioned. This is also exposed as a manual posting and a manual reset on the system.

### 2.11. Utilisation Banding

Each robot system owns a utilization cycle, a reporting band that mirrors one commitment interval of its parent agreement. A cycle's `cycle_end_utc` is its `cycle_start_utc` plus 30 days. Two counts live on it: `scheduled_case_count` counts procedure cases on the system whose `case_started_at` falls inside the half-open interval from `cycle_start_utc` inclusive to `cycle_end_utc` exclusive, in any non-cancelled status; `completed_case_count` counts only those with `case_status` `COMPLETED` in the same window.

The `utilization_band` is derived from `completed_case_count` alone, and only `COMPLETED` rows ever move it — `SCHEDULED` rows count toward the scheduled count but never toward the band. The band is `UNDERUTILIZED` when the completed count is 7 or fewer, `BASELINE` from 8 through 14 inclusive, `HIGH` from 15 through 21 inclusive, and `EXPANSION_READY` at 22 or more. Both lower edges are inclusive where it matters: a count of exactly 8 is `BASELINE` and not `UNDERUTILIZED`, and a count of exactly 22 is `EXPANSION_READY` and not `HIGH`.

A cycle also tracks `same_hour_conflict_count`, the number of other non-cancelled cases on the same system whose occupancy interval — the case's `case_started_at` through its start plus `duration_minutes` plus a 30-minute turn-over buffer, half-open — overlaps this cycle's own scheduled case intervals. And it carries `reporting_period_index`, initialized to 0 and advanced by one at each month-close run; that index drives the eligibility window for expansion reviews.

### 2.12. The Commitment Ledger: Fees, Shortfalls and Compliance Tiers

A placement agreement carries a per-interval utilization commitment. The commitment is `minimum_procedures_per_cycle` procedures per interval of `interval_months` months, with an interval length of 1 month and a committed count of 0 as the defaults; a commitment of 0 disables the commitment entirely and with it the shortfall fee.

The count of procedures actually delivered in the open interval is `committed_cases`, computed over procedure case rows whose `case_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` and whose `case_started_at` falls in the half-open interval from `cycle_start_utc` inclusive to `cycle_end_utc` exclusive. Note that a case that is merely `SCHEDULED` and never delivered still counts toward the commitment in this view; only `CANCELLED` rows are excluded.

At an interval rollover, a shortfall fee is computed for every agreement whose arrangement is `USAGE_BASED` and whose `minimum_procedures_per_cycle` is at least 1. First the shortfall is the committed minimum minus the delivered count, floored at 0. If the agreement's shortfall has been waived for the interval, the fee is 0. Otherwise, if the shortfall is 0 the fee is 0. Otherwise, if the shortfall is 2 or fewer procedures the fee is 0 as well — the grace band is inclusive. Only beyond that does a fee accrue: the fee is the shortfall beyond the grace of 2, multiplied by the agreement's `placement_fee_cents`, multiplied by a shortfall rate of 200 basis points, and the product is divided by 10000 and rounded half-up to a whole number of cents. A commitment of 10 with a shortfall of exactly 2 therefore yields no fee, while a shortfall of 3 yields one procedure's worth of the placement fee at 2 percent. The shortfall itself is recorded as `shortfall_procedures`, the fee as `last_shortfall_fee_cents`.

The compliance status that the commitment produces is a tiered function of the shortfall streak. Let the tier be the closed-cycle shortfall streak multiplied by the agreement's `placement_fee_cents`, divided by a divisor of 1000000, rounded half-up to a whole number. The streak is the count of consecutive prior closed intervals in which `shortfall_procedures` was greater than 0. The resulting `compliance_status` runs `MEETS_COMMITMENT → SHORTFALL_WARNING → SHORTFALL_CHARGEABLE → NON_COMPLIANT → REMEDIATION_REQUIRED → MEETS_COMMITMENT`. `MEETS_COMMITMENT` applies when the current interval's shortfall is 0 or has been waived. `SHORTFALL_WARNING` applies when the tier is 10 or below. `SHORTFALL_CHARGEABLE` applies when the tier is above 10 and at or below 25. `NON_COMPLIANT` applies when the tier exceeds 25. `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` applies when the same agreement occupies a tier above 25 at two consecutive month-close runs. The upper edge of the warning band is strict: a tier of exactly 10 is `SHORTFALL_WARNING`, and a tier of 11 is `SHORTFALL_CHARGEABLE`.

A waiver is recorded on the agreement as `is_shortfall_waived`, set when an operator waives a shortfall and reset at each rollover. While set, it prevents the compliance cascade for the current interval. It also carries a `last_shortfall_fee_cents` that is 0 whenever the interval met its commitment or the shortfall was waived.

### 2.13. Escalation of Chronic Shortfalls

Beyond the compliance tier, a separate escalation rule runs at each month-close over the agreement's streak of consecutive prior closed intervals with a shortfall. It is evaluated in a pinned order. The escalation tier is the largest integer `t` for which 2 raised to the power `t`, taken modulo 7, is not equal to 1, examined with `t` ascending. That tier then selects a period quota: tier 0 gives a quota of 0, tier 1 gives 1, tier 2 gives 2, and tier 3 gives 4. The escalation is effective when the period quota is greater than 0 and the reporting period index of the agreement's current utilization cycle, taken modulo the period quota, equals 0. When the escalation is effective, the agreement is driven to `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` at that run regardless of its tier.

The ladder's first step is the trap. Since 2 raised to the power 3 is 8 and 8 modulo 7 is 1, the ladder stops at tier 2, and the period quota is 2. It is never 4, no matter how long the streak runs. So a chronic agreement escalates to `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` at every second reporting period once its streak reaches two intervals, and never at every fourth.

### 2.14. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write operations require an expected-version field, and each is rejected if the version the client supplies does not match the parent's current `version`.

Accepting a placement agreement requires `expected_hospital_account_version` alongside the agreement's own version field, and the account whose version it names is the `HospitalAccount` that owns the agreement. If the account's version has advanced since the client last observed it, the acceptance is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The accept path gates on the account's posture and its service-line tier, so a client holding a stale account view is holding a stale posture.

Opening an expansion review requires `expected_hospital_account_version` naming the reviewed `HospitalAccount`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The eligible-system selector reads the account's systems and their service priorities, so a stale account view is a stale candidate set.

Creating a service contract requires `expected_robot_system_version` naming the covered `RobotSystem`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The contract's start instant must fall on or after the system's installation instant, so a stale view of the installation state is not admissible.

These three are the complete set of aggregate guards. Separately from them, and applying much more widely, every state-transition endpoint that acts on a specific resource by its identifier requires the caller to supply that resource's own current version in the body, named for the resource in question: `expected_account_version`, `expected_review_version`, `expected_agreement_version`, `expected_robot_system_version`, `expected_deployment_version`, `expected_case_version`, `expected_training_record_version`, `expected_evidence_version`, `expected_contract_version`, `expected_incident_version`, `expected_dispatch_version`, `expected_transfer_version` and `expected_ledger_version`. There is no other endpoint in §4 that takes an expected-parent-version field for concurrency purposes.

### 2.15. Monetary Values and Precision

All monetary values in this API are integer cents. Final amounts are rounded half-up to a whole number of cents, and where a computation involves several steps the rounding is applied once, at the end, on the whole amount rather than at intermediate steps. Where a scaffold rule specifies a rate as a fraction — the volume discount ladder and the shortfall rate — the fraction is carried through the arithmetic at full precision and only the final amount is rounded. Configuration values that are rates or measured quantities rather than settled amounts — `operating_cost_per_minute`-style rate fields, the dimensional minimums, the tolerances — preserve their input precision and are not rounded.

The one non-monetary precision rule concerns the expansion status ratio, which is carried to four decimal places. Counts — case counts, procedure units, penalty points, instrument lives, tier indexes — are plain integers.
## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Account Establishment, Site Qualification and Commercial Agreements

#### 3.1.1. Opening a Hospital System and Clearing the Site

A `RegionalSalesDirector` opens a hospital system by supplying its `account_name`, its `idn_group_code`, its `region_code`, an optional `placement_cap` and an optional `maximum_procedure_commitment`. The account is born in `PROSPECT`, `hospital_owner_user_id` is stamped from the caller's `X-User-ID`, and its `account_posture` computes to `ONBOARDING_GATE` — there is no readiness review on file yet, and that alone is enough to hold the gate closed. The `idn_group_code` matters well beyond identification: systems sharing the code share a commitment pool, and the code is what later lets committed-procedure credit move between member accounts and what scales the volume-discount ladder. The `placement_cap` caps how many concurrently non-terminal placement agreements the account may hold, and a cap of 0 means there is no cap at all.

Before any commercial paperwork can move, the account needs at least one site-readiness review and at least one surgeon training record on file; without them the move from `PROSPECT` to `READINESS` fails with `ERR_READINESS_REVIEW_REQUIRED` and `ERR_TRAINING_PATH_UNINITIALIZED`.

A `ClinicalOperationsLead` opens the site-readiness review against a specific site and a specific system configuration. The `site_code` identifiers the site and must be unique within the account, so a second review reusing a `site_code` already in use fails with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SITE_CODE` (HTTP 409). The review is measured against the configuration named by `evaluated_configuration_id`, and the caller submits the four clear measurements — `operating_room_length_cm`, `operating_room_width_cm`, `table_throw_radius_cm` and `ceiling_clearance_cm` — together with the two integration facts, `has_dedicated_power_circuit` and `has_dark_fiber_run`. The review opens in `PENDING_REVIEW` at `review_round` 1, with its conditional clearance count and its construction uplift already computed.

The configuration being measured against is a manufacturer catalog entry, created by a `PlatformAdmin`, and it carries both commercial and physical attributes. Physically it declares `min_operating_room_length_cm`, `min_operating_room_width_cm`, `min_table_throw_radius_cm` and `min_ceiling_clearance_cm`, plus `integration_tier` (a depth from 1 to 3), `requires_dark_fiber` and `requires_dedicated_power_circuit`. Commercially it declares `base_placement_fee_cents`, `max_instrument_lives`, and a `feature_entitlements` array pairing each `feature_code` with the `min_release_index` at which it unlocks. It also names its `model_code`, drawn from the closed set `DA_V1`, `DA_V2`, `SP_V1`, `SP_V2`, `SP_V3`, and its own lifecycle is carried on a field also called `status`, which runs from `ACTIVE` to `RETIRED`. Creating one that demands a dedicated power circuit or a dark fiber run the target site cannot supply is rejected with `ERR_POWER_CIRCUIT_MISSING` or `ERR_NETWORK_INTEGRATION_MISSING`, both HTTP 422.

Reading a verdict is where the site is actually accepted or held. A `ClinicalOperationsLead` submits a `verdict` against the review, supplying `expected_review_version`. A clean pass requires every dimension and the throw radius to meet or exceed the configuration's minimums and requires the conditional clearance count to be zero; a single measurement short of its minimum fails the submission with `ERR_ROOM_REQUIREMENTS_UNMET` (HTTP 422), and any outstanding conditional clearance fails a pass attempt with `ERR_CONDITIONAL_CLEARANCE_REQUIRES_REVIEW` (HTTP 422) while still leaving a `CONDITIONAL` verdict available. Either verdict requires the power circuit when the configuration demands it and the fiber run when the configuration demands it, and neither may proceed against an account whose posture is `EXPANSION_BLOCKED`.

A `CONDITIONAL` verdict is the middle path: it is the right answer when at least one dimensional or integration check missed exact match but stayed inside the configuration's conditional tolerance band. That band is where a measurement sits at or above the configuration minimum but below the minimum plus 5 cm; the count of such clearances is exposed as `conditional_clearance_count`, and it is what a construction crew later has to close out. A review may be lifted from `CONDITIONAL` to `PASSED` later, once its conditional clearance count has been driven to zero, and that lift shares the same posture guard.

Rejection goes the other way. A `ClinicalOperationsLead` may reject a review with a `remediation_note` describing what the site must fix; an empty or missing note fails with `ERR_REMEDIATION_NOTE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). The note is retained as `remediation_note` on the review. A rejected review is not dead: returning it to review moves it back to `PENDING_REVIEW`, advances `review_round` by one so the revision history is legible, and recomputes the conditional clearance count against the configuration. Re-measuring a single dimensional check does not change the review's status at all, but it does recompute the clearance count and bump the review's version.

One rejection path has no caller at all: if the configuration a review was evaluated against is `RETIRED`, the review is automatically moved to `REJECTED`.

The construction uplift quoted on the review is what the hospital will be charged if it proceeds without closing the dimensional gaps, and it is deliberately progressive. Count the four dimensional checks whose submitted values fall below the configuration minimums, and add to that the count of those same checks whose values sit at or above the minimum but below the minimum plus 5 cm — the two categories together are the uplift units. If there are no uplift units, the `construction_uplift_fee_cents` is zero. Otherwise the fee is 304800 cents for each uplift unit, plus a further 76200 cents for each unit beyond the first, so the second unit and every unit after it costs more than the first: two units cost 685800 cents rather than 609600.

#### 3.1.2. Placement Agreements and the Fee That Shrinks

Commercial placement begins with a `RegionalSalesDirector` drafting a placement agreement against an account, a configuration, and the specific readiness review that authorized the placement. The agreement names its `arrangement_type` — `PURCHASE`, `LEASE` or `USAGE_BASED` — alongside `base_monthly_fee_cents`, the fixed monthly instrument-and-accessory fee that applies under every arrangement. It also carries the commitment terms `minimum_procedures_per_cycle` and `interval_months`, and the arrangement-specific commercial terms: `placement_fee_cents` for a purchase, `lease_monthly_amount_cents` for a lease, and `usage_rate_basis_points` for a usage-based arrangement. The agreement opens in `DRAFT` with `compliance_status` `MEETS_COMMITMENT`, and no system, contract or cycle exists yet.

Submitting the agreement moves it to `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE` and computes its placement fee, which is a forward-looking number evaluated in a pinned order. First form a dimensional uplift by taking the readiness review's `conditional_clearance_count`, dividing it by 2, rounding that quotient up to a whole number, and multiplying by 150000 cents. Second form an integration uplift by multiplying the configuration's `integration_tier` by 250000 cents. Third add the configuration's `base_placement_fee_cents` to those two uplifts to get the gross fee. Fourth count the `idn_scale`: the number of hospital accounts sharing the same `idn_group_code` whose `account_status` is `ACTIVE` or `DORMANT`. Fifth derive the volume discount rate as 0.02 multiplied by the `idn_scale`, capped at 0.04 — so two members of an IDN earn a 4 percent ladder, one member earns 2 percent, and a lone account earns nothing. Sixth multiply the gross fee by that rate to get the discount. Seventh subtract the discount from the gross fee and round half-up to whole cents.

The value of that computation depends on when it is evaluated, and this is the crux of the arrangement's commercial life. At submit the ladder is read while the submitting account is still `CONTRACTED`, so the account does not yet count toward its own `idn_scale` — a single-site IDN is placed at a volume discount rate of exactly 0.00 and the number written to `placement_fee_cents` is the full gross fee that contracts and purchase orders are cut against. At activation the ladder is re-evaluated, and the account has by then become `ACTIVE`, so it now counts 1 toward `idn_scale`, the rate moves to 0.02, and the same agreement re-prices strictly lower. That lower figure is what is actually invoiced, and it is stored separately as `net_placement_fee_cents`. The list fee is retained on the resource; the net fee is null until the agreement reaches `ACTIVE`.

Submission is guarded. The agreement must still be `DRAFT` (`ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_IN_ELIGIBLE_STATE`, HTTP 409, checked first). The readiness review it references must be `PASSED`, failing which the submission is rejected with `ERR_READINESS_REVIEW_NOT_PASSED` (HTTP 409). The account's posture must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_ACCOUNT_POSTURE_GATE`). The arrangement's own commercial term must be present — a `PURCHASE` without `placement_fee_cents`, a `LEASE` without `lease_monthly_amount_cents`, or a `USAGE_BASED` arrangement without `usage_rate_basis_points` is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_TERMS_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422). And the submission may not push the account's count of non-terminal agreements above its `placement_cap`, whenever that cap is at least 1 — a breach is `ERR_PLACEMENT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402). The interface also exposes a generic `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) for a body that is missing a required field, unparseable, or of the wrong JSON type, and a `ERR_REFERENTIAL_INTEGRITY_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409) when a referenced parent row was deleted, has reached a terminal state, or belongs to a different hospital account.

Arrangements may be filtered on listing by their account, their configuration, the readiness review that authorized them, and their `agreement_status`.

#### 3.1.3. Acceptance, Activation and the Cascade That Mints a System

Acceptance is the hospital's side of the bargain and belongs to a `HospitalProcurementOfficer`. The agreement must be `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE` (`ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_IN_ELIGIBLE_STATE`, checked first), the logical clock must be strictly before `acceptance_expires_utc` — otherwise `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409) — and the account's posture must be `ACTIVE`. The `acceptance_expires_utc` was stamped when the agreement was submitted, at 30 days after that instant, and the window is exclusive: an acceptance arriving exactly at the expiry instant is already too late. If nobody accepts in time, the agreement returns to `DRAFT` automatically and `acceptance_expires_utc` is cleared.

Acceptance is more than a status change, because it is the moment a physical machine is promised. On success the agreement moves to `ACTIVE`; `activated_at_utc` and `cycle_start_utc` are stamped from the logical clock; and `cycle_end_utc` becomes `cycle_start_utc` plus `interval_months` multiplied by 30 days. A `RobotSystem` is synthesized in `service_status` `NOT_INSTALLED`, serial-numbered from the agreement, and its identifier is recorded on the agreement as `synthesized_system_id` with `synthesized_system_version` always 1 immediately after synthesis. A `ServiceContract` is synthesized at the same moment in `contract_status` `ACTIVE` with `coverage_tier` `COMPREHENSIVE` and a one-month `contract_months`; its identifier is recorded as `synthesized_service_contract_id`. Both children are reachable by listing the agreement's robot systems and the system's service contracts respectively. The `net_placement_fee_cents` is computed under the write-down variant described above, the posture is recomputed, and the commitment state is recomputed for the newly active agreement. The agreement's version and the account's version both increment.

The synthesized system's `serial_number` must be unique across every robot system in the fleet; a collision fails acceptance with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERIAL_NUMBER` (HTTP 409).

Acceptance also takes the pinned pair of version guards described in §2.14: `expected_agreement_version` for the agreement itself and `expected_hospital_account_version` for the owning account.

An agreement that has gone wrong can be suspended, but only for cause. A `PlatformAdmin` suspends an `ACTIVE` agreement only when its `compliance_status` is `NON_COMPLIANT`; any other compliance state yields `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_SUSPENDABLE` (HTTP 409). Suspension is not a quiet status flip: every robot system on the agreement is pushed to `SERVICE_ACTION_REQUIRED`, and any loaner dispatch that is `PROGRAMMED` or `ACTIVATED` and covers one of those systems is moved to `RECLAIMED`, releasing its loaner unit for other dispatches to claim. Because the systems are now in service-action-required, the account's posture recomputes to `EXPANSION_BLOCKED`.

Reinstatement unwinds that, but the coverage has to be live first. A `RegionalSalesDirector` may reinstate a `SUSPENDED` agreement only when every `ACTIVE` service contract on its systems has the logical clock strictly before its `contract_end_utc`; an expired or otherwise out-of-window contract fails the reinstatement with `ERR_CONTRACT_TEMPORALLY_INVALID`. On success the agreement returns to `ACTIVE`, every system of the agreement currently in `SERVICE_ACTION_REQUIRED` returns to `IN_SERVICE`, and the commitment state is recomputed.

Rejection is the hospital backing out. A `HospitalProcurementOfficer` may reject an agreement in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE` or `ACTIVE`, returning it to `DRAFT`; a rejection from `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE` also clears `acceptance_expires_utc`, and a rejection from `ACTIVE` clears both `cycle_start_utc` and `activated_at_utc` back to null.

Termination is the end of the relationship and it cascades hard. A `PlatformAdmin` may terminate an agreement in `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`; anything else is `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_TERMINABLE` (HTTP 409, checked first). The cascade has three hops and the hops are causal, not independent. First, every non-terminal procedure case on the agreement's systems is cancelled. Then every robot system on the agreement is moved to `DECOMMISSIONED`. Then, because each of those systems has left service, every `ACTIVE` service contract on them is terminated — and once a contract is gone, its response-time commitment is no longer evaluated for that system at all. Then, because those contracts have ended, every incident on those systems that is `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` is resolved. Finally, every `PROGRAMMED` or `ACTIVATED` loaner dispatch on the agreement's systems is cancelled, releasing its loaner unit. The posture is recomputed and the agreement's version increments.

### 3.2. The Physical System: Installation, Commissioning and Withdrawal

A robot system, once minted by acceptance, runs `NOT_INSTALLED → INSTALLED → IN_SERVICE → SERVICE_ACTION_REQUIRED → IN_SERVICE`, with a final `IN_SERVICE → DECOMMISSIONED`. It carries its `serial_number`, its `hospital_account_id`, its `system_configuration_id`, the `placement_agreement_id` that synthesized it and the `site_readiness_review_id` it was installed against. Its derived state includes `installed_software_release_index`, which starts at 0 and is raised only by a completed software deployment; the `entitled_feature_codes` the installed release unlocks; `instrument_lives_remaining`; and an `expansion_eligible` flag whose computation is described in §3.7.

Installation belongs to a `ClinicalOperationsLead`. A system may only be installed from `NOT_INSTALLED`, or the request fails with `ERR_SYSTEM_STATUS_INVALID` (HTTP 409, checked first). Three further conditions must hold. The referenced readiness review must be `PASSED`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_SYSTEM_SITE_NOT_READY` (HTTP 409) — a `CONDITIONAL` review is not good enough to install against, no matter how small the outstanding clearance. The account's posture must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_ACCOUNT_POSTURE_GATE`). And the parent agreement's commitment interval must be live: the logical clock must sit inside the half-open interval from `cycle_start_utc` inclusive to `cycle_end_utc` exclusive, or the cycle must not yet have been closed. A system whose agreement has never had its cycle stamped fails with `ERR_COMMITMENT_CYCLE_NOT_STARTED` (HTTP 409).

Commissioning is what actually puts a machine into clinical service, and it is also the moment the reporting clock starts. A `ClinicalOperationsLead` commissions an `INSTALLED` system, which requires at least one `CREDENTIALED` surgeon training record on the account (`ERR_NO_CREDENTIALED_SURGEON`, HTTP 409) — a hospital with no credentialed operator cannot be allowed to start operating — and an `ACTIVE` service contract on the system (`ERR_SERVICE_CONTRACT_REQUIRED`, HTTP 409). On success the system moves to `IN_SERVICE`. A `UtilizationCycle` is opened for the system's current commitment interval: if none exists for that interval it is created, and if one does exist it is reused as the open cycle rather than duplicated. Its identifier is stamped onto the system as `current_utilization_cycle_id`, and the band is initialized to `UNDERUTILIZED`, which is where an empty cycle naturally falls. The response carries the system's `service_status`, the new `current_utilization_cycle_id`, the `instrument_lives_remaining` and the `expansion_eligible` flag, so a client can chain immediately into case scheduling.

A system in service can be taken out of it when something breaks or when it needs attention. A `ServiceEngineer` invokes the service-action capability on an `IN_SERVICE` system, which moves it to `SERVICE_ACTION_REQUIRED`. That single change has three consequences that ripple across entities. Because the account now has a system in service-action-required, its posture recomputes to `EXPANSION_BLOCKED`. Because the posture is now blocked, every `ACTIVE` placement agreement on that system whose `compliance_status` is `NON_COMPLIANT` is suspended. And a reclaim wave named for expired coverage leaves every `PROGRAMMED` or `ACTIVATED` loaner dispatch on that system `RECLAIMED`. The system's `expansion_eligible` flag is recomputed, and the agreement's version increments alongside the system's.

Coming back is more demanding than going out. Restoring a system to service requires it to currently be in `SERVICE_ACTION_REQUIRED` (`ERR_SYSTEM_STATUS_INVALID`, checked first); requires every incident on the system whose `request_status` is not `RESOLVED` to be resolved, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_SERVICE_ACTION_UNRESOLVED` (HTTP 409); and requires an `ACTIVE` service contract on the system with the logical clock strictly before its `contract_end_utc`, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_SERVICE_CONTRACT_REQUIRED`. On success the system returns to `IN_SERVICE` and the posture recomputes — which, if no other system is in service-action-required, lets the account out of `EXPANSION_BLOCKED`.

A `PlatformAdmin` may decommission a system from either `IN_SERVICE` or `INSTALLED`; any other status fails with `ERR_SYSTEM_STATUS_INVALID`. Decommissioning requires zero non-terminal procedure cases on the system, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_SYSTEM_HAS_OPEN_CASES` (HTTP 409). On success the system moves to `DECOMMISSIONED`, every `ACTIVE` service contract on it is terminated, and the posture recomputes. Note the asymmetry with termination of an agreement: an agreement termination cancels the open cases and then decommissions, while a direct decommission insists the cases are already gone.

Systems may be listed fleet-wide, by account, or by the agreement that placed them, filtered by service status and by the configuration or site review they belong to. A single system is retrievable by identifier, and carries its `service_status`, its `current_utilization_cycle_id`, its `instrument_lives_remaining`, its `installed_software_release_index`, its `entitled_feature_codes` and its `expansion_eligible` flag.

### 3.3. Coverage, Response Commitments, Incidents and Loaners

Coverage is contracted per system by a `ServiceEngineer`. The contract names the `robot_system_id`, a `coverage_tier`, a `contract_start_utc` and a `contract_months`, and it initializes to `contract_status` `ACTIVE`. Its `contract_end_utc` is the start instant advanced by `contract_months` multiplied by 30 days. From the tier come `service_tier_index` and `response_time_commitment_hours`, computed by the pinned clamp order given in §2.8 — the floor at 4 hours is applied before the ceiling at 24, which is what pulls the `COMPREHENSIVE` branch down from 36 hours to 24. A tier outside the recognized set is rejected with `ERR_CONTRACT_TIER_UNRECOGNIZED` (HTTP 422). Contract creation is one of the three operations carrying a parent-version guard, and it requires `expected_robot_system_version`; a stale system version is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Creating it also increments the system's version, because the coverage relationship is part of the system's state.

The temporal validity rule bites twice. A contract whose `contract_start_utc` precedes the system's installation instant, or whose end is not strictly later than its start, is rejected with `ERR_CONTRACT_TEMPORALLY_INVALID` (HTTP 422) at creation, and the same code is raised when an agreement reinstatement is attempted while a contract on its systems has fallen out of window.

Contracts end in one of two ways. A month-close run expires any `ACTIVE` contract whose `contract_end_utc` the logical clock has reached or passed, and that expiry triggers a reclaim wave — every `PROGRAMMED` or `ACTIVATED` loaner dispatch on that system is left `RECLAIMED`, on the reasoning that a system whose coverage has lapsed cannot be carrying a manufacturer-owned loaner unit. Alternatively, a `ServiceEngineer` terminates an `ACTIVE` contract explicitly; the contract must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_SERVICE_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`, checked first), and the termination resolves every incident on the system that is `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`.

Incidents are the operational counterpart. A `ServiceEngineer` files one with the `robot_system_id` and the `incident_started_at`, the instant the failure interrupted serviceable operation. The request opens at `OPEN`; its severity derives from the system's posture and coverage at filing, as `CRITICAL` when the system is in `SERVICE_ACTION_REQUIRED`, `MAJOR` when it is `IN_SERVICE` but the account's posture is not `ACTIVE`, and `MINOR` otherwise. Its `response_due_at_utc` is the incident start advanced by the response-time commitment of the active coverage contract. An incident may be acknowledged, which stops the response clock, or resolved, which scores a penalty and refreshes the account's penalty balance; resolution may carry optional `resolution_notes`. The penalty scale, its grace and escalation bands, and the clamping and monthly decay of the balance are specified in §2.9. The `resolved_at_utc` is stamped from the logical clock at resolution and is null while the request is `OPEN`.

Incidents may be listed fleet-wide, by system, filtered by status. The `resolution_notes` field is the engineer's free-text record of what was done.

Loaner units are the manufacturer's answer to a system being down for longer than a shift. A `ServiceEngineer` programs a dispatch naming the displaced `robot_system_id`, the `loaner_serial_number`, and a `dispatch_window_start_utc` and `dispatch_window_end_utc`. The dispatch opens at `PROGRAMMED` and runs from `PROGRAMMED` through `ACTIVATED` to one of `RETURNED`, `CANCELLED` or `RECLAIMED`. That lifecycle is carried on the dispatch's `reservation_status`, so a listing filtered by `reservation_status` returns exactly the units in a given reservation phase. The window is guarded by half-open interval arithmetic with a mandatory buffer: the occupied interval is the dispatch start through the dispatch start plus 14 days plus 4 hours, and a candidate dispatch on a given loaner unit is rejected with `ERR_LOANER_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409) when that buffered interval overlaps any non-terminal dispatch on the same `loaner_serial_number`. Overlap means the candidate begins before the existing buffered end and the candidate's buffered end falls after the existing start. Adjacency is permitted: a candidate starting exactly at an existing buffered end is not an overlap, because the interval is half-open, while a candidate intruding by a single minute is.

The declared window must also be coherent: `dispatch_window_end_utc` must be strictly later than `dispatch_window_start_utc`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (HTTP 400). A dispatch is moved from `PROGRAMMED` to `ACTIVATED` by an explicit activation. Presenting the window end separately from the buffered occupancy is deliberate: the buffer extends the protected interval beyond the stated return date, and the practical borrowing accommodation adds 3 further days of unreturned hold beyond the 14-day window before the unit is reclaimed.

Two reclaim paths exist with distinct names. The `CONTRACT_EXPIRED` wave fires when a contract expires or when a system is placed into service-action-required, and it leaves dispatches `RECLAIMED`. The `DEFERRED_URGENT` and `CONTRACT_EXPIRED` reasons may be recorded on a dispatch as `reclaim_reason` when a higher-priority dispatch takes over a window, and a dispatch that is cancelled or reclaimed releases its unit for other dispatches to claim.

### 3.4. Scheduling a Case, Occupancy and the Instrument Bill

Bookings belong to the account acting as first party. An `AccountOwner` schedules a procedure case on a robot system, naming the `robot_system_id` in the path, the `training_record_id` of the credentialed operator, the `procedure_family`, the `case_started_at` and the `duration_minutes`, with an optional `instrument_load` and an optional `is_emergency` flag. The attempted load is stored as submitted and the load actually authorized is recorded separately as `approved_instrument_load`; the amount the case will bill is carried as `instrument_billing_cents`, and the case's own identifier is its `id`. The `procedure_family` is drawn from the closed set `UROLOGY`, `GYNECOLOGY`, `GENERAL_SURGERY`, `THORACIC`, `COLORECTAL`. The case opens at `case_status` `SCHEDULED`, with `hospital_account_id` and `placement_agreement_id` inherited from the system, and its `commitment_cycle_index` stamped from the logical clock at creation so it is attributed to the interval it was booked into. Its occupancy interval is the case start through the case start plus `duration_minutes` plus a 30-minute turn-over buffer.

Occupancy is exclusive on a system. The conflict rule is evaluated in a pinned order: compute the candidate's buffered occupancy, then reject the booking when that occupancy overlaps the buffered occupancy of any non-cancelled case on the same `robot_system_id`, using the sibling's own `duration_minutes` and the same 30-minute buffer. A violation is `ERR_CASE_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Adjacency is not a conflict — a case starting exactly at a sibling's buffered end is accepted, because the intervals are half-open. Rescheduling a case moves it to a new `case_started_at` with an optional new `duration_minutes`, and a case may always be rescheduled against its own freed slot: the candidate occupancy replaces the case's own prior occupancy rather than colliding with it.

The case's operator must be legitimate. The named training record is evaluated against the credential-validity step of the training path described in §3.5; a record that is not credentialed with an unexpired evidence grace yields `ERR_SURGEON_NOT_CREDENTIALED` (HTTP 409) at booking and at every subsequent mutation. The account's ownership of the system is enforced too, and a mismatch is `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

Instrument load is clamped at booking rather than refused outright. The caller's `instrument_load` is an attempted figure; the load actually authorized is the smaller of the attempted load and the accessory set's remaining lives at booking, and it is stored as `approved_instrument_load`. It is never above the remaining lives. If the requested load is above zero but clamping leaves nothing available, the booking is rejected with `ERR_INSTRUMENT_LIVES_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409). A case whose approved load is zero never triggers the exhaustion threshold; the surcharge rule that rides on this computation is specified in §2.10.

Billing is where the arrangement type finally matters, and it is evaluated in a pinned order whose order is the specification. If the parent agreement's `arrangement_type` is `PURCHASE`, the amount is the approved instrument-and-accessory load priced at 125000 cents per whole load. If it is `LEASE`, the load is already covered by the lease's `lease_monthly_amount_cents` and the amount is zero. If it is `USAGE_BASED`, the amount is the agreement's `placement_fee_cents` multiplied by its `usage_rate_basis_points`, divided by 10000, and rounded half-up to whole cents — and this per-procedure rate does not vary with the load at all. Two overrides then apply, and they override everything above. A case whose `case_status` is not `COMPLETED` is never billable, so a `SCHEDULED` or `CANCELLED` case reports zero. And a case on a system whose `service_status` is `SERVICE_ACTION_REQUIRED` is never billable, so it reports zero as well. Rounding is applied once, at the end, on the whole amount.

The two-variant structure here is the subtlety. At booking the figure reported is a quote computed from the attempted load, before clamping, and it exists so the hospital's scheduling paperwork can be produced; a case whose attempted load of 3 is clamped to 1 therefore quotes 375000 cents while its eventual bill settles at 125000. At completion the figure is recomputed from the approved, clamped load actually consumed, and the case is billable only because it is now `COMPLETED`. The two numbers coincide only when the attempted load was within what the accessory set could support.

Completing a case is the operation that participants throughout §3 depend on. An `AccountOwner` marks a case completed by supplying `expected_case_version`; the case moves from `SCHEDULED` to `COMPLETED`, its billing is recomputed under the settle variant, the accessory set and instrument lives are reconciled, the ledger is refreshed, the account's service-line tier index is recomputed, the utilization band is recomputed, the account's utilization posture is refreshed, and the expansion status is recomputed. That single call increments four versions: the case's, the parent agreement's, the utilization cycle's, and the account's.

A case runs `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`, `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, and, once invoiced, `COMPLETED` advancing to `BILLED`; a cancelled case may also be returned to `SCHEDULED`. A case may be cancelled by either the `AccountOwner` or a `HospitalProcurementOfficer`, which moves it to `CANCELLED` and releases its instrument load back into the accessory-set accumulator. Bulk completion lets an `AccountOwner` submit a set of `case_ids` and receive a count of cases completed alongside the list of `skipped_case_ids`; each eligible case takes the full per-case completion side effects, while ineligible cases are skipped rather than rolled back, so a partially valid batch still makes progress. Naming a system outside the acting account's scope in that batch is `ERR_BULK_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

Cases are listable fleet-wide and per system, filterable by system, by account, by `case_status`, by a `case_started_from` and `case_started_to` instant range, by the agreement whose commitment they count toward, and by the training record that operated them.

The realization counts that roll up from completed cases are what §3.7 reads. The account's `service_line_tier` is the observed depth of its practice: count the distinct `procedure_family` values among the account's `COMPLETED` cases, subtract one to get the tier index, and clamp that index to the range 0 through 4 — so a single-family account sits at index 0, which is the tier `OBSERVATIONAL`, two families give index 1 `ESTABLISHED`, three give index 2 `DEVELOPING`, four give index 3 `ADVANCED`, and five or more saturate at index 4 `REFERENCE`. Because the index saturates, an account performing eight different procedure families reads the same `REFERENCE` tier as one performing five.

### 3.5. Surgeon Readiness, Credentialing Evidence and Software Entitlement

A surgeon's readiness is a monotone path with one deliberate retreat built into it. A `ClinicalOperationsLead` opens a surgeon training record on an account with a `surgeon_name`, a `required_proctored_cases` count, and an optional `simulation_modules_completed` figure defaulting to 0. The record opens at `training_status` `NOT_STARTED`, and its `surgeon_npi` must be unique within the account, so a duplicate fails with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SURGEON_NPI` (HTTP 409). Its `proctored_cases_completed` starts at 0 and its `proctor_approval_pending` flag starts false.

The path runs `NOT_STARTED → SIMULATION_COMPLETE → PROCTORING → CREDENTIALED`, with a `PROCTORING → SIMULATION_INCOMPLETE` retreat and a re-entry from `SIMULATION_INCOMPLETE` back to `SIMULATION_COMPLETE`. Completing simulation requires the record to be `NOT_STARTED` or `SIMULATION_INCOMPLETE` — anything else is `ERR_TRAINING_STATUS_INVALID` (HTTP 409, checked first) — and requires at least 3 simulation modules to have been completed, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_SIMULATION_MODULES_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422). Moving into proctoring requires the record to be `SIMULATION_COMPLETE` (checked first) and requires a `required_proctored_cases` figure of at least 1, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_PROCTORING_REQUIREMENT_MISSING` (HTTP 422) — a proctoring phase with no required cases is a contradiction and is refused. Beginning proctoring belongs to a `RegionalSalesDirector`.

Proctored cases are logged by a `ClinicalOperationsLead` against a proctoring record, each logging carrying a `case_observation_ref` and the `occurred_at_utc` of the observation. Logging one increments `proctored_cases_completed` and sets `proctor_approval_pending` to true, flagging that a compliance review is outstanding. The `ComplianceOfficer` then approves or rejects. Approval increments the completed count and, when the proctoring requirement is met, carries the record through to `CREDENTIALED`, stamping `credential_expires_utc` at the credential issue instant plus 365 days and `evidence_required_after_utc` at that expiry plus a 60-day grace. Approval requires the record to be in `PROCTORING` (checked first), requires the completed proctored cases to have reached `required_proctored_cases` — otherwise it fails with `ERR_PROCTORING_CASES_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422) — and requires at least one piece of credentialing evidence on the record whose `evidence_status` is `VALID` or `EXPIRING_SOON`, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_CREDENTIALING_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). It also carries the segregation-of-duties guard from §2.1. Rejection of a proctor case is the retreat: `proctored_cases_completed` is reset to 0, the status falls to `SIMULATION_INCOMPLETE`, and the pending flag is cleared.

Credentialing evidence is what the hospital holds to substantiate its own surgeons, and it is attached by the account itself. An `AccountOwner` files an evidence record naming an `evidence_type` — `CREDENTIAL_LETTER`, `PRIVILEGE_ATTESTATION` or `PROCTOR_SIGNOFF_SET` — with an `issued_utc` and an `expires_utc`. The evidence must be temporally coherent: `issued_utc` must not be later than the logical clock, and `expires_utc` must be strictly later than `issued_utc`; a violation is `ERR_EVIDENCE_WINDOW_INVALID` (HTTP 422), both on filing and on renewal. The record opens at `evidence_status` `VALID` or `EXPIRING_SOON` depending on how close the expiry is, and runs `VALID → EXPIRING_SOON → EXPIRED / REVOKED`.

A month-close run sweeps evidence: a record whose `expires_utc` is no more than 30 days after the logical clock is moved from `VALID` to `EXPIRING_SOON`, and a record whose expiry the logical clock has reached or passed moves to `EXPIRED` from either `VALID` or `EXPIRING_SOON`. A `ComplianceOfficer` may renew a record in `VALID`, `EXPIRING_SOON` or `EXPIRED` — statuses outside that set fail with `ERR_EVIDENCE_NOT_RENEWABLE` (HTTP 409, checked first) — supplying a fresh `issued_utc` and `expires_utc`, both of which must be strictly later than the logical clock and whose end must be strictly later than its start. Renewing a lapsed record revives it to `VALID`. Note that `EXPIRED` is renewable while `REVOKED` is not, the latter being the only status the renew path refuses. A `ComplianceOfficer` may revoke evidence from `VALID` or `EXPIRING_SOON`, moving it to `REVOKED`, and revoking is refused from `REVOKED` under the same not-renewable code.

A credential that has lapsed is returned to proctoring rather than silently extended. A `ClinicalOperationsLead` triggers re-evaluation when the record is `CREDENTIALED` (checked first) and either `credential_expires_utc` is earlier than the logical clock instant or the record carries at least one `REVOKED` evidence record; when neither condition holds the request is refused with `ERR_RECERTIFICATION_NOT_DUE` (HTTP 409). Re-evaluation resets `proctored_cases_completed` to 0 and clears both `credential_expires_utc` and `evidence_required_after_utc` back to null, dropping the surgeon back into proctoring for a fresh set of supervised cases.

The whole path feeds one eligibility evaluation, which the test-driving of expiry rules makes worth stating exactly. It is evaluated in a pinned order in which each step's output feeds the next. First, the surgeon is simulation-ready when `simulation_modules_completed` has reached at least 3. Second, proctoring-ready when simulation-ready and `required_proctored_cases` is at least 1. Third, proctoring-satisfied when proctoring-ready and `proctored_cases_completed` has reached at least `required_proctored_cases`. Fourth, credentialing-ready when proctoring-satisfied and at least one piece of evidence on the record has an `evidence_status` of `VALID` or `EXPIRING_SOON`. Fifth, credential-valid when credentialing-ready and the logical clock is at or before `evidence_required_after_utc` — the comparison is inclusive, because the evidence grace instant is itself valid. That 60-day post-expiry grace is what keeps a credential `PROVISIONALLY_VALID` for a further period after `credential_expires_utc` passes, and it is the reason a surgeon who is slightly late renewing evidence can still be scheduled while their paperwork is being refreshed. Sixth, case-eligible when credential-valid and the case's `procedure_family` is present among the account's already-`COMPLETED` procedure families, or the account's service-line tier index is below the activity floor of 2. That last gate only starts biting once the account has reached index 2 — an account at `OBSERVATIONAL` or `ESTABLISHED` may schedule outside its observed families, while a `DEVELOPING` account onward may not.

Software capability is a separate entitlement plane, layered on the system rather than the surgeon. A `PlatformAdmin` publishes a software release against a system configuration, naming a `release_index` — a monotonic ordinal within that configuration — and the `feature_codes` the release unlocks. The release carries a `deployed` flag that is set once a deployment of it completes. Separately, a `ServiceEngineer` programs a deployment of a release onto a system by naming the `software_release_id`, a `scheduled_window_start_utc` and a `scheduled_window_end_utc`; the deployment opens at `deployment_status` `PROGRAMMED` and advances through `PROGRAMMED`, `DELIVERED` and `VALIDATED` to `COMPLETED`, with an escape from `PROGRAMMED` to `CANCELLED`. Three temporal guards apply at programming time: the window end must not precede the window start, so a zero-length window is accepted but a negative one is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (HTTP 400); the window start must not precede the deployment's own creation instant, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_DEPLOYMENT_WINDOW_INVALID` (HTTP 422); and the release's `release_index` must be strictly greater than the target system's `installed_software_release_index`, failing which the deployment is refused with `ERR_RELEASE_DOWNGRADE` (HTTP 409). A further guard is structural: the release's `software_release_id` must target the system's own `system_configuration_id`, and a release built for a different configuration is rejected with `ERR_RELEASE_CONFIGURATION_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409).

Completion is where entitlement actually changes. A `ServiceEngineer` completes a deployment by supplying `expected_deployment_version`; the deployment moves to `COMPLETED`, the release is marked deployed, the target system's `installed_software_release_index` is raised to the delivered release's index, and the system's `entitled_feature_codes` is recomputed to the feature codes of its configuration whose `min_release_index` has been reached by that installed index. An `entitlement_snapshot` of those codes is recorded on the deployment itself so the entitlement state at delivery remains auditable after later releases move the system forward. The deployment's version and the system's version both increment.

### 3.6. Month-Close, Commitment Accounting and Compliance Cascades

The reporting period is closed by a single administrative run that a `PlatformAdmin` drives, optionally supplying an `as_of_utc` instant to close against. It is the widest-reaching operation in the system because it is the only one that sweeps time-dependent state across every entity at once. In one pass it re-bands every utilization cycle from the completed cases inside its window; advances each cycle's `reporting_period_index` by one; computes each agreement's shortfall fee and then drives that agreement's compliance cascade; recomputes each agreement's commitment state; decays the penalty balance and then recomputes it; returns agreements whose acceptance window has expired to `DRAFT`; expires contracts whose `contract_end_utc` the clock has reached; sweeps evidence to `EXPIRING_SOON` or `EXPIRED`; and detects accounts that have gone dormant.

The shortfall computation and its compliance tier are specified in §2.12, and the escalation ladder that can force `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` regardless of tier is specified in §2.13. Two further points about the run matter operationally. The first is that the run reads the commitment count over rows whose `case_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`, which means a case that was booked into the interval but never delivered still credits the commitment at the reconciliation view; a separate actual-at-billing view taken later, over only `COMPLETED` rows, re-opens the shortfall that the reconciliation view had credited. The second is that the provisional compliance view produced at a run ignores `is_shortfall_waived` entirely, so a waived interval still reports its pre-waiver tier at reconciliation time, while the authoritative report read after the waiver window closes treats a waived interval as contributing nothing to the streak and reporting `MEETS_COMMITMENT`.

A shortfall can be waived by a `ComplianceOfficer`, supplying a `reason` for the record. The waiver sets `is_shortfall_waived` to true, recomputes the compliance status, and recomputes the agreement's commitment state. It is reset at the next rollover, so a waiver forgives one interval and not a habit. The `reason` supplied is retained on the agreement so the concession is legible later.

The compliance status is not merely a label: `NON_COMPLIANT` is the sole precondition that makes an agreement suspendable, per §3.1. An agreement that is driven to `NON_COMPLIANT` by the tier ladder therefore becomes eligible for suspension at the next administrative decision, and an agreement driven to `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` by the escalation ladder has been placed on the strongest form of notice the system expresses. Both the tier ladder of §2.12 and the escalation ladder of §2.13 write into the same `compliance_status` field, and the escalation is deliberately able to override the tier.

The commitment state of an agreement is a derived reconciliation that is recomputed whenever anything that could change it moves: when the account reaches `ACTIVE`, when an agreement is accepted or reinstated, when a shortfall is waived, and at every month-close run. Where an agreement's derived commercial state is reconciled, the agreement's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` incremented, using the same rule that governs every derived field in §2.7.

Catalog pricing behaves the same way: when a system configuration is created or updated, its pricing derivations are recomputed, and updating a configuration increments its version. A configuration can be moved to `RETIRED`, and retirement is not inert — a readiness review still awaiting a verdict against a retired configuration is automatically rejected, per §3.1.

### 3.7. Utilization Reporting, Expansion Reviews and Capacity Allocation

Utilization is reported per system per commitment interval through the utilization cycle described in §2.11, and the band it produces is what the commercial side treats as the signal for whether a site deserves another platform. A cycle is retrievable on the system as its current cycle, and cycles are listable by system, by agreement, or across the fleet.

The band is the input to expansion eligibility, which is a three-part conjunction evaluated per system. A system is expansion-eligible when its `service_status` is `IN_SERVICE`, and its `ACTIVE` service contract carries a `service_tier_index` of at least 2 — that is, `STANDARD` or `COMPREHENSIVE` coverage but not `LIMITED` or `PARTS_ONLY` — and the completed-case count of its current utilization cycle has reached the parent agreement's `minimum_procedures_per_cycle`. A commitment of 0 makes that third condition vacuously true, so an account with no utilization commitment is expansion-eligible on service status and coverage tier alone. Eligibility is recomputed when a case completes and at every month-close run, so the flag a client reads is always the current reconciliation rather than a cached judgement.

An expansion review is how the account asks for growth, and it is opened by a `RegionalSalesDirector` against an account and the agreement whose interval is being reviewed, carrying a `support_request_ranking` and a `requested_case_capacity`. It is one of the three operations guarded by a parent-version check and requires `expected_hospital_account_version`, failing with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) when the account has moved on, because the eligible-system selector reads the account's systems and their service priorities and a stale account view is a stale candidate set. Opening a review also increments the account's version.

The review's `eligible_system_id` is selected deterministically from the account's systems whose `expansion_eligible` flag is true. The selector takes the candidate with the highest service priority; ties are broken by the earliest `created_at`, and never by identifier. The requester's own system is excluded from consideration, and the selected system's utilization is recorded on the review as `eligible_system_utilization`, being the completed-case count of that system in its current utilization cycle. The review also carries a `requires_prior_reporting_period` flag, set when the targeted agreement's reference utilization cycle sits one `reporting_period_index` behind the account's newest cycle — the signal that the review is being decided against a period that the account has already moved past.

Capacity allocation is not a greedy distribution and cannot be decomposed into independent per-system decisions. Support requests are binary rather than partial: each review round carries exactly one `support_request_ranking`, equal to its requested service-line tier index, and that round either clears every eligible system or clears none of them. The allocation is evaluated in a pinned order. The ranking is the requested service-line tier index. The supported system count is the number of eligible systems whose parent agreement's `minimum_procedures_per_cycle` is at or below the expansion volume target of 22 at the request instant. The available case capacity is that volume target multiplied by the supported system count. The allocated case capacity is the smaller of the `requested_case_capacity` and the available case capacity. The total allocated case capacity is the smaller of that allocation and the volume target multiplied by the eligible system count. And the unallocated case capacity is the volume target multiplied by the difference between the eligible system count and the supported system count, floored at 0. That final term is what prevents the allocation from being decomposed into independent per-system decisions, because capacity that no supported system can absorb still counts against the round.

The expansion status then falls out of a ratio. The cap ratio is the total allocated case capacity divided by the volume target multiplied by the count of eligible systems, floored at 0 and carried to four decimal places. The status is `CAPACITY_DEFERRED` when that ratio is below 1.0, and `EXPANSION_READY` when the ratio is exactly 1.0 and the unallocated case capacity is 0. Only reviews in `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED` status ever contribute to this computation; `DRAFT` and `REJECTED` rows never contribute. The expansion status is recomputed when a case completes and at every month-close run.

The review itself runs `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → APPROVED / REJECTED`, with a `REJECTED → DRAFT` revision loop. A `RegionalSalesDirector` or the `AccountOwner` submits a draft, supplying `expected_review_version`; a `ComplianceOfficer` approves or rejects a submitted review, stamping `decided_at_utc` from the logical clock in either case, and the rejection carries a `reason` that is retained on the review. Approval is subject to the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.1: the approving `ComplianceOfficer`'s `X-User-ID` may not equal the owning account's `hospital_owner_user_id`, on pain of `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). Approval also recomputes the account's posture, while rejection recomputes the expansion status. Reviews are listable by account and by agreement, filtered by `review_status`.

### 3.8. The IDN Credit Pool

Hospitals that belong to the same integrated delivery network can share committed-procedure credit between member sites, and that sharing is modelled as an explicit consumption ledger on each account plus a transfer transaction between them. An `IDNCoordinator` creates an account's ledger by supplying a `maximum_procedure_commitment` — the account-level procedure commitment across all its agreements, where 0 means the pool is wide open. The ledger opens with `consumed_procedure_units` and `supplied_procedure_units` both at 0, and its `remaining_credit_units` is derived as the `maximum_procedure_commitment` minus the consumed units minus the supplied units, floored at 0 so the accumulator never wraps negative.

A transfer moves credit from one account to another. An `IDNCoordinator` submits a transaction naming the `source_account_id`, the `destination_account_id`, the `procedure_units` being moved, and a `transfer_window_start_utc` and `transfer_window_end_utc` describing the window in which the credit is valid on the destination. The transaction opens at `transaction_status` `COMMITTED` and runs `COMMITTED → SETTLED / REVERSED`. On creation the source ledger's supplied units are increased by the transferred units and the destination ledger's consumed units are increased by the same amount, both ledgers' remaining credit is recomputed, the pool subscription is recomputed, and both ledger versions increment. The credit is attributed to the group through the ledger's `donor_idn_group_code`, which records the IDN group code of the account that supplied the credit. Every parent row referenced by the transaction must still exist, be non-terminal, and share the child's hospital account; a violation is `ERR_REFERENTIAL_INTEGRITY_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409).

The validity windows are closed date ranges, and the arithmetic is the opposite of the loaner rule in §3.3. Both endpoints of the transfer window are inclusive at day granularity, so the candidate window is the closed range from the start date through the end date inclusive. A candidate is rejected with `ERR_TRANSFER_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409) when any non-reversed transaction on the same `destination_account_id` has a closed window overlapping the candidate's, disregarding only gaps of 3 days or fewer. That grace is a merge provision rather than a tolerance: a candidate whose start date falls within 3 days after an existing window's inclusive end date is merged into that window instead of being rejected, and the merged window then spans both. A further capacity rule rides on the same transaction: the sum of the transferred units of the non-reversed transactions on the same `source_account_id` inside their validity windows may not exceed that source account's `maximum_procedure_commitment`, and a breach is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_UTILISATION_CREDIT` (HTTP 402). Credit cannot be sourced from an account that does not have it, and a source whose remaining credit units fall below the requested procedure units is refused on the same code.

The pool itself is conserved, and the conservation is stated as an explicit accounting identity rather than an emergent property. Two named caveats modulate the pool budget. Caveat A applies when the agreement's `placement_fee_cents` is exactly 0, and it is ignored whenever the agreement's committed cases in the current interval are greater than 0 — a zero placement fee is only a genuine anomaly if the agreement is also producing no volume. Caveat B applies when the report is read at a `reporting_period_index` greater than 2 and the ledger's consumed procedure units are greater than 0 at the same read. Given those, the pool budget is the account ledger's `maximum_procedure_commitment` when caveat A is inapplicable, and the surplus pool budget of 30 units when caveat A is applicable. The pool left is that budget minus the sum of the consumed units across the account's consumption ledgers, and it is unsigned — when it is negative the pool is over-consumed and the value must not wrap around. The units available to transfer are the smaller of the requested `procedure_units` and the pool left. The closing consumption is the sum of consumed units minus the sum of supplied units plus the sum of the transferred units of the non-reversed transactions on the account's `idn_group_code`. The aggregate utilization is the agreement's committed cases in its current interval plus the destination account's closing consumption, and the conserved quantity is that aggregate utilization minus the closing consumption multiplied by the unallocated case capacity — and that conserved quantity must be identical before and after every transfer, so that a reversal restores it exactly rather than merely closely. Finally the pool is saturated when the aggregate utilization has reached the ceiling of 25 units; reaching the ceiling blocks further transfers with `ERR_IDN_POOL_SATURATED` (HTTP 402).

Beyond the transfer path, a ledger's opening unused consumption can be seeded through a bootstrap decrement, which recomputes `consumed_procedure_units` and then the pool subscription, and requires `expected_ledger_version`. Note that the pool subscription rule and the transfer window rule both apply to this seeding operation as well, so a bootstrap that would push aggregate utilization to the 25-unit ceiling is refused like any other consumption. A committed transfer may be settled by an `IDNCoordinator` supplying `expected_transfer_version`, which moves it to `SETTLED` and stamps `settlement_certified_at_utc` from the logical clock, or reversed by supplying a `reversal_reason`; a reversal moves the transaction to `REVERSED`, subtracts the transferred units back out of the source ledger's supplied figure and the destination ledger's consumed figure, recomputes both ledgers' remaining credit and the pool subscription, and increments the transaction's version and both ledger versions.

### 3.9. Fleet Maintenance, Instrument Administration and Account Closure

Two administration paths on a robot system exist purely for operational hygiene. A `ServiceEngineer` may post a manual instrument-and-accessory load against the system's accessory set by supplying `approved_load`; the remaining lives are recomputed downward, an accessory-load ledger entry is appended, and the system's version increments. And a `ServiceEngineer` may provision a replacement accessory set, which recomputes `instrument_lives_remaining` back to the configuration's `max_instrument_lives` and increments the system's version. The reset is the only action in the system that raises the remaining lives, which is why the exhaustion surcharge described in §2.10 persists for every case booked after the set runs dry until a replacement is provisioned.

Closing an account is the terminal administrative act and it belongs to a `PlatformAdmin`, taking `expected_account_version`. It is reachable only from `ACTIVE` or `DORMANT` (`ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_CLOSABLE`, HTTP 409, checked first), and it refuses while any agreement remains `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` (`ERR_ACTIVE_AGREEMENT_EXISTS`, HTTP 409). On success the account moves to `CLOSED`, every non-terminal procedure case on its systems is cancelled, every `ACTIVE` service contract on those systems is terminated, the posture is recomputed, and the account's version increments. The systems themselves are not deleted — they persist with their serial numbers and their histories — but they can no longer be scheduled against, since their cases are cancelled and their coverage is gone. Because a closed account no longer counts toward the `idn_scale` figure used by the placement fee ladder, which counts only accounts in `ACTIVE` or `DORMANT`, closing a member of an IDN also quietly re-prices the volume discount available to its peers; and because a closed account no longer contributes non-terminal cases, it cannot hold a service-line tier open for any dependent rule.

A dormant account is recoverable. A `RegionalSalesDirector` reactivates it by supplying `expected_account_version`; the account must currently be `DORMANT` (checked first), and on success it returns to `ACTIVE` with `dormant_since_utc` cleared back to null and its version incremented. Reactivation is the counterpart to the automatic dormancy detection that a month-close run performs when no case was scheduled on any of the account's systems inside the just-closed cycle window, and it is the difference between a site that has paused and a site that has left.

Two invariants hold across the whole fleet regardless of which workflow last touched it. The first is that every transition which mutates a resource refreshes that resource's `updated_at` and increments its `version`, including the derived reconciliations that no client asked for — so a client that reads a version is reading a version that moves whenever anything about the resource changes. The second is that every operation which reads a version must be given one, and the operations that additionally read a parent aggregate through a pinned concurrency guard are the three named in §2.14; those are the only places where the parent's version, rather than the target's own, governs whether the request proceeds.
