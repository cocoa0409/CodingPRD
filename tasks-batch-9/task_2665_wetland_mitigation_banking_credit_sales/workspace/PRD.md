# Wetland Mitigation Banking API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Compensatory mitigation banking sits at an awkward intersection of ecology, real-estate finance and federal permitting. A bank sponsor spends years and considerable capital restoring hydrology, planting native vegetation and suppressing invasive species on a single site, then recovers that investment by selling wetland credits to permit holders whose authorised impacts must be offset. Between the sponsor's spade and the buyer's cheque stands an interagency review team that must be convinced the site was well chosen, that the work plan will actually generate functional lift, that the credits are released only as construction and ecological performance milestones are genuinely attained, and that the site will be protected and funded in perpetuity. Today that entire chain of custody is administered in spreadsheets, e-mail threads and paper ledgers, and the resulting drift — credits sold twice, credits released against a failing monitoring year, credits sold outside the watershed the bank actually serves — is precisely the failure mode the regulators care most about.

This document specifies the API for a mitigation banking administration platform that makes the credit ledger the system of record. Every credit that comes into existence, is placed on hold, is sold, is frozen by a remedial action or is restored by a compensating reversal is written as one immutable ledger entry, and every aggregate the business cares about — credits released, credits sold, credits on hold, credits available — is a projection of that ledger rather than an independently maintained counter. The platform additionally carries the review workflow (prospectus voting rounds, work plan decisions, monitoring report scoring), the fiscal machinery (permittee escrow, long-term stewardship endowment, performance assurance bond) and the commercial layer (service-area-limited quoting, tiered pricing, demand forecasting against recent permitting activity).

Two properties of the design deserve emphasis up front because they shape almost every endpoint. First, credit quantities are conserved: no operation may make credits appear or disappear except through an explicitly enumerated ledger entry, and the invariant that released credits always equal the sum of sold, on-hold and available credits is never permitted to break, not even transiently across a cascade. Second, the platform is deliberately unforgiving about *when* a client last looked at a bank. Because a quote is priced from the bank's credit position and a release is approved against it, both operations carry an optimistic-concurrency token, and a caller working from a stale read is turned away rather than allowed to overwrite a position it has never observed.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

All business endpoints described in this document are mounted under the `/mitigation` prefix. The single exception is the logical-clock administration endpoint, which lives outside that prefix and is described in §4.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller is acting. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five recognised role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Once authenticated, the caller's role must appear in the authorised-role list of the endpoint being invoked, as tabulated in §4; a role acting outside its list is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `Sponsor`, `Permittee`, `IRTMember`, `IRTChair` and `ProgramAdmin`.

Three of these roles are backed by persisted entities, and for them `X-User-ID` is a resource identifier rather than an opaque login. When `X-User-Role` is `Sponsor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Sponsor row; ownership checks on any resource that references a Sponsor compare the resource's sponsor foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a Sponsor write is admissible only when the targeted bank's `sponsor_id` equals `X-User-ID` — whether the bank is named directly in the path, reached through the target row's `bank_id`, or reached through the monitoring report that a remedial action was opened against — and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_BANK_SPONSOR` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `Permittee`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Permittee row; ownership checks compare `X-User-ID` to the sale's `permittee_id`, to the `permittee_id` supplied in the body when a quote is created, or to the permittee identifier in the path when escrow is topped up, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_SALE_PERMITTEE` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `IRTMember`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting IRTMember row; if that identifier does not resolve to a seated member record at all, the request is rejected with `ERR_IRT_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

The remaining two roles are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `IRTChair`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it — it is stored as `decision_recorded_by` on a prospectus whose review round the chair has just closed — but it is never compared to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier, recorded nowhere and compared to nothing.

A `Sponsor` builds and operates bank sites: it registers the site, drafts and submits the prospectus, drafts and submits the mitigation work plan, records the conservation easement, funds the stewardship endowment, adds and claims release milestones, files monitoring reports, performs corrective work, and settles and assigns credit sales. A `Permittee` funds an escrow account, requests quotes, reserves credits and may withdraw its own quote or reservation; it may never settle or assign a sale, because settlement and permit assignment belong to the sponsor. An `IRTMember` votes on prospectus revision rounds, reviews and scores monitoring reports, inspects corrective work, and approves credit releases. An `IRTChair` closes prospectus revision rounds, decides work plans, executes the banking instrument, closes banks and reverses settled sales; the chair never casts a prospectus vote, which keeps the quorum and supermajority tallies free of the deciding official's own opinion, and an attempt by the chair to vote is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. A `ProgramAdmin` registers sponsors, permittees and review-team members, may top up permittee escrow, and drives the logical clock; it holds no bank, review, release or sales authority whatsoever.

Two further authority constraints cut across the review team. A member whose `roster_status` is `RECUSED` may neither vote on a prospectus, nor review a monitoring report, nor inspect a remedial action, nor approve a credit release; all four are rejected with `ERR_MEMBER_RECUSED` (HTTP 403). And the member who verified the clearing reinspection of a remedial action may never be the member who approves the release of the milestone that action had deferred — an attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint documents a different ordering in §4, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Four pagination fields accompany every list response inside `meta`: the echoed `limit`, the echoed `offset`, `total_count` carrying the number of rows matching the filter before pagination is applied, and `has_more` indicating whether any rows remain beyond the current page. These four fields are present only on list responses; single-resource responses carry `meta` without them.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Immutable Ledger Record

Every resource the API returns carries a server-minted `id`, formatted as a UUID with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`; they receive one and use it in subsequent paths and foreign-key references.

Most resources also carry three bookkeeping properties. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped when the row is first written. `updated_at` uses the same format and is refreshed on every mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that begins at 1 and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row; it is the token the optimistic-concurrency machinery of §2.7 is built on.

Two record types are deliberate exceptions because they are immutable events rather than mutable resources. A prospectus vote, once cast, is never edited: it carries only `id` and `created_at`, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from its representation. A credit ledger entry is likewise append-only and never rewritten or deleted: it too carries only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version`. Every other resource in the system — sponsors, permittees, review-team members, banks, prospectuses, work plans, performance standards, release milestones, monitoring reports, remedial actions and credit sales — carries all four common properties.

### 2.5. Credit Quantities, Money and Rounding Discipline

Credit quantities are decimal numbers carried to exactly 2 decimal places. Money is carried as integer cents throughout; there are no fractional-cent values anywhere in the system, and no field expresses money in dollars. Every arithmetic step in this document is performed with exact decimal arithmetic — never with binary floating point — and wherever a value must be shortened, the rounding method is round half up: a discarded remainder of exactly one half rounds the retained digit away from zero.

Rounding placement is normative, not incidental. Several calculations in this specification round intermediate terms *before* summing them, and the resulting number differs from the number obtained by summing unrounded terms and rounding once at the end. Wherever the following sections pin a rounding order, that order is the requirement; an implementation that rounds later and produces a neighbouring value is wrong even though its arithmetic is defensible. Two derived quantities are held at 4 decimal places rather than 2 — the ecological attainment score of a monitoring report and the service-area demand index — and both use the same round-half-up convention. One capitalisation step rounds upward to the next whole cent regardless of remainder; it is called out explicitly where it occurs.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock, Windows and Interval Semantics

All time-dependent behaviour is evaluated against an injectable logical clock rather than wall-clock time. At process start the clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and a `ProgramAdmin` may move it forward or backward by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the resulting `current_utc`. Every timestamp the server stamps, every window comparison, every hold expiry and every demand quarter boundary is computed from that clock.

Interval inclusivity differs by purpose and each case is stated where it applies, but two conventions recur. Performance and monitoring windows are half-open: `window_start` is inclusive, `window_end` is exclusive, and `window_end` must fall strictly after `window_start` on every entity that carries the pair. Reservation holds are closed at both ends: both `hold_start_at` and `hold_expires_at` belong to the hold, so a hold is still live at the instant it expires and only becomes releasable once the clock has moved strictly past `hold_expires_at`.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on the Bank Ledger

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, and both are guarded against the same parent: the mitigation bank. Both require a non-optional integer input field named `expected_bank_version`, carrying the `version` of the bank as the client last read it from the bank's single-resource read. If the value supplied does not equal the bank's current `version` at request time, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BANK_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

The first guarded operation is credit-quote creation. A quote is priced from the bank's available credits, its stewardship funding gap and its demand index as those stand at quote time, and any other writer's release approval, reservation, execution or reversal legitimately supersedes that view; the quote must therefore be re-derived from a fresh read rather than priced off inputs the bank has already moved past. The second is credit-release approval. Releases of two different milestones are each independently valid, so an approval carrying the bank version observed before another release landed is turned away on the stale version rather than on state — the reviewer must re-read the bank and re-submit.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field. Every other mutation, including escrow deposits, easement recording, stewardship contributions, reservations, executions, assignments, cancellations and reversals, is processed without any client-supplied version token.

### 2.8. Sales Posture: The Derived Gate on Every Credit Transaction

Alongside its lifecycle `status`, every mitigation bank carries a second, derived enumeration called `sales_posture`, whose value is one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED`, `SOLD_OUT` or `FROZEN`. It is never written directly by any client. It is recomputed by the server after every mutation that changes the bank's `status`, its `credits_on_hold`, its `credits_available`, its `easement_recorded` flag, or the set of that bank's remedial actions still standing in `OPEN`, `WORK_SUBMITTED`, `REINSPECTION` or `FAILED`.

The recomputation is a first-match-wins ladder and the order of its branches is normative. If the bank's `status` is `DRAFT`, `PROSPECTUS_REVIEW`, `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED`, the posture is `FROZEN`. Otherwise, if the conservation easement has not been recorded, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise, if at least one remedial action reachable through the bank's monitoring reports is in `OPEN`, `WORK_SUBMITTED`, `REINSPECTION` or `FAILED`, the posture is again `RESTRICTED`; cleared actions are ignored by this test. Otherwise, if the bank's available credits are exactly zero, the posture is `SOLD_OUT`. In every remaining case the posture is `OPEN`.

Posture gates the commercial endpoints asymmetrically. Quoting and reserving both require a posture of exactly `OPEN`. Execution is permitted when the posture is either `OPEN` or `SOLD_OUT`, because a sale whose credits are already held may legitimately settle after the last available credit has left the pool. The rejection code is keyed to the offending posture value rather than to the operation: a `FROZEN` bank yields `ERR_BANK_SALES_FROZEN`, a `RESTRICTED` bank yields `ERR_BANK_SALES_RESTRICTED`, and a `SOLD_OUT` bank blocking a quote or a reservation yields `ERR_BANK_SOLD_OUT`. All three carry HTTP 409.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Hierarchical Consistency

Several operations in this system mutate more than one entity, and where they do, the full set of secondary effects is part of the contract rather than an implementation detail. Two cascades are long enough to be worth naming here, and both are specified step-by-step in §3.

Whenever a cascade repositions a bank, the bank's `version` increments by exactly 1 for the whole request — not once per touched field — and its `updated_at` is refreshed. Child rows mutated inside the same cascade increment their own `version` and refresh their own `updated_at` in the same way. Derived aggregates are never left behind: whenever a ledger entry is appended, the bank's `credits_released`, `credits_sold`, `credits_on_hold` and `credits_available` are all recomputed from the ledger, and `sales_posture` is recomputed from the ladder in §2.8. Whenever a sale's status or price changes in a way that affects a permittee, that permittee's `deposited_cents`, `escrow_balance_cents`, `assigned_credits` and `unmet_requirement_credits` are recomputed. Whenever a bank enters or leaves `SUSPENDED`, the owning sponsor's `good_standing` is recomputed, and its `banks_count` is maintained as the count of bank rows naming it as sponsor, counted across every status without exception.

### 2.10. Request Validation Order

When a single request violates several rules at once, the response must name the violation that comes first in the following pipeline; evaluation stops at the first failure. This ordering is observable and is part of the contract.

Authentication comes first (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`), then role authorisation against the endpoint's role list (`ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`). Schema validation follows: required fields present, JSON types correct, enum members recognised, HUC-8 codes matching exactly eight decimal digits, credit quantities strictly positive with at most 2 decimal places, cent amounts non-negative integers, a release percentage greater than zero and no greater than one, `limit` within 1 through 100, `offset` non-negative, `window_end` strictly after `window_start`, a non-empty credit-strata array with no repeated stratum code, and a measurements array with no repeated standard code — any breach yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Next, every identifier embedded in the request path is resolved left to right, each returning its own not-found code: `ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERMITTEE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_IRT_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BANK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROSPECTUS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORK_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MONITORING_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REMEDIAL_ACTION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CREDIT_SALE_NOT_FOUND`. Immediately after the path, every foreign key supplied in a request body — `bank_id`, `permittee_id`, `sponsor_id`, `predecessor_milestone_id` — is resolved against live rows, again returning that entity's own not-found code; this applies to the creation of banks, prospectuses, work plans, milestones, monitoring reports and credit sales.

Ownership and standing checks come next, in this order: sponsor ownership (`ERR_NOT_BANK_SPONSOR`), permittee ownership (`ERR_NOT_SALE_PERMITTEE`), then review-team recusal (`ERR_MEMBER_RECUSED`). After identity comes state: first whether the target row is in a state from which the requested transition exists at all, returning the entity-specific code (`ERR_BANK_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PROSPECTUS_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_WORK_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_MILESTONE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_REMEDIAL_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_SALE_STATE_INVALID`); then the sales-posture gate of §2.8; then the optimistic-concurrency check (`ERR_STALE_BANK_VERSION`). Note the consequence of that last placement: a stale `expected_bank_version` on a quote against a restricted bank surfaces as the posture error, not the concurrency error, because posture is evaluated first.

Regulatory safety checks follow the concurrency check, in this order: the easement precondition (`ERR_EASEMENT_NOT_RECORDED`), the service-area containment rule (`ERR_OUTSIDE_SERVICE_AREA`), the segregation-of-duties rule (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), and the site-watershed membership rule at bank creation (`ERR_SERVICE_AREA_MISSING_SITE_HUC`). Then come the temporal rules, in this order: monitoring window overlap (`ERR_MONITORING_WINDOW_OVERLAP`), reservation hold conflict (`ERR_RESERVATION_HOLD_CONFLICT`), milestone window buffer (`ERR_MILESTONE_WINDOW_BUFFER`), milestone window not yet open (`ERR_MILESTONE_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`) and remedial work past due (`ERR_REMEDIAL_PAST_DUE`).

Uniqueness and aggregate constraints are evaluated next, in this order: duplicate monitoring year (`ERR_DUPLICATE_MONITORING_YEAR`), duplicate milestone code (`ERR_DUPLICATE_MILESTONE_CODE`), an oversubscribed release schedule (`ERR_RELEASE_SCHEDULE_OVERSUBSCRIBED`), a predecessor milestone not yet released (`ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_RELEASED`), a duplicate vote in the round (`ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE`), a duplicate permit number (`ERR_DUPLICATE_PERMIT_NUMBER`), and the single-approved-work-plan rule together with the standard-weight sum rule (`ERR_WORK_PLAN_ALREADY_APPROVED` and `ERR_STANDARD_WEIGHTS_INVALID`). Referential dependency checks follow, in this order: the prospectus and work plan prerequisites (`ERR_WORK_PLAN_MISSING` and `ERR_PROSPECTUS_NOT_APPROVED`), review quorum (`ERR_IRT_QUORUM_NOT_MET`), effective standards and their measurements (`ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_STANDARDS` and `ERR_MEASUREMENT_MISSING`), the monitoring prerequisites of a performance release (`ERR_NO_ACCEPTED_MONITORING_REPORT` and `ERR_COMPLIANCE_FAILED`), and the permit obligation ceiling (`ERR_ASSIGNMENT_EXCEEDS_REQUIREMENT`). The closure completeness rule (`ERR_MILESTONES_INCOMPLETE`) is evaluated after those.

Fiscal checks are evaluated last, in this order: credit availability (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CREDITS`), escrow sufficiency (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ESCROW`), stewardship funding (`ERR_STEWARDSHIP_UNDERFUNDED`) and the performance assurance bond (`ERR_PERFORMANCE_BOND_INSUFFICIENT`). Placing money last is deliberate: a caller should learn that the ecology or the paperwork is wrong before learning that the account is short.

### 2.11. The Credit Ledger and Its Conserved Quantities

The credit ledger is the system's book of record. Each entry belongs to exactly one bank through its `bank_id` and carries a `sequence_number` that starts at 1 for that bank's first entry and increases by one for each subsequent append, in append order. When a single request appends more than one entry, the order in which this document pins those appends determines their `sequence_number` values, and therefore also their running balances.

An entry's `entry_type` is one of `GENERATED`, `RELEASED`, `HOLD_PLACED`, `HOLD_RELEASED`, `SOLD`, `SALE_REVERSED` or `ASSIGNED`, and the type fixes the sign of the entry's `delta_credits` — the signed change the entry makes to the bank's available credits. A `RELEASED` entry carries the released quantity as a positive delta. A `HOLD_PLACED` entry carries the held quantity as a negative delta, and a `HOLD_RELEASED` entry carries the same quantity back as a positive delta. A `SOLD` entry carries the sold quantity as a negative delta, and a `SALE_REVERSED` entry carries it back as a positive delta. A `GENERATED` entry and an `ASSIGNED` entry both carry a delta of exactly zero: the first records that a banking instrument authorised a credit total, the second records that already-sold credits were attached to a permit, and neither moves the balance. `balance_after` records the bank's available credits immediately after the entry was appended, and is therefore the running accumulation of every `delta_credits` from sequence number 1 through this entry inclusive.

Because an entry names the row that caused it, it carries a polymorphic pair: `source_entity_type`, one of `MITIGATION_BANK`, `RELEASE_MILESTONE`, `CREDIT_SALE` or `REMEDIAL_ACTION`, and `source_id`, the identifier of that row resolved against the discriminator. A human-readable `memo` accompanies each entry.

Four bank aggregates are projections of that ledger and are never maintained independently. `credits_released` is the total of the deltas of the bank's `RELEASED` entries. `credits_sold` is the negation of the total of the deltas of its `SOLD` and `SALE_REVERSED` entries taken together, so a reversal genuinely reduces the sold quantity rather than merely annotating it. `credits_on_hold` is the negation of the total of the deltas of its `HOLD_PLACED` and `HOLD_RELEASED` entries taken together. `credits_available` is the released quantity minus the sold quantity minus the on-hold quantity. The invariant no endpoint may ever break is that released credits equal the sum of sold, on-hold and available credits, and that available credits equal the `balance_after` of the bank's highest-numbered ledger entry. The ledger is append-only: no entry is ever amended and none is ever deleted, so every aggregate is recomputable from history alone.

Several read endpoints are computed projections hanging off a bank rather than independent collections: the bank's prospectus revisions, its work plans, its materialised performance standards, its ledger and its credit forecast are all addressed beneath the bank's own identifier, and in each case the bank identifier is resolved first, so an unknown bank yields `ERR_BANK_NOT_FOUND` before any filter or pagination parameter is considered. The bank's ledger read is ordered by `sequence_number` ascending rather than by `created_at`, and accepts an optional `entry_type` filter. The performance-standards read is ordered by `effective_from_year` and then by `standard_code`, and is the only way to observe performance standard rows at all — they have no creation endpoint of their own.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering the Parties: Sponsors, Permit Holders and the Review Team

Nothing in this system can happen before the parties exist, and all three party registrations are the exclusive province of a `ProgramAdmin`. A sponsor is registered with its `legal_name` — a non-empty string of at most 160 characters — and its `legal_entity_type`, which is one of `PRIVATE_ENTREPRENEURIAL`, `PUBLIC_AGENCY` or `NGO`, the three sponsor categories the interagency review team recognises. A `performance_bond_cents` figure may be supplied at registration to record the performance assurance the sponsor has posted; it must not be negative, and when it is omitted it defaults to 0. Two sponsor properties are derived rather than supplied: `banks_count`, the number of bank rows naming this sponsor, counted across every bank status without exception and initialised to 0; and `good_standing`, a boolean that is true precisely when the sponsor owns no bank in `SUSPENDED` status, and therefore initialised to true. Note carefully that `good_standing` is recomputed exclusively from bank statuses and never from the bond figure — an underfunded sponsor is still in good standing until one of its banks is actually suspended.

A permit holder is registered with its `organization_name`, again non-empty and at most 160 characters, its `permit_number`, its `impact_huc8_code` and its `required_credits`. The permit number is the Corps permit identifier and must match the shape of three uppercase letters, a hyphen, four decimal digits, a hyphen and five decimal digits; it is globally unique across all permit holders, and a repeat registration is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PERMIT_NUMBER`. The impact watershed code is exactly eight decimal digits, identifying the HUC-8 watershed in which the authorised impacts occur. Required credits express the total compensatory mitigation obligation the permit imposes; the figure must be strictly greater than zero and carry at most 2 decimal places. Four permit-holder properties are derived. `deposited_cents` accumulates every escrow deposit ever recorded and starts at 0. `escrow_balance_cents` is the deposited total minus the sum of the settled prices of all of this holder's sales standing in `EXECUTED` or `ASSIGNED` status, and likewise starts at 0. `assigned_credits` is the total requested-credit quantity across this holder's sales standing in `ASSIGNED` status, starting at 0. `unmet_requirement_credits` is the required credits minus the assigned credits, floored at zero so it can never go negative, carried to 2 decimal places, and therefore equal to the required credits at registration.

Escrow is funded by a deposit carrying a single `amount_cents` figure, and either a `ProgramAdmin` or the permit holder itself may lodge one; a permit holder may only fund its own account. A deposit is the system's sole money-conservation source event: the deposited total and the escrow balance both rise by the deposited amount, and the permit holder's `version` increments by one.

Review-team members are seated with a `member_name` of at most 120 characters and an `agency`, which is one of `USACE`, `EPA`, `USFWS`, `STATE_DEQ` or `TRIBAL`. The roster deliberately does not enforce one seat per agency; two members from the same agency may both be seated. A member's `roster_status` is either `SEATED` or `RECUSED` and defaults to `SEATED` when not supplied. The `veto_authority` flag is derived, never supplied: it is true precisely when the member's agency is `USACE`, because the chartering agency alone holds veto power over a prospectus.

### 3.2. Site Selection, Service Area Definition and the Stewardship Endowment

A sponsor opens a bank site by registering it under its own `sponsor_id`, together with a `site_name`, the `site_huc8_code` of the watershed the site physically occupies, the `service_area_huc8_codes` list defining where credits from this bank may be sold, the site's `hgm_class`, and its `annual_stewardship_cost_cents`. The service area is an explicit list of between one and eight eight-digit watershed codes with no duplicates, and it must contain the site's own watershed code — a service area that omits the site's watershed is rejected with `ERR_SERVICE_AREA_MISSING_SITE_HUC`, because a bank that cannot sell into its own watershed is a contradiction in terms. The hydrogeomorphic class is one of `RIVERINE`, `DEPRESSIONAL`, `SLOPE`, `FLATS` or `ESTUARINE`, and it is the field that selects the bank's base credit price. The annual stewardship cost is a strictly positive cent figure capturing what it will cost each year to steward the restored site in perpetuity.

A newly registered bank opens in `DRAFT` status with a `sales_posture` of `FROZEN`, and every credit aggregate — `total_authorized_credits`, `credits_released`, `credits_sold`, `credits_on_hold` and `credits_available` — initialised to 0.00. Its `easement_recorded` flag is false with `easement_recorded_at` and `easement_deed_reference` both null; its `stewardship_funded_cents`, `deferral_count` and `open_quote_count` are 0; and `instrument_executed_at` and `closed_at` are null. Registration also raises the owning sponsor's bank count by one.

Registration is also where the long-term stewardship obligation is first quantified, onto `stewardship_required_cents`. The calculation capitalises the annual stewardship cost at a non-wasting payout rate of 3.75 percent: the base endowment is the annual stewardship cost divided by 0.0375, rounded **up** to the next whole cent whatever the remainder. An administrative fee of 1.50 percent of that base endowment is then computed and rounded half up to whole cents. The required endowment is the base endowment plus the administrative fee. This evaluation order is pinned and single-pass, and the reason deserves stating plainly: the administrative fee is itself an annual stewardship cost, so a naive reading would feed the fee back into the capitalisation, raising the endowment, which raises the fee, and so on. That fixed-point reading is forbidden and produces a wildly larger number. The fee is derived once from the fee-free base endowment and added afterwards; no iteration to convergence is performed. The same figure is re-derived from the same input when the banking instrument is executed.

Two funding gates are derived from that requirement and are consulted later. The interim gate is 25 percent of the required endowment, rounded half up to whole cents. The final gate is the required endowment in full. Which gate applies where is set out in §3.6 and §3.9.

### 3.3. Prospectus Submission and the Interagency Review Rounds

With a site registered, the sponsor drafts a prospectus for it, supplying the target `bank_id`, an `objective_summary` of at most 2000 characters narrating the restoration objectives, the site selection rationale and the watershed need, and a `proposed_credits` figure that must be strictly positive with at most 2 decimal places. The proposed figure is informational only and is never the authorised credit total — that number arrives much later, from the approved work plan. A prospectus opens in `DRAFT` status with `revision_round` stamped at 1, all three vote tallies at 0, `veto_recorded` false, and `submitted_at`, `review_opened_at`, `decision_recorded_by` and `decided_at` all null.

Submission by the owning sponsor advances the prospectus `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, stamps `submitted_at` with the current logical clock, and — this is the side effect that starts the regulatory machine — drags the bank from `DRAFT` to `PROSPECTUS_REVIEW`, recomputing the bank's posture (which remains `FROZEN`, since `PROSPECTUS_REVIEW` is not a sales-eligible status) and incrementing the bank's `version` by one. Submission is admissible only while the prospectus is in `DRAFT`, only while the bank is still in `DRAFT`, and only when the bank's service area contains the bank's own site watershed code.

A seated member records a position on the current round, supplying a `position` of `CONCUR`, `OBJECT` or `ABSTAIN` and optionally a `comment` of at most 1000 characters. The vote row records the `prospectus_id` it was cast against, the `irt_member_id` of the caster, and a copy of the prospectus's `revision_round` at cast time, so votes are permanently scoped to a single round. Its `is_veto` flag is derived: true precisely when the position is `OBJECT` **and** the casting member holds veto authority. One vote per member per prospectus per round is allowed; a second attempt in the same round is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE`. The first vote of a round auto-advances the prospectus `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and stamps `review_opened_at` with the clock. Each vote raises exactly one of `concur_count`, `object_count` or `abstain_count` by one — the tallies count only votes bearing the prospectus's current revision round — and a veto vote raises the object tally and additionally sets `veto_recorded` to true.

The chair closes a round on a prospectus that is `UNDER_REVIEW`, and the outcome is decided by three tests applied to the votes of the current round. Quorum first: at least 3 votes must have been cast in the current round, exactly 3 being sufficient; fewer yields `ERR_IRT_QUORUM_NOT_MET`. Then the veto: if any veto was recorded in this round, the prospectus goes `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED`. Then the supermajority: the concur tally must be at least two-thirds of the votes cast in the round, with that two-thirds threshold rounded **up** to a whole number of votes, so five votes cast require four concurrences and four votes cast require three. When quorum is met, no veto stands, the supermajority is reached, and the bank additionally has a work plan in either `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED` status, the prospectus goes `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED`; if no such work plan exists the finalisation is rejected with `ERR_WORK_PLAN_MISSING`. When quorum is met, no veto stands, the supermajority is *not* reached and the prospectus is still in its first review round, it goes `UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED` and its `revision_round` rises by one. When quorum is met and either a veto stands, or the supermajority is missed while the prospectus is already in its second round, it goes `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED`. At most two review rounds exist: a prospectus may pass through `REVISION_REQUESTED` exactly once, so a second failure to reach supermajority is terminal.

Every one of the three finalisation branches records the chair's opaque caller identity into `decision_recorded_by` and stamps `decided_at` with the clock; those two side effects are common to approval, revision request and rejection alike. The revision-request branch additionally resets the round's tallies: the concur, object and abstain counts and the veto flag are all recomputed against the new revision round and are therefore all zero and false until fresh votes arrive. A prospectus that reaches `REJECTED` is terminal, exactly as an `APPROVED` one is, and the rejection branch leaves the bank sitting in `PROSPECTUS_REVIEW` and no banking instrument may ever be executed from a rejected prospectus. A revised prospectus is resubmitted by its sponsor, advancing `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, restamping `submitted_at`, clearing `review_opened_at` back to null because a fresh round has no opened review yet, and again showing all tallies at zero for the new round; resubmission is admissible only while the revision round has not passed the second-round ceiling.

### 3.4. The Mitigation Work Plan: Hydrology, Planting, Invasive Control and Credit Yield

Running in parallel with the prospectus, the sponsor authors the mitigation work plan that says what will actually be built. The plan names its `bank_id` and quantifies three acreages, each non-negative and carried to 2 decimal places: `hydrology_restoration_acres` covering ditch plugging, tile breaking and microtopography work; `planting_acres` covering native woody and herbaceous planting; and `invasive_control_acres` covering the area placed under an invasive-species regime. The regime itself is declared in `invasive_control_protocol`, one of `MECHANICAL`, `HERBICIDE` or `INTEGRATED`.

The plan's commercial substance lives in two arrays. `credit_strata` describes the functional-lift strata that will generate credits: between one and six entries, each an object carrying a `stratum_code` — one of `UPLAND_BUFFER`, `EMERGENT_MARSH`, `FORESTED_WETLAND`, `SCRUB_SHRUB` or `OPEN_WATER`, and unique within the plan — an `acres` figure strictly greater than zero with 2 decimal places, and a `lift_factor` strictly greater than zero and no greater than 1, carried to 3 decimal places. `performance_standards` describes the ecological yardsticks the bank will be held to: between one and six entries, each an object carrying a `standard_code` unique within the plan, a `comparator`, a `threshold_value`, a `weight` and an `effective_from_year`. The weights across a plan's standards must sum to exactly 1.000; any other sum is rejected with `ERR_STANDARD_WEIGHTS_INVALID`.

From the strata array the server derives `total_planned_credits`, and the rounding order here is pinned and consequential. Each stratum's credit contribution is its acreage multiplied by its lift factor, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and the plan total is the sum of those already-rounded per-stratum contributions. Summing the unrounded products and rounding the single total at the end is forbidden and yields a different number in the ordinary case where several strata each land on a half-cent boundary; the per-stratum-first order is normative. Strata are iterated in ascending stratum-code order, which does not change the result once each term is rounded but is pinned regardless. The plan total is computed when the plan is first drafted and re-derived on every submission of that plan.

A plan opens in `DRAFT`, with `submitted_at`, `decided_at` and `return_reason` all null. Only one plan per bank may ever reach `APPROVED`; drafting a further plan for a bank that already has an approved one is rejected with `ERR_WORK_PLAN_ALREADY_APPROVED`, and so is any attempt to approve a second plan. Submission by the owning sponsor advances `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and stamps `submitted_at`, and is admissible only when the hydrology restoration acreage and the planting acreage together come to more than zero — a plan that restores nothing has nothing to submit. A returned plan may be resubmitted, advancing `RETURNED → SUBMITTED`, restamping `submitted_at` and clearing `return_reason` back to null.

The chair decides a submitted plan by supplying a `decision`, and the two branches have deliberately divergent side-effect sets. Approving advances `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, stamps `decided_at`, and materialises exactly one performance-standard row per entry of the plan's standards array, each stamped with the plan's bank identifier; the bank's `version` increments by one. Those materialised rows are the only performance standards that ever exist — there is no way to create one directly — and each carries a `standard_code` that is one of `HYDROPERIOD_DAYS`, `INVASIVE_COVER_PCT`, `NATIVE_COVER_PCT` or `STEM_DENSITY_PER_ACRE` and is unique within its bank, a `comparator` of `GTE` or `LTE`, a strictly positive `threshold_value`, a strictly positive `weight` at 3 decimal places whose siblings on the same bank sum to 1.000, and an `effective_from_year` of at least 1 naming the first monitoring year in which the standard is evaluated. Approval requires the same bank to already have an approved prospectus; without one the decision is rejected with `ERR_WORK_PLAN_MISSING`. Returning instead advances `SUBMITTED → RETURNED`, stamps `decided_at`, and stores the chair's `return_reason` — which must be supplied on this branch, its absence being a malformed request. The return branch materialises no performance standard rows at all and leaves the bank entirely untouched. Deciding a plan that is not in `SUBMITTED` is rejected with `ERR_WORK_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`, as is submitting a plan that is already submitted or approved.

Note what approval does *not* do: it does not write the bank's authorised credit total. The plan's credit yield sits on the plan until the banking instrument is executed, and only then is it copied onto the bank.

### 3.5. Executing the Instrument, Recording the Easement and Funding the Endowment

Instrument execution is the chair's act and the moment the bank becomes a real credit producer. It is admissible only on a bank in `PROSPECTUS_REVIEW`; any other bank status is rejected with `ERR_BANK_STATE_INVALID`. The bank must have a prospectus in `APPROVED` status, and without one the request is rejected with `ERR_PROSPECTUS_NOT_APPROVED`; it must have a work plan in `APPROVED` status, and without one the request is rejected with `ERR_WORK_PLAN_MISSING`; and the owning sponsor's posted performance assurance must be at least 25,000,000 cents — a quarter of a million dollars — with a shortfall rejected as `ERR_PERFORMANCE_BOND_INSUFFICIENT`.

Execution advances the bank `PROSPECTUS_REVIEW → INSTRUMENT_EXECUTED` and performs five further effects. The bank's `total_authorized_credits` is set to the approved work plan's planned credit total, and that copy is the only authorised figure the commercial side of the system ever consults. The bank's required stewardship endowment is re-derived from its annual stewardship cost by the pinned single-pass capitalisation of §3.2. The bank's `instrument_executed_at` is stamped with the logical clock. A ledger entry of type `GENERATED` is appended, carrying a delta of 0.00 and therefore a balance of 0.00, attributed to the bank itself through a source entity type of `MITIGATION_BANK` and the bank's own identifier, with a memo recording the authorised credit total in the form `INSTRUMENT EXECUTED authorized=<total_authorized_credits>`. Finally the posture is recomputed, which lands on `RESTRICTED` for as long as the conservation easement remains unrecorded, and the bank's `version` increments by one.

Recording the conservation easement is the sponsor's act and the hard gate in front of every credit release. The sponsor supplies a single `deed_reference` — the deed book and page reference under which the easement was recorded — and the server sets `easement_recorded` to true, stamps `easement_recorded_at` with the logical clock, stores the supplied reference into `easement_deed_reference`, recomputes the posture, and increments the bank's `version` by one. Until this has happened, the posture ladder holds the bank at `RESTRICTED` no matter how healthy everything else looks, so no credit may be quoted and no release may be approved.

Funding the long-term stewardship endowment is likewise the sponsor's act, performed as often as the sponsor likes with an `amount_cents` figure each time. Each contribution raises `stewardship_funded_cents` by the amount contributed and increments the bank's `version` by one. Both the easement recording and the stewardship contribution are ownership-checked against the bank's sponsor, and both are rejected with `ERR_BANK_STATE_INVALID` when the bank's status does not admit further work on it — a closed bank, in particular, accepts neither.

### 3.6. The Credit Release Schedule: Scheduling, Claiming and Approving Releases

Credits are not released in a lump; they are released in tranches tied to construction and performance milestones, and the sponsor builds that schedule itself. Each milestone names its `bank_id`, a `milestone_code`, a `release_pct`, a `window_start` and a `window_end`, and optionally a `predecessor_milestone_id`. The milestone code is one of `INSTRUMENT_EXECUTION`, `CONSTRUCTION_COMPLETE`, `PERF_YEAR_3`, `PERF_YEAR_5` or `PERF_YEAR_10`, and it must be unique within its bank; a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_MILESTONE_CODE`. The release percentage is the fraction of the bank's authorised credit total that this milestone frees, strictly greater than zero and no greater than 1, carried to 3 decimal places. Summed across all of a bank's milestones — including the one being added — those fractions may not exceed 1.000, and an over-subscription is rejected with `ERR_RELEASE_SCHEDULE_OVERSUBSCRIBED`, because a bank cannot promise to release more credits than it was authorised to generate.

The performance window is a half-open interval: the milestone may be claimed from `window_start` inclusive and the window ends at `window_end` exclusive, with the end required to fall strictly after the start. Consecutive windows on one bank must additionally be separated by a full year of monitoring: a new milestone's window start must fall no earlier than 365 days after the latest window end already on that bank's schedule, and a separation of exactly 365 days is accepted. A tighter spacing is rejected with `ERR_MILESTONE_WINDOW_BUFFER`. The optional predecessor identifier chains a milestone behind another milestone of the same bank; the first milestone of a bank leaves it null, and a predecessor identifier that resolves to nothing is rejected with `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_FOUND`.

A milestone opens in `PENDING` with `claimed_at`, `approved_at` and `deferred_at` null, `attainment_factor_applied` null, and `final_release_credits` at 0.00. Its `provisional_release_credits` is computed straight away, and is recomputed each time the milestone is claimed so that it always reflects the bank's current authorised total. That provisional figure is the bank's authorised credit total multiplied by the milestone's release percentage, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The provisional variant always applies an attainment factor of exactly 1.00 and consults no monitoring report whatsoever, which is why a claim can never fail on ecological grounds.

Claiming is the sponsor's assertion that the milestone has been attained, and it advances the milestone `PENDING → CLAIMED` while stamping `claimed_at` with the logical clock. It is admissible only while the bank is in `INSTRUMENT_EXECUTED` or `MONITORING` status, only once the logical clock has reached or passed the window start — an earlier attempt is rejected with `ERR_MILESTONE_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` — and only when the milestone either has no predecessor or has a predecessor already standing in `APPROVED`; an unreleased predecessor is rejected with `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_RELEASED`.

Approving the release is a seated review-team member's act, and it is the most heavily guarded write in the system. The request carries `expected_bank_version` and is turned away with `ERR_STALE_BANK_VERSION` if that value does not match the bank's current version. The milestone must be in `CLAIMED`; anything else is `ERR_MILESTONE_STATE_INVALID`. The conservation easement must already be recorded, or the request is rejected with `ERR_EASEMENT_NOT_RECORDED`. The acting member must not be the member recorded as having verified the clearing reinspection of any remedial action that had deferred this very milestone, or the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. And the bank's stewardship endowment must clear the gate for this milestone code: for every code other than `PERF_YEAR_10` the funded amount must be at least the interim gate of 25 percent of the required endowment rounded half up to whole cents, while for `PERF_YEAR_10` the funded amount must be at least the required endowment in full. A shortfall is rejected with `ERR_STEWARDSHIP_UNDERFUNDED`.

The ecological gate depends on the milestone code, and this is where the final release quantity is decided. For the two construction milestones, `INSTRUMENT_EXECUTION` and `CONSTRUCTION_COMPLETE`, no monitoring is consulted at all and the attainment factor is exactly 1.00 — earthwork needs no growing season. For the three performance milestones, the server selects the governing monitoring report: among the bank's reports whose review status is `ACCEPTED`, the one with the highest monitoring year, and where two accepted reports share the highest monitoring year, the most recently created of them. If no accepted report exists for the bank, the approval is rejected with `ERR_NO_ACCEPTED_MONITORING_REPORT`. If the governing report's compliance status is `FAIL`, the approval is rejected with `ERR_COMPLIANCE_FAILED` — a failing year defers a release, it never scales one down. Otherwise the factor is selected from the report's attainment score by two thresholds, both of which are inclusive at their boundary: a score of at least 0.95 earns a factor of 1.00, a score of at least 0.85 but below 0.95 earns a factor of 0.90, and a score below 0.85 is rejected with `ERR_COMPLIANCE_FAILED`.

The final release quantity is then the *already-rounded* provisional quantity multiplied by the selected factor, rounded half up to 2 decimal places again. This two-step rounding is pinned: the server does not multiply the authorised total by the release percentage and the attainment factor in one unrounded expression. Where a partial factor of 0.90 applies, the 10 percent difference between the provisional and final quantities is simply never released; it does not linger anywhere and never re-enters the bank's available credits.

Approval then advances the milestone `CLAIMED → APPROVED` and performs a compact cascade. Both `attainment_factor_applied` and `final_release_credits` are stamped from the calculation just described. A ledger entry of type `RELEASED` is appended carrying the final release quantity as a positive delta, attributed through a source entity type of `RELEASE_MILESTONE` and the milestone's own identifier, with a memo naming the release, its milestone code and the attainment factor applied. The bank's `credits_released` and `credits_available` are recomputed from the ledger, and the posture is recomputed — which is typically the moment a bank moves from `RESTRICTED` to `OPEN`, once credits exist and no remedial action is standing. The milestone's `approved_at` is stamped with the clock and the bank's `version` increments by one. What approval does *not* do is move the bank's lifecycle status: only the bank's first accepted monitoring report carries it from `INSTRUMENT_EXECUTED` to `MONITORING`.

### 3.7. Performance Monitoring, Attainment Scoring and Remedial Action

Monitoring is the mechanism by which ecological reality is allowed to contradict the sponsor's optimism, and a monitoring report carries two entirely independent state dimensions on one row. `review_status` walks the paperwork track through `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED` and `RETURNED`. `compliance_status` walks the ecological track through `UNEVALUATED`, `PASS`, `PARTIAL` and `FAIL`, and it is written only by the acceptance branch of review. Neither dimension gates the other: a returned report keeps its compliance status at `UNEVALUATED` indefinitely unless it is resubmitted and accepted, and a report may perfectly well be `ACCEPTED` while carrying a compliance status of `FAIL` — accepting a report means the paperwork is in order, not that the wetland is thriving.

A sponsor opens a report for one `monitoring_year` on one `bank_id`, bounded by a `window_start` and a `window_end` describing the field monitoring window, and carrying a `measurements` array of between one and six objects, each pairing a `standard_code` with a non-negative `measured_value`; codes may not repeat within the array. The monitoring year must be at least 1 and must be unique within the bank among reports whose review status is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` — a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_MONITORING_YEAR`, while a `RETURNED` report releases its year and its window slot back. The monitoring windows of one bank may not overlap. Those windows are half-open, so a new window whose start equals an existing window's end is perfectly acceptable adjacency; only a positive-length intersection with an occupying window is rejected, with `ERR_MONITORING_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Occupying windows are again those of reports in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED`. A report cannot be opened for a bank that has no performance standard effective in that year at all, which is rejected with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_STANDARDS`. A new report opens with its review status at `DRAFT`, its compliance status at `UNEVALUATED`, `return_count` at 0, `attainment_score` null, `failed_standard_codes` empty, and `reviewed_by_member_id`, `submitted_at` and `reviewed_at` all null.

Submission by the owning sponsor advances the review status `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and stamps `submitted_at` with the logical clock; a returned report is resubmitted the same way, advancing `RETURNED → SUBMITTED` and restamping `submitted_at`. Submission is admissible while the bank is in `INSTRUMENT_EXECUTED`, `MONITORING` or `SUSPENDED` status — a suspended bank must still be able to file, since filing is how it recovers — and requires the measurements array to contain one entry for every performance standard of the bank whose `effective_from_year` is no greater than the report's monitoring year. A standard effective in that year with no matching measurement is rejected with `ERR_MEASUREMENT_MISSING`.

A seated member reviews a submitted report by supplying a `decision`. Returning advances the review status `SUBMITTED → RETURNED`, raises `return_count` by one, records the acting member into `reviewed_by_member_id`, stamps `reviewed_at`, and requires a `return_reason`. The return branch performs no scoring at all: the compliance status stays `UNEVALUATED`, no remedial action is created, no milestone is deferred, no posture changes, and the bank's `version` is *not* incremented.

Accepting is the branch with teeth. The report's review status advances `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, `reviewed_by_member_id` records the acting member, `reviewed_at` is stamped, and the report is scored against the standards effective for its monitoring year. Those standards are the bank's performance standards whose effective-from year is no greater than the report's monitoring year, taken in ascending standard-code order; if there are none, the acceptance is rejected with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_STANDARDS`, and if any of them lacks a matching measurement, it is rejected with `ERR_MEASUREMENT_MISSING`. Each standard yields a ratio and a met-or-not verdict. For a standard whose comparator is `GTE`, the ratio is the measured value divided by the threshold, capped at 1.0 so over-performance earns no bonus, and the standard is met when the measured value is greater than or equal to the threshold — equality counts as attainment. For a standard whose comparator is `LTE`, the ratio is exactly 1.0 when the measured value is less than or equal to the threshold; when the measured value exceeds the threshold, the ratio is 1.0 minus the excess over the threshold divided by the threshold, floored at 0.0 so a catastrophic overshoot scores zero rather than negative, and the standard is met when the measured value is less than or equal to the threshold — again, equality counts. Each standard's contribution is its ratio multiplied by its weight, rounded half up to 4 decimal places; this per-standard rounding is pinned and precedes summation. The report's `attainment_score` is the sum of those already-rounded contributions divided by the sum of the effective standards' weights as supplied — the denominator uses the unrounded weights and covers only the standards effective in this year, so a report in an early monitoring year is scored against a denominator smaller than 1.000 — with the quotient rounded half up to 4 decimal places. `failed_standard_codes` lists the codes of the standards not met, in ascending code order.

The compliance status then follows from those two outputs in order: it is `PASS` when no standard failed, otherwise `PARTIAL` when the attainment score is at least 0.85 — the boundary is inclusive — and otherwise `FAIL`. Because a single missed standard on an otherwise excellent year still leaves the score high, `PARTIAL` is the common outcome of a near miss, and it is `PARTIAL` that later halves nothing but shaves a tenth off a performance release.

If the bank has never had an accepted report before, this acceptance additionally carries the bank `INSTRUMENT_EXECUTED → MONITORING`. And if the computed compliance status is `FAIL`, acceptance triggers a four-hop cascade whose order is pinned, because the credit arithmetic at the end depends on it.

The first hop creates a remedial action against this report. It opens in `OPEN` status with `round_number` at 1, its `monitoring_report_id` naming the failing report, its `opened_at` stamped with the logical clock, its `due_at` set to that clock plus 365 days, and its `corrective_scope` auto-generated as the literal text `REMEDIATE: ` followed by the failed standard codes joined by commas in the same ascending order they were listed in. Remedial actions have no creation endpoint; this cascade is the only thing that makes one. The second hop recomputes the bank's posture, which now finds a non-terminal remedial action and therefore lands on `RESTRICTED`. The third hop acts on that posture change: every credit sale of the bank standing in `QUOTED` or `RESERVED` is cancelled with a `cancelled_reason` of `BANK_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` and a `cancelled_at` stamp, and each formerly reserved sale appends a `HOLD_RELEASED` entry that restores its held credits to the bank's available pool. The fourth hop defers a milestone: the bank's earliest non-terminal release milestone, ordered by window start ascending with creation order as the tie-break, transitions to `DEFERRED` with `deferred_at` stamped, and the bank's `deferral_count` rises by one. The remedial action's `deferred_milestone_id` records which milestone it froze, and its `held_credits` is evaluated *at this point in the chain* — that is, after the third hop has already returned the cancelled reservations' credits — as the bank's released credits minus its sold credits. A `HOLD_PLACED` entry carrying that quantity as a negative delta is appended, attributed through a source entity type of `REMEDIAL_ACTION` and the action's own identifier, and it is appended **only** when the quantity is greater than zero. The consequence of the pinned order is exact and worth stating: at the end of such a request the bank's credits on hold equal the remedial action's held credits and its available credits are exactly 0.00 — every unsold released credit of the bank is frozen. Where the deferred milestone had never been claimed, nothing had ever been provisionally computed to freeze: the held credits are 0.00 and no ledger entry is appended at all, and that milestone simply moves `PENDING → DEFERRED`. Throughout, the bank's `version` increments by exactly one for the whole request.

Corrective work is then the sponsor's problem. Submitting the work advances the action `OPEN → WORK_SUBMITTED` and stamps `work_submitted_at` with the clock, and it is admissible only while the logical clock has not passed the action's due date — on the due instant itself submission is still accepted, but past it the request is rejected with `ERR_REMEDIAL_PAST_DUE`. Ownership is checked through the failing report's bank.

A seated member then inspects the work, supplying an `outcome`. The action passes through `REINSPECTION` within the same request and resolves immediately; the inspection is admissible only on an action standing in `WORK_SUBMITTED` when the request arrives, and anything else is `ERR_REMEDIAL_STATE_INVALID`. An outcome of `CLEARED` advances the action `REINSPECTION → CLEARED`, records the acting member into `verified_by_member_id` and stamps `cleared_at`; that recorded member is precisely the person who may never afterwards approve the release of the milestone this action deferred. Because no non-terminal remedial action remains for the bank, the deferred milestone is restored: `DEFERRED → CLAIMED` when it had been claimed before it was deferred, and `DEFERRED → PENDING` when it had not, with a `HOLD_RELEASED` entry appended — again attributed to the remedial action — carrying the previously held quantity as a positive delta, and again only when that quantity is greater than zero. The bank's credits on hold and available credits are recomputed, the posture is recomputed back to `OPEN`, a bank sitting in `SUSPENDED` returns to `MONITORING`, the owning sponsor's `good_standing` is recomputed to true unless another of its banks is still suspended, and the bank's `version` increments by one.

An outcome of `NOT_CLEARED` sends the action round again, so long as rounds remain: with a round number below the ceiling of 3, the action goes `REINSPECTION → OPEN`, its `round_number` rises by one, its `work_submitted_at` is cleared back to null and a fresh due date is set. Nothing else moves — the deferred milestone stays `DEFERRED`, its hold stays exactly where it is, and no ledger entry is written. But an outcome of `NOT_CLEARED` on the third round exhausts the sponsor's patience allowance: the action goes `REINSPECTION → FAILED`, the bank goes `MONITORING → SUSPENDED` (or stays suspended if it already was), its posture is recomputed to `FROZEN`, every credit sale of the bank standing in `QUOTED` or `RESERVED` is cancelled with a reason of `BANK_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` with each formerly reserved sale appending a `HOLD_RELEASED` entry, the owning sponsor's `good_standing` is recomputed to false, and the bank's `version` increments by one. A suspended bank sells nothing until a later remedial action clears and no non-terminal action remains anywhere on it.

### 3.8. Selling Credits: Quoting, Pricing, Reservation and Settlement

Every write in the credit-sales area begins with a housekeeping step before anything else happens: the server sweeps the bank's reserved sales for expired holds. A reserved sale whose `hold_expires_at` lies **strictly** before the current logical clock moves `RESERVED → EXPIRED`, appends a `HOLD_RELEASED` entry returning its requested credits to the available pool, records a `cancelled_reason` of `HOLD_EXPIRED` and stamps `cancelled_at`. At exactly the expiry instant the hold is still live and survives the sweep; only once the clock has moved past it does the hold lapse. This sweep runs at the start of quote creation, reservation, execution, assignment, cancellation and reversal alike, so a caller can never transact against a hold that should already have lapsed.

A quote is opened by either the sponsor or the buying permit holder, naming the `bank_id` whose credits are wanted, the `permittee_id` buying them, the `credits_requested` quantity, the `impact_huc8_code` of the impact site the credits will compensate, and the `expected_bank_version` the caller last observed. The bank's posture must be exactly `OPEN`. The impact watershed must fall inside the bank's service area, or the quote is rejected with `ERR_OUTSIDE_SERVICE_AREA`: this is the service-area limitation that gives mitigation banking its geographic integrity, and it is also what fixes the sale's `service_area_tier`, which is `PRIMARY` when the impact watershed is the bank's own site watershed and `SECONDARY` when it is any other member of the bank's service-area list. The requested quantity must not exceed the bank's available credits — requesting exactly the available quantity is accepted — with a shortfall rejected as `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CREDITS`; and it must not exceed the buyer's outstanding permit obligation, with an excess rejected as `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_EXCEEDS_REQUIREMENT`. That obligation ceiling is checked here at quote time and checked again at assignment, because the obligation can be consumed by another sale in between.

A quote opens in `QUOTED` status with `hold_start_at`, `hold_expires_at`, `executed_price_cents`, `executed_at`, `assigned_at`, `assignment_reference`, `cancelled_reason` and `cancelled_at` all null, raises the bank's `open_quote_count` by one and increments the bank's `version` by one. That counter is a derived tally rather than a hand-maintained number: it is the count of the bank's credit sales standing in either `QUOTED` or `RESERVED`, so a quote that is placed on hold continues to be counted, and a sale leaves the tally only when it settles, is withdrawn or expires. Two figures are frozen onto it at creation: `quoted_demand_index`, a snapshot of the bank's service-area demand pressure, and `quoted_price_cents`, the quoted-variant price.

Demand pressure is measured chronologically over the trailing 360 days, divided into four consecutive half-open 90-day quarters ending at the current logical clock, and weighted so that recent activity dominates: the oldest quarter — the one beginning 360 days ago and ending 270 days ago — carries a weight of 0.10, the next 0.20, the next 0.30, and the most recent quarter, the 90 days immediately preceding the clock, carries 0.40. For each quarter the server sums the requested credits of the bank's sales standing in `EXECUTED` or `ASSIGNED` status whose `executed_at` falls inside that quarter; sales in `QUOTED`, `RESERVED`, `CANCELLED` or `EXPIRED` status are excluded entirely, which means a reversed sale drops straight out of the measurement. Each quarter's sum is multiplied by its weight and rounded half up to 2 decimal places — the per-quarter rounding precedes summation and is pinned — and those four rounded figures are added to give the demand pressure. The `demand_index` is that pressure divided by a normaliser of 10.00 credits, capped at a ceiling of 3.0, with the result rounded half up to 4 decimal places.

Pricing starts from the bank's hydrogeomorphic class, which fixes a base price per credit: 4,250,000 cents for `RIVERINE`, 3,800,000 cents for `DEPRESSIONAL`, 3,600,000 cents for `SLOPE`, 3,300,000 cents for `FLATS` and 5,100,000 cents for `ESTUARINE`. The requested quantity is then split across three marginal tiers, evaluated in order. The first tier takes the requested quantity up to a ceiling of 5.00 credits at a multiplier of 1.000. The second tier takes whatever remains above 5.00 credits, up to a further 10.00 credits, at a multiplier of 0.950 — so it spans the range from 5.00 to 15.00 credits. The third tier takes everything above 15.00 credits at a multiplier of 0.900. Each tier carrying a non-zero quantity contributes its quantity multiplied by the base price and by its multiplier, rounded half up to whole cents, and the tiered subtotal is the sum of those already-rounded tier amounts; a tier carrying zero credits contributes nothing and is not rounded at all.

Two surcharges are then computed from that subtotal, and both are expressed as percentages added together before a single rounding. A `SECONDARY` service-area tier attracts 12 percent, a `PRIMARY` tier attracts nothing. A bank whose stewardship endowment is funded below its requirement attracts a further 8 percent, and a fully funded bank attracts nothing. The combined premium is the subtotal multiplied by the sum of those two percentages, rounded half up to whole cents — the two are never rounded separately and then added. A third adjustment reflects demand: the subtotal is multiplied by the demand sensitivity of 0.10 times the demand index, except that this multiplier is capped at 0.30 however hot the market gets, and the result is rounded half up to whole cents. The gross price is the tiered subtotal plus the premium plus the demand adjustment. Finally a ceiling is applied: the price cap is the tiered subtotal multiplied by 1.15, rounded half up to whole cents, and the price charged is whichever of the gross price and the cap is smaller.

That whole calculation is run twice in a sale's life, and the two runs can legitimately disagree. The quoted variant runs at quote creation, using the bank's stewardship funding state and demand index as they stand at that moment, and the figure it produces never changes afterwards. The executed variant runs again at settlement: it re-reads the stewardship funding state — which may have been cured in the meantime, clearing the 8 percent surcharge — and re-runs the demand measurement against the settlement-time clock, and then applies one further nested ceiling on top of the price cap already described. The settled price is whichever is smaller of the freshly recomputed price and the quoted price multiplied by 1.10 and rounded half up to whole cents. A settled price may therefore land below the quote, when a surcharge has cleared, or above it, when demand has risen — never more than 10 percent above it — so a single sale row quite legitimately holds two different prices.

Reservation belongs exclusively to the buying permit holder acting on its own sale. It advances the sale `QUOTED → RESERVED`, requires the bank's posture to still be exactly `OPEN`, and re-checks the requested quantity against the bank's available credits, because credits may have left the pool since the quote was priced. One further check protects the bank from a buyer sitting on overlapping options: the prospective hold, running from the current clock through 30 days later, may not intersect the hold interval of any other reserved sale belonging to the same permit holder. Both the prospective and the existing intervals are closed at both ends, so touching endpoints do collide — a new hold beginning exactly at an existing hold's expiry instant is rejected, precisely because the expiry sweep only releases a hold once the clock has passed strictly beyond it. A collision is rejected with `ERR_RESERVATION_HOLD_CONFLICT`. Reservation stamps `hold_start_at` with the current clock and `hold_expires_at` 30 days later, appends a `HOLD_PLACED` entry carrying the requested credits as a negative delta and attributed through a source entity type of `CREDIT_SALE` and the sale's own identifier, recomputes the bank's credits on hold and available credits and its posture, recomputes the bank's `open_quote_count`, and increments the bank's `version` by one.

Settlement belongs to the sponsor. It advances the sale `RESERVED → EXECUTED` and requires the bank's posture to be `OPEN` or `SOLD_OUT`, the logical clock to be no later than the hold's expiry instant, and the buyer's escrow balance to be at least the executed-variant price computed for this very request — a shortfall is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ESCROW` and carries HTTP 402. On success the settled price is stamped onto `executed_price_cents`, `executed_at` is stamped with the clock, and **two** ledger entries are appended in a pinned order: first a `HOLD_RELEASED` entry returning the requested credits as a positive delta, then a `SOLD` entry taking the same quantity away as a negative delta. The net effect on the balance is zero, but the two entries receive consecutive sequence numbers and different running balances, and that order is part of the contract. The bank's credits on hold, credits sold and available credits are recomputed, the buyer's escrow balance is recomputed as deposits minus settled prices, the posture is recomputed and lands on `SOLD_OUT` when available credits have reached exactly zero, and the bank's `version` increments by one.

Assignment is the sponsor's final act on a sale and the point at which the credits actually discharge a permit. It advances the sale `EXECUTED → ASSIGNED`, requires the requested quantity not to exceed the buyer's remaining unmet obligation, stamps `assigned_at` with the clock, and generates an `assignment_reference` formed as the buyer's permit number, a forward slash, the bank's site watershed code, another forward slash, and the requested credit quantity. A ledger entry of type `ASSIGNED` is appended for the audit trail carrying a delta of exactly 0.00, so the running balance is unchanged, and the buyer's assigned credits and unmet obligation are recomputed. An assigned sale is terminal.

Withdrawal is available to either party while a sale is still uncommitted, and requires a `cancelled_reason` drawn from `BUYER_WITHDREW`, `SPONSOR_WITHDREW`, `BANK_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`, `HOLD_EXPIRED` or `IRT_REVERSAL`. Cancelling a quote advances `QUOTED → CANCELLED`, stamps the reason and `cancelled_at`, and lowers the bank's open quote count; no ledger entry is written because no hold ever existed. Cancelling a reservation advances `RESERVED → CANCELLED`, and additionally appends a `HOLD_RELEASED` entry returning the held credits to the available pool before recomputing the bank's on-hold and available credits and its posture. Any attempt to reserve a sale that is not quoted, settle one that is not reserved, assign one that is not settled, withdraw one that is already settled, assigned, cancelled or expired, or reverse one that is not settled, is rejected with `ERR_SALE_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.9. Reversal, Demand Forecasting, Observability and Closing the Bank

Occasionally a settled sale has to be undone — a permit is vacated, an authorisation is withdrawn, the review team discovers the transaction should never have completed. Reversal is the chair's exclusive compensating transaction and is available only on a sale standing in `EXECUTED`; an assigned sale is terminal and can never be reversed, because its credits have already discharged a permit obligation. Reversal advances the sale `EXECUTED → CANCELLED` with a `cancelled_reason` of `IRT_REVERSAL` and a `cancelled_at` stamp, appends a `SALE_REVERSED` entry carrying the requested credits back as a positive delta, and recomputes the bank's sold credits and available credits so that the ledger balance returns exactly to its pre-settlement value with no drift whatsoever. Because the reversed sale has left the settled-and-assigned set, the buyer's escrow balance is recomputed and the full settled price returns to escrow. The settled price itself is deliberately **retained** on the sale row for audit rather than nulled out, so the record of what was charged survives the reversal. The posture is recomputed and the bank's `version` increments by one.

Demand forecasting closes the commercial loop by projecting permitting activity forward. A computed read on a bank reports its `demand_index` — the same trailing four-quarter measurement used in pricing — alongside four forward-looking figures and the bank's four ledger aggregates. `trailing_quarter_average_credits` is the total requested credits of the bank's sales standing in `EXECUTED` or `ASSIGNED` whose settlement instant falls inside the trailing 360-day window measured back from the current clock, divided by 4, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. `forecast_horizon_credits` is that quarterly average multiplied by 4 and rounded half up to 2 decimal places, expressing a one-year forward sales expectation at the current rate. `projected_quarters_to_sellout` is 0 whenever the bank has no available credits at all; otherwise it is the available credits divided by the trailing quarterly average, rounded **up** to a whole number of quarters, with the divisor floored at 0.01 credits so that a bank with no sales history yields an enormous rather than an undefined projection, and with the whole result capped at a forecast horizon of 40 quarters. The read also reports `credits_available`, `credits_released`, `credits_sold` and `credits_on_hold` so that a caller can reconcile the projection against the ledger in one round trip.

Observability across the system is provided by filtered flat list reads. Banks may be filtered by `sponsor_id`, by `status` and by `sales_posture`. Release milestones may be filtered by `bank_id`, by `status` and by `predecessor_milestone_id`, the latter being how a caller walks a release chain forward. Monitoring reports may be filtered by `bank_id`, by `reviewed_by_member_id`, by `review_status` and by `compliance_status`, the two status dimensions being independently selectable exactly as they are independently maintained. Remedial actions may be filtered by `monitoring_report_id`, by `deferred_milestone_id`, by `verified_by_member_id` and by `status`. Credit sales may be filtered by `bank_id`, by `permittee_id` and by `status`. A bank's ledger may be filtered by `entry_type`. Prospectus revisions of a bank may be filtered by `status`, as may a bank's work plans, and the votes of a prospectus and the votes of a member are each listable in their own right. All of these accept the standard `limit` and `offset` pagination parameters of §2.3.

Closure is the chair's final act and the strictest gate in the document. It advances the bank `MONITORING → CLOSED` and is admissible only from `MONITORING`. Every release milestone on the bank must stand in `APPROVED` and no remedial action of the bank may be in `OPEN`, `WORK_SUBMITTED`, `REINSPECTION` or `FAILED`; a breach of either is rejected with `ERR_MILESTONES_INCOMPLETE`. The conservation easement must be recorded, or the request is rejected with `ERR_EASEMENT_NOT_RECORDED`. And the stewardship endowment must be funded in full — here the final gate applies, not the interim one, so `stewardship_funded_cents` must be at least `stewardship_required_cents` — with a shortfall rejected as `ERR_STEWARDSHIP_UNDERFUNDED`. On success, `closed_at` is stamped with the logical clock, the posture is recomputed to `FROZEN`, every credit sale of the bank still standing in `QUOTED` or `RESERVED` is cancelled with a `cancelled_reason` of `SPONSOR_WITHDREW` and each formerly reserved sale appends a `HOLD_RELEASED` entry restoring its held credits, and the bank's `version` increments by one. `CLOSED` is terminal: a closed bank accepts no further writes of any kind, and attempts are rejected with `ERR_BANK_STATE_INVALID`.
