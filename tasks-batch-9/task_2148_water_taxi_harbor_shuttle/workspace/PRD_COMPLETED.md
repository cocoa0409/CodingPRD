# Water Taxi & Harbor Shuttle API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A harbor water-taxi operator runs two products off the same fleet: a published timetable of shuttle sailings between waterfront stops, and an on-demand hail service that matches riders to whatever hull is free. Both products share the same scarce physical resources — a small number of certificated vessels, a roster of licensed masters, and berths at docks that are co-leased with other operators. What makes the operation hard is that none of these resources are independent. A rising wind derates every sailing on the day at once. A falling tide can strand a deep-draft hull at a shallow stop. A master whose medical certificate lapsed overnight cannot take a boat out even though the boat itself is perfectly seaworthy. And when a stadium event floods the waterfront with twenty thousand extra people, the operator must plan surge capacity days ahead while still honouring the multi-ride passes sold at last winter's discount.

This document specifies the REST API that runs that operation end to end. The API is mounted under `/harbor`, with a single administrative exception described in §2.5. It covers the physical estate (docks, fare-zone stops, vessels, crew), the daily operating picture (weather, tide, events, and the derived posture that gates every vessel movement), the commercial layer (fare quotation, multi-ride passes, bookings, dispatch), the berth ledger (slot reservations, landing fees, shared-dock rebates and monthly caps), and the aftermath of a bad day (service interruptions, refund claims, and the segregation-of-duties controls that govern who may pay them out).

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, money is conserved: landing fees are posted provisionally at departure and must be reversed before the final posting at completion, so that a dock's running accrual always equals the sum of what its slots actually carry. Second, safety gates are ordered, not parallel: when several conditions fail at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined by the rejection ladder in §2.8. Implementers should read those two sections before anything else, because almost every workflow in §3 leans on them.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the system-wide rules, contracts, and boilerplate that apply across the whole API surface.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is missing or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six recognised role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs before anything else — before path resolution, before payload validation, before any business rule.

The six roles are `HarborAdmin`, `Dispatcher`, `VesselMaster`, `MarineInspector`, `Rider`, and `FareAuditor`. A `HarborAdmin` is the shoreside administrator who owns the physical estate — docks, stops, vessels, crew — and files the daily weather, tide and event observations. A `Dispatcher` runs the sailing plan: creating trips, booking berths, offering trips to riders, and declaring service interruptions. A `VesselMaster` is the licensed master of a specific vessel movement. A `MarineInspector` is an independent surveyor and is the only role permitted to file inspection records; an inspector may never create or move the vessel they survey. A `Rider` is a travelling customer. A `FareAuditor` sits in revenue control and decides refund claims. Once the acting role is known, it is matched against the roles authorised for the endpoint in §4; a role acting outside its remit is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

Identity semantics differ by role, and the difference matters. When `X-User-Role` is `VesselMaster`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CrewMember`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `CrewMember` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, which in practice means a boarding, departure or completion request is honoured only when `X-User-ID` equals the trip's `master_crew_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `HarborAdmin`, `Dispatcher`, `MarineInspector`, `Rider`, or `FareAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator or customer identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — as `inspector_user_id` on an inspection filed by a `MarineInspector`, as `declared_by_user_id` on a `ServiceInterruption` declared by a `Dispatcher`, as `declarer_user_id` and `approver_user_id` on a `RefundClaim`, and as `rider_user_id` on a `Booking` or `RidePass` created by a `Rider` — but it is never compared to any entity's `id`. For `Rider`-owned resources, ownership is nevertheless enforced: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the stored `rider_user_id` of the booking or pass being acted on. Any ownership mismatch, whether on a rider resource or on a master's trip, yields `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

One further access control is structural rather than role-based. The `Dispatcher` who declares a service interruption may never be the `FareAuditor` who decides a claim that interruption created; the comparison is between the acting `X-User-ID` and the claim's `declarer_user_id`, and a match is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside these bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless an endpoint's description in §4 says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The only stated exception is the inspection listing for a vessel, which is ordered by `performed_on` descending so the most recent survey appears first. List responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` in the `meta` envelope, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` — a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied `id` in a creation payload is ignored. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped at creation and never modified thereafter.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. `Dock`, `Stop`, `Vessel`, `CrewMember`, `ServiceDay`, `Trip`, `DockSlot`, `RidePass`, `Booking`, `ServiceInterruption`, and `RefundClaim` all carry the full set. `VesselInspection` is the single exception: an inspection is an immutable event record, so it carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and neither field appears in its representation.

Version increments are not confined to the row the client addressed. A cascade that touches a second entity increments that entity's `version` too, and refreshes its `updated_at`. The individual cascades are described where they occur in §3, but the rule is uniform: if a row's stored data changed as a result of a request, its `version` advanced by 1 and its `updated_at` was refreshed.

### 2.5. The Logical Harbor Clock

The server does not read wall-clock time. All time-dependent behaviour — certificate expiry, inspection validity, layup windows, pass validity, dispatch eligibility, and the departure and arrival stamps — is evaluated against a **logical clock** whose value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` until a `HarborAdmin` moves it, and which never falls back to real time. A `HarborAdmin` may set the clock by supplying `to_iso8601`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant, which is truncated to whole seconds before it is stored; the response body reports the new value under `current_utc`, rendered in ISO 8601 UTC with fractional digits only when the stored instant carries a non-zero millisecond part — which after truncation it never does, so a clock reading is written `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. That same second-resolution rendering is used wherever the clock is later stamped onto a row, namely `actual_departure_utc` and `actual_arrival_utc`. This is the one capability mounted outside the `/harbor` prefix, at the path given in §4.

Moving the clock has no direct write side effect on stored rows, but it changes what those rows derive to: every `Vessel.service_state` and `annual_inspection_expires_on` is re-evaluated against the new instant on the next read as well as at every gate check. A `RidePass.status`, by contrast, is stored rather than derived: the pass-expiry comparison is made only at the confirmation gate of §3.6, so a pass read after its `valid_until` has slipped past still reports `ACTIVE` until a confirmation tenders it and moves it `ACTIVE → EXPIRED`. Where a rule compares a stored calendar date to "today", the comparison uses the calendar date of the logical clock instant in UTC.

### 2.6. Money, Precision and Basis Points

All monetary amounts in this API are integers denominated in whole cents; there are no fractional-cent values anywhere in the system, in storage or in transit. Wherever a rule multiplies a monetary amount by a rate, the rate is expressed in **basis points** — hundredths of a percent — so that a factor of one is written as 10000, a 25 percent uplift as 12500, and a 15 percent rebate as 1500. Applying a basis-point rate means multiplying the amount by the rate and dividing by 10000.

Every division that could produce a fraction is resolved by rounding half up to a whole cent: exactly one half of a cent rounds away from zero, upward. Rounding is applied at the specific points named in the relevant rule in §3 and nowhere else — collapsing two pinned rounding steps into one final rounding is a different number and is wrong. Where physical quantities are involved, the conventions are: metres to two decimals (`draft_m`, `charted_depth_m`, `low_water_m`, `wave_height_m`, and any clearance derived from them, which is itself rounded half up to two decimals before comparison); knots to one decimal (`wind_knots`); berth occupancy floored to whole minutes; seat counts as integers with every derate floored; and calendar comparisons at day resolution. Instants are compared at whole-second resolution.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Berths and Seats

Two write endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent row's `version`, and only those two. No other endpoint accepts or requires a version field.

A berth-reservation request — `POST /harbor/trips/{id}/dock-slots` — must carry `expected_dock_version`, a non-optional integer in the request body carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Dock`. If it does not equal that dock's current `version` at the moment the request is processed, the reservation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DOCK_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A second reservation against the same dock is entirely legitimate whenever `berth_count` still has room, so what the server rejects here is not a conflicting slot but a stale view of the dock: the client is asking to add occupancy to a dock picture it has not actually seen.

A booking-confirmation request — `POST /harbor/bookings/{id}/confirm` — must carry `expected_trip_version`, a non-optional integer in the request body carrying the client's last observed `version` of the offered `Trip`. If it does not equal the trip's current `version`, the confirmation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TRIP_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Because another rider's confirmation supersedes the seat picture the current client is holding, and because the second confirmation would frequently still fit inside the remaining capacity and therefore succeed silently, the server resolves the situation as a stale-version conflict on the trip rather than as a capacity question.

### 2.8. The Order of Rejection

When more than one rule would reject a request, exactly one error is returned, and it is always the one belonging to the lowest-numbered rung of the following ladder. The ladder is evaluated strictly in ascending order and evaluation stops at the first failure.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` one of the six declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorisation.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised list in §4 — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path resolution.** Every id embedded in the URL path resolves to an existing row, validated left to right, so an outer id that does not resolve is reported before any inner id is looked at. The error is the one belonging to the missing resource type: `ERR_DOCK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VESSEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_DAY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRIP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DOCK_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RIDE_PASS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFUND_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_CREW_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint that carries an id in its path.
4. **Body foreign-key resolution.** Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves, validated in the order the fields are listed for that endpoint in §3, yielding `ERR_STOP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VESSEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_DAY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DOCK_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_RIDE_PASS_NOT_FOUND` (all 404). This rung applies to trip creation, berth reservation, and booking creation. A body foreign key that is absent, null, or not a non-empty string cannot be resolved at all and is never reported here: it falls through to rung 6 as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
5. **Ownership.** For rider-owned resources the acting `X-User-ID` equals the booking's or pass's `rider_user_id`; for master actions it equals the trip's `master_crew_id` — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This rung applies to booking decline, booking confirm, pass refund, trip boarding, trip departure, and trip completion.
6. **Payload validity.** All required fields present, of the declared type, inside the declared numeric range, and every enum value a declared member — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
7. **Time windows.** A trip's `scheduled_arrival_utc` is strictly after its `scheduled_departure_utc`; a berth slot's `end_utc` is strictly after its `start_utc`; a vessel's `layup_end_date` is not earlier than its `layup_start_date`; and a completion's clock reading is strictly after the trip's `actual_departure_utc`. Any breach is `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW` (422). This rung applies to trip creation, berth reservation, vessel registration, and trip completion.
8. **Distinct endpoints.** `origin_stop_id` differs from `destination_stop_id` — otherwise `ERR_SAME_ORIGIN_DESTINATION` (422). Applies to trip creation and booking creation.
9. **Calendar uniqueness.** A `ServiceDay` may not be opened for a `service_date` that already has one — otherwise `ERR_SERVICE_DAY_DUPLICATE` (409). Applies to service-day creation.
10. **State preconditions.** The target resource is in the state the requested transition departs from, yielding one of `ERR_TRIP_NOT_SCHEDULED`, `ERR_TRIP_NOT_BOARDING`, `ERR_TRIP_NOT_DEPARTED`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_REQUESTED`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_OFFERED`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_CONFIRMED`, `ERR_PASS_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_REFUND_CLAIM_NOT_OPEN`, or `ERR_INTERRUPTION_NOT_DECLARABLE` (all 409). Applies to every state-changing endpoint. Two of those identifiers are raised by their own endpoint lower down the ladder rather than at this rung: on booking confirmation the tendered pass's `ERR_PASS_NOT_ACTIVE` is checked directly below rung 22, so this rung's `ERR_PASS_NOT_ACTIVE` governs only pass refund; and on trip boarding the manifest's `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_CONFIRMED` is checked below rung 12 and above rung 25.
11. **Optimistic concurrency.** The supplied `expected_dock_version` or `expected_trip_version` equals the parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_DOCK_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_TRIP_VERSION` (412). Applies to berth reservation and booking confirmation.
12. **Suspended service.** The governing day's `posture` is not `SUSPENDED` — otherwise `ERR_SERVICE_SUSPENDED` (409). Applies to trip boarding, trip departure, and booking confirmation.
13. **Vessel availability.** At departure the vessel's `service_state` is `IN_SERVICE`; at trip creation the trip's calendar window does not overlap the vessel's layup window. A breach is `ERR_VESSEL_IN_LAYUP` or `ERR_VESSEL_OUT_OF_SERVICE` (409). Applies to trip creation and trip departure.
14. **Annual inspection.** The latest `ANNUAL` inspection exists, is not a `FAIL`, has not expired, and under `RESTRICTED` posture carries no more than two open deficiencies — otherwise `ERR_VESSEL_INSPECTION_INVALID` (422). Applies to trip departure.
15. **Hull class.** Under `RESTRICTED` posture only an `OFFSHORE` hull may sail — otherwise `ERR_HULL_CLASS_RESTRICTED` (422). Applies to trip departure.
16. **Crew currency.** The master is `ACTIVE` and both certificates are current — otherwise `ERR_CREW_CERT_EXPIRED` (422). Applies to trip departure.
17. **Endorsement rank.** The master's endorsement is at least the vessel's requirement — otherwise `ERR_CREW_ENDORSEMENT_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Applies to trip departure.
18. **Under-keel clearance.** The clearance is at least the safety minimum — otherwise `ERR_UNDER_KEEL_CLEARANCE` (422). Applies to trip departure.
19. **Berth occupancy.** The candidate berth window does not push buffered occupancy on the dock up to `berth_count` — otherwise `ERR_DOCK_BERTH_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to berth reservation.
20. **Pass validity date.** The tendered pass's `valid_until` is not earlier than the logical clock's calendar date — otherwise `ERR_PASS_EXPIRED` (409). Applies to booking confirmation.
21. **Slot on route.** The dock named on a berth slot is the dock of the trip's origin stop or of its destination stop — otherwise `ERR_SLOT_DOCK_NOT_ON_ROUTE` (422). Applies to berth reservation.
22. **Pass zone coverage.** The tendered pass's `zone_tier` covers the booking's zone span — otherwise `ERR_PASS_ZONE_NOT_COVERED` (422). Applies to booking confirmation. Immediately below this rung, and still above rung 25, the tendered pass's `status` must be `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_PASS_NOT_ACTIVE` (409).
23. **Segregation of duties.** The acting auditor's `X-User-ID` differs from the claim's `declarer_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to refund-claim decision.
24. **Dispatch pool.** The candidate pool is non-empty — otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TRIP` (409). Applies to booking dispatch.
25. **Seat capacity.** The confirmation's party fits in the remaining capacity, and a boarding manifest's total party size fits inside `effective_capacity` — otherwise `ERR_TRIP_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to booking confirmation and trip boarding.
26. **Ride balance.** The tendered pass has at least as many rides left as the booking's `party_size` — otherwise `ERR_PASS_NO_RIDES_REMAINING` (409). Applies to booking confirmation.

Three consequences of this ordering are worth spelling out because they are easy to get backwards. A trip that has already departed and is asked to depart again on a day whose under-keel clearance is below the safety minimum is rejected with `ERR_TRIP_NOT_BOARDING`, not `ERR_UNDER_KEEL_CLEARANCE`, because the state rung sits above the safety rungs. An `INNER` pass with zero rides left, tendered for a three-zone hop, is rejected with `ERR_PASS_ZONE_NOT_COVERED`, not `ERR_PASS_NO_RIDES_REMAINING`, because zone coverage is checked before the ride balance. And a confirmation carrying a stale `expected_trip_version` for a party that would also overflow the trip's capacity is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TRIP_VERSION`, not `ERR_TRIP_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, because the concurrency rung sits above the capacity rung.

### 2.9. Conserved Quantities in the Harbor Ledger

Three quantities in this system are conserved, meaning each is defined by an identity that must hold before a forward action, in the window between a forward action and its compensating action, and after the compensation. Implementations that treat any of them as a free-running counter will drift, and the drift is invisible endpoint by endpoint because every individual call still returns a success status.

**Ride credits on a pass.** A pass's `rides_purchased` always equals its `rides_remaining` plus the sum of `party_size` over every booking that names that pass in `ride_pass_id` and whose `redemption_status` is `REDEEMED`. The forward action is a confirmation that tenders the pass; the compensating action is the posture cascade interrupting that booking, which hands the rides back and moves the booking's `redemption_status` to `RESTORED`. A `RESTORED` booking must not be counted in that sum — counting it silently loses a ride.

**Landing-fee money on a dock.** A dock's `accrued_fee_cents` always equals the sum of `landing_fee_cents` over its slots in `CONSUMED` plus the sum of `projected_fee_cents` over its slots in `RESERVED`. The forward action is a departure posting a provisional amount into the accrual; the compensating action is the completion subtracting exactly that provisional amount back out before the final amount is posted. Skip the reversal and the accrual drifts by precisely the provisional figure. The identity is asserted after a voided slot too, where both terms fall to zero.

**Committed seats on a trip.** A trip's `seats_committed` always equals the sum of `party_size` over its bookings in `CONFIRMED` or `BOARDED`. The forward action is a confirmation; the compensating action is the posture cascade moving that booking to `INTERRUPTED`. Boarding and completion recompute the figure from that same sum: boarding leaves it unchanged, because a `BOARDED` booking still counts, while completion drives it to 0, because every booking that was counted in it has by then become `COMPLETED`.

### 2.10. How Cascades Propagate

Two multi-entity cascades run in this system, and both are named here so that §3 can refer to them by name.

**Cascade A** is the suspension cascade. It fires when an operating day's `posture` is recomputed to `SUSPENDED`, and it works through every non-terminal trip on that day in ascending `scheduled_departure_utc`, ties broken by ascending `created_at`. Each such trip is cancelled; each of that trip's reserved berth slots is voided; each of that trip's live bookings is interrupted; and each interrupted booking that had redeemed pass rides gets those rides back in kind.

**Cascade B** is the settlement cascade. It fires when a trip completes, and it reverses each reserved slot's provisional posting out of the dock accrual, posts the final fee, consumes the slot, evaluates the dock's monthly cap, and — if the cap flips — pre-locks the remaining reserved slots on that same dock so that any later posting on them computes to zero.

Both cascades increment the `version` and refresh the `updated_at` of every row they touch, not merely the row the client addressed.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Harbor Estate

Before a single sailing can be planned, a `HarborAdmin` must describe the physical estate. Registering a dock records its `name` (a required string of 1 to 80 characters), its `berth_count` (at least 1 — the number of berths that can be occupied at once), and its `min_turnaround_minutes` (whole minutes, at least 0, defaulting to 15 when omitted), which is the buffer the operator needs between two boats using the same berth. Three tariff fields go on the dock as well: `landing_fee_per_pax_cents`, the per-passenger landing charge, and `berth_minute_rate_cents`, the per-berth-minute occupancy charge, both integers of at least 0; and `monthly_fee_cap_cents`, an integer of at least 1 that is the hard ceiling on what this dock may ever accrue. A seventh field, `shared_operator_count` (at least 1, defaulting to 1), records how many operators share the dock. It exists purely for reporting: no calculation, no rebate tier, and no selector in this system reads it. A newly registered dock starts with `accrued_fee_cents` at 0 and `fee_status` at `ACCRUING`, the other permissible value being `CAPPED`.

Fare-zone stops hang off docks. Adding a stop supplies its `name` (again 1 to 80 characters, required), its `zone_index` — an integer from 1 to 6 that places the stop in the fare-zone ordinal scheme — and its `charted_depth_m`, the charted depth at the stop in metres above chart datum to two decimals, at least 0.00 — which, like the dock's `shared_operator_count`, exists purely for reporting: under-keel clearance is derived from the operating day's `low_water_m` alone, and no calculation, gate or selector in this system reads `charted_depth_m`. The stop's `dock_id` is taken from the dock addressed in the request rather than from the payload, which is how a stop comes to know which berths it lands at. The stop listing for a dock is the reverse view of that relationship and accepts `limit` and `offset`.

Vessels are registered with a `name` of 1 to 80 characters, a `seat_capacity` of at least 1 (the certificated seated passenger capacity), a `draft_m` strictly greater than 0.00 metres to two decimals, a `hull_class` of either `HARBOR` or `OFFSHORE`, and a `required_endorsement` of either `MASTER_25T` or `MASTER_100T` naming the minimum licence a master needs to take her out. A vessel may optionally declare a seasonal winter-layup window through `layup_start_date` and `layup_end_date`, both calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form and both defaulting to null when the vessel has no layup planned; when they are supplied, `layup_end_date` may not be earlier than `layup_start_date`. The window is inclusive at both ends — the vessel is laid up on the start date and still laid up on the end date. Registration derives the vessel's `service_state` immediately, from the logical clock and the layup window.

A vessel's availability is never written directly; `service_state` is re-derived from the logical clock, the layup window, and the vessel's inspection history at every read and at every gate check, and takes one of three values: `IN_SERVICE`, `LAID_UP`, or `OUT_OF_SERVICE`. The derivation is strictly ordered. Take the vessel's inspections whose `inspection_type` is `ANNUAL`, ignoring `PRE_DEPARTURE` rows entirely no matter how recent they are, and sort them by `performed_on` descending with ties broken by `created_at` descending; the first row is the latest annual inspection, and there may be none. If that latest annual inspection exists and its `result` is `FAIL`, the vessel reads `OUT_OF_SERVICE`. Otherwise, if both `layup_start_date` and `layup_end_date` are non-null and the logical clock's calendar date falls on or after `layup_start_date` and on or before `layup_end_date`, the vessel reads `LAID_UP`; a vessel carrying only one of the two dates has no usable window and reads `IN_SERVICE`. Otherwise it reads `IN_SERVICE`. A failed annual inspection therefore outranks a layup window: a failed vessel reads `OUT_OF_SERVICE` even inside its layup dates. The same latest-annual row supplies `annual_inspection_expires_on`, which mirrors that row's `expires_on`, or is null when the vessel has no annual inspection at all.

Only a `MarineInspector` may file an inspection, and inspections are immutable event records rather than editable resources. Filing one names the `inspection_type` (`ANNUAL` or `PRE_DEPARTURE`), the `result` (`PASS`, `PASS_WITH_DEFICIENCIES`, or `FAIL`), the `deficiency_count` (an integer of at least 0, defaulting to 0, counting open deficiencies the survey recorded), and `performed_on`, the calendar date the survey was carried out and the primary sort key of the latest-annual selection above. The `vessel_id` comes from the vessel addressed in the request. On filing, the server computes `expires_on` as `performed_on` plus 365 days for an `ANNUAL` row and leaves it null for a `PRE_DEPARTURE` row; stamps `inspector_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`; re-derives the vessel's `service_state` and `annual_inspection_expires_on`; and increments the vessel's `version` by 1. `PRE_DEPARTURE` rows are advisory and gate nothing at any point in this system. The inspection listing for a vessel returns rows newest `performed_on` first, ties broken by `created_at` descending, and accepts an `inspection_type` filter alongside `limit` and `offset`.

The consequences of filing land on the vessel's derived availability. Filing an `ANNUAL` inspection with `result` `FAIL` that becomes the latest annual row moves the vessel `IN_SERVICE → OUT_OF_SERVICE`, and every subsequent departure attempt on that hull is rejected with `ERR_VESSEL_OUT_OF_SERVICE`; the vessel's `version` increments by 1. Filing a later `ANNUAL` inspection whose `result` is `PASS` or `PASS_WITH_DEFICIENCIES`, on a `performed_on` that makes it the new latest annual row, moves the vessel `OUT_OF_SERVICE → IN_SERVICE` and again increments the vessel's `version` by 1. The layup edges are driven by the clock rather than by any inspection: advancing the logical clock into the inclusive window moves the vessel `IN_SERVICE → LAID_UP` (provided the latest annual inspection is not a `FAIL`), from which point departure attempts are rejected with `ERR_VESSEL_IN_LAYUP`; advancing the clock past `layup_end_date`, or back before `layup_start_date`, moves it `LAID_UP → IN_SERVICE` and the vessel becomes departable again.

Crew registration is the last piece of the estate. A crew member has a `name` of 1 to 80 characters, an `endorsement_class` of `MASTER_25T` or `MASTER_100T`, a `cert_expires_on` giving the last calendar date the licence endorsement remains current, and a `medical_expires_on` giving the last calendar date the medical certificate remains current — both inclusive. The optional `status` is `ACTIVE` by default and may be set to `SUSPENDED`; a suspended crew member may not master a departure. Endorsements rank: `MASTER_25T` is the lower rank and `MASTER_100T` the higher, and wherever this document speaks of an endorsement being sufficient it means the held class is of equal or higher rank than the class demanded. A new crew member starts with `trips_mastered_count` at 0; that counter is advanced only by trips actually reaching completion under that master.

### 3.2. The Operating Day: Posture, Tide and Event Surge

Everything that moves on the water is gated by an operating day. A `HarborAdmin` opens one for a given `service_date` — a calendar date that must be unique across all operating days, a second attempt on the same date being rejected with `ERR_SERVICE_DAY_DUPLICATE` — and files the day's observations: `wind_knots`, the observed sustained wind in knots to one decimal and at least 0.0; `wave_height_m`, the observed significant wave height in metres to two decimals and at least 0.00; and `low_water_m`, the predicted lowest water level of the day in metres above chart datum, to two decimals and at least 0.00. Three optional fields complete the picture: `manual_suspension`, a boolean defaulting to false that lets the operator force the day shut regardless of what the instruments say; `event_name`, defaulting to null, naming the stadium or arena event driving waterfront surge on that date; and `expected_attendance`, an integer of at least 0 defaulting to 0, the forecast crowd for that event.

From those observations alone the server derives the day's `posture`, which is one of `NORMAL`, `RESTRICTED`, or `SUSPENDED`. No trip, booking, or vessel ever feeds back into the posture — it is a function of the day's own row and nothing else. The derivation is a ladder, and the first matching rung wins. If `manual_suspension` is true, the day is `SUSPENDED`. Otherwise, if `wind_knots` is at least 35.0, the day is `SUSPENDED`. Otherwise, if `wave_height_m` is at least 2.50 metres, the day is `SUSPENDED`. Otherwise, if `wind_knots` is at least 22.0, the day is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise, if `wave_height_m` is at least 1.20 metres, the day is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise, if `low_water_m` is strictly below 1.80 metres, the day is `RESTRICTED`. If none of those rungs matches, the day is `NORMAL`. The boundaries are exact: a wind of exactly 22.0 knots restricts, a wind of exactly 35.0 knots suspends, a wave of exactly 1.20 metres restricts, a wave of exactly 2.50 metres suspends, and a predicted low water of exactly 1.80 metres does not restrict at all, because that comparison alone is strict. Comparisons are made on the stored rounded values — wind at one decimal, wave and water at two.

The same request derives the day's event plan. When `event_name` is null the day's `surge_tier` is `NONE`, however large `expected_attendance` may be. When `event_name` is present, an `expected_attendance` of at least 30000 makes the tier `PEAK`, an attendance of at least 15000 but below 30000 makes it `ELEVATED`, and anything below 15000 leaves it at `NONE`; exactly 15000 is `ELEVATED` and exactly 30000 is `PEAK`. The tier then fixes two derived fields outright: `surge_multiplier_bp` is 10000 basis points at `NONE`, 12500 at `ELEVATED`, and 15000 at `PEAK`; and `planned_extra_vessels` — the operator's capacity plan for the event — is 0 at `NONE`, 1 at `ELEVATED`, and 2 at `PEAK`.

Because the posture is derived rather than declared, a day may be born in any of the three postures: a day opened with a 40-knot gale is `SUSPENDED` from the moment it is created. Revising the observations later re-runs both derivations. A `HarborAdmin` may update any subset of `wind_knots`, `wave_height_m`, `low_water_m`, `manual_suspension`, `event_name`, and `expected_attendance`; the recomputed posture then decides what happens next, and the day's `version` increments by 1 in every case. Every edge of the three-by-three posture matrix is reachable and re-reachable. Moving `NORMAL → RESTRICTED` or `RESTRICTED → NORMAL` recomputes the `effective_capacity` of every trip on that day, since the posture factor is one of the inputs to it. Any update whose recomputed posture comes out `SUSPENDED` fires Cascade A across every non-terminal trip on the day, processed in ascending `scheduled_departure_utc` with ties broken by ascending `created_at`, as described in §3.9. The trigger is the recomputed value, not the transition, so `NORMAL → SUSPENDED` and `RESTRICTED → SUSPENDED` fire it and so does a `SUSPENDED → SUSPENDED` update that merely revises the observations of an already-suspended day — on that repeat firing the cascade simply finds no non-terminal trip left and changes nothing. The recovery edges are deliberately asymmetric: moving `SUSPENDED → NORMAL` or `SUSPENDED → RESTRICTED` performs no un-cancellation whatsoever. Trips cancelled by an earlier suspension stay cancelled, their slots stay voided, and their bookings stay interrupted; the day simply becomes operable again for whatever is planned afterwards.

### 3.3. Planning a Sailing and Sizing It

A `Dispatcher` plans sailings. Creating one names the operating day it belongs to through `service_day_id`, the hull through `vessel_id`, the licensed master through `master_crew_id`, the route through `origin_stop_id` and `destination_stop_id`, and the product through `service_mode`, which is `SCHEDULED` for a published timetable sailing or `ON_DEMAND` for a sailing opened up to dispatch of on-demand hails. The timetable itself is `scheduled_departure_utc` and `scheduled_arrival_utc`, both ISO 8601 UTC instants, the arrival strictly after the departure. The two stops must differ.

Trip creation is where the seasonal layup window is enforced against the calendar, and the geometry here is closed at both ends and concerns a single occupant — the vessel itself — with no buffer of any kind. If either `layup_start_date` or `layup_end_date` is null there is no conflict and the trip may be created. Otherwise, take the trip's first calendar day as the UTC date of `scheduled_departure_utc` and its last calendar day as the UTC date of `scheduled_arrival_utc`; the trip conflicts with the layup when its first day falls on or before `layup_end_date` and its last day falls on or after `layup_start_date`, and the creation is then rejected with `ERR_VESSEL_IN_LAYUP`. Because both ends of the layup window are inclusive, a trip whose departure date is exactly `layup_end_date` does conflict — a deliberate contrast with the berth rule in §3.4, where an occupancy ending exactly when the candidate begins does not by itself conflict. Only the layup window stored on this vessel is consulted; no other vessel's window participates.

A newly created trip is `SCHEDULED` on its movement axis and `UNSETTLED` on its money axis, with `seats_committed` at 0, `booked_pax` and `boarded_pax` at 0, `provisional_fee_cents` and `final_fee_cents` at 0, `actual_departure_utc` and `actual_arrival_utc` null, and `cancellation_reason` null. The two axes are genuinely independent: `operational_status` moves through `SCHEDULED`, `BOARDING`, `DEPARTED`, and terminates in either `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`, while `settlement_status` moves through `UNSETTLED`, `PROVISIONAL`, and terminates in either `FINAL` or `VOID`. They are written by different clauses of the same operations, and `UNSETTLED` persists across the `SCHEDULED → BOARDING` move because opening boarding posts no money at all.

The most consequential derived field on a trip is `effective_capacity`, the number of seats actually saleable on that sailing. It is computed when the trip is created, recomputed whenever the day's posture changes, and re-derived on every read. Four independent inputs interact non-linearly, and they are drawn from exactly one vessel, one crew member, and one operating day — bookings never feed back into capacity.

Start with three ceilings, each derived from the vessel's `seat_capacity`. The **posture ceiling** applies a percentage factor of 100 percent under `NORMAL`, 70 percent under `RESTRICTED`, and 0 percent under `SUSPENDED`: multiply `seat_capacity` by that percentage, divide by 100, and round the result down to a whole seat. The **tide ceiling** depends on the under-keel clearance, which is the day's `low_water_m` minus the vessel's `draft_m`, rounded half up to two decimals; when that clearance is strictly below 0.60 metres the ceiling is `seat_capacity` divided by two and rounded down to a whole seat, and otherwise it is the full `seat_capacity`. A clearance of exactly 0.60 metres therefore does not derate. The **crew ceiling** is the full `seat_capacity` when the master's `endorsement_class` is `MASTER_100T`, and a flat 24 seats when it is `MASTER_25T`. The smallest of these three ceilings is the trip's core capacity.

On top of the core comes a standing-room bonus of 12 seats, awarded only when all three of the following hold at once: the day's posture is `NORMAL`, the day's `event_name` is not null, and the day's `expected_attendance` is at least 15000. If any of the three fails, the bonus is 0 — which means the bonus is suppressed entirely once the posture leaves `NORMAL`, even on a `PEAK` event day with a sell-out crowd. Finally, the sum of the core capacity and the bonus is capped at the vessel's `seat_capacity` plus 12, and `effective_capacity` is the smaller of the two. Trips can be listed and filtered by `service_day_id`, `vessel_id`, `master_crew_id`, `origin_stop_id`, `destination_stop_id`, `operational_status`, and `settlement_status`, alongside `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.4. Berths on a Shared Dock

Docks in this harbor are co-leased, so a `Dispatcher` must reserve berth time for each trip rather than assume it. A reservation names the `dock_id` it occupies, its `purpose` — `LANDING` for a billable call or `LAYOVER` for a berth held while the boat waits — and the occupancy window as `start_utc` and `end_utc`, both ISO 8601 UTC instants with the end strictly after the start. The window is half-open: the berth is occupied from `start_utc` inclusive up to `end_utc` exclusive. The request also carries `expected_dock_version` as described in §2.7. The trip whose reservation this is comes from the trip addressed in the request, and the dock must actually be on that trip's route: it has to be the dock of the trip's origin stop or the dock of its destination stop, and any other dock is rejected with `ERR_SLOT_DOCK_NOT_ON_ROUTE`. That route test is the last of the reservation's checks rather than the first: the ladder of §2.8 runs the stale-dock-version check at rung 11, then the berth-occupancy count at rung 19, and only then the route test at rung 21, so a reservation that is both off-route and berth-blocked answers `ERR_DOCK_BERTH_CONFLICT`. The trip's own `operational_status` is never tested here at all; a berth may be reserved against a trip in any state, including one already `DEPARTED`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`.

Berth availability is an occupancy count, not a merge. Take the dock's `min_turnaround_minutes` as a buffer in whole minutes and apply it symmetrically to both ends of both intervals: an existing slot occupies the berth from the buffer before its `start_utc` until the buffer after its `end_utc`, and the candidate is tested over the same widened span. Concretely, an existing slot counts as occupied against the candidate when the candidate's `start_utc` falls strictly before that slot's `end_utc` plus the buffer, and that slot's `start_utc` falls strictly before the candidate's `end_utc` plus the buffer. Only slots on the same dock whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `CONSUMED` are counted; a `VOIDED` slot is invisible to the test and releases its berth immediately. Count the occupying slots: if that count is already at or above the dock's `berth_count`, the reservation is rejected with `ERR_DOCK_BERTH_CONFLICT`. In other words, a dock with a `berth_count` of three accepts two overlapping reservations and rejects the third. Two boundary behaviours follow from the strict comparisons. With `min_turnaround_minutes` at 0, two windows that merely touch — one ending at the exact instant the next begins — do not occupy the berth together. With a non-zero turnaround buffer, that same touching pair does occupy it together, because the buffer pushes each interval past the other's edge.

A slot that survives both checks is written in `RESERVED` status with `projected_fee_cents`, `landing_fee_cents`, `pax_basis`, `berth_minutes`, and `rebate_bp` all at 0, and the dock's `version` increments by 1 — which is precisely why a client holding an older `expected_dock_version` is turned away on the next attempt. A reserved slot subsequently either moves `RESERVED → CONSUMED` when its trip completes, carrying the reversal, final posting and cap sequence described in §3.7, or moves `RESERVED → VOIDED` when a posture cascade cancels its trip, in which case both `projected_fee_cents` and `landing_fee_cents` are zeroed and any provisional amount already posted is subtracted back out of the dock's `accrued_fee_cents`. Both are terminal. Slots may be read individually or listed with `dock_id`, `trip_id`, and `status` filters alongside `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.5. Riders, Multi-Ride Passes and the Frozen Fare

A `Rider` may buy a multi-ride pass ahead of travelling. The purchase declares a `zone_tier` of `INNER`, `OUTER`, or `ALL_ZONE`; a `rides_purchased` count of at least 1; a `purchase_price_cents` of at least 0, being what the rider actually paid; and a `valid_until` calendar date, the last date inclusive on which the pass may be redeemed. The pass's `rider_user_id` is stamped from the acting `X-User-ID` and is the value every later ownership check compares against — it is never matched against any entity's `id`. A fresh pass has `rides_remaining` equal to `rides_purchased`, `rides_consumed` at 0, `status` `ACTIVE`, `refund_basis` null, and `refund_amount_cents` at 0. The tier determines the reach of the pass: `INNER` covers a zone span of at most 1, `OUTER` covers a zone span of at most 3, and `ALL_ZONE` covers any span at all. The zone span between two stops is the absolute difference of their `zone_index` values.

Requesting a ride creates a booking. The rider supplies `origin_stop_id` and `destination_stop_id` (which must differ), a `requested_date` naming the calendar date of travel, a `service_mode` of `SCHEDULED` or `ON_DEMAND`, and a `party_size` between 1 and 12 inclusive. An optional `ride_pass_id` tenders a pass; leaving it null makes this a cash booking. The travel date is resolved to the operating day whose `service_date` equals it, and if no such day has been opened the request fails with `ERR_SERVICE_DAY_NOT_FOUND`. A new booking is stamped with `rider_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, starts in `REQUESTED` with `trip_id` null, `offer_round` at 0, an empty `declined_trip_ids` list, `redemption_status` `NONE`, `interrupted_from_status` null, and `fare_charged_cents`, `fare_refunded_cents`, and `credit_refunded_cents` all at 0.

The fare is quoted once, at request time, and frozen into `fare_quoted_cents`; it is never re-quoted, whatever happens to the booking afterwards, and the trip eventually offered or confirmed never influences it. The quote is built from the two stops named on the booking plus the operating day matching `requested_date`. Begin with a per-seat base of 450 cents plus 275 cents for each zone crossed, where the number of zones crossed is the absolute difference of the destination stop's `zone_index` and the origin stop's `zone_index`; when both stops share a zone that difference is zero and the base is simply 450 cents. Then apply two multipliers, in this order and with a rounding step after each. The mode multiplier is 14000 basis points for an `ON_DEMAND` booking and 10000 basis points for a `SCHEDULED` one: multiply the base by it, divide by 10000, and round half up to whole cents. The surge multiplier is the operating day's `surge_multiplier_bp`: multiply the rounded per-seat amount by it, divide by 10000, and round half up to whole cents again. Only then multiply by `party_size` to obtain `fare_quoted_cents`. This ordering is normative — rounding once at the end, after multiplying the base by both multipliers and by the party size, yields a different number of cents and is incorrect.

A rider who no longer wants a pass may cash it in, choosing a `settlement_basis` of either `PRO_RATA` or `DIFFERENTIAL`. Only the pass's owner may do so, and only while the pass is `ACTIVE` or `EXHAUSTED`; a pass already `REFUNDED` or `EXPIRED` is rejected with `ERR_PASS_NOT_ACTIVE`. Under `PRO_RATA` the operator pays back the rides not taken at the average price the rider actually paid: divide `purchase_price_cents` by `rides_purchased`, round half up to whole cents to get a unit price, then multiply that unit price by `rides_remaining`. Retail value is ignored entirely. Under `DIFFERENTIAL` the operator instead claws back the retail value of the rides already taken: multiply `rides_consumed` by the retail per-ride price of the pass's tier — 1100 cents for `INNER`, 1800 cents for `OUTER`, 2600 cents for `ALL_ZONE` — subtract that from `purchase_price_cents`, and take zero if the subtraction would go negative. Because a multi-ride pass is sold at a discount to retail, `DIFFERENTIAL` returns strictly less than `PRO_RATA` once any ride has been used, while a `PRO_RATA` settlement of a fully exhausted pass pays exactly 0. The chosen basis is recorded in `refund_basis`, the payout in `refund_amount_cents`; `rides_remaining` is set to 0, the pass moves `ACTIVE → REFUNDED` or `EXHAUSTED → REFUNDED`, and its `version` increments by 1. Both `EXPIRED` and `REFUNDED` are terminal.

The count feeding `DIFFERENTIAL` deserves emphasis: `rides_consumed` is the sum of `party_size` over the bookings on this pass whose `redemption_status` is `REDEEMED`, and nothing else. Rides handed back by a service interruption leave their booking at `RESTORED` and are therefore *not* counted as consumed, so an interrupted rider does not pay retail for a leg they never travelled.

### 3.6. Dispatch, Decline and Confirmation

A booking sitting in `REQUESTED` is inert until a `Dispatcher` offers it a trip. The dispatch decision is a pure selector over the trips on the operating day whose `service_date` equals the booking's `requested_date`, and it is fully determined — no discretion is involved. A trip enters the candidate pool only when every one of the following holds: it belongs to that operating day; its `operational_status` is `SCHEDULED`; its `service_mode` equals the booking's `service_mode`, so a `SCHEDULED` booking may only ever be offered a `SCHEDULED` trip and an `ON_DEMAND` booking only an `ON_DEMAND` trip; its `origin_stop_id` equals the booking's `origin_stop_id` and its `destination_stop_id` equals the booking's `destination_stop_id`; its id does not appear in the booking's `declined_trip_ids`; its `scheduled_departure_utc` is at or after the current logical-clock instant; the day's `posture` is `NORMAL` or `RESTRICTED`; and its `effective_capacity` minus its `seats_committed` is at least the booking's `party_size`. Candidates are ranked by `scheduled_departure_utc` ascending, ties broken by the larger remaining capacity — that is, `effective_capacity` minus `seats_committed` descending — and any remaining ties broken by `created_at` ascending. A trip's id participates only as an exclusion-set membership token and never as a ranking or tie-break key. The top-ranked candidate becomes the offer: the booking's `trip_id` is set to it, the booking moves `REQUESTED → OFFERED`, and its `version` increments by 1. No seats are committed by an offer. If the pool is empty the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TRIP` and the booking is left completely untouched — `status` stays `REQUESTED`, `offer_round` unchanged, `declined_trip_ids` unchanged, `version` unchanged. Dispatching a booking that is not in `REQUESTED` is rejected with `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_REQUESTED`.

Declining is the cycle edge, and only the booking's owner may decline. The current `trip_id` is appended to `declined_trip_ids`, `trip_id` is then cleared to null, `offer_round` increments by 1, and the booking's `version` increments by 1. Where the booking lands depends on the round: the operator allows three offers in total, so when the incremented `offer_round` would still be below 3 the booking cycles `OFFERED → REQUESTED` and returns to the dispatch pool with a larger exclusion set, and when the incremented `offer_round` reaches 3 the booking instead falls `OFFERED → UNFULFILLED`, which is terminal. A decline attempted on a booking that is not `OFFERED` is rejected with `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_OFFERED`.

Confirmation is where seats and money finally move, and it is the most heavily guarded operation in the system. Only the booking's owner may confirm, the booking must be `OFFERED`, and the offered trip must still be `SCHEDULED` — a trip that has already opened boarding is rejected with `ERR_TRIP_NOT_SCHEDULED`. The request carries `expected_trip_version`, and a mismatch against the trip's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TRIP_VERSION` before any of the remaining checks run. The day's `posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_SERVICE_SUSPENDED`. When a pass is tendered, four further conditions are checked, in the order given here, which is exactly the order the ladder of §2.8 imposes: the pass's `valid_until` must not be earlier than the logical clock's calendar date, and a stale pass is moved `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` before `ERR_PASS_EXPIRED` is returned, so the rejection leaves a permanent trace on the pass row; the pass's `zone_tier` must cover the zone span between the booking's origin and destination stops, else `ERR_PASS_ZONE_NOT_COVERED`; the pass's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_PASS_NOT_ACTIVE`; and — after the capacity test below — `rides_remaining` must be at least the booking's `party_size`, else `ERR_PASS_NO_RIDES_REMAINING`. Between the pass's status and its ride balance sits the trip's own capacity: `effective_capacity` minus `seats_committed` must be at least `party_size`, else `ERR_TRIP_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The capacity test is the only one of the five that also runs on a cash booking, where it is the sole remaining check.

A confirmation that clears all of that moves the booking `OFFERED → CONFIRMED`. When a pass was tendered, `rides_remaining` is reduced by `party_size`, the booking's `redemption_status` becomes `REDEEMED`, `fare_charged_cents` is set to 0 because the ride was paid for in kind, and if `rides_remaining` has reached exactly 0 the pass moves `ACTIVE → EXHAUSTED`. When no pass was tendered, `fare_charged_cents` is set to the frozen `fare_quoted_cents`. Either way the trip's `seats_committed` is recomputed as the sum of `party_size` over that trip's bookings whose `status` is exactly `CONFIRMED` or `BOARDED` — bookings in `OFFERED`, `REQUESTED`, `INTERRUPTED`, `UNFULFILLED`, or `COMPLETED` are all excluded from that sum — and both the trip's `version` and the booking's `version` increment by 1. Bookings may be read individually or listed with `trip_id`, `ride_pass_id`, `origin_stop_id`, `destination_stop_id`, `rider_user_id`, and `status` filters alongside `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.7. The Movement: Boarding, Sailing, Arrival — and Cancellation

Only the trip's own master may move a boat, and every one of the three movement operations first checks that the acting `X-User-ID` equals the trip's `master_crew_id`.

Opening boarding records the manifest. The master supplies `boarded_booking_ids`, the list of bookings physically taken on board. The trip must be `SCHEDULED`, else `ERR_TRIP_NOT_SCHEDULED`, and the day's `posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_SERVICE_SUSPENDED`. Every id in the manifest must name a booking that belongs to this trip and is currently `CONFIRMED`; a booking that is on another trip or in any other status is rejected with `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_CONFIRMED`, while an id that names no booking at all is an `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`. The manifest is inspected only after the state and suspension gates above have both passed, and it is inspected in two sweeps: every id is resolved first and only then is every resolved booking tested, so a manifest carrying both an unknown id and a booking that is not `CONFIRMED` answers `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`. The total `party_size` across the manifest must not exceed the trip's `effective_capacity`, else `ERR_TRIP_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. On success the trip's `boarded_pax` is set to that total, each named booking moves `CONFIRMED → BOARDED` with its `version` incremented by 1, `seats_committed` is recomputed — `BOARDED` bookings still count towards it — the trip moves `SCHEDULED → BOARDING`, and the trip's `version` increments by 1. Nothing on the money axis moves: `settlement_status` remains `UNSETTLED`.

Sailing carries no payload at all; the master simply declares departure, and the request runs the full safety ladder. The trip must be `BOARDING`, else `ERR_TRIP_NOT_BOARDING`. The day must not be `SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_SERVICE_SUSPENDED`. The vessel's derived `service_state` must be `IN_SERVICE`: a `LAID_UP` hull is rejected with `ERR_VESSEL_IN_LAYUP` and an `OUT_OF_SERVICE` hull with `ERR_VESSEL_OUT_OF_SERVICE`. The vessel must have a latest `ANNUAL` inspection whose `result` is `PASS` or `PASS_WITH_DEFICIENCIES` and whose `expires_on` is not earlier than the logical clock's calendar date — an `expires_on` falling exactly on today is still valid — and when the day's posture is `RESTRICTED` that inspection must additionally carry a `deficiency_count` of no more than 2; any failure here is `ERR_VESSEL_INSPECTION_INVALID`. Under `RESTRICTED` posture only an `OFFSHORE` `hull_class` may sail, a `HARBOR` hull being turned back with `ERR_HULL_CLASS_RESTRICTED`. The master's `status` must be `ACTIVE` and both `cert_expires_on` and `medical_expires_on` must be no earlier than today's date, else `ERR_CREW_CERT_EXPIRED`. The master's `endorsement_class` must rank at least as high as the vessel's `required_endorsement`, so a `MASTER_25T` holder asked to take out a hull requiring `MASTER_100T` is refused with `ERR_CREW_ENDORSEMENT_INSUFFICIENT`. Finally the under-keel clearance — the day's `low_water_m` less the vessel's `draft_m`, rounded half up to two decimals — must be at least 0.30 metres, else `ERR_UNDER_KEEL_CLEARANCE`.

A successful departure stamps `actual_departure_utc` from the logical clock, snapshots `booked_pax` from the trip's current `seats_committed` — this snapshot is the provisional passenger basis — and posts a provisional landing fee on every berth slot of the trip that is still `RESERVED`. Each posted amount is added to the owning dock's `accrued_fee_cents`, and each dock touched has its `version` incremented by 1. The sum of the posted amounts becomes the trip's `provisional_fee_cents`; the money axis moves `UNSETTLED → PROVISIONAL` while the movement axis moves `BOARDING → DEPARTED`; and the trip's `version` increments by 1.

Arrival likewise carries no payload. The clock reading is tested first, at rung 7: whenever the trip already carries an `actual_departure_utc`, the logical clock must read strictly later than it, else `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW` — which is why a trip already `COMPLETED`, asked to complete again on an unmoved clock, answers `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW` rather than `ERR_TRIP_NOT_DEPARTED`. Only then is the state tested: the trip must be `DEPARTED`, else `ERR_TRIP_NOT_DEPARTED`. A trip that has never departed carries no stamp, so the clock test has nothing to compare and is skipped entirely for it, and it falls straight through to `ERR_TRIP_NOT_DEPARTED`. On success `actual_arrival_utc` is stamped from the clock and Cascade B runs: for each still-`RESERVED` slot of the trip, the slot's `projected_fee_cents` is first subtracted back out of the dock's `accrued_fee_cents` and reset to 0, then the final fee is computed and written to `landing_fee_cents` and the slot moves `RESERVED → CONSUMED`. The sum of those final amounts becomes the trip's `final_fee_cents`; the money axis moves `PROVISIONAL → FINAL` and the movement axis `DEPARTED → COMPLETED`; every booking on the trip still in `BOARDED` moves `BOARDED → COMPLETED` with its `version` incremented by 1; the master's `trips_mastered_count` increments by 1; the trip's `seats_committed` is recomputed from the same `CONFIRMED`-or-`BOARDED` sum and therefore lands at 0, every booking that fed it having just become `COMPLETED`; each dock touched has its `version` incremented by 1; and the trip's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.8. Posting Landing Fees: the Provisional and Final Variants

Both postings run the same computation with two differences, and both process the trip's slots in ascending `start_utc` with ties broken by ascending `created_at`. That order is normative, because the rebate tier each slot receives depends on how much the dock had already accrued when its turn came.

The **passenger basis** is 0 for any slot whose `purpose` is `LAYOVER` — a layover occupies a berth but lands nobody. Otherwise it is the trip's `booked_pax` in the provisional variant and the trip's `boarded_pax` in the final variant. The **berth minutes** in the provisional variant are the whole minutes between the slot's `start_utc` and its `end_utc`, any fractional minute discarded downward. In the final variant they run from the slot's `start_utc` to the trip's real `actual_departure_utc`, again floored to whole minutes, and never below 0 — so a boat that sat at the berth past its reserved window bills more minutes than it reserved, while one that left before its window opened bills none. The **gross charge** is the berth minutes multiplied by the dock's `berth_minute_rate_cents`, plus the passenger basis multiplied by the dock's `landing_fee_per_pax_cents`. Both figures are stored on the slot as `berth_minutes` and `pax_basis`, reflecting whatever the most recent posting used.

The rebate looks circular — the tier is keyed on the dock's accrual, and the accrual is grown by the very fee the tier helps compute — so the evaluation order is pinned and must be followed exactly. First, in the final variant only, subtract the slot's `projected_fee_cents` back out of `accrued_fee_cents` and set `projected_fee_cents` to 0. Second, read the dock's `accrued_fee_cents` as it now stands; call this the pre-posting accrual. Third, select the rebate from that pre-posting accrual alone, never from the value this posting is about to produce: if the slot's `rebate_bp` has already been pre-locked to 10000 by a cap flip on this dock the rebate stays 10000; otherwise it is 1500 basis points when the pre-posting accrual is at least 500000 cents, 800 basis points when it is at least 200000 cents but below 500000, and 0 otherwise. Fourth, compute the fee: multiply the gross charge by the rebate basis points, divide by 10000, round half up to whole cents, and subtract that rebate amount from the gross charge — a pre-locked rebate of 10000 basis points therefore yields a fee of exactly 0. Fifth, add the fee to the dock's `accrued_fee_cents`. Sixth, evaluate the monthly cap, and only in the final variant. The selected rebate is stored on the slot as `rebate_bp`.

In the **provisional** variant the fee is written to the slot's `projected_fee_cents`, added to the dock's accrual, and that is the end of it: `monthly_fee_cap_cents` is deliberately not evaluated on a provisional posting, so a provisional posting can push the accrual past the cap without flipping anything. In the **final** variant the fee is added to the accrual and the cap is then tested. If the resulting `accrued_fee_cents` reaches or exceeds the dock's `monthly_fee_cap_cents`, the overflow — the amount by which the accrual overshot the cap — is trimmed off the fee, the accrual is pinned to exactly `monthly_fee_cap_cents`, and the dock's `fee_status` flips `ACCRUING → CAPPED`. The fee, trimmed or not, is written to the slot's `landing_fee_cents`. A slot's `landing_fee_cents` stays 0 until its trip completes, and stays 0 forever if the slot is voided instead.

The cap flip has one further consequence, and it is the third link of Cascade B. Once a dock's `fee_status` has become `CAPPED`, every slot on that same dock still sitting in `RESERVED` has its `rebate_bp` pre-locked to 10000, each slot the pre-lock actually rewrites having its `version` incremented by 1 while a slot already carrying 10000 is left untouched and does not advance, so any later posting on those slots computes a fee of exactly 0 and the capped dock never accrues another cent. The `CAPPED` state is permanent for that dock.

### 3.9. When the Harbor Shuts: Cancellation, Interruption and Refunds

Cascade A is what a suspension actually does. Every trip on the newly suspended day whose movement axis is `SCHEDULED` or `BOARDING` is worked through in ascending `scheduled_departure_utc`, ties broken by ascending `created_at`, and each is taken through the following four links in order.

The first link cancels the trip itself: `operational_status` moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` or `BOARDING → CANCELLED`, `settlement_status` moves `UNSETTLED → VOID`, and `cancellation_reason` is stamped with the exact string `POSTURE_SUSPENDED`. No money is posted on this path. The trip's `seats_committed` is recomputed to 0 and its `version` increments by 1. When the trip was cancelled out of `BOARDING`, its `boarded_pax` is deliberately left at the value boarding gave it, so the audit trail of who was on board survives the cancellation.

The second link voids the berths: every slot of that trip whose `status` is `RESERVED` moves `RESERVED → VOIDED` with `projected_fee_cents` and `landing_fee_cents` both 0, and where a provisional amount had already been posted it is subtracted back out of the dock's `accrued_fee_cents`. Only a dock that actually gave money back is touched: a dock whose voided slot carried no provisional amount keeps its `version` and `updated_at` unchanged. In the same link, every booking on the trip whose `status` is `OFFERED`, `CONFIRMED`, or `BOARDED` is interrupted — `OFFERED → INTERRUPTED`, `CONFIRMED → INTERRUPTED`, or `BOARDED → INTERRUPTED` respectively; the status it held immediately beforehand is stamped into `interrupted_from_status`, which may therefore be `OFFERED`, `CONFIRMED`, or `BOARDED`; and the booking's `trip_id` is retained rather than cleared, because the claim lookup later needs it.

The third link returns ride credits in kind. When an interrupted booking's `redemption_status` was `REDEEMED`, its pass gets `rides_remaining` increased by the booking's `party_size`, the booking's `redemption_status` becomes `RESTORED`, a pass sitting in `EXHAUSTED` returns `EXHAUSTED → ACTIVE`, and both the booking's `version` and the pass's `version` increment by 1. A booking interrupted out of `OFFERED` has nothing to restore — nothing was ever redeemed — and its `fare_charged_cents` stays at 0. `INTERRUPTED` and `UNFULFILLED` are both terminal for a booking, as is `COMPLETED`.

Cancellation alone pays nobody. To open compensation a `Dispatcher` declares a service interruption against the day, supplying only the `cause`, which is one of `WEATHER`, `TIDE`, `MECHANICAL`, or `DOCK_CLOSURE`. The day's `posture` must be `SUSPENDED` at that moment, else the declaration is refused with `ERR_INTERRUPTION_NOT_DECLARABLE`. The interruption records `service_day_id` from the day addressed, stamps `declared_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and starts in `DECLARED`, the only other status being `RESOLVED`.

Declaring opens exactly one refund claim per eligible booking, created in ascending booking `created_at`. A booking is eligible when its `status` is `INTERRUPTED`, when the trip it names sits on this operating day, and when no claim already exists for it — so declaring twice on the same day does not double-pay. Bookings still in `REQUESTED`, never having been offered a trip, are not eligible and receive no claim at all. Each claim records `booking_id` and `service_interruption_id`, copies the interruption's declaring user into `declarer_user_id` as the segregation-of-duties comparand, leaves `approver_user_id` null, and starts in `OPEN`; the other three claim statuses are `APPROVED`, `PAID`, and `DENIED`.

The entitlement on each claim is decided by the branch the booking was interrupted from. A booking interrupted from `OFFERED` or from `CONFIRMED` is entitled to the whole of its `fare_charged_cents`. A booking interrupted from `BOARDED` — a rider who got part of the way — is entitled to a partial-leg share of 5000 basis points, that is half of its `fare_charged_cents`, multiplied out and rounded half up to whole cents. That figure is written to `entitlement_cents`, and the allocation into buckets follows in a fixed order. The first bucket is compensation in kind: when the booking's `redemption_status` is `RESTORED`, the cascade has already handed the rides back, so `ride_restored` is set to true and both `cash_refund_cents` and `credit_refund_cents` stay at 0 — the two money buckets are skipped entirely. Otherwise `ride_restored` is false and the entitlement flows through two ordered money buckets: `cash_refund_cents` takes the entitlement up to a cash ceiling of 5000 cents, taking whichever is smaller, and `credit_refund_cents` receives everything the cash bucket could not absorb, namely the entitlement less the cash bucket. An entitlement of exactly 5000 cents therefore lands wholly in cash and leaves the credit bucket at 0. Once every claim is written, the interruption's `affected_booking_count` is set to the number of claims created and its `total_provisional_refund_cents` to the sum of `entitlement_cents` across them. Interruptions on a day can be listed with `limit` and `offset`.

Deciding a claim belongs to a `FareAuditor`, who supplies a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `DENY`. The claim must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_REFUND_CLAIM_NOT_OPEN`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the claim's `declarer_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; the owning interruption's own status is not separately tested on either decision, because an interruption still holding an `OPEN` claim is necessarily still `DECLARED`. An approval moves the claim `OPEN → APPROVED`, stamps `approver_user_id` with the acting user, and writes the two buckets onto the booking — `fare_refunded_cents` takes the claim's `cash_refund_cents` and `credit_refunded_cents` takes the claim's `credit_refund_cents` — incrementing both the claim's `version` and the booking's `version` by 1. Disbursement then completes inside the very same request, moving the claim `APPROVED → PAID` and incrementing the claim's `version` by 1 again. A denial instead moves the claim `OPEN → DENIED`, stamps `approver_user_id` all the same, leaves both refund buckets on the booking at 0, and increments the claim's `version` by 1. `PAID` and `DENIED` are both terminal.

The parent interruption is re-derived from its children on every such decision. Once the interruption has at least one claim and no claim of that interruption remains `OPEN`, the interruption bubbles up `DECLARED → RESOLVED` and its `version` increments by 1. Claims can be read individually or listed with `booking_id`, `service_interruption_id`, and `status` filters alongside `limit` and `offset`.
