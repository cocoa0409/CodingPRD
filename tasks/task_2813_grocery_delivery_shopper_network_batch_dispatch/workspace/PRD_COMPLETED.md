# Grocery Batch Dispatch API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Same-day grocery delivery is a business of small margins and large coordination costs. A single delivery run — a **batch** — may bundle several customers' orders drawn from more than one store, must be offered to exactly one shopper out of a pool ranked by rating and reliability, and must survive the ordinary chaos of a physical supermarket: an item is gone from the shelf, a bag of loose apples weighs less than the customer asked for, a shopper walks away from a claimed run halfway through. Every one of those events has a money consequence for the customer, for the shopper, and for the platform. The Grocery Batch Dispatch API is the system of record that keeps those consequences consistent.

This document specifies that system. It governs nine collaborating resource families — retailers and their catalogs, customers and their benefit and credit balances, shoppers and their rating tiers, orders and their lines, batches and their membership rows, in-aisle replacement requests, shopper payouts, and the customer credit ledger — and it defines, for each, the exact preconditions under which a state change is permitted and the exact arithmetic by which derived money is produced. Two themes recur throughout. The first is **dual-variant evaluation**: the same formula is run twice at different moments with deliberately different inputs, once to decide how much money may be held and once to decide how much money is actually taken, and once over a shopper's five most recent ratings and again over their whole career. The second is **cascade discipline**: a single abandonment must forfeit a payout, credit every affected customer, reverse a completed capture, re-score a shopper, suspend them, and release every other run they were holding — in that order, with no link skipped.

The intended readers are the engineers who will implement the service. Where this document states a threshold, an inclusivity, an evaluation order, or a rounding rule, that statement is normative: two implementations that both satisfy the prose must agree on every stored value, because downstream reconciliation, shopper pay disputes, and SNAP benefit accounting all depend on the numbers being reproducible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold everywhere in the API: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, identity and versioning of resources, the logical clock, monetary precision, optimistic concurrency, and the order in which rejections are decided. Domain workflows are specified in section 3.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A header present but carrying an empty value counts as absent, and the role name is matched exactly as spelled below, so a value differing only in letter case is not one of the five and is rejected the same way. If the headers are well formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorized for the addressed operation in section 4, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The five recognized roles are `Customer`, `Shopper`, `RetailerManager`, `DispatchAdmin`, and `CustomerSupport`. A `Customer` places grocery orders, adjudicates in-aisle substitutions, rates shoppers, and adjusts tips. A `Shopper` claims batches, shops the aisles, proposes substitutions, checks out, and delivers. A `RetailerManager` owns one retailer's catalog, shelf prices, markup policy, and parity-disclosure posture. A `DispatchAdmin` operates the dispatch board: building batches, offering them, reassigning abandoned work, finalizing payouts, and driving the logical clock. A `CustomerSupport` operator works the back office: registering customers, posting goodwill credits, and reading the whole board.

Three of the five roles are backed by a persisted entity, and for those three `X-User-ID` is a resource identifier that ownership checks compare against.

- When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Customer row. Ownership checks compare `X-User-ID` to the addressed order's `customer_id`; when the addressed resource is an order line or a replacement request, the comparison is against the `customer_id` of the order that owns that line.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Shopper`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Shopper row. For a batch claim, ownership requires `X-User-ID` to equal the batch's `offered_shopper_id`. For starting to shop, picking a line, proposing a replacement, checking out, delivering, and abandoning, ownership requires `X-User-ID` to equal the batch's `claimed_shopper_id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `RetailerManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Retailer row. Ownership checks compare `X-User-ID` to the addressed catalog item's `retailer_id`, and to the addressed retailer's own `id` when the retailer itself is the target of a policy update.

The remaining two roles are operator roles with no backing entity. When `X-User-Role` is `DispatchAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it for audit purposes but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds when `X-User-Role` is `CustomerSupport`: the identifier is opaque and is not matched against any stored row. Consequently no `DispatchAdmin` or `CustomerSupport` request can ever fail an ownership check.

Any ownership mismatch on a resource addressed by a `Customer`, a `Shopper`, or a `RetailerManager` is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

The comparison is made only on operations that create or change state. Reads never perform it: a single-resource read, a filtered list read, and a computed read such as the parity-disclosure read are all answered for any role that section 4 authorizes for that path, without regard to who owns the addressed row and without narrowing a list to the acting principal's own rows, so no read in this API can fail with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the single value in the whole API read from the host's real clock rather than from the logical clock of section 2.5; it is envelope metadata only, and no stored field, deadline or comparison is ever derived from it.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated with two query parameters. `limit` is the maximum number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of rows skipped before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range of 1 through 100 is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); so is a negative `offset`, and so is either parameter supplied as something that is not an integer. Either parameter supplied with an empty value is treated as omitted and falls back to its default.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list endpoint orders its rows by `created_at` ascending before the page window is applied, so paging is stable across calls. Because `created_at` is read from the logical clock, which moves only when a client sets it, rows routinely share one instant; rows that tie on `created_at` are ordered by the sequence in which the server created them, earliest first, which keeps that ordering total. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of rows actually returned on this page is less than `total_count`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource the API stores is addressable by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and no embedded semantics; clients must never construct one. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, written once at creation from the logical clock of section 2.5 rather than from the host's real clock, formatted as an ISO 8601 instant in UTC with exactly three fractional-second digits. Every other instant any payload exposes — a shopping-window bound, an offer or replacement expiry, a delivery deadline, a delivery stamp, a tip-window close, an abandonment stamp — is rendered in exactly that same format, and every instant a client supplies is normalized to UTC before it is stored, a value carrying no zone offset being read as already UTC.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` and `version`. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the same logical clock and refreshed on every mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations that only rewrite derived fields. That increase is counted per row and per request, not per write: a single request that touches one row several times — writing declared fields and then recomputing derived ones, or applying several links of one cascade to the same row — leaves that row's `version` exactly 1 higher than it found it. The retailer, catalog item, customer, shopper, order, order line, batch, replacement request, and shopper payout families all carry the full set: `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, and `version`.

Field presence in a payload is uniform across every family. Every field a family declares is always present in that family's response body; a field that currently holds no value — an unset `offered_shopper_id`, a `delivered_at` before delivery, an unattributed credit entry's `order_id` — is serialized as JSON `null` and is never omitted. Relationships are exposed as identifier fields only: no payload embeds a nested copy of a related resource, so a line reports its `catalog_item_id` rather than the catalog item, and a membership row reports its `batch_id` and `order_id` rather than either row.

Three families are append-only records of something that happened and are never modified after they are written: shopper ratings, batch membership rows, and credit-ledger entries. Rows in those three families carry only `id` and `created_at`. They have no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all, and neither field should appear in their response payloads.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Lazy Expiry

Nothing in this system's domain logic reads wall-clock time; the one exception is the envelope's `api_request_timestamp` of section 2.2, which is stamped from the host's real clock and feeds nothing. All temporal reasoning — offer lifetimes, shopping windows, delivery deadlines, replacement lifetimes, and tip windows — is evaluated against a server-held **logical clock**. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to the host's real time. A `DispatchAdmin` moves it by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting test-driving capability; that call performs no state transition and expires nothing by itself. The supplied instant is stored exactly as given, normalized to UTC, and is not required to be later than the clock's current reading, so the clock may legitimately be wound backwards; the only rejection the setter performs is the malformed-instant one of rank 5.

Expiry is always evaluated **lazily**. Passing a deadline changes no stored row; instead, the endpoint that cares about the deadline compares the current logical-clock instant against the stored instant on its next invocation. Three deadline fields are written from the clock at the moment of the transition that writes them, and they are written from the clock value alone, never from an input:

- A batch's `offer_expires_at` is set to the current logical-clock instant plus 30 minutes each time the batch is offered or re-offered.
- An order's `tip_window_closes_at` is set to the order's `delivered_at` plus 2 hours, and `delivered_at` is itself the logical-clock instant at which delivery was recorded.
- A replacement request's `expires_at` is set to the current logical-clock instant plus 20 minutes when the request is created.

Deadline comparisons are strict: an action guarded by a deadline is permitted only while the clock is strictly before the stored instant, so an action attempted at exactly the deadline instant is rejected. This applies to claiming a batch against `offer_expires_at`, approving a replacement against `expires_at`, and adjusting a tip against `tip_window_closes_at`. Window *openings*, by contrast, are inclusive: shopping may begin once the clock has reached `shop_window_start`, and a payout may be finalized once the clock has reached every member order's `tip_window_closes_at`. Timestamps are compared at whole-minute resolution in ISO 8601 UTC.

### 2.6. Money, Weight and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is an integer count of cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere in a stored field or a response body. Whenever a monetary expression produces a fraction, it is rounded half up to a whole cent — a value of exactly half a cent rounds away from zero.

Three rounding disciplines matter enough to state separately, because getting them wrong produces plausible but wrong totals:

- **Per-line rounding is pinned before summation.** When a weighted item's money is derived by multiplying a per-pound price by a number of pounds, that single line's product is rounded half up to a whole cent *first*, and only then are the lines added together. Summing unrounded products and rounding the total once is explicitly wrong and yields a different number of cents for the same weights.
- **Ratios are compared by integer cross-multiplication, never by floating-point division.** Wherever this document describes a percentage comparison — the tip-baiting eligibility test and the abandonment pick-progress test — the implementation must compare the cross-multiplied integers rather than dividing.
- **Derived non-money quantities have their own precision.** Weights in pounds and driving distances in miles are carried to one decimal place, rounded half up. Shopper rating averages and shopper tier scores are carried to four decimal places, rounded half up. The retailer markup fraction is carried to two decimal places, rounded half up, so a fraction supplied with more precision than that is quantized to two decimals on the way in and it is the quantized value that is stored and that every price derivation reads. Unit counts, line counts, store counts, and every `*_count` field are integers. All of these travel in a response body as JSON numbers — a bare integer for money and for the counts, a decimal number for weights, distances, rating averages, tier scores and the markup fraction — and never as quoted strings.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Orders and Batches

Two write operations legitimately supersede a value that the client reasoned about, so both are guarded by optimistic concurrency control on the parent resource's `version`.

A `POST /orders/{id}/tip-adjustments` request must carry `expected_order_version` as a required body field holding the `version` of that order which the client last observed. If `expected_order_version` does not equal the order's currently stored `version`, the adjustment is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a tip adjustment supersedes the order's current tip: a second adjustment is a perfectly valid product operation, not an illegal transition, so the only way to protect the client is to confirm that the baseline it reasoned about — the tip snapshot in `tip_original_cents` and the count in `tip_adjustment_count` — is still the stored one.

A `POST /batches/{id}/orders` request must carry `expected_batch_version` as a required body field holding the `version` of the target batch which the client last observed. If `expected_batch_version` does not equal the batch's currently stored `version`, the attachment is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BATCH_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Adding an order supersedes the batch's derived composition — `total_units`, `total_weight_lb`, `total_distance_mi`, `store_count`, and `delivery_deadline` — so a stale version means the dispatcher sized the addition against a composition that has since changed.

These are the only two operations that take a version field. No other endpoint in this API accepts `expected_order_version`, `expected_batch_version`, or any other expected-version input, and no other endpoint performs a version check.

### 2.8. Rejection Precedence

Many operations in this API are guarded by several checks at once, and which error the client sees is part of the contract. Checks are evaluated in the following fixed order of decreasing generality, and the first check that fails determines the response. A later check never masks an earlier one, and an earlier check never runs after a later one has already rejected.

1. **Authentication.** Headers present and role recognized, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorization.** The acting role appears in the addressed operation's authorized-role list in section 4, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (403).
3. **Path identifier existence.** Every identifier embedded in the URL resolves to a stored row, otherwise the 404 that names the missing family: `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CATALOG_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHOPPER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYOUT_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_CREDIT_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`.
4. **Ownership.** The entity-backed roles must own the addressed resource as described in section 2.1, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). One refinement applies here: when the ownership comparison targets a field that is only populated in a particular state — a batch's `offered_shopper_id`, which is null except while an offer stands, or a batch's `claimed_shopper_id`, which is null except while the batch is held — the corresponding rank-8 state gate is evaluated *before* the ownership comparison, so that both the state error and the ownership error remain observable. A second refinement concerns *when* rank 4 can run at all: the comparison is made as soon as the identity of the owner is known, which for a resource named by the path is before rank 5, but for the four operations whose owner can only be learned from a body identifier — publishing a catalog item, opening an order, rating a shopper, and proposing a replacement — only after ranks 5 and 6 have passed, so on those four a malformed body or an unresolvable body identifier is reported in preference to `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
5. **Request-body schema.** Required fields present, types correct, enum members recognized, integers inside their declared ranges, and the field-level shape rules stated throughout section 3 satisfied — a per-pound line carrying a positive requested weight and a requested quantity of exactly 1, an each-priced line carrying a requested weight of 0, a shopping window whose end is strictly after its start, a star rating within 1 through 5, a tip within 0 through 20000 cents, and a pagination `limit` within 1 through 100. Any violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Four shape conventions apply at this rank on every endpoint. A request whose body is absent or empty is read as an empty JSON object, so what gets reported is its missing required fields; a body that does not parse as JSON, or parses to anything other than a JSON object, is itself `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A field supplied as JSON `null` is treated exactly as if it had been omitted — an optional field falls back to its default and a required field is reported missing. And a JSON boolean is never accepted where an integer is declared, even though `true` and `false` would coerce; likewise every string field in the API, unless it states its own length bounds, must be non-empty, so the empty string is rejected rather than stored. Finally, a body key the addressed operation does not declare is ignored rather than rejected: an update carries only the fields the operation says it carries, and anything else in the object — including a field that family declares but that this operation does not accept, such as a `sku` or a `pricing_unit` on a catalog-item update — is silently dropped.
6. **Body foreign-key existence.** Identifiers supplied in the request body resolve to stored rows, each mapping to its own family's 404: `customer_id` and then `retailer_id` on order creation, `retailer_id` on catalog-item publication, `catalog_item_id` on line creation, `order_id` on rating creation, an attributing `order_id` on a credit-ledger entry whenever one is supplied, `order_id` on batch attachment, `batch_id` and then `order_line_id` and then `proposed_catalog_item_id` on replacement creation, and `shopper_id` on rating backfill. Where one body carries several of these, they are resolved in the order just listed and the first one that fails to resolve determines the 404, exactly as section 2.9 resolves path identifiers left to right.
7. **Stale version, then one-batch membership.** The optimistic-concurrency checks of section 2.7 are evaluated here, and immediately after them the rule that an order may hold at most one batch membership row for its entire life, whose violation is `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_BATCHED` (409). That membership rule is deliberately ranked above the rank-8 requirement that an attached order be `PLACED`: an order that already sits in a batch necessarily also fails the `PLACED` gate, so unless membership is consulted first the more specific error could never be observed.
8. **State preconditions.** The current status of the addressed rows permits the transition. These are the 409 conflicts named in section 3. Where an operation checks the status of more than one row, the order status is examined before the batch status, and the batch status is examined before the status of a line, a replacement request, or a payout.
9. **Referential business rules.** A proposed substitute must come from the same retailer as the line it replaces (`ERR_REPLACEMENT_RETAILER_MISMATCH`, 422), and a batch being offered or re-offered must have at least one member order (`ERR_BATCH_EMPTY`, 422).
10. **Benefit-program safety.** The two SNAP guards: `ERR_EBT_NOT_ENABLED` (422) and `ERR_EBT_INELIGIBLE_REPLACEMENT` (422).
11. **Temporal rules.** Window and deadline comparisons: `ERR_SHOPPER_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_RETAILER_CONCURRENCY_CAP_REACHED`, `ERR_SHOP_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`, `ERR_TIP_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_PAYOUT_WINDOW_OPEN`, and the expired-approval form of `ERR_REPLACEMENT_NOT_PENDING`.
12. **Eligibility and quota constraints.** `ERR_SHOPPER_SUSPENDED`, `ERR_TIER_BELOW_BATCH_FLOOR`, `ERR_ITEM_OUT_OF_STOCK`, `ERR_WEIGHT_VARIANCE_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_RATING`, `ERR_TIP_ADJUSTMENT_LIMIT`, and `ERR_REINSTATE_SCORE_TOO_LOW`.
13. **Fiscal and capacity ceilings.** `ERR_SHOPPER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409) and `ERR_ORDER_PAYMENT_HELD` (409).
14. **Selector exhaustion.** `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SHOPPER` (422) is evaluated last of all, so every cheaper and more specific rejection is reported in preference to it. A dispatch attempt that would fail both because the batch is empty and because no shopper qualifies reports the empty-batch error.

Ranks 1 and 2 apply to every endpoint in the API, and rank 5 applies to every endpoint that declares a body field or a query parameter. The endpoints that declare neither — every single-resource read, and the ten transitions that carry no payload at all: order placement, order re-authorization, shopper reinstatement, batch dispatch, claim, start-shopping, reassign, checkout, deliver, and payout finalization — never inspect the request body, so a body accompanying one of those that is malformed or is not a JSON object is ignored rather than rejected. The scopes of the remaining ranks are stated alongside the workflows they guard in section 3.

### 2.9. Identifier Resolution and Reverse Lookup

When a path contains more than one identifier, the identifiers are resolved strictly left to right and the first one that fails to resolve determines the 404 that is returned; the server does not attempt to locate a later segment under a parent that does not exist. Two paths in this API list a child collection under a parent identifier — the membership rows of a batch and the membership rows referencing an order — and in both the parent identifier is resolved before the collection is assembled, so an unknown batch reports `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND` and an unknown order reports `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` rather than an empty page.

Every foreign-key relationship in the data model is navigable in reverse through a filtered list endpoint. Catalog items may be filtered by `retailer_id`, `availability`, and `is_ebt_eligible`; credit-ledger entries by `customer_id`, `order_id`, and `reason_code`; shopper ratings by `shopper_id` and `order_id`; orders by `customer_id`, `retailer_id`, `fulfillment_status`, and `payment_status`; order lines by `order_id`, `catalog_item_id`, and `status`; batches by `status`, `service_area_code`, `offered_shopper_id`, and `claimed_shopper_id`; replacement requests by `batch_id`, `order_line_id`, `proposed_catalog_item_id`, and `status`; and shopper payouts by `batch_id`, `shopper_id`, and `status`. Filters combine conjunctively, and every one of them is optional; omitting all of them lists the whole collection. A filter supplied with an empty value is treated as omitted. Every filter but one is matched literally against the stored field and is not itself validated against that field's enum, so an unrecognized status or reason code simply matches no rows and yields an empty page rather than a rejection; the exception is the boolean filter `is_ebt_eligible`, which accepts `true`, `false`, `1`, or `0` in any letter case and rejects any other value with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Every filtered list also honours `limit` and `offset` as described in section 2.3.

### 2.10. Derived Fields, Cascades and Conservation

A large fraction of the fields in this system are **derived**: their stored value is defined as a function of other rows, not as something a client supplies. Derived fields are never allowed to drift. Whenever any input to a derived field changes, the field is recomputed from its definition in the same operation, and the recomputation is a mutation of the owning row — it refreshes that row's `updated_at` and increases its `version`. Two qualifications keep that rule from double-counting. A recomputation that leaves every derived field at the value it already held is not a mutation at all: it neither refreshes `updated_at` nor advances `version`. And, as section 2.4 states, however many fields of one row a single request rewrites, that row's `version` advances by exactly 1 for the whole request. An implementation that computes a derived value once and forgets to recompute it will disagree with this specification even though no client ever wrote the field.

Cascades run to completion inside the operation that triggers them. When an operation touches more than one family — a batch transition that moves member orders, a replacement approval that re-prices a line and then re-authorizes an order and then adjusts a batch counter, an abandonment that forfeits a payout and then credits customers and then re-scores and suspends a shopper and then releases that shopper's other batches — every link of the chain is applied before the response is returned, and the response reflects the post-cascade state. Section 3 names each link explicitly; none of them is optional and none of them is deferred.

Two conservation invariants must hold after every operation, and they are the sharpest test of whether the cascades were implemented correctly.

- **Benefit funds are conserved.** For any customer, the sum of `ebt_snap_balance_cents` and `ebt_captured_total_cents` must equal the value `ebt_snap_balance_cents` held before any capture ever touched that customer. Capture moves money out of the balance and into the captured total; a post-checkout reversal moves it back, restoring the balance and zeroing the order's captured EBT amount so that the captured total shrinks by the same number of cents that the balance grew.
- **Capacity units are conserved.** A shopper's `committed_units` must at all times equal the sum of `total_units` over the batches that shopper currently holds in `CLAIMED`, `SHOPPING`, or `CHECKED_OUT`. Any claim followed by its compensating event — abandonment, a system release, or delivery — must return `committed_units` to exactly the value it held before the claim.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Retailer Catalogs, Shelf Prices and Customer Accounts

A `DispatchAdmin` onboards a store by registering a retailer with its `legal_name` and a `store_code`. The store code is the human-facing handle used on the dispatch board; it must be between 3 and 16 characters long and must be unique across all retailer rows, a duplicate being rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) once every other declared field has passed its own validation. Five policy fields may be supplied at onboarding and each has a default when omitted. `catalog_markup_pct` is the fraction the platform adds to the shelf price to produce the in-app price; it defaults to 0.15, is carried to two decimal places, and must be at least 0.00 and at most 0.60 — the platform's hard ceiling is a sixty percent markup and a request above it is malformed. `parity_disclosure_required` defaults to true and records whether this retailer discloses its catalog-versus-shelf markup to customers. `ebt_snap_enabled` defaults to true; when it is false no SNAP tender may be authorized on any order for that store. `concurrent_shopper_cap` is the number of batches with overlapping shopping windows the store is willing to host at once; it defaults to 2 and must be at least 1 and at most 10. Finally `status` is either `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED` and defaults to `ACTIVE`; only an `ACTIVE` retailer will accept a newly placed order.

The `RetailerManager` who owns a store maintains its policy through a parity-policy update that may carry any subset of `catalog_markup_pct`, `parity_disclosure_required`, and `concurrent_shopper_cap`. That update has a wide blast radius: it re-derives the in-app price of *every* catalog item belonging to that retailer, and it increases the retailer row's `version` by 1. What it deliberately does not do is touch history. Prices already copied onto existing order lines are left exactly as they were, so a markup change tomorrow never re-prices a basket submitted today.

Catalog items are published by the owning `RetailerManager`. Each item names its `retailer_id`, a `sku` that must be unique within that retailer — a duplicate is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), and that uniqueness test is the last gate publication applies, after the named retailer has resolved and the ownership comparison has passed — a display `name`, and a `pricing_unit` of either `EACH` for items sold by the unit or `PER_LB` for items sold by weight. The shelf price is supplied as `in_store_price_cents`, which must be at least 1 cent and is expressed per unit for an `EACH` item and per pound for a `PER_LB` item. From it the system derives `catalog_price_cents`, the customer-facing in-app price: multiply `in_store_price_cents` by one plus the owning retailer's `catalog_markup_pct` as read at write time, and round the product half up to a whole cent. The system also derives `is_weighted`, which is true exactly when `pricing_unit` is `PER_LB` and false otherwise. Both derivations run when the item is first published, when the item is updated, and once per item when the retailer's parity policy changes.

Three optional item attributes round out the catalog. `is_ebt_eligible` defaults to false and marks the item as SNAP-eligible food. `estimated_weight_lb` defaults to 0 and holds the pounds assumed for a weighted item when the customer omits a weight; it must be greater than 0 for a `PER_LB` item and must be exactly 0 for an `EACH` item. `unit_weight_lb` defaults to 0, must not be negative, and carries the shipping weight of a single `EACH` unit, which is what makes heavy-pay totals computable for non-weighted goods. Shelf presence is tracked by `availability`, either `IN_STOCK` or `OUT_OF_STOCK`, defaulting to `IN_STOCK`. A `RetailerManager` may later revise `in_store_price_cents`, `availability`, `is_ebt_eligible`, `estimated_weight_lb`, and `unit_weight_lb` on an item; any such revision re-derives `catalog_price_cents` from the current markup, increases the item's `version` by 1, and again leaves every existing order-line snapshot untouched. It also touches no batch: because `unit_weight_lb` is never snapshotted onto a line, a batch's `total_weight_lb` reads it live from the item at the moment that aggregate is next re-derived, but the item revision does not itself trigger that re-derivation, so an already-composed batch keeps the `total_weight_lb` it already holds until some later operation re-derives that batch's composition.

Customers are registered by `CustomerSupport` with a `display_name` and a `card_last4` consisting of exactly four digits — the card on file is always available as a tender, which is why nothing else about it is stored. An optional `ebt_snap_balance_cents` seeds the customer's remaining SNAP benefit balance; it defaults to 0 and may never be negative. That balance is debited when a capture consumes benefit funds and restored when a capture is reversed.

Two customer fields are pure aggregates and are recomputed whenever any input to them changes. `ebt_captured_total_cents` is the sum of `ebt_captured_cents` over that customer's orders whose `payment_status` is `CAPTURED` or `REFUNDED`; it is the conservation counterpart of the benefit balance described in section 2.10. `credit_balance_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over every credit-ledger entry belonging to the customer, with no filtering by status of any kind, because ledger entries are immutable once written.

A credit-ledger entry records goodwill or restitution owed to a customer. `CustomerSupport` may post one directly, supplying `amount_cents` — a strictly positive credit of at least 1 cent and at most 100000 cents — a `reason_code`, and an optional `order_id` attributing the credit to a specific order. When `order_id` is omitted it is stored as null, which is how un-attributed support goodwill is represented; when it is supplied it must resolve to a stored order, but it is not required to be an order of the customer the entry belongs to and no status of that order is examined. The `reason_code` is one of `ABANDONMENT_INCONVENIENCE`, `REPLACEMENT_REFUND`, `PARITY_ADJUSTMENT`, `POST_CHECKOUT_REVERSAL`, or `SUPPORT_GOODWILL`; of those, only `PARITY_ADJUSTMENT`, `POST_CHECKOUT_REVERSAL` and `ABANDONMENT_INCONVENIENCE` are ever written by the system itself, so `REPLACEMENT_REFUND` and `SUPPORT_GOODWILL` reach the ledger only through a support-posted entry — in particular a rejected replacement writes no ledger entry of its own. Each entry also stores the `customer_id` it belongs to, and posting one immediately recomputes that customer's `credit_balance_cents`.

### 3.2. Composing an Order: Draft Lines, Snapshots and Authorization

An order belongs to exactly one customer and exactly one store. A `Customer` opens a draft by supplying `customer_id`, `retailer_id`, the store-to-door driving distance as `delivery_distance_mi` — at least 0.0, at most 60.0, carried to one decimal place — and a `delivery_deadline` expressed as an ISO 8601 UTC instant giving the latest acceptable hand-off time; that instant is stored exactly as supplied, normalized to UTC, and is never compared against the logical clock at creation, so a deadline that has already passed is accepted and simply flows into the batch aggregates and the window arithmetic as it stands. Two optional fields may be set at creation. `ebt_requested` defaults to false and signals that the customer wants SNAP-eligible lines paid from their benefit balance. `tip_amount_cents` defaults to 0 and must be at least 0 and at most 20000 cents.

Every order carries two independent status dimensions, and the whole point of separating them is that neither constrains the other. `fulfillment_status` is the physical dimension and takes the values `DRAFT`, `PLACED`, `IN_BATCH`, `SHOPPING`, `CHECKED_OUT`, `DELIVERED`, and `REASSIGN_PENDING`; a new order starts at `DRAFT` and `DELIVERED` is its terminal value. `payment_status` is the money dimension and takes the values `UNAUTHORIZED`, `AUTHORIZED`, `REAUTH_HELD`, `CAPTURED`, and `REFUNDED`; a new order starts at `UNAUTHORIZED` and `REFUNDED` is its terminal value. An order can perfectly well be `REAUTH_HELD` while it is `SHOPPING`, or still `AUTHORIZED` while it sits in `REASSIGN_PENDING`, and no implementation may collapse the two dimensions into a single lifecycle.

Lines are added to a draft one at a time by the owning `Customer`, and only while the order's `fulfillment_status` is `DRAFT`; an attempt on any other value is rejected with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_DRAFT` (409). A line names its `order_id` and the `catalog_item_id` it draws from. For an `EACH` item the customer supplies `requested_qty`, which defaults to 1 and must be at least 1 and at most 50, and leaves `requested_weight_lb` at its default of 0. For a `PER_LB` item the customer supplies `requested_weight_lb`, which must be greater than 0, and `requested_qty` must be exactly 1. Getting that pairing wrong — a weight on an each-priced item, a zero weight on a weighted item, a quantity above 1 on a weighted item — is a malformed request.

Adding a line freezes three values onto it so that later catalog edits cannot move a customer's price. `unit_price_cents_snapshot` receives the item's current `catalog_price_cents`, `in_store_price_cents_snapshot` receives the item's current `in_store_price_cents`, and `ebt_eligible_snapshot` receives the item's current `is_ebt_eligible`. The line's `status` begins at `REQUESTED`, and its three outcome fields begin at zero: `picked_qty`, `final_weight_lb`, and `final_price_cents`. A line's `status` moves through the values `REQUESTED`, `REPLACEMENT_PENDING`, `PICKED`, `REPLACED`, and `REFUNDED`, of which `PICKED`, `REPLACED`, and `REFUNDED` are terminal. The three snapshot fields are re-taken from the owning catalog item at the moment the order is placed, and after that they are rewritten only when a substitution is approved. Nothing about the item is checked against the order it joins: a line may legitimately draw from a catalog item published by a retailer other than the order's own `retailer_id`. And adding a line rewrites no field of the order at all — the order's `estimated_subtotal_cents` stays 0 and its `updated_at` and `version` are untouched — because that aggregate is first computed when the order is placed.

Placing the order is the customer's commitment point, and it is guarded by four conditions beyond authentication and ownership. The order's `fulfillment_status` must be `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_NOT_DRAFT`. The order must have at least one line, otherwise the request is malformed. The owning retailer's `status` must still be `ACTIVE`, otherwise the request is likewise malformed. And if `ebt_requested` is true, the owning retailer's `ebt_snap_enabled` must also be true, otherwise the placement is rejected with `ERR_EBT_NOT_ENABLED` (422). Both malformed cases carry `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), and the four conditions are evaluated in exactly the order just given: because placement declares no body field, those two are business preconditions rather than rank-5 schema checks and are therefore evaluated *after* the `DRAFT` gate, so an order that is both already placed and empty reports `ERR_ORDER_NOT_DRAFT`, and an empty draft at a paused retailer reports the malformed-request error for the missing line rather than for the retailer.

When those conditions hold, the system runs its **ESTIMATE** authorization pass and writes four money fields. First, each line's estimated money is computed on its own: for a `PER_LB` line, multiply `unit_price_cents_snapshot` by `requested_weight_lb` and round the product half up to a whole cent; for an `EACH` line, multiply `unit_price_cents_snapshot` by `requested_qty`, which is already whole cents. `estimated_subtotal_cents` is the sum of those per-line amounts over every line of the order whose `status` is not `REFUNDED`. Next, `authorization_ceiling_cents` is built by multiplying `estimated_subtotal_cents` by 1.15 — a fifteen percent headroom buffer that absorbs scale variance and substitutions — rounding that product half up to a whole cent, then adding the platform service fee of 399 cents and the order's current `tip_amount_cents`. Then the benefit slice: if and only if `ebt_requested` is true and the retailer's `ebt_snap_enabled` is true, sum the per-line estimated amounts over the non-`REFUNDED` lines whose `ebt_eligible_snapshot` is true; otherwise that eligible estimate is 0. `authorized_ebt_cents` is the smaller of the customer's current `ebt_snap_balance_cents` and that eligible estimate — the benefit slice is never buffered and never covers fees or tip. Finally `authorized_card_cents` is `authorization_ceiling_cents` minus `authorized_ebt_cents`, so the card carries the fees, the tip, and the entire buffer.

Placement also copies `tip_amount_cents` into `tip_original_cents`, advances `fulfillment_status` from `DRAFT` to `PLACED`, advances `payment_status` from `UNAUTHORIZED` to `AUTHORIZED`, and increases the order's `version` by 1.

The same four-field arithmetic is invoked a second time at the register, and there it behaves differently enough that the two passes must be understood as distinct variants of one rule. The **ESTIMATE** variant, used at placement and at re-authorization, reads requested weights and requested quantities, applies the 1.15 buffer to build the ceiling, and derives the benefit slice from estimated line money; it answers the question *how much may we hold*. The **FINAL** variant, used at checkout, reads the weights actually scanned and the quantities actually picked with approved substitutions already applied, applies **no** buffer at all, and caps the benefit slice a third time against `authorized_ebt_cents`; it answers the question *how much do we take*. For the same order the two variants disagree whenever a weighed item lands off its requested weight or a line ends up replaced or refunded.

Re-authorization exists because an approved substitution can push a basket past the ceiling that was authorized at placement. The owning `Customer` may re-authorize only while `payment_status` is `REAUTH_HELD`; on any other value the request is rejected with `ERR_REAUTH_NOT_REQUIRED` (409). Re-authorization re-runs the **ESTIMATE** pass over the line snapshots as they stand at that moment, rewriting `authorization_ceiling_cents`, `authorized_ebt_cents`, and `authorized_card_cents`; it moves `payment_status` from `REAUTH_HELD` back to `AUTHORIZED`, re-derives the whole composition of the batch holding this order — which decreases that batch's blocked-order counter by 1 — and increases the order's `version` by 1.

### 3.3. The Shopper Network: Enrollment, Ratings and Tier Access

Shoppers are enrolled by a `DispatchAdmin` with a `display_name`, a `service_area_code` naming the dispatch area they work, and a `vehicle_capacity_units` figure of at least 1 and at most 200 that expresses how many batch units they can carry at once. Enrollment initialises `committed_units`, `completed_batch_count`, and `abandonment_count` to 0 and runs the tier-scoring rule against an empty rating history, which lands both `tier` and `lifetime_tier` on `PROBATION`. A shopper's `status` is either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_REVIEW` and starts at `ACTIVE`; only an `ACTIVE` shopper may be offered a batch or claim one.

Three of a shopper's counters are aggregates rather than inputs. `committed_units` is the sum of `total_units` over the batches whose `claimed_shopper_id` is this shopper and whose status is `CLAIMED`, `SHOPPING`, or `CHECKED_OUT`; delivered and abandoned batches hold no capacity and contribute nothing. `completed_batch_count` is any injected historical count plus the number of batches this shopper claimed that reached `DELIVERED`. `abandonment_count` is any injected historical count plus the number of shopper-initiated abandonments; a release performed by the system on the shopper's behalf never increments it, and that asymmetry is what stops the suspension cascade from re-entering itself.

Ratings arrive from customers. A `Customer` rates a shopper by supplying `stars` — an integer of at least 1 and at most 5 — together with the `order_id` of the delivered order being rated. The order's `fulfillment_status` must be `DELIVERED`, otherwise the rating is rejected with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_DELIVERED` (409), and at most one rating row may exist for any given pairing of `shopper_id` and `order_id`; a second attempt is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RATING` (409). The rated shopper is taken from the path and is not required to be the shopper who actually delivered that order — no link between the two is verified — so the duplicate rule bites strictly per pairing: one delivered order may rate two different shoppers, and one shopper may be rated once by each of several delivered orders. `order_id` is a required field here, and ownership is compared against the resolved order's `customer_id` before the delivery gate is examined. Each rating row stores the `shopper_id` it applies to and receives a `sequence_no`, a 1-based index within that shopper's rating history assigned as one more than the highest `sequence_no` the shopper already has. Ratings backfilled by the back office carry a null `order_id`, which is how a rating that is not attributable to a delivered order is represented.

Because tier bands are otherwise reachable only by staging many deliveries, two administrative test-driving capabilities exist. The first injects an ordered star history: a `DispatchAdmin` supplies a `shopper_id` and a `stars_sequence`, and the system writes one rating row per element of the sequence, in list order, each with a null `order_id` and each receiving its `sequence_no` in that same list order, then re-scores the shopper exactly once after the whole sequence has been written. The second sets the reliability history directly: a `DispatchAdmin` may supply `completed_batch_count` and/or `abandonment_count` for a shopper, and the supplied figure becomes that shopper's whole stored counter rather than only its historical component: the injected component is set to the supplied figure minus the deliveries, or the shopper-initiated abandonments, the shopper has already accumulated on the platform, so the counter reads back as exactly the number supplied and not as that number plus those events. A counter the request omits is left exactly as it was. The shopper is then re-scored so that both scores and both tiers reflect the injected history. Injecting history never changes a shopper's `status` by itself — a shopper handed a large `abandonment_count` becomes `PROBATION` but does not thereby become `SUSPENDED_REVIEW`. Both capabilities answer with HTTP 200 and the re-scored shopper row in `data` rather than with the rows they wrote, and both advance that shopper's `version` by 1. On the star injection, `stars_sequence` is required and must be a JSON array whose every element is an integer of 1 through 5, any violation being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) decided before the `shopper_id` is resolved; an empty array is accepted and writes no rating row, re-scoring the shopper exactly as they stand.

Scoring itself is a weighted blend of quality and reliability, and it is evaluated in two variants that differ only in which ratings enter the mean. Both variants are recomputed on every rating, on every history injection, at delivery, at abandonment, and at reinstatement.

The **ROLLING_5** variant looks only at the shopper's five most recent rating rows, selected by ordering the shopper's whole history by `created_at` descending and breaking ties by `sequence_no` descending, then keeping the first five. The mean of those rows' `stars`, rounded half up to four decimal places, is stored as `rolling_recent_avg`; a shopper with no ratings at all has a `rolling_recent_avg` of 0.0. The **LIFETIME** variant takes the mean of `stars` over every rating row the shopper has ever received, rounded half up to four decimal places, and stores it as `lifetime_rating_avg`; again a shopper with no ratings has a `lifetime_rating_avg` of 0.0. Recent behaviour therefore dominates the rolling variant, and a shopper whose early ratings were poor and whose recent ratings are good will read higher on the rolling books than on the lifetime books.

From an average the score is built in three steps. The **quality component** maps a five-star average onto a zero-to-one scale: subtract 1 from the average and divide by 4, except that a shopper with no ratings at all has a quality component of exactly 0. The **reliability component** is 1 minus the shopper's `abandonment_count` divided by 5, and if that expression is negative the component is 0 instead — so a shopper with no abandonments scores 1, one abandonment scores 0.8, and five or more abandonments score 0. The **score** is 0.70 times the quality component plus 0.30 times the reliability component, rounded half up to four decimal places. Applying that to `rolling_recent_avg` produces `rolling_tier_score`; applying it to `lifetime_rating_avg` produces `lifetime_tier_score`. Both are stored to four decimals.

A score becomes a tier through a single band table, evaluated top down. A score of at least 0.9200 earns `DIAMOND`, but only if the shopper's `completed_batch_count` is at least 3; a score of at least 0.8000 earns `PLATINUM`; a score of at least 0.6000 earns `STANDARD`; anything lower is `PROBATION`. Note the shape of the diamond rule: a shopper who clears 0.9200 but has fewer than three completed batches falls through to the next band that their score satisfies, which for such a score is `PLATINUM`.

The two tiers the shopper carries are not symmetric in one respect. `tier` — the authoritative gate for dispatch eligibility and for claiming — is `PROBATION` outright whenever the shopper's `abandonment_count` has reached 2 or more, regardless of how good their ratings are; only if the shopper is below that abandonment threshold is `tier` read from the band table using `rolling_tier_score` and `completed_batch_count`. `lifetime_tier` is always read from the band table using `lifetime_tier_score` and `completed_batch_count`, and the abandonment override does **not** apply to it, so `lifetime_tier` is a display-only reflection of the shopper's career. A shopper can therefore be `PLATINUM` for batch access while reading only `STANDARD` on the lifetime books, or `PROBATION` on `tier` while still `PLATINUM` on `lifetime_tier`.

Wherever a tier is compared against another tier, the comparison is by rank: `PROBATION` ranks 0, `STANDARD` ranks 1, `PLATINUM` ranks 2, and `DIAMOND` ranks 3, and a shopper satisfies a floor when their rank is greater than or equal to the floor's rank.

Reinstatement is the only way back from suspension and it belongs to a `DispatchAdmin`. The shopper's `status` must be `SUSPENDED_REVIEW`, otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_SHOPPER_NOT_SUSPENDED` (409). The shopper's `lifetime_tier_score` must be at least 0.6000, otherwise it is rejected with `ERR_REINSTATE_SCORE_TOO_LOW` (422) — the lifetime books, not the rolling five, decide whether a suspended shopper has earned another chance. A successful reinstatement resets `abandonment_count` to 0, recomputes `rolling_recent_avg`, `lifetime_rating_avg`, `rolling_tier_score`, `lifetime_tier_score`, `tier`, and `lifetime_tier`, moves `status` from `SUSPENDED_REVIEW` to `ACTIVE`, and increases the shopper's `version` by 1. Because the reset clears the abandonment override, a reinstated shopper's `tier` typically jumps at the same instant.

### 3.4. Batch Construction and Store-Level Concurrency

A batch is the unit of work a shopper claims. A `DispatchAdmin` opens an empty one by supplying a `service_area_code`, a `shop_window_start`, and a `shop_window_end` that must be strictly after the start; a window whose end is at or before its start is malformed. An optional `tier_access_floor` restricts which shoppers may be offered or claim the batch and defaults to `STANDARD`; its permitted values are `PROBATION`, `STANDARD`, `PLATINUM`, and `DIAMOND`. A new batch starts with `status` `BUILDING`, every aggregate at 0, an empty `declined_shopper_ids` array, and both `offered_shopper_id` and `claimed_shopper_id` null.

A batch's `status` moves through `BUILDING`, `OFFERED`, `CLAIMED`, `SHOPPING`, `CHECKED_OUT`, `DELIVERED`, and `ABANDONED`. Only `DELIVERED` is terminal. The lifecycle is deliberately cyclic in two places: an `OFFERED` batch whose offer lapsed is re-offered and returns to `OFFERED`, and an `ABANDONED` batch is returned to the pool as `OFFERED`, so a single batch may be offered many times over its life. Alongside the status the batch keeps a small audit trail: `offered_shopper_id` names the shopper currently holding the standing offer and is null before the first dispatch and after a claim; `claimed_shopper_id` names the shopper who accepted and is null before the claim and after any release; `declined_shopper_ids` starts as an empty list and accumulates the identifiers of shoppers who may no longer be re-selected for this batch, in append order with the oldest exclusion first, and is always serialized as that array of bare identifier strings; `offer_expires_at` holds the deadline of the standing offer; `reassignment_count` starts at 0 and counts how many times the batch has been returned to the pool after an abandonment; and `abandoned_at` records the logical-clock instant of the most recent abandonment.

Membership is carried by its own row, one per order, joining a `batch_id` to an `order_id`. Each membership row receives a `sequence_no`, a 1-based index within that batch assigned as one more than the highest `sequence_no` already present, which is equivalently the position of the row when the batch's membership rows are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The row also freezes a `units` figure at attachment time: sum, over the order's lines, the `requested_qty` of each `EACH`-priced line and the value 1 for each `PER_LB` line. That figure is frozen — later line churn does not move an already-recorded membership `units` value, and even a line that ends up `REFUNDED` keeps contributing its frozen units.

Every attachment or member-line change re-derives the batch's composition from its membership rows:

- `total_units` is the sum of `units` over the batch's membership rows. It is the quantity matched against a shopper's `vehicle_capacity_units`.
- `total_distance_mi` is the sum of `delivery_distance_mi` over the member orders, carried to one decimal place.
- `store_count` is the number of distinct `retailer_id` values across the member orders — this is what makes a batch multi-store.
- `delivery_deadline` is the earliest `delivery_deadline` among the member orders, and is null while the batch has no members.
- `total_weight_lb` is a sum over the member orders' lines, skipping every line whose `status` is `REFUNDED`. A `PER_LB` line contributes its `final_weight_lb` when that field is greater than 0 and its `requested_weight_lb` otherwise. An `EACH` line contributes the item's `unit_weight_lb` multiplied by its effective quantity, where the effective quantity is `picked_qty` when the line's `status` is `PICKED` or `REPLACED` and `requested_qty` otherwise. The per-line contributions are accumulated at full precision and it is the *total* that is rounded half up to one decimal place, exactly once and after the summation; the per-line-rounding rule of section 2.6 governs money only and does not apply to this weight sum.
- `checkout_blocked_count` is the number of member orders whose `payment_status` is `REAUTH_HELD`. Checkout requires it to be 0.

Attaching an order is a `DispatchAdmin` operation and it is the most heavily guarded write in the build phase. Beyond the stale-batch-version check of section 2.7, an order may hold at most one membership row for its entire lifetime — a second attachment anywhere is rejected with `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_BATCHED` (409), and as section 2.8 explains this rule is consulted before the status gates so that the specific error is observable. The order's `fulfillment_status` must be `PLACED`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_NOT_PLACED` (409). The target batch's `status` must be `BUILDING`, otherwise `ERR_BATCH_NOT_BUILDING` (409).

The last gate on attachment protects the store itself. No supermarket wants an unbounded number of platform shoppers in its aisles at the same time, so each retailer carries a `concurrent_shopper_cap`. Before the order is attached, the system counts the *other* batches that would put a shopper into that retailer during an overlapping window: take the retailer of the order being added, then count every batch other than the target batch whose `status` is `OFFERED`, `CLAIMED`, or `SHOPPING`, which already carries at least one member order for that same retailer, and whose shopping window intersects the target batch's shopping window. The intersection test here uses **closed** intervals: the two windows collide when the other batch's `shop_window_start` is at or before the target batch's `shop_window_end` and the target batch's `shop_window_start` is at or before the other batch's `shop_window_end`, so windows that merely touch at an endpoint do collide. Batches in `BUILDING`, `DELIVERED`, or `ABANDONED` are ignored entirely. If that count has reached the retailer's `concurrent_shopper_cap` or exceeds it, the attachment is rejected with `ERR_RETAILER_CONCURRENCY_CAP_REACHED` (409). Note that the rule is an aggregate count against the cap, not a pairwise veto: with a cap of 2, one colliding batch is fine and a second is fine, and only the third is refused.

When all gates pass, the membership row is created and returned, the batch's composition is re-derived, the order's `fulfillment_status` moves from `PLACED` to `IN_BATCH`, and the batch's `version` increases by 1. Membership is readable from both directions: the rows of a given batch can be listed, and the rows referencing a given order can be listed as well, where the result has length 0 or 1 by construction.

### 3.5. Dispatch, Offers and the Claim Gate

Dispatch is where the platform's allocation policy lives. A `DispatchAdmin` offers a batch, and the system picks **exactly one** shopper — never a broadcast, never a list. The selection is fully deterministic: no randomness, and the shopper's `id` is never consulted as an ordering key.

Eligibility is a conjunction of six predicates. A shopper is a candidate only if their `status` is `ACTIVE`; their `service_area_code` equals the batch's `service_area_code`; the rank of their `tier` is greater than or equal to the rank of the batch's `tier_access_floor`; their `committed_units` plus the batch's `total_units` is less than or equal to their `vehicle_capacity_units`; the batch's window does not conflict with a window they already hold, as defined below; and their `id` does not appear in the batch's `declined_shopper_ids`.

The surviving candidates are then ranked, and the first row wins: highest `tier` rank first, then highest `rolling_tier_score`, then lowest `committed_units`, then earliest `created_at`, and — because `created_at` is read from the logical clock, so shoppers enrolled without an intervening clock move all share one instant — finally the sequence in which the server created the shopper rows, earliest first. That last key is what makes the ranking total, and it is the same creation sequence that section 2.3 uses to break `created_at` ties in list reads. Note that the *rolling* score is what ranks the pool — `lifetime_tier_score` plays no part in dispatch. If the candidate set is empty the batch is left exactly as it was and the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SHOPPER` (422).

Window conflict deserves its own paragraph because its interval arithmetic is exact and asymmetric. The batch being considered occupies the half-open interval that begins at its `shop_window_start` and ends at its `delivery_deadline`. Each batch the shopper already holds — meaning every other batch whose `claimed_shopper_id` is that shopper and whose `status` is `CLAIMED`, `SHOPPING`, or `CHECKED_OUT` — occupies a half-open interval that begins at *its* `shop_window_start` and ends 15 minutes after *its* `delivery_deadline`. The 15-minute turnaround buffer is applied to the **end** of the held interval only, never to its start and never to the candidate interval. A conflict exists when the candidate interval's start is strictly before the held interval's end **and** the held interval's start is strictly before the candidate interval's end. Both comparisons are strict, so two raw intervals that abut exactly are legal, but a gap shorter than the 15-minute turnaround is not. Batches in `DELIVERED` or `ABANDONED` are never in conflict with anything, since a finished or dropped batch holds no time.

A first dispatch requires the batch's `status` to be `BUILDING`; a re-offer requires the batch's `status` to be `OFFERED` **and** the logical clock to have reached or passed `offer_expires_at`. Any other combination — a batch that is `CLAIMED`, or an `OFFERED` batch whose offer is still live — is rejected with `ERR_BATCH_NOT_OFFERED` (409). In both forms the batch must already carry at least one member order, that is `total_units` must be at least 1; an empty batch is rejected with `ERR_BATCH_EMPTY` (422), and as section 2.8 states this rejection is reported in preference to selector exhaustion.

On a first dispatch the batch moves from `BUILDING` to `OFFERED`, `offered_shopper_id` becomes the winner, `offer_expires_at` becomes the current logical-clock instant plus 30 minutes, and the batch's `version` increases by 1. On a re-offer the batch stays at `OFFERED` and one extra step comes first: the outgoing `offered_shopper_id` is appended to `declined_shopper_ids` **before** the candidate pool is computed, so a shopper who let an offer lapse is excluded from the re-selection and can never be handed the same batch again. Then, exactly as on a first dispatch, `offered_shopper_id` becomes the new winner, `offer_expires_at` is reset to the current logical-clock instant plus 30 minutes, and `version` increases by 1. This exclusion-on-retry behaviour is what lets a batch cycle through a whole service area's roster without repetition.

Claiming is the `Shopper` side of the handshake, and the checks run in a fixed order. The batch's `status` must be `OFFERED`, otherwise `ERR_BATCH_NOT_OFFERED` (409). The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the batch's `offered_shopper_id`, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403) — an offer is personal and a shopper cannot poach a run offered to a colleague. The logical clock must be strictly before `offer_expires_at`; a claim at exactly the expiry instant or later is rejected with `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED` (409). The shopper's window must not conflict with a window they already hold, under exactly the interval arithmetic described above, otherwise `ERR_SHOPPER_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). The shopper's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_SHOPPER_SUSPENDED` (403). The rank of the shopper's `tier` must be greater than or equal to the rank of the batch's `tier_access_floor`, otherwise `ERR_TIER_BELOW_BATCH_FLOOR` (403) — and because `tier` carries the abandonment override described in section 3.3, a shopper whose ratings are excellent but whose `abandonment_count` has reached 2 is `PROBATION` and will be refused a `STANDARD`-floored batch. Last, the shopper's `committed_units` plus the batch's `total_units` must be less than or equal to their `vehicle_capacity_units`, otherwise `ERR_SHOPPER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).

A successful claim moves the batch from `OFFERED` to `CLAIMED`, writes the acting shopper's identifier into `claimed_shopper_id`, clears `offered_shopper_id` back to null, re-derives the shopper's `committed_units` — which rises by the batch's `total_units` — and increases the batch's `version` by 1. Note that the eligibility predicates are checked both at selection time and again at claim time; a batch offered thirty minutes ago may no longer be claimable if the shopper has taken on other work in the meantime.

Arriving at the store is a separate step. The claiming `Shopper` starts shopping, which requires the batch's `status` to be `CLAIMED` — otherwise `ERR_BATCH_NOT_CLAIMED` (409) — and requires the logical clock to have reached `shop_window_start`; an earlier attempt is rejected with `ERR_SHOP_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` (409). Note the inclusivity: starting exactly at `shop_window_start` is permitted. The batch moves from `CLAIMED` to `SHOPPING` and every member order's `fulfillment_status` moves from `IN_BATCH` to `SHOPPING`, which is what makes the member lines pickable.

### 3.6. In-Aisle Execution: Picks, Weights and Substitutions

Once a batch is `SHOPPING`, the claiming `Shopper` works the member orders line by line. Two things can happen to a line in the aisle: it is picked as ordered, or a substitute is proposed for it.

Picking a line as ordered requires the owning batch's `status` to be `SHOPPING` — otherwise `ERR_BATCH_NOT_SHOPPING` (409) — and requires the acting shopper to be that batch's `claimed_shopper_id`. A line whose order holds no batch membership at all is refused with that same `ERR_BATCH_NOT_SHOPPING` rather than with a 404, since the line itself resolved. The line's own `status` must be `REQUESTED`, otherwise `ERR_LINE_NOT_PICKABLE` (409); a line already picked, already replaced, already refunded, or currently awaiting a substitution answer cannot be picked. The line's catalog item must have `availability` of `IN_STOCK`; picking an item the retailer has marked `OUT_OF_STOCK` is rejected with `ERR_ITEM_OUT_OF_STOCK` (409).

For an `EACH` line the shopper reports `picked_qty`, the number of units actually taken off the shelf; if the field is omitted the line's `requested_qty` is used. For a `PER_LB` line the shopper reports `final_weight_lb`, the pounds actually read from the scale; if the field is omitted the line's `requested_weight_lb` is used. `picked_qty` carries the same bounds as `requested_qty` — at least 1 and at most 50 — and `final_weight_lb` may not be negative. Unlike the pairing rules that govern line creation, the aisle is forgiving about the field that does not apply: a `final_weight_lb` supplied on an `EACH` line and a `picked_qty` supplied on a `PER_LB` line are each validated against those bounds and then ignored, never rejected for being the wrong member of the pair. Scales and shoppers are imprecise, so a tolerance applies to weighted lines: the absolute difference between `final_weight_lb` and `requested_weight_lb` must be less than or equal to twenty percent of `requested_weight_lb`. That comparison is made against the weight exactly as the shopper supplied it, before any rounding; only once the tolerance has been cleared is the reported weight rounded half up to one decimal place and stored in `final_weight_lb`. A reading whose raw value falls outside the tolerance is therefore refused even when its one-decimal rounding would have fallen inside it. The bound is inclusive — a deviation of exactly twenty percent is accepted — and a larger deviation is rejected with `ERR_WEIGHT_VARIANCE_EXCEEDED` (422), which is the system's way of forcing the shopper to propose a substitution rather than quietly charging for a very different quantity.

A successful pick prices the line and moves its `status` from `REQUESTED` to `PICKED`. Line money is always computed the same way, wherever it is computed. A line whose `status` is `REFUNDED`, `REQUESTED`, or `REPLACEMENT_PENDING` has a `final_price_cents` of exactly 0. For a `PER_LB` line, `final_price_cents` is `unit_price_cents_snapshot` multiplied by the line's effective weight, rounded half up to a whole cent, where the effective weight is `final_weight_lb` when that field is greater than 0 and `requested_weight_lb` otherwise. For an `EACH` line, `final_price_cents` is `unit_price_cents_snapshot` multiplied by the line's effective quantity, which is `picked_qty` when the line's `status` is `PICKED` or `REPLACED` and `requested_qty` otherwise. An order's `captured_subtotal_cents` is the sum of `final_price_cents` across **all** of that order's lines, including the refunded ones, which contribute exactly 0. As section 2.6 insists, each weighted line is rounded to whole cents before the addition, never after. A pick also re-derives the owning batch's `total_weight_lb`, since the scale reading has just changed the load the shopper is carrying. What a pick does not touch is the owning order: `captured_subtotal_cents` is written only by the register, so between the first pick and checkout an order still reports the figure it already held — 0 for an order that has never been rung up — and neither the order's `updated_at` nor its `version` moves on a pick.

When an item simply is not there, the `Shopper` proposes a substitute instead. The proposal names the `batch_id` being shopped, the `order_line_id` that cannot be filled, and the `proposed_catalog_item_id` found in the aisle. For an each-priced substitute the shopper supplies `proposed_qty`, which defaults to 1 and must be at least 1 and at most 50; for a weight-priced substitute the shopper supplies `proposed_weight_lb`, which defaults to 0. The owning batch must be `SHOPPING` and the acting shopper must be its `claimed_shopper_id`; the target line's `status` must be `REQUESTED`, otherwise `ERR_LINE_NOT_PICKABLE`. The supplied `batch_id` is the batch whose standing claim authorizes the proposal and is not checked against the target line's own membership, so it is that batch's `status` and then its `claimed_shopper_id` that are gated, in that order, before the line's status is examined. As in the aisle and unlike line creation, the inapplicable member of the quantity/weight pair is stored as supplied and ignored rather than rejected: `proposed_qty` is recorded even for a `PER_LB` substitute and `proposed_weight_lb` even for an `EACH` one, and only the one matching the proposed item's `pricing_unit` is read if the request is approved. Two business rules then apply. The proposed item's `retailer_id` must equal the `retailer_id` of the order that owns the target line — you cannot substitute across stores — and a violation is rejected with `ERR_REPLACEMENT_RETAILER_MISMATCH` (422). And if the target line's `ebt_eligible_snapshot` is true while the owning order's `authorized_ebt_cents` is greater than 0, the proposed item's `is_ebt_eligible` must also be true; benefit funds may never migrate onto an ineligible item, and an attempt is rejected with `ERR_EBT_INELIGIBLE_REPLACEMENT` (422).

Creating the proposal writes a replacement request with `status` `PENDING`, a `resolution_effect` of `NONE`, and an `expires_at` of the current logical-clock instant plus 20 minutes, and it moves the target line's `status` from `REQUESTED` to `REPLACEMENT_PENDING`. It also records `price_delta_cents`, which tells the customer how much the swap would cost them: compute the proposed line's money — the proposed item's `catalog_price_cents` multiplied by the proposed weight exactly as supplied — before that weight is rounded half up to one decimal place for storage in `proposed_weight_lb` — and rounded half up to a whole cent when the proposed item is `PER_LB`, or its `catalog_price_cents` multiplied by `proposed_qty` when the proposed item is `EACH` — then subtract the original line's estimated money, computed exactly as at placement from the line's `unit_price_cents_snapshot` against its requested quantity or requested weight. The difference may be negative when the substitute is cheaper.

A replacement request ends in exactly one of three states — `APPROVED`, `REJECTED`, or `EXPIRED` — and each writes a different `resolution_effect` and a different set of downstream money effects. The owning `Customer` decides the first two; the system decides the third.

**Approval.** The acting `X-User-ID` must be the `customer_id` of the order owning the target line; because that field is populated in every state, the ownership comparison keeps its ordinary rank-4 position and is therefore evaluated *before* the state gate, so a stranger addressing an already-resolved request is told `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` and not `ERR_REPLACEMENT_NOT_PENDING`. The request's `status` must then be `PENDING` and the logical clock must be strictly before `expires_at`; both a non-pending request and a still-pending-but-lapsed request are rejected with `ERR_REPLACEMENT_NOT_PENDING` (409). Approval sets `status` to `APPROVED` and `resolution_effect` to `LINE_REPLACED`, then runs three cascade links. First, the line is repointed: its `catalog_item_id` becomes the proposed item, its `unit_price_cents_snapshot`, `in_store_price_cents_snapshot`, and `ebt_eligible_snapshot` are all re-taken from the proposed item, its `picked_qty` or `final_weight_lb` is taken from `proposed_qty` or `proposed_weight_lb` respectively while the other member of that pair is written to 0 — a weight-priced substitute leaves `picked_qty` 0 and an each-priced substitute leaves `final_weight_lb` 0, however those two outcome fields stood beforehand — its `final_price_cents` is written by the line-pricing rule above, and its `status` moves from `REPLACEMENT_PENDING` to `REPLACED`. Second, the order's `estimated_subtotal_cents` is recomputed over the current line snapshots under the estimate rule of section 3.2. Note what the repoint leaves alone: the line's `requested_qty` and `requested_weight_lb` still hold what the customer originally asked for, so that recomputation multiplies the *new* `unit_price_cents_snapshot` by the *originally requested* quantity or weight and never by `proposed_qty` or `proposed_weight_lb`. If the resulting projected total — `estimated_subtotal_cents` plus the 399-cent service fee plus the order's current `tip_amount_cents` — is strictly greater than the order's `authorization_ceiling_cents`, the order's `payment_status` moves from `AUTHORIZED` to `REAUTH_HELD`. Third, the owning batch's whole composition is re-derived from its membership rows exactly as section 3.4 prescribes, not merely its blocked counter: `checkout_blocked_count`, which in the hold case increases by 1 and blocks checkout with `ERR_ORDER_PAYMENT_HELD` until the customer re-authorizes, and `total_weight_lb` alongside it, since repointing the line at a different item and settling it at the proposed weight or quantity has just changed the load the shopper is carrying. Approval increases the order's `version` by 1.

**Rejection.** The acting `X-User-ID` must be the owning order's `customer_id`, compared first exactly as on approval, and the request's `status` must be `PENDING` — otherwise `ERR_REPLACEMENT_NOT_PENDING`. There is no expiry check on a rejection: a customer may always refuse a lapsed proposal. Rejection sets `status` to `REJECTED` and `resolution_effect` to `LINE_REFUNDED`, and applies the platform's **refund fallback**: rather than substituting, the customer is simply not charged for the line. The line's `status` moves from `REPLACEMENT_PENDING` to `REFUNDED`, its `picked_qty` is set to 0, its `final_weight_lb` is set to 0, and its `final_price_cents` is set to 0. The line's snapshot-priced amount — its estimated money computed exactly as at placement, from `unit_price_cents_snapshot` against its requested quantity or requested weight, and not the `final_price_cents` the line is being stripped of — is added to the order's `refunded_cents`, and the order's `estimated_subtotal_cents` is recomputed, which lowers it because a `REFUNDED` line no longer contributes. The owning batch's composition is re-derived from its membership rows as well, most consequentially `total_weight_lb`, which now skips the refunded line. What a rejection never revisits is the order's `payment_status`: an order already sitting in `REAUTH_HELD` stays there even though stripping the line has just pulled its projected total back under the ceiling, and its batch's `checkout_blocked_count` therefore stays as it was, so nothing but an explicit re-authorization clears that hold.

**Expiry.** A request that is still `PENDING` when the owning batch leaves `SHOPPING` is resolved by the system, and which effect it takes depends on how the batch left. On a checkout sweep, `status` becomes `EXPIRED`, `resolution_effect` becomes `LINE_REFUNDED`, and the target line moves from `REPLACEMENT_PENDING` to `REFUNDED` with `final_price_cents` 0 — the same refund fallback as an explicit rejection. On an abandonment, `status` becomes `EXPIRED` but `resolution_effect` becomes `NONE`, and the target line returns from `REPLACEMENT_PENDING` to `REQUESTED` so the next shopper can try again. The distinction matters: a swept request permanently drops the line from the basket, while an abandoned one merely rewinds it.

### 3.7. Checkout: Final Pricing, Split Tender and Parity

Checkout is the single most consequential operation in the API: it is where estimates become charges. The claiming `Shopper` rings up every member order of the batch at once, and is answered with the updated batch row rather than with the member orders the operation re-priced. The batch's `status` must be `SHOPPING`, otherwise `ERR_BATCH_NOT_SHOPPING` (409), and the acting shopper must be its `claimed_shopper_id`. No member line may still be sitting in `REQUESTED` — every line must be picked, replaced, refunded, or at least awaiting a substitution answer before the register will open; a member line still in `REQUESTED` is refused with `ERR_LINE_NOT_PICKABLE` (409), and that refusal is evaluated before the payment-hold one below. And the batch's `checkout_blocked_count` must be 0, meaning no member order sits in `REAUTH_HELD`; otherwise checkout is refused with `ERR_ORDER_PAYMENT_HELD` (409).

Checkout then executes a fixed sequence, and the order of the steps is load-bearing.

The **sweep comes first**. Every replacement request that is still `PENDING` on a member line is resolved as described in section 3.6: `status` `EXPIRED`, `resolution_effect` `LINE_REFUNDED`, and the line moved from `REPLACEMENT_PENDING` to `REFUNDED` with `final_price_cents` 0. When the sweep refunds at least one line it has changed a member line, so the batch's composition is re-derived from its membership rows exactly as section 3.4 prescribes — most consequentially `total_weight_lb`, which skips every line whose `status` is `REFUNDED` — and that re-derivation happens before any pricing; a sweep that refunds nothing leaves the composition as it stands. Because the re-derivation runs inside the same checkout operation it does not advance the batch's `version` a second time, and the corrected `total_weight_lb` is what a later delivery reads when it sizes `heavy_pay_cents`. Sweeping before pricing is what makes an unanswered proposal fall back to a refund rather than blocking the register.

Then **every member line is re-priced** under the line-pricing rule of section 3.6, and each member order's `captured_subtotal_cents` is set to the sum of `final_price_cents` over all of its lines.

Then the **split tender is allocated**, and this is the FINAL variant of the authorization arithmetic. Buckets are ordered: benefit funds first, card second. For each member order, the grand total is `captured_subtotal_cents` plus the 399-cent service fee plus the order's current `tip_amount_cents`. The benefit-eligible money actually in the cart is the sum of `final_price_cents` over the order's lines whose `ebt_eligible_snapshot` is true **and** whose `status` is `PICKED` or `REPLACED`. If and only if the order's `ebt_requested` is true and the retailer's `ebt_snap_enabled` is true, `ebt_captured_cents` is the smallest of three numbers: the customer's live `ebt_snap_balance_cents` at this moment, that benefit-eligible cart money, and the order's `authorized_ebt_cents` ceiling fixed at placement. Otherwise `ebt_captured_cents` is 0. `card_captured_cents` is then the grand total minus `ebt_captured_cents` — the service fee and the tip always ride on the card, never on benefit funds.

The three-way minimum is what makes the FINAL variant differ from the ESTIMATE variant of section 3.2: the estimate was capped twice, by the balance and by estimated eligible money, while the capture is capped a third time by the authorized ceiling, so a customer whose weighed items came in heavier than requested cannot have more benefit money taken than was authorized.

Member orders are processed in ascending `sequence_no` order of their batch membership rows, and each order's benefit debit is applied to the customer's `ebt_snap_balance_cents` **before** the next member order is priced. When one customer has two orders in the same batch, the second order therefore sees a balance that the first has already shrunk. Each capture debits `ebt_snap_balance_cents` by that order's `ebt_captured_cents` and re-derives the customer's `ebt_captured_total_cents`.

With money settled, each member order's `payment_status` moves from `AUTHORIZED` to `CAPTURED` and its `fulfillment_status` moves from `SHOPPING` to `CHECKED_OUT`, and the batch itself moves from `SHOPPING` to `CHECKED_OUT`.

The final step of checkout is the **price-parity accounting**, the platform's answer to the regulatory question "did the customer pay more in the app than they would have paid in the store?" Only the lines that were actually bought count — those whose `status` is `PICKED` or `REPLACED`; refunded and still-requested lines contribute nothing to either side. The catalog subtotal is the sum of `final_price_cents` over those priced lines. The in-store subtotal is computed the same way but from the shelf-price snapshots: for a `PER_LB` line, `in_store_price_cents_snapshot` multiplied by the line's effective weight and rounded half up to a whole cent; for an `EACH` line, `in_store_price_cents_snapshot` multiplied by the line's effective quantity. The difference, catalog subtotal minus in-store subtotal, is stored on the order as `parity_markup_cents`. The effective markup fraction is `parity_markup_cents` divided by the in-store subtotal, rounded half up to four decimal places; it is not itself a stored field and appears nowhere in the order's payload — the order stores only `parity_markup_cents` and `parity_disclosed`, and the fraction is recomputed on demand by the parity-disclosure read. It is exactly 0.0000 when the in-store subtotal is 0 — which is also what an order with no priced lines at all reports, alongside a markup of 0.

Two consequences follow from that fraction, and they are mutually exclusive. When the retailer's `parity_disclosure_required` is true, the retailer has told its customers about the markup, so no money moves: the parity credit is 0. The disclosure duty is triggered when `parity_disclosure_required` is true **and** the effective markup fraction is strictly greater than 0.10 — a markup of exactly ten percent does not trigger it. When the retailer's `parity_disclosure_required` is false, the platform makes the customer whole for the *excess* only: the parity credit is `parity_markup_cents` minus ten percent of the in-store subtotal rounded half up to a whole cent, floored at 0 so a below-threshold markup credits nothing. If that credit is greater than 0, checkout creates a credit-ledger entry with `reason_code` `PARITY_ADJUSTMENT` for that amount and re-derives the customer's `credit_balance_cents`. Checkout also stamps the order's `parity_disclosed` with the value of the retailer's `parity_disclosure_required` as read at that moment. Member orders are visited in this step in the same ascending membership `sequence_no` order as in the tender step, so their `PARITY_ADJUSTMENT` entries land in the credit ledger in that order.

Parity is also readable on demand for a single order, and the read is computed live rather than served from stored fields. It reports the order's `order_id`, the `catalog_total_cents` and `in_store_total_cents` subtotals, the `markup_cents` difference, the `effective_markup_pct` fraction to four decimals, a `disclosure_required` boolean applying the strict ten-percent test above, and a `disclosure_satisfied` boolean. Satisfaction is defined so that a retailer with no duty is trivially compliant: `disclosure_satisfied` is true whenever `disclosure_required` is false, and otherwise it is the stored `parity_disclosed` flag on the order. Because `parity_disclosed` is written only at checkout, an order that is still `SHOPPING` and already past the threshold reads `disclosure_required` true with `disclosure_satisfied` false — that gap is the intended signal to the retailer, not a defect.

### 3.8. Delivery, Tip Windows and Shopper Pay

Handing the groceries over completes the batch. The claiming `Shopper` records the delivery, which requires the batch's `status` to be `CHECKED_OUT` — otherwise `ERR_BATCH_NOT_CHECKED_OUT` (409) — and requires the acting shopper to be its `claimed_shopper_id`. The batch moves from `CHECKED_OUT` to `DELIVERED`, which is terminal, and every member order's `fulfillment_status` moves from `CHECKED_OUT` to `DELIVERED`.

Delivery is also the instant that freezes the tip baseline. For each member order, `delivered_at` is set to the current logical-clock instant, `tip_original_cents` is re-snapshotted from the order's current `tip_amount_cents`, and `tip_window_closes_at` is set to `delivered_at` plus 2 hours. On the shopper's record, `completed_batch_count` rises by 1, `committed_units` falls by the batch's `total_units`, and both tier variants are re-scored, so a run that pushes a shopper's completed count to 3 can promote them into `DIAMOND` on the spot if their `rolling_tier_score` already cleared 0.9200.

Delivery finally writes one payout row, with `status` `PROVISIONAL`, naming the `batch_id` it pays for and the `shopper_id` it pays; that row is not what the delivery answers with — like checkout, delivery returns the batch it advanced, so the new payout must be read back from the payout endpoints. Its money fields come from two rules evaluated together at the delivery instant.

The first rule computes what the platform owes for the *work*, independent of tips:

- `base_pay_cents` is a flat 700 cents plus 25 cents for every unit in the batch's `total_units`.
- `heavy_pay_cents` is a weight-tier bonus, evaluated top down with inclusive thresholds: 900 cents once the batch's `total_weight_lb` reaches 120.0 pounds, otherwise 600 cents once it reaches 80.0 pounds, otherwise 300 cents once it reaches 50.0 pounds, otherwise 0. Only one tier pays, and the heaviest satisfied tier wins.
- `mileage_bonus_cents` compensates long drives beyond a free allowance. Subtract 8.0 miles from the batch's `total_distance_mi`; if the result is negative use 0.0 instead. Multiply that figure by 60 cents per mile and round the single product half up to a whole cent. Then cap the result at 1200 cents — the rounding happens once, before the cap is applied.
- `multi_store_bonus_cents` is 250 cents for every store beyond the first: multiply 250 by the batch's `store_count` minus 1, using 0 whenever `store_count` is 0 or 1.

The sum of those four amounts is the batch work pay, and tips are deliberately not part of it.

The second rule assembles the payable total, and its six steps must be evaluated in exactly this order, because the guarantee top-up is derived from a subtotal that already includes tips and tip protection. Topping up from work pay alone and adding tips afterwards produces a different total for the same inputs and is wrong.

1. Take the batch work pay computed above.
2. Compute `tip_pass_through_cents` as the sum of `tip_amount_cents` over the batch's member orders, read at this instant.
3. Compute the tip baseline as the sum of `tip_original_cents` over the member orders, and the tip cut as the baseline minus `tip_pass_through_cents`, using 0 whenever that difference is negative. Tip protection is eligible only when the baseline is greater than 0 **and** the cut is at least half the baseline. That ratio test is performed by integer cross-multiplication: the cut multiplied by 100 must be greater than or equal to the baseline multiplied by 50.
4. When protection is eligible, `tip_protection_cents` is the smaller of 1000 cents and the tip cut; when it is not eligible, `tip_protection_cents` is 0.
5. Add the batch work pay, `tip_pass_through_cents`, and `tip_protection_cents` to form a subtotal. The guarantee floor is 80 cents multiplied by the batch's `total_units`. `guarantee_top_up_cents` is the guarantee floor minus that subtotal, using 0 whenever the difference is negative.
6. `total_payout_cents` is the subtotal plus `guarantee_top_up_cents`.

That assembly runs in three variants, and they differ in exactly one place plus their triggering moment. In the **PROVISIONAL** variant — used at delivery and again on every accepted tip adjustment while the payout is still `PROVISIONAL` — `tip_protection_cents` is forced to 0. At delivery this falls out of the arithmetic anyway, since `tip_pass_through_cents` equals the baseline and the cut is 0. On a later tip adjustment the cut is real, but protection is deliberately withheld, because the customer may still raise the tip again before the window closes. A provisional recompute therefore rewrites `tip_pass_through_cents` and, through step 5, may rewrite `guarantee_top_up_cents` and `total_payout_cents`, while leaving `tip_protection_cents` at 0. In the **FINAL** variant — used when the payout is closed out — the adjusted tips are re-read, so a reduced tip both lowers `tip_pass_through_cents` and can add `tip_protection_cents`, and can move `guarantee_top_up_cents` as well. In the **FORFEITED** variant — used when a shopper walks away from a batch — every money field is written as 0: `base_pay_cents`, `heavy_pay_cents`, `mileage_bonus_cents`, `multi_store_bonus_cents`, `tip_pass_through_cents`, `tip_protection_cents`, `guarantee_top_up_cents`, and `total_payout_cents`. The per-unit guarantee floor explicitly does **not** apply to a forfeited batch.

Tip adjustment is the customer's post-delivery privilege and the shopper's exposure, which is why it is fenced on four sides. The owning `Customer` supplies a new `tip_amount_cents` — still bounded by 0 and 20000 cents — together with `expected_order_version` as described in section 2.7. The order's `fulfillment_status` must be `DELIVERED`, otherwise `ERR_ORDER_NOT_DELIVERED` (409). The logical clock must be strictly before the order's `tip_window_closes_at`; an adjustment at exactly the closing instant or later is rejected with `ERR_TIP_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409). And the order's `tip_adjustment_count` must be strictly less than 2 — two accepted adjustments per window is the ceiling, and a third is rejected with `ERR_TIP_ADJUSTMENT_LIMIT` (409). An accepted adjustment rewrites `tip_amount_cents`, increases `tip_adjustment_count` by 1, increases the order's `version` by 1, and re-runs the work-pay rule and the PROVISIONAL assembly over the batch's live payout row — the earliest-created row for that batch whose `status` is `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`, forfeited rows left behind by the batch's earlier holders being skipped, and then only while that row's `status` is still `PROVISIONAL`, so a payout that has already been closed out is left untouched — rewriting `tip_pass_through_cents`, `tip_protection_cents`, `guarantee_top_up_cents`, and `total_payout_cents`.

Closing the payout belongs to a `DispatchAdmin`. A payout row for the batch must exist and its `status` must be `PROVISIONAL`; a missing row, or one already `FINAL` or `FORFEITED`, is rejected with `ERR_PAYOUT_NOT_PROVISIONAL` (409). The logical clock must have reached or passed **every** member order's `tip_window_closes_at`; if any window is still open the request is rejected with `ERR_PAYOUT_WINDOW_OPEN` (409). Closing re-runs the six-step assembly in its FINAL variant over the adjusted tips, rewrites `tip_pass_through_cents`, `tip_protection_cents`, `guarantee_top_up_cents`, and `total_payout_cents`, moves the payout's `status` from `PROVISIONAL` to `FINAL`, and increases the payout row's `version` by 1. This is the point at which tip-baiting protection is actually paid: a customer who dropped a 1000-cent tip to 200 cents cut 800 cents, which is at least half the baseline, so the platform contributes 800 cents of make-good — and had they cut a 3000-cent tip to zero, the make-good would be capped at 1000 cents.

### 3.9. Abandonment, Suspension and Reassignment

Sometimes a shopper walks away. Abandonment is legal at three points in a batch's life — while it is `CLAIMED`, while it is `SHOPPING`, and even after it is `CHECKED_OUT` but before hand-off — and illegal everywhere else; an attempt on a batch outside that set is rejected with `ERR_BATCH_NOT_CLAIMED` (409). Only the batch's `claimed_shopper_id` may abandon it. An optional `reason_note` may accompany the request for the operator's benefit; it is not persisted on any field of the batch.

Whatever the starting state, four things always happen to the batch and the shopper. The batch's `abandoned_at` is set to the current logical-clock instant, its `claimed_shopper_id` is cleared to null, and the abandoning shopper's identifier is appended to `declined_shopper_ids` so that the dispatch selector can never hand them the same batch again. A payout row is written for the abandoning shopper with `status` `FORFEITED` and every money field set to 0. Every member order's `fulfillment_status` moves to `REASSIGN_PENDING` — from `IN_BATCH` when the batch was `CLAIMED`, from `SHOPPING` when it was `SHOPPING`, from `CHECKED_OUT` when it was `CHECKED_OUT`. And every member order earns a disruption credit, described below.

Then the **four cascade links** run, in order, and none may be skipped.

1. The shopper's `committed_units` falls by the batch's `total_units`, and their `abandonment_count` rises by 1.
2. Because `abandonment_count` changed, `rolling_tier_score` and `tier` are re-derived. The abandonment override of section 3.3 now bites: once `abandonment_count` has reached 2, `tier` becomes `PROBATION` no matter how strong the ratings are. `lifetime_tier` and `lifetime_tier_score` are re-derived too, and the override does not touch them.
3. Because `tier` became `PROBATION` under that override, the shopper's `status` moves from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED_REVIEW`. A suspended shopper can neither be offered nor claim a batch until a `DispatchAdmin` reinstates them under the lifetime-score gate of section 3.3.
4. Because the shopper is now `SUSPENDED_REVIEW`, every **other** batch they still hold in `CLAIMED`, `SHOPPING`, or `CHECKED_OUT` is released by the system.

A system release looks much like an abandonment but differs in two deliberate respects. It sets that batch's `abandoned_at` to the current logical-clock instant, clears its `claimed_shopper_id`, appends the released shopper's identifier to its `declined_shopper_ids`, moves its `status` to `ABANDONED`, writes a `FORFEITED` payout row with every money field 0 for the released shopper, moves each of its member orders' `fulfillment_status` to `REASSIGN_PENDING` — from whatever value that order held, `IN_BATCH` or `SHOPPING` or `CHECKED_OUT`, since a released sibling may have been in any of the three held states — gives each member order an `ABANDONMENT_INCONVENIENCE` credit sized by exactly the disruption-credit rule described below, and re-derives the shopper's `committed_units`. The first thing it does **not** do is increment `abandonment_count` — and that omission is load-bearing, because incrementing it would re-trigger link 2, re-trigger link 3, and send the cascade back through itself indefinitely. The second is that it performs none of the state-specific unwinding that the two starting states described further below add on top of the common work: a released sibling that was `SHOPPING` keeps every picked line, its `final_price_cents` and its still-`PENDING` replacement requests exactly as they stood, and a released sibling that was `CHECKED_OUT` keeps its captures — its member orders stay `CAPTURED`, no `refunded_cents` is written, no benefit balance is restored, the order's `ebt_captured_cents` is not zeroed and no `POST_CHECKOUT_REVERSAL` entry is posted. Only the batch the shopper abandoned directly is unwound that way.

The disruption credit is sized by how far the shopper had got. Member orders are processed in ascending `sequence_no` order of their membership rows, and for each order the system counts its total lines and, among them, the lines whose `status` is `PICKED` or `REPLACED`, **counted before** the abandonment resets any picked line back to `REQUESTED`. If no line had been picked, the disruption rate is zero. If at least one line had been picked but fewer than half the lines, the rate is five percent. If at least one line had been picked and the picked lines are at least half of all lines — a test performed by integer cross-multiplication, comparing twice the picked-line count against the total line count — the rate is ten percent. The raw credit is a flat 500 cents of inconvenience money plus the order's `estimated_subtotal_cents` multiplied by that rate and rounded half up to a whole cent, and the stored `amount_cents` is that raw credit capped at 2500 cents. Each credit is written as a ledger entry with `reason_code` `ABANDONMENT_INCONVENIENCE`, after which the customer's `credit_balance_cents` is re-derived. An abandonment from `CHECKED_OUT` lands in the ten-percent band whenever at least half the order's lines are `PICKED` or `REPLACED`, which is the usual case because the register only opens once no line is still `REQUESTED`; an order carrying enough `REFUNDED` lines to push its picked count below half of its total line count still lands in the five-percent band, and one every line of which was refunded earns only the flat 500 cents.

Two of the three starting states add their own unwinding on top of the common work. Neither unwinding re-derives the batch's own composition: `total_weight_lb` in particular keeps whatever the aisle work and the register left on it rather than being recomputed from the rewound lines, and the abandoned batch carries that figure — along with `total_units`, `total_distance_mi`, `store_count`, `delivery_deadline` and `checkout_blocked_count` — unchanged into any later reassignment.

Abandoning while `SHOPPING` rewinds the aisle work. Every member line whose `status` is `PICKED` is reset to `REQUESTED` with `picked_qty` 0, `final_weight_lb` 0, and `final_price_cents` 0. Every replacement request still `PENDING` on a member line moves to `EXPIRED` with `resolution_effect` `NONE`, and its line returns from `REPLACEMENT_PENDING` to `REQUESTED`. The next shopper starts from a clean basket.

Abandoning after `CHECKED_OUT` additionally unwinds money that has already been taken, and this is the only path in the system that reaches the `REFUNDED` payment state. Each member order's `payment_status` moves from `CAPTURED` to `REFUNDED`, and its `refunded_cents` is set to the sum of `ebt_captured_cents` and `card_captured_cents` — set, not added to, so it overwrites whatever a rejected replacement had already accumulated there. `card_captured_cents` itself is left exactly as the register wrote it and is never zeroed. The customer's `ebt_snap_balance_cents` is increased by that order's `ebt_captured_cents`, the order's `ebt_captured_cents` is then set to 0, and the customer's `ebt_captured_total_cents` is re-derived — together these three steps are exactly what preserves the benefit-conservation invariant of section 2.10. Two ledger entries are written for the order, in this order: first one with `reason_code` `POST_CHECKOUT_REVERSAL` covering the card slice, which is written only when that card slice is strictly greater than 0 so that a card slice of exactly 0 produces no reversal entry at all, and then one with `reason_code` `ABANDONMENT_INCONVENIENCE` covering the disruption. Every member order's `fulfillment_status` moves from `CHECKED_OUT` to `REASSIGN_PENDING` and every member line returns to `REQUESTED` with `final_price_cents` 0 — every one of them, whatever status it held, refunded lines included. Unlike the `SHOPPING` rewind, this path leaves each line's `picked_qty` and `final_weight_lb` exactly as the aisle left them, and it does not re-derive the order's `captured_subtotal_cents`, which keeps the figure the register wrote. The same four cascade links then run.

Bringing a dropped batch back to life belongs to a `DispatchAdmin`. The batch's `status` must be `ABANDONED`, otherwise `ERR_BATCH_NOT_ABANDONED` (409). It must still carry at least one member order, that is `total_units` of at least 1, otherwise `ERR_BATCH_EMPTY` (422). And the dispatch selector of section 3.5 must find a non-empty candidate pool after excluding every identifier in `declined_shopper_ids` — which by now includes the shopper who dropped it and any shopper who let an earlier offer lapse — otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SHOPPER` (422) and the batch stays `ABANDONED`. On success the batch's `reassignment_count` increases by 1, `offered_shopper_id` becomes the new winner and `offer_expires_at` becomes the current logical-clock instant plus 30 minutes, every member order's `fulfillment_status` moves from `REASSIGN_PENDING` back to `IN_BATCH`, the batch's `status` moves from `ABANDONED` to `OFFERED`, and its `version` increases by 1. From there the batch is claimable again exactly as in section 3.5, and a batch may travel this loop any number of times.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is rooted at the API base with no version or namespace prefix, so all collections sit at the top level of the path space. Endpoints that bring a brand-new resource into existence — a retailer, a catalog item, a customer, a credit-ledger entry, a shopper, a shopper rating, an order, an order line, a batch, a batch membership row, or a replacement request — answer with HTTP 201 and the created resource in `data`. Every other endpoint answers with HTTP 200: reads, list reads, computed reads, state transitions, and the underscore-prefixed test-driving endpoints. A state transition returns the resource it advanced, except where section 3 says otherwise. The logical-clock setter returns an empty `data` object. The three underscore-prefixed endpoints exist purely so that a test can steer the system into states that would otherwise take many days of simulated traffic to reach; they are restricted to `DispatchAdmin` like any other privileged operation.
