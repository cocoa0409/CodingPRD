# Mattress Recycling Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Statewide mattress stewardship programs sit at an awkward intersection of tax administration, waste logistics and industrial certification. A recycling fee is collected by manufacturers and retailers at the point of sale, remitted quarterly to a council that administers the program state by state, and then spent again — as per-unit credits to enrolled collection sites, as per-unit-plus-per-kilometre payments to contract haulers, and as the overhead of certifying and auditing the recycling facilities that actually break the units down. Every one of those money movements has to reconcile against a single reserve balance, and the fee rate itself has to be re-derived from that reserve whenever the balance moves.

This document specifies the API that administers that program. It governs six distinct operator populations — council program staff, an independent audit function, registered manufacturers and retailers, collection-site operators, contract haulers, and certified recycling facilities — and it treats the physical units of mattress and foundation as a conserved quantity that must be accounted for from the moment a site reports collecting them until a recycler either accepts or rejects them. The two hardest requirements the system carries are therefore arithmetic ones: money booked into the reserve must return exactly to its prior value when a payment is compensated by a refund of the same amount, and the five custody buckets on a collection site must always sum to the lifetime units credited into that site's custody.

The API is also a compliance instrument. Short-paid remittances become receivables, receivables make registrants delinquent, and enough delinquent registrants raise the program's enforcement posture and freeze outbound refunds. Contaminated or infested loads accumulate strikes against the site that shipped them, and three strikes suspend the site and cancel its open routes. Segregation of duties is enforced structurally rather than by policy: the administrator who proposes a fee rate may not approve it, and the auditor who sends a remittance back for amendment may not close that audit.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The collections in this API are peers at the root of the URL space — there is no single enclosing container resource. Programs, fee-rate schedules, registrants, remittances, exemption claims, collection sites, volume reports, haulers, recyclers, pickup orders, load inspections, commodity output records and take-back deliveries are joined to one another by explicit reference fields carried in request bodies. The rules in this section apply to every one of those collections unless a later section narrows them.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which it acts. A request that omits either header, or that presents an `X-User-Role` value the server does not recognise, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. A request whose declared role is recognised but is not among the roles permitted for the operation is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows:

- `ProgramAdministrator` — council program staff. Creates state programs and revises their forecasts, proposes, approves and activates fee rates, registers participants, enrolls, suspends and reinstates collection sites, enrolls haulers and recyclers, grants, suspends and restores processing certification, accepts volume reports, finalizes hauler settlements, issues fee refunds, and drives the logical clock.
- `ProgramAuditor` — the independent audit function. Opens fee audits, adjudicates exemption claims, requests amendments, closes audits, and verifies commodity output records.
- `Registrant` — a manufacturer or retailer participant. Opens, files and pays remittances, attaches exemption claims, submits amendments, and records take-back deliveries.
- `CollectionSiteOperator` — a collection-site or transfer-station operator. Submits monthly volume reports, dispatches loads to recyclers, and voids scheduled pickups.
- `Hauler` — a contract hauler. Confirms pickup and delivery of routed loads.
- `Recycler` — a certified processing facility. Records incoming-load inspections and outbound commodity streams.

Two of these roles are **opaque** and four are **entity-backed**, and the distinction determines what `X-User-ID` is compared against.

When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `proposed_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a fee-rate schedule — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds when `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAuditor`: `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier, recorded as `audit_opened_by_user_id` and `amendment_requested_by_user_id` on a remittance, as `adjudicated_by_user_id` on an exemption claim, and as `verified_by_user_id` on a commodity output record, but never resolved against a stored entity.

When `X-User-Role` is `Registrant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting registrant; ownership checks on any resource that references a registrant compare that resource's `registrant_id` to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `CollectionSiteOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting collection site; ownership checks compare the target site's own `id`, or a pickup order's `collection_site_id`, to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Hauler`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting hauler; ownership checks compare a pickup order's `hauler_id` to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Recycler`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting facility; ownership checks compare either the recycler identified in the request path or a pickup order's `recycler_id` to `X-User-ID`, and the value is additionally stamped as `inspected_by_user_id` on the inspection it records. In all four entity-backed cases, an authenticated and role-authorized principal that reaches for a resource it does not own is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE`.

Two operations additionally enforce **segregation of duties** across two distinct `X-User-ID` values holding the same role, and both reject with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Approving a fee-rate schedule requires an `X-User-ID` different from the schedule's `proposed_by_user_id`. Closing a remittance audit requires an `X-User-ID` different from that remittance's `amendment_requested_by_user_id` whenever that field has been set; when no amendment was ever requested the field is null and the check does not apply.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Filtering

List endpoints accept two query-string parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` skips a number of leading records and defaults to 0; its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.

Every list endpoint returns its records sorted by `created_at` in ascending order unless a narrower rule is stated for that collection. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the size of the filtered result set before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether records remain beyond the current window.

Beyond pagination, most list endpoints accept optional equality filters drawn from the record's own fields. Fee-rate schedules may be narrowed by `state_program_id`, `unit_class` and `status`. Remittances may be narrowed by `registrant_id`, `state_program_id`, `amends_remittance_id`, `status` and `period_code`. A remittance's exemption claims may be narrowed by `status`. Collection sites may be narrowed by `state_program_id`, `enrollment_status` and `site_type`; a site's volume reports by `status` and `period_month`. Haulers may be narrowed by `state_program_id` and `region_code`, recyclers by `state_program_id` and `certification_status`, and a recycler's commodity output records by `record_status` and `commodity`. Pickup orders may be narrowed by `collection_site_id`, `hauler_id`, `recycler_id`, `fulfillment_status` and `settlement_status`. Take-back deliveries may be narrowed by `registrant_id`, `collection_site_id` and `status`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at`, which is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the record was first written. Almost all resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of the record. Both of those stamps are read from the logical clock described below rather than from real time, and both always carry their three fractional digits; two records written without the clock moving in between therefore carry identical stamps, and wherever `created_at` orders or tie-breaks records, records sharing a stamp fall in the order they were written. Derived and recomputed fields count as mutations: when a payment moves a program's ledger totals, the program's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments, exactly as it would for a client-supplied edit.

The one exception is the load inspection. An inspection is an append-only event record: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from a load inspection, because an inspection is never modified after it is written.

All date and time comparisons in this system — filing deadlines, quarantine expiry, rate effective dates, pickup-window overlap — read a server-side **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. The clock's value at startup is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and until a client moves it every such comparison uses that instant; the server never falls back to real time. A `ProgramAdministrator` may advance or rewind the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, which responds with the new `current_utc`. Every subsequent read of the current instant — including the stamping of `filed_at`, `accepted_at`, `suspended_at` and `certified_at`, and every quarantine and effective-date comparison — uses the new value. Each field stamped straight off the clock carries the clock's current instant in exactly the form the clock endpoint reports as `current_utc`, which for the startup value is `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, with no fractional digits. An instant *derived* from the clock rather than read straight off it — the infestation quarantine expiry of §3.7 — is rendered in that same style: ISO 8601 UTC ending in `Z`, carrying three fractional digits only when the instant does not land on a whole second.

### 2.5. Reference Resolution Order

Requests in this API commonly name two or three other records: a pickup order names a collection site, a recycler and possibly a hauler; a remittance names a registrant and a program. References are resolved before any business rule runs, and they are resolved in a fixed order — first the identifiers embedded in the URL path, read left to right, then the identifiers carried in the request body, read in the order the body's fields are declared for that operation. The first identifier that fails to resolve determines the response: the request is rejected with the 404 error specific to that entity type, and no later reference is examined. Thus a request naming a non-existent collection site and a non-existent recycler reports `ERR_COLLECTION_SITE_NOT_FOUND` if the site is declared first, and never mentions the recycler.

The per-entity 404 codes are `ERR_STATE_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATE_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REGISTRANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REMITTANCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXEMPTION_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COLLECTION_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VOLUME_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HAULER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECYCLER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PICKUP_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMMODITY_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_TAKE_BACK_DELIVERY_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.6. Money, Weight and Rounding Discipline

Money in this system is expressed exclusively in whole cents as integers; there are no fractional-cent balances anywhere. Wherever a percentage, a ratio or a per-kilometre rate would produce a fractional cent, the result is rounded to the nearest whole cent with ties resolved upward — the "round half up" convention — and the rounded integer is what gets stored and compared. Two rounding placements are deliberate and load-bearing, and are spelled out where they occur: the early-filing discount on a remittance is rounded once per sales line and subtracted inside that line before the lines are summed, and the distance component of a hauler route payment is rounded before it is added to the per-unit and stop components.

Two derived quantities are carried as numbers with two decimal places rather than as integers: a program's `reserve_coverage_months` and a recycler's `diversion_rate_pct`. Both are produced by rounding half up to two decimal places, and in both cases it is the **rounded** value that a threshold gate compares — not the unrounded quotient. A coverage figure whose exact quotient is a shade under three months but which rounds to `3.00` is treated as `3.00` by the rate-increase gate.

Physical units are integers throughout. A route distance (`route_distance_km`) and a commodity weight (`output_weight_kg`) are the only non-integer physical inputs; a distance must be greater than zero and a commodity weight must be greater than zero. Rate-card and forecast fields preserve the integer cent values supplied on input.

### 2.7. The Program Reserve Ledger

Each state program maintains a single reserve balance that every money movement in the system flows through, and that balance is always recomputed from primary records rather than incrementally patched. Fees collected (`fee_collected_cents`) is the sum of `fee_paid_cents` across the program's remittances minus the sum of `fee_refunded_cents` across the same set, counting only remittances whose `status` is one of `PAID`, `UNDER_AUDIT`, `AMENDMENT_REQUIRED`, `AUDIT_CLOSED` or `REFUNDED`; remittances still in `DRAFT` or `FILED` contribute nothing. Site credits paid (`site_credit_paid_cents`) is the sum of `credit_cents` over volume reports whose `status` is `ACCEPTED` and whose owning collection site belongs to this program; reports still in `SUBMITTED` contribute nothing. Hauler payments paid (`hauler_payment_paid_cents`) is the sum of `final_payment_cents` over pickup orders whose `settlement_status` is `FINALIZED` and whose collection site belongs to this program. The reserve balance (`reserve_balance_cents`) is then the opening reserve (`reserve_opening_cents`) plus fees collected, less site credits paid, less hauler payments paid.

This reconciliation runs on exactly four triggers: a remittance moving from `FILED` to `PAID`, a remittance moving from `PAID` to `REFUNDED`, a volume report moving from `SUBMITTED` to `ACCEPTED`, and a pickup order moving from `DELIVERED` to `CLOSED`. Because every total is a re-derivation and not an increment, the ledger obeys a strict compensation invariant: for a forward action and its compensating action of the same amount — a payment of some amount followed by a refund of that same amount — both `fee_collected_cents` and `reserve_balance_cents` return to exactly their pre-action values, with no drift. No rounding occurs anywhere in this reconciliation because every input is already a whole cent.

Alongside the reserve, a program tracks what it is owed. Receivables (`receivable_cents`) is the sum of `balance_due_cents` over the program's remittances whose `status` is `AUDIT_CLOSED`.

### 2.8. Unit Custody Conservation at Collection Sites

Physical units are tracked as a conserved quantity. Each collection site carries five custody buckets — `units_on_hand` for units physically at the site and available to manifest, `units_in_transit` for units manifested onto pickup orders that have not yet been delivered, `units_at_recycler_count` for units delivered to a recycler but not yet inspected, `units_recycled_count` for units a recycler accepted at inspection, and `units_written_off_count` for units lost between manifest and scale plus units rejected at inspection — together with `units_reported_total`, the lifetime count of units credited into that site's custody. After every request, including every compensating one, the lifetime total must equal the sum of the five buckets exactly.

Only two events add to the lifetime total: accepting a volume report adds its credited units, and completing a take-back delivery adds the units actually collected. Both additions land in `units_on_hand`. Every other movement is a transfer between buckets that preserves the total. Creating a pickup order moves its manifested units from `units_on_hand` to `units_in_transit`. Voiding a scheduled order, or cancelling one as a cascade of its site being suspended, moves those same manifested units back from `units_in_transit` to `units_on_hand`. Delivering an order removes the full manifested quantity from `units_in_transit`, adds the delivered quantity to `units_at_recycler_count`, and adds the shortfall between manifest and delivery to `units_written_off_count`. Recording an inspection removes the delivered quantity from `units_at_recycler_count`, adds the accepted quantity to `units_recycled_count`, and adds the rejected remainder to `units_written_off_count`. Every transfer moves whole units; no path in the system mints or destroys a unit.

### 2.9. Version Pinning on Program and Remittance Writes

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a parent record's `version`, and in both cases the version field is a required, non-optional element of the request body. No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field.

A `POST /fee-rate-schedules` request must carry `expected_state_program_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the `state_program_id` it names. If that value does not equal the program's current `version`, the proposal is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The reason the guard exists is that a proposal snapshots the program's recommended rate and the accepted band around it; activating a successor schedule or settling a remittance payment both legitimately move the forecast-and-reserve state those numbers are derived from, so a proposal written from a program view the client has not refreshed would be banded against arithmetic that no longer holds.

A `POST /sales-remittances/{id}/amendments` request must carry `expected_remittance_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the remittance being amended. If that value does not equal the remittance's current `version`, the submission is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REMITTANCE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Each accepted amendment legitimately supersedes the registrant's prior revised figures and consumes one of only two permitted amendment rounds, so a submission written from a remittance view the client has not refreshed would burn a round against the wrong baseline.

### 2.10. Order of Evaluation for Rejections

When more than one rule would reject a request, the response reports the rule that comes first in the following ladder. The ordering is normative: a later check must never mask an earlier one, and a request that violates several rules is diagnosed by the earliest violated rule only.

1. Authentication — both headers present and the role recognised (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`).
2. Role authorization for the operation (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`).
3. Request-body well-formedness on every write operation: every required field present, every value of the declared type, every enumerated value a declared member, every numeric constraint respected (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`).
4. Pagination bounds on every list operation (`ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`).
5. Reference resolution in the order described in §2.5 (the per-entity 404 codes).
6. Ownership for the four entity-backed roles (`ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE`).
7. Program version pinning (`ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION`), then remittance version pinning (`ERR_STALE_REMITTANCE_VERSION`).
8. Segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`).
9. The target record's own lifecycle state — the family of conflict codes named per workflow in §3, evaluated in the order fee-rate schedule, remittance draft, remittance payable, remittance paid, remittance under audit, pending exemption claims, claim already adjudicated, site enrollment, volume report, pickup order, duplicate inspection, settlement readiness, recycler certification, take-back schedule, commodity record.
10. The lifecycle state of a *different* record the operation depends on: recycler certification for a dispatch (`ERR_RECYCLER_NOT_CERTIFIED`), site enrollment (`ERR_SITE_NOT_ENROLLED`), registrant suspension (`ERR_REGISTRANT_SUSPENDED`).
11. Safety interlocks: site infestation quarantine (`ERR_SITE_QUARANTINED`), then program enforcement posture (`ERR_PROGRAM_POSTURE_ELEVATED`).
12. Temporal rules: rate-interval overlap (`ERR_RATE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`), then rate not yet effective (`ERR_RATE_NOT_YET_EFFECTIVE`), then hauler window capacity (`ERR_HAULER_WINDOW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`).
13. Cross-record referential agreement: recycler-versus-site program (`ERR_RECYCLER_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`), then explicit hauler program and region (`ERR_HAULER_REGION_MISMATCH`), then rate availability for the filing period (`ERR_NO_APPLICABLE_RATE`).
14. Uniqueness and quantitative business constraints, in this order: duplicate remittance period (`ERR_DUPLICATE_REMITTANCE_PERIOD`), duplicate volume period (`ERR_DUPLICATE_VOLUME_PERIOD`), amendment limit (`ERR_AMENDMENT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`), exempt units against sales (`ERR_EXEMPT_UNITS_EXCEED_SALES`), site stock against manifest (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SITE_UNITS`), hauler eligibility pool (`ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_HAULER`), delivered or contaminated units against the manifest (`ERR_UNITS_EXCEED_MANIFEST`), take-back offer against delivery (`ERR_TAKEBACK_OFFER_BELOW_DELIVERY`), take-back collection against offer (`ERR_TAKEBACK_COLLECTED_ABOVE_OFFER`), diversion rate at recertification (`ERR_DIVERSION_RATE_BELOW_MINIMUM`).
15. Fiscal rules, in this order: proposed rate inside the recommended band (`ERR_RATE_OUTSIDE_RECOMMENDED_BAND`), reserve coverage ceiling on a rate increase (`ERR_RESERVE_ABOVE_INCREASE_CEILING`), payment amount exactness (`ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`), refund amount against net cash received (`ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_PAYMENT`).

Two consequences of this ladder are worth calling out because they are easy to get backwards. Segregation of duties (step 8) is checked **before** the target record's own state (step 9): an administrator who proposed a schedule and then tries to approve it gets `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` even if the schedule has already left `PROPOSED`, and an auditor who requested an amendment and then tries to close the audit gets `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` even if the remittance is no longer `UNDER_AUDIT`. Likewise remittance version pinning (step 7) precedes the amendment-state check (step 9): a stale `expected_remittance_version` surfaces as `ERR_STALE_REMITTANCE_VERSION` rather than as a state conflict.

### 2.11. Cascade Semantics

Several operations in this system mutate more than one record, and a few mutate records several hops away from the one addressed. Cascades are applied in the same request, synchronously, before the response is produced — there is no deferred settlement and no eventual consistency. Each record touched by a cascade has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented, exactly as if it had been edited directly. Where a cascade's first hop changes a counter that a threshold reads, the threshold is evaluated **after** that hop's own effects have landed, so a single request can carry a chain several links long: an inspection can reject a load, which raises a site's strike count, which suspends the site, which decrements the program's enrolled-site count and cancels every other open pickup order at that site. The individual chains are specified in §3 where they occur.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a State Program and Reading Fee Adequacy

A `ProgramAdministrator` opens the system for a jurisdiction by creating a **state program**. The request carries `state_code`, exactly two uppercase letters identifying the state and unique across all programs, and `program_name`, the statutory name of the program. It also carries the forecast the whole fee-setting apparatus is built on: `forecast_annual_cost_cents`, the projected annual cost of running the program, which must be at least 1; `forecast_annual_units`, the projected annual count of chargeable units, also at least 1; and optionally `forecast_other_revenue_cents`, projected non-fee revenue such as commodity proceeds and grants, which must be zero or greater and defaults to 0 when omitted. Reserve policy arrives as `reserve_target_months`, an integer from 1 through 12 expressing the target reserve as a number of months of program cost, and `reserve_opening_cents`, the opening reserve balance, which must be zero or greater and is immutable for the life of the program. Two payout parameters complete the request: `site_credit_cents_per_unit`, the per-unit credit paid to enrolled collection sites on accepted volume reports, which must be zero or greater, and `volume_bonus_threshold_units`, at least 1, the credited-unit count at or above which a monthly report earns a volume bonus. Finally, `delinquency_alert_threshold` — the count of delinquent registrants at or above which the program's posture escalates — may be supplied, must be at least 1, and defaults to 1 when omitted.

A newly created program is fully quiet. Its `enforcement_posture` begins at `NORMAL`, `enrolled_site_count` at 0, `reported_units_total` at 0, `delinquent_registrant_count` at 0, and the three cash aggregates `fee_collected_cents`, `site_credit_paid_cents` and `hauler_payment_paid_cents` at 0, along with `receivable_cents`. The creation itself computes `reserve_balance_cents` from the opening reserve, then `reserve_coverage_months`, then `recommended_rate_cents`.

**Reserve coverage** is deliberately simple, because it is a gate rather than a report. The monthly cost is the annual cost forecast divided by twelve, rounded half up to whole cents. Coverage is then the reserve balance divided by that monthly cost, rounded half up to two decimal places. It is recomputed whenever either input moves — that is, on every mutation of the reserve balance and on every revision of the annual cost forecast.

**The recommended rate** is where the program's fiscal policy is encoded, and its seven steps run exactly once, in order, on every trigger. First, the reserve target in cents is the annual cost forecast multiplied by the reserve target in months, divided by twelve, rounded half up. Second, the reserve gap is the reserve target less the current reserve balance, or zero if the reserve already meets or exceeds the target. Third, the recoverable amount is the annual cost forecast, plus the reserve gap amortised over two years — that is, the gap divided by two and rounded half up — less the forecast other revenue. Fourth, the base rate per unit is the recoverable amount divided by the forecast annual units, rounded *up* to the next whole cent. Fifth, an administrative surcharge percentage is selected from that base rate: six percent when the base rate is 800 cents or less, four percent when the base rate is from 801 through 1600 cents, and two percent when the base rate is 1601 cents or more. The tier is chosen from the base rate and only from the base rate; it is never re-selected after the surcharge has been applied. Sixth, the recommended rate is the base rate plus the base rate multiplied by that surcharge percentage, rounded half up to whole cents. Seventh, the accepted proposal band is derived from the recommended rate: `band_low_cents` is eighty percent of the recommended rate rounded *down* to a whole cent, and `band_high_cents` is one hundred and twenty percent of the recommended rate rounded *up* to a whole cent.

That seven-step computation is re-run into `recommended_rate_cents` on program creation, on any revision of the cost forecast, when a remittance is paid, when a fee refund is issued, when a fee-rate schedule is activated, when a volume report is accepted, and when a pickup order's settlement is finalized. The same computation is snapshotted onto a proposal at the moment it is created.

Forecasts change, and revising them is a distinct administrator capability. A revision may carry any subset of `forecast_annual_cost_cents`, `forecast_annual_units`, `forecast_other_revenue_cents` and `reserve_target_months`, leaving unnamed fields untouched; it recomputes `reserve_coverage_months` and `recommended_rate_cents` and increments the program's `version` by one. Note what a revision cannot touch: the opening reserve is fixed at creation, and the two payout parameters and the delinquency threshold are not part of the forecast revision surface.

Administrators and auditors also have a computed read that exposes the whole rate derivation without mutating anything. It returns `recommended_rate_cents`, `band_low_cents` and `band_high_cents` together with the intermediate values that produced them — `base_rate_cents`, `admin_surcharge_pct`, `reserve_target_cents` and `reserve_gap_cents` — plus the current `reserve_balance_cents` and `reserve_coverage_months`. This is the read a program administrator uses before proposing a rate, and it is the read whose `version` must then be pinned into the proposal.

### 3.2. Setting the Per-Unit Fee: Proposal, Approval, Activation, Supersession

A recycling fee is set per state and per unit class, and `unit_class` is either `MATTRESS` or `FOUNDATION`. A `ProgramAdministrator` proposes a rate by naming the `state_program_id`, the `unit_class`, the `fee_cents_per_unit` to charge (at least 1), an inclusive `effective_from` instant, and optionally an exclusive `effective_to` instant which, when present, must be strictly later than `effective_from`. Omitting `effective_to` proposes an open-ended rate. The proposal also carries the program version pin described in §2.9.

A proposal starts in `PROPOSED`, records the acting administrator as `proposed_by_user_id`, and leaves `approved_by_user_id` null. It snapshots the program's recommended rate at that instant into `recommended_rate_cents_at_proposal` and derives `band_low_cents` and `band_high_cents` from that snapshot, and it increments the program's `version` by one. Two rules police the proposal itself.

The first is **interval non-overlap**, and it treats a rate's effective interval as half-open: the start instant is inside the interval and the end instant is not. A proposal is compared against every existing schedule for the same program and unit class whose `status` is `PROPOSED`, `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE`; schedules already `SUPERSEDED` are historical and never conflict. Treating an absent `effective_to` on either side as infinitely far in the future, two intervals overlap when the proposal's start is strictly earlier than the existing schedule's end and the existing schedule's start is strictly earlier than the proposal's end. Any overlap is rejected with `ERR_RATE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. The practical consequence is that a successor is proposed by abutting its predecessor: a proposal whose `effective_from` equals the predecessor's exclusive `effective_to` is accepted, while one instant earlier is not. It also means a second open-ended proposal for a pair that already holds an open-ended `PROPOSED`, `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE` schedule always overlaps and is always refused.

The second is the **recommended band**. The proposed `fee_cents_per_unit` must be at least `band_low_cents` and at most `band_high_cents` as snapshotted on this proposal; a value outside that inclusive range is rejected with `ERR_RATE_OUTSIDE_RECOMMENDED_BAND`.

Approval moves a schedule `PROPOSED → APPROVED` and requires a second `ProgramAdministrator` user as described in §2.1. Approval is only legal from `PROPOSED`; from any other status the request is rejected with `ERR_RATE_SCHEDULE_NOT_APPROVABLE`. Approval also carries a fiscal interlock. If an `ACTIVE` schedule already exists for the same program and unit class and this schedule's `fee_cents_per_unit` is greater than that active schedule's `fee_cents_per_unit` — that is, if the proposal is a rate *increase* — then the program's `reserve_coverage_months` must be strictly less than three months. A program sitting at exactly `3.00` months of coverage is already well enough funded that a rate increase is refused with `ERR_RESERVE_ABOVE_INCREASE_CEILING`; the comparison is strict, not inclusive, and it compares the two-decimal rounded coverage figure. A proposal that lowers or matches the current active rate, or for which no active rate exists, is not subject to this interlock at all. On success, the approving administrator's identifier is recorded as `approved_by_user_id`.

Activation moves a schedule `APPROVED → ACTIVE`. It is only legal from `APPROVED` — otherwise `ERR_RATE_SCHEDULE_NOT_APPROVABLE` — and only once the logical clock has reached or passed the schedule's `effective_from`; activating early is refused with `ERR_RATE_NOT_YET_EFFECTIVE`. Activation cascades. Any schedule for the same program and unit class currently in `ACTIVE` transitions `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, and if that superseded schedule's `effective_to` was null it is set to the newly activated schedule's `effective_from`; a superseded schedule that had an explicitly proposed `effective_to` keeps it, preserving the half-open interval it was proposed with. Activation then recomputes the program's `recommended_rate_cents` and `reserve_coverage_months` and increments the program's `version` by one.

A `SUPERSEDED` schedule is not dead. It remains the rate of record for dates that fall inside its own interval, which matters because remittances are priced for historical quarters. Whenever a fee rate is needed for a program, a unit class and a particular date, the candidate set is every schedule of that program and unit class whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED`, whose `effective_from` is at or before the date in question, and whose `effective_to` is either null or strictly later than that date. Schedules in `PROPOSED` or `APPROVED` are never the rate of record. If the candidate set is empty, the lookup fails with `ERR_NO_APPLICABLE_RATE`. Non-overlap is enforced at proposal time so the candidate set holds exactly one schedule; should more than one ever match, the one with the latest `effective_from` wins, and among equal starts the one created most recently wins. The winning schedule's `fee_cents_per_unit` is the rate.

### 3.3. Registrants, Quarterly Remittances and Payment

A `ProgramAdministrator` registers each participant, supplying `legal_name`, the registered legal entity name; `registrant_type`, which is `MANUFACTURER`, `RETAILER` or `BOTH`; and `home_state_code`, exactly two uppercase letters naming the principal place of business. A registrant's `registrant_type` matters downstream: `RETAILER` and `BOTH` participants carry the take-back-with-delivery obligation of §3.9. A new registrant begins with `compliance_status` at `COMPLIANT`, `delinquent_balance_cents` at 0 and `takeback_breach_count` at 0.

Compliance is derived, never set directly. A registrant's `delinquent_balance_cents` is the sum of `balance_due_cents` across that registrant's `AUDIT_CLOSED` remittances plus the sum of `breach_penalty_cents` across that registrant's `OBLIGATION_BREACHED` take-back deliveries. `compliance_status` follows from that balance: `COMPLIANT` while the balance is zero, `DELINQUENT` once the balance is greater than zero, and `SUSPENDED` once the balance reaches 1,000,000 cents — ten thousand dollars of unpaid obligation — or more. `takeback_breach_count` independently counts the registrant's `OBLIGATION_BREACHED` deliveries.

A `Registrant` opens a quarterly filing by naming its own `registrant_id`, the `state_program_id` it is remitting into, a `period_code`, and the two sales lines `units_sold_mattress` and `units_sold_foundation`, each of which must be zero or greater. The `period_code` is a reporting quarter written as four digits, the letter Q, and a digit from 1 to 4 — `2026Q1` and the like — and from it the server derives `period_start`, the first instant of that quarter in UTC, and `period_end`, the last day of that quarter at `23:59:59.999Z`. The four quarters run January 1 to March 31, April 1 to June 30, July 1 to September 30, and October 1 to December 31. Only one *original* filing may exist per registrant, program and quarter: a second attempt where no amendment parent is involved is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REMITTANCE_PERIOD`. A new filing starts in `DRAFT` with `amendment_round` at 0, `amends_remittance_id` null, `filed_at` null, `early_filing_qualified` false, `fee_self_assessed_cents` at 0, `fee_paid_cents` at 0, `fee_refunded_cents` at 0, `balance_due_cents` at 0, and both `fee_reconciled_cents` and the audit-trail fields `audit_opened_by_user_id` and `amendment_requested_by_user_id` null.

While a remittance is in `DRAFT` the registrant may attach exemption claims to it, as described in §3.4, and may then file it. Filing is only legal from `DRAFT`; attempting to file — or to attach a claim — to a remittance in any other status is rejected with `ERR_REMITTANCE_NOT_DRAFT`. Two further gates guard the `DRAFT → FILED` transition. A registrant whose `compliance_status` is `SUSPENDED` may not file at all, and is refused with `ERR_REGISTRANT_SUSPENDED`. And a rate must exist to price the filing: for every unit class whose corresponding sales line is greater than zero, an applicable schedule in `ACTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED` must cover both `period_start` and `period_end`; if any required lookup comes up empty the filing is refused with `ERR_NO_APPLICABLE_RATE`. A class with zero units sold requires no rate.

Filing stamps `filed_at` from the logical clock, determines `early_filing_qualified`, and stamps `fee_self_assessed_cents`.

**The fee assessment** is one calculation with two variants, and the self-assessed variant is the one stamped at filing. Its skeleton is fixed. First, the filing is early when `filed_at` falls at or before twenty days after `period_end`; `early_filing_qualified` records that determination. The discount percentage is one and a half percent when the filing is early and the discount has not been forfeited, and zero otherwise. The fee then accumulates over the two sales lines in a **pinned order — foundation first, then mattress**. For each line: take that class's units sold; subtract the credited exempt units, which is the sum of `units` over this remittance's exemption claims for that unit class whose status falls in the variant's inclusion set; treat the chargeable count as zero if the subtraction would go negative. Multiply the chargeable count by the applicable rate for that class at the variant's rate date to get the line's gross amount in cents. Round the line's discount — the gross amount multiplied by the discount percentage — half up to whole cents, subtract it from that line's gross amount, and add the difference to the running total. The per-line rounding placement is normative: rounding a single aggregate discount over the summed gross amounts is explicitly not the rule, and can differ by one cent for each pair of lines whose discounts land on a half cent.

The self-assessed variant prices both lines at the rate applicable on `period_start`, counts exemption claims whose status is `PENDING` or `SUBSTANTIATED` — a claim counts as filed even before an auditor has looked at it — and never forfeits the discount.

Registrants are expected to file within forty-five days of a quarter's end as a matter of statute; the API attaches no automatic penalty to that due window, and the twenty-day early-filing discount is the only date-driven adjustment to a remittance's fee.

Payment moves `FILED → PAID` and must be exact. The request carries `amount_cents`, which must equal `fee_self_assessed_cents` precisely; anything else is rejected with `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. Paying a remittance that is not in `FILED` is rejected with `ERR_REMITTANCE_NOT_PAYABLE`. On success `fee_paid_cents` is set to the amount paid, and the program's `fee_collected_cents` and `reserve_balance_cents` are reconciled per §2.7, after which `reserve_coverage_months` and `recommended_rate_cents` are recomputed.

Refund is the compensating action of payment and belongs to a `ProgramAdministrator`, not to the registrant. It moves `PAID → REFUNDED` and carries `amount_cents` together with a `reason`. A refund is only legal from `PAID`; from any other status the answer is `ERR_REMITTANCE_NOT_PAID`. Money may not leave a program whose posture has escalated: if the program's `enforcement_posture` is `ELEVATED` rather than `NORMAL`, the refund is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_POSTURE_ELEVATED`. The amount must also not exceed the net cash the program is actually holding against this remittance — `fee_paid_cents` less `fee_refunded_cents` — or it is refused with `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_PAYMENT`. On success the refunded amount is added to `fee_refunded_cents`, and the program's `fee_collected_cents` and `reserve_balance_cents` are reconciled, followed by `reserve_coverage_months` and `recommended_rate_cents`. Because both the payment and the refund path re-derive the program totals from primary records rather than adjusting them, a refund of the full amount paid restores the reserve to exactly its pre-payment value.

### 3.4. Exemption Claims, the Audit Cycle and Delinquency

Not every unit sold is chargeable. While a remittance sits in `DRAFT`, its `Registrant` attaches **exemption claims** against it, each naming a `claim_type` — `EXEMPT_SALE` for a statutorily exempt buyer, or `OUT_OF_STATE_SHIPMENT` for a unit shipped outside the program state — a `unit_class` of `MATTRESS` or `FOUNDATION` identifying the sales line it credits, a `units` count of at least 1, and optionally a `documentation_reference` holding the exemption certificate or bill-of-lading reference, which is left null when the registrant has none. Each claim carries a `remittance_id` identifying the filing it belongs to. A claim begins in `PENDING` with `adjudicated_by_user_id` and `disallow_reason` both null. Claims cannot over-claim a line: for a given unit class, the total `units` across this remittance's `PENDING` and `SUBSTANTIATED` claims may not exceed that class's reported units sold, and a claim that would breach that ceiling is refused with `ERR_EXEMPT_UNITS_EXCEED_SALES`.

Once a remittance has been paid, a `ProgramAuditor` may open an audit on it, moving `PAID → UNDER_AUDIT` and recording the acting auditor as `audit_opened_by_user_id`. Opening an audit on a remittance that is not in `PAID` is rejected with `ERR_REMITTANCE_NOT_PAID`.

With the remittance under audit, the auditor adjudicates each claim. Adjudication carries a `decision` and, when disallowing, a `disallow_reason` of `MISSING_DOCUMENTATION`, `UNITS_UNSUPPORTED` or `OUT_OF_SCOPE`. A claim may only be adjudicated while it is `PENDING` and while its parent remittance is `UNDER_AUDIT`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`. The two decisions fork:

- Deciding to substantiate moves the claim `PENDING → SUBSTANTIATED`. It requires that `documentation_reference` is not null — an undocumented claim cannot be substantiated — and it records `adjudicated_by_user_id` as the acting auditor. It has no other effect.
- Deciding to disallow moves the claim `PENDING → DISALLOWED`. It records `adjudicated_by_user_id` as the acting auditor and stores the supplied `disallow_reason`. It additionally marks the parent remittance's early-filing discount as forfeit for the reconciled assessment: a single disallowed claim anywhere on the remittance removes the one-and-a-half-percent discount from the audit-reconciled arithmetic, regardless of how early the filing was.

An auditor who is not satisfied with the reported figures sends the remittance back. Requesting an amendment moves `UNDER_AUDIT → AMENDMENT_REQUIRED`, carries a `note` explaining what is wanted, and records the requesting auditor as `amendment_requested_by_user_id`, overwriting any previous value. It is legal only from `UNDER_AUDIT` — otherwise `ERR_REMITTANCE_NOT_UNDER_AUDIT` — and only while `amendment_round` is less than 2; a third request is refused with `ERR_AMENDMENT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

The registrant answers with revised figures, supplying new `units_sold_mattress` and `units_sold_foundation` values alongside the remittance version pin of §2.9. This is legal only while the remittance is in `AMENDMENT_REQUIRED` — otherwise `ERR_REMITTANCE_NOT_UNDER_AUDIT` — and only while `amendment_round` is less than 2, or else `ERR_AMENDMENT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Accepting the submission does three things: it increments the audited remittance's `amendment_round` by one, increments that remittance's `version` by one, and creates a **new** remittance row in `DRAFT` whose `amends_remittance_id` points at the audited parent and which copies the parent's `registrant_id`, `state_program_id` and `period_code` while carrying the revised sales figures. The audited parent returns `AMENDMENT_REQUIRED → UNDER_AUDIT`, and it is the parent — not the newly created draft — that comes back in the response body. Because the round counter lives on the parent, the loop `UNDER_AUDIT → AMENDMENT_REQUIRED → UNDER_AUDIT` can run at most twice.

Closing the audit moves `UNDER_AUDIT → AUDIT_CLOSED` and is the most consequential single request in the system. It is legal only from `UNDER_AUDIT`, or else `ERR_REMITTANCE_NOT_UNDER_AUDIT`. It additionally requires that no exemption claim of this remittance is still `PENDING`; an unadjudicated claim blocks closure with `ERR_PENDING_EXEMPTION_CLAIMS`. And it requires the segregation-of-duties separation of §2.1: an auditor whose identifier matches `amendment_requested_by_user_id` cannot close the audit they sent back.

Closure stamps `fee_reconciled_cents` using the **audit-reconciled variant** of the fee assessment. That variant shares the pinned foundation-then-mattress line order and the per-line discount rounding of §3.3, and differs in exactly three respects. It prices both lines at the rate applicable on `period_end`, so a mid-period rate change prices the entire quarter at the later rate. It counts only claims whose status is `SUBSTANTIATED`, so disallowed and (impossibly, given the closure gate) pending claims contribute no credited exempt units. And the discount is treated as forfeit — and therefore the discount percentage is zero — whenever any exemption claim of this remittance ended in `DISALLOWED`, no matter what `early_filing_qualified` says.

Closure then stamps `balance_due_cents` as the reconciled fee less the net cash actually received, that is less `fee_paid_cents` minus `fee_refunded_cents`, with the result floored at zero: an over-collected remittance closes with a balance due of zero rather than a negative one.

When that balance due is greater than zero, a four-hop cascade fires inside the same request. First, the closure itself stamps the reconciled fee and the balance due. Second, because the balance due is positive, the amount is added to the registrant's `delinquent_balance_cents` and the registrant's `compliance_status` becomes `DELINQUENT` — or `SUSPENDED` outright if the accumulated balance has reached 1,000,000 cents. Third, because that registrant is now delinquent or suspended, the program's `delinquent_registrant_count` increases by one and the balance due is added to the program's `receivable_cents`; the count is the number of distinct registrants that are `DELINQUENT` or `SUSPENDED` and hold at least one `AUDIT_CLOSED` remittance of this program with a positive balance due. Fourth, because the delinquent-registrant count has reached the program's `delinquency_alert_threshold`, the program's `enforcement_posture` flips from `NORMAL` to `ELEVATED` — and an elevated posture blocks every subsequent `PAID → REFUNDED` transition in that program until it clears. With the default threshold of 1, a single short-paid audit closure is enough to freeze refunds program-wide.

A remittance closed with a balance due of zero performs none of hops two through four. `AUDIT_CLOSED` and `REFUNDED` are the two terminal statuses of a remittance.

### 3.5. Collection Site Enrollment and Monthly Volume Reporting

A `ProgramAdministrator` brings a drop-off point into the program by applying it, supplying the `state_program_id` it belongs to, the `operator_name` of the operating organisation, a `site_type` of `COLLECTION_SITE`, `TRANSFER_STATION` or `PERMANENT_DROPOFF`, and a `region_code`. The region code is the routing key: a hauler is eligible for this site's loads only when its service regions contain that value. A newly applied site has `enrollment_status` `APPLIED`, all five custody buckets at zero along with `units_reported_total`, `contamination_strike_count` at 0, and both `infestation_quarantine_until` and `suspended_at` null.

Enrollment moves a site `APPLIED → ENROLLED` and increments the program's `enrolled_site_count` by one. Suspension moves it `ENROLLED → SUSPENDED`, carries a `reason`, stamps `suspended_at` from the logical clock, and decrements the program's `enrolled_site_count` by one; it also reaches into logistics, cancelling every pickup order of this site whose `fulfillment_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `PICKED_UP` — each such order's `fulfillment_status` becomes `CANCELLED`, its `settlement_status` becomes `VOIDED`, and its manifested units move back out of the site's `units_in_transit` into `units_on_hand`. Reinstatement moves the site `SUSPENDED → ENROLLED`, resets `contamination_strike_count` to 0 so the strike counter restarts, and increments the program's `enrolled_site_count` by one; it is blocked while an infestation quarantine is still running, which is to say it requires either that `infestation_quarantine_until` is null or that the logical clock has reached or passed it, and a site still inside its quarantine window is refused with `ERR_SITE_QUARANTINED`. Each of these three transitions insists on the right starting status — `APPLIED` to enroll, `ENROLLED` to suspend, `SUSPENDED` to reinstate — and rejects a mismatch with `ERR_SITE_ENROLLMENT_STATE_INVALID`. Enrollment cycles freely: `ENROLLED → SUSPENDED → ENROLLED` may repeat, and a collection site has no terminal status.

A `CollectionSiteOperator` reports what its site collected each month, supplying a `period_month` written as four digits, a hyphen and a two-digit month from `01` through `12`, plus `units_mattress` and `units_foundation`, each zero or greater. The report records the `collection_site_id` of the site it was submitted against. Only one report may exist per site and month; a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOLUME_PERIOD`. A report may only be submitted for a site whose `enrollment_status` is `ENROLLED`, and a submission against a site in any other enrollment status is refused with `ERR_SITE_NOT_ENROLLED`. A new report starts in `SUBMITTED` with `credited_units` and `credit_cents` at 0 and `accepted_at` null — nothing is credited and no custody moves until an administrator accepts it.

Acceptance moves a report `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, the report's only transition and its terminal status. It requires the report to be in `SUBMITTED`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_VOLUME_REPORT_NOT_SUBMITTED`, and it requires the owning site to still be `ENROLLED`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_SITE_NOT_ENROLLED` — a report submitted before a suspension cannot be accepted after it. Acceptance stamps `accepted_at` from the logical clock and computes the credit.

**The site volume credit** works as follows. The credited units are simply the mattress units plus the foundation units reported. A volume bonus of five percent applies when the credited units reach or exceed the program's `volume_bonus_threshold_units`, and no bonus applies below that threshold. The credit in cents is the credited units multiplied by the program's `site_credit_cents_per_unit`, multiplied by one plus the bonus percentage, with the round-half-up applied once to that bonus-multiplied product. A report of zero units is entirely legal: it credits zero cents, earns no bonus, and still reaches `ACCEPTED`.

Acceptance then moves money and units together. The credited units are added to the site's `units_on_hand` and to its `units_reported_total`. The credit in cents is added to the program's `site_credit_paid_cents`, after which the program's `reserve_balance_cents`, `reserve_coverage_months`, `recommended_rate_cents` and `reported_units_total` are all recomputed. A program's `reported_units_total` is the sum of `credited_units` over the `ACCEPTED` reports of its sites plus the sum of `units_takeback_collected` over the `OBLIGATION_MET` and `OBLIGATION_BREACHED` take-back deliveries into those sites.

### 3.6. Routing Loads: Hauler Enrolment, Selection and Dispatch

A `ProgramAdministrator` enrols a contract hauler under a `state_program_id`, with a `company_name`, a `service_regions` array of at least one region code, and a rate card: `per_unit_rate_cents` and `per_km_rate_cents`, each zero or greater, and `max_route_payment_cents`, at least 1, being the contractual cap on any single route payment. Optionally `max_concurrent_routes` sets how many buffer-overlapping pickup windows the hauler may hold at once; it must be at least 1 and defaults to 2. A new hauler starts with `active_route_count` and `total_paid_cents` at 0. Its `active_route_count` is the number of its pickup orders whose `fulfillment_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `PICKED_UP` or `DELIVERED`, and its `total_paid_cents` is the sum of `final_payment_cents` over its orders whose `settlement_status` is `FINALIZED`. Haulers have no lifecycle status of their own.

A `CollectionSiteOperator` dispatches a load by creating a **pickup order**. The request names the `collection_site_id` the load leaves from, the `recycler_id` it is bound for, the `units_manifested` being loaded out of the site's on-hand stock (at least 1), the `route_distance_km` the route covers (greater than zero), and an agreed window bounded by an inclusive `pickup_window_start` and an inclusive `pickup_window_end`, which must be strictly later than the start. The `hauler_id` may be supplied explicitly, or omitted to let the server choose; the resulting order records which happened in `hauler_auto_selected`, true when the server resolved the hauler and false when the client named one.

Dispatch is the most heavily guarded creation in the system, and the checks fire in the order set out in §2.10. The named recycler must be `CERTIFIED`, or the dispatch is refused with `ERR_RECYCLER_NOT_CERTIFIED`. The source site must be `ENROLLED`, or `ERR_SITE_NOT_ENROLLED`. The source site must not be under infestation quarantine — meaning `infestation_quarantine_until` is null or the logical clock has reached or passed it — or the dispatch is refused with `ERR_SITE_QUARANTINED`. The resolved hauler's window capacity is then tested. The named recycler's `state_program_id` must equal the source site's `state_program_id`, or the dispatch is refused with `ERR_RECYCLER_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`. An explicitly supplied hauler must belong to the site's program and its `service_regions` must contain the site's `region_code`, or the dispatch is refused with `ERR_HAULER_REGION_MISMATCH`. The manifested quantity must not exceed the site's `units_on_hand`, or the dispatch is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SITE_UNITS`. And when the hauler was left to the server, at least one hauler must survive the eligibility filter, or the dispatch is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_HAULER`.

**Automatic hauler selection** considers only haulers whose `state_program_id` matches the source site's program, whose `service_regions` contains the site's `region_code`, and whose count of buffer-conflicting routes against the requested window is strictly below their own `max_concurrent_routes`. If that pool is empty the dispatch fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_HAULER`. Otherwise the pool is ranked by `per_unit_rate_cents` ascending — cheapest per unit wins — then by `active_route_count` ascending as the first tie-break, favouring the less loaded hauler, then by `created_at` ascending as the final tie-break, favouring the longer-standing contract. A hauler's `id` is never a ranking input. The first hauler in that ranking is chosen.

**Window capacity** is evaluated for whichever hauler ends up resolved, whether named explicitly or selected. Both ends of a pickup window are inclusive, and a symmetric turnaround buffer of sixty minutes is applied to the *new* window only: the padded start is the requested `pickup_window_start` one hour earlier and the padded end is the requested `pickup_window_end` one hour later. The server then counts the resolved hauler's existing pickup orders whose `fulfillment_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `PICKED_UP` or `DELIVERED` and whose own window overlaps the padded window — meaning the padded start is at or before the existing window's end and the existing window's start is at or before the padded end. Because both ends are inclusive, windows that merely touch do conflict. Orders in `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` never conflict. This is an aggregate capacity test rather than an exclusive-occupancy test: the dispatch is refused with `ERR_HAULER_WINDOW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` only once the conflicting count reaches the hauler's `max_concurrent_routes`, so a hauler configured for two concurrent routes may legitimately hold two overlapping windows and is refused the third.

A dispatched order starts on two independent status axes. Its `fulfillment_status` begins at `SCHEDULED` and its `settlement_status` at `UNSETTLED`, with `units_delivered`, `units_accepted`, `estimated_payment_cents` and `final_payment_cents` all null. Creation moves the manifested units out of the site's `units_on_hand` into `units_in_transit` and increments the hauler's `active_route_count` by one.

The two axes advance separately on the same record. `fulfillment_status` runs `SCHEDULED → PICKED_UP → DELIVERED → CLOSED`, or diverts to `CANCELLED`; `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are its terminal values. `settlement_status` runs `UNSETTLED → ESTIMATED`, then either straight to `FINALIZED` or via `DISPUTED` to `FINALIZED`, or diverts from `UNSETTLED` to `VOIDED`. Recording an inspection can move `settlement_status` from `ESTIMATED` to `DISPUTED` while `fulfillment_status` stays at `DELIVERED`; finalizing the settlement moves both axes in a single request. Every operation on a pickup order requires the right `fulfillment_status` to start from — `SCHEDULED` to confirm pickup or to void, `PICKED_UP` to deliver, `DELIVERED` to inspect or to finalize settlement — and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_PICKUP_STATE_INVALID`.

The assigned `Hauler` confirms collection, moving `SCHEDULED → PICKED_UP`. Only the hauler named on the order may do so, per the ownership rule of §2.1. Confirmation moves no units — the load is already counted in the site's `units_in_transit` — and leaves `settlement_status` untouched.

The hauler then reports delivery, moving `PICKED_UP → DELIVERED` and supplying `units_delivered`, which may not exceed the order's `units_manifested`; an over-report is refused with `ERR_UNITS_EXCEED_MANIFEST`. Delivery settles the custody arithmetic for the leg: the full manifested quantity leaves the site's `units_in_transit`, the delivered quantity lands in `units_at_recycler_count`, and any shortfall between manifest and delivery — shrinkage on the road — lands in `units_written_off_count`. The receiving recycler's `units_received_count` grows by the delivered quantity. Delivery also advances `settlement_status` from `UNSETTLED` to `ESTIMATED` and stamps `estimated_payment_cents`.

**The route payment** is one calculation with two variants, and the estimate is the first of them. The distance component is the `route_distance_km` multiplied by the hauler's `per_km_rate_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents. The raw payment is the billable units multiplied by the hauler's `per_unit_rate_cents`, plus that distance component, plus a flat stop fee of 2,500 cents. The raw payment is then capped at the hauler's `max_route_payment_cents` — the lesser of the two is kept — and the capped figure is then floored at a minimum route payment of 4,000 cents, the greater of the two being kept. A rejection-handling deduction of 300 cents per rejected unit is subtracted from the floored figure, and the payment is that difference or zero, whichever is greater. The ordering here is deliberate and normative: the contractual cap is applied *before* the minimum floor and *before* the rejection deduction, so a route that hits its cap still pays the full handling deduction out of the capped amount rather than out of the raw one.

The **route-estimate** variant, stamped at delivery into `estimated_payment_cents`, bills the manifest rather than the scale ticket: its billable units are `units_manifested`, and because nothing has been inspected yet its rejected units are zero, so no handling deduction applies.

A `CollectionSiteOperator` may withdraw a load before it is collected. Voiding carries a `reason`, requires `fulfillment_status` `SCHEDULED` and `settlement_status` `UNSETTLED`, moves `fulfillment_status` to `CANCELLED` and `settlement_status` to `VOIDED`, returns the manifested units from the site's `units_in_transit` to its `units_on_hand`, and decrements the hauler's `active_route_count` by one. It is the exact compensating action of dispatch, and it restores both the site's custody buckets and the hauler's load to their pre-dispatch values.

The same cancellation happens automatically, without any client request against the order, when the owning collection site enters `SUSPENDED` — whether by administrator action or by the strike cascade of §3.7. Every order of that site whose `fulfillment_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `PICKED_UP` moves to `CANCELLED` with `settlement_status` `VOIDED`, its manifested units return from `units_in_transit` to `units_on_hand`, and the hauler's `active_route_count` decrements by one. Note that this automatic arm reaches orders already in `PICKED_UP`, which the operator-driven void does not.

### 3.7. Recycler Certification, Load Inspection and Route Settlement

A `ProgramAdministrator` enrols a processing facility under a `state_program_id`, with a `facility_name` and an `accepted_unit_classes` array of at least one element drawn from mattress and foundation. A new facility starts in `certification_status` `PROVISIONAL` with `certified_at` null, `units_received_count`, `units_processed_count`, `units_rejected_count` and `verified_output_weight_kg` at 0, and `diversion_rate_pct` at 0. Certification moves it `PROVISIONAL → CERTIFIED` and stamps `certified_at` from the logical clock. Suspending certification moves it `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED`, carries a `reason`, and clears `certified_at` to null; while a facility is suspended, new pickup orders naming it are refused. Recertification moves it `SUSPENDED → CERTIFIED` and stamps `certified_at` afresh, but only once the facility's verified diversion rate has recovered: `diversion_rate_pct` must be at least 75.00, and a facility below that is refused with `ERR_DIVERSION_RATE_BELOW_MINIMUM`. All three transitions insist on the correct starting status — `PROVISIONAL` to certify, `CERTIFIED` to suspend, `SUSPENDED` to recertify — rejecting a mismatch with `ERR_RECYCLER_CERT_STATE_INVALID`. Certification cycles: `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED → CERTIFIED` may repeat, and a recycler has no terminal status.

A `Recycler` documents what it ships out by recording commodity output. Each record names a `commodity` of `STEEL`, `FOAM`, `FIBER` or `WOOD`, an `output_weight_kg` greater than zero, and a `source_period_month` written as four digits, a hyphen and a two-digit month from `01` through `12`, attributing the output to a processing month. The record carries the `recycler_id` of the facility it belongs to and starts in `record_status` `SUBMITTED` with `verified_by_user_id` null; unverified weight does not count toward anything. A `ProgramAuditor` verifies a record, moving `SUBMITTED → VERIFIED` and recording `verified_by_user_id`; verification is legal only from `SUBMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_COMMODITY_RECORD_NOT_SUBMITTED`. Verification recomputes the facility's `verified_output_weight_kg` — the sum of `output_weight_kg` over that facility's `VERIFIED` records — and its `diversion_rate_pct`.

**The diversion rate** compares verified outbound weight against the weight the facility is presumed to have taken in. The basis in kilograms is the facility's `units_processed_count` — its accepted units — multiplied by an assumed average unit weight of 28.0 kilograms. When that basis is zero the diversion rate is 0.00; otherwise it is the verified output weight divided by the basis, multiplied by one hundred, rounded half up to two decimal places. Only `VERIFIED` records contribute weight; `SUBMITTED` weight is excluded entirely. The recertification gate compares that rounded figure against 75.00 inclusively, so a facility sitting exactly at 75.00 percent qualifies.

When a load arrives, the receiving `Recycler` records an **inspection** against the pickup order. This is an append-only event, so it carries only an `id` and a `created_at` alongside its data, and it records the `pickup_order_id` it inspected and the acting facility as `inspected_by_user_id`. An inspection has a single lifecycle state, `RECORDED`, which is both where it begins and where it ends: creation is its only event, it is never advanced, amended or withdrawn, and the whole of its effect lands in the request that writes it. The request supplies `contaminated_unit_count`, the number of units found wet, soiled or otherwise unprocessable, which must be zero or greater and may not exceed the order's `units_delivered` — an over-count is refused with `ERR_UNITS_EXCEED_MANIFEST` — and an `infestation_finding` of `NONE`, `BED_BUG` or `MOLD`. The order must be in `fulfillment_status` `DELIVERED` with `settlement_status` `ESTIMATED`, and any other combination is refused with `ERR_PICKUP_STATE_INVALID`. Exactly one inspection may exist per pickup order: a second recording is refused with `ERR_LOAD_ALREADY_INSPECTED`. Only the facility named as the order's recycler may record it.

**The disposition** is decided in a fixed order. First the contamination percentage is computed as the contaminated unit count divided by the order's delivered units, multiplied by one hundred, rounded half up to two decimal places. Then: if the `infestation_finding` is anything other than `NONE`, the `disposition` is `FULL_REJECT`, the `rejection_reason` is `INFESTATION`, and `units_accepted` is 0 — an infestation finding overrides the contamination arithmetic entirely, however clean the load otherwise looks. Otherwise, if the contamination percentage is *strictly greater* than 5.00, the `disposition` is `FULL_REJECT`, the `rejection_reason` is `CONTAMINATION_THRESHOLD`, and `units_accepted` is 0. Otherwise the `disposition` is `PARTIAL_ACCEPT`, the `rejection_reason` is null, and `units_accepted` is the order's delivered units less the contaminated count — the clean remainder is taken and only the contaminated units are lost. The comparison is against the *rounded* two-decimal percentage and it is strict: exactly 5.00 percent is a partial acceptance and 5.01 percent is a full rejection, so twelve contaminated units out of two hundred and forty is accepted while thirteen out of two hundred and forty is rejected.

Recording an inspection then unwinds through the system. The inspection's own `contamination_pct`, `disposition`, `units_accepted` and `rejection_reason` are stamped, and the order's `units_accepted` is set from the inspection. The recycler's `units_processed_count` grows by the accepted units, its `units_rejected_count` grows by the difference between delivered and accepted units, and its `diversion_rate_pct` is recomputed. The source site's `units_at_recycler_count` falls by the order's delivered units, its `units_recycled_count` grows by the accepted units, and its `units_written_off_count` grows by the rejected remainder.

On a `FULL_REJECT` a four-hop cascade continues from there. The first hop moves the order's `settlement_status` from `ESTIMATED` to `DISPUTED`, leaving `fulfillment_status` at `DELIVERED`. The second hop, because the load was rejected, increments the source site's `contamination_strike_count` by one, and when the `rejection_reason` is `INFESTATION` it additionally sets that site's `infestation_quarantine_until` to fourteen days after the inspection instant, blocking any new dispatch from the site until the quarantine elapses. The third hop applies when the strike count has thereby reached three: the site's `enrollment_status` moves `ENROLLED → SUSPENDED` with `suspended_at` set from the logical clock. The threshold is evaluated after the second hop's increment has landed, so the third strike suspends within the same request that recorded it. The fourth hop follows from the suspension: the program's `enrolled_site_count` decrements by one, and every other pickup order of that site whose `fulfillment_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `PICKED_UP` becomes `CANCELLED` with `settlement_status` `VOIDED`, its manifested units returning from the site's `units_in_transit` to `units_on_hand`. A `PARTIAL_ACCEPT` triggers none of these four hops; its settlement stays at `ESTIMATED` and the site accrues no strike.

Settlement is the administrator's closing act on a route. Finalizing moves `fulfillment_status` `DELIVERED → CLOSED` and `settlement_status` to `FINALIZED`, and it requires all three of: the order in `DELIVERED`, its `settlement_status` in either `ESTIMATED` or `DISPUTED`, and an inspection already recorded against it. A wrong fulfillment status is `ERR_PICKUP_STATE_INVALID`; a settlement status outside those two values, or a missing inspection, is `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_READY`. Note that a disputed route is still settleable — a full rejection reduces the payment, it does not block it.

Finalizing stamps `final_payment_cents` from the **final-settlement** variant of the route payment described in §3.6. That variant differs from the estimate in two respects: its billable units are the `units_accepted` established by the inspection rather than the manifest, and its rejected units are the delivered units less the accepted units, so the 300-cents-per-unit handling deduction genuinely applies. Every other step — the rounded distance component, the 2,500-cent stop fee, the contractual cap, then the 4,000-cent floor, then the deduction, then the floor at zero — is identical and in the same order. The payment is then added to the hauler's `total_paid_cents` and to the program's `hauler_payment_paid_cents`, after which the program's `reserve_balance_cents`, `reserve_coverage_months` and `recommended_rate_cents` are recomputed.

### 3.8. The Retailer Take-Back-with-Delivery Obligation

Retailers and dual-role participants owe a one-for-one take-back: when they deliver new units to a customer, they must offer to remove at least as many old ones and bring them to a collection site. A `Registrant` records each such delivery by naming its own `registrant_id`, the `collection_site_id` that will receive the taken-back units, a `delivery_reference` holding the retailer's delivery ticket reference which must be unique among that registrant's deliveries, `units_delivered_new` being the count of new units delivered and therefore the size of the obligation (at least 1), and `units_takeback_offered`, the units the retailer offered to remove, which must be zero or greater and must be at least as large as `units_delivered_new`. An offer below the obligation is refused outright with `ERR_TAKEBACK_OFFER_BELOW_DELIVERY`. A new delivery starts in `SCHEDULED` with `units_takeback_collected` null, `waiver_reference` null, and `breach_units` and `breach_penalty_cents` at 0.

A delivery leaves `SCHEDULED` in exactly one of three ways, and `OBLIGATION_MET`, `OBLIGATION_BREACHED` and `WAIVED` are all terminal. Any of the three requires the delivery to still be in `SCHEDULED`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_TAKEBACK_NOT_SCHEDULED`.

Reporting the actual removal supplies `units_takeback_collected`. That figure may not exceed `units_takeback_offered`, or the report is refused with `ERR_TAKEBACK_COLLECTED_ABOVE_OFFER`, and the receiving collection site must be `ENROLLED`, or the report is refused with `ERR_SITE_NOT_ENROLLED`. The comparison of collected units against the obligation then selects the outcome, and the two branches carry genuinely different consequences.

When the collected count is at least `units_delivered_new`, the delivery moves `SCHEDULED → OBLIGATION_MET`. The collected count is stored, `breach_units` is set to 0 and `breach_penalty_cents` to 0. The collected units are added to the receiving site's `units_on_hand` and to its `units_reported_total`, and the program's `reported_units_total` is recomputed. Nothing is charged to the registrant.

When the collected count falls short of `units_delivered_new`, the delivery moves `SCHEDULED → OBLIGATION_BREACHED`. The collected count is stored, `breach_units` is set to the obligation less the collected count, and `breach_penalty_cents` is set to 2,500 cents — twenty-five dollars — for each breached unit. The collected units still flow into the receiving site's `units_on_hand` and `units_reported_total`, and the program's `reported_units_total` is still recomputed, because whatever was actually brought in is real. Beyond that, the registrant is charged: its `takeback_breach_count` increases by one and the penalty is added to its `delinquent_balance_cents`, which flips its `compliance_status` to `DELINQUENT` as soon as that balance is greater than zero — and, as §3.3 establishes, all the way to `SUSPENDED` once the accumulated balance reaches 1,000,000 cents. A suspended registrant can no longer file remittances at all, so a pattern of short take-backs eventually locks a retailer out of the filing workflow entirely.

The third exit is a documented customer declination. Waiving supplies a `waiver_reference` recording the declination, and moves the delivery `SCHEDULED → WAIVED` with `units_takeback_collected` set to 0, `breach_units` at 0 and `breach_penalty_cents` at 0. No units move into the receiving site, nothing is added to any lifetime total, and no penalty is charged — a documented declination discharges the obligation cleanly. A waiver is the one exit that does not require the receiving site to be `ENROLLED`, since it moves no units.
