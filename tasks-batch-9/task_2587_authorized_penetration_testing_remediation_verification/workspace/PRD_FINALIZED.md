# Pentest Assurance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Enterprise security assurance programmes live or die on evidence. A large organisation runs dozens of authorized penetration tests every year against applications, internal networks and cloud environments, and each one has to be justified before it starts, bounded while it runs, and proven closed after it finishes. Today that evidence lives in spreadsheets, ticket threads and email chains: nobody can answer, on demand, whether a given tester was cleared and provisioned when they filed a finding, whether the asset they touched was inside the signed authorization envelope at that instant, or whether the fix that closed a critical finding was ever independently retested by somebody other than the person who wrote it. The Pentest Assurance API exists to make those answers a matter of record rather than reconstruction.

The service models the whole programme as a single flat, cross-referenced graph of records mounted under the `/pentest` path prefix: an inventory of testable assets, an annual test plan whose candidate scope is scored and ranked against a declared risk appetite, engagements chartered out of approved scope, rules-of-engagement documents whose counter-signature is the act that authorizes testing at all, per-target testing windows with technique allowlists, tester records with background checks and clearance expiry, least-privilege access grants that reserve hours out of an engagement budget, findings with server-computed severity, real-time alerts for critical findings, remediation assignments with regulator-aware due dates, retest verifications with evidence, and closure sign-off under strict segregation of duties. On top of that graph the service runs trend analysis, rolling recurring root-cause themes up into training recommendations that feed the secure-development curriculum.

Two design commitments shape every requirement that follows. First, **authorization is a precondition, not a courtesy**: no finding may be filed against an asset that is not covered by an active authorized target inside an open window, held by a tester whose least-privilege grant names that asset. Second, **the risk ledger is conserved**: every finding carries an integer point value fixed at the moment it is rated, and every subsequent triage, acceptance, duplication or closure moves those points between buckets without ever creating or destroying them, so a plan's total risk exposure is always reconcilable.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service — without exception, including the logical-clock capability — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role they are acting in. If either header is absent, empty, or contains only whitespace, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` and HTTP 401. If both headers are present but `X-User-Role` is not one of the five recognised role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ROLE`, also HTTP 401. If the role is recognised but is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403 — and that decision is made **before** any identifier in the URL is looked up, so a caller lacking permission who addresses a resource that does not exist receives 403 rather than 404.

The five roles, spelled exactly as the service expects them, are:

- **`PlatformAdmin`** — the programme registrar. Owns the asset inventory, the tester roster, background-check recording and the logical clock.
- **`ProgramManager`** — runs the annual programme: opening plans, proposing and deciding scope, chartering engagements, drafting rules of engagement, adding authorized targets, requesting testers, provisioning and revoking access, and assigning remediation.
- **`ComplianceOfficer`** — the independent assurance authority. Counter-signs rules of engagement, resumes or terminates engagements, and owns closure sign-off. Segregation of duties forbids this role from signing a document it drafted or a remediation it owns.
- **`Tester`** — a cleared, provisioned penetration tester. The only role that may file findings.
- **`RemediationOwner`** — the accountable owner of a fix, the only role that may submit remediation evidence.

Identity resolution differs by role, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `Tester`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Tester` record; ownership and attribution checks on any resource that references a tester compare that resource's tester foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a reported finding's `discovered_by_tester_id` is taken from the header and never from the request body, the check that the reporter actually holds a provisioned assignment on the engagement resolves the assignment by `X-User-ID`, a recorded verification's `verifier_tester_id` is taken from the header, and the rule that a tester may not verify their own discovery compares `X-User-ID` to the finding's `discovered_by_tester_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `ProgramManager`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `RemediationOwner`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared to any record's `id`. The server does record such identifiers where the specification says so — a drafted document's `drafted_by_user_id`, a signed document's `signed_by_user_id`, an acknowledged alert's `acknowledged_by_user_id`, a signed-off verification's `signed_off_by_user_id`, and the `owner_user_id` supplied when remediation is assigned — and it compares those stored strings against `X-User-ID` for the segregation-of-duties and ownership rules described later, but it never resolves them to an entity row.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection-listing endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls how many records are skipped before the page begins: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0, with no upper bound. Both must be integers. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` and HTTP 400.

Unless an endpoint's description states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest record first. List responses set `response_type` to `"array"` and populate four pagination fields inside `meta`: `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective skip), `total_count` (the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied) and `has_more` (true when records remain beyond the current page). Single-resource reads set `response_type` to `"object"` and omit those four fields from `meta`.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Audit Stamps and Versioning

Every record the service persists carries a server-minted `id` in UUID form. Identifiers carry no type prefix and are never supplied by the client; a client-provided `id` in a request body is ignored. Every record also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits.

Most records additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed to the current instant on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the record is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record. Assets, test plans, scope items, engagements, rules-of-engagement documents, authorized targets, testers, tester assignments, findings, critical alerts, remediations and retest verifications all carry the full set. **Training recommendations are the single exception**: they are immutable analytics records, and they carry only `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from their representation entirely, because nothing ever mutates them after generation.

The `version` counter is not cosmetic. It is the value clients echo back on the writes described in §2.7, and the specification is explicit about which operations advance it: proposing a scope candidate, deciding a scope candidate, switching a plan's prioritization mode, activating a plan and creating an engagement all bump the plan's `version`; starting, completing, reporting or closing fieldwork, emergency-stopping, resuming, signing the rules of engagement, filing a finding, requesting an assignment and provisioning or revoking access all bump the engagement's `version`. Where a single request mutates several records — and cascades in this system routinely do — every mutated record's `version` advances by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed.

### 2.5. The Logical Programme Clock

Every temporal rule in this specification — testing-window containment, cooldown collision detection, clearance expiry, alert acknowledgement deadlines, remediation due dates and overdue evaluation, asset staleness, and the recency decay in trend analysis — reads a single server-side logical clock, referred to throughout as the current instant. The clock never falls back to wall-clock time. Before any caller has set it, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`.

A `PlatformAdmin` may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting capability. Setting the clock changes the value every subsequent request observes and mutates no entity whatsoever: no counters shift, no state machine advances, no `version` increases, and no overdue flag is recomputed as a side effect of the call itself. Derived values that are described as computed at read time — most notably a remediation's `overdue_flag` — reflect the clock as it stands when the record is next read or evaluated.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision

Unless a rule says otherwise, every monetary-style or score-style number the server computes is rounded to **2 decimal places using round-half-up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero. Rounding is applied once, at the end of a calculation, never per intermediate component, except where a rule explicitly pins a different rounding order (the training-priority calculation in §3.9 is the one place where per-item rounding precedes summation, and that ordering is observable in the result).

Three families of numbers deviate deliberately. Risk points and rank numbers are integers and are never rounded, which is what makes the plan risk ledger reconcile exactly. Settled tester hours are whole numbers of hours obtained by rounding a fractional elapsed duration **up** to the next whole hour, while the engagement's hour-ledger fields themselves carry at most 2 decimals. Remediation service-level windows are whole numbers of days added to the clock, so a due instant always shares the time-of-day component of the instant it was derived from.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Programme Writes

Three create operations sit on parent records whose derived state moves underneath the client, and each of them therefore requires the client to declare which version of that parent it was looking at. The declared field is **not optional** on those three requests; omitting it is a malformed body and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 400.

- `POST /pentest/scope-items` requires `expected_test_plan_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `TestPlan`. If it does not equal the plan's current `version`, the proposal is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The reason is that every accepted candidate rescores and re-ranks the plan's whole candidate population and advances the plan's `version`, so a client holding an older version is scoring its candidate against a ranking population that no longer exists. Two managers may both legitimately propose different assets into the same plan, so no state precondition orders them — the stale-version guard is what does.
- `POST /pentest/findings` requires `expected_engagement_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Engagement`. A mismatch with the engagement's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ENGAGEMENT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Each accepted finding, each emergency stop and each hour settlement advances the engagement's `version`, so a stale value means the tester has not observed a suspension or a budget change that may already forbid the report.
- `POST /pentest/tester-assignments` also requires `expected_engagement_version` against the target `Engagement`, and a mismatch is likewise rejected with `ERR_STALE_ENGAGEMENT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Reserved, consumed and available hours all move with every assignment and every settlement, so a stale value means the planner is budgeting against hours another assignment has already taken.

The stale-version comparison is evaluated **before** the parent's own state preconditions. A client whose `expected_engagement_version` is out of date and whose engagement is also suspended sees the 412, not the state conflict — the version guard is the more specific complaint, because the client has demonstrably not seen the suspension yet. No other endpoint in this specification accepts a version field; supplying one elsewhere has no effect.

### 2.8. Order of Rejection

Many operations are guarded by several rules at once, and the service is precise about which complaint surfaces first. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the first one that fails ends the request. Reading this ladder top to bottom is the definitive answer to "which error does this request get".

**Band 1 — authentication and authorization (in this order):** header presence (`ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`, 401), then role recognition (`ERR_INVALID_ROLE`, 401), then endpoint permission (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, 403). All three apply to every endpoint.

**Band 2 — path resolution.** Every identifier embedded in the URL is resolved left to right, and the first one that fails to resolve determines the error: `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEST_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCOPE_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TARGET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TESTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALERT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REMEDIATION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_RETEST_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404. This band applies to every endpoint that has an identifier in its path.

**Band 3 — request shape.** For every request that carries a body, the body is checked against the endpoint's declared inputs: required fields present and non-blank, types correct, enumerated values drawn from the known member list, numeric values inside their declared ranges, arrays free of duplicate members, every target window ending strictly after it starts, and every assignment interval ending strictly after it starts. Any violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 400. For every list endpoint, `limit` and `offset` are checked in this band and a violation is `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` with HTTP 400.

**Band 4 — body-referenced resource resolution.** Every foreign key supplied in a body must resolve: `test_plan_id`, `asset_id`, `scope_item_id`, `engagement_id`, `roe_id`, `tester_id`, `finding_id`, `remediation_id`, `duplicate_of_finding_id`, and each member of `granted_asset_ids`. This band guards scope-item creation, engagement creation, rules-of-engagement drafting, authorized-target creation, tester-assignment requests, finding reports, remediation assignment, retest verification and finding triage. The failure carries the matching not-found identifier from Band 2's list at HTTP 404 — an unresolvable `asset_id` in a body, or inside `granted_asset_ids`, yields `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`.

**Band 5 — stale-version guards.** `expected_test_plan_version` on scope-item creation (`ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION`, 412), then `expected_engagement_version` on finding reports and on tester-assignment requests (`ERR_STALE_ENGAGEMENT_VERSION`, 412). As §2.7 states, this band precedes every state precondition.

**Band 6 — state preconditions,** evaluated in this relative order:

1. The plan must be `DRAFT` (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_DRAFT`, 409) — for scope-item creation, scope decisions, prioritization-mode switching and plan activation.
2. The plan must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`, 409) — for engagement creation.
3. The scope item must be `PROPOSED` or `DEFERRED` (`ERR_SCOPE_ITEM_NOT_PENDING`, 409) — for scope decisions; a `CONSUMED` or `WITHDRAWN` candidate accepts no further decision.
4. The referenced scope item must be `APPROVED` and must belong to the same plan as the engagement being created (`ERR_SCOPE_ITEM_NOT_APPROVED`, 409).
5. The engagement's authorization axis must not be `SUSPENDED` (`ERR_ENGAGEMENT_SUSPENDED`, 409) — for finding reports, execution transitions, access provisioning or revocation, and authorized-target creation. This state complaint outranks every business rule on those four operations.
6. The engagement's authorization axis must be `AUTHORIZED` (`ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, 409) — for finding reports, for starting fieldwork, and for provisioning access. Both `ROE_PENDING` and `TERMINATED` fail this check; `SUSPENDED` was already caught one step earlier.
7. The engagement's execution axis must be `EXECUTING` (`ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_EXECUTING`, 409) — for finding reports.
8. The requested execution action must be declared for the engagement's current execution state (`ERR_INVALID_EXECUTION_TRANSITION`, 409).
9. Resumption requires the authorization axis to be `SUSPENDED` and the execution axis not to be `CLOSED` (`ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_SUSPENDED`, 409).
10. An emergency stop requires the authorization axis to be `AUTHORIZED` or `SUSPENDED` (`ERR_ENGAGEMENT_ALREADY_TERMINATED`, 409); an already-terminated engagement cannot be stopped again.
11. Signing requires the rules-of-engagement document to be `DRAFT` (`ERR_ROE_ALREADY_SIGNED`, 409).
12. Target creation and target exclusion require the parent document to be `DRAFT` or `SIGNED` (`ERR_ROE_NOT_MODIFIABLE`, 409); a `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED` document is frozen.
13. Exclusion requires the target to be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_TARGET_NOT_ACTIVE`, 409).
14. Access provisioning requires the assignment to be `REQUESTED`, and revocation requires it to be `REQUESTED` or `PROVISIONED` (`ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`, 409).
15. A background-check result of `CLEARED` requires the tester to be `PENDING_CLEARANCE` or `SUSPENDED`, while a result of `FLAGGED` or `EXPIRED` requires the tester to be `AVAILABLE` or `ASSIGNED` (`ERR_TESTER_STATE_INVALID`, 409).
16. Triage requires a compatible finding status: `REPORTED` or `TRIAGED` for a duplicate marking, `RISK_ACCEPTED` for revoking a risk acceptance, and `REPORTED` specifically for acceptance (`ERR_FINDING_NOT_TRIAGEABLE`, 409).
17. Remediation assignment requires the finding to be `TRIAGED`, and moving an engagement to reported requires that no finding of that engagement is still `REPORTED` (`ERR_FINDING_NOT_TRIAGED`, 409).
18. Submission and extension require the remediation to be `ASSIGNED` (`ERR_REMEDIATION_NOT_ASSIGNED`, 409).
19. Recording a retest verification requires the remediation to be `SUBMITTED` (`ERR_REMEDIATION_NOT_SUBMITTED`, 409).
20. Sign-off requires the verification to be `VERIFIED` (`ERR_RETEST_ALREADY_SIGNED_OFF`, 409), and then requires the verification outcome to be `PASS` with the remediation `VALIDATED` (`ERR_REMEDIATION_NOT_VALIDATED`, 409).
21. Acknowledgement requires the alert to be `RAISED` or `ESCALATED` (`ERR_ALERT_ALREADY_ACKNOWLEDGED`, 409).

**Band 7 — uniqueness.** No competing scope item for the same plan-and-asset pair may hold `PROPOSED` or `APPROVED` status (`ERR_DUPLICATE_SCOPE_ITEM`, 409); no competing rules-of-engagement document for the same engagement may hold `DRAFT`, `SIGNED` or `SUSPENDED` status (`ERR_ROE_ALREADY_EXISTS`, 409); no competing remediation on the same finding may hold any status other than `CLOSED` (`ERR_REMEDIATION_ALREADY_EXISTS`, 409).

**Band 8 — testing-authorization safety,** in this relative order: no open authorized window at all (`ERR_NO_OPEN_TESTING_WINDOW`, 422), then explicit exclusion of the asset (`ERR_TARGET_EXCLUDED`, 422), then absence of an active authorized target for the asset (`ERR_TARGET_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, 422), then absence of a provisioned assignment for the reporting tester (`ERR_TESTER_NOT_ASSIGNED`, 422), then the least-privilege grant check (`ERR_ASSET_NOT_GRANTED`, 422). Provisioning safety follows: clearance status (`ERR_TESTER_NOT_CLEARED`, 422), then clearance expiry (`ERR_CLEARANCE_EXPIRED`, 422), then the grant-scope subset check (`ERR_GRANT_EXCEEDS_AUTHORIZED_SCOPE`, 422). Target creation enforces the technique subset rule (`ERR_TECHNIQUE_NOT_ALLOWED`, 422); signing enforces the presence of at least one active target (`ERR_NO_AUTHORIZED_TARGETS`, 422); and completing or closing fieldwork enforces that no critical alert of the engagement is still `RAISED` (`ERR_UNACKNOWLEDGED_CRITICAL_ALERT`, 409).

**Band 9 — segregation of duties and referential rules,** in this relative order: the document signer may not be its drafter (`ERR_SOD_ROE_SIGNER_CONFLICT`, 422); a verifying tester may not be the finding's discoverer (`ERR_SOD_VERIFIER_CONFLICT`, 422); a closure signer may not be the remediation owner (`ERR_SOD_SIGNOFF_CONFLICT`, 422); an evidence submitter must be the remediation owner (`ERR_ACTOR_NOT_OWNER`, 403); a duplicate marking may not point a finding at itself (`ERR_DUPLICATE_SELF_REFERENCE`, 422) and may not point at a finding that is already `DUPLICATE` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CHAIN_NOT_ALLOWED`, 422); and a remediation whose validation requirement is `RETEST_REQUIRED` may only be verified by a `Tester` (`ERR_VERIFIER_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, 422).

**Band 10 — temporal and capacity rules,** in this relative order: the authorized target window must lie inside the engagement's window (`ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_ENGAGEMENT`, 422); then the cooldown-widened collision check on the same asset (`ERR_TARGET_WINDOW_COLLISION`, 409); then the assignment interval must lie inside the engagement's window (`ERR_INTERVAL_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`, 422); then the tester's aggregate concurrency cap (`ERR_TESTER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, 409); then the lead-qualification gate (`ERR_LEAD_QUALIFICATION_NOT_MET`, 422); then, for starting fieldwork and for filing findings, the engagement's own inclusive testing window against the clock (`ERR_OUTSIDE_TESTING_WINDOW`, 422). Also in this band: the below-threshold override requirement on scope approval (`ERR_BELOW_RISK_THRESHOLD`, 422); the requirement that activation have at least one approved candidate (`ERR_EMPTY_SCOPE_SELECTION`, 422) before the coverage gate (`ERR_COVERAGE_BELOW_MINIMUM`, 422); the extension cap (`ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_REACHED`, 409); and the residual-note requirement on non-passing verifications (`ERR_RESIDUAL_NOTE_REQUIRED`, 422).

**Band 11 — fiscal rules, checked last of all.** The plan's engagement count must be below its planned engagement count (`ERR_PLAN_ENGAGEMENT_LIMIT_REACHED`, 409), and an assignment's requested hours must fit the engagement's available hours (`ERR_ENGAGEMENT_HOURS_EXCEEDED`, 422). Placing the hour ledger last is deliberate: a request that violates both a capacity rule and the budget reports the capacity problem.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Cross-Aggregate Consistency

Because the records in this system are peers rather than a nesting hierarchy, consistency is maintained by explicit cascades that run inside the same request as the operation that triggered them. All cascades are immediate: by the time a response is returned, every record the cascade touches already shows its new state, its refreshed `updated_at` and its incremented `version`. Three invariants must hold after every single request, and they are the yardstick for whether a cascade was implemented correctly.

The **hour-ledger invariant** on an engagement is that `hours_allocated` always equals `hours_reserved` plus `hours_consumed` plus `hours_available`. Requesting an assignment adds its reserved hours; settling an assignment moves hours out of the reserved bucket and, where access was actually provisioned, into the consumed bucket; available hours are always the allocation minus the other two.

The **risk-point invariant** on a test plan is that `total_risk_points` always equals `open_risk_points` plus `closed_risk_points` plus `accepted_risk_points` plus `duplicate_risk_points`. Points enter the ledger exactly once, when a finding is rated, and thereafter only move between the four buckets.

The **alert-existence invariant** is that a critical alert record exists for a finding precisely when that finding's `immediate_action_required` is true, which is precisely when its severity band is `CRITICAL`. No other finding has an alert, and no critical finding lacks one.

Two cascades in particular are long. Suspending an engagement runs a six-link chain — engagement suspended, then its document suspended, then that document's active targets suspended, then the intersecting provisioned assignments revoked, then hours settled on the engagement ledger and tester counters decremented, then testers whose live-assignment count reached zero returned to availability. Closure sign-off runs a five-link chain — verification signed off, then remediation closed, then finding closed, then the plan ledger and the asset counters adjusted, then the asset's exposure posture cleared if it holds no more open critical findings. §3 describes both chains link by link in their business context.

Parent bubble-up runs in the same immediate fashion but only upward and only in one place: a test plan is never closed by any direct call. It closes by itself, as a consequence of its last engagement closing, provided the plan has at least one engagement and every engagement of that plan has reached the closed execution state.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

The programme's credibility rests on three separations, each enforced by comparing `X-User-ID` against a stored user identifier at the moment of the sensitive action rather than at any earlier point. The officer who counter-signs a rules-of-engagement document must not be the manager who drafted it. The tester who verifies a retest must not be the tester who discovered the finding being retested — and when a remediation's `validation_requirement` is `RETEST_REQUIRED`, the verifier must be a `Tester` at all, since a `ComplianceOfficer` may only verify remediations whose requirement is `EVIDENCE_ONLY`. The officer who signs closure off must not be the remediation's own owner. Conversely, one comparison runs the other way: only the recorded owner may submit remediation evidence, and a `RemediationOwner` whose `X-User-ID` differs from the remediation's `owner_user_id` is refused with HTTP 403 rather than 422, because this is a matter of who the caller is rather than of programme policy.

### 2.11. Read Channels and Path Resolution

Every collection in this system is addressed directly beneath the `/pentest` prefix; no record is created through another record's URL. Two read channels are nested, and they exist purely so that records created as side effects remain reachable: the training recommendations generated for a plan are listable beneath that plan, and every other side-effect record — critical alerts above all — is reachable through its own filtered collection listing.

Where a path does contain more than one identifier, the identifiers are resolved strictly left to right and the leftmost failure is the one reported. A request naming a plan that does not exist reports the missing plan even if the nested identifier is also invalid.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Programme Foundations: The Asset Inventory and the Tester Roster

Nothing can be tested that has not first been registered. A `PlatformAdmin` builds the inventory of testable things, giving each one a human label in `name` (between 1 and 120 characters, and not merely whitespace) and classifying it in `asset_class` as one of `APPLICATION`, `NETWORK` or `CLOUD`. That class is more than a label: it decides how much breathing room the scheduler insists on between two authorized tests of the same thing, as §3.4 explains. Each asset also declares its `criticality_tier`, an integer that must be 1, 2, 3 or 4 where 1 denotes the most business-critical estate; its `compliance_regimes`, a duplicate-free and possibly empty subset of `PCI_DSS`, `HIPAA`, `SOX`, `SOC2` and `FEDRAMP`; and its `exposure_score`, an attack-surface metric between 0 and 100 inclusive carrying at most two decimal places. Where the previous authorized test of the asset finished, `last_tested_at` records that instant; the field is optional at registration and stays null for an asset that has never been tested.

Registration also seeds the posture fields the server maintains from then on. A fresh asset opens with `exposure_state` set to `MONITORED` and with `open_finding_count`, `open_critical_finding_count` and `remediated_finding_count` all at 0, and its `last_cleared_at` is null. Those three counters have precise definitions the server must keep true at all times: `open_finding_count` is the number of findings on this asset whose status is none of `CLOSED`, `DUPLICATE` or `RISK_ACCEPTED`; `open_critical_finding_count` is the number of those open findings whose severity band is `CRITICAL`; and `remediated_finding_count` is the number of findings on this asset that have reached `CLOSED`.

An asset's exposure posture is a cycle rather than a ladder, and it is driven entirely by open critical findings. An asset sitting at `MONITORED` or `CLEARED` moves to `ELEVATED` the moment a newly rated `CRITICAL` finding pushes its critical count to at least 1. An asset at `ELEVATED` moves to `CLEARED` when its last open critical finding leaves the open set — whether by being closed, risk-accepted or marked duplicate — that is, when the critical count reaches 0 after the decrement, and at that moment `last_cleared_at` is stamped with the current instant. A `CLEARED` asset returns to `ELEVATED` when a new critical finding is reported against it, and also when a previously granted risk acceptance on a critical finding is revoked, since revocation puts those points and that finding back into the open set. The cycle `MONITORED` → `ELEVATED` → `CLEARED` → `ELEVATED` may repeat indefinitely; there is no terminal posture. The asset collection can be browsed with optional filters on `asset_class`, on `criticality_tier` and on `exposure_state`.

The tester roster is the other foundation, and it is also the registrar's responsibility. A tester record carries `full_name` (1 to 120 characters), a duplicate-free and possibly empty `certifications` list drawn from `OSCP`, `GPEN`, `CREST` and `CISSP`, a `clearance_expires_at` instant after which the tester's clearance is no longer valid, and `max_concurrent_engagements` — the aggregate cap on how many interval-overlapping assignments the tester may hold, which must be between 1 and 5 and defaults to 2 when the registrar does not state it. From the certification list the server derives `qualification_score`: award 45 points for `OSCP`, 35 for `GPEN`, 25 for `CREST` and 15 for `CISSP`, add up the points for the certifications the tester actually holds, and cap the total at 100. Because duplicate members are rejected as a malformed request, each certification can contribute at most once. A tester whose certification list is later replaced wholesale is rescored the same way from the new list; that is the only way the score changes.

A newly registered tester is not yet usable. Their `background_check_status` opens at `PENDING`, their `background_check_at` is null, their `availability_state` opens at `PENDING_CLEARANCE`, and their `active_assignment_count` — the number of assignments they hold in the provisioned access state — opens at 0. Recording a background check supplies a `result` of `CLEARED`, `FLAGGED` or `EXPIRED`, and either a `PlatformAdmin` or a `ComplianceOfficer` may record it. A `CLEARED` result requires the tester to be at `PENDING_CLEARANCE` or `SUSPENDED` and additionally requires that `clearance_expires_at` lies strictly after the current instant — a clearance whose expiry falls exactly on the clock instant is already expired and the request is refused with `ERR_CLEARANCE_EXPIRED`. On success the result is written to `background_check_status`, `background_check_at` is stamped with the current instant, and `availability_state` advances `PENDING_CLEARANCE` → `AVAILABLE` (or `SUSPENDED` → `AVAILABLE`, which is how a re-cleared tester rejoins the roster).

A `FLAGGED` or `EXPIRED` result is the mirror image and carries equal weight. It requires the tester to be at `AVAILABLE` or `ASSIGNED`, writes the supplied result into `background_check_status`, stamps `background_check_at`, and drives `availability_state` `AVAILABLE` → `SUSPENDED` (an `ASSIGNED` tester likewise lands at `SUSPENDED`). It then reaches across into live work: every assignment of this tester that is currently provisioned is revoked, each revocation settling its hours against its own engagement's ledger exactly as §3.5 describes, and `active_assignment_count` is reset to 0. Availability is a cycle, not a one-way door — a suspended tester who passes a later check returns to `AVAILABLE` through the cleared transition. The tester collection can be browsed with optional filters on `background_check_status` and on `availability_state`.

### 3.2. Opening the Annual Plan and Scoring Candidate Scope

A `ProgramManager` opens the year's programme by creating a test plan for a `fiscal_year` between 2026 and 2099 inclusive. Two numbers set the plan's character. `risk_appetite_score`, an integer from 0 to 100 inclusive, expresses how much low-scoring scope the programme is willing to admit, and from it the server immediately derives `admission_threshold` as 100 minus the appetite — a hungry programme with an appetite of 90 admits candidates scoring as low as 10, while a conservative programme with an appetite of 20 demands 80. `planned_engagement_count`, an integer from 1 to 50 inclusive, caps how many engagements the programme intends to run this year and is enforced later, at engagement creation. The plan also declares `prioritization_mode`, either `RISK_DRIVEN` or `COMPLIANCE_DRIVEN`, defaulting to `RISK_DRIVEN` when the manager does not say. A new plan opens at `DRAFT` status with `coverage_score` at 0 and all five risk-point buckets — `total_risk_points`, `open_risk_points`, `closed_risk_points`, `accepted_risk_points` and `duplicate_risk_points` — at 0. Plans can be browsed with optional filters on `fiscal_year` and `status`.

Candidates are proposed into the plan one asset at a time. A proposal names its `test_plan_id` and its `asset_id`, explains itself in `justification` (1 to 500 characters), estimates the tester effort it would need in `requested_engagement_hours` (strictly greater than 0 and at most 400), and — as §2.7 requires — declares `expected_test_plan_version`. The plan must still be at `DRAFT`; scope is not editable once the programme is running. No other candidate for the same plan-and-asset combination may currently hold `PROPOSED` or `APPROVED` status, though candidates that have been deferred, withdrawn or consumed do not occupy that slot, so an asset can legitimately be re-proposed after an earlier candidate for it fell away. A newly accepted candidate opens at `PROPOSED` status with a null `decision_note`.

Scoring happens immediately, and it is the heart of the prioritisation story. Four ingredients feed the score of a single candidate. **Tier points** come from the asset's criticality: 100 points for tier 1, 75 for tier 2, 50 for tier 3 and 25 for tier 4. **Regime points** reward regulatory footprint at 40 points for each compliance regime the asset is subject to, capped at 100 points however many regimes it carries — so two regimes give 80 and three or more give 100. **Staleness points** measure how long it has been since the asset was last tested: an asset whose `last_tested_at` is null scores the full 100 points, and otherwise the server takes the whole number of days elapsed from `last_tested_at` to the current instant, discarding any partial day, multiplies that day count by 100, divides by 365, discards the fractional part of that result, and caps the outcome at 100 points. **Exposure** contributes the asset's `exposure_score` directly.

The four ingredients are combined as a weighted sum, and the weights are exactly what `prioritization_mode` selects. Under `RISK_DRIVEN` — the default — exposure is weighted 0.45, tier points 0.30, staleness points 0.20 and regime points 0.05, so attack surface and business criticality dominate. Under `COMPLIANCE_DRIVEN` the weights become 0.20 for exposure, 0.25 for tier points, 0.15 for staleness points and 0.40 for regime points, so regulatory footprint dominates instead — which is enough to invert the relative ranking of the same two assets. The weighted sum is rounded half-up to two decimal places **once, at the end**; no individual component is rounded on the way. The rounded result is written to `priority_score`, and the mode that produced it is recorded in `scored_mode` so a reader can always tell which weighting a stored score reflects. Finally `below_threshold_flag` is set to true when `priority_score` is strictly less than the plan's `admission_threshold` — a score exactly equal to the threshold is not below it and the flag stays false.

Ranking is separate from scoring and covers a narrower population. Only candidates of the plan whose status is `PROPOSED` or `APPROVED` are ranked. They are sorted by `priority_score` descending; where two candidates tie on score, the one with the smaller `requested_engagement_hours` ranks ahead, on the principle that the cheaper candidate wins a tie; where they still tie, the earlier `created_at` ranks ahead. The candidate's own identifier is never consulted as a tie-break. The sorted population receives `priority_rank` values of 1, 2, 3 and so on with no gaps, and every candidate whose status is `DEFERRED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `CONSUMED` has `priority_rank` set to null. Ranking is recomputed for the whole plan population after every event that changes a score or the membership of that population, so deferring, withdrawing or consuming a candidate renumbers the remainder contiguously.

Mid-planning, a `ProgramManager` may switch the plan's `prioritization_mode`, which is only permitted while the plan is at `DRAFT`. Switching rescores **every** candidate of the plan whose status is `PROPOSED`, `APPROVED` or `DEFERRED` under the new weighting, refreshing each one's `priority_score`, `scored_mode` and `below_threshold_flag`, and then re-ranks the `PROPOSED` and `APPROVED` population. Deferred candidates are rescored but remain unranked, while withdrawn and consumed candidates are never rescored at all — their stored score is a historical fact. Candidates can be browsed with optional filters on `test_plan_id`, `asset_id` and `status`.

### 3.3. Scope Decisions and the Coverage Gate

Both a `ProgramManager` and a `ComplianceOfficer` may decide a candidate's fate, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `DEFER` or `WITHDRAW` together with an optional free-text `note`, and — where the rules demand it — an `override_note`. Every decision requires the plan to still be at `DRAFT`, and requires the candidate to be at `PROPOSED` or `DEFERRED`; a consumed or withdrawn candidate is beyond decision.

Approving moves a candidate `PROPOSED` → `APPROVED`, or `DEFERRED` → `APPROVED` for a candidate that was previously set aside — the deferral loop deliberately allows a candidate to be re-admitted without recreating the row. Approval carries one extra condition: if the candidate's `below_threshold_flag` is true, meaning it scored strictly below the plan's `admission_threshold`, then `override_note` must be present and non-blank, and its absence is refused with `ERR_BELOW_RISK_THRESHOLD`. The note the server records in `decision_note` is the `override_note` when one was supplied, and otherwise the plain `note`. Approval then recomputes the plan's coverage and advances the plan's `version`.

Deferring moves a candidate `PROPOSED` → `DEFERRED`, records the supplied `note` in `decision_note`, and drops the candidate out of the ranked population — which means the remaining proposed and approved candidates are renumbered so their ranks stay contiguous. Withdrawing moves a candidate at `PROPOSED` or `DEFERRED` to `WITHDRAWN`, sets its `priority_rank` to null, and likewise triggers a renumbering of the remainder. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal: a withdrawn candidate accepts no further decision.

**Coverage** is the plan's answer to "have we actually planned to test the estate that matters?", and it counts only the top two criticality tiers. The denominator is the number of registered assets whose `criticality_tier` is 1 or 2, counted across the whole inventory regardless of which plan they belong to. The numerator is the number of **distinct** assets of tier 1 or 2 that this plan has candidates for in either `APPROVED` or `CONSUMED` status. `coverage_score` is 100 multiplied by the numerator, divided by the denominator, rounded half-up to two decimal places; and in the degenerate case where the inventory holds no tier-1 or tier-2 asset at all, `coverage_score` is defined as exactly 100.00 rather than being undefined. Tier-3 and tier-4 assets influence neither side of the fraction. Coverage is recomputed whenever a candidate enters or leaves `APPROVED` or `CONSUMED`.

Activating the plan moves it `DRAFT` → `ACTIVE` and is a `ProgramManager` action. It first requires that at least one candidate of the plan is `APPROVED`, refusing an empty selection with `ERR_EMPTY_SCOPE_SELECTION`. It then recomputes `coverage_score` from scratch and requires the recomputed value to be at least 60.00 percent, refusing anything lower with `ERR_COVERAGE_BELOW_MINIMUM` — a plan sitting at exactly 60.00 activates. Activation advances the plan's `version`. From that point on the plan's scope is frozen: no candidate may be proposed or decided, and the mode may not be switched, since all three of those operations demand a `DRAFT` plan.

### 3.4. Chartering an Engagement and Authorizing the Test

Once the plan is `ACTIVE`, a `ProgramManager` charters engagements out of it. Chartering names the `test_plan_id`, names the `scope_item_id` it is drawing down, declares the `engagement_type` as one of `APPLICATION_TEST`, `NETWORK_TEST` or `CLOUD_REVIEW`, fixes the engagement's testing window with an inclusive `window_start` and an inclusive `window_end` that must fall strictly after the start, and grants a tester-hour budget in `hours_allocated`, strictly greater than 0 and at most 2000. The referenced candidate must be `APPROVED` and must belong to the very plan named in the request; a candidate from a different plan is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_ITEM_NOT_APPROVED`. The plan must also have room: if the number of engagements already chartered under the plan has reached `planned_engagement_count`, the request is refused with `ERR_PLAN_ENGAGEMENT_LIMIT_REACHED`.

Chartering consumes the candidate. The scope item moves `APPROVED` → `CONSUMED`, which both records that the plan's intent has been acted on and releases the plan-and-asset uniqueness slot so a follow-up candidate for the same asset may be proposed later. The new engagement opens with `execution_state` at `PLANNED` and `authorization_state` at `ROE_PENDING`, with a null `suspension_reason` and with both `provisional_assurance_score` and `final_assurance_score` null. Its hour ledger opens with `hours_reserved` at 0, `hours_consumed` at 0 and `hours_available` equal to the full `hours_allocated`. The plan's `version` advances, and — because a consumed candidate leaves the ranked population — the plan's coverage and remaining ranks reflect the change immediately.

An engagement carries **two independent state axes**, and confusing them is the most common modelling error in this domain. The execution axis, `execution_state`, tracks the work: `PLANNED`, `EXECUTING`, `FIELDWORK_COMPLETE`, `REPORTED` and finally `CLOSED`. The authorization axis, `authorization_state`, tracks the legal permission to touch anything: `ROE_PENDING`, `AUTHORIZED`, `SUSPENDED` and finally `TERMINATED`. The two axes move independently. Suspending an engagement mid-fieldwork leaves it `EXECUTING` on the execution axis while making it unable to accept a single finding, and that asymmetry is deliberate — the work is paused, not rewound. Engagements can be browsed with optional filters on `test_plan_id`, `scope_item_id`, `execution_state` and `authorization_state`.

Permission comes from a **rules-of-engagement document**, which the same `ProgramManager` drafts against the engagement. The draft names its `engagement_id`, lists out-of-scope hosts, paths or accounts in `exclusions` (from 0 up to 50 strings), declares which techniques the testers may use in `allowed_techniques` — a non-empty, duplicate-free subset of `SQLI`, `XSS`, `AUTHZ_BYPASS`, `PHISHING`, `LATERAL_MOVEMENT`, `IAM_ESCALATION` and `DOS` — spells out the agreed stop procedure in `emergency_stop_procedure` (1 to 500 characters), and gives a contact reachable at any hour for critical findings in `escalation_contact` (1 to 120 characters). The server stamps `drafted_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, opens the document at `DRAFT` status, and sets `active_target_count` to 0. An engagement may hold only one live document: if a document for the same engagement already exists at `DRAFT`, `SIGNED` or `SUSPENDED`, the draft is refused with `ERR_ROE_ALREADY_EXISTS`. Revoked documents do not block a replacement. Documents can be browsed with optional filters on `engagement_id` and `status`.

Inside the document, each thing that may actually be attacked is enumerated as an **authorized target**. A target names its `roe_id` and the `asset_id` it authorises, carries its own inclusive `window_start` and inclusive `window_end` (again, the end strictly after the start), and narrows permission further with `technique_allowlist` — a non-empty list that must be a subset of the parent document's `allowed_techniques`, since a target can never permit a technique the engagement itself does not allow. A technique outside that set, or an empty list, is refused with `ERR_TECHNIQUE_NOT_ALLOWED`. The target's window must lie entirely inside the engagement's own window, and a window that pokes out at either end is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_ENGAGEMENT`. Targets may be added while the document is `DRAFT` or `SIGNED`; a `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED` document is frozen and rejects additions with `ERR_ROE_NOT_MODIFIABLE`, and a suspended engagement rejects them outright. On acceptance the target opens at `ACTIVE` state with a null `exclusion_reason`, the parent document's `active_target_count` is incremented, and the document's `version` advances.

Two authorized tests of the same asset must not crowd each other, and how much space they need depends on what kind of asset it is. Each target records `cooldown_hours`, resolved from the asset's class: an `APPLICATION` asset needs no buffer at all, a `CLOUD` asset needs half a day of quiet on each side, and a `NETWORK` asset needs a full day on each side. A candidate target collides with an existing target when that existing target is on the same asset, is currently `ACTIVE`, and the two widened windows touch — specifically, when the candidate's `window_start` is at or before the existing target's `window_end` extended forward by the cooldown, **and** the candidate's `window_end` is at or after the existing target's `window_start` pulled back by the cooldown. Both comparisons are inclusive, so a new window that begins exactly one cooldown period after an existing window ends still collides, while a window beginning one second later does not. The comparison spans every document in the system, not just this one, because the constraint protects the asset rather than the paperwork. A collision is refused with `ERR_TARGET_WINDOW_COLLISION`. Targets that are `EXCLUDED` or `SUSPENDED` release their window and take no part in the check. Targets can be browsed with optional filters on `roe_id`, `asset_id` and `state`.

A target can be taken off the table mid-programme. Either a `ProgramManager` or a `ComplianceOfficer` may exclude an `ACTIVE` target on a `DRAFT` or `SIGNED` document, supplying a mandatory `reason`. Exclusion moves the target `ACTIVE` → `EXCLUDED`, stamps the supplied text into `exclusion_reason`, decrements the document's `active_target_count`, and advances the document's `version`. Two consequences follow immediately: the asset becomes untestable, so any subsequent finding filed against it on this engagement is refused with `ERR_TARGET_EXCLUDED`; and the window the target occupied is released, so a competing target on the same asset may now be created in that slot. `EXCLUDED` is terminal — an excluded target is never revived, not even by resuming a suspended engagement.

**Signing is the act that authorizes testing.** Only a `ComplianceOfficer` may counter-sign, optionally attaching a `signature_note`. The document must still be at `DRAFT`, or the signature is refused with `ERR_ROE_ALREADY_SIGNED`. It must have at least one target in `ACTIVE` state, or the signature is refused with `ERR_NO_AUTHORIZED_TARGETS` — an engagement cannot be authorized to test nothing. And the signer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the document's `drafted_by_user_id`, or the signature is refused with `ERR_SOD_ROE_SIGNER_CONFLICT`. On success the document moves `DRAFT` → `SIGNED`, `signed_by_user_id` is stamped from `X-User-ID` and `signed_at` from the current instant, and — the cross-record effect that matters most — the engagement's authorization axis moves `ROE_PENDING` → `AUTHORIZED`. Both the document's `version` and the engagement's `version` advance.

Fieldwork begins with a start action on the engagement, which either a `ProgramManager` or a `Tester` may issue and which moves the execution axis `PLANNED` → `EXECUTING`. It requires the authorization axis to be `AUTHORIZED`: an engagement still at `ROE_PENDING` or already at `TERMINATED` is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, while a `SUSPENDED` engagement is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_SUSPENDED`. It also requires the current instant to lie inside the engagement's window with both bounds inclusive, refusing anything earlier or later with `ERR_OUTSIDE_TESTING_WINDOW`. Starting advances the engagement's `version`.

### 3.5. Staffing, Least-Privilege Access and the Hour Ledger

Testers are booked onto an engagement by a `ProgramManager`. A booking names the `engagement_id` and the `tester_id`, declares the `assignment_role` as either `LEAD` or `MEMBER`, occupies the tester over an interval running from an inclusive `interval_start` to an **exclusive** `interval_end` that must fall strictly after the start, reserves `requested_hours` out of the engagement budget (strictly greater than 0 and at most 400), and enumerates the least-privilege grant in `granted_asset_ids` — a duplicate-free list of between 0 and 20 asset identifiers, every one of which must resolve to a registered asset. As §2.7 requires, the booking also declares `expected_engagement_version`.

Four gates guard a booking, and they are evaluated in a fixed order so that the most structural complaint always wins. First, the occupation interval must lie inside the engagement's own testing window; an interval that extends beyond it is refused with `ERR_INTERVAL_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`. Second comes the tester's concurrency cap. The server counts the tester's existing assignments whose `access_state` is either `REQUESTED` or `PROVISIONED` — across every engagement and every plan, not just this one — and treats an existing assignment as overlapping the request when its `interval_start` falls strictly before the requested `interval_end` **and** its `interval_end` falls strictly after the requested `interval_start`. Because both comparisons are strict, intervals are half-open: a booking that begins at the exact instant another one ends does **not** overlap it. Note that this is the opposite boundary convention from the authorized-target cooldown check in §3.4, where both comparisons are inclusive. If the number of overlapping live assignments has already reached the tester's `max_concurrent_engagements`, the booking is refused with `ERR_TESTER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Revoked assignments release their interval and are not counted.

Third comes the lead-qualification gate: a booking whose `assignment_role` is `LEAD` requires the tester's `qualification_score` to be at least 60, so a tester holding exactly 60 points qualifies while 59 does not, and a shortfall is refused with `ERR_LEAD_QUALIFICATION_NOT_MET`. A `MEMBER` booking has no qualification floor. Fourth and last comes the money: `requested_hours` must be no greater than the engagement's current `hours_available`, with equality passing, and an over-draw is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_HOURS_EXCEEDED`. The hour ledger is checked after every capacity and qualification rule precisely so that a booking which is both unqualified and unaffordable reports the qualification problem.

An accepted booking opens at `REQUESTED` access state, with `provisioned_at` and `revoked_at` null and `settled_hours` at 0. Its `requested_hours` are added to the engagement's `hours_reserved`, `hours_available` is recomputed as the allocation minus reserved minus consumed, and the engagement's `version` advances. Stated as standing definitions rather than as increments: an engagement's `hours_reserved` is the sum of `requested_hours` over its assignments whose `access_state` is `REQUESTED` or `PROVISIONED`; its `hours_consumed` is the sum of `settled_hours` over its assignments whose `access_state` is `REVOKED`; and its `hours_available` is `hours_allocated` minus `hours_reserved` minus `hours_consumed`. Assignments can be browsed with optional filters on `engagement_id`, `tester_id` and `access_state`.

Booking a tester is not the same as giving them access. Access is granted by a separate `ProgramManager` step carrying an `action` of `PROVISION` or `REVOKE`, and both actions advance the engagement's `version` because both move the hour ledger — which is exactly why the stale-version guard on new bookings can observe them. Provisioning requires the assignment to be at `REQUESTED` (`ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID` otherwise) and the engagement to be `AUTHORIZED` rather than suspended or terminated. It then re-verifies the human: the tester's `background_check_status` must be `CLEARED`, refused with `ERR_TESTER_NOT_CLEARED` if it is anything else, and their `clearance_expires_at` must lie strictly after the current instant, refused with `ERR_CLEARANCE_EXPIRED` when the expiry has arrived or passed. Finally it re-verifies the scope: every member of `granted_asset_ids` must be the `asset_id` of an authorized target that belongs to this engagement's rules-of-engagement document and that is currently in `ACTIVE` state. Granting an asset outside that set — including one whose target has since been excluded or suspended — is refused with `ERR_GRANT_EXCEEDS_AUTHORIZED_SCOPE`. This deliberate re-check at provisioning time, rather than at booking time, is what keeps least privilege honest: authorization can shrink between the two moments.

On success the assignment moves `REQUESTED` → `PROVISIONED`, `provisioned_at` is stamped with the current instant, the tester's `active_assignment_count` is incremented, and the tester's `availability_state` moves `AVAILABLE` → `ASSIGNED` (a tester already at `ASSIGNED` stays there, since the state reflects "holds at least one live grant" rather than a count).

Revoking is where hours are settled, and the arithmetic is worth stating exactly because it is observable. A revocation of an assignment that was **never provisioned** — one still at `REQUESTED` — settles `settled_hours` at 0, since no access ever existed and nothing was consumed; it stamps `revoked_at`, subtracts the assignment's `requested_hours` from the engagement's `hours_reserved`, and recomputes `hours_available`. A revocation of a **provisioned** assignment computes the hours actually held. The baseline is the later of the assignment's `interval_start` and its `provisioned_at`; the effective end is the earlier of its `revoked_at` and its `interval_end`; the elapsed duration is the effective end minus the baseline expressed in hours, floored at 0 so a revocation that lands before the baseline yields nothing. That elapsed figure is rounded **up** to the next whole hour, and `settled_hours` is the smaller of that rounded figure and the assignment's `requested_hours` — a tester can never consume more hours than were reserved for them, however long the interval ran.

The ledger is then updated in a fixed order on the parent engagement: `hours_reserved` is reduced by the assignment's `requested_hours`, `hours_consumed` is increased by the freshly computed `settled_hours`, and `hours_available` is recomputed as `hours_allocated` minus the new `hours_reserved` minus the new `hours_consumed`. Applied that way, the invariant that allocated hours always equal reserved plus consumed plus available survives every revocation. The tester's `active_assignment_count` is then decremented, and when it reaches 0 the tester's `availability_state` moves `ASSIGNED` → `AVAILABLE`. `REVOKED` is a terminal access state: a revoked grant is never restored, and re-admitting the tester means booking and provisioning again from scratch.

Revocation is reachable four ways, and every one of them runs the settlement above. A `ProgramManager` may revoke directly. The emergency-stop cascade revokes intersecting grants. Closing the engagement revokes every grant still live. And a `FLAGGED` or `EXPIRED` background check revokes every provisioned grant the affected tester holds, on every engagement they were working.

### 3.6. Fieldwork: Reporting Findings, Rating Severity and Real-Time Critical Alerts

Only a `Tester` may file a finding, and filing one is the most heavily guarded write in the system because it is the moment the programme asserts that a real attack was performed against a real asset under real authority. A report names its `engagement_id` and the `asset_id` it concerns, gives a short `title` of 1 to 160 characters, records ordered `reproduction_steps` of 1 to 2000 non-blank characters, supplies the mandatory `business_impact_narrative` of 1 to 2000 non-blank characters — the programme accepts no finding without a business impact story — states a `technical_base_score` between 0.0 and 10.0 inclusive with at most two decimal places, classifies reachability in `exposure_class` as `INTERNAL`, `PARTNER` or `INTERNET`, and attributes cause in `root_cause_theme` as one of `INPUT_VALIDATION`, `ACCESS_CONTROL`, `CRYPTO_MISCONFIG`, `PATCH_MANAGEMENT`, `SECRETS_HANDLING` or `CLOUD_IAM`. As §2.7 requires, the report also declares `expected_engagement_version`. The tester is never asked who they are: `discovered_by_tester_id` is taken from `X-User-ID` and a value supplied in the body is ignored.

After the version guard, the engagement itself must be in a fit state. A `SUSPENDED` engagement rejects the report with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_SUSPENDED`; an engagement at `ROE_PENDING` or `TERMINATED` rejects it with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_AUTHORIZED`; and an engagement whose execution axis is anything other than `EXECUTING` rejects it with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_EXECUTING`. Then come five authorization-of-testing checks, in this order. First, at least one authorized target of the engagement's document must cover the current instant, meaning the instant falls between that target's `window_start` and `window_end` with both bounds inclusive; **the target's state is deliberately not consulted here**, so even an excluded target proves that an authorized window covered this moment, and this check refuses only reports filed when no authorized window of the document is open at all, with `ERR_NO_OPEN_TESTING_WINDOW`. Second, if the finding's asset has a target on this engagement's document in `EXCLUDED` state, the report is refused with `ERR_TARGET_EXCLUDED` — an explicit exclusion is reported ahead of every other scope, temporal or fiscal complaint, because it is the most specific statement the programme can make. Third, the asset must have a target on that document in `ACTIVE` state, or the report is refused with `ERR_TARGET_NOT_AUTHORIZED`. Fourth, the reporting tester must hold an assignment on this engagement whose `access_state` is `PROVISIONED`, or the report is refused with `ERR_TESTER_NOT_ASSIGNED`; this precedes the grant check because the grant check needs that assignment to exist. Fifth, the finding's `asset_id` must appear in that assignment's `granted_asset_ids`, or the report is refused with `ERR_ASSET_NOT_GRANTED`. Only after all five does the engagement's own inclusive testing window get checked against the clock, with `ERR_OUTSIDE_TESTING_WINDOW` for a report filed before the window opens or after it closes.

Severity is the server's judgement, never the tester's. The rating starts from `technical_base_score` and multiplies it by an exposure multiplier — 0.95 for an `INTERNAL` finding, exactly 1.00 for a `PARTNER` finding, and 1.15 for an `INTERNET` finding. **The multiplier applies to the technical base score alone, before anything is added.** To that product the server adds a criticality bonus taken from the asset's tier: 0.50 for tier 1, 0.30 for tier 2, 0.10 for tier 3 and nothing at all for tier 4. It then adds a regulatory bonus of 0.20 for each compliance regime the asset carries, capped at 0.60 however many regimes there are — so three or more regimes all yield the same 0.60. The resulting total is **clamped down to 10.00 first and only then rounded** half-up to two decimal places, and the result is `severity_score`. Neither the tier bonus nor the regime bonus is ever multiplied by the exposure factor.

From that score the server derives three frozen values. `severity_band` is `CRITICAL` when `severity_score` is at least 9.00, otherwise `HIGH` when it is at least 7.00, otherwise `MEDIUM` when it is at least 4.00, and otherwise `LOW`. `severity_points` is the band's conserved ledger weight: 40 points for `CRITICAL`, 20 for `HIGH`, 8 for `MEDIUM` and 2 for `LOW`. And `immediate_action_required` is true exactly when the band is `CRITICAL`. All four values — score, band, points and flag — are computed once at creation from the single finding and its asset row, with no other finding participating, and are never re-derived afterwards. A later change to the asset's tier or regimes leaves a previously rated finding untouched.

An accepted finding opens at `REPORTED` status with `discovered_at` stamped from the current instant, `closed_at` null and `duplicate_of_finding_id` null. Its points are added to the plan's `total_risk_points` and to its `open_risk_points`. Its asset's `open_finding_count` is incremented, and for a `CRITICAL` finding `open_critical_finding_count` is incremented too, which may drive the asset's posture from `MONITORED` or `CLEARED` to `ELEVATED` as described in §3.1. The engagement's `version` advances. Findings can be browsed with optional filters on `engagement_id`, `asset_id`, `discovered_by_tester_id`, `duplicate_of_finding_id`, `status`, `severity_band` and `root_cause_theme`.

A `CRITICAL` finding demands real-time communication, so the same request that creates it also raises a **critical alert**. The alert record references the finding through `finding_id`, stamps `raised_at` with the same instant as the finding's `discovered_at`, opens at `RAISED` state with `escalation_level` at 1, and leaves `acknowledged_at`, `acknowledged_by_user_id` and `containment_action` null. Its `ack_deadline_at` is `raised_at` advanced by a window that depends on the finding's `exposure_class`: an internet-facing critical finding must be acknowledged within one hour, that is 60 minutes, while a partner-facing or internal critical finding is allowed four hours, that is 240 minutes. No alert is ever created for a finding whose band is not `CRITICAL`, and every `CRITICAL` finding has exactly one. Alerts can be browsed with optional filters on `finding_id` and `state`.

Acknowledging an alert records the containment step actually taken in a mandatory `containment_action`, and either a `ProgramManager` or a `ComplianceOfficer` may do it. The outcome forks purely on the clock. Acknowledging a `RAISED` alert at or before its `ack_deadline_at` moves it `RAISED` → `ACKNOWLEDGED`, stamps `acknowledged_at` with the current instant and `acknowledged_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, records the `containment_action`, and leaves `escalation_level` at 1 — an acknowledgement landing exactly on the deadline instant still counts as on time. Acknowledging a `RAISED` alert strictly after its `ack_deadline_at` moves it `RAISED` → `ESCALATED` instead, stamping the same `acknowledged_at` and `acknowledged_by_user_id` but setting `escalation_level` to 2. An escalation is not the end of the matter: a `ComplianceOfficer` may acknowledge a second time to close it out, which moves the alert `ESCALATED` → `ACKNOWLEDGED`, updates `containment_action` with the newly supplied text, and leaves `escalation_level` at 2 as the permanent record that the deadline was missed. An alert already sitting at `ACKNOWLEDGED` accepts no further acknowledgement and is refused with `ERR_ALERT_ALREADY_ACKNOWLEDGED`.

Unacknowledged alerts hold the engagement open. Neither completing fieldwork nor closing the engagement is permitted while any alert of any finding of that engagement is still `RAISED`, and both attempts are refused with `ERR_UNACKNOWLEDGED_CRITICAL_ALERT`. An `ESCALATED` alert does not block those transitions — it has been seen, however late.

### 3.7. Triage and the Conserved Risk-Point Ledger

Every reported finding must be triaged, and triage is where the plan's risk ledger does its work. Either a `ProgramManager` or a `ComplianceOfficer` may triage, supplying an `action` of `ACCEPT`, `MARK_DUPLICATE`, `RISK_ACCEPT` or `REVOKE_RISK_ACCEPTANCE`, together with an optional `note` and, for a duplicate marking, a `duplicate_of_finding_id`. Two of the four actions are reserved: risk acceptance and its revocation may be performed only by a `ComplianceOfficer`, because accepting residual risk is an assurance decision rather than a programme-management one. Throughout what follows, the finding's points mean its frozen `severity_points`.

**Accepting** a finding moves it `REPORTED` → `TRIAGED` and requires the status to be exactly `REPORTED`. It moves no points at all: the finding stays in the open bucket, since triage confirms the finding rather than resolving it. The finding's `version` advances.

**Marking a duplicate** moves a finding at either `REPORTED` or `TRIAGED` to `DUPLICATE`. It requires `duplicate_of_finding_id` to be present and to resolve to an existing finding; that target must not be the finding being triaged, and a self-reference is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SELF_REFERENCE`; and the target must not itself already be `DUPLICATE`, since duplicate-of-a-duplicate chains are forbidden and are refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHAIN_NOT_ALLOWED`. On success the supplied identifier is written to the finding's `duplicate_of_finding_id`. The plan's ledger subtracts the finding's points from `open_risk_points` and adds the same points to `duplicate_risk_points`, leaving `total_risk_points` untouched. The asset's `open_finding_count` is decremented, and for a `CRITICAL` finding its `open_critical_finding_count` is decremented too, which may move the asset `ELEVATED` → `CLEARED` with `last_cleared_at` stamped. And because a duplicate needs no independent containment, any alert of this finding still sitting at `RAISED` is moved to `ACKNOWLEDGED` with `containment_action` set to the literal text `DUPLICATE_OF_PRIOR_FINDING` and `escalation_level` left exactly as it was.

**Accepting the risk** moves a finding at either `REPORTED` or `TRIAGED` to `RISK_ACCEPTED` and requires the `note` to be present and non-blank — an accepted risk with no rationale is not acceptable evidence. The ledger subtracts the finding's points from `open_risk_points` and adds them to `accepted_risk_points`, again leaving the total untouched. The asset's `open_finding_count` is decremented and, for a `CRITICAL` finding, so is its `open_critical_finding_count`, with the same potential `ELEVATED` → `CLEARED` consequence.

**Revoking a risk acceptance** is the exact compensating inverse, and it carries the same specification weight as the acceptance it undoes. It moves a finding from `RISK_ACCEPTED` back to `TRIAGED`. The ledger subtracts the finding's points from `accepted_risk_points` and adds them back to `open_risk_points`, once more leaving the total untouched. The asset's `open_finding_count` is incremented and, for a `CRITICAL` finding, so is its `open_critical_finding_count` — which can drive a `CLEARED` asset straight back to `ELEVATED`. That is the second half of the invariant that makes the ledger trustworthy: after every one of these five events — rating, duplicate marking, risk acceptance, revocation and closure sign-off — the plan's `total_risk_points` must still equal the sum of its open, closed, accepted and duplicate buckets.

Triage is also what unblocks reporting. Moving an engagement's execution axis `FIELDWORK_COMPLETE` → `REPORTED` requires that no finding of the engagement is still sitting at `REPORTED` status; while any remains untriaged the transition is refused with `ERR_FINDING_NOT_TRIAGED`. A triage action that does not fit the finding's current status — accepting something already triaged, revoking an acceptance that was never granted — is refused with `ERR_FINDING_NOT_TRIAGEABLE`.

### 3.8. Remediation, Retest Verification and Closure

A `ProgramManager` assigns remediation for a finding that has reached `TRIAGED`, naming the accountable owner in `owner_user_id` (1 to 64 characters, an opaque operator identifier rather than a tester row), choosing a `validation_requirement` of either `RETEST_REQUIRED` or `EVIDENCE_ONLY`, and writing the intended corrective action into `remediation_plan` (1 to 2000 characters). The request names the finding through `finding_id`. A finding may hold only one live remediation: if another remediation on the same finding exists in any status other than `CLOSED`, the assignment is refused with `ERR_REMEDIATION_ALREADY_EXISTS`. As §2.8's ladder requires, the finding's `TRIAGED` precondition is evaluated before that uniqueness scan, so an assignment naming a finding whose status is anything other than `TRIAGED` is refused with `ERR_FINDING_NOT_TRIAGED` whether or not a live remediation also exists on that finding. Assignment moves the finding `TRIAGED` → `REMEDIATION_ASSIGNED` and opens the remediation at `ASSIGNED` status with `extension_count` at 0, `overdue_flag` false, and `submitted_at` and `evidence_uri` null.

The due date is where severity meets regulation. The severity default gives a `CRITICAL` finding one week — 7 days — a `HIGH` finding 30 days, a `MEDIUM` finding 60 days and a `LOW` finding 120 days. Regulation can only tighten that, never loosen it: if the finding's asset carries no compliance regime at all, `sla_days` is simply the severity default; otherwise the server takes the strictest cap among the regimes the asset is subject to — `FEDRAMP` caps at 21 days, `PCI_DSS` at 30, `HIPAA` at 45, `SOX` at 60 and `SOC2` at 90 — and `sla_days` becomes the smaller of the severity default and that strictest regime cap. A `LOW` finding on a FedRAMP asset therefore gets 21 days rather than 120, while a `CRITICAL` finding on a SOC2 asset keeps its 7 days because the severity default is already tighter. `due_at` is the creation instant advanced by `sla_days` whole days, so it shares the time-of-day component of the moment the remediation was assigned. Both `sla_days` and `due_at` are derived from the finding's frozen `severity_band` and the asset's regimes **as they stand at assignment time**; later changes to the asset never re-derive them.

`overdue_flag` is true when the current instant is strictly later than `due_at` **and** the remediation's status is neither `VALIDATED` nor `CLOSED`. A remediation sitting exactly at its `due_at` instant is not yet overdue, and a validated or closed remediation is never overdue however late the clock runs. Remediations can be browsed with optional filters on `finding_id`, `status` and `overdue_flag`.

Deadlines can move, but not without limit. A `ProgramManager` may grant an extension on a remediation that is still `ASSIGNED`, supplying `additional_days` and a mandatory `reason`; the extension adds those days to the current `due_at`, increments `extension_count`, re-evaluates the overdue flag, and leaves the remediation at `ASSIGNED` — it is a self-loop rather than a state change. A remediation whose `extension_count` has already reached 2 accepts no third extension and the request is refused with `ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_REACHED`.

Only the recorded owner may submit. A `RemediationOwner` whose `X-User-ID` matches the remediation's `owner_user_id` supplies an `evidence_uri` and moves the remediation `ASSIGNED` → `SUBMITTED`, stamping `submitted_at` with the current instant and recording the evidence pointer; anyone else is refused with `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_OWNER`. Submission carries the finding along, moving it `REMEDIATION_ASSIGNED` → `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`, and it also tidies history: every earlier verification of this remediation that is still sitting at `VERIFIED` state is moved to `SUPERSEDED`, so only the verification of the latest evidence bundle can ever be signed off. Such earlier verifications are necessarily ones whose `outcome` was `FAIL` or `PARTIAL`, since a passing verification would have validated the remediation rather than returning it to the owner.

Verification is performed by a `Tester` or, in the narrow `EVIDENCE_ONLY` case, by a `ComplianceOfficer`. It names the `remediation_id`, points at the retest evidence bundle through `evidence_uri` (1 to 500 characters), declares an `outcome` of `PASS`, `FAIL` or `PARTIAL`, and describes residual exposure in `residual_note`. The remediation must be at `SUBMITTED`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_REMEDIATION_NOT_SUBMITTED`. Three rules then apply. When the acting user is a `Tester`, their `X-User-ID` must differ from the finding's `discovered_by_tester_id`, since the tester who found a weakness may not be the one who certifies it fixed, and a self-verification is refused with `ERR_SOD_VERIFIER_CONFLICT`. When the remediation's `validation_requirement` is `RETEST_REQUIRED`, the verifier must be a `Tester`; a `ComplianceOfficer` may only verify remediations whose requirement is `EVIDENCE_ONLY`, and overreach is refused with `ERR_VERIFIER_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. And `residual_note` must be present and non-blank whenever the `outcome` is `FAIL` or `PARTIAL`, refused with `ERR_RESIDUAL_NOTE_REQUIRED`; a `PASS` needs no residual note.

Every accepted verification opens at `VERIFIED` state, stamps `verified_at` with the current instant, and leaves `signed_off_by_user_id`, `signed_off_at` and `closure_note` null. Its `verifier_tester_id` is taken from `X-User-ID` when the actor is a `Tester` and is null when a `ComplianceOfficer` verified an `EVIDENCE_ONLY` remediation. It also records lateness in `days_late`: the server takes `verified_at` minus the remediation's `due_at`, expresses the difference in whole days discarding any partial day, and floors the result at 0 — a verification recorded on or before the due instant reports 0 days late. Verifications can be browsed with optional filters on `remediation_id`, `verifier_tester_id`, `outcome` and `state`.

The three outcomes fork from the same submitted state with three different consequences, and all three are specified to the same depth. A `PASS` moves the remediation `SUBMITTED` → `VALIDATED` and the finding `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` → `RETEST_PASSED`; nothing moves in the risk ledger yet, because the finding is not closed until it is signed off. A `FAIL` moves the remediation `SUBMITTED` → `REJECTED` and the finding `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` → `RETEST_FAILED`, and then immediately re-opens both: the remediation returns to `ASSIGNED` with its `evidence_uri` and `submitted_at` cleared, and the finding returns to `REMEDIATION_ASSIGNED`, ready for another attempt. The ledger is untouched, since the finding was open throughout. A `PARTIAL` does everything a `FAIL` does — moving the remediation `SUBMITTED` → `PARTIALLY_VALIDATED`, the finding to `RETEST_FAILED`, and then both back to `ASSIGNED` and `REMEDIATION_ASSIGNED` with the evidence pointer and submission instant cleared — and adds one thing: the remediation's `due_at` is pushed forward by a fortnight, that is 14 days, as a grace allowance for the work that was demonstrably partly done, and `extension_count` is deliberately **not** incremented, so a partial credit never consumes one of the owner's two extensions. The loop from `REMEDIATION_ASSIGNED` through `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` to `RETEST_FAILED` and back to `REMEDIATION_ASSIGNED` may repeat without bound.

Closure is a `ComplianceOfficer` act and the final link in the chain. Signing off supplies a mandatory `closure_note` and requires three things: the verification must still be at `VERIFIED` state, and one already `SIGNED_OFF` or `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_RETEST_ALREADY_SIGNED_OFF`; the verification's `outcome` must be `PASS` with its remediation at `VALIDATED`, and anything else is refused with `ERR_REMEDIATION_NOT_VALIDATED`; and the signer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the remediation's `owner_user_id`, with a self-sign-off refused by `ERR_SOD_SIGNOFF_CONFLICT`. On success the verification moves `VERIFIED` → `SIGNED_OFF`, stamping `signed_off_by_user_id` from the header, `signed_off_at` from the current instant, and the supplied `closure_note`.

That signature triggers the five-link closure cascade, and every link must land in the same request. Because the verification is signed off, its remediation moves `VALIDATED` → `CLOSED`. Because the remediation is closed, its finding moves `RETEST_PASSED` → `CLOSED` with `closed_at` stamped from the current instant. Because the finding is closed, the plan's ledger subtracts its points from `open_risk_points` and adds them to `closed_risk_points`, the asset's `open_finding_count` is decremented, the asset's `remediated_finding_count` is incremented, and for a `CRITICAL` finding the asset's `open_critical_finding_count` is decremented as well. And because that critical count may now have reached 0, the asset moves `ELEVATED` → `CLEARED` with `last_cleared_at` stamped from the current instant. `SIGNED_OFF` and `SUPERSEDED` are both terminal verification states, and `CLOSED` is terminal for both the remediation and the finding.

### 3.9. Emergency Stop, Engagement Wind-Down and Programme Trend Analysis

Every rules-of-engagement document commits to an `emergency_stop_procedure`, and the service implements it. Invoking the stop supplies a `stop_code` of either `PAUSE` or `TERMINATE` and a mandatory `reason`, which is recorded in the engagement's `suspension_reason`. Either a `ProgramManager` or a `ComplianceOfficer` may pause; termination is reserved to a `ComplianceOfficer`. The engagement's authorization axis must be `AUTHORIZED` or `SUSPENDED`; an already-terminated engagement, or one still at `ROE_PENDING`, is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_ALREADY_TERMINATED`. `PAUSE` specifically operates on an `AUTHORIZED` engagement.

A `PAUSE` is reversible and moves the authorization axis `AUTHORIZED` → `SUSPENDED`, then runs a six-link cascade. Because the engagement is now suspended, its rules-of-engagement document moves `SIGNED` → `SUSPENDED`. Because that document is suspended, each of its targets in `ACTIVE` state moves to `SUSPENDED`, and the document's `active_target_count` is decremented once per target moved. Because those targets are suspended, every assignment of this engagement whose `access_state` is `PROVISIONED` and whose `granted_asset_ids` intersect the suspended targets' assets moves to `REVOKED`. Because those assignments are revoked, each one settles exactly as §3.5 specifies — `settled_hours` stamped, the engagement's `hours_reserved` reduced by the assignment's `requested_hours`, its `hours_consumed` increased by the settled figure, and `hours_available` recomputed — and each affected tester's `active_assignment_count` is decremented. And because a tester's live-assignment count may have reached 0, that tester moves `ASSIGNED` → `AVAILABLE`. The engagement's `version` advances. Crucially, **the execution axis is untouched**: an engagement that was `EXECUTING` remains `EXECUTING` while suspended. What changes is that finding reports, execution transitions, access provisioning and target creation are all refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_SUSPENDED` until the pause is lifted.

Lifting a pause is a `ComplianceOfficer` act, optionally annotated with a `resume_note`. It requires the authorization axis to be `SUSPENDED` and the execution axis not to be `CLOSED`, and anything else is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_SUSPENDED`. Resumption moves the authorization axis `SUSPENDED` → `AUTHORIZED`, clears `suspension_reason` back to null, moves the rules-of-engagement document `SUSPENDED` → `SIGNED`, and moves each of that document's targets in `SUSPENDED` state back to `ACTIVE`, incrementing `active_target_count` once per target revived. Targets that were explicitly `EXCLUDED` are **not** revived — an exclusion is a decision, not a side effect. Revoked assignments are likewise **not** restored: access must be requested and provisioned again from scratch, because a revocation already settled hours that cannot be un-settled. The engagement's `version` advances. The authorization axis is therefore a cycle: `AUTHORIZED` → `SUSPENDED` → `AUTHORIZED` may repeat as often as the programme needs.

A `TERMINATE` is the divergent branch of the same invocation and is terminal on both axes. It records the `reason` in `suspension_reason`, moves the authorization axis to `TERMINATED`, moves the rules-of-engagement document to `REVOKED`, and moves **every** target of that document to `SUSPENDED` regardless of which ones were active. It revokes every assignment of the engagement whose `access_state` is `REQUESTED` or `PROVISIONED`, each with the settlement arithmetic of §3.5 — which means an assignment that never got provisioned settles at 0 hours while a provisioned one settles at its computed figure. It then forces the execution axis to `CLOSED` and stamps `final_assurance_score`, and it evaluates the plan bubble-up exactly as an ordinary close would. The engagement's `version` advances. Where `PAUSE` is reversible, `TERMINATE` admits no resumption.

The ordinary wind-down runs along the execution axis. Completing fieldwork moves the engagement `EXECUTING` → `FIELDWORK_COMPLETE` and may be issued by a `ProgramManager` or a `Tester`; it is blocked while any alert of the engagement is still `RAISED`. It stamps `provisional_assurance_score`. Moving to reported takes the engagement `FIELDWORK_COMPLETE` → `REPORTED` and is a `ProgramManager` act, blocked while any finding of the engagement is still at `REPORTED` status. Closing takes the engagement `REPORTED` → `CLOSED`, is likewise a `ProgramManager` act, and is again blocked while any alert is still `RAISED`. Closing stamps `final_assurance_score`, revokes every assignment of the engagement whose access state is `REQUESTED` or `PROVISIONED` with full hour settlement, and then evaluates the plan bubble-up. An execution action that is not the one declared for the engagement's current execution state — closing something that is still executing, starting something already reported — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EXECUTION_TRANSITION`. Every execution transition advances the engagement's `version`.

**Assurance scoring** exists to give each engagement a single number, and the same underlying data deliberately yields two different numbers depending on when it is measured. Both variants start from a base of 100.00 and subtract a deduction for each finding in their input set: 25 for each `CRITICAL` finding, 12 for each `HIGH`, 5 for each `MEDIUM` and 1 for each `LOW`. The **provisional** variant, stamped into `provisional_assurance_score` when fieldwork completes and before any remediation has been verified, counts every finding of the engagement whose status is anything other than `DUPLICATE` — so findings that have already been closed still count against it — and grants no credit whatsoever. Its result is the base minus the deductions, floored at 0.00, rounded half-up to two decimal places once at the end. The **final** variant, stamped into `final_assurance_score` when the engagement closes or is terminated, excludes both `DUPLICATE` and `CLOSED` findings from its deduction set and then adds back a retest credit of 5 points for each finding of the engagement that has reached `CLOSED`, capped at 20 points however many closures there were. Its result is the base minus the deductions plus the retest credit, capped above at 100.00 and floored at 0.00, rounded half-up to two decimal places once at the end.

A test plan closes only by consequence. There is no operation that closes a plan directly: the plan moves `ACTIVE` → `CLOSED` when the bubble-up condition holds — the plan has at least one engagement, and every engagement of the plan has reached `CLOSED` on its execution axis. The plan's `version` advances when it closes. `CLOSED` is terminal for a plan.

**Trend analysis** closes the programme loop by turning recurring root causes into training. A `ProgramManager` or a `ComplianceOfficer` runs the analysis over a plan, supplying an inclusive `window_start` and an inclusive `window_end`, and the service emits one immutable training recommendation for each root-cause theme that has at least one contributing finding in that window. A finding contributes to a theme when it belongs to some engagement of this plan, its `root_cause_theme` equals the theme, its status is neither `DUPLICATE` nor `RISK_ACCEPTED`, and its `discovered_at` falls between the supplied `window_start` and `window_end` with both bounds inclusive. Findings that have reached `CLOSED` still contribute — the theme was exercised whether or not it was later fixed. Contributing findings are ordered by `discovered_at` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending, and that same ordering is what `contributing_finding_ids` records.

The score weighs severity against recency. Each contributing finding starts from a severity weight of 5.00 for `CRITICAL`, 3.00 for `HIGH`, 1.20 for `MEDIUM` and 0.40 for `LOW`. Its recency multiplier is computed by taking the whole number of days from the finding's `discovered_at` to the generation instant discarding any partial day, dividing that day count by 90, multiplying the result by 0.60, subtracting that product from 1, and then raising the outcome to 0.40 if it fell below — so a finding discovered on the day of generation contributes at full weight, one discovered 90 days earlier contributes at 0.40, and nothing ever decays below 0.40. Each finding's contribution is its severity weight multiplied by its recency multiplier, **rounded half-up to two decimal places before anything is summed**. The rounded contributions are then added together, the sum is capped at 20.00, and the capped figure is rounded half-up to two decimal places once more to give `priority_score`. The rounding order is pinned and observable: summing the unrounded contributions and rounding once at the end produces a different score for the same data, and that is not the required behaviour.

Each recommendation also records `recurrence_count`, the number of contributing findings for the theme in the window; `source_engagement_count`, the number of distinct engagements those contributing findings came from; and `generated_at`, the logical-clock instant of generation, against which the recency decay was measured. `urgency_band` is `MANDATORY` when `recurrence_count` is at least 3 **and** `priority_score` is at least 8.00, and `RECOMMENDED` in every other case — both conditions must hold, so a single devastating finding is never mandatory training on its own. Each record also carries the `test_plan_id` it analysed and the `window_start` and `window_end` it was run over. The emitted collection is ordered by `priority_score` descending and then by `root_cause_theme` ascending. Recommendations are never mutated afterwards, and the collection can be browsed with optional filters on `urgency_band` and on `root_cause_theme`.

Creating a new resource returns HTTP 201 with the created resource in `data`; every other successful operation, including all state-driving actions and all reads, returns HTTP 200 with the affected or requested resource in `data`. Action endpoints return the resource whose state they moved, not the resource they cascaded into.
