# Career Agency Field Force API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

This document specifies the product requirements for the Career Agency Field Force API, the back-office system of a career-agency distribution organisation. The agency makes its money through people: representatives are recruited from campuses and from career-changing professionals, assessed for fit, offered contracts, licensed across a lattice of insurance and securities registrations, appointed by carriers, trained against production milestones, and finally validated as full producers. Every one of those steps produces a durable record and a financial consequence, and the API exists to keep that record and those consequences mutually consistent.

The central design problem this document addresses is *posture-driven gating*. An advisor's ability to be paid, to accrue renewal income, to participate in joint work, and to inherit the policyholders of a departed colleague is not a property of the request being made — it is a property of the advisor's derived standing at the instant the request arrives. That standing is itself the product of several parallel, independently evolving dimensions (contract lifecycle, insurance licensing, securities registration, carrier appointment), and it is continuously recomputed by cascades that reach across the whole hierarchy: a policy that lapses charges back the commission that produced the sponsor's override, which moves a district office's override ledger, which is capped per measurement. A second design problem, equal in importance, is *determinism*: wherever a threshold, a rounding order, an interval boundary, or a tie-break could be read two ways, this document states the single reading the system must implement.

The system is a hierarchical field organisation. A `FinancialRep` produces; a `DistrictDirector` recruits, offers, and remediates; a `ManagingDirector` contracts, validates, terminates, and moves money. Each rank may do everything the ranks below it may do, plus exclusive powers of its own. Read access is universal across authenticated roles. The API is mounted under the top-level path `/agency`.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` names the acting capacity. If either header is absent or blank, or if `X-User-Role` names a role the system does not recognise, the request is refused with `ERR_MISSING_OR_INVALID_USER_CONTEXT_HEADER` and a client-error status. If the headers are well formed but the named role does not hold the requested operation in its exclusive-operation or shared-operation grant, the request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PERMISSIONS`.

The recognised roles, spelled exactly as they must appear in `X-User-Role`, are `PlatformAdmin`, `ManagingDirector`, `DistrictDirector`, `FinancialRep`, `RecruitingOfficer`, `LicensingCoordinator`, and `Any`. `PlatformAdmin` is a home-office operator who drives the logical clock and holds no business operation beyond clock control. `ManagingDirector` sits at the top of the field hierarchy and controls contracts, validation, termination, and all money movement. `DistrictDirector` is the mid-level field leader who recruits, extends and rejects offers, and remediates at-risk advisors. `FinancialRep` is the producing representative who opens and submits joint work cases and disputes persistency measurements. `RecruitingOfficer` is a recruiting-operations specialist who drives pipeline stages and assessment scoring. `LicensingCoordinator` sequences insurance licences, securities registrations, and carrier appointments. `Any` is the read-only projection shared by every authenticated role: every list and detail read is available to `Any`, regardless of which role drove the writes, and `Any` carries no write operation of its own.

The `identity` of a role determines how `X-User-ID` is interpreted, and the distinction matters because several operations are ownership-scoped. When `X-User-Role` is `ManagingDirector`, `DistrictDirector`, or `FinancialRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Advisor`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Advisor` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely: a joint work case may be opened, submitted, or reopened only by the `FinancialRep` who is the case's `lead_advisor_id`; only the `FinancialRep` whose advisor `id` equals a persistency record's `advisor_id` may dispute that record; and a `ManagingDirector`'s or `DistrictDirector`'s authority is likewise carried by an `Advisor` row, so the same comparison applies wherever one of those roles performs an ownership-scoped action. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `RecruitingOfficer`, `LicensingCoordinator`, or `Any`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it but does not compare it to any entity's `id`. In particular, operations driven by a `RecruitingOfficer` or a `LicensingCoordinator` are never ownership-scoped, because those roles belong to the agency's central operations staff rather than to the field hierarchy.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept a `limit` and an `offset` query parameter. `limit` is the maximum number of items to return; when the client omits it the server uses a default of 10, and the value must lie between 1 and 100 inclusive. `offset` is the number of items to skip and defaults to 0; a negative `offset` is invalid. A request whose `limit` falls outside the inclusive range 1 to 100, or whose `offset` is negative, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION_PARAMETERS`. Unless a rule elsewhere in this document states otherwise, every list endpoint returns resources sorted by `created_at` ascending. The pagination fields carried in the `meta` object of a list response are `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`.

### 2.4. Field Hierarchy, Sponsoring Chains, and Pipeline Lineage

The organisation is a graph rather than a tree in three separate senses, and each carries its own integrity rule. First, an `Advisor` carries a self-referential `sponsoring_advisor_id` that names the sponsor whose downline the advisor joins; a home-office hire has no sponsor and therefore a null `sponsoring_advisor_id`, which is also the create-time default. The server must refuse any write that would make an advisor's sponsoring chain reach that same advisor: when a `sponsoring_advisor_id` supplied at advisor creation would close such a cycle, the request is refused with `ERR_SPONSORING_CYCLE_DETECTED`. Second, a `SecuritiesRegistration` carries a self-referential `prerequisite_registration_id` that names another registration which must reach `ACTIVE` first; this forms a sequencing dependency graph whose members may not be activated out of order, and whose default is null (meaning the registration depends on the advisor's insurance licence rather than on another registration). Third, a `RecruitingCandidate` carries a self-referential `prior_candidate_id` that names the earlier pipeline entry a re-engagement continues; it defaults to null.

Nested retrieval follows the URL hierarchy. Whenever a request path contains more than one resource identifier, the server validates the existence of each identifier from left to right and rejects with the not-found error belonging to the **first** identifier that has no matching resource, without attempting to resolve the identifiers that follow it. The same left-to-right discipline governs identifiers supplied in a request body: every identifier that names an existing resource of the agency is resolved in the order the endpoint's input list declares them, and the first one that fails produces that resource type's not-found error. Each resource type has its own not-found identifier: `ERR_DISTRICT_OFFICE_NOT_FOUND` for district offices, `ERR_ADVISOR_NOT_FOUND` for advisors, `ERR_CANDIDATE_NOT_FOUND` for recruiting-candidate pipeline entries, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND` for selection assessments, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND` for employment contracts, `ERR_VALIDATION_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND` for validation periods, `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_FOUND` for product licences, `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND` for securities registrations, `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND` for carrier appointments, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND` for policies, `ERR_JOINT_CASE_NOT_FOUND` for joint work cases, `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_FOUND` for commission credits, `ERR_PERSISTENCY_NOT_FOUND` for persistency records, and `ERR_ORPHAN_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND` for orphan policy assignments. All of these carry the not-found status.

Deactivating a container does not automatically dislodge its members, and one specific containment rule applies to offices: a district office may never be closed while any advisor assigned to it is in a live contract state. The live states for this purpose are `ACTIVE_TRAINING`, `VALIDATING`, and `AT_RISK`. An attempt to close an office that still holds such an advisor is refused with `ERR_OFFICE_HAS_LIVE_ADVISORS`; the office never closes itself as a side effect of anything else. Suspending an office, by contrast, changes only the office's own `status` and leaves every advisor's `contract_status` untouched.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Every entity in this system carries the full common field set. Each resource has an `id` that is a server-minted UUID string carrying no type prefix, and a `created_at` that is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. Each resource also carries an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the resource, and a `version` integer that begins at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on each modification. Derived fields — aggregate statuses, posture fields, monetary rollups, and counters — count as modifications like any other: recomputing them refreshes `updated_at` and advances `version` on the row that owns them. No entity in this system declares `common_fields: false`, so there is no exception to carry the `updated_at` and `version` pair.

Two timestamp conventions recur and must not be confused. Where this document says a field is set to "the clock", it means the server's logical clock value at the moment the operation executes. The logical clock is a settable, test-driving facility: an admin-only capability accepts a `current_utc` instant and installs it as the clock, and the clock's startup default is exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`. Before any such call the clock reads that default and must never fall back to real wall-clock time. The clock advance re-evaluates licence and registration expiry and the derived postures on the next read. Where this document instead says a field is set from "the request", it means the value the client supplied in that field.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Parent-Anchored Writes

Three write operations in this system are anchored to a parent resource whose `version` the client must pin. Each requires an extra, non-optional request field carrying the version of the parent the client last observed, and each is refused when the supplied value no longer matches the parent's current `version`.

Creating an employment contract from a pipeline entry requires `expected_candidate_version`, matching the target `RecruitingCandidate`'s current `version`. When the candidate's version has advanced since the client last read it, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at status 412. This check exists because two contract requests against the same candidate can both look legitimate — a renegotiated offer is a real product event — so no stage field distinguishes the stale writer; only the parent's version does.

Opening a validation window requires `expected_contract_version`, matching the target `EmploymentContract`'s current `version`. When the contract's version has advanced since the client last read it, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at status 412. A second window is likewise a legitimate product action — a remediation window — so again only the parent's version identifies the stale request.

Opening a joint work case requires `expected_policy_version`, matching the target `Policy`'s current `version`. When the policy's version has advanced since the client last read it, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at status 412. Opening a second joint case on a policy is a legitimate product action, so the precondition on case counts cannot identify the stale writer and only the parent's version can.

Endpoints that are not one of these three do not accept a version field in their request body at all, whether or not they mutate a parent.

### 2.7. Money, Rounding, and the Credit Ledger Invariants

All monetary values in this system are integers in cents. Rates and percentages are decimal numbers. Wherever a computation produces a monetary amount, a percentage, or a score, it is rounded half up, per term, at the exact point named in the rule that describes it; the half-up rule is applied to the exact decimal value of the computation and never to a binary floating-point approximation of it, and a value that falls exactly halfway between two representable results is rounded up rather than to the nearest even digit. The rules that follow pin their own evaluation order, but three system-wide conventions hold everywhere. First, "rounded to two decimal places, half up" applies to percentage and score quantities such as `weighted_score`, `persistency_pct`, and the intermediate products of any formula that says `round_2`; these statements of precision are exact and are never hedged. Second, "rounded to the nearest whole cent, half up" applies to every integer-cents conversion, and where a formula defines one operand as the *remainder* of another, that operand is computed by subtraction from the total rather than independently rounded. Third, a rounding order stated in a rule as pinned is load-bearing: rounding each term and then combining is not the same as combining and then rounding, and this document states for every formula which of the two applies.

Three accounting invariants hold across the whole ledger and are the acceptance criteria for any recomputation.

The first is the per-policy commission conservation invariant. Every commission credit row belonging to a policy carries `case_gross_commission_cents`, and that value is the policy's first-year gross commission, identical on every credit row of that policy. On every path — before a joint case exists, with a joint case submitted, after a joint case is voided, and after any recomputation — the sum of `gross_commission_cents` over that policy's non-`VOIDED` credits of type `PRIMARY`, `SPLIT_LEAD`, and `SPLIT_SUPPORT` must equal `case_gross_commission_cents` exactly, to the cent. Override rows, training-allowance rows, and renewal rows are outside this invariant; the three primary and split channels are its entire scope.

The second is the chargeback balance invariant. An advisor's `chargeback_balance_cents` is always equal to the sum, over that advisor's commission credits whose `status` is `CHARGED_BACK`, of `chargeback_assessed_cents` minus `recovered_cents`. Because a reversal returns `chargeback_assessed_cents` to zero while leaving `recovered_cents` untouched, reversing a partly recovered chargeback leaves a credit in the advisor's favour equal to exactly the recovered amount; money already recovered is never refunded, and any reversal that pays recovered money back breaks the invariant.

The third is that `DistrictOffice.override_paid_cents` is always the sum of `override_income_cents` over every advisor whose `district_office_id` is that office, recomputed whenever any member advisor's override income changes.

All rollup fields in this system — `team_production_cents`, `override_income_cents`, `override_paid_cents`, `chargeback_balance_cents`, `renewal_commission_cents`, and `training_allowance_paid_cents` — are recomputed from live source rows rather than incremented in place, with the single exception that `renewal_commission_cents` on the advisor accrues by addition at finalization as §3 describes. Recomputing from live rows is what makes reversal exact: when a descendant's credit leaves the live set, its contribution to an ancestor's accrual disappears at the next recompute, with no compensating subtraction to get wrong.

### 2.8. Interval and Window Conventions

Three interval types recur, and their boundary conventions differ deliberately.

A `ValidationPeriod` measures a half-open window `[start_date, end_date)`. The window's `start_date` is inclusive and its `end_date` is exclusive, so a credit whose `created_at` equals the window's `start_date` is inside the window and a credit whose `created_at` equals the window's `end_date` is outside it. A window must have a `start_date` strictly before its `end_date`, and must lie wholly within its contract's live span; a window that violates either is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION_WINDOW_INVALID`. A new window must not overlap an existing `OPEN` or `EXTENDED` window of the same contract, and a violation is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Because the convention is half-open, two windows that merely touch at a shared boundary instant are adjacent rather than overlapping and are allowed.

A `ProductLicense` interval is also half-open, `[effective_date, expires_at)`. A new licence for the same advisor and the same `product_line` must not overlap an existing licence interval; an overlap is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`. A shared boundary instant is *not* an overlap for licences.

A `SecuritiesRegistration` interval is closed at both ends, `[effective_date, expires_at]`. A new registration for the same advisor and the same `registration_type` must not share *any* instant with an existing registration interval; an overlap, including an overlap consisting of a single shared boundary instant, is refused with `ERR_REGISTRATION_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`. A shared boundary instant *is* an overlap for registrations.

A `PersistencyRecord` measurement window is likewise closed at both ends, `[measurement_start, measurement_end]`. Two measurement windows of the same advisor must not share any instant, including a single boundary instant; a violation is refused with `ERR_PERSISTENCY_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.

A policy's `months_inforce` counts *completed* calendar months between the policy's `issue_date` and its `lapse_date`, with a partial trailing month not counting, and it reads zero while the policy is in force. The completed-month count is the year difference times twelve plus the month difference, minus one when the lapse day-of-month falls earlier in the month than the issue day-of-month. Under this rule a policy lapsed on the same month and day one year later reads twelve completed months, not eleven.

### 2.9. Derived Postures and the Parallel Licensing Dimensions

An `Advisor` row carries four status dimensions that evolve independently and one derived posture that depends on several of them.

The four parallel dimensions are `contract_status`, `insurance_license_status`, `securities_registration_status`, and `appointment_status`. Each is an aggregate over the advisor's child rows of the corresponding type, and each takes values from its own value set. `contract_status` is `NOT_CONTRACTED`, `ACTIVE_TRAINING`, `VALIDATING`, `AT_RISK`, `VALIDATED`, or `TERMINATED`. `insurance_license_status` is `NONE`, `PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, or `LAPSED` and is derived from the advisor's `ProductLicense` rows. `securities_registration_status` is `NONE`, `PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, or `SUSPENDED` and is derived from the advisor's `SecuritiesRegistration` rows. `appointment_status` is `NONE`, `REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, or `TERMINATED` and is derived from the advisor's `CarrierAppointment` rows. A newly created advisor starts with `contract_status` of `NOT_CONTRACTED` and all three licensing dimensions at `NONE`.

The derived `validation_posture` takes one of `VALIDATING`, `VALIDATED`, `AT_RISK`, or `FAILED_POSTURE`. It is recomputed on every child mutation and it is what gates the advisor's economics: it gates commission payout, renewal accrual, joint-work participation, and orphan reception. It is not an independent input; it follows `contract_status` as the state machines in §3 describe, and a fresh advisor therefore starts at `VALIDATING` in step with a freshly contracted one.

A licence may lapse purely by the passage of time. When the logical clock reaches a `ProductLicense`'s `expires_at`, the licence moves to `LAPSED` and the advisor's `insurance_license_status` follows, which in turn blocks any policy of that advisor still in `ISSUED` from reaching `ACTIVE`. Two derived flags ride alongside these dimensions. The `orphan_pool_access` flag records whether the advisor may receive reassigned orphan policyholders; it is false at creation, becomes true only on contract validation, and returns to false on every demotion and on termination. The `licensing_locked` flag is a deliberate test-driving control: while it is true, demotions that would move an advisor from `ACTIVE_TRAINING` to `AT_RISK` because a licence expired are skipped, so that an unrelated cascade may be driven against a validated advisor without a licence-expiry demotion firing at the same time. It is false by default and is set and cleared by an admin-only locking and unlocking capability.

A `Policy` carries one derived flag of the same kind. Its `orphaned` flag records whether the advisor of record has terminated and left the policyholder unserved; it is false by default and becomes true through the termination cascade described in §3.

### 2.10. Validation Evaluation Order and Error Precedence

When more than one rule could reject a request, exactly one error is returned, chosen by this order. Authentication is evaluated first: a missing, blank, or unrecognised `X-User-ID` or `X-User-Role` yields `ERR_MISSING_OR_INVALID_USER_CONTEXT_HEADER`, and a recognised role acting outside its grant yields `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PERMISSIONS` — both before any resource is even looked up. Existence follows, first along the path from left to right and then through the body in declared input order, so a request that names a nonexistent parent and also carries a bad payload fails on the parent and never reaches the payload. State is evaluated after existence: a resource that exists but sits in a state from which the requested transition is not declared is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and a resource that sits in a terminal state and admits no further transition is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. Structural payload validity — a missing required body field, a field of the wrong type, an out-of-range enum member, a `competency_scores` map that does not carry exactly the four required keys, a `split_pct_lead` outside its legal range, a legal-entity identifier used where its own type does not apply — is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` where the enum or field is simply malformed, and as the more specific identifier `ERR_ASSESSMENT_INCOMPLETE_SCORES`, `ERR_SPLIT_PCT_INVALID`, or `ERR_SELF_JOINT_CASE` where one of those exists.

Business rules are evaluated after structural validity, and among them the order is fixed. Licensing, registration, and appointment compliance is evaluated first, producing `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_PREREQUISITE_REGISTRATION_NOT_ACTIVE`, or `ERR_TERMINATED_ADVISOR` as the case may be. Posture and interval constraints come next, producing `ERR_VALIDATION_POSTURE_BLOCKS_PAYOUT`, `ERR_JOINT_WORK_POSTURE_INELIGIBLE`, `ERR_LICENSE_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`, `ERR_REGISTRATION_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`, or `ERR_PERSISTENCY_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Fiscal caps and balance blocks follow, producing `ERR_ALLOWANCE_CAP_EXCEEDED` or `ERR_CHARGEBACK_BALANCE_BLOCKS_NEW_BUSINESS`. Natural-key uniqueness follows, producing `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY`. Temporal window validity is evaluated last, producing `ERR_VALIDATION_WINDOW_INVALID`, `ERR_VALIDATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, or `ERR_PERSISTENCY_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Because compliance is evaluated before posture, a request that is blocked both by an expired licence and by an at-risk posture reports the licence error and not the posture error. Because compliance is evaluated before the fiscal block, a request refused for both a licensing failure and an excessive chargeback balance reports the licensing failure.

One semantics is orthogonal to this ordering and pre-empts it. The three parent-anchored creates described in §2.6 are pinned to the parent's `version`, and a stale pin is decided before the state-check path runs on the affected parent. A client whose `expected_candidate_version`, `expected_contract_version`, or `expected_policy_version` no longer matches the parent's current `version` receives `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at status 412 even when the parent's state would independently have satisfied the transition's preconditions — the version pin is the outermost check on those three writes, and no state-based diagnosis is offered in its place.

### 2.11. District Office Structure and Override Parameters

A `DistrictOffice` is the reporting and override unit that sits above the advisor population. Each office is created by a `ManagingDirector` with a home-office `office_code` — a natural key unique across all offices, so a reuse is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY` — a display `name`, a two-letter `region_code` used for reporting rollups, and the `state_code` in which the office is domiciled. It also carries two override parameters that default when the client omits them: `override_rate_pct`, the override rate applied to the gross commission production of every advisor whose `district_office_id` is that office, defaulting to 0.05; and `override_cap_cents`, the per-measurement cap applied to each such advisor's override income, defaulting to 300000 cents. The office's `override_paid_cents` is a computed ledger equal to the sum of `override_income_cents` across every advisor whose `district_office_id` is that office, and it is recomputed whenever any member advisor's override income changes. The office's `status` starts `ACTIVE` and moves among `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, and `CLOSED`. A `ManagingDirector` may suspend an `ACTIVE` office, which sets its status to `SUSPENDED` and changes no advisor's `contract_status` — advisors keep their own contract state entirely; may reactivate a `SUSPENDED` office back to `ACTIVE`; or may close an `ACTIVE` office, which sets its status to `CLOSED` and is the office's terminal state. Closing is refused with `ERR_OFFICE_HAS_LIVE_ADVISORS` whenever any advisor with a `contract_status` of `ACTIVE_TRAINING`, `VALIDATING`, or `AT_RISK` is still assigned to the office. A suspended office may not be closed directly — the transition to `CLOSED` is declared only from `ACTIVE`, so a request to close a `SUSPENDED` office fails the state check instead.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Recruiting a Campus or Career-Changer Candidate

Every prospective representative enters the agency as a `RecruitingCandidate`, created by a `RecruitingOfficer` in the `SOURCED` stage with a `reengagement_count` of zero and `entered_stage_at` stamped with the clock. The pipeline entry records the recruit's `full_name`, the `source_channel` it arrived through — `CAMPUS`, `CAREER_CHANGER`, `REFERRAL`, or `INTERNAL` — the `district_office_id` of the recruiting office that owns it, and free-text `notes` that default to null. A candidate sourced from a campus also carries `campus_name`; it defaults to null and is only meaningful for `CAMPUS` candidates. A re-engagement may name the earlier entry it continues in `prior_candidate_id`, which defaults to null.

Screening is the first gate. A `RecruitingOfficer` screens a candidate forward out of `SOURCED`, or out of `REENGAGED` on the second time around; either move sets the stage to `SCREENED` and refreshes `entered_stage_at`. For a `CAMPUS` candidate the campus `campus_name` must be present: screening a campus candidate whose `campus_name` is null is refused with `ERR_CAMPUS_NAME_REQUIRED`, and that refusal happens before the transition is attempted. Screening a candidate in any other stage is refused because the transition is not declared from there.

Scheduling an assessment is what moves a screened candidate to `ASSESSMENT_PENDING`. The `RecruitingOfficer` supplies the instrument `assessment_version`, which is either `V1` or `V2` and which selects the competency weight table used later, and the `scheduled_date` the instrument will be administered. The system creates a `SelectionAssessment` in status `SCHEDULED` against that candidate, as §3.2 describes, and advances the candidate. The candidate's `stage` and its `entered_stage_at` move together on every one of these steps: any stage change refreshes `entered_stage_at` with the clock.

Once an assessment has been scored, the candidate sits at `ASSESSED`, and the next move is to `INTERVIEW_READY`. Advancing to interview is permitted only when the candidate's *selected* assessment is not a failure. The selected assessment is the latest by `scored_date` descending, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, drawn from among the candidate's assessments currently in status `SCORED`; its `outcome` must be `PASS` or `CONDITIONAL`. When that outcome is `FAIL`, the advance is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_PASSED`.

Offers are a `DistrictDirector`'s prerogative. Extending an offer from `INTERVIEW_READY` additionally requires that the candidate's `district_office_id` office be in status `ACTIVE`; the transition sets the stage to `OFFER_EXTENDED`. From there a `RecruitingOfficer` either records acceptance, moving the candidate to `OFFER_ACCEPTED`, or records a decline, moving it to `OFFER_DECLINED`.

### 3.2. The Assessment Fork: Scoring, Superseding, Invalidating, and Re-engaging

A `SelectionAssessment` is a child of its `RecruitingCandidate` and begins in `SCHEDULED`. Scoring it is the joint work of two state machines: the assessment moves from `SCHEDULED` to `SCORED` and, in the same operation, its owning candidate moves from `ASSESSMENT_PENDING` to `ASSESSED`. A `RecruitingOfficer` supplies `raw_score`, an integer from 0 to 100 inclusive, and `competency_scores`, a map that must carry *exactly* the four keys `MARKET_KNOWLEDGE`, `RELATIONSHIP`, `RESILIENCE`, and `ETHICS`, each an integer from 0 to 10. A map missing a key, or carrying a fifth key, is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_INCOMPLETE_SCORES`, and a `raw_score` outside 0 to 100 is a malformed field. If the owning candidate has meanwhile left `ASSESSMENT_PENDING`, the candidate-side advance is skipped and only the assessment is scored.

The `weighted_score` is then computed and the three response-visible fields `weighted_score`, `outcome`, and `locked_weights` are written on the assessment, together with `scored_date` set to the clock. The `raw_score` is stored on the record but never enters the weighted score. The arithmetic is pinned, and the order matters more than usual here. First, the instrument version selects the weight table, and this selection is evaluated before anything else. Under `V1` the weights are `MARKET_KNOWLEDGE` 0.175, `RELATIONSHIP` 0.275, `RESILIENCE` 0.25, and `ETHICS` 0.30. Under `V2` they are `MARKET_KNOWLEDGE` 0.30, `RELATIONSHIP` 0.15, `RESILIENCE` 0.20, and `ETHICS` 0.35. Second, the competency average is formed as the sum, over the four competency keys, of that key's sub-score multiplied by its weight from the selected table. Third, the weighted component is that average multiplied by 0.70, rounded half up to two decimal places. Fourth, the raw component is the `ETHICS` sub-score multiplied by 0.305, rounded half up to two decimal places. Fifth, the `weighted_score` is the sum of those two already-rounded components, itself rounded half up to two decimal places. The two intermediate products are rounded *before* they are added; adding first and rounding afterwards is a different, incorrect reading that shifts results by a cent — for competency values of 9, 9, 8, and 9 under the `V1` table the pinned order yields a `weighted_score` of 8.88, while the sum-then-round order yields 8.87. The `locked_weights` field records the weight table actually applied, frozen at scoring time, so a later change to what the instrument version means cannot retroactively move a scored result.

The `outcome` is derived from the `weighted_score` against fixed cut scores: `PASS` when the `weighted_score` is at least 8.00, `CONDITIONAL` when it is at least 5.50 and strictly below 8.00, and `FAIL` when it is strictly below 5.50. The instrument version is genuinely discriminating, not decorative: the same competency profile of 4, 10, 10, and 8 yields a `weighted_score` of 8.29 and a `PASS` under `V1`, but a `weighted_score` of 7.69 and a `CONDITIONAL` under `V2`.

Three forks lead out of scoring, and they are not interchangeable. A `RecruitingOfficer` may **supersede** a scored assessment; this is legal only while the assessment is `SCORED` and the candidate is `ASSESSED`, and it moves the assessment to `SUPERSEDED` and pushes the candidate *back* to `ASSESSMENT_PENDING`, stamping `entered_stage_at`. A superseded assessment therefore does not end the candidate's assessment phase; a fresh `SCHEDULED` assessment must be produced before the candidate can reach `INTERVIEW_READY` again. Separately, a `RecruitingOfficer` may **invalidate** a scheduled assessment; this is legal only while the assessment is `SCHEDULED`, moves the assessment to `INVALIDATED`, and returns the candidate to `SCREENED`. The third fork is a cascade rather than a direct call: when the candidate is rejected, every one of its assessments currently in `SCHEDULED` or `SCORED` is set to `INVALIDATED` by the system, and the candidate's `ASSESSED` station is never re-entered. Rejection is the join point that closes the fork permanently.

A declined or rejected candidate is not necessarily lost. A `RecruitingOfficer` may re-engage a candidate whose stage is `OFFER_DECLINED` or `REJECTED`. Re-engaging sets the stage to `REENGAGED`, increments `reengagement_count` by one, and supersedes — not invalidates — every one of the candidate's assessments currently in `SCHEDULED` or `SCORED`. From `REENGAGED` the candidate re-enters the pipeline at screening, reusing the `SOURCED` to `SCREENED` shape, and the cycle may repeat as many times as the agency wishes. A `DistrictDirector` may also reject a candidate from any stage that is not already terminal, moving it to `REJECTED`; the terminal pipeline stages are `CONTRACTED`, `REJECTED`, and `OFFER_DECLINED`.
### 3.3. Contracting an Accepted Candidate and the Training Allowance Ledger

Before a candidate can be contracted, the representative's own record must exist. A `ManagingDirector` creates an `Advisor` with the representative's `full_name`, an agency `email` that is unique per advisor across the whole population, the `district_office_id` of the office the advisor joins, a `rank` of `REP`, `DISTRICT_DIRECTOR`, or `MANAGING_DIRECTOR` that drives override entitlement, and an optional `sponsoring_advisor_id` naming the sponsor whose downline the advisor joins — which defaults to null, as it does for a home-office hire. The new advisor starts at `NOT_CONTRACTED` with all three licensing dimensions at `NONE`, `team_production_cents` and `chargeback_balance_cents` at zero, `orphan_pool_access` false, and `terminated_at` null. If the supplied `sponsoring_advisor_id` would make the advisor's sponsoring chain reach itself, the creation is refused with `ERR_SPONSORING_CYCLE_DETECTED`. An advisor's `employment_contract` is created separately, and it is that action which contracts them.

An accepted candidate becomes a contracted representative through a single `ManagingDirector` action that writes an `EmploymentContract`. The payload names the `advisor_id` of the representative being contracted, the `candidate_id` of the pipeline entry that produced the contract, the `contract_type` — one of `FULL_TIME`, `PART_TIME`, or `INTERN` — the `start_date` the contract takes effect, the `training_allowance_total_cents` that budgets the training allowance, the `validation_requirement_cents` of production the contract must reach, the `training_allowance_mode` that selects the draw variant (`FLAT_MONTHLY` or `PRODUCTION_DRAW`), and the `expected_candidate_version` that pins the pipeline entry's version as §2.6 describes. The candidate must be at stage `OFFER_ACCEPTED` and must not already have an employment contract, and the advisor must be at `NOT_CONTRACTED`; a contract request that fails either the state check or the version pin is refused accordingly, with a stale `expected_candidate_version` producing `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at status 412 ahead of the state diagnosis.

A contract begins life at `ACTIVE_TRAINING`, mirroring the advisor, and its `contract_status` moves with the advisor through `ACTIVE_TRAINING`, `VALIDATING`, `AT_RISK`, `VALIDATED`, and `TERMINATED`; a contract that has not yet been activated sits at `DRAFT`, which is the state a contract would carry before its effective start. The effects are wide but deliberately narrow in one direction. The new contract starts at `contract_status` `ACTIVE_TRAINING`, mirroring the advisor, and the advisor's own `contract_status` moves to `ACTIVE_TRAINING` with `validation_posture` set to `VALIDATING`, and `orphan_pool_access` forced to false. The pipeline entry advances to `CONTRACTED`, its `entered_stage_at` is stamped with the clock, and its `version` is incremented by one — which is precisely why any other pending contract request pinned to the candidate's previous version will now be refused. The contract itself carries `end_date` null until termination, and `training_allowance_paid_cents` starting at zero.

What the contract does *not* do is equally binding. No `ProductLicense`, no `SecuritiesRegistration`, and no `CarrierAppointment` rows are created here. The newly contracted advisor begins with all three licensing dimensions at `NONE` — `insurance_license_status` `NONE`, `securities_registration_status` `NONE`, `appointment_status` `NONE` — and must acquire each dimension through the separate licensing workflows of §3.5. Contracting is the root of the first great cascade in this system: this one transition drives the advisor's `contract_status`, the advisor's `validation_posture`, the training-allowance ledger described below, and the dictated-tier recomputation that follows from posture.

A contract's `training_allowance_paid_cents` is the amount of allowance already drawn, capped at `training_allowance_total_cents`. An allowance instalment is drawn by a `ManagingDirector`-only capability that names the target contract and draws one window's instalment under the contract's mode. The draw logic is a threshold computation with a pinned evaluation order, and the order is the point: the product requirement is tested *before* the mode is selected, so a contract that has already met its requirement draws zero under either mode.

The requirement test compares the advisor's credited production against a fraction of the contract's validation requirement. The threshold is the contract's `validation_requirement_cents` multiplied by 0.35, rounded half up to the nearest whole cent; that is 35 percent of the requirement, so a 300000-cent requirement sets the threshold at 105000 cents. The production credited toward it is the sum of `gross_commission_cents` over the advisor's `PRIMARY`, `SPLIT_LEAD`, and `SPLIT_SUPPORT` commission credits whose `status` is `ACCRUED` or `PAID` — `CHARGED_BACK` credits do not count toward the training threshold, and neither do override, training-allowance, or renewal rows. When that sum reaches or exceeds the threshold, the product requirement is met.

Given the test result and the mode, the per-window draw is decided in this order. If the contract's `contract_status` is `TERMINATED`, the draw is zero. Otherwise, if the product requirement is met, the draw is zero: the allowance *pauses*, it does not repay, and no clawback is taken from an advisor who has begun producing. Otherwise, if `training_allowance_mode` is `FLAT_MONTHLY`, the draw is a flat 200000 cents per window regardless of how large or small the requirement is, continuing until the allowance total is exhausted. Otherwise — that is, under `PRODUCTION_DRAW` — the draw is the contract's `validation_requirement_cents` multiplied by 0.25 and rounded half up to the nearest cent, which is 75000 cents for a 300000-cent requirement. The two modes are genuinely different products: the flat mode draws 200000 cents per window and the production-draw mode draws 75000 cents for the same contract figures.

After the per-window amount is determined, the contract's `training_allowance_paid_cents` becomes the smaller of `training_allowance_total_cents` and the sum of its prior value and the per-window draw. A draw that would push `training_allowance_paid_cents` beyond `training_allowance_total_cents` is refused with `ERR_ALLOWANCE_CAP_EXCEEDED` at the payment-required status rather than being silently truncated. Each successful draw also creates a commission credit row of type `TRAINING_ALLOWANCE` for the advisor, which is an allowance ledger entry and is excluded from production attainment and from override accrual. The same computation additionally derives a recruiter override amount, `recruiter_override_cents`, equal to the contract's `validation_requirement_cents` multiplied by 0.05 and rounded half up to the nearest cent.

### 3.4. Validation Windows, Production Attainment, and the Contract Lifecycle

Validation is measured window by window. A `ManagingDirector` opens a `ValidationPeriod` against a contract by supplying the `contract_id`, the `period_number` (a sequential window number starting at one), the `start_date` and `end_date` bounding a half-open measurement window, the `required_production_cents` the window demands, and the `expected_contract_version` pinning the contract's version exactly as §2.6 describes; a stale pin yields `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at status 412 before the state path is consulted. The contract must be in `ACTIVE_TRAINING` or `VALIDATING` when the window is opened. Opening the first window moves the contract to `VALIDATING`, and moves its advisor's `contract_status` and `validation_posture` to `VALIDATING` in the same breath; opening a window while the contract is already `VALIDATING` is a legitimate self-loop that leaves the contract's state alone and merely grows the window set. Either way the new `ValidationPeriod` starts at `OPEN`, the contract's `version` is incremented by one, and the window carries `achieved_production_cents` and `extension_count` at zero and `extended_end_date` and `evaluated_at` null.

The window's bounds are validated before it is created. The `start_date` must be strictly before the `end_date`, and the window must lie wholly within the contract's live span; a violation is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION_WINDOW_INVALID`. The window must not overlap an existing `OPEN` or `EXTENDED` window of the same contract, and a violation is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Because the comparison is on half-open intervals, two windows that share only a boundary instant are adjacent, not overlapping, and are permitted — a window ending at an instant and the next beginning at that same instant is a legal pair, on whichever path the contract currently sits.

Attainment is recomputed when the window is evaluated, and the recomputation has a precise scope. The credited set is every commission credit whose `advisor_id` is the window's advisor, whose `credit_type` is `PRIMARY`, `SPLIT_LEAD`, `SPLIT_SUPPORT`, or `RENEWAL`, whose `status` is `ACCRUED`, `PAID`, or `CHARGED_BACK`, and whose `created_at` falls inside the half-open window — at or after the window's `start_date` and strictly before its `end_date`. `CHARGED_BACK` credits count here, unlike in the training-allowance test. The window's `achieved_production_cents` is the sum of `gross_commission_cents` over that set, in integer cents with no rounding. Two exclusions are absolute: `OVERRIDE_DO`, `OVERRIDE_MD`, and `TRAINING_ALLOWANCE` rows never count toward attainment. A credit whose `created_at` equals the window's `start_date` is counted and one whose `created_at` equals the window's `end_date` is not; where several credits must be processed in order, the order is `created_at` ascending with `id` ascending as the final deterministic tie-break.

Evaluating an `OPEN` window whose attainment reaches or exceeds its `required_production_cents` passes it: the window's `status` becomes `PASSED`, its `evaluated_at` is stamped with the clock, and the contract validates outright — contract `contract_status` becomes `VALIDATED`, and the advisor's `contract_status` and `validation_posture` both become `VALIDATED`. This is the moment the advisor becomes economically whole. `orphan_pool_access` becomes true, so the advisor may henceforth receive reassigned orphan policyholders; `team_production_cents` is recomputed; and a two-level cascade fires. At depth one, every commission credit of that advisor still in `ACCRUED` has its `gross_commission_cents` and `net_commission_cents` recomputed as §3.6 describes, now that the post-validation branch of the allocation rule applies. At depth two, the sponsoring advisor's and the district office's override income are recomputed as §3.6 describes.

Evaluating an `OPEN` window whose attainment falls short of its requirement is a failure, but only while the window still has extension headroom. When the window's `extension_count` is below the maximum of two, a missed evaluation sets the window to `FAILED`, increments its `extension_count` by one, stamps `evaluated_at` with the clock, and demotes the whole contract: the contract's `contract_status` and the advisor's `contract_status` both become `AT_RISK`, and the advisor's `validation_posture` follows to `AT_RISK`. A window whose `extension_count` has already reached the maximum of two cannot be failed through this path, because the transition is not declared from that condition.

An `OPEN` window that has missed its requirement may instead be extended rather than failed. A `ManagingDirector` supplies a `new_end_date`, and the window must be `OPEN`, must have drawn fewer than the maximum of two extensions, and must currently be short of its requirement. Extending sets `extended_end_date` to the supplied `new_end_date`, widens `end_date` to that same instant, increments `extension_count` by one, and sets the window's `status` to `EXTENDED`. Attempting to extend a window whose `extension_count` has reached the maximum of two is refused with `ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_REACHED`. Once a window is `EXTENDED`, evaluating it recomputes attainment over the widened window and returns the window to `OPEN` when it is still short of its requirement; when the widened window's attainment has reached the requirement, the window passes instead. Evaluation of a window that is neither `OPEN` nor `EXTENDED` — and, for a contract with no `OPEN` window in the sense §3 expects — is refused with `ERR_NO_OPEN_VALIDATION_WINDOW`.

The contract cycle closes through remediation. An `AT_RISK` advisor returns to `ACTIVE_TRAINING` when a `DistrictDirector` — or a `ManagingDirector` — remediates the record. Remediation is permitted only when the advisor's `contract_status` is `AT_RISK`, the advisor's `insurance_license_status` is `ACTIVE`, the advisor's `appointment_status` is `APPROVED`, and the advisor has an employment contract whose `contract_status` is likewise `AT_RISK`; satisfying all four sets the contract and the advisor to `ACTIVE_TRAINING` and the advisor's `validation_posture` to `VALIDATING`, ready for the next window to open.

One ordering hazard sits inside the licensing dimension and belongs here because it demotes the contract. Approving a carrier appointment whose `requires_license_id` points at a `ProductLicense` whose `expires_at` the clock has already reached demotes an `ACTIVE_TRAINING` advisor to `AT_RISK`, with `validation_posture` following to `AT_RISK`. This is deliberate: the licence is locked to the appointment at activation time, so a licence that expired between its activation and the appointment's approval still turns the approval into a demotion event. The demotion is suppressed while the advisor's `licensing_locked` flag is true.

### 3.5. Insurance Licensing, Securities Registration, and Appointment Sequencing

The three licensing dimensions are owned by a `LicensingCoordinator` and evolve independently of the contract lifecycle, though the contract reacts to them.

An insurance licence is recorded by a `LicensingCoordinator` as a `ProductLicense` naming the `advisor_id` it belongs to, the `product_line` it covers — `LIFE`, `ANNUITY`, `DISABILITY`, or `LONG_TERM_CARE` — the state `license_number`, the `effective_date` the licence takes effect, the `expires_at` instant it lapses, and an `appointment_required` flag that defaults to true and records whether a carrier appointment must precede writing business under it. A new licence is created in status `PENDING` and the advisor's `insurance_license_status` becomes `PENDING`. The interval rules of §2.8 apply: a licence for the same advisor and `product_line` whose half-open `[effective_date, expires_at)` interval overlaps an existing one is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`, and a shared boundary instant is not an overlap. The `license_number` is also a natural key, unique per state and `product_line`, so a reuse is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY`.

A pending licence is activated by a `LicensingCoordinator`. Activation is permitted only while the licence is `PENDING` and the clock is strictly before its `expires_at`; a licence whose `expires_at` the clock has already reached is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_ACTIVE`. Activation sets the licence to `ACTIVE`, sets the advisor's `insurance_license_status` to `ACTIVE`, and cascades to the advisor's appointments: every `CarrierAppointment` of that advisor whose `appointment_status` is `REQUESTED` and whose `requires_license_id` is this licence is re-evaluated against the appointment sequencing gate below, since the licence it was waiting on has just become available.

A licence may lapse on its own. When the logical clock reaches a licence's `expires_at`, the system moves the licence to `LAPSED` and the advisor's `insurance_license_status` to `LAPSED`, and every policy of that advisor still in status `ISSUED` becomes unable to reach `ACTIVE`, because the issuance gate requires an active licence. A lapsed licence is restorable by a `LicensingCoordinator` who supplies a fresh `expires_at` strictly after the clock; a renewal sets the licence's `expires_at` to that value, returns the licence to `ACTIVE`, and returns the advisor's `insurance_license_status` to `ACTIVE`.

A securities registration is recorded in the same shape. A `LicensingCoordinator` creates a `SecuritiesRegistration` naming the `advisor_id`, the `registration_type` — `SERIES_6`, `SERIES_7`, `SERIES_63`, `SERIES_65`, or `SERIES_66` — the `crd_number`, the `effective_date`, the `expires_at` instant, and an optional `prerequisite_registration_id` that defaults to null. A new registration starts `PENDING` and sets the advisor's `securities_registration_status` to `PENDING`. The interval rule for registrations is the strict one: a new registration for the same advisor and `registration_type` whose *closed* `[effective_date, expires_at]` interval shares any instant with an existing one, including a single boundary instant, is refused with `ERR_REGISTRATION_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`. The `crd_number` is unique per `registration_type`, so a reuse is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY`.

Activating a registration is where sequencing bites, and the gate has two alternative forms. A registration whose `prerequisite_registration_id` is null may reach `ACTIVE` only when the clock is strictly before its own `expires_at`. A registration whose `prerequisite_registration_id` is set may reach `ACTIVE` only when that referenced registration's `status` is itself `ACTIVE`. Attempting to activate a registration before its prerequisite has reached `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_PREREQUISITE_REGISTRATION_NOT_ACTIVE`. This is the shape of a real registration ladder: `SERIES_66` may be registered directly off an active insurance licence with no prerequisite, while `SERIES_63` and `SERIES_65` require an already-active prerequisite registration and are refused until it exists. Successful activation sets the registration to `ACTIVE`, recomputes the advisor's `securities_registration_status`, and cascades: every dependent registration of that advisor whose `prerequisite_registration_id` is this registration is re-evaluated, since the gate that was blocking it may now be open. A registration is suspended rather than lapsed when its time runs out — when the clock reaches a registration's `expires_at` while it is `ACTIVE`, the system moves it to `SUSPENDED` and the advisor's `securities_registration_status` to `SUSPENDED`, and every policy of that advisor whose `product_line` is `ANNUITY` and whose status is `ISSUED` becomes unable to reach `ACTIVE`, because annuity business requires an active registration.

A carrier appointment is requested by a `LicensingCoordinator` with the `advisor_id`, the `requires_license_id` naming the licence the appointment depends on, the `carrier_name` granting it, the `state_code` it covers, and the `requested_date`. The referenced licence must exist and must belong to that advisor. The new appointment starts at `appointment_status` `REQUESTED`, with `appointment_date` null, and the advisor's `appointment_status` becomes `REQUESTED`. Approving a requested appointment re-checks the licence gate at approval time, not merely at request time: approval requires the appointment to be `REQUESTED`, requires the referenced licence to be in status `ACTIVE`, and requires the clock to be strictly before that licence's `expires_at`. When the licence has gone stale, the approval is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_ACTIVE`, and — as §3.4 notes — the advisor's contract is demoted from `ACTIVE_TRAINING` to `AT_RISK` in the same operation. When the gate passes, the appointment becomes `APPROVED`, its `appointment_date` is stamped with the clock, and the advisor's `appointment_status` becomes `APPROVED`. An appointment may instead be rejected, which requires it to be `REQUESTED` and moves it to `REJECTED`; the advisor's `appointment_status` returns to `NONE` when no other appointment of that advisor remains `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`.

The three gates compose into one sequencing rule, and its evaluation order is pinned: the licence or registration gate is evaluated first, then the advisor's parallel-dimension aggregate is recomputed, and only then is the advisor's contract-status demotion check run. Reversing the first and third steps changes which error surfaces for a request that is both unlicensed and demoted, so the order is not cosmetic.

### 3.6. Writing Business: Commission Allocation, Overrides, and Chargebacks

A policy is written by a `ManagingDirector` supplying the `policy_number`, the `writing_advisor_id` of the advisor of record, the `carrier_name`, the `product_line`, the `annual_premium_cents`, the `issue_date`, and an `is_advanced_commission` flag that defaults to true and records whether first-year commission was advanced at issue. The `policy_number` is a natural key unique across all policies, so a reuse is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NATURAL_KEY`. The new policy starts at status `ISSUED` with `lapse_date` and `reinstatement_date` null, `months_inforce` at zero, and `orphaned` false. Writing the policy produces its commission credits at once, accrues the overrides along the sponsoring chain, and recomputes `team_production_cents`.

A policy cannot be put in force without the licensing furniture around it. Activating an `ISSUED` policy requires that the writing advisor's `insurance_license_status` be `ACTIVE`, that the advisor's `appointment_status` be `APPROVED`, and that the advisor's `contract_status` not be `TERMINATED`. For `ANNUITY` business specifically, the writing advisor's `securities_registration_status` must additionally be `ACTIVE`, because a variable annuity is a dual-registered product; a registration that has been suspended or terminated does not satisfy this. The first failing gate produces its own error: `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_APPROVED`, or `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_ACTIVE` as applicable, and an operation targeting a terminated advisor produces `ERR_TERMINATED_ADVISOR`. Activation sets the policy to `ACTIVE` and its `months_inforce` to zero. A separate fiscal block sits in front of new business: while the writing advisor's `chargeback_balance_cents` exceeds `CHARGEBACK_BLOCK_CENTS`, which is 500000 cents, both writing a new policy and submitting a joint work case are refused with `ERR_CHARGEBACK_BALANCE_BLOCKS_NEW_BUSINESS` at the payment-required status.

Commission allocation is a waterfall over a policy's credits. The policy's case-level gross commission, `case_gross_commission_cents`, is the policy's `annual_premium_cents` multiplied by the first-year commission rate of 0.40, rounded half up to the nearest cent. That value is stored identically on every credit row of the policy, and it is the basis of the conservation invariant of §2.7.

When the policy has no joint work case in status `SUBMITTED` — whether none was ever opened or the one opened was `VOIDED` — the allocation is simple: a single `PRIMARY` credit goes to the policy's `writing_advisor_id` with `credit_pct` of 1.00 and `gross_commission_cents` equal to the case gross. When the policy *does* have a joint case in `SUBMITTED`, the allocation splits, and the split is defined asymmetrically. With a lead share fraction `split_pct_lead` of s, the lead's raw share is the case gross multiplied by s. The `SPLIT_LEAD` credit to the joint case's `lead_advisor_id` takes the lead's raw share *rounded half up to the nearest cent*. The `SPLIT_SUPPORT` credit to the case's `support_advisor_id` takes the remainder — the case gross minus that rounded lead amount — and is never rounded independently. The one-cent residual, which may be either plus or minus one cent, lands on the support advisor by construction. The `PRIMARY` credit is simultaneously voided, set to `gross_commission_cents` zero and `credit_pct` 0.00. The rounding order here is load-bearing: because the support share is defined as the remainder rather than as its own rounded product, the invariant holds exactly on every path; rounding both shares independently can overshoot the case gross by a cent and break it. Voiding a submitted joint case reverses this precisely: the `SPLIT_LEAD` and `SPLIT_SUPPORT` credits are set to `VOIDED`, and the `PRIMARY` credit is restored to `credit_pct` 1.00 with `gross_commission_cents` recomputed to the case gross.

Override income accrues up the sponsoring chain, and its computation is a full recomputation rather than an increment. The rate an advisor's rank commands on the production of descendants is 0.03 when the advisor's `rank` is `DISTRICT_DIRECTOR` and 0.02 when it is `MANAGING_DIRECTOR` — the district-office override rate and the managing-director override rate respectively — and an advisor whose `rank` is `REP` accrues no override on its own behalf.

Four steps are evaluated in a pinned order. First, the ancestor's own posture gate: when the ancestor's `contract_status` is `TERMINATED`, or when the ancestor's `validation_posture` is `AT_RISK` or `FAILED_POSTURE`, the ancestor's `override_income_cents` is zero outright. Second, the descendant eligibility filter: a descendant contributes to an ancestor only when the descendant is a different advisor whose `sponsoring_advisor_id` chain reaches the ancestor and whose *own* `contract_status` is `VALIDATED`; a descendant passes nothing up the chain until its own contract has validated. Third, per-credit rank accrual: for every accruing descendant, each of the descendant's qualifying credits contributes an accrual equal to that credit's gross multiplied by the ancestor's rank rate, rounded half up to the nearest cent, and the per-credit accruals are then summed. Fourth, and last, the office cap is applied once to the summed accrual: the result is the smaller of that sum and the ancestor's district office's `override_cap_cents`, which defaults to 300000 cents when the office was created without a stated cap. The cap is applied *after* summing and not before; capping each accrual first would let a chain of individually-small credits exceed the cap, so the sum-then-cap order is pinned. The office used for the cap is the `DistrictOffice` whose `id` equals the ancestor's `district_office_id`.

Because descendant eligibility is a *contract* dimension and not a posture dimension, the consequences are specific: neither a policy lapse nor a persistency demotion ever retroactively destroys an already-accrued override. Only contract validation on the descendant's part, termination, or a credit leaving the live set changes an ancestor's accrual — and because the accrual is fully recomputed on every trigger, a descendant's credit leaving the live set removes its contribution exactly, with no residue. The triggers that force the recomputation are writing a policy, submitting or voiding a joint case, terminating an advisor, assigning an orphan policy, and evaluating a validation window on pass.

Team production rolls up the same chain but over a different credit set. An advisor's `team_production_cents` is the sum of `gross_commission_cents` over the `PRIMARY`, `SPLIT_LEAD`, and `SPLIT_SUPPORT` credits whose `status` is not `VOIDED`, for that advisor and for every advisor whose sponsoring chain reaches that advisor. It excludes override, training-allowance, and renewal rows, and it excludes voided credits. The district office's `override_paid_cents` is the sum of `override_income_cents` over every advisor whose `district_office_id` is that office.

A joint work case is how two representatives share a case. It is created by a `FinancialRep` with the `policy_id` being worked jointly, the `lead_advisor_id` and `support_advisor_id`, and the `split_pct_lead` fraction; it also requires the `expected_policy_version` pin of §2.6, and a stale pin yields `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at status 412 ahead of the state path. The new case starts at status `OPEN` with `submitted_at` null, and the policy's `version` is incremented by one. Two structural rules guard it: the lead and support advisors must be different advisors, and a case whose `lead_advisor_id` equals its `support_advisor_id` is refused with `ERR_SELF_JOINT_CASE`; and the lead share must be strictly greater than 0 and at most 1, so a `split_pct_lead` outside that range is refused with `ERR_SPLIT_PCT_INVALID`. A second case may not be opened on a policy that already carries a case in `OPEN` or `SUBMITTED`, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_JOINT_CASE_OPEN_EXISTS`.

Submitting a joint case is a `FinancialRep` action, and it is scoped to the acting advisor: `X-User-ID` must equal the case's `lead_advisor_id`. The case must be `OPEN`; both the lead's and the support's `validation_posture` must be `VALIDATING` or `VALIDATED`, so an at-risk or failed-posture advisor on either side blocks the submission with `ERR_JOINT_WORK_POSTURE_INELIGIBLE` and the chargeback-balance block described above may also apply; the case's policy must be `ISSUED` or `ACTIVE`; and the policy must have no *other* joint work case in status `OPEN` or `SUBMITTED`. The submission sets the case to `SUBMITTED` and stamps `submitted_at` with the clock, then re-splits the policy's credits exactly as the waterfall above describes, recomputes both advisors' `team_production_cents`, cascades at depth two to the sponsoring advisors' `override_income_cents`, and cascades at depth three to the district office's override income for that measurement. Submitting a joint case is the root of the third great cascade in this system. A `ManagingDirector` closes a submitted case, moving it to `CLOSED`, and a closed case admits nothing further.

Voiding is available on two different terms depending on who is doing it and from which status. From `OPEN`, a `FinancialRep` who is the case's lead advisor may void it: the case becomes `VOIDED`, the policy's split credits are set to `VOIDED`, the `PRIMARY` credit is restored to `credit_pct` 1.00, and the involved advisors' `team_production_cents` are recomputed. From `SUBMITTED`, voiding is a shared power held by the `FinancialRep` lead and by a `ManagingDirector`, and it carries two extra guard conditions: the clock must be no more than `JOINT_VOID_WINDOW_DAYS` past the case's `submitted_at`, and no commission credit of the policy may have status `RECOVERED`. When those hold, the case becomes `VOIDED`, the split credits are voided and the `PRIMARY` credit is restored to `credit_pct` 1.00 with its `gross_commission_cents` recomputed, the involved advisors' team production is recomputed, and the sponsoring advisors' override income is recomputed at depth two. Once the void window has closed, or once any credit has been recovered, a submitted case may only go to `CLOSED`. A voided case is not dead, however: a `FinancialRep` may reopen it, and reopening requires the case to be `VOIDED` and its policy to be `ISSUED` or `ACTIVE`; reopening sets the case to `OPEN`, clears `submitted_at` to null, and leaves credit allocation free to move again on a later submission. Credit allocation in this system is therefore not monotone.

A lapse is the event that turns commission into a chargeback, and its arithmetic has three disjoint completed-month bands. The completed-month count follows §2.8's rule. For a surrender, the assessed amount is the credit's full `gross_commission_cents`, regardless of how many months the policy was in force. For a lapse, when the completed-month count is at most 11, the assessed amount is the credit's gross multiplied by one minus the count divided by 24, rounded half up to the nearest cent — that is, the unearned portion on a straight-line 24-month schedule, so a credit on a policy lapsing in its first month is assessed 23/24 of its gross. When the completed-month count is exactly 12, the assessed amount is the credit's gross multiplied by 0.50, rounded half up to the nearest cent. When the completed-month count is 13 or more, the assessed amount is the credit's gross multiplied by 0.50, rounded half up to the nearest cent, but only when the policy's `is_advanced_commission` flag is true; when it is false, the assessed amount is zero. In every case the assessed amount is finally capped at the credit's own `gross_commission_cents`, so no assessment ever exceeds the credit. Override rows are assessed by exactly this same formula, and an override row's own reassessment cascades onward to the next ancestor up the chain.

When a policy lapses or is surrendered, the cascade reaches every credit of that policy. A credit currently `PAID` whose assessment yields a positive amount moves to `CHARGED_BACK` with its `chargeback_assessed_cents` set to the assessed amount and its `chargeback_reason` set to `LAPSE_WITHIN_12MO` for a lapse or `SURRENDER` for a surrender, and the advisor's `chargeback_balance_cents` is increased by the assessed amount less what has already been recovered. The credit's `advanced_paid_cents` records the amount advanced to the advisor at issue and is what a recoverable chargeback is measured against; `net_commission_cents` is the credit's payable amount after chargeback netting. The `chargeback_reason` values this system records are `LAPSE_WITHIN_12MO`, `SURRENDER`, `REPLACEMENT`, and `TERMINATION`, of which the lapse and surrender paths described here write the first two. A credit's `status` moves among `ACCRUED`, `PAID`, `CHARGED_BACK`, `RECOVERED`, and `VOIDED`, and its `credit_type` among `PRIMARY`, `SPLIT_LEAD`, `SPLIT_SUPPORT`, `OVERRIDE_DO`, `OVERRIDE_MD`, `TRAINING_ALLOWANCE`, and `RENEWAL`; `credit_pct` is the fraction of the case gross credited to this advisor. A credit currently `ACCRUED` — never paid — moves to `CHARGED_BACK` on a lapse with `chargeback_reason` `LAPSE_WITHIN_12MO` and the advisor's balance recomputed. From a charged-back row, the deepest cascade fires: where the charged-back credit is an `OVERRIDE_DO` or `OVERRIDE_MD` row, the dependent override row of the sponsoring advisor is recomputed and, if it was `PAID`, is itself set to `CHARGED_BACK`. A lapse within twelve completed months of issue is the core chargeback trigger; a policy issued at least thirteen completed months earlier is charged back only when its `is_advanced_commission` flag is true, and only for the half-share the band specifies.

Charged-back money is recovered through a `ManagingDirector` capability that names an `amount_cents`. Recovery requires the credit to be `CHARGED_BACK` and the amount to be strictly greater than zero and at most the credit's `chargeback_assessed_cents` minus its `recovered_cents`; an amount beyond that remainder is refused with `ERR_RECOVERY_EXCEEDS_ASSESSED`. A successful recovery adds the amount to `recovered_cents` and subtracts it from the advisor's `chargeback_balance_cents`, and when `recovered_cents` has come to equal `chargeback_assessed_cents` the credit's status becomes `RECOVERED`. Paying is how an accrued credit becomes money, and it is where the posture gate bites hardest. A `ManagingDirector` pays a credit only when the credit's status is `ACCRUED` and its advisor's `validation_posture` is `VALIDATING` or `VALIDATED`; an advisor whose posture is `AT_RISK` or `FAILED_POSTURE` is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION_POSTURE_BLOCKS_PAYOUT`, and that refusal follows the precedence of §2.10, in which posture is evaluated before the fiscal block. A successful payment sets the credit's status to `PAID` and its `advanced_paid_cents` to the credit's `net_commission_cents`. Separately, an `ACCRUED` credit that was never paid may be voided, which requires its `recovered_cents` to be zero; voiding sets it to `VOIDED`, sets its `gross_commission_cents` to zero, and recomputes the advisor's `team_production_cents`.

Reinstatement is the compensating action for a lapse, and it does not refund what has been recovered. A `ManagingDirector` may reinstate a `LAPSED` policy when the clock is less than `REINSTATEMENT_WINDOW_DAYS` — 90 days — past the policy's `lapse_date`; a request beyond that window is refused with `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Reinstatement returns the policy to `ACTIVE`, sets its `reinstatement_date` to the clock, and clears `lapse_date` to null. Every credit of that policy that is currently `CHARGED_BACK` reverts to `PAID`, with `chargeback_assessed_cents` reset to zero and `chargeback_reason` reset to null, while `recovered_cents` is left **unchanged**. The advisor's `chargeback_balance_cents` is recomputed so that only the unrecovered remainder is subtracted — the recovered portion is never refunded — and the balance invariant of §2.7 therefore leaves the advisor in credit by exactly the recovered amount when a partly recovered chargeback is reversed.

A policy may be surrendered from either `ACTIVE` or `LAPSED`, and the two branches are not the same operation. Surrendering an `ACTIVE` policy requires only that the policy be `ACTIVE`; it sets the policy to `SURRENDERED` and runs the full chargeback assessment with reason `SURRENDER`, which assesses the credit's entire `gross_commission_cents` regardless of how many months the policy was in force. Surrendering a `LAPSED` policy requires only that the policy be `LAPSED`; its chargeback exposure was already assessed when the policy lapsed, so this branch sets the policy to `SURRENDERED` and writes no further assessment. What the two branches share is the terminal destination: a surrendered policy is terminated through a final `ManagingDirector` action that requires the policy to be `SURRENDERED`. Termination sets the policy to `TERMINATED`, and if the policy's `orphaned` flag is true and an `OrphanPolicyAssignment` for it is still `PENDING`, that assignment is set to `REJECTED` with its `rejection_reason` recorded as `POLICY_TERMINATED`.

### 3.7. Persistency Measurement and Renewal Income

Renewal income is earned on the persistence of an advisor's book, and it is measured in windows the agency declares rather than in windows the calendar imposes. A `ManagingDirector` opens a `PersistencyRecord` with the `advisor_id` being measured, a `measurement_period` label such as `2026-H1`, a `measurement_start` and `measurement_end` bounding a *closed* window, and the two counters `policies_issued` and `policies_persisted`. The record starts `PROVISIONAL`, with `persistency_pct`, `persistency_tier`, `renewal_rate_pct`, and `renewal_commission_cents` unwritten until finalization. The closed-window rule of §2.8 applies: two measurement windows of the same advisor must not share any instant, boundary instants included, and a violation is refused with `ERR_PERSISTENCY_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.

An advisor may contest a measurement. A `FinancialRep` disputes a provisional record, and the dispute is scoped to ownership: `X-User-ID` must equal the record's `advisor_id`. A successful dispute moves the record to `DISPUTED`. A `ManagingDirector` resolves the dispute, which returns the record to `PROVISIONAL`, where it may be re-measured before it is finalized. Both of these are cycle edges; a disputed record is not dead, and a provisional record is not final.

Finalization is the `ManagingDirector` action that turns the counters into money, and it is gated on the advisor's standing. A provisional record may be finalized only when the advisor's `contract_status` is `VALIDATED` and the advisor's `validation_posture` is `VALIDATED`. A disputed record may be finalized without re-resolution only when the advisor's `contract_status` is `VALIDATED`. Finalization computes four values in a fixed order. First, `persistency_pct` is one hundred times `policies_persisted` divided by `policies_issued`, rounded half up to two decimal places; when `policies_issued` is zero the percentage is 0.00. Second, the tier is selected against inclusive lower bounds, evaluated top down: a `persistency_pct` of at least 95.00 selects `TIER_1`; otherwise a `persistency_pct` of at least 90.00 selects `TIER_2`; otherwise a `persistency_pct` of at least 85.00 selects `TIER_3`; otherwise the tier is `INELIGIBLE`. Third, the renewal rate follows the tier: `TIER_1` selects a `renewal_rate_pct` of 0.050, `TIER_2` selects 0.035, `TIER_3` selects 0.020, and `INELIGIBLE` selects 0.000. Fourth, the renewal base is the sum of `annual_premium_cents` over the advisor's policies whose status is `ACTIVE` at the instant of the window's `measurement_end` within the closed window, and `renewal_commission_cents` is that base multiplied by the selected `renewal_rate_pct`, rounded half up to the nearest cent.

The accrual side effects are as specific as the arithmetic. Finalization writes `persistency_pct`, `persistency_tier`, `renewal_rate_pct`, and `renewal_commission_cents` onto the record and sets its `status` to `FINALIZED`; the advisor's `renewal_commission_cents` is increased by the record's `renewal_commission_cents`; and a `CommissionCredit` of type `RENEWAL` is created for the advisor with `gross_commission_cents` equal to the same renewal amount. Provisional and disputed records accrue nothing — only finalization pays.

The tier carries a consequence beyond money. When the finalized record's `persistency_tier` is `INELIGIBLE`, the advisor's contract is demoted: an advisor whose contract is `VALIDATED` moves to `AT_RISK`, its `validation_posture` moves to `AT_RISK`, and `orphan_pool_access` returns to false. In the same operation the advisor's `renewal_commission_cents` is recomputed — no renewal accrues at the `INELIGIBLE` tier — every commission credit of the advisor still in `ACCRUED` is recomputed without any change of attribution, and the advisor's `team_production_cents` is recomputed. An `INELIGIBLE` measurement is therefore not merely unpaid; it costs the advisor its validated standing, and therefore its override entitlement and its orphan-pool access, until remediation returns it.

### 3.8. Termination and Orphan Policy Reassignment

Termination is the terminal event for an advisor and the broadest cascade in the system. A `ManagingDirector` terminates an advisor whose `contract_status` is `ACTIVE_TRAINING`, `VALIDATING`, `AT_RISK`, or `VALIDATED`; a fresh advisor who has never been contracted may not be terminated through this path. Termination sets the advisor's `contract_status` to `TERMINATED` and its `validation_posture` to `FAILED_POSTURE`, forces `orphan_pool_access` to false, and stamps `terminated_at` with the clock.

The cascade that follows is deterministic and total. Every `EmploymentContract` of that advisor whose `contract_status` is `ACTIVE_TRAINING` or `VALIDATING` is set to `TERMINATED` with its `end_date` stamped with the clock, and every `ValidationPeriod` under those contracts whose status is `OPEN` or `EXTENDED` is set to `FAILED`. Every `ProductLicense` of that advisor whose status is not already `REVOKED` is set to `REVOKED`. Every `SecuritiesRegistration` of that advisor whose status is not already `TERMINATED` is set to `TERMINATED`. Every `CarrierAppointment` of that advisor whose status is not already `TERMINATED` is set to `TERMINATED`.

The policyholder book is then handled explicitly. For every `Policy` whose `writing_advisor_id` is the terminated advisor and whose status is `ISSUED` or `ACTIVE`, the policy's `orphaned` flag is set to true, and a new `OrphanPolicyAssignment` is created for that policy with status `PENDING`, `assignment_method` `ROUND_ROBIN`, and `from_advisor_id` set to the terminated advisor. The assignment's `to_advisor_id` is null and its `assigned_at` is null until the selector described below resolves it. A policy that was already `LAPSED`, `SURRENDERED`, or `TERMINATED` is not orphaned by this cascade.

Joint work and renewal money are then unwound. Every `JointWorkCase` of that advisor whose status is `OPEN` or `SUBMITTED` is set to `VOIDED`. Every `CommissionCredit` of that advisor with status `ACCRUED` and `credit_type` `RENEWAL` is set to `VOIDED`, because no renewal survives the representative. Every `CommissionCredit` whose `credit_type` is `OVERRIDE_DO` or `OVERRIDE_MD` and whose credit depends on a now-orphaned policy is recomputed and reassessed under the chargeback formula of §3.6. Finally the advisor's `chargeback_balance_cents` and `team_production_cents` are recomputed, and — because override accrual is a full recomputation over live credits — the removal of the terminated advisor's production removes its contribution to every ancestor's override at the next recompute, with no residual.

An orphaned policyholder is reassigned by a deterministic selector rather than by judgement. A `ManagingDirector` assigns a `PENDING` orphan assignment, and the assignment requires the policy's `orphaned` flag to still be true. The eligible pool is built from a precise filter: an eligible advisor is one whose `district_office_id` equals the writing office of the policy being reassigned; whose `contract_status` is `VALIDATING` or `VALIDATED`; whose `validation_posture` is `VALIDATING` or `VALIDATED`; whose `appointment_status` is `APPROVED`; and whose `id` differs from the assignment's `from_advisor_id`, so the departed advisor can never receive its own book back. The pool is then ranked by `team_production_cents` ascending, ties broken by `created_at` ascending, and remaining ties broken by `id` ascending as the final deterministic fallback. The first advisor after ranking is chosen. The assignment's `assignment_method` records the selector policy that was used, one of `ROUND_ROBIN`, `SENIORITY`, or `MANUAL`; the cascade that creates an assignment for an orphaned policy always creates it under `ROUND_ROBIN`, which is also the create-time default, while `SENIORITY` and `MANUAL` are alternative selector policies the assignment record can carry.

When the pool is non-empty, the assignment's `to_advisor_id` is set to the chosen advisor, the policy's `writing_advisor_id` is changed to that advisor, the policy's `orphaned` flag is cleared to false, the assignment's `status` becomes `ASSIGNED`, and its `assigned_at` is stamped with the clock. The receiving advisor's `team_production_cents` is recomputed, and at depth two the receiving advisor's sponsoring advisors' `override_income_cents` are recomputed. When the pool is empty, the operation does not silently no-op and never falls back to the terminated advisor: the assignment's `status` becomes `REJECTED`, its `rejection_reason` is recorded as `NO_ELIGIBLE_ADVISOR`, and the policy's `orphaned` flag remains true. This empty-pool rejection is the same `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ADVISOR` condition the error catalogue carries, reported at the unprocessable-entity status rather than as a success.

A rejected orphan assignment may be retried once circumstances change. A `ManagingDirector` reopens a `REJECTED` assignment when the policy's `orphaned` flag is still true; reopening sets the assignment's status back to `PENDING` and clears its `rejection_reason` to null, freeing it for a later assignment attempt once a new advisor has become eligible.
