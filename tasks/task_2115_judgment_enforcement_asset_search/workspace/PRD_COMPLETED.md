# Judgment Enforcement API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A judgment recovery firm buys or is retained on money judgments that courts have already entered but that nobody has actually collected. Winning in court is the easy half; the hard half is everything that follows — docketing the judgment with the right county recorder so the lien attaches, hunting down the bank account or the paycheck or the parcel that can satisfy it, getting a sheriff to serve a writ, answering the debtor's exemption claim, and keeping a running, defensible number for what is owed on any given day while statutory interest compounds against a moving payment history. Today that work lives in spreadsheets, calendar reminders, and the memory of whichever case manager last touched the file. When a payoff quote is wrong by a dollar, a settlement collapses. When a renewal deadline slips, a seven-figure judgment simply evaporates.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces that patchwork. The service is the system of record for eleven kinds of object — creditors, debtors, judgments, county filings, asset findings, enforcement actions, exemption claims, debtor examinations, settlement offers, installment agreements, and payments — and, more importantly, it is the system of record for the *relationships* between them. Recording a county filing changes what enforcement the firm may attempt. Verifying an asset changes the debtor's recoverability score. Accepting a settlement suspends every live levy without releasing the money it holds. Reversing a single payment rewinds the entire interest history back to the date the judgment was entered. Every one of those consequences is a server-side obligation, not a client-side convention.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and are worth stating up front. First, **the ledger is always replayed, never patched**: balances are recomputed from the original principal forward through the surviving payment history, so a correction can never leave a stale interest segment behind. Second, **the firm's own staff are segregated by duty**: the investigator who swears an asset is real may not be the officer who levies on it, and the officer who fights an exemption claim may not be the officer who decides it. Both commitments are enforced by the server, on every request, without exception.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Section 2 is the contract layer. Everything here applies to every endpoint unless a later section names an explicit exception. Collections are mounted flat at the root — there is no nesting in URL paths, and a child object is always discovered by filtering its own collection on the foreign key it carries.

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which they act. Exactly six roles exist, and their spellings are fixed: `CaseManager`, `AssetInvestigator`, `EnforcementOfficer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `Creditor`, and `Debtor`. A request that omits `X-User-Role`, or supplies a value that is not one of those six, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. A request whose role is `Creditor` or `Debtor` must also carry `X-User-ID`; omitting it draws the same `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` response. Once the caller is authenticated, the service checks the role against the endpoint's authorized list from Section 4; a role that does not appear there is turned away with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` and HTTP 403.

Four of the six roles are firm staff and their identities are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `CaseManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example as `writ_requested_by_user_id` on an enforcement action) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `AssetInvestigator`, whose identifier is stamped onto `verified_by_user_id` when a finding is verified; for `EnforcementOfficer`, whose identifier is stamped onto `writ_requested_by_user_id` at writ request and onto `opposed_by_user_id` when an exemption claim is opposed; and for `ComplianceOfficer`, whose identifier is stamped onto `ruled_by_user_id` when a claim is decided. None of those four identifiers is a foreign key, and none of them is ever resolved against a stored row — but two of them are compared against *each other*, which is the subject of Section 2.9.

The remaining two roles are **entity-backed** portal users. When `X-User-Role` is `Creditor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Creditor` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Creditor` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Debtor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Debtor` row, and ownership checks compare the resource's debtor foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely: a `Creditor` caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the governing judgment's `creditor_id`, and a `Debtor` caller's `X-User-ID` must equal that judgment's `debtor_id`. The governing judgment is found by following the target resource's own foreign-key chain — an exemption claim reaches its enforcement action through `enforcement_action_id` and that action reaches the judgment through `judgment_id`; a settlement offer, a payment, and an enforcement action each carry `judgment_id` directly; a judgment is its own governing judgment. When the target of the request is a debtor row itself, that row's `id` plays the same part, so a `Debtor` caller may read only their own debtor record. A mismatch anywhere along that chain is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` with HTTP 403. The same scoping applies to list reads, and it takes the form of a narrowed result set rather than a refusal: a `Creditor` sees only judgments whose `creditor_id` is their own identifier, and a `Debtor` sees only judgments whose `debtor_id` is their own, and every other collection an entity-backed caller may list — county filings, enforcement actions, exemption claims, settlement offers, installment agreements and payments — is filtered down to the rows whose governing judgment that caller owns, with the non-owned rows silently absent and `total_count` counting only what survives the filter. A list read therefore never answers `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; only a read or write aimed at one named row does.

Responsibilities divide as follows. A `CaseManager` opens files, dockets judgments, drives county filings, schedules examinations and subpoenas, drafts enforcement actions, records missed installments, and books money in. An `AssetInvestigator` runs skip traces and records and verifies asset findings, and touches no enforcement instrument. An `EnforcementOfficer` requests, serves and releases writs and opposes exemption claims. A `ComplianceOfficer` rules on exemption claims, renews judgments, reinstates defaulted agreements, reverses payments, releases writs, and is the only role permitted to accept a settlement below its floor. `Creditor` and `Debtor` negotiate and, for the debtor, file exemption claims and pay.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Filtered Discovery

Because the topology is flat, filtered list reads are the only way to walk from a parent to its children, and they carry a large share of the traffic. Every list endpoint accepts `limit` and `offset` as query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` and HTTP 400. Results are always ordered by `created_at` ascending; no endpoint offers a different sort. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied) and `has_more` (true when `offset` plus the number of returned rows is still less than `total_count`).

Alongside pagination, each list endpoint exposes the filters that make reverse reachability possible. Judgments may be filtered by `creditor_id`, `debtor_id`, `status` and `enforcement_posture`. County filings accept `judgment_id`, `county_code` and `status`. Asset findings accept `judgment_id`, `asset_type`, `status` and `source`. Enforcement actions accept `judgment_id`, `asset_finding_id` and `status`. Exemption claims accept `enforcement_action_id` and `status`. Debtor examinations accept `judgment_id`, `status` and `subpoena_status`. Settlement offers accept `judgment_id`, the self-referential `responds_to_offer_id`, and `status`. Installment agreements accept `judgment_id`, `settlement_offer_id` and `status`. Payments accept `judgment_id`, `installment_agreement_id`, `enforcement_action_id` and `status`. Every filter is optional; when several are supplied they combine conjunctively.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and the Version Counter

Every stored object carries an `id` that the server mints as a UUID at creation time. Identifiers are bare UUIDs with no type prefix and no embedded semantics; clients must never parse them. Every object also carries `created_at` and `updated_at`, both ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits, and a `version` that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. All eleven object types in this system carry the full set — `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version` — so there is no entity for which `updated_at` or `version` is absent. At creation `updated_at` holds exactly the same instant as `created_at`, and both are stamped from the server's real wall clock rather than from the logical clock of Section 2.5, strictly increasing from row to row so that `created_at` ascending is precisely insertion order. Temporal rendering is fixed by provenance rather than by field: an instant the server computes — `created_at`, `updated_at`, an offer's `decided_at` — is always written with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`, whatever precision the clock was set with; a datetime the client supplies and the server merely echoes — an examination's `scheduled_at`, an offer's `expires_at` — is stored and returned as the very characters supplied, unnormalised; and every `_on` field is a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date, a supplied datetime being normalised down to its date before storage.

`version` is the anchor of the concurrency scheme described in Section 2.7, and it is important to understand which operations move it. A judgment's `version` advances when the judgment is created, when a county filing on it is recorded, when an enforcement action is drafted against it, when a writ against it is served, when a settlement offer on it is tendered or accepted, when an installment agreement on it defaults or is reinstated, when a payment is booked or reversed, when it is renewed, and whenever a cascade moves its `status` or its `enforcement_posture`. A debtor's `version` advances on exactly four operations: a skip trace against that debtor; the creation of an asset finding on one of that debtor's judgments; the verification of such a finding; and the recording of a `NO_SHOW` outcome on one of that debtor's examinations. Any mutation, cascaded or direct, also refreshes `updated_at` on the row it touches. The converse is worth stating, because several events rewrite a row's payload without being version-bearing: stamping `certified_recoverability_score` on a debtor at writ request, and recomputing a debtor's `recoverability_score` when a quash exhausts a finding, each refresh that debtor's `updated_at` alone and leave its `version` exactly where it stood; and on the judgment side a writ request, the rejection or clock-driven expiry of a settlement offer, and a writ release that does not actually move the posture all refresh `updated_at` while leaving `version` untouched.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and the Docket Sweep

Time in this system is logical, not wall-clock. The server holds a single logical instant that reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` before any client has moved it, and it must never fall back to real wall-clock time. Firm staff advance it through `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, supplying `to_iso8601`; the four staff roles `CaseManager`, `AssetInvestigator`, `EnforcementOfficer` and `ComplianceOfficer` may all call it. The clock may only move forward or stay where it is: a `to_iso8601` earlier than the current logical instant is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REGRESSION` and HTTP 422. Wherever this document speaks of "the current clock date" it means the calendar date of that logical instant.

Advancing the clock is not a passive act. It runs the docket sweep, whose four steps execute in a pinned order, each completing entirely before the next begins, and which processes rows within each step in `created_at` ascending order.

- **Step 1 — expiry.** Every `ACTIVE` judgment whose `expires_on` is on or before the new clock date becomes `EXPIRED`, cascading as described in Section 3.9.
- **Step 2 — unopposed exemption claims.** Every exemption claim still in `FILED` whose `opposition_deadline_on` falls strictly before the new clock date becomes `DEFAULTED`, cascading as described in Section 3.6.
- **Step 3 — stale offers.** Every settlement offer still in `OPEN` whose `expires_at` is at or before the new clock instant becomes `EXPIRED`.
- **Step 4 — lapsed subpoenas.** Every subpoena in `ISSUED` on an examination whose own status dimension is still `SCHEDULED`, and whose `subpoena_service_deadline_on` falls strictly before the new clock date, becomes `EXPIRED`.

The response to a clock advance is not a stored object but a summary: `current_utc` echoes the new logical instant by reproducing the caller's `to_iso8601` string exactly as supplied — the same characters, with no renormalisation of its fractional-second digits or of its UTC designator — and `expired_judgment_count`, `defaulted_claim_count`, `expired_offer_count`, `expired_subpoena_count` and `terminated_agreement_count` report how many rows each step touched, with `terminated_agreement_count` counting the installment agreements terminated by the step-1 cascade.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points, and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are whole numbers of cents, transported as integers. There are no decimal money fields and no currency codes. Rates are expressed in basis points, where one basis point is one hundredth of one percent, so a rate applied to an amount is that amount multiplied by the basis-point figure and divided by ten thousand.

Rounding is pinned per calculation and is never left to the implementer's discretion, because the order in which rounding is applied changes the answer. Statutory interest is rounded to a whole number of cents, half up, **once per accrual segment**, and the rounded segment amount is what is added to the running interest balance; accumulating exact fractional interest across segments and rounding a single time at the end produces a different and incorrect figure. The recovery firm's contingency fee is rounded to a whole number of cents, half up, **once per payment**, and the per-payment slices are then summed; multiplying the aggregate collection by the fee rate and rounding once produces a different and incorrect figure. Three quantities round downward to the nearest whole unit — the asset component of a recoverability score, the percentage cap on a wage garnishment, and the minimum acceptable settlement amount. One quantity rounds upward to the nearest whole cent: the monthly installment. Capitalizing interest into principal at renewal is an exact integer transfer and rounds not at all, as is the movement of funds between an asset's custody buckets.

### 2.7. Stale-Read Protection on Judgment and Debtor Writes

Three write endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the `version` of a parent row. Each requires a non-optional version field in its request body, and each rejects a value that does not match the parent's current `version`. Endpoints not named here take no version field at all.

**Tendering a settlement offer** — `POST /settlement-offers` — must carry `expected_judgment_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the target judgment. A new offer legitimately supersedes whatever offer is currently `OPEN` on the same judgment, moving that sibling to `COUNTERED` or `REJECTED`, so a writer whose view of the judgment predates that supersede is negotiating against a balance and an offer chain that no longer exist. If `expected_judgment_version` does not equal the judgment's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_JUDGMENT_VERSION` and HTTP 412.

**Drafting an enforcement action** — `POST /enforcement-actions` — must likewise carry `expected_judgment_version` holding the client's last observed judgment `version`. Nothing forbids a second action against the same judgment or even the same asset finding, so a writer working from a stale read would otherwise succeed while sizing a levy against a payoff, an `enforcement_posture` and an `application_order` that have already moved — a recorded county filing, a served sibling levy, an accepted settlement, or a renewal all advance the judgment's `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_JUDGMENT_VERSION` and HTTP 412.

**Running a skip trace** — `POST /debtors/{debtor_id}/skip-trace` — must carry `expected_debtor_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of that debtor. A completed trace supersedes the debtor's earlier skip-trace-sourced asset findings and rewrites `locate_status` and `recoverability_score`, so a second trace built on a stale read would re-stale findings that a newer trace already refreshed. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEBTOR_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Because the debtor's `version` is advanced by asset-finding creation and verification and by a recorded no-show as well as by tracing, a client should read the debtor immediately before tracing it.

None of these three rules is about requests overlapping in time. Each is a comparison between the version the client last observed and the version the row currently holds; a stale value is stale whether it was read a millisecond or a month ago.

### 2.8. Screening Order: How a Request Is Rejected

When several checks would fail on the same request, the response is determined by the **first** failing check in the following fixed order. This ordering is normative: a request that violates both a segregation-of-duties rule and a fiscal floor reports the segregation failure, never the floor.

1. `X-User-Role` is present and names one of the six roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. `X-User-ID` is present whenever the acting role is entity-backed, meaning `Creditor` or `Debtor` — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
3. The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized list — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
4. Entity-backed ownership holds for `Creditor` and `Debtor` callers — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
5. Every identifier embedded in the URL path resolves to a stored row, validated left to right, each reported with its own code: `ERR_JUDGMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEBTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COUNTY_FILING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSET_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXEMPTION_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXAMINATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSTALLMENT_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND`.
6. Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves, validated in this field order: `creditor_id`, `debtor_id`, `judgment_id`, `asset_finding_id`, `enforcement_action_id`, `responds_to_offer_id`, `installment_agreement_id`. This applies to creating a judgment, a county filing, an asset finding, an enforcement action, an exemption claim, a debtor examination, a settlement offer and a payment, and the code is the per-entity not-found code — `ERR_CREDITOR_NOT_FOUND` for a bad `creditor_id`, and the corresponding codes listed above for the others.
7. Every non-optional field in the endpoint's input is present and carries the declared JSON type — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400. The same code covers a value that falls outside a declared range, such as an `interest_rate_bps` above 3000, an `installment_count` above 60, or a `state_code` that is not exactly two uppercase letters.
8. Every enum-typed input carries a value from that field's declared set — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` and HTTP 400.
9. Monetary and count inputs satisfy their sign constraints — otherwise `ERR_NON_POSITIVE_AMOUNT` and HTTP 400. `original_principal_cents`, `amount_cents`, `claimed_amount_cents`, `installment_count` and `hits_count` must be strictly greater than zero; `fee_cents`, `filing_costs_cents`, `estimated_value_cents`, `weekly_disposable_earnings_cents` and `interest_rate_bps` must be zero or greater.
10. Pagination inputs are within bounds — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
11. On the clock endpoint, `to_iso8601` is not earlier than the current logical clock — otherwise `ERR_CLOCK_REGRESSION`.
12. `expected_judgment_version` matches the judgment's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_JUDGMENT_VERSION`. This guards offer tender and enforcement-action drafting.
13. `expected_debtor_version` matches the debtor's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_DEBTOR_VERSION`. This guards the skip trace.
14. The target resource sits in one of the states from which the requested transition is legal — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` and HTTP 409. This applies to every state-transition endpoint.
15. The governing judgment's `status` is not `EXPIRED` — otherwise `ERR_JUDGMENT_EXPIRED` and HTTP 409. This applies to every write endpoint that touches a judgment or any of its children.
16. The governing judgment's `status` is not `SATISFIED` — otherwise `ERR_JUDGMENT_SATISFIED` and HTTP 409, with the same breadth as the previous check.
17. The writ requester's `X-User-ID` differs from the linked finding's `verified_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403, on writ request.
18. The ruling officer's `X-User-ID` differs from the claim's `opposed_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403, on ruling an exemption claim.
19. The judgment's `enforcement_posture` is not `STAYED` — otherwise `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_STAYED` and HTTP 409. This guards exactly three operations: drafting an enforcement action, requesting its writ, and serving it.
20. No exemption claim on the referenced enforcement action sits in `FILED` or `OPPOSED` — otherwise `ERR_EXEMPTION_PENDING` and HTTP 409, on booking a payment that names an `enforcement_action_id`.
21. An exemption claim is filed no earlier than the writ's `served_on` and no later than the inclusive deadline described in Section 3.6 — otherwise `ERR_EXEMPTION_WINDOW_CLOSED` and HTTP 422.
22. Renewal is attempted on or after the first day of the renewal window — otherwise `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` and HTTP 422.
23. A scheduled or continued examination respects the cooling separation from every other live examination on the same debtor — otherwise `ERR_EXAM_COOLING_PERIOD` and HTTP 409. This applies both to scheduling a new examination and to recording a `CONTINUED` outcome on an existing one.
24. A writ's occupation window does not overlap that of another live writ on the same asset — otherwise `ERR_LEVY_WINDOW_CONFLICT` and HTTP 409, on service.
25. A subpoena is issued on or before its service deadline — otherwise `ERR_SUBPOENA_DEADLINE_PASSED` and HTTP 422.
26. A payment's `received_on` falls within the judgment's life — otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_DATE_INVALID` and HTTP 422.
27. The judgment carries a perfected lien in the debtor's state — otherwise `ERR_NO_PERFECTED_LIEN` and HTTP 409, on writ request.
28. The referenced asset finding is `VERIFIED` when the enforcement action is drafted — otherwise `ERR_FINDING_NOT_VERIFIED` and HTTP 409.
29. The linked asset finding has not been staled since drafting — otherwise `ERR_FINDING_STALE` and HTTP 409, on writ request.
30. The debtor is located when the examination order is served — otherwise `ERR_DEBTOR_NOT_LOCATED` and HTTP 409.
31. A counter-offer comes from the opposite side to the offer it answers — otherwise `ERR_SAME_PARTY_COUNTER` and HTTP 409, on tendering an offer that supplies `responds_to_offer_id`.
32. The instrument matches the asset class — otherwise `ERR_ACTION_ASSET_TYPE_MISMATCH` and HTTP 409, on drafting an enforcement action.
33. `court_case_number` is unique within `state_code` across all judgments — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CASE_NUMBER` and HTTP 409.
34. No live county filing duplicates this one — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_COUNTY_FILING` and HTTP 409.
35. The negotiation has rounds left — otherwise `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` and HTTP 409, on tendering a counter-offer.
36. The examination has continuances left — otherwise `ERR_CONTINUANCE_LIMIT` and HTTP 409, on recording a `CONTINUED` outcome.
37. The judgment has renewals left — otherwise `ERR_RENEWAL_LIMIT_REACHED` and HTTP 409.
38. The agreement has reinstatements left — otherwise `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_LIMIT` and HTTP 409.
39. The accepted offer clears its settlement floor, unless the acting role is `ComplianceOfficer` — otherwise `ERR_SETTLEMENT_BELOW_FLOOR` and HTTP 422.
40. A wage garnishment yields a strictly positive weekly withholding — otherwise `ERR_GARNISHMENT_BELOW_FLOOR` and HTTP 422, on writ request when the instrument is a wage garnishment.
41. A bank levy yields a strictly positive hold — otherwise `ERR_NO_LEVIABLE_FUNDS` and HTTP 422. This is checked both at writ request and again at service, whenever the instrument is a bank levy.
42. A payment does not exceed the judgment's payoff as of the date the money arrived — otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_PAYOFF` and HTTP 422.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties Across the Enforcement Chain

Two conflicts of interest are structurally forbidden, and both are keyed on `X-User-ID` rather than on role, because both parties in each pair hold the same role. An `AssetInvestigator` who verified a finding — whose identifier therefore sits in that finding's `verified_by_user_id` — may not be the `EnforcementOfficer` who requests the writ that rests on it; if the requesting officer's `X-User-ID` equals `verified_by_user_id`, the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Likewise the `EnforcementOfficer` whose identifier sits in an exemption claim's `opposed_by_user_id` may not be the `ComplianceOfficer` who rules on that claim; an equal comparison there draws the same refusal. Note that these are identity comparisons, not role comparisons: the same human operating under two roles is still the same `X-User-ID` and is still blocked.

### 2.10. The Two Axes of a Judgment

A judgment carries two independent state dimensions on the same row, and confusing them is the most common modelling mistake in this domain. The first is `status`, the lifecycle axis, which starts at `ACTIVE` and ends at either `SATISFIED` or `EXPIRED`; both of those are terminal and nothing revives a judgment from them. The second is `enforcement_posture`, which starts at `NONE` and moves among `NONE`, `LIEN_PERFECTED`, `ENFORCING` and `STAYED` without ever terminating while the judgment lives. Posture gates enforcement: a judgment at `NONE` has no perfected lien and cannot support a writ; at `LIEN_PERFECTED` it can; at `ENFORCING` at least one writ is live; at `STAYED` an installment agreement holds enforcement in abeyance. The posture axis is genuinely cyclic — it moves `ENFORCING` to `STAYED` and back to `ENFORCING` as agreements activate and default, and `ENFORCING` to `LIEN_PERFECTED` and back to `ENFORCING` as writs conclude and new ones are served.

Debtor examinations carry a second dimension in the same way: the appearance order runs on `status`, while the duces-tecum subpoena runs independently on `subpoena_status`.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline and Custody Conservation

Many operations here mutate more than one row, and the specification is exact about the order and the accounting. Whenever a cascade touches a set of sibling rows — releasing every recorded filing on a satisfied judgment, suspending every served writ when a settlement activates, staling every skip-trace finding after a new trace — those rows are processed in `created_at` ascending order. Every row a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, exactly as a direct write would.

Asset custody obeys a conservation law that must hold at every instant: for any asset finding, `available_cents` plus `frozen_cents` plus `swept_cents` equals `estimated_value_cents`. Serving a bank levy moves cents from available to frozen; releasing or quashing that levy moves the same cents back from frozen to available; collecting on it moves them from frozen to swept, permanently. Serving and releasing therefore form a compensating pair that restores the split exactly — even though the sheriff's fee charged to the judgment at service is never refunded.

The ledger obeys a parallel discipline. A judgment's `gross_exposure_cents` — the sum of `principal_balance_cents`, `accrued_interest_cents`, `costs_balance_cents` and `collected_to_date_cents` — is the conserved quantity across the payment and reversal pair, because booking a payment moves value out of the three balances and into collections while reversing it moves value back. Balances are never patched by an inverse delta; they are always recomputed by replaying the surviving payment history from the judgment's `entered_on`, which is what keeps the interest segments correct.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening a File: Creditors, Debtors and the Docketed Judgment

A `CaseManager` starts by onboarding the client. A creditor record needs only a `name` — the legal name of the judgment creditor — and a `contact_email`, which is the address to which payoff quotes and settlement decisions are directed. The creditor also carries `active_judgment_count`, which the server maintains rather than the client: it starts at 0 and always equals the number of judgments whose `creditor_id` is this creditor and whose lifecycle `status` is `ACTIVE`. It is recomputed from that definition — never incremented or decremented by a delta — when a judgment is docketed and again on every judgment lifecycle transition, so a judgment reaching `SATISFIED` or `EXPIRED` reduces it by one; each such recompute also increments the creditor row's own `version` by one and refreshes its `updated_at`.

Registering the debtor captures the facts that will later decide how much of a paycheck can be reached. `full_name` is the debtor's legal name. `state_code` is the two-letter USPS code of the debtor's state of residence, written as exactly two uppercase letters; it drives both the garnishment cap and the perfected-lien test, so it matters far more than a mailing address normally would. `head_of_household` is the debtor's household declaration and defaults to false when omitted. `weekly_disposable_earnings_cents` is the debtor's weekly disposable earnings in cents, defaults to 0, and may not be negative. Four investigative counters are server-owned: a freshly registered debtor has `locate_status` of `UNLOCATED`, a `skip_trace_count` of 0, a `no_show_count` of 0, and a `last_skip_trace_on` that is present but null until the first trace runs. Its `recoverability_score` is 20 on the day it is created — the cooperation component alone, since no assets are known and the debtor is not yet located — and its `certified_recoverability_score` is null and stays null until a writ is first requested against one of that debtor's judgments.

Docketing the judgment itself binds a creditor and a debtor together through `creditor_id` and `debtor_id` and records what the court decided. The payload carries `court_case_number`, the docket number the entering court assigned; `state_code`, the two-letter code of the state whose court entered the judgment; `original_principal_cents`, the principal as entered, which must be strictly greater than zero; `interest_rate_bps`, the statutory post-judgment simple interest rate in basis points per annum, which must be at least 0 and no more than 3000; `entered_on`, the calendar date of entry, which is the instant from which interest begins to run; and optionally `filing_costs_cents`, the recoverable court costs taxed at entry, which defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `court_case_number` must be unique within its `state_code` across every judgment in the system — the same docket number in two different states is perfectly normal, but a repeat within one state is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CASE_NUMBER`.

Upon docketing, the server stamps the opening position. `expires_on` is set to ten calendar years after `entered_on`, where calendar-year arithmetic means the same month and day that many years later, with the twenty-ninth of February mapping to the twenty-eighth. The lifecycle `status` begins at `ACTIVE`, the `enforcement_posture` at `NONE`, and the `application_order` at `STATUTORY_ORDER`. `principal_balance_cents` is seeded from `original_principal_cents` and `costs_balance_cents` from `filing_costs_cents`, `accrued_interest_cents` starts at zero, and the balances are then replayed forward to the current clock date so that `total_payoff_cents`, `gross_exposure_cents` and `accrued_through_on` are correct from the first read. `collected_to_date_cents`, `forgiven_cents`, `contingency_fee_cents`, `net_to_creditor_cents`, `renewal_count`, `active_enforcement_count`, `open_offer_count` and `perfected_county_count` all begin at 0, and `renewal_dates` begins as an empty list. Finally the creditor's `active_judgment_count` is recomputed from its definition, which raises it by one.

### 3.2. Perfecting the Lien: County Recording and Priority

A money judgment is worth very little until it is recorded with a county recorder, because recording is what attaches the lien and fixes its position against every other creditor in that county. The `CaseManager` drafts one filing per county, naming the `judgment_id` it docketed, the `county_code` of the recorder — a code such as `CA-LOS-ANGELES` — the two-letter `state_code` of that county's state, the `filing_type`, which is either `ABSTRACT_OF_JUDGMENT` or `JUDGMENT_LIEN`, and the `fee_cents` the recorder will charge, which may not be negative. A new filing is born in `DRAFT`. The firm may not carry two live filings of the same instrument in the same county for the same judgment: if another filing already shares this filing's `judgment_id`, `county_code` and `filing_type` while sitting in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `RECORDED`, the draft is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_COUNTY_FILING`. Filings that have been `REJECTED`, `RELEASED` or `LAPSED` do not block a new one.

Submission moves the filing `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and stamps `submitted_on` with the current clock date; the judgment must still be `ACTIVE`, and the same no-live-duplicate condition holds at submission as at drafting. The recorder's answer arrives through adjudication, which takes an `outcome`. An `outcome` of `REJECTED` moves the filing `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, requires a `rejection_reason` in the payload, increments `rejection_count` by one, and stores the reason on the row; crucially it leaves the judgment's `costs_balance_cents` untouched, because a rejected instrument was never recorded and its fee is not taxable. A rejected filing is not dead — correcting the defect and submitting again moves it `REJECTED → SUBMITTED` (the judgment must still be `ACTIVE`) and re-stamps `submitted_on`, and a filing may bounce between those two states any number of times, with `rejection_count` accumulating each rebuff.

An `outcome` of `RECORDED` moves the filing `SUBMITTED → RECORDED` and is where the interesting work happens. The payload must supply `recorded_on`, the recorder's acceptance date, and that date must fall on or after `submitted_on` and on or before the current clock date; a date outside that span is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The judgment must be `ACTIVE`, and for the lien to perfect the filing's `state_code` must equal the `state_code` of the debtor reached through the judgment's `debtor_id`. Recording sets `lien_expires_on` to ten calendar years after `recorded_on`. It then adds this filing's `fee_cents` to the judgment's `costs_balance_cents` and replays the judgment's balances to the current clock date, so the recorder's fee becomes recoverable taxed costs. It then recomputes the judgment's `perfected_county_count` as the number of that judgment's filings currently sitting in `RECORDED`, a count that takes no notice of state and so is raised just as much by a recording in a county outside the debtor's state as by one inside it. The `enforcement_posture` is a separate question, decided not by that count but by the two conditions that the posture is still `NONE` and that this filing's `state_code` equals the debtor's: when both hold the posture moves `NONE → LIEN_PERFECTED`, the moment at which enforcement first becomes possible. An out-of-state recording therefore leaves the posture at `NONE` however high the count has climbed, and a later in-state recording perfects the lien even though by then the count is rising from one to two rather than from zero to one. The judgment's `version` is incremented by one.

Recording also settles **priority**, and it does so for an entire county at once rather than merely stamping the new arrival. The cohort is every county filing in the system — across all judgments, not merely this creditor's — whose `county_code` and `state_code` match this filing's and whose status is exactly `RECORDED` immediately after this recording, including the filing just recorded. That cohort is sorted by `recorded_on` ascending and, for filings sharing a recording date, by `created_at` ascending; identifiers are never used as a tie-break. Each member is then assigned `priority_rank` by its position in that order, starting at 1; a filing that has never been recorded carries no `priority_rank` at all. Because the whole cohort is renumbered, a filing that was created later but recorded on the same day as an existing senior filing can take the senior position and demote its peer within the same request, whenever creation order and recording order disagree. Filings that subsequently leave `RECORDED` drop out of the cohort, but the survivors keep their existing ranks until the next recording renumbers them. Renumbering writes only where it must: a cohort member whose `priority_rank` genuinely changes has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, a member that keeps the rank it already held is left entirely untouched, and the filing being recorded carries only the single `version` increment of its own recording however the renumbering falls out.

Recorded filings end their life by cascade rather than by request. When the judgment is satisfied, each `RECORDED` filing on it moves `RECORDED → RELEASED`; when the judgment expires, each moves `RECORDED → LAPSED`. In both cases the filings are processed in `created_at` ascending order and each departure decrements the judgment's `perfected_county_count`, which drives that counter to zero once the sweep is complete.

### 3.3. Locating the Money: Skip Tracing, Asset Findings and Recoverability

An `AssetInvestigator` owns the search. A skip trace records what a locating vendor came back with: `vendor` names the vendor, `located` is a boolean saying whether the debtor was found, `hits_count` is the number of matching records the vendor returned and must be strictly positive, and `current_state_code` is an optional two-letter code that, when supplied, replaces the debtor's `state_code` outright. The trace is guarded by `expected_debtor_version` as described in Section 2.7. A trace with `located` true sets the debtor's `locate_status` to `LOCATED`. A trace with `located` false sets it to `STALE` if the previous value was `LOCATED`, and to `UNLOCATED` otherwise — a debtor who was never found in the first place does not become stale, they simply remain unlocated. Every trace increments `skip_trace_count` by one, sets `last_skip_trace_on` to the current clock date, recomputes the debtor's `recoverability_score` under the preliminary variant, and increments the debtor's `version` by one.

A completed trace also supersedes what earlier traces found. Every asset finding whose `source` is `SKIP_TRACE`, which hangs off a judgment whose `debtor_id` is the traced debtor, and whose own status is `OPEN` or `VERIFIED`, is moved to `STALE` — `OPEN → STALE` or `VERIFIED → STALE` as the case may be — in `created_at` ascending order. Findings sourced from `PUBLIC_RECORD`, `CREDIT_HEADER` or `DEBTOR_EXAM` are untouched by a trace, and a finding already `STALE` or `EXHAUSTED` stays where it is. Each staling recomputes the preliminary recoverability score.

Recording a discovered asset names the `judgment_id` the search was run under and describes the asset: `asset_type` is one of `REAL_PROPERTY`, `BANK_ACCOUNT`, `EMPLOYMENT` or `VEHICLE`; `institution_name` is the bank, employer, county recorder or motor-vehicle agency holding it; `identifier_last4` is the last four characters of the account, parcel or plate identifier; `estimated_value_cents` is the investigator's valuation and may not be negative; and `source` is the investigative channel, one of `SKIP_TRACE`, `PUBLIC_RECORD`, `CREDIT_HEADER` or `DEBTOR_EXAM`. One further field applies to employment findings only: `weekly_disposable_earnings_cents`, the weekly disposable earnings the finding establishes, which may not be negative, must be supplied when `asset_type` is `EMPLOYMENT`, and must be omitted for every other asset class. When it is supplied, its value is copied onto the debtor reached through the judgment, overwriting whatever the debtor record previously held, and the debtor's `version` is incremented by one; the finding row keeps the field as well, carrying null on every non-employment class. A new finding begins in `OPEN`, with `available_cents` initialised to `estimated_value_cents`, `frozen_cents` at 0 and `swept_cents` at 0, and its creation recomputes the debtor's preliminary recoverability score.

Verification is what turns an investigator's belief into something a writ can rest on. Verifying moves a finding `OPEN → VERIFIED`, or `STALE → VERIFIED` when a superseding trace had staled it, and in both cases the judgment must be `ACTIVE`. Verification stamps `verified_by_user_id` with the calling investigator's `X-User-ID` and `verified_on` with the current clock date, re-stamping both on a re-verification, and recomputes the preliminary recoverability score. The fourth and final destination for a finding is `EXHAUSTED`, reached only by cascade: when an exemption claim of type `HEAD_OF_HOUSEHOLD` or `HOMESTEAD` succeeds and quashes the writ that reached this asset, the finding moves `VERIFIED → EXHAUSTED` and thereafter contributes nothing to any score. `EXHAUSTED` is terminal.

The **recoverability score** is a 0-to-100 integer summarising how collectable a debtor looks, and it exists in two variants computed from the same three components. The population for both is every asset finding whose `judgment_id` belongs to a judgment whose `debtor_id` is this debtor — the debtor's entire book, not one judgment's worth.

Under the **preliminary** variant, which is the one written to `recoverability_score` on every investigative event, the weighted asset total is the sum, over findings whose status is `OPEN` or `VERIFIED`, of each finding's `estimated_value_cents` taken at full weight when the finding is `VERIFIED` and at sixty percent when it is merely `OPEN`. Findings that are `STALE` or `EXHAUSTED` contribute nothing. The asset component is sixty multiplied by that weighted total, divided by a target asset value of fifty thousand dollars — five million cents — with the result rounded down to a whole number and then capped at 60. The locate component is 20 when the debtor's `locate_status` is `LOCATED` and 0 otherwise. The cooperation component starts at 20 and loses 10 points for each recorded no-show, counting at most two no-shows, so it is 20 at zero no-shows, 10 at one, and 0 at two or more. The three components are added and the sum is clamped into the range 0 through 100.

The **certified** variant is stricter, and it is computed and written to `certified_recoverability_score` at exactly one moment: when a writ is requested. Its weighted asset total counts only findings whose status is `VERIFIED`, at full weight — `OPEN` findings are excluded outright rather than discounted, as are `STALE` and `EXHAUSTED` ones. Its asset component is derived from that total exactly as before: sixty times the weighted total divided by five million cents, rounded down, capped at 60. Its locate component is identical, 20 when `LOCATED` and 0 otherwise. Its cooperation component, however, starts at 20 and loses the full 20 points on the first no-show, counting at most one, so it is 20 at zero no-shows and 0 at one or more. The sum is clamped to 0 through 100 in the same way. The weighted total is carried as an exact decimal throughout and the only rounding anywhere in either variant is the downward rounding inside the asset component. Identical asset and examination data therefore produce a certified score that is never higher, and usually strictly lower, than the preliminary one.

### 3.4. Compelling Disclosure: Examinations and Subpoenas

When the paper trail runs out, the firm brings the debtor into court. A `CaseManager` schedules an examination against a `judgment_id`, supplying `scheduled_at` as an ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the debtor must appear. The debtor being examined is the one reached through the judgment's `debtor_id`, which matters because the scheduling constraint is per debtor rather than per judgment: the candidate instant must be separated from the `scheduled_at` of every **other** examination on that same debtor whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `SERVED` by at least 120 days, measured as an absolute difference of at least 2,880 hours on the raw instants. A separation of exactly 120 days is permitted — the bound is inclusive — while one second less is refused with `ERR_EXAM_COOLING_PERIOD`. A new examination begins with `status` of `SCHEDULED`, `subpoena_status` of `NOT_ISSUED`, `continuance_count` of 0, and `subpoena_service_deadline_on` set to the calendar date of `scheduled_at` less 10 days.

The subpoena runs on its own axis. Issuing a duces-tecum subpoena requires the examination's own status dimension to still be `SCHEDULED` and the current clock date to fall on or before `subpoena_service_deadline_on`; issuing later is refused with `ERR_SUBPOENA_DEADLINE_PASSED`. The payload names the recipient with `recipient_type`, which is one of `DEBTOR`, `THIRD_PARTY_BANK` or `EMPLOYER`, and `recipient_name`, the person or institution commanded to produce records. Issuance moves `subpoena_status` `NOT_ISSUED → ISSUED` and stamps those two values onto the examination row as `subpoena_recipient_type` and `subpoena_recipient_name` respectively. If the examination is never served and the clock passes the deadline while the examination's status dimension is still `SCHEDULED`, the sweep moves the subpoena `ISSUED → EXPIRED`, which is terminal on that axis.

Serving the appearance order moves the examination `SCHEDULED → SERVED` and takes `served_on`, the calendar date of service. The judgment must be `ACTIVE`, the debtor's `locate_status` must be `LOCATED` — one cannot serve someone nobody can find, and attempting it is `ERR_DEBTOR_NOT_LOCATED` — and `served_on` must fall on or after the date 30 days before the current clock date and on or before the current clock date itself, with anything outside that span rejected as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Service stamps `served_on`, and if the subpoena is sitting in `ISSUED` it moves `ISSUED → SERVED` on the same call, so one act of service disposes of both dimensions.

Recording the result takes an `outcome` and closes or re-arms the examination. `HELD` moves it `SERVED → HELD` and stamps `outcome_recorded_on`; the debtor appeared and the matter is finished. `NO_SHOW` moves it `SERVED → NO_SHOW`, stamps `outcome_recorded_on`, increments the debtor's `no_show_count` by one, and — because that counter feeds the cooperation component — recomputes the debtor's preliminary recoverability score and increments the debtor's `version` by one. Both `HELD` and `NO_SHOW` are terminal. `CONTINUED` is the cyclic branch: it requires a `new_scheduled_at` in the payload, requires `continuance_count` to be strictly less than the ceiling of 2 continuances (a third attempt draws `ERR_CONTINUANCE_LIMIT`), and requires `new_scheduled_at` to satisfy the same 120-day separation against every other live examination on the debtor. It moves the examination `SERVED → SCHEDULED`, replaces `scheduled_at` with `new_scheduled_at`, increments `continuance_count` by one, clears `served_on`, and recomputes `subpoena_service_deadline_on` as the calendar date of the new `scheduled_at` less 10 days, while leaving `subpoena_status` exactly where it stands and `outcome_recorded_on` unstamped. An examination may therefore cycle `SCHEDULED → SERVED → SCHEDULED` at most twice before it must resolve.

### 3.5. Executing: Writs, Service, Suspension and Release

An enforcement action is the instrument that actually reaches an asset, and it passes through three sets of hands. The `CaseManager` drafts it, naming the `judgment_id` being enforced, the `asset_finding_id` the writ will reach, and the `action_type`, which is either `WAGE_GARNISHMENT` or `BANK_LEVY`; the draft also carries `expected_judgment_version` as described in Section 2.7. Two structural checks apply at drafting. The referenced finding must be in status `VERIFIED` at that moment, and a finding that is `OPEN`, `STALE` or `EXHAUSTED` draws `ERR_FINDING_NOT_VERIFIED`. The instrument must also match the asset class: `WAGE_GARNISHMENT` demands a finding whose `asset_type` is `EMPLOYMENT` and `BANK_LEVY` demands one whose `asset_type` is `BANK_ACCOUNT`, and any other pairing is `ERR_ACTION_ASSET_TYPE_MISMATCH`. Drafting is also one of the three operations blocked while the judgment's `enforcement_posture` is `STAYED`, which surfaces as `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_STAYED`. A successful draft creates the action in `DRAFT` and increments the judgment's `version` by one. Nothing prevents two actions being drafted against the same judgment or the same finding, which is exactly why the stale-version guard exists.

An `EnforcementOfficer` then requests the writ, moving the action `DRAFT → WRIT_REQUESTED`. The judgment must be `ACTIVE`. The officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the linked finding's `verified_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The judgment's `enforcement_posture` must be `LIEN_PERFECTED` or `ENFORCING`; a posture of `NONE` means no county filing has been recorded in the debtor's state, which is `ERR_NO_PERFECTED_LIEN`, and a posture of `STAYED` is `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_STAYED`. The linked finding must not have been staled by a superseding skip trace between drafting and this moment, which is a separate failure from the drafting check and is reported as `ERR_FINDING_STALE`. Finally the instrument must be fiscally worth issuing, which is the subject of the two calculations below. When all of that holds, the request stamps `writ_requested_on` with the current clock date and `writ_requested_by_user_id` with the officer's `X-User-ID`; for a wage garnishment it stores the computed `weekly_withholding_cents` on the action, while for a bank levy it leaves `levy_hold_cents` at 0 until service actually freezes money; it stamps the debtor's `certified_recoverability_score` with the certified variant described in Section 3.3, refreshing that debtor's `updated_at` without advancing its `version`; and it recomputes the judgment's `active_enforcement_count`, which is never moved by a delta but always derived afresh as the number of that judgment's enforcement actions sitting in `WRIT_REQUESTED`, `SERVED` or `SUSPENDED` — so the newly requested writ raises it by one here. The judgment's own `version` does not move on a writ request; only its `updated_at` is refreshed.

The **weekly withholding** on a wage garnishment is the smallest of three independent caps, all measured against the debtor reached through the action's judgment. The first is the percentage cap: the debtor's `weekly_disposable_earnings_cents` multiplied by a cap rate and divided by ten thousand, rounded down to a whole cent. That cap rate is normally 2,500 basis points — a quarter of disposable earnings — but drops to 1,000 basis points, a tenth, when the debtor's `head_of_household` flag is true **and** their `state_code` is `FL`; both conditions must hold for the lower cap to apply. The second is the federal floor: disposable earnings less thirty times the federal minimum hourly wage of 725 cents, that is, less 21,750 cents, with any negative result treated as zero. The third is the outstanding debt itself: the judgment's `total_payoff_cents` from a payoff replay as of the current clock date. The withholding is the minimum of those three. The floor boundary is exact — weekly disposable earnings of exactly 21,750 cents yield a federal-floor cap of zero and therefore a withholding of zero, while 21,751 cents yield a cap of one cent and a valid writ. A withholding of zero is refused with `ERR_GARNISHMENT_BELOW_FLOOR`. Which of the three caps binds shifts non-linearly with the debtor's earnings, the head-of-household branch, and the outstanding payoff.

The **bank levy hold** is computed against the linked finding, whose `asset_type` must be `BANK_ACCOUNT`. Take the finding's `available_cents`, subtract an automatic statutory exemption of 175,000 cents, and take the smaller of that difference and the judgment's `total_payoff_cents` from a payoff replay as of the current clock date; if the result is negative, the hold is zero. A hold of zero is refused with `ERR_NO_LEVIABLE_FUNDS`, and that check is applied twice — once as a feasibility test when the writ is requested, and again at service, because the account balance or the payoff may have moved in between.

Service moves the action `WRIT_REQUESTED → SERVED` and takes `served_on`, the calendar date on which the garnishee or bank was actually served. The judgment must be `ACTIVE`, the `enforcement_posture` must not be `STAYED`, and `served_on` must fall on or after `writ_requested_on` and on or before the current clock date, with a date outside that span rejected as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Service also opens an occupation window and that window may not collide: `expires_on` is set to 120 days after `served_on`, and the half-open interval running from `served_on` up to but not including `expires_on` must not overlap the equivalent interval of any other enforcement action on the same `asset_finding_id` whose status is `SERVED` or `SUSPENDED`. Touching endpoints do not overlap, so a new levy that begins exactly on a prior levy's `expires_on` is permitted; anything genuinely overlapping is `ERR_LEVY_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

The order of the service side effects is pinned, because they interact. For a bank levy, `levy_hold_cents` is computed first, against the payoff **before** the sheriff's fee is added to the judgment, and that many cents are moved out of the finding's `available_cents` and into its `frozen_cents`. Only afterwards is `sheriff_fee_cents` — which stands at 0 from drafting until this moment — set to 12,500 cents, added to the judgment's `costs_balance_cents`, and the judgment's balances replayed. If the `enforcement_posture` was `LIEN_PERFECTED` it moves `LIEN_PERFECTED → ENFORCING`, the judgment's `active_enforcement_count` is recomputed from the definition above — which leaves it unchanged, because the action was already counted while it sat in `WRIT_REQUESTED` — and the judgment's `version` is incremented by one.

A served writ can be paused rather than ended. When an installment agreement activates and drives the posture `ENFORCING → STAYED`, every enforcement action on that judgment sitting in `SERVED` moves `SERVED → SUSPENDED`, in `created_at` ascending order — and the money stays where it is: `levy_hold_cents` is untouched and the finding's custody split between `available_cents` and `frozen_cents` is left exactly as it was. When that agreement later defaults and the posture returns `STAYED → ENFORCING`, every `SUSPENDED` action returns `SUSPENDED → SERVED`, again in `created_at` ascending order and again with no custody change. A levy may cycle through `SERVED → SUSPENDED → SERVED` as many times as agreements default and are reinstated.

Three exits end a writ. **Release** is requested by an `EnforcementOfficer` or a `ComplianceOfficer` and is legal from three states, as `WRIT_REQUESTED → RELEASED`, `SERVED → RELEASED` or `SUSPENDED → RELEASED`. From `SERVED` or `SUSPENDED` it returns `levy_hold_cents` from the finding's `frozen_cents` to its `available_cents`, leaves `swept_cents` alone, and zeroes `levy_hold_cents`; from `WRIT_REQUESTED` there is nothing frozen to return. In every case the judgment's `active_enforcement_count` is recomputed, which drops it by one, and the sheriff's fee already charged is **not** refunded. **Collection** happens when a payment naming this `enforcement_action_id` is booked, and it is conditional: no exemption claim on the action may still be `FILED` or `OPPOSED`, and either the payment's `source` must be `GARNISHMENT` — a wage remittance always collects — or, for a `BANK_LEVY`, the payment's `amount_cents` added to the action's existing `collected_cents` must reach `levy_hold_cents`. `collected_cents` rises by the payment amount whenever a payment names the action, collecting or not, and the action must additionally be sitting in `SERVED` for the collection itself to fire — a `SUSPENDED` action takes the credit without ending. When it does collect, the bank levy's own custody move follows: the finding's `frozen_cents` falls by `levy_hold_cents` while its `swept_cents` rises by the same amount, leaving `available_cents` untouched so that the three-way custody identity still holds, after which `levy_hold_cents` is set to 0 and the action moves `SERVED → COLLECTED`, `active_enforcement_count` being recomputed and thereby falling by one. **Quashing** is imposed from outside, when an exemption claim succeeds; it is described in Section 3.6. All three of `COLLECTED`, `RELEASED` and `QUASHED` are terminal.

Whenever an enforcement action reaches `COLLECTED`, `RELEASED` or `QUASHED`, the judgment's `active_enforcement_count` and `perfected_county_count` are both recomputed and three conditions are then tested together: the `enforcement_posture` must at that moment be exactly `ENFORCING`, the recomputed `active_enforcement_count` must be 0, and the recomputed `perfected_county_count` must be at least 1. Only when all three hold does the posture step back `ENFORCING → LIEN_PERFECTED` and the judgment's `version` increment by one. If any one of them fails — a posture of `STAYED` or `LIEN_PERFECTED`, another writ still live, or no filing left in `RECORDED` — the posture is left exactly where it stands and the judgment's `updated_at` is refreshed without its `version` moving at all. Enforcement is over for the moment; the lien is not.

### 3.6. The Debtor's Answer: Exemption Claims

Once a writ has been served, the debtor gets a statutory window in which to object. A `Debtor` files a claim naming the `enforcement_action_id` it attacks, the `claim_type` asserted — one of `HEAD_OF_HOUSEHOLD`, `SOCIAL_SECURITY`, `WAGE_CAP` or `HOMESTEAD` — and `claimed_amount_cents`, the amount the debtor says is exempt, which must be strictly greater than zero. The writ must actually have been served for there to be a window at all: an enforcement action whose `served_on` has never been stamped cannot anchor one, and a claim naming it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. That test looks at the stamp and nothing else, so an action that was served and has since been released, collected or quashed still accepts a claim for as long as its window runs. Filing stamps `filed_on` with the current clock date, sets `filing_deadline_on` to 20 days after the writ's `served_on`, and sets `opposition_deadline_on` to 10 days after `filed_on`. The filing window is bounded on both ends: a claim may not be filed before the writ's `served_on`, and it may not be filed after `filing_deadline_on`, which is itself an inclusive last day. Filing outside that window is `ERR_EXEMPTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A new claim begins in `FILED`.

From `FILED` the claim has exactly two exits, and which one it takes depends entirely on whether the creditor answers in time. An `EnforcementOfficer` may **oppose** it, moving `FILED → OPPOSED` and stamping `opposed_on` with the current clock date and `opposed_by_user_id` with the officer's `X-User-ID`; the linked enforcement action must be in `SERVED` or `SUSPENDED` at that moment. The opposition window need not be re-checked at that point, because the docket sweep has already moved every claim whose `opposition_deadline_on` has passed out of `FILED` — reaching this transition at all proves the window is still open. If nobody opposes, the sweep moves the claim `FILED → DEFAULTED` once `opposition_deadline_on` falls strictly before the new clock date. **An unopposed claim is granted by default, with the same consequences as one that is sustained.**

An opposed claim goes to a `ComplianceOfficer`, who supplies an `outcome` of either `SUSTAINED` or `OVERRULED`. In both branches the officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from `opposed_by_user_id` or the ruling is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and in both branches `ruled_by_user_id` is stamped with the ruling officer's identifier. From there the two branches diverge completely, and the difference is worth spelling out symmetrically because it is the sharpest fork in the domain.

An outcome of `SUSTAINED` moves the claim `OPPOSED → SUSTAINED` and unwinds the levy end to end. The linked enforcement action is quashed — `SERVED → QUASHED` or `SUSPENDED → QUASHED`, depending on where it was sitting. That quash returns `levy_hold_cents` from the finding's `frozen_cents` to its `available_cents` and sets `levy_hold_cents` to 0, and sets `weekly_withholding_cents` to 0. When the `claim_type` is `HEAD_OF_HOUSEHOLD` or `HOMESTEAD` the underlying asset is placed permanently out of reach and the finding moves `VERIFIED → EXHAUSTED`; for `SOCIAL_SECURITY` and `WAGE_CAP` claims the finding keeps its `VERIFIED` status, because only this particular writ was defeated. The debtor's preliminary `recoverability_score` is recomputed, refreshing that debtor's `updated_at` without advancing its `version`, the judgment's `active_enforcement_count` is recomputed and thereby drops by one, and the `enforcement_posture` steps back `ENFORCING → LIEN_PERFECTED` under exactly the three conditions set out at the end of Section 3.5. Should the linked action have already left `SERVED` or `SUSPENDED` by the time the ruling lands, the claim still reaches `SUSTAINED` and the cascade simply finds nothing to unwind.

An outcome of `OVERRULED` moves the claim `OPPOSED → OVERRULED` and changes nothing outside the claim row. The linked enforcement action keeps its status, keeps its `levy_hold_cents`, and the finding's custody split between `available_cents`, `frozen_cents` and `swept_cents` is untouched. The judgment's `active_enforcement_count` and `enforcement_posture` are both unchanged. Only `ruled_by_user_id` is written. All three of `SUSTAINED`, `OVERRULED` and `DEFAULTED` are terminal.

The default branch travels the same cascade as `SUSTAINED`: when the sweep moves a claim `FILED → DEFAULTED`, the linked action moves `SERVED → QUASHED`, the quash returns the hold to `available_cents`, a `HEAD_OF_HOUSEHOLD` or `HOMESTEAD` claim exhausts the finding, `active_enforcement_count` is recomputed and falls by one, and the posture steps back to `LIEN_PERFECTED` under the same three conditions.

One more consequence of a live claim: while any exemption claim on an enforcement action sits in `FILED` or `OPPOSED`, money may not be booked against that action. A payment naming that `enforcement_action_id` is refused with `ERR_EXEMPTION_PENDING` until every claim on it has resolved.

### 3.7. Negotiated Resolution: Offers, Counters and Installment Agreements

Most judgments are settled rather than collected in full, and the negotiation runs as an alternating chain of offers. A `CaseManager`, a `Creditor` or a `Debtor` may tender one, naming the `judgment_id` under negotiation, `offered_by` to say which side is tendering — `CREDITOR` or `DEBTOR` — `kind`, which is `LUMP_SUM` or `INSTALLMENT`, `amount_cents`, the total tendered, which must be strictly greater than zero, and `expires_at`, the ISO 8601 UTC instant after which the sweep will kill the offer. Three optional fields shape the deal: `installment_count`, the number of monthly instalments, which defaults to 1, must be at least 1 and no more than 60, and is forced to 1 whenever `kind` is `LUMP_SUM`; `application_order_concession`, which is `STATUTORY_ORDER` or `PRINCIPAL_FIRST` and defaults to `STATUTORY_ORDER`, recording the payment-application order the creditor concedes if the offer is taken; and `responds_to_offer_id`, which points at the offer this one counters and is absent on the opening offer of a negotiation. Every tender carries `expected_judgment_version` as described in Section 2.7.

The chain is disciplined in two ways. `round_number` is 1 for an opening offer and one more than the parent offer's `round_number` for a counter, and a counter whose `round_number` would exceed 4 is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` — four rounds is the whole negotiation. Sides must alternate: a counter whose `offered_by` equals the `offered_by` of the offer it responds to is refused with `ERR_SAME_PARTY_COUNTER`. The parent offer must still be `OPEN` — countering one that has left that state is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and tendering the counter moves it `OPEN → COUNTERED` and stamps its `decided_at` with the current logical instant, rendered in the canonical three-fractional-digit `Z` form of Section 2.4 rather than reproducing whatever characters last set the clock. Because a counter closes one offer and opens another in the same breath, the judgment's `open_offer_count` is unchanged by a counter; it is recomputed on every tender, and the judgment's `version` is incremented by one.

At creation the offer snapshots two derived figures. `monthly_cents` is `amount_cents` divided by `installment_count`, rounded **up** to a whole cent. `min_acceptable_cents` is the settlement floor: take a payoff replay of the judgment as of the current clock date, multiply its `total_payoff_cents` by 6,000 basis points — sixty percent — divide by ten thousand, and round down to a whole cent. That floor is frozen at creation and never re-quoted; interest that accrues between tender and acceptance does not move it.

Acceptance is deliberately cross-party. The accepting party must be on the side that did **not** tender the offer, and side is fixed by role: `CaseManager`, `ComplianceOfficer` and `Creditor` all act for the creditor side, while `Debtor` acts for the debtor side. An offer whose `offered_by` is `CREDITOR` may therefore be accepted only by a `Debtor`, and an offer whose `offered_by` is `DEBTOR` only by a `CaseManager`, a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `Creditor`; accepting one's own side's offer is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The judgment must be `ACTIVE`. The offer's `amount_cents` must be at least its snapshotted `min_acceptable_cents`, and a shortfall is `ERR_SETTLEMENT_BELOW_FLOOR` — except when the acting role is `ComplianceOfficer`, who alone may take a deal below the floor.

Acceptance moves the offer `OPEN → ACCEPTED`, stamps `decided_at`, decrements the judgment's `open_offer_count` by one, and sets off the longest cascade in the system. First an installment agreement is created in status `ACTIVE`, carrying the accepted offer's `judgment_id` and `settlement_offer_id`, with `total_cents` copied from the offer's `amount_cents`, `installment_count` copied from the offer, `monthly_cents` equal to `total_cents` divided by `installment_count` rounded up to a whole cent, `final_installment_cents` equal to `total_cents` less `monthly_cents` multiplied by one fewer than `installment_count`, `first_due_on` set to 30 days after the current clock date, `application_order_concession` copied from the offer, and `paid_to_date_cents`, `missed_count` and `reinstatement_count` all at 0. This agreement is never created directly by a client and is discoverable only by listing agreements and filtering on `judgment_id` or `settlement_offer_id`.

Second, because an agreement has become `ACTIVE`, the judgment's `application_order` is set to that agreement's `application_order_concession` and, **if and only if** the `enforcement_posture` is currently `ENFORCING`, the posture moves `ENFORCING → STAYED`. A judgment sitting at `NONE` or `LIEN_PERFECTED` when an agreement activates keeps its posture unchanged — there is no served writ to stay — but its `application_order` still switches to the concession. Third, when the posture does become `STAYED`, every enforcement action on the judgment in status `SERVED` moves to `SUSPENDED` in `created_at` ascending order, holding its frozen funds. Fourth, every **other** settlement offer on the same judgment still in `OPEN` moves `OPEN → REJECTED`, in `created_at` ascending order, each stamping its `decided_at` and decrementing `open_offer_count`. Finally the judgment's `version` is incremented by one.

An offer can also be declined outright without a counter, moving it `OPEN → REJECTED`, stamping `decided_at` and decrementing `open_offer_count`; `CaseManager`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `Creditor` and `Debtor` may all do this. And an offer nobody answers dies on the clock: the sweep moves any `OPEN` offer whose `expires_at` is at or before the new logical instant along `OPEN → EXPIRED`, stamping `decided_at` and decrementing `open_offer_count`. `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED`, `EXPIRED` and `COUNTERED` are all terminal for an offer.

Living with an agreement is where the cycle appears. A `CaseManager` records a missed instalment; each recording increments `missed_count` by one, and the third miss — the miss that brings `missed_count` to 3 — defaults the agreement, moving it `ACTIVE → DEFAULTED`. That default returns the judgment's `enforcement_posture` `STAYED → ENFORCING`, reverts `application_order` to `STATUTORY_ORDER`, resumes every `SUSPENDED` enforcement action on the judgment to `SERVED` in `created_at` ascending order, replays the judgment's balances under the reverted application order — which genuinely changes `principal_balance_cents` and `accrued_interest_cents`, since the two orders produce different histories — and increments the judgment's `version` by one.

A `ComplianceOfficer` may reinstate a defaulted agreement, but only once: `reinstatement_count` must be strictly less than 1, and a second attempt is `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_LIMIT`. The judgment must be `ACTIVE`. Reinstatement resets `missed_count` to 0, increments `reinstatement_count` by one, moves the agreement `DEFAULTED → ACTIVE`, returns the posture `ENFORCING → STAYED`, re-applies the agreement's `application_order_concession` to the judgment's `application_order`, suspends every `SERVED` enforcement action on the judgment in `created_at` ascending order, and increments the judgment's `version` by one. The agreement may therefore cycle `ACTIVE → DEFAULTED → ACTIVE` exactly once.

Two terminal outcomes remain. An agreement moves `ACTIVE → COMPLETED` when its `paid_to_date_cents` reaches or exceeds `total_cents`; at that instant the judgment's `forgiven_cents` is set to whatever `total_payoff_cents` the replay still shows outstanding — the balance the creditor has agreed to write off — and the judgment's own `status` moves `ACTIVE → SATISFIED`, which releases the county filings and enforcement actions as described in Section 3.9. An agreement moves `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` when the owning judgment expires under the sweep.

### 3.8. Applying Money: the Chronological Replay, Contingency Accounting and Reversal

Money arrives through several channels and is recorded the same way each time. A `CaseManager` or a `Debtor` books a payment naming the `judgment_id` credited, `amount_cents`, the gross amount received, which must be strictly greater than zero, `received_on`, the calendar date the money actually arrived, and `source`, which is `DEBTOR_DIRECT`, `GARNISHMENT`, `LEVY` or `SETTLEMENT`. The payment may additionally be attributed to exactly one producer: `installment_agreement_id` when it is an instalment under a settlement, or `enforcement_action_id` when it is a garnishment or levy remittance. A payment begins in `APPLIED`. `received_on` must fall on or after the judgment's `entered_on` and on or before the current clock date, inclusive at both ends, and anything else is `ERR_PAYMENT_DATE_INVALID`. Payments may be recorded out of chronological order, which is precisely why `received_on` rather than arrival order is the replay key.

The heart of the system is the **payoff replay**, which reconstructs every balance from first principles rather than adjusting them incrementally. It takes two inputs. The reference date is the `as_of` value when a caller asks for a quote and the current clock date otherwise; every persisting recompute uses the current clock date and writes it into the judgment's `accrued_through_on`. The application order is the `application_order` supplied on a quote request, and otherwise the judgment's own `application_order`.

The event list is assembled from two sources: every payment on this judgment whose `status` is `APPLIED`, keyed on its `received_on`, and every date in the judgment's `renewal_dates`. Any event falling after the reference date is dropped entirely — a quote as of an earlier date must ignore money that arrived later. The surviving events are sorted by date ascending; where a payment and a renewal share a date, the payment is applied first and the capitalization second; where two payments share a date, `created_at` ascending breaks the tie, never the identifier.

The replay then walks that list. Principal starts at `original_principal_cents`. Costs start at `filing_costs_cents` plus every recorded county filing fee, plus every sheriff's fee charged at service — and nothing else. Renewal fees are deliberately **not** seeded here, because the walk itself adds 5,000 cents as it reaches each renewal date below; seeding them as well would charge every renewal twice. Interest starts at zero, and a cursor starts at `entered_on`. For each event in turn, the number of whole days from the cursor up to but not including the event date is computed; when that count is greater than zero, interest for the segment is the current principal multiplied by `interest_rate_bps`, multiplied by the number of days, divided by ten thousand and then by a day basis of 365, **rounded half up to a whole cent and added to the running interest balance**, after which the cursor advances to the event date. If the event is a payment, its amount is consumed bucket by bucket: under `STATUTORY_ORDER` the buckets are taken in the order costs, then accrued interest, then principal; under `PRINCIPAL_FIRST` they are taken in the order principal, then accrued interest, then costs. Each bucket absorbs the smaller of the amount still unconsumed and that bucket's current balance, that balance falls by the amount absorbed, and the remainder moves to the next bucket; the amount is always fully consumed, which is guaranteed by the rule that a payment may never exceed the payoff. If the event is a renewal, the entire accrued interest balance is added to principal, the interest balance is set to zero, and 5,000 cents of renewal fee are added to costs. After the final event, one more segment is accrued from the cursor to the reference date under the same rule.

Three properties of that procedure are load-bearing. **Interest accrues on principal only** — never on unpaid costs, and never on unpaid interest except through a renewal capitalization event. **Rounding happens once per segment, before accumulation**; summing exact fractional segment interest and rounding a single time at the end is a different and wrong answer. And **segment boundaries are only the events, plus `entered_on` and the reference date** — adding taxed costs never opens a segment, because costs earn no interest. When the walk finishes, `total_payoff_cents` is principal plus interest plus costs, and `gross_exposure_cents` is that sum plus `collected_to_date_cents`. The same identical payment history produces a different `total_payoff_cents` under the two application orders, because `PRINCIPAL_FIRST` drives principal down faster and so accrues less interest in later segments while leaving unpaid interest and costs behind, whereas `STATUTORY_ORDER` clears costs and interest first and so keeps principal — and therefore future interest — higher for longer.

Anyone with an interest in the file may pull a **non-persisting payoff quote** — `CaseManager`, `EnforcementOfficer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `Creditor` and `Debtor` all may. The quote accepts `as_of`, which defaults to the current clock date, and `application_order`, which defaults to the judgment's own and lets a caller price the other variant against an identical payment history. It answers with `as_of`, `application_order`, `principal_balance_cents`, `accrued_interest_cents`, `costs_balance_cents`, `total_payoff_cents`, `collected_to_date_cents`, `gross_exposure_cents`, and `segment_count`, the number of interest segments the walk produced. The echoed `as_of` comes back normalised to a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date whatever form it arrived in, and `collected_to_date_cents` is the judgment's stored figure rather than one re-derived as of the reference date, so the quoted `gross_exposure_cents` is the quoted `total_payoff_cents` plus that stored collection. It stores nothing.

Booking a payment runs that replay as a persisting recompute and writes this payment's own split into `applied_to_costs_cents`, `applied_to_interest_cents` and `applied_to_principal_cents`. It also refuses money the judgment cannot absorb: `amount_cents` may not exceed the `total_payoff_cents` produced by a replay whose reference date is the payment's own `received_on` — a point-in-time test, not a test against today's balance — and an overpayment is `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_PAYOFF`. When `enforcement_action_id` is supplied, no exemption claim on that action may still be `FILED` or `OPPOSED`, on pain of `ERR_EXEMPTION_PENDING`; the action's `collected_cents` rises by the amount; the action moves `SERVED → COLLECTED` when the payment's `source` is `GARNISHMENT`, or, for a `BANK_LEVY`, once `amount_cents` plus the action's prior `collected_cents` reaches `levy_hold_cents`; and on collection the levy hold shifts from the finding's `frozen_cents` into its `swept_cents`. When `installment_agreement_id` is supplied, the agreement's `paid_to_date_cents` rises by the amount and the agreement completes once `total_cents` is reached. If the replayed `total_payoff_cents` reaches zero, the judgment moves `ACTIVE → SATISFIED`. The judgment's `version` is incremented by one.

Every booking and every reversal also recomputes the firm's **contingency accounting** from scratch, never incrementally. Take every `APPLIED` payment on the judgment, ordered by `received_on` ascending and then `created_at` ascending, and sum their amounts; that sum is `collected_to_date_cents`. The fee tier is selected from that gross figure **before any cost reimbursement**: 4,000 basis points when the gross collection is below 1,000,000 cents, 3,300 basis points when it is below 5,000,000 cents, and 2,500 basis points at or above 5,000,000 cents — the boundaries are exclusive on the upper end of each lower tier, so a gross of exactly 5,000,000 cents sits in the top tier. Each payment's own `contingency_fee_cents` is then its `amount_cents` multiplied by that tier rate and divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to a whole cent, and the judgment's `contingency_fee_cents` is the sum of those per-payment slices. Only then are advanced costs reimbursed: the judgment's `net_to_creditor_cents` is `collected_to_date_cents` less `contingency_fee_cents` less `filing_costs_cents`. The evaluation order is pinned and the apparent circularity is illusory — the tier depends only on the gross collection and nothing downstream feeds back into it. Keying the tier on the gross less filing costs is a different and wrong answer, and multiplying the aggregate gross by the tier rate and rounding once instead of rounding each payment is likewise a different and wrong answer.

Reversal is the compensating action, available to a `ComplianceOfficer` and requiring a `reason` in the payload. The payment must be `APPLIED` and the judgment must be `ACTIVE`. Reversal moves the payment `APPLIED → REVERSED`, stamps `reversal_reason` and `reversed_on` with the current clock date, and removes the payment from the replay input set entirely; the judgment's balances are then replayed from `entered_on` over the remaining `APPLIED` payments to the current clock date. It is never patched by an inverse delta, which would leave the interest segments wrong. Because `collected_to_date_cents` falls, `contingency_fee_cents` and `net_to_creditor_cents` are recomputed from scratch — and the tier itself may drop, changing every surviving payment's own slice. If the reversed payment carried an `enforcement_action_id`, that action's `collected_cents` is reduced and, if the action had reached `COLLECTED`, it returns `COLLECTED → SERVED` and the judgment's `active_enforcement_count` is recomputed and thereby rises by one; the asset custody split is not rewound, so cents already moved into `swept_cents` stay swept and the action's `levy_hold_cents` stays at 0. If it carried an `installment_agreement_id`, that agreement's `paid_to_date_cents` is reduced. The judgment's `version` is incremented by one. `REVERSED` is terminal; a reversed payment is never reinstated.

### 3.9. Renewal, Capitalization and the End of the Judgment

A judgment is not immortal. It lives for ten calendar years from `entered_on`, and unless it is renewed it dies on `expires_on` no matter how much is still owed. Renewal is a `ComplianceOfficer` privilege and takes no payload at all. It is permitted only inside a window that opens 180 days before `expires_on` and closes the day `expires_on` itself arrives: the current clock date must be on or after the date 180 days before `expires_on` — the lower bound is inclusive, so exactly 180 days before expiry is the first legal day — and strictly before `expires_on`. An attempt made earlier than that window is `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. An attempt made once the clock has reached `expires_on` does not surface as a window error at all, because the sweep will already have moved the judgment to `EXPIRED`; it surfaces as `ERR_JUDGMENT_EXPIRED`. A judgment may be renewed at most twice — `renewal_count` must be strictly less than 2 — and a third attempt is `ERR_RENEWAL_LIMIT_REACHED`.

Renewal begins by replaying the balances to the renewal date, which is the current clock date, so that `accrued_interest_cents` is current. It then **capitalizes**: the entire `accrued_interest_cents` figure is added to `principal_balance_cents` and `accrued_interest_cents` is set to zero. This is an exact integer move with no rounding, and it conserves the sum of principal and interest — only the split changes — but it changes everything that follows, because interest accrues on principal alone and the capitalized interest now earns interest itself. A renewal fee of 5,000 cents is added to `costs_balance_cents`. The renewal date is appended to `renewal_dates`, where it becomes a permanent capitalization boundary in every future replay, so a second renewal re-walks the first one. `expires_on` is pushed out a further ten calendar years, `renewal_count` is incremented by one, `accrued_through_on` is set to the renewal date, and the judgment's `version` is incremented by one.

When renewal does not happen in time, the sweep's first step ends the judgment. Every `ACTIVE` judgment whose `expires_on` is on or before the new clock date moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED`. Its `enforcement_posture` is driven to `NONE` from wherever it stood — `LIEN_PERFECTED → NONE`, `ENFORCING → NONE` or `STAYED → NONE`. Every county filing on it in status `RECORDED` moves `RECORDED → LAPSED`, in `created_at` ascending order, driving `perfected_county_count` to 0. Every enforcement action on it in `WRIT_REQUESTED`, `SERVED` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `RELEASED`, again in `created_at` ascending order, while actions still in `DRAFT` are left untouched; each release credits that action's `levy_hold_cents` back to its finding's `available_cents` and out of `frozen_cents`, after which the judgment's `active_enforcement_count` stands at 0. Every installment agreement on it in `ACTIVE` moves to `TERMINATED`. The creditor's `active_judgment_count` is decremented by one, and the judgment's `version` is incremented by one.

Satisfaction is the happier ending and cascades in parallel. A judgment moves `ACTIVE → SATISFIED` at the end of a payment booking or a payment reversal, whenever the replayed balances leave `principal_balance_cents`, `accrued_interest_cents` and `costs_balance_cents` all at zero, and also when an installment agreement completes. Its `enforcement_posture` is driven to `NONE` in the same way, as `LIEN_PERFECTED → NONE`, `ENFORCING → NONE` or `STAYED → NONE`. Every `RECORDED` county filing on it moves `RECORDED → RELEASED`, in `created_at` ascending order, and because those filings have left `RECORDED` the judgment's `perfected_county_count` is recomputed to 0. Every enforcement action in `WRIT_REQUESTED`, `SERVED` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `RELEASED` in `created_at` ascending order, each returning its `levy_hold_cents` to its finding, and `active_enforcement_count` is recomputed to 0. Every `ACTIVE` installment agreement moves to `COMPLETED`. The creditor's `active_judgment_count` is decremented by one, and the judgment's `version` is incremented by one. Both `SATISFIED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal; no operation revives a judgment from either, and every write endpoint touching a dead judgment or any of its children answers `ERR_JUDGMENT_SATISFIED` or `ERR_JUDGMENT_EXPIRED` as appropriate.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

All collections are mounted at the root of the service; there is no nesting in URL paths and no API prefix. Endpoints that bring a new resource into existence answer with HTTP 201 and the created object; every other successful call answers with HTTP 200 and, unless the description says otherwise, the object the call modified. Sub-resource actions that only change the state of an existing row — submitting a filing, serving a writ, accepting an offer, reversing a payment — are modifications and therefore answer 200, even though they are expressed as POST. Where such a call also mutates related rows, the object returned in `data` is always the resource named in the path — the county filing for an adjudication, the enforcement action for a service, the settlement offer for an acceptance, the installment agreement for a recorded miss, the debtor for a skip trace — never the judgment or the cascade-created row the side effects reached.
