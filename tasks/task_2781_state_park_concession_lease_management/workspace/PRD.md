# State Park Concession Lease Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A state parks system does not run its lodges, marinas, dining rooms and guided-activity programs itself. It leases them. Each lease is a long-dated commercial contract in which a private concessionaire is granted the exclusive right to serve the visiting public at a specific park, in exchange for a percentage of everything it collects, a floor on that percentage expressed as a minimum annual guarantee, a schedule of capital improvements it must build at its own expense, and a bond that the agency may cash if the concessionaire stops performing. The money moves in both directions and rarely in a straight line: the concessionaire remits a fee every reporting period, the agency reconciles those remittances against a tiered annual ladder once the lease year closes, an auditor may later substantiate a different receipts figure and force the whole year to be re-priced with penalty and interest, and at the end of the term the agency may owe the outgoing concessionaire for the undepreciated portion of the buildings it leaves behind.

Today that lifecycle is administered on spreadsheets, and the failures are predictable. A solicitation is awarded to an offeror whose surety cannot write the bond the term requires. Two construction closures land on the same park in the same summer. A lease year is reconciled twice from two different views of the ledger and the second overwrites figures the first never saw. An audit is finalized, a deficiency notice is issued, the findings are later withdrawn, and nobody can say which invoices are still live. This API exists to make each of those steps an explicit, ordered, auditable state transition on a persisted record, so that the answer to "what does this concessionaire owe us right now" is a field on a row rather than an argument between two departments.

The service is a headless, deterministic HTTP API. Every rule below is a hard requirement: the states, the ordering of validation checks, the rounding locations inside every money formula, and the exact cascade an operation triggers across sibling records. Nothing depends on wall-clock time — a server-controlled logical clock is the only source of "now" — so a full ten-year concession term can be exercised end to end in a single test run.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Program Roles and Request Identity

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting under. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names defined below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being called — the authorized roles for each endpoint are listed in §4 — the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). Authentication is always evaluated before authorization, and both are evaluated before anything else in the request is examined.

The six roles are `ProgramAdministrator`, `ConcessionSpecialist`, `ContractingOfficer`, `EvaluationPanelist`, `AuditorGeneral` and `Concessionaire`.

A `ProgramAdministrator` is the statewide concessions program administrator: it maintains the park register and the offeror register, debars offerors, and positions the logical clock. A `ConcessionSpecialist` is program staff — it identifies opportunities, performs the market analysis, drafts and issues the prospectus, closes the solicitation, and shepherds proposals through review; it can neither approve an opportunity for solicitation nor award one. A `ContractingOfficer` is the warranted officer: it approves solicitation, awards, executes and then administers the live lease, which covers operating-plan and capital-improvement approvals, acceptance of receipts reports, the annual reconciliation, compliance notices and bond draws. An `EvaluationPanelist` is a subject-matter scorer who files exactly one score sheet per proposal. An `AuditorGeneral` is the independent auditor exercising the lease's audit-rights clause. A `Concessionaire` is the offeror side of every contract.

For `ProgramAdministrator`, `ConcessionSpecialist`, `ContractingOfficer`, `EvaluationPanelist` and `AuditorGeneral`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any row's `id`. It is nevertheless recorded where a transition says so: the officer who awards a lease is stored as `awarded_by_user_id`, the officer who executes it as `executed_by_user_id`, and the panelist who files a score sheet as `panelist_user_id`. Those recorded identifiers are what the segregation-of-duties rules later compare against the acting `X-User-ID`; they are never resolved to an entity.

For `Concessionaire`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Offeror`. Ownership checks compare that value to the offeror that owns the target resource: for proposal routes, the proposal's `offeror_id`; for a performance bond, capital improvement, operating plan, gross receipts report, fee reconciliation or audit engagement, the `offeror_id` of the lease reached through the resource's `lease_id`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Ownership is checked after role authorization but before the request body is validated, so a `Concessionaire` reaching for another offeror's lease is turned away regardless of what it sent.

Read access is broad and write access is narrow. Every role may call every `GET` endpoint, with one exception: the two score-sheet read endpoints are closed to `Concessionaire` — panel score sheets are not disclosed to offerors — while `ProgramAdministrator`, `ConcessionSpecialist`, `ContractingOfficer`, `EvaluationPanelist` and `AuditorGeneral` may all read them.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Filters

Every list endpoint is paginated by two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when absent, may not be smaller than 1 and may not be larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. Results are ordered by `created_at` ascending on every list endpoint; where two rows share a `created_at`, insertion order breaks the tie. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` as they were applied, `total_count` as the number of rows matching the filters before pagination, and `has_more` as true when rows remain beyond the current page.

Because the resource graph is flat — every collection is addressed at the top level — the way to reach a record's children is to filter the child collection. Each list endpoint therefore exposes the foreign keys of its own entity as optional equality filters, alongside a status filter. Parks may be narrowed by `region_code` and by `status`. Concession opportunities accept `park_id`, `status` and `service_category`. Prospectuses accept `opportunity_id` and `status`; offerors accept `status`; proposals accept `prospectus_id`, `offeror_id` and `status`; score sheets accept `proposal_id`. Leases accept `park_id`, `prospectus_id`, `offeror_id`, `selected_proposal_id`, `predecessor_lease_id`, `performance_bond_id`, and — because a lease carries two independent status dimensions — `contract_status` and `compliance_status` separately. Performance bonds, operating plans and audit engagements each accept `lease_id` and `status`. Capital improvements accept `lease_id`, `park_id` and `status`. Gross receipts reports accept `lease_id`, `operating_plan_id` and `status`. Fee reconciliations accept `lease_id`, `audit_engagement_id`, `superseded_by_id` and `status`. Compliance actions accept `lease_id`, `reconciliation_id`, `status` and `origin`. Unrecognized query parameters are ignored.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

The server maintains a logical clock, and that clock is the only source of "now" anywhere in this specification. Every submission deadline, cure deadline, term boundary, insurance expiry, reconciliation window and interest computation reads it. Before it has ever been repositioned the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and under no circumstance does the implementation fall back to the host's wall-clock time.

A `ProgramAdministrator` repositions the clock through `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, supplying the target instant as `current_utc`. The call returns 200 and mutates no entity: no `status` field moves, no `version` counter advances, and no cascade fires. Moving the clock backwards is permitted. Where a rule compares a date-only field against the clock, the comparison uses the clock's UTC calendar date.

### 2.5. Record Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every persisted record carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix — clients never supply it — and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with exactly three fractional-second digits. Most records additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and an integer `version`. A newly created record has `version` 1 and `updated_at` equal to `created_at`; every subsequent mutation of that record advances `version` by exactly one and refreshes `updated_at` to the logical clock instant at which the mutation was applied. This is true of mutations a client requests directly and equally true of mutations a cascade applies to a record the client never named: when awarding a solicitation drags a losing proposal to `NOT_SELECTED`, that proposal's `version` advances too.

The single exception is the panel score sheet. A `ProposalEvaluation` is append-only and immutable, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all. Nothing in the system ever rewrites a filed score sheet.

Field visibility follows a simple rule: every field named in this document, whether the client supplied it, the system stamped it, or a formula computed it, is present in the resource object returned for that entity. Computed and system fields that have not yet been established are returned as null rather than omitted.

### 2.6. Money, Percentages and Rounding

All monetary amounts in this API are integer counts of cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere in a stored value, in a request, or in a response. Every money formula that divides — every percentage application, every proration, every amortization — rounds its result to a whole number of cents using round-half-up (a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero, so 1,250.5 cents becomes 1,251 cents).

Percentages are decimal numbers carrying at most two decimal places, and a percentage is always applied by multiplying by the percentage figure and then dividing by one hundred. `base_fee_pct`, `offered_franchise_fee_pct`, `minimum_franchise_fee_pct`, `market_capture_pct`, `min_technical_score`, `composite_score`, `underreport_pct` and `financial_capacity_ratio` are all two-decimal figures; the five prospectus evaluation weights are whole percentage points. A request carrying a percentage with three or more decimals, or one outside the bound stated for that field, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Where a formula sums several rounded components, **the rounding location is part of the rule and is never optimized away**. The period franchise fee rounds each of its four category products to whole cents and then adds the four rounded values; summing the raw products and rounding once at the end is wrong and will differ by one or two cents. The annual tier ladder rounds each of its three tier slices and then adds the three rounded values. The bond requirement rounds each of its two scaled components before comparing them and rounds again after applying the long-term multiplier. Per-criterion score contributions round twice under one scoring mode and once under the other. Each of these locations is stated explicitly where the formula is described.

### 2.7. Stale Lease Version Rejection

Two write operations take the lease's `version` as an input and refuse to proceed against a stale view of it. In both cases the client must send `expected_lease_version` — a required, non-optional integer field carrying the `version` value the client last observed on the `Lease` named by the request's `lease_id`. If that number does not equal the lease's current `version`, the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LEASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is created or modified.

The first is the creation of a fee reconciliation. A second reconciliation for the same lease year is a legitimate operation — it supersedes the first — so the write cannot be refused on the basis of state alone; the version check is what stops a writer whose picture of the lease ledger predates the supersede from re-basing the year on figures it never saw. The second is the submission of an operating plan. A newly submitted plan is legitimate from any active lease, so no state precondition covers a client working from an outdated view of the lease's season calendar, rate ceilings or compliance posture; requiring the version forces that client to re-read the lease first.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Both of these writes advance `Lease.version` by one when they succeed, which means a client performing two of them in sequence must re-read the lease between them.

### 2.8. Interval Conventions

Two interval conventions coexist in this domain and they are not interchangeable.

**Half-open intervals** — construction closure windows, gross receipts reporting periods and audit windows — run from an inclusive start to an exclusive end. `construction_window_end` is the day the site reopens; `period_end` is the day after the last day reported; `audit_period_end` is the day after the last day audited. A half-open interval requires its start to be strictly before its end. Two half-open intervals that merely touch — one ending on the same day the next begins — are adjacent, not overlapping.

**Closed intervals** — operating seasons and lease years — run from an inclusive start to an inclusive end. `season_end` is the last day of the season and `lease_year_end` is the last day of the lease year. A closed interval requires its start to be at or before its end. Two closed intervals that share even a single calendar day overlap, and touching endpoints therefore do collide: a season ending 2027-09-30 and a season starting 2027-09-30 are in conflict.

Any interval violating its own ordering requirement is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (HTTP 422). All dates in this API are `YYYY-MM-DD` strings unless the field is described as an instant, in which case it is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp; `submission_deadline` and `cure_deadline` are instants, everything else is a date.

### 2.9. The Validation Ladder

Most operations in this system have several ways to fail, and which failure a client sees is not left to implementation convenience. Checks are grouped into ordered bands, evaluated strictly from the lowest band upward, and **the lowest-numbered failing check is the one reported**. A request that violates a fiscal limit and also names a lease in the wrong state always comes back with the state error, never the fiscal one. Every precondition listed against a state transition in §3 is written in this same order.

The bands, in evaluation order, are:

1. **Authentication** — both headers present and `X-User-Role` a declared role, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorization** — the acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized list, else `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path resource existence** — the `{id}` path segment resolves to a row of the addressed collection, else the collection's own not-found code: `ERR_PARK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OPPORTUNITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROSPECTUS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFEROR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_IMPROVEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OPERATING_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECONCILIATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_COMPLIANCE_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`. All are HTTP 404 and each covers the single-resource read plus every action route hanging off that resource.
4. **Body foreign-key existence** — every reference carried in the request body resolves, and references are resolved left to right in the order the fields are listed for that endpoint. A `park_id` that does not resolve on opportunity creation yields `ERR_PARK_NOT_FOUND`; an `opportunity_id` on prospectus creation yields `ERR_OPPORTUNITY_NOT_FOUND`; on proposal creation `prospectus_id` is resolved first and yields `ERR_PROSPECTUS_NOT_FOUND`, and only then is `offeror_id` resolved, yielding `ERR_OFFEROR_NOT_FOUND`; a `lease_id` on capital-improvement, operating-plan, gross-receipts-report, fee-reconciliation, audit-engagement or compliance-action creation yields `ERR_LEASE_NOT_FOUND`; a supplied `reconciliation_id` on compliance-action creation must resolve to a reconciliation whose `lease_id` equals the body's `lease_id`, else `ERR_RECONCILIATION_NOT_FOUND`; a supplied `successor_lease_id` at close-out must resolve to a lease other than the one in the path, else `ERR_LEASE_NOT_FOUND`.
5. **Resource ownership** — when the acting role is `Concessionaire`, `X-User-ID` must equal the owning offeror's `id`, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. This band guards proposal creation, resubmission and withdrawal, bond posting and reinstatement, capital-improvement creation, resubmission and place-in-service, operating-plan creation and resubmission, gross-receipts-report creation and amendment, reconciliation settlement, and the contesting of audit findings.
6. **Request shape** — every required field present and non-null, every enum value a declared member, every `*_cents` field an integer inside its declared bound, every `*_pct` field carrying at most two decimals inside its declared bound, every date field in `YYYY-MM-DD` form and every instant field a valid ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Unknown fields are ignored rather than rejected. Applies to every write endpoint.
7. **Weight normalization** — the five prospectus evaluation weights total exactly 100, else `ERR_WEIGHTS_NOT_NORMALIZED` (HTTP 422).
8. **Interval sanity** — the ordering rules of §2.8, else `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. Applies to capital-improvement creation and resubmission, operating-plan creation and resubmission, gross-receipts-report creation, fee-reconciliation creation and audit-engagement creation.
9. **Lease version currency** — `expected_lease_version` equals the lease's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_LEASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Applies to fee-reconciliation creation and operating-plan creation.
10. **The path resource's own status** — the addressed row's own state admits the requested transition, else that entity's status code: `ERR_OPPORTUNITY_STATUS_INVALID`, `ERR_PROSPECTUS_STATUS_INVALID`, `ERR_OFFEROR_STATUS_INVALID`, `ERR_PROPOSAL_STATUS_INVALID`, `ERR_LEASE_STATUS_INVALID`, `ERR_BOND_STATUS_INVALID`, `ERR_IMPROVEMENT_STATUS_INVALID`, `ERR_OPERATING_PLAN_STATUS_INVALID`, `ERR_REPORT_STATUS_INVALID`, `ERR_RECONCILIATION_STATUS_INVALID`, `ERR_AUDIT_STATUS_INVALID` or `ERR_COMPLIANCE_ACTION_STATUS_INVALID`, all HTTP 409.
11. **The referenced lease's contract posture** — `ERR_LEASE_NOT_ACTIVE` when a capital improvement, operating plan, gross receipts report or fee reconciliation is filed against a lease whose `contract_status` is not `ACTIVE`, and equally when an improvement or a plan is approved; `ERR_LEASE_STATUS_INVALID` when a compliance action names a lease outside `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER`, or an audit engagement names a lease outside `ACTIVE`, `HOLDOVER` or `EXPIRED`.
12. **Opportunity clearance** — `ERR_OPPORTUNITY_NOT_APPROVED` when the prospectus being issued hangs off an opportunity that has not reached `APPROVED_FOR_SOLICITATION`.
13. **Bond posture** — `ERR_BOND_NOT_ACTIVE` when the lease's bond is not `ACTIVE` at lease execution, or is neither `ACTIVE` nor `PARTIALLY_DRAWN` at an improvement approval or a bond draw.
14. **Offeror standing** — `ERR_OFFEROR_DEBARRED` when the offeror named in a new proposal is not `ELIGIBLE`.
15. **Audit interference** — `ERR_AUDIT_IN_PROGRESS` when a lease close-out is attempted while any audit on the lease is `OPENED`, `FIELDWORK`, `FINDINGS_ISSUED` or `CONTESTED`.
16. **Successor already running** — `ERR_SUCCESSOR_ALREADY_ACTIVE` when holdover is requested on a lease that already has an `ACTIVE` successor naming it as predecessor.
17. **Audit settlement** — `ERR_AUDIT_ALREADY_SETTLED` when findings are withdrawn but the reconciliation the audit minted is no longer `PENDING_PAYMENT`.
18. **Reconciliation coverage** — `ERR_NO_RECONCILIATION_FOR_PERIOD` when an audit is finalized and no `PENDING_PAYMENT` or `SETTLED` reconciliation exists for the lease year the audit window falls in.
19. **Audit substance** — `ERR_AUDIT_PERIOD_EMPTY` when fieldwork begins and no `ACCEPTED` report lies inside the audit window.
20. **Evaluation not yet started** — `ERR_EVALUATION_ALREADY_STARTED` when a revision is requested on a proposal that already carries at least one score sheet.
21. **Panel capacity** — `ERR_PANEL_ALREADY_COMPLETE` when a score sheet is filed on a proposal whose panel is already complete.
22. **Season coverage** — `ERR_NO_APPROVED_OPERATING_PLAN` when a reporting period is not fully covered by an `APPROVED` season on the same lease.
23. **Responsiveness** — `ERR_NO_RESPONSIVE_PROPOSAL` (HTTP 422) when an award is attempted and the candidate set is empty.
24. **Insurance in force** — `ERR_INSURANCE_LAPSED` when the certificate of insurance is not strictly beyond the clock's date: the submitted `insurance_expires_on` at lease execution, and the lease's stored `insurance_expires_on` at a capital-improvement approval.
25. **Segregation of duties** — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the acting officer performed the paired step: at award, when any score sheet on any proposal of that prospectus carries `panelist_user_id` equal to the acting `X-User-ID`; at execution, when `awarded_by_user_id` equals the acting `X-User-ID`.
26. `ERR_SUBMISSION_WINDOW_CLOSED` — the clock must be strictly before the prospectus `submission_deadline` to issue the prospectus, to submit a proposal, or to resubmit one.
27. `ERR_SUBMISSION_WINDOW_OPEN` — the clock must be at or after `submission_deadline` before the solicitation may be closed.
28. `ERR_TERM_NOT_ELAPSED` — the clock's date must be at or after `term_end` to enter holdover, and strictly after `term_end` to expire the lease.
29. `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_OPEN` — the clock must be strictly after `cure_deadline` before a bond draw may be ordered.
30. `ERR_RECONCILIATION_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED` — the clock's date must be strictly after the requested `lease_year_end` before that year may be reconciled.
31. `ERR_IN_SERVICE_BEFORE_WINDOW` — the reported `in_service_date` must be at or after `construction_window_start`.
32. `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_TERM` (HTTP 422) — a reporting period must sit inside the contract term.
33. `ERR_CONSTRUCTION_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_TERM` (HTTP 422) — a closure window must sit inside the contract term, checked on both improvement creation and improvement resubmission.
34. `ERR_SEASON_OVERLAP` — an approved season may not share a calendar day with another `APPROVED` season on the same lease.
35. `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_OVERLAP` — a new reporting period may not intersect a live report on the same lease.
36. `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROPOSAL` — an offeror may hold only one live proposal per prospectus.
37. `ERR_DUPLICATE_PANELIST_EVALUATION` — a panelist may file only one score sheet per proposal.
38. `ERR_PARK_CLOSURE_CAPACITY` — the park's concurrent approved-closure ceiling.
39. `ERR_RATE_EXCEEDS_CEILING` (HTTP 422) — a maximum public rate above the park's benchmark ceiling.
40. `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` — the proposal's revision allowance is exhausted.
41. `ERR_OPPORTUNITY_NOT_VIABLE` (HTTP 422) — the projected receipts are below the viability floor.
42. `ERR_FEE_BELOW_MINIMUM` (HTTP 422) — on proposal submission and resubmission.
43. `ERR_GUARANTEE_BELOW_MINIMUM` (HTTP 422) — on proposal submission and resubmission.
44. `ERR_IMPROVEMENT_EXCEEDS_OBLIGATION` (HTTP 422) — capital-funded improvement approval past the obligation.
45. `ERR_MAINTENANCE_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422) — reserve-funded improvement approval past the accrued reserve.
46. `ERR_REMITTANCE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) — the logged remittance does not equal the period's assessed fee.
47. `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) — the settlement payment does not equal the amount due.
48. `ERR_BOND_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422) — the posted penal sum is below the required amount.
49. `ERR_BOND_EXCEEDS_CAPACITY` (HTTP 422) — the posted penal sum exceeds the offeror's attested capacity.
50. `ERR_DRAW_EXCEEDS_BOND` (HTTP 422) — the ordered draw exceeds the bond's available balance.
51. `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_EXCEEDS_DRAWN` (HTTP 422) — the reinstatement is not strictly positive, or exceeds what was drawn.

The business meaning of each of these conditions, and the precise comparison it makes, is developed in §3 alongside the workflow it guards. Where an operation is subject to several bands, the operation's own narrative names them in ladder order.

### 2.10. Cascades, Consistency and Two-Dimensional Lease State

This system is immediately consistent. When one request touches several rows, every one of those rows is written before the response is produced, and a read issued immediately afterwards — by any role — observes the finished picture. There are no deferred jobs and no eventual convergence.

Cascades are described in §3 as ordered hops, and the ordering matters because later hops read what earlier hops wrote. When a bond draw is ordered, hop one records the draw amount on the notice, hop two debits the bond, hop three reads the bond's resulting status to decide whether the lease has defaulted, and hop four unwinds the defaulted lease's live plans and improvements. Two kinds of cascade appear. An **unconditional** cascade always fires when its transition fires: issuing a prospectus always advances its opportunity. A **conditional** cascade fires only when its own predicate holds: accepting a cure returns the lease to compliance only when no other notice on that lease is still outstanding, and closing out a lease releases its bond only when nothing is owed. Where a conditional cascade's predicate fails, the target row is left exactly as it was — no status change, no `version` advance, no `updated_at` refresh.

A `Lease` is unusual: it carries two independent state dimensions. `contract_status` tracks the contract's lifecycle through `PENDING_EXECUTION`, `ACTIVE`, `HOLDOVER`, `EXPIRED`, `CLOSED_OUT` and `TERMINATED_DEFAULT`. `compliance_status` tracks the concessionaire's standing through `COMPLIANT`, `DEFICIENT` and `DEFAULT`. Neither dimension is ever read as a precondition for a transition of the other, and they advance on entirely different triggers, so a lease may legitimately be `HOLDOVER` and `DEFICIENT` at the same time, or `ACTIVE` and `DEFAULT`. Both start at their initial values the moment the lease is minted: `PENDING_EXECUTION` and `COMPLIANT`. A single request may advance both, and when it does the lease's `version` still advances by exactly one.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Park Register and Opportunity Identification

Everything begins with the park. A `ProgramAdministrator` registers each state park with its `name`, its administrative `region_code` — one of `NW`, `NE`, `SW` or `SE` — and its prior-year visitor count as `annual_visitation`, which must not be negative. Three further figures are the reason the park register exists at all: `comparable_lodging_rate_cents`, `comparable_meal_rate_cents` and `comparable_activity_rate_cents` are the regional benchmark rates for a night's lodging, a meal and a guided activity, each of which must be strictly greater than zero. Those benchmarks are what later cap the prices a concessionaire may charge the visiting public. A newly registered park has `status` `OPERATING` (the alternative value being `CLOSED`) and an `open_closure_window_count` of 0.

That counter deserves an early word, because it is maintained by operations several sections away. `open_closure_window_count` is the number of capital improvements on the park whose status is `APPROVED` — in other words, the number of construction closure windows the agency currently has reserved at that park. It rises by one when an improvement is approved and falls by one whenever an approved improvement leaves that status, whether because the asset was placed in service or because a lease event withdrew it.

A `ConcessionSpecialist` opens the commercial conversation by creating a **concession opportunity** against a park: the payload names the `park_id`, gives the opportunity a `title`, declares the `service_category` — `LODGING`, `FOOD_SERVICE`, `RETAIL`, `GUIDED_ACTIVITY` or `MARINA` — and states `required_capital_investment_cents`, the capital investment the agency intends to require of whoever wins, which may be zero but not negative. The opportunity is born in `DRAFT` with no market figures at all: `comparable_gross_receipts_cents`, `market_capture_pct`, `estimated_annual_gross_receipts_cents` and `analyst_note` are all null.

The market analysis fills them in. The same specialist supplies `comparable_gross_receipts_cents`, the annual gross receipts of the closest market comparable, and `market_capture_pct`, the share of that comparable's business this site is projected to capture — a two-decimal percentage that must be strictly greater than zero and no greater than 200.00, since a site may plausibly out-perform its comparable but not without limit. An `analyst_note` narrative may accompany the figures and is optional. The projection itself is deliberately simple: `estimated_annual_gross_receipts_cents` is the comparable's receipts multiplied by `market_capture_pct` and divided by one hundred, with the result rounded half up to whole cents. No other record is consulted. Recording the analysis moves a `DRAFT` opportunity to `ANALYSIS_COMPLETE`, and on an opportunity already at `ANALYSIS_COMPLETE` it is a self-loop that leaves the status where it is while recomputing the projection from the new comparables — the analysis may be redone any number of times until an officer clears the opportunity. Either way the three supplied fields are persisted, the projection is rewritten, and the opportunity's `version` advances by one.

Clearing the opportunity for solicitation is a `ContractingOfficer` decision and moves `ANALYSIS_COMPLETE` to `APPROVED_FOR_SOLICITATION`. It takes no payload and enforces one substantive test: the projected annual gross receipts must be at least one hundred thousand dollars. A projection strictly below that viability floor is rejected with `ERR_OPPORTUNITY_NOT_VIABLE`; a projection exactly at the floor passes. The opportunity's `version` advances by one.

The remaining two hops on an opportunity are never requested directly. Issuing the solicitation drives `APPROVED_FOR_SOLICITATION` to `SOLICITED`, and awarding it drives `SOLICITED` to `AWARDED`, which is terminal. Each of those cascaded hops advances the opportunity's `version` by one, exactly as a directly requested transition would.

### 3.2. The Competitive Solicitation

The **prospectus** is the solicitation document, and drafting one is where the agency commits to the commercial and procedural terms it will hold every offeror to. A `ConcessionSpecialist` drafts it against an `opportunity_id` and states: `minimum_franchise_fee_pct`, the lowest base franchise fee percentage of gross receipts the agency will accept, greater than zero and no more than 100.00; `minimum_annual_guarantee_cents`, the floor an offeror's annual guarantee must at least match, strictly positive; `term_years`, the contract length in whole years, at least 1 and at most 30; and `submission_deadline`, the instant before which proposals must be received.

The evaluation framework is fixed in the same document. Five weights — `weight_experience`, `weight_operating_plan`, `weight_capital_investment`, `weight_financial_capacity` and `weight_fee_offer` — are whole percentage points, each between 0 and 100 inclusive, and their sum must be exactly 100. A set of weights totalling 99 or 101 is refused with `ERR_WEIGHTS_NOT_NORMALIZED`, and that check runs ahead of every interval, concurrency and business rule in the request. `scoring_mode` selects which of the two composite-score variants will be applied to every proposal on this solicitation and is either `PER_CRITERION_ROUNDING` or `FINAL_ROUNDING`; the choice is made once, at drafting time, and cannot be revisited. `min_technical_score` is the composite an offeror must reach to be considered responsive, a two-decimal figure between 0 and 100.00 inclusive. The prospectus is created in `DRAFT`.

Publication opens the submission window. Issuing a `DRAFT` prospectus takes no payload and enforces two conditions in ladder order: the opportunity behind it must have reached `APPROVED_FOR_SOLICITATION`, else `ERR_OPPORTUNITY_NOT_APPROVED`, and the logical clock must be strictly before `submission_deadline`, else `ERR_SUBMISSION_WINDOW_CLOSED` — the agency does not publish a solicitation nobody can answer. On success the prospectus moves `DRAFT → ISSUED`, its opportunity is dragged from `APPROVED_FOR_SOLICITATION` to `SOLICITED`, and both rows advance their `version` by one.

Closing the window is the mirror image: an `ISSUED` prospectus moves to `CLOSED` only once the logical clock has reached or passed `submission_deadline`, and an attempt made while proposals could still legitimately arrive is refused with `ERR_SUBMISSION_WINDOW_OPEN`. Closing has one cascade, and it is narrow. Every proposal on this prospectus sitting in `REVISION_REQUESTED` is moved to `NOT_SELECTED`, because the window in which it could have been revised is now gone; each such proposal advances its own `version` by one. Proposals in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `SCORED` are deliberately left untouched — they remain in play for the award. The prospectus advances its `version` by one.

### 3.3. Offerors, Proposals and the Revision Cycle

Before anyone can bid, a `ProgramAdministrator` registers the bidder. An **offeror** record carries its `legal_name`, a `contact_email` for correspondence, and `bond_capacity_cents` — the maximum penal sum its surety has attested it can post, which must be strictly positive. Offerors are created `ELIGIBLE`. The `id` minted for an offeror is significant beyond the register: it is the value a `Concessionaire` presents in `X-User-ID`, which is how the ownership rule of §2.1 knows which leases and proposals that caller may touch.

Debarment is the program's blunt instrument and it is reserved to the `ProgramAdministrator`. An `ELIGIBLE` offeror moves to `DEBARRED`, which is terminal; a second attempt on an already-debarred offeror is refused with `ERR_OFFEROR_STATUS_INVALID`. Debarment reaches into the procurement in one direction only. Every proposal held by that offeror whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `SCORED` is moved to `WITHDRAWN`, each such proposal advancing its own `version` by one; proposals already in a terminal status are untouched, which makes this a conditional cascade rather than a blanket sweep. Leases the offeror already holds are explicitly **not** altered — debarment bars new awards, it does not terminate a running concession. The offeror's own `version` advances by one.

Submitting a proposal is a `Concessionaire` act. The payload names the `prospectus_id` being answered and the `offeror_id` submitting, then states the four commercial and qualification figures the panel will score: `offered_franchise_fee_pct`, the base franchise fee percentage offered, strictly above zero and no more than 100.00; `proposed_capital_investment_cents`, the capital the offeror commits to spend, which may be zero; `proposed_annual_guarantee_cents`, strictly positive; `experience_years`, whole years of comparable concession operating experience, not negative; and `financial_capacity_ratio`, a two-decimal current-ratio style solvency figure that must be strictly positive. A new proposal is `SUBMITTED`, with `revision_round` at 0, `evaluation_count` at 0 and `composite_score` null.

Four substantive gates stand in front of a new proposal, and they are evaluated in this order. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `offeror_id` in the body, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — an offeror cannot bid in someone else's name. The named offeror must still be `ELIGIBLE`, else `ERR_OFFEROR_DEBARRED`. The logical clock must be strictly before the prospectus `submission_deadline`, else `ERR_SUBMISSION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The offeror must not already hold a live proposal on this same prospectus, where "live" means any status other than `WITHDRAWN` or `NOT_SELECTED`; a second live bid is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROPOSAL`, and note that an offeror whose earlier attempt was withdrawn is free to bid again. Only then are the two fiscal floors applied: `offered_franchise_fee_pct` must be at least `minimum_franchise_fee_pct`, with equality passing — a proposal offering exactly the minimum is responsive — else `ERR_FEE_BELOW_MINIMUM`; and `proposed_annual_guarantee_cents` must be at least `minimum_annual_guarantee_cents`, again with equality passing, else `ERR_GUARANTEE_BELOW_MINIMUM`.

Review begins when a `ConcessionSpecialist` takes the proposal from `SUBMITTED` to `UNDER_REVIEW`, which requires only that the solicitation behind it be `ISSUED` or `CLOSED` — otherwise `ERR_PROSPECTUS_STATUS_INVALID`. From `UNDER_REVIEW` the same specialist may send the proposal back, moving `UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED`. Three conditions guard that move, in order: the proposal's `evaluation_count` must still be 0, because once any panelist has filed a score sheet the bid is frozen and the attempt fails with `ERR_EVALUATION_ALREADY_STARTED`; the prospectus must still be `ISSUED`, since a revision is pointless once the window has closed; and `revision_round` must be strictly below two, else `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` — an offeror gets two bites and no more. On success `revision_round` rises by one and the proposal's `version` advances by one.

The offeror closes the loop by resubmitting, moving `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` and overwriting exactly three fields from the request body: `offered_franchise_fee_pct`, `proposed_capital_investment_cents` and `proposed_annual_guarantee_cents`. Resubmission is confined to the owning offeror, requires the prospectus to still be `ISSUED`, requires the logical clock to still be strictly before `submission_deadline`, and re-applies both fiscal floors — a revised fee below `minimum_franchise_fee_pct` earns `ERR_FEE_BELOW_MINIMUM` and a revised guarantee below `minimum_annual_guarantee_cents` earns `ERR_GUARANTEE_BELOW_MINIMUM`. The full cycle is therefore `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, traversable at most twice.

Withdrawal is the offeror's own exit and is available from `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` and `SCORED`, in every case landing on `WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal. It requires no payload and is confined to the owning offeror. `SELECTED`, `NOT_SELECTED` and `WITHDRAWN` are the proposal's three terminal states; nothing moves a proposal out of them.

### 3.4. Panel Evaluation, Composite Scoring and Award Selection

Scoring is done by panel, one score sheet per panelist, and the sheets are immutable once filed. An `EvaluationPanelist` files a sheet against a proposal that is `UNDER_REVIEW`, supplying five raw integer scores between 0 and 100 inclusive: `score_experience`, `score_operating_plan`, `score_capital_investment`, `score_financial_capacity` and `score_fee_offer`. The sheet is stored with `proposal_id` pointing at the proposal and `panelist_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`. Two guards apply after the proposal's own status is checked: the panel must not already be complete — a proposal that already carries two sheets rejects a third with `ERR_PANEL_ALREADY_COMPLETE` — and the acting panelist must not already have filed on this proposal, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_PANELIST_EVALUATION`. Filing a sheet raises the proposal's `evaluation_count` by one and advances the proposal's `version` by one.

The panel is complete at two sheets, and the second sheet is the one that scores the proposal: `composite_score` is computed and the proposal moves `UNDER_REVIEW → SCORED` in the same request. Until that moment `composite_score` remains null.

The composite is a weighted blend, and its arithmetic is specified to the decimal place because the prospectus chooses between two rounding disciplines that can differ in the final hundredth of a point. For each of the five criteria, first form the criterion's raw mean: add that criterion's score across every score sheet filed on the proposal — there is no status filter on score sheets, since they are append-only and immutable — and divide by `evaluation_count`. Keep that mean exact for now; it is not yet rounded. The weight for each criterion is the matching prospectus weight (`weight_experience` for experience, `weight_operating_plan` for the operating plan, `weight_capital_investment` for capital investment, `weight_financial_capacity` for financial capacity and `weight_fee_offer` for the fee offer), and those weights are guaranteed to total 100.

Under `PER_CRITERION_ROUNDING`, each criterion's raw mean is first rounded half up to two decimal places, then multiplied by that criterion's weight and divided by one hundred, and that product is itself rounded half up to two decimal places; `composite_score` is the exact sum of the five resulting two-decimal contributions. Under `FINAL_ROUNDING`, each criterion's **unrounded** raw mean is multiplied by its weight and divided by one hundred, the five unrounded products are added, and only that total is rounded half up to two decimal places. Identical score sheets on two prospectuses that differ only in `scoring_mode` can therefore produce composites one hundredth of a point apart, and that is intended, not a defect.

Awarding is a `ContractingOfficer` act on a `CLOSED` prospectus and is the single busiest operation in the system. It carries one input, `term_start`, the contract start date. Three preconditions are evaluated in order. The prospectus must be `CLOSED`. The candidate set must be non-empty, else `ERR_NO_RESPONSIVE_PROPOSAL` and nothing at all is changed. And the acting officer must be clean of the scoring: if any score sheet on any proposal of this prospectus carries a `panelist_user_id` equal to the acting `X-User-ID`, the award is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A panelist who has scored one bid can never award that solicitation, even for a different bid.

The candidate set is built from the proposals on this prospectus that satisfy every one of the following at once: the proposal's status is `SCORED`; the offeror behind it is `ELIGIBLE`; its `composite_score` is at least the prospectus `min_technical_score`; its `offered_franchise_fee_pct` is at least `minimum_franchise_fee_pct`; its `proposed_capital_investment_cents` is at least the opportunity's `required_capital_investment_cents`; and the offeror's `bond_capacity_cents` is at least the performance bond this proposal's own terms would require, computed exactly as §3.5 describes. No proposal in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `SELECTED`, `NOT_SELECTED` or `WITHDRAWN` can enter the pool. Candidates are then ranked by `composite_score` from highest to lowest; ties are broken by `offered_franchise_fee_pct` from highest to lowest, then by `proposed_capital_investment_cents` from highest to lowest, then by `created_at` from earliest to latest. Ranking never falls back on record identifiers. The rank-one candidate wins.

A successful award fans out. The winning proposal moves `SCORED → SELECTED`. Every other proposal on this prospectus still in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `SCORED` moves to `NOT_SELECTED` — a proposal that was never scored is non-responsive by definition, and a proposal stuck `UNDER_REVIEW` with fewer than two sheets is treated the same way. A `Lease` is minted, in `contract_status` `PENDING_EXECUTION` and `compliance_status` `COMPLIANT`, carrying the `park_id` inherited from the opportunity, the `prospectus_id` of this solicitation, the `offeror_id` and `selected_proposal_id` of the winner, `awarded_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, `executed_by_user_id` still null, and `predecessor_lease_id` null — a lease is first-generation until a close-out elsewhere names it as a successor. The winning terms are copied onto the lease at this moment and never re-derived: `base_fee_pct` from the winner's `offered_franchise_fee_pct`, `minimum_annual_guarantee_cents` from its `proposed_annual_guarantee_cents`, `capital_investment_obligation_cents` from its `proposed_capital_investment_cents`, and `term_years` from the prospectus. A `PerformanceBond` is minted alongside it and the lease's `performance_bond_id` points at it. Finally the opportunity moves `SOLICITED → AWARDED` and the prospectus itself moves `CLOSED → AWARDED`, which is terminal for both. Every row the award touches advances its own `version` by one, and the response is the new lease.

The lease's calendar is fixed at award from `term_start` and `term_years` alone. `term_end` is obtained by adding the whole number of `term_years` to `term_start` and then subtracting one day — a ten-year term starting 2027-03-01 therefore ends 2037-02-28. The same arithmetic defines every lease year: the Nth lease year, counting from one, begins on the date `term_start` plus N minus one whole years and ends on the date `term_start` plus N whole years minus one day, and both endpoints are inclusive. A given date belongs to the one lease year whose closed window contains it, and that index is what a reconciliation records as `lease_year_number`.

### 3.5. Bonding, Insurance and Lease Execution

An awarded lease is not yet a running concession. Two instruments must be in place first — a performance bond the agency can cash and a certificate of insurance that has not lapsed — and a second officer must sign.

The bond row is created by the award itself, in status `PENDING`, with `lease_id` pointing at the new lease, `surety_name` and `penal_sum_cents` still null, `available_balance_cents` at 0 and `drawn_total_cents` at 0. What it must eventually be worth is decided at award and stored twice: as `required_bond_amount_cents` on the lease and, copied from it, as `required_amount_cents` on the bond. The requirement is built from three inputs — the winning proposal's annual guarantee and capital commitment, and the prospectus term length — and its rounding locations matter. Take one quarter of the lease's `minimum_annual_guarantee_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents; take one tenth of the lease's `capital_investment_obligation_cents`, also rounded half up to whole cents; then take whichever is greatest of those two rounded components and a hard floor of twenty-five thousand dollars. For a term of ten years or fewer that greatest value is the requirement as it stands. For a term strictly longer than ten years the requirement is that value multiplied by one and a half, rounded half up to whole cents a second time. The same computation, applied to a candidate proposal's own figures, is what the award's eligibility predicate compares against an offeror's `bond_capacity_cents`.

The concessionaire posts the bond, moving it `PENDING → ACTIVE`. The payload states `penal_sum_cents`, the face amount as posted, and `surety_name`, the surety company of record. Beyond ownership, two bounds apply in order: the penal sum must be at least `required_amount_cents`, with equality passing, else `ERR_BOND_INSUFFICIENT`; and it must not exceed the lease-holding offeror's attested `bond_capacity_cents`, else `ERR_BOND_EXCEEDS_CAPACITY`. On success the penal sum and surety are persisted, `available_balance_cents` is set equal to `penal_sum_cents`, `drawn_total_cents` is set to 0, and the bond's `version` advances by one.

Execution is a `ContractingOfficer` act taking a lease from `PENDING_EXECUTION` to `ACTIVE`, and it carries the terms of the physical relationship: `insurance_expires_on`, the expiry date of the certificate of insurance on file; `carrier_name`, the insurer of record; and `agency_maintenance_scope`, the portion of physical maintenance the agency retains, which is `NONE`, `STRUCTURAL_ONLY` or `STRUCTURAL_AND_UTILITIES` and defaults to `STRUCTURAL_ONLY`. Whatever the agency does not retain is the concessionaire's responsibility, funded out of the maintenance reserve that accrues from its own receipts. Three preconditions are checked in order after the lease's own `contract_status`: the lease's bond must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_BOND_NOT_ACTIVE` — a bond that has already been drawn down is not good enough to start a contract; the submitted `insurance_expires_on` must be strictly after the logical clock's date, else `ERR_INSURANCE_LAPSED`, so a certificate expiring today does not qualify; and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the lease's `awarded_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The officer who awarded may never be the officer who executes. On success the three supplied fields are persisted, `executed_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, and the lease's `version` advances by one.

Insurance is re-certified independently of any state change. A `ContractingOfficer` may record the certificate currently on file at any point while the lease's `contract_status` is `PENDING_EXECUTION`, `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER`, supplying a new `insurance_expires_on` and optionally a new `carrier_name`. The agency records what the carrier files — including a certificate whose expiry has already passed — so this is also the mechanism by which a lapse is registered against a running lease. Neither of the lease's two status dimensions moves, but the lease's `version` advances by one, which matters to any client holding an `expected_lease_version` from before the filing.

### 3.6. Operating Seasons, Service Levels and Public Rate Standards

A concession does not operate year-round by default; it operates in **seasons**, and each season is a plan the agency has approved. The concessionaire submits one for a lease that is `ACTIVE` — any other `contract_status` earns `ERR_LEASE_NOT_ACTIVE` — naming the `lease_id`, the closed season window `season_start` through `season_end`, the `service_level` committed for that window, which is `FULL`, `REDUCED` or `WINTER_LIMITED`, and the three price ceilings it will hold itself to: `max_lodging_rate_cents`, the highest nightly lodging rate it may charge the public; `max_meal_rate_cents`, the highest meal rate; and `max_activity_rate_cents`, the highest guided-activity rate. All three must be strictly positive. Because a submission is legitimate from any active lease, the request must also carry `expected_lease_version` as described in §2.7, and a successful submission advances the lease's `version` by one in addition to creating the plan in `SUBMITTED`. A client submitting two seasons back to back must therefore re-read the lease in between.

Approval belongs to a `ContractingOfficer` and moves the plan `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`. It takes no payload and enforces three things after the plan's own status: the lease must still be `ACTIVE`; the season must not collide with another `APPROVED` season on the same lease; and every rate must sit under its ceiling. Season collision is judged on closed intervals, so sharing even one calendar day is a collision and touching endpoints do conflict — a plan running to 2027-09-30 and a plan starting on 2027-09-30 cannot both be approved, and the second attempt earns `ERR_SEASON_OVERLAP`. Plans in `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED` do not reserve a season; only `APPROVED` ones do.

The rate ceilings are the operating-standards clause made mechanical. For each of the three categories, the ceiling is the park's regional benchmark for that category multiplied by one and fifteen hundredths (1.15), rounded half up to whole cents: `max_lodging_rate_cents` is tested against `comparable_lodging_rate_cents`, `max_meal_rate_cents` against `comparable_meal_rate_cents`, and `max_activity_rate_cents` against `comparable_activity_rate_cents`. A rate exactly at its ceiling passes; a rate one cent above it fails the whole approval with `ERR_RATE_EXCEEDS_CEILING`. All three categories are tested, so a plan may be refused for a lodging rate even when its meal and activity rates are conservative.

The agency may instead return the plan, moving `SUBMITTED → RETURNED` with a `deficiency_note` explaining why. The concessionaire then revises and resubmits, moving `RETURNED → SUBMITTED` and overwriting six fields from the request body — `season_start`, `season_end`, `service_level`, `max_lodging_rate_cents`, `max_meal_rate_cents` and `max_activity_rate_cents` — while `deficiency_note` is cleared back to null. Resubmission is confined to the owning offeror and, unlike the original submission, carries no version field and does not touch the lease. The `SUBMITTED → RETURNED → SUBMITTED` cycle is unbounded; what ends it is approval, or the lease event that supersedes every live plan. `SUPERSEDED` is the plan's only terminal state, and no request produces it directly: it arrives only as a cascade from lease expiry or from a bond-draw default.

### 3.7. Capital Improvements, Closure Windows and the Maintenance Split

Every concession lease obliges the concessionaire to build. `capital_investment_obligation_cents`, copied onto the lease from the winning bid, is the total the concessionaire has committed to spend over the term, and `capital_investment_completed_cents` is how much of that obligation it has actually discharged — the sum, over the lease's improvements that have reached `COMPLETED`, `VESTED_IN_AGENCY` or `COMPENSABLE`, of the lesser of each improvement's `actual_cost_cents` and its `approved_cost_cents`. Spending more than the agency approved earns no extra credit against the obligation.

A proposal for work is filed by the concessionaire against an `ACTIVE` lease and states the `lease_id`, a `description` of the scope of work, the `funding_source`, `proposed_cost_cents` (strictly positive), `useful_life_years` for straight-line amortization (at least 1 and at most 40), the half-open closure window `construction_window_start` through `construction_window_end` — the second being the day the site reopens — and `ownership_at_expiration`, which is either `AGENCY` or `CONCESSIONAIRE` and decides who owns the asset when the contract ends. `funding_source` is the fiscal fork: `CONCESSIONAIRE_CAPITAL` charges the work against the capital obligation, while `MAINTENANCE_RESERVE` spends the reserve that the concessionaire's own receipts have accrued. The improvement is created in `PROPOSED`, `approved_cost_cents`, `actual_cost_cents`, `in_service_date`, `unamortized_value_cents` and `deficiency_note` all null, and `park_id` copied from the lease so that closure windows can be counted per park without walking the lease graph. The closure window must sit inside the contract term: `construction_window_start` at or after the lease's `term_start`, and `construction_window_end` no later than the day after `term_end` — since the window's end is exclusive, the day after the last day of the term is the largest admissible value. A window that pokes outside the term is refused with `ERR_CONSTRUCTION_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_TERM`, and the same test is re-applied on every resubmission.

Approval is where the money and the park calendar are actually committed, and it is a `ContractingOfficer` act moving the improvement `PROPOSED → APPROVED`. Its only input is `approved_cost_cents`, the cost the agency is willing to stand behind, and that figure — not the proposed cost and not the eventual actual cost — becomes the amortization base. Five conditions are evaluated in this order. The lease must still be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_LEASE_NOT_ACTIVE`. The lease's performance bond must be `ACTIVE` or `PARTIALLY_DRAWN`, else `ERR_BOND_NOT_ACTIVE` — the agency does not authorize construction behind an exhausted bond. The lease's `insurance_expires_on` must be strictly after the logical clock's date, else `ERR_INSURANCE_LAPSED`. Then the park's construction capacity: counting the improvements on the same `park_id` whose status is `APPROVED` and whose half-open closure window intersects the one being approved, that count must be strictly below two, else `ERR_PARK_CLOSURE_CAPACITY`. A park tolerates two overlapping approved closures; a third simultaneous one would shut too much of the park at once. Because the windows are half-open, an improvement whose window ends the day another's begins does not count against it.

The fifth condition depends on the funding fork, and exactly one branch applies. For `CONCESSIONAIRE_CAPITAL`, add the lease's `capital_investment_completed_cents`, the `approved_cost_cents` already committed by this lease's improvements currently in `APPROVED`, and the `approved_cost_cents` on this request; that total must be no greater than the lease's `capital_investment_obligation_cents`, else `ERR_IMPROVEMENT_EXCEEDS_OBLIGATION`. Equality passes — a project that exactly exhausts the obligation is approvable. For `MAINTENANCE_RESERVE`, the request's `approved_cost_cents` must be no greater than the lease's `maintenance_reserve_balance_cents`, else `ERR_MAINTENANCE_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT`; again equality passes. On success `approved_cost_cents` is persisted, the park's `open_closure_window_count` rises by one, and — only on the reserve-funded branch — the lease's `maintenance_reserve_balance_cents` is reduced by `approved_cost_cents` immediately, at approval rather than at completion. Both the improvement and the park advance their `version` by one, as does the lease when its reserve was debited.

Not every proposal is approved. The agency may return it instead, moving `PROPOSED → RETURNED` and persisting a `deficiency_note` that states what was wrong. The concessionaire revises and resubmits, moving `RETURNED → PROPOSED` while overwriting `proposed_cost_cents`, `useful_life_years`, `construction_window_start` and `construction_window_end` from the request body and clearing `deficiency_note` back to null. Note what resubmission does *not* touch: `description`, `funding_source` and `ownership_at_expiration` are fixed at creation. Resubmission is confined to the owning offeror, and the `PROPOSED → RETURNED → PROPOSED` cycle may repeat as often as the parties need.

Completion is reported by the concessionaire, moving `APPROVED → COMPLETED` with two inputs: `actual_cost_cents`, the cost actually incurred, and `in_service_date`, the date the asset was placed in service, which starts its amortization clock. The reported date must be at or after `construction_window_start`, else `ERR_IN_SERVICE_BEFORE_WINDOW` — an asset cannot enter service before construction was permitted to begin. On success both fields are persisted, the park's `open_closure_window_count` falls by one because the site has reopened, and the lease's `capital_investment_completed_cents` rises by the lesser of `actual_cost_cents` and `approved_cost_cents`. The improvement, the park and the lease each advance their `version` by one.

An improvement's three terminal states — `VESTED_IN_AGENCY`, `COMPENSABLE` and `WITHDRAWN` — are all reached by cascade rather than by request, and §3.9 describes when.

### 3.8. Gross Receipts Reporting, Annual Reconciliation and Audit Rights

The financial heart of a concession lease is percentage rent, and percentage rent requires the concessionaire to report what it took in. Reporting is periodic. The concessionaire files a **gross receipts report** naming the `lease_id` and the half-open period `period_start` through `period_end`, and breaking the period's takings into four categories, each of which must be a non-negative integer: `lodging_receipts_cents`, `food_beverage_receipts_cents`, `retail_receipts_cents` and `activity_receipts_cents`. The report is created in `SUBMITTED`.

Four checks stand between the filing and the record, in this order. The lease must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_LEASE_NOT_ACTIVE`. Exactly one `APPROVED` operating plan on the same lease must have a closed season window `season_start` through `season_end` that covers every calendar day of the half-open reporting period; that plan's identifier is written onto the report as `operating_plan_id`, and if no approved season covers the period the filing is refused with `ERR_NO_APPROVED_OPERATING_PLAN`. The period must sit inside the contract term — `period_start` at or after the lease's `term_start`, and `period_end` no later than the day after `term_end`, the exclusive end again making the day after the term's last day the largest admissible value — else `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_TERM`. And the period must not intersect another report on the same lease whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED` or `RESTATED`, else `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`; because reporting periods are half-open, a period ending 2027-07-01 and a period starting 2027-07-01 are adjacent rather than overlapping and both may stand. A previously `REJECTED` report reserves nothing.

Two figures are computed the moment the report is created. `total_receipts_cents` is simply the four category amounts added together. `assessed_fee_cents` — the franchise fee owed for this period — applies a different rate to each category, and the rates are the lease's own `base_fee_pct` shifted by an agency-wide differential: lodging pays one and a half percentage points above the base rate, food and beverage pays exactly the base rate, retail pays half a percentage point *below* the base rate, and guided activities pay two percentage points above it. So a lease with a `base_fee_pct` of 8.00 charges 9.50 percent on lodging, 8.00 on food and beverage, 7.50 on retail and 10.00 on activities. **The evaluation order is pinned**: for each of the four categories, multiply that category's receipts by that category's rate, divide by one hundred, and round half up to whole cents; then add the four rounded category fees together in the order lodging, food and beverage, retail, activities. Summing the four raw products and rounding once at the end is wrong and can differ from the correct answer by a cent or two.

The third computed figure funds the maintenance split. `maintenance_reserve_due_cents` is one half of one percent of `total_receipts_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents. The reserve exists because everything outside the lease's `agency_maintenance_scope` is the concessionaire's to maintain, and it is spendable in exactly one way: through a capital improvement whose `funding_source` is `MAINTENANCE_RESERVE`. The reserve is an accrual, not cash — its running balance, `maintenance_reserve_balance_cents` on the lease, is the sum of `maintenance_reserve_due_cents` over the lease's `ACCEPTED` and `RESTATED` reports less the `approved_cost_cents` of every reserve-funded improvement that has reached `APPROVED` or beyond.

Acceptance is a `ContractingOfficer` act moving a report `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, and it logs the cash: `remittance_received_cents` must equal the report's `assessed_fee_cents` to the cent, else `ERR_REMITTANCE_MISMATCH`. There is no tolerance and no partial acceptance. On success the remittance is persisted and three lease ledger fields move at once — `reported_receipts_ytd_cents` rises by the report's `total_receipts_cents`, `remitted_fee_ytd_cents` rises by the logged remittance, and `maintenance_reserve_balance_cents` rises by `maintenance_reserve_due_cents` — with the report and the lease each advancing their `version` by one. Alternatively the officer rejects the report, moving `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` with a `rejection_note`. The concessionaire may then amend, moving `REJECTED → SUBMITTED` while overwriting all four category amounts, clearing `rejection_note` to null, and recomputing `total_receipts_cents`, `assessed_fee_cents` and `maintenance_reserve_due_cents` from the new figures. The `SUBMITTED → REJECTED → SUBMITTED` amendment cycle has no bound, and a report has no terminal state at all.

Once a lease year has run out, the officer reconciles it. This is the operation that decides what the concessionaire actually owed for the year, as against the sum of what it remitted period by period, and the two figures are computed on deliberately different bases. A reconciliation names the `lease_id`, the closed lease year `lease_year_start` through `lease_year_end`, and — per §2.7 — the `expected_lease_version` the client last observed. The lease must be `ACTIVE`, and the logical clock's date must be strictly after `lease_year_end`, else `ERR_RECONCILIATION_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`: a lease year cannot be reconciled before it is over. `lease_year_number` is recorded as the one-based index of that year within the term, derived from `lease_year_start` and the lease's `term_start`.

The year's receipts are assembled first. The period set is every gross receipts report on this lease whose status is `ACCEPTED` or `RESTATED` and whose period lies inside the year — `period_start` at or after `lease_year_start`, and `period_end` no later than the day after `lease_year_end` — taken in order of `period_start` ascending and, for ties, `created_at` ascending. Each report contributes its `restated_receipts_cents` if it is `RESTATED` and its `total_receipts_cents` otherwise, and the sum is `annual_gross_receipts_cents`. Reports still `SUBMITTED`, and reports that were `REJECTED`, contribute nothing at all — so a month the agency rejected and the concessionaire never amended silently lowers the year's receipts.

That annual total is then priced on a marginal tier ladder that ignores the per-category differentials entirely. The first five hundred thousand dollars of annual receipts pay the lease's `base_fee_pct`. Receipts above five hundred thousand dollars and up to one and a half million dollars pay `base_fee_pct` plus two and a half percentage points. Receipts above one and a half million dollars pay `base_fee_pct` plus five percentage points. Concretely: the first slice is the lesser of the annual total and five hundred thousand dollars; the second slice is the lesser of the annual total and one and a half million dollars, minus five hundred thousand dollars, floored at zero; the third slice is the annual total minus one and a half million dollars, floored at zero. Each slice is multiplied by its own rate, divided by one hundred and rounded half up to whole cents *independently*, and the tiered fee is the sum of the three rounded slice fees.

The minimum annual guarantee then acts as a floor. `mag_applied_cents` is the lease's full `minimum_annual_guarantee_cents` when the requested window is exactly the lease year window that the term arithmetic of §3.4 produces; when a shorter window is reconciled, the guarantee is prorated — the full guarantee multiplied by the inclusive number of days from `lease_year_start` through `lease_year_end` and divided by three hundred and sixty-five, rounded half up to whole cents. `annual_fee_due_cents` is whichever is greater, the tiered fee or `mag_applied_cents`.

What was actually paid is `remitted_total_cents`, the sum of `remittance_received_cents` over that same period set. `base_delta_cents` is `annual_fee_due_cents` less `remitted_total_cents`, and a negative value means the concessionaire overpaid across the year. On a reconciliation the officer creates directly, `penalty_cents` and `interest_cents` are both zero; `additional_fee_due_cents` is `base_delta_cents` plus `penalty_cents` plus `interest_cents`. `due_date` is thirty days after `lease_year_end`. The row is created in `PENDING_PAYMENT` when `additional_fee_due_cents` is strictly positive and directly in `CREDIT_ISSUED` otherwise — the agency does not invoice a credit, it records one.

The gap between the two bases is the whole point of the exercise, and it is worth stating plainly. The in-year view the lease surfaces as `remitted_fee_ytd_cents` is the running total of the period fees, each period priced on its own category mix at the flat category rates with no tier ladder anywhere. The reconciliation prices the same money on the annual ladder, blended across categories. For identical receipts these are different numbers, and the difference *is* `base_delta_cents` — the amount the reconciliation invoices or credits.

Creating a reconciliation has two further effects. Any existing `PENDING_PAYMENT` reconciliation for the same lease and the same `lease_year_start` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, with its `superseded_by_id` set to the new row's identifier. The lease's `outstanding_fee_balance_cents` — the sum of `additional_fee_due_cents` over the lease's reconciliations still in `PENDING_PAYMENT` — is recomputed, and the lease's `version` advances by one. The concessionaire clears the invoice by settling it, moving `PENDING_PAYMENT → SETTLED`, and the payment must be exact: `payment_amount_cents` must equal `additional_fee_due_cents`, else `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. Settlement recomputes the lease's outstanding balance, and if the reconciliation carries an `audit_engagement_id` whose audit is in `FINAL`, that audit is closed out in the same request, moving `FINAL → CLOSED`. `SETTLED` and `VOIDED` are the reconciliation's terminal states.

The agency's audit rights let an `AuditorGeneral` test the reported figures after the fact. An engagement names the `lease_id`, the half-open window `audit_period_start` through `audit_period_end`, and a `scope_note`; the lease may be `ACTIVE`, `HOLDOVER` or `EXPIRED`, and any other `contract_status` earns `ERR_LEASE_STATUS_INVALID`. The engagement opens in `OPENED`. Beginning fieldwork moves it `OPENED → FIELDWORK` and freezes the baseline: `reported_gross_receipts_cents` becomes the sum of `total_receipts_cents` over the lease's `ACCEPTED` reports whose half-open periods lie inside the audit window. At least one such report must exist, else `ERR_AUDIT_PERIOD_EMPTY` — there is nothing to audit. Reports that were `REJECTED` or are still `SUBMITTED` never enter an audit.

Issuing findings moves `FIELDWORK → FINDINGS_ISSUED` and carries `adjusted_gross_receipts_cents`, the receipts the auditor substantiates for the window, which must not be negative, together with a `finding_note`. Two figures follow. `underreport_pct` is zero when the frozen reported figure is zero; otherwise it is the adjusted figure less the reported figure, multiplied by one hundred, divided by the reported figure, and rounded half up to two decimal places — so an adjustment upward yields a positive percentage and one downward a negative percentage. `penalty_applies` is true when `underreport_pct` is at or above five percent; five percent exactly does trigger it. Nothing is restated at this stage. The concessionaire may contest, moving `FINDINGS_ISSUED → CONTESTED` with a `contest_basis`; contesting delays finalization but does not prevent it.

Finalizing moves either `FINDINGS_ISSUED → FINAL` or `CONTESTED → FINAL` — the cascade is identical from both — and requires that a reconciliation in `PENDING_PAYMENT` or `SETTLED` already exist for the lease year the audit window falls in, else `ERR_NO_RECONCILIATION_FOR_PERIOD`. Four hops follow, in order. **Hop one:** every `ACCEPTED` report whose half-open period lies inside the audit window moves `ACCEPTED → RESTATED` and receives its share of the adjusted total as `restated_receipts_cents`. The allocation is proportional and exact: taking the covered reports in order of `period_start` ascending then `created_at` ascending, every report except the last gets the adjusted total multiplied by that report's own `total_receipts_cents`, divided by the frozen reported total, rounded half up to whole cents; the last report in that order absorbs whatever remains, so that the restated figures sum to `adjusted_gross_receipts_cents` exactly. **Hop two:** because those periods have been restated, the lease year's `PENDING_PAYMENT` reconciliation moves to `SUPERSEDED` and a replacement is minted, carrying `audit_engagement_id` pointing at this audit and `annual_gross_receipts_cents` equal to the adjusted figure, re-run through the same tier ladder, guarantee floor and remittance comparison as any other reconciliation. Its penalty and interest are not zero. `penalty_cents` is ten percent of the replacement row's own `base_delta_cents`, rounded half up, but only when `penalty_applies` is true and that delta is strictly positive; otherwise it is zero. `interest_cents` is simple interest at one percent per month: the replacement row's `base_delta_cents` multiplied by one percent and then by the number of whole calendar months from the superseded reconciliation's `due_date` to the logical clock's date, rounded half up — that month count is zero when the clock is at or before the old `due_date`, and the interest is zero whenever the delta is not strictly positive. **Hop three:** because a new `PENDING_PAYMENT` row now exists, the lease's `outstanding_fee_balance_cents` is recomputed. **Hop four:** because that recomputed balance exceeds twenty thousand dollars, the lease's `compliance_status` moves `COMPLIANT → DEFICIENT` and a compliance action is minted for it automatically, with `origin` `SYSTEM_ISSUED`, `action_type` `FEE_DELINQUENCY`, `reconciliation_id` pointing at the replacement row, and a `cure_deadline` thirty days after the logical clock. Every row touched by the four hops advances its own `version` by one.

An auditor may withdraw final findings, moving `FINAL → WITHDRAWN`, provided the reconciliation the audit minted is still `PENDING_PAYMENT`; once the concessionaire has paid it, the findings are locked and the attempt fails with `ERR_AUDIT_ALREADY_SETTLED`. Withdrawal unwinds the entire restatement in the reverse order it was applied. The reconciliation the audit minted moves `PENDING_PAYMENT → VOIDED`. The reconciliation it had superseded moves `SUPERSEDED → PENDING_PAYMENT` with its `superseded_by_id` reset to null, so the original invoice is live again. Every `RESTATED` report inside the audit window returns to `ACCEPTED` with `restated_receipts_cents` reset to null. Every compliance action whose `origin` is `SYSTEM_ISSUED`, whose status is still `NOTICE_ISSUED` and whose `reconciliation_id` points at the voided row moves to `RESCINDED`. The lease's `outstanding_fee_balance_cents` is recomputed, and its `compliance_status` returns `DEFICIENT → COMPLIANT` — but only when no compliance action on that lease remains in `NOTICE_ISSUED`; an officer-issued notice for an unrelated deficiency keeps the lease deficient. The supplied `withdrawal_basis` is persisted and every affected row advances its `version` by one. `WITHDRAWN` and `CLOSED` are the engagement's terminal states.

### 3.9. Default Remedies, Expiration and the Transfer of Possessory Interest

When a concessionaire falls short, the agency's first move is a notice, not a termination. A `ContractingOfficer` issues a **compliance action** against a lease whose `contract_status` is `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER` and whose `compliance_status` is `COMPLIANT`, naming the `lease_id`, an `action_type` of `FEE_DELINQUENCY`, `OPERATING_STANDARD` or `INSURANCE_LAPSE`, a `deficiency_note` describing the shortfall, a `cure_deadline` instant, and optionally the `reconciliation_id` of the unpaid invoice that provoked it. The action is created in `NOTICE_ISSUED` with `origin` set to `AGENCY_ISSUED` — the value that distinguishes an officer's notice from the `SYSTEM_ISSUED` notices the audit cascade of §3.8 mints — and the lease's `compliance_status` moves `COMPLIANT → DEFICIENT` with its `version` advancing by one. `draw_amount_cents` and `cure_note` are null at issuance.

A lease may carry several notices at once, and this is why the return to compliance is conditional. When the officer accepts a cure, moving the action `NOTICE_ISSUED → CURE_ACCEPTED` and persisting a `cure_note`, the lease returns `DEFICIENT → COMPLIANT` only if no other compliance action on that lease is still in `NOTICE_ISSUED`; if one is, the action closes but the lease stays deficient and its `version` does not move. Note also what deficiency does *not* do: a `DEFICIENT` lease is still `ACTIVE`, so it may still file reports, submit operating plans, propose improvements and be reconciled. The two status dimensions of §2.10 remain independent right up to the point of default.

If the cure period lapses without a cure, the officer orders a draw against the performance bond, moving the action `NOTICE_ISSUED → BOND_DRAW_ORDERED`. Three conditions are checked in order: the lease's bond must be in `ACTIVE` or `PARTIALLY_DRAWN`, else `ERR_BOND_NOT_ACTIVE`; the logical clock must be strictly after the action's `cure_deadline`, else `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_OPEN` — a draw ordered at the very instant the deadline falls is premature; and the requested `draw_amount_cents` must not exceed the bond's current `available_balance_cents`, else `ERR_DRAW_EXCEEDS_BOND`, which is tested against the live balance so that a second draw sees what the first one left.

The draw then runs four hops. **Hop one** persists `draw_amount_cents` on the action. **Hop two** moves the money on the bond: `available_balance_cents` falls by the drawn amount and `drawn_total_cents` rises by the same amount, while `penal_sum_cents` never changes — the penal sum is the face value of the instrument, not a running balance. Where the bond lands depends on a sufficiency floor: half of `required_amount_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents. If the remaining `available_balance_cents` is at or above that floor the bond lands in `PARTIALLY_DRAWN`, and the agency's security is still adequate; if it falls strictly below the floor the bond lands in `INSUFFICIENT`. A bond already in `PARTIALLY_DRAWN` follows the same rule on the next draw. **Hop three** fires only on `INSUFFICIENT`: the lease's `compliance_status` moves `DEFICIENT → DEFAULT` and, at the same time, its `contract_status` moves from `ACTIVE` or `HOLDOVER` to `TERMINATED_DEFAULT`. This is the one place in the system where the two dimensions move together, and it is the only path to either terminal value. **Hop four** cleans up after the termination: every operating plan on the lease in `SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED` or `APPROVED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, and every capital improvement in `PROPOSED`, `RETURNED` or `APPROVED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, with the park's `open_closure_window_count` decremented once for each row that was `APPROVED` — the same closure-capacity bookkeeping as §3.7, now unwound in bulk. Separately, and regardless of where the bond landed, if the action carries a `reconciliation_id` and `draw_amount_cents` exactly equals that reconciliation's `additional_fee_due_cents`, the draw is treated as payment: that reconciliation moves `PENDING_PAYMENT → SETTLED` and the lease's `outstanding_fee_balance_cents` is recomputed. A draw for any other amount settles nothing, even a draw for more than the invoice.

The surety can restore drawn capacity. A reinstatement names a `reinstatement_amount_cents` that must be strictly positive and no greater than the bond's current `drawn_total_cents`, else `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_EXCEEDS_DRAWN`. `available_balance_cents` rises and `drawn_total_cents` falls by that amount, `penal_sum_cents` again untouched. A **full** reinstatement — one whose amount equals `drawn_total_cents`, bringing it to zero — returns the bond to `ACTIVE` from either `PARTIALLY_DRAWN` or `INSUFFICIENT`; a **partial** one leaves the bond in `PARTIALLY_DRAWN` as a self-loop. Critically, restoring the bond does not restore the lease: a lease already in `DEFAULT` and `TERMINATED_DEFAULT` stays there, both dimensions unchanged, no matter how completely the bond is made whole. Default is a declared status, not a derived one. `RELEASED` is the bond's only terminal state and is reachable solely by lease close-out.

At the other end of the lifecycle, a lease that has run its course expires rather than terminating. When the term is up but a successor has not yet been selected, the officer may move the lease `ACTIVE → HOLDOVER`, which keeps the operating obligation alive: it requires that the logical clock's date be at or after `term_end` — the day `term_end` itself is early enough, unlike expiration — else `ERR_TERM_NOT_ELAPSED`, and that no other lease naming this one as its `predecessor_lease_id` be `ACTIVE` already, else `ERR_SUCCESSOR_ALREADY_ACTIVE`. Holdover is not a default and not a deficiency; a lease in `HOLDOVER` reports receipts, is reconciled and is audited exactly as an `ACTIVE` one.

Expiration may be ordered from `ACTIVE` or from `HOLDOVER`, and requires the clock's date to be strictly after `term_end` — one day later than holdover's threshold. **The cascade is identical from both source states, in this order.** Each capital improvement in `COMPLETED` has its `unamortized_value_cents` computed and then moves to `VESTED_IN_AGENCY` if its `ownership_at_expiration` is `AGENCY` or to `COMPENSABLE` if it is `CONCESSIONAIRE`. Each improvement still in `PROPOSED`, `RETURNED` or `APPROVED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, with the park's `open_closure_window_count` decremented once per row that was `APPROVED`. Each operating plan in `SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED` or `APPROVED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`. Finally the lease's `possessory_interest_value_cents` is frozen as the sum of `unamortized_value_cents` over its `COMPENSABLE` improvements only, and the lease's `version` advances by one along with every row the cascade touched.

The amortization is straight line by whole months and deliberately coarse. The useful life in months is `useful_life_years` multiplied by twelve. The months elapsed are counted from the improvement's `in_service_date` to the lease's `term_end`: twelve times the difference in calendar years, plus the difference in calendar months, plus one — the plus one makes the in-service month itself count as elapsed, so a partial month at either end counts as a whole month. That count is then clamped to no less than zero and no more than the useful life in months, so an improvement whose life is fully run shows no remaining value rather than a negative one. The amortized amount is `approved_cost_cents` multiplied by the clamped month count, divided by the useful life in months, rounded half up to whole cents; `unamortized_value_cents` is `approved_cost_cents` less that amortized amount, floored at zero. The base is always `approved_cost_cents` — the figure the agency authorized — and never `actual_cost_cents`, even when the concessionaire spent more. An improvement placed in service on 2030-06-14 with a fifteen-year life against a `term_end` of 2032-06-30 has 2 × 12 + 0 + 1 = 25 elapsed months out of 180.

Closing out an expired lease is the last act. It requires that no audit engagement on the lease remain in `OPENED`, `FIELDWORK`, `FINDINGS_ISSUED` or `CONTESTED`, else `ERR_AUDIT_IN_PROGRESS` — the agency will not settle a possessory interest it may still restate. The officer may supply a `successor_lease_id`, which must resolve to a lease other than this one; when supplied, that lease's `predecessor_lease_id` is set to this lease, forming the chain that holdover's successor check reads. The bond moves `ACTIVE → RELEASED` only when the lease's `outstanding_fee_balance_cents` is zero; when money is still owed the bond keeps whatever status it has, and the close-out proceeds regardless.

The settlement itself is a waterfall, and **its step order is part of the rule** because the administrative fee is superficially circular — a percentage of a settlement that is itself net of the fee. It is pinned to the gross figure and evaluated before any offset. Step one: the gross possessory interest is the sum of `unamortized_value_cents` over this lease's `COMPENSABLE` improvements *only*; a `VESTED_IN_AGENCY` improvement is worth nothing to the outgoing concessionaire even when its unamortized value is large, because that value vested in the state. Step two: the administrative fee is three percent of that gross figure, rounded half up to whole cents. Step three: the raw net is the gross figure less the administrative fee less the lease's `outstanding_fee_balance_cents`. Step four: `transfer_settlement_net_cents` is the raw net when it is positive and zero otherwise, while `transfer_settlement_deficiency_cents` is the raw net negated when the raw net is negative and zero otherwise. At most one of the two is non-zero, and when the raw net is exactly zero both are zero. The lease then moves `EXPIRED → CLOSED_OUT`, which along with `TERMINATED_DEFAULT` is a terminal `contract_status`: nothing further can be filed, reconciled or audited against it.
