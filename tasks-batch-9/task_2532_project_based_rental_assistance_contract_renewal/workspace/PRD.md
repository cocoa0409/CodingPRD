# Project-Based Rental Assistance Renewal API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every year, thousands of Housing Assistance Payments (HAP) contracts covering project-based rental assistance reach the end of their term, and each one has to be renewed, restructured, or allowed to expire under a body of statutory rules that touch the owner-agent, the performance-based contract administrator, the HUD field office, and the local public housing agency at different moments. The work is unforgiving in a very specific way: the renewal rent that gets written onto a contract depends on which renewal option the owner elected, on whether a state-certified appraiser's rent comparability study survived reviewer challenge, on whether the owner chose an operating-cost adjustment factor or a line-item budget, and on whether a mark-up-to-market or mark-to-market restructuring determination intervened. Get the sequence wrong and the wrong rent is paid for a full term.

This document specifies a back-office API for that entire lifecycle. It carries the contract expiration calendar and the renewal window that gates when a case may be opened; the option-to-contract-type compatibility matrix; the comparability study negotiation cycle with its bounded number of reviewer challenges; the two competing rent-adjustment computations and the market-rent ceilings that constrain them; the tenant notification requirements with their statutory lead times; the opt-out path that converts project-based units into tenant-based enhanced vouchers; the utility allowance analysis that recertifies every household in a project; and the monthly voucher and special-claim machinery that draws against a finite HAP authorization.

Two properties of the domain drove the design more than anything else. First, money and units are **conserved**: the HAP authorization must always equal the ledger balance plus everything paid, and a property's assisted unit count must always equal the sum of its three unit counters, so every forward operation that moves either quantity has a compensating operation that restores it exactly. Second, several decisions are **adversarial by design** — the reviewer who challenged a study is not permitted to approve the rents that study supports, and a utility allowance swing beyond ten percent escalates out of the contract administrator's hands entirely. The API is mounted under `/pbra`.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Roles, Identity and Access Control

Every request to this API — without exception, including reads and including the clock endpoint — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not exactly one of the four recognised role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The four roles are `OwnerAgent`, `ContractAdministrator`, `HudFieldOffice`, and `PhaAdministrator`; role names are case-sensitive and no other value is accepted.

An `OwnerAgent` is the assisted-housing owner or its management agent. It opens renewal cases, elects the renewal option and the adjustment method, commissions comparability studies and utility allowance analyses, issues and rescinds tenant notices, registers and certifies households, records move-outs, and files the monthly vouchers and special claims. A `ContractAdministrator` is the performance-based contract administrator acting for HUD: it registers owners and contracts, challenges and decides comparability studies, approves renewal rents, executes renewals, finalizes opt-outs, applies approved utility allowances, opens and evaluates restructuring determinations, and disburses vouchers and claims. A `HudFieldOffice` user is the sole approver of restructuring determinations and the sole approver of a utility allowance change that exceeds the field-office threshold. A `PhaAdministrator` is the public housing agency that issues and rescinds the enhanced vouchers minted for households in an opting-out property.

When `X-User-Role` is `OwnerAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Owner`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Owner` compare the resource's owning-owner field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, the owner reached from the target resource — a `Property` via its `owner_id`, or the `Property` reached through a resource's property/contract chain — must have an `id` equal to `X-User-ID` on every `OwnerAgent` write. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_PROPERTY_OWNER` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `ContractAdministrator`, `HudFieldOffice`, or `PhaAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it in specific audit fields — the reviewer identity behind a study challenge, the approver of renewal rents, the approver of a utility allowance analysis, the field-office approver of a restructuring determination, and the issuing agency on an enhanced voucher — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Authorisation is a two-step test. The acting role must appear in the endpoint's authorized-role list as published in §4; if it does not, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Only after that does the ownership test above run for `OwnerAgent` writes. Two further role restrictions are not simple list membership and are described where the business rules live: the separation of duties on renewal-rent approval, which yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and the escalation of utility allowance approval authority, which yields `ERR_UA_CHANGE_REQUIRES_FIELD_OFFICE`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. When `limit` is omitted it defaults to 10; the smallest accepted value is 1 and the largest is 100. When `offset` is omitted it defaults to 0, and the smallest accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint description in §4 states a different ordering, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Two endpoints do state a different ordering: the monthly voucher submissions of a contract come back ordered by `period_month` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending, and the certifications of a household come back ordered by `effective_date` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending.

List responses populate four pagination fields inside `meta`: `limit` and `offset` echo the values in force for the request, `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filter before the window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window. Single-resource responses omit all four.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and Calendar Arithmetic

Nothing in this system reads the wall clock. Every date-dependent computation — the renewal window, notice lead days, voucher period elapse checks, vacancy day counts, certification spacing, utility allowance coverage intervals, and every stamped date field — reads a **logical clock** that begins at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A single administrative capability, available to all four roles, replaces that value with a supplied `to_iso8601` instant and returns the new value as `current_utc`. Moving the clock mutates no entity and advances no state machine; it only changes what every subsequent computation considers "now". Where this document says "the clock date", it means the calendar date of the logical clock in UTC.

Day counts are whole calendar days and never exclude weekends or holidays. The convention used throughout is half-open: the starting day is not counted and the ending day is counted. So the days from a contract's `expiration_date` back to the clock date, the `lead_days` from a notice's `issued_date` to its `effective_date`, and the `days_to_expiration_at_open` on a renewal case all follow that same rule. Vacancy day counting is the mirror image and is stated explicitly where it applies: the first vacant day counts and the re-rental day does not. Month arithmetic ("advanced by twelve whole months", "advanced by `term_months` whole months") advances the month component and keeps the day-of-month component.

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an `updated_at` timestamp, and an integer `version`. Timestamps are ISO 8601 UTC strings with exactly three fractional-second digits. The `version` of a freshly created resource is 1; every subsequent mutation of that resource increments its `version` by exactly one and refreshes its `updated_at`. All fourteen resource kinds in this system — owners, properties, HAP contracts, renewal cases, comparability studies, comparables, utility allowance analyses, tenant households, tenant certifications, tenant notices, restructuring determinations, enhanced vouchers, voucher submissions, and special claims — carry the full set of common properties, so none of them is missing `updated_at` or `version`.

Version increments are not confined to the resource named in the request path. Many operations in this system mutate a parent or a sibling as a documented side effect, and each such mutation increments that resource's own `version` and refreshes its own `updated_at`. Registering a property, for example, increments the owning owner's `version` because it also increments that owner's `portfolio_property_count`. The individual cascades are described in §3, and they matter directly to clients because the optimistic concurrency mechanism in §2.8 compares against these same counters.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Foreign-Key Referencing

Resources in this system are flat: each of the fourteen kinds lives at its own top-level collection under `/pbra`, and creation never nests one resource's identifier inside another's URL. Relationships are carried by explicit identifier fields in request bodies — a property names its owner, a contract names its property, a renewal case names its contract, a study names its case, a comparable names its study, and so on. The nested read paths that do exist (a property's contracts, a case's studies, a household's certifications) are reverse-reachability read channels only.

Path identifiers are resolved left to right, and the first unresolvable segment determines the error. Each resource kind has its own dedicated not-found code: `ERR_OWNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RENEWAL_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UA_ANALYSIS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TENANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENHANCED_VOUCHER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VOUCHER_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, all HTTP 404.

The same codes govern body-borne references. Every foreign key supplied in a request body must resolve before any business rule is evaluated: `owner_id`, `property_id`, `contract_id`, `renewal_case_id`, `study_id`, `tenant_id`, `supersedes_study_id`, and every identifier inside `struck_comparable_ids`. A `study_id` or a `supersedes_study_id` that names nothing yields `ERR_STUDY_NOT_FOUND`; an identifier inside `struck_comparable_ids` that names no comparable — or names a comparable belonging to a different study — yields `ERR_COMPARABLE_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.7. Cent Arithmetic and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integer cents, and every field carrying money ends in `_cents`. There are no floating-point money values anywhere in a stored resource or in a response body. The only non-integer stored values are the two rate figures on a utility allowance analysis — `utility_rate_change_pct` and `change_pct`.

Wherever a computation produces a fractional intermediate, the result is rounded **half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. Almost every rounding in this system lands on whole cents (zero decimal places). Two exceptions are called out where they occur: the per-comparable adjusted rent inside a comparability study rounds half up to whole **dollars**, meaning to the nearest multiple of 100 cents; and the `change_pct` of a utility allowance analysis rounds half up to four decimal places before it is compared to any threshold.

The order in which rounding is applied is part of the specification, not an implementation detail, because several of the formulas in §3 produce different answers under different orderings. Where a calculation pins the sequence — round each term then aggregate, or apply a factor to a part then add the remainder back — the §3 narrative states the sequence explicitly and that sequence is normative. Aggregations of already-rounded per-item terms are exact integer sums with no further rounding.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Aggregates

Three write operations in this system depend on the client having an up-to-date view of a parent resource, and each requires the client to echo back the `version` it last observed on that parent. In all three cases the version field is a required, non-optional part of the request body, and in all three cases a value that does not equal the parent's current `version` is rejected with HTTP 412 and a code specific to that parent.

Creating a tenant certification requires `expected_tenant_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `TenantHousehold`. Chained recertifications are entirely legitimate — a later certification supersedes an earlier one — so the write is never refused merely because a certification already exists; what the check catches is a client whose picture of the household (its income figures, its status, its currently active certification) predates an intervening recertification or an intervening utility-allowance apply cascade. A mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_TENANT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Creating a monthly voucher submission requires `expected_contract_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `HapContract`. Vouchers for distinct billing months coexist happily on one contract, so a later create is never refused on state grounds; the stale version identifies a client whose rent basis and ledger balance predate an intervening payment, reversal, paid claim, or renewal execution. A mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The same field and the same code guard the execution of a renewal, which writes the approved rent and the new expiration date onto the contract.

Applying an approved utility allowance analysis requires `expected_property_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the analysis's `Property`. Successive allowance applications are legitimate once the coverage interval has elapsed, so the apply is never refused on state grounds; the stale version identifies an applier whose baseline allowance and unit counters predate an intervening apply or an intervening enhanced-voucher issuance. A mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_PROPERTY_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these three operations must not be expected to carry one, and none is validated.

### 2.9. The Order in Which Requests Are Rejected

A single malformed or ill-timed request frequently breaks more than one rule at once, and clients depend on getting a stable, predictable answer about which one. The checks below are evaluated in the stated order, and the **first** one that fails determines the response; no later check is reached. The order is normative — it is not a description of a convenient implementation.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the four declared roles, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role permission.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Owner ownership.** On every `OwnerAgent` write endpoint, `X-User-ID` equals the `id` of the owner reachable from the target resource — otherwise `ERR_NOT_PROPERTY_OWNER`.
4. **Path existence.** Every path-addressed resource exists, resolved left to right, on every endpoint that carries a path identifier — otherwise the resource-specific 404 code from §2.6.
5. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign key in the body resolves, on every endpoint carrying one — otherwise the resource-specific 404 code from §2.6.
6. **Request shape.** Required body fields present, types correct, enum members legal, constrained integers non-negative, dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, billing months in `YYYY-MM` form, `cert_type` not `UA_ADJUSTMENT` on a client write, `utility_rate_change_pct` present exactly when the analysis method is `FACTOR_BASED` and `engineering_estimate_cents` present exactly when it is `ENGINEERING_BASED`, and `move_out_date` not earlier than the effective date of the household's `ACTIVE` certification. Applies to every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
7. **Pagination bounds.** On every list endpoint, `limit` within 1 through 100 and `offset` at least 0 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
8. **Uniqueness of a registered owner's taxpayer number.** On owner registration only — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_TIN`.
9. **Uniqueness of a unit number within a property.** On household registration only — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_UNIT_NUMBER`.
10. **Household version match.** On certification creation only — otherwise `ERR_STALE_TENANT_VERSION`.
11. **Contract version match.** On voucher-submission creation and on renewal execution — otherwise `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION`.
12. **Property version match.** On utility-allowance apply only — otherwise `ERR_STALE_PROPERTY_VERSION`.
13. **Transition legality.** On every action endpoint, the target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — including terminal-state lockdown and the rejection of a repeated trigger that has already been applied — otherwise `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`.
14. **No open renewal case.** On renewal-case creation only — otherwise `ERR_OPEN_RENEWAL_CASE_EXISTS`.
15. **Supersession legality.** On study creation, a supplied `supersedes_study_id` names a study in state `REJECTED` belonging to the same renewal case — otherwise `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`.
16. **Challenge round budget.** On study challenge only, evaluated after the state precondition and before any comparable is struck — otherwise `ERR_MAX_CHALLENGE_ROUNDS`.
17. **No analysis in flight.** On utility-allowance submit only — otherwise `ERR_UA_ANALYSIS_IN_FLIGHT`.
18. **No duplicate billing period.** On voucher-submission creation only — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOUCHER_PERIOD`.
19. **Accepted study present.** On renewal-rent approval, when the case requires a study — otherwise `ERR_RCS_NOT_ACCEPTED`. This cross-entity guard outranks every business rule below it, so a case that both lacks an accepted study and would breach a rent ceiling reports this code rather than a ceiling code.
20. **Covering voucher paid.** On special-claim payment only — otherwise `ERR_COVERING_VOUCHER_NOT_PAID`.
21. **No paid claim on the period.** On voucher reversal only — otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_PAID_BLOCKS_REVERSAL`.
22. **No issued enhanced voucher.** On notice rescission of a one-year opt-out notice — otherwise `ERR_ENHANCED_VOUCHER_ISSUED_BLOCKS_RESCIND`.
23. **Separation of duties.** On renewal-rent approval only — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. This check is deliberately ordered after all the state guards above, so a wrong-state approval attempt reports the state error even when the caller also breaches separation of duties.
24. **Field-office authority.** On utility-allowance decision only — otherwise `ERR_UA_CHANGE_REQUIRES_FIELD_OFFICE`.
25. **Option permitted for contract type.** On option selection only — otherwise `ERR_OPTION_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_TYPE`.
26. **Adjustment method compatible with option.** On adjustment election only — otherwise `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_METHOD_CONFLICT`.
27. **Determination branch matches the elected option.** On restructuring approval only — otherwise `ERR_MUTM_OPTION_MISMATCH`.
28. **Inside the renewal window.** On renewal-case creation only — otherwise `ERR_OUTSIDE_RENEWAL_WINDOW`.
29. **Notice lead time met.** On notice issuance only — otherwise `ERR_NOTICE_WINDOW_NOT_MET`.
30. **Opt-out notice period elapsed.** On opt-out finalization only — otherwise `ERR_OPT_OUT_NOTICE_PERIOD_INCOMPLETE`. This temporal check outranks the voucher-resolution check at step 34, so a premature finalization on a property with unissued vouchers reports the temporal code.
31. **Allowance coverage interval clear.** On utility-allowance apply only — otherwise `ERR_UA_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`.
32. **Certification spacing respected.** On certification creation only — otherwise `ERR_CERT_INTERVAL_CONFLICT`.
33. **Billing period elapsed.** On voucher submission only — otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`.
34. **One-year opt-out notice present.** On opt-out recording only — otherwise `ERR_OPT_OUT_NOTICE_MISSING`.
35. **At least one surviving comparable.** On study submission only — otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_COMPARABLES`.
36. **Every noticed household resolved.** On opt-out finalization only — otherwise `ERR_TENANT_VOUCHER_UNRESOLVED`.
37. **Aggregate vacancy day cap.** On special-claim submission only — otherwise `ERR_VACANCY_DAYS_CAP_EXCEEDED`.
38. **Operating-cost-adjustment rent within the comparable ceiling.** On renewal-rent approval only — otherwise `ERR_RENT_EXCEEDS_COMPARABLE`.
39. **Budget-based rent within its ceiling.** On renewal-rent approval only — otherwise `ERR_BUDGET_RENT_EXCEEDS_CAP`.
40. **Positive enhancement entitlement.** On enhanced-voucher issuance only — otherwise `ERR_NO_ENHANCEMENT_ENTITLEMENT`.
41. **Positive voucher request.** On voucher submission only — otherwise `ERR_ZERO_VOUCHER_REQUEST`.
42. **Positive claim amount.** On special-claim submission only — otherwise `ERR_ZERO_CLAIM_AMOUNT`.
43. **Sufficient HAP ledger balance.** On voucher payment and on special-claim payment — otherwise `ERR_HAP_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402).

### 2.10. Conserved Ledgers: HAP Money and Assisted Units

Two quantities in this system are invariants. They are audited after every operation that touches them, and every drift is a defect.

The **money invariant** lives on a HAP contract. At all times, the contract's `hap_ledger_balance_cents` plus the sum of `paid_amount_cents` over its voucher submissions in state `PAID` plus the sum of `paid_amount_cents` over its special claims in state `PAID` must equal `authorized_hap_budget_cents`. Equivalently, `hap_ledger_balance_cents` is always `authorized_hap_budget_cents` reduced by `paid_hap_total_cents` and reduced again by `paid_claims_total_cents`, where `paid_hap_total_cents` sums the paid vouchers and `paid_claims_total_cents` sums the paid claims. A reversed voucher contributes nothing to either sum even though its `paid_amount_cents` is retained for audit, which is precisely what makes the pay-then-reverse pair exactly self-cancelling: reversing a payment must restore both `paid_hap_total_cents` and `hap_ledger_balance_cents` to the values they held before that payment.

The **unit invariant** lives on a property. At all times, `units_under_hap_count` plus `enhanced_voucher_unit_count` plus `released_unit_count` must equal `assisted_unit_count`. A property is created with all of its assisted units under project-based HAP and the other two counters at zero. Only three families of events move units between the counters: enhanced-voucher issuance and its rescission, household move-out, and opt-out finalization. Enhanced vouchers in state `PENDING` or `RESCINDED` move no units at all, which is what makes issue-then-rescind exactly self-cancelling: rescinding an issued voucher must restore all three counters to their pre-issuance values.

### 2.11. Separation of Duties and Escalated Approval Authority

Two decisions in this system are removed from the operator who would otherwise make them, and both are structural rather than advisory.

The first is a separation of duties on renewal rents. Whenever a `ContractAdministrator` challenges a comparability study, that operator's `X-User-ID` is recorded on the renewal case as `challenge_reviewer_user_id`. The operator whose identifier sits in that field may never approve the renewal rents of that same case; an attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Any other `ContractAdministrator` may approve, and the approving identifier is recorded on the case as `rents_approver_user_id`. Because the field records the **most recent** challenger, a case challenged first by one reviewer and then by another locks out only the second.

The second is an escalation of approval authority on utility allowances. Each utility allowance analysis computes a `change_pct` and a boolean `requires_field_office_approval`. When that boolean is true, only a `HudFieldOffice` user may decide the analysis; a `ContractAdministrator` attempt is refused with `ERR_UA_CHANGE_REQUIRES_FIELD_OFFICE` (HTTP 403). When the boolean is false, either role may decide it. Note carefully that the escalation threshold is a strict comparison on the magnitude of the change, so a change whose magnitude lands exactly on the threshold stays with the contract administrator.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Building the Portfolio: Owners, Properties and HAP Contracts

An assisted-housing owner enters the system when a `ContractAdministrator` or a `HudFieldOffice` user registers it with a `legal_name`, a nine-digit `tin`, and a `contact_email`. The taxpayer identification number must be unique across all owners; a repeat yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_TIN`. A newly registered owner starts with `portfolio_property_count` of 0 and a `compliance_flag` of `OK`. That flag is a durable audit marker with exactly two values, `OK` and `REVIEW_REQUIRED`: it flips to `REVIEW_REQUIRED` the moment any comparability study anywhere under this owner's properties is rejected by a reviewer, and it is never cleared automatically thereafter. The `id` returned from registration is the value that this owner's staff subsequently present as `X-User-ID`.

Registering a property is the owner-agent's own act. The payload names the `owner_id`, a HUD `project_name`, a two-letter `state_code` that must be either `IL` or `TX`, a `total_units` count of at least 1, an `assisted_unit_count` of at least 1 that may not exceed `total_units`, a `published_fmr_cents` of at least 1 cent, a `baseline_utility_allowance_cents` of at least 0, and an optional `has_fha_insured_mortgage` flag that defaults to false. The state code is not decorative: it selects the operating-cost adjustment factor used later in the renewal arithmetic, and the FHA flag is a hard precondition of a mark-to-market referral. At creation the property's `current_utility_allowance_cents` equals `baseline_utility_allowance_cents`, its `units_under_hap_count` equals `assisted_unit_count`, its `enhanced_voucher_unit_count` and `released_unit_count` are both 0, and its `last_applied_ua_effective_date` is null — so the unit invariant of §2.10 holds from the first instant. The act also increments the owning owner's `portfolio_property_count` by 1 and that owner's `version` by 1.

A HAP contract is recorded by a `ContractAdministrator`, not by the owner. Its payload names the `property_id`, the statutory `contract_type` — one of `NEW_CONSTRUCTION`, `STATE_AGENCY_FINANCED`, `LOAN_MANAGEMENT_SET_ASIDE`, or `PROPERTY_DISPOSITION` — the `expiration_date` on which the current term ends, a `term_months` of at least 12, a `contract_rent_per_unit_cents` of at least 1, and three per-unit budget components: `monthly_debt_service_per_unit_cents`, `operating_expense_per_unit_cents`, and `reserve_deposit_per_unit_cents`, each at least 0. The payload also carries `authorized_hap_budget_cents`, the total authorization out of which every voucher and claim on this contract will be paid. A new contract begins with `lifecycle_status` `ACTIVE`, with `paid_hap_total_cents` and `paid_claims_total_cents` both 0, and with `hap_ledger_balance_cents` equal to the full authorization. Its three post-renewal fields — `renewed_rent_per_unit_cents`, `renewed_expiration_date`, and `renewed_option` — are all null until a renewal is executed.

Recording a contract deliberately touches none of the property's unit counters. The project-based unit ledger is seeded once at property creation and is moved only by voucher issuance, by move-out, and by opt-out finalization; a contract is a financial and calendar instrument layered on top of a unit ledger that already balances.

Read access across the portfolio is broad. All four roles may read a single owner, a single property, a single contract, and may page the properties of an owner, the contracts of a property, the households of a property, and the utility allowance analyses of a property. The household listing accepts an optional `status` filter and the analysis listing an optional `state` filter, each alongside `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.2. Opening a Renewal Case Inside the Expiration Calendar

The expiration calendar is what drives the whole workflow. An owner-agent opens a renewal case by supplying only the `contract_id`; everything else about the case is derived. The case may be opened only while the contract's expiration is genuinely approaching: the whole days from the clock date to the contract's `expiration_date` must be strictly greater than zero and at most 420 days — a window of a little under fourteen months. A contract that expired yesterday, a contract expiring today, and a contract whose expiration is still more than 420 days out are all refused with `ERR_OUTSIDE_RENEWAL_WINDOW`. Only one live case is permitted per contract: if a case already exists on the contract whose rent track has not reached `EXECUTED`, the attempt is refused with `ERR_OPEN_RENEWAL_CASE_EXISTS`.

A successfully opened case records its `opened_on_date` as the clock date and its `days_to_expiration_at_open` as the whole days from that date to the contract's expiration (the open day excluded, the expiration day counted). It begins with `renewal_option` and `adjustment_method` both null, `rcs_required` false, and all four rent figures — `comparable_market_rent_cents`, `ocaf_rent_per_unit_cents`, `budget_rent_per_unit_cents`, and `approved_rent_per_unit_cents` — null. The audit fields `challenge_reviewer_user_id`, `rents_approver_user_id`, and `opt_out_notice_id` are likewise null. On the contract side, opening a case moves `lifecycle_status` from `ACTIVE` to `RENEWAL_PENDING` and increments the contract's `version` by 1.

What makes this case unusual is that it carries **two independent state dimensions that advance separately**. The rent track, `rent_track_status`, runs `TERMS_PENDING` → `OPTION_SELECTED` → `RCS_IN_PROGRESS` → `RENTS_APPROVED` → `EXECUTED`, with `EXECUTED` terminal. The notice track, `notice_track_status`, runs `NOTICES_PENDING` → `RENEWAL_NOTICED` on the renewal path, or `NOTICES_PENDING` → `OPT_OUT_NOTICED` → `OPT_OUT_FINALIZED` on the opt-out path, with `OPT_OUT_FINALIZED` terminal and with a return edge from `OPT_OUT_NOTICED` back to `NOTICES_PENDING` when the governing notice is rescinded. Neither track constrains the other except at one single coupling point: executing the renewal reads both. Rents may be approved with no notice yet issued, and notices may be issued before any option has been elected.

The contract's own lifecycle mirrors the case at a coarser grain: `ACTIVE` → `RENEWAL_PENDING` when the case opens, `RENEWAL_PENDING` → `RENEWED` when the renewal executes, `RENEWAL_PENDING` → `OPT_OUT_PENDING` when the owner records an opt-out, `OPT_OUT_PENDING` → `RENEWAL_PENDING` when the opt-out notice is rescinded, and `OPT_OUT_PENDING` → `EXPIRED_OPT_OUT` when the opt-out is finalized. `RENEWED` and `EXPIRED_OPT_OUT` are both terminal. Every one of those five transitions increments the contract's `version` by 1.

### 3.3. Electing the Option and the Adjustment Method

The first substantive act on an open case is the owner-agent's election of a `renewal_option`, supplied in a payload with that single field. Four options exist — `OPTION_1_MARK_UP_TO_MARKET`, `OPTION_2_OCAF`, `OPTION_3_MARK_TO_MARKET`, and `OPTION_4_PORTFOLIO_REENGINEERING` — but only some are lawful for a given contract family, and the compatibility table is closed and exhaustive. A `NEW_CONSTRUCTION` contract admits `OPTION_1_MARK_UP_TO_MARKET`, `OPTION_2_OCAF`, and `OPTION_3_MARK_TO_MARKET`. A `STATE_AGENCY_FINANCED` contract admits `OPTION_2_OCAF` and `OPTION_4_PORTFOLIO_REENGINEERING`. A `LOAN_MANAGEMENT_SET_ASIDE` contract admits `OPTION_2_OCAF` and nothing else. A `PROPERTY_DISPOSITION` contract admits `OPTION_2_OCAF` and `OPTION_4_PORTFOLIO_REENGINEERING`. Any other pairing is refused with `ERR_OPTION_NOT_PERMITTED_FOR_TYPE` (HTTP 422). The election is available only while the rent track is still `TERMS_PENDING`; a second attempt after the track has advanced is a `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`.

A lawful election advances `rent_track_status` from `TERMS_PENDING` to `OPTION_SELECTED`, writes `renewal_option`, and recomputes `rcs_required` as true when the elected option is `OPTION_1_MARK_UP_TO_MARKET` or the already-recorded `adjustment_method` is `BUDGET_BASED`, and false otherwise. Electing mark-up-to-market therefore makes an accepted comparability study mandatory before rents can be approved. The election also recomputes both candidate rents on the case from the contract's current per-unit components, as described below.

Separately, and as a one-shot decision, the owner-agent elects an `adjustment_method` of either `OCAF` or `BUDGET_BASED`. This election is available only while the rent track sits at `OPTION_SELECTED`, and only while `adjustment_method` is still null — once recorded it cannot be changed, and a second attempt is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. Mark-up-to-market admits the operating-cost adjustment factor only: electing `BUDGET_BASED` on a case whose `renewal_option` is `OPTION_1_MARK_UP_TO_MARKET` is refused with `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_METHOD_CONFLICT` (HTTP 422). Recording the election leaves `rent_track_status` at `OPTION_SELECTED` — this is a self-transition on the rent-track dimension, in which the status label is unchanged but the election and both candidate rents are written — and it recomputes `rcs_required` as true when `adjustment_method` is `BUDGET_BASED` or `renewal_option` is `OPTION_1_MARK_UP_TO_MARKET`, and false otherwise, so an `OCAF` election on a non-mark-up-to-market option releases the study requirement.

**The operating-cost adjustment computation.** The figure written to `ocaf_rent_per_unit_cents` is derived from the contract's rent and debt service and the property's state. Begin with the non-debt portion of the current rent: the contract's `contract_rent_per_unit_cents` less its `monthly_debt_service_per_unit_cents`, floored at zero so that a debt service exceeding the rent yields a non-debt portion of zero rather than a negative number. Multiply that non-debt portion by the operating-cost adjustment factor for the property's `state_code` — 1.032 for `IL`, meaning a 3.2 percent escalation, and 1.028 for `TX`, meaning 2.8 percent — and round that product half up to whole cents. Then add the full `monthly_debt_service_per_unit_cents` back on. The order is pinned: the factor applies to the non-debt portion **only**, and the rounding happens **before** the debt-service add-back. Escalating the whole rent, or adding the debt service first and rounding once at the end, produces a different number and is not permitted. No ceiling is applied here — an over-market escalation is deliberately allowed to stand in `ocaf_rent_per_unit_cents` so that the contract administrator sees it and the approval-time ceiling check can refuse it outright rather than silently trimming it.

**The budget-based computation.** The figure written to `budget_rent_per_unit_cents` reads only the three per-unit budget components on the contract and no property or tenant data at all. First form the pre-fee subtotal: `operating_expense_per_unit_cents` plus `monthly_debt_service_per_unit_cents` plus `reserve_deposit_per_unit_cents`, an exact integer sum. Then levy the management fee of 8 percent **on that subtotal**, rounding the fee half up to whole cents. The budget-based rent is the subtotal plus that single rounded fee, again an exact integer sum. This is a single forward pass and it is deliberately not an algebraic fixed point: the management fee is nominally a percentage of the total rent and the total rent nominally includes the fee, but dividing the subtotal by one-minus-the-fee-percentage, or re-levying the fee against the new total for a second iteration, both produce different numbers and neither is permitted. Exactly one rounding occurs, and it applies to the fee term alone.

Both figures are recomputed together, from scratch, whenever the option is selected and whenever the adjustment method is elected. Because they read entirely different inputs, the same stored contract typically yields two materially different candidate rents, and the elected `adjustment_method` is what decides which one becomes the approved rent.

### 3.4. Comparability Studies, Comparables and the Challenge Cycle

A rent comparability study is the owner-agent's evidentiary filing about what the market would pay for the assisted unit type. Commissioning one requires a payload naming the `renewal_case_id`, the `appraiser_name` of the state-certified appraiser who prepared it, and optionally a `supersedes_study_id`. A study is born in state `DRAFT` with `challenge_round` at 0, `accepted_comparable_count` at 0, an `estimated_market_rent_cents` of 0, `last_challenge_reason` null, and `submitted_on_date` null. Filing the **first** study on a case whose rent track sits at `OPTION_SELECTED` and whose `adjustment_method` has already been recorded advances `rent_track_status` from `OPTION_SELECTED` to `RCS_IN_PROGRESS`; a case whose adjustment method is still null cannot host a study, and later studies on a case already at `RCS_IN_PROGRESS` leave the track where it is.

The `supersedes_study_id` field exists for the replacement of a rejected filing. When present, it must name a study in state `REJECTED` whose `renewal_case_id` equals the new study's `renewal_case_id`; naming a study in any other state, or a rejected study belonging to a different case, is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. A lawful supersession moves the named predecessor from `REJECTED` to `SUPERSEDED`, increments its `version` by 1, and leaves the new study in `DRAFT` with its own `challenge_round` at 0. `SUPERSEDED` and `ACCEPTED` are the two terminal states of a study; `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `CHALLENGED`, and `REJECTED` are all live.

Comparables are added to a study one at a time, and only while the study is still in `DRAFT` — adding one to a study that has left `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. Each comparable's payload names the `study_id`, an `address_label` for the comparable property, a `raw_rent_cents` of at least 1 for the unadjusted asking rent, an `adjustment_total_cents` capturing the net appraisal adjustment, a `utility_adjustment_cents` capturing the utility-configuration adjustment, and an appraiser `weight` that is an integer from 1 through 5. The two adjustment fields may be negative. A comparable is created with `status` `ACCEPTED`, and it holds a computed `adjusted_rent_cents`.

**Deriving the estimated market rent.** The study's `estimated_market_rent_cents` is a weighted mean over its surviving comparables, computed in a strictly pinned order. First, for each comparable of the study whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`, taken in `created_at` ascending order, sum its `raw_rent_cents`, its `adjustment_total_cents`, and its `utility_adjustment_cents`, and round that gross figure half up to the nearest whole dollar — that is, to the nearest multiple of 100 cents — storing the result in that comparable's `adjusted_rent_cents`. Second, over those already-rounded values, form the weighted sum of each comparable's `adjusted_rent_cents` multiplied by its `weight`, and the total of the weights. Third, divide the weighted sum by the total weight and round that quotient half up to whole cents; that single number is `estimated_market_rent_cents`. Comparables whose `status` is `STRUCK` are excluded from both the weighted sum and the weight total. Computing the weighted mean over the unrounded gross figures and rounding only once at the end yields a different answer and is not permitted — the per-comparable dollar rounding must happen first. When no comparable of the study is `ACCEPTED`, so that the total weight would be zero, `estimated_market_rent_cents` is null. Both `estimated_market_rent_cents` and `accepted_comparable_count` are refreshed, and the study's `version` incremented, every time a comparable is added, every time the study is submitted, and every time a challenge strikes comparables.

Submission is the owner-agent's act and moves the study from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED`, or from `CHALLENGED` back to `SUBMITTED` when a challenged study is refiled. Either way it requires at least one comparable of the study to be in `status` `ACCEPTED`; a study whose every comparable has been struck, or which never had one, is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_COMPARABLES` (HTTP 422). Submission recomputes the estimated market rent over the surviving accepted comparables — including any added after a challenge — stamps `submitted_on_date` with the clock date, and writes the recomputed estimate onto the case's `comparable_market_rent_cents`, incrementing the case's `version` by 1. Refiling after a challenge closes the negotiation cycle and expressly does **not** reset `challenge_round`.

A `ContractAdministrator` reviewing a `SUBMITTED` study has two moves. The first is to **challenge** it, supplying a free-text `reason` and a list of `struck_comparable_ids`. Every identifier in that list must name a comparable of this study whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`; anything else yields `ERR_COMPARABLE_NOT_FOUND`. The negotiation is bounded: a study may be challenged at most three times, and a fourth attempt against a study whose `challenge_round` already equals 3 is refused with `ERR_MAX_CHALLENGE_ROUNDS` (HTTP 409) — that budget check runs after the state precondition and before any comparable is struck, so a refused challenge leaves every comparable intact. A lawful challenge moves the study `SUBMITTED` → `CHALLENGED`, sets `status` to `STRUCK` on every named comparable and increments each of their `version` counters by 1, increments `challenge_round` by 1, records `last_challenge_reason` from the supplied `reason`, recomputes `accepted_comparable_count` and `estimated_market_rent_cents` over the remaining accepted comparables, writes that recomputed estimate onto the case's `comparable_market_rent_cents`, sets the case's `challenge_reviewer_user_id` to the challenging `X-User-ID`, and increments the case's `version` by 1. That last side effect is the separation-of-duties lock of §2.11: the challenger has now disqualified itself from approving this case's rents.

The reviewer's second move is to **decide** the study, supplying a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` and an optional `reason`. Acceptance moves the study `SUBMITTED` → `ACCEPTED`, requires at least one accepted comparable, and neither advances either track of the case nor changes `comparable_market_rent_cents`, which already carries this study's estimate from the submission; the case's two candidate rents are likewise left exactly as they stand, because neither the operating-cost-adjustment figure nor the budget-based figure reads the comparable market rent. What acceptance does accomplish is to satisfy the accepted-study guard on rent approval. Rejection moves the study `SUBMITTED` → `REJECTED`, records the supplied `reason` in `last_challenge_reason`, and leaves the case's `comparable_market_rent_cents` holding the rejected study's value until a superseding study is submitted. Rejection also reaches across aggregates: the `Owner` of the case's property has its `compliance_flag` set to `REVIEW_REQUIRED`, and that flag is never cleared automatically.

Studies are readable individually, listable per case, and listable across the system with optional `renewal_case_id`, `supersedes_study_id`, and `state` filters; a study's comparables are listable with an optional `status` filter.

### 3.5. Restructuring Eligibility: Mark-Up-to-Market versus Mark-to-Market

A restructuring determination asks a narrow question: does this project's market position justify raising its rents to market, or does it instead call for a referral into debt restructuring? A `ContractAdministrator` opens one with a payload naming only the `renewal_case_id`. The determination is created in `determination_state` `DRAFT` with a null `eligibility_reason_code`, a null `post_restructure_rent_per_unit_cents`, a null `approved_by_user_id`, and its three rent snapshots taken.

Those snapshots are what make a determination stable. `market_rent_per_unit_cents` copies the case's `comparable_market_rent_cents`, `current_rent_per_unit_cents` copies the contract's `contract_rent_per_unit_cents`, and `fmr_per_unit_cents` copies the property's `published_fmr_cents`. Evaluation re-takes all three from the case, the contract, and the property, then decides the branch from those values alone; once evaluated, later changes to the study or the property do not retroactively alter the determination.

**The eligibility decision.** Evaluation is a `ContractAdministrator` act on a `DRAFT` determination and forks it into exactly one of three outcomes, tested in this order. If the contract's `contract_type` is `LOAN_MANAGEMENT_SET_ASIDE`, the determination is `INELIGIBLE` with `eligibility_reason_code` `CONTRACT_TYPE_EXCLUDED`, and no rent comparison is made at all. Otherwise, if the snapshot market rent is strictly greater than the snapshot current rent, the project is in the mark-up-to-market family: when the market rent is also at least the Fair Market Rent floor — that is, at least the snapshot Fair Market Rent multiplied by 1.00 and rounded half up to whole cents, an inclusive comparison — the determination becomes `MUTM_ELIGIBLE` with reason `MARKET_ABOVE_CONTRACT_AND_FMR`; when the market rent falls below that floor, the determination is `INELIGIBLE` with reason `MARKET_BELOW_FMR_FLOOR`. Otherwise, if the snapshot current rent is strictly greater than the snapshot market rent **and** the property's `has_fha_insured_mortgage` is true, the determination becomes `M2M_REFERRED` with reason `CONTRACT_ABOVE_MARKET_FHA_INSURED`. In every remaining situation the determination is `INELIGIBLE` with reason `MARKET_NOT_ABOVE_CONTRACT`. Both boundaries are load-bearing: the market-versus-contract test is strict, so a market rent exactly equal to the contract rent is not mark-up-to-market eligible, and the market-versus-Fair-Market-Rent floor test is inclusive, so a market rent exactly at the floor is eligible. `INELIGIBLE` is terminal.

The two live branches join back at `APPROVED`, which is a `HudFieldOffice` prerogative and no one else's, but they carry divergent arithmetic and divergent option requirements. Approving a `MUTM_ELIGIBLE` determination requires the case's `renewal_option` to be `OPTION_1_MARK_UP_TO_MARKET`; approving an `M2M_REFERRED` determination requires it to be `OPTION_3_MARK_TO_MARKET`. A mismatch on either branch is refused with `ERR_MUTM_OPTION_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). On the mark-up-to-market branch, `post_restructure_rent_per_unit_cents` is set to the snapshot `market_rent_per_unit_cents` unchanged. On the mark-to-market branch, `post_restructure_rent_per_unit_cents` is set to 90 percent of the snapshot `market_rent_per_unit_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents — the debt-restructuring haircut. Both branches set `determination_state` to `APPROVED`, record the approving `X-User-ID` in `approved_by_user_id`, and then write `post_restructure_rent_per_unit_cents` onto the case's `ocaf_rent_per_unit_cents`, incrementing the case's `version` by 1. That overwrite is the whole point of the exercise: it replaces the operating-cost-adjustment candidate with the restructured rent so that a later rent approval on the operating-cost-adjustment track settles at the restructured figure. `APPROVED` is terminal.

Determinations are readable individually and listable per case; the read paths are open to `OwnerAgent`, `ContractAdministrator`, and `HudFieldOffice` users, but a public housing agency has no business with them and is not authorised.

### 3.6. Approving the Renewal Rents and Executing the Renewal

Approving rents is a `ContractAdministrator` act that takes no payload at all: every input it needs is already on the case. It is reachable from two different rent-track states, and which one applies depends on `rcs_required`.

The ordinary path runs from `RCS_IN_PROGRESS` to `RENTS_APPROVED` and demands a comparability study on the case in state `ACCEPTED`. If `rcs_required` is true and no accepted study exists, the request is refused with `ERR_RCS_NOT_ACCEPTED` (HTTP 409), and because that guard outranks the fiscal ceilings, a case that both lacks an accepted study and would breach its ceiling reports this code. The fork alternative runs directly from `OPTION_SELECTED` to `RENTS_APPROVED` and exists for the simple case: it requires `rcs_required` to be false and `adjustment_method` to be `OCAF`, and it settles at the uncapped operating-cost-adjustment rent precisely because no accepted study — and therefore no comparable ceiling — exists. Both paths require the approving `X-User-ID` to differ from the case's `challenge_reviewer_user_id`, on pain of `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

**Choosing and testing the rent.** The approved rent is selected, never recomputed. When `adjustment_method` is `OCAF`, the candidate is the case's stored `ocaf_rent_per_unit_cents`, and the ceiling is the case's `comparable_market_rent_cents` when `rcs_required` is true and no ceiling at all when it is false. When `adjustment_method` is `BUDGET_BASED`, the candidate is the case's stored `budget_rent_per_unit_cents`, and the ceiling is 110 percent of `comparable_market_rent_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents before the comparison. Where a ceiling exists and the candidate strictly exceeds it, the approval is refused rather than trimmed: the operating-cost-adjustment track reports `ERR_RENT_EXCEEDS_COMPARABLE` and the budget-based track reports `ERR_BUDGET_RENT_EXCEEDS_CAP`, both HTTP 422. A candidate exactly equal to its ceiling is accepted. Neither candidate rent is re-derived at approval time — whatever the last election or the last restructuring approval wrote is what gets tested.

A successful approval writes the selected candidate into `approved_rent_per_unit_cents`, records the approving `X-User-ID` in `rents_approver_user_id`, and sets `rent_track_status` to `RENTS_APPROVED`.

Execution is the single coupling point between the two tracks. A `ContractAdministrator` executes the renewal with a payload carrying `expected_contract_version`, and the request requires the case's `notice_track_status` to be exactly `RENEWAL_NOTICED` — a case whose notice track sits anywhere else, including a case on the opt-out path, is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. The version must match the contract's current `version` or the request is refused with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Execution then moves `rent_track_status` from `RENTS_APPROVED` to the terminal `EXECUTED` and, on the contract, moves `lifecycle_status` from `RENEWAL_PENDING` to the terminal `RENEWED` while writing four things: `renewed_rent_per_unit_cents` and `contract_rent_per_unit_cents` both take the case's `approved_rent_per_unit_cents`, `renewed_option` takes the case's `renewal_option`, `renewed_expiration_date` takes the old `expiration_date` advanced by `term_months` whole months, and `expiration_date` itself is advanced to that same new date. The contract's `version` increments by 1. From that moment the renewed rent is the contract's in-force per-unit rent, and every downstream computation that reads a rent basis — certification snapshots, special-claim valuations — sees the new figure.

### 3.7. Tenant Notices, the Opt-Out Path and Enhanced Vouchers

Statutory notification is the owner-agent's obligation, and it is transacted through notices issued under a renewal case. A notice payload names the `renewal_case_id`, a `notice_type`, an `effective_date` on which the noticed action takes effect, and an optional `tenant_id`; when `tenant_id` is omitted the notice is project-wide rather than addressed to a single household. Four notice types exist, and each carries its own statutory lead time: `RENEWAL_INTENT` requires 120 days, `OPT_OUT_ONE_YEAR` requires 365 days, `TERMINATION_120_DAY` requires 120 days, and `RENT_CHANGE` requires 30 days.

Issuance stamps `issued_date` with the clock date and computes two figures. `lead_days` is the whole calendar days from `issued_date` to `effective_date`, with the issue day excluded and the effective day counted; weekends and holidays are not excluded. `required_lead_days` is the statutory figure for the notice's `notice_type`. The notice is compliant when `lead_days` is at least `required_lead_days` — exactly meeting the requirement is compliant — and a notice that falls short is refused outright with `ERR_NOTICE_WINDOW_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422). A surviving notice is created with `status` `ISSUED` and a null `rescind_reason`.

Issuance has two conditional side effects on the case. When `notice_type` is `RENEWAL_INTENT` and the case's notice track is `NOTICES_PENDING`, the track advances `NOTICES_PENDING` → `RENEWAL_NOTICED` and the case's `version` increments by 1 — this is what unlocks execution later. When `notice_type` is `OPT_OUT_ONE_YEAR`, the case's `opt_out_notice_id` is set to this notice's identifier and the case's `version` increments by 1, but the notice track does **not** move yet; the one-year notice merely arms the opt-out, which the owner must separately record.

**Recording the opt-out.** With an `ISSUED` `OPT_OUT_ONE_YEAR` notice on the case and the notice track still at `NOTICES_PENDING`, the owner-agent records the opt-out; a case with no such notice is refused with `ERR_OPT_OUT_NOTICE_MISSING` (HTTP 422). This is the deepest cascade in the system and it runs in four connected hops. The notice track moves `NOTICES_PENDING` → `OPT_OUT_NOTICED` and `opt_out_notice_id` is confirmed as that notice's identifier. Caused by that, the contract's `lifecycle_status` moves `RENEWAL_PENDING` → `OPT_OUT_PENDING` and its `version` increments by 1. Caused by that in turn, every household of the property whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE` → `OPT_OUT_NOTICED`, has `ev_eligible` set to true, and has its own `version` incremented by 1. Caused by that in turn, one enhanced voucher row in state `PENDING` is created for each household that just became eligible, processed in household `created_at` ascending order. No unit counter moves at this stage: a noticed household is still project-based assisted until its voucher is actually issued or it moves out.

**Computing the enhancement.** Each newly minted enhanced voucher records the `tenant_id` of its household and the `renewal_case_id` of the opting-out case, and carries three computed figures. `payment_standard_cents` is the greater of the case's `comparable_market_rent_cents` and the property's `published_fmr_cents`, plus the property's `current_utility_allowance_cents`. `ttp_snapshot_cents` is the `ttp_cents` of that household's certification whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, or zero when the household has never been certified. `entitlement_cents` is the payment standard less the greater of the snapshot total tenant payment and the 2,500-cent minimum rent, floored at zero. Every term is already an integer, so no intermediate rounding arises. A household whose entitlement computes to zero still receives a `PENDING` row — the record exists for audit — but its voucher cannot be issued.

**Issuing and rescinding.** A `PhaAdministrator` issues a `PENDING` voucher, and three things must hold: the voucher's `state` is `PENDING`, its `entitlement_cents` is strictly greater than zero, and the case's contract is in `lifecycle_status` `OPT_OUT_PENDING`. A zero entitlement is refused with `ERR_NO_ENHANCEMENT_ENTITLEMENT` (HTTP 422). Issuance moves the voucher `PENDING` → `ISSUED`, records the issuing `X-User-ID` in `issuing_pha_user_id` and the clock date in `issued_on_date`, moves the household `OPT_OUT_NOTICED` → `EV_ISSUED` with its `version` incremented, and moves one unit across the property ledger: `units_under_hap_count` decrements by 1, `enhanced_voucher_unit_count` increments by 1, and the property's `version` increments by 1, so the unit invariant holds. Rescission is the exact compensation. A `PhaAdministrator` rescinds an `ISSUED` voucher with a payload carrying a `reason`, provided the household's `status` is still `EV_ISSUED` and not `MOVED_OUT`; the voucher moves `ISSUED` → `RESCINDED`, the household returns `EV_ISSUED` → `OPT_OUT_NOTICED` with its `version` incremented, `enhanced_voucher_unit_count` decrements by 1, `units_under_hap_count` increments by 1, and the property's `version` increments by 1 — all three counters back to their pre-issuance values. `RESCINDED` is terminal for a voucher.

**Rescinding a notice, and unwinding the cascade.** Any `ISSUED` notice may be rescinded by the owner-agent with a payload carrying a `reason`, which moves it `ISSUED` → `RESCINDED` and records `rescind_reason`. What else happens depends on which notice it was. Rescinding the notice that is the case's `opt_out_notice_id`, while the notice track is `OPT_OUT_NOTICED`, compensates the entire opt-out cascade: the notice track returns `OPT_OUT_NOTICED` → `NOTICES_PENDING`, `opt_out_notice_id` is cleared to null and the case's `version` increments by 1; the contract's `lifecycle_status` returns `OPT_OUT_PENDING` → `RENEWAL_PENDING` with its `version` incremented by 1; every household on the case with `status` `OPT_OUT_NOTICED` returns to `ACTIVE` with `ev_eligible` set back to false and its `version` incremented by 1; and every enhanced voucher on the case in state `PENDING` moves `PENDING` → `RESCINDED` with its `version` incremented by 1, moving no unit counter because a pending voucher never moved one. This unwinding is only available while nothing has been issued: if even one enhanced voucher on the case is already in state `ISSUED`, the rescission of a one-year opt-out notice is refused with `ERR_ENHANCED_VOUCHER_ISSUED_BLOCKS_RESCIND` (HTTP 409), and the agency must rescind the vouchers first. Rescinding a `RENEWAL_INTENT` notice, by contrast, changes only the notice row: `notice_track_status` stays at `RENEWAL_NOTICED`, because the renewal-intent notification requirement is discharged by the act of noticing and no return transition out of `RENEWAL_NOTICED` is declared.

**Finalizing the opt-out.** A `ContractAdministrator` finalizes an opt-out on a case whose notice track is `OPT_OUT_NOTICED`, and two conditions must hold. The clock date must have reached or passed the `effective_date` of the notice named by `opt_out_notice_id` — the full one-year notice period must have elapsed — otherwise `ERR_OPT_OUT_NOTICE_PERIOD_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422). And every household of the property still in `status` `OPT_OUT_NOTICED` must hold an enhanced voucher in state `ISSUED`, otherwise `ERR_TENANT_VOUCHER_UNRESOLVED` (HTTP 422). The temporal check is evaluated first, so a premature finalization on a property with unissued vouchers reports the temporal code. Finalization moves the notice track `OPT_OUT_NOTICED` → the terminal `OPT_OUT_FINALIZED`, moves the contract's `lifecycle_status` `OPT_OUT_PENDING` → the terminal `EXPIRED_OPT_OUT` with `hap_ledger_balance_cents` zeroed and the contract's `version` incremented by 1, and closes the property's unit ledger: whatever remained in `units_under_hap_count` is added to `released_unit_count`, `units_under_hap_count` is set to 0, and the property's `version` increments by 1, preserving the unit invariant.

Notices are readable individually, listable per case with optional `notice_type` and `status` filters, and listable per household with an optional `status` filter. Enhanced vouchers are readable individually and listable both per case and per household with an optional `state` filter.

### 3.8. Utility Allowances, Household Certifications and Departures

Households enter the system through the owner-agent, with a payload naming the `property_id`, a `unit_number`, a `household_name` for the head of household, an `annual_adjusted_income_cents` of at least 0, an `annual_gross_income_cents` that may not be lower than the adjusted figure, and a `family_size` of at least 1. Unit numbers must be unique within a property across all households whose `status` is not `MOVED_OUT`; a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_UNIT_NUMBER` (HTTP 409), and a unit vacated by a move-out may be reused. A new household is `ACTIVE`, with `move_out_date`, `current_ttp_cents`, and `current_hap_cents` all null and `ev_eligible` false. Its lifecycle spans `ACTIVE`, `OPT_OUT_NOTICED`, `EV_ISSUED`, and the terminal `MOVED_OUT`.

**Certifying a household.** A certification is what turns income into money. The payload names the `tenant_id`, a `cert_type`, an `effective_date`, and `expected_tenant_version`. Client writes may use `INITIAL`, `ANNUAL`, or `INTERIM` only; `UA_ADJUSTMENT` is reserved to the utility-allowance apply cascade and a client that supplies it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Certifications must be spaced: a new certification's `effective_date` must be strictly more than 30 days after the `effective_date` of the household's currently `ACTIVE` certification, so a certification landing exactly 30 days later is refused with `ERR_CERT_INTERVAL_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409) and only day 31 onward is accepted. Each certification freezes two figures at creation: `utility_allowance_cents` snapshots the property's `current_utility_allowance_cents`, and `contract_rent_per_unit_cents` snapshots the household's contract's in-force per-unit rent, which is `renewed_rent_per_unit_cents` when that field is non-null and `contract_rent_per_unit_cents` otherwise.

**The total tenant payment.** From those inputs the certification's money fields are derived as follows. Divide the household's `annual_adjusted_income_cents` by twelve and round half up to whole cents to get the monthly adjusted income; divide its `annual_gross_income_cents` by twelve and round half up to whole cents to get the monthly gross income. Apply 30 percent to the monthly adjusted income and round that product half up to whole cents; apply 10 percent to the monthly gross income and round that product half up to whole cents. The `ttp_cents` is the greatest of those two rounded products and the minimum rent of 2,500 cents — a three-way maximum. The rounding order is pinned: each monthly income is rounded before its percentage is applied, and each percentage product is rounded before the three-way comparison; applying the percentage to the annual figure and dividing afterwards is not permitted. From the total tenant payment three further figures follow. `gross_rent_cents` is the certification's `contract_rent_per_unit_cents` plus its `utility_allowance_cents`. `tenant_rent_cents` is the total tenant payment less the utility allowance, floored at zero. `utility_reimbursement_cents` is the utility allowance less the total tenant payment, floored at zero — the mirror figure, paid to the tenant when the allowance exceeds what the tenant can be asked to pay. `hap_cents` is the gross rent less the total tenant payment, floored at zero, and it is the per-household term that the monthly voucher aggregates. Income is read from the household once, at certification time, and never re-read afterwards.

Creating a certification makes it `ACTIVE`, flips the household's previously `ACTIVE` certification to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` and increments that older certification's `version` by 1, re-points the household's `current_ttp_cents` at the new `ttp_cents` and its `current_hap_cents` at the new `hap_cents`, and increments the household's `version` by 1. The supersession transition is a system act with one precondition worth naming: the new certification's `effective_date` is at or after the superseded one's, which the spacing rule already guarantees for client writes.

**Analysing the utility allowance.** An owner-agent opens a utility allowance analysis with a payload naming the `property_id`, a `method` of either `FACTOR_BASED` or `ENGINEERING_BASED`, an `effective_date`, and exactly one method-specific figure: `utility_rate_change_pct` for a factor-based analysis, or `engineering_estimate_cents` for an engineering-based one. Supplying the wrong one, or neither, is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The rate-change factor is signed and bounded to the range from negative 0.5 through positive 0.5 inclusive — a value of 0.09 means a nine percent increase — and the engineering estimate must be at least 0. Creation snapshots the property's `current_utility_allowance_cents` into `baseline_allowance_cents` and then computes three things. `proposed_allowance_cents` is, for a factor-based analysis, the baseline multiplied by one plus `utility_rate_change_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents; for an engineering-based analysis it is simply `engineering_estimate_cents`. `change_pct` is the proposed allowance less the baseline, divided by the baseline, rounded half up to four decimal places. `requires_field_office_approval` is true exactly when the magnitude of `change_pct` is strictly greater than 0.10, so a swing whose magnitude is exactly ten percent stays with the contract administrator. A baseline allowance of zero would make the change ratio undefined and is rejected upstream as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The analysis is created in state `DRAFT` with `approver_user_id` and `applied_on_date` null and `recertified_tenant_count` at 0.

An analysis moves through `DRAFT` → `SUBMITTED` → `APPROVED` → the terminal `APPLIED`, with a rejection loop: a rejected analysis returns to `SUBMITTED` on resubmission rather than to `DRAFT`. Submission — from `DRAFT` or from `REJECTED` — is the owner-agent's act and requires that no **other** analysis on the same property is in state `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`; a second in-flight analysis is refused with `ERR_UA_ANALYSIS_IN_FLIGHT` (HTTP 409). Deciding a `SUBMITTED` analysis takes a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` with an optional `reason`: acceptance moves it to `APPROVED` and rejection to `REJECTED`, and either way `approver_user_id` records the deciding `X-User-ID`. Authority depends on the escalation flag of §2.11 — when `requires_field_office_approval` is true only a `HudFieldOffice` user may decide, and a `ContractAdministrator` attempt is refused with `ERR_UA_CHANGE_REQUIRES_FIELD_OFFICE`.

**Applying an allowance, and the recertification cascade.** Applying an `APPROVED` analysis is a `ContractAdministrator` act with a payload carrying `expected_property_version`. Two guards precede it. The coverage interval must be clear: the analysis's `effective_date` must be at or after the property's `last_applied_ua_effective_date` advanced by twelve whole months, an inclusive boundary, so an effective date landing exactly on the anniversary is accepted while anything earlier is refused with `ERR_UA_INTERVAL_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409); a property that has never had an analysis applied always satisfies this. And the version must match the property's current `version`, or the request is refused with `ERR_STALE_PROPERTY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The apply then runs a four-hop cascade. The analysis moves `APPROVED` → `APPLIED` and stamps `applied_on_date` with the clock date. Caused by that, the property's `current_utility_allowance_cents` takes `proposed_allowance_cents`, its `last_applied_ua_effective_date` takes the analysis's `effective_date`, and its `version` increments by 1. Caused by that, every household of the property whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `OPT_OUT_NOTICED`, or `EV_ISSUED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, has its `ACTIVE` certification flipped to `SUPERSEDED` and a replacement certification created with `cert_type` `UA_ADJUSTMENT` and `effective_date` equal to the analysis's effective date, whose `ttp_cents`, `tenant_rent_cents`, `utility_reimbursement_cents`, `gross_rent_cents`, and `hap_cents` are all recomputed against the new allowance; the household's `current_ttp_cents` and `current_hap_cents` are re-pointed and its `version` increments by 1; and `recertified_tenant_count` is set to the number of certifications created. Caused by that, every voucher submission of the property's contract still in state `DRAFT` has its `requested_hap_cents` and `included_certification_count` recomputed against the new certifications, so a draft month never bills a superseded allowance.

Because the property's `current_utility_allowance_cents` is defined as the `proposed_allowance_cents` of the applied analysis with the greatest `effective_date` — with a tie broken by `created_at` ascending, the last row winning — and as `baseline_utility_allowance_cents` when no analysis has ever been applied, the field is always reconstructible from the analysis history.

**Departures.** A move-out is recorded by the owner-agent with a payload carrying a `move_out_date`, and it is available from `ACTIVE` and from `EV_ISSUED`. The date may not be earlier than the `effective_date` of the household's `ACTIVE` certification; an earlier date is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The household moves to the terminal `MOVED_OUT` with `move_out_date` recorded. The unit ledger then moves according to where the household stood: `released_unit_count` always increments by 1, and the offsetting decrement lands on `enhanced_voucher_unit_count` when the household was `EV_ISSUED` and on `units_under_hap_count` otherwise; the property's `version` increments by 1. Crucially, the household's certifications are left completely untouched — the `ACTIVE` certification stays `ACTIVE` — so that a voucher period which the household partly occupied can still be prorated and already-closed periods still reconcile.

Certifications are readable individually and listable per household with optional `status` and `cert_type` filters; households are readable individually and listable per property with an optional `status` filter.

### 3.9. Monthly Vouchers, Special Claims and Subsidy Reconciliation

The monthly voucher is how an owner bills HUD for the assistance actually delivered. Opening one requires a payload naming the `contract_id`, a `period_month` in `YYYY-MM` form, and `expected_contract_version`. Only one live voucher per billing month is permitted per contract: if a voucher for the same `contract_id` and `period_month` exists in any state other than `REVERSED`, the request is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOUCHER_PERIOD` (HTTP 409) — a reversed month may be re-billed. A stale contract version is refused with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION`. The voucher is created in state `DRAFT` with `paid_amount_cents`, `paid_on_date`, and `reversal_reason` all null, and with its request amount already computed; creating it also increments the contract's `version` by 1.

**Aggregating the month's HAP.** The `requested_hap_cents` of a voucher is built from the certifications governing the billed month. Establish the first and the last calendar day of `period_month` and the number of days that month contains. The candidate households are those of the contract's property whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `OPT_OUT_NOTICED`, or `MOVED_OUT`, and, for the moved-out ones, only those whose `move_out_date` falls strictly after the first day of the month — a household that left on or before the first day contributes nothing. Households whose `status` is `EV_ISSUED` are excluded entirely, because their assistance has become tenant-based and is no longer billed on this contract. For each candidate household, taken in `created_at` ascending order, select the governing certification: among that household's certifications whose `effective_date` is at or before the last day of the month, take the one with the greatest `effective_date`, breaking a tie by `created_at` ascending with the last row winning. The certification's `status` is irrelevant to this selection — a `SUPERSEDED` certification still governs a month that closed before its successor took effect. A household with no such certification is skipped. If the household is `MOVED_OUT` with a `move_out_date` at or before the last day of the month, its term is prorated: count the whole days from the first day of the month to the `move_out_date`, not counting the move-out day itself, multiply the certification's `hap_cents` by that day count, divide by the number of days in the month, and round half up to whole cents. Otherwise the term is the certification's `hap_cents` in full. `requested_hap_cents` is the exact integer sum of those per-household terms, and `included_certification_count` is the number of households that contributed one. The per-household rounding is mandatory and comes first: summing unrounded prorated terms and rounding once at the end is not permitted. This aggregation runs when the voucher is created, again when it is submitted, and again for every `DRAFT` voucher swept by a utility-allowance apply.

Submission moves the voucher `DRAFT` → `SUBMITTED` and is the owner-agent's act. The billed month must have fully closed: the clock date must be at or after the first day of the month following `period_month`, and an early submission is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED` (HTTP 422). The recomputed `requested_hap_cents` must also be strictly greater than zero, or the submission is refused with `ERR_ZERO_VOUCHER_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). Payment is a `ContractAdministrator` act on a `SUBMITTED` voucher and is the point where the money invariant bites: `requested_hap_cents` must not exceed the contract's `hap_ledger_balance_cents`, and an over-draw is refused with `ERR_HAP_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402). Payment moves the voucher `SUBMITTED` → `PAID`, sets `paid_amount_cents` to `requested_hap_cents` and `paid_on_date` to the clock date, adds the paid amount to the contract's `paid_hap_total_cents`, subtracts it from `hap_ledger_balance_cents`, and increments the contract's `version` by 1.

Reversal is the compensating move, available to a `ContractAdministrator` on a `PAID` voucher with a payload carrying a `reason`. It is blocked while any special claim whose `claim_period_month` equals this voucher's `period_month` is already in state `PAID`: reversing the covering voucher out from under a paid claim would break the ordering the claim relied on, so the attempt is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_PAID_BLOCKS_REVERSAL` (HTTP 409). A permitted reversal moves the voucher `PAID` → the terminal `REVERSED`, records `reversal_reason`, retains `paid_amount_cents` for audit while stopping it counting toward the ledger, subtracts that retained amount from the contract's `paid_hap_total_cents`, adds it back to `hap_ledger_balance_cents`, and increments the contract's `version` by 1 — restoring both figures to exactly their pre-payment values.

**Special claims.** A claim covers a loss the owner incurred, and its payload names the `contract_id`, a `claim_type` of `VACANCY`, `UNPAID_RENT`, or `TENANT_DAMAGES`, the `unit_number` the claim concerns, a `claim_period_month` in `YYYY-MM` form, and then the fields its type needs: `vacancy_start_date` and optionally `vacancy_end_date` and `estimated_vacancy_days` for a vacancy claim, `claimed_amount_cents` for the other two, and an optional `security_deposit_balance_cents` that defaults to 0. Creation lands the claim in state `DRAFT` with `paid_amount_cents` and `decision_reason` null and with `vacancy_days_claimed`, `valuation_variant`, and `computed_amount_cents` already derived.

The valuation reads the contract's in-force per-unit rent — `renewed_rent_per_unit_cents` when that field is non-null, otherwise `contract_rent_per_unit_cents` — and then branches into one of three variants recorded in `valuation_variant`. A vacancy claim whose `vacancy_end_date` is non-null, meaning the unit has been re-rented, takes the `ACTUAL_CHRONOLOGICAL` variant: `vacancy_days_claimed` is the lesser of 60 days and the whole days from `vacancy_start_date` to `vacancy_end_date` (the first vacant day counted, the re-rental day not), and the amount is built by walking the calendar months spanned by the vacancy from the start date forward across exactly that many days, in ascending month order. For each month, count the vacant days falling inside it, compute that month's daily rate by dividing the unit rent by the number of days in that month and rounding half up to whole cents, and multiply the daily rate by the vacant day count in that month; `computed_amount_cents` is the exact sum of those month subtotals. The month-by-month divisor matters: February's 28-day divisor yields a higher daily rate than January's 31-day divisor, so identical vacancy lengths in different months produce different amounts. A vacancy claim whose `vacancy_end_date` is null, meaning the unit is still vacant, takes the `PROJECTED_ESTIMATE` variant instead: `vacancy_days_claimed` is the lesser of 60 days and the owner's `estimated_vacancy_days` (which must be at least 1), and `computed_amount_cents` is 80 percent of the unit rent multiplied by that day count and divided by a flat 30-day month, with a single half-up rounding to whole cents at the end. The calendar is ignored entirely on this branch, which is why the two vacancy variants diverge materially on the same number of vacant days.

Both remaining claim types take the `DIRECT_LEDGER` variant with `vacancy_days_claimed` of 0. For an `UNPAID_RENT` claim, take the lesser of `claimed_amount_cents` and one month of unit rent, subtract `security_deposit_balance_cents`, and floor the result at zero. For a `TENANT_DAMAGES` claim, take the lesser of `claimed_amount_cents` and two months of unit rent, subtract `security_deposit_balance_cents`, and floor at zero. The deposit is deducted after the cap, not before it.

Submitting a claim re-runs the whole valuation against the contract's in-force rent and then applies two gates. The amount must be strictly positive, so a claim fully offset by the security deposit or a zero-day vacancy is refused with `ERR_ZERO_CLAIM_AMOUNT` (HTTP 422). And the vacancy day cap is enforced in aggregate, not per claim: this claim's `vacancy_days_claimed` plus the sum of `vacancy_days_claimed` over every other claim on the same `contract_id` and the same `unit_number` in state `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, or `PAID` must not exceed 60 days, and a breach is refused with `ERR_VACANCY_DAYS_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Submission then moves the claim `DRAFT` → `SUBMITTED`.

A `ContractAdministrator` decides a submitted claim with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY` and an optional `reason`, moving it to `APPROVED` or to the terminal `DENIED` and recording `decision_reason` either way. Payment of an `APPROVED` claim is ordered behind the covering voucher: the voucher submission whose `contract_id` matches the claim's and whose `period_month` equals the claim's `claim_period_month` must exist and be in state `PAID`, otherwise the payment is refused with `ERR_COVERING_VOUCHER_NOT_PAID` (HTTP 409). The claim's `computed_amount_cents` must also fit inside `hap_ledger_balance_cents`, on pain of `ERR_HAP_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. Payment moves the claim `APPROVED` → the terminal `PAID`, sets `paid_amount_cents` to `computed_amount_cents`, adds that amount to the contract's `paid_claims_total_cents`, subtracts it from `hap_ledger_balance_cents`, and increments the contract's `version` by 1.

**Reconciliation.** Finally, a read-only projection reconciles a contract's subsidy position and persists nothing. It walks that contract's voucher submissions ordered by `period_month` ascending, with a tie broken by `created_at` ascending, and reports eight figures. `requested_total_cents` sums `requested_hap_cents` over vouchers in state `SUBMITTED` or `PAID`, so drafts are excluded. `paid_hap_total_cents` sums `paid_amount_cents` over vouchers in state `PAID`. `reversed_total_cents` sums `paid_amount_cents` over vouchers in state `REVERSED`, reported separately precisely because those amounts are retained but no longer count. `paid_claims_total_cents` sums `paid_amount_cents` over special claims in state `PAID`. `authorized_hap_budget_cents` echoes the contract's authorization. `variance_cents` is the requested total less the paid total. `hap_ledger_balance_cents` is the authorization less the paid voucher total less the paid claim total, and it must always equal the contract's own stored `hap_ledger_balance_cents` — a divergence between the projection and the stored field is a defect. `paid_period_count` is the number of billing periods paid. Every sum is an exact integer cent total with no rounding anywhere.
