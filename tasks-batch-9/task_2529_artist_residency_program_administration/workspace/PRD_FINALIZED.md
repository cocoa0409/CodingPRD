# Artist Residency Program Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An artist residency organization runs one competitive intake a year, and almost every problem it has is a coordination problem rather than a creative one. A single cohort passes through an application cycle with its own discipline categories and portfolio rules, a scoring panel whose members must declare conflicts of interest before they can be trusted, a slate that has to respect both discipline minimums and a promise made to returning alumni, and then — for the artists who accept — a physical residency with a studio that must actually be capable of the work, a bed, a meal plan, a stipend that is taxed differently depending on citizenship, a visa timeline that starts months before anybody arrives, and a set of public engagement obligations that the funder will ask about at the end of the year. Today all of this is tracked in spreadsheets that disagree with each other. The organization does not know, on any given morning, how much of the stipend pool is still uncommitted, which residents are cleared to enter a welding studio, or which visa cases are about to miss their filing deadline.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces those spreadsheets. The service is the single system of record for cycles, applications, panel review, residencies, facilities, sponsorship, money and outcomes, and it is deliberately opinionated: every number that a human might otherwise compute by hand is computed by the server and read back, every state change that ought to have consequences elsewhere has those consequences applied in the same request, and every conserved quantity — committed stipend money, reserved studio-days, occupied bed-nights, cohort slots — is maintained as a live ledger rather than reconstructed on demand. Where two reasonable readings of a rule exist, this document names the one the service implements.

Two constraints shape the whole design. First, the service is **time-driven but not wall-clock-driven**: submission windows, session dates, visa deadlines and payment periods are all evaluated against a logical clock that operators can set, so that a cycle can be rehearsed end to end before it is run for real. Second, the service enforces **segregation of duties** in the places where an organization of this size is structurally tempted to cut corners — the panelist who scored an application may not be the chair who locks the slate it appears on, and the finance officer who built a payment schedule may not be the one who releases the money.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Participants, Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service, without exception, carries two headers: `X-User-ID` identifying the acting user and `X-User-Role` naming the capacity in which they are acting. A request that omits `X-User-ID`, or supplies it empty, is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_USER_ID` (HTTP 401) before anything else is considered — this is the very first check in the service's validation ladder. The second check is `X-User-Role`: it must be present and must be spelled exactly as one of the eight roles the system recognises — `ProgramAdmin`, `Applicant`, `Panelist`, `PanelChair`, `FinanceOfficer`, `VisaCoordinator`, `FacilitiesManager`, `Funder` — and anything else, including a missing header or a role name that differs only in case, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ROLE` (HTTP 401). Only once both headers are well-formed does the service ask whether that role is permitted to perform the requested operation at all; when it is not, the answer is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). The per-operation permission table is given in §4.

The eight roles divide into two identity families, and the distinction matters because it decides whether `X-User-ID` is compared against stored data.

- When `X-User-Role` is `Applicant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Applicant` row; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Applicant` compare the resource's applicant foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Panelist`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Panelist` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Panelist` compare the resource's panelist foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `PanelChair`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Panelist` row — chair is a privilege carried on a panelist record, not a separate directory of people — and ownership and segregation checks compare panelist foreign keys to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `FinanceOfficer`, `VisaCoordinator`, `FacilitiesManager` or `Funder`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — a stipend disbursement stores the acting finance officer's identifier in `scheduled_by_user_id` when its schedule is generated and in `paid_by_user_id` when it is paid — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

For the three entity-backed roles (`Applicant`, `Panelist`, `PanelChair`), the supplied `X-User-ID` must resolve to an existing row of the backing entity — an `Applicant` row for `Applicant`, a `Panelist` row for both `Panelist` and `PanelChair`. When it does not resolve, the request fails with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403); this check is evaluated on every endpoint whose permitted roles include one of those three, and it runs after the plain role-permission check and before any resource is loaded. A caller presenting `PanelChair` faces one extra gate: the `Panelist` row behind their `X-User-ID` must carry `chair_eligible` set to true. A chair-ineligible panelist attempting the slate lock is rejected with `ERR_CHAIR_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 403), and that rejection is evaluated immediately after the backing-row check, ahead of any lookup of the cycle itself.

Ownership is not merely advisory. An acting `Applicant` may only operate on an application or residency whose `applicant_id` equals their `X-User-ID`; this governs editing an application, submitting it, responding to its offer, attaching or deleting a portfolio item on it, and withdrawing from a residency. An acting `Panelist` may only record scores on, or declare a conflict for, a panel assignment whose `panelist_id` equals their `X-User-ID`. Both families of violation surface as `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Separately from ownership, the service refuses operations whose acting user has already performed an incompatible earlier step in the same workflow; those refusals carry `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403) and are described in §2.10.

`Funder` deserves a note because its narrowness is deliberate: the funding body reads aggregate outcomes, the residency record and the facilities record, but never the rubric detail of individual reviews and never any money-settlement operation.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection-listing endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when the caller omits it, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` outside the range of 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a subsection below says otherwise, every list is returned in ascending `created_at` order — oldest row first — and pagination is applied after that ordering. List responses populate four fields in the `meta` object: the effective `limit`, the effective `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filter before the page window is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows matching the filter remain beyond the current page). Single-resource responses omit all four.

Filters offered by a list endpoint narrow `total_count` as well as the returned page. All filters combine conjunctively: a request naming both a residency and a status returns only rows matching both.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every resource the service persists is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients must never parse them, and — importantly for the selection and ranking rules in §3 — the service itself never uses an `id` as a tie-break or ordering key. Where a deterministic tie-break is needed, an operator-assigned business key such as a studio's `code` is used instead.

Most resources also carry three bookkeeping fields. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the row was created, rendered with three fractional digits. `updated_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the row's most recent mutation, in the same format; on a freshly created row it equals `created_at`. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, whether the mutation was requested directly or applied as a side effect of an operation on another resource. Any statement in §3 of the form "the residency's revision counter advances" means precisely this: `version` increases by 1 and `updated_at` is refreshed.

Two resources are deliberately immutable and therefore carry neither counter: a `Discipline` row and a `PortfolioItem` row expose only `id` and `created_at`. They have no `updated_at` and no `version`, because the service offers no operation that edits them — a discipline is configured once while its cycle is still a draft, and a portfolio item is either attached or removed, never amended. Every other resource in the system — `Cycle`, `Applicant`, `Application`, `Panelist`, `PanelAssignment`, `Residency`, `Studio`, `StudioAssignment`, `HousingUnit`, `HousingAssignment`, `VisaCase`, `StipendDisbursement` and `Obligation` — carries all four common fields.

### 2.5. The Logical Programme Clock

Nothing in this system reads the wall clock. Submission windows, session start and end dates, visa filing deadlines, consulate windows, disbursement dates and every comparison the specification phrases as "now" are all evaluated against a single server-side **logical clock**. At service startup that clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; this startup default is mandatory and the service must never fall back to real time if the clock has not been set. An administrative test-driving capability moves the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and echoes the instant it now holds. Setting the clock writes no entity row and increments no `version`, but it does change the answer to every subsequent read that depends on the current moment — most visibly a visa case's `days_to_filing_deadline`, which is recomputed on every read rather than stored.

Where a rule compares a date-only value (a session start date, a filing deadline) against "now", the comparison uses the calendar date of the logical clock instant in UTC. Where a rule compares an instant (a submission window bound) it uses the full instant.

### 2.6. Path Resolution, Foreign Keys and Reverse Reachability

Collections in this API are flat: cycles, disciplines, applicants, applications, portfolio items, panelists, panel assignments, residencies, studios, studio assignments, housing units, housing assignments, visa cases, stipend disbursements and obligations are each addressed at their own top-level collection, and no resource is reachable only through a parent path. Ownership is expressed through foreign-key fields in request bodies and through the filters offered on each list endpoint, so every relationship declared in §3 can be walked in reverse: the applications of a cycle, the portfolio items of an application, the panel assignments of a panelist, the disbursements of a residency, and so on.

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first unresolvable segment decides the error. Each resource type has its own not-found code — `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISCIPLINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PORTFOLIO_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PANELIST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PANEL_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESIDENCY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDIO_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDIO_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HOUSING_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HOUSING_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VISA_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_OBLIGATION_NOT_FOUND` — and all of them are HTTP 404.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved by the same rule, using the same catalogue of not-found codes, in the order in which this document declares the fields of that request. Creating a discipline, an application, a portfolio item, a panel assignment, a residency, a studio assignment, a housing assignment or a visa case therefore fails on the *first* unresolvable reference in declaration order, not on some arbitrary one. Foreign-key resolution happens after path resolution and before any shape or range validation of the remaining body fields.

### 2.7. Money, Counts, Percentages and Rounding

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in **cents**. There are no fractional cents and no floating-point currency anywhere in a request or a response: an application fee, a stipend pool, a weekly rate, a gross payment, a withheld amount and a net amount are all whole numbers of cents. Slot counts, portfolio item counts, bed counts, headcounts, week counts, day counts and event counts are likewise integers.

Two families of derived numbers do carry decimals, and both use the same rounding convention — **round half up**, meaning a value exactly on a half boundary rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude.

- **Rubric scores and composite scores** are carried to two decimal places. Rounding is applied *twice* in the scoring pipeline, once when an individual reviewer's weighted total is formed and again when reviewer totals are averaged into an application score; §3.4 gives the exact sequence, and the double rounding is intentional rather than an artefact.
- **Percentages in the funder outcome report** are carried to two decimal places, again rounded half up. Where the denominator of a percentage is zero the service reports `null` rather than zero — an absence of data is not the same as a rate of nought.

Withholding is the one place where a rate meets cents. A withholding rate is a decimal with two decimal places; when it is applied to a taxable amount the product is rounded half up to whole cents at the moment of application. Because that rounding happens per payment rather than once per year, the sum of the amounts actually withheld can differ from the year-end statutory figure by a cent or two; §3.9 specifies how the difference is reported rather than hidden.

Alumni reserve arithmetic rounds in the opposite direction on purpose: the reserved slot count is the fraction multiplied by the cohort target, rounded **up** to the next whole slot. Workshop obligation counts round **down** to the next whole workshop. Weeks served on an early termination round **up**, so a partial week is paid as a full week. Each of these is stated again at the point of use.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Guards on Facility and Finance Writes

Three write operations in this system can legitimately supersede work that already exists — reassigning a studio on top of a residency's previous studio, booking a further bed in a multi-bed unit that already has tenants, and regenerating a stipend schedule to apply a corrected weekly rate. Because each of these is a *valid* product operation, no state precondition can refuse it, and a client working from a stale picture of the world would silently overwrite something it never saw. The service therefore guards all three with optimistic concurrency control on the **parent** resource's `version`.

- Creating a studio assignment requires the non-optional field `expected_residency_version`, carrying the `version` of the target `Residency` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the residency's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written. A release, a compliance suspension or another reassignment performed by someone else since the client's last read will all have advanced the residency's `version` and will all therefore be caught here.
- Creating a housing assignment requires the non-optional field `expected_housing_unit_version`, carrying the `version` of the target `HousingUnit` as the client last observed it. A mismatch against the unit's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This is what prevents a bed from being booked against an occupancy picture — the unit's bed-night ledger, its released rows — that has already moved on.
- Generating a residency's stipend schedule requires the non-optional field `expected_residency_version`, carrying the `version` of that `Residency` as the client last observed it. A mismatch against the residency's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), which stops a regeneration from being priced against an out-of-date session length, weekly rate or payment history.

No other endpoint in this system takes a version field, and no other endpoint may reject a request with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. Where a version field is required it is a required field: omitting it is a shape violation reported as `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400), not a stale-version rejection. The stale-version comparison itself is evaluated early — after path and foreign-key resolution and after the request body has been checked for shape and range, but before any domain state, safety, temporal, capacity or fiscal rule is consulted.

### 2.9. The Order in Which Rejections Are Reported

Many requests in this system violate more than one rule at once, and which error the caller receives is part of the contract, not an implementation detail. The service evaluates checks in a fixed ladder and reports the **first** failure it finds. The ladder runs:

1. **Authentication and authorization** — the `X-User-ID` header, then the `X-User-Role` header, then role permission for the operation, then backing-row resolution for entity-backed roles, then chair eligibility.
2. **Existence** — path parameters left to right, then request-body foreign keys in declaration order.
3. **Request shape and declared ranges** — required fields present with the declared JSON type, enum members recognised, dates well-formed, numeric and string ranges respected, pagination bounds respected, then business-key uniqueness, then the cross-entity referential check that a named discipline belongs to the named cycle.
4. **Stale-version guards** — as described in §2.8.
5. **Ownership** — an acting `Applicant` owning the application or residency, an acting `Panelist` owning the panel assignment.
6. **State preconditions** — cycle stage, application status, panel-assignment status, panelist recusal, residency status, visa-case status, disbursement status, obligation status, facility availability.
7. **Safety and compliance** — training requirements, compliance suspension, studio capability and ventilation matching.
8. **Cross-entity referential rules** — visa approval before check-in, visa eligibility, document completeness, predecessor-residency eligibility.
9. **Temporal rules** — submission windows, offered session lengths, session start not in the past, assignment windows inside the session, single-occupancy interval conflicts, visa filing deadline and consulate window, disbursement payment order.
10. **Domain constraints** — fee settlement and waiver eligibility, then portfolio counts and media coverage (an unsettled fee is reported ahead of a portfolio problem, as §3.3 sets out), duplicate associations, meal-plan availability, affiliation conflict, segregation of duties, panel-review completeness, discipline minimum feasibility, alumni reserve sanity, cohort minimum sanity, studio availability for auto-selection, outstanding obligations.
11. **Capacity and money — always last** — shared-studio headcount, multi-bed housing headcount, open cohort slots, and finally the stipend pool.

The rationale is that a caller should be told about the most fundamental thing that is wrong. A resident who has not finished equipment training must hear about the training, not about a calendar clash. A residency that has not even been scheduled must hear that before it hears about a meal plan. And money is always the last thing checked, so an ineligible offer is reported as an offer problem rather than as a budget problem.

Three conflicts are worth calling out explicitly, because a naïve implementation gets them backwards:

- An `ACCEPT` on an application that is not in `OFFERED` status *and* whose stipend would not fit the remaining pool is reported as `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_OFFERED` (HTTP 409), never as `ERR_STIPEND_POOL_EXHAUSTED`.
- A studio assignment request for a residency whose `compliance_status` is `SUSPENDED`, where the requested window also collides with an existing occupancy, is reported as `ERR_COMPLIANCE_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 422), never as `ERR_STUDIO_INTERVAL_CONFLICT`.
- A housing request naming a unit without meals for a residency that is no longer `SCHEDULED` is reported as `ERR_RESIDENCY_NOT_SCHEDULED` (HTTP 409), never as `ERR_MEAL_PLAN_NOT_AVAILABLE`.

### 2.10. Cascades, Ledger Synchronisation and Segregation of Duties

A great deal of this system's behaviour lives in the *consequences* of an operation rather than in the operation itself. Three cascades in particular are named in §3 and are the deepest chains in the system: the offer-acceptance cascade that brings a residency and, where needed, a visa case into being while committing money on the cycle; the severe-conflict recusal cascade that can strip a panelist out of an entire cycle and demote already-scored applications back into review; and the termination cascade that voids future payments, re-prices the award and releases the difference back to the funding cycle. In every case, each link of the cascade is applied inside the same request as the triggering operation — there is no deferred or eventual settlement anywhere in this system — and every entity a cascade touches has its own `version` advanced by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed.

Four quantities behave as conserved ledgers and are recomputed, never incrementally patched, whenever anything that feeds them changes: a cycle's `stipend_committed_cents` and its mirror `stipend_uncommitted_cents`; a cycle's `slots_filled_count` and its mirror `slots_open_count`; a studio's `reserved_resident_days` and `active_assignment_count`; and a housing unit's `occupied_bed_nights`. The invariant the organization cares about is reversibility: when a residency is withdrawn, the committed stipend returns to exactly the figure it held before the offer was accepted; when a studio assignment is released, the studio's reserved days return to exactly the figure they held before that assignment existed, provided no other active assignment was added in between; and when a payment is reversed, the residency's paid and withheld totals return to exactly their pre-payment values.

Segregation of duties is enforced at three points and always with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). First, the acting chair locking a cycle's slate must not be the panelist on **any** panel assignment in that cycle whose status is `SCORED` or `CONFLICT_DECLARED` — a chair who merely holds unscored assignments is still allowed to lock. Second, the finance officer marking a disbursement paid must not be the finance officer recorded in that row's `scheduled_by_user_id`. Third, the finance officer reversing a payment must not be the one recorded in `paid_by_user_id`. Because `PanelChair` and `Panelist` share a single backing record, the first of these three is a real constraint rather than a formality: every chair is also a potential reviewer.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Configuring a Cycle and Its Discipline Categories

Everything in this system hangs off a **cycle** — one annual intake, with its own money, its own calendar and its own rules. A `ProgramAdmin` opens a cycle in status `DRAFT` by supplying eleven configuration values: a human-readable `code` of 1 to 32 characters that must be unique across all cycles (a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`, HTTP 409); the inclusive submission window bounds `open_at` and `close_at` as ISO 8601 UTC instants — each normalised to UTC when stored and read back in the three-fractional-digit rendering of §2.4 rather than byte-for-byte as supplied, so a bound given as `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z` is returned as `2026-09-01T00:00:00.000Z` — where `close_at` must fall strictly later than `open_at`; the `application_fee_cents` charged per application unless waived, which may be zero but never negative; the `panel_quorum`, the minimum number of completed reviews an application needs before it can be scored, which must be at least 2; the `cohort_target_size`, the total number of residency slots the cycle awards, which must be at least 1; the `alumni_reserve_pct`, a fraction between 0 and 1 inclusive carrying at most two decimal places, naming the share of the cohort target held back for returning alumni; the `stipend_pool_cents` the funder awarded; the `slate_min_score`, a number between 0.00 and 100.00 inclusive with two decimal places that sets the floor for entering the slate candidate pool; the `stipend_weekly_rate_cents` that residencies of this cycle are paid by default; and `session_length_options_weeks`, a non-empty array of whole-week integers, each of which must be at least 4 and at most 16 weeks. Any of these ranges being violated is `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400). On creation the cycle's ledger fields are initialised so that `stipend_committed_cents` is 0 and `stipend_uncommitted_cents` equals `stipend_pool_cents`.

While the cycle is still `DRAFT` its configuration remains editable — the same eleven values minus the `code`, each optionally supplied — and every such edit recomputes `stipend_uncommitted_cents` and `slots_open_count` and advances the cycle's `version` by 1. Cycles can be listed with an optional filter on `status` or on `code`.

**Discipline categories** are the second half of a cycle's configuration, and they may only be created while the cycle is `DRAFT`; attempting to add one to a cycle that has moved on is `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409). Each carries a payload containing the `cycle_id` it configures, a `code` of 2 to 16 uppercase letters, digits or underscores that must be unique inside that cycle (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`, HTTP 409), a display `name`, and the rules that will later govern portfolios, cohort balance and studio matching. Portfolio bounds are `portfolio_min_items`, which must be at least 1, and `portfolio_max_items`, which must be at least `portfolio_min_items`; `required_media_types` names every media type that must appear at least once among an application's work samples and is a possibly-empty subset of `IMAGE`, `VIDEO`, `AUDIO`, `TEXT` and `MODEL_3D`. Cohort balance is `cohort_min_slots`, the number of slots the allocator must fill from this discipline before anything else, which may be 0, and `cohort_max_slots`, the hard ceiling on slots this discipline may occupy, which must be at least `cohort_min_slots`. Facility matching is `required_capability_tags`, a possibly-empty subset of `WELDING`, `KILN`, `DARKROOM`, `WET_LAB`, `PRINT_PRESS` and `CLEAN_ONLY`, together with `required_ventilation_class`, one of `NONE`, `LOCAL` or `FUME_HOOD` and defaulting to `NONE`. Finally, `requires_hazmat_review` defaults to false; when true, residents in this discipline who declared hazardous materials will additionally owe hazardous-material handling training. Disciplines are immutable once created and can be listed filtered by `cycle_id` or by `code`.

A cycle advances through six stages — `DRAFT`, `OPEN`, `SCREENING`, `PANEL_REVIEW`, `SLATE_LOCKED` and `CLOSED` — and `CLOSED` is terminal. A `ProgramAdmin` drives four of the five edges by naming a `to_status`; the fifth, into `SLATE_LOCKED`, belongs to the chair and is described in §3.5. Any requested stage that is not an edge of this pipeline from the cycle's current stage is `ERR_CYCLE_STAGE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), and so is any stage-guard precondition failure that does not carry a more specific code of its own.

- `DRAFT → OPEN` requires that at least one discipline exists for the cycle and that the sum of `cohort_min_slots` across all of the cycle's disciplines is no greater than `cohort_target_size`. A cycle with no disciplines, or whose discipline minimums together exceed the cohort target, is refused with `ERR_COHORT_MINIMUMS_EXCEED_TARGET` (HTTP 422). The edge advances the cycle's `version` by 1. Applications may only be created and first submitted while the cycle is `OPEN`.
- `OPEN → SCREENING` requires that no application of the cycle is still in status `DRAFT` — everyone has either submitted or been left out. The cycle's `version` advances by 1.
- `SCREENING → PANEL_REVIEW` requires that no application of the cycle is still in status `SUBMITTED`; every submission must have been advanced, sent back for revision, or rejected. The cycle's `version` advances by 1.
- `SLATE_LOCKED → CLOSED` requires that no application of the cycle is still in status `OFFERED`, meaning every offer has been accepted or declined. Closing the cycle rejects the entire remaining waitlist: every application still in `WAITLISTED` moves `WAITLISTED → REJECTED`, has its `waitlist_rank` cleared to null, and has its own `version` advanced by 1. The cycle then recomputes `slots_open_count` and advances its `version` by 1.

Six counters on the cycle are maintained by the server and are never writable. `stipend_committed_cents` is the sum of `stipend_total_cents` over every residency of the cycle whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_RESIDENCE`, `COMPLETED` or `TERMINATED`; `stipend_uncommitted_cents` is `stipend_pool_cents` less that sum. `slots_filled_count` is the number of residencies of the cycle in those same four statuses, and `slots_open_count` is the cohort target less the filled count, floored at zero so it never reports a negative. Residencies in `WITHDRAWN` leave both ledgers entirely — money and slot alike — whereas residencies in `TERMINATED` remain in both, but with their award already reduced to the settled figure. `slate_ready_count` is the number of applications of the cycle whose `status` is exactly `SCORED`. `alumni_reserved_slots` is set when the slate is allocated. And `panel_review_complete` is true precisely when `slate_ready_count` is at least 1 **and** no application of the cycle remains in status `PANEL_REVIEW`. All of these figures are recomputed from their inputs rather than patched incrementally, so a cascade that changes several rows at once leaves them consistent.

### 3.2. Registering Artists, Opening Applications and Settling the Fee

An artist joins the system as an **applicant**, either self-registering or entered by program staff, with a `full_name`, an `email` that must be unique across all applicants (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`, HTTP 409), an `affiliation_org` naming their current institutional home, a `citizenship_country` given as exactly two uppercase letters in ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 form, an optional `treaty_country` flag defaulting to false that records whether their citizenship country has a reduced-rate tax treaty with the host country, and a `declared_annual_income_cents` figure of zero or more that the fee-waiver test will later consult. The host country is the United States, written `US`; from `citizenship_country` the server derives `is_international`, which is true exactly when the applicant's citizenship country is anything other than `US`. A second derived field, `completed_residency_count`, counts the residencies of this applicant whose status is `COMPLETED`; it is the alumni signal the slate allocator reads, and it is why the same artist returning for a second residency is treated differently from a newcomer. Profile edits may change `full_name`, `affiliation_org`, `citizenship_country`, `treaty_country` or `declared_annual_income_cents`; such an edit re-derives `is_international` and advances the applicant's `version` by 1, and because a residency's `visa_required` and `tax_form_type` are themselves derived from the applicant, those fields shift on subsequent reads too. A profile edit is not ownership-scoped: the ownership rule of §2.1 reaches applications and residencies rather than applicant rows, so an acting `Applicant` — who must still resolve to an applicant row of their own, as §2.1 requires of every entity-backed role — is not confined to their own row here and may edit another artist's profile without being refused `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Applicants can be listed filtered by `email` or `citizenship_country`.

Opening an **application** requires the cycle to be `OPEN`; anything else is `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409). The payload names the `cycle_id` being competed in, the `applicant_id` of the applying artist, the `discipline_id` of the category applied to, a `requested_session_length_weeks` figure, a `project_statement` narrative of 1 to 4000 characters, and an optional `fee_waiver_requested` flag defaulting to false. Two cross-checks apply. The discipline named must belong to the cycle named: a discipline configured under a different cycle is `ERR_DISCIPLINE_CYCLE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). And the requested session length must be one of the values in the cycle's `session_length_options_weeks`; anything else is `ERR_SESSION_LENGTH_NOT_OFFERED` (HTTP 422). At most one application may exist per cycle-and-applicant pair, and a second attempt is `ERR_DUPLICATE_ASSOCIATION` (HTTP 409). A new application begins with `status` set to `DRAFT` and `fee_status` set to `PENDING`. While it is in `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` the artist may revise its `discipline_id`, `requested_session_length_weeks`, `project_statement` or `fee_waiver_requested`, each advancing the application's `version` by 1; an edit attempted from any other status, and in particular from one of the three terminal statuses `REJECTED`, `ACCEPTED` and `DECLINED`, is `ERR_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Applications can be listed filtered by `cycle_id`, `applicant_id`, `discipline_id`, `status` or `fee_status`.

The application fee is settled by a `ProgramAdmin` in a single decision that carries an `action` of `PAY`, `WAIVE` or `DENY_WAIVER` together with an optional `reason`. A fee may only be settled once: if `fee_status` is already `PAID` or `WAIVED`, the request is `ERR_FEE_ALREADY_SETTLED` (HTTP 409). `PAY` sets `fee_status` to `PAID`. `WAIVE` sets it to `WAIVED`, but only when the artist actually asked — `fee_waiver_requested` must be true — and only when their `declared_annual_income_cents` is at or below the waiver income ceiling of 3,500,000 cents, which is thirty-five thousand dollars a year; a request that fails either condition is `ERR_FEE_WAIVER_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422), and note that an income sitting exactly on the ceiling qualifies. `DENY_WAIVER` records the decision without settling anything, leaving `fee_status` at `PENDING`. In all three cases the supplied reason is stored on the application as `fee_waiver_reason`, which is null until the first fee decision is recorded, and the application's `version` advances by 1.

**Portfolio items** are the work samples that make an application reviewable, and they may only be attached while the application is in `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`. Each carries the `application_id` it belongs to, a `media_type` drawn from `IMAGE`, `VIDEO`, `AUDIO`, `TEXT` and `MODEL_3D`, a `title` of 1 to 200 characters, a `file_size_mb` figure that must be strictly greater than 0 and no greater than 200, and an optional `hazardous_materials_declared` flag defaulting to false that records whether producing the work involves hazardous materials. Attaching an item that would push the application past its discipline's `portfolio_max_items` is refused with `ERR_PORTFOLIO_ITEM_COUNT` (HTTP 422). Work samples are immutable — there is no edit — but they can be removed from an application that has not yet been submitted, and that removal returns no body. Both attaching and removing an item recompute three derived fields on the parent application and advance the application's `version` by 1: `portfolio_item_count`, the number of work samples attached; `portfolio_media_types`, the distinct media types present sorted in ascending order; and `hazardous_materials_declared` on the application, which is true when at least one of its work samples declares hazardous materials. Work samples can be listed filtered by `application_id` or `media_type`.

### 3.3. Submission, Screening and the Bounded Revision Loop

Submitting an application is the artist's own act (program staff may perform it on their behalf) and it takes no request body at all — every input it needs is already on the application. A **first** submission drives `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and is the most heavily guarded transition in the intake phase. The cycle must be `OPEN`, or the answer is `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409). The logical clock must fall inside the inclusive window running from `open_at` to `close_at`; an instant before the window opens or after it closes yields `ERR_SUBMISSION_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422), and an instant landing exactly on either bound is inside the window. The application's `fee_status` must already be `PAID` or `WAIVED`; a still-`PENDING` fee is `ERR_FEE_UNSETTLED` (HTTP 422). The `portfolio_item_count` must lie at or above the discipline's `portfolio_min_items` and at or below its `portfolio_max_items`, with both bounds inclusive; outside that band the answer is `ERR_PORTFOLIO_ITEM_COUNT` (HTTP 422). And every media type listed in the discipline's `required_media_types` must appear among the application's `portfolio_media_types`; a missing one is `ERR_PORTFOLIO_MEDIA_MISSING` (HTTP 422). A successful submission recomputes `portfolio_item_count`, `portfolio_media_types` and `hazardous_materials_declared` from the attached work samples and advances the application's `version` by 1.

Once the window has closed, a `ProgramAdmin` moves the cycle into `SCREENING` and works through the submissions. Each screening decision names a `decision` and may carry a `note`. Three of the four decisions belong to this phase, and all three require the cycle to be in `SCREENING` — a decision attempted in the wrong stage is `ERR_CYCLE_STAGE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), while a decision that is not an edge from the application's current status, including any decision at all on an application in `REJECTED`, `ACCEPTED` or `DECLINED`, is `ERR_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

- `ADVANCE` drives `SUBMITTED → PANEL_REVIEW`, putting the application in front of the selection panel, and advances the application's `version` by 1.
- `REQUEST_REVISION` drives `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED`, handing the application back to the artist for rework. It increments `revision_round` by 1 and advances `version` by 1. The loop is bounded at two rounds: a revision request against an application whose `revision_round` has already reached 2 is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409).
- `REJECT` drives `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` and has a cascade of its own. Every panel assignment on that application still in status `ASSIGNED` is moved `ASSIGNED → VACATED`, each such assignment's `version` advancing by 1, and the application's `assigned_panelist_count` is recomputed accordingly. The application's `version` advances by 1.

Resubmission closes the loop: an application in `REVISION_REQUESTED` returns along `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` through the same submission act. This second pass differs from the first in exactly one respect that implementers routinely get wrong. The cycle must be in `SCREENING` rather than `OPEN`, and the submission window is deliberately **not** re-checked — screening happens after `close_at` by construction, so re-testing the window would make every resubmission impossible. The portfolio count band and the required-media coverage check both still apply, with the same two error codes, and the three portfolio-derived fields are recomputed again. Because the revision loop is bounded at two rounds, the longest possible path through intake is `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` before the application must be advanced or rejected.

### 3.4. Panel Review: Assignment, the Rubric, Quorum and Conflict Recusal

Reviewers exist independently of cycles. A `ProgramAdmin` registers a **panelist** with a `full_name`, a unique `email` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`, HTTP 409), an `affiliation_org` that will be compared against applicants' affiliations when conflicts are assessed, a `discipline_expertise` array of discipline `code` values the reviewer is credentialed in, which may be empty, and an optional `chair_eligible` flag defaulting to false. A panelist's `status` starts at `ACTIVE` and may become `RECUSED_FOR_CYCLE`, at which point `recused_cycle_id` records which cycle froze them; it is null otherwise. Two counters are maintained on the reviewer: `assigned_count`, the number of their panel assignments in status `ASSIGNED` or `SCORED`, and `conflict_count`, the number in status `CONFLICT_DECLARED`. Reviewer records can be edited to change `full_name`, `affiliation_org`, `discipline_expertise` or `chair_eligible`, advancing the panelist's `version` by 1; such an edit affects how expertise is judged on *subsequent* scoring only — reviewer totals already recorded are never restated. Reviewers can be listed filtered by `recused_cycle_id`, `status` or `chair_eligible`.

Pairing a reviewer with an application creates a **panel assignment**, the many-to-many junction between the two, and the payload carries only `application_id` and `panelist_id`. The cycle must be in `PANEL_REVIEW` (`ERR_CYCLE_STAGE_INVALID`, HTTP 409); the application must be in `PANEL_REVIEW` or `SCORED`, since a further reviewer may legitimately be added to an application that has already reached quorum, and anything else is `ERR_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409); the reviewer's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, because a panelist frozen as `RECUSED_FOR_CYCLE` takes no further work in that cycle and the refusal is `ERR_PANELIST_RECUSED` (HTTP 409); and at most one assignment may exist per application-and-panelist pair, a second attempt being `ERR_DUPLICATE_ASSOCIATION` (HTTP 409). A new assignment begins in status `ASSIGNED`. On creation the server computes `expertise_matched`, which is true exactly when the `discipline_code` of the application's discipline appears in the reviewer's `discipline_expertise`, then recomputes the application's `assigned_panelist_count` (its assignments in status `ASSIGNED` or `SCORED`) and the reviewer's `assigned_count`, and advances the application's `version` by 1. Assignments can be listed filtered by `application_id`, `panelist_id`, `status` or `conflict_type`.

Scoring is strictly self-service: a `Panelist` records their own four rubric criteria — `score_artistic_merit`, `score_feasibility`, `score_community_fit` and `score_technical_readiness` — with an optional `notes` field, and the transition is `ASSIGNED → SCORED`. Each of the four must be an integer no less than 0 and no greater than 10; a non-integer or an out-of-range figure is `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400). The cycle must be in `PANEL_REVIEW`, the application must be in `PANEL_REVIEW` or `SCORED`, the reviewer's `status` must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_PANELIST_RECUSED`), and the assignment must still be in `ASSIGNED`: an assignment already in `SCORED`, or one that has reached `CONFLICT_DECLARED` or the terminal `VACATED`, admits no scoring and yields `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). One further check has no analogue anywhere else in the system: a reviewer whose `affiliation_org` equals the applicant's `affiliation_org` may not record scores at all and must resolve the shared affiliation through a conflict declaration first, which is `ERR_AFFILIATION_CONFLICT` (HTTP 422). Recording scores stores the four figures, the notes, and a `scored_at` instant taken from the logical clock.

The rubric arithmetic runs in two legs, and the reason it is worth stating precisely is that rounding happens in **both** of them. The reviewer's own leg produces `panelist_total_100`, a number on a 0.00 to 100.00 scale. Take the artistic-merit score multiplied by its weight of 35, plus the feasibility score multiplied by its weight of 27, plus the community-fit score multiplied by its weight of 23, plus the technical-readiness score multiplied by its weight of 15; divide that sum by 10. Because the four weights total 100 and each criterion runs from 0 to 10, the quotient already sits on a 0 to 100 scale. Multiply it by the expertise multiplier — exactly 1.00 when `expertise_matched` is true, and 0.90 when it is false, so a reviewer working outside their credentialed disciplines carries ninety percent of the weight of one working inside them — and round the product half up to two decimal places. That rounded figure is the reviewer's total, and it is null unless the assignment is in `SCORED`.

The aggregation leg turns reviewer totals into the two application-level scores, and **only** assignments in status `SCORED` contribute. Assignments still in `ASSIGNED` have not been reviewed; assignments in `CONFLICT_DECLARED` and `VACATED` have had their scores cleared to null. Let the contributing set be every `panelist_total_100` on the application's `SCORED` assignments, and let its size be `scored_panelist_count`. When that count is zero, both `provisional_screening_score` and `final_panel_score` are null.

- `provisional_screening_score` is the **untrimmed** arithmetic mean: sum the contributing reviewer totals, divide by their count, round half up to two decimal places. It is maintained from the very first recorded review onward, has no quorum gate and no trim, and is the only score a `ProgramAdmin` can see mid-review.
- `final_panel_score` is the authoritative slate number and is **quorum-gated and trimmed**. When the contributing count is below the cycle's `panel_quorum`, it is null. When the count reaches at least four reviewers, remove exactly one occurrence of the highest reviewer total and exactly one occurrence of the lowest reviewer total from the contributing set, then average what remains and round half up to two decimal places. When the count meets quorum but is fewer than four, no trim is applied and the mean is taken over all contributors, rounded the same way. On identical data the two variants therefore disagree, and the trimmed figure is the one that decides the slate.

Reaching quorum is a state change, not just a recalculation. On the review that raises `scored_panelist_count` to the cycle's `panel_quorum`, the application moves `PANEL_REVIEW → SCORED`: `final_panel_score` is computed, `rank_in_discipline` is derived, the cycle's `slate_ready_count` increases by 1 and its `panel_review_complete` flag is re-evaluated, the assignment's and the application's `version` each advance by 1, and on that quorum edge the cycle's `version` advances by 1 as well. `rank_in_discipline` is the 1-based position of the application inside its own discipline, ordering applications of the same `discipline_id` whose `status` is `SCORED` by `final_panel_score` descending and then, for ties, by `created_at` ascending; it is null whenever `final_panel_score` is null. Every review recorded, quorum edge or not, recomputes `scored_panelist_count` and `provisional_screening_score` and advances both the assignment's and the application's `version` by 1.

**Conflict declaration** is the reviewer's escape hatch, and it is the head of the system's second cascade. A `Panelist` declares a conflict on their own assignment with a `conflict_type` drawn from `SAME_AFFILIATION`, `PRIOR_MENTORSHIP`, `FAMILIAL` and `FINANCIAL`, plus a free-text `conflict_detail`. The cycle must be in `PANEL_REVIEW` and the assignment must be in `ASSIGNED` or `SCORED`; from `CONFLICT_DECLARED` or `VACATED` the answer is `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). Both `ASSIGNED → CONFLICT_DECLARED` and `SCORED → CONFLICT_DECLARED` run the same first three links.

1. The declaration is recorded: `conflict_type` and `conflict_detail` are stored, and the four rubric score fields, `scored_at` and `panelist_total_100` are all cleared back to null. The application's `assigned_panelist_count` is recomputed, the reviewer's `conflict_count` rises, and the assignment's and application's `version` each advance by 1.
2. Only when the declared `conflict_type` is `FAMILIAL` or `FINANCIAL` — the two the organization treats as severe — the reviewer auto-recuses: the panelist's `status` becomes `RECUSED_FOR_CYCLE` and `recused_cycle_id` is set to the `cycle_id` of the application under review. The panelist's `version` advances by 1. A `SAME_AFFILIATION` or `PRIOR_MENTORSHIP` declaration stops at link 1 and touches nothing else.
3. Caused by the recusal, every **other** panel assignment held by that reviewer whose application belongs to the same cycle and whose status is `ASSIGNED` or `SCORED` is moved to `VACATED`. Where the vacated assignment had been `SCORED`, its four rubric scores, `scored_at` and `panelist_total_100` are cleared to null as compensation. Each vacated assignment's `version` advances by 1.
4. For every application touched by link 3 — and for the declaring assignment's own application when it left `SCORED` — `scored_panelist_count` and `provisional_screening_score` are recomputed over the *remaining* `SCORED` assignments. An application in status `SCORED` whose contributing count has now fallen below the cycle's `panel_quorum` is demoted along the reverse edge `SCORED → PANEL_REVIEW`, with `final_panel_score` and `rank_in_discipline` both set to null. Each affected application's `version` advances by 1.
5. Each such demotion decrements the cycle's `slate_ready_count` by 1 and re-evaluates `panel_review_complete`, advancing the cycle's `version` by 1.

This is the intended serialization point of the review phase: a severe conflict declared late can pull a whole cycle back out of readiness, and because the slate lock demands `panel_review_complete`, the chair simply cannot lock a slate whose scores have just been invalidated.

### 3.5. Locking the Slate, Offers and the Waitlist

Locking a cycle's slate is a single chair action that decides the fate of every scored application at once, drives the cycle `PANEL_REVIEW → SLATE_LOCKED`, and takes no request body. It belongs exclusively to `PanelChair`, and it is guarded three ways before any allocation runs. The cycle must be in `PANEL_REVIEW`, or the answer is `ERR_CYCLE_STAGE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The acting chair's backing panelist row must carry `chair_eligible` set to true, or `ERR_CHAIR_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 403). The acting chair's `X-User-ID` must not be the `panelist_id` of **any** panel assignment in the cycle whose status is `SCORED` or `CONFLICT_DECLARED` — a chair who reviewed or declared a conflict in the cycle cannot also adjudicate its outcome — and a violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Finally the cycle's `panel_review_complete` flag must be true, meaning at least one application has reached `SCORED` and none remains in `PANEL_REVIEW`; otherwise the answer is `ERR_PANEL_REVIEW_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409).

Allocation itself is a three-bucket waterfall over a deterministic candidate ordering. The **candidate pool** is every application of the cycle whose `status` is `SCORED` and whose `final_panel_score` is at or above the cycle's `slate_min_score`; a score exactly equal to the floor is in the pool. Applications still in `PANEL_REVIEW` cannot appear, because the readiness precondition has already excluded that possibility. **Merit order** throughout means `final_panel_score` descending, and for ties `created_at` ascending — an earlier-submitted application beats a later one at equal merit. No entity `id` participates in any part of this decision, and neither does any random value.

Before the buckets run, two integers are fixed. `alumni_reserved_slots` is the cycle's `alumni_reserve_pct` multiplied by its `cohort_target_size`, rounded **up** to the next whole slot. The general allotment is `cohort_target_size` less `alumni_reserved_slots`; if that difference would be negative — equivalently, if the rounded-up reserve exceeds the cohort target — the whole lock is refused with `ERR_ALUMNI_RESERVE_EXCEEDS_COHORT` (HTTP 422) and nothing is written. The reserve is **pinned to the cohort target**, not recomputed against whatever slots survive the first bucket: with a cohort target of 10 and a reserve fraction of 0.25 the reserve is 3 slots, and it stays 3 slots even after discipline minimums have already consumed 2 of the 10 — a reading that recomputed the reserve against the 8 remaining slots would yield 2 and hand an extra slot to general merit, and that reading is wrong.

The selected set is built in order, and it never exceeds `cohort_target_size` entries.

1. **Discipline minimums.** Walk the cycle's disciplines in ascending `code` order — an alphabetical walk over the operator-assigned codes, not over identifiers. For each discipline, order its not-yet-selected candidates by merit and take up to `cohort_min_slots` of them, stopping early if the selected set has already reached the cohort target. If, after that, the discipline has fewer selections than its `cohort_min_slots` — including the degenerate case where it had no candidates at all — the entire lock is refused with `ERR_DISCIPLINE_MINIMUM_UNFILLABLE` (HTTP 422).
2. **Alumni reserve.** Count how many already-selected applications belong to artists whose `completed_residency_count` is at least 1; that is the reserve already satisfied by bucket 1. Then walk the remaining candidates who are alumni by the same test, in merit order, adding each one until either the alumni count reaches `alumni_reserved_slots` or the selected set reaches `cohort_target_size`. Skip — do not stop at — any candidate whose discipline has already reached its `cohort_max_slots` in the selected set.
3. **General merit.** Walk every remaining candidate in merit order, adding each until the selected set reaches `cohort_target_size`, again skipping any candidate whose discipline has already reached its `cohort_max_slots`. This bucket fills whatever the first two left.

The outcomes follow mechanically, and each one advances the affected application's `version` by 1. Every selected application moves `SCORED → OFFERED`. Every candidate that was in the pool but not selected moves `SCORED → WAITLISTED` and receives a `waitlist_rank`: the 1-based cycle-wide position among all `WAITLISTED` applications of the cycle, ordered by `final_panel_score` descending and then `created_at` ascending. Every application in `SCORED` that never entered the pool — that is, whose `final_panel_score` falls below `slate_min_score` — moves `SCORED → REJECTED`. The cycle then stores `alumni_reserved_slots`, recomputes `slate_ready_count`, and advances its own `version` by 1.

An offer is answered by the artist (or by staff on their behalf) with a `response` of `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE`, and the cycle must be in `SLATE_LOCKED` for either. The application must be in `OFFERED`; anything else is `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_OFFERED` (HTTP 409), and — as §2.9 sets out — that state answer is given even when the money would also not have fitted. `DECLINE` drives `OFFERED → DECLINED`, recomputes the cycle's `slots_open_count`, and advances the application's `version` by 1.

`ACCEPT` drives `OFFERED → ACCEPTED` and is the head of the acceptance cascade. Two resource checks precede it: the cycle's `slots_open_count` must be at least 1, or `ERR_NO_SLOTS_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 409); and the cycle's `stipend_uncommitted_cents` must be at least the amount this acceptance would commit, which is the application's `requested_session_length_weeks` multiplied by the cycle's `stipend_weekly_rate_cents`, or `ERR_STIPEND_POOL_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409) — the last check in the entire ladder. When both pass, three linked effects fire inside the same request.

1. A residency is created for the artist in `status` `SCHEDULED` and `compliance_status` `TRAINING_PENDING`, carrying the `cycle_id`, the `application_id` of the accepted application, the `applicant_id` of the artist, the `discipline_code` copied from the accepted application's discipline, a `stipend_weekly_rate_cents` copied from the cycle's default weekly rate, a `stipend_basis` of `PROVISIONAL`, and no session dates yet — `session_start_date`, `session_end_date` and `session_length_weeks` are all null until the residency is scheduled.
2. Caused by link 1, the provisional award is committed against the cycle: `stipend_committed_cents` increases by the requested session length in weeks multiplied by the cycle's weekly rate, and `stipend_uncommitted_cents`, `slots_filled_count` and `slots_open_count` are all recomputed.
3. Also caused by link 1, when the new residency's `visa_required` is true — that is, when the artist's `is_international` is true — a visa case is created for it in status `INITIATED` with a `case_type` of `B1_SHORT_TERM`.

The application's `version` and the cycle's `version` each advance by 1.

The fourth screening decision belongs to this phase rather than to screening proper. `PROMOTE_FROM_WAITLIST` drives `WAITLISTED → OFFERED` and requires the cycle to be in `SLATE_LOCKED` (`ERR_CYCLE_STAGE_INVALID`, HTTP 409) and the application to be in `WAITLISTED` (`ERR_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID`, HTTP 409). It also requires the cycle's `slots_open_count` to be at least 1, or `ERR_NO_SLOTS_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 409) — there is no point offering a place that does not exist. Promotion sets the promoted application's `waitlist_rank` to null, re-ranks every application still in `WAITLISTED` so the sequence stays contiguous, and advances the promoted application's `version` by 1. Because promotion creates a fresh `OFFERED` application, a cycle cannot be closed while any promotion is still outstanding; closing the cycle, as §3.1 describes, rejects whatever remains on the waitlist.

### 3.6. Residencies: Formation, Scheduling, Obligations and the Lifecycle Axis

Most residencies arrive through the acceptance cascade of §3.5, but not all of them do. A `ProgramAdmin` may also create a residency directly — an invited or commissioned residency, or a **follow-on** residency extending one an artist has already completed — by supplying the `cycle_id`, the `applicant_id`, the `discipline_id` whose code the residency will carry, and optionally a `predecessor_residency_id` and an `application_id`. The cycle must be neither `DRAFT` nor `CLOSED`. When `predecessor_residency_id` is supplied it must name a residency whose `status` is `COMPLETED` and whose `applicant_id` equals the new residency's `applicant_id` — the follow-on chain is self-referential and strictly per artist — and when `discipline_id` is supplied its `cycle_id` must equal the new residency's `cycle_id`; either violation is `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_COMPLETED` (HTTP 422). A directly created residency starts in `status` `SCHEDULED` and `compliance_status` `TRAINING_PENDING`, with `discipline_code` copied from the named discipline, `stipend_basis` `PROVISIONAL` and `stipend_total_cents` 0. The server derives `visa_required` from the artist's `is_international` and `tax_form_type` from the same flag — `W9` for a domestic artist, `1042S` for an international one — and, when the artist is international, opens a visa case in `INITIATED`. The cycle's `slots_filled_count` and `slots_open_count` are recomputed and the cycle's `version` advances by 1. Residencies can be listed filtered by `cycle_id`, `application_id`, `applicant_id`, `predecessor_residency_id`, `status` or `compliance_status`.

**Scheduling** is the moment a residency acquires a calendar, and it provisions four different things at once. A `ProgramAdmin` supplies a `session_start_date` and a `session_length_weeks`, and the residency must be in `status` `SCHEDULED` — a residency that has already checked in, completed, terminated or withdrawn cannot be rescheduled, and the answer is `ERR_RESIDENCY_NOT_SCHEDULED` (HTTP 409). The length must be one of the cycle's `session_length_options_weeks` values, or `ERR_SESSION_LENGTH_NOT_OFFERED` (HTTP 422). The start date must not be earlier than the current date on the logical clock, a start date in the past being `ERR_SESSION_START_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422); a start date equal to today is acceptable. A residency that already has a stipend disbursement in status `PAID` may not be rescheduled at all, because money has already moved against the old calendar: that is `ERR_RESIDENCY_ALREADY_PAID` (HTTP 409). And the cycle must still have enough uncommitted stipend to cover the confirmed length at the residency's effective weekly rate, or `ERR_STIPEND_POOL_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409).

A successful scheduling call does the following, in one request. It derives `session_end_date` as the start date plus the session length in weeks multiplied by seven days, minus one day, so that the session is a closed inclusive interval — an eight-week session beginning on a Monday ends on a Sunday fifty-five days later. It derives `required_training_types` as a sorted ascending list built from three sources: the single entry `SAFETY_ORIENTATION`, always present; one entry per capability tag on the residency's discipline, written as `EQUIPMENT_` followed by the tag, so a discipline requiring welding and a kiln yields `EQUIPMENT_WELDING` and `EQUIPMENT_KILN`; and the entry `HAZMAT_HANDLING`, present only when the discipline's `requires_hazmat_review` is true **and** the source application's `hazardous_materials_declared` is also true. It provisions the engagement obligations described below. It re-bases `stipend_total_cents` to the provisional award for the confirmed length and recomputes the cycle's stipend ledger. And it re-derives the visa case's `case_type`, `filing_deadline_date` and `earliest_entry_date` from the new calendar. The residency's `version` advances by 1, the cycle's `version` advances by 1, and the visa case's `version` advances by 1 when one exists.

**Engagement obligations** are the public-facing commitments the residency owes, and they are provisioned from the confirmed session length. Three of them are flat: one `OPEN_STUDIO`, one `EXHIBITION` and one `ARTIST_TALK`, each with a `required_count` of 1. The fourth scales with the session: the number of `COMMUNITY_WORKSHOP` events owed is the session length in weeks divided by four, rounded **down** to a whole number, and the row is only created when that figure is at least 1. An eight-week residency therefore owes two workshops and carries four obligation rows totalling five required events; a five-week and a four-week residency each owe one workshop and carry four rows totalling four events; a residency shorter than four weeks would owe no workshop at all and carry only three rows. Every provisioned row starts with `status` `PENDING`, `completed_count` 0, `total_attendance` 0 and a `due_date` equal to the residency's `session_end_date` at the moment of provisioning. Rescheduling does not duplicate rows: an existing `PENDING` row of the same `obligation_type` has its `required_count` and `due_date` re-derived in place, while rows already in `SATISFIED`, `WAIVED` or `BREACHED` are never re-derived. Obligations can be listed filtered by `residency_id`, `obligation_type` or `status`.

Progress against an obligation is recorded by a `ProgramAdmin` with an `action` of `LOG_COMPLETION` or `WAIVE`, plus an optional `attendance` figure or an optional `waived_reason`. Either way the obligation must be in `PENDING`; a row already `SATISFIED`, `WAIVED` or `BREACHED` is `ERR_OBLIGATION_NOT_PENDING` (HTTP 409). `LOG_COMPLETION` increases `completed_count` by 1 and adds the supplied attendance to `total_attendance`; when `completed_count` reaches or passes `required_count`, the row moves `PENDING → SATISFIED`. `WAIVE` requires the residency's `status` to be `SCHEDULED`, `IN_RESIDENCE` or `WITHDRAWN`, records the `waived_reason`, and moves the row `PENDING → WAIVED`. Both actions recompute the residency's three obligation counters and advance both the obligation's and the residency's `version` by 1. Those counters are worth stating exactly, because waived rows leave the sums entirely: `obligations_required_total` is the sum of `required_count` over the residency's obligations whose status is anything other than `WAIVED`; `obligations_completed_total` is the sum of `completed_count` over the same set; and `obligations_breached_count` counts only the rows in `BREACHED`.

The residency's **lifecycle axis** carries five statuses — `SCHEDULED`, `IN_RESIDENCE`, `COMPLETED`, `TERMINATED` and `WITHDRAWN` — of which the last three are terminal and admit no further action of any kind. A lifecycle call names an `action` and may carry a `reason`. An action that is not an edge from the residency's current status, including any action at all on a terminal residency, is `ERR_RESIDENCY_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

`CHECK_IN` drives `SCHEDULED → IN_RESIDENCE` and is where the safety and sponsorship axes finally bind to the calendar. The residency must have a `session_start_date`, the current date on the logical clock must be at or after that date, and both failures are reported as `ERR_RESIDENCY_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The residency's `compliance_status` must be exactly `CLEARED`; a resident still in `TRAINING_PENDING`, or one whose clearance has been withdrawn to `SUSPENDED`, is refused with `ERR_TRAINING_NOT_COMPLETE` (HTTP 422). And when `visa_required` is true, the residency's visa case must be in status `APPROVED`, or the answer is `ERR_VISA_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 422). Check-in advances the residency's `version` by 1 and changes nothing else.

`COMPLETE` drives `IN_RESIDENCE → COMPLETED` and requires the current date on the logical clock to be at or after `session_end_date`. Completion settles five things at once. `actual_weeks_in_residence` is set to the residency's `session_length_weeks` — the full planned session, because the resident stayed to the end. Every obligation of the residency still in `PENDING` whose `completed_count` is below its `required_count` moves `PENDING → BREACHED`, each advancing its own `version` by 1, and `obligations_breached_count` is recomputed. Every studio assignment of the residency in status `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → RELEASED` with `released_reason` recorded as `RESIDENCY_COMPLETED`, and each affected studio's `reserved_resident_days` is recomputed. Every housing assignment in `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → RELEASED` and each affected unit's `occupied_bed_nights` is recomputed. And the artist's `completed_residency_count` is recomputed, which is what makes them an alumnus for the purposes of any future cycle's alumni reserve. The residency's `version` advances by 1, as does the `version` of every assignment, studio, housing unit and obligation the completion touched.

`TERMINATE` drives `IN_RESIDENCE → TERMINATED` and requires the current date on the logical clock to be at or before `session_end_date` — a residency that has run its full course is completed, not terminated. It is the head of the termination cascade, whose four links run in order.

1. `actual_weeks_in_residence` is set to the number of days from `session_start_date` up to and including the settlement date, divided by seven and rounded **up** to a whole number of weeks. Nineteen days served therefore count as three weeks.
2. Caused by link 1, every stipend disbursement of the residency still in status `SCHEDULED` moves `SCHEDULED → VOIDED`, each with its `withholding_cents` and `net_cents` set to 0 and its `version` advanced by 1.
3. Caused by link 2, because the surviving schedule no longer covers the planned session, `stipend_basis` becomes `FINAL_SETTLED` and `stipend_total_cents` is re-priced on the settled basis described in §3.9.
4. Caused by link 3, the cycle's `stipend_committed_cents` is reduced by the difference between the old award and the new one, and `stipend_uncommitted_cents` is recomputed, releasing the unspent balance back to the funding pool.

Alongside those four links, every studio assignment of the residency in `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → RELEASED` with `released_reason` recorded as `RESIDENCY_TERMINATED`, the affected studio's `reserved_resident_days` is recomputed and `assigned_studio_code` on the residency is cleared to null; every housing assignment in `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → RELEASED`, the affected unit's `occupied_bed_nights` is recomputed and `assigned_housing_unit_code` is cleared to null. The residency's `version` and the cycle's `version` each advance by 1, as does the `version` of every disbursement, assignment, studio and housing unit touched.

`WITHDRAW` drives `SCHEDULED → WITHDRAWN` and is the artist's own action: the acting `Applicant`'s `X-User-ID` must equal the residency's `applicant_id`, and the residency must still be in `SCHEDULED` (`ERR_RESIDENCY_NOT_SCHEDULED`, HTTP 409). Withdrawal is designed as the exact inverse of acceptance. The cycle's `stipend_committed_cents` is reduced by the residency's full `stipend_total_cents`, restoring the committed figure to precisely its pre-acceptance value; every stipend disbursement in `SCHEDULED` moves `SCHEDULED → VOIDED`; every studio assignment and housing assignment in `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → RELEASED`, with the studio's `reserved_resident_days` and the unit's `occupied_bed_nights` recomputed; every obligation in `PENDING` moves `PENDING → WAIVED` with `waived_reason` recorded as `RESIDENCY_WITHDRAWN`; and the residency's visa case, when one exists and its status is neither `APPROVED` nor `DENIED`, is driven to `DENIED` with the reason recorded as sponsorship withdrawn. The cycle's `slots_filled_count` and `slots_open_count` are recomputed — a withdrawn residency leaves the slot ledger as well as the money ledger — and the residency's and cycle's `version` each advance by 1, along with the `version` of every row the withdrawal touched. `actual_weeks_in_residence` remains null on a withdrawn residency, as it does on one still merely `SCHEDULED`.

### 3.7. Facilities: Safety Clearance, Studio Matching, Housing and Meals

A **studio** is registered by a `FacilitiesManager` or a `ProgramAdmin` with a `code` unique across all studios (uppercase letters, digits, underscore or hyphen, two to sixteen characters; a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`, HTTP 409), a non-empty `capability_tags` list drawn from `WELDING`, `KILN`, `DARKROOM`, `WET_LAB`, `PRINT_PRESS` and `CLEAN_ONLY`, a `ventilation_class` of `NONE`, `LOCAL` or `FUME_HOOD` defaulting to `NONE`, a `max_concurrent_residents` headcount of at least 1, and a `status` of `AVAILABLE`, `MAINTENANCE` or `DECOMMISSIONED` defaulting to `AVAILABLE`. The studio also carries two server-maintained figures: `reserved_resident_days`, the sum of `occupied_days` across the studio's studio assignments in status `ACTIVE`, and `active_assignment_count`, the number of such assignments. Both start at 0. A `FacilitiesManager` may later revise `capability_tags`, `ventilation_class`, `max_concurrent_residents` or `status`; each such change advances the studio's `version` by 1. Studios may be listed filtered by `status`, by `ventilation_class` or by a single `capability_tag`, the last matching any studio whose `capability_tags` contains that tag. Studios are readable by `FacilitiesManager`, `ProgramAdmin` and `Funder`.

A **housing unit** is registered by a `ProgramAdmin` with a `code` unique across all housing units under the same character rules, a `bed_count` of at least 1, a `meal_plan_included` flag defaulting to false, an `accessibility_features` list drawn from `STEP_FREE`, `ROLL_IN_SHOWER`, `VISUAL_ALARM` and `GROUND_FLOOR` which may be empty, and a `status` of `AVAILABLE` or `MAINTENANCE` defaulting to `AVAILABLE`. Its one server-maintained figure is `occupied_bed_nights`, the sum of `bed_nights` across the unit's housing assignments in status `ACTIVE`, starting at 0. A `ProgramAdmin` may revise `bed_count`, `meal_plan_included`, `accessibility_features` or `status`, each change advancing the unit's `version` by 1, and may list units filtered by `status` or `meal_plan_included`.

**Safety clearance.** Recording a training is how a residency earns its way off `TRAINING_PENDING`. A `FacilitiesManager` or `ProgramAdmin` submits one `training_type`, which must be a member of the residency's `required_training_types` — a training the residency does not owe is `ERR_TRAINING_TYPE_NOT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422) — and the residency must not be in one of the three terminal lifecycle statuses (`ERR_RESIDENCY_STATE_INVALID`, HTTP 409). The type is appended to `completed_training_types`, which is kept sorted ascending and de-duplicated, so recording the same training twice is accepted and idempotent in effect. When, after the append, every member of `required_training_types` appears in `completed_training_types`, the compliance axis moves `TRAINING_PENDING → CLEARED` in the same request. The residency's `version` advances by 1.

The compliance axis can also be driven by hand. A `FacilitiesManager` or `ProgramAdmin` submits an `action` of `SUSPEND` or `REINSTATE` with an optional `reason`; in either case the residency's lifecycle `status` must be `SCHEDULED` or `IN_RESIDENCE`, and a terminal residency is `ERR_RESIDENCY_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). `SUSPEND` is an edge from **either** non-suspended compliance state: both `CLEARED → SUSPENDED` and `TRAINING_PENDING → SUSPENDED` exist, so a resident can be suspended before they were ever cleared. Suspension from `CLEARED` releases the residency's studio: every studio assignment of the residency in `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → RELEASED` with `released_reason` recorded as `COMPLIANCE_SUSPENDED`, the studio's `reserved_resident_days` and `active_assignment_count` are recomputed, and `assigned_studio_code` on the residency is cleared to null. `REINSTATE` drives `SUSPENDED → CLEARED` and requires that every member of `required_training_types` still appears in `completed_training_types`, or the answer is `ERR_TRAINING_NOT_COMPLETE` (HTTP 422). Note the deliberate asymmetry that follows from those two rules together: a residency suspended out of `TRAINING_PENDING` before its trainings were complete cannot be reinstated until they are, and reinstatement always lands in `CLEARED`, never back in `TRAINING_PENDING`. Note also that reinstatement is **not** a restoration: the released studio assignment stays released, and a new assignment must be booked if the resident is to have a studio again. Both actions advance the residency's `version` by 1, and a suspension that released a studio advances the studio's and the assignment's `version` by 1 as well.

**Booking a studio.** A `FacilitiesManager` or `ProgramAdmin` supplies a `residency_id`, a `start_date`, an `end_date` no earlier than the start, an `expected_residency_version`, and optionally a `studio_id`. The interval is **closed**: `occupied_days` is the end date minus the start date plus one, so a single-day booking occupies one day. Setting aside the shape, authorisation and existence checks, the guards apply in this order.

- The `expected_residency_version` must equal the residency's current `version`, or `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- The residency must not be in a terminal lifecycle status, or `ERR_RESIDENCY_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409) — a studio may be booked for a residency that is `SCHEDULED` or already `IN_RESIDENCE`.
- The residency must have a `session_start_date`; an unscheduled residency has no session to book inside, and the answer is `ERR_RESIDENCY_NOT_SCHEDULED` (HTTP 409).
- When a `studio_id` was named explicitly, that studio's `status` must be `AVAILABLE`; a studio in `MAINTENANCE` or `DECOMMISSIONED` is `ERR_FACILITY_UNAVAILABLE` (HTTP 409).
- The residency's `compliance_status` must not be `SUSPENDED`, or `ERR_COMPLIANCE_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 422). As §2.9 records, this check is deliberately placed **ahead** of the interval-conflict check, so a suspended resident hears about the suspension even when the window they asked for was never bookable.
- The named studio's `capability_tags` must be a superset of the `required_capability_tags` of the discipline whose `code` matches the residency's `discipline_code` within the residency's cycle, or `ERR_STUDIO_CAPABILITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).
- The named studio's ventilation must be sufficient, or `ERR_VENTILATION_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). Ventilation is graded on the ordered scale where `NONE` ranks 0, `LOCAL` ranks 1 and `FUME_HOOD` ranks 2, and the studio's rank must be at least the discipline's `required_ventilation_class` rank. Independently of the discipline, whenever the residency's source application declared hazardous materials the studio's rank must also be at least the rank of `FUME_HOOD` — that is, the studio must be a fume-hood studio, whatever the discipline nominally requires.
- The window must lie inside the session: `start_date` at or after `session_start_date` and `end_date` at or before `session_end_date`, or `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_OUTSIDE_SESSION` (HTTP 422).
- Then one of two mutually exclusive occupancy rules applies, selected by the studio's own `max_concurrent_residents`. On a **single-occupancy** studio, where `max_concurrent_residents` is exactly 1, the requested window must not overlap any `ACTIVE` assignment of that studio under closed-interval semantics **extended by a two-day turnaround buffer** on each existing assignment: an existing assignment blocks from its own `start_date` through its `end_date` plus two days, and the requested window is likewise treated as extending two days past its `end_date`. A collision is `ERR_STUDIO_INTERVAL_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Concretely, a studio holding an active assignment ending on the 27th of a month is blocked through the 29th, so a request starting on the 29th conflicts while an otherwise identical request starting on the 30th does not. On a **shared** studio, where `max_concurrent_residents` is greater than 1, the interval-conflict rule does not apply at all and overlapping tenants are permitted; instead, for every single calendar date of the requested window, the number of `ACTIVE` assignments of that studio covering that date, counting the request itself, must not exceed `max_concurrent_residents`. Each individual overlap may be legal while the collection is not, and the excess is `ERR_STUDIO_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). Only assignments in `ACTIVE` occupy a studio; a `RELEASED` assignment frees its window immediately, buffer included.

When `studio_id` is omitted the server chooses the studio itself, and `selection_mode` on the created assignment is recorded as `AUTO_SELECTED` rather than `EXPLICIT`. Selection proceeds in two stages. First an eligibility filter retains every studio that satisfies all of the following: its `status` is `AVAILABLE`, so studios in `MAINTENANCE` or `DECOMMISSIONED` are never candidates; its `capability_tags` are a superset of the discipline's `required_capability_tags`; its ventilation rank is at least the discipline's required rank; its ventilation rank is at least the rank of `FUME_HOOD` whenever the residency declared hazardous materials; and it has room for the requested window under whichever of the two occupancy rules its own `max_concurrent_residents` selects — the buffered closed-interval rule for a single-occupancy studio, the aggregate headcount rule for a shared one. Then the survivors are ranked and the first is chosen. The ranking is ascending on every key in turn, which means the selector deliberately prefers the **least** over-provisioned studio that will do the job: first by ventilation rank ascending, so a `LOCAL` studio is preferred over a `FUME_HOOD` studio when `LOCAL` suffices; then by the number of entries in `capability_tags` ascending, so a narrowly equipped studio is preferred over a general-purpose one; then by `max_concurrent_residents` ascending, so a private studio is preferred over a shared one; and finally by `code` ascending as the deterministic tie-break. That last key is an operator-assigned business key: the studio's `id` is never consulted, so when two studios tie on every other key the winner is fully predictable from their codes alone. If the eligibility filter retains nothing, the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_STUDIO_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 409).

A created studio assignment starts in `status` `ACTIVE` with `released_reason` null. The server recomputes the studio's `reserved_resident_days` and `active_assignment_count`, sets the residency's `assigned_studio_code` to the chosen studio's `code`, and advances the studio's and the residency's `version` by 1 each. Assignments may be listed filtered by `studio_id`, `residency_id`, `status` or `selection_mode`. Releasing an assignment by hand — a `FacilitiesManager` or `ProgramAdmin` action carrying an optional `reason` — requires the assignment to be `ACTIVE`, a released row being `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409); it moves `ACTIVE → RELEASED`, records the `released_reason`, recomputes the studio's `reserved_resident_days` and `active_assignment_count`, clears the residency's `assigned_studio_code` to null, and advances the assignment's, the studio's and the residency's `version` by 1 each. Because the studio's day ledger is recomputed from the surviving `ACTIVE` rows rather than adjusted by arithmetic, releasing the only assignment restores `reserved_resident_days` to exactly its pre-assignment value.

**Booking a bed.** A `ProgramAdmin` supplies a `housing_unit_id`, a `residency_id`, a `check_in_date`, a `check_out_date` strictly after it, an `expected_housing_unit_version`, and optionally a `meal_plan_selected` flag defaulting to false. Housing intervals are **half-open**, written `[check_in_date, check_out_date)`: the resident occupies a bed on the check-in night and every night up to but **not** including the check-out date, so `bed_nights` is simply the check-out date minus the check-in date. An assignment from the 2nd of one month to the 28th of the next occupies fifty-six bed-nights, the nights of the 2nd through the 27th. There is no turnaround buffer on housing. The guards are these.

- The `expected_housing_unit_version` must equal the unit's current `version`, or `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Note that the housing OCC guard is taken on the **unit**, not on the residency — the opposite parent from the studio booking's guard.
- The residency's `status` must be exactly `SCHEDULED`; a residency that has already checked in, or one in a terminal status, is `ERR_RESIDENCY_NOT_SCHEDULED` (HTTP 409). As §2.9 records, this check beats the meal-plan check, so a request naming a no-meal unit for an already `IN_RESIDENCE` residency is reported as a residency-state problem, not a meal-plan problem.
- The residency must have a `session_start_date`, or `ERR_RESIDENCY_NOT_SCHEDULED` (HTTP 409).
- The named unit's `status` must be `AVAILABLE`; a unit in `MAINTENANCE` is `ERR_FACILITY_UNAVAILABLE` (HTTP 409).
- The window must lie inside the session, which for housing means `check_in_date` at or after `session_start_date` and `check_out_date` at or before one day past `session_end_date` — the exclusive check-out date may fall on the morning after the session's last night. A violation is `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_OUTSIDE_SESSION` (HTTP 422).
- `meal_plan_selected` may be true only when the named unit's `meal_plan_included` is true, or `ERR_MEAL_PLAN_NOT_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 422).
- Then, as with studios, one of two mutually exclusive occupancy rules applies, selected by the unit's own `bed_count`. On a **single-bed** unit, where `bed_count` is exactly 1, the requested window must share no night with any `ACTIVE` assignment of that unit: two half-open intervals overlap exactly when each one's check-in date falls strictly before the other's check-out date. Adjacency is explicitly **not** a conflict — one resident checking out on a date and another checking in on the same date share no night — and a genuine overlap is `ERR_HOUSING_INTERVAL_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). On a **multi-bed** unit that per-pair rule does not apply; instead, for every single night of the requested window, the number of `ACTIVE` assignments of that unit whose half-open interval contains that night, counting the request itself, must not exceed `bed_count`, and the excess is `ERR_HOUSING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). The boundary here is the exact opposite of the studio rule's: on a one-bed unit whose active assignment runs to a check-out on the 28th, a new booking checking in on the 28th is accepted while one checking in on the 27th is rejected. Only `ACTIVE` rows occupy beds; a `RELEASED` row frees its nights immediately.

A created housing assignment starts in `status` `ACTIVE` with `released_reason` null. The server recomputes the unit's `occupied_bed_nights`, sets the residency's `assigned_housing_unit_code` to the unit's `code`, and advances the unit's and the residency's `version` by 1 each. Bookings may be listed filtered by `housing_unit_id`, `residency_id`, `status` or `meal_plan_selected`. Releasing one — a `ProgramAdmin` action carrying an optional `reason` — requires `ACTIVE` status (`ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`, HTTP 409), moves `ACTIVE → RELEASED`, records the `released_reason`, recomputes the unit's `occupied_bed_nights`, clears `assigned_housing_unit_code` to null, and advances the assignment's, the unit's and the residency's `version` by 1 each. `RELEASED` is terminal for both kinds of assignment: neither can be reactivated, and a resident who needs a studio or a bed again needs a new booking.

### 3.8. Visa Sponsorship for International Residents

Every residency whose `visa_required` is true carries exactly one visa case. The acceptance cascade of §3.5 opens it automatically, and direct residency creation does the same; a `VisaCoordinator` or `ProgramAdmin` may also open one by hand for a follow-on residency or a re-opened sponsorship, supplying only the `residency_id`. The residency's `visa_required` must be true and no visa case may already exist for it — a domestic residency or a second case is `ERR_VISA_NOT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). A new case starts with `status` `INITIATED`, `case_type` `B1_SHORT_TERM`, `rfe_round` 0, empty `requested_doc_list` and `submitted_doc_list`, and `consulate_appointment_date` null. Cases may be listed filtered by `residency_id`, `status` or `case_type`, and are visible to `VisaCoordinator` and `ProgramAdmin` only.

**The timeline.** Four of the case's fields are derived, never supplied, and are re-derived on creation, whenever the sponsored residency is rescheduled, on filing, and on every read of the case.

- `backlog_days` is 45 when the sponsored artist's `citizenship_country` is one of `IN`, `CN`, `NG` or `BR` — the high-volume posts — and 21 for every other citizenship. It is derived even before the residency has a calendar.
- `filing_deadline_date` is the residency's `session_start_date` minus a lead time of 90 days plus `backlog_days`: 135 days for a high-volume citizenship, 111 days otherwise. A session starting on 2 November 2026 therefore has a filing deadline of 20 June 2026 for an Indian citizen and 14 July 2026 for a French one.
- `earliest_entry_date` is the residency's `session_start_date` minus 30 days — the first date on which the resident may lawfully enter the host country.
- `days_to_filing_deadline` is `filing_deadline_date` minus the current date on the logical clock, in whole days, and goes negative once the deadline has passed. It is recomputed on every read rather than stored, so advancing the clock changes it without touching the case.

While the residency has no `session_start_date`, `filing_deadline_date`, `earliest_entry_date` and `days_to_filing_deadline` are all null and `backlog_days` alone is populated. `case_type` is `J1_RESEARCH_SCHOLAR` when the residency's `session_length_weeks` is at least 9 and `B1_SHORT_TERM` otherwise, which means an eight-week session is a short-term case and a nine-week session is a research-scholar case; until the residency is scheduled the case remains `B1_SHORT_TERM` by default. Because the whole timeline is derived from the residency, rescheduling a residency can move a case from inside its filing window to outside it — or the reverse — without the case itself being touched by anybody.

**Documents.** A `VisaCoordinator` submits an `action` of `REQUEST` or `SUBMIT` together with a `doc_list`. In both cases the sponsored residency's `status` must be `SCHEDULED`, or the answer is `ERR_RESIDENCY_NOT_SCHEDULED` (HTTP 409), and the action must be an edge of the case's pipeline from its current status, an inapplicable action — including any action at all on a case already `APPROVED` or `DENIED`, both terminal — being `ERR_VISA_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). `REQUEST` requires a non-empty `doc_list` and drives `INITIATED → DOCS_REQUESTED`, replacing `requested_doc_list` with the supplied list. `SUBMIT` drives `DOCS_REQUESTED → DOCS_SUBMITTED` and requires the supplied `doc_list` to cover every member of `requested_doc_list`; a short submission is `ERR_VISA_DOCS_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422), while extra documents beyond those requested are harmless. A successful submit records `submitted_doc_list`. `REQUEST` is also an edge **out of** `DOCS_SUBMITTED`, closing a cycle: re-requesting after a submission drives `DOCS_SUBMITTED → DOCS_REQUESTED`, replaces `requested_doc_list` with the new list and increases `rfe_round` by 1. Those request-for-evidence rounds are unbounded — unlike the application revision loop of §3.3, which stops at two — so `rfe_round` simply counts how many times the coordinator has gone back to the resident. Every document action advances the case's `version` by 1.

**Adjudication.** A `VisaCoordinator` submits an `action` of `FILE`, `APPROVE` or `DENY`, with a `consulate_appointment_date` on a filing and an optional `reason`. The residency's `status` must be `SCHEDULED` (`ERR_RESIDENCY_NOT_SCHEDULED`, HTTP 409), the residency must have a `session_start_date` so that the timeline is defined (also `ERR_RESIDENCY_NOT_SCHEDULED`, HTTP 409), and the action must be an edge from the case's current status (`ERR_VISA_STATE_INVALID`, HTTP 409). `FILE` drives `DOCS_SUBMITTED → FILED` and adds two window checks: the current date on the logical clock must be at or before `filing_deadline_date`, a late filing being `ERR_VISA_FILING_DEADLINE_PASSED` (HTTP 422); and the supplied `consulate_appointment_date` must fall at or after the current date and at or before `earliest_entry_date`, an appointment in the past or one after the earliest lawful entry date being `ERR_CONSULATE_DATE_OUT_OF_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). A successful filing records `consulate_appointment_date` and re-derives `case_type` from the residency's `session_length_weeks` as it then stands. `APPROVE` drives `FILED → APPROVED` with no further precondition, and is what unblocks the residency's check-in described in §3.6. `DENY` drives `FILED → DENIED`. Each adjudication action advances the case's `version` by 1.

Finally, a withdrawal reaches the visa case from outside. When a residency moves from `SCHEDULED` to `WITHDRAWN`, its visa case is driven to `DENIED` from whichever non-terminal state it occupies — there is an edge to `DENIED` from `INITIATED`, from `DOCS_REQUESTED`, from `DOCS_SUBMITTED` and from `FILED` — with the reason recorded as sponsorship withdrawn and the case's `version` advanced by 1. A case already `APPROVED` or `DENIED` is left untouched, so an approved visa survives the withdrawal of the residency it was granted for.

### 3.9. Stipend Administration, Withholding, Tax Reporting and Funder Outcomes

The residency's award figure, `stipend_total_cents`, is priced two different ways, and which one is in force is recorded on the residency itself as `stipend_basis`.

- The **provisional award** is in force while `stipend_basis` is `PROVISIONAL` — from offer acceptance until the residency completes or terminates. It is the residency's `stipend_weekly_rate_cents` multiplied by the planned week count, so the cycle commits the whole award up front. The planned week count is the residency's `session_length_weeks`; before the residency has been scheduled it falls back to the source application's `requested_session_length_weeks`; and for an invited residency that has neither a confirmed session nor a source application it is 0, making the provisional award 0 as well.
- The **final settled award** is in force while `stipend_basis` is `FINAL_SETTLED`, which the termination cascade of §3.6 sets. It is the same weekly rate multiplied by the weeks actually served, that figure being the number of days from `session_start_date` up to and including the settlement date, divided by seven and rounded **up**. A partial week therefore counts as a full week. At a weekly rate of 70,000 cents an eight-week session prices provisionally at 560,000 cents; terminated after nineteen days served, three whole weeks, it re-prices at 210,000 cents and releases the 350,000-cent difference back to the cycle's uncommitted pool.

Neither variant reads the residency's disbursement rows. The schedule is derived **from** the award, not the other way round, which is precisely why a voided disbursement can never double-count against the award. The residency's effective `stipend_weekly_rate_cents` is the cycle's `stipend_weekly_rate_cents` unless a schedule generation supplied a `weekly_rate_override_cents`, in which case it is the override.

**Generating the schedule.** A `FinanceOfficer` — and only a `FinanceOfficer` — generates a residency's disbursement schedule, supplying an `expected_residency_version` and optionally a `weekly_rate_override_cents`. The guards are the OCC match on the residency's `version` (`ERR_STALE_VERSION`, HTTP 412), a residency `status` of exactly `SCHEDULED` and a non-null `session_start_date` (either failure being `ERR_RESIDENCY_NOT_SCHEDULED`, HTTP 409), and finally sufficient uncommitted stipend in the cycle to cover what the schedule commits (`ERR_STIPEND_POOL_EXHAUSTED`, HTTP 409). The split then runs chronologically over two-week periods. The number of periods is the residency's `session_length_weeks` divided by two, rounded **up**. Periods are emitted in strictly ascending `period_index` starting at 1; each period's `weeks_in_period` — a field the disbursement row stores and returns — is two, except that the last period takes only the weeks that remain, so a five-week session splits into two, two and one. Each row's `period_start_date` is `session_start_date` plus fourteen days for every period already emitted; its `period_end_date` is thirteen days after its own start, or the residency's `session_end_date` if that falls sooner, so the final period never runs past the session. Its `gross_cents` is the effective weekly rate multiplied by that period's `weeks_in_period` — a short final period is priced at its true week count, never at a full period — and eight weeks at 70,000 cents per week therefore yields four rows of 140,000 cents summing to 560,000, while five weeks at the same rate yields 140,000, 140,000 and 70,000 summing to 350,000. Every row is created with `status` `SCHEDULED`, `withholding_cents` and `net_cents` 0, `withholding_rate` and `cumulative_taxable_cents` null, `paid_on_date` and `paid_by_user_id` null, `tax_form_type` copied from the residency as it stands at generation time, and `scheduled_by_user_id` set to the acting officer's `X-User-ID` — an opaque operator identifier, not the id of any entity row. Generation also sets the residency's `stipend_weekly_rate_cents` to the effective rate, re-bases `stipend_total_cents` to the provisional award for that rate, recomputes the cycle's stipend ledger, and advances the residency's and the cycle's `version` by 1 each. Because the rows it creates are the disbursements rather than the residency, this call answers HTTP 201 with `response_type` `"array"` and `data` carrying the disbursement rows it has just created, in the ascending `period_index` order they were emitted in; it is not a list endpoint, so `meta` carries no pagination fields.

Generation may be repeated. On a regeneration every existing `SCHEDULED` row of the residency is first driven `SCHEDULED → VOIDED`, while rows in `PAID` and `REVERSED` are left completely untouched. The new rows then restart at `period_index` 1, reusing the `period_index` values that the surviving paid and reversed rows already hold — the index is an ordinal within a generation, not a globally unique key for the residency. Disbursements are created by no other means, so listing them, filtered by `residency_id`, `status` or `period_index`, is the only way to discover them; a `FinanceOfficer`, a `ProgramAdmin` or an `Applicant` may read them.

**Settling a disbursement.** A `FinanceOfficer` submits an `action` of `PAY`, `VOID` or `REVERSE`, with an optional `paid_on_date` and an optional `reason`. `PAY` and `VOID` both require the row to be `SCHEDULED` and `REVERSE` requires it to be `PAID`; any other combination is `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). `VOIDED` and `REVERSED` are terminal.

`PAY` drives `SCHEDULED → PAID` under four further guards. The residency's `status` must be `IN_RESIDENCE` or `COMPLETED` — a residency that has not yet checked in, or one terminated or withdrawn, admits no payment, and the answer is `ERR_RESIDENCY_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The residency's `compliance_status` must not be `SUSPENDED`, or `ERR_COMPLIANCE_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 422). Every disbursement of the same residency with a lower `period_index` must already have left `SCHEDULED` — that is, must be `PAID`, `VOIDED` or `REVERSED` — so payment runs strictly in order, and a gap is `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_OUT_OF_ORDER` (HTTP 409). And when the row being paid carries the highest `period_index` among the residency's non-voided schedule, the residency's `obligations_breached_count` must be 0: the final payment is withheld until the public-engagement commitments are settled, and an outstanding breach is `ERR_OBLIGATIONS_OUTSTANDING` (HTTP 422). The acting officer's `X-User-ID` must also differ from the row's `scheduled_by_user_id` (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, HTTP 403): whoever generated the schedule may not pay it.

**Withholding** is computed at the moment of payment, and the rate depends on the resident's tax status rather than on the amount. When the residency's `tax_form_type` is `W9` the `withholding_rate` is 0.00. Otherwise, when the sponsored artist's `treaty_country` is true the rate is 0.15, and when it is not the rate is 0.30. The taxable amount is then found from a **cumulative** exemption of 60,000 cents across the residency's paid disbursements, not a per-payment exemption. Take the sum of `gross_cents` over the residency's disbursements that are already `PAID` and whose `period_index` is lower than this row's — call that the cumulative gross before this payment — and add this row's `gross_cents` to obtain the cumulative gross after it. The taxable slice of this payment is the amount by which the cumulative-after figure exceeds 60,000 cents, less the amount by which the cumulative-before figure exceeds 60,000 cents, where each excess is treated as 0 when the figure is at or below the threshold. That difference is stored as `cumulative_taxable_cents`; `withholding_cents` is the taxable slice multiplied by the rate, rounded half up to whole cents; and `net_cents` is `gross_cents` minus `withholding_cents`. The consequence worth stating twice is that a single payment can be partly exempt and partly taxable, and that at a cumulative total of exactly 60,000 cents nothing is withheld — only the first cent above the threshold is taxable. For a non-treaty international resident receiving four payments of 30,000 cents, the first two are wholly exempt, the second bringing the cumulative total to exactly the threshold, and the third and fourth are each fully taxable at 30,000 cents, withholding 9,000 cents apiece.

A successful payment also records `paid_on_date` and sets `paid_by_user_id` to the acting officer's `X-User-ID`, then recomputes the residency's `stipend_paid_gross_cents` — the sum of `gross_cents` over the residency's `PAID` rows — and `withholding_to_date_cents` — the sum of `withholding_cents` over the same set. Both the disbursement's and the residency's `version` advance by 1.

`VOID` drives `SCHEDULED → VOIDED`, sets `withholding_cents` and `net_cents` to 0, and advances the row's `version` by 1. `REVERSE` drives `PAID → REVERSED` and is the second segregation point: the acting officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the row's `paid_by_user_id`, or `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). A reversal is a compensating action, not an erasure. The row keeps its `gross_cents`, `withholding_cents`, `net_cents`, `withholding_rate`, `cumulative_taxable_cents` and `paid_on_date` for tax-reporting history, but it is excluded from both residency ledgers, which are recomputed without it and thereby restored to exactly their pre-payment values. The row's and the residency's `version` each advance by 1. Note that `SCHEDULED` rows are not yet income, `VOIDED` rows were never paid, and `REVERSED` rows are excluded from every live total — only `PAID` rows contribute.

**The tax report** is a computed read available to a `FinanceOfficer` or a `ProgramAdmin` that writes nothing and returns eight fields. `tax_form_type` and `withholding_rate` are the residency's form type and the rate selected as above. `annual_gross_cents` is the sum of `gross_cents` over the residency's `PAID` disbursements and `paid_period_count` is how many such rows there are. `annual_taxable_cents` is the amount by which `annual_gross_cents` exceeds the 60,000-cent exemption, or 0 when it does not. `withheld_actual_cents` is the sum of `withholding_cents` over those same paid rows — the money actually held back, with each payment's rounding already baked in. `statutory_liability_cents` is a genuinely different calculation of the same liability: the rate is applied **once** to `annual_taxable_cents` and the result is rounded half up to whole cents **once**. Because the first variant rounds every payment and the second rounds only the total, the two can disagree by a cent or two, and `reconciliation_delta_cents` reports the gap as `withheld_actual_cents` minus `statutory_liability_cents`, a positive value meaning the programme over-withheld. For a treaty resident paid four periods of 87,502 cents at 0.15, the per-payment withholdings are 4,125, 13,125, 13,125 and 13,125 cents, totalling 43,500, while the statutory line is the rate applied once to 290,008 cents of taxable income, rounding to 43,501, so the delta is −1.

**The funder outcome report** is a computed read available to a `ProgramAdmin` or a `Funder` that writes nothing and rolls the whole cycle up into thirteen fields, all computed over the cycle's residencies. `awarded_count` counts the residencies whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_RESIDENCE`, `COMPLETED` or `TERMINATED`; `completed_count`, `terminated_count` and `withdrawn_count` count those in `COMPLETED`, `TERMINATED` and `WITHDRAWN` respectively. Withdrawn residencies are reported in `withdrawn_count` but excluded from `awarded_count` and therefore from every ratio that divides by it, so a single withdrawal moves both a numerator and a denominator. `completion_rate_pct` is one hundred times `completed_count` divided by `awarded_count`, rounded half up to two decimal places; `international_share_pct` is one hundred times the number of awarded residencies whose `visa_required` is true, over `awarded_count`, on the same rounding; and `alumni_share_pct` is one hundred times the number of awarded residencies whose artist's `completed_residency_count` is at least 1, over `awarded_count`, likewise. Each of those three is `null` rather than 0 when `awarded_count` is 0. `follow_on_count` is the number of residencies whose `predecessor_residency_id` is not null. `obligation_fulfilment_pct` is one hundred times the sum of `obligations_completed_total` across the cycle's residencies divided by the sum of `obligations_required_total`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `null` when that denominator is 0; because waived obligations are already out of both residency sums, they neither help nor hurt the percentage. `breached_obligation_count` is the sum of `obligations_breached_count`, and `total_attendance` is the sum of `total_attendance` across every obligation of the cycle's residencies. Finally `stipend_paid_cents` is the sum of `stipend_paid_gross_cents` and `withholding_cents` the sum of `withholding_to_date_cents`, both across the cycle's residencies and both in integer cents.
