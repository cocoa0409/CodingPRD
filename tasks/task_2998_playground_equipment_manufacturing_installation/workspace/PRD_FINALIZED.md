# Playground Equipment Manufacturing & Installation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Commercial playground equipment is one of the few products where a design error is simultaneously a safety incident, a regulatory finding and a warranty liability. Our manufacturing business begins long before the first post is extruded: a design consultant sits with a school district, a municipality or a homeowners' association, measures a site, learns which age bands the finished playground must serve, discovers that the subgrade is clay rather than sandy loam, and is told the budget ceiling and the date on which the community's fundraising drive closes. From that conversation the business must produce a rendering the community can vote on, a quote that is defensible to the cent, a manufacturing order that can be kitted and bagged on the plant floor, a booking with a certified installer whose crew calendar is genuinely free, and finally an independent post-installation safety audit. Today that chain lives in spreadsheets, PDF renderings emailed around a school board, and a warranty binder in a filing cabinet, and the seams between those artefacts are exactly where compliance failures hide.

This document specifies the API that replaces that chain with a single system of record. Its central organising idea is that a **playground safety standard edition** governs everything: exactly one edition is ACTIVE at any moment, component models are certified against that edition, renderings pin the edition they were drawn under, and when a new edition supersedes the old one the system must automatically walk the consequences outward — decertifying models, demoting the placements that used them, reopening the designs that contained those placements, and expiring the renderings and quotes that were built on them. A second organising idea is that money and geometry are both computed, never asserted: use-zone intervals, critical fall heights, required surfacing depths, quote subtotals, fundraising allocations, crew-day commitments, warranty coverage percentages and retrofit prices are all derived by the server from rules written here, so that two readers of the same design cannot disagree about them.

The API is mounted under the `/playground` prefix. It is designed for six distinct operator populations — plant administration, design consultancy, compliance, the commissioning community, the installer network and independent safety auditors — whose duties deliberately do not overlap. Where a segregation-of-duties boundary exists in the real business (a consultant may not certify their own design; the auditor who inspected an installation may not also audit it), the API enforces that boundary rather than trusting policy.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads, writes and the test-driving clock endpoint alike — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The same rejection applies when `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the six roles defined below: an unrecognised role name is an authentication failure, not an authorisation failure.

Six roles exist, and their names must be spelled exactly as follows:

- `ManufacturerAdmin` — plant administration. Owns the component catalogue, registers standard editions, drives the production floor and manages membership of the installer network.
- `DesignConsultant` — runs the design consultation. Owns projects, play areas, placements, renderings, quotes, manufacturing orders and installer bookings.
- `ComplianceOfficer` — owns safety certification. Activates and supersedes standard editions, certifies component models and finished designs, and decides warranty claims.
- `ClientRepresentative` — acts for the commissioning community. Runs fundraising, records community input, accepts quotes, buys replacement parts and files warranty claims.
- `InstallerOperator` — a company in the certified installer network, acting on its own bookings only.
- `SafetyAuditor` — an independent inspector who files installation inspections and post-installation safety audits.

Once the headers are accepted, the acting role must appear in the authorised-role list of the endpoint being called, as tabulated in §4. A role that is legitimate but not authorised for that endpoint receives `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

Identity semantics differ between the roles, and this difference matters because it decides what ownership means. When `X-User-Role` is `InstallerOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CertifiedInstaller`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `CertifiedInstaller` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, an `InstallerOperator` may start, complete or drive the rework loop of a job only when that job's `installer_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`; acting on another company's booking is rejected with `ERR_NOT_JOB_OWNER` (HTTP 403). For the other five roles — `ManufacturerAdmin`, `DesignConsultant`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `ClientRepresentative` and `SafetyAuditor` — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record such an identifier (for example as `consultant_user_id` on a `DesignProject` when a `DesignConsultant` opens it, as `performed_by_user_id` on every inspection record a `SafetyAuditor` files, or as `install_inspector_user_id` on an `InstallationJob`) but it never compares those opaque identifiers to any entity's `id`.

Two segregation-of-duties rules ride on those recorded identifiers, and both reject with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). First, the `ComplianceOfficer` who certifies a project's compliance must not be the `DesignConsultant` who created it: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the project's stored `consultant_user_id`. Second, the `SafetyAuditor` who files a post-installation audit must not be the auditor recorded in the job's `install_inspector_user_id` — that is, the auditor who filed the job's installation inspection is permanently barred from auditing the same job.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` that is not an integer, or is below 1, or above 100, and an `offset` that is not an integer or is below 0, are both rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 422).

Unless a specific endpoint's narrative says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The inspection log of an installation job is the one place where an additional ordering guarantee is stated explicitly: its records come back in `sequence_number` order, which for an append-only log is the same ordering as `created_at` ascending.

The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filter before pagination was applied, and `has_more` indicating whether any row remains beyond the current page. Filter query parameters listed for an endpoint in §4 narrow `total_count` accordingly; they never change the sort order.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every resource the API creates is assigned an `id` that is a server-minted UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and no embedded semantics — a caller must never parse an `id` to learn what kind of resource it points at. Clients never supply an `id`; a body field that happens to end in `_id` is always a reference to an already-existing row, never a request to mint one.

Most resources also carry three common properties. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the row was inserted, rendered with exactly three fractional digits. `updated_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the row's most recent mutation, in the same format; on a freshly created row it equals `created_at`. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations the caller did not directly request, such as those a cascade performs. When a narrative in §3 says an operation "bumps" or "increments" a row's version, it means exactly this: one increment per row actually mutated by that operation, and `updated_at` refreshed at the same time.

`InstallationInspection` is the single exception. Inspection records are immutable append-only evidence, so they carry only `id` and `created_at`; they have neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and neither field appears in their JSON representation. Every other entity — `PlaygroundStandard`, `ComponentModel`, `DesignProject`, `PlayArea`, `ComponentPlacement`, `DesignRendering`, `Quote`, `FundraisingPledge`, `ManufacturingOrder`, `CertifiedInstaller`, `InstallationJob` and `WarrantyClaim` — carries all four of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

### 2.5. The Logical Plant Clock

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time for business purposes. Instead the server keeps a single **logical clock**, and every deadline, expiry, elapsed-warranty, effective-date and booking-window rule in this document reads it. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; this startup default is mandatory, and the server must not fall back to real time under any circumstance. An administrative test-driving capability moves the clock to an explicit ISO-8601 UTC instant supplied as `current_utc`, and the response body reports the clock's new `current_utc` value rather than a business row.

Time may be moved forward freely, but it may not be rewound past history the system has already written down: if the requested instant is earlier than any `installed_on` date already recorded on any project, the request is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 422). Setting the clock mutates no business rows and bumps no versions.

Where a rule compares against a calendar date rather than an instant — a fundraising deadline, a quote's last acceptable acceptance day, an installer's certification expiry, a job's first startable day, a pledge date, a failure date — the comparison uses the date portion of the logical clock in UTC. Where a rule compares against a calendar year — the last year in which replacement parts for a model are sold — it uses the year portion of that same date.

### 2.6. Nested Path Resolution and Referential Integrity

Several resources are addressed through nested paths — a project's play areas, a play area's placements, a job's inspection log. Path segments are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the error. Asking for the placements of a play area that does not exist yields `ERR_PLAY_AREA_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) even when the placement identifier further right is also nonsense; asking for a rendering under a project that does not exist yields `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND` before the rendering is ever looked up. Each entity has its own not-found code, and all of them are HTTP 404: `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAY_AREA_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RENDERING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLEDGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSTALLER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_JOB_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`.

The same catalogue governs references supplied in a request body rather than in the path. Every foreign key a caller sends must resolve to an existing row, and an unresolvable reference produces the not-found code of the referenced entity's type: `component_model_id`, `supersedes_model_id` and `replacement_model_id` produce `ERR_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`; `attached_to_placement_id` and `placement_id` produce `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`; `pinned_standard_id` and `successor_standard_id` produce `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_FOUND`; `rendering_id` produces `ERR_RENDERING_NOT_FOUND`; `project_id` produces `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`; `quote_id` produces `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND`; `installer_id` produces `ERR_INSTALLER_NOT_FOUND`; and `order_id` produces `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`.

Beyond existence, request bodies are checked for shape. Required fields must be present, values must be of the declared type, enum-typed values must be members of their declared set, strings must respect their declared length limits and numbers their declared ranges. Any failure of that kind is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). Fields the endpoint does not recognise are ignored rather than rejected.

### 2.7. Money, Geometry and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integer counts of cents. There are no floating-point money fields, no currency codes and no fractional cents in stored data. Where a calculation multiplies an amount by a percentage and the exact product is not a whole number of cents, the result is rounded **half up** — a value whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero to the next higher cent. Rounding happens only at the points this document names; intermediate products are carried exactly, as rational arithmetic, until such a point is reached. The distinction is not cosmetic: §3.5 describes two quote variants that differ *only* in where the single rounding point sits, and on the same placement data they can legitimately produce subtotals that differ by up to one cent per priced line less one.

All geometry — site extents, play-area intervals, anchor coordinates, use-zone extents, deck heights, surfacing depths, clearances — is expressed in whole centimetres. Those calculations never round because every term in them is already an integer. Percentages that are exposed as data rather than used as a multiplier follow their own stated precision: the community approval percentage is carried to exactly one decimal place, rounded half up, while a warranty coverage percentage is a whole percent, also rounded half up.

Crew-day figures are whole numbers, and every date interval in this system that is described as a booking window is **closed on both ends**: both the first and the last calendar day count, so a window whose start and end are the same day is one day long. Use-zone intervals are likewise closed on both ends. Play-area axis intervals, by contrast, are **half-open**: the origin coordinate is inside the interval and the coordinate one past the interval's length is outside it. Mixing these two conventions up is the most common source of off-by-one geometry defects, so §3.3 and §3.4 restate them where they bite.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Protection on Quoting and Rescheduling

Two write operations require the client to declare which revision of a *parent* row it was looking at when it decided to write, and both reject a mismatch with HTTP 412.

A `POST /playground/quotes` request must carry `expected_project_version`, an integer holding the client's last observed `version` of the `DesignProject` named by `project_id`. If that value does not equal the project's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason is that issuing a quote legitimately expires the project's prior ISSUED quote; a writer whose view of the project predates another writer's successful issuance would therefore silently expire a quote it had never seen. `expected_project_version` is a required field of that request, not an optional one.

A `POST /playground/installation-jobs/{id}/reschedule` request must carry `expected_installer_version`, an integer holding the client's last observed `version` of the `CertifiedInstaller` the job is booked with. If that value does not equal the installer's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INSTALLER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A reschedule supersedes the job's previous booking window and re-derives the installer's committed crew days, so a writer holding a stale view of the installer's booking book would evaluate the overlap, travel-buffer and crew-capacity checks against calendar occupancy that no longer exists. `expected_installer_version` is likewise required.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field. In particular, quote acceptance, placement validation, order progression, inspection filing and warranty decisions carry no `expected_*_version` input, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.9. One Governing Edition, and How Cascades Propagate

At most one `PlaygroundStandard` row may be in status `ACTIVE` at any instant. That invariant is what makes the phrase "the ACTIVE edition" a deterministic singleton everywhere else in this document: operations that must resolve it never have to choose between candidates, and if there is no candidate at all they fail rather than guess, with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_STANDARD` (HTTP 409). The operations that must resolve the ACTIVE edition are model certification, placement creation and rendering issuance.

Cascades in this system are **immediate and complete**. When an operation's side effects include changes to rows other than the one addressed, those changes land inside the same request, before the response is written; there is no deferred queue and no eventual reconciliation. Two cascades are large enough to be described hop by hop in §3 — the supersession of a standard edition (§3.1) and the failure of a post-installation audit (§3.8) — and two more are structural (project cancellation in §3.9, installer revocation in §3.7). In all of them, every row actually mutated has its `version` incremented by exactly 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed, including rows the caller never named. Rows that a cascade examines but does not change are not bumped.

Derived fields are always **recomputed from their inputs**, never patched incrementally. A play area's `critical_fall_height_cm` and `required_surfacing_depth_cm` are re-derived from the area's current placement set every time that set or its members change. A project's `funds_collected_cents` and its four allocation buckets are re-derived from the project's collected pledges on every collection and every refund. An installer's `committed_crew_days` is re-derived from the installer's current non-excluded job set on every event that could change it. This rule is what makes compensating actions exact: refunding a collected pledge restores the buckets to precisely their pre-collection values, and cancelling a job restores `committed_crew_days` to precisely its pre-booking value, with no accumulated drift.

### 2.10. Order of Rejection

When a request violates more than one rule, the response must carry the error of the **highest-priority** violated rule, evaluated in the following order. This ordering is normative; a fair implementation that checks rules in a different order will report the wrong code on requests that break several rules at once.

1. **Header presence and role recognition.** Missing or empty `X-User-ID` or `X-User-Role` first, then an `X-User-Role` outside the six declared roles — both `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Endpoint authorisation.** The acting role must be listed for the endpoint — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. **Ownership and duty separation.** Job ownership for installer-driven job operations (`ERR_NOT_JOB_OWNER`) is checked next; then the consultant-versus-officer separation on compliance certification, and then the inspector-versus-auditor separation on a post-installation audit (both `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`).
4. **Path existence,** left to right, then **body foreign-key existence** — the not-found codes of §2.6.
5. **Request shape,** then **pagination bounds** — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, then `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
6. **Stale parent version** — `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_INSTALLER_VERSION`.
7. **State admissibility of the transition itself:** whether the target row's state field permits the requested move at all. A trigger fired from a state that declares no such transition — including firing the same trigger twice — is rejected here with that entity's state error: `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_MODEL_NOT_CERTIFIABLE`, `ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_EDITABLE`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_RENDERING_NOT_ISSUED`, `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_ACCEPTED`, `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_IN_PRODUCTION`, `ERR_JOB_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PLEDGE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE` or `ERR_INSTALLER_INVALID_STATE`.
8. **Editability and locking of design content:** design content may be mutated only while the owning project's `design_status` is `CONSULTATION` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` (`ERR_PROJECT_NOT_EDITABLE`), and a `LOCKED` play area accepts no further placement writes (`ERR_PLAY_AREA_LOCKED`).
9. **Cross-entity state gates:** the placed model must be certified and not discontinued, an attachment parent must be validated, a quote's rendering must be ISSUED, an order's quote must be an ACCEPTED FIRM quote, a job's installer must be ACTIVE, and a rendering's pinned edition must still be ACTIVE when compliance is certified.
10. **Standard-registry invariants:** at most one ACTIVE edition (`ERR_ACTIVE_STANDARD_EXISTS`), an ACTIVE edition must exist where one is required (`ERR_NO_ACTIVE_STANDARD`), and a supersession's successor must be DRAFT and already effective (`ERR_SUCCESSOR_NOT_DRAFT`, then `ERR_SUCCESSOR_NOT_EFFECTIVE`).
11. **Design-completeness gates for rendering issuance:** every required age band must have a play area (`ERR_AGE_GROUP_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE`) and every play area must hold at least one placement in `DRAFT` or `VALIDATED` (`ERR_PLAY_AREA_EMPTY`).
12. **Safety checks,** in this order: fall height against the ACTIVE edition's ceiling for the area's age band (`ERR_FALL_HEIGHT_EXCEEDS_STANDARD`), age-group use-zone separation (`ERR_AGE_GROUP_SEPARATION_VIOLATION`), model-to-area age-band agreement (`ERR_AGE_GROUP_MISMATCH`), surfacing depth sufficiency (`ERR_SURFACING_DEPTH_INSUFFICIENT`), and accessible-route provision (`ERR_ACCESSIBLE_ROUTE_MISSING`).
13. **Business constraints,** in this order: the rework ceiling (`ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`), use-zone overlap inside an area (`ERR_USE_ZONE_OVERLAP`), use-zone containment inside the area's axis interval (`ERR_USE_ZONE_OUT_OF_BOUNDS`), attachment direction (`ERR_INVALID_ATTACHMENT_TARGET`), installer booking conflicts (`ERR_INSTALLER_BOOKING_CONFLICT`), crew capacity (`ERR_CREW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`), installer category coverage (`ERR_INSTALLER_CATEGORY_NOT_CERTIFIED`) and the minimum community turnout (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_COMMUNITY_INPUT`).
14. **Temporal and referential business rules,** in this order: the fundraising deadline (`ERR_FUNDRAISING_DEADLINE_PASSED`), quote validity (`ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`), parts availability (`ERR_PARTS_NO_LONGER_AVAILABLE`), installer certification expiry and job start eligibility (`ERR_INSTALLER_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`, then `ERR_JOB_NOT_YET_STARTABLE`), retrofit model eligibility (`ERR_RETROFIT_MODEL_MISMATCH`), warranty claim placement ownership and project installation (`ERR_WARRANTY_PLACEMENT_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_INSTALLED`), failure-date bounds (`ERR_INVALID_FAILURE_DATE`), rendering-to-project agreement on a quote (`ERR_RENDERING_PROJECT_MISMATCH`), an empty pricing scope (`ERR_QUOTE_EMPTY_SCOPE`) and the requirement that every placement an order's quote priced is still validated (`ERR_PLACEMENTS_NOT_VALIDATED`).
15. **Fiscal checks last.** Only after every safety and constraint check has passed does the system look at money: a FIRM quote against the client's budget ceiling (`ERR_QUOTE_EXCEEDS_BUDGET`), funding sufficiency when an order is raised (`ERR_FUNDING_SHORTFALL`), committed funds blocking a refund (`ERR_FUNDS_ALREADY_COMMITTED`) and decayed warranty coverage blocking an approval (`ERR_WARRANTY_EXPIRED`).
16. **Standalone rules that guard a single operation** sit at the end of their own operation's chain: an installer may not be reinstated while audit failures remain open (`ERR_AUDIT_FAILURES_OPEN`), a project may be cancelled only from a cancellable status and may accept pledges only while fundable (`ERR_PROJECT_NOT_CANCELLABLE`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FUNDABLE`), a model whose commercial lifecycle has ended may not be placed (`ERR_MODEL_DISCONTINUED`), a model that is not certified may not be placed, validated or used as a retrofit replacement (`ERR_MODEL_NOT_CERTIFIED`), a placement's attachment parent must itself be validated (`ERR_PARENT_PLACEMENT_NOT_VALIDATED`), a PROVISIONAL quote may not be accepted (`ERR_PROVISIONAL_QUOTE_NOT_ACCEPTABLE`), an inactive installer may not be booked or started (`ERR_INSTALLER_NOT_ACTIVE`) and the logical clock may not be rewound past a recorded installation date (`ERR_CLOCK_REWIND_FORBIDDEN`).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Governing Safety Editions and the Component Catalogue

Everything the plant builds is measured against a **playground safety standard edition**. Registering one is a records-keeping act, open to both `ManufacturerAdmin` and `ComplianceOfficer` operators, and it captures the edition's family `code` — a string of 1 to 40 characters restricted to uppercase letters, digits and the underscore character, such as the family that governs public playground equipment — together with the `edition_year` of this particular revision, an integer between 2000 and 2100 inclusive. The pair of `code` and `edition_year` must be globally unique: the same family may be registered many times, but never twice for the same revision year. The edition also carries `effective_from`, a calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form marking the earliest day on which the revision may take effect, and four numeric requirements: `min_use_zone_clearance_cm`, the minimum number of clear centimetres the edition demands between use zones belonging to different age bands, which may be zero or more; and three fall-height ceilings, `max_fall_height_cm_toddler`, `max_fall_height_cm_preschool` and `max_fall_height_cm_school_age`, each of which must be greater than zero and each of which caps the designated play surface height permitted for the age band named in its field. Finally the boolean `requires_accessible_route` records whether the edition mandates an accessible transfer point in every play area; when the registrant omits it, it defaults to true. A newly registered edition is born in `DRAFT` status with `superseded_by_standard_id` null, and governs nothing at all until it is activated. Registry listings may be narrowed by `status` and by `code`, and — because `superseded_by_standard_id` doubles as a reverse lookup — by the identifier of the edition that superseded them.

Activation is a `ComplianceOfficer` privilege and moves an edition `DRAFT → ACTIVE`. Two things can go wrong. Activating an edition whose status is anything other than `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_DRAFT`, and activating a genuine `DRAFT` while some other edition already sits in `ACTIVE` is rejected with `ERR_ACTIVE_STANDARD_EXISTS` — the registry admits at most one `ACTIVE` edition at any instant, and that invariant is load-bearing throughout §3. A successful activation sets `status` to `ACTIVE` and bumps the edition's `version` by one.

**Supersession is the largest single operation in this API.** A `ComplianceOfficer` retires the `ACTIVE` edition by nominating a `successor_standard_id` in the request body. The edition being retired must itself be `ACTIVE`, or the request fails with `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_ACTIVE`; the nominated successor must be in `DRAFT`, or the request fails with `ERR_SUCCESSOR_NOT_DRAFT`; and the successor's `effective_from` must be on or before the logical clock's current date, or the request fails with `ERR_SUCCESSOR_NOT_EFFECTIVE` — a revision that has not yet come into force cannot be pressed into service early. When all three hold, the retiring edition takes `status` `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` and records the successor in its `superseded_by_standard_id`, while the successor is atomically promoted to `ACTIVE`, so the single-`ACTIVE` invariant never lapses even momentarily.

The consequences then propagate outward in four hops, all inside the same request. **Hop one:** because the retired edition no longer governs, every `ComponentModel` whose `certified_against_standard_id` points at it and whose `certification_status` is `CERTIFIED` moves `CERTIFIED → DECERTIFIED`; the historical pin in `certified_against_standard_id` is deliberately retained, so the record of what the model was once certified against survives. **Hop two:** because those models are now decertified, every `ComponentPlacement` whose `component_model_id` is one of them moves to `NEEDS_RECERTIFICATION`, provided its own `status` was `DRAFT` or `VALIDATED` and its owning project's `design_status` is one of `CONSULTATION`, `RENDERED`, `COMMUNITY_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `DESIGN_APPROVED`, `CERTIFIED` or `ORDERED`. **Hop three:** because those placements now need recertification, each owning `DesignProject` has its `compliance_status` set to `RETROFIT_REQUIRED`; and additionally, any such project whose `design_status` is `RENDERED`, `COMMUNITY_REVIEW` or `CERTIFIED` is reverted to `REVISION_REQUESTED`. A project already sitting in `DESIGN_APPROVED` is a deliberate exception: its community sign-off is preserved and its `design_status` is *not* reverted, though its `compliance_status` still becomes `RETROFIT_REQUIRED`. **Hop four:** for exactly those projects whose `design_status` hop three reverted to `REVISION_REQUESTED`, every `DesignRendering` of theirs in `ISSUED` status moves to `SUPERSEDED` and every `Quote` of theirs in `ISSUED` status moves to `EXPIRED`. The `DESIGN_APPROVED` project keeps its `ISSUED` rendering — which now pins an edition that is no longer `ACTIVE`, so its later attempt at compliance certification will fail with `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_ACTIVE`. That is the intended trap, not an oversight.

After the hops, every play area touched by hop two has its `critical_fall_height_cm` and `required_surfacing_depth_cm` recomputed, because placements that have left `DRAFT` and `VALIDATED` also leave the input set of those two derived numbers. Version counters are incremented by one on the retired edition, on the promoted successor, and on every model, placement, project, rendering, quote and play area the four hops actually mutated.

Turning to the catalogue: a `ManufacturerAdmin` adds a component model with a `sku` of 1 to 40 characters that must be globally unique, a `category` drawn from `POST`, `DECK`, `PLASTIC_SLIDE`, `METAL_CLIMBER` and `HARDWARE_KIT`, and an `age_group` drawn from `TODDLER_6_23M`, `PRESCHOOL_2_5` and `SCHOOL_AGE_5_12`. Geometry comes from two integers: `deck_height_cm`, the designated play surface height this model contributes, which ranges from 0 (for ground-level components) to 400 inclusive; and `use_zone_footprint_cm`, the half-extent of the model's use zone measured outward from its anchor point along the site axis, which must be zero or more. Commercial terms come from `unit_price_cents`, a list price greater than zero that excludes both colour uplift and freight; `hardware_bag_count`, the number of fastener bags shipped per unit, zero or more; `warranty_years`, a term between 1 and 50 inclusive; and `parts_support_until_year`, the last calendar year between 2000 and 2200 inclusive in which replacement parts for the model are still sold. The `color_options` array holds between 1 and 8 distinct uppercase strings, and its **first element is the no-cost default colour** — any other selection is chargeable, as §3.5 explains. The boolean `accessible_transfer_ready` records whether the model provides a compliant accessible transfer point and defaults to false. Where the model replaces an older one for retrofit purposes, `supersedes_model_id` names that predecessor; for a new family it is omitted and stored as null, and it doubles as a reverse-lookup filter on catalogue listings. A newly added model is `UNCERTIFIED` with `certified_against_standard_id` null and `lifecycle_status` `ACTIVE`. Catalogue listings may be filtered by `category`, `age_group`, `certification_status`, `lifecycle_status`, `supersedes_model_id` and `certified_against_standard_id`.

Certification of a model is a `ComplianceOfficer` act that resolves the single `ACTIVE` edition itself — the caller supplies no body. If no edition is `ACTIVE`, the attempt fails with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_STANDARD`. A model may be certified from `UNCERTIFIED` and re-certified from `DECERTIFIED`; attempting it on a model that is already `CERTIFIED`, or on one whose `lifecycle_status` has reached `DISCONTINUED`, fails with `ERR_MODEL_NOT_CERTIFIABLE`. The substantive check is dimensional: the model's `deck_height_cm` must not exceed the resolved edition's ceiling for the model's own `age_group` — `max_fall_height_cm_toddler` for `TODDLER_6_23M`, `max_fall_height_cm_preschool` for `PRESCHOOL_2_5`, `max_fall_height_cm_school_age` for `SCHOOL_AGE_5_12` — and a model that stands taller than its band permits is refused with `ERR_FALL_HEIGHT_EXCEEDS_STANDARD`. On success the model's `certification_status` becomes `CERTIFIED`, `certified_against_standard_id` is set to the resolved edition, and `version` is incremented by one. Re-certification after a supersession closes the `DECERTIFIED → CERTIFIED` half of the model's certification cycle, and its new pin points at the new edition.

A model's commercial availability is a second, independent dimension. A `ManufacturerAdmin` moves it along by supplying a `lifecycle_status` value: `ACTIVE → LEGACY` marks the model as an older-system part, after which every replacement-part sale of it prices in an eighteen-percent surcharge (§3.9); `LEGACY → DISCONTINUED` retires it from new designs altogether, and a placement attempt against a discontinued model is refused with `ERR_MODEL_DISCONTINUED`. `DISCONTINUED` is terminal, and a lifecycle move that no transition declares — jumping from `ACTIVE` straight to `DISCONTINUED`, or moving a model that is already `DISCONTINUED` — is rejected as an inadmissible transition with HTTP 409. Certification status and lifecycle status never affect one another: a `LEGACY` model can be perfectly `CERTIFIED`, which is exactly what makes compliance retrofits to legacy parts possible.

### 3.2. Opening a Consultation: Site Constraints, Age Bands and Play Areas

A `DesignConsultant` opens the engagement by creating a project for the commissioning `client_name`, a string of 1 to 120 characters naming the school, municipality or homeowners' association footing the bill. The site is described by two extents, both greater than zero and both no larger than 100000 centimetres: `site_length_cm` along the layout axis, inside which every play area must fit, and `site_width_cm` perpendicular to it, which is recorded for the record but never enters a geometric check in this specification. Subgrade classification arrives as `site_soil_type`, one of `SANDY_LOAM`, `GRAVEL` or `CLAY`, and clay subgrade carries a surfacing consequence described in §3.3. Commercial and calendar boundaries arrive as `budget_cents`, a hard ceiling greater than zero that a contractual quote may not breach, and `fundraising_deadline`, a `YYYY-MM-DD` date after which pledges may no longer be collected. The `required_age_groups` array names between 1 and 3 distinct age bands drawn from `TODDLER_6_23M`, `PRESCHOOL_2_5` and `SCHOOL_AGE_5_12` that the finished playground must serve. The optional boolean `accessible_route_required` records whether the client's contract independently mandates an accessible transfer point in every play area and defaults to true when omitted.

The created project starts with `design_status` `DRAFT` and `compliance_status` `NOT_ASSESSED`, and it stores the acting consultant's `X-User-ID` in `consultant_user_id` — the value the segregation-of-duties check in §3.7 later compares against. Its counters start at zero: `revision_rounds` is 0, `funds_collected_cents` is 0, and all four allocation buckets — `alloc_permit_freight_cents`, `alloc_surfacing_cents`, `alloc_structure_cents` and `alloc_contingency_cents` — are 0. Its `installed_on` is null and stays null until the day the installation passes inspection. Project listings can be narrowed by `design_status` and by `compliance_status`, and a single project's representation exposes `funds_collected_cents` alongside those four buckets.

Two state dimensions run on a project and they are genuinely independent. `design_status` walks the design lifecycle through `DRAFT`, `CONSULTATION`, `RENDERED`, `COMMUNITY_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `DESIGN_APPROVED`, `CERTIFIED`, `ORDERED`, `INSTALLED`, `AUDITED` and `CANCELLED`, of which `AUDITED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. `compliance_status` tracks regulatory posture through `NOT_ASSESSED`, `COMPLIANT`, `RETROFIT_REQUIRED` and `NON_COMPLIANT`, and it advances on its own triggers. Because they are independent, combinations that look contradictory are legal and expected: a project can sit at `design_status` `DESIGN_APPROVED` while its `compliance_status` reads `RETROFIT_REQUIRED`, which is precisely the state hop three of a supersession leaves behind.

Starting the consultation moves the project `DRAFT → CONSULTATION` and is what makes design content editable at all. It requires exactly one edition to be in `ACTIVE` status; with no `ACTIVE` edition the attempt is refused with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_STANDARD`. On success `design_status` becomes `CONSULTATION` and the project's `version` is incremented by one. Firing the same trigger a second time, or firing it from any status other than `DRAFT`, is rejected with `ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE`.

**Design content is editable only in two statuses.** Creating a play area, creating a placement, validating a placement and removing a placement are all permitted only while the owning project's `design_status` is `CONSULTATION` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`; in any other status they are refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_EDITABLE`. That second permitted status is what lets a project reopened by a below-threshold community review, or by hop three of a supersession, actually be repaired.

Inside an editable project, a consultant carves out play areas. Each area serves a single `age_group` from the same three bands, and a project may hold at most one area per band — the combination of owning project and `age_group` is unique, and the area's `project_id` is taken from the request path rather than the body. The area occupies a **half-open** interval on the site axis: `axis_origin_cm`, which must be zero or more, is inside the interval, and the interval runs for `axis_length_cm` centimetres, which must be greater than zero, with the coordinate at origin plus length lying outside it. The sum of `axis_origin_cm` and `axis_length_cm` must not exceed the project's `site_length_cm`. Protective surfacing is specified per area by `surfacing_material`, either `ENGINEERED_WOOD_FIBER` or `POURED_RUBBER`, and by `surfacing_depth_cm`, the depth the design commits to, between 0 and 120 centimetres inclusive. A newly created area is `PLANNED`, with `critical_fall_height_cm` at 0 and `required_surfacing_depth_cm` at the absolute floor of 15 centimetres; creating it increments the owning project's `version` by one. Area listings under a project may be filtered by `age_group`.

An area's own lifecycle has three states. `PLANNED` becomes `ZONED` automatically the moment the area's first placement reaches `VALIDATED`, and `ZONED` becomes `LOCKED` automatically when the owning project is ordered. `LOCKED` is terminal: a locked area accepts no further placement creation, validation or removal, and such attempts are refused with `ERR_PLAY_AREA_LOCKED`. A single area's representation exposes both derived numbers, `critical_fall_height_cm` and `required_surfacing_depth_cm`, so a consultant can see the surfacing obligation the design has accumulated.

### 3.3. Placing Components: Use Zones, the Attachment Graph and Validation

Placing a catalogue component inside a play area is the atom of design work. The consultant names the `component_model_id` being placed, picks a `color_choice`, anchors it at `x_start_cm` — a coordinate on the site axis that must be zero or more — and states a `quantity` of identical units between 1 and 50 inclusive. Where the component bolts onto another, `attached_to_placement_id` names the parent placement; free-standing components omit it and it is stored as null. The `color_choice` must be a member of the model's `color_options`; any value other than the first element of that array incurs the colour uplift when the design is priced. A placement's `play_area_id` is taken from the request path, never from the body, and the placement is born in `DRAFT`.

Creation is gated by a run of checks, all of which read the single `ACTIVE` edition — if none is `ACTIVE`, the attempt fails with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_STANDARD`. The model must be `CERTIFIED`, or the placement is refused with `ERR_MODEL_NOT_CERTIFIED`; it must not have reached `DISCONTINUED`, or the placement is refused with `ERR_MODEL_DISCONTINUED`. The model's `age_group` must equal the owning area's `age_group`, otherwise `ERR_AGE_GROUP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) — a preschool slide cannot be dropped into a toddler area. The model's `deck_height_cm` must not exceed the `ACTIVE` edition's ceiling for the area's age band, otherwise `ERR_FALL_HEIGHT_EXCEEDS_STANDARD` (HTTP 422).

**Use-zone geometry** is derived, never supplied. For the placement being written, `use_zone_start_cm` is the anchor coordinate `x_start_cm` less the model's `use_zone_footprint_cm`. `use_zone_end_cm` is the anchor coordinate plus the model's `use_zone_footprint_cm`, plus a further 122 centimetres when and only when the model's `category` is `PLASTIC_SLIDE` — a slide needs clear run-out past its exit that a post or a deck does not. The resulting interval from `use_zone_start_cm` to `use_zone_end_cm` is **closed at both ends**. That whole interval must lie inside the owning area's half-open axis interval, meaning the start must be at or after `axis_origin_cm` and the end must be strictly before `axis_origin_cm` plus `axis_length_cm`; a zone that pokes outside its area is refused with `ERR_USE_ZONE_OUT_OF_BOUNDS` (HTTP 422).

Attachment direction follows the catalogue, and violating it yields `ERR_INVALID_ATTACHMENT_TARGET` (HTTP 422). A `PLASTIC_SLIDE` and a `METAL_CLIMBER` may attach only to a placement whose model `category` is `DECK`. A `DECK` may attach only to a `POST`. A `POST` and a `HARDWARE_KIT` attach to nothing at all, so supplying a parent for either of them is a violation. Because decks hang off posts and slides hang off decks, the placements of a project form a directed acyclic graph through `attached_to_placement_id`, and the attachment parent must be a placement in the same play area.

Validating a `DRAFT` placement is the consultant's assertion that it is buildable, and it is where the expensive geometry runs. The placement itself must be in `DRAFT`; validating one that is already `VALIDATED`, or one that has been demoted to `NEEDS_RECERTIFICATION`, is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. The project must be editable and the area must not be `LOCKED`. The model must still be `CERTIFIED`, or the attempt fails with `ERR_MODEL_NOT_CERTIFIED` — a model decertified by a supersession blocks validation even though the placement was created while it was fine. If the placement declares an attachment parent, that parent must itself already be `VALIDATED`, otherwise `ERR_PARENT_PLACEMENT_NOT_VALIDATED`; the practical consequence is that a structure must be validated bottom-up, posts before decks before slides.

Two spatial checks then run, and both are deliberately restricted to placements already in `VALIDATED` status — `DRAFT` and `NEEDS_RECERTIFICATION` placements are invisible to them, which is why **the order in which a consultant validates placements changes the outcome**. The first is overlap inside the area: two closed use-zone intervals overlap when the first's start is at or before the second's end *and* the second's start is at or before the first's end. Two zones that merely touch at a single coordinate — where one interval's end equals the other's start — therefore *do* overlap, while a one-centimetre gap between them does not. Any overlap with another `VALIDATED` placement in the same area is refused with `ERR_USE_ZONE_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409), with exactly one exemption: a pair is exempt when one of the two is the other's attachment parent, because a slide bolted to its deck must by construction overlap that deck's zone. The second check reaches across areas: for every `VALIDATED` placement sitting in a *different* play area of the same project — therefore serving a different age band — the two zones must be separated by at least the `ACTIVE` edition's `min_use_zone_clearance_cm` clear centimetres. Clear separation is counted as the number of whole centimetres strictly between the two intervals: take the later interval's start, subtract the earlier interval's end, subtract one more, and the result must be greater than or equal to `min_use_zone_clearance_cm`. Falling short is refused with `ERR_AGE_GROUP_SEPARATION_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409).

A successful validation sets the placement's `status` to `VALIDATED`, promotes the owning area from `PLANNED` to `ZONED` if it was still `PLANNED`, recomputes the area's `critical_fall_height_cm` and `required_surfacing_depth_cm`, and increments both the placement's and the area's `version` by one.

**The two derived numbers on a play area** deserve their own statement, because five different operations read them. `critical_fall_height_cm` is the largest `deck_height_cm` among the models of the area's placements whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `VALIDATED`; when that set is empty the value is 0. If that maximum ever exceeds 305 centimetres, the operation that would have produced it is refused with `ERR_FALL_HEIGHT_EXCEEDS_STANDARD`. `required_surfacing_depth_cm` is then derived from the critical fall height, the area's surfacing product and two independent uplifts. Start with a base attenuation depth. When the critical fall height is 0 there is nothing to fall from and the base is 0. Otherwise, for `ENGINEERED_WOOD_FIBER` the base is 23 centimetres when the critical fall height is at most 150 centimetres, 30 centimetres when it is at most 200, and 38 centimetres above that; for `POURED_RUBBER` the base is 5 centimetres at or below 150, 8 centimetres at or below 200, and 13 centimetres above that. Two uplifts then apply, and **they are not additive**: each is applied to the base on its own to form a candidate, and the requirement is the largest candidate. The first candidate is the base plus 5 centimetres when the area's `age_group` is `TODDLER_6_23M`, and the base unchanged otherwise. The second candidate is the base plus 3 centimetres when the owning project's `site_soil_type` is `CLAY`, and the base unchanged otherwise. `required_surfacing_depth_cm` is the greatest of those two candidates and the absolute minimum of 15 centimetres — which is why an area with no placements at all still reads 15. All terms are whole centimetres and nothing is rounded. Both numbers are recomputed on every placement creation, every validation, every removal and every retrofit under the area.

Removing a placement is available to a `DesignConsultant` while the project is editable and the area is not `LOCKED`, and it returns HTTP 204 with no body. Removal deletes the placement, clears `attached_to_placement_id` on any child placement that pointed at it — the children survive as free-standing placements rather than being deleted — recomputes the area's two derived numbers, and increments the area's `version` by one.

A placement's `status` takes one of three values, `DRAFT`, `VALIDATED` and `NEEDS_RECERTIFICATION`, and its representation also carries the pricing of the most recent replacement-part or retrofit purchase made against it: `retrofit_model_id`, `retrofit_quantity`, `retrofit_unit_price_cents`, `retrofit_handling_cents`, `retrofit_total_cents` and the boolean `legacy_surcharge_applied`, all of which start at null, zero, zero, zero, zero and false respectively and are explained in §3.9. Placement listings across the catalogue may be filtered by `play_area_id`, `component_model_id`, `attached_to_placement_id`, `retrofit_model_id` and `status`; listings scoped to one area may be filtered by `status`.

### 3.4. Renderings, Community Input and Revision Rounds

Issuing a design rendering is how a consultant freezes a design for the community to look at. The request carries a single field, `pinned_standard_id`, and it must name the edition that is currently `ACTIVE`; pinning anything else — a `DRAFT` edition, or one already `SUPERSEDED` — is refused with `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_ACTIVE`, and issuing while no edition is `ACTIVE` at all is refused with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_STANDARD`. The project must be in `CONSULTATION` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`; from any other `design_status` the attempt fails with `ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE`.

Three completeness gates then run in this order. Every age band listed in the project's `required_age_groups` must have a play area of that `age_group`, or the request is refused with `ERR_AGE_GROUP_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE`. Every play area of the project — including any extra area beyond the required bands — must hold at least one placement whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `VALIDATED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PLAY_AREA_EMPTY`. And accessibility: when either the project's `accessible_route_required` is true **or** the `ACTIVE` edition's `requires_accessible_route` is true, every play area must hold at least one placement in `DRAFT` or `VALIDATED` whose model has `accessible_transfer_ready` set to true; a shortfall is refused with `ERR_ACCESSIBLE_ROUTE_MISSING` (HTTP 422). Note the disjunction: a client contract that waives the accessible route cannot override an edition that mandates one.

A successful issuance creates a rendering in `ISSUED` status whose `round_number` is one greater than the highest `round_number` any previous rendering of the project holds, or 1 when this is the project's first. It freezes `use_zone_snapshot`, a map keyed by each play area's identifier whose value records that area's `critical_fall_height_cm`, its `required_surfacing_depth_cm`, its specified `surfacing_depth_cm` and its `placement_count` as they stood at issue time. It freezes `pinned_standard_id`, and that pin is never rewritten afterwards even when the pinned edition is later superseded. Any other rendering of the same project still in `ISSUED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`. The project's `design_status` becomes `RENDERED` and its `version` is incremented by one. Renderings are returned with HTTP 201 and their vote tallies start at zero: `yes_votes` 0, `no_votes` 0 and `community_approval_pct` 0.0. Rendering listings may be filtered by `project_id`, `pinned_standard_id` and `status`; a rendering's own representation exposes the frozen `use_zone_snapshot` alongside the tallies.

Community input arrives from a `ClientRepresentative` as batches rather than individual ballots. Each batch supplies `yes_votes` and `no_votes`, and the values are **added to** the rendering's running `yes_votes` and `no_votes` rather than replacing them. The rendering must be in `ISSUED` status, or the batch is refused with `ERR_RENDERING_NOT_ISSUED`, and the owning project must be in `RENDERED` or `COMMUNITY_REVIEW`. After the batch is absorbed, `community_approval_pct` is re-derived: with no votes at all it is 0.0; otherwise it is one hundred multiplied by the accumulated `yes_votes`, divided by the sum of `yes_votes` and `no_votes`, rounded half up to exactly one decimal place. Recording a batch also sets the project's `design_status` to `COMMUNITY_REVIEW`, which is idempotent when the project is already there, and increments both the rendering's and the project's `version` by one.

Closing the review is a `DesignConsultant` act, and it is a **branch, not a pass/fail gate**. The rendering must still be `ISSUED` (otherwise `ERR_RENDERING_NOT_ISSUED`) and the project must be in `COMMUNITY_REVIEW`. Turnout is checked first: the sum of `yes_votes` and `no_votes` must be at least 25, and a review closed on thinner participation is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_COMMUNITY_INPUT` (HTTP 422). Once turnout clears, `community_approval_pct` selects the branch and never itself raises an error, and the comparison is **inclusive** — exactly 60.0 percent approves.

The two branches have deliberately unequal consequences, and both must be implemented in full. On the **approve branch**, where `community_approval_pct` is greater than or equal to 60.0, the project's `design_status` moves `COMMUNITY_REVIEW → DESIGN_APPROVED` and the rendering is left in `ISSUED` status precisely so that it can go on to back a quote; `revision_rounds` is not touched, no quote is expired, and the rendering is not withdrawn. On the **revise branch**, where `community_approval_pct` is below 60.0, three further things happen beyond the status move: the rendering's `status` becomes `WITHDRAWN`, the project's `design_status` becomes `REVISION_REQUESTED` and its `revision_rounds` counter is incremented by one, and every `Quote` of the project still in `ISSUED` status is moved to `EXPIRED`. Either way, every row actually touched has its `version` incremented by one, and the operation's response body is the project rather than the rendering.

Because a project in `REVISION_REQUESTED` is editable again and may have a new rendering issued from it, the cycle `RENDERED → COMMUNITY_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED → RENDERED` may run as many times as the community demands, each pass minting a rendering whose `round_number` is one higher than the last and incrementing `revision_rounds` once per completed round that ended in revision. A rendering's own lifecycle has two terminal outcomes, `SUPERSEDED` when a newer rendering displaces it or a supersession cascade retires it, and `WITHDRAWN` when a review closes below threshold.

### 3.5. Quoting: Two Pricing Variants, Budget Discipline and Acceptance

A quote is a `DesignConsultant`'s priced offer against a specific rendering, and it is computed once at issue time and frozen thereafter. The request identifies the `project_id` being quoted and the `rendering_id` being priced, selects a `variant` of either `PROVISIONAL` or `FIRM`, sets `valid_until` — the last `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar day on which the quote may still be accepted — and carries the `expected_project_version` described in §2.8. The named rendering must belong to the named project, or the request is refused with `ERR_RENDERING_PROJECT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422), and it must be in `ISSUED` status, or refused with `ERR_RENDERING_NOT_ISSUED`.

Pricing proceeds in six steps, and the first and third of them depend on the variant.

**Step one, the input set.** Gather every `ComponentPlacement` whose owning play area belongs to the quoted project and whose `status` is included by the variant, ordered by `created_at` ascending — that ordering is the only sanctioned one and identifier ordering must never be substituted for it. A `PROVISIONAL` quote includes placements in `DRAFT` *and* `VALIDATED`, because an early budgetary number is meant to price a design that is still being validated. A `FIRM` quote includes only placements in `VALIDATED`. Placements demoted to `NEEDS_RECERTIFICATION` are priced by neither variant. If the resulting set is empty there is nothing to price, and the request is refused with `ERR_QUOTE_EMPTY_SCOPE` (HTTP 422).

**Step two, the per-line raw amount.** For each placement in that ascending order, take its model's `unit_price_cents`, multiply by the placement's `quantity`, and call the product the base line. When the placement's `color_choice` differs from the first element of the model's `color_options`, the raw line is the base line increased by seven percent — that is, the base line multiplied by one plus seven hundredths. When the colour choice is the default first element, the raw line is the base line unchanged. These raw amounts are carried exactly, with no rounding, into step three.

**Step three, the subtotal — and this is the only place the two variants differ arithmetically.** For a `PROVISIONAL` quote, each raw line is rounded half up to whole cents first, and `subtotal_cents` is the sum of those rounded lines. For a `FIRM` quote, the exact unrounded raw lines are summed first and the single total is rounded half up to whole cents once, at the end. On identical placement data the two orders can differ by as much as one cent for every priced line beyond the first: three lines whose exact values each end in half a cent give the `PROVISIONAL` order a subtotal one cent above the `FIRM` order's. Both behaviours are required; neither is an approximation of the other.

**Step four, freight.** `priced_unit_count` is the sum of `quantity` over the placements the variant included. `freight_cents` is a flat base of 48000 cents plus 1250 cents for every priced unit.

**Step five, the volume discount.** The tier is selected on `subtotal_cents` **alone** — never on the subtotal plus freight — and freight itself is never discounted. When `subtotal_cents` is at least 5000000 cents the rate is six percent; otherwise, when it is at least 2000000 cents the rate is three percent; otherwise the rate is zero. `discount_cents` is `subtotal_cents` multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents.

**Step six.** `total_cents` is `subtotal_cents` plus `freight_cents` less `discount_cents`.

A `FIRM` quote carries three additional obligations that a `PROVISIONAL` quote does not, because a firm quote is the contractual instrument a manufacturing order may be raised against. Every play area of the project must have a specified `surfacing_depth_cm` greater than or equal to its `required_surfacing_depth_cm`, or the issuance is refused with `ERR_SURFACING_DEPTH_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). The accessible-route provision of §3.4 is re-checked: with either the project's `accessible_route_required` or the `ACTIVE` edition's `requires_accessible_route` true, every play area must hold an accessible-transfer-ready placement, or the issuance is refused with `ERR_ACCESSIBLE_ROUTE_MISSING`. And the resulting `total_cents` must not exceed the project's `budget_cents`, or the issuance is refused with `ERR_QUOTE_EXCEEDS_BUDGET` (HTTP 422) — fiscal checks of this kind run only after every safety and constraint check has already passed.

Issuing a quote creates it in `ISSUED` status with `subtotal_cents`, `freight_cents`, `discount_cents`, `total_cents` and `priced_unit_count` all frozen, moves every other `Quote` of the project still in `ISSUED` to `EXPIRED`, and increments the project's `version` by one. That version bump is exactly why the stale-version guard of §2.8 exists on this operation. Quotes are returned with HTTP 201, and quote listings may be filtered by `project_id`, `rendering_id`, `status` and `variant`.

Acceptance belongs to the `ClientRepresentative`. The quote must be in `ISSUED` status; its `variant` must be `FIRM`, since a budgetary number is not contractually acceptable and attempting it is refused with `ERR_PROVISIONAL_QUOTE_NOT_ACCEPTABLE`; the logical clock's date must be on or before `valid_until`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`; and the rendering backing the quote must still be in `ISSUED` status, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_RENDERING_NOT_ISSUED`. On success the quote's `status` becomes `ACCEPTED`, every other quote of the same project still in `ISSUED` becomes `EXPIRED`, and the `version` of every quote touched — the accepted one and each expired sibling — is incremented by one. `ACCEPTED` and `EXPIRED` are both terminal, so a quote is never revived; a project that needs a different number needs a new quote.

Quotes expire from `ISSUED` for five distinct reasons, and all five must be implemented: a newer quote is issued for the same project, a sibling quote is accepted, a community review closes below threshold, the project is cancelled, or hop four of a standard supersession fires. Each of those expirations increments the expired quote's `version` by one.

### 3.6. Community Fundraising and the Allocation Waterfall

Community playgrounds are paid for by pledge drives, and the API models the drive explicitly so that ordering can be gated on money actually in hand. A pledge is recorded against a project by either a `ClientRepresentative` or a `DesignConsultant`, carrying `amount_cents` greater than zero and `pledged_at`, the `YYYY-MM-DD` date on which the pledge was made, which must not be later than the logical clock's current date. The pledge's `project_id` comes from the request path. A project accepts pledges only while its `design_status` is outside `CANCELLED`, `INSTALLED` and `AUDITED`; in any of those three it is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FUNDABLE`. A recorded pledge is born `PLEDGED` and returned with HTTP 201, and — importantly — it contributes nothing to the project's money until it is collected. Pledge listings under a project may be filtered by `status`.

Collecting a pledge is a `ClientRepresentative` act. The pledge must be in `PLEDGED` status, and collecting one that is not is refused with `ERR_PLEDGE_INVALID_STATE`. The logical clock's date must be on or before the project's `fundraising_deadline`, or the collection is refused with `ERR_FUNDRAISING_DEADLINE_PASSED`. The owning project's `design_status` must not be `CANCELLED` or `AUDITED`, or the collection is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FUNDABLE`. On success the pledge's `status` becomes `COLLECTED`, the project's `funds_collected_cents` and all four allocation buckets are recomputed from scratch, and both the pledge's and the project's `version` are incremented by one.

**The allocation waterfall** is recomputed in full on every collection and every refund, never patched incrementally. Begin by summing `amount_cents` over every pledge of the project whose `status` is `COLLECTED`; that sum is `funds_collected_cents`. Then find the project's most recently created `Quote` whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `ACCEPTED` — order the candidates by `created_at` ascending and take the last one. When no such quote exists, all three targeted buckets are zero and `alloc_contingency_cents` absorbs the entire collected amount. When one does exist, it supplies three targets in strict fill order. The first target, for permits and freight, is the quote's `freight_cents`. The second target, for surfacing, is the quote's `subtotal_cents` multiplied by twenty percent, rounded half up to whole cents. The third target, for structure, is the quote's `subtotal_cents` less that surfacing target — defined by subtraction precisely so that the surfacing and structure targets sum to `subtotal_cents` exactly, with no rounding remainder left stranded. Contingency has no target and absorbs whatever survives the first three.

Fill the buckets in that order. `alloc_permit_freight_cents` receives the smaller of the remaining funds and the first target, and that amount leaves the remaining funds. `alloc_surfacing_cents` receives the smaller of what now remains and the second target, and that amount leaves the remainder. `alloc_structure_cents` receives the smaller of what now remains and the third target, and that amount leaves the remainder. `alloc_contingency_cents` receives every cent still remaining. The invariant that must hold after every collection and every refund is that the four buckets sum to exactly `funds_collected_cents` — no cent is created and none is lost.

Refunding is the compensating action, available to a `ClientRepresentative` on a pledge whose `status` is `PLEDGED` or `COLLECTED`; attempting it on one already `REFUNDED` is refused with `ERR_PLEDGE_INVALID_STATE`. Money that has already been committed to production cannot be clawed back: once the project's `design_status` has reached `ORDERED`, `INSTALLED` or `AUDITED`, a refund is refused with `ERR_FUNDS_ALREADY_COMMITTED`. A successful refund sets the pledge's `status` to `REFUNDED` and recomputes `funds_collected_cents` and the four buckets from scratch. Because the recompute is total rather than incremental, refunding a collected pledge restores the buckets to exactly their pre-collection values — the buckets drain in reverse fill order purely as a consequence of the recompute, with no drift. Refunding a pledge that was still merely `PLEDGED` leaves `funds_collected_cents` untouched, because such a pledge had never entered it; in that case only the pledge's own `version` is incremented, whereas refunding a `COLLECTED` pledge increments both the pledge's and the project's `version` by one. `REFUNDED` is terminal.

### 3.7. Compliance Certification, Ordering, and the Production Floor

Compliance certification is the checkpoint between a design the community likes and a design the business is willing to build. Only a `ComplianceOfficer` may perform it, and the officer must not be the project's own consultant: the acting `X-User-ID` is compared against the project's stored `consultant_user_id`, and a match is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The project must be in `design_status` `DESIGN_APPROVED`; from anywhere else the attempt is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE`. Four substantive gates then run. The rendering of the project still in `ISSUED` status must have a `pinned_standard_id` whose edition is *still* `ACTIVE` — if a supersession has moved on since the rendering was drawn, certification is refused with `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_ACTIVE`, which is the mechanism that catches the `DESIGN_APPROVED` project hop three deliberately spared. Every `ComponentPlacement` of the project must be in `VALIDATED` status; one left in `DRAFT`, or demoted to `NEEDS_RECERTIFICATION`, is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENTS_NOT_VALIDATED`. Every play area's specified `surfacing_depth_cm` must be greater than or equal to its `required_surfacing_depth_cm`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_SURFACING_DEPTH_INSUFFICIENT`. And the accessible-route provision is checked once more: with either the project's `accessible_route_required` or the `ACTIVE` edition's `requires_accessible_route` true, each play area must hold at least one placement whose model is `accessible_transfer_ready`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_ACCESSIBLE_ROUTE_MISSING`.

A successful certification advances both project dimensions at once: `design_status` moves `DESIGN_APPROVED → CERTIFIED` and `compliance_status` moves to `COMPLIANT`, and the project's `version` is incremented by one. The `compliance_status` axis is entered from either `NOT_ASSESSED` or `RETROFIT_REQUIRED`, so a project whose compliance posture was spoiled by a supersession and then repaired is certifiable again. For completeness, that axis has five moves in total and only the first is client-driven: `NOT_ASSESSED → COMPLIANT` on certification; `COMPLIANT → RETROFIT_REQUIRED` when hop three of a supersession demotes at least one of the project's placements; `RETROFIT_REQUIRED → COMPLIANT` when a retrofit clears the project's last `NEEDS_RECERTIFICATION` placement; `COMPLIANT → NON_COMPLIANT` when a post-installation audit fails; and `NON_COMPLIANT → COMPLIANT` when a subsequent post-installation audit passes.

Raising the manufacturing order is the consultant's final design act. The request names a single `quote_id`. The project must be in `CERTIFIED` status; the named quote must belong to the project, be in `ACCEPTED` status and have `variant` `FIRM`, or the request is refused with `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_ACCEPTED`; every placement that quote priced must still be `VALIDATED`, with any demotion refused as `ERR_PLACEMENTS_NOT_VALIDATED`; and the money must actually be in hand — the project's `funds_collected_cents` must be greater than or equal to the quote's `total_cents`, and a shortfall is refused with `ERR_FUNDING_SHORTFALL`, the one HTTP 402 in this API. On success a `ManufacturingOrder` is created with HTTP 201 in `production_status` `CREATED`, `kitting_status` `PENDING` and `bagging_status` `PENDING`, with `kit_line_count`, `hardware_bag_total` and `instruction_package_revision` all at 0. The project's `design_status` becomes `ORDERED`, and every play area of the project is driven `ZONED → LOCKED`, after which placements can no longer be created, validated or removed there. The project's `version` and each locked area's `version` are incremented by one.

The plant floor runs on three parallel state dimensions of the same order row, and `ManufacturerAdmin` drives all of them. `production_status` is the primary axis and walks `CREATED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, `READY_TO_SHIP`, `SHIPPED` and `CANCELLED`, of which `SHIPPED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. Starting production moves the order `CREATED → IN_PRODUCTION` and additionally requires that the backing quote's project is still in `design_status` `ORDERED`; firing it from any other `production_status` is refused with `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`. **That transition is the fork**: only once the order is `IN_PRODUCTION` do the two prep dimensions become writable, and advancing either of them while `production_status` is anything else is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_IN_PRODUCTION`.

The two prep dimensions are genuinely independent — either may be advanced first, in any interleaving, and advancing one never mutates the other. Kitting is driven by supplying a `kitting_status` value and walks `PENDING → IN_PROGRESS → DONE`, with a fast path straight from `PENDING → DONE` for an order where no partial kitting was ever recorded. Reaching `DONE` writes exactly one derived field and no other: `kit_line_count` becomes the count of distinct placement lines the backing quote priced. Hardware bagging is driven by supplying a `bagging_status` value over the same three states, with the same fast path, and reaching `DONE` writes exactly one derived field and no other: `hardware_bag_total` becomes the sum, over every `VALIDATED` placement under the ordered quote's project taken in `created_at` ascending order, of that placement's model's `hardware_bag_count` multiplied by the placement's `quantity`. Because an order is always raised from a `FIRM` quote, that `VALIDATED` set is precisely the set the quote priced. Whole bags only; nothing is rounded. The two branches thus touch disjoint fields — kitting never writes `hardware_bag_total`, bagging never writes `kit_line_count` — and each advance increments the order's `version` by one.

**The join** is a system transition rather than a client call: the moment the second of `kitting_status` and `bagging_status` reaches `DONE`, and only then, the order's `production_status` moves `IN_PRODUCTION → READY_TO_SHIP` and `instruction_package_revision` is set to the backing rendering's `round_number`. That field is written nowhere else — not on order creation, not by either prep branch on its own — so an order that ships against the third rendering of a much-revised design carries revision 3 in its installation instruction package. Whichever branch happens to complete second carries out the join inside the same request, so a caller sees `production_status` already `READY_TO_SHIP` in that response. Shipping then moves the order `READY_TO_SHIP → SHIPPED`, dispatching the kit together with its instruction package, and increments `version` by one. Order listings may be filtered by `quote_id`, `production_status`, `kitting_status` and `bagging_status`, and a single order's representation exposes both prep dimensions alongside `kit_line_count`, `hardware_bag_total` and `instruction_package_revision`.

### 3.8. The Installer Network, Installation, Inspection and the Post-Installation Audit

Admission to the certified installer network is a `ManufacturerAdmin` act. The company's `company_name` is 1 to 120 characters and must be globally unique. Its `certification_level` is one of `APPRENTICE`, `CERTIFIED` or `MASTER`. Its `certified_categories` array names between 1 and 5 distinct component categories drawn from `POST`, `DECK`, `PLASTIC_SLIDE`, `METAL_CLIMBER` and `HARDWARE_KIT` — the categories the company is qualified to erect. Its `crew_capacity_per_day` is the maximum number of crew members it can field on any single calendar day, between 1 and 200 inclusive. Its `certification_expires_on` is the last `YYYY-MM-DD` date on which its certification remains valid, and its `region_code` is exactly two uppercase letters. A newly admitted installer is `ACTIVE` with `committed_crew_days` at 0 and `open_audit_failures` at 0, and is returned with HTTP 201. Installer listings may be filtered by `status`, `region_code` and `certification_level`, and a single installer's representation exposes both `committed_crew_days` and `open_audit_failures`.

**The crew-day ledger** is derived, never asserted. Each job contributes `booked_crew_days`, which is its `crew_size` multiplied by the inclusive day count of its booking window — the number of calendar days from `scheduled_start_date` to `scheduled_end_date` counting both ends, so a window whose two dates are equal counts as one day. An installer's `committed_crew_days` is the sum of `booked_crew_days` over every job booked with that installer whose `status` is **not** one of `AUDIT_PASSED`, `AUDIT_FAILED` or `CANCELLED`; a finished or cancelled job no longer occupies the calendar. That total is recomputed from scratch whenever a job is created, rescheduled, cancelled or started, whenever an inspection outcome changes a job's status, and on any change to the installer's own `status`. Because it is a full recompute, cancelling a job restores `committed_crew_days` to exactly the value it held before that job was created.

Booking a job is a `DesignConsultant` act. The request names the `order_id` whose kit is being installed and the `installer_id` doing the work, and sets `scheduled_start_date`, `scheduled_end_date` — which must not be earlier than the start date — and `crew_size`, an integer of at least 1 committed for every day of the window. An order may carry several phased jobs. Four admission checks then run against the installer's **other** jobs whose status is not one of the three excluded ones. First, the installer must be in `ACTIVE` status; booking a `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED` company is refused with `ERR_INSTALLER_NOT_ACTIVE`. Second, the installer's `certified_categories` must cover every component `category` appearing among the models of the placements the order's quote priced; a gap is refused with `ERR_INSTALLER_CATEGORY_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 409). Third, the requested closed date interval must not intersect any of those other bookings, **and** it must leave one fully clear calendar day of travel buffer between consecutive bookings: the new window's first day must fall on or after the earlier booking's last day plus one day plus the one-day buffer — so a booking that ends on the tenth of a month admits a next booking starting no earlier than the twelfth — and symmetrically, the new window's last day must sit at least that same clear day before any later booking's first day. Either an intersection or an insufficient gap is refused with `ERR_INSTALLER_BOOKING_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Fourth, `crew_size` must not exceed the installer's `crew_capacity_per_day`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_CREW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). A successful booking creates the job with HTTP 201 in `SCHEDULED` status with `rework_cycles` at 0 and `last_failed_inspection_kind` null, writes its `booked_crew_days`, recomputes the installer's `committed_crew_days`, and increments the installer's `version` by one.

Rescheduling moves a `SCHEDULED` job to a new window. Besides the `expected_installer_version` of §2.8, it carries a new `scheduled_start_date` and `scheduled_end_date`, and optionally a new `crew_size`; when `crew_size` is omitted the existing crew size is retained. Firing it against a job in any other status is refused with `ERR_JOB_INVALID_STATE`. The same overlap, travel-buffer and crew-capacity checks re-run against the installer's other non-excluded jobs, with the same three error codes. On success the booking window and `booked_crew_days` are rewritten, `committed_crew_days` is recomputed, and both the job's and the installer's `version` are incremented by one. Cancelling a job is the exact compensating action for creating one: it is permitted while the job's `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, sets `status` to `CANCELLED`, recomputes `committed_crew_days` back to its pre-booking value, and increments both the job's and the installer's `version` by one. Job listings may be filtered by `order_id`, `installer_id` and `status`.

Membership status is its own axis with three values, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` and `REVOKED`, the last being terminal, and the `ManufacturerAdmin` drives it by supplying a `status` value. **The two kinds of suspension differ, and the difference is deliberate.** An *administrative* suspension moves the installer `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and does **not** cancel any existing booking; it only blocks the creation of new jobs and blocks a `SCHEDULED` job from being started. A *compliance* suspension, driven by the audit-failure cascade described below, moves the installer to `SUSPENDED` and **does** cancel that installer's other `SCHEDULED` and `IN_PROGRESS` jobs. Reinstatement moves `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` but only while `open_audit_failures` is 0; while any audit failure remains open the reinstatement is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_FAILURES_OPEN`. Reinstatement does not resurrect jobs a prior compliance suspension cancelled. Revocation moves the installer to `REVOKED` from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, cancels every job of that installer whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, and then recomputes `committed_crew_days`; the installer's `version` and each cancelled job's `version` are each incremented by one. A status change that no transition declares — attempting to move a `REVOKED` installer back to `ACTIVE`, for instance — is refused with `ERR_INSTALLER_INVALID_STATE`.

On site, the `InstallerOperator` drives its own job and nobody else's; every one of the three operator-driven job triggers compares the acting `X-User-ID` against the job's `installer_id` and refuses a mismatch with `ERR_NOT_JOB_OWNER`. Starting the job moves it `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS` under three further conditions: the installer's own `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the start is refused with `ERR_INSTALLER_NOT_ACTIVE`; the installer's `certification_expires_on` must be on or after the logical clock's date, or the start is refused with `ERR_INSTALLER_CERTIFICATION_EXPIRED`; and the logical clock's date must be on or after the job's `scheduled_start_date`, since a crew cannot start early, with a premature attempt refused as `ERR_JOB_NOT_YET_STARTABLE`. Reporting the work finished moves the job `IN_PROGRESS → INSPECTION_PENDING` and hands it to the auditors.

Inspection records are filed by a `SafetyAuditor` and form an immutable append-only log per job. Each record states its `kind`, either `INSTALL_INSPECTION` or `POST_INSTALL_AUDIT`, and its `result`, either `PASS` or `FAIL`, along with three measurements: `deficiency_count`, the number of deficiencies noted, zero or more; `fall_height_measured_cm`, the highest designated play surface height actually measured on site, zero or more; and `surfacing_depth_measured_cm`, the shallowest protective surfacing depth actually measured on site, zero or more. The record's `job_id` comes from the request path, its `performed_by_user_id` is set from the acting `X-User-ID`, and its `sequence_number` is 1 for the job's first record and one greater than the highest existing sequence number thereafter. Records are returned with HTTP 201, and a job's log is listed in `sequence_number` order with optional filters on `kind` and `result`. Because these records are evidence rather than working state, they carry only `id` and `created_at` and are never updated. Of the three measurements, `surfacing_depth_measured_cm` is the one that gates: on an `INSTALL_INSPECTION` it must be greater than or equal to the largest `required_surfacing_depth_cm` across the project's play areas, and a shallower measurement is refused with `ERR_SURFACING_DEPTH_INSUFFICIENT`. `deficiency_count` and `fall_height_measured_cm` are recorded for the audit trail and gate nothing.

An `INSTALL_INSPECTION` may only be filed while the job is in `INSPECTION_PENDING`, and a `POST_INSTALL_AUDIT` only while it is in `AUDIT_PENDING`; filing in the wrong phase is refused with `ERR_JOB_INVALID_STATE`. Filing a post-installation audit additionally requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the job's recorded `install_inspector_user_id`, with a match refused as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The four outcomes have sharply different consequences, and each must be implemented in full.

An **installation inspection that passes** creates the record, sets the job's `install_inspector_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID` if it is still null, moves the job `INSPECTION_PENDING → AUDIT_PENDING`, and reaches across into the design: the owning project's `design_status` moves `ORDERED → INSTALLED` and its `installed_on` is set to the logical clock's current date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form — the date from which every warranty term is later measured. Both the job's and the project's `version` are incremented by one.

An **installation inspection that fails** creates the record, likewise sets `install_inspector_user_id` if it is still null — so the auditor who first touched the job is barred from the eventual audit whichever way the inspection went — moves the job `INSPECTION_PENDING → INSPECTION_FAILED`, and records `last_failed_inspection_kind` as `INSTALL_INSPECTION`. The job's `version` is incremented by one; the project is not touched.

A **post-installation audit that passes** creates the record, moves the job `AUDIT_PENDING → AUDIT_PASSED`, and advances the project on both axes at once: `design_status` becomes `AUDITED` and `compliance_status` becomes `COMPLIANT`. Because `AUDIT_PASSED` is one of the excluded statuses, the installer's `committed_crew_days` is recomputed — the job releases the calendar — and `open_audit_failures` is recomputed as well. The job's, the project's and the installer's `version` are each incremented by one. `AUDITED` is terminal for the project and `AUDIT_PASSED` terminal for the job.

A **post-installation audit that fails** fires a four-hop cascade. Hop one: the record is created and the job moves `AUDIT_PENDING → AUDIT_FAILED` with `last_failed_inspection_kind` set to `POST_INSTALL_AUDIT`. Hop two: because the job has failed its audit, the owning project's `compliance_status` becomes `NON_COMPLIANT` — note that its `design_status` is left alone, so a project can be `AUDITED`-bound yet non-compliant. Hop three: because that project is now non-compliant, the installer's `open_audit_failures` is incremented, and the suspension threshold is **one** — a single failed post-installation audit is enough — so the installer's `status` becomes `SUSPENDED` as a compliance suspension. Hop four: because the installer is now compliance-suspended, every *other* job of that installer whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` moves to `CANCELLED`, and `committed_crew_days` is then recomputed over what remains. Version counters are incremented by one on the job, the project, the installer and every cancelled sibling job. The installer's `open_audit_failures` is the count of its own jobs currently sitting in `AUDIT_FAILED`, which is what makes reinstatement contingent on those jobs being worked back out of that status.

The rework loop is how a failed job recovers, and it is driven by the `InstallerOperator` supplying an `action` of either `START` or `COMPLETE`. `START` is admissible while the job's `status` is `INSPECTION_FAILED` or `AUDIT_FAILED` and moves it to `REWORK_IN_PROGRESS`, but only while `rework_cycles` is strictly less than 2; at two completed cycles the loop is closed and a further attempt is refused with `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. `COMPLETE` is admissible only from `REWORK_IN_PROGRESS`, increments `rework_cycles` by one, and **returns the job to whichever check it failed**: when `last_failed_inspection_kind` is `INSTALL_INSPECTION` the job lands back in `INSPECTION_PENDING`, closing the cycle `INSPECTION_PENDING → INSPECTION_FAILED → REWORK_IN_PROGRESS → INSPECTION_PENDING`; when it is `POST_INSTALL_AUDIT` the same trigger lands the job in `AUDIT_PENDING` instead. Each rework transition increments the job's `version` by one.

### 3.9. Warranty, Replacement Parts, Compliance Retrofits and Cancellation

Warranty runs by component category, not by system, and a claim is filed by either a `ClientRepresentative` or a `ComplianceOfficer` once a playground is in the ground. The request names the `project_id` the failure occurred on, the `placement_id` that failed, the `quantity` of failed units claimed — at least 1 — and the `failure_date` on which the failure occurred. Three referential and temporal gates run. The named placement must belong to a play area of the named project, or the claim is refused with `ERR_WARRANTY_PLACEMENT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The project must actually have been installed, meaning its `installed_on` is non-null; a claim against an uninstalled project is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_INSTALLED` (HTTP 422). And `failure_date` must fall on or after the project's `installed_on` and on or before the logical clock's current date, with anything outside that window refused as `ERR_INVALID_FAILURE_DATE` (HTTP 422).

At claim time the claim freezes its own copy of the commercial facts so that later catalogue edits cannot rewrite history. `component_model_id` is copied from the placement's current `component_model_id` and frozen; `claim_category` is copied from that model's `category` and is what selects the coverage variant. `elapsed_years` is the whole number of years between installation and failure: take the number of days from the project's `installed_on` to the claim's `failure_date`, divide by 365, and discard any fraction, rounding down.

**Coverage then follows one of two variants, selected purely by category.** For a `POST`, a `DECK` or a `METAL_CLIMBER` — structural steel and lumber, which depreciate continuously — the variant is `PRORATED`. `covered_pct` is 0 when `elapsed_years` has reached or passed the model's `warranty_years`; otherwise it is one hundred multiplied by the model's `warranty_years` less `elapsed_years`, divided by that same `warranty_years`, rounded half up to a whole percent. Coverage therefore declines by one warranty-term fraction per elapsed year: a ten-year model claimed in elapsed year 3 covers 70 percent. For a `PLASTIC_SLIDE` or a `HARDWARE_KIT` — rotomoulded plastics and fasteners — the variant is `STEP`. Take half the model's `warranty_years`, rounded **up** to a whole number of years. `covered_pct` is 100 when `elapsed_years` is at or below that halfway point; otherwise it is 50 while `elapsed_years` is still strictly less than `warranty_years`; otherwise it is 0. Coverage is thus a step function rather than a ramp, and on identical inputs the two variants genuinely diverge: the same ten-year term at elapsed year 3 pays 70 percent under `PRORATED` and 100 percent under `STEP`. The chosen variant is recorded on the claim in `coverage_variant`, whose value is either `PRORATED` or `STEP`.

`reimbursement_cents` is then the model's `unit_price_cents` multiplied by the claim's `quantity` multiplied by `covered_pct`, divided by one hundred, rounded half up to whole cents. All four of `elapsed_years`, `coverage_variant`, `covered_pct` and `reimbursement_cents` are computed once and frozen. A claim whose `covered_pct` computes to 0 is still created and returned with HTTP 201 in `SUBMITTED` status — the record of an out-of-warranty failure has value in its own right. Claim listings may be filtered by `project_id`, `placement_id`, `component_model_id` and `status`.

Deciding a claim belongs to the `ComplianceOfficer`, who supplies a `decision` of either `APPROVED` or `DENIED`. The claim must be in `SUBMITTED` status; deciding one already decided is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`. Approval is refused when coverage has decayed away entirely — a `covered_pct` of 0 cannot be approved and yields `ERR_WARRANTY_EXPIRED` — whereas denial is always available regardless of coverage. Either decision sets `status` accordingly and increments the claim's `version` by one; `APPROVED` and `DENIED` are both terminal.

**Buying a replacement part and performing a compliance retrofit are the same operation**, available to a `ClientRepresentative` or a `DesignConsultant`, and it names a `replacement_model_id` and a `quantity`. Two eligibility gates run before any pricing. The logical clock's current year must be no later than the replacement model's `parts_support_until_year`, since the plant stops sourcing parts for a family after that year, and a late purchase is refused with `ERR_PARTS_NO_LONGER_AVAILABLE`. The replacement must be either the placement's own current model or a model whose `supersedes_model_id` points at the placement's current model; anything else is refused with `ERR_RETROFIT_MODEL_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). When the purchase is being used to repair a placement in `NEEDS_RECERTIFICATION`, the stricter form applies: the replacement must genuinely supersede the placement's model, must itself be in `certification_status` `CERTIFIED` — an uncertified replacement is refused with `ERR_MODEL_NOT_CERTIFIED` — and its `age_group` must still match the owning area's band, with a mismatch refused as `ERR_AGE_GROUP_MISMATCH` and a `deck_height_cm` above the `ACTIVE` edition's ceiling for that band refused as `ERR_FALL_HEIGHT_EXCEEDS_STANDARD`.

Pricing the purchase has three steps. First the surcharge: when the replacement model's `lifecycle_status` is `LEGACY`, `retrofit_unit_price_cents` is the model's `unit_price_cents` plus eighteen percent of that `unit_price_cents` rounded half up to whole cents, and `legacy_surcharge_applied` is set true; otherwise `retrofit_unit_price_cents` is simply the model's `unit_price_cents` and `legacy_surcharge_applied` is false. Second the line: the line amount is `retrofit_unit_price_cents` multiplied by the requested `quantity`. Third the handling waiver, and its evaluation point is the subtle part — **the waiver is decided on the line amount, before handling is added, and never on the resulting total.** When the line amount is greater than or equal to 250000 cents, `retrofit_handling_cents` is 0; otherwise it is a flat 3500 cents. A line of exactly 250000 cents therefore waives handling, while a line of 247000 cents attracts the 3500-cent charge and totals 250500 cents — above the threshold, yet handling still applies, because the decision was made on 247000. `retrofit_total_cents` is the line amount plus `retrofit_handling_cents`. The purchase also records `retrofit_model_id` as the replacement model and `retrofit_quantity` as the requested quantity.

Where the placement was in `NEEDS_RECERTIFICATION` and the replacement supersedes its model, the placement's `component_model_id` is rewritten to the replacement and its `status` moves `NEEDS_RECERTIFICATION → VALIDATED`. The owning area's `critical_fall_height_cm` and `required_surfacing_depth_cm` are recomputed in every case, since the model behind the placement may have changed height. And there is a cascade upward: once the owning project holds no remaining placement in `NEEDS_RECERTIFICATION`, its `compliance_status` returns to `COMPLIANT`. The placement's, the area's and the project's `version` are each incremented by one.

Cancellation is the project-level compensating action, driven by the `DesignConsultant`. It is available from any of `DRAFT`, `CONSULTATION`, `RENDERED`, `COMMUNITY_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `DESIGN_APPROVED`, `CERTIFIED` and `ORDERED`; a project already `INSTALLED`, `AUDITED` or `CANCELLED` cannot be cancelled and the attempt is refused with `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_CANCELLABLE`. On success the project's `design_status` becomes `CANCELLED`, and the consequences fan out inside the same request. Hop one: the project's `ManufacturingOrder`, if it has one and its `production_status` is `CREATED` or `IN_PRODUCTION`, moves to `CANCELLED`. Hop two: because that order is cancelled, every `InstallationJob` of the order whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` moves to `CANCELLED`. Hop three: because those jobs are cancelled, each affected installer has its `committed_crew_days` recomputed. Alongside the hops, every `FundraisingPledge` of the project whose `status` is `PLEDGED` or `COLLECTED` moves to `REFUNDED`, after which `funds_collected_cents` and all four allocation buckets are recomputed and therefore all become 0; and every `Quote` of the project still in `ISSUED` moves to `EXPIRED`. Version counters are incremented by one on the project and on every order, job, installer, pledge and quote the cancellation actually mutated. `CANCELLED` is terminal on the project's design axis.
