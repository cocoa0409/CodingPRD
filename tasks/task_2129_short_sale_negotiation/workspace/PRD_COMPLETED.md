# Short Sale Negotiation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A short sale is the only workout in the distressed-property playbook that requires every party at the table to lose something on purpose. The seller gives up their home and any equity they imagined they had; the listing brokerage accepts a haircut on its commission; the subordinate lien holders release recorded security interests for a fraction of what they are owed; the mortgage insurance carrier writes a cheque it would rather not write; and the servicer, acting for an investor it does not own, signs off on a payoff below the unpaid balance of the note. None of those concessions is worth anything on its own. They are worth something only when they arrive together, in the right order, priced against the same opinion of value, and inside a closing window that has not yet lapsed. The negotiators who run these files spend most of their day not negotiating but reconciling — chasing a bank statement that went stale, re-running a net sheet because a junior lien holder moved its number, discovering that the approval letter they were counting on was quietly voided when a broker price opinion came back revised.

This document specifies the API for a short sale negotiation platform that makes that reconciliation the server's job rather than the negotiator's. The system tracks one **short sale case** per distressed property from intake through either a closed transaction or a declined file. Around that case it hangs the artefacts the workout actually turns on: the seller's hardship package and its supporting documentation, the marketing listing and its days on market, the buyer offers and the counter/revise rounds they pass through, the broker price opinions and the disputes filed against them, the junior lien demands and responses, the mortgage insurance claim, the settlement net sheets, and finally the approval letter with its conditions, its commission cap, its relocation incentive, its deficiency-waiver treatment, and its closing deadline.

Two properties drive most of the specification that follows. The first is that a case carries **two independent state dimensions** — a negotiation dimension and a documentation dimension — that advance on separate schedules and must never be collapsed into a single status field. The second is that value is **conserved and derived**: the minimum net the servicer will accept, the junior contribution ledger, the cumulative extension budget, and the net proceeds on a settlement sheet are all computed from primary records, recomputed the moment any of those records move, and cascaded outward to invalidate downstream commitments that no longer hold. An approval letter that was fiscally sound when it was issued must stop being sound the instant a sustained valuation dispute raises the floor above it. Getting those recomputations and their cascades exactly right — not approximately right — is the substance of this build.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every route in this specification is mounted under the `/short-sale` prefix, with a single exception: the logical-clock control endpoint lives under `/_clock`. All request and response bodies are JSON.

### 2.1. Participants and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent, present but empty, or `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the seven role names below, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. Once the headers are accepted, the role is checked against the set of roles the addressed endpoint admits; a role outside that set yields `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403.

The seven roles, spelled exactly as they must appear in `X-User-Role`, are:

- **`Negotiator`** — the short sale negotiator who owns the file end to end. The negotiator opens the case, assembles the hardship package and files the seller's documentation, sends junior lien payoff demands, opens the mortgage insurance claim, prepares net sheets, submits offers to the servicer, files valuation disputes, files closing extension requests, and closes the transaction under the approval letter.
- **`ListingAgent`** — the listing brokerage. It owns the marketing window and the buyer-facing contract: it creates and withdraws listings, records price reductions, files buyer offers, and revises countered offers.
- **`ServicerAnalyst`** — the loss-mitigation analyst at the servicing shop. The analyst reviews seller documentation, decides the hardship package, orders valuations, counters or rejects submitted offers, adjudicates valuation disputes, issues approval letters, and decides extension requests. Two segregation-of-duties bars constrain this role; see §2.10.
- **`JuniorLienHolder`** — a subordinate lien holder deciding whether to release its lien for a partial payoff.
- **`MiUnderwriter`** — the mortgage insurance carrier's underwriter, sole decider of the MI claim and of the cash contribution the carrier demands as a condition of approving it.
- **`BpoVendor`** — the valuation vendor firm that performs broker price opinions and appraisals and records the resulting opinion of value.
- **`PlatformAdmin`** — the operations role. It registers vendor firms and drives the logical clock. It holds no business decision rights on any case; it may read across the system but may not decide anything on a file.

Identity binding differs by role, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `BpoVendor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting vendor record itself; ownership checks compare the `vendor_id` recorded on a valuation to that `X-User-ID`, and a vendor may record a value only on valuations it holds. When `X-User-Role` is any of `Negotiator`, `ListingAgent`, `ServicerAnalyst`, `JuniorLienHolder`, `MiUnderwriter`, or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records those identifiers wherever the specification below says a transition stamps one — `assigned_negotiator_user_id` on a case, `assembled_by_user_id` on a hardship package, `reviewed_by_user_id` on a seller document, `listing_agent_user_id` on a listing, `prepared_by_user_id` on a net sheet, `ordered_by_user_id` on a valuation, `filed_by_user_id` and `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a dispute, `decided_by_user_id` on an MI claim or an extension request, `issued_by_user_id` on an approval letter, and `requested_by_user_id` on an extension request — but it never compares them to any entity's `id`. The one opaque identifier that is compared to a stored value is the junior lien holder's: a `JuniorLienHolder` may act on a lien only when its `X-User-ID` equals the `holder_user_id` the negotiator recorded on that lien when the lien was entered.

When an ownership binding fails — a `BpoVendor` whose `X-User-ID` does not match the addressed valuation's `vendor_id`, or a `JuniorLienHolder` whose `X-User-ID` does not match the addressed lien's `holder_user_id` — the request is refused with `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_OWNER` and HTTP 403. Note that this is a distinct failure from `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`: the role is right, the principal is wrong.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination, Filtering, and List Ordering

Collection endpoints accept two query-string parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds, or one that is not an integer, is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Because the logical clock stands still between the operator's moves, rows created in the same window share an identical `created_at` to the millisecond, so the ordering is completed by creation sequence: rows come back in the order the server minted them. Exactly one collection orders differently and says so where it is described: the net sheets attached to an offer come back newest first, ordered by `created_at` descending, with the same creation sequence breaking the ties in reverse.

Every list response carries the four pagination fields inside `meta`: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values actually applied, `total_count` reports the number of rows matching the filter before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window. Every reverse lookup is a list response in this sense, `response_type` of `array` and all, even where the parent can hold at most one child: the case-to-hardship-package and case-to-MI-claim lookups answer with an array of zero or one element rather than with a bare object.

Several collections accept optional filters, and a filter that names an unrecognised enum member is a malformed request. Cases may be filtered on either state dimension, by `negotiation_status` or by `documentation_status` or both. Seller documents on a package may be filtered by `doc_type` and by `status`. Listings on a case may be filtered by `marketing_status`. Offers on a case, disputes on a valuation, junior liens on a case, and extension requests on a letter may each be filtered by `status`. Net sheets on an offer may be filtered by `variant` and by `status`. The system-wide valuation list may be narrowed by `case_id`, by `vendor_id`, and by `status`. The system-wide approval-letter list may be narrowed by `case_id`, by `offer_id`, by `supersedes_approval_id`, and by `status`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields, and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` — a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind — and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with exactly three fractional-second digits, read from the logical clock of this section rather than from the host's wall clock, as is every `updated_at`. The envelope's own `api_request_timestamp` is the single exception: it is a real wall-clock instant and moves independently of the logical clock. A client never supplies an `id`; one supplied in a request body is ignored, and so is any other field in a request body that this specification does not name — unrecognised body fields are discarded, never refused.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed to the current instant on every mutation of that resource, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation. Both fields move together: any write that changes a resource's persisted state bumps its `version` and refreshes its `updated_at`, including writes that reach the resource indirectly as a cascade from an operation addressed at some other record. The single exception is the vendor firm record. A vendor firm is immutable configuration — it has no update and no delete route — and it therefore carries only `id` and `created_at`. Neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on a vendor firm, and neither field should appear in its representation.

Time in this system is not wall-clock time. Every deadline, every freshness window, every days-on-market figure, and every expiry check is evaluated against a **logical clock** that the platform operator drives. The clock's startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; until it is moved, every read of "now" returns that instant, and the server never falls back to the host's real clock. A `PlatformAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock control endpoint, which answers HTTP 200 with the new `current_utc` value carried as the single field of the standard envelope's `data` object. Moving the clock persists nothing and cascades nothing; in particular, approval letters that have passed their deadline are not swept at that moment — they expire lazily, on the next write that addresses them, as §3.9 describes.

### 2.5. Path Resolution and Left-to-Right Existence Checking

Resource identity in this API is flat. Every record is addressed at a single-segment collection path plus its own `id` — a document is reached as a document, a lien as a lien, an extension request as an extension request — even though each of them is linked to a parent by a foreign-key field. That field is named for the parent it points at and is part of the child's representation: `case_id` on a hardship package, a listing, an offer, a valuation, a junior lien, an MI claim and an approval letter; `package_id` on a seller document; `offer_id` on a net sheet and on an approval letter; `valuation_id` on a dispute; `vendor_id` on a valuation; and `approval_letter_id` on an extension request. Two-segment paths appear in exactly two situations: when a child is created beneath its parent, and when a client walks from a parent to its children as a reverse lookup.

Where a path carries more than one identifier, the identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that fails to resolve determines the error. Each entity type has its own not-found code: `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LISTING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VALUATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LIEN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MI_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPROVAL_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_EXTENSION_NOT_FOUND`. All eleven answer with HTTP 404.

Identifiers that arrive in a request body rather than in the path are resolved too, and after the path has been resolved. A `vendor_id` in a valuation order that names no registered vendor firm is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND` and HTTP 404. An `offer_id` on an approval letter that names no offer — or that names an offer belonging to some other case than the one in the path — is refused with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND` and HTTP 404.

### 2.6. The Order in Which a Request Is Judged

Many operations in this system can fail for several reasons at once. Which reason the caller is told about is not a matter of taste; it is a fixed ladder, and a request is refused by the **first** rung it fails. Implementations that evaluate the cheap checks last, or that let a business-readiness failure mask an authentication failure, will report the wrong code. The ladder, from first-evaluated to last, is:

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present, non-empty, and `X-User-Role` naming a declared role — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). This applies to every endpoint in the system.
2. **Role admission.** The presented role appears in the addressed endpoint's authorized set — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). This too applies to every endpoint.
3. **Ownership binding.** On vendor value recording and on junior lien holder responses, the acting `X-User-ID` matches the record's owner field — otherwise `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_OWNER` (403). This rung and the next necessarily presuppose that the record whose owner field they read has been found, so the addressed record's own path identifier resolves ahead of them: a bad owner or a barred analyst presented against an identifier that names nothing is still reported as that entity's not-found code, not as a 403.
4. **Segregation of duties.** On dispute adjudication and on extension decisions, the acting analyst is not the barred one — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
5. **Path existence.** Every identifier in the path resolves, left to right — otherwise the entity-specific not-found code (404).
6. **Body reference existence.** Every identifier carried in the body resolves — `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND` on valuation ordering, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND` on approval-letter issuance (404).
7. **Request shape.** Required fields present, types correct, enum members recognised, numeric bounds honoured — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
8. **Optimistic concurrency.** The supplied `expected_case_version` or `expected_offer_version` matches the parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_OFFER_VERSION` (412). See §2.8.
9. **State admissibility.** The addressed record is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — otherwise the entity-specific code: `ERR_CASE_NOT_ACTIONABLE`, `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_ACTIONABLE`, `ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_REVIEWABLE`, `ERR_LISTING_NOT_ACTIONABLE`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_ACTIONABLE`, `ERR_VALUATION_NOT_ACTIONABLE`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_LIEN_NOT_ACTIONABLE`, `ERR_MI_CLAIM_NOT_DECIDABLE`, `ERR_APPROVAL_NOT_ACTIVE`, or `ERR_EXTENSION_NOT_PENDING` (all 409).
10. **Approval-letter currency.** The addressed approval letter has not passed its closing deadline — otherwise `ERR_APPROVAL_EXPIRED` (409). This rung guards exactly three operations: closing under a letter, filing an extension request against a letter, and deciding an extension request.
11. **Uniqueness.** No duplicate hardship package on a case, no duplicate MI claim on a case, no second junior lien at the same recording position on a case — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` (409). This rung guards exactly those three creation operations.
12. **Interval admission.** A new marketing window does not overlap an existing one on the case (`ERR_LISTING_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, 409); a new inspection slot clears the vendor's buffered conflict test (`ERR_VENDOR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`, 409).
13. **Required documentation.** Every required document type is represented on the package at submission — otherwise `ERR_REQUIRED_DOCUMENT_MISSING` (422). This rung guards package submission only.
14. **Document freshness.** A document too old at review time may not be verified — otherwise `ERR_DOCUMENT_EXPIRED` (422). This rung guards document review only.
15. **Deficiency-waiver eligibility.** A letter may waive the deficiency only on a case whose investor permits it — otherwise `ERR_DEFICIENCY_WAIVER_NOT_PERMITTED` (422). This rung guards approval-letter issuance only.
16. **Vendor daily capacity.** The vendor has not already filled its allowance of inspections for that UTC calendar date — otherwise `ERR_VENDOR_DAILY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422). This rung guards valuation ordering only, and is evaluated only after the buffered conflict test at rung 12 has passed.
17. **Controlling valuation present.** The case has a valuation with a recorded value, so its derived floor is not null — otherwise `ERR_CONTROLLING_VALUATION_MISSING` (422). This rung guards approval-letter issuance only.
18. **Hardship accepted.** The case's hardship package exists and has been accepted — otherwise `ERR_HARDSHIP_NOT_ACCEPTED` (422). This rung guards exactly two operations: offer submission and approval-letter issuance.
19. **Current final net sheet.** The offer named by the letter has a current settlement sheet of the auditable variant — otherwise `ERR_STALE_NET_SHEET` (409). This rung guards approval-letter issuance only.
20. **Junior liens resolved.** Every junior lien on the case has been approved — otherwise `ERR_JUNIOR_LIEN_UNRESOLVED` (422). This rung guards approval-letter issuance only.
21. **MI decided.** The case's MI claim, if one exists, has been approved or declared not required — otherwise `ERR_MI_APPROVAL_PENDING` (422). This rung guards approval-letter issuance only.
22. **Marketing period.** The case has a controlling listing and, on offer submission, that listing has been on the market long enough — otherwise `ERR_MARKETING_PERIOD_NOT_MET` (422). This rung guards exactly two operations: offer submission and net-sheet preparation. Net-sheet preparation needs the controlling listing only for its commission rate, so it checks that a controlling listing exists but does not check the day count; offer submission checks both.
23. **Price reduction outstanding.** The case's price-reduction gate is clear — otherwise `ERR_PRICE_REDUCTION_REQUIRED` (422). This rung guards offer submission only.
24. **Negotiation round budget.** Re-submitting a revised offer stays inside the round allowance — otherwise `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (422). This rung guards offer submission only.
25. **Dispute variance floor.** The filed variance clears the minimum — otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_VARIANCE_TOO_SMALL` (422). This rung guards dispute filing only.
26. **Dispute evidence floor.** The comparable count clears the minimum — otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT` (422). This rung guards dispute filing only, and is evaluated after the variance floor, so a filing that fails both is reported as a variance failure.
27. **Hardship score floor.** An accept decision requires an audit score at or above the minimum — otherwise `ERR_HARDSHIP_SCORE_BELOW_MINIMUM` (422). This rung guards the package decision only.
28. **Lien contribution cap.** A demanded payoff or an approved contribution does not exceed the lien's outstanding balance — otherwise `ERR_LIEN_CONTRIBUTION_EXCEEDS_CAP` (422). This rung guards exactly two operations: sending a lien demand and a holder's response.
29. **Commission cap.** The commission ceiling written into the letter does not exceed the servicer's own cap — otherwise `ERR_COMMISSION_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422). This rung guards approval-letter issuance only.
30. **Net floor.** The net proceeds the letter commits to are at or above the case's minimum acceptable net — otherwise `ERR_NET_BELOW_MINIMUM` (422). This rung guards approval-letter issuance only.
31. **Extension budget.** Granting the request keeps the chain's cumulative extension days inside the allowance — otherwise `ERR_EXTENSION_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (422). This rung guards the extension decision only.

Read that ladder with approval-letter issuance in mind, since it is the operation most heavily guarded: eleven distinct rungs can refuse it, and they are evaluated in exactly the order listed — concurrency before state, state before deficiency eligibility, deficiency eligibility before the readiness of the valuation, the package, the net sheet, the liens and the MI claim, and all of those before the commission cap and the net floor.

### 2.7. Money, Days, and Rounding

All monetary amounts in this API are integers denominated in **whole cents**. Percentages and rates are decimal fractions rather than percentage points: a commission of five and a half percent is carried as `0.055`, and a coverage of twenty-five percent as `0.25`.

Wherever a monetary product must be rounded, the method is **round half up** — a value exactly on the halfway mark rounds away from zero, upward. Two rounding disciplines apply, and confusing them produces off-by-one-cent errors that the settlement arithmetic will not forgive:

- On a settlement net sheet, **each individual line is rounded to whole cents on its own, and only the already-rounded lines are added together.** Computing the raw products first and rounding the aggregate once at the end is wrong and yields a different total.
- On the hardship score, the opposite discipline holds: the weighted terms are accumulated at full precision and **a single rounding to two decimal places is applied once, at the very end**, to the accumulated raw value.

Where a percentage-derived floor is computed, each floor is rounded to whole cents separately before the floors are compared and before any credit is subtracted from the winner. The variance figure on a valuation dispute is the one quantity carried to four decimal places, again rounded half up.

Durations expressed in days are whole days. Where a day count is derived from two instants, the elapsed seconds are divided by 86,400 and the result is truncated downward to a whole number, never rounded. Where a whole number of calendar days is added to an instant to produce a deadline, the time-of-day component is preserved exactly.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on the Case and on the Offer

Two write operations in this system operate on a parent record that other writers routinely move underneath them, and both are protected by an optimistic-concurrency check on that parent's `version`.

**Issuing an approval letter** requires the request body to carry `expected_case_version`. This field is not optional and has no default; a request that omits it is malformed. Its value must equal the current `version` of the short sale case named in the path. A newly issued letter legitimately supersedes the servicer's earlier view of the file — the offer it accepts, the junior-lien ledger it prices, and the minimum acceptable net it clears are all case-level state — so if the case's `version` has advanced since the client last read it, the client is working from a view of the file that no longer holds and the issuance is refused with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Successful issuance itself increments the case's `version` by 1.

**Preparing a net sheet** requires the request body to carry `expected_offer_version`, likewise non-optional, whose value must equal the current `version` of the offer named in the path. A new sheet of a given variant supersedes the prior current sheet of that variant, so a writer working from a stale view of the offer price, the round index, or the existing sheet set would otherwise mint a superseding sheet from inputs that no longer hold. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_OFFER_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Successful preparation increments the offer's `version` by 1.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field of any kind, and supplying one elsewhere has no effect.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline

A great deal of what this system does happens to records the caller did not address. Three named cascades recur throughout §3, and each is specified there in full, but the discipline they share belongs here: a cascade is **synchronous, complete, and ordered**. Every hop runs inside the request that triggered it, before the response is written. Every record a hop touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented. A hop that is described as caused by an earlier hop runs only when that earlier hop actually fired — if the condition guarding hop *n* is false, hops *n+1* onward do not run. And a compensating action must restore the ledger it compensates to **exactly** its prior value, not to a recomputed approximation of it: revoking a document verification restores the package's counters to their pre-verification values, and a lien holder withdrawing its approval returns the case's junior contribution ledger to exactly the total it held before the approval.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Two bars separate the analyst who creates an obligation from the analyst who reviews it, and both compare opaque operator identifiers rather than entity ids.

The analyst who ordered a valuation — recorded as `ordered_by_user_id` when the order was placed — is permanently barred from adjudicating any dispute filed against that valuation. The analyst who issued an approval letter — recorded as `issued_by_user_id` at issuance — is barred from deciding any extension request filed against that letter. Either violation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403. Both bars survive supersession: a successor letter minted by an extension grant copies the predecessor's `issued_by_user_id`, so the original issuer remains barred from deciding extensions against the successor too.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening a File and Assembling the Seller's Hardship Package

A `Negotiator` opens a short sale file by recording the `property_address` of the distressed property, the `servicer_name` of the shop that will review it, the `unpaid_principal_balance_cents` outstanding on the first-lien note — a whole-cent integer of at least 1 — and the `investor_type` that owns that note. Only three investor types are recognised: `GSE`, `PORTFOLIO`, and `FHA`. The choice is consequential twice over. It fixes the ratio the servicer's net floor is computed at, as §3.9 explains, and it decides once and for all whether the deficiency balance may be forgiven: `deficiency_waiver_permitted` is set to true when the investor is `GSE` or `FHA`, and to false when the investor is `PORTFOLIO`. A portfolio investor keeps its right to pursue the deficiency, and no approval letter on such a case may waive it.

The opening write stamps `assigned_negotiator_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and starts the case's two independent state dimensions. `negotiation_status` begins at `INTAKE` and travels through `PACKAGE_REVIEW`, `MARKETED`, `OFFER_UNDER_REVIEW`, `APPROVAL_ACTIVE` and possibly `RENEGOTIATION` before settling at one of the two terminal values, `CLOSED` or `DECLINED`. `documentation_status` begins at `DOC_PENDING` and moves only between `DOC_PENDING`, `DOC_COMPLETE` and `DOC_STALE`; it has no terminal value at all. The two dimensions never gate each other directly. A case can sit in `APPROVAL_ACTIVE` while its documentation dimension reads `DOC_STALE`, and the documentation dimension is driven exclusively by seller-document reviews and package revalidation — never by offers, valuations, liens, or approval letters. Alongside them the case carries a set of derived ledger quantities that begin empty and are maintained by the server thereafter: `junior_lien_count` and `failed_offer_count` at 0, `junior_contribution_total_cents` at 0, `price_reduction_required` at false, and both `final_value_cents` and `minimum_acceptable_net_cents` at null, since no opinion of value has yet been recorded. Once a case reaches `CLOSED` or `DECLINED` it accepts no further work hung directly off the file: the seven case-scoped creation routes — assembling the hardship package, creating a listing, recording an offer, ordering a valuation, recording a junior lien, opening the MI claim, and issuing an approval letter — are refused with `ERR_CASE_NOT_ACTIONABLE` at rung 9 of §2.6. Operations addressed at a child record that already exists carry no such check and are judged solely on that child's own state: reviewing a document, submitting or deciding the package, reducing a price, withdrawing a listing, submitting, answering or revising an offer, preparing a net sheet, recording a value, filing or adjudicating a dispute, demanding on or answering a lien, deciding the MI claim, closing under a letter, and filing or deciding an extension all remain available on a terminal case.

Each case admits **at most one** hardship package. A second attempt to assemble one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The package the negotiator assembles carries the seller's declared `hardship_reason`, drawn from `JOB_LOSS`, `MEDICAL`, `DIVORCE`, `INCOME_REDUCTION`, `RELOCATION` or `OTHER`; the documented `monthly_income_cents` and `monthly_expenses_cents` of the household, each at least 1 cent; and `liquid_assets_cents`, the seller's reserves, which may be 0 but never negative. Creation stamps `assembled_by_user_id` from the acting header, links the package to its case through the case's own identifier, and starts all four document counters — `document_count`, `pending_document_count`, `verified_document_count` and `rejected_document_count` — at 0. Both score fields, `hardship_score_intake` and `hardship_score_audit`, start null; neither is computed at assembly time. The package's `status` begins at `DRAFT` and moves through `SUBMITTED` to either `ACCEPTED` or the terminal `REJECTED`, with a revalidation loop back through `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` described in §3.5.

### 3.2. Filing and Reviewing Seller Documentation

Documentation hangs off the package, not off the case. A `Negotiator` files each piece against the package it supports, supplying its `doc_type`, the `issued_on` instant at which its originator produced it, and a `reference_label` — the human-readable name the negotiator files it under. Six document kinds are recognised: `HARDSHIP_LETTER`, `FINANCIAL_WORKSHEET`, `BANK_STATEMENT`, `AUTHORIZATION_FORM`, `PAY_STUB` and `TAX_RETURN`. Filing a document records the owning package's identifier as the document's `package_id`, raises the package's `document_count` by one and its `pending_document_count` by one, and starts the document's `status` at `PENDING`. Filing is gated on nothing at all beyond request shape: a document may be filed against a package in any status, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` and the terminal `REJECTED` included. Its `age_days_at_review` and `reviewed_by_user_id` both begin null, because nobody has looked at it yet.

Four of the six kinds are **required documentation**: `HARDSHIP_LETTER`, `FINANCIAL_WORKSHEET`, `BANK_STATEMENT` and `AUTHORIZATION_FORM`. A pay stub or a tax return is welcome supporting material, but it never satisfies a requirement and it never contributes to a score. The other two kinds are what the submission gate and the completeness rule are written against.

A `ServicerAnalyst` reviews a filed document by supplying an `outcome` — one of `VERIFY`, `REJECT` or `REVOKE` — and, optionally, a free-text `note`. Every review stamps `reviewed_by_user_id` with the reviewing analyst's identifier and stamps `age_days_at_review`, the whole number of days between the document's `issued_on` and the review instant on the logical clock, computed by dividing the elapsed seconds by 86,400 and truncating downward. No review of any kind is permitted while the owning package has been rejected outright; such an attempt is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_REVIEWABLE`, as is a verify or reject aimed at a document that is not currently `PENDING`, and as is a revoke aimed at a document that is not currently `VERIFIED`.

`VERIFY` moves a `PENDING` document to `VERIFIED`, but only if the document is still fresh. A document whose age at review exceeds **90 days** may not be verified; the review is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENT_EXPIRED`. A document exactly 90 days old is still fresh and passes. That refusal lands ahead of the stamping, so a document turned away as expired keeps its `age_days_at_review` and `reviewed_by_user_id` at null; unlike the audit score of §3.3, a review that fails persists nothing at all. A successful verification raises the package's `verified_document_count` by one and lowers its `pending_document_count` by one, leaving `document_count` untouched. It may also move the case's documentation dimension: if, **after** this verification, every one of the four required document kinds has at least one `VERIFIED` document on the package, `documentation_status` advances from `DOC_PENDING` to `DOC_COMPLETE`.

`REJECT` moves a `PENDING` document to the terminal `REJECTED` state, raising `rejected_document_count` by one and lowering `pending_document_count` by one. `document_count` is again untouched. A rejected document never returns to review.

`REVOKE` is the deliberate compensating action for a verification: it moves a `VERIFIED` document back to `PENDING`, lowers `verified_document_count` by one and raises `pending_document_count` by one, restoring every package counter to precisely the value it held before the verification it undoes — `document_count` having never moved at all. Revocation can also walk the documentation dimension backwards: if, after the revoke, at least one of the four required kinds no longer has any `VERIFIED` document on the package, `documentation_status` falls from `DOC_COMPLETE` back to `DOC_PENDING`. Notice the asymmetry between the two directions and respect it: verification advances the dimension only when the whole required set is satisfied after the write, while revocation retreats it as soon as any single required kind is left uncovered after the write.

### 3.3. Scoring the Hardship and the Servicer's Package Decision

Submission is the negotiator's assertion that the file is complete enough to review. Before a `DRAFT` package can move to `SUBMITTED`, every one of the four required document kinds must have at least one document on the package whose status is either `PENDING` or `VERIFIED` — a filed-but-unreviewed document counts here, a rejected one does not. A submission attempted with a gap is refused with `ERR_REQUIRED_DOCUMENT_MISSING`. Submitting a package that is neither `DRAFT` nor `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` is refused with `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_ACTIONABLE`. When a `DRAFT` package is submitted the case's negotiation dimension advances from `INTAKE` to `PACKAGE_REVIEW`; when a `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` package is re-submitted the negotiation dimension is left alone and the documentation dimension instead recovers from `DOC_STALE` to `DOC_PENDING`.

Every submission stamps `hardship_score_intake`. Every servicer decision stamps `hardship_score_audit`. Both are produced by the same weighted formula, evaluated under different admission rules for the documentation term — and the two scores routinely disagree, which is the point of having both.

The formula has four terms. The first is a **reason weight** taken directly from the seller's declared hardship: 40 points for `JOB_LOSS`, 35 for `MEDICAL`, 30 for `DIVORCE`, 25 for `INCOME_REDUCTION`, 20 for `RELOCATION` and 10 for `OTHER`. The second is a **deficit term** worth up to 30 points. Take the amount by which monthly expenses exceed monthly income, divide it by monthly income, and cap the resulting ratio at 1; if expenses do not exceed income the ratio is instead floored at 0, so a household running a surplus contributes nothing here. Multiply the resulting ratio by 30. The third is a **reserve-relief term** worth up to 20 points. Divide the seller's liquid reserves by their monthly expenses to obtain a number of months of runway; cap that figure at 6 months; divide the capped figure by 6 to get the fraction of a full six-month cushion the seller holds; subtract that fraction from 1 to get the relief fraction, floored at 0. Multiply the relief fraction by 20. A seller with six months or more of reserves earns nothing from this term; a seller with no reserves at all earns the full 20. The fourth is a **documentation-support term** worth up to 10 points. Count how many **distinct** required document kinds qualify, divide that count by 4 — the number of required kinds — and multiply by 10. Documents of a kind outside the required four never count, and two documents of the same kind count once.

The four terms are added at full precision and the sum is then rounded, once, half up, to two decimal places. That single final rounding is the whole of the precision rule for this calculation; rounding the individual terms first is wrong.

What differs between the two evaluations is only which documents qualify for the fourth term. Under the **intake** evaluation performed at submission, a required kind qualifies when the package holds at least one document of that kind whose status is `PENDING` or `VERIFIED`, and document age is ignored entirely. Under the **servicer-audit** evaluation performed at decision time, a required kind qualifies only when the package holds at least one document of that kind that is `VERIFIED` *and* whose age at that moment — again the elapsed seconds since `issued_on` divided by 86,400 and truncated downward — is at most 90 days. A still-pending document, or one that has gone stale-dated in the meantime, drops out of the audit count and pulls the documentation term down with it.

A `ServicerAnalyst` decides a `SUBMITTED` package by supplying an `outcome` of either `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`; deciding a package in any other status is refused with `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_ACTIONABLE`. The audit score is computed and stamped into `hardship_score_audit` **before** the outcome is evaluated, so it is persisted even when the decision that follows fails. An `ACCEPT` requires that audit score to be at least **55.00**; a package scoring below that floor cannot be accepted and the attempt is refused with `ERR_HARDSHIP_SCORE_BELOW_MINIMUM`. A score of exactly 55.00 passes. A successful `ACCEPT` moves the package to `ACCEPTED` and carries the case's negotiation dimension from `PACKAGE_REVIEW` to `MARKETED`. A `REJECT` carries no score floor at all: it moves the package to the terminal `REJECTED` and carries the case's negotiation dimension from `PACKAGE_REVIEW` to `DECLINED`, ending the file.

### 3.4. Marketing the Property: Windows, Days on Market, and Price Reductions

The listing brokerage puts the property on the market. A `ListingAgent` creates a listing against the case with a `list_price_cents` asking price of at least 1 cent, a `commission_pct` agreed in the listing agreement, and a `listed_at` instant that opens the marketing window. Commission is a decimal fraction, strictly greater than 0 and no greater than `0.10` — a total brokerage commission above ten percent is not a valid listing agreement here and is rejected as a malformed request. Creation stamps `listing_agent_user_id` from the acting header, starts `price_reduction_count` at 0, sets `marketing_status` to `ACTIVE`, and leaves `ended_at` null, meaning the window is open-ended. Creating a listing has **no effect whatsoever** on either case dimension: the negotiation dimension reaches `MARKETED` because the servicer accepted the hardship package, not because a listing appeared, and a listing may perfectly well be created while the case is still in `INTAKE` or `PACKAGE_REVIEW`.

A case's marketing windows may not overlap. Each window is half-open: it includes its `listed_at` instant and excludes its `ended_at` instant, and a null `ended_at` extends the window forward without limit. Because a newly created listing always has a null `ended_at`, a new window conflicts with an existing one on the same case exactly when the new `listed_at` falls strictly before that existing window's `ended_at` — or when that existing window has a null `ended_at` of its own, in which case any new window conflicts. Adjacency is deliberately admissible: a new window that starts at precisely the instant a prior window ended does not conflict, because the comparison is strict. The check considers **every** listing on the case in every marketing status, withdrawn ones included. A conflict is refused with `ERR_LISTING_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.

`days_on_market` is not stored; it is computed fresh every time a listing is read and every time an offer submission needs it. Take the window's end — the listing's `ended_at` if it has one, otherwise the current logical-clock instant. Take the earlier of the current instant and that window end. Subtract `listed_at` from it, divide the elapsed seconds by 86,400, and truncate downward to a whole number of days. If the result would be negative — which happens when a listing is dated into the future — report 0 instead.

Where an operation needs "the case's listing", it means the case's **controlling listing**, selected the same way every time: among the listings on that case whose `marketing_status` is `ACTIVE` or `UNDER_CONTRACT`, take the one with the latest `listed_at`, breaking a tie in favour of the later `created_at`. Withdrawn listings are never controlling. Because the window-overlap rule prevents two open windows on one case, the candidate set never holds more than one row in practice; if it holds none, the operation that needed it fails with `ERR_MARKETING_PERIOD_NOT_MET`.

Recording a price reduction is the listing side's answer to a stalled file. The agent supplies `new_list_price_cents`, a whole-cent integer of at least 1 which the server does not require to be any lower than the price it replaces; the server replaces `list_price_cents` with it, raises `price_reduction_count` by one, and — this is the part that matters upstream — clears the owning case's `price_reduction_required` gate back to false. Withdrawing a listing closes its window at a supplied `ended_at` instant and moves `marketing_status` to the terminal `WITHDRAWN`, from either `ACTIVE` or `UNDER_CONTRACT`, freeing the case for a fresh window that may begin at exactly that instant. The supplied `ended_at` must fall strictly after `listed_at`; a withdrawal that would close the window at or before it opened is refused with `ERR_LISTING_NOT_ACTIONABLE`, as is any price reduction or withdrawal addressed at a listing already `WITHDRAWN`.

### 3.5. Offers: Submission Gates and the Counter/Revise Loop

A `ListingAgent` records a buyer's purchase contract against the case, capturing `buyer_name`, the `buyer_financing` declared on it — `CASH`, `CONVENTIONAL` or `FHA_LOAN` — the `offer_price_cents` contract price of at least 1 cent, the `earnest_money_cents` deposit, which may be 0, and `requested_closing_days`, the number of days to close the buyer is asking for, at least 1. The offer is born in `DRAFT` with `round_index` at 1, `submitted_at` null and `servicer_counter_price_cents` null. Recording an offer moves nothing else; it is the negotiator, not the agent, who puts an offer in front of the servicer.

Submission is the most heavily gated routine operation in the system. An offer may be submitted only from `DRAFT` or from `REVISED`; from anywhere else the attempt is refused with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_ACTIONABLE`. Beyond that, four business gates apply, and they are evaluated in the ladder order §2.6 fixes. First, the case's hardship package must exist and be `ACCEPTED` — this is the real gate, and the case's negotiation dimension reading `MARKETED` or `RENEGOTIATION` is a consequence of that acceptance rather than a second, separate check; a case without an accepted package is refused with `ERR_HARDSHIP_NOT_ACCEPTED`. Second, the case must have a controlling listing and that listing's `days_on_market` must be **at least 15** — a property that has been exposed to the market for exactly 15 days qualifies, one at 14 does not, and either the missing listing or the short exposure is reported as `ERR_MARKETING_PERIOD_NOT_MET`. Third, the case's `price_reduction_required` gate must be clear; a case carrying an outstanding price-reduction obligation is refused with `ERR_PRICE_REDUCTION_REQUIRED` until the brokerage records a reduction. Fourth, when the offer being submitted is a `REVISED` one, the round budget must accommodate another round: the offer's `round_index` incremented by one must be no greater than **3**, and a fourth round is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`.

A successful submission moves the offer to `SUBMITTED`, stamps `submitted_at` with the current logical-clock instant, moves the controlling listing from `ACTIVE` to `UNDER_CONTRACT`, and carries the case's negotiation dimension to `OFFER_UNDER_REVIEW` — from `MARKETED` on a first pass, or from `RENEGOTIATION` when the file has come back around after an approval was voided. Nothing forbids a second offer on the same case being submitted while a first is still under review: the gates above are all the gates there are, and a submission that finds the dimension already at `OFFER_UNDER_REVIEW` and the controlling listing already `UNDER_CONTRACT` simply leaves both where they stand. Re-submitting from `REVISED` additionally raises `round_index` by one; a first submission from `DRAFT` leaves it at 1.

The `ServicerAnalyst` answers a `SUBMITTED` offer with an `outcome` of `COUNTER` or `REJECT`, optionally accompanied by a `note`. Acceptance is deliberately **not** an outcome here: an offer becomes `ACCEPTED` only as a side effect of an approval letter being issued against it, as §3.9 describes. A `COUNTER` requires a `counter_price_cents` of at least 1; the server stamps it into `servicer_counter_price_cents` and moves the offer to `COUNTERED`. Answering an offer that is not currently `SUBMITTED` is refused with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_ACTIONABLE`.

A `REJECT` fires the four-hop chain that this system calls **Cascade B**, and every hop must land. The first hop moves the offer to the terminal `REJECTED`. The second hop, caused by the first, returns the controlling listing from `UNDER_CONTRACT` to `ACTIVE` and raises the case's `failed_offer_count` by one; the case's negotiation dimension falls back from `OFFER_UNDER_REVIEW` to `MARKETED` and the property is on the market again. The third hop, caused by the second, fires only when the case's `failed_offer_count` has now reached **2 or more** — that is, on the second rejection and on every rejection after it — and sets `price_reduction_required` to true, which blocks any further offer submission until the brokerage records a price reduction. The fourth hop, caused by the third and therefore firing only when the third fired, invalidates the hardship file — and only where there is a hardship file in `ACCEPTED` to invalidate: the case's hardship package moves from `ACCEPTED` to `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`, its `hardship_score_audit` is cleared back to null, and the case's documentation dimension moves from `DOC_COMPLETE` to `DOC_STALE`, that last step firing only from `DOC_COMPLETE` and leaving any other documentation status alone. A case with no package, or one whose package sits in any status other than `ACCEPTED`, sees none of hop four — its documentation dimension included, which therefore does not go stale on its own. The seller must re-submit the package, which is exactly what carries the documentation dimension from `DOC_STALE` back to `DOC_PENDING` and re-computes `hardship_score_intake`.

Closing the negotiation loop is the listing side's move. Given a `COUNTERED` offer, a `ListingAgent` revises it, supplying a replacement `offer_price_cents` and optionally a replacement `earnest_money_cents`. The offer moves to `REVISED`, the price is replaced, and **every** settlement sheet on that offer whose status is `CURRENT` — of either variant — is demoted to `STALE`, because the price the sheets were computed against no longer holds. `servicer_counter_price_cents` is left standing at whatever the servicer last countered with rather than cleared, and neither the counter nor the revision moves either case dimension: the case sits in `OFFER_UNDER_REVIEW` for the whole `SUBMITTED → COUNTERED → REVISED → SUBMITTED` loop. Revising an offer that is not `COUNTERED` is refused with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_ACTIONABLE`. From `REVISED`, the negotiator submits again, and the cycle `SUBMITTED → COUNTERED → REVISED → SUBMITTED` may run until the round budget is exhausted.

### 3.6. Establishing Value: Vendor Scheduling, Recorded Opinions, and Disputes

Valuation vendors are platform configuration rather than case data. A `PlatformAdmin` registers a broker-price-opinion firm with its `vendor_name`, the `license_state` two-letter code it is licensed in — carried as an ordinary non-empty string, with no length or alphabet check applied to it — and its `daily_inspection_capacity` — the greatest number of inspections, at least 1, that the firm may hold on any single UTC calendar date. A vendor firm record is immutable once written: there is no route to change one and no route to delete one, and consequently the record carries only its `id` and `created_at` and neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

A `ServicerAnalyst` orders an opinion of value against a case, naming the `vendor_id` of the firm that will perform it, the `valuation_type` — `BPO_EXTERIOR`, `BPO_INTERIOR` or `APPRAISAL` — the `effective_date` the opinion will speak as of, and the `inspection_start` and `inspection_end` bracketing the vendor's site visit, where the end must fall strictly after the start, an end at or before the start being a malformed request refused at rung 7 with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Ordering stamps `ordered_by_user_id` with the ordering analyst's identifier, which permanently bars that analyst from adjudicating any dispute against this valuation. The valuation is born `ORDERED` with `value_cents` and `recorded_at` both null.

Two scheduling constraints guard the order, and they are evaluated in this sequence. The first is a **buffered slot conflict** measured across the vendor's entire book of work — every valuation that firm holds, on every case, in every status. The vendor needs an hour of travel and setup either side of a site visit, so a requested slot conflicts with an existing one when the requested start falls strictly before the existing slot's end extended forward by 60 minutes **and** the requested end falls strictly after the existing slot's start pulled back by 60 minutes. Both comparisons are strict, which means a gap of exactly 60 minutes between two slots is admissible and a gap of 59 minutes is not. A conflict is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. The second constraint, evaluated only once the first has passed, is the vendor's **aggregate daily allowance**: count the valuations already held by that vendor whose `inspection_start` falls on the same UTC calendar date as the requested one, and if that count has already reached the firm's `daily_inspection_capacity`, refuse the order with `ERR_VENDOR_DAILY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A vendor whose capacity is 3 may hold a third inspection on a date but not a fourth.

Only the vendor named on a valuation may record its result, and only while the valuation is still `ORDERED`; recording against a valuation in any later status is refused with `ERR_VALUATION_NOT_ACTIONABLE`. The vendor supplies `value_cents`, a whole-cent integer of at least 1; the server stamps it, stamps `recorded_at` with the current logical-clock instant, and moves the valuation to `RECEIVED`. That recording is what first gives the case a number to negotiate against. It recomputes the two case-level figures described below and stops there: recording a value never voids an approval letter and never moves a case dimension, even where the fresh opinion lifts the floor above a letter already committed to. Only the two dispute branches that move a value carry the chain that far.

**The controlling valuation.** A case may accumulate several opinions of value, and exactly one of them controls at any moment. A valuation is eligible to control when it belongs to the case, when its status is `RECEIVED`, `DISPUTED`, `REVISED` or `FINAL`, and when it actually carries a recorded `value_cents`. Among the eligible ones, the controlling valuation is the one with the latest `effective_date`, breaking a tie in favour of the later `created_at`. Note that `effective_date`, not `recorded_at`, is the ranking key: a later-ordered opinion that speaks as of an earlier date does not displace an earlier-ordered one that speaks as of a later date. If no valuation on the case is eligible, the case has no controlling valuation, both `final_value_cents` and `minimum_acceptable_net_cents` remain null, and any attempt to issue an approval letter fails with `ERR_CONTROLLING_VALUATION_MISSING`.

**The servicer's floor.** Whenever the controlling valuation changes — a value is recorded, or a dispute is sustained or partially sustained — and whenever the case's mortgage insurance claim leaves its pending state, the server recomputes two case-level figures. `final_value_cents` is simply the controlling valuation's `value_cents`. `minimum_acceptable_net_cents` is derived from it in four steps. First, a **value floor**: multiply `final_value_cents` by the ratio the investor demands — `0.88` for a `GSE` investor, `0.84` for a `PORTFOLIO` investor, `0.86` for an `FHA` investor — and round half up to whole cents. Second, a **balance floor**: multiply the case's `unpaid_principal_balance_cents` by `0.60` and round half up to whole cents. Each floor is rounded on its own, before anything is compared. Third, take the **greater** of the two floors. Fourth, subtract the mortgage-insurance credit described in §3.7 — the carrier's `required_contribution_cents` when the case has an approved MI claim, and zero otherwise. The result is the minimum net proceeds the servicer will accept on this file; it is taken as the arithmetic leaves it and is not floored at zero, so a credit larger than the winning floor yields a negative `minimum_acceptable_net_cents`.

**Disputing a value.** Either a `Negotiator` or a `ServicerAnalyst` may file a dispute against a valuation whose status is `RECEIVED` or `REVISED`; a dispute filed against a valuation in any other status — including one already locked — is refused with `ERR_VALUATION_NOT_ACTIONABLE`. The dispute records the disputed valuation's identifier as its `valuation_id`. The filer asserts a `claimed_value_cents` of at least 1 cent, attaches a `comparable_count` of supporting comparable sales — an integer of at least 0, a negative count being a malformed request rather than an evidence failure — and writes a `rationale` narrative. Filing stamps `filed_by_user_id` and computes `variance_pct`: take the absolute difference between the claimed value and the valuation's current recorded value, divide it by that recorded value, and round half up to **four** decimal places. Two admission thresholds then apply, in this order. The variance must be **at least `0.05`** — a disagreement of less than five percent is not worth the servicer's time and is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_VARIANCE_TOO_SMALL`; a variance of exactly `0.05` is admissible. Then the evidence must be sufficient: **at least 3** comparable sales, or the filing is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT`. Because the variance test runs first, a filing that fails both is reported as a variance failure. An admissible filing creates the dispute in `OPEN` and moves the valuation to `DISPUTED`.

**Adjudication.** A `ServicerAnalyst` other than the one who ordered the valuation adjudicates an `OPEN` dispute by supplying a `resolution` of `SUSTAINED`, `PARTIAL` or `DENIED`, optionally with a `note`; adjudicating a dispute that is not `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_OPEN`. The adjudicating identifier is stamped into `adjudicated_by_user_id`, and the dispute's own `status` leaves `OPEN` for the terminal value naming the branch taken — `SUSTAINED`, `PARTIAL` or `DENIED`, spelled exactly as the supplied `resolution` was, which is also what the dispute-list `status` filter matches on. The three branches produce three different `resolved_value_cents` and, critically, three different side-effect sets:

- **`SUSTAINED`** adopts the filer's number outright: `resolved_value_cents` becomes the `claimed_value_cents`, and the valuation moves from `DISPUTED` to `REVISED` carrying that value.
- **`PARTIAL`** splits the difference: `resolved_value_cents` becomes the average of the claimed value and the valuation's current recorded value — their sum divided by two, rounded half up to whole cents — and the valuation likewise moves from `DISPUTED` to `REVISED` carrying that value.
- **`DENIED`** keeps the original: `resolved_value_cents` is set equal to the valuation's existing `value_cents`, which is itself left untouched, and the valuation moves from `DISPUTED` to the terminal `FINAL`. A `FINAL` valuation is locked — no further dispute may ever be filed against it — and because the value did not move, the case's `final_value_cents` and `minimum_acceptable_net_cents` are not recomputed and nothing downstream is disturbed.

The two branches that move the value fire **Cascade A**, a five-hop chain. Hop one writes `resolved_value_cents` on the dispute. Hop two, caused by hop one, writes that resolved value into the valuation's `value_cents` and moves the valuation to `REVISED`. Hop three, caused by hop two, recomputes the case's `final_value_cents` and `minimum_acceptable_net_cents` from the new controlling valuation. Hop four, caused by hop three, examines every approval letter on the case whose status is `ACTIVE`: any letter whose `approved_net_proceeds_cents` is now strictly below the newly computed `minimum_acceptable_net_cents` is under water and moves from `ACTIVE` to `VOIDED`. Hop five, caused by hop four, cleans up behind the voided letter — every extension request against it whose status is `REQUESTED` moves to `CANCELLED` — and, where the voided letter was the case's only active one, carries the case's negotiation dimension from `APPROVAL_ACTIVE` back to `RENEGOTIATION`, returning the file to the negotiating table with its offers still intact.

### 3.7. Clearing the Subordinate Debt: Junior Liens and Mortgage Insurance

Everything recorded behind the first lien has to be bought off before a short sale can close, and the two mechanisms for doing that behave very differently.

A `Negotiator` records each junior lien encumbering the property against the case, giving its `lien_position` — the recording position behind the first lien, so at least 2 — the `holder_name` of the institution that holds it, the opaque `holder_user_id` that the holder must present in order to act on it, and the outstanding `balance_cents` of at least 1 cent. Recording positions are unique per case: a second lien entered at a position already taken on that case is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. Creation raises the case's `junior_lien_count` by one, starts the lien at `IDENTIFIED` with `demand_cents` null and `approved_contribution_cents` at 0. The lien then travels through `DEMAND_SENT` and possibly `NEGOTIATING` to either `APPROVED` or the terminal `REFUSED`.

Sending the payoff demand is the negotiator's move and is available only from `IDENTIFIED`; from anywhere else it is refused with `ERR_LIEN_NOT_ACTIONABLE`. The demanded payoff, supplied as `demand_cents`, is a whole-cent integer of at least 0 — a demand of exactly 0 is admissible, a negative one is malformed — and may not exceed the lien's outstanding `balance_cents` — nobody demands that a holder accept more than it is owed — and a demand above the balance is refused with `ERR_LIEN_CONTRIBUTION_EXCEEDS_CAP`. A demand exactly equal to the balance is fine. The demand is stamped, the lien moves to `DEMAND_SENT`, and because a demanded payoff is precisely what the negotiator's forward-looking settlement estimate prices, **every preliminary-variant settlement sheet on any offer of this case whose status is `CURRENT` is demoted to `STALE`**.

The `JuniorLienHolder` whose `holder_user_id` matches its `X-User-ID` answers with an `outcome`. `NEGOTIATE`, available from `DEMAND_SENT`, simply moves the lien to `NEGOTIATING` and changes nothing else. `APPROVE`, available from either `DEMAND_SENT` or `NEGOTIATING`, carries a `contribution_cents` figure of at least 0 that likewise may not exceed the lien's `balance_cents`, on pain of `ERR_LIEN_CONTRIBUTION_EXCEEDS_CAP`; it writes that figure into `approved_contribution_cents`, moves the lien to `APPROVED`, and demotes every **final**-variant sheet on any offer of the case that was `CURRENT` to `STALE`. `REFUSE`, available from either `DEMAND_SENT` or `NEGOTIATING`, zeroes `approved_contribution_cents` and moves the lien to the terminal `REFUSED`. `WITHDRAW_APPROVAL`, available only from `APPROVED`, is the compensating action for an approval: it zeroes `approved_contribution_cents`, returns the lien to `NEGOTIATING`, and demotes the final-variant current sheets to `STALE`. An outcome that is not declared from the lien's present status is refused with `ERR_LIEN_NOT_ACTIONABLE`.

Every one of those responses recomputes the case's conserved ledger. `junior_contribution_total_cents` is always the sum of `approved_contribution_cents` over the liens on that case whose status is `APPROVED`; liens in any other status contribute nothing. Because a withdrawal zeroes the contribution and drops the lien out of the `APPROVED` set, the ledger returns to exactly the total it carried before the approval it undoes — not to a value that merely resembles it.

Mortgage insurance runs on a single claim per case, and a second attempt to open one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The `Negotiator` opens it with the `mi_provider` carrier name, the `coverage_pct` on the first lien as a decimal fraction between `0` and `1` inclusive, and the carrier's `claim_reference` number. The claim starts `PENDING` with `required_contribution_cents` at 0, `contribution_form` at `NONE` and `decided_by_user_id` null — and a pending claim blocks approval-letter issuance outright.

Only the `MiUnderwriter` decides it, and only while it is `PENDING`; a decision on an already-decided claim is refused with `ERR_MI_CLAIM_NOT_DECIDABLE`. The `outcome` is one of three. `APPROVE` requires a `required_contribution_cents` figure of at least 0 and a `contribution_form` of either `CASH` or `PROMISSORY_NOTE`; both are stamped onto the claim, which moves to the terminal `APPROVED`. `DECLINE` moves the claim to the terminal `DECLINED` and forces `required_contribution_cents` back to 0. `NOT_REQUIRED` moves it to the terminal `NOT_REQUIRED`, forces `required_contribution_cents` to 0 and `contribution_form` back to `NONE`, and is the carrier's way of saying the coverage does not apply. Every decision stamps `decided_by_user_id`.

Because the claim leaving `PENDING` changes what the servicer must net, each of the three outcomes triggers a recomputation of the case's `minimum_acceptable_net_cents` as §3.6 describes — with the carrier's contribution credited against the floor when and only when the claim ended `APPROVED`, and with no credit at all when it ended `DECLINED` or `NOT_REQUIRED`. An `APPROVE` additionally demotes every final-variant settlement sheet on any offer of the case that was `CURRENT` to `STALE`, since the credit those sheets did or did not carry has just changed. A pending or declined claim leaves the file unable to proceed to approval, which surfaces as `ERR_MI_APPROVAL_PENDING`.

### 3.8. Net Sheets: The Waterfall to the First Lien

A settlement net sheet answers one question — what actually reaches the first lien if this contract closes — and it is prepared twice, in two variants that differ in exactly three places. A `Negotiator` prepares a sheet against an offer, naming the `variant` (`PRELIMINARY` or `FINAL`), the flat `title_escrow_cents` charge, the delinquent `property_tax_arrears_cents` to be paid at closing, and the delinquent `hoa_arrears_cents`, all three of which may be 0 but never negative, together with the `expected_offer_version` §2.8 requires. Preparation stamps `prepared_by_user_id`, births the sheet in `CURRENT`, demotes any prior sheet on that offer **of the same variant** that was `CURRENT` to `SUPERSEDED` — sheets of the other variant are untouched — and increments the parent offer's `version`. The case must have a controlling listing, because the sheet needs its commission rate; if there is none, preparation fails with `ERR_MARKETING_PERIOD_NOT_MET`. Beyond that listing and the concurrency check, preparation is gated on nothing: the offer's own status is never consulted, so a sheet may be prepared against an offer in `DRAFT`, in `COUNTERED`, or in the terminal `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED` just as readily as against a `SUBMITTED` one. Note that preparation does **not** check the day count on that listing; only offer submission does.

The calculation runs in four steps, and its evaluation order is not negotiable.

**Step one — the commission rate.** A preliminary sheet charges the controlling listing's full `commission_pct` as agreed with the brokerage. A final sheet charges the lesser of that agreed rate and the servicer's own cap of `0.055`, so a brokerage that agreed to seven percent is priced at five and a half on the auditable sheet. Either way, the rate actually used is recorded on the sheet as `commission_pct_applied`.

**Step two — six deduction lines, in this order.** Each entry in `line_items` records its `line_type`, its `basis`, the `rate_or_amount` it was computed from, and its own `amount_cents` — on a `PCT_OF_PRICE` line `rate_or_amount` is the decimal rate applied, while on a `FLAT` line it is the whole-cent figure itself, so there it simply equals `amount_cents`. Line 1 is `COMMISSION`, on a `PCT_OF_PRICE` basis at the applied commission rate: the offer's price multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents. Line 2 is `TITLE_AND_ESCROW`, on a `FLAT` basis, equal to the supplied `title_escrow_cents`. Line 3 is `TRANSFER_TAX`, on a `PCT_OF_PRICE` basis at a rate of `0.0075` — seventy-five basis points of the contract price — again rounded half up to whole cents. Line 4 is `PROPERTY_TAX_ARREARS`, `FLAT`, equal to the supplied arrears. Line 5 is `HOA_ARREARS`, `FLAT`, equal to the supplied dues. Line 6 is `JUNIOR_LIEN_PAYOFF`, `FLAT`, and is the whole of the divergence between the variants. On a **preliminary** sheet it is the sum of `demand_cents` across the case's liens whose status is `DEMAND_SENT`, `NEGOTIATING` or `APPROVED` — a lien still merely `IDENTIFIED` has no demand yet and is excluded, a `REFUSED` lien is excluded, and a lien in an included status whose `demand_cents` is still null contributes 0. On a **final** sheet it is the sum of `approved_contribution_cents` across the case's liens whose status is `APPROVED` and no other; every other status contributes 0. Both variants walk the case's liens in ascending `lien_position` order, breaking ties by earlier `created_at` — positions being unique per case, that tie-break is never actually reached, but the order is pinned so it is total. `gross_deductions_cents` is then the sum of those six **already-rounded** line amounts. Summing the raw products and rounding once at the end is wrong and will produce a different total.

**Step three — the mortgage-insurance credit.** A preliminary sheet always carries a `mi_contribution_credit_cents` of 0. A final sheet carries the carrier's `required_contribution_cents` when the case has an MI claim in `APPROVED` status, and 0 in every other situation — no claim at all, a pending one, a declined one, or one declared not required.

**Step four — the relocation incentive and the net.** The seller's relocation incentive is tiered on the net, and the net is reduced by the incentive, so the order in which that circle is broken is pinned. First compute `base_net_cents`: the offer's price, less `gross_deductions_cents`, plus `mi_contribution_credit_cents`. Select the incentive tier from **that** figure, before any incentive has been deducted. A `base_net_cents` of at least 20,000,000 cents — two hundred thousand dollars — earns `relocation_incentive_cents` of 300,000 cents, three thousand dollars. Otherwise, a `base_net_cents` of at least 10,000,000 cents earns 200,000 cents. Below that, the incentive is 100,000 cents, one thousand dollars — there is no tier that pays nothing. Finally, `net_proceeds_cents` is `base_net_cents` less `relocation_incentive_cents`. Selecting the tier from the post-deduction figure is wrong.

Sheets go stale as the file moves, and every demotion described elsewhere in §3 lands here: revising an offer stales both variants on that offer; a junior lien demand stales preliminary sheets across the case; a lien approval, a lien approval withdrawal, and an MI approval stale final sheets across the case. A sheet is `SUPERSEDED` when a newer sheet of its own variant replaces it and `STALE` when an input it depended on moved. Neither a `SUPERSEDED` nor a `STALE` final sheet can support an approval letter.

### 3.9. Approval Letters: Issuance, Deadlines, Extensions, Expiry, and Closing

Issuing the approval letter is the moment the servicer commits, and it is guarded by more preconditions than any other operation in the system. A `ServicerAnalyst` issues one against a case, naming in the body the `offer_id` of the offer being approved, the `approved_net_proceeds_cents` the servicer commits to accept, the `commission_cap_pct` ceiling the letter allows, the `seller_relocation_incentive_cents` the letter grants the seller at closing — a whole-cent integer of at least 0 — the `deficiency_waiver` treatment, an optional array of free-text `conditions` attached to closing which defaults to an empty array when omitted, and the `expected_case_version` §2.8 requires. The named offer must belong to the case in the path or the issuance is refused as a not-found offer.

Beyond the concurrency check, the readiness gates are these, evaluated in the §2.6 ladder order. The named offer must currently be `SUBMITTED`. The `deficiency_waiver` may read `WAIVED` only on a case whose `deficiency_waiver_permitted` is true — that is, a `GSE` or `FHA` file — and a `WAIVED` letter on a `PORTFOLIO` case is refused with `ERR_DEFICIENCY_WAIVER_NOT_PERMITTED`; `RESERVED` and `PROMISSORY_NOTE` are always available. The case must have a controlling valuation, so that its `final_value_cents` and `minimum_acceptable_net_cents` are not null, or the issuance fails with `ERR_CONTROLLING_VALUATION_MISSING`. The case's hardship package must exist and be `ACCEPTED`, or `ERR_HARDSHIP_NOT_ACCEPTED`. The named offer must have a settlement sheet of the final variant whose status is `CURRENT`, or `ERR_STALE_NET_SHEET`. Every junior lien on the case must be `APPROVED` — a case with no junior liens at all satisfies this vacuously — or `ERR_JUNIOR_LIEN_UNRESOLVED`. The case's MI claim, if one exists, must be `APPROVED` or `NOT_REQUIRED`; a `PENDING` or `DECLINED` claim yields `ERR_MI_APPROVAL_PENDING`, while a case with no MI claim at all passes. The `commission_cap_pct` must be strictly above 0 — a zero or negative ceiling is a malformed request caught back at rung 7 with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and no greater than the servicer's own cap of `0.055`, that upper ceiling alone being the one enforced at rung 29 with `ERR_COMMISSION_CAP_EXCEEDED`. And finally the `approved_net_proceeds_cents`, which may be 0 or more, must be **at least** the case's `minimum_acceptable_net_cents`; a letter below the floor is refused with `ERR_NET_BELOW_MINIMUM`, and a letter exactly at the floor is fine.

A successful issuance stamps `issued_by_user_id`, stamps `issued_at` with the current logical-clock instant, leaves `supersedes_approval_id` null because this is an original rather than a successor, starts `cumulative_extension_days_used` at 0, and computes `closing_deadline` as `issued_at` plus **45 calendar days**, preserving the time of day exactly. The letter is born `ACTIVE`. Its issuance moves the approved offer to the terminal `ACCEPTED`, carries the case's negotiation dimension from `OFFER_UNDER_REVIEW` to `APPROVAL_ACTIVE`, and increments the case's `version`.

Closing under the letter is the negotiator's move, and it is available only while the letter is `ACTIVE` and only on or before its `closing_deadline` — a close attempted at exactly the deadline instant still succeeds. The letter moves to the terminal `CLOSED`, the case's negotiation dimension moves from `APPROVAL_ACTIVE` to the terminal `CLOSED`, and the case's controlling listing is withdrawn: whether it stood at `ACTIVE` or at `UNDER_CONTRACT`, it moves to `WITHDRAWN` with its `ended_at` set to the current logical-clock instant. In the ordinary course the controlling listing is in `UNDER_CONTRACT` at that point, because the accepted offer put it there.

**Expiry is lazy.** Nothing sweeps deadlines on a schedule and moving the logical clock forward does not expire anything by itself. Instead, whenever a write is addressed at a letter and the current instant has passed that letter's `closing_deadline`, the server first moves the letter from `ACTIVE` to the terminal `EXPIRED`, cancels every extension request against it that was still `REQUESTED`, and then refuses the observing write with `ERR_APPROVAL_EXPIRED`. That refusal applies to exactly three operations: closing under the letter, filing an extension request against it, and deciding an extension request against it. Addressing any of those three at a letter that is not `ACTIVE` for some other reason — already closed, already voided, already superseded — yields `ERR_APPROVAL_NOT_ACTIVE` instead.

When a closing is going to slip, the `Negotiator` files an extension request against the active letter, supplying `requested_days` of at least 1 and a `reason`. Filing records the letter's identifier as the request's `approval_letter_id`, stamps `requested_by_user_id`, and creates the request in `REQUESTED` with `granted_days`, `decided_by_user_id` and `new_closing_deadline` all null.

A `ServicerAnalyst` — necessarily one other than the letter's `issued_by_user_id` — decides it with an `outcome` of `GRANT` or `DENY`; deciding a request that is not `REQUESTED` is refused with `ERR_EXTENSION_NOT_PENDING`, and that test runs ahead of the letter's own currency, so a request that has already been cancelled behind a superseded letter is reported as `ERR_EXTENSION_NOT_PENDING` rather than as `ERR_APPROVAL_NOT_ACTIVE`. `decided_by_user_id` is stamped onto the request before the budget below is tested, so — exactly as `hardship_score_audit` is stamped before the package outcome is judged — it survives on the record even when the grant it precedes is refused. A `GRANT` additionally requires that the chain's extension budget accommodate it: the letter's `cumulative_extension_days_used` plus the `requested_days` must be **no more than 60 days** in total, and a request that would push the chain past sixty days is refused with `ERR_EXTENSION_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. A request landing exactly on 60 is granted. That budget is a conserved quantity computed over the letter's whole supersede chain: it is the sum of `granted_days` over every extension request filed against any letter in the chain rooted at the original letter — the one whose `supersedes_approval_id` is null — whose status is `GRANTED`, accumulated in `created_at` ascending order. Denied and cancelled requests contribute nothing and must leave both the running total and the deadline exactly where they were.

A grant fires **Cascade C**, three hops deep. Hop one sets the request's `granted_days` equal to its `requested_days` and mints a **successor** approval letter: its `supersedes_approval_id` points at the predecessor, its `closing_deadline` is the predecessor's `closing_deadline` advanced by `granted_days` calendar days, its `cumulative_extension_days_used` is the predecessor's total plus those granted days, and every other field — the case, the approved offer, the approved net, the commission cap, the relocation incentive, the deficiency-waiver treatment, the conditions, the issuing analyst's identifier and the predecessor's `issued_at`, which the successor inherits verbatim rather than re-stamping at the grant instant — is copied from the predecessor. The successor is a freshly minted resource for audit purposes all the same: its own `created_at` and `updated_at` are the grant instant and its `version` starts at 1. The successor is `ACTIVE`. Hop two, caused by hop one, moves the predecessor from `ACTIVE` to the terminal `SUPERSEDED`. Hop three, caused by hop two, cancels the collateral damage: every **other** extension request against the predecessor that was still `REQUESTED` moves to `CANCELLED`. The granted request itself records `new_closing_deadline`, the successor's deadline, and moves to the terminal `GRANTED`.

A `DENY` is deliberately inert on the ledger. It sets `granted_days` to 0, stamps `decided_by_user_id`, moves the request to the terminal `DENIED`, and leaves the letter's `closing_deadline`, its `cumulative_extension_days_used` and its `ACTIVE` status entirely untouched. Extension requests also reach `CANCELLED` without anyone deciding them, whenever the letter they were filed against leaves `ACTIVE` for `VOIDED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `EXPIRED`.
