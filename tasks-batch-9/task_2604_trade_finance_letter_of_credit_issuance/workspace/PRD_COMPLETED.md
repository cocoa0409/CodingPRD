# Trade Finance Letter Of Credit Issuance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A documentary credit is the bank's own irrevocable undertaking to pay an exporter against documents, and every one of them is written against a limit somebody in the bank has already promised to somebody else. The operational problem this service exists to solve is not the paperwork — it is the bookkeeping underneath the paperwork. When a trade finance officer issues a credit, the bank simultaneously commits an amount of the applicant's facility, prices a commission it will earn over the credit's validity, screens three counterparties against sanctions lists, and starts a clock that will eventually decide whether the bank still has the right to refuse discrepant documents. Today those five things live in five different places, are reconciled by spreadsheet, and disagree with each other as often as they agree.

This document specifies a single service that owns the whole instrument lifecycle: applicant onboarding and facility limits, collateral posting and release, credit drafting with its documentary requirements and incoterm alignment, credit approval and issuance, advising and confirming bank selection under country-risk and appetite constraints, amendment negotiation with beneficiary consent, document examination inside the five-banking-day period, discrepancy identification, waiver requests to the applicant, the single refusal notice, and finally payment, acceptance, negotiation or reimbursement with the charges that go with each. The service is deliberately conservative with money: exposure is committed at issuance rather than at drawing, every rounding step that could shave a cent chooses the direction that favours the bank's reserve, and no allocation is ever edited in place — it is released and re-cut, so the ledger is never observed in a half-written state.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. First, **time is explicit**: the service reads a logical clock that only an operator can move, so that presentation windows, consent windows, examination deadlines and maturity dates are reproducible rather than wall-clock dependent. Second, **authority is split**: the officer who drafts an instrument is never the officer who approves it, and the officer who runs a sanctions screening is never the officer who releases it. Both commitments show up throughout the specification as concrete rejection rules rather than as aspirations.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Principals, Roles and Ownership

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity it acts in. If either header is missing, empty, or made up entirely of whitespace the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); surrounding whitespace is trimmed from both values before either is compared to anything. This check runs before anything else, so an unauthenticated request never learns whether the resource it addressed exists.

Eight roles exist, and their names are spelled exactly as follows: `CreditOfficer`, `TradeFinanceOfficer`, `DocumentExaminer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `ApplicantUser`, `BeneficiaryUser`, `CorrespondentBank` and `SystemOperator`. If `X-User-Role` is not one of those eight strings, or is one of them but is not listed among the roles authorised for the addressed endpoint, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). There is no hierarchical override anywhere in this system: a `CreditOfficer` cannot do a `TradeFinanceOfficer`'s work and a `TradeFinanceOfficer` cannot do a `CreditOfficer`'s, regardless of seniority.

Roles fall into two identity families, and the difference determines what `X-User-ID` actually means.

Four roles carry **opaque operator identities**. When `X-User-Role` is `CreditOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition writes it (for example as `onboarded_by_user_id` on an applicant record) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `TradeFinanceOfficer`, whose identifier is recorded as `drafted_by_user_id` and `issued_by_user_id` on credits and amendments and as `settled_by_user_id` on settlement claims; for `DocumentExaminer`, recorded as `raised_by_user_id`, `examined_by_user_id` and `requested_by_user_id`; and for `ComplianceOfficer`, recorded as `screened_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id`. For these four roles the only comparisons the server ever makes against `X-User-ID` are the segregation-of-duties comparisons described in §2.10. `SystemOperator` is likewise opaque, and nothing at all is recorded from it.

Three roles are **entity-backed**, and for them `X-User-ID` is the primary key of the row the principal acts for. When `X-User-Role` is `ApplicantUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Applicant`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Applicant` compare the resource's `applicant_id` — reached directly on a credit, or through the presentation's credit on a waiver decision — to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `BeneficiaryUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Beneficiary`, and ownership checks compare the addressed credit's `beneficiary_id` to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `CorrespondentBank`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Bank`; on a confirmation response the comparison is against the credit's `confirming_bank_id`, and on every presentation write it is against the presentation's `presenting_bank_id`. Any of these comparisons failing yields `ERR_PRINCIPAL_NOT_PARTY` (HTTP 403). Reads are scoped the same way where the role is admitted at all: an `ApplicantUser` may read only its own applicant row, only facilities under that applicant, and only credits, amendment lists, presentations and presentation sub-collections whose credit's `applicant_id` is its own; a `BeneficiaryUser` may read only its own beneficiary row and only credits, presentations and presentation sub-collections whose credit's `beneficiary_id` is its own. A `CorrespondentBank` read is the deliberate exception: no ownership comparison is made on any read it is authorised for, so it may fetch any bank row and any credit or documentary-requirement list without being a party to it. A comparison that has no row to read at all — because the addressed identifier does not resolve — is skipped rather than failed, and the request goes on to fail with the matching 404.

A separate 403, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, is reserved for the four-eyes rules of §2.10 and is never used for a plain ownership mismatch.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, and it is the one field in the whole service taken from the host wall clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.5.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- A request body that is not a JSON object — malformed JSON, a JSON value that is not an object, or an unsupported content type — is rejected with the catalogued `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422); an absent body is read as the empty object, so an endpoint whose fields are all optional accepts a request with no body at all. Three further `error_id` values fall outside the catalogue of §5 because they are produced before any endpoint is entered and belong to no band of §2.9: a path that matches no route returns `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (404), a routed path addressed with a method it does not offer returns `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` (405), and an unhandled server fault returns `ERR_INTERNAL` (500). Route matching ignores a trailing slash, so `/credits` and `/credits/` address the same collection.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection-returning endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` outside its permitted range, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422), as is either parameter supplied as anything other than a base-ten integer. A parameter supplied as an empty or all-whitespace string is treated as absent and takes its default, and an `offset` at or beyond `total_count` is not an error — it returns an empty `data` array with the true `total_count`.

List responses report all four pagination fields in `meta`: the effective `limit`, the effective `offset`, the `total_count` of rows matching the filter before the page window is applied, and `has_more`, which is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window.

Unless an endpoint's own description in §4 says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Two endpoints deviate deliberately and their ordering is part of their contract: the bank directory is ordered by `eligibility_score` descending, then by `country_risk_score` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending; and the amendment list of one credit is ordered by `seq` ascending. Because `created_at` is drawn from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from a wall clock, many rows legitimately carry the very same `created_at`; `created_at` ascending is therefore realised as creation order, with rows sharing a timestamp appearing in the order they were inserted. The same tie-break governs every cascade this document describes as processing rows "in `created_at` ascending order".

Filter query parameters, where an endpoint offers them, narrow the result set before pagination is applied and never change the ordering. Every filter but the bank directory's `max_country_risk_score` — which admits banks whose `country_risk_score` is at most the supplied value — is an exact equality test against the stored field, and a filter value that matches nothing, including a value outside the field's own enumeration, yields an empty page rather than an error. A filter over a boolean field accepts `true`, `false`, `1` and `0` in any letter case and rejects anything else with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422); a filter supplied as an empty string is treated as absent.

### 2.4. Instrument Records, Versions and Timestamps

Every persisted record carries a server-minted `id` in canonical UUID form. Identifiers never carry a type prefix, and clients never supply them — an `id` present in a request body is ignored, as is any other field a request body carries that the addressed endpoint does not declare. Unknown fields are never an error.

Almost every record also carries three system-maintained fields. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with exactly three fractional digits, both read from the logical clock of §2.5 and not from the host wall clock — so every row created before the operator first moves the clock is stamped `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 the first time that row is mutated within a request, and by nothing further for the rest of that same request: a row that one request's cascade writes to two or three times still ends the request exactly one version higher than it began. `updated_at` is refreshed with the same first touch, including when the mutation is a downstream consequence of a write aimed at some other record. The rule is worth stating plainly because this domain is dense with cascades: if a credit's issuance drives a facility's exposure figures upward, the facility's `version` increments and its `updated_at` is refreshed even though nobody addressed the facility directly.

One entity is exempt. Presented documents are immutable evidence: a document row carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, there is no endpoint that updates one and none that deletes one. A correction is made by presenting another document, not by editing an existing row.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Deterministic Date Arithmetic

Nothing in this service reads the host wall clock except the response envelope's own `api_request_timestamp`, which is metadata about the call rather than a fact about the domain. Every deadline, window boundary, maturity date and date-stamped field — `created_at` and `updated_at` included — derives from a single logical clock that starts at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and moves only when a `SystemOperator` sets it. Advancing the clock mutates no entity — deadlines are re-evaluated only when a business write or the operational sweep runs — and an attempt to set the clock to an instant earlier than its current value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REGRESSION` (HTTP 422); the clock is monotonic by construction.

Date-typed fields (`facility_expiry_date`, `latest_shipment_date`, `expiry_date`, `issue_date`, `presentation_deadline_date`, `received_date`, `maturity_date`, `consent_window_start`, `consent_deadline_date`, `window_end_effective`, `confirmation_window_start`, `confirmation_window_end`) are plain calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form and are compared as dates. Instant-typed fields (`received_at`, `submitted_at`, `examination_deadline`, `refusal_notice_sent_at`, `requested_at`, `deadline_at`, `responded_at`, `resolved_at`, `released_at`, `settled_at`, `reviewed_at`) are full ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits and are compared as instants. Where a deadline is expressed as an instant derived from a date, it lands at `23:59:59.999Z` on that date.

Banking-day arithmetic appears in exactly one place — the examination period — and uses a fixed weekday rule with no holiday calendar: Monday through Friday are banking days, Saturday and Sunday are not.

### 2.6. Minor Units, Rounding and Ledger Conservation

All monetary amounts are integers in the minor unit of their currency, which this document calls cents; a field name ending in `_cents` is always an integer and never a decimal. Because every stored amount is already an exact whole number of cents, the specification's two-decimal-place presentation and its half-up rounding rule coincide: wherever a computation produces a fraction of a cent, that fraction is resolved by rounding half up — to the nearest whole cent, with an exact half rounded away from zero.

Two rounding disciplines run side by side and must not be confused. Exposure arithmetic — everything that decides how much of a facility a credit occupies — uses integer floor division throughout, never half-up rounding, so that the bank never under-reserves by a fraction. Charge arithmetic — commissions, confirmation charges, discount charges and document fees — uses half-up rounding, applied once per charge component and never to a running total. Where a charge is assessed per document, the rounding happens per document and the rounded values are then summed; summing exact fractions and rounding once at the end is a different and wrong answer, and the difference grows with the number of documents.

Percentages and basis points are integers. A basis-point figure is a rate in ten-thousandths, so a rate of 15 basis points applied to an amount means that amount multiplied by 15 and divided by 10,000.

The ledgers in this system are pure aggregations, never incrementally patched counters. A facility's occupied exposure is always the sum over its currently open allocation rows; a credit's drawable position is always derived from its own presentations. This is what makes the conservation property hold to the cent: releasing an allocation restores the facility to exactly the value it held before that allocation was opened, and voiding a drawing restores the credit to exactly its pre-submission drawable position.

### 2.7. Path Resolution and Reference Integrity

Several resources are addressed through a parent: a facility under an applicant, a documentary requirement or an amendment under a credit, documents and discrepancies and waiver requests and settlement claims under a presentation. Path parameters are always resolved strictly left to right. The parent identifier is looked up first, and if it does not resolve the request fails there with that parent's own not-found error — the child identifier is not even examined. Only once the parent resolves is the child looked up, and a child that resolves but belongs to a different parent is treated as not found rather than as a permission problem.

Path identifiers are resolved before payload foreign keys. Within a payload, foreign keys are resolved in the order the fields are declared for that endpoint. Each unresolvable reference maps to its own dedicated 404: `ERR_APPLICANT_NOT_FOUND` for an applicant path id or an `applicant_id`, `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND` for a facility path id or a `facility_id`, `ERR_BANK_NOT_FOUND` for a bank path id or any of `nominated_bank_id`, `advising_bank_id`, `confirming_bank_id` and `presenting_bank_id`, `ERR_BENEFICIARY_NOT_FOUND` for a beneficiary path id or a `beneficiary_id`, `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_FOUND` for a credit path id or a `credit_id`, `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_FOUND` for an amendment path id, `ERR_PRESENTATION_NOT_FOUND` for a presentation path id or a `presentation_id`, `ERR_WAIVER_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` for a waiver-request path id, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` for a settlement-claim path id and `ERR_SCREENING_CHECK_NOT_FOUND` for a screening-check path id.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Credits and Facilities

Two write operations in this service are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, and only two. Both are creates that the product rules deliberately allow to stack on or supersede a prior one, which means no state check and no business rule can catch a writer working from a stale view — a stale-version check is the only mechanism that can.

A `POST /credits/{id}/amendments` request must carry `expected_credit_version` as a required, non-optional field holding the `version` of the `DocumentaryCredit` the client last observed. If that value does not equal the credit's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CREDIT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason this matters is structural: drafting a new amendment legitimately moves any earlier still-draft amendment on the same credit to `SUPERSEDED`, so a writer holding a stale view of the credit would silently discard an amendment it never saw. Note also that drafting an amendment is itself a write on the credit and therefore advances the credit's `version`, so a second amendment drafted from the same captured version is rejected.

A `POST /facilities/{id}/collateral-postings` request must carry `expected_facility_version` as a required, non-optional field holding the `version` of the `CreditFacility` the client last observed. If that value does not equal the facility's current `version`, the posting is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FACILITY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Collateral postings stack additively, so a second sequential posting would always succeed on its own merits; the check earns its place because the facility's `version` also advances whenever an issuance cascade recomputes its occupied exposure, and that recomputation changes how much collateral credit a given posting will actually buy.

No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field of any kind, and no other endpoint may be made to fail on one.

### 2.9. The Order of Rejection

When one request violates several rules, exactly one error is returned, and which one is not a matter of implementation convenience. Checks are evaluated in strictly ascending priority bands, and the first band that produces a failure decides the response. Within a single band, checks are evaluated left to right in payload order, then in path order.

The bands, in order:

1. **Authentication** — both headers present and non-empty, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Authorization** — the role is one of the eight and is permitted on this endpoint (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, 403); entity-backed principals own the addressed row (`ERR_PRINCIPAL_NOT_PARTY`, 403); segregation of duties holds (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403). Both of those comparisons need a row to read: when the addressed identifier does not resolve there is nothing to compare, the comparison is skipped, and the request falls through to band 3 and reports the matching 404.
3. **Shape, then existence** — required fields present, correctly typed, enum members declared, money and day-count integers non-negative except where a field explicitly permits a negative value, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). A field supplied as JSON `null` counts as absent everywhere: a required field sent as `null` reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and an optional one sent as `null` simply takes its default. An integer field demands a true JSON integer, so a number carrying a fractional part and a boolean standing in for a number are both `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and so is a number or a boolean standing in for an enum. Then every path identifier and payload foreign key resolves, path identifiers first and payload keys in declaration order, else the matching 404 from §2.7.
4. **State preconditions and concurrency** — the addressed row is in a state from which the requested transition exists, else the specific 409 for that instrument (`ERR_CREDIT_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_ISSUED`, `ERR_CREDIT_TERMINAL`, `ERR_PRESENTATION_NOT_EXAMINABLE`, `ERR_PRESENTATION_ALREADY_CONCLUDED`, `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_PENDING_CONSENT`, `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_LODGED` or `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_ACTIVE`); cross-entity state guards hold, which for issuance means the facility is `ACTIVE` and its posture is not `FROZEN` (`ERR_FACILITY_NOT_ACTIVE`, 409), for settlement means the governing presented-documents screening is not still awaiting review (`ERR_SCREENING_NOT_CLEARED`, 409) and for amendment consent means the parent credit is still `ISSUED` or `AMENDMENT_PENDING` (`ERR_CREDIT_NOT_ISSUED`, 409); and the optimistic-concurrency field of §2.8 matches, else `ERR_STALE_CREDIT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_FACILITY_VERSION` (412). The cross-entity screening guard covers only the awaiting-review result — a blocked or escalated screening is deliberately excluded here and is adjudicated one band later.
5. **Compliance safety** — sanctions and dual-use prohibitions first, then the two counterparty-standing bars: `ERR_SANCTIONS_BLOCK` (403), `ERR_SCREENING_REVIEW_PENDING` (409), `ERR_DUAL_USE_ESCALATED` (409), then `ERR_APPLICANT_KYC_NOT_CLEARED` (422) and then `ERR_ADVISING_BANK_INELIGIBLE` (422). Those last two live here rather than in the shape or fiscal bands, so a facility state failure of band 4 outranks an uncleared KYC file and an uncleared KYC file outranks any temporal, structural, referential or fiscal complaint.
6. **Temporal windows** — `ERR_SHIPMENT_AFTER_EXPIRY`, `ERR_PRESENTATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_EXAMINATION_PERIOD_EXPIRED`, `ERR_WAIVER_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_CONSENT_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_CONFIRMATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_CLOCK_REGRESSION`.
7. **Structural constraints** — overlap, capacity, completeness and uniqueness: `ERR_AMENDMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_CONFIRMING_LINE_WINDOW_FULL`, `ERR_CONFIRMING_BANK_INELIGIBLE`, `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CONFIRMING_BANK`, `ERR_INCOTERM_DOCUMENT_MISMATCH`, `ERR_PRESENTATION_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`, `ERR_LIVE_REQUEST_EXISTS`, `ERR_EMPTY_CHANGE_SET`.
8. **Referential coherence between parties and instrument** — `ERR_CLAIM_TYPE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_BANK_NOT_NOMINATED`, `ERR_FACILITY_APPLICANT_MISMATCH`, `ERR_FACILITY_EXPIRED`, `ERR_DISCREPANCY_CODE_UNKNOWN`, `ERR_CLAIM_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, `ERR_BENEFICIARY_CONSENT_REQUIRED`.
9. **Fiscal capacity, checked last of all** — `ERR_FACILITY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_CREDIT_DRAWABLE_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_CHARGES_EXCEED_PROCEEDS`, `ERR_COLLATERAL_RELEASE_EXCEEDS_POSTED`, `ERR_COLLATERAL_RELEASE_BLOCKED`.

The ordering of the last five bands is a deliberate compliance decision rather than a generic default: compliance safety is evaluated **before** temporal windows, which are evaluated **before** structural constraints, which are evaluated **before** referential coherence, which is evaluated **before** fiscal capacity. A sanctions or dual-use prohibition is an absolute legal bar, and the bank must refuse before it tells the presenter anything about which document was wrong, whether a bank was nominated, or whether the facility had headroom. Fiscal capacity comes last precisely because a facility breach is a curable commercial condition while a sanctions hit is not.

Two consequences are worth spelling out because they are easy to get backwards. An issuance attempt on a credit that is still in `DRAFT` and whose committed allocation would also exceed the facility's headroom returns `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_APPROVED` (409) — the state band outranks the fiscal band — and not `ERR_FACILITY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). A settlement claim lodged on a presentation whose documents screened `BLOCKED`, sent with a claim form the credit's availability does not admit, returns `ERR_SANCTIONS_BLOCK` (403) and not `ERR_CLAIM_TYPE_MISMATCH` (422).

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Three steps in this service are four-eyes controlled, and in each the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the identifier recorded against the step being reviewed. Credit approval requires an identifier different from the credit's `drafted_by_user_id`. Amendment issuance requires an identifier different from the amendment's own `drafted_by_user_id`. Screening review requires an identifier different from the check's `screened_by_user_id`. A violation of any of the three yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Note the shape of the rule: it compares operator identifiers, not roles, so two different `TradeFinanceOfficer` principals satisfy it and one principal wearing the same role twice does not.

### 2.11. Window and Interval Conventions

This domain runs on windows, and the boundary conventions differ between them on purpose. Getting them wrong is the single most common source of off-by-one behaviour, so each is stated explicitly here and again in the workflow that owns it.

- The **presentation window** ends on `presentation_deadline_date`, and that date is **inclusive**: a submission on the deadline date itself is timely.
- The **confirmation window** runs from `confirmation_window_start` **inclusive** to `confirmation_window_end` **exclusive**. A request on the start date is inside the window; a request on the end date is outside it.
- The **examination period** ends at `examination_deadline`, an instant, and that instant is **inside** the period: an action stamped at exactly `23:59:59.999Z` on the final banking day is timely, and one millisecond later is not.
- The **waiver window** ends at `deadline_at`, an instant, **inclusive**.
- The **amendment consent window** is a closed interval running from `consent_window_start` to `consent_deadline_date`, both ends **inclusive**.
- **Confirming-line occupancy** is measured over half-open intervals and against a **peak concurrent** figure, not a lifetime total; equality with the appetite is admitted.
- **Amendment consent windows** on one credit are compared as closed intervals widened by a symmetric buffer on both ends, and touching endpoints do collide.

Capacity comparisons throughout this service admit exact equality: an allocation that consumes precisely the available headroom succeeds, a drawing that consumes precisely the drawable balance succeeds, and a confirming line whose peak occupancy exactly equals the bank's appetite is admitted.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Parties, Facilities and Collateral

Nothing can be issued until three kinds of counterparty exist in the system. A `CreditOfficer` onboards the **applicant** — the importer who asks the bank to issue a credit — by supplying its registered `legal_name` (1 to 200 characters, not blank), the `country_code` of incorporation as exactly two uppercase letters, a four-digit `industry_code`, the obligor grade `internal_risk_grade`, which is one of `AAA`, `AA`, `A`, `BBB`, `BB`, `B` or `CCC`, and the outcome of the onboarding file as `kyc_status`, which is one of `PENDING`, `CLEARED` or `REJECTED`. A `sanctions_flag` may be supplied and defaults to false when it is omitted. The server records the acting operator as `onboarded_by_user_id` and sets the applicant's `status` to `ACTIVE`; the field's other two declared values, `SUSPENDED` and `CLOSED`, are never produced at onboarding. Applicants are unique on the pair of `legal_name` and `country_code`, compared character for character with no case folding and no trimming, and a second onboarding of the same pair is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (409).

Opening a **credit facility** for that applicant is also a `CreditOfficer` act. The facility is denominated in a `currency` given as exactly three uppercase letters, carries an approved `limit_cents` of at least 100,000 minor units, a `collateral_required_pct` between 0 and 100 inclusive, and a `facility_expiry_date` — the last date, inclusive, on which the facility may support a new issuance. The new facility starts with `status` `ACTIVE`, `collateral_posted_cents` and `allocated_cents` at zero, `available_cents` equal to the whole `limit_cents`, `suspended_reason` unset, and `utilisation_posture` `OPEN`. Its `restrict_threshold_cents` is derived once from the limit as ninety-five percent of `limit_cents` using integer floor division — that is, `limit_cents` multiplied by 95, divided by 100 with any remainder discarded. The facility's `applicant_id` is the applicant it was opened under.

The facility's occupied exposure is never patched incrementally. Whenever an allocation row is inserted, has its charges changed, or is released, `allocated_cents` is recomputed as the sum, over every allocation row on this facility whose `status` is `OPEN`, of that row's `allocated_cents` plus its `charge_cents`; released rows are excluded from the sum entirely and their `released_cents` is a historical record only. Rows are summed in `created_at` ascending order. From that figure, `available_cents` is `limit_cents` minus `allocated_cents`, stored exactly as that subtraction lands rather than clamped at zero: the headroom tests of §3.3, §3.4 and §3.5 are what normally keep it non-negative, and where one of them does not apply — the consent-free amendment branch of §3.5 makes no headroom test at all — a facility legitimately reports a negative `available_cents`. `utilisation_posture` is then re-derived: it is `FROZEN` whenever the facility's `status` is `CLOSED`; otherwise it is `RESTRICTED` whenever `allocated_cents` has reached or passed `restrict_threshold_cents`; otherwise it is `OPEN`. No rounding occurs anywhere in this recomputation, which is what allows the conservation property to hold exactly.

Crossing the restriction threshold has consequences beyond the posture field. When a recomputation leaves `allocated_cents` at or above `restrict_threshold_cents` on a facility that was `ACTIVE`, the facility moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` set to `RESTRICT_THRESHOLD_BREACH` and `utilisation_posture` set to `RESTRICTED`, its `version` increments, and every credit sitting on that facility in status `APPROVED` is moved to `ON_HOLD`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, each such credit's `version` incrementing by one. The reverse is symmetric: when a recomputation leaves `allocated_cents` strictly below `restrict_threshold_cents` on a facility that is `SUSPENDED` for reason `RESTRICT_THRESHOLD_BREACH`, the facility returns `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, `suspended_reason` is cleared to unset, `utilisation_posture` returns to `OPEN`, and every `ON_HOLD` credit on that facility returns to `APPROVED` in `created_at` ascending order.

A `CreditOfficer` can also drive the facility's lifecycle by hand, supplying an `action` of `SUSPEND`, `REINSTATE` or `CLOSE` together with an optional `reason` — which, like a collateral posting's note, is accepted, validated for length and then discarded: the facility carries no free-text reason field and no response echoes it. Each action demands a starting status and reports `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_ACTIVE` (409) when the facility is not in it: `SUSPEND` needs `ACTIVE`, `REINSTATE` needs `SUSPENDED`, and `CLOSE` needs `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, so every action on an already-`CLOSED` facility reports that same error. A manual suspension takes an `ACTIVE` facility to `SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` set to `MANUAL_SUSPENSION`; note the asymmetry with the automatic path — `utilisation_posture` is recomputed from the exposure figures as usual but is **not** forced to `RESTRICTED`. The same `APPROVED → ON_HOLD` cascade over sibling credits runs, in `created_at` ascending order. Reinstatement takes a `SUSPENDED` facility back to `ACTIVE`, clears `suspended_reason`, recomputes `utilisation_posture` from the exposure figures, and returns every `ON_HOLD` credit on the facility to `APPROVED` in `created_at` ascending order — but the gate it must pass is a pure exposure test rather than a question about why the facility was suspended. While `allocated_cents` remains at or above `restrict_threshold_cents` the request is rejected with `ERR_FACILITY_STILL_RESTRICTED` (409), whatever the `suspended_reason` says, because a facility over its threshold cannot be talked back into life; and once `allocated_cents` has fallen below the threshold the reinstatement succeeds, again whatever the `suspended_reason` says, so a threshold-breach suspension is manually reinstatable exactly once the exposure that caused it has been released. Closing takes an `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` facility to the terminal `CLOSED` state, sets `utilisation_posture` to `FROZEN` and clears `suspended_reason` to unset, and it requires that no allocation row on the facility is still `OPEN` — equivalently that `allocated_cents` is zero — failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_FACILITY_HAS_OPEN_ALLOCATION` (409). All three actions return the updated `CreditFacility` row with HTTP 200.

**Collateral** moves the other lever. A `CreditOfficer` posts collateral by supplying an `amount_cents`, which is the one money field in this service that may legitimately be negative: a positive value posts collateral, a negative value releases it. An optional `posting_note` may accompany it, and the request must also carry `expected_facility_version` exactly as described in §2.8. The facility's `collateral_posted_cents` becomes its previous value plus the supplied `amount_cents`, postings being applied in arrival order, and the facility's `version` increments. A posting is recorded as a change to the facility rather than as an instrument in its own right: no collateral-posting record is created, and none is exposed by any endpoint — the `posting_note` is accepted but is not reflected in any response. A successful call therefore returns the updated `CreditFacility` row itself with HTTP 200, carrying the facility's own fields, among them the new `collateral_posted_cents` and the incremented `version`, and no `amount_cents` of its own. Two guards protect a release. A release that would drive `collateral_posted_cents` below zero is rejected with `ERR_COLLATERAL_RELEASE_EXCEEDS_POSTED` (422). A release that would leave `collateral_posted_cents` below the total collateral credit already granted to the facility's `OPEN` allocations is rejected with `ERR_COLLATERAL_RELEASE_BLOCKED` (422), because that collateral is already doing work; that total is read off the rows rather than stored, as the sum over the facility's `OPEN` allocation rows of each row's credit's `gross_exposure_cents` less that row's own `allocated_cents`, which is exactly the collateral credit that row was cut with. Critically, a posting is forward-looking only: it never retroactively changes `committed_allocation_cents` on a credit that has already been issued. Collateral bought headroom for the credits issued after it, not before.

The **correspondent bank network** is registered by a `CreditOfficer` too. A bank carries a `bank_name` of 1 to 200 non-blank characters, a `swift_bic` of either 8 or 11 uppercase alphanumeric characters which must be unique across all banks (a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`, 409), a `country_code` of exactly two uppercase letters, a `country_risk_score` between 0 and 100 inclusive where higher means riskier, an external `credit_rating` drawn from `AA`, `A`, `BBB` and `BB`, a `confirmation_appetite_cents` of at least zero representing the maximum peak concurrent confirmed exposure the bank will carry, and an optional `sanctions_flag` defaulting to false. A new bank starts with `status` `ACTIVE` — the only other permitted value is `SUSPENDED` — and `confirming_line_used_cents` at zero. Its `eligibility_score` is derived immediately: take 100 minus `country_risk_score`, multiply by 6, and add the rating points multiplied by 4, where the rating points are 100 for `AA`, 75 for `A`, 50 for `BBB` and 25 for `BB`. That score is the sort key of the bank directory, which orders by `eligibility_score` descending, then `country_risk_score` ascending, then `created_at` ascending, and which can be narrowed by `country_code`, `status`, `max_country_risk_score` — an inclusive ceiling, admitting a bank whose `country_risk_score` equals the supplied value — and `credit_rating`.

A bank's risk posture is not frozen at registration. Either a `CreditOfficer` or a `ComplianceOfficer` may revise its `status`, `sanctions_flag`, `country_risk_score`, `confirmation_appetite_cents` or `credit_rating`, and `eligibility_score` is re-derived from the new `country_risk_score` and `credit_rating` on the spot. All five fields are optional and only those present are written; a revision that names none of them is accepted all the same, re-deriving `eligibility_score` and advancing the bank's `version` exactly as a substantive one does. Such a revision is forward-looking as well: it does **not** unwind confirmations the bank has already given. A bank that becomes ineligible simply stops being a candidate for new confirmation requests.

Finally, a `TradeFinanceOfficer` registers each **beneficiary** — the exporter in whose favour credits are issued — with a `beneficiary_name` of 1 to 200 non-blank characters, a two-uppercase-letter `country_code`, a `nominated_bank_id` naming the beneficiary's own bank (which must resolve to a registered bank, else `ERR_BANK_NOT_FOUND`, 404, but which is not vetted any further — a `SUSPENDED` or sanctions-flagged bank may be nominated), and an optional `sanctions_flag` defaulting to false. The beneficiary's `status` starts at `ACTIVE`; its only other value, `BLOCKED`, is reached solely through the document-screening cascade described in §3.8, and a blocked beneficiary is barred from being named on any new credit. The nominated bank matters later: it is always an acceptable presenting bank. Beneficiary and facility listings can be narrowed by `nominated_bank_id`, `country_code` and `status`, and the allocation listings by `status` and `credit_id`.

### 3.2. Drafting a Documentary Credit

A `TradeFinanceOfficer` drafts the credit. The draft names its four counterparties — `applicant_id`, `beneficiary_id`, `facility_id` and `advising_bank_id`, the last being the bank that will advise the credit to the beneficiary — and states the commercial terms: a `currency` of exactly three uppercase letters, an `amount_cents` of at least 100,000 minor units, an `available_by` drawn from `SIGHT_PAYMENT`, `ACCEPTANCE`, `NEGOTIATION` and `DEFERRED_PAYMENT`, an `incoterm` drawn from `EXW`, `FCA`, `FOB`, `CFR`, `CIF`, `CPT`, `CIP`, `DAP`, `DPU` and `DDP`, the `incoterm_place` that must accompany it (1 to 120 non-blank characters — the port of loading for `FOB`, `CFR` and `CIF`, the destination for `DAP` and `DDP`), a `latest_shipment_date`, an `expiry_date`, an `expiry_place` of 1 to 120 non-blank characters naming where documents must be presented, and a `presentation_period_days` between 1 and 30 inclusive. Four inputs are optional: `tolerance_pct`, the upward drawing tolerance, between 0 and 10 inclusive and defaulting to 0; `tenor_days`, between 0 and 360 inclusive and defaulting to 0; and the two shipment permissions `partial_shipment_allowed` and `transhipment_allowed`, both booleans defaulting to false.

Availability and tenor are paired, and the pairing is enforced at draft time. A credit available by `SIGHT_PAYMENT` must have `tenor_days` of exactly 0; a credit available by any of the three usance forms must have `tenor_days` of at least 1. Either violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422).

The new draft is stamped with `status` `DRAFT` and `confirmation_status` `UNCONFIRMED` — the two lifecycle dimensions a credit carries, which evolve independently of each other for the whole life of the instrument. Its `amendment_seq` starts at 0, its `drafted_by_user_id` records the acting operator, and `issued_by_user_id`, `issue_date`, `confirming_bank_id`, `confirmation_window_start`, `confirmation_window_end` and `cancelled_reason` all start unset. Every other money figure the credit carries starts at 0 and stays there until issuance: `gross_exposure_cents`, `committed_allocation_cents`, `commission_bps_applied`, `base_commission_cents`, `confirmation_charge_cents`, `total_charge_cents`, `chargeable_utilisation_cents`, `reserved_cents`, `drawn_settled_cents` and `available_cents` — so a draft reports `available_cents` of 0 even though its `max_drawable_cents` is already derived, because the drawable ledger is first computed at issuance. The full enumeration of the primary dimension is `DRAFT`, `PENDING_CREDIT_APPROVAL`, `APPROVED`, `ON_HOLD`, `ISSUED`, `AMENDMENT_PENDING`, `UTILISED`, `EXPIRED` and `CANCELLED`, of which `UTILISED`, `EXPIRED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal; the confirmation dimension enumerates `UNCONFIRMED`, `CONFIRMATION_REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED` and `CONFIRMATION_DECLINED`, and has no terminal state at all. Any transition attempted against a credit already in a terminal state is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_TERMINAL` (409).

Three figures are derived on the spot. The drawing ceiling `max_drawable_cents` is `amount_cents` multiplied by the quantity 100 plus `tolerance_pct`, divided by 100 with the remainder discarded — the credit's nominal amount grown by its upward tolerance. The `presentation_deadline_date` is the earlier of the `expiry_date` and the date obtained by adding `presentation_period_days` calendar days to `latest_shipment_date`; that resulting date is an inclusive upper bound. And a **provisional exposure estimate** is written to `provisional_allocation_cents`, which is important to understand for what it is not: it never debits the facility, no allocation row is created, and the facility's `allocated_cents` is untouched.

The provisional estimate is computed by the same five-step exposure rule the bank uses at issuance, but run with the tolerance and the tenor deliberately forced to zero, because a draft is not yet an undertaking. Concretely: the drawable ceiling for this run is `amount_cents` itself (a zero tolerance grows nothing); the usance risk add-on for this run is zero (a zero tenor buys no months); so the gross exposure for this run equals `amount_cents`. Against that gross figure the bank recognises collateral credit as the **smaller** of two caps — the first being ninety percent of the facility's `collateral_posted_cents`, computed by multiplying by 90 and dividing by 100 with the remainder discarded, which is what the applicant has actually posted after the advance-rate haircut; the second being the facility's `collateral_required_pct` applied to the gross figure the same way, which is what the facility's own collateral policy will recognise for an exposure of that size. The provisional figure is the gross exposure minus that collateral credit. Every division in this rule discards its remainder rather than rounding to nearest, in whichever direction that falls, so the bank never under-reserves.

Drafting a credit is also the first point at which the bank refuses business. If the named applicant, the named beneficiary or the named advising bank carries `sanctions_flag` set to true, or if the named beneficiary's `status` is already `BLOCKED`, the draft is refused outright with `ERR_SANCTIONS_BLOCK` (403). An applicant whose `kyc_status` is anything other than `CLEARED` yields `ERR_APPLICANT_KYC_NOT_CLEARED` (422). An advising bank whose `status` is not `ACTIVE` yields `ERR_ADVISING_BANK_INELIGIBLE` (422) — a flagged advising bank never reaches this check, having already been refused with `ERR_SANCTIONS_BLOCK` above. A `latest_shipment_date` falling after the `expiry_date` is a logically impossible credit and yields `ERR_SHIPMENT_AFTER_EXPIRY` (422). No check on the drafting, approval or issuance path compares `expiry_date` against the clock or against the eventual `issue_date`: a credit whose `expiry_date` has already gone by is drafted, approved and issued without complaint, and prices its commission on the one-quarter floor of §3.3. The named facility must belong to the named applicant and be denominated in the credit's own `currency`, failing which the draft is refused with `ERR_FACILITY_APPLICANT_MISMATCH` (422); and separately, a facility whose `facility_expiry_date` falls earlier than the credit's `expiry_date` yields `ERR_FACILITY_EXPIRED` (422), since the facility must outlive the instrument it supports.

**Documentary requirements** are drafted into the credit one at a time, and only while the credit is still `DRAFT` — an attempt on a credit in any other non-terminal status is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_DRAFT` (409), and one on a terminal credit with `ERR_CREDIT_TERMINAL` (409), the terminal check taking precedence wherever both could apply. Each requirement names a `doc_type` from `COMMERCIAL_INVOICE`, `BILL_OF_LADING`, `AIR_WAYBILL`, `INSURANCE_CERTIFICATE`, `PACKING_LIST`, `CERTIFICATE_OF_ORIGIN`, `INSPECTION_CERTIFICATE` and `DRAFT_BILL_OF_EXCHANGE`, and each `doc_type` may appear at most once per credit — a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (409). It states `originals_required` and `copies_required`, both integers between 0 and 5 inclusive, an `issuer_constraint` of up to 120 characters naming who must issue the document (for example "any insurance company" or "chamber of commerce") which is a required field but may be sent as the empty or blank string, and an optional `is_mandatory` flag that defaults to true; when a mandatory requirement's `doc_type` is absent from a presentation, that absence becomes a missing-document discrepancy. Requirements drafted before issuance carry `amended_in_seq` of 0, which records the amendment round at which the requirement was added or last altered. Adding requirements does not disturb `provisional_allocation_cents` — documentary terms are free.

Sending the draft to the credit officer moves it `DRAFT → PENDING_CREDIT_APPROVAL`, optionally accompanied by a `submission_note`, and demands the same `DRAFT` starting status on the same two errors as a documentary requirement does. It re-derives `provisional_allocation_cents` by the same provisional rule against the facility's collateral posture as it stands at submission — a collateral posting made between drafting and submission therefore shows up here. The submission is also where the credit's paperwork is checked against its own delivery term. A credit must carry at least one mandatory `COMMERCIAL_INVOICE` requirement and at least one transport requirement, meaning a requirement whose `doc_type` is `BILL_OF_LADING` or `AIR_WAYBILL`. On top of that, a credit whose `incoterm` is `CIF` or `CIP` must carry a mandatory `INSURANCE_CERTIFICATE` requirement, because under those terms the seller insures the goods; conversely, a credit whose `incoterm` is `FOB`, `FCA` or `EXW` must **not** carry an `INSURANCE_CERTIFICATE` requirement, because under those terms it is not the seller's insurance to produce. Every one of these failures reports `ERR_INCOTERM_DOCUMENT_MISMATCH` (422). A returned draft may be resubmitted, so the `DRAFT → PENDING_CREDIT_APPROVAL → DRAFT` loop can repeat without limit.

The credit register can be narrowed by `applicant_id`, `beneficiary_id`, `facility_id`, `advising_bank_id`, `confirming_bank_id`, `status` and `confirmation_status`; a credit's requirement list can be narrowed by `doc_type` and `is_mandatory`.

### 3.3. Approval, Issuance and the Facility Exposure Cascade

Approval is the `CreditOfficer`'s decision on whether the applicant's facility should carry this credit, and it is expressed as a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `RETURN_TO_DRAFT`, optionally with an `approval_note`. Either branch requires the credit to be in `PENDING_CREDIT_APPROVAL`; a credit in any other non-terminal status is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_DRAFT` (409) and a terminal one with `ERR_CREDIT_TERMINAL` (409). The four-eyes rule sits earlier still: because it belongs to band 2 of §2.9 it is applied before the payload is even read, so the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the credit's `drafted_by_user_id` on **both** branches — returning a draft one drafted oneself is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403) just as approving it is, and that 403 outranks a missing or unrecognised `decision`. Returning the credit takes it `PENDING_CREDIT_APPROVAL → DRAFT` and increments its `version`; that is the whole of that branch's effect, and it is what makes the drafting loop cyclic. Approving takes it `PENDING_CREDIT_APPROVAL → APPROVED` and likewise increments `version`, but carries two further guards, evaluated in this order: the facility named by `facility_id` must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_ACTIVE` (409); and then the applicant's `kyc_status` must be `CLEARED`, else `ERR_APPLICANT_KYC_NOT_CLEARED` (422). Approval grants permission and nothing more — **no facility debit happens here**. The limit is consumed at issuance.

Issuance is the moment the bank binds itself, and it is the single densest operation in this service. A `TradeFinanceOfficer` triggers it, optionally with an `issue_note`, and the credit moves `APPROVED → ISSUED`. Six preconditions gate it. The credit must be `APPROVED`, else `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_APPROVED` (409) — and by the ordering of §2.9 that state failure is reported even when the credit would also have blown the facility limit. The facility must be `ACTIVE` and its `utilisation_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, else `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_ACTIVE` (409); the credit's own state is judged before this facility gate is reached, so a credit that a facility suspension cascade has already carried from `APPROVED` into `ON_HOLD` is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_APPROVED` and not with `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_ACTIVE`, even though its facility is itself no longer `ACTIVE`. The facility's `facility_expiry_date` must be on or after the current clock date, else `ERR_FACILITY_EXPIRED` (422). The advising bank must have `status` `ACTIVE` and `sanctions_flag` false, else `ERR_ADVISING_BANK_INELIGIBLE` (422). The committed allocation plus the issuance charges must fit inside the facility's `available_cents`, with exact equality admitted, else `ERR_FACILITY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). And the screening of the credit's parties must not come back blocked, else `ERR_SANCTIONS_BLOCK` (403).

The **committed exposure** is the same five-step rule that produced the provisional estimate, now run in earnest: the tolerance and the tenor both count. Step one recomputes `max_drawable_cents` as `amount_cents` multiplied by the quantity 100 plus `tolerance_pct`, divided by 100 with the remainder discarded. Step two prices the usance risk add-on in whole months rounded **up**: for a credit available by `SIGHT_PAYMENT` the month count is zero, and otherwise it is `tenor_days` divided by 30 and rounded up to the next whole number; the add-on is then `max_drawable_cents` multiplied by 25, multiplied by that month count, divided by 10,000 with the remainder discarded — twenty-five basis points of the drawable ceiling for every whole or part month of usance. Step three sets `gross_exposure_cents` to `max_drawable_cents` plus that add-on. Step four computes the eligible collateral credit as the smaller of ninety percent of the facility's `collateral_posted_cents` (multiply by 90, divide by 100, discard the remainder) and the facility's `collateral_required_pct` applied to `gross_exposure_cents` the same way; the facility's `collateral_posted_cents` is read at the instant of this calculation, and postings accepted later never retroactively shrink an allocation already made. Step five sets `committed_allocation_cents` to `gross_exposure_cents` minus that collateral credit. For identical inputs this always returns a figure at least as large as the provisional estimate, and a strictly larger one whenever `tolerance_pct` or `tenor_days` is above zero. No other credit's figures enter the computation; the inputs are exactly this credit and the facility it names. `provisional_allocation_cents` is left exactly as the last provisional run wrote it — issuance neither re-derives it nor clears it, so an issued credit keeps reporting the estimate it carried at submission alongside its committed figure.

**Pricing** runs next, and its evaluation order is normative because the rule looks circular and is not. First, the validity of the credit in days is the number of days from `issue_date` to `expiry_date` counting **both ends inclusive** — that is, the day difference plus one. That day count is divided by 90 and rounded up to the next whole number to give the number of chargeable quarters, with a floor of one quarter. Second, the commission tier is selected from `gross_exposure_cents` **alone** and is recorded in `commission_bps_applied`: 15 basis points when `gross_exposure_cents` is at most 5,000,000 cents, 10 basis points when it is above that but at most 50,000,000 cents, and 7 basis points above that. The tier is emphatically **not** selected from `committed_allocation_cents`, not from `chargeable_utilisation_cents`, and not from any figure that already contains a charge. Third, `base_commission_cents` is the larger of a 25,000-cent floor and the result of multiplying `gross_exposure_cents` by `commission_bps_applied` and by the quarter count, dividing by 10,000 and rounding half up. Fourth, `confirmation_charge_cents` is zero whenever `confirming_bank_id` is unset or `confirmation_status` is anything other than `CONFIRMED`; the priced case belongs to §3.4. Fifth and last, `total_charge_cents` is `base_commission_cents` plus `confirmation_charge_cents`, and `chargeable_utilisation_cents` is `committed_allocation_cents` plus `total_charge_cents`. Those last two feed nothing above them.

Issuance also opens the credit's windows. `issue_date` is set to the clock date, `issued_by_user_id` to the acting operator. The `presentation_deadline_date` is re-derived as the earlier of `expiry_date` and `latest_shipment_date` advanced by `presentation_period_days` days, inclusive. The confirmation window is opened with `confirmation_window_start` equal to `issue_date`, inclusive, and `confirmation_window_end` equal to `expiry_date` advanced by `presentation_period_days` days — and that end date is **exclusive**, which is a different convention from the presentation deadline on purpose.

A screening of the credit's parties is created as part of issuance, with `scope` `CREDIT_PARTIES`, `credit_id` pointing at this credit, `presentation_id` unset, and `screened_by_user_id` recorded as the literal value `system` rather than any operator. Its `result` will be `CLEAR` or `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW`; a blocked score aborts the entire issuance request with `ERR_SANCTIONS_BLOCK` (403) and persists nothing at all — no screening row, no allocation, no status change. The scoring rule itself is described in §3.8.

What follows is a four-hop cascade, and each hop is caused by the hop above it rather than directly by the request. **Hop one** creates a facility allocation row: its `facility_id` and `credit_id` name the facility and the credit, its `basis` is `COMMITTED_ISSUANCE`, its `status` is `OPEN`, its `allocated_cents` is the credit's `committed_allocation_cents` and its `charge_cents` is the credit's `total_charge_cents`; its `released_cents` starts at 0 and its `released_at` is unset. **Hop two** is caused by hop one: the new open row re-drives the facility's exposure ledger, so the facility's `allocated_cents` rises by exactly the credit's `chargeable_utilisation_cents`, its `available_cents` falls by the same amount, and its `version` increments by one. **Hop three** is caused by hop two: if the facility's new `allocated_cents` has reached or passed `restrict_threshold_cents`, `utilisation_posture` flips `OPEN → RESTRICTED` and the facility's `status` flips `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` set to `RESTRICT_THRESHOLD_BREACH`. **Hop four** is caused by hop three: the suspension itself moves every sibling credit on the same facility whose status is `APPROVED` — this credit excluded, since it is now `ISSUED` — to `ON_HOLD`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, each sibling's `version` incrementing by one. Finally the issued credit's own `version` increments.

`ON_HOLD` is a narrow state by design: it is reachable only from `APPROVED` and it returns only to `APPROVED`, so no memory of a prior state is ever needed. The return happens automatically whenever the facility's exposure falls back below its restriction threshold and the suspension reason was a threshold breach, or when a credit officer manually reinstates a manually suspended facility.

Cancellation is the compensating action for all of this, and it is available from `DRAFT`, `PENDING_CREDIT_APPROVAL`, `APPROVED`, `ON_HOLD` and `ISSUED`, always landing on the terminal `CANCELLED`. A credit in `AMENDMENT_PENDING` is not cancellable and is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_ISSUED` (409) — the amendment must resolve first — while a credit already terminal is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_TERMINAL` (409). A `reason` is required, must be 1 to 500 non-blank characters, and is recorded as `cancelled_reason`. Cancelling an unissued credit needs nothing further — an unissued credit has created no beneficiary rights. Cancelling an `ISSUED` credit is different, because an irrevocable undertaking cannot be withdrawn unilaterally: the request must carry a present and non-blank `beneficiary_consent_reference` of up to 120 characters, else it is refused with `ERR_BENEFICIARY_CONSENT_REQUIRED` (422). It must also find the credit clean of drawings, and that test is applied **first**, ahead of the consent-reference test, so a live presentation is reported even when the consent reference is missing too. The test is made presentation by presentation: every presentation on the credit must have `examination_status` `REFUSED` or a `settlement_status` among `VOID`, `PAID`, `NEGOTIATED` and `REIMBURSED`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_HAS_LIVE_PRESENTATION` (409). The credit's own `reserved_cents` and `drawn_settled_cents` are not themselves consulted, so a credit whose every drawing has been settled or voided may be cancelled even though `drawn_settled_cents` is not zero.

When cancellation of an issued credit succeeds, the issuance cascade is run backwards. Every `OPEN` allocation row of the credit flips to `RELEASED` with `released_cents` set to that row's own `allocated_cents` plus its `charge_cents` and `released_at` set to the clock instant, and the row's `version` increments. That release is hop two's cause: the facility's `allocated_cents` falls by exactly `released_cents`, restoring the precise pre-issuance value, and the facility's `version` increments. Hop three follows if the facility's `allocated_cents` is now below `restrict_threshold_cents` and its `suspended_reason` is `RESTRICT_THRESHOLD_BREACH`: `status` returns `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` and `utilisation_posture` returns `RESTRICTED → OPEN`. Hop four follows from that: every `ON_HOLD` sibling credit on the facility returns to `APPROVED` in `created_at` ascending order. Any live confirmation occupation is released as well, and the confirming bank's `confirming_line_used_cents` is re-derived and its `version` incremented; the credit's `confirmation_status` and `confirming_bank_id` are themselves left exactly as they stood, the occupation lapsing simply because a `CANCELLED` credit is no longer of an occupying status. When the credit had no `OPEN` allocation to release the facility is not recomputed and not touched at all. The cancelled credit's own `version` increments.

### 3.4. Confirmation and the Confirming-Line Window

Confirmation is the second, independent lifecycle dimension of a credit. It moves `confirmation_status` without ever touching `status`: a credit can be `ISSUED` and `UNCONFIRMED`, `ISSUED` and `CONFIRMED`, or `AMENDMENT_PENDING` and `CONFIRMATION_REQUESTED`, and the two dimensions never interfere.

A `TradeFinanceOfficer` asks a correspondent bank to add its confirmation. The request may name a `confirming_bank_id` explicitly, or omit it and let the selector policy choose, and may carry a `request_note`. The credit must be in status `ISSUED` or `AMENDMENT_PENDING`, else `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_ISSUED` (409) — or `ERR_CREDIT_TERMINAL` (409) if it is terminal — and its `confirmation_status` must be `UNCONFIRMED` or `CONFIRMATION_DECLINED`, the latter being the retry edge; a `confirmation_status` of `CONFIRMATION_REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED` reports that same `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_ISSUED` (409), the two dimensions sharing one error on this endpoint. The credit's parties are then re-scored on the `CREDIT_PARTIES` scope exactly as at issuance, and a `BLOCKED` score refuses the request with `ERR_SANCTIONS_BLOCK` (403): a party sanctioned since issuance gets no further confirmation cover. That re-scoring persists nothing whatever the result — a confirmation request never writes a `ScreeningCheck` row. Next, the clock date must fall inside the credit's half-open confirmation window, on or after `confirmation_window_start` and strictly before `confirmation_window_end`, else `ERR_CONFIRMATION_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422).

Whichever bank ends up resolved must be eligible: its `status` must be `ACTIVE`, its `sanctions_flag` must be false, its `country_risk_score` must be at most 60, and it must not be domiciled in the beneficiary's own country — that is, its `country_code` must differ from the beneficiary's `country_code`, since a confirmation from within the exporter's own jurisdiction adds no country-risk cover. A named bank failing any of these is refused with `ERR_CONFIRMING_BANK_INELIGIBLE` (422). Note one thing the rule does **not** require: the confirming bank need not differ from the advising bank. The bank that advises a credit is entirely free to confirm it too.

The bank's appetite is measured as **peak concurrent occupancy** of its confirming line, not as a lifetime total and not by any pairwise comparison. Each credit that names the bank as its `confirming_bank_id`, whose `confirmation_status` is `CONFIRMATION_REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED`, and whose `status` is `ISSUED`, `AMENDMENT_PENDING` or `UTILISED`, occupies the line with a weight equal to its own `gross_exposure_cents` over the half-open interval from its `confirmation_window_start` up to but not including its `confirmation_window_end`. Cancelled credits, expired credits and credits whose confirmation was declined release their occupation immediately and do not appear. To measure the peak, form two events for each occupation — one at the window start adding that occupation's weight, one at the window end subtracting it — sort all events by date ascending and, where two events fall on the same date, by the signed weight ascending so that subtractions are applied before additions, then walk the events in order maintaining a running total and record the highest running total reached. That highest value is written to the bank's `confirming_line_used_cents`. When the measurement is an admission check for a new request, the candidate credit's own occupation is included in the events before the sweep runs, and the request is refused with `ERR_CONFIRMING_LINE_WINDOW_FULL` (409) only when the resulting peak is **strictly greater** than the bank's `confirmation_appetite_cents`; a peak exactly equal to the appetite is admitted. The reason subtractions sort before additions on a shared date is precisely that the intervals are half-open: an occupation ending on a given date frees the line before an occupation starting on that same date takes it.

When `confirming_bank_id` is omitted, the selector picks a bank. The candidate pool is every registered bank whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `sanctions_flag` is false, whose `country_risk_score` is at most 60, whose `country_code` differs from the beneficiary's `country_code`, which has not already returned a decline on this very credit, and whose peak concurrent occupancy including this candidate credit is at most its own `confirmation_appetite_cents`. Each surviving candidate is ranked by its `eligibility_score`: 100 minus its `country_risk_score`, multiplied by 6, plus its rating points multiplied by 4, where the rating points are 100 for `AA`, 75 for `A`, 50 for `BBB` and 25 for `BB`. The highest score wins. Ties are broken first by the lower `country_risk_score` and then by the earlier `created_at` — never by identifier. If the candidate pool is empty the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CONFIRMING_BANK` (422); there is no deferred queue and no retry scheduling, and the credit simply stays `UNCONFIRMED` or `CONFIRMATION_DECLINED` as it was.

A successful request sets the credit's `confirming_bank_id` to the named or selected bank, moves `confirmation_status` to `CONFIRMATION_REQUESTED`, re-derives that bank's `confirming_line_used_cents` and increments the bank's `version` by one, and increments the credit's own `version` by one. The credit's `status` dimension is untouched.

The nominated bank answers for itself. Acting as `CorrespondentBank`, with its `X-User-ID` equal to the credit's `confirming_bank_id` — anything else is `ERR_PRINCIPAL_NOT_PARTY` (403) — it supplies a `decision` of `CONFIRM` or `DECLINE`, with a `declined_reason` of up to 500 characters that is required and must be non-blank on the decline branch (a missing or blank one is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`). The credit must still be `ISSUED` or `AMENDMENT_PENDING` — `ERR_CREDIT_TERMINAL` (409) if it has gone terminal, `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_ISSUED` (409) otherwise — and its `confirmation_status` must be exactly `CONFIRMATION_REQUESTED`; a credit that is already `CONFIRMED`, back to `UNCONFIRMED` or already `CONFIRMATION_DECLINED` reports that same `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_ISSUED` (409), so answering twice is refused. No party screening is re-run on the response path.

Confirming moves `confirmation_status` `CONFIRMATION_REQUESTED → CONFIRMED` and finally prices the confirmation charge. The rate is 20 basis points plus one further basis point for each point of the confirming bank's `country_risk_score`; `confirmation_charge_cents` is then `gross_exposure_cents` multiplied by that rate, multiplied by the same inclusive-both-ends quarter count used at issuance, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up — computed from `gross_exposure_cents` and never from any running total. Headroom is tested before anything is written, and it is tested on the **increment** only: the newly priced `confirmation_charge_cents` less whatever the credit already carried in that field, compared against the facility's current `available_cents`, and refused with `ERR_FACILITY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422) only when the increment is **strictly greater** than that headroom, so an increment exactly equal to it is admitted. A credit whose `facility_id` no longer resolves skips the test altogether. The charge then goes in by re-forming the whole charge block from the credit as it now stands rather than by adding to the stored totals: `total_charge_cents` becomes a freshly derived base commission — the tier taken again from the current `gross_exposure_cents` and the quarter count taken again from the current `issue_date` and `expiry_date` — plus the new `confirmation_charge_cents`, and `chargeable_utilisation_cents` becomes `committed_allocation_cents` plus that new `total_charge_cents`. The stored `base_commission_cents` and `commission_bps_applied` are **not** rewritten in the process, so on a credit whose exposure or expiry an amendment has since moved, `total_charge_cents` will no longer equal the recorded `base_commission_cents` plus `confirmation_charge_cents`; the re-derivation wins and the recorded commission stays as issuance left it. That triggers a cascade of its own: the credit's `OPEN` allocation row has its `charge_cents` **set** to the credit's new `total_charge_cents` — not incremented, so any amendment fee the row had accumulated is displaced by that assignment — and the facility's `allocated_cents` is then re-derived from its `OPEN` rows, which may itself flip the facility's `utilisation_posture` to `RESTRICTED` and its `status` to `SUSPENDED`, which in turn moves that facility's `APPROVED` siblings to `ON_HOLD` in `created_at` ascending order; a credit with no `OPEN` allocation row simply skips that step and the facility is re-derived anyway. The confirming bank's `confirming_line_used_cents` is re-derived on this branch too and its `version` increments, the credit now occupying the line as `CONFIRMED` rather than as `CONFIRMATION_REQUESTED` at the same weight. The credit's `version` increments.

Declining moves `confirmation_status` `CONFIRMATION_REQUESTED → CONFIRMATION_DECLINED`, clears `confirming_bank_id` back to unset, re-derives the declining bank's `confirming_line_used_cents` — its occupation is removed — and increments that bank's `version` by one, as well as the credit's. What keeps that bank out of the candidate pool for any later automatic selection on this credit is the declining bank's identity, remembered privately against the credit and appearing in no response body; the `declined_reason` itself, though mandatory, is persisted on no row and exposed nowhere, and the credit carries no `declined_reason` field for it. Because the exclusion is consulted only by the selector, a bank that declined can still be asked again by naming it explicitly in a later request — it is only the automatic choice that will never land on it. A fresh request may then be made against a different bank, closing the retry cycle `CONFIRMATION_REQUESTED → CONFIRMATION_DECLINED → CONFIRMATION_REQUESTED`, which carries exactly the same eligibility, country-risk and confirming-line preconditions and exactly the same side effects as the first request did.

### 3.5. Amendment Negotiation and Beneficiary Consent

An issued credit's terms are altered only through an Amendment, which is a two-stage instrument: it is drafted, then issued, and only on issuance does it either bind at once or open a consent window for the beneficiary.

A `TradeFinanceOfficer` drafts an amendment against a credit by supplying a `change_set`, a `reason` of 1 to 500 non-blank characters, and an `expected_credit_version`. The `change_set` is a map from credit field name to new value carrying between 1 and 5 keys, and every key must be drawn from the amendable set: `amount_cents`, `tolerance_pct`, `expiry_date`, `latest_shipment_date`, `incoterm`, `incoterm_place`, `presentation_period_days`, `partial_shipment_allowed`, `transhipment_allowed`. Nothing else about a credit can be amended — not the beneficiary, not the facility, not `available_by`, not the nominated bank. A `change_set` that is not a JSON object at all, or that carries no keys or more than five, is a shape failure of band 3 reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422) rather than as `ERR_EMPTY_CHANGE_SET`; and the value sitting under each **known** amendable key is shape-checked in that same band against exactly the bounds the drafting endpoint of §3.2 applies to that same field — `amount_cents` at least 100,000, `tolerance_pct` 0 to 10, `presentation_period_days` 1 to 30, `expiry_date` and `latest_shipment_date` well-formed `YYYY-MM-DD` dates, `incoterm` a member of the ten-incoterm enumeration, `incoterm_place` 1 to 120 non-blank characters, and the two shipment permissions true JSON booleans — a violation being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422) reported before the credit path identifier is even resolved. Values sitting under keys outside the amendable set are not shape-checked at all; they are left alone and adjudicated later as `ERR_EMPTY_CHANGE_SET`. `expected_credit_version` must additionally be an integer of at least 1, a zero or negative one being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422) rather than `ERR_STALE_CREDIT_VERSION`. Before the set is judged, every key whose new value equals the credit's current value is discarded as a no-op; if the surviving set is empty, or if any key names a field outside the amendable set, the draft is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_CHANGE_SET` (422). Drafting is permitted only while the credit's `status` is `ISSUED` or `AMENDMENT_PENDING`, else `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_ISSUED` (409); and the supplied `expected_credit_version` must equal the credit's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_CREDIT_VERSION` (412). Drafting also runs the same temporal coherence test the issue step runs, and runs it before the change set's structure is judged: against the credit merged with the `change_set`, `latest_shipment_date` must not fall after `expiry_date`, and `expiry_date` must fall strictly after the credit's `issue_date`, either violation being `ERR_SHIPMENT_AFTER_EXPIRY` (422) — which therefore outranks an unknown key or an all-no-op set, both of which are band 7. A date that is unset or unparseable on either side of a comparison simply skips that comparison.

A successful draft sets `status` to `DRAFT`, records `drafted_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`, and assigns `seq` as one plus the number of Amendment rows that already exist for this credit. The row stores the `change_set` exactly as submitted, no-op keys included: the no-op pruning above governs the judgements made about the set and the figures derived from it, never the map echoed back. A draft starts with `amendment_fee_cents` 0 — the fee belongs to issuance — and with `issued_by_user_id`, `resolved_at`, `consent_window_start`, `consent_deadline_date` and `window_end_effective` all unset, the window likewise belonging to issuance. That counter is strictly monotone per credit and numbers are never reused, even when an earlier amendment was rejected, lapsed or superseded. `requires_beneficiary_consent` and `exposure_delta_cents` are computed at draft time as well. Because drafting is itself a write on the credit, the credit's own `version` increments — which is exactly why a second draft attempted from the same captured version is rejected. When a newer amendment is created for a credit while an earlier one is still `DRAFT`, the earlier one moves `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED` as a system event, taking `resolved_at` at the clock instant and `window_end_effective` at the clock date, and incrementing its own `version`. A superseded amendment never occupied a consent window and is invisible to the overlap rule.

`requires_beneficiary_consent` follows a narrow product rule rather than a blanket one: a change that can only improve the beneficiary's position binds on issuance, and only a change that reduces the beneficiary's rights is held for consent. It is true when any one of the following holds of the surviving `change_set`: `amount_cents` is present and lower than the credit's current `amount_cents`; `tolerance_pct` is present and lower than the current `tolerance_pct`; `expiry_date` is present and earlier than the current `expiry_date`; `latest_shipment_date` is present and earlier than the current `latest_shipment_date`; `presentation_period_days` is present and lower than the current `presentation_period_days`; `incoterm` is present and different from the current `incoterm`; `incoterm_place` is present and different from the current `incoterm_place`; `partial_shipment_allowed` is present, newly false, and currently true; or `transhipment_allowed` is present, newly false, and currently true. Otherwise it is false. Note the asymmetry between the field families: a numeric or date field triggers consent only when it moves against the beneficiary, whereas any change at all to `incoterm` or `incoterm_place` triggers consent, and the two boolean permissions trigger consent only when a permission is withdrawn — granting one that was previously withheld does not. Because no-op keys are discarded first, a key restated at its current value never triggers consent.

`exposure_delta_cents` is the committed-issuance exposure of the credit as it would stand after applying the `change_set`, minus the credit's current `committed_allocation_cents`. It is positive for increases and negative for decreases.

A different `TradeFinanceOfficer` then issues the amendment, optionally with an `issue_note`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `drafted_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). The amendment's `status` must be `DRAFT`, else `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_DRAFT` (409), and the credit must still be `ISSUED` or `AMENDMENT_PENDING`, else `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_ISSUED` (409). The credit's parties are then re-scored on the `CREDIT_PARTIES` scope exactly as at issuance and at a confirmation request, and a `BLOCKED` score refuses the issue with `ERR_SANCTIONS_BLOCK` (403): an amendment cannot be pushed through against a party sanctioned since issuance. That re-scoring persists nothing whatever the result, so issuing an amendment never writes a `ScreeningCheck` row. The prospective terms are then re-validated: the same two-part coherence test the draft ran is applied again, so a `change_set` that would leave `latest_shipment_date` after `expiry_date`, or `expiry_date` on or before `issue_date`, is refused with `ERR_SHIPMENT_AFTER_EXPIRY` (422); and the incoterm-to-insurance-document pairing is re-checked against the amended incoterm, a failure being `ERR_INCOTERM_DOCUMENT_MISMATCH` (422). That pairing check runs if and only if the `change_set` carries an `incoterm` key at all — a restatement at the credit's current value included — and never when it does not, so an amendment that leaves the incoterm alone never has the credit's paperwork re-examined; and being band 7 it is evaluated after the window-overlap test described next, not before it.

On both branches, and before the branch is taken, the amendment acquires its window: `consent_window_start` is set to the clock date, `consent_deadline_date` to the clock date plus ten days, and `window_end_effective` to `consent_deadline_date`. Every issued amendment owns an interval — a consent-free amendment is not exempt from the cooling-off buffer. Issuance also records `issued_by_user_id` as the acting operator and **re-derives** both `requires_beneficiary_consent` and `exposure_delta_cents` from the credit as it now stands, overwriting the figures the draft computed, so an amendment is always judged and priced on its issuance-time position rather than on the one captured when it was drafted.

That interval must not collide with any other amendment interval on the same credit. The comparison is **pairwise and over closed intervals widened by a symmetric two-day cooling-off buffer**; one conflicting neighbour is enough, and there is no capacity to aggregate. Let the new interval run from the clock date to the clock date plus ten days, both ends inclusive. Then, over every other Amendment row of the same credit ordered by `created_at` ascending, skip any row whose `status` is `SUPERSEDED` (it never occupied a window) and any row whose `consent_window_start` is unset (it is still a draft). For each remaining row, take its `consent_window_start` as the neighbour's start and its `window_end_effective` as the neighbour's end — the deadline date while the amendment is live, the resolution date once it is terminal. The intervals collide, and the issue is refused with `ERR_AMENDMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409), when the new start is on or before the neighbour's end plus two days **and** the new end is on or after the neighbour's start minus two days. Both comparisons admit equality, so touching endpoints do collide here — the opposite of the confirming-line sweep, where an interval ending on a date frees capacity for one starting on it. Terminal amendments keep occupying their window up to `window_end_effective` plus the buffer, which is what enforces a genuine cooling-off period between negotiation rounds.

The amendment fee is priced once, here at issuance, and is not recomputed if the beneficiary later rejects the amendment. When `exposure_delta_cents` is zero or negative, `amendment_fee_cents` is the flat 7,500 cents. When it is positive, `amendment_fee_cents` is 7,500 cents plus `exposure_delta_cents` multiplied by a recharge rate in basis points, multiplied by the remaining quarter count, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up. The remaining quarter count is the number of days from the clock date to the **amended** `expiry_date` — the surviving change set's value where it carries one, the credit's stored value otherwise — inclusive of both ends, divided by 90 and rounded up, and it carries a floor of one quarter, so an expiry already at or behind the clock date still prices a single quarter rather than none. The recharge rate is selected by the same three-tier ladder used at issuance — 15 basis points at or below 5,000,000 cents, 10 basis points at or below 50,000,000 cents, 7 basis points above — but keyed on the **new** `gross_exposure_cents` alone. That recharge rate is a purely local value used to price this one fee; it is never written back to the credit's `commission_bps_applied`, which keeps the tier chosen at issuance for the life of the credit.

Issuance then forks, and the two branches have genuinely divergent side-effect sets.

When `requires_beneficiary_consent` is false, the amendment traverses `DRAFT → ISSUED → ACCEPTED` inside the single issue call — a favourable amendment binds on issuance. `ISSUED` is never observable as a resting state on either branch. Because the window closed the instant it opened, `window_end_effective` is immediately pulled back from `consent_deadline_date` to the clock date, and `resolved_at` is stamped at the clock instant exactly as a consented acceptance stamps it. The `change_set` is applied to the credit and the full exposure and charge cascade described below runs. That cascade carries no fiscal test of its own on this branch: the consent-free path never consults the facility's headroom, so a favourable amendment that nevertheless raises exposure binds and re-cuts the allocation even when the facility is thereby driven over its limit — `ERR_FACILITY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` belongs to the consent branch alone. The credit's `status` stays `ISSUED` throughout; it never enters `AMENDMENT_PENDING` on this branch.

When `requires_beneficiary_consent` is true, the amendment traverses `DRAFT → ISSUED → PENDING_CONSENT`, `window_end_effective` stays at `consent_deadline_date`, the credit moves `ISSUED → AMENDMENT_PENDING`, and the `change_set` is **not** applied. No credit term, no exposure figure and no ledger changes yet; the consent-required branch defers every field change.

The beneficiary answers with a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` and an optional `note` of up to 500 characters, which is validated for length and then discarded — the Amendment carries no field for it and no response echoes it. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the credit's `beneficiary_id`, else `ERR_PRINCIPAL_NOT_PARTY` (403). The amendment's `status` must be `PENDING_CONSENT`, else `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_PENDING_CONSENT` (409). The clock date must be on or before `consent_deadline_date` — the interval is closed, so a response landing exactly on the deadline date is in time — and a later response is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409).

`ACCEPT` moves `PENDING_CONSENT → ACCEPTED`, sets `resolved_at` to the clock instant, and pulls `window_end_effective` back to the clock date, which is the point from which the cooling-off buffer for any subsequent amendment is measured. Then a four-hop cascade runs. Hop one applies the `change_set` to the credit, sets the credit's `amendment_seq` to this amendment's `seq`, and returns the credit `AMENDMENT_PENDING → ISSUED`. Exactly these credit fields are re-derived: `presentation_deadline_date` and the confirmation window bounds, `max_drawable_cents`, `gross_exposure_cents`, `committed_allocation_cents`, `chargeable_utilisation_cents` and `available_cents`. Exactly these are **not** re-priced: `commission_bps_applied`, `base_commission_cents`, `confirmation_charge_cents` and `total_charge_cents`. `provisional_allocation_cents` is not re-derived either, on this branch or any other, so an amended credit goes on reporting the estimate its last pre-approval submission wrote. The commission is earned once at issuance and an amendment only adds `amendment_fee_cents` on top of it.

Hop two re-cuts the allocation rather than editing it in place. The credit's existing `OPEN` FacilityAllocation row flips to `RELEASED`, with `released_cents` set to that row's own `allocated_cents` plus its `charge_cents` and `released_at` set to the clock instant, and exactly one new row is opened whose `allocated_cents` is the credit's new `committed_allocation_cents`, whose `charge_cents` is the released row's `charge_cents` plus the amendment's `amendment_fee_cents`, and whose `released_cents` starts at 0 with `released_at` unset exactly as an issuance row's does; where the credit somehow carries no `OPEN` row to release, the retained charge is nil and the fresh row carries the amendment fee alone. The new row's `basis` is `AMENDED_INCREASE` when `exposure_delta_cents` is strictly positive and `AMENDED_DECREASE` otherwise — a zero delta takes the `AMENDED_DECREASE` label. Note the deliberate asymmetry this creates: the amendment fee occupies the facility through the allocation's `charge_cents`, but the credit's own `chargeable_utilisation_cents` remains `committed_allocation_cents` plus `total_charge_cents` under the pinned charge order and therefore excludes amendment fees altogether. The two figures are meant to differ after an amendment.

Hop three re-derives the facility's `allocated_cents`, `available_cents` and `utilisation_posture` from the new open-row set. Hop four is bidirectional: crossing `restrict_threshold_cents` upward suspends the facility and holds its `APPROVED` siblings, while falling back below the threshold reactivates the facility and returns its `ON_HOLD` siblings to `APPROVED`, in either case in `created_at` ascending order. A decreasing amendment is therefore a legitimate route out of `SUSPENDED`. If the accepted amendment's new exposure would breach the facility's headroom, the whole acceptance is refused with `ERR_FACILITY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422) and the amendment stays `PENDING_CONSENT` — it is not rejected, and the beneficiary may accept again once headroom is restored. That headroom test is band 9 and is therefore made on the re-cut position before any of the four hops is written: the facility's `limit_cents` less the sum of `allocated_cents` plus `charge_cents` over its `OPEN` allocation rows **other than this credit's own row**, which the re-cut is about to release, gives the headroom, and it is compared against the credit's post-amendment `committed_allocation_cents` plus the released row's retained `charge_cents` plus this amendment's `amendment_fee_cents`. Exact equality is admitted and only a strict excess refuses. A credit whose `facility_id` no longer resolves skips the test altogether. Finally, when the credit names a `confirming_bank_id`, that bank's `confirming_line_used_cents` is re-derived from the credit book — the occupation's weight is the credit's `gross_exposure_cents`, which the amendment has just moved — and the bank's `version` increments; this runs on both `ACCEPTED` edges, the consent-free one of the previous paragraph included.

`REJECT` moves `PENDING_CONSENT → REJECTED`, sets `resolved_at` to the clock instant and `window_end_effective` to the clock date, returns the credit `AMENDMENT_PENDING → ISSUED` on its original terms, and sets `amendment_fee_cents` to 0 — no fee is charged for a rejected amendment. No credit field, no FacilityAllocation row and no facility field changes at all.

If neither answer arrives, the amendment lapses. When the clock date is strictly past `consent_deadline_date`, the expiry sweep moves it `PENDING_CONSENT → EXPIRED_NO_CONSENT`, sets `resolved_at` to the clock instant, sets `amendment_fee_cents` to 0, and — unlike the accept and reject branches — leaves `window_end_effective` at `consent_deadline_date` rather than pulling it back to the clock date, so a lapsed amendment continues to block the calendar for its full ten days plus buffer. The credit returns `AMENDMENT_PENDING → ISSUED` with no field changes. `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED`, `EXPIRED_NO_CONSENT` and `SUPERSEDED` are all terminal.

### 3.6. Presentation of Documents and the Examination Period

A Presentation is the file of documents tendered under a credit. It carries two independent status dimensions that evolve without disturbing each other: `examination_status`, which records where the documents stand against the credit's terms, and `settlement_status`, which records where the money stands. `examination_status` is one of `RECEIVED`, `UNDER_EXAMINATION`, `COMPLYING`, `DISCREPANT`, `WAIVER_REQUESTED`, `REFUSED` or `PRECLUDED`, starting at `RECEIVED`, with `COMPLYING`, `REFUSED` and `PRECLUDED` terminal. `settlement_status` is one of `UNSETTLED`, `CLAIM_LODGED`, `PAID`, `ACCEPTED`, `MATURED`, `NEGOTIATED`, `REIMBURSED` or `VOID`, starting at `UNSETTLED`, with `PAID`, `NEGOTIATED`, `REIMBURSED` and `VOID` terminal. Submission, examination and waiver handling move `examination_status` while `settlement_status` stays pinned at `UNSETTLED`; claiming and settling move `settlement_status` while `examination_status` stays pinned at `COMPLYING` or `PRECLUDED`. The one place the dimensions interact is refusal.

A `CorrespondentBank` opens a presentation file by naming the `credit_id`, the `presenting_bank_id` and optionally a `cover_letter_reference` of up to 60 characters. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `presenting_bank_id` — a bank may not present on another bank's behalf — else `ERR_PRINCIPAL_NOT_PARTY` (403). Because that comparison reads a payload field rather than a stored row, it is made once the body's own shape has been accepted and before either identifier is resolved: a missing, null or non-string `credit_id` or `presenting_bank_id` reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422) first, and a bank naming somebody else then outranks both `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_BANK_NOT_FOUND`. Of the two foreign keys the `credit_id` resolves first. The credit must be `ISSUED` or `AMENDMENT_PENDING`, else `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_ISSUED` (409): documents may be presented while an amendment is out for consent. The presenting bank must be nominated, meaning `presenting_bank_id` equals the beneficiary's `nominated_bank_id`, or the credit's `advising_bank_id`, or the credit's `confirming_bank_id`; any other bank is refused with `ERR_BANK_NOT_NOMINATED` (422). The new file takes `examination_status` `RECEIVED`, `settlement_status` `UNSETTLED`, `received_at` at the clock instant, `received_date` as the clock date, `document_count` 0, `drawn_amount_cents` 0 and `discrepancy_count` 0, with `submitted_at`, `maturity_date`, `refusal_notice_sent_at`, `disposal_instruction` and `examined_by_user_id` all unset and `cover_letter_reference` null whenever it was omitted. Opening the file writes nothing at all on the credit: no drawable figure is recomputed and the credit's `version` does not move, because nothing is reserved until submission. `examination_deadline` deliberately stays unset until submission: the five banking days run from the day the documents are actually presented as a complete set, not from the day the file was opened.

The same bank then adds documents one at a time, supplying `doc_type`, `originals_presented` and `copies_presented` (each 0 to 5 inclusive), `issue_date`, and optionally `shipment_date`, `invoice_amount_cents`, `goods_description` (up to 500 characters), `hs_code` (either empty or 6 to 10 digits), `consignee_country` (either empty or exactly two uppercase letters), `transit_country_codes` (an array of two-letter codes, empty by default) and `issuer_name` (up to 200 characters). Each of those optional strings defaults to the **empty string** rather than to null when it is omitted or sent as `null` — `goods_description`, `hs_code`, `consignee_country` and `issuer_name` are all stored as `""` in that case — while an omitted `shipment_date` is stored as null. `doc_type` is one of `COMMERCIAL_INVOICE`, `BILL_OF_LADING`, `AIR_WAYBILL`, `INSURANCE_CERTIFICATE`, `PACKING_LIST`, `CERTIFICATE_OF_ORIGIN`, `INSPECTION_CERTIFICATE` or `DRAFT_BILL_OF_EXCHANGE`. `shipment_date` is the on-board or dispatch date and is expected for `BILL_OF_LADING` and `AIR_WAYBILL` and unset otherwise; that expectation is documentary only and is enforced in neither direction — a transport document may omit it and any other type may carry one. `invoice_amount_cents` is required and must be strictly greater than zero when `doc_type` is `COMMERCIAL_INVOICE`, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422); for every other document type it is optional and defaults to zero, and a non-zero value supplied on one is stored as given but never reaches the drawn amount, which sums `COMMERCIAL_INVOICE` rows alone. Documents may be added only while `examination_status` is `RECEIVED`, else `ERR_PRESENTATION_NOT_EXAMINABLE` (409). Each accepted document increments the presentation's `document_count`. Presented documents are immutable once recorded: there is no update path, no delete path, and — as noted in the record conventions — no `updated_at` and no `version` on them at all. A mis-tendered document is corrected by opening a fresh presentation, not by editing this one.

When the file is complete the presenting bank submits it, optionally with a `submission_note`. Submission requires `examination_status` to be `RECEIVED` and the acting `X-User-ID` to equal `presenting_bank_id`, and those are its only state preconditions: the credit's own `status` is deliberately not re-examined here, so a file opened while the credit was `ISSUED` may still be submitted after the credit has moved elsewhere, the presentation window below being what actually closes that door. The file must carry at least one document, and every mandatory `DocumentaryRequirement` of the credit must be answered by at least one document of the matching `doc_type` whose `originals_presented` is at least the requirement's `originals_required`; a file with no documents or an unanswered mandatory requirement is refused with `ERR_PRESENTATION_INCOMPLETE` (422). The clock date must be on or before the credit's `presentation_deadline_date`, that date itself included, else `ERR_PRESENTATION_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422).

Submission freezes the money figures. `drawn_amount_cents` becomes the sum of `invoice_amount_cents` over the file's `COMMERCIAL_INVOICE` documents and is never re-summed afterwards, and `document_count` is likewise frozen. That frozen drawn amount must be less than or equal to the credit's `available_cents`; exact equality is admitted, and only a strict excess is refused, with `ERR_CREDIT_DRAWABLE_EXCEEDED` (422). `submitted_at` is set to the clock instant, `received_date` is set to the clock date at submission, and `maturity_date` becomes that just-stamped `received_date` advanced by the credit's `tenor_days`. The test that decides it is made on `tenor_days` alone rather than on `available_by`: a zero tenor leaves `maturity_date` unset, which by the draft-time availability-and-tenor pairing is exactly the `SIGHT_PAYMENT` case.

`examination_deadline` is then computed as **five banking days following the day of presentation**, at `23:59:59.999Z` on the fifth such day. Start from `received_date` and walk forward one calendar day at a time, counting a day only when it is Monday through Friday, until five have been counted; the day the walk lands on is the deadline date. The day of presentation itself is never counted, even when it is a banking day. There is no holiday calendar: Saturday and Sunday are the only non-banking days, so the deadline is reproducible from the date alone. The deadline instant is inside the period — a conclusion or refusal stamped at exactly `23:59:59.999Z` on the fifth banking day is timely, and one millisecond later is not.

Submission also creates a `PRESENTED_DOCUMENTS` ScreeningCheck over the file, scored as described in the screening section and carrying `screened_by_user_id` as the literal `system`; on a `CLEAR` or `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW` score that row is written after the presentation's own fields have been stamped. A `BLOCKED` outcome aborts the submission with `ERR_SANCTIONS_BLOCK` (403) and leaves the presentation at `RECEIVED` — but not without a trace, because the scoring is the first check the submission makes after its state guard: the `PRESENTED_DOCUMENTS` row is persisted all the same, and the beneficiary is cascaded `ACTIVE → BLOCKED`, before the refusal is returned. Only the `CREDIT_PARTIES` scope of §3.8 leaves no screening history behind.

Finally, submission moves `examination_status` `RECEIVED → UNDER_EXAMINATION` and re-derives the credit's drawable ledger, which is a pure aggregation over that credit's presentations. The live set is every presentation of the credit whose `settlement_status` is `UNSETTLED`, `CLAIM_LODGED`, `ACCEPTED` or `MATURED` **and** whose `examination_status` is `UNDER_EXAMINATION`, `COMPLYING`, `DISCREPANT`, `WAIVER_REQUESTED` or `PRECLUDED`. The settled set is every presentation whose `settlement_status` is `PAID`, `NEGOTIATED` or `REIMBURSED`. The credit's `reserved_cents` is the sum of `drawn_amount_cents` over the live set, `drawn_settled_cents` is the sum over the settled set, and `available_cents` is `max_drawable_cents` minus `reserved_cents` minus `drawn_settled_cents`. A presentation still at `RECEIVED` has not frozen its drawn amount and belongs to neither set; a presentation whose `settlement_status` is `VOID` belongs to neither set either, which is precisely what makes refusal a clean compensating action. The credit's `version` increments on the submit, and so does the presentation's.

Presentation files can be listed with the filters `credit_id`, `presenting_bank_id`, `examination_status` and `settlement_status`; a file's documents can be listed filtered by `doc_type`.

### 3.7. Discrepancies, Waiver Requests, Refusal and Preclusion

A `DocumentExaminer` records each defect found on comparing the documents with the credit as a Discrepancy, supplying a `code`, a `severity` of `MATERIAL` or `MINOR`, and a `description` of 1 to 500 non-blank characters. The `code` is one of the fixed list `LATE_SHIPMENT`, `LATE_PRESENTATION`, `CREDIT_EXPIRED`, `MISSING_DOCUMENT`, `INSUFFICIENT_ORIGINALS`, `INVOICE_OVERDRAWN`, `GOODS_DESCRIPTION_MISMATCH`, `INCOTERM_MISMATCH`, `INSURANCE_UNDERVALUED` or `TRANSHIPMENT_NOT_ALLOWED`, and that fixed list is the whole of it: the credit's own documentary requirements are never consulted, so any `code` outside the ten is refused with `ERR_DISCREPANCY_CODE_UNKNOWN` (422). The `code` is shape-checked first as a non-blank string of 1 to 60 characters, so an over-long one reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422) rather than the unknown-code error. A code already recorded on this presentation is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (409) — the pair of presentation and code is unique, so one defect is cited once. Discrepancies may be raised only while `examination_status` is `UNDER_EXAMINATION`, else `ERR_PRESENTATION_NOT_EXAMINABLE` (409), and only while the clock instant is at or before `examination_deadline`, else `ERR_EXAMINATION_PERIOD_EXPIRED` (409). Each new row takes `status` `IDENTIFIED`, records `raised_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`, sets the presentation's `examined_by_user_id` to the same value, and increments the presentation's `discrepancy_count`. A discrepancy's own dimension is small: `IDENTIFIED` is the initial state and `WAIVED` and `UPHELD` are both terminal, and neither is ever reached by direct call — only as a consequence of a waiver response, a waiver expiry, or a refusal.

`discrepancy_count` is a pure aggregation: the number of Discrepancy rows of the presentation whose `status` is not `WAIVED`. `UPHELD` rows therefore still count, which is why denying a waiver leaves the count exactly where it was while granting one lowers it.

The examiner then concludes the examination, optionally with a `conclusion_note`. The presentation must be `UNDER_EXAMINATION` and the clock instant must be at or before `examination_deadline`; past that instant the conclusion is refused with `ERR_EXAMINATION_PERIOD_EXPIRED` (409) and the presentation must instead be swept to `PRECLUDED`. The conclusion is also barred while the newest `PRESENTED_DOCUMENTS` screening for the presentation still sits at `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW`, which returns `ERR_SCREENING_REVIEW_PENDING` (409). The outcome is decided purely by the count: `discrepancy_count` equal to zero moves `UNDER_EXAMINATION → COMPLYING`, and a count of one or more moves `UNDER_EXAMINATION → DISCREPANT`. Either way `examined_by_user_id` records the concluding examiner. A conclusion attempted from any other status splits two ways, and the split is not by terminality: only `RECEIVED` — a file whose documents were never submitted — reports `ERR_PRESENTATION_NOT_EXAMINABLE` (409), while every other status reports `ERR_PRESENTATION_ALREADY_CONCLUDED` (409), `DISCREPANT` and `WAIVER_REQUESTED` as much as `COMPLYING`, `REFUSED` and `PRECLUDED`.

From `DISCREPANT` the examiner may ask the applicant to waive named defects, supplying `discrepancy_codes` — between 1 and 10 elements with no duplicates — and an optional `request_note`. Every element must match a Discrepancy of this presentation whose `status` is `IDENTIFIED` **and** whose `severity` is `MINOR`; a `MATERIAL` defect cannot be waived by the applicant at all, and citing one, or citing a code that is not an identified discrepancy of this presentation, is refused with `ERR_DISCREPANCY_CODE_UNKNOWN` (422). At most one waiver request per presentation may be live: a second attempt while one is `PENDING` is refused with `ERR_LIVE_REQUEST_EXISTS` (409), and this one uniqueness test is deliberately evaluated **ahead of** the state guard rather than in its band 7 position, because a presentation parked in `WAIVER_REQUESTED` by that very live request could otherwise never report it. Only once no request is pending is the status judged: `RECEIVED` and `UNDER_EXAMINATION` report `ERR_PRESENTATION_NOT_EXAMINABLE` (409), and anything else that is not `DISCREPANT` — including a `WAIVER_REQUESTED` file whose request has already been answered — reports `ERR_PRESENTATION_ALREADY_CONCLUDED` (409). A request is likewise barred while the newest `PRESENTED_DOCUMENTS` screening sits at `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW`, with `ERR_SCREENING_REVIEW_PENDING` (409).

The request takes `status` `PENDING`, `requested_at` at the clock instant and `requested_by_user_id` as the acting examiner, and moves the presentation `DISCREPANT → WAIVER_REQUESTED`. Its own `deadline_at` is the **earlier** of two instants: the date part of `requested_at` plus two days at `23:59:59.999Z`, and the presentation's `examination_deadline`. Requesting a waiver does **not** extend `examination_deadline` — the five banking days keep running underneath the waiver window, so a waiver raised late in the examination period gets a window shorter than two days, and a waiver raised on the last banking day gets a window ending at the same instant as the examination period itself. The cited discrepancies stay `IDENTIFIED` for the whole duration of the window; nothing moves them until an answer arrives.

The applicant answers with a `decision` of `GRANT` or `DENY` and an optional `applicant_note` of up to 500 characters, which is one of only two narrative fields this service actually stores — it is written to the waiver request's own `applicant_note`, the other being a screening review's `review_note`. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `applicant_id` of the presentation's credit, else `ERR_PRINCIPAL_NOT_PARTY` (403). The request must be `PENDING`, else `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_PENDING` (409), and the clock instant must be at or before `deadline_at` — that instant included — else `ERR_WAIVER_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409), in which case the request must instead be swept to `EXPIRED`. Either answer sets `responded_at` to the clock instant.

`GRANT` moves the request `PENDING → GRANTED` and every cited Discrepancy `IDENTIFIED → WAIVED`, which lowers `discrepancy_count`. Where the presentation lands then depends on whether the waiver was total: if `discrepancy_count` reaches zero the presentation moves `WAIVER_REQUESTED → COMPLYING` and may be settled, but if at least one `IDENTIFIED` discrepancy remains uncited and therefore unwaived, the presentation returns `WAIVER_REQUESTED → DISCREPANT` instead. A partial grant is not a compliance event.

`DENY` moves the request `PENDING → DENIED`, moves every cited Discrepancy `IDENTIFIED → UPHELD`, leaves `discrepancy_count` unchanged because upheld rows still count, and returns the presentation `WAIVER_REQUESTED → DISCREPANT`. This closes the cycle `DISCREPANT → WAIVER_REQUESTED → DISCREPANT`, and a second waiver request naming the remaining codes is permitted. Raising a waiver request is in fact the one examination-side write that applies no examination-deadline gate of its own, so a request made after `examination_deadline` has already passed is still accepted with HTTP 201 — it simply lands with its `deadline_at` clamped back to that elapsed instant, which makes it unanswerable and leaves it for the sweep to expire. If no answer arrives at all, the expiry sweep moves the request `PENDING → EXPIRED`, moves the cited discrepancies `IDENTIFIED → UPHELD`, and returns the presentation to `DISCREPANT` exactly as a denial would. `GRANTED`, `DENIED` and `EXPIRED` are all terminal for a waiver request.

A discrepant presentation is finally refused by the examiner, who supplies a `disposal_instruction` of `HOLD_AT_DISPOSAL` or `RETURN_TO_PRESENTER` and an optional `refusal_note`. The presentation must be `DISCREPANT` with `discrepancy_count` of at least one, and the clock instant must be at or before `examination_deadline`, else `ERR_EXAMINATION_PERIOD_EXPIRED` (409) — past that instant the bank is preclusion-barred and must honour, whatever the documents look like. No waiver request may still be `PENDING`; because a pending waiver leaves the presentation in `WAIVER_REQUESTED`, that situation returns the specific `ERR_WAIVER_RESPONSE_PENDING` (409) rather than the generic state error. Only one refusal notice may ever be sent per presentation, so a second attempt is refused with `ERR_PRESENTATION_ALREADY_CONCLUDED` (409), as is an attempt on a `COMPLYING` or `PRECLUDED` file; a refusal attempted from `RECEIVED` or `UNDER_EXAMINATION` reports `ERR_PRESENTATION_NOT_EXAMINABLE` (409) instead.

Refusal moves `examination_status` `DISCREPANT → REFUSED`, which is terminal, sets `refusal_notice_sent_at` to the clock instant, records the `disposal_instruction`, and moves every remaining `IDENTIFIED` Discrepancy to `UPHELD`. The refusing examiner's own identifier is not recorded anywhere: `examined_by_user_id` keeps whichever examiner last raised a discrepancy or concluded the examination. Then the three-hop compensating cascade runs. Hop one moves `settlement_status` `UNSETTLED → VOID`, and only from `UNSETTLED` — a presentation whose money dimension has already moved on keeps whatever it holds. Hop two follows from hop one: a void presentation leaves the drawable ledger's live set, so the credit's `reserved_cents` falls by exactly this presentation's `drawn_amount_cents`. Hop three follows from hop two: `available_cents` is re-derived as `max_drawable_cents` minus `reserved_cents` minus `drawn_settled_cents`, restoring precisely the value the credit had before the submission, and the credit's `version` increments. The facility is deliberately untouched: `allocated_cents` does not move, because facility exposure is committed at issuance and not at drawing, and the credit itself remains outstanding.

If the examination period runs out with the file still open, the expiry sweep precludes the presentation, and the bank loses its right to assert non-compliance. Three edges lead there, all system-driven and all requiring the clock instant to be strictly past `examination_deadline`. From `UNDER_EXAMINATION` the presentation moves straight to `PRECLUDED` and the drawing **stays** reserved — the bank must honour, so the money remains committed. From `DISCREPANT` it moves to `PRECLUDED` only while `refusal_notice_sent_at` is still unset, and every `IDENTIFIED` Discrepancy stays `IDENTIFIED`: on record, but no longer actionable. From `WAIVER_REQUESTED` it moves to `PRECLUDED` too, and the preclusion is applied **first**: the presentation pass of §3.9 runs ahead of the waiver pass, so the open waiver request is only moved `PENDING → EXPIRED` afterwards in that same sweep, and finding the presentation no longer at `WAIVER_REQUESTED` it upholds its cited discrepancies without demoting the examination dimension back to `DISCREPANT`. Preclusion outranks the demotion. A precluded presentation is settled exactly as a complying one is.

Discrepancies can be listed filtered by `status` and `severity`, and waiver requests filtered by `status`.

### 3.8. Sanctions and Dual-Use Screening

A ScreeningCheck is an immutable-by-convention scoring record. Every check carries the `credit_id` it belongs to — always set, including for document-scope checks, where it is taken from the presentation's credit — a `presentation_id` set only when the `scope` is `PRESENTED_DOCUMENTS` and unset for `CREDIT_PARTIES`, the `scope` itself, the four hit counters `country_hits`, `dual_use_hits`, `keyword_hits` and `transit_hits`, the resulting `risk_score`, and a `result` that is one of `PENDING`, `CLEAR`, `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW`, `BLOCKED`, `RELEASED` or `ESCALATED`, nominally starting at `PENDING` though no persisted row is ever observed there. `screened_by_user_id` records the `ComplianceOfficer` who ran the check, or the literal string `system` when the check was created by a system cascade rather than by an officer — the issuance cascade and a presentation submission are the two that create one that way. `reviewed_by_user_id`, `review_note` and `reviewed_at` stay unset until a review happens.

Scoring uses four fixed reference lists. The sanctioned country codes are `IR`, `KP`, `SY` and `CU`. The high-risk transit codes are `AE`, `HK` and `TR`. The dual-use tariff prefixes are `8471`, `8542`, `9013` and `2811`, matched against the first four characters of a document's `hs_code`. The controlled keywords are `centrifuge`, `maraging` and `gyroscope`, matched as case-insensitive substrings of a document's lowercased `goods_description`.

For the `PRESENTED_DOCUMENTS` scope the inputs are every PresentedDocument row of the presentation, invoices and non-invoices alike. `country_hits` is the number of documents whose `consignee_country` is a sanctioned code **or** any element of whose `transit_country_codes` is a sanctioned code; a document matching in several ways at once counts exactly once. `dual_use_hits` is the number of documents whose `hs_code` begins with one of the dual-use prefixes. `keyword_hits` is the number of documents whose `goods_description` contains any controlled keyword. `transit_hits` is counted differently from the others: it is the number of document-and-transit-code **pairs** where the transit code is a high-risk transit code and is not also a sanctioned code, so one document routed through two high-risk countries contributes two transit hits, while a transit through a sanctioned country contributes to `country_hits` and never to `transit_hits`.

For the `CREDIT_PARTIES` scope the inputs are exactly three parties — the credit's applicant, its beneficiary and its advising bank. The confirming bank is not screened here; it is vetted separately at confirmation time through the confirming-bank eligibility precondition. `country_hits` is the number of those three parties whose `country_code` is a sanctioned code, plus one further hit for each of those three parties whose `sanctions_flag` is true. A party that is both domiciled in a sanctioned country and flagged therefore contributes two. `dual_use_hits`, `keyword_hits` and `transit_hits` are all zero on this scope.

`risk_score` is 100 per country hit, plus 60 per dual-use hit, plus 40 per keyword hit, plus 20 per transit hit, and the total is then capped at 200 — a score never exceeds 200 however many hits accumulate. The `result` follows from the score against two inclusive lower bounds: a score of 100 or more gives `BLOCKED`, a score of 40 or more but below 100 gives `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW`, and anything below 40 gives `CLEAR`. So exactly 40 reviews and exactly 100 blocks. A check is minted with its scored `result` already in place and with all four hit counters and the `risk_score` already persisted, so `PENDING` is a declared value that is never observable on any row and a newly created check reports `version` 1: nothing is transitioned out of `PENDING` and creation increments no version.

When a document-scope check scores `BLOCKED`, the beneficiary of the credit is moved `ACTIVE → BLOCKED`, which itself bars that beneficiary from being named on any new credit. When a `CREDIT_PARTIES` check would score `BLOCKED`, the behaviour is deliberately different: the issuance is aborted with `ERR_SANCTIONS_BLOCK` and **no** ScreeningCheck row is persisted at all, so a blocked issuance leaves no screening history behind.

A `ComplianceOfficer` may re-run screening over a presentation's documents at any time, supplying a `scope` and an optional `screening_note`. Each run creates a new row — screening history is append-only, rows are never updated in place — and `screened_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`. The endpoint is addressed under a presentation, but the supplied `scope` decides what is actually screened and what the row records: `PRESENTED_DOCUMENTS` scores that presentation's own document rows and stores the row with `presentation_id` set, whereas `CREDIT_PARTIES` scores the parent credit's applicant, beneficiary and advising bank and stores the row with `presentation_id` **unset**, carrying only the `credit_id`. Either scope succeeds with HTTP 201 and the new row whatever it scores: a `BLOCKED` result is reported in the row's own `result` field and is never raised as an error here, and — unlike at issuance — a `BLOCKED` `CREDIT_PARTIES` row raised this way is persisted rather than suppressed. The beneficiary cascade `ACTIVE → BLOCKED` fires on the `PRESENTED_DOCUMENTS` scope only. The newest check for a given scope is the one every gate reads; older rows are history.

A check sitting at `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW` is adjudicated by a `ComplianceOfficer` with a `decision` of `RELEASE`, `BLOCK` or `ESCALATE` and an optional `review_note`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `screened_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403) — the officer who ran a screening may not clear it. Only a check at `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW` is reviewable; any other `result` gives `ERR_SCREENING_NOT_REVIEWABLE` (409). All three branches set `reviewed_by_user_id`, `reviewed_at` and `review_note` and increment the check's `version`, and the three side-effect sets then diverge deliberately. `RELEASE` moves `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW → RELEASED` and unblocks settlement. `BLOCK` moves `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW → BLOCKED` and — when the `scope` is `PRESENTED_DOCUMENTS` — cascades the beneficiary `ACTIVE → BLOCKED` just as an automatic block does; it writes to no presentation and no claim of its own, so the freeze on live presentations and claims is realised entirely by the settlement gate below reading the newest check, never by a status change on the frozen rows themselves. `ESCALATE` moves `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW → ESCALATED` and is the third branch of the fork: unlike `RELEASE` it does not unblock settlement, and unlike `BLOCK` it does **not** cascade to the beneficiary's `status` — the beneficiary stays `ACTIVE` and the presentation stays settleable in principle, barred only pending an external licence decision. `CLEAR`, `BLOCKED`, `RELEASED` and `ESCALATED` are all terminal; in particular there is no edge back from `ESCALATED` to `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW`, because the dual-use question has left the bank's hands.

The three non-clear results map onto three distinct settlement gate errors, and the mapping is exact. A newest `PRESENTED_DOCUMENTS` check at `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW` blocks settlement with `ERR_SCREENING_NOT_CLEARED` (409). One at `ESCALATED` blocks settlement with `ERR_DUAL_USE_ESCALATED` (409). One at `BLOCKED` blocks settlement with `ERR_SANCTIONS_BLOCK` (403). Settlement proceeds only when the newest check is `CLEAR` or `RELEASED`. `ERR_SCREENING_REVIEW_PENDING` (409) is a different error with a different scope: it belongs to concluding an examination and to raising a waiver request, and is never returned by settlement.

Screening checks can be listed per credit filtered by `scope`, `result` and `presentation_id`, and per presentation filtered by `result`.

### 3.9. Settlement, Reimbursement and the Clock-Driven Sweep

A complying or precluded presentation is turned into money by a SettlementClaim. The presenting bank lodges it, naming a `claim_type` of `PAYMENT`, `ACCEPTANCE`, `NEGOTIATION`, `DEFERRED_PAYMENT` or `REIMBURSEMENT`, a `claimed_amount_cents` of at least 1, and an optional `claim_note`. A claim's own `status` is `LODGED`, `SETTLED` or `REJECTED`, starting at `LODGED`; `REJECTED` is a declared value that no endpoint and no sweep ever produces, so a claim only ever rests at `LODGED` or `SETTLED`. Every fee and proceeds field — `commission_bps_applied`, `commission_cents`, `discount_cents`, `doc_examination_fee_cents`, `days_to_maturity` and `net_proceeds_cents` — starts at zero and stays there until the claim is settled, as do `settled_at` and `settled_by_user_id`, which start unset. `variant_applied` is the single exception: it is already populated at lodging and is merely rewritten with the same value at settlement.

Lodging requires the presentation's `examination_status` to be `COMPLYING` or `PRECLUDED`; a precluded bank has lost the right to refuse and must honour, so it stands in the same position as a complying one. Anything else is refused with `ERR_PRESENTATION_NOT_COMPLYING` (409). The `settlement_status` must likewise be `UNSETTLED` or `MATURED`, and any other value — `CLAIM_LODGED`, `PAID`, `ACCEPTED`, `NEGOTIATED`, `REIMBURSED` or `VOID` — reports that same `ERR_PRESENTATION_NOT_COMPLYING` (409), the examination dimension being judged before the settlement one. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the presentation's `presenting_bank_id`, else `ERR_PRINCIPAL_NOT_PARTY` (403), and that bank must still be a nominated bank of the credit — its `presenting_bank_id` must equal the credit's `advising_bank_id`, the credit's `confirming_bank_id` or the beneficiary's `nominated_bank_id` — else `ERR_BANK_NOT_NOMINATED` (422). At most one claim per presentation may be live, so a second attempt while one is `LODGED` is refused with `ERR_LIVE_REQUEST_EXISTS` (409); exactly as with the waiver-request uniqueness test of §3.7, this one test is evaluated **ahead of** both state guards rather than in its band 7 position, because a presentation carrying a `LODGED` claim already sits at `CLAIM_LODGED` and a state-first reading would report `ERR_PRESENTATION_NOT_COMPLYING` and make the uniqueness error unreachable. If the newest `PRESENTED_DOCUMENTS` screening is `BLOCKED` the claim is refused with `ERR_SANCTIONS_BLOCK` (403), and that safety check is evaluated **before** the claim-type coherence check, so a blocked presentation lodged with an inadmissible `claim_type` returns the sanctions error and not the mismatch error. The same band-5 step refuses a newest check sitting at `ESCALATED` with `ERR_DUAL_USE_ESCALATED` (409). A newest check still at `HIT_PENDING_REVIEW`, by contrast, does **not** bar lodging at all: the awaiting-review guard is the settle step's alone, so a claim may be lodged over a presentation whose screening is out for review and simply cannot be settled until the review lands.

`claim_type` must match what the credit's `available_by` admits, else `ERR_CLAIM_TYPE_MISMATCH` (422). The admission table is exact: `SIGHT_PAYMENT` admits `PAYMENT` and `REIMBURSEMENT`; `NEGOTIATION` admits `NEGOTIATION` and `REIMBURSEMENT`; `ACCEPTANCE` admits `ACCEPTANCE` and `REIMBURSEMENT`; `DEFERRED_PAYMENT` admits `DEFERRED_PAYMENT` and `REIMBURSEMENT`. Every availability admits `REIMBURSEMENT`, and no availability admits any other form than its own. `claimed_amount_cents` must equal the presentation's frozen `drawn_amount_cents` exactly — no partial claim and no over-claim — else `ERR_CLAIM_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422).

Lodging sets the claim's `status` to `LODGED`, derives `variant_applied` from the `claim_type` **alone** — `PAYMENT` and `REIMBURSEMENT` give `SIGHT_HONOUR`, `NEGOTIATION` gives `NEGOTIATION_UNDER_DISCOUNT`, and `ACCEPTANCE` and `DEFERRED_PAYMENT` give `ACCEPTANCE_AT_MATURITY`, the credit's `available_by` never entering the derivation — and moves the presentation's `settlement_status` `UNSETTLED → CLAIM_LODGED`. Nothing at all is written on the credit: its drawable ledger is not re-derived and its `version` does not move, because `CLAIM_LODGED` sits in the very same live set as the `UNSETTLED` or `MATURED` state it replaced. A post-maturity reimbursement claim instead moves it `MATURED → CLAIM_LODGED`: the settlement dimension is cyclic through `CLAIM_LODGED` for usance credits, and this second visit is a fresh claim row with `claim_type` `REIMBURSEMENT`, checked for the same acting-principal identity and the same amount equality.

A `TradeFinanceOfficer` then settles the claim, optionally with a `settlement_note`. The claim must be `LODGED`, else `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_LODGED` (409), and the presentation must be at `CLAIM_LODGED`, else that same `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_LODGED` (409) — the claim's own state is judged first and the presentation's second. The newest `PRESENTED_DOCUMENTS` screening must be `CLEAR` or `RELEASED`, with the three gate errors as described in the screening section. On the acceptance and deferred-payment forms the presentation's `maturity_date` must be set, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422) — a sight-only presentation cannot be accepted at maturity; being a fact about the presentation rather than about the payload, that one check is deliberately made after both screening gates rather than in band 3.

The proceeds are then priced as a strict waterfall, in three costing steps and one deduction step. Step one is commission, taken as a single rounding on the whole drawn amount: `commission_bps_applied` is 12 basis points on the `SIGHT_HONOUR` variant and 20 basis points on the other two, so the rate is keyed on the variant and never on the credit's `available_by` — a `REIMBURSEMENT` claim is a sight honour whatever the credit's availability and is therefore priced at 12 even on a usance credit — and `commission_cents` is `drawn_amount_cents` multiplied by `commission_bps_applied`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up. Step two is the per-document examination fee, and its rounding order is pinned: walk the presentation's PresentedDocument rows in `created_at` ascending order, and for each one round its own fee to whole cents **before** adding it to the running total. A `COMMERCIAL_INVOICE` contributes its `invoice_amount_cents` multiplied by 5 basis points, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up; every other document type contributes a flat 3,500 cents. Every row contributes, not only invoices. Summing exact per-document amounts and rounding once at the end is wrong and can differ from the correct answer by up to half the `document_count`. Step three is the discount charge, which applies only to the usance forms: for a sight honour, `days_to_maturity` is 0 and `discount_cents` is 0, while for the usance forms `days_to_maturity` is the whole number of days from the clock date to the presentation's `maturity_date` — a **signed** figure that is neither floored at zero nor rejected, so a maturity date already behind the clock date gives a negative `days_to_maturity` and correspondingly negative `discount_cents`, which raises the net rather than lowering it, an exact half on a negative amount rounding away from zero as §2.6 requires — and `discount_cents` is `drawn_amount_cents` multiplied by 450 basis points, multiplied by `days_to_maturity`, divided by the product of 10,000 and a 360-day year basis, rounded half up. Step four deducts the charges from the drawn amount in the fixed order commission, then discount, then document fees: `net_proceeds_cents` is `drawn_amount_cents` minus `commission_cents` minus `discount_cents` minus `doc_examination_fee_cents`. The net may never go below zero, and a result that would be negative is refused with `ERR_CHARGES_EXCEED_PROCEEDS` (422). The drawn amount used throughout is the value frozen at submission, never a re-sum at settlement time.

Three variants use that arithmetic differently, and which one applied is recorded on the claim as `variant_applied`, whose value is `SIGHT_HONOUR`, `NEGOTIATION_UNDER_DISCOUNT` or `ACCEPTANCE_AT_MATURITY`. `SIGHT_HONOUR` applies to a `PAYMENT` claim on a `SIGHT_PAYMENT` credit and to every `REIMBURSEMENT` claim: commission at 12 basis points and no discount, which for an identical presentation yields a strictly larger net than the negotiation variant whenever the maturity date is still ahead of the settlement date. `NEGOTIATION_UNDER_DISCOUNT` applies to a `NEGOTIATION` claim: commission at 20 basis points and a discount charged for the whole run from the settlement date to `maturity_date` on the 360-day basis, so the same drawn amount and the same documents yield materially smaller proceeds. `ACCEPTANCE_AT_MATURITY` applies to `ACCEPTANCE` and `DEFERRED_PAYMENT` claims and uses arithmetic identical to `NEGOTIATION_UNDER_DISCOUNT`, but with a different money movement.

Settling sets `status` `LODGED → SETTLED`, `settled_at` to the clock instant and `settled_by_user_id` to the acting officer, and writes `variant_applied`, `commission_bps_applied`, `commission_cents`, `discount_cents`, `doc_examination_fee_cents`, `days_to_maturity` and `net_proceeds_cents`. Then the presentation's settlement dimension moves according to the `claim_type` rather than to the variant — the two part company on `REIMBURSEMENT`, which is priced as a `SIGHT_HONOUR` but lands on `REIMBURSED` and not on `PAID`. A sight honour on a `PAYMENT` claim moves `CLAIM_LODGED → PAID`, and the drawing moves out of the credit's `reserved_cents` into its `drawn_settled_cents` — both by exactly `drawn_amount_cents`, so `available_cents` is unchanged by settlement. A negotiation moves `CLAIM_LODGED → NEGOTIATED` with the same money movement. An acceptance or deferred payment moves `CLAIM_LODGED → ACCEPTED` and deliberately does **not** release `reserved_cents` and does not raise `drawn_settled_cents`: the bank's undertaking runs until maturity, so the money stays committed. Once the clock date reaches or passes `maturity_date`, the expiry sweep moves that presentation `ACCEPTED → MATURED`, the presenting bank lodges its `REIMBURSEMENT` claim, and settling that claim moves `CLAIM_LODGED → REIMBURSED` and finally moves the drawing from `reserved_cents` into `drawn_settled_cents`. The reimbursement leg is priced as a sight honour with `discount_cents` fixed at 0, because the discount was already taken at acceptance. On every one of these branches the only rows settlement writes are the claim, the presentation and — through the drawable re-derivation — the credit: the facility is untouched, exposure being committed at issuance rather than at drawing, and no confirming bank's line is re-derived either.

Settlement can also finish the credit. The credit moves `ISSUED → UTILISED` once its `available_cents` is exactly zero and no presentation of the credit is still in `examination_status` `RECEIVED`, `UNDER_EXAMINATION`, `DISCREPANT` or `WAIVER_REQUESTED`. The fiscal half of the test reads `available_cents` **alone**, and `reserved_cents` need not itself be zero: a credit whose reserved and settled drawings together exhaust `max_drawable_cents` is utilised even while an accepted drawing is still reserved against it. Both conditions must hold: a fully drawn credit with another presentation still under examination stays `ISSUED`. The test is applied only from `ISSUED` and only where a settlement has just moved the drawable ledger — no other write and no sweep pass ever utilises a credit.

Claims can be listed per presentation filtered by `status` and `claim_type`.

Time itself is operated explicitly. A `SystemOperator` advances the logical clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant. The service starts at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and this is the only way the clock ever moves. Setting the clock mutates no entity at all — deadlines are re-evaluated only when a sweep or an ordinary write runs — and an instant earlier than the current logical clock is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_REGRESSION` (422), while an instant exactly equal to the current value is accepted and changes nothing. A `to_iso8601` that is absent, null, not a string, longer than 40 characters or not parseable as an ISO 8601 instant is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422), judged before the regression comparison is attempted. The endpoint returns no resource body.

Every clock-driven transition is then applied by a single operational sweep, which a `SystemOperator` runs with an optional `dry_run` flag and which reports the counters `credits_expired`, `presentations_precluded`, `waiver_requests_expired`, `amendments_lapsed`, `allocations_released` and `facilities_reactivated`. When `dry_run` is true the counts are reported and nothing is mutated: the five passes are genuinely executed against a snapshot taken of the whole store beforehand, and that snapshot is restored before the response is written, so the counters a dry run reports are exactly the ones a real run at the same clock would report. The sweep runs five passes in a fixed order, and each pass processes its rows in `created_at` ascending order, which makes the whole sweep deterministic and idempotent — a second sweep at the same clock returns all zeros and mutates nothing at all, each pass re-testing its own precondition and the facility pass additionally skipping any facility whose stored ledger figures already agree with its rows, so no `version` is bumped and no `updated_at` refreshed by a no-op sweep.

Pass one precludes presentations: every presentation whose `examination_deadline` has passed while its `examination_status` is `UNDER_EXAMINATION`, `DISCREPANT` or `WAIVER_REQUESTED` moves to `PRECLUDED`, and because the bank lost its right to refuse and must honour, the drawing stays reserved and no credit ledger is re-derived. The maturity flip rides in the same walk over the same rows: a presentation whose `settlement_status` is `ACCEPTED` and whose `maturity_date` is on or before the clock date moves `ACCEPTED → MATURED` here, and it is the one sweep transition with no counter of its own. Pass two expires waiver requests: every request past its `deadline_at` while `PENDING` moves to `EXPIRED`, its `responded_at` staying unset because an expiry is not an answer, and each cited discrepancy still at `IDENTIFIED` moves to `UPHELD`. The presentation is then demoted `WAIVER_REQUESTED → DISCREPANT` **only** while it still sits at `WAIVER_REQUESTED` — pass one may already have precluded it, and that preclusion outranks the demotion — while its `discrepancy_count` is re-aggregated and its `version` advanced either way. Pass three lapses amendments: every amendment past its `consent_deadline_date` while `PENDING_CONSENT` moves to `EXPIRED_NO_CONSENT`, keeps `window_end_effective` at `consent_deadline_date`, and returns its credit `AMENDMENT_PENDING → ISSUED` on the original terms — but only when no other amendment of that same credit is still `PENDING_CONSENT`; while one is, the credit stays `AMENDMENT_PENDING` and is left for a later sweep. Pass four expires credits: every credit whose `presentation_deadline_date` is strictly before the clock date, whose `status` is `ISSUED`, and whose `reserved_cents` is zero moves to `EXPIRED`, which is terminal, releasing its `OPEN` FacilityAllocation and any confirmation occupation it held; a credit whose `presentation_deadline_date` is unset or unparseable is skipped rather than expired. `allocations_released` counts one for each such credit that actually had an `OPEN` row to release and nothing for one that had none. The facility is re-derived by that release, but the `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` flip is deliberately withheld from this pass so that pass five owns it and `facilities_reactivated` is counted exactly once; the `ON_HOLD` siblings are likewise released only there. Where the credit named a `confirming_bank_id`, that bank's `confirming_line_used_cents` is re-derived, its `eligibility_score` refreshed and its `version` advanced. Only `ISSUED` credits are scanned here, and that is sufficient precisely because pass three has already returned every credit whose consent window lapsed from `AMENDMENT_PENDING` to `ISSUED`, so the same sweep still catches it. Pass five re-derives facility ledgers: every facility is examined, not only the suspended ones, and one whose stored `allocated_cents`, `available_cents` and `restrict_threshold_cents` already agree with its `OPEN` rows and whose status needs no flip is skipped entirely and not even touched. For the rest the ledger is recomputed exactly as §3.1 describes, so a facility that has fallen back below its `restrict_threshold_cents` returns `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` with its `ON_HOLD` credits returning to `APPROVED` in `created_at` ascending order, while one found at or above the threshold while still `ACTIVE` is suspended here and holds its `APPROVED` siblings. `facilities_reactivated` counts only the `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` flips, and only on facilities whose `suspended_reason` was `RESTRICT_THRESHOLD_BREACH`.

The pass order matters and is normative. A presentation precluded in pass one keeps its drawing reserved, which is exactly what stops pass four from expiring its credit in the same sweep; an allocation released in pass four is what allows pass five to reactivate the facility in the same sweep.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every collection lives at the root of the API: there is no common prefix, and each record is reachable either from its own collection or as a sub-resource of its parent. Endpoints that create a new record return HTTP 201; endpoints that modify or transition an existing record, and all reads, return HTTP 200. The clock endpoint returns 200 with an empty data object. Every endpoint requires the `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` headers, and all list endpoints accept `limit` and `offset` in addition to the filters named in their description. Every free-text narrative field the API accepts — each `*_note`, each `reason` and `declined_reason`, and a document's `goods_description` and a discrepancy's `description` — is capped at 500 characters, and exceeding the cap is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422); the length is counted on the value exactly as submitted, before any trimming, and the value is stored exactly as submitted too. The reference-style free-text fields carry their own tighter caps, named where each appears: 60 characters for a presentation's `cover_letter_reference` and a discrepancy `code`, 120 for `beneficiary_consent_reference`, `issuer_constraint`, `incoterm_place` and `expiry_place`, and 200 for `issuer_name` and for the party name fields.
