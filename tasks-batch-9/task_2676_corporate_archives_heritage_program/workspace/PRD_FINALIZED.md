# Corporate Archives & Heritage Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

The corporate archives and heritage office sits at an uncomfortable intersection. It is simultaneously a records-compliance function answerable to counsel and the retention schedule, a preservation laboratory answerable to environmental standards and a finite conservation budget, a reading room answerable to researchers and journalists who want material the company may not be free to release, and a brand-heritage service that must prove, decades after the fact, that a mark has been used continuously in commerce. Today these obligations are tracked in disconnected spreadsheets: an appraisal scored in one workbook, a litigation hold emailed by counsel, a donation to an external repository recorded on a paper receipt. When a legal hold lands on a series that already has three researcher requests in screening, nobody can say with confidence which requests were frozen, which screenings were invalidated, or what happens to them when the hold lifts.

This document specifies the API that replaces that patchwork. The system governs the full arc of an archival holding: appraisal for historical value against the governing retention schedule, accession processing with documented provenance, arrangement and finding-aid publication, physical and digital preservation planning with format migration inside environmentally compliant storage zones, access policy across internal business users, external researchers and media, trademark and brand-heritage evidence packages, anniversary and museum exhibit loans, the oral history program with its consent and transcription tracks, and finally deaccession by destruction or donation to an external repository. Every resource is addressed at the top level of the API — there is no URL nesting — and relationships between resources are carried by explicit identifier fields in request bodies and by filtered list queries.

Two properties of the specification deserve emphasis up front, because they shape almost every rule that follows. First, **consistency is immediate**: whenever an operation has consequences for other records — a hold suspending a series and freezing its access requests, a deaccession approval invalidating published finding aids, a completed migration retiring a preservation plan's at-risk census — those consequences are applied inside the same request, so the response already reflects a fully reconciled world. Second, **time is server-controlled**: retention expiry, oral-history embargoes, exhibit display windows and migration treatment windows are all evaluated against a logical UTC clock that the platform advances explicitly, never against wall-clock time.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across the entire API: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, audit fields, the logical clock, the order in which rejections are decided, the stale-version guards, cascade discipline, segregation of duties, numeric precision, and the environmental standard that governs storage zones.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that operator acts. The system recognises exactly seven roles, spelled here as they must appear in the header: `ArchivesDirector`, `Archivist`, `PreservationOfficer`, `AccessServicesOfficer`, `LegalCounsel`, `Requester`, and `SystemOperator`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` carries a value outside that set of seven, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well formed but the presented role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called — the authorisation column of the endpoint table in §4 is definitive — the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six business roles hold largely disjoint capability sets, in the manner of a segregation-of-duties model rather than a hierarchy: no role is a superset of another, and elevation is not possible. The `ArchivesDirector` heads the office and owns retention-schedule configuration, appraisal finalisation, finding-aid publication, accession rejection, series disposal, heritage clearance of a deaccession, and deaccession approval and rejection. The `Archivist` is the processing archivist: creating series, accessions, archival items, finding aids, oral history interviews, deaccession proposals and appraisal drafts, submitting aids for review, screening appraisals, and carrying out physical custody movements such as exhibit check-out, return and deaccession execution. The `PreservationOfficer` owns storage zones and their environmental readings, preservation plans, the entire migration-job lifecycle, and the conservation confirmation of an exhibit loan. The `AccessServicesOfficer` registers and suspends requesters, may file requests on their behalf, opens and completes confidentiality screenings, and begins, approves, denies and closes access requests. The `LegalCounsel` owns legal holds, certification of trademark evidence packages, and legal clearance of deaccessions. The `Requester` is the external researcher, media representative or internal business user on whose behalf access is sought. The `SystemOperator` holds no business capability whatsoever; it exists solely to drive the logical clock.

Identity semantics differ between the `Requester` role and all others, and the distinction is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `Requester`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Requester` row that access services created; ownership checks compare a resource's requester reference to `X-User-ID`, so a `Requester` may file an access request only when the `requester_id` in the body equals `X-User-ID`, and may revise only a request whose `requester_id` equals `X-User-ID`. A violation of either is rejected with `ERR_NOT_REQUEST_OWNER` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `ArchivesDirector`, `Archivist`, `PreservationOfficer`, `AccessServicesOfficer`, `LegalCounsel` or `SystemOperator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow demands an audit stamp — as `issued_by` on a legal hold, `appraised_by` on an appraisal, `requested_by` and `approved_by` on a migration job, `screened_by` on a screening, `decided_by` on an access request, and `legal_cleared_by`, `heritage_cleared_by` and `approved_by` on a deaccession proposal — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`. Those recorded opaque identifiers are, however, compared against each other: see §2.9.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when the client omits it the server applies a default of 10, and a supplied value must be at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0. Every list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope; `total_count` counts the rows matching the filters before pagination is applied.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order.

Beyond `limit` and `offset`, most list endpoints accept optional equality filters that stand in for the URL nesting this API deliberately avoids. Retention schedules may be filtered by `disposition_action`. Record series accept `retention_schedule_id`, `current_appraisal_id`, `disposition_state` and `business_unit`. Legal holds accept `record_series_id` and `status`; appraisals accept `record_series_id` and `status`; accessions accept `record_series_id` and `status`. Archival items accept `accession_id`, `storage_zone_id`, `status` and `original_format`. Storage zones accept `environmental_status`. Finding aids accept `accession_id` and `status`. Preservation plans accept `storage_zone_id` and `status`, and migration jobs accept `preservation_plan_id`, `archival_item_id` and `status`. Requesters accept `requester_class` and `status`. Access requests accept `requester_id`, `record_series_id`, `finding_aid_id` and `status`; confidentiality screenings accept `access_request_id` and `status`. Oral history interviews accept `accession_id`, `consent_status`, `transcription_status` and `release_state`. Trademark evidence packages accept `record_series_id` and `status`; exhibit loans accept `archival_item_id` and `status`; deaccession proposals accept `archival_item_id`, `status` and `route`. All of these filters are optional, and omitting one means the corresponding column is not constrained.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and the Immutable Configuration Row

Every resource the API mints carries an `id` that is a server-generated UUID string with no type prefix and no client influence, together with a `created_at` timestamp expressed as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits (for example `2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z`).

Every instant-valued field the API returns is rendered in exactly that form — normalised to UTC and carried to exactly three fractional digits — and not only `created_at`. That covers `updated_at`, every audit stamp listed in §2.5 such as `checked_out_at` and `returned_at`, every derived instant such as `retention_expiry_utc` and a loan's `recovery_until`, and every instant the client itself supplied and the resource carries — `effective_from`, `interview_date`, `embargo_until`, and each `window_start` and `window_end` — which are echoed back in that normalised form rather than byte-for-byte as sent, so a client that supplies `2026-08-15T00:00:00Z` reads the stored field back as `2026-08-15T00:00:00.000Z`. A field whose value is null is returned as null.

All resources except one also carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade, and including mutations that only touch derived counters. A record series whose `blocked_access_request_count` moves because a hold cascade blocked one of its access requests has been mutated: its `version` increments and its `updated_at` is refreshed, exactly as if a client had written to it directly.

The single exception is the retention schedule. A retention schedule is an immutable configuration row: it carries `id` and `created_at` only, has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and there is no endpoint that modifies one. Once registered, its `code`, `retention_years`, `disposition_action`, `legal_citation` and `min_appraisal_score_for_permanent` stand for the life of the deployment.

### 2.5. The Logical Archive Clock

Every temporal comparison in this specification — retention expiry, oral-history embargo release, the opening of an exhibit display window, the placement of a migration treatment window, and every audit stamp such as `activated_at`, `released_at`, `published_at`, `satisfied_at`, `certified_at`, `consent_signed_at`, `completed_at`, `checked_out_at` and `returned_at` — reads a single server-held logical UTC clock. The clock never falls back to wall-clock time. Before any operator sets it, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`.

Only a `SystemOperator` may move the clock, by supplying the target instant as `current_utc`. Moving the clock mutates no entity; it simply changes the instant that every subsequent request observes. Comparisons against the clock are inclusive wherever this document says "at or after": an instant that exactly equals a retention expiry counts as elapsed, an instant that exactly equals an embargo end counts as released, and an instant that exactly equals a display window's start counts as open.

### 2.6. Rejection Precedence Across the Rule Ladder

Many operations in this system are guarded by several independent rules at once, and which error a caller sees is part of the contract rather than an implementation detail. Rules are therefore evaluated in a fixed ladder, and the **first** rule that fails determines the response. The ladder, from earliest to latest, is:

1. **Header authentication** — `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present, and the role one of the seven declared in §2.1. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorisation** — the presented role appears in the called endpoint's authorised list. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Payload shape** — every required body field present, every enum value a declared member of its set, every integer and number inside the range declared for it in §3, `requested_item_ids` and `evidence_item_ids` free of duplicate elements, and, on every windowed create, `window_end` strictly later than `window_start`. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint that accepts a body.
4. **Path resolution** — the `{id}` in the request path resolves to an existing row of the endpoint's own resource type. Failure: that resource type's not-found code (404), listed in §5 — for example `ERR_MIGRATION_JOB_NOT_FOUND` for a migration job path, `ERR_ACCESS_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` for an access-request path. Applies to every endpoint with a path parameter.
5. **Reference resolution** — every identifier in the request body that points at another resource must resolve. These are checked strictly left to right in the order the endpoint declares those fields, and the first unresolved reference determines the error, which is the *referenced* type's not-found code (404) — for example `ERR_STORAGE_ZONE_NOT_FOUND` when an archival-item create names a zone that does not exist. Applies to `POST /record-series`, `POST /legal-holds`, `POST /appraisals`, `POST /accessions`, `POST /archival-items`, `POST /finding-aids`, `POST /preservation-plans`, `POST /migration-jobs`, `POST /access-requests`, `POST /confidentiality-screenings`, `POST /oral-history-interviews`, `POST /trademark-evidence-packages`, `POST /exhibit-loans` and `POST /deaccession-proposals`. Individual elements of `requested_item_ids` and `evidence_item_ids` that name no existing item are reported as `ERR_ARCHIVAL_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`.
6. **Business-key uniqueness** — a retention schedule's `code`, a legal hold's `matter_number` and a storage zone's `zone_code` must each be unique across all existing rows of their type. Failure: `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE_KEY` (409). Applies to `POST /retention-schedules`, `POST /legal-holds` and `POST /storage-zones`.
7. **Requester ownership** — when the acting role is `Requester`, `X-User-ID` must equal the `requester_id` in the body on a create, or the target request's `requester_id` on a revision. Failure: `ERR_NOT_REQUEST_OWNER` (403). Applies to `POST /access-requests` and `POST /access-requests/{id}/revise`.
8. **Legal clearance present** — on a deaccession approval, `legal_cleared_by` must not be null. Failure: `ERR_LEGAL_CLEARANCE_MISSING` (422). This sits deliberately *ahead* of the generic state guard at rung 11, so that an approval attempted on a proposal that has only heritage clearance names the missing legal clearance rather than returning an opaque state error. Applies to `POST /deaccession-proposals/{id}/approve`.
9. **Heritage clearance present** — on a deaccession approval, `heritage_cleared_by` must not be null. Failure: `ERR_HERITAGE_CLEARANCE_MISSING` (422). This sits after the legal-clearance rung and still ahead of the generic state guard, so that an approval attempted on a proposal that has only legal clearance names the missing heritage clearance. Applies to `POST /deaccession-proposals/{id}/approve`.
10. **Stale-version guard** — the expected-version field required by §2.7 must equal the current `version` of the named parent row. Failure: `ERR_STALE_SERIES_VERSION` on `POST /appraisals`, `ERR_STALE_ACCESS_REQUEST_VERSION` on `POST /confidentiality-screenings`, both HTTP 412.
11. **Provenance documented** — an accession may be processed only once `provenance_documented` is true. Failure: `ERR_PROVENANCE_NOT_DOCUMENTED` (422). This too sits ahead of the generic state guard, so that processing an accession still in `RECEIVED` names the undocumented provenance rather than a generic state error. Applies to `POST /accessions/{id}/process`.
12. **State guard** — the target row must be in a state from which the requested transition is declared in §3. This rung also covers the derived-state guards: a finding aid must have `stale_item_count` equal to 0 before it can be published, and a preservation plan must have no job still open before it can be closed. Failure: the resource's own state-invalid code (409) — `ERR_SERIES_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_HOLD_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_APPRAISAL_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ACCESSION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_FINDING_AID_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_JOB_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_REQUESTER_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_SCREENING_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_INTERVIEW_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PACKAGE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_LOAN_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_PROPOSAL_STATE_INVALID`. Applies to every action endpoint that drives a state transition.
13. **Single active hold per series** — a hold may not be activated while a different hold on the same series is already `ACTIVE`. Failure: `ERR_SERIES_ALREADY_ON_HOLD` (409). Applies to `POST /legal-holds/{id}/activate`.
14. **Segregation of duties** — see §2.9. Failure: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to `POST /migration-jobs/{id}/approve`, `POST /access-requests/{id}/approve`, `POST /access-requests/{id}/deny` and `POST /deaccession-proposals/{id}/approve`.
15. **Series not frozen** — the governing record series must not be in `SUSPENDED` disposition state, and its `legal_hold_active` flag must be false. Failure: `ERR_SERIES_DISPOSITION_SUSPENDED` (409). This guard covers seven operations: `POST /appraisals/{id}/screen`, `POST /appraisals/{id}/finalize`, `POST /accessions/{id}/verify-provenance`, `POST /access-requests/{id}/begin-screening`, `POST /access-requests/{id}/approve`, `POST /deaccession-proposals/{id}/legal-clearance` and `POST /record-series/{id}/dispose`.
16. **Series not permanent** — the governing series must not be in `PERMANENT` disposition state, because permanently designated heritage records may neither be cleared for removal nor disposed. Failure: `ERR_SERIES_PERMANENT` (409). This guard covers three operations: `POST /deaccession-proposals/{id}/legal-clearance`, `POST /deaccession-proposals/{id}/heritage-clearance` and `POST /record-series/{id}/dispose`.
17. **Consent signed** — a transcript may be approved only when the interview's `consent_status` is `SIGNED`. Failure: `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_SIGNED` (422). Applies to `POST /oral-history-interviews/{id}/transcription` when the requested action is `APPROVE`.
18. **Embargo elapsed** — the interview's `embargo_until` must be null, or the logical clock must be at or after it. Failure: `ERR_EMBARGO_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to `POST /oral-history-interviews/{id}/transcription` when the requested action is `APPROVE`. Note the ordering against rung 17: an interview that is both unconsented and embargoed reports the consent failure.
19. **Retention elapsed** — the logical clock must be at or after the series' `retention_expiry_utc`. Failure: `ERR_RETENTION_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED` (422). Applies to `POST /record-series/{id}/dispose`.
20. **Display window open** — the logical clock must be at or after the loan's `window_start`. Failure: `ERR_LOAN_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` (422). Applies to `POST /exhibit-loans/{id}/check-out`.
21. **Non-empty item set** — `requested_item_ids` must contain at least one element. Failure: `ERR_NO_ITEMS_REQUESTED` (422). Applies to `POST /access-requests` and `POST /access-requests/{id}/revise`. An empty array is well-formed shape, so it survives rung 3 and is caught here as a business rejection.
22. **Requested items belong to the series** — every archival item named in `requested_item_ids` must sit under an accession whose `record_series_id` equals the request's `record_series_id`. Failure: `ERR_ITEM_NOT_IN_SERIES` (422). Applies to `POST /access-requests` and `POST /access-requests/{id}/revise`.
23. **Cited finding aid published** — when a request's `finding_aid_id` is not null, the referenced aid must be in `PUBLISHED` status. Failure: `ERR_FINDING_AID_NOT_PUBLISHED` (422). Applies to `POST /access-requests/{id}/approve`.
24. **Declared units reconciled** — `processed_unit_count` must equal `declared_unit_count`. Failure: `ERR_UNIT_COUNT_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to `POST /accessions/{id}/process`.
25. **Requester in good standing** — the request's requester must be in `ACTIVE` status. Failure: `ERR_REQUESTER_SUSPENDED` (409). Applies to `POST /access-requests/{id}/begin-screening` and `POST /access-requests/{id}/approve`.
26. **Screening decision available** — the request's `current_screening_id` must resolve to a screening in `COMPLETED` status whose `decision` is `GRANT_FULL` for a `FULL` approval, or one of `GRANT_FULL` or `GRANT_REDACTED` for a `REDACTED` approval. Failure: `ERR_SCREENING_DECISION_MISSING` (422). Applies to `POST /access-requests/{id}/approve` and `POST /access-requests/{id}/deny`.
27. **Donation recipient named** — a proposal whose `route` is `DONATION` must carry a non-empty `recipient_repository`. Failure: `ERR_RECIPIENT_REPOSITORY_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to `POST /deaccession-proposals` and `POST /deaccession-proposals/{id}/approve`.
28. **Item condition suitable for display** — the item's `condition_grade` must be `GOOD` or `FAIR`. Failure: `ERR_ITEM_CONDITION_UNSUITABLE` (422). Applies to `POST /exhibit-loans/{id}/confirm`.
29. **No live loan on the item** — no exhibit loan on the item may be in `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `OUT`. Failure: `ERR_ITEM_HAS_ACTIVE_LOAN` (409). Applies to `POST /deaccession-proposals/{id}/approve`.
30. **Sufficient shelved units** — the item's `units_on_shelf` must be at least the number of units the operation moves. Failure: `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SHELF_UNITS` (422). This guard covers three operations: `POST /exhibit-loans/{id}/confirm`, `POST /exhibit-loans/{id}/check-out` and `POST /deaccession-proposals/{id}/execute`.
31. **Display window free** — the new loan's window, extended by the light-recovery buffer described in §3.8, must not collide with any live loan on the same item. Failure: `ERR_LOAN_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to `POST /exhibit-loans`.
32. **Zone environmentally compliant** — the item's storage zone must be in `COMPLIANT` environmental status. Failure: `ERR_ZONE_NOT_COMPLIANT` (422). Applies to `POST /migration-jobs/{id}/start`.
33. **Zone treatment capacity** — inserting this job's treatment window must not push the zone's simultaneous approved-or-in-progress job count above `max_overlapping_migration_jobs` at any instant. Failure: `ERR_ZONE_MIGRATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to `POST /migration-jobs/{id}/approve`.
34. **Qualifying evidence present** — a trademark evidence package must have at least one qualifying evidence item. Failure: `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_EVIDENCE` (422). Applies to `POST /trademark-evidence-packages/{id}/certify`.
35. **Redaction required above the risk threshold** — a `FULL` approval requires the request's `post_screen_risk_score` to be strictly below the redaction threshold of 75; at or above it, only a `REDACTED` scope is permitted. Failure: `ERR_RISK_THRESHOLD_REQUIRES_REDACTION` (422). Applies to `POST /access-requests/{id}/approve`.
36. **Preservation budget ceiling** — the plan's `budget_committed_cents` plus the job's `committed_cost_cents` must not exceed the plan's `budget_cents`; equality is permitted. Failure: `ERR_PRESERVATION_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to `POST /migration-jobs/{id}/approve`.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Creation of Superseding Children

Two creation operations in this system silently retire an existing sibling, and both are protected by an explicit stale-version guard.

Creating an appraisal for a record series supersedes every earlier appraisal on that series. A client that has not observed the series' latest state would therefore discard an appraisal it never read. To prevent that, `POST /appraisals` requires `expected_series_version` as a non-optional body field, carrying the `version` of the record series as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the series' current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SERIES_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written.

Creating a confidentiality screening for an access request supersedes the prior screening on that request. `POST /confidentiality-screenings` therefore requires `expected_access_request_version` as a non-optional body field, carrying the `version` of the access request as the client last observed it. If it does not equal the request's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ACCESS_REQUEST_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no screening is opened.

No other endpoint in this API takes an expected-version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. Note that the guard is against staleness, not against contention: any write that has advanced the parent's `version` since the client's last read — a cascade, a counter refresh, another operator's action — is enough to make a previously valid expected value stale.
### 2.8. Cascade Discipline and Immediate Reconciliation

Three operations in this system reach well beyond the resource named in the path, and in each case the whole cascade is applied inside the triggering request. Nothing is deferred, queued or reconciled later; by the time the response is written every derived field named below already holds its post-cascade value.

Activating a legal hold runs a three-hop suspension cascade. Hop one suspends the governing record series, capturing the state it was in so it can be restored later. Hop two blocks the series' in-flight access requests. Hop three supersedes the open confidentiality screenings on the requests that hop two blocked. Completing a migration job runs a two-hop preservation cascade: hop one returns the treated item to the shelf with its new preservation format and its digital surrogate flag raised, and hop two re-censuses the funding plan's at-risk holdings, which may in turn retire the plan. Approving a deaccession proposal runs a three-hop staleness cascade: hop one moves the item into pending disposition, hop two marks every published finding aid over that item's accession as needing revision, and hop three forces every access request citing one of those aids and already decided in the affirmative back into revalidation. Section 3 specifies each hop's preconditions, its side effects, and the counters that record how far it reached.

Each entity a cascade touches is mutated in its own right: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments by 1. A single hold activation can therefore bump the version of the hold, the series, several access requests and several screenings, all within one request.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties Within a Role

Three approvals in this system must not be performed by the operator who set them up, even though both operators legitimately hold the same role. The comparison is made on `X-User-ID`, and a collision is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

The officer approving a migration job must present an `X-User-ID` different from the job's `requested_by`. The officer approving or denying an access request must present an `X-User-ID` different from the `screened_by` of the request's current screening. And the director approving a deaccession proposal must present an `X-User-ID` different from the proposal's `legal_cleared_by`. There is no override, and no role that is exempt.

### 2.10. Money, Units and Numeric Precision

Two quantities in this system are strictly conserved, with zero tolerance for drift.

The first is money. All monetary amounts are integer counts of cents; there are no fractional cents anywhere in the API, and every rounding step named in §3 uses the round-half-up convention. A preservation plan's `budget_committed_cents` must, after every single request including every cascade, equal the exact sum of `committed_cost_cents` over the migration jobs on that plan whose status is `APPROVED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. Its `budget_spent_cents` must equal the exact sum of `committed_cost_cents` over the jobs on that plan whose status is `COMPLETED`. Jobs in `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `REJECTED` and `CANCELLED` contribute to neither figure. Because approval commits exactly a job's `committed_cost_cents` and cancellation from either `APPROVED` or `IN_PROGRESS` releases exactly the same amount, an approve-then-cancel sequence returns `budget_committed_cents` to precisely its pre-approval value.

The second is physical units. For every archival item in any status other than `REJECTED_ITEM`, `unit_count` must equal `units_on_shelf` plus `units_on_loan` plus `units_deaccessioned` after every request. Checking out an exhibit loan moves units from the shelf column to the loan column and returning it moves the identical number back, so the triple is restored exactly. Executing a deaccession moves units from the shelf column to the deaccessioned column and is deliberately irreversible. Items in `REJECTED_ITEM` carry `units_on_shelf` of 0 and are exempt from the identity.

Scores carry their own precision rules. Appraisal scores are rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and the threshold comparison that derives a disposition recommendation is made against the rounded value, not the unrounded one. Confidentiality risk scores are pure integer arithmetic, clamped into the range 0 through 100 inclusive at both ends. A trademark package's continuous-use coverage percentage is rounded half up to 2 decimal places, while its gap arithmetic is in whole years. Temperature and humidity readings carry one decimal place. Instant comparisons are made at millisecond resolution on ISO 8601 UTC values.

### 2.11. Environmental Standards for Storage Zones

Every storage zone carries the latest recorded dry-bulb temperature as `temperature_c` and the latest recorded relative humidity as `relative_humidity_pct`, each to one decimal place, and a derived `environmental_status` that is one of `COMPLIANT`, `MARGINAL` or `NON_COMPLIANT`. The derivation is re-run from scratch on the reading supplied at zone creation and again on every subsequent reading, and it reads nothing but that single latest pair — historical readings are not retained, only counted, in `reading_count`.

A zone is `COMPLIANT` when its temperature is at most 18.0 degrees Celsius **and** its relative humidity is at least 30.0 percent and at most 50.0 percent. Otherwise, a zone is `MARGINAL` when its temperature is at most 21.0 degrees Celsius **and** its relative humidity is at least 30.0 percent and at most 55.0 percent. If neither of those holds, the zone is `NON_COMPLIANT`. Every one of those bounds is inclusive: a reading of 18.0 degrees at 50.0 percent humidity is `COMPLIANT`, whereas 18.1 degrees is not, and a humidity of 29.9 percent is `NON_COMPLIANT` no matter how cool the vault is. Only a `COMPLIANT` zone may host the start of a format migration; see §3.6.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Retention Schedules, the Series Register and Disposal

Everything in the archive hangs off a **record series**, and every record series is governed by a **retention schedule** that the `ArchivesDirector` registers as immutable configuration. A schedule is identified by a `code` such as `FIN-07` or `LEG-12`, which must be between 3 and 32 characters long and unique across all schedules; a duplicate `code` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE_KEY`. The schedule states `retention_years`, a whole number of years from 0 to 200 inclusive that a series must be kept after its latest record year; a `disposition_action` of `DESTROY`, `REVIEW` or `TRANSFER` prescribing what happens once retention elapses; a `legal_citation` of 1 to 200 characters naming the statute or policy behind the rule; and `min_appraisal_score_for_permanent`, a number from 0.00 to 100.00 carried to exactly two decimal places. That last figure is the inclusive score at or above which appraisal designates a series permanent instead of following `disposition_action`.

An `Archivist` registers a series by supplying its `title` (1 to 200 characters), the owning `business_unit` code (1 to 64 characters), the `retention_schedule_id` of its governing schedule, a `sensitivity_ceiling` of `PUBLIC`, `INTERNAL`, `CONFIDENTIAL` or `RESTRICTED` naming the highest sensitivity any record in the series may carry, `annual_volume_linear_feet` as a non-negative whole number of linear feet accruing per year, and the calendar-year bounds `earliest_record_year` and `latest_record_year`. Both years must fall between 1800 and 2100 inclusive, and `earliest_record_year` must not exceed `latest_record_year`.

At creation the series enters `disposition_state` `ACTIVE`, its `legal_hold_active` flag is false, its `current_appraisal_id` and `pre_hold_disposition_state` and `disposition_certificate_reference` are all null, its `blocked_access_request_count` is 0, and its `version` is 1. The lifecycle states a series may occupy are `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `ELIGIBLE_FOR_DISPOSITION`, `PERMANENT` and `DISPOSED`, with `DISPOSED` terminal.

Creation also derives the retention clock. The base instant is the last moment of the series' latest record year — the ISO 8601 UTC instant whose date is 31 December of `latest_record_year` and whose time is 23:59:59.000. The series' `retention_expiry_utc` is that base instant advanced by `retention_years` whole calendar years, meaning the year field is incremented while the month, day and time-of-day are preserved verbatim. A series is retention-elapsed once the logical clock stands at or after `retention_expiry_utc`; the comparison is inclusive, so the expiry instant itself counts as elapsed. The value is recomputed whenever `latest_record_year` or the referenced schedule changes, and reads nothing beyond those two inputs.

Disposal is the `ArchivesDirector`'s act and the only route to the terminal `DISPOSED` state. It is declared only from `ELIGIBLE_FOR_DISPOSITION`, so a series that is still `ACTIVE`, already `PERMANENT`, currently `SUSPENDED` or already `DISPOSED` is refused with `ERR_SERIES_STATE_INVALID`. Beyond the state guard, four conditions must hold: `legal_hold_active` must be false and the state must not be `SUSPENDED` (otherwise `ERR_SERIES_DISPOSITION_SUSPENDED`); the state must not be `PERMANENT` (otherwise `ERR_SERIES_PERMANENT`); the logical clock must stand at or after `retention_expiry_utc` (otherwise `ERR_RETENTION_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`); and no archival item under any accession of the series may be in `ON_LOAN`. The director supplies `disposition_certificate_reference`, the certificate of destruction or transfer, which is stored on the series. Disposal also has one cascade: every finding aid whose accession belongs to this series and whose status is `PUBLISHED` transitions to `WITHDRAWN`, from which no further transition is possible. The series' `version` increments.

### 3.2. Appraising Historical Value

Appraisal is how the archive decides whether a series is heritage or overhead. An `Archivist` opens an appraisal by naming the `record_series_id` under appraisal and rating four dimensions, each a whole number from 0 to 10 inclusive: `informational_value`, `evidential_value`, `brand_heritage_value` and `uniqueness_factor`. The archivist also records `provenance_verified`, a boolean stating whether documented provenance was confirmed. The create carries `expected_series_version` per §2.7. The new row enters `status` `DRAFT`, stamps `appraised_by` from `X-User-ID`, and leaves `screening_score`, `final_score`, `recommended_disposition` and `superseded_by_appraisal_id` null with `brand_heritage_counted` false. No score is computed at creation.

Creating an appraisal is a superseding act. Every earlier appraisal on the same series that sits in `DRAFT`, `SCREENED` or `FINAL` moves to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` state, with `superseded_by_appraisal_id` set to the new row's identifier. If the superseded set included the appraisal the series had designated final, the series' `current_appraisal_id` is cleared back to null. Either way the series' `version` increments by 1. This is precisely why the stale-version guard exists on this endpoint: without it, a client working from an out-of-date view of the series would destroy an appraisal it had never read.

Screening is the archivist's own read of the series before the director looks at it, and it advances the appraisal `DRAFT → SCREENED`. It is refused with `ERR_APPRAISAL_STATE_INVALID` from any other status, and refused with `ERR_SERIES_DISPOSITION_SUSPENDED` when the governing series is `SUSPENDED` or carries an active hold. Screening writes `screening_score`.

Finalisation is the `ArchivesDirector`'s record of decision and advances the appraisal `SCREENED → FINAL`. It requires the series to be in `ACTIVE` disposition state with `legal_hold_active` false; a suspended or held series yields `ERR_SERIES_DISPOSITION_SUSPENDED`. Finalisation writes `final_score`, sets `brand_heritage_counted`, derives `recommended_disposition`, sets the series' `current_appraisal_id` to this appraisal, and increments the series' `version`.

**The scoring formula, and the one place the two variants disagree.** Both the screening pass and the final pass compute a score from the same skeleton. The span of the series is the number of calendar years it covers inclusively: the latest record year minus the earliest record year, plus one. The age bonus is one point for every complete five years of that span, discarding any remainder, capped at 10 points. The volume penalty is zero when the series accrues 50 linear feet per year or less; above that floor it is one point for every complete 10 linear feet by which the annual volume exceeds 50, again discarding any remainder, capped at 15 points and never negative. The core score is the informational rating multiplied by 3, plus the evidential rating multiplied by 3, plus the admitted brand-heritage rating multiplied by 2, plus the uniqueness rating multiplied by 2. The raw score is the core plus the age bonus minus the volume penalty, clamped into the range 0 through 100 inclusive. The score written to the record is the raw score multiplied by the provenance multiplier, rounded half up to two decimal places. Inputs are read from the appraisal and its series alone.

The screening variant and the final variant differ on exactly two inputs. On the admitted brand-heritage rating: the screening variant admits `brand_heritage_value` unconditionally, whereas the final variant admits it only when a trademark evidence package for the same series stands in `CERTIFIED` status at the moment of finalisation, and otherwise substitutes zero. Packages in `DRAFT` or `REJECTED` status are ignored entirely. When the final variant does admit the rating it sets `brand_heritage_counted` to true, otherwise false. On the provenance multiplier: the screening variant always uses 1.00, even when `provenance_verified` is false, whereas the final variant uses 1.00 when `provenance_verified` is true and 0.80 when it is false. Nothing else differs — the same weights, the same age bonus, the same volume penalty, the same clamp and the same rounding apply to both.

Disposition derivation runs only in the final pass. When the rounded `final_score` is greater than or equal to the governing schedule's `min_appraisal_score_for_permanent`, `recommended_disposition` is `PERMANENT`; otherwise it is the schedule's `disposition_action`, so one of `DESTROY`, `REVIEW` or `TRANSFER`. The permitted values of `recommended_disposition` are therefore `PERMANENT`, `DESTROY`, `REVIEW` and `TRANSFER`, or null before finalisation.

That recommendation then drives the series. From `ACTIVE`, a recommendation of `PERMANENT` moves the series to `PERMANENT`. From `ACTIVE`, a recommendation of `DESTROY`, `REVIEW` or `TRANSFER` moves the series to `ELIGIBLE_FOR_DISPOSITION` — but only if the logical clock already stands at or after the series' `retention_expiry_utc`. When the recommendation is non-permanent and the retention clock has not yet elapsed, the series stays `ACTIVE` and only `current_appraisal_id` changes. In all three outcomes the series' `version` increments.

### 3.3. Legal Hold Coordination and the Suspension Cascade

When counsel opens a matter, records must stop moving. A `LegalCounsel` operator drafts a hold by supplying a `matter_number` of 3 to 64 characters, unique across all holds and otherwise rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE_KEY`; the `record_series_id` of the single series the hold freezes; and `effective_from`, the ISO 8601 UTC instant from which counsel considers the hold effective. The hold is created in `DRAFT` with `issued_by` stamped from `X-User-ID`, `activated_at` and `released_at` and `release_note` null, and `suspended_access_request_count` 0. Drafting has no effect whatsoever on the series. The hold's lifecycle is `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` and the terminal `RELEASED`.

Activation is where the freeze bites, and it is declared only from `DRAFT` — any other status yields `ERR_HOLD_STATE_INVALID`. Activation additionally requires that no other hold on the same series is already `ACTIVE`; a second concurrent hold is refused with `ERR_SERIES_ALREADY_ON_HOLD`. Activation stamps `activated_at` from the logical clock and runs the three-hop cascade in full.

Hop one suspends the series. Whichever of `ACTIVE`, `ELIGIBLE_FOR_DISPOSITION` or `PERMANENT` the series currently occupies is copied verbatim into `pre_hold_disposition_state`, the series moves to `SUSPENDED`, `legal_hold_active` is set true, and the series' `version` increments with `updated_at` refreshed. All three source states are treated identically — the captured value is exactly the state the series was in, which is why `pre_hold_disposition_state` is constrained to `ACTIVE`, `ELIGIBLE_FOR_DISPOSITION` or `PERMANENT`, and is null whenever the series is not `SUSPENDED`.

Hop two blocks the series' live access requests. Every access request whose `record_series_id` is the suspended series and whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `SCREENING` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED` moves to `BLOCKED` with `legal_hold_blocked` set true, and each such move increments the series' `blocked_access_request_count` by 1. All three source statuses behave the same way. The number of requests moved is stamped onto the hold as `suspended_access_request_count`.

Hop three cleans up the screenings underneath. For every request that hop two moved out of `SCREENING` specifically, each confidentiality screening on that request still in `OPEN` moves to `SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_reason` set to `LEGAL_HOLD_BLOCK`. Screenings under requests blocked from `SUBMITTED` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED` are unaffected, because no screening is open there.

Release is the mirror image, declared only from `ACTIVE`. Counsel supplies `release_note`, which is stored, and `released_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Provided no other hold on the series remains `ACTIVE` after this release, the series restores `disposition_state` from `pre_hold_disposition_state` verbatim — a series that was `ACTIVE` before the hold returns to `ACTIVE`, one that was `ELIGIBLE_FOR_DISPOSITION` returns to `ELIGIBLE_FOR_DISPOSITION`, and one that was `PERMANENT` returns to `PERMANENT` — then `pre_hold_disposition_state` is set back to null and `legal_hold_active` becomes false, with the series' `version` incrementing. Every access request on the series that sits in `BLOCKED` with `legal_hold_blocked` true returns to `SUBMITTED` with `legal_hold_blocked` cleared to false, and the series' `blocked_access_request_count` decrements by 1 for each. Unblocking always lands on `SUBMITTED` regardless of which status the request was blocked from, so a request that was mid-screening must begin a fresh screening cycle; its superseded screening is not revived. The `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` round trip on a series is therefore a genuine cycle that a series may traverse any number of times.

While a series is suspended or carries an active hold, `ERR_SERIES_DISPOSITION_SUSPENDED` blocks appraisal screening, appraisal finalisation, accession provenance verification, the beginning of access screening, access approval, deaccession legal clearance and series disposal — the same seven operations listed at rung 15 of §2.6.
### 3.4. Accession Processing, Provenance and the Custody Register

Custody arrives in the archive as an **accession**. An `Archivist` records one against a series by supplying the `record_series_id`, the `donor_name` naming the donor, department or estate transferring custody (1 to 160 characters), the `transfer_instrument` conveying custody — one of `DEED_OF_GIFT`, `INTERNAL_TRANSFER`, `PURCHASE` or `BEQUEST` — and `declared_unit_count`, the number of boxes or carriers the transferring party declared, which must be at least 1. The new accession enters `status` `RECEIVED` with `provenance_documented` false, `processed_unit_count` 0, `deaccessioned_unit_count` 0, and `provenance_statement`, `transfer_instrument_reference` and `rejection_reason` all null. The accession lifecycle runs `RECEIVED`, `PROVENANCE_VERIFIED`, and the two terminal states `PROCESSED` and `REJECTED`.

Provenance verification is the archivist's act of writing down where the material came from, and it advances the accession `RECEIVED → PROVENANCE_VERIFIED`. The archivist supplies `provenance_statement`, the custodial-history narrative, and `transfer_instrument_reference`, the signed-instrument reference; both are stored on the accession and `provenance_documented` is set true. Verification is refused with `ERR_ACCESSION_STATE_INVALID` from any status other than `RECEIVED`, and refused with `ERR_SERIES_DISPOSITION_SUSPENDED` while the governing series is `SUSPENDED` or under an active hold.

Physical containers are registered as **archival items** under an accession. The archivist supplies the `accession_id`, the `storage_zone_id` of the physical zone that will shelve the container, an `item_label` of 1 to 120 characters serving as the shelf label, a `unit_count` of at least 1, a `condition_grade` of `GOOD`, `FAIR`, `POOR` or `UNSTABLE`, an `original_format` describing the carrier as received — `PAPER`, `PHOTO`, `FILM`, `MAGNETIC_TAPE` or `BORN_DIGITAL` — a `sensitivity_level` of `PUBLIC`, `INTERNAL`, `CONFIDENTIAL` or `RESTRICTED`, and optionally `brand_use_year`, the calendar year between 1800 and 2100 in which this item evidences use of a mark in commerce. `brand_use_year` is null for items that are not brand evidence. The item is created in `status` `UNPROCESSED` with `preservation_format` equal to `original_format`, `units_on_shelf` equal to `unit_count`, `units_on_loan` and `units_deaccessioned` both 0, `digital_surrogate_available` false and `last_loan_window_end` null. The item's `preservation_format` may later take any of `PAPER`, `PHOTO`, `FILM`, `MAGNETIC_TAPE`, `BORN_DIGITAL`, `PRESERVATION_TIFF`, `PRESERVATION_WAV` or `PRESERVATION_MKV`; its `status` may take any of `UNPROCESSED`, `ACCESSIONED`, `ON_LOAN`, `IN_TREATMENT`, `PENDING_DISPOSITION`, and the terminal `DEACCESSIONED` and `REJECTED_ITEM`.

Creating an item forces the parent accession to recompute `processed_unit_count`, which is the sum of `unit_count` over all items under that accession whose status is anything other than `REJECTED_ITEM`. That figure is the gate on processing: the accession may be closed out only when `processed_unit_count` exactly equals `declared_unit_count`, and a mismatch in either direction is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_COUNT_MISMATCH`. Processing also requires `provenance_documented` to be true, and because the provenance rule sits ahead of the state guard in the ladder, an attempt to process an accession still in `RECEIVED` reports `ERR_PROVENANCE_NOT_DOCUMENTED` rather than a state error. Processing advances the accession `PROVENANCE_VERIFIED → PROCESSED`, and every item under that accession still in `UNPROCESSED` moves to `ACCESSIONED` with `units_on_shelf` set to `unit_count`, `units_on_loan` set to 0, `units_deaccessioned` set to 0 and `preservation_format` set to `original_format`.

Rejection is the `ArchivesDirector`'s escape hatch for material that must go back to the transferring party. It is declared from `RECEIVED` and from `PROVENANCE_VERIFIED`, stores the supplied `rejection_reason`, and moves the accession to the terminal `REJECTED`. Every item under the accession still in `UNPROCESSED` moves to the terminal `REJECTED_ITEM` with `units_on_shelf` set to 0 — those rows are the sole exemption from the unit-conservation identity of §2.10, and they are also excluded from `processed_unit_count`.

Two further counters live on the accession and are maintained by other workflows. `deaccessioned_unit_count` is the sum of `units_deaccessioned` over the accession's items and is recomputed whenever a deaccession executes. Note also that `ERR_ACCESSION_STATE_INVALID` is raised not only for the accession's own transitions but also when a child operation whose precondition names the parent accession's status is attempted while that accession is not `PROCESSED` — specifically, submitting a finding aid for review.

### 3.5. Arrangement, Finding Aids and the Revision Cycle

A **finding aid** is the descriptive instrument a researcher actually reads. An `Archivist` drafts one over a single accession by supplying the `accession_id`, a `title` of 1 to 200 characters, an `arrangement_level` naming the deepest level described — `COLLECTION`, `SERIES`, `FILE` or `ITEM` — and a human-readable `version_label` of 1 to 16 characters such as `v1` or `v2`. The aid is created in `status` `DRAFT` with `described_item_count` and `stale_item_count` both 0, `revision_cycle_count` 0 and `published_at` null. Its lifecycle states are `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `PUBLISHED`, `NEEDS_REVISION` and the terminal `WITHDRAWN`.

Submitting an aid for review is declared from two states, and the archivist may optionally supply a fresh `version_label` with the submission. From `DRAFT` the aid advances to `UNDER_REVIEW`, and this path additionally requires the parent accession to be in `PROCESSED` status — a submission over an unprocessed accession is refused with `ERR_ACCESSION_STATE_INVALID`. From `NEEDS_REVISION` the aid also advances to `UNDER_REVIEW`, and this path increments `revision_cycle_count` by 1, closing the `PUBLISHED → NEEDS_REVISION → UNDER_REVIEW → PUBLISHED` cycle that an aid may traverse repeatedly. Both paths recompute `described_item_count` and `stale_item_count`. Any other starting status yields `ERR_FINDING_AID_STATE_INVALID`.

The two counters read the accession's holdings. `described_item_count` is the number of items under the aid's accession whose status is anything other than `REJECTED_ITEM`. `stale_item_count` is the number of items under that same accession whose status is `PENDING_DISPOSITION` or `DEACCESSIONED`. Both are refreshed by every cascade that touches those items.

Publication belongs to the `ArchivesDirector` and is declared only from `UNDER_REVIEW`. It additionally requires `stale_item_count` to be exactly 0: an aid that describes material already sliding towards disposal may not be published, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_FINDING_AID_STATE_INVALID`. Publication stamps `published_at` from the logical clock — on every publication, so a republished aid carries the most recent instant — and recomputes `described_item_count`.

Two events pull an aid back out of `PUBLISHED`. A deaccession approval moves it to `NEEDS_REVISION` (see §3.9), and a series disposal moves it to the terminal `WITHDRAWN` (see §3.1). Only the first is recoverable.

### 3.6. Preservation Planning, Format Migration and Zone Capacity

Physical preservation begins with a **storage zone**. A `PreservationOfficer` creates one with a `zone_code` of 2 to 32 characters that must be unique across all zones, an initial `temperature_c` and `relative_humidity_pct` reading, and `max_overlapping_migration_jobs`, a whole number from 1 to 20 inclusive that caps how many treatment windows may cover any single instant inside that zone. Creation derives `environmental_status` from the creation reading per §2.11 and sets `reading_count` to 1. Recording a further environment reading overwrites both `temperature_c` and `relative_humidity_pct`, re-derives `environmental_status` from the new pair alone, and increments `reading_count` by 1.

A **preservation plan** funds work in one zone. The officer supplies the `storage_zone_id`, a `plan_name` of 1 to 120 characters, and `budget_cents`, a non-negative integer count of cents. The plan is created in `status` `DRAFT` with `budget_committed_cents`, `budget_spent_cents` and `at_risk_unit_count` all 0 and `satisfied_at` null. Plan states are `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `SATISFIED` and the terminal `CLOSED`.

Activation is declared only from `DRAFT` and additionally requires `budget_cents` to be at least 1 — a zero-budget plan cannot be activated and is refused with `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. Activation takes the plan's first at-risk census of its zone. **The at-risk census** sums `unit_count` over every archival item whose `storage_zone_id` matches the plan's zone, whose status is one of `UNPROCESSED`, `ACCESSIONED` or `IN_TREATMENT`, whose `original_format` is `MAGNETIC_TAPE` or `FILM`, and whose `digital_surrogate_available` is false. Items in `ON_LOAN`, `PENDING_DISPOSITION`, `DEACCESSIONED` or `REJECTED_ITEM` are excluded outright, as are items in every format other than magnetic tape and film. The result is a whole number of units and is order-independent. If that census comes back as exactly 0 at activation, the plan does not linger in `ACTIVE`: it transitions straight through to `SATISFIED` and stamps `satisfied_at` from the logical clock.

Closing a plan is declared from `ACTIVE` and from `SATISFIED`, and both paths carry the identical additional condition: no migration job on the plan may still be in `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `APPROVED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. A plan with any such open job is refused with `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. `CLOSED` is terminal.

A **migration job** is one item's format migration inside one treatment window. The officer supplies the `preservation_plan_id` funding it, the `archival_item_id` being migrated, a `target_format` of `PRESERVATION_TIFF`, `PRESERVATION_WAV` or `PRESERVATION_MKV`, a `units` count of at least 1, and the window bounds `window_start` and `window_end`, where `window_end` must be strictly later than `window_start`. The job is created in `status` `PENDING_APPROVAL` with `requested_by` stamped from `X-User-ID`, `approved_by` and `cancel_reason` null. Job states are `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `APPROVED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, and the three terminal states `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` and `REJECTED`.

**The cost waterfall is computed once, at creation, and frozen for the life of the job.** It has six steps and they must be executed in exactly this order; in particular, each tier lookup reads only what the step above it produced, never a later figure. Step one sets `subtotal_cents` to the per-unit rate for the item's `original_format` multiplied by the job's `units`. The rates are 900 cents per unit for `PAPER`, 1,800 for `PHOTO`, 3,600 for `FILM`, 4,200 for `MAGNETIC_TAPE` and 600 for `BORN_DIGITAL`; the rate is keyed on the carrier being migrated *from*, never on `target_format`. Step two selects `volume_discount_pct` from `subtotal_cents` and from nothing else: 0.00 when the subtotal is 50,000 cents or less, 0.06 when it lies between 50,001 and 200,000 cents inclusive, and 0.11 when it is 200,001 cents or more. Step three sets `discounted_cents` to the subtotal multiplied by one minus the discount rate, rounded half up to whole cents. Step four selects `handling_fee_cents` from the step-three output: 1,500 cents when `discounted_cents` is 25,000 or less, 4,000 cents when it lies between 25,001 and 100,000 inclusive, and 9,000 cents when it is 100,001 or more. Step five sets `condition_surcharge_cents` to the sum of `discounted_cents` and `handling_fee_cents`, multiplied by the surcharge rate for the item's `condition_grade` — 0.00 for `GOOD`, 0.05 for `FAIR`, 0.12 for `POOR` and 0.20 for `UNSTABLE` — rounded half up to whole cents. Step six sets `committed_cost_cents` to `discounted_cents` plus `handling_fee_cents` plus `condition_surcharge_cents`. Rounding happens at step three and step five only, in that order. The inputs are exactly one job plus the item it names, read at creation time.

Approval is where money is actually committed, and it is declared only from `PENDING_APPROVAL`. Four further conditions apply. The funding plan must be in `ACTIVE` status. The approving `X-User-ID` must differ from the job's `requested_by`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The plan's `budget_committed_cents` plus this job's `committed_cost_cents` must not exceed the plan's `budget_cents`, with equality permitted; exceeding it yields `ERR_PRESERVATION_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. And the zone must have room for the window. On success, `approved_by` is stamped from `X-User-ID` and the job's `committed_cost_cents` is added to the plan's `budget_committed_cents`.

**Zone treatment capacity** is an aggregate rule, not a pairwise one: windows in a zone are allowed to overlap freely until the number of jobs covering some single instant would exceed the zone's ceiling. A migration window is treated as **closed on both ends**, so two windows that merely touch — one ending at the exact instant another begins — do overlap. The candidate set is every other migration job whose item shares the zone and whose status is `APPROVED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; jobs in `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` and `REJECTED` occupy nothing. From that set, take those whose closed window intersects the candidate job's closed window. Now consider the boundary instants formed by the candidate job's `window_start` together with the `window_start` of each intersecting job, and at each such instant count how many intersecting jobs' closed windows cover it; the peak after insertion is one plus the largest of those counts. The approval is refused with `ERR_ZONE_MIGRATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` only when that peak is strictly greater than the zone's `max_overlapping_migration_jobs`; a peak exactly equal to the ceiling is permitted.

Starting a job is declared only from `APPROVED`. It requires the item to be in `ACCESSIONED` status and the item's zone to be in `COMPLIANT` environmental status; a `MARGINAL` or `NON_COMPLIANT` zone is refused with `ERR_ZONE_NOT_COMPLIANT`. On success the job moves to `IN_PROGRESS` and the item moves `ACCESSIONED → IN_TREATMENT`, with no unit movement at all — the item stays shelved in its zone while under treatment.

Completion is declared only from `IN_PROGRESS` and drives the two-hop preservation cascade. Hop one returns the item `IN_TREATMENT → ACCESSIONED` with `preservation_format` set to the job's `target_format` and `digital_surrogate_available` set true. Hop two re-runs the at-risk census on the plan covering that item's zone, because the newly created surrogate takes the item out of the at-risk population; if the recomputed `at_risk_unit_count` reaches exactly 0 while the plan is `ACTIVE`, the plan auto-transitions `ACTIVE → SATISFIED` and stamps `satisfied_at` from the logical clock. Completion also moves the job's `committed_cost_cents` out of the plan's `budget_committed_cents` and into `budget_spent_cents`.

Cancellation covers three starting states and its consequences differ across them, so the branches must be implemented separately. From `PENDING_APPROVAL` the job lands in the terminal `REJECTED`, stores the supplied `cancel_reason`, and leaves `budget_committed_cents` untouched, because nothing had been committed. From `APPROVED` the job lands in the terminal `CANCELLED`, stores `cancel_reason`, and subtracts exactly `committed_cost_cents` from the plan's `budget_committed_cents`, restoring the precise pre-approval figure. From `IN_PROGRESS` the job also lands in `CANCELLED`, stores `cancel_reason`, subtracts exactly `committed_cost_cents` from `budget_committed_cents`, and additionally returns the item `IN_TREATMENT → ACCESSIONED` with `preservation_format` and `digital_surrogate_available` both left exactly as they were — an abandoned treatment produces no surrogate. Any other starting status yields `ERR_JOB_STATE_INVALID`.
### 3.7. Access Policy, Confidentiality Screening and Service Fees

Access services mediates between the archive and everyone who wants to read it. An `AccessServicesOfficer` registers a **requester** with a `display_name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `requester_class` of `INTERNAL_BUSINESS`, `EXTERNAL_RESEARCHER` or `MEDIA`, an `affiliation` of 1 to 160 characters naming the employing business unit, institution or media outlet, and optionally `nda_on_file`, a boolean that defaults to false when omitted and records whether a signed non-disclosure agreement is held. The requester is created in `status` `ACTIVE` with `suspend_reason` null. The row's `id` is the value a `Requester`-role caller presents as `X-User-ID`. Suspension is declared only from `ACTIVE`, stores the supplied `suspend_reason`, and moves the requester to `SUSPENDED`; suspending an already-suspended requester yields `ERR_REQUESTER_STATE_INVALID`. Suspension leaves existing access requests in whatever status they occupy, but they can no longer be moved into screening or approved — both are refused with `ERR_REQUESTER_SUSPENDED`.

An **access request** may be filed either by the `AccessServicesOfficer` on a requester's behalf or by the `Requester` for itself. It carries the `requester_id` on whose behalf it is filed, the `record_series_id` whose holdings are wanted, a `purpose` of 1 to 500 characters stating the research or business rationale, `requested_item_ids` as an array of 0 to 50 archival-item identifiers with no duplicates, and optionally `finding_aid_id` naming the aid the requester cited — null for an unmediated request. A `Requester`-role caller may only file for itself, and a mismatch between `X-User-ID` and the body's `requester_id` is rejected with `ERR_NOT_REQUEST_OWNER`.

Two checks on the item set deserve care because they sit at different rungs of the ladder. A duplicate identifier inside `requested_item_ids`, or more than 50 elements, is a shape failure and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An empty array, by contrast, is perfectly well-formed shape and passes that check, only to be refused as a business matter with `ERR_NO_ITEMS_REQUESTED`. Separately, every item named must sit under an accession whose `record_series_id` matches the request's own, or the filing is refused with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_IN_SERIES`; an identifier naming no item at all is reported as `ERR_ARCHIVAL_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` during reference resolution.

The new request enters `status` `SUBMITTED` with `legal_hold_blocked` false, `revision_round` 0, `current_screening_id` null, and `post_screen_risk_score`, `service_fee_cents`, `decided_by`, `approval_scope` and `deny_reason` all null. Its statuses across the whole lifecycle are `SUBMITTED`, `SCREENING`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `PARTIALLY_APPROVED`, `DENIED`, `BLOCKED`, `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` and the terminal `CLOSED`. Creation also stamps two derived figures. `requested_page_count` is eleven pages for every unit in the requested items — that is, eleven multiplied by the sum of `unit_count` across the items named in `requested_item_ids`. And a preliminary risk score is stamped into `prelim_risk_score`.

**Confidentiality risk is scored twice, with different information available each time.** Both passes start from the requester's class weight: 10 points for `INTERNAL_BUSINESS`, 30 for `EXTERNAL_RESEARCHER` and 45 for `MEDIA`. Both subtract an NDA credit of 12 points when the requester's `nda_on_file` is true, and nothing when it is false. Both clamp the result into the range 0 through 100 inclusive, and both work in whole integers with no rounding.

The preliminary pass runs at filing and again on every revision, before any item has been inspected. It adds the sensitivity weight of the series' `sensitivity_ceiling` as a single upper-bound proxy for the holdings — 0 points for `PUBLIC`, 15 for `INTERNAL`, 35 for `CONFIDENTIAL` and 60 for `RESTRICTED` — and it ignores legal holds and redaction entirely. Its output is written to `prelim_risk_score`.

The post-screen pass runs when a screening completes and replaces the proxy with reality. It adds the greatest sensitivity weight found across the actual items named in `requested_item_ids`, using each item's own `sensitivity_level` and the same four weights, with no filter on item status — an item that has since drifted into `PENDING_DISPOSITION` still contributes its sensitivity. On top of that it adds a legal-hold surcharge of 25 points when the governing series carries `legal_hold_active` true, and it subtracts a redaction credit of 10 points when the completing screening set `redaction_required` true. Its output, after the same clamp, is written to `post_screen_risk_score`. Because the item set is guaranteed non-empty, the maximum across items always has at least one term to work with.

Moving a request into screening is the officer's act, and it is declared from two states. From `SUBMITTED` the request advances to `SCREENING`, and the previously stamped `prelim_risk_score` is retained untouched — no calculation runs. From `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` the request also advances to `SCREENING`, but this path additionally clears `service_fee_cents` and `post_screen_risk_score` back to null, so a fresh screening must be created and completed before the request can be decided again. Both paths require the governing series not to be `SUSPENDED` and the requester to be in `ACTIVE` status. Any other starting status yields `ERR_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`.

A **confidentiality screening** is opened by an `AccessServicesOfficer` over a request that is under screening, naming the `access_request_id` and carrying `expected_access_request_version` per §2.7. The new screening stamps `screened_by` from `X-User-ID` and `max_item_sensitivity` — the highest `sensitivity_level` across the request's requested items, one of `PUBLIC`, `INTERNAL`, `CONFIDENTIAL` or `RESTRICTED` — and enters `status` `OPEN` with `redaction_required` false, and `decision`, `screening_note`, `superseded_reason` and `completed_at` all null. Opening a screening supersedes its predecessor: any earlier screening on the same request sitting in `OPEN`, and equally any sitting in `COMPLETED`, moves to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_reason` set to `NEW_SCREENING`. The request's `current_screening_id` is repointed at the new row and the request's `version` increments by 1. `current_screening_id` always means the most recently created screening on that request that is in `OPEN` or `COMPLETED`, ordered by `created_at` ascending, and is null when no such screening exists. Screening statuses are `OPEN` and the two terminal states `COMPLETED` and `SUPERSEDED`.

Completing a screening is declared only from `OPEN` and additionally requires the parent request to be in `SCREENING` status; anything else yields `ERR_SCREENING_STATE_INVALID`. The officer supplies `decision`, one of `GRANT_FULL`, `GRANT_REDACTED`, `DENY` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, and may supply `redaction_required` — which defaults to false — and a free-text `screening_note`. All three are stored, `completed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the post-screen risk pass runs onto the parent request's `post_screen_risk_score`. When and only when the decision is `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, the parent request moves `SCREENING → CHANGES_REQUESTED`.

The requester answers a changes-requested screening with a **revision**, which is the one operation exclusive to the `Requester` role. It is declared only from `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, requires `X-User-ID` to equal the request's `requester_id`, and carries a replacement `requested_item_ids` array plus an optional replacement `purpose`. The same non-empty, no-duplicates and same-series rules apply to the revised array as to the original, with the same three error codes. On success the request returns to `SCREENING`, `revision_round` increments by 1, `requested_page_count` and `prelim_risk_score` are both recomputed from the revised item set, and every screening on the request in `OPEN` or in `COMPLETED` moves to `SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_reason` set to `REQUESTER_REVISION`. This closes the `SCREENING → CHANGES_REQUESTED → SCREENING` cycle, which a request may traverse any number of times, with `revision_round` counting the completed loops.

Deciding a request is the officer's act and comes in three flavours, all declared only from `SCREENING`. The `approval_scope` supplied on an approval determines the destination: `FULL` drives `SCREENING → APPROVED`, and `REDACTED` drives `SCREENING → PARTIALLY_APPROVED`. Both approval branches require the same five things, specified here with equal force. First, the officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the `screened_by` of the request's current screening, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Second, the requester must be in `ACTIVE` status, or `ERR_REQUESTER_SUSPENDED`. Third, the governing series must be in neither `SUSPENDED` nor `DISPOSED` disposition state, and must not carry an active hold, or `ERR_SERIES_DISPOSITION_SUSPENDED`. Fourth, the request's `finding_aid_id` must be null or else name an aid in `PUBLISHED` status, or `ERR_FINDING_AID_NOT_PUBLISHED`. Fifth, `current_screening_id` must resolve to a screening in `COMPLETED` status whose decision authorises the requested scope, or `ERR_SCREENING_DECISION_MISSING`.

That fifth condition is where the two branches diverge, and so is the risk gate. A `FULL` approval demands a completed screening whose `decision` is exactly `GRANT_FULL`; a `GRANT_REDACTED`, `DENY` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED` decision cannot support it. A `REDACTED` approval accepts a completed screening whose `decision` is either `GRANT_FULL` or `GRANT_REDACTED`. On top of that, a `FULL` approval requires `post_screen_risk_score` to be strictly below 75; at 75 or above, the only permitted outcome is a `REDACTED` approval, and an attempt at full scope is refused with `ERR_RISK_THRESHOLD_REQUIRES_REDACTION`. No risk gate applies to the redacted branch. Both branches stamp `decided_by` from `X-User-ID`, store the supplied `approval_scope`, and compute and store `service_fee_cents`.

**The service fee is billed per line item, and the rounding order is pinned.** The line items are the archival items named in `requested_item_ids`, processed in ascending `created_at` order with ties broken by `created_at` alone and never by identifier, and with no filter on item status — a requested item that has since moved to `PENDING_DISPOSITION` still bills. Each line's page count is that item's `unit_count` multiplied by eleven pages per unit; the total page count across all lines is the request's `requested_page_count`. A single volume tier is selected once from that total and applied to every line: no discount when the total is 100 pages or fewer, 7.5 percent when it lies between 101 and 500 pages inclusive, and 12.5 percent when it is 501 pages or more. Each line's gross amount is its page count multiplied by the per-page rate for the requester's class — 0 cents per page for `INTERNAL_BUSINESS`, 35 cents for `EXTERNAL_RESEARCHER` and 90 cents for `MEDIA`. Each line's billable amount is its gross amount multiplied by one minus the tier rate, **rounded half up to whole cents at that point, before any summation**; the alternative of summing the gross amounts first and rounding once at the end is explicitly not used and will produce off-by-one-cent answers. The fee subtotal is the sum of those already-rounded lines. `service_fee_cents` is that subtotal plus a base handling charge of 2,500 cents, plus a media licensing surcharge of 15,000 cents when and only when the requester's class is `MEDIA`.

Denial is declared only from `SCREENING` and requires the current screening to resolve to a `COMPLETED` row — any completed decision will do — with the same segregation-of-duties comparison against `screened_by`. It stores the supplied `deny_reason`, stamps `decided_by`, moves the request to `DENIED`, and leaves `service_fee_cents` null: a denied request is never billed. Closing out is declared from `APPROVED`, from `PARTIALLY_APPROVED` and from `DENIED`, and all three land in the terminal `CLOSED`, from which no further transition is possible.

Two cascades reach into access requests from elsewhere. A hold activation drives live requests to `BLOCKED` and a hold release returns them to `SUBMITTED`, as §3.3 describes. A deaccession approval drives already-decided requests to `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`, as §3.9 describes.
### 3.8. The Heritage Programme: Brand Evidence, Oral History and Exhibits

Three programmes draw on the collections for outward-facing purposes, and each has its own gating logic.

**Trademark and brand-heritage support.** When the trademark group needs to prove that a mark has been used continuously in commerce, it assembles a **trademark evidence package** from dated brand-evidence items. Either an `Archivist` or a `LegalCounsel` operator may assemble one, naming the `record_series_id` supplying the evidence, the `mark_name` whose continuous use is at issue (1 to 120 characters), the `claimed_first_use_year` between 1800 and 2100 that opens the coverage span, and `evidence_item_ids`, an array of 0 to 80 archival-item identifiers with no duplicates. The package is created in `status` `DRAFT` with `qualifying_evidence_count`, `covered_year_count`, `max_gap_years` and `continuous_use_coverage_pct` all 0, and `certified_at` and `rejection_code` null. Package statuses are `DRAFT` and the two terminal states `CERTIFIED` and `REJECTED`.

Certification is the `LegalCounsel` operator's act, declared only from `DRAFT` — anything else yields `ERR_PACKAGE_STATE_INVALID` — and it runs the coverage arithmetic before either outcome is chosen. The coverage span opens at `claimed_first_use_year` and closes at the calendar year of the logical clock read in UTC, and the span length is the closing year minus the opening year, plus one. An evidence item qualifies only when all four conditions hold: it is named in `evidence_item_ids`, its `brand_use_year` is not null, its status is not `DEACCESSIONED`, and its `brand_use_year` falls inside the closed span, meaning at or after the opening year and at or before the closing year. `qualifying_evidence_count` is the number of items that qualify. If that count is zero the arithmetic stops right there, before any gap or coverage figure is computed: the request is refused with `ERR_NO_QUALIFYING_EVIDENCE` and the package remains in `DRAFT`, untransitioned.

With at least one qualifying item, take the distinct `brand_use_year` values of the qualifying items in ascending order — duplicate years collapse into a single covered year — and let `covered_year_count` be how many there are. The gaps are the runs of uncovered years, and there are three kinds, all of which count: the leading run, which is the earliest covered year minus the opening year; each interior run, which for every consecutive pair of covered years is the later year minus the earlier year, minus one; and the trailing run, which is the closing year minus the latest covered year. `max_gap_years` is the largest of all those runs. `continuous_use_coverage_pct` is 100 multiplied by `covered_year_count`, divided by the span length, rounded half up to two decimal places.

Two gates then decide the outcome, and they are evaluated strictly in this order. First, `max_gap_years` must be at most 3 whole years. Second, `continuous_use_coverage_pct` must be at least 25.00. When both pass, the package moves `DRAFT → CERTIFIED` and `certified_at` is stamped from the logical clock. When either fails, the package moves `DRAFT → REJECTED` and `rejection_code` records which gate stopped it: `GAP_EXCEEDED` when the gap gate failed, otherwise `COVERAGE_BELOW_MINIMUM`. Because the gap gate is evaluated first, a package that fails both gates is stamped `GAP_EXCEEDED`. Both outcomes store the four computed coverage figures. A `CERTIFIED` package is what makes `brand_heritage_value` admissible to the final appraisal pass on the same series, as §3.2 sets out.

**The oral history programme** runs on two state axes that advance independently of one another, and confusing them is the commonest way to get this workflow wrong. An `Archivist` records an interview under an accession by supplying the `accession_id` holding the recordings, the `narrator_name` (1 to 120 characters), the `interview_date` as an ISO 8601 UTC instant, and optionally `embargo_until`, the ISO 8601 UTC instant before which the transcript may not be released; null means no embargo. The interview is created with `consent_status` `PENDING`, `transcription_status` `NOT_STARTED`, `release_state` `WITHHELD`, `consent_signed_at` null and `withdrawal_count` 0.

The consent axis is driven by a `consent_action` of `SIGN`, `RESTRICT` or `WITHDRAW`, and takes the values `PENDING`, `SIGNED`, `RESTRICTED` and the terminal `WITHDRAWN`. Signing from `PENDING` moves consent to `SIGNED` and stamps `consent_signed_at` from the logical clock. Restricting from `SIGNED` moves consent to `RESTRICTED`. Signing again from `RESTRICTED` moves it back to `SIGNED`, so `SIGNED` and `RESTRICTED` form a cycle the narrator's wishes may traverse repeatedly; note that `consent_signed_at` records when consent *first* became signed. Withdrawing is declared from `SIGNED` and equally from `RESTRICTED`; either path moves consent to `WITHDRAWN`, increments `withdrawal_count` by 1, and forces `release_state` to `WITHHELD` irrespective of how far transcription has progressed. Every consent action recomputes `release_state` and leaves `transcription_status` completely untouched. An action not declared from the interview's current consent value yields `ERR_INTERVIEW_STATE_INVALID`.

The transcription axis is driven by a `transcription_action` of `START_DRAFT`, `MARK_REVIEWED` or `APPROVE`, and takes the values `NOT_STARTED`, `DRAFT`, `REVIEWED` and the terminal `APPROVED`. Starting a draft moves transcription `NOT_STARTED → DRAFT`; marking reviewed moves it `DRAFT → REVIEWED`. Both leave `consent_status` untouched, which means drafting a transcript is entirely legal while consent is still `PENDING`. Approving moves it `REVIEWED → APPROVED` and is the single point where the two axes interact: approval additionally requires `consent_status` to be exactly `SIGNED`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_SIGNED`, and requires `embargo_until` to be null or the logical clock to stand at or after it, refusing otherwise with `ERR_EMBARGO_ACTIVE`. The consent check is evaluated before the embargo check, so an interview that is both unconsented and still embargoed reports the consent failure. Every transcription action recomputes `release_state`.

**The derived release state** is the joint reading of both axes plus the embargo clock, and it takes one of `WITHHELD`, `RESEARCH_ROOM_ONLY` or `PUBLIC`. When consent is `WITHDRAWN`, or still `PENDING`, the release state is `WITHHELD` regardless of transcription. When consent is `RESTRICTED`, the release state is `RESEARCH_ROOM_ONLY` if transcription has reached `REVIEWED` or `APPROVED`, and `WITHHELD` otherwise. When consent is `SIGNED`, the release state is `PUBLIC` if transcription has reached `APPROVED` **and** either there is no embargo or the logical clock stands at or after `embargo_until`; failing that, it is `RESEARCH_ROOM_ONLY` if transcription has reached `REVIEWED` or `APPROVED`; and `WITHHELD` in every remaining case. Nothing outside the interview row itself is read.

**Anniversary, museum and exhibit projects** borrow physical units through an **exhibit loan**, which either an `ArchivesDirector` or an `Archivist` may request. The request names the `archival_item_id` wanted for display, an `exhibit_name` of 1 to 160 characters, a `units` count of at least 1, and the display window bounds `window_start` and `window_end`, where the end must be strictly later than the start. The loan is created in `status` `REQUESTED` with `returned_condition_grade`, `checked_out_at` and `returned_at` null, and its `recovery_until` derived as `window_end` plus a 45-day light-recovery buffer — the effective end of this loan's occupation of the item, during which the material rests out of the light. Creation also refreshes the item's `last_loan_window_end`, which always holds the `window_end` of the most recently created loan on that item whose status is `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED`, `OUT` or `RETURNED`, ordered by `created_at` ascending, and is null when no such loan exists. Loan statuses are `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED`, `OUT`, and the two terminal states `RETURNED` and `CANCELLED`.

**Display-window conflict is checked before the row is created, and this rule's interval geometry is deliberately different from the migration-window rule of §3.6.** A display window is **half-open**: it includes its `window_start` and excludes its `window_end`. An existing loan's occupation, however, runs from its own `window_start` up to but not including its `window_end` plus the 45-day recovery buffer — the buffer is appended to the end only, never to the front. The candidates are the loans on the same item whose status is `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `OUT`; loans in `RETURNED` or `CANCELLED` release their occupation entirely, buffer included. The new loan conflicts with a candidate when the new window's start falls strictly before that candidate's buffered end **and** that candidate's start falls strictly before the new window's end. Rejection is pairwise, so one colliding loan is enough, and the create is refused with `ERR_LOAN_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Three consequences of this geometry are worth spelling out: a new window starting at the exact instant an existing loan's `window_end` falls **does** conflict, because it lands inside the recovery buffer; a new window ending at the exact instant an existing loan's `window_start` falls does **not** conflict, because the new window is half-open at its end and no buffer is applied to the front of the existing loan; and a new window starting exactly 45 days after an existing loan's `window_end` does **not** conflict, because the comparison against the buffered end is strict.

Confirmation is the `PreservationOfficer`'s conservation review, declared only from `REQUESTED`. It requires the item to be in `ACCESSIONED` status, the item's `condition_grade` to be `GOOD` or `FAIR` — `POOR` and `UNSTABLE` material may not go on display, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_ITEM_CONDITION_UNSUITABLE` — and the item's `units_on_shelf` to be at least the loan's `units`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SHELF_UNITS`. No units move at confirmation.

Check-out is the `Archivist`'s physical release, declared only from `CONFIRMED`. It requires `units_on_shelf` to be at least the loan's `units`, and requires the logical clock to stand at or after the loan's `window_start`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_LOAN_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. On success the loan moves to `OUT`, `checked_out_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the item moves `ACCESSIONED → ON_LOAN` with the loan's `units` subtracted from `units_on_shelf` and the same number added to `units_on_loan`, preserving the conservation identity of §2.10 exactly.

Return is check-out's compensating action, declared only from `OUT`. The archivist supplies `returned_condition_grade`, one of `GOOD`, `FAIR`, `POOR` or `UNSTABLE`, which is stored on the loan and also overwrites the item's `condition_grade` — a piece returning in worse shape is thereby disqualified from future display. The loan moves to `RETURNED`, `returned_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the item moves `ON_LOAN → ACCESSIONED` with the loan's `units` subtracted from `units_on_loan` and the same number added back to `units_on_shelf`, restoring the pre-check-out split exactly and with zero drift.

Cancellation is available to the `ArchivesDirector` and to the `Archivist` and is declared from `REQUESTED` and equally from `CONFIRMED`; both paths land the loan in the terminal `CANCELLED`, move no units, and free the item's occupation window including its recovery buffer so that later loans may be scheduled over it. A loan already `OUT`, `RETURNED` or `CANCELLED` cannot be cancelled, and the attempt yields `ERR_LOAN_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.9. Deaccession: Dual Clearance, Approval Cascade and Disposal Routes

Removing material from the collection is the one workflow the archive treats as irreversible, and it is built as a fork-join: two independent clearances must both land before approval is possible, and the order in which they land is free.

An `Archivist` proposes removal by naming the `archival_item_id`, a curatorial `rationale` of 1 to 500 characters, a `route` of either `DESTRUCTION` or `DONATION`, and a `units` count of at least 1. When the route is `DONATION` the proposal must also carry `recipient_repository`, the external repository receiving the material, as a non-empty string; a donation-route proposal without one is refused with `ERR_RECIPIENT_REPOSITORY_REQUIRED`, and the same rule is re-checked at approval time. The proposal is created in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `legal_cleared_by`, `heritage_cleared_by`, `approved_by`, `clearance_note` and `transfer_reference` all null, and `stale_finding_aid_count` and `revalidated_request_count` both 0. Proposal statuses are `SUBMITTED`, `LEGAL_CLEARED`, `HERITAGE_CLEARED`, `DUALLY_CLEARED`, `APPROVED`, and the two terminal states `EXECUTED` and `REJECTED`.

**Fork branch A is legal clearance**, granted by a `LegalCounsel` operator. It is declared from `SUBMITTED`, advancing the proposal to `LEGAL_CLEARED`, and equally from `HERITAGE_CLEARED`, advancing it to the join state `DUALLY_CLEARED`. Both paths stamp `legal_cleared_by` from `X-User-ID` and store the optional `clearance_note`. Both paths require the item's governing series to have `legal_hold_active` false and to be in neither `SUSPENDED` disposition state, refusing otherwise with `ERR_SERIES_DISPOSITION_SUSPENDED`, nor `PERMANENT`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_SERIES_PERMANENT`.

**Fork branch B is heritage clearance**, granted by the `ArchivesDirector`. It is declared from `SUBMITTED`, advancing the proposal to `HERITAGE_CLEARED`, and equally from `LEGAL_CLEARED`, advancing it to the join state `DUALLY_CLEARED`. Both paths stamp `heritage_cleared_by` from `X-User-ID` and store the optional `clearance_note`. Both paths require the item's governing series not to be in `PERMANENT` disposition state, refusing otherwise with `ERR_SERIES_PERMANENT`. The two branches are symmetric: `SUBMITTED → LEGAL_CLEARED → DUALLY_CLEARED` and `SUBMITTED → HERITAGE_CLEARED → DUALLY_CLEARED` are equally valid routes to the join, and neither branch may be granted twice.

**Approval is the join point** and belongs to the `ArchivesDirector`. It is declared only from `DUALLY_CLEARED`, and it enforces five things. Both `legal_cleared_by` and `heritage_cleared_by` must be non-null — and because those two checks sit ahead of the state guard in the ladder, an approval attempted on a proposal holding only heritage clearance reports `ERR_LEGAL_CLEARANCE_MISSING` while one holding only legal clearance reports `ERR_HERITAGE_CLEARANCE_MISSING`, rather than a generic state error. The approving `X-User-ID` must differ from `legal_cleared_by`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A `DONATION`-route proposal must carry `recipient_repository`. And no exhibit loan on the item may be in `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `OUT` status, refusing otherwise with `ERR_ITEM_HAS_ACTIVE_LOAN` — material promised to an exhibit cannot simultaneously be promised to a skip.

Approval stamps `approved_by` from `X-User-ID` and runs the three-hop staleness cascade. Hop one moves the item `ACCESSIONED → PENDING_DISPOSITION`, with no unit movement yet. Hop two takes every finding aid over that item's accession sitting in `PUBLISHED` and moves it to `NEEDS_REVISION`, incrementing each aid's `stale_item_count` by 1; the number of aids moved is recorded on the proposal as `stale_finding_aid_count`. Hop three takes every access request whose `finding_aid_id` names one of the aids hop two just moved and which sits in `APPROVED` or in `PARTIALLY_APPROVED`, and moves it to `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED`; both source statuses behave identically, and the number of requests moved is recorded on the proposal as `revalidated_request_count`. That last hop is the intended serialisation point between disposal and access: a researcher already cleared against a finding aid that no longer describes reality must be re-screened before reading, which is why beginning screening from `REVALIDATION_REQUIRED` clears the earlier fee and post-screen risk figures.

**Execution is the `Archivist`'s irreversible act**, declared only from `APPROVED`. It requires the item to be in `PENDING_DISPOSITION` status and the proposal's `units` to be no greater than the item's `units_on_shelf`, refusing otherwise with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SHELF_UNITS`. The archivist supplies `transfer_reference`. The two routes then diverge in their side-effect sets. On the `DESTRUCTION` route, the proposal's `units` move out of `units_on_shelf` and into `units_deaccessioned` on the item, and `transfer_reference` records the destruction certificate reference. On the `DONATION` route, the same unit movement occurs, `transfer_reference` records the receiving repository's receipt, and `recipient_repository` is copied onto the item's audit trail so the external custodian is permanently traceable. Either way the conserved sum of §2.10 is unchanged by the movement. The item's destination depends on what is left: when `units_on_shelf` reaches exactly 0 the item lands in the terminal `DEACCESSIONED`; when units remain, the item returns to `ACCESSIONED`, which is how a partial deaccession works. The parent accession then recomputes `deaccessioned_unit_count`, and the proposal moves to the terminal `EXECUTED`.

Rejection is the director's abort and is declared from `SUBMITTED`, `LEGAL_CLEARED`, `HERITAGE_CLEARED` and `DUALLY_CLEARED` — any point before approval. The director may record a `clearance_note`. The proposal lands in the terminal `REJECTED` and the item is left entirely untouched in `ACCESSIONED`, with no unit movement and no cascade. A proposal already `APPROVED`, `EXECUTED` or `REJECTED` cannot be rejected, and the attempt yields `ERR_PROPOSAL_STATE_INVALID`.
