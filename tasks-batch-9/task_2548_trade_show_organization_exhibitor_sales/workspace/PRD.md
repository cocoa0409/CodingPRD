# Trade Show Exhibitor Sales API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A trade show producer does not sell a product; it sells a rectangle of concrete for four days, and it sells that rectangle many times over to companies that all want the same corner by the entrance. Every commitment in that business is simultaneously physical, financial and calendar-bound: the hall is leased for years at a time under an agreement that penalises the producer when the floor does not sell, the floor plan is a fixed and finite inventory that must still leave enough width for a fire lane, and the right to pick a booth first is a currency that exhibitors accumulate over a decade of loyalty. Today the producer runs all of this on spreadsheets that are copied between the sales desk, the operations trailer and the accounting office, and every copy disagrees about which booths are still open.

This document specifies the API that replaces those spreadsheets with a single authoritative record. It covers the whole arc of an edition: contracting a hall for multiple consecutive years with an attrition allowance and a day-banded cancellation schedule; laying out booth inventory with aisle deductions, premium-location pricing and physical combines into islands; running space-selection day as a ranked, timed appointment process driven by certified priority points; negotiating and signing space contracts inside a bounded counter-offer cycle; packaging and selling sponsorship inventory that carries delivery obligations the producer owes back to the sponsor; acquiring and qualifying a buyer audience; taking exhibitor service orders at advance or floor rates; enforcing show-floor rules through citations that a second operator must adjudicate; and closing the edition with an attrition settlement and a post-show report on attendance and lead quality.

Two engineering constraints shape almost every rule that follows. First, booth inventory and sponsorship inventory are finite and are competed for, which means the server — not the client — is the arbiter of what is still available, and every write that consumes inventory must prove it was composed against a floor plan the client had actually read. Second, nearly every quantity of consequence is a denormalised aggregate: a show carries its own area ledger, an exhibitor carries its own point ledger, a package carries its own unit counters. Those ledgers are cheap to read and easy to corrupt, so this specification names four conserved quantities that must hold after every single write and must return exactly to their prior values when a compensating operation (a release, a cancellation, a reversal) is applied.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operator Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API carries an `X-Role` header naming the operational capacity of the caller. Exactly five roles are recognised, spelled precisely as follows: `ShowDirector`, `SalesRep`, `FloorManager`, `Exhibitor`, and `Registrant`. When `X-Role` is absent, or when it holds any string other than these five, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is considered. When `X-Role` is present and recognised but the role is not among those the addressed endpoint permits, the rejection is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). That same code also covers one role-shaped restriction inside an otherwise-permitted endpoint: a `FloorManager` may resolve a sponsorship obligation as delivered but may never waive one, so a waive disposition arriving from a `FloorManager` is answered with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

Requests also carry an `X-User-ID` header identifying the individual operator behind the role. For `ShowDirector`, `SalesRep`, `FloorManager` and `Registrant` this identifier is opaque: it is not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to any row's `id`. It is, however, recorded wherever the specification names an operator — as `reported_by_user_id` and `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a citation, as `proposed_by_user_id` and `signed_by_user_id` on a sponsorship deal, as `resolved_by_user_id` on a fulfillment obligation, and as `last_offer_by_user_id` on a space contract — and three separation rules described in §2.7 compare a request's `X-User-ID` against one of those recorded values.

The `Exhibitor` role is different: it is entity-backed. When `X-Role` is `Exhibitor`, the request must additionally carry `X-Role-Id`, and that value is the `id` of the acting `Exhibitor` row. If `X-Role-Id` is absent, or does not resolve to an existing exhibitor, the request fails with `ERR_IDENTITY_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403). Ownership checks on any resource that references an exhibitor compare the resource's exhibitor foreign key to `X-Role-Id`: a space contract's `exhibitor_id`, a selection appointment's `exhibitor_id`, a sponsorship deal's `exhibitor_id`, and — for a booth — the `exhibitor_id` of the contract its `space_contract_id` resolves to. A mismatch is `ERR_EXHIBITOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403), and it guards exactly these operations: `POST /space-contracts/{id}/negotiate`, `POST /space-contracts/{id}/sign`, `POST /space-contracts/{id}/deposits`, `POST /space-contracts/{id}/cancel`, `POST /space-contracts/{id}/rebook`, `POST /space-contracts/{id}/service-orders`, `POST /space-contracts/{id}/lead-scans`, `POST /selection-appointments/{id}/exercise`, and `POST /sponsorship-deals/{id}/cancel`.

The five roles divide authority as follows. A `ShowDirector` acts for the producer and holds every irreversible power: registering, executing and terminating venue contracts; creating shows, publishing floor plans and driving the show lifecycle; posting manual priority-point adjustments and reinstating suspended exhibitors; packaging sponsorship inventory and signing sponsorship deals; adjudicating citations; overriding a waitlisted registration; approving a discount above the sales threshold; and waiving a fulfillment obligation. A `SalesRep` sells space: opening contracts, re-offering within the ordinary discount threshold, booking selection appointments, advancing selection rounds, exercising an appointment on an exhibitor's behalf at the desk, proposing sponsorship deals, posting deposits and driving rebooking — but a `SalesRep` may never sign a sponsorship deal and may never approve a discount above the threshold. A `FloorManager` runs the physical floor: adding and combining booths, taking service orders, writing citations, recording lead scans and marking sponsorship deliverables delivered. An `Exhibitor` may touch only its own rows, and two operations belong to it alone — signing its own space contract, and filing a counter in the negotiation cycle. A `Registrant` submits its own registration for screening and holds no other authority anywhere in the API. All five roles may set the logical clock through the test-driving capability described in §2.4, and read access is broadly shared as tabulated in §4.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside those bounds is a structural validation failure and is answered with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — the oldest row first. Every list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filter before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows matching the filter remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps, Versions and the Logical Clock

Every resource in this system is addressed by an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply one. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row — and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation. The `version` field is the substrate of the concurrency control described in §2.5, and the specification below is explicit about which rows have their `version` bumped by each cascade, because a cascade that touches a row without advancing its `version` would let a stale client write over it. One entity is deliberately exempt: a lead scan is an append-only record with no update endpoint, so a lead scan carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all.

Time in this system is a logical clock, not wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; the server must never fall back to real system time. An administrative test-driving capability accepts a `current_utc` instant and moves the logical clock to it, modifying no business row and incrementing no `version`. Every deadline, appointment window, day band, service-order rate variant and point-decay rule in this document reads that clock, and the timestamps the system writes from it — `executed_at`, `signed_at`, `ordered_at` and `scanned_at` — are recorded from the logical clock at the moment the write is accepted.

### 2.5. Version Pinning on Inventory-Consuming Writes

Booth inventory is finite and contested, so any request that converts available floor into a held or reshaped commitment must prove it was composed against a floor plan the client had actually read. Six write operations therefore take a non-optional expected-version field in their request body, and each one pins a specific parent row.

Four of them pin the show whose floor is being consumed and require `expected_show_version` to equal that show's current `version`:

- `POST /space-contracts` — opening a contract off-appointment converts available booths into held booths, so the client's picture of what is still available, and of the committed ratio that gates the write, must be current.
- `POST /selection-appointments/{id}/exercise` — an exercise picks booths out of the available pool, and a booth chosen against a superseded floor plan may already have been held by an earlier exercise.
- `POST /space-contracts/{id}/sign` — signing converts held inventory into assigned inventory and can push the show's committed ratio across a posture threshold, so the decision must be made against a current ratio.
- `POST /booths/{id}/combine` — a combine rewrites the show's sellable inventory, and must not silently absorb a booth another writer has already held.

If the supplied `expected_show_version` does not equal the show's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SHOW_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written.

The fifth show-pinned rule is `POST /space-contracts/{id}/rebook`, whose expected-version field is named `expected_target_show_version`. A rebook creates a contract on the *target* edition and holds booths there, so the field pins the target show's `version`, never the version of the show being rebooked from. A mismatch is again `ERR_STALE_SHOW_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

The sixth rule pins a venue contract. `POST /venue-contracts/{id}/terminate` requires `expected_venue_contract_version` to equal the venue contract's current `version`. Termination cascades to every non-terminal show under the agreement, so the blast radius is a function of the contract's current child set; a terminate authorised against an older version was authorised against a smaller blast radius than the one it would actually execute, and is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VENUE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint accepts an expected-version field. In particular, negotiating, depositing, cancelling, adjudicating, resolving an obligation, screening a registration and recording a lead scan carry no version input, because none of them claims new inventory.

### 2.6. Precision, Rounding and the Arithmetic of Cents, Feet and Points

Money is stored and computed in whole cents. Wherever this document says an amount is rounded, the method is round half up — a value exactly on the half boundary rounds away from zero, upward. The rounding *positions* matter as much as the method, and three of them are pinned:

- Booth space pricing rounds **once per booth line**, and the rounded lines are then summed. Summing exact line values and rounding the total once produces a different number and is wrong.
- The bundle discount on a contract invoice rounds exactly once, at the point where the discount percentage is applied to the pre-discount subtotal.
- A floor-rate service order rounds the rate product first and then adds the flat expedite fee; the fee is never included in the rounded product.

Area is exact integer square feet, and the area ledger permits no rounding anywhere. The single exception is the aisle deduction that produces a show's sellable area, which floors rather than rounds — an aisle may never be narrowed by rounding. The venue's attrition allowance is likewise floored, which favours the venue.

Percentages that the system derives are integers rounded half up: a show's `committed_ratio_pct`, a registration's `qualification_score`, and every percentage in the post-show report. Percentages that a client supplies or that come from a rate card are integers in the range 0 through 100 unless a narrower bound is stated. Two derived percentages are exceptions and may carry a fractional part: `volume_tier_pct` and `bundle_discount_pct` are drawn from fixed tier tables whose members include a value of two and a half.

Priority points are integers. Every division in the point formulas floors, and each term is floored independently before the terms are summed, which makes the sum order-insensitive and removes any need for a processing-order tie-break. The published totals are then clamped at zero, so an exhibitor's standing can never be reported as negative — but the clamp applies only to the published totals, never to the underlying `penalty_points` counter, which keeps accumulating.

### 2.7. Segregation of Duties

Role membership is never by itself sufficient authority for the second step of a two-step commitment. Three pairings are enforced by comparing the request's `X-User-ID` against an operator identifier recorded earlier on the row.

First, the operator who packaged and priced a sponsorship may not be the operator who commits the show to it: signing a sponsorship deal requires an `X-User-ID` different from the deal's `proposed_by_user_id`, and a match is rejected with `ERR_SOD_SPONSORSHIP_SIGN` (HTTP 403). Second, the floor manager who wrote a citation may not rule on it, for either disposition: adjudicating a rule violation requires an `X-User-ID` different from that violation's `reported_by_user_id`, and a match is `ERR_SOD_VIOLATION_ADJUDICATION` (HTTP 403). Third, an operator may not self-approve a deep discount on the offer that follows its own: when a re-offer carries an `approved_discount_pct` greater than 10, the approving `X-User-ID` must differ from the contract's `last_offer_by_user_id`, and a match is `ERR_SOD_DISCOUNT_APPROVAL` (HTTP 403).

### 2.8. Floor Posture: The Derived Gate over Inventory

Each show publishes a derived `floor_posture` that summarises how much of its floor is already committed and whether the floor is under enforcement action. It is recomputed after every write that changes the show's `held_net_sqft`, `assigned_net_sqft`, `sellable_booth_sqft`, `status` or `upheld_violation_count`, and it takes one of exactly three values: `OPEN`, `SPACE_LOCKED`, or `FROZEN`.

The computation starts from the committed ratio. When `sellable_booth_sqft` is zero, `committed_ratio_pct` is zero. Otherwise `committed_ratio_pct` is one hundred multiplied by the sum of `held_net_sqft` and `assigned_net_sqft`, divided by `sellable_booth_sqft`, rounded half up to a whole percent. The posture is then `FROZEN` when `committed_ratio_pct` is at least 100, and also when the show is `ON_SITE` and its `upheld_violation_count` has reached three; otherwise it is `SPACE_LOCKED` when `committed_ratio_pct` is at least 85; otherwise it is `OPEN`. In the same pass, `available_net_sqft` is set to `sellable_booth_sqft` less `held_net_sqft` less `assigned_net_sqft`.

Each posture level blocks a different set of operations, and each returns its own error rather than a generic one. A `FROZEN` floor blocks the four operations that would consume new inventory — opening a space contract, exercising a selection appointment, rebooking onto this show, and adding a booth to the floor plan — each with `ERR_SHOW_FLOOR_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). A floor that is not `OPEN` blocks booth creation with `ERR_SHOW_FLOOR_SPACE_LOCKED` (HTTP 409), and that same code answers a re-offer whose `approved_discount_pct` exceeds 10 while the posture is `SPACE_LOCKED`, effectively capping a locked floor's discretionary discounting at the ordinary sales threshold. Posture never blocks signing, depositing, cancelling, adjudicating or closing out an existing hold — a frozen floor must always remain workable back down to `OPEN`.

### 2.9. Four Conserved Quantities

Because the show, the exhibitor and the sponsorship package all carry denormalised ledgers rather than recounting their children on every read, this specification names four quantities that must hold after **every** write, and that must return exactly to their pre-operation values once a compensating operation is applied. A compensating pair is one of: hold and release, sign and cancel, propose and cancel, or credit and reverse. "Exactly" means to the square foot, the unit, the cent and the point — no drift is tolerable, and no implementation may reconcile a discrepancy by iterating.

**The floor area partition.** A show's `sellable_booth_sqft` must always equal the sum of its `available_net_sqft`, `held_net_sqft` and `assigned_net_sqft`. Each of those three terms must independently equal the summed `area_sqft` of that show's booths in the corresponding status: `held_net_sqft` over booths whose `status` is `HELD`, `assigned_net_sqft` over booths whose `status` is `ASSIGNED`, and `available_net_sqft` over booths whose `status` is `AVAILABLE`. Booths whose `status` is `MERGED` are excluded from every term of both statements, because a combine sets each child booth to `MERGED` and adds that child's `area_sqft` to the surviving booth's `area_sqft` — so `sellable_booth_sqft` is unchanged by a combine, the merged area having left the partition exactly as it entered the survivor. Finally, for any contract, a sign followed by a cancel returns all three of `available_net_sqft`, `held_net_sqft` and `assigned_net_sqft` to the exact values they held immediately before that contract's holds were taken. These statements are asserted after every write that touches a booth's `status` or a contract's booth set: opening a contract, exercising an appointment, re-offering, rebooking, signing, cancelling, the close-out sweep at show close, adding a booth, and combining booths.

**Sponsorship unit conservation.** A package's `inventory_total` must always equal `inventory_committed` plus `inventory_available`, and `inventory_committed` must always equal the count of that package's deals whose `deal_status` is either `PROPOSED` or `SIGNED`. A unit is consumed when a deal is *proposed*, not when it is signed — a proposal holds the slot. `inventory_available` is never negative and never exceeds `inventory_total`. Proposing and then cancelling restores `inventory_committed` and `inventory_available` to their exact pre-proposal values. These statements are asserted after every proposal, signature and cancellation of a sponsorship deal.

**Deposit conservation.** For any space contract, `deposit_paid_cents` must always equal `refund_cents` plus `fee_applied_to_deposit_cents`, so every cent collected is accounted for; and `cancellation_fee_cents` must always equal `fee_applied_to_deposit_cents` plus `balance_due_cents`, so the fee is fully split. At most one of `refund_cents` and `balance_due_cents` is ever non-zero. While the contract has not been cancelled, `deposit_paid_cents` equals the sum of the deposit amounts the system has accepted, and all three of `refund_cents`, `fee_applied_to_deposit_cents` and `balance_due_cents` are zero. The split is computed with a minimum and two subtractions only, so it cannot lose a cent to rounding, and no netting is ever performed across contracts. These statements are asserted after every deposit and every cancellation.

**Point ledger conservation.** An exhibitor's `provisional_priority_points` must always equal its `ledger_points` plus `space_points_provisional` plus `sponsorship_fulfillment_points` less `penalty_points`, clamped at zero, where `space_points_provisional` is the accumulation of the space contributions of the exhibitor's contracts in the provisional set together with the sponsorship spend term described in §3.3. Per contract, `points_awarded` equals that contract's space-point contribution while the contract sits in the provisional set, and is exactly zero once the contract is `CANCELLED`. A sign followed by a cancel returns `provisional_priority_points` and `priority_tier` to their exact pre-sign values and resets the contract's `points_awarded` to zero. And `penalty_points` is purely additive: it equals the summed `penalty_points` of the exhibitor's violations whose `status` is `UPHELD`, plus three points for each `FORFEITED` selection appointment, plus twenty points for each deal that reached a breached fulfillment state — with waiving a violation subtracting exactly what upholding it would have added. These statements are asserted after every write that can move a point: contract signing, contract cancellation, the close-out cascade at show close, sponsorship signing and cancellation, obligation resolution, citation adjudication, appointment forfeiture, and manual ledger adjustment.

### 2.10. Cascades, Nested Reads and Aggregate Recomputation

Aggregates are never lazily derived at read time. Whenever a write changes a child row that feeds a parent's ledger, the parent's ledger fields are rewritten in the same request and the parent's `version` is incremented. This applies in both directions of a hierarchy: adding a booth rewrites the owning show's `sellable_booth_sqft`, `available_net_sqft`, `committed_ratio_pct` and `floor_posture` and bumps the show's `version`; resolving a fulfillment obligation rewrites the owning deal's `resolved_obligation_count` and `fulfillment_status` and bumps the deal's `version`; recording a lead scan increments the scanned registration's `lead_scan_count` and its `version`.

Cascades that touch many rows are processed in a deterministic order so that two servers executing the same request produce identical results. Where a cascade fans out over a collection, the collection is walked in `created_at` ascending order — shows under a terminating venue contract, space contracts under a cancelling show, and fulfillment obligations under a closing show are all processed oldest-first. Cascades are transitive: terminating a venue contract cancels its shows, and each of those show cancellations then fires its own downstream links over appointments, space contracts, booths, service orders, sponsorship deals and exhibitor points. Every row a cascade actually mutates has its own `version` incremented.

Read paths that nest one collection under another resource are resolved strictly left to right. For a path such as the one that resolves a single fulfillment obligation, the deal named by the first path identifier is looked up and validated first; only then is the obligation named by the second path identifier considered, and it must genuinely belong to that deal. An obligation that exists but whose `sponsorship_deal_id` points at a different deal is reported as `ERR_OBLIGATION_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), not as a validation error — under a nested path, the path *is* the identity. The same left-to-right discipline governs every reverse-reachability list: a request for the shows under a venue contract, the booths of a show, the service orders of a contract or the scans of a registration first resolves the parent and reports the parent's own not-found error if it is missing.

### 2.11. Order of Evaluation

When a single request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. The server evaluates in the following order, stopping at the first failure.

1. **Request shape.** The body must parse; every input field the endpoint requires must be present; every enumerated field must hold a declared value; every field naming cents, square feet, a percentage or a quantity must be an integer within range (percentage fields from 0 through 100, money and area fields at zero or above, quantities of at least 1); and every timestamp or date field must be a valid ISO 8601 UTC value. Failures here are `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422). These are structural, single-field checks that consult no stored data, which is why they outrank even existence.
2. **Authentication**, then **role authorisation**, then **exhibitor identity**, then **exhibitor scope** — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, `ERR_IDENTITY_REQUIRED`, `ERR_EXHIBITOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` in that sequence, as specified in §2.1.
3. **Segregation of duties**, in the order sponsorship signing, then citation adjudication, then discount approval: `ERR_SOD_SPONSORSHIP_SIGN`, then `ERR_SOD_VIOLATION_ADJUDICATION`, then `ERR_SOD_DISCOUNT_APPROVAL`.
4. **Existence**, path resources before body references, in this order: venue contract (`ERR_VENUE_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`), show (`ERR_SHOW_NOT_FOUND`), exhibitor (`ERR_EXHIBITOR_NOT_FOUND`), booth (`ERR_BOOTH_NOT_FOUND`), space contract (`ERR_SPACE_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`), selection appointment (`ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`), sponsorship package (`ERR_SPONSORSHIP_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`), sponsorship deal (`ERR_SPONSORSHIP_DEAL_NOT_FOUND`), fulfillment obligation (`ERR_OBLIGATION_NOT_FOUND`), registration (`ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`), rule violation (`ERR_RULE_VIOLATION_NOT_FOUND`), each HTTP 404. Body references are checked here too: `venue_contract_id` on show creation, `show_id` and `exhibitor_id` on contract creation, `exhibitor_id` on appointment booking and on deal proposal, every element of `booth_ids`, and `registration_id` on a lead scan.
5. **Per-row state preconditions**, in this order: venue contract status (`ERR_VENUE_CONTRACT_NOT_EXECUTED` or `ERR_VENUE_CONTRACT_NOT_DRAFT`), show status (`ERR_INVALID_SHOW_STATUS`), show transition reachability (`ERR_INVALID_SHOW_TRANSITION`), show-closed requirement (`ERR_SHOW_NOT_CLOSED`), contract status (`ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATUS`), appointment state (`ERR_INVALID_APPOINTMENT_STATE`), deal status (`ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STATUS`), obligation already resolved (`ERR_OBLIGATION_ALREADY_RESOLVED`), citation already adjudicated (`ERR_VIOLATION_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`), and merged-booth rejection (`ERR_BOOTH_MERGED`) — all HTTP 409.
6. **Version pinning.** Every expected-version field supplied in the body is compared to the current `version` of the parent row it names, yielding `ERR_STALE_SHOW_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_VENUE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Concurrency control sits deliberately *after* existence and per-row state — a stale version on a non-existent or wrongly-stated row still reports the sharper error — and strictly *before* every business rule.
7. **Standing and floor safety**, in this order: exhibitor suspension (`ERR_EXHIBITOR_SUSPENDED`), frozen floor (`ERR_SHOW_FLOOR_FROZEN`), locked floor (`ERR_SHOW_FLOOR_SPACE_LOCKED`), booth height limits (`ERR_BOOTH_HEIGHT_LIMIT`).
8. **Referential coherence**, in this order: appointment ownership (`ERR_APPOINTMENT_EXHIBITOR_MISMATCH`), booth-to-show membership (`ERR_BOOTH_NOT_IN_SHOW`), registration-to-show membership (`ERR_REGISTRATION_SHOW_MISMATCH`), rebook target validity (`ERR_REBOOK_TARGET_INVALID`), combine-set validity (`ERR_INVALID_COMBINE_SET`).
9. **Inventory, uniqueness and bounded loops**, in this order: booth availability (`ERR_BOOTH_NOT_AVAILABLE`), empty selector pool (`ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_BOOTH`), aisle ratio (`ERR_AISLE_RATIO_VIOLATION`), floor-plan aggregate checks (`ERR_FLOOR_INVENTORY_OVERCOMMITTED` and `ERR_FLOOR_PLAN_EMPTY`), booth-number uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_BOOTH_NUMBER`), duplicate active contract (`ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_CONTRACT`), negotiation budget (`ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`), sponsorship inventory (`ERR_SPONSORSHIP_INVENTORY_EXHAUSTED`), exclusivity conflict (`ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_CATEGORY_TAKEN`), duplicate lead scan (`ERR_DUPLICATE_LEAD_SCAN`), missing waiver or override reason (`ERR_WAIVER_REASON_REQUIRED`).
10. **Calendars, windows and overlap**, in this order: show date ordering (`ERR_INVALID_SHOW_WINDOW`), contract term containment (`ERR_SHOW_OUTSIDE_CONTRACT_TERM`), hall occupancy overlap (`ERR_HALL_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT`), appointment window shape (`ERR_INVALID_APPOINTMENT_WINDOW`), appointment window closed (`ERR_APPOINTMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED`), selection desk capacity (`ERR_SELECTION_DESK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`), service-order window (`ERR_SERVICE_ORDER_WINDOW_CLOSED`), lead-scan window (`ERR_LEAD_SCAN_OUTSIDE_SHOW_WINDOW`).
11. **Money last**, in this order: discount approval authority (`ERR_DISCOUNT_APPROVAL_REQUIRED`), discount cap (`ERR_DISCOUNT_EXCEEDS_CAP`), sponsorship price floor (`ERR_SPONSORSHIP_PRICE_BELOW_FLOOR`), deposit minimum at signing (`ERR_DEPOSIT_BELOW_MINIMUM`), deposit ceiling (`ERR_DEPOSIT_EXCEEDS_INVOICE`).

Four consequences of this ladder are worth spelling out, because they are the cases an implementation is most likely to get backwards. A sign request against an `ON_SITE` show whose deposit is also below the minimum reports `ERR_INVALID_SHOW_STATUS`, not `ERR_DEPOSIT_BELOW_MINIMUM`: the show being past selling closes the door before the money is even counted. An exercise by a suspended exhibitor whose appointment window has also already shut reports `ERR_EXHIBITOR_SUSPENDED`, not `ERR_APPOINTMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED`: standing is a compliance gate, while a window is only a schedule. A booth reference that belongs to another show *and* is already held there reports `ERR_BOOTH_NOT_IN_SHOW`, not `ERR_BOOTH_NOT_AVAILABLE`: a cross-show reference is a broken relationship, not an inventory shortage. And on an exercise, an appointment whose own show does not match the booths being claimed reports `ERR_APPOINTMENT_EXHIBITOR_MISMATCH` rather than `ERR_BOOTH_NOT_IN_SHOW`, because on that operation the appointment is the identity of the request.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Contracting the Hall and Scheduling Editions

Everything begins with a master agreement between the producer and a venue. A `ShowDirector` registers one by naming the venue with a `venue_code` and the specific exhibit hall inside it with a `hall_code`; both are one to thirty-two characters drawn only from uppercase A through Z, the digits 0 through 9, and the underscore. That pair is the physical resource whose date occupancy is exclusive, and §3.1's calendar rules below turn on it. The hall's own dimensions come next: `hall_gross_sqft` is the gross contracted floor area and must be at least one square foot; `min_aisle_ratio_pct` is the fire marshal's minimum share of gross area that must remain aisle, an integer from 0 through 90 that a show may exceed but never undercut; and `max_booth_height_ft` is the hall's rigging limit, at least one foot.

The multi-year shape of the agreement is expressed by `contract_year_start`, the first edition year covered, which must be 2026 or later, and `contract_year_count`, the number of consecutive edition years covered, from 1 through 10. The commercial terms follow: `committed_net_sqft_per_year` is the net sold square footage the producer promises the venue each edition year and is at least one, and `attrition_allowance_pct` — from 0 through 100 — is the share of that commitment forgiven before attrition damages begin to accrue. The exhibitor-services rate card is stored on the same row: `advance_order_discount_pct`, an integer from 0 through 60, is the discount applied to service orders placed by a show's advance-order deadline, while `labor_rate_cents_per_hour`, `electrical_rate_cents_per_amp` and `material_handling_rate_cents_per_cwt` are the straight-time installation labour rate per hour, the utility rate per ordered amp of 120-volt service, and the freight rate per hundredweight — each at least one cent.

Finally the agreement carries a `cancellation_fee_schedule`: an ordered array of buckets, each an object with a `min_days_out` integer and a `pct` integer. Between one and six buckets are permitted; every `min_days_out` must be at least zero and distinct from the others; every `pct` must lie from 0 through 100; and exactly one bucket must have a `min_days_out` of zero, so that a cancellation on or after the first show day always finds a band. When the field is omitted at registration, it defaults to three buckets — 25 percent at 180 days out, 50 percent at 90 days out, and 100 percent at zero days out. A newly registered agreement has a `status` of `DRAFT`, a null `executed_at`, a null `terminated_effective_date`, and a `show_count` of zero. `show_count` is thereafter maintained as the number of shows whose `venue_contract_id` is this agreement and whose own status is anything other than `CANCELLED`.

Countersigning the agreement moves it `DRAFT → EXECUTED`. The operation requires that the agreement currently be `DRAFT` — a replayed countersignature on an already-executed or already-terminated agreement is answered with `ERR_VENUE_CONTRACT_NOT_DRAFT` — and it re-verifies that the `cancellation_fee_schedule` holds exactly one bucket whose `min_days_out` is zero. On success the system writes `executed_at` from the logical clock and increments the agreement's `version`. No show may be created under an agreement that is not `EXECUTED`; an attempt reports `ERR_VENUE_CONTRACT_NOT_EXECUTED`.

Termination moves the agreement `EXECUTED → TERMINATED`. The request supplies `expected_venue_contract_version` as described in §2.5, a `terminated_effective_date` as an ISO 8601 date, and optionally a free-text `reason`. The effective date is stored in `terminated_effective_date`, the agreement's `version` is incremented, and `show_count` is recomputed. The first and largest cascade link fires here: every show whose `venue_contract_id` is this agreement and whose status is `PLANNING`, `SPACE_SELECTION`, `SELLING` or `ON_SITE` is transitioned to `CANCELLED` with its `cancellation_reason` set to the literal `VENUE_CONTRACT_TERMINATED`, processed oldest-first by `created_at`. Each of those show cancellations then fires the full show-cancellation cascade described in §3.9, so a single termination can release booths, cancel space contracts and sponsorship deals, and reverse exhibitor points across several editions at once.

Creating an edition of the show is likewise a `ShowDirector` action. The request names the `venue_contract_id` it is booked under, a marketing `name` of one to one hundred and twenty characters, an `edition_year` of 2026 or later, four ISO 8601 dates — `move_in_start`, `event_start`, `event_end` and `move_out_end` — the `gross_sqft` this edition takes in the hall, the `aisle_ratio_pct` this floor plan reserves for aisles, cross-aisles and fire lanes, a published `base_rate_cents_per_sqft` of at least one cent, and a `selection_desk_capacity` of at least one. The `gross_sqft` is at least one square foot and may not exceed the hall's `hall_gross_sqft`; the `aisle_ratio_pct` is an integer from 0 through 90 and may not fall below the hall's `min_aisle_ratio_pct`, which is checked at creation and again at publication and reported as `ERR_AISLE_RATIO_VIOLATION`.

Three calendar rules then run, in the order given. The four dates must be non-decreasing — `move_in_start` on or before `event_start`, `event_start` on or before `event_end`, `event_end` on or before `move_out_end`, all read as UTC dates — or the request fails with `ERR_INVALID_SHOW_WINDOW`. The whole occupancy window must then fall inside the agreement's term: the calendar year of `move_in_start` and the calendar year of `move_out_end` must both lie between `contract_year_start` and `contract_year_start` plus `contract_year_count` minus one inclusive, and `edition_year` must equal the calendar year of `event_start`; otherwise `ERR_SHOW_OUTSIDE_CONTRACT_TERM`. Last comes the hall itself. Each edition occupies its hall over the closed interval that runs from two days before its `move_in_start` to two days after its `move_out_end` — a symmetric dark-day buffer of two days on each side, giving the crews time to strike one show and set the next. If that buffered interval overlaps the equally buffered interval of any other show in the same `venue_code` and `hall_code` pairing whose status is `PLANNING`, `SPACE_SELECTION`, `SELLING`, `ON_SITE` or `CLOSED`, the request fails with `ERR_HALL_OCCUPANCY_CONFLICT`. Because the intervals are closed and the buffer is symmetric, a move-in exactly two days after another show's move-out still collides, and one further day is clear. A `CANCELLED` show releases its hall and never conflicts.

A newly created edition starts with a `status` of `PLANNING`, a `selection_round` of 0, a `floor_posture` of `OPEN`, a null `cancellation_reason`, and every area and money ledger — `net_sellable_sqft`, `sellable_booth_sqft`, `held_net_sqft`, `assigned_net_sqft`, `available_net_sqft`, `committed_ratio_pct`, `upheld_violation_count`, `attrition_shortfall_sqft` and `attrition_settlement_cents` — initialised to zero, with `advance_order_deadline` unset until the floor plan is published. Creation increments the parent agreement's `show_count` and its `version`. Shows may be listed under their agreement, filtered by `status`.

### 3.2. Designing the Floor: Booth Inventory, Combines and Publication

While an edition is still `PLANNING`, a `ShowDirector` or a `FloorManager` builds its floor plan one booth at a time. Each booth carries a `booth_number` — one to sixteen characters of uppercase A through Z and digits 0 through 9 — which must be unique among the booths of the same show; a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_BOOTH_NUMBER`. Its geometry is given as `width_ft`, the frontage, and `depth_ft`, the depth, each at least one foot, from which the system computes `area_sqft` as the frontage multiplied by the depth. Its pricing geometry is given as a `location_class`, one of `INLINE`, `CORNER`, `PENINSULA` or `ISLAND`, and an optional `premium_zone`, one of `NONE`, `MAIN_AISLE`, `ENTRANCE` or `FOOD_COURT`, which defaults to `NONE` when omitted. Finally each booth declares its own `max_height_ft`, the tallest structure permitted inside it, which must be at least one foot and may not exceed the hall's `max_booth_height_ft`; a booth that reaches above the rigging is refused with `ERR_BOOTH_HEIGHT_LIMIT`.

A new booth's `status` is `AVAILABLE`, with a null `space_contract_id` and a null `combined_parent_booth_id`. Adding a booth is only legal while the show is `PLANNING`, and the floor must be genuinely `OPEN`: a `SPACE_LOCKED` floor refuses new inventory with `ERR_SHOW_FLOOR_SPACE_LOCKED`, and a `FROZEN` floor refuses it with `ERR_SHOW_FLOOR_FROZEN`. One aggregate rule also applies to booth creation, and it is an aggregate rule precisely because each individual booth is legal while the collection is not: the summed `area_sqft` of the show's booths whose status is anything other than `MERGED` may never exceed the show's sellable area — the value derived from `gross_sqft` and `aisle_ratio_pct` described below, evaluated on demand even before publication has written it into `net_sellable_sqft`. A booth that would push the collection past that ceiling is refused with `ERR_FLOOR_INVENTORY_OVERCOMMITTED`. Every accepted booth rewrites the show's `sellable_booth_sqft`, `available_net_sqft`, `committed_ratio_pct` and `floor_posture` and increments the show's `version`.

Larger island spaces are built by physically combining adjacent booths rather than by editing dimensions. A `FloorManager` or `ShowDirector` names a surviving booth in the path and supplies `expected_show_version` together with a `booth_ids` array of the children to absorb. Every child must currently be `AVAILABLE` with a null `combined_parent_booth_id`, must share the surviving booth's `show_id`, and must not be the surviving booth itself. A child that is already `MERGED` is refused with `ERR_BOOTH_MERGED`; a child belonging to another show is refused with `ERR_BOOTH_NOT_IN_SHOW`; a set that names a booth of another show or lists the survivor as its own child is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COMBINE_SET`; a `booth_ids` element that resolves to nothing is `ERR_BOOTH_NOT_FOUND`. On success each child moves `AVAILABLE → MERGED` with its `combined_parent_booth_id` pointing at the survivor, and each child's `area_sqft` is added to the survivor's `area_sqft`. Both the child rows and the survivor row have their `version` incremented, and the show's `sellable_booth_sqft`, `available_net_sqft`, `committed_ratio_pct` and `floor_posture` are recomputed along with the show's `version`. `MERGED` is terminal: a merged booth can never again be held, assigned, or un-merged, and its area now lives permanently inside the survivor. As §2.9 states, a combine leaves `sellable_booth_sqft` unchanged — area is conserved, never created.

Publishing the floor plan closes the design phase and opens selection, moving the edition `PLANNING → SPACE_SELECTION`. The operation requires that the show still be `PLANNING`, that its venue agreement still be `EXECUTED`, that its `aisle_ratio_pct` be at least the hall's `min_aisle_ratio_pct`, that at least one booth of the show be in a status other than `MERGED` — a show with nothing but merged shells, or with no booths at all, is refused with `ERR_FLOOR_PLAN_EMPTY` — and that the summed `area_sqft` of those non-merged booths not exceed the sellable area, else `ERR_FLOOR_INVENTORY_OVERCOMMITTED`. On success the system writes `net_sellable_sqft` as the show's `gross_sqft` multiplied by one hundred less its `aisle_ratio_pct`, divided by one hundred, with any fractional square foot discarded downward. Flooring rather than rounding is deliberate: rounding up would narrow an aisle. Merged children are excluded from the booth-area side of that comparison, because their area has already been folded into their survivors and counting it twice would overstate the floor.

Publication also computes the edition's `advance_order_deadline` as the date of `move_in_start` less twenty-one days — three weeks before the trucks arrive — which is the boundary that later decides whether an exhibitor service order is priced at advance or floor rates. The show's `floor_posture` is recomputed and its `version` incremented. Booths are readable individually and as a filtered list, narrowable by `show_id`, by `space_contract_id`, by `combined_parent_booth_id` for discovering what a survivor absorbed, and by `status`, `premium_zone` or `location_class`.

### 3.3. Exhibitor Standing and the Priority-Point Ledger

An exhibitor account is opened by a `ShowDirector` or a `SalesRep` with a `legal_name` of one to one hundred and sixty characters, which must be unique across all exhibitors when compared case-insensitively, and a `contact_email` for the primary sales contact, which must contain exactly one at-sign. A `consecutive_years` count may be supplied to migrate the company's history out of the legacy system — the number of consecutive prior editions it has exhibited at, at least zero, defaulting to zero when omitted — and it is thereafter incremented by exactly one on every successful rebook. A new account has a `status` of `ACTIVE` and every point counter at zero: `ledger_points`, `sponsorship_fulfillment_points` and `penalty_points`.

Priority points are the currency of space selection, and this system publishes two different standings from the same underlying ledger. `provisional_priority_points` is the forward-looking number used for tiering, sales conversations and dashboards. `certified_priority_points` is the closed-book number and is the *only* input to space-selection ranking. Both are computed from the same shared terms — the exhibitor's `ledger_points`, its `sponsorship_fulfillment_points`, and its `penalty_points` — and both use the same two building blocks. A space contract's contribution is its effective net square footage divided by one hundred with the remainder discarded, multiplied by two points per whole hundred-square-foot block, plus a flat five-point rebooking bonus when the contract's `rebooked_from_space_contract_id` is set. A sponsorship deal's contribution is its `negotiated_price_cents` divided by five hundred thousand cents — one point for every five thousand dollars of sponsorship spend — with the remainder discarded.

The provisional variant looks at live business. Its contract set is the exhibitor's space contracts whose `status` is `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED` or `SIGNED` and whose show is in `PLANNING`, `SPACE_SELECTION`, `SELLING` or `ON_SITE`. Within that set, a contract's effective net square footage is its `assigned_net_sqft` when the contract is `SIGNED`, and its `offered_net_sqft` otherwise — an unsigned hold counts at what it holds. The summed contract contributions are published as `space_points_provisional`. The provisional spend term sums the deal contributions of every sponsorship deal of this exhibitor whose `deal_status` is `SIGNED`. `provisional_priority_points` is then `ledger_points` plus `space_points_provisional` plus that spend term plus `sponsorship_fulfillment_points`, less `penalty_points`, with the result clamped at zero.

The certified variant looks only at history, and it decays. Its contract set is the exhibitor's space contracts whose `status` is `CLOSED_OUT` and whose show is `CLOSED`. Each such contract's edition age is the calendar year of the current logical clock less that contract's show's `edition_year`; when the edition age is strictly greater than three, the contract's contribution is halved with the remainder discarded, and otherwise it counts in full. The summed contributions are published as `space_points_certified`. The certified spend term sums the deal contributions of the exhibitor's `SIGNED` sponsorship deals whose package belongs to a show that is `CLOSED`. `certified_priority_points` is then `ledger_points` plus `space_points_certified` plus that certified spend term plus `sponsorship_fulfillment_points`, less `penalty_points`, again clamped at zero. Because the two variants filter contracts differently, the same exhibitor's two standings diverge whenever any of its contracts is still open — which is the entire point: an exhibitor cannot buy its way up the selection order with paper it has not yet honoured. The two status filters are exhaustive in the sense that a contract belonging to neither set contributes to neither variant.

Both standings share the property that every division floors and every term is floored independently before the terms are summed, so no processing order can change the answer. `priority_tier` is a band over the *provisional* number: `PLATINUM` at 1000 points or more, `GOLD` at 600 or more, `SILVER` at 300 or more, and `BRONZE` below that.

The whole ledger is recomputed on every event that can move a point: a space contract signing, cancelling, or closing out at show close; a sponsorship deal being signed or cancelled; a deal's fulfillment reaching completion; a citation being upheld; a selection appointment being forfeited; and a manual adjustment. Manual adjustments are a `ShowDirector` privilege and exist for loyalty grants, negotiated make-goods and corrections: the request carries a signed `points_delta` and a mandatory `reason`, the delta is added to `ledger_points`, both standings and `priority_tier` are recomputed, and the exhibitor's `version` is incremented.

Standing has teeth. When an exhibitor's `penalty_points` reaches thirty, the exhibitor transitions `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` as part of whichever write pushed it over. A `SUSPENDED` exhibitor may not acquire new space at all: opening a space contract, signing one, rebooking one, exercising a selection appointment, being booked a selection appointment, having a sponsorship deal proposed, and having a sponsorship deal signed are all refused with `ERR_EXHIBITOR_SUSPENDED`. Only a `ShowDirector` can undo this, by reinstating the account with a mandatory `reason`: reinstatement returns `status` to `ACTIVE`, resets `penalty_points` to zero, recomputes both standings and `priority_tier`, and increments the exhibitor's `version`. Note what reinstatement does *not* do — it does not revisit the citations that produced the penalties, and it does not restore points that the clamp at zero had already absorbed.

### 3.4. Space Selection Day: Windows, Ranking and Exercise

Space selection is a scheduled, ranked and timed event: exhibitors are given desk appointments in numbered rounds, the round's appointments are ordered by certified standing, and each exhibitor picks from what is still on the floor when its turn opens. A `ShowDirector` or `SalesRep` books an appointment against a show by naming the `exhibitor_id`, the `round_index` the appointment belongs to (at least 1), and the desk window as `window_start` and `window_end`, both ISO 8601 UTC instants. The window is half-open: it includes `window_start` and excludes `window_end`.

Two rules police the booking. The window must be well-formed and timely — `window_start` strictly before `window_end`, and both falling on or before the show's `event_start` — or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPOINTMENT_WINDOW`. And the desk itself is a finite resource: counting the `SCHEDULED` appointments of the same show and the same `round_index` whose half-open windows overlap the requested one, plus the requested appointment itself, the total may not exceed the show's `selection_desk_capacity`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SELECTION_DESK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. This is an aggregate temporal check: each appointment is individually legal while the concurrent set is not. Half-open semantics mean an appointment starting at the exact instant another ends does not overlap it. A booked appointment starts in state `SCHEDULED`, with `rank` and `certified_points_at_rank` both null until its round opens. Appointments are readable individually and as a filtered list, narrowable by `show_id`, `exhibitor_id`, `round_index` or `state`.

Opening a round is a `ShowDirector` or `SalesRep` action against a show that is in `SPACE_SELECTION`. It increments the show's `selection_round` by one and then does four things. First it assembles the candidate set: appointments of this show whose `state` is `SCHEDULED`, whose `round_index` equals the newly incremented `selection_round`, whose exhibitor's `status` is `ACTIVE`, and whose `window_end` is strictly later than the current logical clock. Second it orders that set — by snapshotted certified standing descending, then by the exhibitor's `consecutive_years` descending, then by the exhibitor's own `created_at` ascending, so that a dead heat is won by the longest-registered exhibitor. The exhibitor's `id` is never consulted as a tie-break. Each candidate is assigned a 1-based `rank` in that order, and each has `certified_points_at_rank` written from its exhibitor's `certified_priority_points` at that instant — snapshotting the standing so that a citation upheld mid-round cannot retroactively reorder a round already in progress. Third, only the first `selection_desk_capacity` candidates in rank order transition `SCHEDULED → ACTIVE`; candidates beyond that cap remain `SCHEDULED` with `rank` and `certified_points_at_rank` still null, waiting for a later round. Fourth, appointments that were not eligible are swept: any appointment of this show still `SCHEDULED` whose window has already elapsed, or whose exhibitor is no longer `ACTIVE`, transitions `SCHEDULED → EXPIRED` with `rank` and `certified_points_at_rank` left null and **no** penalty charged; and any appointment left over in state `ACTIVE` from the previous round transitions `ACTIVE → FORFEITED`, which adds three penalty points to that exhibitor's `penalty_points` and increments both the appointment's and the exhibitor's `version`. A forfeiture does not disturb booths already converted into a contract by an earlier exercise — those holds survive. Every appointment the round touches has its `version` incremented, and the show's `version` is incremented once. If the candidate set is empty the round still advances: `selection_round` increments, no ranks are written, and the request succeeds with 200.

Exercising an `ACTIVE` appointment is how an exhibitor actually claims space, and it may be performed by the `Exhibitor` itself or by a `SalesRep` standing at the desk on its behalf. The request supplies `expected_show_version`, the `requested_sqft` the exhibitor wants, an optional `premium_placement_requested` flag defaulting to false, and optionally an explicit `booth_ids` array. The appointment must be in state `ACTIVE`, or the request fails with `ERR_INVALID_APPOINTMENT_STATE`. The logical clock must be at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end`; an exercise at the exact instant of `window_end` is late and is refused with `ERR_APPOINTMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The appointment's own `exhibitor_id` must be the acting exhibitor and its `show_id` must be the show whose booths are being claimed, or the request fails with `ERR_APPOINTMENT_EXHIBITOR_MISMATCH`. The show's `floor_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, the exhibitor's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, and the exhibitor must not already hold another space contract on this show in `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED` or `SIGNED` status — that last is `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_CONTRACT`.

A successful exercise transitions the appointment `ACTIVE → EXERCISED` and creates one space contract in `OFFERED` status for that show and exhibitor, with `offered_booth_ids` set to the booths resolved as described in §3.5. Each selected booth transitions `AVAILABLE → HELD` with its `space_contract_id` pointing at the new contract, and the new contract's `volume_tier_pct`, `space_fee_cents`, `min_deposit_cents`, `bundle_discount_pct`, `bundle_discount_cents` and `invoice_total_cents` are all computed. The appointment's `version` and the show's `version` are both incremented. The two branches out of `ACTIVE` are deliberately asymmetric: exercising consumes inventory and charges no penalty, while forfeiting consumes no inventory and charges a penalty of three points.

Selection ends when a `ShowDirector` moves the edition `SPACE_SELECTION → SELLING`. That transition is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SHOW_TRANSITION` while any appointment of the show is still in state `ACTIVE` — the desk must be clear before the general sale opens. On success, every appointment of the show still in state `SCHEDULED` transitions to `EXPIRED` with no penalty, the show's `floor_posture` is recomputed, and its `version` is incremented.

### 3.5. Selling Space: Choosing Booths, Pricing the Offer, Negotiating and Signing

Not every sale comes through the selection desk. A `SalesRep`, a `ShowDirector` or an `Exhibitor` may open a space contract directly against a show that is in `SPACE_SELECTION` or `SELLING`, naming the `show_id`, the `exhibitor_id`, the `expected_show_version` required by §2.5, the `requested_sqft` wanted, an optional `premium_placement_requested` flag defaulting to false, and optionally an explicit `booth_ids` array. The show's `floor_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, the exhibitor's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, and the exhibitor must not already hold another contract on this show in `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED` or `SIGNED` status. A new contract opens in `OFFERED` status with `negotiation_round` at 0, `requested_discount_pct` and `approved_discount_pct` at 0, `deposit_paid_cents` at 0, `points_awarded` at 0, a null `signed_at`, a null `last_offer_by_user_id`, a null `rebooked_from_space_contract_id`, a null `cancellation_reason`, and an `assigned_net_sqft` of 0 until it is signed. Contracts are readable individually and as a filtered list, narrowable by `show_id`, `exhibitor_id`, `rebooked_from_space_contract_id` or `status`.

**Which booths.** When `booth_ids` is supplied, those booths are taken as named — each must exist, must belong to the target show, must not be `MERGED`, and must be `AVAILABLE` (or already `HELD` by this very contract, in the re-offer case), failing with `ERR_BOOTH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOOTH_NOT_IN_SHOW`, `ERR_BOOTH_MERGED` and `ERR_BOOTH_NOT_AVAILABLE` respectively. When `booth_ids` is omitted, the server chooses, and its choice is fully determined. The candidate pool is every booth of the target show whose `status` is `AVAILABLE`, whose `combined_parent_booth_id` is null, whose `area_sqft` is at least the `requested_sqft`, and which is either not in the `ENTRANCE` premium zone or is being selected for a contract where `premium_placement_requested` is true. Held, assigned and merged booths are never candidates, and a booth belonging to a different show is never a candidate even when a caller names it explicitly.

The pool is then ordered into a total order in which no identifier, UUID or insertion pointer participates. When `premium_placement_requested` is false, candidates are ordered by `area_sqft` ascending first, then by `location_class` in the sequence `INLINE`, `CORNER`, `PENINSULA`, `ISLAND`, then by `booth_number` ascending. When `premium_placement_requested` is true, the primary key inverts to the traffic zone, ordered `ENTRANCE`, then `MAIN_AISLE`, then `FOOD_COURT`, then `NONE`, followed by `area_sqft` ascending, then the same `location_class` sequence, then `booth_number` ascending. The final tie-break is always `booth_number` ascending, compared as a case-sensitive byte-wise string; since `booth_number` is unique per show the order is total and the winner is unique. Exactly one booth — the first-ranked candidate — is selected. The steering is intentional: a buyer who has not asked and paid for placement can never be handed an entrance booth by the server, while a buyer who has asked for placement gets the highest-traffic zone available. If the candidate pool is empty, no booth is held, no partial write is made, and the whole request fails atomically with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_BOOTH`.

**What it costs.** The contract's `offered_booth_ids` is the ordered list of booths it currently holds or has assigned, and it is rewritten on every offer, counter-response and re-offer. `offered_net_sqft` is the summed `area_sqft` of those booths. From that total the system selects a `volume_tier_pct`, and the thresholds are inclusive at the bottom: 12 percent at 4000 square feet or more, 8 percent at 2000 or more, 5 percent at 1000 or more, and 0 percent below 1000. Exactly 1000 square feet therefore earns 5 percent, which is why a well-advised exhibitor buys the thousandth foot. The `volume_tier_pct` is added to the contract's current `approved_discount_pct`, and the sum is capped at 40 percent, giving the total discount applied to every line.

Each booth is then priced individually, walking `offered_booth_ids` in array order. A booth's exact gross line is its `area_sqft` multiplied by the show's `base_rate_cents_per_sqft`, multiplied by a location multiplier — 1.00 for `INLINE`, 1.10 for `CORNER`, 1.20 for `PENINSULA`, 1.30 for `ISLAND` — and multiplied by a zone multiplier: 1.00 for `NONE`, 1.08 for `MAIN_AISLE`, 1.15 for `ENTRANCE`, and 1.05 for `FOOD_COURT`. That exact gross line, carrying full precision, is then reduced by the total discount — multiplied by one hundred less the total discount percentage, divided by one hundred — and the *result of that reduction* is rounded half up to whole cents. `space_fee_cents` is the sum of those already-rounded per-booth lines. As §2.6 insists, rounding once per line and then summing is the definition; summing exact lines and rounding the total once is a different number and is wrong. The contract's `min_deposit_cents` is then 25 percent of `space_fee_cents`, rounded half up. Because the volume tier is a step function of the summed area rather than of any individual line, the same one thousand square feet can legitimately cost less than nine hundred and ninety-nine.

**What is invoiced.** The invoice is larger than the space fee, because a sponsor's attached spend is bundled into it, and the evaluation order is pinned. Step one takes `space_fee_cents` as just computed. Step two computes `sponsorship_attached_cents` as the summed `negotiated_price_cents` of every sponsorship deal whose `deal_status` is `SIGNED`, whose `exhibitor_id` matches this contract's exhibitor, and whose package belongs to this contract's show; proposed and cancelled deals contribute nothing. Step three adds those two figures into a pre-discount subtotal. Step four selects `bundle_discount_pct` from that **pre-discount** subtotal: 4 percent when the subtotal is at least five million cents (fifty thousand dollars), two and a half percent when it is at least two million cents (twenty thousand dollars), and 0 percent below that. Step five computes `bundle_discount_cents` as the subtotal multiplied by `bundle_discount_pct`, divided by one hundred, rounded half up — the single rounding in this calculation. Step six sets `invoice_total_cents` to the subtotal less `bundle_discount_cents`. The tier looks circular, since the discount reduces the very subtotal that selected it, and the resolution is that the order above *is* the definition: `invoice_total_cents` may legitimately end up below the threshold that earned the discount, and re-selecting the tier from `invoice_total_cents` is wrong. Every recomputation of the space fee reprices the invoice, and so does the signing or cancellation of any sponsorship deal for the same exhibitor on the same show.

**Negotiation.** The contract can cycle between `OFFERED` and `COUNTERED` a bounded number of times, and the two legs of the cycle belong to different parties. The exhibitor's leg is a counter: it requires an `action` of `COUNTER`, is available only to the `Exhibitor` that owns the contract, requires the contract to be `OFFERED` and the show to be in `SPACE_SELECTION` or `SELLING`, and requires the contract's `negotiation_round` to be strictly below three. It writes `requested_discount_pct` from the request — a percentage from 0 through 40 — increments `negotiation_round` by one, moves the contract `OFFERED → COUNTERED`, and increments the contract's `version`. A fourth counter, attempted when `negotiation_round` already equals three, is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`; this is the termination condition of the cycle.

The producer's leg is a re-offer: it requires an `action` of `REOFFER`, is available to a `SalesRep` or a `ShowDirector`, requires the contract to be `COUNTERED` and the show to be in `SPACE_SELECTION` or `SELLING`, and moves the contract `COUNTERED → OFFERED`. It writes `approved_discount_pct` — the discount the producer is actually granting — and records the acting operator in `last_offer_by_user_id`. Deep discounting is fenced three ways. A discount above 10 percent may only be approved by a `ShowDirector`; a `SalesRep` attempting it is refused with `ERR_DISCOUNT_APPROVAL_REQUIRED`. No role, the `ShowDirector` included, may approve more than 40 percent; beyond that the answer is `ERR_DISCOUNT_EXCEEDS_CAP`. And a discount above 10 percent requires an `OPEN` floor, so on a `SPACE_LOCKED` show the deep discount is refused with `ERR_SHOW_FLOOR_SPACE_LOCKED` — a nearly sold-out floor has no need to discount. The separation-of-duties rule of §2.7 also applies to any above-threshold approval. A re-offer may optionally supply `booth_ids`, which *replaces* the contract's booth set: booths dropped from the set transition `HELD → AVAILABLE` with their `space_contract_id` cleared, and booths added transition `AVAILABLE → HELD` with their `space_contract_id` set. The contract's `volume_tier_pct`, `space_fee_cents`, `min_deposit_cents`, `bundle_discount_pct`, `bundle_discount_cents` and `invoice_total_cents` are all recomputed over the new set, and both the contract's and the show's `version` are incremented. Critically, a re-offer does **not** increment `negotiation_round`: only the exhibitor's counters consume the round budget, so the producer can re-offer as often as it likes within three exhibitor counters.

**Signing.** Only the owning `Exhibitor` may sign, and only a contract that is `OFFERED` on a show still in `SPACE_SELECTION` or `SELLING`. The request supplies `expected_show_version` and the `deposit_paid_cents` being tendered. The exhibitor's `status` must be `ACTIVE`; `offered_booth_ids` must be non-empty and every booth it names must currently be `HELD` with its `space_contract_id` pointing at this contract; the tendered deposit must be at least `min_deposit_cents`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_BELOW_MINIMUM`; and the tendered deposit may not exceed `invoice_total_cents`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_EXCEEDS_INVOICE`.

A successful signature moves the contract `OFFERED → SIGNED` and freezes its commercial terms: `space_fee_cents`, `volume_tier_pct`, `bundle_discount_pct`, `bundle_discount_cents` and `invoice_total_cents` are held at their current values and are not repriced afterwards. `deposit_paid_cents` and `signed_at` are written, the latter from the logical clock. Every booth in `offered_booth_ids` transitions `HELD → ASSIGNED`, which makes the contract's `assigned_net_sqft` — the summed `area_sqft` of the booths whose `space_contract_id` is this contract and whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` — non-zero for the first time. `points_awarded` is written as that assigned footage divided by one hundred with the remainder discarded, multiplied by two, and is added to the exhibitor's space-point base, after which `space_points_provisional`, `provisional_priority_points` and `priority_tier` are recomputed. The show's `assigned_net_sqft`, `held_net_sqft`, `committed_ratio_pct` and `floor_posture` are recomputed, and the contract's, the exhibitor's and the show's `version` are each incremented.

Contract status gates every subsequent operation, and a mismatch is always `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATUS`: a counter and a signature require `OFFERED`, a re-offer requires `COUNTERED`, a cancellation accepts `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED` or `SIGNED`, deposits and service orders and lead scans and citations all require `SIGNED`, and rebooking requires `CLOSED_OUT`.

### 3.6. Deposits, Cancellation and the Money Waterfall

A signed contract can take further money at any time. An `Exhibitor` or a `SalesRep` posts a payment as an `amount_cents` figure against a contract that is `SIGNED`; the amount is added to `deposit_paid_cents`, the deposit invariants of §2.9 are asserted, and the contract's `version` is incremented. The one ceiling is the invoice: the existing `deposit_paid_cents` plus the new `amount_cents` may not exceed `invoice_total_cents`, and an over-payment is refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_EXCEEDS_INVOICE`. The same ceiling applies at signing, when the tendered deposit is the first payment.

Cancellation is available to the owning `Exhibitor` or to a `ShowDirector`, against a contract that is `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED` or `SIGNED`, on a show in `SPACE_SELECTION`, `SELLING` or `ON_SITE`. The request carries a mandatory `cancellation_reason`, which is stored on the row. A signed contract may not be cancelled once its show is `CLOSED` — at that point the contract has already been closed out and the floor is history.

The liability is computed in two steps, and the first step has three branches. When the cancellation arrives as a cascade from a show cancellation or a venue-contract termination, `cancellation_fee_cents` is forced to zero: the producer cancelled, not the exhibitor. When the contract was `OFFERED` or `COUNTERED` at the moment of cancellation, `cancellation_fee_cents` is likewise zero, because an unsigned hold carries no penalty. Only when the contract was `SIGNED` and the exhibitor is walking away does a fee accrue. In that case the system computes the days out — the number of whole days from the date of the current logical clock to the date of the show's `event_start`, which is to say the event date minus today — and then selects a band from the venue agreement's `cancellation_fee_schedule`. The buckets are scanned in `min_days_out` **descending** order, and the first bucket whose `min_days_out` is less than or equal to the days out is the one that applies; if the days out is negative, because the show has already opened, the bucket whose `min_days_out` is zero is used. `cancellation_fee_cents` is then the contract's frozen `space_fee_cents` multiplied by that bucket's `pct`, divided by one hundred, rounded half up — the single rounding in this calculation. Note that the basis is the space fee and not `invoice_total_cents`: attached sponsorship is billed on its own deal and is not liquidated by a space cancellation. With the seeded default schedule, a cancellation two hundred days before the doors open costs 25 percent of the space fee, one one hundred and twenty days out costs 50 percent, and one inside ninety days costs the whole thing.

The second step is a waterfall against money already collected, and its order is pinned. `fee_applied_to_deposit_cents` is the smaller of `cancellation_fee_cents` and `deposit_paid_cents`. `refund_cents` is `deposit_paid_cents` less `fee_applied_to_deposit_cents` — the cash actually returned to the exhibitor. `balance_due_cents` is `cancellation_fee_cents` less `fee_applied_to_deposit_cents` — the unpaid remainder that is invoiced after the fact. The deposit bucket fills the fee first, only the overflow becomes a balance due, and only the remainder is refunded, which is why at most one of `refund_cents` and `balance_due_cents` can ever be non-zero. For an unsigned cancellation the arithmetic degenerates cleanly: the fee and the applied portion are both zero, `refund_cents` equals the whole `deposit_paid_cents`, and `balance_due_cents` is zero.

Cancelling an unsigned contract moves it `OFFERED → CANCELLED` (identically from `COUNTERED`), releases every booth in `offered_booth_ids` from `HELD → AVAILABLE` with `space_contract_id` cleared, recomputes the show's `held_net_sqft`, `available_net_sqft`, `committed_ratio_pct` and `floor_posture`, and increments the contract's and the show's `version`.

Cancelling a signed contract moves it `SIGNED → CANCELLED` and fires three compensating links, each of which is exactly the reverse of something signing did. The first releases inventory: every booth of the contract transitions `ASSIGNED → AVAILABLE` with `space_contract_id` cleared. The second releases services: every service order of the contract whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` transitions to `CANCELLED`. The third releases points: `points_awarded` is subtracted from the exhibitor's space-point base and then reset to zero, after which `provisional_priority_points` and `priority_tier` are recomputed. The show's `assigned_net_sqft`, `available_net_sqft`, `committed_ratio_pct` and `floor_posture` are recomputed, and the `version` of the contract, the exhibitor, the show and every booth and service order the cascade actually touched is incremented. These three links are the compensating legs named in §2.9: after a sign followed by a cancel, the show's three area figures, the exhibitor's provisional standing and tier, and the contract's `points_awarded` are all exactly back where they started.

### 3.7. Sponsorship: Packaging, Deals, Obligations and Fulfillment Credit

Sponsorship is inventory that has nothing to do with square footage — a keynote stage, a lanyard, a registration-area banner — and it is sold with delivery promises attached. A `ShowDirector` packages it against a show that is `PLANNING`, `SPACE_SELECTION` or `SELLING`. Each package carries a `package_code`, one to thirty-two characters of uppercase A through Z, digits and underscores, unique among the packages of that show; a `list_price_cents` of at least one cent, being the published price of one unit; a `price_floor_cents`, at least one cent and no greater than the list price, being the author-set floor for one unit; and an `inventory_total` of at least one, being the number of units available for sale. An optional `exclusivity_category`, drawn from the same character set and defaulting to the literal `NONE`, names a market category the package locks up — the literal `NONE` means the package is non-exclusive. The delivery promises are given as `obligation_codes`, an array of between one and eight distinct codes of one to thirty-two uppercase alphanumeric-or-underscore characters each, and `priority_point_value`, at least zero, is the point award the sponsor earns once every obligation of a signed deal has been resolved. A new package has `inventory_committed` at zero and `inventory_available` equal to `inventory_total`. Packages are listable per show and filterable by `exclusivity_category`.

Selling a unit begins with a proposal, filed by a `SalesRep` or `ShowDirector` against a package, naming the `exhibitor_id` of the sponsor and a `negotiated_price_cents` of at least one cent. Three gates apply. The package must have at least one unit left, or the request is refused with `ERR_SPONSORSHIP_INVENTORY_EXHAUSTED`. Where the package names a real `exclusivity_category`, no *other* package of the same show sharing that category may already carry a deal in `PROPOSED` or `SIGNED` status for a **different** exhibitor; a conflict is `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_CATEGORY_TAKEN`, and note that the conflict lives on a sibling package, not on the package being sold. And the price must clear two independent floors: `negotiated_price_cents` must be at least the package's `price_floor_cents`, and it must also be at least 80 percent of `list_price_cents` — that is, the list price multiplied by one hundred less twenty, divided by one hundred, rounded half up — so that no package can be discounted by more than a fifth. The binding floor is whichever of the two is higher, and a price below it is `ERR_SPONSORSHIP_PRICE_BELOW_FLOOR`. The sponsor's own `status` must be `ACTIVE`.

A proposal consumes a unit **immediately**: `inventory_committed` rises by one and `inventory_available` falls by one, because a proposal holds the slot even before anyone commits to it. The new deal opens with a `deal_status` of `PROPOSED`, a `fulfillment_status` of `NOT_STARTED`, `obligation_count` and `resolved_obligation_count` at zero, `fulfillment_credited` false, `proposed_by_user_id` recorded from the acting operator's `X-User-ID`, a null `signed_by_user_id` and a null `cancellation_reason`. Deals are readable individually and as a filtered list, narrowable by `sponsorship_package_id`, `exhibitor_id`, `deal_status` or `fulfillment_status`.

A deal carries two status axes that evolve independently. `deal_status` is the commercial axis and moves only through explicit commercial acts; `fulfillment_status` is the delivery axis and is purely derived from the deal's obligations. Signing moves only the commercial axis. It is a `ShowDirector` act, requires the deal to be `PROPOSED`, requires the show to be `PLANNING`, `SPACE_SELECTION` or `SELLING`, requires the sponsor to be `ACTIVE`, requires the package to still have a unit available counting this deal's own proposed hold, and requires an `X-User-ID` different from `proposed_by_user_id` as §2.7 sets out. On success the deal moves `PROPOSED → SIGNED`, `signed_by_user_id` is written, and the obligations are materialised: one fulfillment obligation is created per entry of the package's `obligation_codes`, **in the declared array order**, each with its `obligation_code` copied from the package, a `status` of `PENDING`, a null `resolved_by_user_id`, a null `note`, and a `due_date` set to the show's `event_start` less fourteen days — two weeks before the doors open, so there is still time to fix a missed deliverable. The deal's `fulfillment_status` is then recomputed, landing on `NOT_STARTED`, and the package's `inventory_committed` and `inventory_available` are recomputed. Signing also moves money and points elsewhere: the sponsor's `provisional_priority_points` and `priority_tier` are recomputed because sponsorship spend now counts, and every non-terminal space contract of this exhibitor on this show has its `sponsorship_attached_cents`, `bundle_discount_pct`, `bundle_discount_cents` and `invoice_total_cents` recomputed. The deal's and the exhibitor's `version` are both incremented.

Cancelling a deal is available to a `ShowDirector` or to the sponsoring `Exhibitor` and carries a mandatory `cancellation_reason`, and the same act has two shapes — `PROPOSED → CANCELLED` and `SIGNED → CANCELLED`. From `PROPOSED`, the effect is narrow: the reason is stored, the held unit is released so `inventory_committed` and `inventory_available` return to their exact pre-proposal values, and the deal's `version` is incremented. From `SIGNED` — which is only permitted while the show is not `CLOSED` — the effect is wider. The reason is stored and the unit is released as before. Obligations still `PENDING` stay `PENDING`, and because their deal is no longer `SIGNED` they are excluded from the missed-obligation sweep that runs at show close. `fulfillment_status` keeps whatever derived value it already had: a commercial cancellation does not rewind the delivery axis, so a deal that had already delivered two of three obligations still reports a partial delivery record. The points are reversed: this deal's sponsorship spend contribution is subtracted from the sponsor's provisional base, and where `fulfillment_credited` is true the `sponsorship_fulfillment_points` this deal credited are subtracted as well, after which `provisional_priority_points` and `priority_tier` are recomputed. Every non-terminal space contract of this exhibitor on this show is repriced. The deal's and the exhibitor's `version` are both incremented.

Resolving an obligation is how the producer discharges what it promised. A `FloorManager` or `ShowDirector` addresses an obligation under its deal — the deal identifier first, the obligation identifier second, resolved left to right as §2.10 requires — and supplies a `disposition` of either `DELIVERED` or `WAIVED`, plus an optional `note`. The deal must be `SIGNED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STATUS`; the obligation must still be `PENDING`, or the request is refused with `ERR_OBLIGATION_ALREADY_RESOLVED`, which is the guard that stops a second resolve from double-crediting the sponsor. A `WAIVED` disposition demands two extra things: the `note` must be present and non-empty, else `ERR_WAIVER_REASON_REQUIRED`, and only a `ShowDirector` may waive — a `FloorManager` attempting it receives `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Either disposition writes `resolved_by_user_id` and `note`, moves the obligation `PENDING → DELIVERED` or `PENDING → WAIVED` respectively, and increments the obligation's `version`. Both dispositions then bubble upward identically, because a waived obligation counts as resolved: the deal's `obligation_count` and `resolved_obligation_count` are recomputed, and `fulfillment_status` is re-derived. Obligations are listable per deal and filterable by `status`.

The derivation of `fulfillment_status` is evaluated in a fixed order, and the order is what makes it correct. If `obligation_count` is zero the value is `NOT_STARTED`. Otherwise, if any obligation of the deal has a `status` of `MISSED` the value is `BREACHED` — a single missed obligation cannot be papered over by waiving the rest, so `BREACHED` outranks completion-by-count. Otherwise, if `resolved_obligation_count` equals `obligation_count` the value is `COMPLETE`. Otherwise, if `resolved_obligation_count` is zero the value is `NOT_STARTED`. Otherwise the value is `PARTIAL`. In transition terms, a deal with at least two obligations of which between one and all-but-one are resolved and none is missed sits at `NOT_STARTED → PARTIAL`; a deal whose last obligation is resolved with none missed moves `PARTIAL → COMPLETE`; and a single-obligation deal jumps `NOT_STARTED → COMPLETE` in one step. All three of these are derived moves that leave `deal_status` untouched and increment only the deal's `version` — except on the two edges that arrive at `COMPLETE`, which additionally carry the fulfillment credit.

That credit is the sponsor's reward for a fully honoured package, and it fires identically on both edges into `COMPLETE`: when `fulfillment_credited` is false, the package's `priority_point_value` is added to the exhibitor's `sponsorship_fulfillment_points`, `fulfillment_credited` is set to true, and the exhibitor's `provisional_priority_points` and `priority_tier` are recomputed from the new total, with the deal's and the exhibitor's `version` both incremented. The `fulfillment_credited` guard is what makes re-entering `COMPLETE` — after, say, an obligation set is re-derived — incapable of double-crediting.

The unhappy edge is the mirror image. When the show-close sweep marks a `PENDING` obligation of a `SIGNED` deal as `MISSED`, that deal's `fulfillment_status` becomes `BREACHED` — `NOT_STARTED → BREACHED` or `PARTIAL → BREACHED`, depending on where the delivery axis stood. Breach costs the sponsor twenty penalty points, added to its `penalty_points`; then `provisional_priority_points`, `certified_priority_points` and `priority_tier` are all recomputed, and if the accumulated `penalty_points` has reached thirty the exhibitor transitions `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` in the same pass. The deal's and the exhibitor's `version` are both incremented.

### 3.8. Attendee Acquisition, Buyer Screening and Lead Capture

An exhibitor pays for a qualified audience, not a crowd, so registration and screening happen in the same request. A `Registrant` submitting on their own behalf, or a `ShowDirector` entering a registration on someone else's, files it against a show that is `SPACE_SELECTION`, `SELLING` or `ON_SITE`. The submission names a `category` — one of `BUYER`, `VIP_BUYER`, `PRESS`, `STUDENT` or `EXHIBITOR_STAFF` — the registrant's employer as `company_name` (one to one hundred and sixty characters), their `job_title` (one to one hundred and twenty characters), and two screening answers: `purchasing_authority`, one of `NONE`, `INFLUENCER`, `RECOMMENDER` or `DECISION_MAKER`, and `annual_spend_band`, one of `UNDER_50K`, `K50_250K`, `K250K_1M` or `OVER_1M`. An `email` is required, must contain exactly one at-sign, and must be unique among the registrations of the same show when compared case-insensitively.

Two categories are credentialed rather than scored. When `category` is `PRESS` or `EXHIBITOR_STAFF`, the `qualification_score` is set to zero and the `qualification_status` is set directly to `QUALIFIED`: a press badge is a credential, not a buyer score, and a zero score on such a row must not be read as a failing grade.

Every other category is scored. Authority contributes 0 points for `NONE`, 15 for `INFLUENCER`, 25 for `RECOMMENDER` and 40 for `DECISION_MAKER`. The spend band contributes 5 points for `UNDER_50K`, 15 for `K50_250K`, 25 for `K250K_1M` and 35 for `OVER_1M`. The title contributes a flat 10 points when any of eight keywords — `CHIEF`, `VP`, `VICE PRESIDENT`, `DIRECTOR`, `HEAD OF`, `BUYER`, `OWNER`, `PARTNER` — occurs as a case-insensitive substring of `job_title`, and nothing otherwise; matching any one keyword earns the ten points exactly once, not once per match. Those three contributions are summed into a raw total, that single raw total is multiplied by the category weight — 1.00 for `BUYER`, 1.15 for `VIP_BUYER`, and 0.50 for `STUDENT` — and the product is rounded half up to an integer, which is the stored `qualification_score`. The weight is applied to the summed raw points exactly once: weighting each component separately and then summing produces a different number and is wrong.

The score then sorts the registrant into one of three outcomes, and the boundaries are inclusive at the bottom. A score of 60 or more transitions the row `PENDING → QUALIFIED`. A score of at least 40 but below 60 transitions it `PENDING → WAITLISTED`. A score below 40 transitions it `PENDING → REJECTED`, which is terminal. A new registration carries a `lead_scan_count` of zero and a null `screening_override_reason`, and its `version` is incremented by the screening write.

Waitlisted registrants can be resolved by hand. A `ShowDirector` may override a registration whose `qualification_status` is `WAITLISTED`, on a show that is `PLANNING`, `SPACE_SELECTION`, `SELLING` or `ON_SITE`, by supplying a `decision` of either `QUALIFIED` or `REJECTED` together with a mandatory non-empty `reason`; an override arriving without one is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_REASON_REQUIRED`. The decision is written straight into `qualification_status`, moving the row `WAITLISTED → QUALIFIED` or `WAITLISTED → REJECTED`, the justification is stored in `screening_override_reason`, and the registration's `version` is incremented. The previously computed `qualification_score` is deliberately preserved untouched, so that the post-show report can always tell a registrant who scored and passed from one who was let in by judgement. Registrations are readable individually and listable per show, filterable by `category` or `qualification_status`.

Lead capture is what turns a badge into evidence that the audience was real. An `Exhibitor` or a `FloorManager` records a scan against a `SIGNED` space contract on a show that is `ON_SITE`, naming the `registration_id` of the badge and the `scanned_at` instant of the scan. Four things are checked: the registration must exist; its `show_id` must be the same show as the contract's, or the request is refused with `ERR_REGISTRATION_SHOW_MISMATCH`; no scan may already exist pairing this same contract with this same registration, or the request is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LEAD_SCAN`; and the date of `scanned_at` must fall inside the closed interval from the show's `move_in_start` through its `move_out_end`, or the request is refused with `ERR_LEAD_SCAN_OUTSIDE_SHOW_WINDOW`. The one-badge-one-exhibitor-one-scan rule is what keeps the registration's attendance counter and the report's per-contract denominators honest. A successful scan increments the registration's `lead_scan_count` and its `version`. The scan row itself is immutable, append-only, and carries no `version` of its own, as §2.4 explains. Scans are listable both from the exhibitor's side, per contract, and from the attendee's side, per registration.

### 3.9. On the Floor: Services, Rule Enforcement, Rebooking and Closing the Edition

Once selling is done a `ShowDirector` moves the edition `SELLING → ON_SITE` by naming `ON_SITE` as the `target_status` of a lifecycle transition — the same request shape that later drives `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED`. The only precondition is that the show is currently `SELLING`; the transition recomputes `floor_posture`, which is how the on-site violation branch of that derivation first becomes reachable, and increments the show's `version`. Rebooking, lead scanning and floor-rule citation are legal only while the edition is `ON_SITE`.

Exhibitor services are ordered against a `SIGNED` space contract by the `Exhibitor` itself or by a `FloorManager` acting at the service desk, on a show that is `SPACE_SELECTION`, `SELLING` or `ON_SITE`. The order names a `service_type` of `LABOR`, `ELECTRICAL` or `MATERIAL_HANDLING`, a `quantity` of at least one, and the `ordered_at` instant at which the order is accepted — supplied by the request, or taken from the logical clock when the request omits it. The date part of `ordered_at` may not fall after the show's `move_out_end`; ordering into a hall that has already been struck is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_ORDER_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The order is created with `status` `SUBMITTED`.

Pricing an order is a four-step derivation, and the whole rate card comes from the show's venue agreement. First, `unit_rate_cents` is copied from that agreement according to type: `labor_rate_cents_per_hour` for `LABOR`, `electrical_rate_cents_per_amp` for `ELECTRICAL`, `material_handling_rate_cents_per_cwt` for `MATERIAL_HANDLING`. Second, `billable_quantity` is derived: for `MATERIAL_HANDLING` it is the larger of the ordered `quantity` and a two-hundredweight minimum, because freight is billed in whole hundredweight with a floor; for `LABOR` and `ELECTRICAL` it equals `quantity` exactly. Third, `rate_variant` is decided by the calendar: `ADVANCE` when the date of `ordered_at` is on or before the show's `advance_order_deadline`, and `FLOOR` when it falls after it.

Fourth, the variant decides the arithmetic, and the two branches are not symmetric. Under `ADVANCE`, `line_total_cents` is the product of `billable_quantity`, `unit_rate_cents` and the quantity (one hundred minus the venue agreement's `advance_order_discount_pct`) divided by one hundred, rounded half up to whole cents; no expedite fee applies. Under `FLOOR`, the product of `billable_quantity`, `unit_rate_cents` and one point three five is rounded half up first, and a flat seventy-five dollars — 7500 cents — is added to that rounded figure afterwards. The order of those last two steps is pinned: adding the flat fee before rounding would give a different total. Because the multiplier exceeds one and the fee is strictly positive, the same order always costs strictly more on the floor than it would have in advance, for every positive rate. A cancelled order keeps the `line_total_cents` it was priced at, for the record, but is excluded from every billing aggregate. Orders are listable per contract, filterable by `status` or `service_type`.

Floor rules are enforced by citation. A `FloorManager` or a `ShowDirector` writes a citation against a `SIGNED` contract naming a `violation_type` of `BOOTH_HEIGHT`, `NOISE`, `AISLE_ENCROACHMENT` or `SALES_CONDUCT`, and a `notes` narrative of one to five hundred characters. A `measured_value`, the measured structure height in feet, is required when the type is `BOOTH_HEIGHT` and is ignored for every other type; for a height citation the measured figure must be strictly greater than the cited booth's own `max_height_ft`, because a structure within its permitted height is not a violation, and a measurement that does not exceed the limit is refused with `ERR_BOOTH_HEIGHT_LIMIT`. The new citation opens with `status` `OPEN` and `penalty_points` of zero, records the reporting operator's identity in `reported_by_user_id`, and leaves `adjudicated_by_user_id` and `waiver_reason` null. Citations are listable per contract, filterable by `status` or `violation_type`.

Only a `ShowDirector` adjudicates, and only a citation still `OPEN` can be adjudicated — a second ruling on the same row is refused with `ERR_VIOLATION_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`. The adjudicator's operator identity must differ from the `reported_by_user_id` recorded at citation time, as §2.7 requires. The request carries a `disposition`, and the two dispositions of this one operation are deliberately asymmetric in how far they propagate.

An `OPEN → UPHELD` disposition writes `adjudicated_by_user_id` and sets the citation's `penalty_points` from the penalty schedule: ten for `BOOTH_HEIGHT`, five for `NOISE`, eight for `AISLE_ENCROACHMENT` and fifteen for `SALES_CONDUCT`. Those points then travel along three cascade links. They are added to the exhibitor's accumulated `penalty_points`. The exhibitor's `provisional_priority_points`, `certified_priority_points` and `priority_tier` are all recomputed, and if the accumulated `penalty_points` has reached thirty the exhibitor transitions `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` in the same request. Finally the show's `upheld_violation_count` is recomputed and its `floor_posture` re-derived, which is how a third upheld citation freezes an `ON_SITE` floor. The citation's, the exhibitor's and the show's `version` are all incremented.

An `OPEN → WAIVED` disposition is the mirror image and propagates nothing. It requires a present, non-empty `waiver_reason`; a waiver without one is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_REASON_REQUIRED`. It writes `adjudicated_by_user_id` and the `waiver_reason`, and leaves the citation's `penalty_points` at zero. The exhibitor's `penalty_points`, standings and `priority_tier` are untouched, the exhibitor is never suspended by a waiver, and the show's `upheld_violation_count` and `floor_posture` do not move — a waived citation can never contribute to the three-violation freeze. Only the citation's own `version` is incremented. Both dispositions are terminal.

Rebooking is the producer's most valuable on-site motion: selling next year's floor while this year's is still standing. A `SalesRep`, a `ShowDirector` or the `Exhibitor` itself rebooks from a space contract that has already reached `CLOSED_OUT`; any other source status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATUS`. The request names the `target_show_id` of the edition being rebooked into, an `expected_target_show_version` pinning the target edition's floor plan as described in §2.5, a `requested_sqft` for the new engagement, and optionally a `premium_placement_requested` flag and an explicit `booth_ids` list; when `booth_ids` is omitted the target floor is auto-allocated by the same ranked walk that serves a first-time offer.

The target edition must genuinely be a later edition of the same event: a different show, sitting under the same venue agreement as the source contract's show, with a strictly greater `edition_year`, and in `PLANNING`, `SPACE_SELECTION` or `SELLING`. Naming the source show itself, an edition under a different venue agreement, an edition whose `edition_year` is not strictly later, or an edition that is no longer open for sale all fail with `ERR_REBOOK_TARGET_INVALID`. Because a rebook is also a contract-opening write, the target edition must additionally be open for selling — `SPACE_SELECTION` or `SELLING` — and a target still in `PLANNING` is caught earlier in the ladder by `ERR_INVALID_SHOW_STATUS`. The exhibitor must be `ACTIVE`, the target edition's `floor_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, and the exhibitor must not already hold an `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED` or `SIGNED` contract on the target edition, which would otherwise be refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_CONTRACT`.

A successful rebook creates a fresh space contract in `OFFERED` on the target edition with `rebooked_from_space_contract_id` set to the source contract, moves the chosen target-edition booths `AVAILABLE → HELD`, prices the new offer by the ordinary offer pricing chain, and increments the target show's `version`. Two pieces of loyalty accounting ride along: the new contract earns the five-point rebook bonus inside its point contribution, which flows straight into the exhibitor's provisional standing, and the exhibitor's `consecutive_years` is incremented by one, which is what keeps a long-running exhibitor's streak intact across editions.

Closing the edition is a single director-initiated transition with a long tail. `ON_SITE → CLOSED` requires only that the show is `ON_SITE`. Every `SIGNED` contract of the show transitions `SIGNED → CLOSED_OUT`; every contract still `OFFERED` or `COUNTERED` transitions to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` `SHOW_CLOSED_UNSIGNED` and a waived fee, meaning `cancellation_fee_cents` of zero, because an exhibitor who never signed owes nothing. Then every `FulfillmentObligation` still `PENDING` whose deal is `SIGNED` transitions `PENDING → MISSED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, and each of those transitions bubbles its deal to `BREACHED` and charges the sponsor as §3.7 describes. Finally `attrition_shortfall_sqft` and `attrition_settlement_cents` are computed and frozen onto the show, `floor_posture` is re-derived, and the show's `version` is incremented.

Closing out a signed contract is deliberately gentle on the floor plan: the booths keep `status` `ASSIGNED`, because after close the floor is history rather than inventory and must remain attributable in the report. What does change is the standing: the contract's `assigned_net_sqft` now enters the certified side of the priority-point derivation, so the exhibitor's `space_points_certified` and `certified_priority_points` are recomputed, and both the contract's and the exhibitor's `version` are incremented.

The attrition settlement is the venue's protection against a producer who commits more space than it sells. The commitment is the venue agreement's `committed_net_sqft_per_year`. The forgiven allowance is that commitment multiplied by `attrition_allowance_pct` divided by one hundred, floored to whole square feet — floored, which favours the venue by shrinking the forgiveness. The forgiven floor is the commitment minus that allowance. Actual performance is the sum of `assigned_net_sqft` across the show's contracts whose status is `SIGNED` or `CLOSED_OUT`, and no other status counts: a contract that was cancelled no longer counts toward the commitment even though it once did, which is exactly how a late cancellation manufactures an attrition bill. The `attrition_shortfall_sqft` is the forgiven floor minus actual performance, or zero when actual performance meets or exceeds it. The `attrition_settlement_cents` is that shortfall multiplied by eighteen dollars — 1800 cents — per square foot. The floor is inclusive: actual performance exactly equal to the forgiven floor owes nothing at all, while one square foot below it owes a full 1800 cents.

An edition can also die rather than close. A `ShowDirector` may cancel a show in `PLANNING`, `SPACE_SELECTION`, `SELLING` or `ON_SITE` — `PLANNING → CANCELLED` and its three siblings from the later statuses are one and the same transition — and the same transition is driven by the system when a venue agreement is terminated. The show's `cancellation_reason` is taken from the request's `cancellation_reason` when a director cancels directly, and set to `VENUE_CONTRACT_TERMINATED` when the cancellation arrives as a cascade from the venue agreement. Every selection appointment of the show that is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` transitions to `EXPIRED`, with no forfeit penalty, because the exhibitor did nothing wrong. Every space contract of the show that is `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED` or `SIGNED` transitions to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` `SHOW_CANCELLED` and `cancellation_fee_cents` forced to zero, processed in `created_at` ascending order — the producer cancelled, not the exhibitor, so no liability may be charged even to a signed contract standing days from opening. Every sponsorship deal on a package of this show that is `PROPOSED` or `SIGNED` transitions to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` `SHOW_CANCELLED`. The show's `floor_posture` and its whole area ledger — `sellable_booth_sqft`, `held_net_sqft`, `assigned_net_sqft`, `available_net_sqft` — are recomputed, and its `version` is incremented. Any target status that is not reachable from the show's current status by one of the transitions above is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SHOW_TRANSITION`.

The post-show report is the artefact the whole edition is judged by. It is a computed read available to a `ShowDirector` or a `SalesRep`, and only for an edition whose status is `CLOSED`; asking for it earlier is refused with `ERR_SHOW_NOT_CLOSED`. On the attendance side it reports `total_registrations`, the count of all registrations of the show; `qualified_registrations`, those whose `qualification_status` is `QUALIFIED`; `attended_registrations`, those with a `lead_scan_count` of one or more; and `qualified_attended`, those that are both `QUALIFIED` and scanned at least once. The `attendance_quality_index` is zero when nobody attended, and otherwise one hundred times `qualified_attended` divided by `attended_registrations`, rounded half up to an integer percent.

On the lead-generation side the report walks the show's contracts whose status is `SIGNED` or `CLOSED_OUT`, in contract `created_at` ascending order, and emits one entry per contract into `exhibitor_lines`. Each entry carries a `lead_count`, the number of scans recorded against that contract; a `qualified_lead_count`, how many of those scans belong to a registration whose `qualification_status` is `QUALIFIED`; and an `exhibitor_lead_quality_pct`, which is zero when the contract has no scans and otherwise one hundred times `qualified_lead_count` divided by `lead_count`, rounded half up. `total_lead_scans` is the sum of `lead_count` across all of those contracts.

The show-level `show_lead_quality_index` has a pinned two-pass rounding order. The contracts that scored at least one lead form the scored set, and `scored_contract_count` is its size. The index is zero when that set is empty, and otherwise the sum of the already-rounded `exhibitor_lead_quality_pct` values over the scored contracts, divided by `scored_contract_count`, and that mean rounded half up. Each contract's percentage is rounded first and the mean is taken of the rounded percentages: pooling every scan in the show and dividing total qualified scans by total scans yields a different number and is wrong. A signed exhibitor who scanned nothing still appears in `exhibitor_lines`, with `lead_count` and `exhibitor_lead_quality_pct` both zero, but is excluded from the index's denominator, because including it would drag every diligent exhibitor's number toward zero. Cancelled contracts and any scans orphaned with them are excluded from every figure in the report. The report closes by restating the frozen `attrition_shortfall_sqft` and `attrition_settlement_cents` as they were written at close.
