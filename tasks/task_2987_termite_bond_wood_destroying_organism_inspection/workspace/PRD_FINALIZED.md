# Termite Bond & Wood-Destroying-Organism Inspection API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A termite bond is a long-lived promise. When a pest-control branch signs one, it commits — for as long as the homeowner keeps paying — either to re-treat a structure that termites return to, or to pay for the wood they eat, or both. That promise is only as good as the paperwork underneath it: the graphed inspection that recorded what the property looked like on day one, the licence of the inspector who signed that graph, the pesticide record that proves what chemical went into the soil and when, the state form that a real-estate closing depends on, and the arithmetic that decides how much of a homeowner's claim was already rotting before coverage began. Today that paperwork lives in six disconnected places, and the branch discovers its exposure only when a claim arrives.

This document specifies the API that consolidates it. The service is the single system of record for structures and their substructure characteristics, for licensed inspectors and applicators and the lifecycle of their state credentials, for graphed inspections and the individual grid-cell notations that make up a graph, for treatment plans and the chemical actually discharged against them, for bonds and their annual renewal cycle including lapse and reinstatement, for the statutory wood-destroying-organism reports that real-estate transactions turn on, for bait-station monitoring visits and the activity escalations they surface, and for damage claims and their adjudication against pre-existing-damage exclusions.

Three constraints shape almost every rule that follows. First, **compliance outranks money**: when a request is simultaneously unlicensed and underpaid, the server reports the licensing failure, because a branch that takes payment for work an unlicensed applicator performed has a regulatory problem, not a billing problem. Second, **every monetary quantity on a bond is conserved**: the repair-liability ceiling is partitioned at all times into money already paid, money reserved against an open claim, and money still available, and no transition may let those three drift apart. Third, **time is explicit and driven by a server-side logical clock**, never by wall-clock time, so that a 365-day term, a 30-day grace period, a 90-day reinstatement window and a 24-hour pre-closing cut-off are all exactly reproducible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Callers, Roles and Identity

Every request except the logical-clock read must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller is acting. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else about it is examined. If `X-User-Role` is not one of the six declared roles, or is a declared role that the target operation does not admit, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `ComplianceOfficer`, `BranchManager`, `FieldInspector`, `Applicator`, `ClaimsAdjudicator` and `PropertyOwner`. A `ComplianceOfficer` is the state-compliance operator: it owns the registry of structures and inspectors, drives licence status, files and issues statutory reports, runs the bond term-review sweep, and sets the logical clock. A `BranchManager` performs underwriting and authorisation: proposing, approving and abandoning treatment plans, issuing bonds, opening and completing renewals, and reinstating lapsed bonds. A `FieldInspector` is a licensed wood-destroying-organism inspector who opens and signs graphs, notates individual graph cells, services bait stations, and attaches a verifying graph to a damage claim. An `Applicator` is a licensed pesticide applicator who records applications against approved plans and closes those plans out. A `ClaimsAdjudicator` decides verified claims. A `PropertyOwner` is the bonded homeowner, who requests statutory reports, files claims against their own bond, and withdraws them.

Identity binding differs by role, and it matters because ownership checks compare `X-User-ID` against persisted foreign keys. When `X-User-Role` is `FieldInspector`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Inspector`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Inspector` compare the resource's inspector foreign key to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Applicator`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Inspector` row — inspectors and applicators are the same licensed population, distinguished by the endorsement categories on their licence rather than by separate records. When `X-User-Role` is `PropertyOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Property`; a homeowner acting on a claim must be the property that the claim's bond covers. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `BranchManager` or `ClaimsAdjudicator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity: the server records it where a transition says so — as `issued_by_user_id` on a `Bond` when a `BranchManager` underwrites it, and as `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a `DamageClaim` when a `ClaimsAdjudicator` resolves it — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Ownership is enforced on five write operations: opening a graph, adding a cell notation to a graph, signing a graph, recording a pesticide application, and recording a bait-station service visit. In the first three, the acting `FieldInspector`'s `X-User-ID` must equal the `inspector_id` carried by the graph. In the fourth, the acting `Applicator`'s `X-User-ID` must equal the `applicator_id` on the plan being discharged. In the fifth, the acting `FieldInspector`'s `X-User-ID` must equal the `applicator_id` of the treatment plan that installed the station being serviced. A mismatch in any of the five is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403), as is a withdrawal attempt by a `PropertyOwner` whose `X-User-ID` is not the `property_id` of the claim's bond.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Collection Reads: Pagination, Ordering and Filters

Every collection read accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. When `limit` falls outside the range 1 through 100, or `offset` is negative, or either value is not an integer, the read is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400). Both values are echoed back in `meta` alongside `total_count`, the number of rows matching the request before paging, and `has_more`, which is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page.

Collections are returned in ascending `created_at` order — oldest row first — so that repeated paging over a stable dataset is deterministic. The one exception is the station listing beneath a treatment plan, which is ordered by `station_index` ascending, matching the physical installation order around the structure.

Filters narrow a collection before paging is applied, and every filter is an exact-match equality test. Structures may be filtered by `state_code` and by `construction_type`. Inspectors may be filtered by `status` and by `license_state`. Graphs may be filtered by `property_id`, by `inspector_id`, by `supersedes_inspection_id`, by `status` and by `purpose`. The cell notations of one graph may be filtered by `finding_type`. Treatment plans may be filtered by `property_id`, `inspection_id`, `applicator_id`, `status` and `method`. The stations of one plan may be filtered by `status`. Bonds may be filtered by `property_id`, `treatment_plan_id`, `status` and `coverage_tier`. The renewal invoices of one bond may be filtered by `status`, and the global renewal-invoice collection additionally by `bond_id` and `inspection_id`. Statutory reports may be filtered by `inspection_id`, `status` and `sla_breached`. Damage claims may be filtered by `bond_id`, `verifying_inspection_id`, `status` and `claim_type`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix and no embedded meaning. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied one is ignored. Every resource also carries `created_at`, the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the row came into existence, rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z`.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation to the instant of that mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations caused indirectly, as a cascade from some other resource's transition. Structures, inspectors, graphs, treatment plans, bonds, renewal invoices, statutory reports and bait stations all behave this way, as do damage claims.

Three record types are immutable by design and therefore carry neither `updated_at` nor `version`: a graph cell notation, a pesticide application record, and a bait-station service visit. Each is written once and never edited, and each exposes only `id` and `created_at` in addition to its own domain fields. There is no update or delete operation for any of the three, and the absence of `updated_at` and `version` on them is deliberate rather than an omission.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

All temporal reasoning in this system reads a server-side logical clock rather than the host's wall clock. The clock's mandatory startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`: before any client moves it, every read of the clock returns that instant, and the server must never fall back to real wall-clock time. A `ComplianceOfficer` may set the clock to any UTC instant by supplying `current_utc`, and any caller may read the clock's present value without supplying authentication headers at all — the clock read is the single endpoint exempt from the header requirement.

Every instant the server stamps comes from this clock: `created_at` and `updated_at`, the `graph_completed_at` of a signed graph, the `requested_at` and `issued_at` of a statutory report, the `applied_at` of a pesticide application, the `serviced_at` of a station visit, the `reported_at` of a claim, the `completed_at` of a plan, and the `paid_at` of a renewal payment. Every window comparison — term boundaries, renewal windows, grace periods, retention deadlines, service intervals, amendment windows, pre-closing lead times — is evaluated against the same clock.

Moving the clock never retro-fires a transition. A bond whose grace period elapsed while the clock jumped forward does not become `LAPSED` on its own; it becomes `LAPSED` only when a compliance operator actually reviews it. The clock changes what a business call will decide, not what has already been decided.

### 2.6. Rejection Precedence: How the Server Chooses an Error

A single malformed or ill-timed request frequently violates several rules at once. The response must be predictable, so the checks below are evaluated in exactly this order, and the **first** violated check determines the response. No later check is evaluated once one has failed. The ordering is not an implementation hint; it is part of the contract, and it exists so that safety and compliance failures always surface ahead of money failures.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present and non-empty on every request except the logical-clock read — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Authorisation.** `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles and that role is admitted by the target operation — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Ownership.** For opening a graph, adding a cell notation, signing a graph, recording a pesticide application, recording a station service and attaching a verifying graph to a claim, the acting inspector or applicator owns the resource as described in §2.1; for withdrawing a claim, the acting homeowner is the `property_id` of the bond the claim was filed against. A failure is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
4. **Path existence.** Every identifier segment in the request path resolves to a persisted row of that segment's type, validated left to right; the leftmost segment that fails to resolve reports its own type's not-found code — `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TREATMENT_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOND_RENEWAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WDO_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BAIT_STATION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_DAMAGE_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` — all at HTTP 404. A nested path is therefore never evaluated against a child when the parent does not exist.
5. **Request shape.** On every write operation: each field the operation declares as required is present and non-null; every field holds the declared JSON type; every enumerated field holds a declared member; every money field is a JSON number, at least zero, with at most two decimal places; every instant field is an RFC 3339 UTC timestamp ending in `Z`; every calendar-date field matches the four-digit-year, two-digit-month, two-digit-day form; a state code is two uppercase letters; a `grid_ref` is a single column letter from `A` through `J` immediately followed by a row number from 1 through 10. Numeric values outside their declared range fail here too. Unknown fields are ignored rather than rejected. Any failure is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
6. **Cross-field shape.** On graph creation and cell notation only: `scheduled_end_at` strictly after `scheduled_start_at`; `closing_date` present exactly when `purpose` is `REAL_ESTATE` and null otherwise; `supersedes_inspection_id` present exactly when `purpose` is `RENEWAL` or `REINSTATEMENT`; `conducive_code` set to a value other than `NONE` exactly when `finding_type` is `CONDUCIVE_CONDITION` and `NONE` otherwise; `damage_estimate_usd` greater than zero exactly when `finding_type` is `DAMAGE`; `pre_existing` supplied exactly when `finding_type` is `DAMAGE`. Any failure is `ERR_FIELD_COMBINATION_INVALID` (422).
7. **Pagination shape.** On every collection read, `limit` and `offset` are valid as described in §2.3 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).
8. **Body-reference existence.** Every identifier carried in a request **body** resolves to a persisted row: `property_id`, `inspector_id`, `supersedes_inspection_id`, `inspection_id`, `treatment_plan_id`, `bond_id`, `verifying_inspection_id` and `reinstatement_inspection_id`. The applicable not-found code is reported at HTTP 404. This check runs after shape validation and, critically, **before** the stale-version check of stage 9: a claim filing that carries both an unknown bond reference and a stale expected version reports the 404, never the 412.
9. **Stale parent version.** The two version-guarded write operations described in §2.7 compare the client's supplied expectation against the parent's current `version` — a mismatch is `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
10. **Target state.** The row named by the path is in a status from which the requested transition is declared. Signing a graph requires a `DRAFT` graph; approving a plan requires a `PROPOSED` plan; adjudicating requires an `UNDER_ADJUDICATION` claim; servicing requires a station that is `INSTALLED`, `ACTIVE_HIT` or `ESCALATED`; a licence transition must be one of the four declared licence moves. The code names the entity whose state is wrong: `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTOR_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_BOND_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_RENEWAL_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_STATION_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`, each at HTTP 409. This stage outranks stage 22 below: opening a renewal invoice on a `LAPSED` bond that is also outside its renewal window reports `ERR_INVALID_BOND_STATE`, not `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`.
11. **Licence active.** The acting inspector's or applicator's licence `status` is `ACTIVE`; both `SUSPENDED` and `EXPIRED` fail with `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). This applies to five operations: opening a graph, signing a graph, recording a pesticide application, recording a station service, and completing a renewal invoice.
12. **Licence unexpired.** For every inspector or applicator whose work the request records, `license_expires_on` is on or after the logical clock's UTC date — an expiry falling exactly on the clock date still passes. A failure is `ERR_LICENSE_EXPIRED` (409), and this applies to signing a graph, recording a pesticide application, recording a station service, and completing a renewal invoice. Because this stage precedes stage 27, a renewal completion carrying both an expired applicator licence and an underpayment reports `ERR_LICENSE_EXPIRED`: compliance outranks money.
13. **Licence reinstatement requirements.** Checked only on a licence-status change whose requested `status` is `ACTIVE` while the inspector is currently `EXPIRED`, as detailed in §3.2 — otherwise `ERR_LICENSE_REINSTATEMENT_UNMET` (422).
14. **Licence jurisdiction.** The acting inspector's `license_state` equals the `state_code` of the structure being inspected or treated; a licence issued by another state may neither sign a graph nor discharge a pesticide. A failure on graph creation or on a pesticide application is `ERR_LICENSE_STATE_MISMATCH` (422).
15. **Statutory form match.** A statutory report's `form_code` is the form its state mandates, per §2.10 — otherwise `ERR_FORM_CODE_MISMATCH` (422).
16. **Category endorsement.** The acting inspector's `license_categories` carries the endorsement the operation demands, per §2.9 — otherwise `ERR_CATEGORY_NOT_LICENSED` (422), on graph signing and on pesticide applications.
17. **Segregation of duties on adjudication.** The adjudicating `X-User-ID` differs from the bond's `issued_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
18. **Segregation of duties on verification.** The verifying graph's `inspector_id` differs from the `applicator_id` on the bonded plan — otherwise `ERR_VERIFIER_IS_APPLICATOR` (422).
19. **Retention lock.** A plan may not be abandoned while any of its pesticide records is still inside its state retention period — otherwise `ERR_RETENTION_LOCK_ACTIVE` (409).
20. **Referential state of consumed rows.** A graph consumed as evidence is `GRAPHED`; its `purpose` matches the consumer; a bonded plan is `COMPLETED`; the requested `method` is permitted for the structure's `construction_type`; referenced rows belong to the same structure; a bond accepting a new claim is `ACTIVE`. In order within this stage: `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_GRAPHED` (409), `ERR_INSPECTION_PURPOSE_MISMATCH` (422), `ERR_PLAN_NOT_COMPLETED` (409), `ERR_METHOD_NOT_PERMITTED` (422), `ERR_PROPERTY_MISMATCH` (422), `ERR_BOND_NOT_ACTIVE` (409).
21. **Uniqueness, capacity and completeness.** In order within this stage: `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_BOND` (409), `ERR_GRAPH_INCOMPLETE` (422), `ERR_DUPLICATE_GRID_FINDING` (409), `ERR_PRODUCT_METHOD_INCOMPATIBLE` (422), `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_RESERVED` (409), `ERR_TIER_EXCLUDES_REPAIR` (422), `ERR_RENEWAL_ALREADY_PENDING` (409). Each condition is stated with its workflow in §3.
22. **Windows, intervals and chronology.** In order within this stage: `ERR_INSPECTOR_SCHEDULE_OVERLAP` (409), `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` (409), `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (409), `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_INSPECTION_STALE` (422), `ERR_APPLICATION_BEFORE_PERMIT_WINDOW` (422), `ERR_SERVICE_TIME_INVALID` (422), `ERR_CLOSING_LEAD_TIME_MISSED` (409), `ERR_AMENDMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409), `ERR_DAMAGE_OUTSIDE_TERM` (422). Each condition is stated with its workflow in §3.
23. **Money.** Last of all, and only once everything above has passed: `ERR_PAYMENT_INSUFFICIENT` (402) on a renewal completion, `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_PAYMENT_INSUFFICIENT` (402) on a reinstatement, and `ERR_REPAIR_CAP_EXHAUSTED` (409) both when a claim verification finds no repair headroom left to reserve against and when a repair approval is attempted on a claim whose projected payout came out at 0.00.

### 2.7. Stale-Parent Guards on Applications and Claim Filings

Two write operations attach a new child row to a parent that legitimately accepts many such children. Because no state precondition can distinguish a well-informed second write from a blind one, both are guarded by an explicit version expectation on the parent.

Recording a pesticide application — `POST /treatment-plans/{id}/applications` — requires the non-optional field `expected_treatment_plan_version`, carrying the `version` of the treatment plan as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the plan's current `version`, the application is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A plan accepts several applications by design, so nothing about the plan's status blocks a later one; the version guard is what stops an applicator from adding gallons on top of a plan view that no longer reflects the true `gallons_applied_total`, or whose status has since moved to `COMPLETED`.

Filing a damage claim — `POST /damage-claims` — requires the non-optional field `expected_bond_version`, carrying the `version` of the bond as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the bond's current `version`, the filing is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A bond accepts several claims over its life, so a second filing is not blocked by any state rule; the version guard is what stops a homeowner from filing against a stale view of `remaining_repair_cap_usd` that an earlier reserve or approved payout has already consumed.

No other operation takes a version field, and no other operation may reject a request for a version reason. Both guards sit at stage 9 of §2.6 — after body-reference existence, before target-state evaluation.

### 2.8. Money, Rounding and the Repair-Liability Invariant

Every monetary amount in this system is a decimal quantity carried to exactly two decimal places in responses, and every rounding step is **half up**: a value whose third decimal digit is 5 or greater rounds the second decimal digit away from zero. Where a calculation names several rounding steps, each is applied exactly once and exactly where it is named. Intermediate quantities that are not named as rounding points carry full precision — collapsing them early, or rounding once at the end instead of at the named steps, produces materially different cents on many inputs and is wrong.

Counts are integers and are never rounded. Where a calculation calls for rounding **up** to a whole number — trench gallons, station counts, whole hours of turnaround, a station-escalation threshold — the value is raised to the next integer unless it is already an integer. Where it calls for rounding **down** — whole elapsed days between two instants — any partial day is discarded.

The most important arithmetic rule in the system is a conservation law on every bond. At every instant, in every bond status, the bond's `repair_liability_cap_usd` equals the sum of `claims_paid_to_date_usd`, `reserved_claim_amount_usd` and `remaining_repair_cap_usd` — exact equality on two-decimal values, with no tolerance window. The three components are defined so that this holds: `claims_paid_to_date_usd` is the sum of `approved_payout_usd` over the bond's claims in status `APPROVED_REPAIR`; `reserved_claim_amount_usd` is the sum of `projected_payout_usd` over the bond's claims in status `UNDER_ADJUDICATION`; and `remaining_repair_cap_usd` is the ceiling less those two sums. Claims in status `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED_RETREAT_ONLY`, `DENIED` or `WITHDRAWN` feed neither sum.

This invariant must be re-established after every transition that touches a bond's money: a claim moving from `SUBMITTED` to `UNDER_ADJUDICATION`, and a claim moving from `UNDER_ADJUDICATION` to any of `APPROVED_REPAIR`, `APPROVED_RETREAT_ONLY`, `DENIED` or `WITHDRAWN`. Verification and withdrawal form a compensating pair: verification reserves an amount, withdrawal releases exactly that amount, so `remaining_repair_cap_usd` returns to its pre-verification value with zero drift.

A parallel conservation law governs counts rather than money, on every structure: `open_conducive_condition_count` plus `waived_conducive_condition_count` always equals `total_conducive_condition_count`. Bond issuance and bond termination form that pair — issuance suppresses the waived conditions, termination restores them — and the total is untouched by either.

### 2.9. Licence Discipline and Segregation of Duties

An inspector record is the system's representation of a state credential. It carries the licensee's `full_name` as printed on state forms (1 to 80 characters, not blank), a `license_number` of exactly two uppercase letters, a hyphen, and five digits, unique within its `license_state`, and the `license_state` itself — one of `AL`, `FL`, `GA`, `TX` or `CA`. It carries `license_categories`, an array of between one and three distinct endorsements drawn from `WDO_INSPECTOR`, `TERMITE_APPLICATOR` and `FUMIGATION_CERTIFIED`; a `license_expires_on` calendar date; a `fumigation_endorsement` flag that defaults to false; and `ce_credits`, the continuing-education balance on file, an integer between 0 and 99 that defaults to 0. Licence `status` is one of `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `EXPIRED` and begins at `ACTIVE`.

Endorsement requirements are checked per operation, and a missing endorsement blocks the request even when the licence is `ACTIVE` and unexpired. Signing a graph requires `WDO_INSPECTOR` among the acting inspector's `license_categories`. Recording a pesticide application against a `LIQUID_BARRIER` or `BAIT_STATION` plan requires `TERMITE_APPLICATOR`. Recording a pesticide application against a `FUMIGATION` plan requires both `FUMIGATION_CERTIFIED` among the categories **and** `fumigation_endorsement` set to true — the certification and the endorsement are separate facts and both are demanded. Any shortfall is `ERR_CATEGORY_NOT_LICENSED` (422).

Two separations of duty are enforced, and neither can be waived. The first protects adjudication: the operator identifier recorded as `issued_by_user_id` when a bond was underwritten may never be the `X-User-ID` that adjudicates a claim against that bond, on pain of `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). The second protects verification: the inspector who signed the graph offered as verification of claimed damage must not be the same person as the `applicator_id` named on the treatment plan the bond was written against, because the applicator who treated a structure cannot be allowed to certify the damage claimed against that treatment. Violations are `ERR_VERIFIER_IS_APPLICATOR` (422).

### 2.10. State Jurisdiction: Forms, Turnaround Clocks and Record Retention

Five jurisdictions are modelled — `AL`, `FL`, `GA`, `TX` and `CA` — and each brings its own statutory form, its own report turnaround expectation, and its own pesticide-record retention period. A structure's `state_code` selects all three, and the selection is snapshotted onto the derived records so that later regulatory change cannot rewrite history.

Each state mandates exactly one report form: Alabama requires `NPMA_33`, Florida requires `FL_DACS_13645`, Georgia requires `GA_100`, Texas requires `TX_SPCS_TWDIR`, and California requires `CA_NPCA_1`. A report filed on any other state's form is not a compliant filing and is refused with `ERR_FORM_CODE_MISMATCH` (422).

Turnaround expectations, measured in whole hours from the moment a report is requested, are 120 hours in Alabama, 72 hours in Florida, 96 hours in Georgia, 96 hours in Texas and 168 hours in California. These hour counts serve two purposes: they set the report's `due_at` at request time, and they are the threshold that the measured `turnaround_hours` is compared against to decide `sla_breached`.

Retention periods are expressed in fixed 365-day years, never in calendar years, so a leap day shortens the calendar span rather than moving the anniversary. Alabama, Georgia and Texas each require 2 years of retention, California requires 3, and Florida requires 4; the retention deadline is always computed as that year count multiplied by 365 days added to the date on which the chemical was applied.

### 2.11. Cascades, Ledger Conservation and Version Propagation

Several operations in this system change more than one row. Where they do, the changes form a **chain**: each hop is caused by the hop before it, not by the original request, and a hop that does not fire cannot cause the hop beneath it. Three chains run three levels deep and are called out explicitly in §3 — graph supersession, bait-station escalation, and bond lapse.

Every row a cascade touches is a mutation of that row: its `updated_at` advances to the current logical-clock instant and its `version` increases by exactly 1, exactly as if it had been written directly. A single request can therefore raise the `version` of a station, its plan and the covering bond all at once. Rows that carry no `version` — cell notations, pesticide records, station visits — are only ever created, never cascaded into.

Cascade effects are computed rather than remembered. A structure's four conducive-condition counters and its `active_infestation_on_record` flag are always recomputed from the structure's current latest signed graph and its current bond of record, never patched incrementally; a bond's money components are always brought back into agreement with the conservation law of §2.8 rather than adjusted by guesswork. When a cascade releases a reserve, it releases exactly the amount that was reserved, to the cent.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering Structures and the Conducive-Condition Ledger

A `ComplianceOfficer` registers each structure the branch works on before anything else can happen. The registration payload describes the building well enough to size a treatment and price a bond: `address_line1`, the street address of the inspected structure, between 1 and 120 characters and not blank; `state_code`, the jurisdiction whose pesticide and wood-destroying-organism regulations govern the structure; `construction_type`, the substructure type, one of `SLAB`, `CRAWLSPACE`, `BASEMENT` or `PIER_AND_BEAM`; `square_footage`, the heated floor area, an integer between 200 and 12000; `linear_footage`, the perimeter in linear feet, an integer between 20 and 2000; and `year_built`, an integer between 1850 and 2026. Two further inputs are optional: `crawl_clearance_inches`, the vertical clearance beneath the structure, which is either absent or an integer between 0 and 60 and is null for a `SLAB` building that has no crawl space at all; and `has_attached_structures`, which defaults to false and states whether attached garages or porches extend the treated perimeter.

Registration is deliberately inert. A freshly registered structure has `total_conducive_condition_count`, `waived_conducive_condition_count` and `open_conducive_condition_count` all equal to 0, and `active_infestation_on_record` false. Those four values are never supplied by a client and never edited directly; they are a **ledger**, recomputed from scratch whenever a graph is signed, a bond is issued, or a bond terminates.

The ledger recomputation works as follows. First the structure's **latest signed graph** is identified: among the structure's graphs in status `GRAPHED`, the one with the greatest `graph_completed_at`; when two share the same instant, the one with the greater `sequence_no` wins, which makes the choice total and non-random. Graphs still in `DRAFT`, and graphs already `SUPERSEDED`, contribute nothing at all. Then the distinct `conducive_code` values are collected over that graph's cell notations whose `finding_type` is `CONDUCIVE_CONDITION` and whose `conducive_code` is something other than `NONE`; the number of distinct codes found is `total_conducive_condition_count`, and it is 0 when the structure has no signed graph. Next the structure's **bond of record** is identified — the single bond on that structure whose status is `ACTIVE` or `LAPSED`, of which at most one may ever exist. The number of the structure's distinct conducive codes that also appear in that bond's `conducive_waiver_codes` array is `waived_conducive_condition_count`, and it is 0 when there is no bond of record. Finally `open_conducive_condition_count` is `total_conducive_condition_count` less `waived_conducive_condition_count`.

Note carefully that the structure-level counters count **distinct codes**, whereas the graph-level counter described in §3.3 counts **rows**. Two `EXCESS_MOISTURE` notations in different grid cells raise the graph's row count by two but the structure's distinct-code count by one. This distinction matters twice: the renewal surcharge is priced per row, and the waiver arithmetic is applied per code.

Separately, `active_infestation_on_record` is true exactly when the structure has a latest signed graph and that graph's own `active_infestation_found` flag is true. A structure with no signed graph has the flag false.

Any caller may read one structure, including its live ledger counters, and any caller may list structures.

### 3.2. Licensing, Suspending and Reinstating the Field Force

A `ComplianceOfficer` registers each licensee, supplying `full_name`, `license_number`, `license_state`, `license_categories` and `license_expires_on`, optionally `fumigation_endorsement` and `ce_credits`. The new record's `status` is `ACTIVE`.

Licence status thereafter moves only through a compliance action that names the target `status`, and only along four declared paths. `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` requires the licence to be `ACTIVE` and simply sets it `SUSPENDED`. `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` likewise requires `ACTIVE`. `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` requires the licence to be `SUSPENDED` **and** its existing `license_expires_on` to fall on or after the logical clock's UTC date — a suspension cannot be lifted on a credential that has meanwhile run out. `EXPIRED → ACTIVE` is the reinstatement path and is the strictest of the four: the request must carry a `license_expires_on` strictly after the logical clock's UTC date, and the **effective** continuing-education balance — the request's `ce_credits` when the request supplies one, otherwise the balance already on file — must be at least 8. An expired licence is never reactivated on the strength of its own stale expiry date, and never on an unmet continuing-education balance; a failure of either condition is `ERR_LICENSE_REINSTATEMENT_UNMET`. On a successful reinstatement the record's `license_expires_on` becomes the supplied date and its `ce_credits` becomes the supplied balance when one was supplied, remaining unchanged otherwise. All four moves set the new `status` and raise the record's `version` by one.

Any other pairing — a second `ACTIVE → ACTIVE` request, a move from `SUSPENDED` straight to `EXPIRED` — is not a declared transition and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTOR_STATE`.

The practical effect of a suspension is immediate but not retroactive: a `SUSPENDED` inspector can neither sign a graph nor record a pesticide application, and rows already written are left exactly as they are. The same is true of expiry. Both statuses fail the licence-active check with `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_ACTIVE`, and a licence whose `license_expires_on` is strictly before the clock date fails the separate unexpired check with `ERR_LICENSE_EXPIRED` — an expiry landing exactly on the clock date is still good for that day. Any caller may read one licensee; every role except the homeowner may list them.

### 3.3. Graphing a Structure: Drafts, Cells, Signing and Supersession

Everything downstream of a structure begins with a graph. A `FieldInspector` opens one by naming the structure through `property_id`, naming themselves through `inspector_id`, declaring a `purpose` — `INITIAL`, `RENEWAL`, `REAL_ESTATE` or `REINSTATEMENT` — and bounding the on-site visit with `scheduled_start_at`, the inclusive start of the inspector's occupation of the site, and `scheduled_end_at`, its exclusive end, which must fall strictly after the start. A `RENEWAL` or `REINSTATEMENT` graph must additionally name the graph it replaces through `supersedes_inspection_id`, and must not be opened without one; an `INITIAL` or `REAL_ESTATE` graph must not carry that field at all. A `REAL_ESTATE` graph must carry `closing_date`, the calendar date of the real-estate closing it serves, and no graph of any other purpose may carry it. Violations of these pairings are `ERR_FIELD_COMBINATION_INVALID`, as is an end instant that is not strictly after the start.

The new graph is created in status `DRAFT` and is stamped with a `sequence_no`: a 1-based counter that is one greater than the highest `sequence_no` yet issued for that structure, so numbering increases monotonically within a structure and supplies a total tie-break wherever two graphs of a structure would otherwise be indistinguishable. Opening a graph also enforces jurisdiction and calendar hygiene. The inspector's `license_state` must equal the structure's `state_code`, or the request is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_STATE_MISMATCH`; the licence must be `ACTIVE`, though the expiry-date test is deferred to the moment the graph is signed. When `supersedes_inspection_id` is supplied, the graph it names must belong to the same structure, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROPERTY_MISMATCH`. And the requested occupation must not collide with the inspector's own diary: treating each of that inspector's graphs as the half-open interval running from its `scheduled_start_at` up to but not including its `scheduled_end_at`, no other interval of that inspector may overlap the requested one. Intervals that merely touch — one visit ending at the exact instant the next begins — are permitted; a genuine overlap is `ERR_INSPECTOR_SCHEDULE_OVERLAP`.

Supplying `supersedes_inspection_id` retires the prior graph immediately, and does so as a three-level chain. The first hop moves the named graph to `SUPERSEDED`, whether it was `GRAPHED` or still `DRAFT`. The second hop is caused by the first, not by the request: every statutory report transcribed from the now-superseded graph, in status `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `AMENDED`, is moved to `VOID`, because a filing whose underlying graph has been replaced can no longer be relied on at a closing. A `DRAFT` graph that was never signed typically has no reports to void, and in that case the second hop simply finds nothing. The third hop is caused by the change of the structure's latest signed graph: the structure's four conducive counters and `active_infestation_on_record` are recomputed as described in §3.1. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal — a retired graph never returns to service, and it is not evidence for anything.

Cell notations are added one at a time to a graph while it is still `DRAFT`; adding one to a graph in any other status is `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_STATE`. Each notation records a `finding_type` — `ACTIVE_INFESTATION`, `PREVIOUS_TREATMENT_EVIDENCE`, `DAMAGE` or `CONDUCIVE_CONDITION` — and a `grid_ref`, the cell on the printed graph, written as a single column letter from `A` through `J` followed by a row number from 1 through 10. Four further inputs are optional and each has a default. `organism` names the wood-destroying organism found at the cell and is one of `SUBTERRANEAN_TERMITE`, `DRYWOOD_TERMITE`, `WOOD_BORING_BEETLE`, `FUNGUS`, `CARPENTER_ANT` or `NONE`, defaulting to `NONE`; it must be something other than `NONE` whenever `finding_type` is `ACTIVE_INFESTATION`. `severity` is an integer weight from 1 to 5 defaulting to 1, and it is the weighting used by the proration form of the claim-exclusion arithmetic. `conducive_code` names the conducive condition at the cell and is one of `WOOD_TO_GROUND`, `EXCESS_MOISTURE`, `DEBRIS_UNDER_STRUCTURE`, `INSUFFICIENT_CLEARANCE`, `FAULTY_GRADE` or `NONE`, defaulting to `NONE`; it must be a real code exactly when `finding_type` is `CONDUCIVE_CONDITION` and must be `NONE` for every other finding type. `damage_estimate_usd` is the repair estimate for that cell, from 0.00 to 250000.00 with at most two decimal places, defaulting to 0.00; it must be strictly greater than zero exactly when `finding_type` is `DAMAGE` and must be exactly 0.00 otherwise. `pre_existing` is a flag, defaulting to false, that marks damage or infestation at the cell as predating bond coverage, and it is supplied exactly when `finding_type` is `DAMAGE`; it is the switch the whole claim-exclusion arithmetic turns on.

Within one graph the pair of `grid_ref` and `finding_type` must be unique. The same cell may legitimately carry both a `DAMAGE` notation and a `CONDUCIVE_CONDITION` notation — rotten sill plate above a wood-to-ground contact — but never two notations of the same type; the second attempt is `ERR_DUPLICATE_GRID_FINDING`. Notations are immutable once written and are listed back per graph, optionally narrowed to a single `finding_type`.

Signing the graph advances it `DRAFT → GRAPHED` and is the point at which every rollup is computed. The signing inspector must own the graph, must hold an `ACTIVE` licence whose `license_expires_on` is not earlier than the clock date, and must carry the `WDO_INSPECTOR` endorsement. A graph with no cell notations at all may only be signed when the request sets `no_findings_attested` to true, which is the inspector's formal attestation that the structure was examined and nothing was found; without that attestation a zero-notation graph is refused with `ERR_GRAPH_INCOMPLETE`. The submitted value of `no_findings_attested` is persisted on the graph, defaulting to false when the request omits it.

Signing stamps `graph_completed_at` with the current logical-clock instant — it stays null for as long as the graph is a draft — and then recomputes four rollups from the graph's own notations. `findings_count` is the number of notations on the graph. `conducive_condition_count` is the number of notations whose `finding_type` is `CONDUCIVE_CONDITION`, counted **per row rather than per distinct code**, and it is the basis of the renewal conducive surcharge. `active_infestation_found` is true when at least one notation has `finding_type` equal to `ACTIVE_INFESTATION`. `damage_estimate_total_usd` is the sum of `damage_estimate_usd` over the graph's `DAMAGE` notations, and it is 0.00 when there are none. Signing then recomputes the structure's ledger exactly as §3.1 describes, and raises the graph's `version` by one.

### 3.4. Real-Estate Transactions: Statutory Reports, Turnaround and Amendments

When a sale is pending, either the homeowner or a `ComplianceOfficer` opens a statutory wood-destroying-organism report against a `REAL_ESTATE` graph, naming it through `inspection_id` and declaring the `form_code` being filed. The report is created in status `DRAFT`, its `requested_at` stamped with the current clock instant, and three values are snapshotted onto it at that moment: `state_code` from the inspected structure, `closing_date` from the graph, and `due_at`, computed as `requested_at` plus that state's turnaround allowance in hours from §2.10. Snapshotting matters — a later change of jurisdiction or closing date cannot rewrite a filing already in flight. The declared `form_code`, one of `NPMA_33`, `FL_DACS_13645`, `GA_100`, `TX_SPCS_TWDIR` or `CA_NPCA_1`, must be the form the snapshotted state mandates, or the request is refused with `ERR_FORM_CODE_MISMATCH`.

Issuance, which a `ComplianceOfficer` performs, moves the report `DRAFT → ISSUED`. The underlying graph must be in status `GRAPHED` — a draft or superseded graph is not evidence, and offering one is `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_GRAPHED` — and its `purpose` must be `REAL_ESTATE`, or the attempt is `ERR_INSPECTION_PURPOSE_MISMATCH`. A closing-lead gate then applies: taking the report's snapshotted `closing_date` at midnight UTC and subtracting 24 hours gives the latest instant at which a report may still be issued, and issuance is permitted when the clock instant is at or before that deadline. An attempt that would land inside the final 24 hours before closing is refused with `ERR_CLOSING_LEAD_TIME_MISSED` rather than issued late.

A successful issuance sets `status` to `ISSUED` and stamps `issued_at` with the clock instant of that issuance. On the **first** issuance only, two measurements are taken: `turnaround_hours`, the number of whole hours from `requested_at` to `issued_at` rounded up so that any partial hour counts as a full one, and `sla_breached`, true exactly when `turnaround_hours` **strictly exceeds** the state's allowance — a report issued in exactly the allowed number of hours is on time.

An issued report may be corrected. Amendment moves it `ISSUED → AMENDED`, requires an `amendment_reason_code` in the request — one of `SECONDARY_EVIDENCE_ADDED`, `INACCESSIBLE_AREA_CLARIFIED`, `DAMAGE_SCOPE_REVISED` or `CLERICAL_CORRECTION` — and is only available while the clock's UTC date is no later than 30 days after the date of the report's current `issued_at`; later attempts are `ERR_AMENDMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The amendment raises `amendment_count` by one and records the supplied reason on the report, and it explicitly does **not** recompute `turnaround_hours` or `sla_breached`: both stay pinned to the first issuance for ever, because the branch's service-level performance is judged on when it first delivered, not on how many corrections followed.

From `AMENDED` the report is re-issued, returning it to `ISSUED` and closing the loop; `amendment_count` records how many times the loop has run. A re-issue re-checks the same 24-hour closing-lead gate and the same requirement that the graph still be `GRAPHED`, and it refreshes `issued_at` to the instant of this issuance — which, since the amendment window is measured from `issued_at`, restarts the 30-day amendment window. It leaves `turnaround_hours` and `sla_breached` at their first-issuance values. Attempting to issue a report that is neither `DRAFT` nor `AMENDED`, or to amend one that is not `ISSUED`, or to touch one that is already `VOID`, is `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_STATE`.

`VOID` is the report lifecycle's terminal state and is only ever reached from outside: as the second hop of the supersession chain in §3.3, every report of a graph that becomes `SUPERSEDED` is voided wherever it stood — `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `AMENDED`.

### 3.5. Choosing and Pricing a Treatment

A `BranchManager` proposes the treatment. The proposal names the structure through `property_id`, the graph that justifies the work through `inspection_id`, and the licensed applicator who will do it through `applicator_id`. It declares a `method` — `LIQUID_BARRIER`, `BAIT_STATION` or `FUMIGATION` — and a `chemical_product_code`, one of `FIPRO_9`, `IMID_4`, `HEXA_BAIT_2` or `SULFURYL_F`. The graph named must belong to the same structure as `property_id`, or the proposal is refused with `ERR_PROPERTY_MISMATCH`. The new plan is created in status `PROPOSED`, and the act of proposing retires competition: every other plan on that same structure still sitting in `PROPOSED` is moved to `ABANDONED`, each such row taking a `version` increment of its own. Plans already `APPROVED`, `COMPLETED` or `ABANDONED` are untouched by this sweep.

Two compatibility gates apply at proposal time and are the reason a branch cannot simply sell its most profitable method everywhere. The first is physical: a slab building admits all three methods; a crawlspace admits `LIQUID_BARRIER` and `BAIT_STATION` only, because a vented crawlspace cannot be sealed tightly enough to hold fumigant; a basement admits `BAIT_STATION` and `FUMIGATION` only, because a continuous exterior trench cannot be cut below a basement footing; and a pier-and-beam building admits all three. A method outside the structure's permitted set is `ERR_METHOD_NOT_PERMITTED`. The second gate is chemical: a `LIQUID_BARRIER` plan may only be loaded with `FIPRO_9` or `IMID_4`, a `BAIT_STATION` plan only with `HEXA_BAIT_2`, and a `FUMIGATION` plan only with `SULFURYL_F`. A soil termiticide may not be packed into a bait cartridge and a fumigant may not be trenched into the ground; a mismatch is `ERR_PRODUCT_METHOD_INCOMPATIBLE`.

A `FUMIGATION` plan carries three further inputs, and they are required exactly for that method: `fumigation_permit_number`, the state permit, written as the letters `FUM`, a hyphen, and six digits; `fumigation_start_at`, the inclusive instant at which the tenting occupation of the property begins; and `fumigation_reentry_at`, the inclusive clearance-and-re-entry instant that ends it, which must fall strictly after the start. A plan of any other method must carry none of the three. Supplying a fumigation field on a non-fumigation plan, omitting one on a fumigation plan, breaking the permit pattern, or ordering the two instants wrongly is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Sizing and pricing happen exactly once, here at proposal, and are then frozen for the life of the plan. Nothing later — approval, an application, completion, a bond, a claim — ever recomputes them. Both draw on exactly one structure row and exactly one graph row, and no other plan participates in the arithmetic.

The volume target, `target_gallons`, is the termiticide volume a liquid plan must deliver before it may be closed out, and it is 0.00 for a plan of any method other than `LIQUID_BARRIER`. For a liquid plan it is built from two parts. The trench part is the structure's `linear_footage` multiplied by a per-foot trench rate that depends on the substructure: 1.0 gallon per linear foot for a slab, 1.5 for a crawlspace, 2.0 for a basement, and 1.25 for pier-and-beam construction. The drilling part applies to slab construction alone and is 0.5 gallons for every 100 square feet of heated area, where the number of 100-square-foot blocks is the structure's `square_footage` divided by 100 and then raised to the next whole number if it is not already whole; for every other substructure the drilling part is zero. The two parts are added together and the sum is raised to the next whole gallon.

The station target, `target_station_count`, is the number of bait stations to install and is 0 for any method other than `BAIT_STATION`. For a bait plan it is the structure's `linear_footage` divided by a station spacing of 10 feet, raised to the next whole number if not already whole, plus 2 extra stations when the structure has attached garages or porches — that is, when `has_attached_structures` is true.

The price, `quoted_price_usd`, is computed in six steps whose order is fixed, each running exactly once:

1. A floor-area subtotal is accumulated across three brackets of heated area: the first 1500 square feet are charged at 0.42 per square foot; the portion above 1500 up to and including 3000 square feet at 0.35 per square foot; and the portion beyond 3000 square feet at 0.28 per square foot. A 2200-square-foot house therefore contributes 1500 square feet at the first rate and 700 at the second, and nothing at the third.
2. The method's base fee is added to that subtotal: 275.00 for `LIQUID_BARRIER`, 390.00 for `BAIT_STATION`, 640.00 for `FUMIGATION`.
3. The running figure is multiplied by a construction multiplier: 1.00 for `SLAB`, 1.15 for `CRAWLSPACE`, 1.25 for `BASEMENT`, 1.10 for `PIER_AND_BEAM`.
4. When the justifying graph's `active_infestation_found` is true, the running figure is increased by a further 12 percent — that is, multiplied by 1.12; when it is false the figure is carried forward unchanged.
5. Only now is the running figure rounded, half up, to two decimal places. Steps 1 through 4 carry full precision, and rounding anywhere earlier is wrong.
6. Finally the rounded figure is capped: the quote is the lesser of that figure and the method's ceiling, which is 4200.00 for `LIQUID_BARRIER`, 3600.00 for `BAIT_STATION` and 9800.00 for `FUMIGATION`. The cap is applied after rounding, never before.

Approval, also a `BranchManager` action, advances the plan `PROPOSED → APPROVED`. It requires the justifying graph to have reached `GRAPHED` — a plan justified by a draft or superseded graph cannot be authorised, and the refusal is `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_GRAPHED` — and it requires the named applicator's licence to be `ACTIVE`, refusing with `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_ACTIVE` otherwise, and to carry a `license_expires_on` on or after the clock date, refusing with `ERR_LICENSE_EXPIRED` otherwise. Approving a plan that is not `PROPOSED` is `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`. Any caller may read one plan, with its frozen sizing and quote alongside its live escalation counters, and any caller may list plans.

### 3.6. Discharging Chemical, Record Retention and Plan Closeout

Each application of chemical against an approved plan is recorded as its own immutable row by the `Applicator` named on that plan. The request supplies `product_epa_number`, the EPA registration number printed on the label, written as three to five digits, a hyphen, then two to four digits, and which must correspond to the plan's `chemical_product_code`; `gallons_applied`, the finished dilution discharged on this visit, from 0.10 to 2000.00 gallons with at most two decimal places; `concentration_pct`, the finished-dilution strength as a percentage, from 0.01 to 5.00 with at most two decimal places; and the version expectation `expected_treatment_plan_version` described in §2.7. The plan must be `APPROVED`; recording against a plan in any other status is `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`.

The acting applicator must be the plan's `applicator_id`, must hold an `ACTIVE` licence whose `license_expires_on` is not before the clock date, must hold a `license_state` equal to the treated structure's `state_code`, and must carry the endorsement the method demands as set out in §2.9. For a fumigation plan there is one further temporal gate: the clock instant that will stamp the record must be at or after the plan's `fumigation_start_at`, because fumigant cannot be recorded as discharged before the permitted start of the tenting window; an earlier instant is `ERR_APPLICATION_BEFORE_PERMIT_WINDOW`.

A successful application creates a record stamped `applied_at` with the current clock instant and snapshots three facts that must survive any later churn in the personnel or property registry: `applicator_license_number` and `applicator_license_state` from the acting applicator's licence, and `state_code` from the treated structure. It also computes `retention_until`, the last calendar date on which the state requires the record to be kept, as the UTC date of `applied_at` plus the state's retention year count multiplied by 365 days, per §2.10. The plan's `gallons_applied_total` — the running sum of `gallons_applied` over the plan's records — is increased by the gallons just discharged, and the plan's `version` rises by one. The record itself never changes again: it has no `updated_at`, no `version`, and no update operation, and the records of one plan are listed back in creation order.

Closing the plan out advances it `APPROVED → COMPLETED`, is performed by the plan's own applicator, and is gated differently for each method. A `LIQUID_BARRIER` plan may only be completed once `gallons_applied_total` has reached at least `target_gallons` — the volume target is a floor, and exceeding it is fine. A `BAIT_STATION` plan may only be completed once at least one application record exists against it, since the cartridges have to have been loaded. A `FUMIGATION` plan may only be completed once the clock instant has reached or passed `fumigation_reentry_at`, because a structure still under tent has not been cleared. Failing the gate for the plan's method leaves the plan `APPROVED`; completing a plan that is not `APPROVED` is `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`.

Completion stamps `completed_at` with the clock instant, and for a `BAIT_STATION` plan it also installs the hardware: exactly `target_station_count` station rows are created, numbered by `station_index` from 1 upward, each in status `INSTALLED`, each with `last_serviced_at` set to the plan's `completed_at` and `next_due_at` set to 90 days after that instant. Each station's `grid_ref` is derived from its ordinal rather than supplied: stations 1 through 10 take column `A` with rows 1 through 10, stations 11 through 20 take column `B` with rows 1 through 10, and so on — the column letter advances one place in the alphabet for each complete block of ten stations, and the row is the position within the block. These cascade-created stations are discoverable only through the station listing beneath their plan.

Abandonment is the other exit from `APPROVED`, available to a `BranchManager`, and it is deliberately hard once chemical has gone into the ground. A plan may not be abandoned while **any** of its pesticide records still has a `retention_until` on or after the clock's UTC date; the refusal is `ERR_RETENTION_LOCK_ACTIVE`, and the practical consequence is that a plan which has discharged chemical can only be abandoned once every one of its records has aged out of its state retention period. A plan that never discharged anything has no records and abandons freely. Abandoning a plan that is not `APPROVED` is `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`. Both `COMPLETED` and `ABANDONED` are terminal.

### 3.7. Underwriting: Issuing the Bond, Its Premium and Its Repair Ceiling

Only a completed treatment earns a bond. A `BranchManager` issues one by naming the structure through `property_id` and the treatment it is written against through `treatment_plan_id`, and by choosing a `coverage_tier`. `RETREAT_ONLY` covers re-treatment labour and nothing else; `REPAIR_AND_RETREAT` adds a monetary ceiling on repair liability. The named plan must belong to the named structure — otherwise `ERR_PROPERTY_MISMATCH` — and must be in status `COMPLETED`; a `PROPOSED`, `APPROVED` or `ABANDONED` plan cannot be bonded, and the refusal is `ERR_PLAN_NOT_COMPLETED`. The graph that justified that plan must have reached `GRAPHED`, failing which the issuance is `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_GRAPHED`, and its `purpose` must be `INITIAL`, failing which it is `ERR_INSPECTION_PURPOSE_MISMATCH`. Finally a structure may hold at most one bond in status `ACTIVE` or `LAPSED` at any instant, and a second issuance is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_BOND` even when the first bond is mid-lapse — a lapsed bond is still an encumbrance on the structure until it terminates.

The issuance request may also carry `conducive_waiver_codes`, an array of conducive-condition codes drawn from the same domain the graph notations use, excluding `NONE`, which defaults to an empty array. Each code in it is a condition the homeowner formally accepts responsibility for at signing — a persistent wood-to-ground contact the branch will not be blamed for. Issuance immediately recomputes the structure's ledger as §3.1 describes, so the waived codes move out of `open_conducive_condition_count` and into `waived_conducive_condition_count` on the spot, leaving `total_conducive_condition_count` untouched.

The bond is created `ACTIVE`, records the issuing operator's identifier as `issued_by_user_id`, and sets `term_index` to 1. Its money is computed here and frozen for the life of the bond, in three ordered steps that must not be reordered:

1. `base_premium_usd` is the bonded plan's `quoted_price_usd` multiplied by 0.18 — 18 percent of the treatment price — rounded half up to two decimal places.
2. `repair_liability_cap_usd` is `base_premium_usd` multiplied by the tier's cap multiplier, rounded half up to two decimal places. For `RETREAT_ONLY` that multiplier is zero, so the ceiling is 0.00 and the bond carries no monetary repair liability at all. For `REPAIR_AND_RETREAT` it is 60, so the ceiling is sixty times the base premium.
3. `annual_premium_usd` is `base_premium_usd` plus a cap loading of 0.004 — four tenths of one percent — of `repair_liability_cap_usd`, the sum rounded half up to two decimal places. This is the figure renewal and reinstatement bill from.

The dependency between steps 2 and 3 looks circular and is not: the ceiling is derived from the **base** premium and is never re-derived from the loaded annual premium. Deriving it the other way round yields a materially larger ceiling and is wrong. The remaining money fields follow mechanically: `claims_paid_to_date_usd` and `reserved_claim_amount_usd` both start at 0.00 and `remaining_repair_cap_usd` starts equal to `repair_liability_cap_usd`, which satisfies the conservation law of §2.8 at issuance.

The coverage calendar is computed at the same moment and uses whole-day offsets throughout; calendar months are never used anywhere in this system. `issued_on` and `current_term_start` are both the logical clock's UTC date at issuance. `current_term_end` is 365 days after `current_term_start` and is the inclusive last date of the term. `renewal_window_opens_on` is 60 days before `current_term_end`, and it is recomputed to keep that relationship whenever the term advances. `lapse_date` starts null, `reinstatement_count` starts at 0, and the three watch flags — `retreat_required`, `coverage_watch` and the counter `retreat_credit_count` — start false, false and 0 respectively. `retreat_required` becomes true once any retreat obligation has been raised, whether by a claim outcome or by a station escalation; `coverage_watch` becomes true only when a station escalation reaches the bond, and it flags the bond for mandatory review at its next renewal.

### 3.8. The Renewal Cycle: Invoicing, Lapse, Reinstatement and Termination

A bond stays alive by being renewed once per 365-day term, and the renewal window is generous but bounded: the clock's UTC date must be on or after `renewal_window_opens_on` — that is, no earlier than 60 days before `current_term_end` — and no later than 30 days after `current_term_end`, both bounds inclusive. That trailing 30 days is the grace period. Outside the window the attempt is `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. Only one invoice may be open at a time: a bond with an existing renewal in status `PENDING` refuses a second with `ERR_RENEWAL_ALREADY_PENDING`, so the open invoice must be paid or voided first. The bond must also be `ACTIVE`; a lapsed bond that is additionally outside its window reports `ERR_INVALID_BOND_STATE`, because the state check outranks the window check.

Opening an invoice creates a renewal row in status `PENDING` that snapshots the bond's `term_index` — the term this invoice closes out — and sets `due_on` to the bond's `current_term_end`. The invoice is then priced **provisionally**, from four components each rounded half up to two decimal places on its own before any addition happens. `base_renewal_fee_usd` is the bond's `annual_premium_usd` multiplied by 0.345. `inspection_fee_usd` is a flat 85.00 plus 2.155 for each 100 square feet of heated area, computed as the structure's `square_footage` divided by 100 and multiplied by 2.155 at full precision before that component is rounded. At creation `conducive_surcharge_usd` and `late_fee_usd` are both forced to 0.00, because no renewal graph has been attached yet and nothing is past due yet. `amount_paid_usd` is likewise 0.00 at creation, and it stays 0.00 for as long as no payment has been recorded against the invoice — an invoice that never gets paid still reports 0.00 rather than null. `invoice_total_usd` is the exact sum of the four already-rounded components — which at creation is simply the first two. Rounding each component before summing, rather than summing at full precision and rounding once, is mandatory: the two orders differ by a cent on many inputs, and only the first is correct.

Completing the invoice is where the renewal actually happens, and it reprices before it charges. A `BranchManager` supplies `inspection_id`, the renewal graph to attach, and `amount_usd`, the payment tendered. The invoice must be `PENDING`, or the attempt is `ERR_INVALID_RENEWAL_STATE`; the bond must be `ACTIVE`; the clock must still be inside the renewal window; the attached graph must belong to the bond's structure, or `ERR_PROPERTY_MISMATCH`; it must be in status `GRAPHED`, or `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_GRAPHED`; and its `purpose` must be `RENEWAL`, or `ERR_INSPECTION_PURPOSE_MISMATCH`. The inspector who signed that graph must hold an `ACTIVE` licence (`ERR_LICENSE_NOT_ACTIVE`) whose `license_expires_on` is not earlier than the clock date (`ERR_LICENSE_EXPIRED`) — and because compliance outranks money, a completion that is both unlicensed and underpaid reports the licence failure.

Two of the four components are then recomputed against the attached graph and the clock. `conducive_surcharge_usd` becomes 7.335 for each conducive-condition notation on the attached renewal graph — that is, 7.335 multiplied by the graph's `conducive_condition_count`, which counts notation **rows**, including rows whose `conducive_code` the bond has waived, and the product rounded half up to two decimals. `late_fee_usd` becomes 0.415 for each whole UTC day by which the clock's date has passed the bond's `current_term_end`, floored at zero days when the clock has not yet reached the term end, again rounded half up to two decimals. `base_renewal_fee_usd` and `inspection_fee_usd` keep the same definitions as at creation, and `invoice_total_usd` is again the exact sum of the four rounded components. The payment check compares `amount_usd` against **this** recomputed total, not the provisional figure stored earlier; a shortfall is `ERR_PAYMENT_INSUFFICIENT`. On success the invoice records the attached `inspection_id`, moves to `PAID`, stamps `paid_at` with the clock instant, and records `amount_paid_usd` as the amount tendered.

Payment cascades into the bond, which takes its own self-transition and stays `ACTIVE` while its coverage window rolls forward one term: `current_term_start` becomes the previous `current_term_end`, `current_term_end` becomes the previous `current_term_end` plus 365 days, `renewal_window_opens_on` becomes 60 days before the new term end, and `term_index` rises by one. One further effect rides along: when the attached renewal graph's `active_infestation_found` is true, the bond's `retreat_required` is set true; when it is false the flag is left exactly as it was rather than cleared. The bond's `version` rises by one, as does the invoice's.

A bond that misses its grace deadline does not lapse spontaneously — a `ComplianceOfficer` must review it. Review of an `ACTIVE` bond whose clock date has passed `current_term_end` plus 30 days moves it `ACTIVE → LAPSED` and starts a three-level chain. The bond's `lapse_date` is set to `current_term_end` plus 30 days — the grace deadline that was actually missed, **not** the date on which the review happened to run, so a review three weeks late still records the true deadline. The second hop, caused by the lapse, does two things: every renewal invoice of that bond still in `PENDING` moves to `VOID`, and every damage claim of that bond in `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_ADJUDICATION` moves to `DENIED` with `denial_reason_code` set to `BOND_LAPSED`. The third hop is caused only by those claim denials that came from `UNDER_ADJUDICATION`: each such claim's `projected_payout_usd` is subtracted from the bond's `reserved_claim_amount_usd` and added back to its `remaining_repair_cap_usd`, which keeps the conservation law of §2.8 intact. A claim denied out of `SUBMITTED` had reserved nothing and releases nothing.

Review of a bond that is already `LAPSED` and whose clock date has passed `lapse_date` plus 90 days moves it `LAPSED → TERMINATED`, which is terminal. The second hop of that chain removes the hardware and unwinds the waivers: every bait station belonging to the bond's treatment plan that is still `INSTALLED`, `ACTIVE_HIT` or `ESCALATED` moves to `REMOVED`, and the covered structure's `waived_conducive_condition_count` returns to 0 with `open_conducive_condition_count` restored to the full `total_conducive_condition_count`, because a terminated bond's `conducive_waiver_codes` no longer suppress anything. The bond's money fields — `repair_liability_cap_usd`, `claims_paid_to_date_usd`, `reserved_claim_amount_usd` and `remaining_repair_cap_usd` — are deliberately left frozen at their pre-termination values as a permanent record of what the bond did. A review that finds neither deadline passed changes nothing and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BOND_STATE`.

Between lapse and termination sits a 90-day window in which the bond can be bought back. Reinstatement is a `BranchManager` action on a `LAPSED` bond, supplying `reinstatement_inspection_id` and `amount_usd`. The clock's UTC date must be no later than `lapse_date` plus 90 days, or the attempt is `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_WINDOW_EXPIRED` and the bond's only remaining path is termination. The named graph must belong to the bond's structure (`ERR_PROPERTY_MISMATCH`), must have `purpose` equal to `REINSTATEMENT` (`ERR_INSPECTION_PURPOSE_MISMATCH`) and status `GRAPHED` (`ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_GRAPHED`), must have been signed recently — its `graph_completed_at` no earlier than 30 days before the current clock instant, otherwise `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_INSPECTION_STALE` — and must show a clean structure, meaning its `active_infestation_found` must be false. A graph showing live termites cannot support reinstatement.

The settlement amount depends on how long the bond was down. `lapse_days` is the number of whole UTC days from `lapse_date` to the clock's UTC date, discarding any partial day and never below zero. When `lapse_days` is 30 or fewer, the **prorated-remainder** treatment applies: the premium charge is `annual_premium_usd` multiplied by the unexpired fraction of the term — 365 less `lapse_days`, divided by 365 — rounded half up to two decimals; the bond's `current_term_start` becomes the old `lapse_date` and its `current_term_end` becomes the **previous** `current_term_end` plus 365 days, so the bond's anniversary date never moves. When `lapse_days` exceeds 30, the **full-term-restart** treatment applies instead: the premium charge is the whole `annual_premium_usd` with no proration, `current_term_start` becomes the clock's UTC date and `current_term_end` becomes 365 days after the clock's UTC date, which moves the anniversary. For otherwise identical bonds the restart treatment always costs strictly more and lands on a different term end than the prorated one. Either way, the amount owed is the premium charge plus a flat reinstatement fee of 95.00 plus 1.75 for each day of lapse, the whole sum rounded half up to two decimal places; the premium charge is rounded before it enters that sum. If `amount_usd` is less than the amount owed, the attempt is `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_PAYMENT_INSUFFICIENT`.

A successful reinstatement returns the bond to `ACTIVE`, clears `lapse_date` back to null, raises `reinstatement_count` by one and `term_index` by one, sets the two term endpoints according to the treatment selected above, and recomputes `renewal_window_opens_on` as 60 days before the new `current_term_end`. The cycle is repeatable: a bond may lapse and be reinstated many times, and `reinstatement_count` is the running tally. Reinstating a bond that is not `LAPSED` is `ERR_INVALID_BOND_STATE`.

### 3.9. Bait-Station Monitoring and the Escalation Cascade

Bait stations exist only as the product of completing a `BAIT_STATION` plan; there is no way to create one directly, and each carries the `treatment_plan_id` of the plan that installed it, its `station_index`, and its derived `grid_ref`. A station's `status` is `INSTALLED`, `ACTIVE_HIT`, `ESCALATED` or `REMOVED`, and the status does double duty: besides describing what the station has found, it selects how often the station must be visited. An `INSTALLED` station is due every 90 days, an `ACTIVE_HIT` station every 30 days, and an `ESCALATED` station every 14 days. `next_due_at` always states the current expectation, computed as `last_serviced_at` plus the interval belonging to the station's present status.

A `FieldInspector` records each visit as an immutable service row. The request carries `activity_level` — `NONE`, `LIGHT`, `MODERATE` or `HEAVY` — describing the termite activity found in the bait matrix, and optionally `matrix_replaced`, defaulting to false, stating whether the bait cartridge was changed. The station must not be `REMOVED`, or the visit is `ERR_INVALID_STATION_STATE`; the installing plan must be `COMPLETED`; the acting inspector must be that plan's `applicator_id` and must hold an `ACTIVE`, unexpired licence. One chronological rule is absolute: the clock instant that will stamp `serviced_at` must be **strictly after** the station's current `last_serviced_at`, so that a station's visit history forms a strictly increasing chain. A clock rewound to, or exactly onto, the previous visit is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_TIME_INVALID`.

The visit row records the `bait_station_id` of the station visited and `serviced_at` from the clock, snapshots the acting inspector's `license_number` as `serviced_by_license_number` so the visit record survives any later change to the personnel registry, and computes two derived values. `days_since_last_service` is the number of whole UTC days from the station's `last_serviced_at` **as it stood before this visit** to `serviced_at`, discarding any partial day. `interval_compliance` grades that gap against the interval belonging to the station's status **before** this visit, not after it: a gap no greater than the interval is `ON_TIME`; a gap greater than the interval but no greater than the interval plus a 7-day grace is `LATE`; anything beyond that is `MISSED`. Both graded edges are inclusive — a gap of exactly the interval is `ON_TIME` rather than `LATE`, and a gap of exactly the interval plus seven days is `LATE` rather than `MISSED`.

The activity level then drives the station's own state. A `NONE` visit resets `consecutive_activity_count` to 0 and returns the station to `INSTALLED`, which is the cyclic part of the design: a station that goes quiet drops back to the relaxed 90-day rhythm no matter how agitated it had become. A `LIGHT` visit is recorded but changes nothing about the station's condition — `consecutive_activity_count` is left exactly as it was and the status is left exactly as it was. A `MODERATE` or `HEAVY` visit raises `consecutive_activity_count` by one; if the resulting run length is 3 or more the station becomes `ESCALATED`, and otherwise it becomes `ACTIVE_HIT` — which for a station already in `ACTIVE_HIT` is a self-transition that merely extends the run. Whatever the outcome, `next_due_at` is recomputed as `serviced_at` plus the interval of the station's **new** status, `service_count` rises by one, and `matrix_replacement_count` rises by one as well when `matrix_replaced` was true.

Escalation is the origin of the system's deepest cascade, and each hop is caused by the hop above it rather than by the request. The first hop is the station's own move into `ESCALATED`. The second hop fires only when a station newly enters `ESCALATED` from some other status: the installing plan's `escalated_station_count` rises by one, and the plan's `retreat_required` is then set true when `escalated_station_count` has reached the plan's escalation threshold, which is 5 percent of the plan's `target_station_count` raised to the next whole number — a threshold that, for any station count from 1 to 20, evaluates to a single escalated station. The third hop fires only when that `retreat_required` flag actually flipped: the bond written against that same plan, if it is `ACTIVE`, has both `coverage_watch` and `retreat_required` set true, marking it for mandatory review at its next renewal. Each of the three rows touched takes its own `version` increment.

Stations end their lives passively. When a bond terminates, as described in §3.8, every station of its plan still in `INSTALLED`, `ACTIVE_HIT` or `ESCALATED` moves to the terminal `REMOVED`. A station may be read individually, and the visit history of a station is listed back in creation order.

### 3.10. Damage Claims: Filing, Verification, Exclusion Arithmetic and Adjudication

A homeowner files a claim by supplying `bond_id`, a `claim_type` of either `REPAIR`, which seeks money for repairs, or `RETREAT`, which seeks re-treatment labour only, a `claimed_damage_usd` amount between 0.01 and 250000.00 with at most two decimal places, and the bond version expectation `expected_bond_version` from §2.7. The bond must be `ACTIVE`: `LAPSED` and `TERMINATED` bonds accept no new claims, and the refusal is `ERR_BOND_NOT_ACTIVE`. The clock's UTC date, which stamps `reported_at`, must also fall inside the bond's current coverage window — on or after `current_term_start` and on or before `current_term_end`, both inclusive — because damage reported outside the current term is not covered by it; a filing outside the window is `ERR_DAMAGE_OUTSIDE_TERM`. The claim is created in status `SUBMITTED` with `verifying_inspection_id` null, every money field at 0.00, `exclusion_basis`, `denial_reason_code` and `adjudicated_by_user_id` all null. Filing also raises the bond's `version` by one, which is what makes a second blind filing against the same bond fail its version check.

Nothing is priced until a graph verifies the damage. A `FieldInspector` attaches one by supplying `verifying_inspection_id`, which advances the claim `SUBMITTED → UNDER_ADJUDICATION`; attaching to a claim in any other status is `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`. The bond must still be `ACTIVE`; the graph must belong to the bond's structure (`ERR_PROPERTY_MISMATCH`) and be in status `GRAPHED` (`ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_GRAPHED`); its `purpose` may be anything, since damage can be discovered on any kind of visit. The graph's `inspector_id` must be the acting inspector, or the attempt is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, and it must **not** be the `applicator_id` of the plan the bond was written against, or the attempt is `ERR_VERIFIER_IS_APPLICATOR`. A bond may hold only one claim in `UNDER_ADJUDICATION` at a time, so a second verification while one claim still holds a reserve is `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_RESERVED`. And on a `REPAIR_AND_RETREAT` bond the `remaining_repair_cap_usd` must be strictly greater than 0.00, since an exhausted ceiling leaves nothing to reserve against; the refusal is `ERR_REPAIR_CAP_EXHAUSTED`. That headroom test does not apply to a `RETREAT_ONLY` bond, whose ceiling is 0.00 by construction.

Verification is where pre-existing damage is separated from new damage, and the method is chosen by the verifying graph's own lineage rather than by anybody's judgement. Only the graph's notations whose `finding_type` is `DAMAGE` participate; `CONDUCIVE_CONDITION`, `ACTIVE_INFESTATION` and `PREVIOUS_TREATMENT_EVIDENCE` notations are excluded from every sum, and no notation from any other graph participates, superseded or otherwise. When the verifying graph carries a `supersedes_inspection_id` — meaning it replaced an earlier graph, so a cell-by-cell baseline exists — the `exclusion_basis` recorded on the claim is `FINDING_LEVEL`. When it supersedes nothing, the basis is `AGGREGATE_PRORATION`.

Under `FINDING_LEVEL`, the whole dollar estimate of every cell marked `pre_existing` is excluded: the `damage_estimate_usd` values of the graph's `DAMAGE` notations whose `pre_existing` is false are summed, and `eligible_damage_usd` is the lesser of that sum and the claim's `claimed_damage_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Under `AGGREGATE_PRORATION`, no dollar figure is trusted and a severity-weighted proportion of the claim is excluded instead: the `severity` weights of the `DAMAGE` notations whose `pre_existing` is false are summed, the `severity` weights of **all** the graph's `DAMAGE` notations are summed, and `eligible_damage_usd` is `claimed_damage_usd` multiplied by the first sum divided by the second, carried at full precision and rounded half up to two decimal places exactly once. When the total severity sum is zero — no `DAMAGE` notations at all — `eligible_damage_usd` is 0.00 and no division is attempted. Either way, `excluded_pre_existing_usd` is `claimed_damage_usd` less `eligible_damage_usd`, rounded half up to two decimals. For identical findings the two bases produce genuinely different eligible amounts; the lineage of the graph decides which one governs.

The payout waterfall then runs, in this fixed order:

1. The eligible amount is the `eligible_damage_usd` just computed.
2. A deductible rate is selected from the **pre-deductible** eligible amount: 10 percent when `eligible_damage_usd` is below 2000.00; 7 percent when it is at least 2000.00 but below 10000.00; and 5 percent at 10000.00 and above. The tier is keyed on the eligible amount and is never re-keyed on the post-deductible or post-cap figure.
3. `deductible_usd` is `eligible_damage_usd` multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to two decimal places.
4. The net claim is `eligible_damage_usd` less `deductible_usd`, computed exactly with no rounding.
5. The cap headroom is the bond's `repair_liability_cap_usd` less its `claims_paid_to_date_usd` less its `reserved_claim_amount_usd` — the last of which excludes the claim now being priced — computed exactly with no rounding.
6. `projected_payout_usd` is the lesser of the net claim and the cap headroom, rounded half up to two decimal places, and then floored at 0.00 so that a negative headroom can never produce a negative reserve.

The claim then moves to `UNDER_ADJUDICATION` with `verifying_inspection_id`, `exclusion_basis`, `excluded_pre_existing_usd`, `eligible_damage_usd`, `deductible_usd` and `projected_payout_usd` all recorded, and the bond reserves the money: `reserved_claim_amount_usd` rises by `projected_payout_usd` and `remaining_repair_cap_usd` falls by exactly the same amount, leaving the conservation law of §2.8 satisfied and raising the bond's `version` by one.

Adjudication is a `ClaimsAdjudicator` decision on an `UNDER_ADJUDICATION` claim, supplied as `decision` with one of three values, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the bond's `issued_by_user_id`. The bond must still be `ACTIVE`. The three branches settle the reserve in three different ways, and each records `adjudicated_by_user_id` as the acting identifier and raises both the claim's and the bond's `version` by one:

- `APPROVE_REPAIR` moves the claim to `APPROVED_REPAIR` and pays. It requires the bond's `coverage_tier` to be `REPAIR_AND_RETREAT`, since a retreat-only bond cannot pay for repairs at all — the refusal is `ERR_TIER_EXCLUDES_REPAIR` — and it requires `projected_payout_usd` to be greater than 0.00, since a waterfall that produced nothing leaves nothing to approve; that refusal is `ERR_REPAIR_CAP_EXHAUSTED`. `approved_payout_usd` is set to the same figure the waterfall produced, recomputed identically at adjudication. On the bond, `claims_paid_to_date_usd` rises by that amount and `reserved_claim_amount_usd` falls by it, while `remaining_repair_cap_usd` is left exactly where it was — the money moves from reserved to paid, not from available to paid.
- `APPROVE_RETREAT_ONLY` moves the claim to `APPROVED_RETREAT_ONLY`, sets `approved_payout_usd` to 0.00, and releases the reserve: `reserved_claim_amount_usd` falls by `projected_payout_usd` and `remaining_repair_cap_usd` rises by exactly that amount, with `claims_paid_to_date_usd` untouched. No money leaves the bond, but an obligation is raised: the bond's `retreat_required` is set true and its `retreat_credit_count` rises by one. This branch is available whatever the coverage tier.
- `DENY` moves the claim to `DENIED`, sets `approved_payout_usd` to 0.00, and releases the reserve exactly as the retreat-only branch does — `reserved_claim_amount_usd` down, `remaining_repair_cap_usd` up by the same amount — but raises **no** retreat obligation and leaves `retreat_credit_count` alone. A denial must carry `denial_reason_code`, one of `PRE_EXISTING_DAMAGE`, `NO_ELIGIBLE_DAMAGE`, `BOND_LAPSED` or `OUTSIDE_COVERAGE`; omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A homeowner may withdraw their own claim, and the effect depends on where the claim stood. Withdrawing from `SUBMITTED` simply moves it to `WITHDRAWN` and moves no money, because nothing had been reserved before verification. Withdrawing from `UNDER_ADJUDICATION` also sets `approved_payout_usd` to 0.00 and releases precisely the amount that verification reserved: `reserved_claim_amount_usd` falls by `projected_payout_usd` and `remaining_repair_cap_usd` rises by the identical figure, so `remaining_repair_cap_usd` returns to its exact pre-verification value with zero drift — verification and withdrawal are a matched pair. Withdrawal by a homeowner who is not the bond's `property_id` is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, and withdrawing a claim that has already reached `APPROVED_REPAIR`, `APPROVED_RETREAT_ONLY`, `DENIED` or `WITHDRAWN` is `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`; all four of those, along with `WITHDRAWN` itself, are terminal.

One claim outcome arrives without an adjudicator: as described in §3.8, a bond's lapse denies every claim in `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_ADJUDICATION` with `denial_reason_code` set to `BOND_LAPSED`, releasing the reserve of any claim that held one. Any caller with a stake in the bond may read one claim, with its exclusion basis and full money breakdown, or list claims.
