# Seed Production & Conditioning API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Certified seed is not a commodity that can be recovered after the fact. Once two varieties have shared a combine hopper, once a field with a volunteer wheat history has been planted to a foundation-class increase, or once a bagged lot has been tagged against a germination test that has since lapsed, the identity of the seed is gone and no downstream paperwork can restore it. The seed program described here exists because that identity has to be defended at every hand-off — at the contracting table, in the field, on the combine, on the conditioning line, in the laboratory, and finally on the grower's payment statement. Every one of those hand-offs is an opportunity for varietal mixing or for a compliance claim that cannot be substantiated, and the current practice of tracking them on spreadsheets and clipboard count sheets has produced a steady trickle of lots that must be downgraded to commercial grade after conditioning has already been paid for.

This document specifies the API for a seed production and conditioning platform that carries a production contract from a draft acreage commitment through to a closed grower settlement. The platform contracts production acreage with growers under explicit isolation-distance and field-history restrictions; it records certification field inspections for varietal purity, restricted noxious weeds and seed-borne disease, and drives the rogueing follow-up loop when a field is salvageable; it coordinates harvest against machines that must be documented clean for the contracted variety; it runs the lot through cleaning, sizing, optional treating and bagging while preserving lot identity and reconciling every kilogram of shrink; it hosts the certification agency's sampling, germination and purity testing and its tag issuance; it manages carryover inventory with mandatory retesting before the seed may be offered in a following season; and it computes grower payment on clean seed units with moisture dockage, quality adjustments, conditioning charges and cash-advance recovery.

Two properties distinguish this system from an ordinary workflow tracker, and both are load-bearing throughout the specification. The first is **segregation of duties enforced on the acting user identity, not merely on role**: the field officer who recorded a failing inspection may not be the officer who signs off on the grower's rogueing, and the accountant who computed a settlement may not be the accountant who approves it. The second is **conservation**: seed mass, cash advance and tag counts are ledgers, and every operation that moves them — including every compensating reversal — must leave the ledger balanced to the cent, to the kilogram and to the bag. Where the specification pins an evaluation order, a rounding step or an interval-boundary convention, that pinning is deliberate and part of the contract.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

All resource endpoints described in this document are mounted under the `/seed-ops` prefix, with the single exception of the test-driving clock capability documented in §2.5.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user, and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that user is acting. If either header is absent, or if the value of `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs before every other check in the system, including request-shape parsing. Once the caller is authenticated, the role in `X-User-Role` must appear on the endpoint being addressed; a caller whose role is not authorized for that endpoint is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). The authorized roles for each endpoint are listed in §4.

The six roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `FieldOfficer`, `Grower`, `ConditioningManager`, `CertificationAgency` and `SettlementAccountant`.

A `ProgramAdmin` is the seed program administrator. This role owns all reference data — varieties, treatment products, growers, production fields, harvest machines and conditioning lines — drafts, offers and cancels production contracts, and drives the logical production clock. A `ProgramAdmin` may read every resource in the system but may not inspect fields, condition lots, issue certification tags or touch settlements. A `FieldOfficer` is a certification field officer who registers production fields and their crop history, records inspection count sheets and verifies grower rogueing. A `ConditioningManager` runs the conditioning plant: registering machines and lines, certifying equipment cleanout, recording harvest intake, planning, completing and reversing conditioning runs, and minting carryover lots. A `CertificationAgency` operator draws official samples, posts laboratory results and issues or voids certification tags, but cannot condition seed, contract acreage or settle money. A `SettlementAccountant` computes, approves and pays grower settlements. A `Grower` is the contracted seed grower, who signs offered contracts, declares rogueing work performed and disputes settlements.

Identity semantics differ by role, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `Grower`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Grower` entity; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Grower` compare the resource's grower foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, for the three grower-actor operations — signing an offered contract, filing a rogueing declaration on an inspection, and disputing a settlement — the platform resolves the `ProductionContract` that owns the target resource and requires that its `grower_id` equal `X-User-ID`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_GROWER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). For the remaining five roles — `ProgramAdmin`, `FieldOfficer`, `ConditioningManager`, `CertificationAgency` and `SettlementAccountant` — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any entity's `id`. The server does record it where a workflow demands an audit trail: a field inspection stores the recording officer's identifier as `inspector_user_id` and, later, the verifying officer's identifier as `rogueing_verified_by_user_id`; an official sample stores the drawing operator's identifier as `sampler_user_id`; and a settlement stores `computed_by_user_id` and, on approval, `approved_by_user_id`. These recorded identifiers are used for the segregation-of-duties comparisons described in §2.9 and §3, never for ownership resolution.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two pagination parameters. `limit` controls the page size; it defaults to 10 when absent, may not be smaller than 1 and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls how many records are skipped before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range of 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List responses order records by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any matching records remain beyond the current page.

Collections additionally accept optional equality filters, which combine conjunctively with each other and with pagination. Varieties may be filtered by `crop_kind`. Treatment products may be filtered by `restricted_use`. Growers may be filtered by `status` and `region_code`. Production fields may be filtered by `grower_id`. Field history records may be filtered by `production_field_id` and `crop_year`. Production contracts may be filtered by `grower_id`, `variety_id`, `production_field_id`, `status` and `season_year`. Field inspections may be filtered by `production_contract_id`, `inspector_user_id` and `inspected_on`. Harvest machines may be filtered by `last_cleanout_variety_id` and `status`. Harvest events may be filtered by `production_contract_id`, `harvest_equipment_id` and `seed_lot_id`. Conditioning lines may be filtered by `last_cleanout_variety_id` and `status`. Conditioning runs may be filtered by `seed_lot_id`, `conditioning_line_id`, `treatment_product_id`, `stage` and `status`. Seed lots may be filtered by `variety_id`, `production_contract_id`, `parent_seed_lot_id`, `conditioning_status`, `certification_status` and `season_year`. Official samples may be filtered by `seed_lot_id`, `sample_kind` and `result`. Certification tags may be filtered by `seed_lot_id`, `lot_sample_id` and `status`. Settlements may be filtered by `production_contract_id` and `status`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Columns and the Version Counter

Every resource the platform persists is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. The identifier carries no type prefix and is never supplied by the client. Alongside `id`, most resources carry three further common properties: `created_at`, the ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits at which the row was inserted; `updated_at`, the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row; and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on insertion and increments by exactly one on every mutation of the row.

Four resource kinds are deliberate exceptions because they are immutable or append-only records rather than living state. A `Variety`, a `TreatmentProduct`, a `FieldHistoryRecord` and a `HarvestEvent` each carry only `id` and `created_at`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from those four resources entirely — they are not present-and-null, they are not part of the response shape at all.

The `version` counter is more than an audit column: it is the coordination primitive described in §2.8, and it is also the observable signal that a cascade fired. When an operation on one resource mutates a second resource as a side effect, the second resource's `version` increments and its `updated_at` refreshes just as if it had been written directly. Section 3 states, for every operation, exactly which resources' versions move.

### 2.5. The Logical Production Clock

Every deadline, window-elapsed check, germination-validity comparison and retest-due comparison in this specification reads a single server-side logical UTC clock rather than real wall-clock time. Before any client touches it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time under any circumstance.

A `ProgramAdmin` may reposition the clock through the administrative test-driving capability `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, supplying the target instant as `current_utc`. The response echoes `{ current_utc }`. This endpoint is the one exception to the `/seed-ops` mount prefix. Every temporal rule in §2 and §3 that speaks of "the current instant" or "the current date" means the value of this logical clock at the moment the request is evaluated.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Referential Integrity Order

Nested paths are resolved strictly left to right. For a request addressed at a path that names more than one resource, the leftmost path parameter is resolved first and, if it does not resolve to an existing row, the request fails immediately with that resource's own not-found error before any deeper parameter is examined. The path parameters and their errors are: `variety_id` yields `ERR_VARIETY_NOT_FOUND`; `treatment_product_id` yields `ERR_TREATMENT_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`; `grower_id` yields `ERR_GROWER_NOT_FOUND`; `production_field_id` yields `ERR_PRODUCTION_FIELD_NOT_FOUND`; `field_history_record_id` yields `ERR_FIELD_HISTORY_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`; `production_contract_id` yields `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`; `field_inspection_id` yields `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`; `harvest_equipment_id` yields `ERR_EQUIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`; `harvest_event_id` yields `ERR_HARVEST_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`; `conditioning_line_id` yields `ERR_LINE_NOT_FOUND`; `seed_lot_id` yields `ERR_SEED_LOT_NOT_FOUND`; `conditioning_run_id` yields `ERR_RUN_NOT_FOUND`; `lot_sample_id` yields `ERR_SAMPLE_NOT_FOUND`; `certification_tag_id` yields `ERR_TAG_NOT_FOUND`; and `settlement_id` yields `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_FOUND`. All fifteen carry HTTP 404.

Foreign keys supplied inside a request body are resolved after every path parameter has resolved, and they are resolved in the order in which the endpoint's input fields are listed in this document, again returning the corresponding not-found error for the first unresolvable reference. This body-level resolution applies to field registration, contract drafting, inspection recording, equipment cleanout certification, harvest recording, conditioning-run planning, sample drawing, tag issuance, settlement computation, and the standalone field eligibility assessment.

### 2.7. Measurement Precision, Rounding and the Clean Seed Unit

The platform's single commercial unit of account is the **clean seed unit**, which is exactly 25.0 kilograms of clean seed. Every price in the system is quoted per clean seed unit and every monetary field is an integer count of cents; no monetary value is ever expressed as a fraction of a cent.

Unless a rule says otherwise, every rounding step in this specification is round-half-up: a value exactly halfway between two representable results rounds away from zero. Clean mass figures in kilograms are rounded to three decimal places. Clean seed unit figures are rounded to two decimal places. Inspection rates, incidence percentages, dockage percentages and contamination risk figures are rounded to two decimal places. Monetary results are rounded to whole cents. Bag counts are whole numbers obtained by truncation toward zero — that is, by discarding any fractional bag rather than rounding it. Two mass fields on a seed lot, its presently held mass and its cumulative shrink, are carried exactly at two decimal places of kilograms and no rounding is applied to them; only the derived dockage percentage is rounded.

Where a threshold comparison appears, its inclusivity is stated explicitly and is part of the contract. Where an evaluation order is pinned — most consequentially in the settlement waterfall of §3.9 and the per-line rounding of the settlement rollup — computing the same quantities in a different order is a defect even when the arithmetic looks equivalent, because the results can differ by cents.

### 2.8. Version Pinning on Settlement Computation and Carryover Minting

Two write operations in this system are protected by optimistic concurrency control, because in both cases the operation is legitimately repeatable and therefore no state precondition can distinguish a deliberate second call from a call built on a stale read.

A settlement computation request — `POST /seed-ops/settlements` — must carry `expected_contract_version` as a required, non-optional input field, holding the `version` of the target `ProductionContract` as the accountant last observed it. If that value does not equal the contract's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This matters because computing a settlement legitimately supersedes any earlier settlement of the same contract that is still in `COMPUTED` or `DISPUTED` state, so the create is not blocked by any state check; the version pin is the only thing that prevents an accountant from superseding a settlement with figures derived from an out-of-date view of the contract's tagged lots and outstanding advance.

A carryover minting request — `POST /seed-ops/seed-lots/{seed_lot_id}/carryover` — must likewise carry `expected_seed_lot_version` as a required, non-optional input field, holding the `version` of the parent `SeedLot` as the manager last observed it. A mismatch against the parent lot's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A parent lot may legitimately carry several successors across seasons, so a repeated carryover call satisfies every state precondition; the version pin is what stops a successor from being derived from an out-of-date view of the parent's mass and germination validity.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field of any kind. Absence of a version field on an endpoint is not an oversight — those endpoints are not version-checked.

### 2.9. Rule Evaluation Order

Because many operations in this domain are guarded by a dozen or more checks, the platform evaluates them in a single fixed priority ladder and returns the error of the first failing check. Two requests that violate several rules at once must therefore produce a deterministic, predictable error, and a mid-flight condition can never be masked by a later check. The ladder, from highest precedence to lowest, is:

1. Header presence and role validity — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Endpoint role authorization — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. Request shape: every required input field present, JSON types correct, enum members valid, single-field numeric ranges honoured, and pagination bounds respected — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Cross-field arithmetic checks such as component percentage sums, mass equality and bag totals are explicitly **not** part of this rule and fire later at their own priorities.
4. Existence of every path-templated resource, left to right — the fifteen not-found errors of §2.6.
5. Existence of every foreign key supplied in the request body, in the order the endpoint's inputs are listed — the corresponding not-found errors.
6. Grower scope: for the three grower-actor operations, `X-User-ID` must equal the `grower_id` of the owning contract — `ERR_GROWER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
7. Contract state permits the operation — `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATE`. This guards contract offer, contract signing, contract cancellation, inspection recording, harvest recording and settlement computation.
8. Inspection state permits the requested rogueing step — `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_STATE`. For a declaration, the inspection must be in `RECORDED` status with outcome `ROGUEING_ORDERED`. For a verification, the outcome must be `ROGUEING_ORDERED` and the status must not already be `ROGUEING_VERIFIED`. Note that a verification attempted while the inspection is still in `RECORDED` status — that is, with no declaration on file — passes this rule and is instead caught at priority 26.
9. Conditioning run state permits the operation: `PLANNED` for completion, and either `PLANNED` or `COMPLETED` for reversal — `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`.
10. Settlement state permits the operation: `COMPUTED` for approval, `APPROVED` for payment, and either `COMPUTED` or `APPROVED` for dispute — `ERR_INVALID_SETTLEMENT_STATE`.
11. The official sample has not yet been resulted — `ERR_SAMPLE_ALREADY_RESULTED`.
12. The certification tag is still `ISSUED` — `ERR_TAG_ALREADY_VOIDED`.
13. The seed lot's two status axes permit the operation — `ERR_INVALID_LOT_STATE`. Sampling requires a `BAGGED` conditioning status together with an `UNSAMPLED` certification status for an `INITIAL` sample or a certification status of either `RETEST_DUE` or `TAGGED` for a `RETEST` sample; carryover requires the parent lot to be `TAGGED`; and planning a new conditioning run requires the lot not to be `BAGGED` already.
14. `expected_contract_version` matches the contract's current `version` — `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION`.
15. `expected_seed_lot_version` matches the parent lot's current `version` — `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION`.
16. Rogueing self-verification: the verifying officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the inspection's `inspector_user_id` — `ERR_SOD_ROGUEING_SELF_VERIFICATION`.
17. Settlement self-approval: the approving accountant's `X-User-ID` must differ from the settlement's `computed_by_user_id` — `ERR_SOD_SETTLEMENT_SELF_APPROVAL`.
18. Treatment field presence symmetry on run planning — `ERR_TREATMENT_FIELDS_NOT_ALLOWED`.
19. Treatment product labeled for the lot's crop — `ERR_TREATMENT_CROP_NOT_LABELED`.
20. Treatment rate within the label maximum — `ERR_TREATMENT_RATE_EXCEEDS_LABEL`.
21. Treated-seed label text complete — `ERR_TREATMENT_LABEL_INCOMPLETE`.
22. Harvest machine documented clean for the contract's variety — `ERR_EQUIPMENT_CLEANOUT_REQUIRED`.
23. No restricted prior crop under the applied eligibility variant — `ERR_FIELD_HISTORY_RESTRICTED`.
24. Field isolation distance meets the variety minimum — `ERR_ISOLATION_DISTANCE_INSUFFICIENT`.
25. Grower standing is not `SUSPENDED` — `ERR_GROWER_SUSPENDED`.
26. A rogueing declaration is on file before verification is accepted — `ERR_ROGUEING_NOT_DECLARED`.
27. The referenced sample belongs to the tagged lot and passed — `ERR_SAMPLE_LOT_MISMATCH`.
28. The lot is tag-eligible, meaning both `BAGGED` and `PASSED` — `ERR_LOT_NOT_TAG_ELIGIBLE`.
29. The settlement rollup produced at least one line — `ERR_NO_SETTLEABLE_LOTS`.
30. The requested stage is the immediate successor of the lot's conditioning status — `ERR_STAGE_OUT_OF_ORDER`.
31. The run's declared input mass equals the lot's current mass — `ERR_INPUT_MASS_MISMATCH`.
32. Output mass is within the run's input mass — `ERR_OUTPUT_EXCEEDS_INPUT`.
33. Reported component percentages do not exceed a whole sample — `ERR_COMPONENT_PCT_SUM_INVALID`.
34. Requested tag bag count fits the lot's untagged bags — `ERR_TAG_BAG_COUNT_EXCEEDS_LOT`.
35. Requested tag class does not outrank the variety's ceiling — `ERR_TAG_CLASS_EXCEEDS_VARIETY`.
36. Carryover generation limit respected — `ERR_CARRYOVER_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.
37. The completed run being reversed is the latest completed run on its lot — `ERR_RUN_NOT_LATEST`.
38. The lot is not tagged when its conditioning history would be altered — `ERR_LOT_TAGGED_IMMUTABLE`.
39. Contracted acres fit within the field — `ERR_CONTRACTED_ACRES_EXCEED_FIELD`.
40. Window start strictly precedes window end — `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`.
41. Backward-looking dates are not in the future — `ERR_DATE_IN_FUTURE`.
42. Harvest machine window does not collide — `ERR_EQUIPMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.
43. Conditioning line window does not collide — `ERR_LINE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.
44. Inter-variety cleanout buffer on the line has elapsed — `ERR_LINE_CLEANOUT_BUFFER_VIOLATION`.
45. Inspector daily capacity not exceeded — `ERR_INSPECTOR_DAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
46. Field-season exclusivity respected — `ERR_FIELD_SEASON_ALREADY_CONTRACTED`.
47. Harvest window has already elapsed — `ERR_HARVEST_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`.
48. Run window has already elapsed — `ERR_RUN_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`.
49. Germination validity has not lapsed at tag issuance — `ERR_GERMINATION_VALIDITY_EXPIRED`.
50. Germination validity has lapsed at carryover — `ERR_RETEST_NOT_DUE`.
51. The settlement's provisional net is not negative — `ERR_NET_PAYABLE_NEGATIVE`.

The first two rules and the request-shape rule apply to every endpoint in the system. The two existence rules apply to every endpoint carrying path parameters and to every endpoint carrying body foreign keys respectively. All remaining rules apply only to the operations named alongside them here and in §3.

### 2.10. Occupation Windows and Interval Boundary Conventions

Two kinds of physical asset in this system are occupied for a span of time rather than at an instant: harvest machines and conditioning lines. Both take a `window_start` and a `window_end`, and in both cases the start must be strictly earlier than the end — a zero-length or inverted window is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). Beyond that shared rule, the two assets use deliberately different boundary conventions, and the difference is part of the contract rather than an accident of implementation.

A harvest machine window is a **closed interval**: both endpoints belong to the interval. A requested harvest window conflicts with an existing harvest window on the same machine whenever the requested start is at or before the existing end and the existing start is at or before the requested end. Two windows that merely touch at a single instant therefore *do* collide, and no buffer notion applies to machines beyond the cleanout certification described in §3.4. The collision is reported as `ERR_EQUIPMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).

A conditioning line window is a **half-open interval**: the start belongs to the interval and the end does not. A requested run window conflicts with an existing run on the same line whenever the requested start is strictly before the existing end and the existing start is strictly before the requested end. Back-to-back windows that touch at a single instant therefore do *not* collide. Only runs in `PLANNED` or `COMPLETED` status occupy a line; runs in `ABORTED` or `REVERSED` status have released their window and are ignored by the conflict scan. The collision is reported as `ERR_LINE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).

A field inspection occupies neither an instant nor a window but a calendar date, and its capacity rule is an aggregate rather than an overlap: at most two field inspections may share the same `inspector_user_id` and the same `inspected_on` calendar date. Inspections that share a date may otherwise coexist freely. A third inspection on the same officer and the same date is rejected with `ERR_INSPECTOR_DAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).

### 2.11. Conserved Quantities: Mass, Bags and Advance

Three ledgers in this system carry an invariant that must hold after every single mutation, including after every compensating reversal. These invariants are the reason the platform exists rather than a spreadsheet, and they are asserted at the end of every operation described in §3.

The **lot mass ledger** conserves the mass that entered conditioning. For every seed lot, the mass entering conditioning must always equal the mass presently held plus the cumulative shrink recorded against the lot. Completing a conditioning run moves mass from held to shrink; reversing a completed run moves exactly the same quantity back, restoring the identity with zero drift. Only runs in `COMPLETED` status contribute to cumulative shrink — runs in `PLANNED`, `ABORTED` or `REVERSED` status contribute nothing.

The **tag bag ledger** conserves bag counts. For every seed lot, the tagged bag count must always equal the sum of the bag counts of that lot's tags whose status is `ISSUED`, summed in `created_at` ascending order; tags in `VOIDED` status contribute zero. The lot's untagged bags are its bag count minus its tagged bag count, and that difference must never be allowed to become negative.

The **advance ledger** conserves the pre-season cash advance. For every production contract, the advance still outstanding plus the sum of advance recoveries booked by that contract's settlements in `APPROVED` or `PAID` status — summed in `created_at` ascending order — must always equal the total advance originally extended. Settlements in `COMPUTED`, `DISPUTED` or `SUPERSEDED` status contribute zero to that sum. Approving a settlement books its recovery; disputing a settlement that had already been approved returns exactly the same amount. All three ledger quantities are integer cents and are never rounded during these movements.

### 2.12. Cascades, Deterministic Ordering and Derived-Field Reconciliation

Several operations in this system propagate through more than one hop, and consistency is immediate: by the time a request returns, every hop of its cascade has been applied and every affected resource's `version` has moved. Two cascades in particular reach four hops beyond the resource the client addressed — the grower-standing cascade described in §3.3 and the failed-retest cascade described in §3.7 — and both are specified hop by hop in those subsections.

Wherever a cascade touches a set of sibling rows rather than a single row, the rows are processed in `created_at` ascending order. This applies to the closing of a cancelled contract's open inspections, to the flagging of a grower's other draft and offered contracts when standing degrades, to the voiding of a lot's issued tags, and to the superseding of a contract's computed settlements. Deterministic ordering matters because each row's mutation is observable through its own `version`.

Derived roll-up fields are reconciled rather than incrementally patched wherever the scaffold of the business rule is a sum over a filtered set. A contract's certified unit total is a roll-up over that contract's lots whose certification status is `TAGGED`; a lot's tagged bag count is a roll-up over its issued tags; a field's history record count is a roll-up over its history records. When a cascade invalidates a member of one of those sets, the roll-up is recomputed from the surviving members, not adjusted by guesswork.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Reference Data, Grower Onboarding and Field Registry

Before any acreage can be contracted, a `ProgramAdmin` must load the varietal reference data that every downstream tolerance check reads. Registering a variety records its `crop_kind` — one of `WHEAT`, `TRITICALE`, `SOYBEAN`, `SUNFLOWER`, `ALFALFA` or `CANOLA` — together with its `variety_name` and the highest class it may ever be certified as, `max_seed_class`, which is one of `FOUNDATION`, `REGISTERED` or `CERTIFIED`. The agronomic guard rails follow: `isolation_min_m` is the minimum distance in metres that must separate a production field from the nearest incompatible pollen source, while `restricted_prior_crop_kinds` lists the crop kinds that disqualify a field when they were grown on it recently and `restricted_prior_crop_years` gives the look-back window in crop years, which must be at least 1.

The certification tolerances are recorded on the same payload. `germination_min_pct` and `purity_min_pct` are the floors a laboratory result must clear for the lot to be certifiable at all. `max_off_type_per_10000` is the greatest tolerated number of off-type plants per ten thousand plants inspected, and `max_restricted_weed_per_10000` the equivalent tolerance for restricted noxious weed plants. `max_disease_incidence_pct` caps seed-borne disease incidence. `max_rogueing_rounds` bounds how many rogueing rounds a contract may consume before the field is written off, and defaults to 2 when the caller omits it. `estimated_conditioning_shrink_pct` is the book shrink percentage the provisional harvest accounting deducts before any real conditioning has happened. `germ_validity_days` is the number of whole days a germination result stays valid before a retest becomes mandatory, and `max_carryover_generations` bounds how deep a lot lineage may be carried across seasons, defaulting to 2. Finally, `treatment_required` declares whether a treating stage is mandatory before bagging for this variety; it defaults to false.

Seed treatment products are registered on their own reference table because treated seed is a regulated article. A treatment product carries a `product_code`, which must be unique across the registry, and the `epa_registration_number` that must be printed on any treated-seed label. `allowed_crop_kinds` lists the crop kinds the product is labeled for. `max_rate_g_per_100kg` is the label maximum application rate in grams per hundred kilograms of seed. Two boolean flags shape the label text the plant must produce: `requires_dye`, which defaults to false and forces an explicit dye disclosure, and `restricted_use`, which also defaults to false and forces the product's `label_signal_word` — one of `CAUTION`, `WARNING` or `DANGER` — to appear verbatim in the label text. Neither varieties nor treatment products are ever mutated after registration.

Registering a contract grower captures `grower_name`, `region_code` and `contact_email`. Two further properties are computed by the platform and never supplied by the caller: `status`, the grower's compliance standing, which is one of `GOOD_STANDING`, `WATCH` or `SUSPENDED` and starts at `GOOD_STANDING`; and `compliance_flag_count`, which counts the number of times a contract of this grower has entered a rogueing-required or rejected condition and starts at 0.

A production field belongs to exactly one grower, so registering a field — a capability a `ProgramAdmin` and a `FieldOfficer` share — carries the owning `grower_id` in the payload alongside `field_code`, a grower-scoped identifier that must be unique among that grower's fields, the tillable `acres`, and `isolation_distance_m`, the surveyed distance in metres to the nearest incompatible pollen source. Two computed properties round out the field: `last_assessed_risk_score`, which holds the contamination risk score of the field's most recent eligibility assessment and is null until the first assessment has run, and `history_record_count`, which counts the field's attached cropping-history rows and starts at 0.

Crop history is append-only. Attaching a history record to a field records the `crop_kind` grown and the `crop_year` in which it was grown; the year must fall between 1990 and 2100 inclusive, and a field may not carry two records for the same crop year. An optional `source_note` records the provenance of the claim and defaults to `GROWER_DECLARED` when the caller omits it. Each appended record increments the owning field's `history_record_count` by 1. A history record is immutable once written and carries no status of its own, which is why the eligibility calculation described next reads every record of the field without any status filter.

### 3.2. Field History, Contamination Risk and the Contract Offer Gate

Drafting a production contract is a bookkeeping act; offering it to the grower is the compliance gate. A `ProgramAdmin` drafts a contract by binding three independent parents together in one payload — the `grower_id` of the contracted grower, the `variety_id` of the variety under production, and the `production_field_id` of the field dedicated to this contract — together with the `season_year`, which must fall between 2000 and 2100 inclusive, and the `seed_class` being produced, which is one of `FOUNDATION`, `REGISTERED`, `CERTIFIED` or `COMMERCIAL_INCREASE`. Note that the contract's class enumeration is deliberately wider than the variety's class ceiling: `COMMERCIAL_INCREASE` is a contract class, not a certifiable tag class. The commercial terms follow: `contracted_acres` must be greater than zero and may not exceed the field's `acres`, or the draft is rejected with `ERR_CONTRACTED_ACRES_EXCEED_FIELD`; `base_price_per_unit_cents` must be greater than zero; `conditioning_charge_per_unit_cents`, the charge deducted per clean seed unit, may be zero but not negative; and `advance_total_cents`, the pre-season cash advance extended to the grower, may likewise be zero but not negative. A freshly drafted contract lands in `DRAFT` status with `advance_outstanding_cents` initialised to the full `advance_total_cents`, `inspection_round` and `rogueing_rounds_used` at 0, `provisional_clean_units_total` and `certified_units_total` at 0, `requires_field_officer_review` false, and `eligibility_variant_applied`, `eligibility_risk_score`, `planted_on` and `cancel_reason` all null.

The eligibility question the offer gate answers is whether this field may legally and safely grow this variety in this season. Both a `ProgramAdmin` and a `FieldOfficer` may also ask that question directly, without touching contract state, through a computed read-through assessment that names a candidate `variety_id`, `season_year` and `seed_class` against a field. That assessment begins by selecting the field's **restricted history records**: every history record of the field whose `crop_kind` appears in the variety's `restricted_prior_crop_kinds` and whose `crop_year` is strictly greater than the season year minus the variety's `restricted_prior_crop_years`. The strictness of that comparison is the whole rule: with a three-year look-back and a season year of 2026, a crop grown in 2023 falls outside the window and a crop grown in 2024 falls inside it. Records whose crop kind is not on the variety's restricted list are excluded entirely. The assessment then computes the **isolation shortfall in metres** as the variety's `isolation_min_m` minus the field's `isolation_distance_m`, floored at zero so that a field with surplus isolation reports a shortfall of exactly 0.

Two variants then evaluate the same inputs to potentially different verdicts, and the assessment reports both. Under the **strict history** variant, the field is ineligible if it carries even one restricted record inside the look-back window, no matter how long ago that crop was grown; if there is no restricted record but the isolation shortfall is greater than zero, the field is also ineligible; otherwise it is eligible. The strict variant's blocking error is `ERR_FIELD_HISTORY_RESTRICTED` whenever at least one restricted record exists, and `ERR_ISOLATION_DISTANCE_INSUFFICIENT` when there is no restricted record but the shortfall is positive — history is examined before isolation, so a field failing both reports the history error. When the strict verdict is eligible, there is no blocking error.

Under the **decay weighted** variant, an old transgression is forgiven gradually rather than absolutely. Each restricted record contributes a history component of 100.0 multiplied by one half raised to the power of the number of elapsed years, where the elapsed years are the season year minus that record's crop year, and each record's contribution is rounded half-up to two decimal places before it is added in. A record from the season year itself therefore contributes the full 100.00, a record one year old contributes 50.00, a record two years old contributes 25.00, and so on. The history component is the sum of those per-record contributions. The isolation component is the isolation shortfall in metres multiplied by 0.5 risk points per metre, rounded half-up to two decimal places. The **contamination risk score** is the sum of the history component and the isolation component, again rounded half-up to two decimal places. Under this variant the field is eligible while the contamination risk score is at or below 25.00, and ineligible once it rises above 25.00. When the decay-weighted verdict is ineligible, the blocking error is `ERR_FIELD_HISTORY_RESTRICTED` if the history component is strictly greater than the isolation component, and `ERR_ISOLATION_DISTANCE_INSUFFICIENT` otherwise; when the verdict is eligible there is no blocking error. Because halving forgives distant history, the very same field, variety and season data can be eligible under the decay-weighted variant while being ineligible under the strict variant.

Which variant actually binds is selected by the contract's `seed_class`: the strict history variant applies when the class is `FOUNDATION`, `REGISTERED` or `CERTIFIED`, and the decay weighted variant applies when the class is `COMMERCIAL_INCREASE`. The read-through assessment response makes all of this explicit, returning `strict_history_result` and `decay_weighted_result` as the two variant verdicts, `restricted_record_count` as the number of restricted history records selected, `history_component`, `isolation_component` and `contamination_risk_score` as the decay-weighted intermediates, `isolation_shortfall_m` as the shortfall in metres, `applied_variant` as the variant the supplied class selects, `applied_result` as that variant's verdict, and `blocking_error_id` as the error the applied variant would raise, or null when the applied verdict is eligible. The assessment does not mutate any contract, but it is not entirely side-effect free: it writes the computed contamination risk score onto the field's `last_assessed_risk_score` and increments the field's `version` by 1.

Offering a drafted contract, which is a `ProgramAdmin` operation, advances the contract `DRAFT → OFFERED` only after five gates pass in the priority order of §2.9. The grower's standing must not be `SUSPENDED`, or the offer is refused with `ERR_GROWER_SUSPENDED`; standings of `GOOD_STANDING` and `WATCH` both permit an offer. The field's `isolation_distance_m` must be at least the variety's `isolation_min_m`, or the offer is refused with `ERR_ISOLATION_DISTANCE_INSUFFICIENT`. The eligibility assessment under the variant selected by the contract's `seed_class` must return an eligible verdict, and when it does not the offer is refused with the variant's own blocking error — `ERR_FIELD_HISTORY_RESTRICTED` when a restricted prior crop is the cause. And no other contract may already occupy the same `production_field_id` for the same `season_year`: an occupying contract is one whose status is any of `OFFERED`, `PLANTED`, `ROGUEING_REQUIRED`, `INSPECTION_PASSED`, `HARVESTED`, `SETTLED` or `CLOSED`, and a collision is refused with `ERR_FIELD_SEASON_ALREADY_CONTRACTED`. Contracts in `DRAFT`, `CANCELLED` or `REJECTED` status do not occupy a field. The contract must itself be in `DRAFT` status; an offer attempted from any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATE`.

A successful offer records what it decided. It sets `eligibility_variant_applied` to `STRICT_HISTORY` when the contract's class is `FOUNDATION`, `REGISTERED` or `CERTIFIED` and to `DECAY_WEIGHTED` when the class is `COMMERCIAL_INCREASE`; it sets `eligibility_risk_score` to the contamination risk score the assessment produced; it writes that same score onto the field's `last_assessed_risk_score` and increments the field's `version` by 1; and it increments the contract's own `version` by 1.

Cancellation is available to a `ProgramAdmin` at any point before the crop has been harvested. A cancellation carries a `cancel_reason` and is accepted only while the contract's status is `DRAFT`, `OFFERED`, `PLANTED` or `ROGUEING_REQUIRED`; from any other status, including `INSPECTION_PASSED` and every terminal status, it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATE`. Cancelling stores the reason, moves the contract to `CANCELLED`, and closes the trailing paperwork: every field inspection of that contract whose status is `RECORDED` or `ROGUEING_DECLARED` moves to `CLOSED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. The contract's `version` increments by 1. `CANCELLED` is terminal, as are `REJECTED` and `CLOSED`.

### 3.3. Signing, the Inspection Cycle, Rogueing and Grower Standing

A `Grower` accepts an offered contract by signing it, which advances the contract `OFFERED → PLANTED` and records `planted_on`, the ISO 8601 date on which the field was actually planted. The signing grower's `X-User-ID` must equal the contract's `grower_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_GROWER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. The planting date may not be in the future relative to the logical clock: a `planted_on` after the current logical clock date is refused with `ERR_DATE_IN_FUTURE`. Signing a contract that is not in `OFFERED` status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATE`. The contract's `version` increments by 1.

Once a contract is `PLANTED`, a `FieldOfficer` may walk the field and record a count sheet. The count sheet names the `production_contract_id` being inspected, the `inspected_on` date of the walk, the number of plants counted as `sampled_plants` (which must be greater than zero), and four observations: `off_type_plants`, the count of varietally impure plants; `restricted_weed_plants`, the count of restricted noxious weed plants; `diseased_plants`, the count showing seed-borne disease — each of these three may be zero but not negative — and `isolation_verified_m`, the isolation distance re-measured during the walk, in metres. The recording officer's `X-User-ID` is stored on the inspection as `inspector_user_id`, and the inspection is stamped with an `inspection_round` equal to the contract's current `inspection_round` plus one, so the first walk of a contract is round 1. Recording an inspection against a contract in any status other than `PLANTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATE`; an `inspected_on` after the current logical clock date is refused with `ERR_DATE_IN_FUTURE`; an `inspected_on` earlier than the contract's `planted_on` is not a valid field walk and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; and a third walk by the same officer on the same calendar date is refused with `ERR_INSPECTOR_DAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` as described in §2.10. A newly recorded inspection lands in `RECORDED` status with `rogueing_plants_removed`, `rogueing_declared_on`, `rogueing_verified_by_user_id` and `verified_remaining_off_type_plants` all null.

From the raw counts the platform derives three rates, each rounded half-up to two decimal places. `off_type_rate_per_10000` is the off-type plant count divided by the sampled plant count, multiplied by ten thousand. `weed_rate_per_10000` is the restricted weed plant count divided by the sampled plant count, multiplied by ten thousand. `disease_incidence_pct` is the diseased plant count divided by the sampled plant count, multiplied by one hundred. Each rate is compared only against the variety's own tolerance, and every one of those comparisons is strictly greater-than: a rate exactly equal to the tolerance passes.

The `outcome` of the walk — one of `PASSED`, `ROGUEING_ORDERED` or `FAILED` — is then determined by a single ordered decision, and the ordering matters because a field can breach several tolerances at once. A **purity breach** exists when the off-type rate is strictly greater than the variety's `max_off_type_per_10000`; a **weed breach** exists when the weed rate is strictly greater than the variety's `max_restricted_weed_per_10000`. If either breach is present, the platform computes a **worst ratio** as the larger of two quotients: the off-type rate divided by the off-type tolerance, and the weed rate divided by the weed tolerance. Both quotients are formed regardless of which of the two tolerances was actually breached. When that worst ratio is at or below three times the tolerance *and* the contract's `rogueing_rounds_used` is strictly less than the variety's `max_rogueing_rounds`, the field is judged salvageable by hand-rogueing and the outcome is `ROGUEING_ORDERED`; otherwise the outcome is `FAILED`. If neither a purity nor a weed breach is present, the platform next checks disease: a `disease_incidence_pct` strictly greater than the variety's `max_disease_incidence_pct` yields `FAILED`. If disease is within tolerance, the platform checks isolation: an `isolation_verified_m` strictly below the variety's `isolation_min_m` yields `FAILED`. Only when all four checks are satisfied is the outcome `PASSED`. The determination reads exactly the counts on the count sheet being recorded, the contract's variety tolerances, and the contract's `rogueing_rounds_used` as it stands at request time; earlier inspections of the same contract are never re-read.

The three outcome branches have deliberately disjoint side-effect sets, and all three are reachable from the single act of recording a count sheet. On the `PASSED` branch the contract advances `PLANTED → INSPECTION_PASSED`, the inspection remains in `RECORDED` status, the contract's `inspection_round` increments by 1, the contract's `version` increments by 1, and no compliance flag is raised against the grower. On the `ROGUEING_ORDERED` branch the contract moves `PLANTED → ROGUEING_REQUIRED`, the contract's `inspection_round` increments by 1 *and* its `rogueing_rounds_used` increments by 1, the grower-standing cascade described below runs, the inspection stays in `RECORDED` status awaiting the grower's declaration, and the contract's `version` increments by 1. On the `FAILED` branch the contract moves `PLANTED → REJECTED`, the contract's `inspection_round` increments by 1, the grower-standing cascade runs, the inspection moves directly to `CLOSED`, and the contract's `version` increments by 1. `REJECTED` is terminal: no harvest event may ever be recorded against a rejected contract.

The **grower-standing cascade** is the system's four-hop compliance propagation, and it fires on every system transition that moves a contract into `ROGUEING_REQUIRED` or `REJECTED`. On the first hop the offending contract's status changes. On the second hop, because the contract entered one of those two conditions, the grower's `compliance_flag_count` increments by 1 and the grower's `version` increments by 1. On the third hop, because the counter moved, the grower's `status` is re-derived from the counter alone: a count of at least 4 sets the standing to `SUSPENDED`, otherwise a count of at least 2 sets it to `WATCH`, and otherwise it remains `GOOD_STANDING`. Both thresholds are inclusive, the counter is monotonically increasing, and it is never decremented — a rogueing round that is later verified successfully does not clear the flag it raised. On the fourth hop, because the grower's standing became `WATCH` or `SUSPENDED`, every *other* production contract of that same grower whose status is `DRAFT` or `OFFERED` has `requires_field_officer_review` set to true and its `version` incremented by 1, processed in `created_at` ascending order. The re-derivation reads only the single grower's own counter; contract rows are never re-scanned to recount it.

After a rogueing order, the ball is in the grower's court. The `Grower` files a rogueing declaration against the inspection, reporting `rogueing_plants_removed`, the number of plants pulled out of the field, and `rogueing_declared_on`, the ISO 8601 date on which the work was finished. A declaration is accepted only when the inspection's `outcome` is `ROGUEING_ORDERED` and its `status` is still `RECORDED`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_STATE`. The declaring grower's `X-User-ID` must equal the owning contract's `grower_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_GROWER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, and a `rogueing_declared_on` after the current logical clock date is refused with `ERR_DATE_IN_FUTURE`. A successful declaration stores both values, moves the inspection `RECORDED → ROGUEING_DECLARED`, and increments the inspection's `version` by 1.

Verification closes the loop, and it is where segregation of duties bites. A `FieldOfficer` re-walks the field and reports `verified_remaining_off_type_plants`, the number of off-type plants still standing. The verifying officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the inspection's `inspector_user_id`; a self-verification is refused with `ERR_SOD_ROGUEING_SELF_VERIFICATION`. A verification attempted against an inspection whose `outcome` is not `ROGUEING_ORDERED`, or whose `status` is already `ROGUEING_VERIFIED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_STATE`; a verification attempted while the inspection is still in `RECORDED` status, meaning the grower has filed no declaration, is refused with `ERR_ROGUEING_NOT_DECLARED` instead. The verified remaining off-type rate is computed exactly as the original rate was — the remaining off-type plant count divided by the inspection's `sampled_plants`, multiplied by ten thousand, rounded half-up to two decimal places — and compared against the variety's `max_off_type_per_10000`.

Verification forks, and the two branches are symmetric in their bookkeeping. When the remaining rate is at or below the variety's off-type tolerance *and* the contract's `rogueing_rounds_used` has not exceeded the variety's `max_rogueing_rounds`, the rogueing is accepted: the inspection moves `ROGUEING_DECLARED → ROGUEING_VERIFIED`, `rogueing_verified_by_user_id` is set from the verifying officer's `X-User-ID`, the contract returns `ROGUEING_REQUIRED → PLANTED` so that a further inspection round may be recorded, the inspection's `version` increments by 1, and the contract's `version` increments by 1. This edge is what closes the cyclic `PLANTED → ROGUEING_REQUIRED → PLANTED` loop that lets a field be given a second chance. When instead the remaining rate is strictly above the tolerance, or the contract's `rogueing_rounds_used` is strictly greater than the variety's `max_rogueing_rounds`, the rogueing is rejected: the inspection moves `ROGUEING_DECLARED → CLOSED`, the contract moves `ROGUEING_REQUIRED → REJECTED`, the grower-standing cascade runs in full, the inspection's `version` increments by 1, and the contract's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.4. Harvest Coordination and Dedicated Equipment Cleanout

The single most expensive mistake in seed production is cutting a certified field with a machine that still holds another variety in its hopper. The platform therefore models cleanout as a posture that a machine holds for exactly one variety and loses the moment it is used. Registering a harvest machine — a capability shared by a `ProgramAdmin` and a `ConditioningManager` — records an `equipment_code` unique across the fleet, an `equipment_kind` of `COMBINE`, `TRUCK` or `AUGER`, and `cleanout_minutes_required`, the documented number of minutes a full varietal cleanout of that machine takes, which may be zero but not negative. A newly registered machine starts in `DIRTY` status with `cleanout_certified` false and both `last_cleanout_at` and `last_cleanout_variety_id` null; its `status` is one of `AVAILABLE`, `DIRTY` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE`.

Certifying a cleanout is a shared responsibility of the `ConditioningManager` and the `FieldOfficer`, and it names the variety the machine has been cleaned out *for* by supplying `variety_id` together with `cleaned_at`, the instant at which the cleanout was completed. A `cleaned_at` after the current logical clock instant is refused with `ERR_DATE_IN_FUTURE`, and an unresolvable variety reference is refused with `ERR_VARIETY_NOT_FOUND`. A successful certification sets the machine's `status` to `AVAILABLE`, sets `cleanout_certified` to true, sets `last_cleanout_variety_id` to the supplied variety and `last_cleanout_at` to the supplied instant, and increments the machine's `version` by 1. The certification clears the machine for that one variety only; `cleanout_certified` is true only in the interval between a certification and the next harvest event that consumes it.

Recording a harvest is a capability shared by a `FieldOfficer` and a `ConditioningManager`. The payload names the `production_contract_id` being harvested and the `harvest_equipment_id` of the machine that cut the field, brackets the machine occupation with `window_start` and `window_end`, and reports `gross_weight_kg`, the gross field weight delivered in kilograms, which must be greater than zero, and `moisture_pct`, the moisture percentage measured at intake, which must fall between 0 and 40 inclusive. An optional `bag_size_kg` declares the nominal bag size the resulting lot will eventually be bagged into, in kilograms; it must be greater than zero and defaults to 25.0 when omitted. Because a harvest event is an immutable record of work already done, it carries only an `id` and a `created_at`: it has no `updated_at` and no `version` at all.

Four gates guard the recording. The contract must be in `INSPECTION_PASSED` status, or already in `HARVESTED` status for a subsequent load; from any other status the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATE`. The machine must be simultaneously in `AVAILABLE` status, carry `cleanout_certified` true, and have a `last_cleanout_variety_id` equal to the contract's `variety_id`; if any of those three fails the request is refused with `ERR_EQUIPMENT_CLEANOUT_REQUIRED`, which is what makes a machine cleaned for the wrong variety as useless as a machine never cleaned at all. The window must be well-formed, with `window_start` strictly before `window_end`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`, and it must already have elapsed — a `window_end` after the current logical clock instant is refused with `ERR_HARVEST_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`. Finally, the window must not collide with another harvest window on the same machine under the closed-interval convention of §2.10, or the request is refused with `ERR_EQUIPMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

A successful harvest event books the grower's provisional receipt using the **provisional harvest basis** variant of clean seed unit accounting. First, a book moisture dockage percentage is derived as the intake moisture percentage minus a 12.0 percent base moisture, multiplied by a shrink factor of 1.5, and floored at zero so that seed at or below base moisture takes no dockage; that value is stored on the harvest event as `moisture_dockage_pct`. Next, `provisional_clean_kg` is the gross field weight reduced by the moisture dockage percentage and then reduced again by the variety's book `estimated_conditioning_shrink_pct`, each reduction applied as a proportional factor of one minus the percentage divided by one hundred, with the result rounded half-up to three decimal places. Finally, `provisional_clean_units` is that provisional clean mass divided by the clean seed unit of 25.0 kilograms, rounded half-up to two decimal places. This variant reads exactly one harvest event and the variety of its contract; it never reads any conditioning run, and it never sees how the seed actually performed on the plant.

Recording the harvest also mints the lot that carries the seed's identity for the rest of its life. The new `SeedLot` is created in conditioning status `RAW` and certification status `UNSAMPLED`, with a system-assigned `lot_code` of the deterministic form `L-{season_year}-{4-digit per-contract sequence}` — the season year of the originating contract followed by a four-digit, zero-padded sequence number that counts lots within that contract, so a contract's first lot in season 2026 is `L-2026-0001`. The lot copies `variety_id` from the originating contract and holds it immutable for the rest of its life, records the originating `production_contract_id` and `season_year`, carries `carryover_generation` 0 and a null `parent_seed_lot_id` because it was minted by a harvest rather than derived from a predecessor, takes its `gross_kg` and its initial `current_kg` from the harvest's gross field weight, starts with `cumulative_shrink_kg` and `dockage_pct` at 0, inherits `bag_size_kg` from the harvest event and `intake_moisture_pct` from the harvest's measured moisture, inherits `provisional_clean_units` from the harvest event's provisional figure, starts with `bag_count` and `tagged_bag_count` at 0, carries `final_clean_units`, `germination_pct`, `purity_pct`, `weed_seed_count_per_kg`, `germination_valid_until` and `adjusted_price_per_unit_cents` all null, and starts with `disposition` `NONE`. The harvest event records the identifier of the lot it minted as its own `seed_lot_id`.

Three further side effects complete the operation. The harvest event's `provisional_clean_units` is added to the contract's `provisional_clean_units_total`. The contract advances `INSPECTION_PASSED → HARVESTED`, and its `version` increments by 1. The machine is dirtied again: its `status` returns to `DIRTY`, its `cleanout_certified` returns to false, and its `version` increments by 1 — so a second load, even from the very same field, demands a fresh cleanout certification. A subsequent harvest event recorded against a contract that is already in `HARVESTED` status is permitted and re-runs every one of these side effects except the status change, which has nowhere left to go.

### 3.5. Conditioning: The Stage Ladder, Line Scheduling and the Mass Ledger

A seed lot carries two entirely independent status axes, and keeping them independent is a design commitment rather than an implementation detail. The `conditioning_status` axis tracks physical processing and runs `RAW`, `CLEANED`, `SIZED`, `TREATED`, `BAGGED`. The `certification_status` axis tracks the certification agency's verdict and runs `UNSAMPLED`, `SAMPLED`, `PASSED`, `FAILED`, `TAGGED`, `RETEST_DUE`. Every conditioning transition leaves the certification axis untouched, and every certification transition leaves the conditioning axis untouched.

Conditioning happens on registered lines. Registering a conditioning line — shared between a `ProgramAdmin` and a `ConditioningManager` — records a `line_code` unique across the plant, `cleanout_minutes_required`, the number of minutes of cleanout that must separate runs of *different* varieties on that line, which may be zero but not negative, and `capacity_kg_per_hour`, the line's rated throughput, which must be greater than zero. An optional `status` of `AVAILABLE` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE` defaults to `AVAILABLE`. Two computed properties track the line's history: `last_cleanout_variety_id`, the variety of the most recently completed run on the line, and `last_run_end_utc`, that run's window end. Both are null before the line's first completion.

A `ConditioningManager` plans a run by naming the `seed_lot_id` being conditioned, the `conditioning_line_id` the run will occupy, the `stage` being performed — one of `CLEANING`, `SIZING`, `TREATING` or `BAGGING` — the occupation window as `window_start` and `window_end`, and `input_kg`, the mass fed into the run, which must be greater than zero. Three optional treatment fields, discussed in §3.6, are supplied only for a treating run. A planned run lands in `PLANNED` status with `output_kg`, `shrink_kg` and `bag_count` all null and `reversal_reason` null; its `status` is one of `PLANNED`, `COMPLETED`, `ABORTED` or `REVERSED`.

Stage order is a strict ladder with exactly one branch. The requested stage must be the immediate successor of the lot's current conditioning status: a `RAW` lot may only be sent to `CLEANING`, a `CLEANED` lot only to `SIZING`, a `SIZED` lot to `TREATING`, and a `TREATED` lot to `BAGGING`. The one branch is that a `SIZED` lot may go directly to `BAGGING`, skipping treatment entirely, but only when the lot's variety has `treatment_required` false; when the variety requires treatment, the direct `SIZED`-to-`BAGGING` shortcut is not available. Any stage that is not the immediate successor under these rules is refused with `ERR_STAGE_OUT_OF_ORDER`. A lot already in `BAGGED` conditioning status has no successor stage and admits no further run at all, which is refused with `ERR_INVALID_LOT_STATE`. A lot whose certification status is `TAGGED` has had its conditioning history frozen by the tags hanging off it, and planning a new run on such a lot is refused with `ERR_LOT_TAGGED_IMMUTABLE`.

The declared input mass is not a free parameter: `input_kg` must equal the lot's `current_kg` as it stands at request time, exact to two decimal places, or the request is refused with `ERR_INPUT_MASS_MISMATCH`. This is what makes the mass ledger auditable — every run picks up precisely what the previous run put down.

Scheduling the line applies three temporal rules. The window must be well-formed, with `window_start` strictly before `window_end`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. The window must not collide with another run on the same line under the half-open convention of §2.10, considering only runs in `PLANNED` or `COMPLETED` status, or the request is refused with `ERR_LINE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. And the inter-variety cleanout buffer must have elapsed: when the line's `last_cleanout_variety_id` is non-null and differs from the requested lot's `variety_id`, the requested `window_start` must fall at or after the line's `last_run_end_utc` plus the line's `cleanout_minutes_required` minutes, or the request is refused with `ERR_LINE_CLEANOUT_BUFFER_VIOLATION`. When the line's last variety is the same as the lot's variety, no cleanout is needed, the required buffer is zero, and a run starting at the exact instant the previous run ended is accepted. Whichever buffer the rule demanded is recorded on the run as `cleanout_buffer_applied_minutes`: zero when the line's last variety matched the lot's variety, and the line's full `cleanout_minutes_required` otherwise.

Completing a run is where mass actually moves. The `ConditioningManager` supplies `output_kg`, the measured mass leaving the run. The run must be in `PLANNED` status, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`; the run's `window_end` must be at or before the current logical clock instant, or it is refused with `ERR_RUN_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`; and the output mass must be at least zero and no greater than the run's `input_kg`, or it is refused with `ERR_OUTPUT_EXCEEDS_INPUT`. On success the run advances `PLANNED → COMPLETED`, its `shrink_kg` is set to the input mass minus the output mass, the lot's `current_kg` is replaced by the output mass, the run's shrink is added to the lot's `cumulative_shrink_kg`, and the lot's `dockage_pct` is recomputed as the cumulative shrink divided by the lot's `gross_kg`, multiplied by one hundred, rounded half-up to two decimal places. The lot's conditioning axis advances one rung — `RAW → CLEANED` for a cleaning run, `CLEANED → SIZED` for a sizing run, `SIZED → TREATED` for a treating run, and either `SIZED → BAGGED` or `TREATED → BAGGED` for a bagging run — and the lot's `version` increments by 1. The line's history is updated in the same breath: its `last_cleanout_variety_id` is set to the lot's `variety_id`, its `last_run_end_utc` is set to the run's `window_end`, and the line's `version` increments by 1. Neither the lot's mass presently held nor its cumulative shrink is ever rounded; only the derived dockage percentage is.

A completing bagging run does two extra things, and they are the bridge into settlement. The lot's `bag_count` becomes the run's output mass divided by the lot's `bag_size_kg`, truncated toward zero so that a partial bag is simply not counted, and the run records the identical figure as its own `bag_count`. And the lot's `final_clean_units` is set from the **final bagged basis** variant of clean seed unit accounting, which is the measured bagged output mass divided by the clean seed unit of 25.0 kilograms, rounded half-up to two decimal places. This variant reads exactly the single completed bagging run of the lot and nothing else — it never looks at the harvest's gross field weight, its intake moisture, or the variety's book conditioning shrink, and neither variant ever reads a run in `ABORTED` or `REVERSED` status. Because it measures rather than estimates, a plant that performed better or worse than the variety's book shrink will produce a final figure that diverges from the provisional figure computed at intake for the very same harvest inputs, and it is the final figure — never the provisional one — that drives every settlement line.

Reversal is the compensating operation, and it forks on the run's status. Reversing a run that is still in `PLANNED` status simply aborts it: the supplied `reversal_reason` is stored, the run moves `PLANNED → ABORTED`, the line window it held is released so a later run may occupy it, and the lot's mass fields are left completely untouched. Reversing a run that is in `COMPLETED` status unwinds the mass movement: the lot's `current_kg` is restored to the run's `input_kg`, the run's `shrink_kg` is subtracted back out of the lot's `cumulative_shrink_kg`, the lot's `dockage_pct` is recomputed, the lot's conditioning status steps back one rung to the stage it held before the run, the run moves `COMPLETED → REVERSED`, the reason is stored, and the lot's `version` increments by 1. The conserved identity of §2.11 — entering mass equals held mass plus cumulative shrink — holds exactly after the reversal, with zero drift. When the reversed run was the bagging run, the lot's `bag_count` returns to 0, its `final_clean_units` returns to null, and its `dockage_pct` is recomputed from the restored figures. Two guards protect reversal: a completed run may only be reversed when it is the latest completed run on its lot, ordered by `window_end` descending with `created_at` descending as the tie-break, or the request is refused with `ERR_RUN_NOT_LATEST`; and no run on a lot whose certification status is `TAGGED` may be reversed at all, which is refused with `ERR_LOT_TAGGED_IMMUTABLE`. Attempting to reverse a run that is already `ABORTED` or `REVERSED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`.

### 3.6. Treated Seed: Product Selection, Rate Limits and Label Disclosure

Treated seed is a regulated article, so the treating stage carries four compliance checks that no other stage carries, evaluated in the order given here. All three treatment inputs live on the run-planning payload rather than on the completion payload, which means the plant commits to a compliant treatment before the line is ever occupied.

The three inputs are `treatment_product_id`, naming the registered treatment product; `treatment_rate_g_per_100kg`, the rate actually to be applied in grams per hundred kilograms of seed; and `label_text`, the treated-seed label text that will be printed on the bag tag. Presence of the three is symmetric with the stage: all three must be present when the run's `stage` is `TREATING`, and all three must be absent for every other stage. Supplying treatment fields on a cleaning, sizing or bagging run, or omitting any of them on a treating run, is refused with `ERR_TREATMENT_FIELDS_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 422). An unresolvable `treatment_product_id` is refused earlier, with `ERR_TREATMENT_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`.

Once the product resolves, its label governs. The `crop_kind` of the lot's variety must appear in the product's `allowed_crop_kinds`, or the run is refused with `ERR_TREATMENT_CROP_NOT_LABELED` — a product labeled only for `WHEAT` and `TRITICALE` may not be applied to a `CANOLA` lot no matter how the rate is set. The requested `treatment_rate_g_per_100kg` may not exceed the product's `max_rate_g_per_100kg`; a rate exactly at the label maximum is permitted, and anything above it is refused with `ERR_TREATMENT_RATE_EXCEEDS_LABEL`.

The label text check is a content check with up to three parts. The supplied `label_text` must always contain the product's `epa_registration_number` as a substring. When the product's `requires_dye` flag is true, the label text must additionally contain the literal string `DYED`, because treated seed that must be dyed has to be disclosed as such on the bag. When the product's `restricted_use` flag is true, the label text must additionally contain the product's `label_signal_word` — the exact `CAUTION`, `WARNING` or `DANGER` string registered against that product. A label text missing any applicable one of those three elements is refused with `ERR_TREATMENT_LABEL_INCOMPLETE`. Where a product has both flags false, only the registration number is required.

### 3.7. Agency Sampling, Laboratory Verdicts, Quality Pricing and Tag Issuance

Certification is the certification agency's business, not the plant's, and it operates entirely on the lot's second status axis. A `CertificationAgency` operator draws an official sample from a lot by naming the `seed_lot_id`, the `sample_kind` — `INITIAL` for a lot's first test or `RETEST` for a carryover retest — and `sampled_on`, the ISO 8601 date on which the sample was physically drawn. Only a fully bagged lot may be sampled: the lot's `conditioning_status` must be `BAGGED`. The certification axis must also permit it, and the two sample kinds have different entry points: an `INITIAL` sample requires the lot's `certification_status` to be `UNSAMPLED`, while a `RETEST` sample requires it to be either `RETEST_DUE` or `TAGGED`. Any other combination is refused with `ERR_INVALID_LOT_STATE`, and a `sampled_on` after the current logical clock date is refused with `ERR_DATE_IN_FUTURE`. Drawing a sample records the drawing operator's `X-User-ID` as `sampler_user_id`, puts the sample in `PENDING` result state with `germination_pct`, `purity_pct`, `inert_pct`, `weed_seed_count_per_kg`, `tested_at` and `germination_valid_until` all null, moves the lot's certification status to `SAMPLED` while leaving its conditioning status exactly where it was, and increments the lot's `version` by 1.

Posting laboratory findings against a `PENDING` sample supplies four measurements and a timestamp: `germination_pct` and `purity_pct`, each between 0 and 100 inclusive; `inert_pct`, the inert matter percentage, also between 0 and 100 inclusive; `weed_seed_count_per_kg`, the weed seed count per kilogram, which may be zero but not negative; and `tested_at`, the instant the laboratory finished the test. A sample that has already been resulted is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_ALREADY_RESULTED`. Because a report cannot describe more than a whole sample, the sum of `purity_pct` and `inert_pct` may not exceed 100; a report that breaches that is refused with `ERR_COMPONENT_PCT_SUM_INVALID`. A `tested_at` after the current logical clock instant is refused with `ERR_DATE_IN_FUTURE`.

The verdict is a three-part conjunction against the variety's floors. The sample passes when its germination percentage is at least the variety's `germination_min_pct`, *and* its purity percentage is at least the variety's `purity_min_pct`, *and* its weed seed count per kilogram is at most 30. If any one of those three fails — germination below the floor, purity below the floor, or more than 30 weed seeds per kilogram — the sample fails. The sample's `result` moves from `PENDING` to `PASS` or `FAIL` accordingly.

On the passing branch the findings are copied forward. The lot's `germination_pct`, `purity_pct` and `weed_seed_count_per_kg` are set from the sample's figures, so the lot always reflects its most recently resulted test. The lot's certification status advances `SAMPLED → PASSED`. Both the sample's and the lot's `germination_valid_until` are set to the sample's `tested_at` advanced by the variety's `germ_validity_days` in whole calendar days, computed exactly on the UTC instant. The lot's `adjusted_price_per_unit_cents` is computed by the quality pricing rule below. The lot's `version` increments by 1. Samples in `PENDING` or `FAIL` state never move a lot's germination validity.

**Quality pricing** turns the four measurements into a per-unit price, and it is a sum of independent adjustment percentages applied to the contract's base price. The germination adjustment is a bonus of 6.0 percent when the germination percentage is at least 95.0, otherwise a bonus of 2.0 percent when it is at least 90.0, and otherwise 0.0 percent. The purity adjustment is a bonus of 2.0 percent when the purity percentage is at least 99.5, otherwise 0.0 percent when it is at least 98.0, and otherwise a penalty of 4.0 percent. The weed penalty is 0.0 percent for a perfectly clean report of zero weed seeds per kilogram, otherwise a penalty of 3.0 percent at up to and including 10 weed seeds per kilogram, and otherwise a penalty of 9.0 percent up to and including 30. Every one of those tier boundaries is an inclusive lower bound. A fourth term is a moisture dockage percentage, derived from the lot's `intake_moisture_pct` — the moisture measured at harvest intake, inherited by the lot and never re-derived from any later sample — as that intake moisture minus a 12.0 percent base moisture, multiplied by a factor of 1.5, floored at zero. The raw adjustment percentage is the germination adjustment plus the purity adjustment plus the weed penalty, minus the moisture dockage percentage; bonuses are additions and penalties are subtractions. That raw adjustment is then clamped into a band whose ceiling is a bonus of 8.0 percent and whose floor is a penalty of 35.0 percent — first raised to the floor if it sits below it, then lowered to the ceiling if it sits above it. Finally, `adjusted_price_per_unit_cents` is the contract's `base_price_per_unit_cents` scaled by one plus the clamped adjustment percentage divided by one hundred, rounded half-up to whole cents. This calculation reads only the four quality figures of the single sample being resulted, the lot's intake moisture, and the base price of the lot's originating contract. Cases below the variety's germination floor, below its purity floor, or above 30 weed seeds per kilogram never reach this calculation at all, because such a sample takes the failing branch instead.

Once a lot is `PASSED`, a `CertificationAgency` operator issues tags against it, naming the `seed_lot_id`, the `lot_sample_id` of the passing sample the tag certifies, the `tag_class` to be printed — one of `FOUNDATION`, `REGISTERED` or `CERTIFIED` — and `bag_count`, the number of bags the tag block covers, which must be greater than zero. Five gates apply. The lot must be simultaneously `BAGGED` on its conditioning axis and `PASSED` on its certification axis, or the request is refused with `ERR_LOT_NOT_TAG_ELIGIBLE`. The referenced sample must belong to that very lot and must carry a `PASS` result, or the request is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_LOT_MISMATCH`. The germination result must still be live: the current logical clock instant must be at or before the lot's `germination_valid_until`, and at the exact boundary instant tagging is still permitted; past it the request is refused with `ERR_GERMINATION_VALIDITY_EXPIRED`. The requested bag count may not exceed the lot's untagged bags, computed as the lot's `bag_count` minus its `tagged_bag_count`, or the request is refused with `ERR_TAG_BAG_COUNT_EXCEEDS_LOT`. And the class printed on the tag may not outrank the variety's ceiling: ranking `CERTIFIED` lowest, `REGISTERED` above it and `FOUNDATION` highest, the requested `tag_class` rank must be at or below the rank of the variety's `max_seed_class`, or the request is refused with `ERR_TAG_CLASS_EXCEEDS_VARIETY`.

Issuing a tag mints a deterministic `tag_serial` of the form `TAG-{season_year}-{6-digit issuance sequence}` — the lot's season year followed by a six-digit, zero-padded issuance sequence number — stamps `issued_at` with the current logical clock instant, and puts the tag in `ISSUED` status with `void_reason` null. The tag's `bag_count` is added to the lot's `tagged_bag_count`, and the lot's certification status advances `PASSED → TAGGED`. The lot's `final_clean_units` is added to the originating contract's `certified_units_total` and the contract's `version` increments by 1. The lot's `version` increments by 1.

Voiding an issued tag is the compensating operation. The operator supplies a `void_reason`; a tag that is already `VOIDED` is refused with `ERR_TAG_ALREADY_VOIDED`. Voiding stores the reason, moves the tag `ISSUED → VOIDED`, and subtracts the tag's `bag_count` back out of the lot's `tagged_bag_count`, restoring the invariant that the lot's tagged bag count equals the sum of bag counts over its still-issued tags. When that subtraction brings the lot's `tagged_bag_count` down to 0, the lot's certification status returns `TAGGED → PASSED` and the lot's `final_clean_units` is subtracted back out of the contract's `certified_units_total`. While the tagged bag count remains above zero the lot stays `TAGGED` and the contract's certified total is untouched. The tag's `version` increments by 1 and the lot's `version` increments by 1.

The failing branch of a laboratory result is the system's deepest cascade, and it reaches five hops. On the first hop the sample's `result` is set to `FAIL`. On the second hop, because the sample failed, the lot's certification status moves to `FAILED` — from `SAMPLED` on an initial test, or from `TAGGED` when a carryover retest fails on an already-tagged lot — the lot's `disposition` is set to `DOWNGRADE_TO_COMMERCIAL`, and the lot's `adjusted_price_per_unit_cents` is set back to null, because a failed lot has no certified price. On the third hop, because the lot became `FAILED`, every certification tag on that lot whose status is `ISSUED` is moved to `VOIDED` in `created_at` ascending order with `void_reason` set to `RETEST_FAILURE`, and the lot's `tagged_bag_count` is driven to 0. On the fourth hop, because the tagged bag count dropped, the contract's `certified_units_total` is recomputed from the contract's remaining lots that are still `TAGGED`, and the contract's `version` increments by 1. On the fifth hop, because the contract's certified unit total changed, every settlement of that contract whose status is `COMPUTED` has its `quality_hold` set to true and its status moved to `SUPERSEDED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order — the quality hold is set *before* the status moves, so the superseded settlement records why it was withdrawn. Settlements already in `APPROVED`, `PAID`, `DISPUTED` or `SUPERSEDED` status are not touched by this hop. The lot's `version` increments by 1. Note that the lot's copied `germination_pct`, `purity_pct` and `weed_seed_count_per_kg` are refreshed from the failing sample on this branch too, so the lot always shows the findings of its most recent resulted test.

### 3.8. Carryover Inventory and the Retest Obligation

Seed that does not sell in the season it was produced can be carried over, but it may not be carried over on the strength of a stale germination test. The platform models this by requiring the parent lot's germination result to have *lapsed* before a successor may be minted, which makes the carryover gate and the tag-issuance gate exact complements of one another: at the precise boundary instant equal to the lot's `germination_valid_until`, tag issuance is still allowed and carryover is not yet due. One instant later, tagging is refused and carryover becomes available.

A `ConditioningManager` mints a carryover successor against a parent lot, supplying `expected_seed_lot_version` — the parent's `version` as last observed, per §2.8 — and `next_season_year`, the season in which the successor will be offered for sale. Four gates apply, in the priority order of §2.9. The parent lot's `certification_status` must be `TAGGED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_LOT_STATE`. The supplied version must match the parent's current `version`, or the request is refused with `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION`. The current logical clock instant must be strictly after the parent's `germination_valid_until`, or the request is refused with `ERR_RETEST_NOT_DUE`. And the successor's generation depth must stay within the variety's ceiling: the parent's `carryover_generation` plus one may not exceed the variety's `max_carryover_generations`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CARRYOVER_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

The successor lot is a new resource in its own right, and it is deliberately born already bagged but not yet certified. Its `parent_seed_lot_id` points at the parent, establishing the self-referential lineage that lets a lot be traced back through every season it survived. Its `carryover_generation` is the parent's generation plus one. Its `season_year` is the supplied `next_season_year`, and it receives its own system `lot_code` minted the same way as any other lot, from its own season year and the next four-digit sequence number within the originating contract. Its `variety_id` and `production_contract_id` are copied from the parent, preserving lot identity across the generation boundary. Its `conditioning_status` is `BAGGED` — no conditioning run will ever be planned against it — and its `certification_status` is `RETEST_DUE`, which is the one certification state that exists purely to say "this seed is physically ready but its paperwork has expired". Its `gross_kg` and its `current_kg` both take the parent's `current_kg`, with `cumulative_shrink_kg` and `dockage_pct` starting at 0, so the conserved mass identity of §2.11 holds on the successor from birth. Its `bag_size_kg`, `bag_count`, `intake_moisture_pct`, `provisional_clean_units` and `final_clean_units` are inherited from the parent — the final clean unit figure is unchanged because the mass is unchanged, being that same held mass divided by the clean seed unit of 25.0 kilograms rounded half-up to two decimal places. Its `tagged_bag_count` starts at 0, its `germination_pct`, `purity_pct`, `weed_seed_count_per_kg`, `germination_valid_until` and `adjusted_price_per_unit_cents` are all null because a fresh retest must establish them, and its `disposition` starts at `NONE`.

The parent lot itself is left in place. Its `certification_status` stays `TAGGED`, its tags stay `ISSUED`, and its `tagged_bag_count` is unchanged — the only thing minting a successor does to the parent is increment the parent's `version` by 1. This is precisely why a parent may legitimately father several successors across seasons, and precisely why the version pin of §2.8 is the only guard against a successor derived from an out-of-date view.

Bringing a carryover successor back into certification means drawing a `RETEST` sample against it, which its `RETEST_DUE` certification status admits, and posting a fresh laboratory result. A passing retest walks the successor through `SAMPLED → PASSED` and re-establishes its germination validity and adjusted price exactly as an initial test would. A failing retest drives the five-hop cascade of §3.7 against the successor. A `RETEST` sample may also be drawn against a lot that is still `TAGGED`, which is how a lot whose tags are already in the trade can be re-verified in place; when such a retest fails, the cascade voids those tags with a `void_reason` of `RETEST_FAILURE` and withdraws the affected settlements.

### 3.9. Grower Settlement, Advance Recovery and Contract Closure

Settlement is the point where certified seed becomes money, and it runs on the final bagged basis only. A `SettlementAccountant` computes a settlement by naming the `production_contract_id` and supplying `expected_contract_version` per §2.8. The contract must be in `HARVESTED` status, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATE`.

The rollup selects its lines by a narrow filter: every seed lot of that contract whose `certification_status` is `TAGGED` and whose `final_clean_units` is not null, ordered by `created_at` ascending with `lot_code` ascending as the tie-break. Lots sitting in `UNSAMPLED`, `SAMPLED`, `PASSED`, `FAILED` or `RETEST_DUE` are excluded, and so are lots whose tags have all been voided, because voiding the last tag returned them to `PASSED`. When the filter selects nothing at all, the request is refused with `ERR_NO_SETTLEABLE_LOTS` rather than being settled at zero.

**The rounding order of the rollup is pinned.** Each line is rounded to whole cents *first*, and only then are the rounded line values summed. For each line, the line's gross amount in cents is that lot's `final_clean_units` multiplied by that lot's `adjusted_price_per_unit_cents`, rounded half-up to whole cents; and the line's conditioning charge in cents is that same unit figure multiplied by the contract's `conditioning_charge_per_unit_cents`, again rounded half-up to whole cents. Summing the exact products and rounding once at the end is wrong and can differ by cents from the required answer. The settlement then records `line_count` as the number of lines folded in, `settled_units_total` as the sum of the lines' clean unit figures rounded half-up to two decimal places, `gross_payable_cents` as the sum of the already-rounded line gross amounts, and `conditioning_charges_cents` as the sum of the already-rounded line charges.

**The advance-recovery waterfall runs in a pinned order too**, and it runs exactly once with no fixed-point iteration, because the recovery cap is keyed on the very total that the recovery then reduces. Step one computes `provisional_net_cents` as `gross_payable_cents` minus `conditioning_charges_cents`; a negative provisional net is refused with `ERR_NET_PAYABLE_NEGATIVE`. Step two selects `recovery_cap_pct` from a tier table keyed on `provisional_net_cents` and on nothing else — not on the net payable, and not on the gross payable. A provisional net of at least fifty thousand dollars, which is 5,000,000 cents, admits a cap of 100.0 percent; otherwise at least twenty thousand dollars, or 2,000,000 cents, admits 60.0 percent; otherwise at least five thousand dollars, or 500,000 cents, admits 35.0 percent; and anything below that admits 15.0 percent. Every tier bound is an inclusive lower bound. Step three computes `advance_recovery_cents` as the smaller of two quantities: the contract's `advance_outstanding_cents` as read at request time, and the provisional net scaled by the recovery cap percentage divided by one hundred and rounded half-up to whole cents before the comparison is made. Step four computes `net_payable_cents` as the provisional net minus the advance recovery. Because the outstanding advance is read at request time, it is already net of any recovery booked by an earlier approved or paid settlement of the same contract.

A computed settlement records the computing accountant's `X-User-ID` as `computed_by_user_id`, lands in `COMPUTED` status with `quality_hold` false, and carries `approved_by_user_id`, `dispute_reason` and `payment_reference` all null. Computing a settlement also increments the contract's `version` by 1, which is what makes a second computation against a version the accountant has not re-read fail the version pin. Creating a settlement legitimately supersedes the contract's earlier work: every earlier settlement of that contract whose status is `COMPUTED` or `DISPUTED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, with no monetary movement of any kind, because the superseding settlement carries the recomputed figures. `SUPERSEDED` and `PAID` are the two terminal settlement states.

Approval is the second pair of eyes. A `SettlementAccountant` approves a settlement whose status is `COMPUTED` and whose `quality_hold` is false; a settlement in any other status, or one carrying a quality hold, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SETTLEMENT_STATE`. The approving accountant's `X-User-ID` must differ from the settlement's `computed_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SOD_SETTLEMENT_SELF_APPROVAL`. The owning contract must be in `HARVESTED` status. On success the settlement moves `COMPUTED → APPROVED`, `approved_by_user_id` is set from the approving accountant's identifier, the settlement's `advance_recovery_cents` is subtracted from the contract's `advance_outstanding_cents`, the contract advances `HARVESTED → SETTLED`, the settlement's `version` increments by 1 and the contract's `version` increments by 1. This is the one operation that books an advance recovery.

Payment closes the contract. A `SettlementAccountant` pays a settlement whose status is `APPROVED`, supplying the bank `payment_reference`; any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SETTLEMENT_STATE`. The reference is stored, the settlement moves `APPROVED → PAID`, the contract advances `SETTLED → CLOSED`, and both versions increment by 1. `CLOSED` is terminal for the contract.

Dispute is the grower's compensating lever, and it is symmetric with approval in exactly one respect: it returns whatever approval booked. A `Grower` whose `X-User-ID` equals the contract's `grower_id` may dispute a settlement whose status is `COMPUTED` or `APPROVED`, supplying a `dispute_reason`; any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SETTLEMENT_STATE`, and a mismatched grower identity is refused with `ERR_GROWER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Disputing a settlement that was in `COMPUTED` status stores the reason and moves it `COMPUTED → DISPUTED` with no advance movement at all, because no recovery had been applied. Disputing a settlement that was in `APPROVED` status stores the reason, moves it `APPROVED → DISPUTED`, adds the settlement's `advance_recovery_cents` back to the contract's `advance_outstanding_cents` — restoring the advance conservation identity of §2.11 exactly — and returns the contract `SETTLED → HARVESTED` so that a corrected settlement can be computed. In both cases the settlement's `version` and the contract's `version` each increment by 1. The contract's `certified_units_total` is a derived roll-up over its `TAGGED` lots and is never touched by any settlement money movement, in either direction.
