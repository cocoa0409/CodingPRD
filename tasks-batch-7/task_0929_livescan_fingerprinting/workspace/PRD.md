# Livescan Fingerprinting & Background Service API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Applicant fingerprinting is a service industry that sits between two masters. On one side stand the agencies — school districts, hospital systems, firearms dealers, licensing boards — each of which submits under a nine-character Originating Agency Identifier issued to it under a specific statute. On the other side stand the destinations: the state criminal-history repository and, when federal law reaches the applicant, the FBI. A commercial livescan vendor runs the capture floor in between: storefront sites, mobile event crews, calibrated scanners, and a front counter that takes money. This document specifies the API that vendor runs.

The hard part of the domain is that almost nothing about a fingerprint transaction is a property of the fingerprints. The very same ten-finger card, captured on the very same scanner by the very same technician, produces a different set of required destinations, a different fee total, a different response projection back to the agency, and a different rap-back eligibility depending only on which ORI it is submitted under. The statutory authority attached to the ORI is therefore the parameterizing spine of the whole system: it fixes the destination requirement, the dissemination tier, whether the record may be retained, and whether continuous rap-back monitoring may be subscribed. A submission then advances along three independent axes at once — the state destination's result, the FBI destination's result, and the retention election's lifecycle — which means the interesting operational cases are the ones where the destinations disagree with each other.

Two further pressures shape the design. First, money and access are coupled: an agency that lets its invoices age past a statutory threshold is suspended, and suspension is a global write gate over every ORI that agency owns — but a gate with a carefully enumerated exclusion list, because an applicant's right to challenge a determination and a destination's duty to deliver a rap-back event do not evaporate because a school district was slow to pay. Second, calibration is retroactive. When an audit discovers that a scanner's calibration certificate had in fact lapsed earlier than the record showed, every card captured after the corrected expiry is invalidated after the fact, the prints are redone at vendor cost, and an onsite event that looked comfortably above its contracted minimum can be pushed into a shortfall charge weeks after the crew went home. Every instant in this system comes from a server-held logical clock, never from wall-clock time, so all of these windows, expiries and sweeps are exactly reproducible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The entire API is mounted under the `/livescan` prefix. Every endpoint in this specification is a JSON-over-HTTP endpoint; every request that carries a body carries `Content-Type: application/json`.

### 2.1. Actors, Credentials and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, or is present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. This check runs before anything else, including before the server looks at the URL's resource identifiers.

Six roles exist, and the spelling below is exact:

- `VendorAdmin` — vendor operations. Registers agencies, ORIs, service types, capture sites and devices; records calibrations and runs calibration audits; opens, issues and settles invoices; decides purge requests; drives the nightly batch and the logical clock.
- `LivescanTechnician` — the front-counter operator. Checks applicants in, marks no-shows, opens fingerprint cards, records scored attempts and exception codes, finalizes cards, transmits them, and takes point-of-service payment.
- `SiteSupervisor` — the floor supervisor. Grants exception overrides, authorizes reprints after a rejection, and manages device rosters, including releasing a rostered device.
- `AgencyAdjudicator` — agency staff. Issues fitness determinations, resolves challenges, subscribes to and renews rap-back, and acknowledges rap-back events.
- `StateRepository` — the destinations themselves. Posts destination results and delivers rap-back notifications.
- `Applicant` — the person being printed. Books and reschedules appointments, cancels them, pays applicant-borne assessments, challenges a determination, and may request that their own retained record be purged.

If `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of those six, or carries one of the six but that role is not listed for the endpoint being called in the endpoint table of §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403. Role membership is checked immediately after authentication and before any resource in the path is looked up, so a caller with the wrong role receives 403 rather than 404 even when the resource named in the path does not exist.

The two headers mean different things for different roles. When `X-User-Role` is `Applicant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Applicant` row; ownership checks on any resource that references an applicant compare the resource's applicant foreign key — `applicant_id` on an appointment — to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `VendorAdmin`, `LivescanTechnician`, `SiteSupervisor`, `AgencyAdjudicator` or `StateRepository`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity: the server records it wherever a transition writes an actor field — `captured_by_user_id` and `exception_override_by_user_id` on a fingerprint card, `issued_by_user_id` and `resolved_by_user_id` on a determination, `purge_requested_by_user_id` and `purge_decided_by_user_id` on a submission, `acknowledged_by_user_id` on a rap-back notification — but never compares it against any row's `id`.

Segregation of duties is enforced by comparing those recorded actor strings against the acting `X-User-ID` of a later request. Three such pairings exist and they are described where the operations that create them are described; all three reject with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Filtering and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when it is not supplied, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0; it has no upper bound. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or an `offset` below 0, or either parameter supplied as something other than an integer, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400.

Responses from list endpoints carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`. `total_count` counts the rows that match the request's filters before `limit` and `offset` are applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when the sum of `offset` and the number of rows actually returned is smaller than `total_count`.

Unless an endpoint's own description says otherwise, every list is ordered by `created_at` ascending, and rows sharing an identical `created_at` are broken by `id` ascending in lexicographic byte order. Every other query parameter named for a list endpoint in §4 is an optional equality filter on the field of the same name; supplying several filters conjoins them. The one filter that is not a plain field match is `ori_code` on the rap-back notification listing, which matches the plain ORI code string the destination stamped on the notification rather than any foreign key.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Datetime Contract

All seventeen persisted resource types — agencies, ORIs, service types, capture sites, devices, device assignments, applicants, appointments, fingerprint cards, position captures, submissions, criminal-history responses, fitness determinations, rap-back subscriptions, rap-back notifications, fee assessments and invoices — carry the same four common properties, with no exceptions and no entity that carries a reduced set:

- `id` — a server-minted UUID string. It carries no type prefix; a card's identifier and an invoice's identifier are indistinguishable by shape. Client-supplied identifiers are ignored.
- `created_at` — the logical-clock instant at which the row was created.
- `updated_at` — the logical-clock instant of the most recent mutation of the row. At creation it equals `created_at`.
- `version` — an integer that is 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on each request that mutates the row. §2.5 states, operation by operation, what "mutates" means for multi-row requests.

Every datetime this API returns — `created_at`, `updated_at`, `suspended_at`, `last_calibrated_at`, `calibration_expires_at`, `calibration_expires_at_snapshot`, `window_start`, `window_end`, `event_window_start`, `event_window_end`, `scheduled_start`, `scheduled_end`, `checked_in_at`, `first_capture_at`, `last_capture_at`, `closed_at`, `transmitted_at`, `state_resulted_at`, `fbi_resulted_at`, `purge_requested_at`, `routed_at`, `issued_at`, `challenge_filed_at`, `term_start`, `term_end` and any other datetime — is rendered as ISO 8601 UTC with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z`, for example `2026-06-02T10:00:00.000Z`. This holds regardless of how the value arrived: a request that supplies `2026-06-02T10:00:00Z` stores that instant and reads it back as `2026-06-02T10:00:00.000Z`.

Six fields are dates, not datetimes, and they are never widened into datetimes. `issued_on` and `due_on` on an invoice, `date_of_birth` and `id_document_expires_on` on an applicant, and `event_date` on a rap-back notification are all plain `YYYY-MM-DD` strings. `period_key` on an invoice is a plain `YYYY-MM` string. All six are returned exactly in those formats.

### 2.5. The Version-Bump Ledger

Version-bump granularity is specified here once, exhaustively, because most operations in this API write more than one row.

**The governing rule.** A row's `version` advances by exactly 1, and its `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock, in any request that changes at least one of its persisted field values — *once per request per row*, never once per cascade leg. A row touched by three different hops of the same cascade in one request still advances its `version` by exactly 1 for that request. A row that a request examines but does not change advances nothing.

**Reads never bump.** No `GET` advances any `version` or `updated_at`. This holds even for the reads that re-evaluate a clock-driven status: reading a rap-back subscription re-derives `ACTIVE` → `RENEWAL_DUE` and `RENEWAL_DUE` → `LAPSED` against the logical clock, reading a device re-derives `CALIBRATED` → `EXPIRED`, and reading a device assignment re-derives `SCHEDULED` → `ACTIVE` when the clock has entered its window — and none of those three system-driven re-derivations is a version-bumping mutation. The same holds for `aging_days` on an invoice, which is recomputed on every read.

The per-operation ledger follows. Rows created by a request start at `version` 1; that is a creation, not a bump.

- **Registering an agency, an ORI, a service type, a capture site, a device, an applicant** — one new row at `version` 1. No other row is touched.
- **Correcting an applicant** — the applicant row bumps once. No other row.
- **Suspending or reinstating an ORI's authority** — the ORI row bumps once. The owning agency does not bump.
- **Rostering a device assignment** — one new assignment row at `version` 1. Neither the device nor the site bumps.
- **Releasing a device assignment** — the assignment row bumps once. The site does not bump, because `billable_print_count` on the *assignment* is what is recomputed, not the site's counter.
- **Recording a calibration** — the device row bumps once. No card, assignment or site is touched, and in particular a card that an earlier audit already superseded is never restored and never bumps.
- **Running a calibration audit** — the device row bumps exactly once even though hop 1 rewrites its calibration fields and hop 6 writes `tainted_card_count`. Each device assignment that moves to `REVOKED_TAINTED` bumps once. Each fingerprint card that the sweep moves to `SUPERSEDED` bumps once. Each criminal-history response of those cards' submissions that moves to `SUPERSEDED` bumps once. Each fee assessment that moves to `CREDITED` bumps once. Each capture site whose `billable_print_count` is recomputed to a different number bumps once; a site whose recomputed count is unchanged does not bump. An assignment that moves to `REVOKED_TAINTED` with `tainted_print_count` left at 0 still bumps, because its `status` changed.
- **Booking an appointment** — one new appointment row at `version` 1. The site does not bump.
- **Rescheduling** — the appointment bumps once, covering both the new interval and the incremented `reschedule_count`.
- **Cancelling an appointment** — the appointment bumps once. No fee assessment is created, so no agency and no assessment row is touched; this is a named non-bump.
- **Marking a no-show** — the appointment bumps once. One new fee assessment row is created at `version` 1. The agency bumps only if `mtd_billed_cents` actually changes, which happens when the assessment's `payer_regime` is `AGENCY_BILLED` or `FEE_WAIVED`; for an `APPLICANT_PAID` no-show fee the agency does not bump.
- **Checking in** — the appointment bumps once.
- **Opening a fingerprint card** — one new card at `version` 1 and ten new position rows at `version` 1 each. The appointment bumps once (it moves to `IN_CAPTURE`). The device bumps once, because `capture_count` changes.
- **Recording a scored attempt** — the position row bumps once and the card bumps once, on every attempt, including an attempt that does not change any aggregate. This is why a client holding a card version from before a capture round cannot transmit that card.
- **Exception-coding a position** — the position row bumps once and the card bumps once.
- **Granting an exception override** — the card bumps once. No position row is touched.
- **Finalizing a card** — the card bumps once. The ten position rows are frozen against further writes but are not themselves rewritten, so they do not bump.
- **Transmitting a card** — one new submission at `version` 1 and one new fee assessment at `version` 1. The card bumps once (it moves to `SUBMITTED`). The appointment bumps once (it moves to `COMPLETED`). The capture site bumps once, because `billable_print_count` grows. The ORI bumps once **only when the transmission actually consumes transaction control numbers**; a reprint that preserves the original root consumes none, so on that branch the ORI is written by nothing and does not bump — a named non-bump. The agency bumps once when `mtd_billed_cents` changes, that is when the assessment's regime is `AGENCY_BILLED` or `FEE_WAIVED`, and does not bump at all under `APPLICANT_PAID`.
- **Posting a destination result** — the submission bumps once, covering the named axis, its result timestamp and the recomputed `composite_disposition`. On an acceptance, one new criminal-history response row is created at `version` 1 and is routed inside the same request, so its creation and its move to `ROUTED` together still leave it at `version` 1. On a rejection no response row exists to touch.
- **Authorizing a reprint** — the submission bumps once, covering `reprint_count` and, when the reprint is free, `free_reprint_count`. The original card bumps once on **every** reprint authorization, including the second and third: even when its `status` leg is a no-op because it is no longer `SUBMITTED`, its `superseded_by_card_id` is re-pointed at the newest replacement card, and that is a field change. Each criminal-history response of the original submission that moves to `SUPERSEDED` bumps once. One new replacement card is created at `version` 1 with ten new position rows at `version` 1.
- **Requesting a purge** — the submission bumps once.
- **Deciding a purge — deny** — the submission bumps once. Nothing else is touched: responses, subscriptions, notifications and determinations are all named non-bumps on this branch.
- **Deciding a purge — grant** — the submission bumps once even though hop 1 sets `retention_status` and hop 6 participates in the ledger reconciliation. Each criminal-history response moving from `RECEIVED` or `ROUTED` to `VOIDED` bumps once. The rap-back subscription moving to `TERMINATED` bumps once, covering both its `status` and the `notifications_voided_count` that hop 4 rewrites; a subscription already `CANCELLED` or `LAPSED` is untouched and does not bump. Each bound notification moving from `ROUTED` to `VOIDED` bumps once; a notification already `ACKNOWLEDGED` is untouched and does not bump. The fitness determination moving to `VACATED` bumps once; a determination already `VACATED` is untouched and does not bump. The ORI bumps once when `tcn_released_count` changes — which happens only when the purged submission is a root transmission — and does not bump when the purged submission is a suffix reprint, because a suffix reprint released nothing. The submission's fingerprint card moving to `SUPERSEDED` bumps once.
- **Issuing a fitness determination** — one new determination at `version` 1. The submission bumps once.
- **Filing a challenge** — the determination bumps once.
- **Resolving a challenge** — the determination bumps once, on both the reaffirming and the amending branch.
- **Subscribing to rap-back** — one new subscription at `version` 1. The submission does not bump.
- **Renewing a subscription** — the subscription bumps once.
- **Cancelling a subscription** — the subscription bumps once. Already-routed notifications are untouched and do not bump; this is a named non-bump.
- **Delivering a rap-back notification** — one new notification row at `version` 1. When it binds, the subscription bumps once because its `notifications_received_count` and `notifications_routed_count` change. When it lands orphaned there is no subscription to touch and nothing else bumps.
- **Acknowledging a notification** — the notification bumps once, and the subscription does not bump, because an acknowledgment moves a notification from `ROUTED` to `ACKNOWLEDGED` and both statuses are counted the same way by `notifications_routed_count`, so no stored counter changes value.
- **Reconciling a mobile event** — the site bumps once, covering `billable_print_count`, `shortfall_charge_cents` and `reconciliation_status`. Each device assignment released by the reconciliation bumps once. One fee assessment is created at `version` 1 **only when the shortfall charge is above zero**; when the shortfall is exactly zero no assessment row is created at all — a named non-creation. The agency bumps once when the shortfall assessment changes `mtd_billed_cents`, and not at all when no assessment was created.
- **Paying a fee assessment at the counter** — the assessment bumps once. No invoice and no agency is touched, because only `APPLICANT_PAID` assessments may be settled this way.
- **Opening an invoice** — one new invoice at `version` 1. The agency bumps once.
- **Issuing an invoice** — the invoice bumps once. Each attached fee assessment moving to `INVOICED` bumps once. The agency bumps once, because `outstanding_balance_cents` grows.
- **Paying an invoice** — the invoice bumps once. Each attached assessment moving from `INVOICED` to `PAID` bumps once. The agency bumps once when any of `outstanding_balance_cents`, `max_aging_days` or `billing_status` changes value, and does not bump when all three are already what the payment leaves them at.
- **Running the nightly batch** — this is the widest operation in the system, and it bumps strictly what it writes. An invoice bumps once if the run changes its `aging_days`, accrues a late fee, or moves its `status`; an invoice whose recomputed `aging_days` is unchanged and which accrues nothing does not bump. An agency bumps once if the run changes any of `outstanding_balance_cents`, `max_aging_days`, `billing_status` or `suspended_at`, and does not bump otherwise. Each appointment cancelled by an agency suspension cascade bumps once; appointments at fixed sites are untouched by that cascade and do not bump. Each subscription the run moves to `LAPSED` bumps once. Each submission whose purge request the run auto-denies bumps once. A batch run that finds nothing to do bumps nothing at all.
- **Setting the logical clock** — bumps nothing. No row is written.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock

The server holds a single logical clock and every temporal computation in this specification — window admission, expiry, aging, term arithmetic, retroactive sweeps and the `created_at` / `updated_at` stamps themselves — reads that clock. Wall-clock time is never consulted. Before any call sets it, the clock reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`.

A `VendorAdmin` moves the clock by posting `to_iso8601` to `POST /livescan/_clock/set_current_utc`. The response body is an object carrying a single `current_utc` string. Setting the clock writes no row and bumps no version; it simply changes what every subsequent computation sees. There is no restriction that the clock move only forwards.

Two conventions govern date arithmetic throughout. "Calendar days" means whole days added to or subtracted from an instant or a date, with no weekend or holiday skipping. "The clock date" means the UTC date part of the current logical clock instant, used whenever a date-typed field such as `id_document_expires_on` or `due_on` is compared against the clock.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write endpoints require the client to prove it has observed the current state of a *parent* row before it writes a *child*. Each takes a mandatory version field in its request body — mandatory, not optional: omitting it is a rung-5 body-validity failure, not a concurrency failure.

- `POST /livescan/submissions` requires `expected_card_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `FingerprintCard` named by `card_id`. If that number does not equal the card's current `version`, the transmission is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A card legitimately accepts more than one transmission over its life, and its `version` advances on every scored attempt, every exception coding, every override and every finalize, so a create built on a stale view of the card would otherwise mint a duplicate transaction control number for the same print.
- `POST /livescan/fitness-determinations` requires `expected_submission_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Submission` named by `submission_id`. A mismatch with the submission's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A submission's version advances when a destination result lands, when retention moves toward a purge, and when a reprint supersedes its responses — all of which change which criminal-history response may legitimately serve as `basis_response_id`, so an adjudicator acting on a stale read would pin the wrong basis.
- `POST /livescan/invoices` requires `expected_agency_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Agency` named by `agency_id`. A mismatch with the agency's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. An agency's version advances on every aging run, every payment and every posture change, so a create built on a stale agency view would attach the wrong assessments to the wrong period.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field of any kind, and no other endpoint may reject on version grounds.

### 2.8. Money, Percentages and Rounding

Every monetary amount in this API is an integer number of whole cents. There are no decimal currency values anywhere in a request or a response.

Wherever a percentage is applied to a money amount, the product is rounded to whole cents by the **round-half-up** rule: the fractional part is examined and a value of exactly one half rounds away from zero, so 162.365 becomes 162, 72.1375 becomes 72, and 176.5 would become 177. The rounding order is pinned and it matters: **each percentage component is rounded to whole cents on its own, before any component enters a sum**. A reading that adds the unrounded percentage products together and rounds the sum once is not what this API implements — for a transaction whose processing surcharge is 162.365 cents and whose vendor tax is 72.1375 cents, this API contributes 162 plus 72, that is 234 cents, and not the 235 cents that a single rounding of 234.5025 would produce.

One quantity in the system is not money and is not whole: `mean_quality_score` on a fingerprint card is a number carried to two decimal places, rounded half up.

### 2.9. The Ordered Validation Ladder

Every request is evaluated against an ordered ladder of forty rungs. The ladder is strictly ordered: the **first** rung whose condition fails determines the response, and no later rung is evaluated. Rungs whose scope does not include the endpoint being called are skipped without being evaluated. Three orderings in this ladder are deliberate and consequential, and they are called out where they occur: the agency billing freeze at rung 7 pre-empts every state, temporal and fiscal rung below it; the concurrency check at rung 9 pre-empts the state-precondition rung at 10, so a client holding a stale parent version learns that before it learns that the target is in the wrong state; and segregation of duties at rung 11 sits *below* the state rung, so an operator who tries to decide their own purge request on a submission that is not awaiting a decision is told about the state, not about the conflict of interest.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and non-empty. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, HTTP 401. Scope: every endpoint.
2. **Authorization.** `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared role names *and* that role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list in §4. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, HTTP 403. Scope: every endpoint.
3. **Path existence.** Every resource named by a path segment exists, validated left to right, so an outer identifier that does not resolve is reported before an inner one is even looked at. A fingerprint card named in a path that does not exist raises `ERR_CARD_NOT_FOUND`; a submission named in a path that does not exist raises `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND`; every other missing path resource — agency, ORI, service type, capture site, device, device assignment, applicant, appointment, position capture, criminal-history response, fitness determination, rap-back subscription, rap-back notification, fee assessment or invoice — raises `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`. All three carry HTTP 404. Scope: every endpoint that has a path parameter.
4. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to an existing row; otherwise `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`, HTTP 404. Scope: `POST /livescan/oris`, `POST /livescan/service-types`, `POST /livescan/sites`, `POST /livescan/devices`, `POST /livescan/device-assignments`, `POST /livescan/appointments`, `POST /livescan/cards`, `POST /livescan/submissions`, `POST /livescan/fitness-determinations`, `POST /livescan/rapback-subscriptions` and `POST /livescan/invoices`.
5. **Body validity.** Every required body field is present and carries its declared type, and every declared numeric range holds: a capture attempt's `score` is between 0 and 100 inclusive, a site's `concurrent_capacity` is between 1 and 50 inclusive, a mobile event's `min_billable_prints` is between 1 and 500 inclusive, a response's `record_count` is between 0 and 99 inclusive, its `disqualifying_offense_count` is at most its `record_count`, and any `amount_cents` is at least 1. A device assignment's `window_end` is strictly after its `window_start`, and a mobile event's `event_window_end` is strictly after its `event_window_start`. A site created with `site_kind` `MOBILE_EVENT` supplies all four of `host_agency_id`, `event_window_start`, `event_window_end` and `min_billable_prints`; a site created with `site_kind` `FIXED` supplies none of the four. Violations raise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, HTTP 400. This rung never checks whether a payment amount *matches* an outstanding balance — that is rung 40's business. Scope: every write endpoint.
6. **Enum membership.** This rung's list is exhaustive and closed. Exactly these request fields route here when they carry a value outside their declared member set: an agency's `payer_regime_default`; an ORI's `statutory_authority`; a service type's `applicant_type`, the members of its `required_document_codes` and the members of its `accepted_id_document_types`; a capture site's `site_kind`; an appointment's `booking_channel` and its `presented_document_type`; an applicant's `sex` and `id_document_type`; a position closure's `exception_code`; a destination result's `destination` and `result_code`; a fitness determination's `outcome` and a challenge resolution's `amended_outcome`; a challenge resolution's `resolution`; a purge decision's `decision`; a rap-back notification's `event_type`; and an ORI authority change's `to_status`. Violations raise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`, HTTP 422. **No other field routes here** — an unrecognized value in any field not on that list is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at rung 5. Scope: every write endpoint.
7. **Agency billing freeze.** The agency that owns the target ORI, or that hosts the target mobile-event site, does not have `billing_status` `SUSPENDED`. Violations raise `ERR_AGENCY_BILLING_SUSPENDED`, HTTP 403. The frozen and excluded sets are enumerated in full in §2.10.
8. **ORI authority.** The ORI the operation transmits under has `authority_status` `ACTIVE`. Otherwise `ERR_ORI_AUTHORITY_SUSPENDED`, HTTP 409. Scope: exactly four endpoints — `POST /livescan/submissions`, `POST /livescan/submissions/{id}/reprints`, `POST /livescan/rapback-subscriptions` and `POST /livescan/rapback-subscriptions/{id}/renew`.
9. **Parent version.** The supplied `expected_card_version`, `expected_submission_version` or `expected_agency_version` equals the parent's current `version`. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, HTTP 412. Scope: `POST /livescan/submissions`, `POST /livescan/fitness-determinations`, `POST /livescan/invoices`.
10. **State precondition.** The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared. An appointment in the wrong state raises `ERR_APPOINTMENT_STATE_INVALID`; a fingerprint card in the wrong state raises `ERR_CARD_STATE_INVALID`, and that includes any write attempted against a `SUPERSEDED` card; a submission, criminal-history response, fitness determination, rap-back subscription, rap-back notification, device assignment, fee assessment or invoice in the wrong state raises `ERR_RECORD_STATE_INVALID`. All three carry HTTP 409. Two carve-outs keep lower rungs reachable. *(a)* On `POST /livescan/submissions/{id}/destination-results` this rung ignores an `fbi_status` of `NOT_REQUIRED` entirely and fires only when the named axis has already reached a terminal value — that is, when a second result is posted for the same destination. A destination that is not in the submission's `destinations` at all is rung 27's business, not this rung's. *(b)* On `POST /livescan/rapback-subscriptions/{id}/renew` this rung fires only when the subscription's `status` is `LAPSED`, `CANCELLED` or `TERMINATED`; a subscription that is still `ACTIVE` but outside its renewal window is rung 32's business. Scope: every state-transition endpoint.
11. **Segregation of duties.** The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the recorded first actor: the exception-override actor must differ from the card's `captured_by_user_id`, the challenge resolver must differ from the determination's `issued_by_user_id`, and the purge decider must differ from the submission's `purge_requested_by_user_id`. Otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, HTTP 403. Scope: `POST /livescan/cards/{id}/exception-override`, `POST /livescan/fitness-determinations/{id}/challenge-resolutions`, `POST /livescan/submissions/{id}/purge-decisions`.
12. **Check-in window.** The logical clock is at or after the appointment's `scheduled_start` minus 30 minutes and at or before its `scheduled_start` plus 10 minutes, inclusive at both ends. Otherwise `ERR_CHECKIN_WINDOW_CLOSED`, HTTP 422. Scope: `POST /livescan/appointments/{id}/check-in`.
13. **Reschedule window.** The logical clock is at or before the appointment's current `scheduled_start` minus 24 hours. Otherwise `ERR_RESCHEDULE_WINDOW_CLOSED`, HTTP 422. Scope: `POST /livescan/appointments/{id}/reschedule`.
14. **Reschedule budget.** The appointment's `reschedule_count` is strictly less than 2. Otherwise `ERR_APPOINTMENT_STATE_INVALID`, HTTP 409. Scope: `POST /livescan/appointments/{id}/reschedule`.
15. **Identity document type.** The `presented_document_type` is a member of the appointment's service type's `accepted_id_document_types`. Otherwise `ERR_REQUIRED_DOCUMENT_MISSING`, HTTP 422. Scope: `POST /livescan/appointments/{id}/check-in`.
16. **Identity document currency.** The applicant's `id_document_expires_on` is on or after the clock date; a document expiring exactly today is still valid. Otherwise `ERR_IDENTITY_DOCUMENT_EXPIRED`, HTTP 422. Scope: `POST /livescan/appointments/{id}/check-in`.
17. **Required document set.** The supplied `presented_documents` contains every code listed in the service type's `required_document_codes`; extra codes are permitted. Otherwise `ERR_REQUIRED_DOCUMENT_MISSING`, HTTP 422. Scope: `POST /livescan/appointments/{id}/check-in`.
18. **Site capacity.** The candidate interval is admitted for its booking channel by the site concurrency rule described in §3.3. Otherwise `ERR_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 409. Scope: `POST /livescan/appointments`, `POST /livescan/appointments/{id}/reschedule`.
19. **Device calibration.** The capture device's `calibration_status` is `CALIBRATED`, which is to say the logical clock is at or before its `calibration_expires_at`. Otherwise `ERR_DEVICE_CALIBRATION_EXPIRED`, HTTP 409. Scope: `POST /livescan/cards`, `POST /livescan/cards/{id}/positions/{position_code}/attempts`.
20. **Mobile-event device binding.** When the card's site has `site_kind` `MOBILE_EVENT`, a device assignment whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` binds that device to that site and the logical clock lies inside that assignment's window. Otherwise `ERR_DEVICE_ASSIGNMENT_CONFLICT`, HTTP 409. Scope: `POST /livescan/cards`, `POST /livescan/cards/{id}/positions/{position_code}/attempts`.
21. **Roster overlap.** No conflicting window exists on the same device under the buffered-occupancy rule of §3.2. Otherwise `ERR_DEVICE_ASSIGNMENT_CONFLICT`, HTTP 409. Scope: `POST /livescan/device-assignments`.
22. **Position already closed.** The target position's `status` is not `PASSED` and not `EXCEPTION_CODED`. Otherwise `ERR_POSITION_ALREADY_CLOSED`, HTTP 409. Scope: `POST /livescan/cards/{id}/positions/{position_code}/attempts`, `POST /livescan/cards/{id}/positions/{position_code}/exception`.
23. **Attempt ceiling.** The target position's `attempt_count` is strictly less than 3. Otherwise `ERR_POSITION_ATTEMPT_CEILING_REACHED`, HTTP 409. Scope: `POST /livescan/cards/{id}/positions/{position_code}/attempts`.
24. **Hard exception ceiling.** The card's `positions_exception_count` plus one is at most 4. No override can lift this bound. Otherwise `ERR_EXCEPTION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 422. Scope: `POST /livescan/cards/{id}/positions/{position_code}/exception`.
25. **Soft exception ceiling at finalize.** Either the card's `positions_exception_count` is at most 2, or its `exception_override_by_user_id` is not null. Otherwise `ERR_EXCEPTION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 422. Scope: `POST /livescan/cards/{id}/finalize`.
26. **Card submittability.** Every one of the card's ten position rows is `PASSED` or `EXCEPTION_CODED`, and the card's `mean_quality_score` is at least 65. Otherwise `ERR_CARD_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`, HTTP 409. Scope: `POST /livescan/cards/{id}/finalize`.
27. **Destination authorization.** The `destination` named in the request is a member of the submission's `destinations`. Otherwise `ERR_DESTINATION_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, HTTP 422. Scope: `POST /livescan/submissions/{id}/destination-results`.
28. **Retention permission.** A `retention_requested` of true requires both the ORI's `retention_permitted` and the service type's `retention_offered` to be true. Otherwise `ERR_RETENTION_NOT_PERMITTED`, HTTP 422. Scope: `POST /livescan/submissions`.
29. **Retention immutability.** When the transmitted card carries a `reprint_of_submission_id`, the supplied `retention_requested` must equal the original submission's `retention_requested`. Otherwise `ERR_RETENTION_IMMUTABLE`, HTTP 409. Scope: `POST /livescan/submissions`, and only when the card's `reprint_of_submission_id` is not null.
30. **Rap-back authority.** The submission's ORI has `rapback_authority` true. Otherwise `ERR_RAPBACK_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, HTTP 403. This rung sits deliberately above the retention rung below it: an ORI without federal reach can never reach a granted retention at all, so checking retention first would make this error unreachable. Scope: `POST /livescan/rapback-subscriptions`, `POST /livescan/rapback-subscriptions/{id}/renew`.
31. **Rap-back requires retention.** The subscribed submission's `retention_status` is `GRANTED`, `PURGE_REQUESTED` or `PURGE_DENIED`. Otherwise `ERR_RAPBACK_REQUIRES_RETENTION`, HTTP 409. Scope: `POST /livescan/rapback-subscriptions`.
32. **Renewal window.** The subscription is inside its renewal window — its `status` is `RENEWAL_DUE`, which is to say the clock is at or after `term_end` minus 60 days and at or before `term_end`. Otherwise `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`, HTTP 422. Scope: `POST /livescan/rapback-subscriptions/{id}/renew`.
33. **Notification routability.** The notification's `subscription_id` is not null and its `status` is `ROUTED`. Otherwise `ERR_NOTIFICATION_UNROUTABLE`, HTTP 409. Scope: `POST /livescan/rapback-notifications/{id}/acknowledge`.
34. **Dissemination entitlement.** An `include_detail` of true is honoured only for an `AgencyAdjudicator` reading a response whose ORI's `dissemination_tier` is `FULL_CHR`; every other combination of role and tier is refused with `ERR_DISSEMINATION_NOT_ENTITLED`, HTTP 403. Scope: `GET /livescan/chr-responses/{id}`.
35. **Challenge window.** The logical clock is at or before the determination's `issued_at` plus 21 days. Otherwise `ERR_CHALLENGE_WINDOW_CLOSED`, HTTP 422. Scope: `POST /livescan/fitness-determinations/{id}/challenges`.
36. **Challenge budget.** The determination's `challenge_count` is strictly less than 2. Otherwise `ERR_CHALLENGE_LIMIT_REACHED`, HTTP 409. Scope: `POST /livescan/fitness-determinations/{id}/challenges`.
37. **Reprint entitlement.** The submission has at least one destination axis reading `REJECTED_TECHNICAL` or `REJECTED_ADMIN`. Otherwise `ERR_REPRINT_NOT_ENTITLED`, HTTP 422. Scope: `POST /livescan/submissions/{id}/reprints`.
38. **Reprint budget.** The submission's `reprint_count` is strictly less than 3. Otherwise `ERR_REPRINT_LIMIT_REACHED`, HTTP 409. Scope: `POST /livescan/submissions/{id}/reprints`.
39. **Payer regime.** The assessment's `payer_regime` is `APPLICANT_PAID`; assessments under `AGENCY_BILLED` or `FEE_WAIVED` are settled through their invoice and never at the counter. Otherwise `ERR_PAYER_REGIME_MISMATCH`, HTTP 422. Scope: `POST /livescan/fee-assessments/{id}/payments`.
40. **Payment exactness.** The supplied `amount_cents` equals the fee assessment's `total_cents` minus its `paid_cents` exactly, or the invoice's `total_cents` minus its `paid_cents` exactly. Neither underpayment nor overpayment is accepted. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, HTTP 400. Scope: `POST /livescan/fee-assessments/{id}/payments`, `POST /livescan/invoices/{id}/payments`.

### 2.10. The Agency Billing Freeze and Its Exclusion List

When an agency's `billing_status` reaches `SUSPENDED`, a write gate closes over every ORI that agency owns and over every mobile event that agency hosts. The gate is **not** universal, and it must not be implemented as "every write is blocked". It is a closed enumeration of thirteen frozen operations and a closed enumeration of the operations that stay open.

**Frozen while the owning agency is suspended** — these thirteen return `ERR_AGENCY_BILLING_SUSPENDED` with HTTP 403:

`POST /livescan/service-types`, `POST /livescan/appointments`, `POST /livescan/cards`, `POST /livescan/cards/{id}/positions/{position_code}/attempts`, `POST /livescan/cards/{id}/positions/{position_code}/exception`, `POST /livescan/cards/{id}/exception-override`, `POST /livescan/cards/{id}/finalize`, `POST /livescan/submissions`, `POST /livescan/submissions/{id}/reprints`, `POST /livescan/rapback-subscriptions`, `POST /livescan/rapback-subscriptions/{id}/renew`, `POST /livescan/fitness-determinations`, and `POST /livescan/device-assignments`.

**Explicitly excluded — allowed to proceed normally while suspended**: every `GET` in the API without exception, plus `POST /livescan/rapback-notifications`, `POST /livescan/rapback-notifications/{id}/acknowledge`, `POST /livescan/fitness-determinations/{id}/challenges`, `POST /livescan/fitness-determinations/{id}/challenge-resolutions`, `POST /livescan/submissions/{id}/purge-requests`, `POST /livescan/submissions/{id}/purge-decisions`, `POST /livescan/submissions/{id}/destination-results`, `POST /livescan/rapback-subscriptions/{id}/cancel`, `POST /livescan/appointments/{id}/cancel`, `POST /livescan/appointments/{id}/no-show`, `POST /livescan/appointments/{id}/check-in`, `POST /livescan/device-assignments/{id}/release`, `POST /livescan/sites/{id}/event-reconciliation`, `POST /livescan/invoices`, `POST /livescan/invoices/{id}/issue`, `POST /livescan/invoices/{id}/payments`, `POST /livescan/fee-assessments/{id}/payments`, `POST /livescan/devices/{id}/calibration`, `POST /livescan/devices/{id}/calibration-audit`, `POST /livescan/oris/{id}/authority-status`, `POST /livescan/_ops/aging-run` and `POST /livescan/_clock/set_current_utc`.

The shape of the exclusion list is the policy: an already-checked-in applicant may still be finished up to the point of capture, an applicant's appeal rights survive their agency's delinquency, destinations may still deliver results and rap-back events, and the vendor may still take the money that lifts the suspension. What is frozen is new intake and new transmission — the work the vendor would otherwise perform on credit.

Note also that the freeze is agency-wide but keyed to the *owning* agency of the resource in hand. Suspending one agency has no effect on the ORIs of any other agency, and a technician may continue to capture and transmit for a solvent agency at the same site on the same device in the same minute.

### 2.11. Conservation Invariants

Four ledgers must balance at all times. Each is stated as an equality that holds after every request that could disturb it, and each is reconciled inside the same request that disturbs it — never by a later sweep.

**Transaction control number conservation.** For each ORI, the number of transaction control numbers consumed minus the number released — that is, `tcn_consumed_count` minus `tcn_released_count` — equals the total number of destinations across that ORI's *root* submissions that have not been purged. A root submission is one whose `tcn_suffix` is 0. Suffix reprints appear on neither side of this equality: they never consumed an allocation, so purging one releases nothing. Checked after every transmission, every reprint and every granted purge.

**Billable print conservation.** For each capture site, the site's `billable_print_count` plus the sum of `tainted_print_count` over every device assignment at that site equals the number of fingerprint cards at that site whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `CLOSED` or `REJECTED_CLOSED`, together with those whose `status` is `SUPERSEDED` with a `taint_reason` of `CALIBRATION_LAPSE`. A lapse audit therefore moves each tainted card from the billable side of the ledger to the tainted side, leaving the sum unchanged. Checked after every transmission, every calibration audit and every event reconciliation.

**Fee component conservation.** For a fee assessment, `total_cents` equals the sum of `state_fee_cents`, `federal_fee_cents`, `processing_surcharge_cents`, `vendor_convenience_fee_cents`, `vendor_tax_cents`, `trip_surcharge_cents` and `reprint_fee_cents`, less `waived_cents` and less `discount_cents`. Additionally, once an assessment's `status` is `PAID` or `CREDITED`, its `paid_cents` plus its `credited_cents` equals its `total_cents` exactly — a vendor-borne credit must equal the total precisely, so the payer's net is zero. Checked after every assessment creation, payment and credit. **An open point the specification does not resolve:** the two assessment kinds that carry no statutory or vendor components — a no-show fee and an onsite shortfall charge — are specified in §3.9 as stamping `total_cents` directly from a flat fee or a shortfall computation while all seven named components remain 0. Under one reading the component equality above is scoped only to the `SUBMISSION` and `REPRINT` kinds, and a no-show row legitimately reads `total_cents` 1500 against seven zero components; under the other reading the equality is universal and those two kinds would have to carry their charge inside one of the named components. This document pins neither reading.

**Notification routing conservation.** For each rap-back subscription, `notifications_received_count` equals `notifications_routed_count` plus `notifications_voided_count`, where the routed counter counts bound notifications whose `status` is `ROUTED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`. Orphaned notifications — those whose `subscription_id` is null — are counted on neither side of that equality, and are not counted by `notifications_received_count` either. The purge cascade moves routed rows to voided, which leaves the sum unchanged. Checked after every notification arrival, acknowledgment and purge.

### 2.12. Nested Paths and Left-to-Right Resolution

Several resources are addressed through nested paths — the ten finger positions of a card at `/livescan/cards/{id}/positions/{position_code}`, the responses of a submission at `/livescan/submissions/{id}/responses`. Path parameters are always resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the error. Requesting an attempt against position `R_THUMB` of a card that does not exist returns `ERR_CARD_NOT_FOUND` and never a position error, because the card segment is resolved first. The `{position_code}` segment carries one of the ten finger position codes — `R_THUMB`, `R_INDEX`, `R_MIDDLE`, `R_RING`, `R_LITTLE`, `L_THUMB`, `L_INDEX`, `L_MIDDLE`, `L_RING` and `L_LITTLE` — and a code outside that set, or a code that names a position belonging to a different card, resolves to no row and therefore raises `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`.

Nesting also implies scoping: a position reached through a card's path must belong to that card, and a criminal-history response reached through a submission's path must belong to that submission.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Agencies, ORIs and the Authority Profile

A `VendorAdmin` opens the relationship by registering the agency. The registration carries the agency's legal `name` and its vendor billing `account_code`, which must be unique across all agencies. Two optional settings may accompany it: `payer_regime_default`, which is `APPLICANT_PAID` when omitted and is otherwise `AGENCY_BILLED` or `FEE_WAIVED`, and `invoice_terms_days`, the calendar-day payment terms stamped onto each invoice at issue, which is 30 when omitted and must fall between 1 and 90 inclusive. Everything else about an agency is derived: `billing_status` starts at `CURRENT`, `outstanding_balance_cents` and `max_aging_days` and `mtd_billed_cents` all start at 0, and `suspended_at` starts null and holds the logical-clock instant at which the agency's posture most recently became `SUSPENDED`.

Under an agency hang its ORIs, and the ORI is where the statute enters the system. Registration supplies the owning `agency_id`, the nine-character `ori_code` — exactly nine characters, unique across all ORIs — the `statutory_authority` the ORI submits under, and optionally `fee_waiver_eligible`, which is false when omitted and records whether a statutory fee waiver may be elected for submissions under this ORI. The `statutory_authority` is one of `CRIMINAL_JUSTICE`, `NONCRIMINAL_STATUTORY` or `NONCRIMINAL_NOFED`, and from that single value four further properties are derived once, at registration, and never change again:

- `CRIMINAL_JUSTICE` yields `destination_requirement` `STATE_AND_FBI`, `dissemination_tier` `FULL_CHR`, `retention_permitted` true and `rapback_authority` true.
- `NONCRIMINAL_STATUTORY` yields `destination_requirement` `STATE_AND_FBI`, `dissemination_tier` `FITNESS_CODE_ONLY`, `retention_permitted` true and `rapback_authority` true.
- `NONCRIMINAL_NOFED` yields `destination_requirement` `STATE_ONLY`, `dissemination_tier` `FITNESS_CODE_ONLY`, `retention_permitted` false and `rapback_authority` false.

Those three rows are the complete mapping; no other combination of the four derived values exists. If a registration request supplies `destination_requirement`, `dissemination_tier`, `retention_permitted` or `rapback_authority` directly, the supplied values are ignored and the derived ones are stored. An ORI additionally carries `authority_status`, which starts at `ACTIVE`, and two ledger counters that both start at 0: `tcn_consumed_count`, the number of transaction control numbers this ORI's transmissions have consumed — one per destination per allocating transmission — and `tcn_released_count`, the number released back by purge reconciliation.

Transmission authority can be pulled and restored. A `VendorAdmin` supplies `to_status` naming either `SUSPENDED` or `ACTIVE`; the ORI moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` only from `ACTIVE`, and `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` only from `SUSPENDED`. While an ORI sits at `SUSPENDED`, transmissions and rap-back operations under it are refused with `ERR_ORI_AUTHORITY_SUSPENDED`, and — this is the point of the axis — the other ORIs of the same agency remain fully usable. Suspending one ORI is a scalpel; suspending an agency's billing posture is a hammer.

Each ORI then carries service types, and a service type is what an applicant actually books. It names its `ori_id`, a short service `code` unique within that ORI, and an `applicant_type` drawn from `TEACHER`, `VOLUNTEER`, `HEALTHCARE`, `FIREARMS` and `EMPLOYMENT_OTHER`. It fixes the appointment length through `slot_duration_minutes`, which must be exactly 15, 30 or 45. It fixes the paperwork through two arrays: `required_document_codes`, a non-empty subset of `PHOTO_ID`, `SSN_CARD`, `AGENCY_AUTH_FORM` and `LIVESCAN_REQUEST_FORM`, every member of which must be produced at check-in; and `accepted_id_document_types`, a non-empty subset of `DRIVER_LICENSE`, `PASSPORT`, `STATE_ID` and `MILITARY_ID`, which constrains which identity document the counter will take. It fixes the statutory money through `state_fee_cents` and `federal_fee_cents`, each between 0 and 20000 inclusive; the federal component is charged only when the transmission actually includes the FBI destination. Finally, `retention_offered` — false when omitted — records whether retention may be elected for this service type at all; a retention election needs *both* this flag and the ORI's `retention_permitted`. A service type's `status` starts at `ACTIVE` and may read `RETIRED`, in which case it refuses new appointments; no operation in this specification moves a service type to `RETIRED`, so in practice it stays `ACTIVE` for its whole life.

### 3.2. Capture Sites, Scanners and the Roster

A capture site is either a fixed vendor storefront or a mobile onsite session hosted at an agency's own address, and `site_kind` — `FIXED` or `MOBILE_EVENT` — decides which. Both kinds carry a `name` and a `concurrent_capacity` between 1 and 50 inclusive, the maximum number of appointment intervals that may overlap any single instant at that site. From that capacity a second ceiling is derived at creation for every site of either kind: `walkin_usable_capacity` is `concurrent_capacity` reduced by twenty percent of `concurrent_capacity` rounded **up** to a whole number. A site with a capacity of 5 therefore reserves 1 slot and admits 4 walk-ins; a site with a capacity of 10 reserves 2 and admits 8; a site with a capacity of 1 reserves 1 and admits none.

A mobile event carries four further inputs, all four required and all four forbidden on a fixed site: the `host_agency_id` whose address the crew visits, an `event_window_start` and an `event_window_end` with the end strictly after the start, and `min_billable_prints` between 1 and 500 inclusive, the contracted minimum the agency has agreed to pay for. A mobile event begins life with `reconciliation_status` `PENDING`, moving `NOT_APPLICABLE → PENDING` at creation; a fixed site stays at `NOT_APPLICABLE` for ever, which is why that value is not a terminal one. Both kinds start with `billable_print_count` and `shortfall_charge_cents` at 0.

Scanners are registered with a `serial_number` unique across all devices, a `home_site_id` naming the site the device normally lives at, and `calibrated_at`, the instant of its most recent calibration certificate. The registration stores that instant as `last_calibrated_at` and derives `calibration_expires_at` as that instant plus 180 days. The device's `calibration_status` starts at `CALIBRATED` and is re-derived against the logical clock on every read and on every capture attempt: once the clock passes `calibration_expires_at`, the device reads `EXPIRED` and further capture attempts on it are refused with `ERR_DEVICE_CALIBRATION_EXPIRED`. A third value, `QUARANTINED`, is reached only through the retroactive lapse audit described in §3.8. Two counters ride along: `capture_count`, the number of fingerprint cards ever opened on this device in any status, and `tainted_card_count`, the number of cards this device's lapse audits have superseded.

Recording a fresh calibration takes a new `calibrated_at` and is admitted only when the device's `calibration_status` is `EXPIRED` or `QUARANTINED`; it moves the device `EXPIRED → CALIBRATED` or `QUARANTINED → CALIBRATED`, rewrites `last_calibrated_at` to the supplied instant and `calibration_expires_at` to that instant plus 180 days. It never un-supersedes a card that an earlier audit tainted. **A point the specification leaves open, rendered here without adjudication:** the `QUARANTINED → CALIBRATED` leg additionally declares that the supplied `calibrated_at` must not be earlier than the device's current `last_calibrated_at`, while the `EXPIRED → CALIBRATED` leg does not restate that ordering condition — so one reading applies the ordering condition to both legs and the other applies it only when the device is coming out of quarantine. Relatedly, the ladder's state rung names a dedicated error code for appointments and for fingerprint cards and a general record-state code for eight other entity types, but names no code at all for a device, an ORI, a service type or a capture site whose current state does not admit a requested transition; the general record-state code `ERR_RECORD_STATE_INVALID` at HTTP 409 and the body-validity code `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at HTTP 400 are both readings the scaffold's own text supports, and this document pins neither.

Rostering puts a device at a site for a window. The roster entry names its `device_id` and `site_id` and its `window_start` and `window_end`, the end strictly after the start, and it starts at `status` `SCHEDULED`. At creation it also copies the device's `calibration_expires_at` into `calibration_expires_at_snapshot` — a historical record only. When a lapse audit later runs, it compares captures against the device's *live, recomputed* expiry, never against this snapshot; the snapshot exists so an auditor can see what the roster believed at the time. Two counters start at 0: `billable_print_count`, the number of cards on this device at this site whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `CLOSED` or `REJECTED_CLOSED`, and `tainted_print_count`, the number of cards on this device at this site that a lapse audit superseded.

A device may serve only one site at a time, and it needs travel time between engagements. A candidate roster window conflicts with an existing entry for the same device whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` when the candidate's `window_start` falls strictly before that entry's `window_end` extended by 30 minutes, **and** the candidate's `window_end` extended by 30 minutes falls strictly after that entry's `window_start`. Entries already `RELEASED` or `REVOKED_TAINTED` free their windows entirely and are never considered. The buffer boundary is strict in the operator's favour: a gap of exactly 30 minutes between two windows is accepted, a gap of 29 minutes is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_ASSIGNMENT_CONFLICT`.

A roster entry moves `SCHEDULED → ACTIVE` on its own, without any call, as soon as the logical clock is at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end`; that is what makes captures on that device at that site permitted. A `SiteSupervisor` may release an entry from either `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE`. Releasing from `ACTIVE` recomputes the entry's `billable_print_count` over the cards on that device at that site whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `CLOSED` or `REJECTED_CLOSED`; releasing from `SCHEDULED` frees the window so that a later overlapping roster entry is accepted. Both legs land on `RELEASED`, which is terminal, as is `REVOKED_TAINTED`.

Closing out a mobile event is the `VendorAdmin`'s job and it is permitted only when the site's `site_kind` is `MOBILE_EVENT`, the logical clock has reached or passed `event_window_end`, and `reconciliation_status` is still `PENDING`. The reconciliation moves the site `PENDING → RECONCILED` — and `RECONCILED` is terminal, so an event may be closed out exactly once — recomputes `billable_print_count` as the number of cards at that site whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `CLOSED` or `REJECTED_CLOSED` — cards in `SUPERSEDED`, whether from a lapse audit or a purge, are excluded, which is precisely what allows a lapse audit to manufacture a shortfall after the crew has gone home — and then computes the charge. The shortfall is the event's `min_billable_prints` less the recomputed `billable_print_count`, floored at zero, multiplied by 2200 cents per missing print, and the result is stamped as `shortfall_charge_cents`. The minimum is per event, not per device: an event with a `min_billable_prints` of 25 that produced 26 billable cards across two rostered scanners — twenty on one and six on the other — clears its minimum and is charged nothing, whereas the reading that split the minimum across the rostered devices as thirteen each would have charged seven missing prints against the under-used scanner. An event with a `min_billable_prints` of 25 that produced 18 billable cards is charged seven times 2200, that is 15400 cents. When the shortfall is above zero, the reconciliation creates exactly one fee assessment whose `assessment_kind` is `ONSITE_SHORTFALL` and whose `payer_regime` is `AGENCY_BILLED`; when the shortfall is exactly zero, it creates none. Finally, every roster entry at that site whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` is moved to `RELEASED`.

### 3.3. Applicants, Booking and the Counter

Registering an applicant is deliberately permissive, and one omission is load-bearing. The registration carries `full_name`, `date_of_birth` as a plain `YYYY-MM-DD` string, `sex` as one of `M`, `F` or `X`, the `id_document_type` the applicant intends to present — `DRIVER_LICENSE`, `PASSPORT`, `STATE_ID` or `MILITARY_ID` — and `id_document_expires_on` as a plain date. `place_of_birth`, the state or country of birth, is optional. From those four demographic values the server derives `demographics_complete`, which is true exactly when `full_name`, `date_of_birth`, `sex` and `place_of_birth` are all present and non-empty, and false otherwise; because `place_of_birth` may be omitted, an applicant can be registered, booked, captured and transmitted with incomplete demographics — and an administrative rejection from a destination is exactly what cites that omission weeks later. Either the applicant themselves or a `LivescanTechnician` at the counter may register the record, and either may correct it afterwards by supplying any of `place_of_birth`, `id_document_type` or `id_document_expires_on`; a correction recomputes `demographics_complete`.

Booking names the `applicant_id`, the `site_id` where the capture happens, the `service_type_id` that fixes duration, paperwork, fees and ORI, and a `scheduled_start`. An optional `booking_channel` chooses between `BOOKED`, the default, and `WALK_IN`. The server derives `scheduled_end` by adding the service type's `slot_duration_minutes` to `scheduled_start`, and every appointment interval is treated as half-open — it includes its start instant and excludes its end instant, so an appointment ending at 10:30 and one beginning at 10:30 do not overlap and adjacency is free.

Capacity is evaluated over intervals, not over slots. For a candidate interval at a site, count the appointments at that same site, other than the candidate itself, whose `status` is `BOOKED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `IN_CAPTURE` and whose interval genuinely overlaps the candidate's — that is, whose `scheduled_start` is strictly before the candidate's `scheduled_end` and whose `scheduled_end` is strictly after the candidate's `scheduled_start`. Appointments already `CANCELLED`, `NO_SHOW` or `COMPLETED` have released their intervals and are not counted. If the candidate's `booking_channel` is `BOOKED`, that count plus one must not exceed the site's `concurrent_capacity`; if it is `WALK_IN`, that count plus one must not exceed the site's `walkin_usable_capacity`. Either violation is `ERR_SLOT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Consider a site with a `concurrent_capacity` of 5, and therefore a `walkin_usable_capacity` of 4, already carrying four booked thirty-minute appointments across 10:00 to 10:30. A fifth `BOOKED` appointment in that window is accepted, because five does not exceed five; a `WALK_IN` in the same window is refused, because five exceeds four. That is the whole point of the reserve: the walk-in channel may never consume the last fifth of the floor.

An appointment's own lifecycle starts at `BOOKED` with `reschedule_count` 0, `presented_document_type` and `presented_documents` and `checked_in_at` all null, and `no_show_fee_assessed` false. Rescheduling is the one cycle in this axis: it loops `BOOKED → BOOKED`, is available to the applicant or to a technician, and takes a single new `scheduled_start`. It is admitted only while the clock is at or before the appointment's *current* `scheduled_start` minus 24 hours, only while `reschedule_count` is below 2, and only when the new interval clears the capacity rule for the target site — with the appointment's own current interval excluded from the count, so that moving an appointment thirty minutes later does not have it collide with itself. Acceptance rewrites `scheduled_start`, re-derives `scheduled_end` from the service type's `slot_duration_minutes`, and increments `reschedule_count` by one. A third reschedule attempt is `ERR_APPOINTMENT_STATE_INVALID`; an attempt inside the final 24 hours is `ERR_RESCHEDULE_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

Cancellation reaches the terminal `CANCELLED` from two places. From `BOOKED` the applicant or a technician may cancel unconditionally. From `CHECKED_IN` only a technician may cancel, and only while no fingerprint card has yet been opened for that appointment. Both legs release the interval back to the site's capacity pool, and — stated explicitly because the parallel branch does bill — **cancellation creates no fee assessment of any kind**.

Check-in is a technician-only operation that moves the appointment `BOOKED → CHECKED_IN` and unlocks capture. It is admitted only while the logical clock lies between the `scheduled_start` less 30 minutes and the `scheduled_start` plus 10 minutes, inclusive at both ends; outside that band it is `ERR_CHECKIN_WINDOW_CLOSED`. It carries the `presented_document_type` actually produced and a `presented_documents` array of the document codes actually produced. Three checks then run in this order and each has its own code: the presented identity document type must be a member of the service type's `accepted_id_document_types`, failing which the response is `ERR_REQUIRED_DOCUMENT_MISSING`; the applicant's `id_document_expires_on` must be on or after the clock date, with a document expiring exactly today still valid and an earlier one failing as `ERR_IDENTITY_DOCUMENT_EXPIRED`; and the presented document set must cover every code in the service type's `required_document_codes`, failing which the response is again `ERR_REQUIRED_DOCUMENT_MISSING`. A successful check-in stamps `checked_in_at` with the logical clock and records both `presented_document_type` and `presented_documents`.

An applicant who does not appear is written off. A technician may mark a still-`BOOKED` appointment `BOOKED → NO_SHOW` once the logical clock has passed the `scheduled_start` plus 10 minutes — the same grace boundary the check-in window ends on, so there is no instant at which both operations are available. The no-show releases the interval, sets `no_show_fee_assessed` to true, and creates exactly one fee assessment whose `assessment_kind` is `NO_SHOW` and whose `total_cents` is the flat 1500 cents, with `payer_regime` copied from the owning agency's `payer_regime_default`. That assessment is not returned by the no-show response; it is found by filtering the fee-assessment listing on `appointment_id`.

### 3.4. Capture: Ten Positions, Two Ceilings and One Mean

A technician opens a fingerprint card against a `CHECKED_IN` appointment, naming that `appointment_id` and the `device_id` of the scanner the whole card will be captured on; an optional `reprint_of_submission_id` marks the card as the replacement print for an earlier transmission and is null on a first print. Opening the card moves the appointment `CHECKED_IN → IN_CAPTURE`, creates the card at `status` `OPEN`, copies the appointment's site into the card's `site_id` for onsite billable-print accounting, stamps `captured_by_user_id` with the acting technician's `X-User-ID`, and creates all ten `PositionCapture` rows at once, each at `status` `PENDING`. The ten `position_code` values are fixed and complete: `R_THUMB`, `R_INDEX`, `R_MIDDLE`, `R_RING`, `R_LITTLE`, `L_THUMB`, `L_INDEX`, `L_MIDDLE`, `L_RING`, `L_LITTLE`.

A freshly opened card carries `positions_passed_count` 0, `positions_exception_count` 0, `mean_quality_score` 0.00, `worst_position_score` null, `submittable` false, `exception_override_by_user_id` null, `superseded_by_card_id` null, `free_reprint_granted` false, `taint_reason` null, and both `first_capture_at` and `last_capture_at` null. Each position row carries `attempt_count` 0, `best_score` null, `exception_code` null and `closed_at` null.

Scoring is per position and bounded. A scored attempt supplies a single `score` between 0 and 100 inclusive. Whatever the outcome, the attempt increments the position's `attempt_count` by one and sets `best_score` to the larger of its previous value and the new score — the first attempt simply sets it. A score of at least 60 closes the position at `PASSED` and stamps `closed_at`; the position reaches `PASSED` either directly from `PENDING` or from `FAILED_RETRYABLE`. A score below 60 leaves the position open at `FAILED_RETRYABLE` — moving `PENDING → FAILED_RETRYABLE` on the first such attempt and looping `FAILED_RETRYABLE → FAILED_RETRYABLE` on the second — as long as the attempt just recorded is not the third. The third consecutive sub-60 attempt is different: it moves the position `FAILED_RETRYABLE → CEILING_REACHED`, leaving `attempt_count` at 3, and any further attempt against that position is refused with `ERR_POSITION_ATTEMPT_CEILING_REACHED`. A position at `CEILING_REACHED` is not closed and leaves the whole card unsubmittable until it is exception-coded. An attempt or an exception aimed at a position that is already `PASSED` or `EXCEPTION_CODED` is refused with `ERR_POSITION_ALREADY_CLOSED`; those two are the terminal values of the position axis.

Where a finger cannot be captured at all, it is closed administratively instead. Exception coding supplies an `exception_code` of `AMPUTATED`, `BANDAGED` or `UNCLASSIFIABLE`; it is admitted from `PENDING` — including before any attempt has ever been scored, which is the natural path for an amputated digit — and from `FAILED_RETRYABLE` and from `CEILING_REACHED`. It sets `exception_code`, stamps `closed_at`, moves the position to `EXCEPTION_CODED`, and removes that position from the quality mean entirely. It is bounded by a hard ceiling that nothing can lift: if the card's `positions_exception_count` plus the position being coded would exceed 4, the request is refused with `ERR_EXCEPTION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

The card's own axis follows the positions. The first scored attempt on a card moves it `OPEN → CAPTURING` and stamps **both** `first_capture_at` and `last_capture_at`. The first *exception* on a card that has never had a scored attempt also moves it `OPEN → CAPTURING`, but on that path `first_capture_at` stays null and only `last_capture_at` is stamped — a distinction that matters enormously in §3.8, because a card with a null `first_capture_at` is invisible to the retroactive calibration sweep. Every subsequent attempt, exception or supervisor override loops the card `CAPTURING → CAPTURING` and recomputes its aggregates. Opening a card and recording an attempt both require the device's `calibration_status` to be `CALIBRATED`, and at a mobile-event site both additionally require a roster entry whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` binding that device to that site with the logical clock inside its window.

The aggregates are recomputed on every attempt, every exception coding and at finalize, from the ten position rows of that one card:

- `positions_passed_count` is the number of positions whose `status` is `PASSED`.
- `positions_exception_count` is the number whose `status` is `EXCEPTION_CODED`.
- `mean_quality_score` is the arithmetic mean of `best_score` over the `PASSED` positions only, carried to two decimal places and rounded half up. Exception-coded positions are excluded from both the numerator and the divisor. When no position has passed, it reads 0.00.
- `worst_position_score` is the smallest `best_score` among the `PASSED` positions, and is null when no position has passed.
- `submittable` is true when all three of the following hold: every one of the ten positions has reached `PASSED` or `EXCEPTION_CODED`; `positions_exception_count` is at most the applicable exception ceiling, which is 4 when `exception_override_by_user_id` is not null and 2 otherwise; and `mean_quality_score` is at least 65.

That last clause is the governing quality rule and it is stricter than it looks. Eight positions passing at 68 with the remaining two exception-coded give a mean of 68.00 over a divisor of eight, not ten, and the card is submittable. Ten positions each passing at 62 give a mean of 62.00 and the card is **not** submittable, even though every individual position cleared the 60 pass threshold — the rejected reading, in which it is the lowest passing score rather than the mean that must clear a bar, would wave that card through, and this API does not.

Exceeding two exception-coded positions requires a supervisor. A `SiteSupervisor` grants the override on a card that is `CAPTURING` and whose `positions_exception_count` already exceeds 2, supplying a written `justification`. The acting supervisor's `X-User-ID` must differ from the card's `captured_by_user_id`, or the request is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the technician who took the prints may not be the person who blesses their shortfall. Acceptance stores that `X-User-ID` in `exception_override_by_user_id` and raises the applicable ceiling from 2 to 4. The hard ceiling of 4 still stands: an override never permits a fifth exception-coded position.

Finalizing closes the card. A technician moves it `CAPTURING → READY_FOR_SUBMISSION`, which freezes all ten position rows against any further attempt or exception. Three conditions guard it. Every position must be `PASSED` or `EXCEPTION_CODED`, failing which the response is `ERR_CARD_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`. The `positions_exception_count` must be at most 2 unless `exception_override_by_user_id` is set, failing which the response is `ERR_EXCEPTION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. And `mean_quality_score` must be at least 65, failing which the response is again `ERR_CARD_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`.

### 3.5. Transmission, Transaction Control Numbers and Destination Results

Transmitting is the technician's last act on a card. The request names the `card_id` of a card sitting at `READY_FOR_SUBMISSION`, the irrevocable `retention_requested` election, and the `expected_card_version` described in §2.7. Acceptance moves the card `READY_FOR_SUBMISSION → SUBMITTED`, moves its appointment `IN_CAPTURE → COMPLETED`, adds one to the capture site's `billable_print_count`, creates the submission row, and creates exactly one fee assessment for it.

The submission copies its `ori_id` from the ORI of the card's appointment's service type — that is the resolution chain, and it is resolved at transmission, not earlier. Its `destinations` come straight from that ORI's `destination_requirement`: an ORI at `STATE_ONLY` transmits to the single destination `STATE`, and an ORI at `STATE_AND_FBI` transmits to both `STATE` and `FBI`. The `state_status` axis starts at `PENDING` in every case. The `fbi_status` axis starts at `PENDING` for a two-destination ORI, but for a `STATE_ONLY` ORI it is set to `NOT_REQUIRED` at creation and never leaves that value — and, as a direct consequence, that submission's fee assessment carries a `federal_fee_cents` of 0. The `composite_disposition` starts at `INCOMPLETE`.

Transaction control numbers are a scarce ledgered resource and their allocation is exact. On a first print — a submission with no `original_submission_id` — the server counts the existing submissions of this ORI whose `tcn_suffix` is 0, adds one, and forms `tcn_root` as the ORI's `ori_code`, then a hyphen, then that sequence number zero-padded to six digits. The `tcn_suffix` is 0, and the ORI's `tcn_consumed_count` increases by the number of entries in `destinations` — two for a state-and-FBI ORI, one for a state-only ORI. An ORI with the code `CA0111111` transmitting its first card therefore mints `CA0111111-000001`.

On a reprint the arithmetic branches. The server finds the head of the reprint chain — the submission with `tcn_suffix` 0 that the chain descends from — and asks two questions. Is the logical clock at or before that original's `transmitted_at` plus 90 days, the boundary being inclusive so that a reprint exactly 90 days later still qualifies? And is the next suffix, which is the original submission's `reprint_count` as it stands after the authorization already incremented it — so the first reprint takes 1, the second 2 — at most 2? If both hold, the reprint **preserves** the root: it copies the original's `tcn_root`, takes that next suffix, and consumes no new allocation at all, leaving `tcn_consumed_count` untouched. If either fails, the reprint mints a brand-new root exactly as a first print would, with `tcn_suffix` 0 and `tcn_consumed_count` increased by the number of destinations. The rendered `tcn_full` is the bare `tcn_root` when `tcn_suffix` is 0, and otherwise the root, a hyphen, and the suffix digit. The submission also records `reprint_sequence`, which is 0 on a first print and otherwise equals that same next-suffix number — 1, then 2, then 3 for the third reprint — and `original_submission_id`, which is null on a first print and otherwise names the chain head.

Take the concrete case. An original is transmitted on 2026-06-01 by ORI `CA0111111` as `CA0111111-000001`. A technical rejection arrives on 2026-07-11, forty days later. A reprint authorized on 2026-07-21 — day fifty — is inside the 90-day preservation window and takes `CA0111111-000001-1`, consuming no new number. Had the same reprint been authorized instead on 2026-09-10 — day one hundred and one — it would fall outside the window, mint the fresh root `CA0111111-000002` with suffix 0, and consume as many further numbers as the ORI has destinations.

Results arrive one destination at a time, from the `StateRepository` role, and each posting advances **only** the axis it names. The posting supplies the `destination` — `STATE` or `FBI` — and a `result_code` of `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED_TECHNICAL` or `REJECTED_ADMIN`, together with the optional payload fields `record_count`, `disqualifying_offense_count`, `record_detail` and `rejection_reason`. A destination that is not among the submission's `destinations` is refused with `ERR_DESTINATION_NOT_AUTHORIZED`; a second result for an axis that has already reached a terminal value is refused with `ERR_RECORD_STATE_INVALID`.

On the state axis, `PENDING → ACCEPTED` stamps `state_resulted_at` with the logical clock, creates exactly one criminal-history response for the `STATE` destination, derives that response's fitness code, and routes it. `PENDING → REJECTED_TECHNICAL` stamps `state_resulted_at` — which is what anchors the free-reprint window — and, stated explicitly because the sibling branch does the opposite, **creates no response row at all**: only an acceptance yields one. `PENDING → REJECTED_ADMIN` also stamps `state_resulted_at`, creates no response, and additionally marks the submission ineligible for a no-fee reprint however quickly that reprint is authorized. On the FBI axis the three legs mirror those: `PENDING → ACCEPTED` stamps `fbi_resulted_at` and creates one criminal-history response for the `FBI` destination; `PENDING → REJECTED_TECHNICAL` stamps `fbi_resulted_at` and creates none; `PENDING → REJECTED_ADMIN` stamps `fbi_resulted_at`, creates none, and blocks the no-fee entitlement for the federal leg. Every one of the six recomputes the composite disposition.

That composite is derived, never supplied, and it is recomputed on every result and on every read of the submission. Let "the FBI is required" mean that `FBI` appears in `destinations`. The derivation runs in this order:

1. If `state_status` is still `PENDING`, or the FBI is required and `fbi_status` is still `PENDING`, the disposition is `INCOMPLETE`. A `STATE_ONLY` submission with an accepted state result is therefore never held at `INCOMPLETE` by its `NOT_REQUIRED` federal axis.
2. Otherwise, call the state axis rejected when it reads `REJECTED_TECHNICAL` or `REJECTED_ADMIN`, and call the FBI axis rejected when the FBI is required and it reads `REJECTED_TECHNICAL` or `REJECTED_ADMIN`.
3. If the state axis is rejected and either the FBI axis is also rejected or the FBI is not required at all, the disposition is `REJECTED`.
4. If exactly one of the two axes is rejected and the other is not, the disposition is `MIXED`.
5. Otherwise neither is rejected, and the disposition turns on whether any record came back: if any criminal-history response of this submission whose `routing_status` is `RECEIVED` or `ROUTED` carries a `record_count` of at least 1, the disposition is `HIT`; otherwise it is `CLEARED`. Responses that have been moved to `VOIDED` or `SUPERSEDED` are excluded from that test, so a purge or a reprint can turn a `HIT` back into a `CLEARED`.

A record count of exactly zero is a clearance, not a hit, even though a response row exists. Three worked cases pin the shape: a state-and-FBI ORI whose state axis accepted with `record_count` 0 and whose FBI axis rejected technically reads `MIXED`; a state-and-FBI ORI whose state axis accepted with `record_count` 2 and whose FBI axis accepted with `record_count` 0 reads `HIT`; a state-only ORI whose state axis accepted with `record_count` 0 and whose federal axis reads `NOT_REQUIRED` reads `CLEARED`.

Once every required destination has returned a non-rejection result — that is, once `composite_disposition` becomes `CLEARED` or `HIT` — the fingerprint card behind the submission moves `SUBMITTED → CLOSED`, and no further capture on that card is possible.

### 3.6. Dissemination, Fitness Determination and Challenge

A criminal-history response is created only by an acceptance, and it belongs to exactly one `submission_id` and one `destination`. It carries the `record_count` the destination returned, between 0 and 99 inclusive; the `disqualifying_offense_count`, the number of those records that are statutorily disqualifying, which may never exceed `record_count`; and `record_detail`, the verbatim rap-sheet text. From the first two the server derives `fitness_code` by a strict order of tests: if `disqualifying_offense_count` is at least 1 the code is `INELIGIBLE`; otherwise, if `record_count` is at least 1 the code is `ELIGIBLE_WITH_NOTICE`; otherwise the code is `ELIGIBLE`. Both boundaries sit at one — zero records is `ELIGIBLE`, and one record carrying zero disqualifying offences is `ELIGIBLE_WITH_NOTICE`.

Routing happens inside the same request that creates the response. The row is created at `routing_status` `RECEIVED` and, provided its parent submission's `retention_status` is not `PURGED`, immediately moves `RECEIVED → ROUTED`, stamping `routed_at` and setting `routed_recipient_roles`. That array is the same for both dissemination tiers: every routed response is routed to both `AgencyAdjudicator` and `Applicant`.

What differs by tier is not who receives the response but *what they see when they read it*. The stored row is never mutated by a read; the projection is computed per request from the reading role, the ORI's `dissemination_tier`, and the optional `include_detail` flag on the single-response read:

- An `AgencyAdjudicator` reading a response under a `FULL_CHR` ORI sees `fitness_code`, `record_count` and `disqualifying_offense_count`, and additionally sees `record_detail` when — and only when — `include_detail` is true.
- An `AgencyAdjudicator` reading a response under a `FITNESS_CODE_ONLY` ORI sees `fitness_code` alone; `record_count`, `disqualifying_offense_count` and `record_detail` all read null. Asking for `include_detail` here is refused with `ERR_DISSEMINATION_NOT_ENTITLED`.
- An `Applicant` sees `fitness_code` alone under either tier, and asking for `include_detail` is refused with `ERR_DISSEMINATION_NOT_ENTITLED`.
- A `VendorAdmin` sees only the operational shell — `destination`, `routing_status` and `routed_at` — with `fitness_code` reading null, and asking for `include_detail` is refused with `ERR_DISSEMINATION_NOT_ENTITLED`.
- Regardless of role, once a response's `routing_status` is `VOIDED`, its `record_detail` reads null and its `record_count` reads 0.

**A point left open, rendered without adjudication:** the projection rule above is declared for the single-response read, which is the one operation that accepts `include_detail` and the one operation the entitlement rung guards. The submission-scoped listing of responses — available to `AgencyAdjudicator`, `VendorAdmin` and `StateRepository` — accepts no `include_detail` parameter and has no projection rule declared for it, and the `StateRepository` role does not appear in the projection mapping at all. One reading applies the same per-role projection to the listing; the other returns the stored rows unprojected there. This document pins neither.

Adjudication is the agency's decision, not the destination's. An `AgencyAdjudicator` issues a fitness determination against a `submission_id`, supplying an `outcome` of `FIT`, `NOT_FIT` or `FIT_WITH_CONDITIONS` together with the `expected_submission_version` of §2.7. Issuance pins `basis_response_id` to the submission's `STATE` criminal-history response whose `routing_status` is `ROUTED`, copies `ori_id` from the submission, stamps `issued_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and `issued_at` with the logical clock, and starts the determination at `status` `ISSUED` with `challenge_count` 0, `challenge_filed_at` null and `resolved_by_user_id` null.

The applicant may push back, twice at most. Filing a challenge supplies written `grounds` and is admitted from `ISSUED` and from `REAFFIRMED`; it is refused with `ERR_CHALLENGE_WINDOW_CLOSED` once the logical clock has passed `issued_at` plus 21 days, and with `ERR_CHALLENGE_LIMIT_REACHED` once `challenge_count` has reached 2. Note that the 21-day window is anchored to the **original** issuance instant and is never re-anchored by a reaffirmation, so a slow first cycle can close the door on the second. Filing moves the determination `ISSUED → CHALLENGED` or `REAFFIRMED → CHALLENGED`, increments `challenge_count`, stamps `challenge_filed_at`, and suspends the determination's effect while it stands.

Resolution is a different adjudicator's job — the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `issued_by_user_id`, or the request is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The resolver supplies a `resolution` of either `REAFFIRM` or `AMEND`, and on the amending branch also an `amended_outcome`. `REAFFIRM` moves the determination `CHALLENGED → REAFFIRMED`, sets `resolved_by_user_id`, clears `challenge_filed_at`, and re-opens the cycle so long as `challenge_count` remains below 2. `AMEND` moves it `CHALLENGED → AMENDED`, overwrites `outcome` with the supplied `amended_outcome`, sets `resolved_by_user_id`, and is terminal — an amended determination admits no further challenge. `VACATED` is the other terminal value, and it is reached only through the retention purge of §3.7.

### 3.7. Retention, Purge and Continuous Rap-Back

Retention is the third axis a submission carries, and it moves entirely independently of the two destination axes. It starts at `NOT_REQUESTED`. A `retention_requested` of true at transmission moves it `NOT_REQUESTED → GRANTED`, and that election requires two permissions to line up: the ORI's `retention_permitted` must be true *and* the service type's `retention_offered` must be true. If either is false the transmission is refused with `ERR_RETENTION_NOT_PERMITTED`. The election is irrevocable — there is no operation that changes it — and when a replacement card from a reprint is later transmitted, it must carry exactly the same `retention_requested` value as its original submission, or the transmission is refused with `ERR_RETENTION_IMMUTABLE`. A granted retention is what unlocks rap-back subscription for that submission.

Destruction of a retained record is a two-person process. The applicant themselves or an `AgencyAdjudicator` files a purge request with a written `reason`; it is admitted from `GRANTED` and from `PURGE_DENIED`, and it stamps `purge_requested_at` with the logical clock and `purge_requested_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`. Filing again after a denial is the cycle in this axis: `PURGE_DENIED → PURGE_REQUESTED` re-stamps both fields and clears `purge_decided_by_user_id` back to null, so the next decision starts clean.

A `VendorAdmin` then decides, supplying a `decision` of `GRANT` or `DENY`. On either branch the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from the recorded `purge_requested_by_user_id`, or the request is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A denial moves `PURGE_REQUESTED → PURGE_DENIED`, stamps `purge_decided_by_user_id`, and — stated explicitly against the grant branch below — leaves every criminal-history response, every subscription, every notification and every determination completely untouched. A request that is never decided is denied by default: the nightly batch moves any submission whose `purge_requested_at` is more than 14 days behind the logical clock from `PURGE_REQUESTED` to `PURGE_DENIED`, and marks the automatic nature of that denial by setting `purge_decided_by_user_id` to null.

A grant, by contrast, is the widest cascade in the system. It runs six hops in this order, all inside the one request:

1. The submission's `retention_status` becomes `PURGED` — a terminal value — and `purge_decided_by_user_id` is stamped with the deciding `X-User-ID`.
2. Because the retention authority is revoked, every criminal-history response of this submission whose `routing_status` is `RECEIVED` or `ROUTED` moves to `VOIDED` and has its `record_detail` cleared to null. Responses already `VOIDED` or `SUPERSEDED` are left alone.
3. Because the retained responses are void, the rap-back subscription of this submission moves to `TERMINATED` if its `status` is `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_DUE`. A subscription already `CANCELLED` or `LAPSED` is left exactly as it is — this leg does not fire for it.
4. Because the subscription terminated, every rap-back notification bound to it whose `status` is `ROUTED` moves to `VOIDED`, and the subscription's `notifications_voided_count` is updated accordingly. A notification that was already `ACKNOWLEDGED` stays `ACKNOWLEDGED` — this leg does not fire for it — and an orphaned notification has no subscription to be reached through at all.
5. Because its evidentiary basis is void, the fitness determination whose `basis_response_id` names one of the responses voided in hop 2 moves to `VACATED`, provided its `status` is `ISSUED`, `CHALLENGED`, `REAFFIRMED` or `AMENDED`. Vacating from `ISSUED` clears `outcome` to null, because the determination has lost the record it rested on. Vacating from `CHALLENGED` additionally abandons the open challenge without a resolution and clears `challenge_filed_at`. A determination already `VACATED` is untouched.
6. Because the record itself is gone, the ORI's `tcn_released_count` increases by the number of entries in the submission's `destinations` — but only when the purged submission is a root transmission, one whose `tcn_suffix` is 0. Purging a suffix reprint releases nothing, because a preserved-root reprint never consumed an allocation. In the same hop, the submission's fingerprint card moves to `SUPERSEDED` with a `taint_reason` of `PURGED_RECORD`.

Rap-back is the other thing retention buys. An `AgencyAdjudicator` subscribes against a `submission_id`; the subscription copies `ori_id` from the submission, sets `term_start` to the logical clock at subscribe time, and derives `term_end` as `term_start` plus 365 days. It starts `ACTIVE` with `renewal_count` 0 and all three notification counters — `notifications_received_count`, `notifications_routed_count` and `notifications_voided_count` — at 0. Two guards apply in this order: the submission's ORI must have `rapback_authority` true, failing which the answer is `ERR_RAPBACK_NOT_AUTHORIZED`; and the submission's `retention_status` must be `GRANTED`, `PURGE_REQUESTED` or `PURGE_DENIED`, failing which the answer is `ERR_RAPBACK_REQUIRES_RETENTION`. That ordering is deliberate: an ORI without rap-back authority is also an ORI whose submissions can never reach a granted retention, so testing retention first would make the authority error impossible to reach.

The term is a fixed day count, not a calendar anniversary, and the difference bites in leap years. A term starting `2027-03-01T00:00:00Z` runs 365 days to `2028-02-29T00:00:00Z`, and its renewal window opens 60 days earlier at `2027-12-31T00:00:00Z`. Under the rejected reading — same month and day one year later — that term would end `2028-03-01T00:00:00Z`, and a renewal attempted at `2028-02-29T12:00:00Z` would be inside the window; under the rule this API implements, that attempt arrives after `term_end` and the subscription has lapsed.

The subscription's status is re-derived against the logical clock on every read and on every renewal attempt. It moves `ACTIVE → RENEWAL_DUE` once the clock is at or after `term_end` less 60 days and at or before `term_end`, both boundaries inclusive; that is what unlocks renewal. It moves to `LAPSED` once the clock passes `term_end`, and it does so from `RENEWAL_DUE` **and** directly from `ACTIVE` — the second leg exists precisely so that a subscription nobody ever read during its renewal window still lapses rather than hanging at `ACTIVE` for ever. `LAPSED` is terminal and can never be renewed; recovering monitoring after a lapse requires a fresh print and a fresh transmission.

Renewing is admitted only while the subscription reads `RENEWAL_DUE`, and only while the ORI's `rapback_authority` is true and the parent submission's `retention_status` is still exactly `GRANTED`. It restarts the term from the renewal instant rather than extending the old one: `term_start` becomes the logical clock, `term_end` becomes that instant plus 365 days, `renewal_count` increases by one, and the subscription returns `RENEWAL_DUE → ACTIVE`. A renewal attempted against a still-`ACTIVE` subscription outside its window is `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`; a renewal attempted against a `LAPSED`, `CANCELLED` or `TERMINATED` subscription is `ERR_RECORD_STATE_INVALID`. An adjudicator may also cancel from either `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_DUE`, which closes the subscription — before its renewal decision, if it was due — while leaving already-routed notifications exactly as they are; later events for that submission simply land as orphans.

Notifications arrive from the destinations. Each carries the `ori_code` the destination stamped on it — a plain string, not a foreign key — the `subject_submission_tcn`, which is the `tcn_full` of the retained transmission the event concerns, an `event_type` of `ARREST`, `DISPOSITION` or `WARRANT`, and an `event_date` as a plain date string. Binding is decided at creation and only at creation: the server looks for a subscription whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_DUE`, whose parent submission's `tcn_full` equals the supplied `subject_submission_tcn`, and whose ORI's `ori_code` equals the supplied `ori_code`. If one is found the notification is created at `ROUTED` with `subscription_id` set, and that subscription's `notifications_received_count` is incremented. If none is found the notification is created at `ORPHANED` with `subscription_id` null; it is retained for audit and can never be acknowledged. The fork happens in the creating request; there is no later re-binding.

Acknowledging is the adjudicator's receipt. It is admitted only when the notification's `status` is `ROUTED` and its `subscription_id` is not null, moves it `ROUTED → ACKNOWLEDGED`, and stamps `acknowledged_by_user_id`. Acknowledging an `ORPHANED` or `VOIDED` notification is `ERR_NOTIFICATION_UNROUTABLE`. Acknowledgment is one of the operations explicitly excluded from the agency billing freeze: a destination's warrant notice must be actionable whether or not the agency has paid its invoices.

### 3.8. Rejections, Reprints and the Retroactive Calibration Sweep

A rejected transmission has to be re-run, and who pays turns on two facts decided at the moment of authorization. A `SiteSupervisor` authorizes the reprint against the rejected submission, naming the `device_id` the replacement print will be captured on. Two guards run first: the submission must have at least one destination axis reading `REJECTED_TECHNICAL` or `REJECTED_ADMIN`, failing which the answer is `ERR_REPRINT_NOT_ENTITLED`; and the submission's `reprint_count` must be below 3, failing which the answer is `ERR_REPRINT_LIMIT_REACHED`. A submission whose state axis is `ACCEPTED` and whose federal axis is `ACCEPTED` or `NOT_REQUIRED` has nothing to reprint and is likewise `ERR_REPRINT_NOT_ENTITLED`.

Entitlement to a *free* reprint is then evaluated once, at that instant, and stamped onto the replacement card as `free_reprint_granted`; the fee assessment created when that card is later transmitted reads the stamped flag rather than re-evaluating the window. A reprint is free when three things hold together: at least one destination returned `REJECTED_TECHNICAL`; the logical clock is at or before the anchor instant plus 30 days, the boundary inclusive so that a reprint exactly 30 days after the rejection is still free; and the submission's `free_reprint_count` is below 2. The anchor is the result timestamp of the earliest destination that returned a technical rejection, and when both destinations returned one it is `state_resulted_at`, because the state axis is evaluated first. An administrative rejection never earns a free reprint however quickly the reprint is authorized: if no destination returned `REJECTED_TECHNICAL` and at least one returned `REJECTED_ADMIN`, the reprint is chargeable. Note what the pinned anchor buys: a card captured on day 0 and rejected on day 40 is still free to reprint on day 50, because the clock runs from the rejection, not from the capture. Under the rejected reading — 45 days measured from the card's `first_capture_at` — that same day-50 reprint would be chargeable at the 1800-cent reprint fee.

The authorization itself does five things. It creates a replacement fingerprint card at `OPEN` with ten fresh `PENDING` position rows, reusing the original appointment and stamping the new card's `reprint_of_submission_id` with the rejected submission. It stamps that replacement card's `captured_by_user_id` with the acting supervisor's `X-User-ID` — which means a later exception override on the replacement card needs a *third* person, because the override actor must differ from the recorded capturer. It increments the submission's `reprint_count`, and, when the reprint is free, also its `free_reprint_count`. It moves the original card `SUBMITTED → REJECTED_CLOSED` when that card is still `SUBMITTED` — on the second and third reprint of the same submission that leg is a no-op, because the card is already `REJECTED_CLOSED` — and in every case re-points the original card's `superseded_by_card_id` at the **newest** replacement. And it moves the original submission's criminal-history responses from `ROUTED` to `SUPERSEDED`, so that only the reprint's own responses remain routable.

**A point the specification leaves open, rendered without adjudication:** the fee rule of §3.9 names the reprint authorization among the four operations that create a fee assessment, while the reprint authorization's own effect list creates only the replacement card and the transmission of a replacement card is separately described as creating a `REPRINT`-kind assessment that reads the stamped `free_reprint_granted`. One reading therefore places the `REPRINT` assessment at the authorization; the other places it at the transmission of the replacement card. This document pins neither.

The other way a card dies is retroactive, and it is the sharpest edge in the system. When a `VendorAdmin` discovers that a scanner's calibration certificate had actually lapsed earlier than the record showed, they run a calibration audit supplying `corrected_last_calibrated_at`. Two conditions gate it: the corrected instant must be at or before the device's current `last_calibrated_at` — an audit may only move the true calibration instant **backwards**, and a forward correction is refused as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and the logical clock must already be past that corrected instant plus 180 days, which is to say the corrected certificate must already have lapsed.

The audit then runs six hops, all in one request:

1. The device's `last_calibrated_at` is rewritten to `corrected_last_calibrated_at`, its `calibration_expires_at` is recomputed as that instant plus 180 days, and its `calibration_status` moves `CALIBRATED → QUARANTINED`.
2. Because the device is quarantined, every roster entry on it whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` moves to `REVOKED_TAINTED`.
3. Because those roster entries are revoked, every fingerprint card on this device whose `first_capture_at` is **strictly after** the newly recomputed `calibration_expires_at`, and whose `status` is `OPEN`, `CAPTURING`, `READY_FOR_SUBMISSION`, `SUBMITTED`, `CLOSED` or `REJECTED_CLOSED`, moves to `SUPERSEDED` with a `taint_reason` of `CALIBRATION_LAPSE`. The sweep processes them ordered by `first_capture_at` ascending and, where those tie, by `created_at` ascending. Two exclusions are exact and load-bearing: a card whose `first_capture_at` is null — the exception-only card of §3.4, which was never scored — is never swept, and a card whose `first_capture_at` equals the recomputed expiry exactly is **not** tainted, because the comparison is strictly after.
4. Because those cards are void, every criminal-history response belonging to their submissions moves to `SUPERSEDED`, from `RECEIVED` or from `ROUTED` alike, and can no longer support any fitness determination.
5. Because the prints must be redone at vendor cost, every `SUBMISSION`-kind or `REPRINT`-kind fee assessment of those cards' submissions whose `status` is `ASSESSED` or `INVOICED` moves to `CREDITED` with `credited_cents` set equal to its `total_cents`, so the applicant or agency owes exactly nothing. Where such an assessment was already attached to an invoice, that invoice's `subtotal_cents` and `total_cents` drop by the credited amount and the owning agency's `outstanding_balance_cents` is recomputed. A card swept while still `CAPTURING` produces **no** credit assessment at all, because no submission fee was ever assessed against it — that leg simply does not fire.
6. Because the billable set has shrunk, each affected capture site's `billable_print_count` is recomputed and each revoked roster entry's `tainted_print_count` is set to the number of cards swept on that device at that site. A roster entry revoked from `SCHEDULED` keeps a `tainted_print_count` of 0, because a scheduled window has no captures behind it yet. Finally the device's `tainted_card_count` is set to the number of cards hop 3 moved.

Two downstream consequences deserve naming. First, because a superseded card no longer counts toward a site's billable prints, a lapse audit run after a mobile event has been reconciled cannot revise that event's charge, but an audit run *before* reconciliation can push a comfortably-over-minimum event into a shortfall. Second, a fresh calibration recorded afterwards clears the quarantine for future work but never restores a card the audit tainted.

### 3.9. Fees, Invoicing, Aging and the Delinquency Posture

Four operations create money rows, and each creates exactly one: transmitting a card, authorizing a reprint, marking a no-show, and reconciling a mobile event. Every fee assessment records who it belongs to and why. Its `agency_id` names the agency that owns the ORI or hosted the event. Its `submission_id` names the transmission it settles and is null for `NO_SHOW` and `ONSITE_SHORTFALL` rows. Its `appointment_id` is set for `SUBMISSION`, `REPRINT` and `NO_SHOW` rows and null for `ONSITE_SHORTFALL` rows. Its `invoice_id` is null until the row is attached to an agency invoice. Its `assessment_kind` is `SUBMISSION`, `REPRINT`, `NO_SHOW` or `ONSITE_SHORTFALL`, and its `payer_regime` — `APPLICANT_PAID`, `AGENCY_BILLED` or `FEE_WAIVED` — is copied from the owning agency's `payer_regime_default` at the instant of creation and never re-read afterwards. It starts at `status` `ASSESSED` with `paid_cents` and `credited_cents` both 0.

The total is assembled in a pinned order, and each stage is worth stating on its own.

**The component stage.** A reprint counts as free when its `assessment_kind` is `REPRINT` and the card being transmitted carries `free_reprint_granted` true. On a free reprint the statutory components are dropped and nothing else is: a free reprint re-transmits a transaction the destinations already refused, so they do not charge again, but the vendor still did the work. Concretely, `state_fee_cents` is 0 on a free reprint and is otherwise the service type's `state_fee_cents` for a `SUBMISSION` or `REPRINT` row and 0 for any other kind. `federal_fee_cents` is 0 on a free reprint and is otherwise the service type's `federal_fee_cents` — but only for a `SUBMISSION` or `REPRINT` row whose submission has `FBI` among its `destinations`, and 0 in every other case. `processing_surcharge_cents` is three and a half percent of the sum of those two statutory components, rounded half up to whole cents. `vendor_convenience_fee_cents` is a flat 995 cents on a `SUBMISSION` or `REPRINT` row and 0 otherwise, and it is never covered by a statutory waiver. `vendor_tax_cents` is seven and a quarter percent of that convenience fee, rounded half up on its own. `trip_surcharge_cents` is 500 cents when the capture site's `site_kind` is `MOBILE_EVENT` and the row is a `SUBMISSION` or `REPRINT`, and 0 otherwise. `reprint_fee_cents` is 1800 cents when the row is a `REPRINT` that is *not* free, and 0 on a free reprint and on every first print.

**The two flat kinds.** A `NO_SHOW` row takes `total_cents` of exactly 1500 and an `ONSITE_SHORTFALL` row takes `total_cents` equal to the site's computed `shortfall_charge_cents`. Both skip the waiver, the invoiceable base and the volume discount entirely — but both still run the ledger step below, so an agency-side flat charge does move that agency's discount tier for later assessments.

**Step one, the statutory waiver.** The statutory subtotal is `state_fee_cents` plus `federal_fee_cents` plus `processing_surcharge_cents`. When the `payer_regime` is `FEE_WAIVED`, `waived_cents` is set to that whole statutory subtotal and the statutory contribution afterwards is zero; under any other regime `waived_cents` is 0 and the statutory subtotal survives in full. The waiver reaches exactly those three components and no others.

**Step two, the invoiceable base.** The base is the post-waiver statutory contribution plus `vendor_convenience_fee_cents` plus `vendor_tax_cents` plus `trip_surcharge_cents` plus `reprint_fee_cents`.

**Step three, the agency volume discount.** The tier is read from the agency's `mtd_billed_cents` **as it stands before this assessment joins it** — the order is pinned precisely because the figure the tier is keyed on is a running total this very row will become part of. A `mtd_billed_cents` of at least 200000 selects six percent; otherwise a `mtd_billed_cents` of at least 40000 selects three percent; otherwise the discount is zero. Both floors are inclusive: exactly 40000 selects the three-percent tier and exactly 200000 selects the six-percent tier. `discount_cents` is that percentage of the base, rounded half up — but only when the `payer_regime` is `AGENCY_BILLED` or `FEE_WAIVED`; under `APPLICANT_PAID` the discount is 0, because the agency is not paying.

**Step four, the total.** `total_cents` is the base less `discount_cents`.

**Step five, the ledger.** When the `payer_regime` is `AGENCY_BILLED` or `FEE_WAIVED`, `total_cents` is added to the agency's `mtd_billed_cents`. Under `APPLICANT_PAID` nothing is added.

Work a real service type through it: `state_fee_cents` 3289, `federal_fee_cents` 1350, a state-and-FBI ORI, a fixed site, a first print. The processing surcharge is three and a half percent of 4639, which is 162.365 and rounds to 162. The vendor tax is seven and a quarter percent of 995, which is 72.1375 and rounds to 72. Under `APPLICANT_PAID` the total is 3289 plus 1350 plus 162 plus 995 plus 72, that is 5868 cents. Under `AGENCY_BILLED` against an agency standing at `mtd_billed_cents` 60000, the tier is three percent, the discount is three percent of 5868, which is 176.04 and rounds to 176, and the total is 5692. Under `FEE_WAIVED` against that same agency, `waived_cents` is 4801, the base is 1067, the discount is three percent of 1067, which is 32.01 and rounds to 32, and the total is 1035. Now reprint that submission at a fixed site under `APPLICANT_PAID`: with `free_reprint_granted` true the statutory components vanish and the total is 995 plus 72, that is 1067; with `free_reprint_granted` false the total is 3289 plus 1350 plus 162 plus 995 plus 72 plus 1800, that is 7668.

Two rejected readings are worth naming because both look plausible. Adding the unrounded percentage products together and rounding once would make the surcharge-and-tax contribution 235 rather than 234, so the `APPLICANT_PAID` total would come out 5869 instead of 5868 — this API rounds per component. And applying the volume discount to the pre-waiver base would make the `FEE_WAIVED` discount 176 rather than 32, so that total would come out 891 instead of 1035 — this API waives first and discounts afterwards.

**A point left open, rendered without adjudication:** the ledger step above describes `mtd_billed_cents` as a running accumulator that each agency-side assessment adds its `total_cents` to, while the tier lookup describes the same field as the sum of `total_cents` over that agency's assessments with `payer_regime` `AGENCY_BILLED` or `FEE_WAIVED` created in the current calendar month of the logical clock, excluding `CREDITED` rows. The two descriptions diverge when the logical clock crosses a month boundary and when a row is later credited: the accumulator neither resets nor subtracts, the month-scoped sum does both. This document pins neither reading.

Applicant-borne assessments are settled at the counter. A `LivescanTechnician` or the applicant supplies `amount_cents`; the assessment's `payer_regime` must be `APPLICANT_PAID`, failing which the answer is `ERR_PAYER_REGIME_MISMATCH`, and the amount must equal `total_cents` less `paid_cents` exactly, failing which the answer is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Acceptance sets `paid_cents` to `total_cents` and moves the assessment `ASSESSED → PAID`.

Agency-borne assessments are settled through an invoice. A `VendorAdmin` opens one against an `agency_id` for a `period_key` in `YYYY-MM` form — unique per agency among that agency's invoices that are not `WRITTEN_OFF` — together with the `expected_agency_version` of §2.7. It starts at `DRAFT` with `issued_on` and `due_on` null and `subtotal_cents`, `late_fee_cents`, `total_cents`, `paid_cents` and `aging_days` all 0.

Issuing it moves `DRAFT → ISSUED` and is admitted only when at least one fee assessment exists whose `status` is `ASSESSED`, whose `payer_regime` is `AGENCY_BILLED` and whose `agency_id` matches the invoice's. Every such assessment is attached, ordered by `created_at` ascending, each having its `invoice_id` set and its `status` moved `ASSESSED → INVOICED`. `issued_on` is stamped with the logical-clock date and `due_on` is derived as `issued_on` plus the agency's `invoice_terms_days` calendar days. `subtotal_cents` is recomputed as the sum of `total_cents` over the attached assessments whose `status` is `INVOICED` or `PAID`, `total_cents` is recomputed as `subtotal_cents` plus `late_fee_cents`, and the agency's `outstanding_balance_cents` grows by that total. Note the scope of the attachment rule: only `AGENCY_BILLED` assessments are attached, so an assessment created under `FEE_WAIVED` is reached by neither the counter path — rung 39 refuses it — nor the invoice path, and remains at `ASSESSED`. **This is an open point** and this document pins neither reading: the attachment condition names `AGENCY_BILLED` alone, while the `invoice_id` field is described as null for `APPLICANT_PAID` rows, which reads as though `FEE_WAIVED` rows were expected to be attached too.

Aging is computed on the calendar. An invoice whose `status` is neither `ISSUED` nor `PAST_DUE`, or whose `due_on` is null, has `aging_days` 0. Otherwise `aging_days` is the number of whole calendar days from `due_on` to the clock date, floored at zero; the invoice is past due exactly when that count is at least 1, so an invoice on its `due_on` date is **not** yet past due. An invoice issued on 2026-06-01 with 30-day terms is due 2026-07-01 and still shows an `aging_days` of 0 on 2026-07-01. Under the rejected reading — 21 business days, Monday through Friday, for both the term and the count — that same invoice would have been due 2026-06-30 and would already show one day of aging and a late fee on 2026-07-01. This API counts calendar days for both.

The nightly batch is what makes aging happen. Run by a `VendorAdmin`, it optionally accepts `write_off_over_days` and returns an object with four counters: `invoices_aged`, `agencies_suspended`, `subscriptions_lapsed` and `purges_auto_denied`. For each invoice whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `PAST_DUE`, it recomputes `aging_days`; where that count is at least 1 and `paid_cents` is below `total_cents`, the invoice moves `ISSUED → PAST_DUE` and a late fee accrues — one and a half percent of the unpaid balance, that is of `total_cents` less `paid_cents`, rounded half up, added to `late_fee_cents`, after which `total_cents` is recomputed as `subtotal_cents` plus `late_fee_cents`. Because the fee is charged on the unpaid balance including previously accrued late fees, it compounds across runs, and a single run never charges the same invoice twice. An invoice with a `subtotal_cents` of 5692 and no payment accrues one and a half percent of 5692, which is 85.38 and rounds to 85, making its `total_cents` 5777 on its first past-due run. When `write_off_over_days` is supplied, any invoice whose `aging_days` has reached that number moves `PAST_DUE → WRITTEN_OFF`: its unpaid balance is removed from the agency's `outstanding_balance_cents`, every attached assessment whose `status` is `INVOICED` moves to `CREDITED` with `credited_cents` set to its `total_cents`, and the agency's `max_aging_days` is recomputed, which may lift the agency's posture back to `CURRENT`. The same run also lapses every rap-back subscription whose term has ended and auto-denies every purge request older than 14 days, as described in §3.7.

The agency's posture is a projection over its open invoices — those whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `PAST_DUE`; `DRAFT`, `PAID` and `WRITTEN_OFF` invoices are excluded entirely. Over that set, `max_aging_days` is the largest `aging_days`, or 0 when the set is empty, and `outstanding_balance_cents` is the sum of `total_cents` less `paid_cents`. A `max_aging_days` of at least 45 makes `billing_status` `SUSPENDED`; otherwise a `max_aging_days` of at least 1 makes it `PAST_DUE`; otherwise it is `CURRENT`. The suspension boundary is inclusive — exactly 45 suspends, 44 does not. This projection is recomputed by the nightly batch, by every invoice payment and by every invoice issuance.

Moving `CURRENT → PAST_DUE` happens when a batch run leaves the agency's `max_aging_days` at 1 or more but still below 45, and it also refreshes `outstanding_balance_cents`. Moving `PAST_DUE → SUSPENDED` happens when `max_aging_days` reaches 45, and it does three further things: it stamps `suspended_at` with the logical clock; it closes the write gate of §2.10 over every ORI the agency owns; and it cancels appointments — but only some of them. Every appointment whose `status` is `BOOKED` and whose site is a `MOBILE_EVENT` capture site hosted by this agency moves to `CANCELLED`. Appointments at `FIXED` sites are left exactly as they are, whichever ORI they were booked under; that conditional leg does not fire for them.

Coming back from delinquency is a payment. Recording a payment against an invoice takes an `amount_cents` that must equal that invoice's `total_cents` less its `paid_cents` exactly; it sets `paid_cents`, moves the invoice `ISSUED → PAID` or `PAST_DUE → PAID` — the past-due leg additionally resetting `aging_days` to 0 — moves every attached assessment whose `status` is `INVOICED` to `PAID`, and recomputes the agency's balances and posture, which can lift a suspension. **A point left open, rendered without adjudication:** the agency's return to `CURRENT`, from either `SUSPENDED` or `PAST_DUE`, is described in two ways that do not always agree. One reading conditions the return on there being no remaining invoice of that agency whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `PAST_DUE` with `total_cents` above `paid_cents`, and sets both `outstanding_balance_cents` and `max_aging_days` to 0 on arrival — under which an agency still holding an unpaid but not-yet-due invoice stays suspended. The other reading is the posture projection above, under which an agency whose largest `aging_days` has fallen to 0 is `CURRENT` regardless of any unpaid balance, and whose `outstanding_balance_cents` is the sum over its remaining open invoices rather than 0. This document pins neither, and the same divergence applies to the `SUSPENDED → CURRENT` leg's clearing of `suspended_at` back to null, which is described only on the first reading.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Seventy-three endpoints are exposed, all beneath the `/livescan` prefix. Status codes follow one rule with no exceptions: an operation that brings a brand-new resource into existence answers **201 Created**, and every other successful operation — a read, a state transition, a payment, a batch run, a clock move — answers **200 OK**. Errors carry the HTTP status listed against their error identifier in §5.
