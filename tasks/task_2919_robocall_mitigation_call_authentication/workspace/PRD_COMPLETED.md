# Robocall Mitigation & Call Authentication API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Illegal robocalling is a network-scale problem that no single voice service provider can solve alone, which is why the regulatory regime around it is built out of obligations that chain from one provider to the next. A provider must authenticate the calls it originates, prove to a policy administrator that it holds a valid signing credential, describe in a filed plan how it prevents illegal traffic from entering its network, know who its origination customers actually are, answer traceback requests about specific calls within a fixed number of hours, and stop carrying traffic once a regulator tells it that traffic is illegal. Failure at any one of those links is not a private matter between the provider and its customer — it degrades the authentication signal every downstream carrier relies on, and it is therefore assessed as a forfeiture.

This document specifies the backend service that a regulator, a certificate policy administrator, and the providers themselves use to run that regime. The service is mounted under `/rmd`. Its central design idea is that a provider's ability to sign calls is never a stored flag that an operator sets; it is a **derived posture** recomputed from the provider's own compliance record — whether a mitigation plan is on file and accepted, whether a signing certificate is currently active, how many traceback requests have gone unanswered past their deadline, and how many compliance penalty points have accrued. Every call-signing request is gated on that derived posture, and every attestation level assigned to a signed call is a function of the posture, the customer's know-your-customer evidence, the customer's recent traffic risk, and whether the provider can actually prove it owns the calling number.

A second design idea runs alongside the first. Compliance is adversarial and time-bound, so the system keeps two kinds of counterweight. Segregation-of-duties gates ensure that the operator who advances a compliance artifact to a decision point is never the operator who decides it. And erroneous blocking — the failure mode that punishes legitimate callers rather than robocallers — has a first-class dispute path that reverses the block and unwinds every downstream consequence it caused, restoring the customer and its numbers to the exact state they held before. Because deadlines, expiries and windows are load-bearing throughout, the service evaluates all of them against an explicit server-side logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that any compliance timeline can be reproduced deterministically.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capability set that principal is exercising. If either header is missing, is present but empty or holds nothing but whitespace, or if `X-User-Role` is not exactly one of the seven role names declared below — the comparison is case-sensitive and surrounding whitespace is not trimmed away before it is made — the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs before every other check in the system. When the headers are well-formed but the named role does not appear on the authorised-role list for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `Regulator`, `PolicyAdministrator`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `KycAnalyst`, `FraudAnalyst`, `ProviderAdmin`, and `Caller`.

A `Regulator` is the enforcement authority. It registers providers, decides mitigation-plan filings, serves traceback requests and illegal-traffic notices, assesses and adjudicates forfeitures, drives origination state on any customer, and runs the deadline sweep. A `PolicyAdministrator` is the certificate authority function: it records certificate requests and issues, renews and revokes SHAKEN certificates. A `ComplianceOfficer` is the provider's compliance function: it drafts, submits and withdraws mitigation plans, answers tracebacks, investigates and mitigates illegal-traffic notices, contests forfeitures, and generates, certifies and files effectiveness reports. A `KycAnalyst` submits customer know-your-customer packages for review and decides them — never both for the same customer. A `FraudAnalyst` records analytics-driven blocks and reviews and resolves the disputes filed against them — never resolving a dispute over a block it applied itself. A `ProviderAdmin` is the provider's operations admin: it activates certificates, onboards customers, drives origination state, assigns telephone numbers, records traffic-monitoring windows, signs calls, and pays forfeitures. A `Caller` is a legitimate calling customer that disputes an erroneous block and may withdraw its own dispute.

Identity semantics differ by role, and they matter because ownership enforcement depends on them. When `X-User-Role` is `Regulator`, `PolicyAdministrator`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `KycAnalyst`, or `FraudAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `filed_by_user_id` on a mitigation plan, `kyc_submitted_by_user_id` or `kyc_decided_by_user_id` on a customer, `blocked_by_user_id` on a blocking event, `recorded_by_user_id` on a traffic observation, `resolved_by_user_id` on a dispute, or `signed_off_by_user_id` on an effectiveness report — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`.

When `X-User-Role` is `ProviderAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Provider` compare the resource's `provider_id` field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Caller`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Customer` compare the resource's `customer_id` field to `X-User-ID`. For both of these entity-backed roles, acting on a row the principal does not own is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403), and read endpoints exposed to these roles are likewise restricted to rows the principal owns. Three details of that comparison are fixed. When the target row is itself the `Provider` a `ProviderAdmin` acts as, or the `Customer` a `Caller` acts as, the comparison is made against that row's own `id`. When a row carries no provider reference of its own — a `BlockingDispute` — the provider it belongs to is resolved through the disputed blocking event's `provider_id`. And the restriction bites differently on the two read shapes: a single-row read of a row the principal does not own is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, whereas a list read is silently narrowed to the principal's own rows before any query filter and before pagination, so `total_count` counts only the rows visible to that principal and an unowned row is simply absent rather than an error.

A distinct class of authorisation failure applies where two steps of the same workflow must be performed by different people. Where such a gate exists, the rejection is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403); the three specific gates are described in §2.12.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- A request to a path the service does not route, or to a routed path with a method that path does not accept, is answered in this same error envelope carrying `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, but with the transport status of the routing failure itself — 404 for an unrouted path and 405 for an unaccepted method — rather than the 400 that §5 lists against that code. A trailing slash on a declared path resolves to the same route as the path without it.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when absent, may not be lower than 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds — or supplied as anything that is not a base-ten integer — is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); either parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as absent and takes its default.

Unless a subsection states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with ties broken by insertion order so that rows created within the same millisecond still come back in the order they were created. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective starting position), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied), and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page). All other query parameters described in §4 are filters; supplying several narrows the result set by every filter simultaneously. A filter whose value is not a declared member of that field's enumeration is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; a filter supplied as an empty string is treated as absent, and a query parameter the endpoint does not declare is ignored.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource the service persists carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID. Identifiers are never prefixed with a type marker and are never supplied by the client. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the instant of insertion. `created_at` and `updated_at` are the service's own real-time (wall-clock) stamps, as is the envelope's `api_request_timestamp`; they are the only instants in the API not drawn from the logical clock of §2.5, which supplies every domain instant a workflow stamps — `issued_at`, `activated_at`, `revoked_at`, `submitted_at`, `decided_at`, `kyc_decided_at`, `received_at`, `responded_at`, `notified_at`, `resolved_at`, `blocked_at`, `reversed_at`, `filed_at`, `contested_at`, `adjudicated_at`, `paid_at` and `signed_off_at`. Instants the client supplies in a request body — a certificate's `not_before` and `not_after`, an assignment's `effective_from` and `effective_to`, a call's `origination_time`, an observation's `window_start` and `window_end`, a report's `period_start` and `period_end` — are accepted at any ISO 8601 fractional precision and with either `Z` or a numeric UTC offset, are normalised to UTC for every comparison the rules make, and are echoed back in responses exactly as the client wrote them rather than reformatted; only the server-stamped instants are rendered in the canonical three-fractional-digit form.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format that is refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Whenever a workflow in §3 says a row's state advances, or a derived counter on that row is recomputed to a new value, that row's `version` increments and its `updated_at` is refreshed. This applies to `Provider`, `Certificate`, `MitigationPlan`, `Customer`, `NumberAssignment`, `TracebackRequest`, `IllegalTrafficNotice`, `BlockingEvent`, `BlockingDispute`, `EnforcementAction`, and `EffectivenessReport`.

Two record types are deliberate exceptions because they are immutable evidence rather than mutable state. A signed-call record (`CallAttestation`) and a traffic-monitoring window (`TrafficObservation`) each carry only `id` and `created_at`: they have no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and no endpoint exists to modify or delete them. A correction to either is made by writing a new record, not by editing an old one. This is why the customer risk index in §3.7 is a pure function of the observation history: the history itself can only grow.

### 2.5. The Logical Compliance Clock

Deadlines, expiries, validity intervals and reporting periods are the substance of this domain, so the service does not read wall-clock time when evaluating them. It maintains a single server-side **logical clock**, and every temporal rule in this document — certificate validity, traceback response deadlines, notice mitigation deadlines, dispute resolution deadlines, observation windows, assignment intervals and report periods — is evaluated against that clock.

Before the clock is ever set explicitly it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. That is the fixed startup default; the service never falls back to wall-clock time, not even on the first request. Any of the seven roles may advance or rewind the clock through a test-driving capability that accepts a single `current_utc` instant; the call modifies no entity, answers 200 with an empty `data` object, and every subsequent read of the logical clock returns the supplied value. Wherever this document says "the current logical clock", it means this value.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on the Provider Record

Two write operations are built on a client's prior observation of a provider, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the `Provider` row's `version`.

Filing a new robocall mitigation plan requires an `expected_provider_version` field in the request body. The field is not optional. If the value does not equal the target provider's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROVIDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a plan filing legitimately supersedes any prior operative filing for that provider and immediately changes the provider's derived `rmd_filing_status` and `auth_posture`; a filer whose view of the provider is out of date would be superseding a filing it never saw. A second filing that carries a freshly read `version` is accepted and supersedes again, which is why this cannot be reduced to a state precondition on the plan.

Generating an effectiveness report likewise requires a non-optional `expected_provider_version` in its request body, and likewise rejects a mismatch against the provider's current `version` with `ERR_STALE_PROVIDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Report metrics are computed from provider-scoped counters and a newer report supersedes any overlapping-period report, so a generation request built on a stale provider view would publish effectiveness numbers that no longer describe the provider.

No other endpoint in the system takes a version field. In particular, certificate renewal, plan submission, plan decisions, customer state changes, number revocation, dispute resolution and enforcement adjudication carry no expected-version input and are guarded only by their own state preconditions.

### 2.7. Numeric Precision, Rounding and Monetary Units

Three numeric families appear in this domain, and each has a fixed precision.

Scores in the unit interval are carried to **four decimal places, rounded half up**. This covers `traffic_risk_index`, `resulting_risk_index`, `analytics_score`, `blocking_false_positive_rate`, `attestation_component`, `traceback_component`, `notice_component` and `effectiveness_score`. A supplied score outside the range 0.0000 to 1.0000 inclusive is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; a supplied score inside the range but carrying more than four decimals is accepted and rounded half up to four decimals as it is persisted, so the value echoed back is the rounded one. Every score travels as a JSON number rather than a string, which makes the four-decimal rule a rounding rule and not a formatting one: a score whose trailing decimals are zero serialises as `0.738` or `1.0`, never as `0.7380` or `1.0000`.

One intermediate score is deliberately coarser: a traffic observation's `window_subscore` is rounded half up to **two decimal places**, persisted at that precision, and every later computation reads the persisted two-decimal value rather than recomputing from raw ratios. The rounding stage is part of the specification, not an implementation detail.

Money is carried exclusively as **integer cents**. `base_assessment_cents`, `base_capped_cents`, `mitigation_credit_cents`, `total_forfeiture_cents`, `paid_amount_cents` and `outstanding_forfeiture_cents` are all whole numbers of cents; there is no decimal currency anywhere in the API. In the forfeiture computation the credit is the only rounding step — it is rounded half up to a whole number of cents — and neither the base nor the total is rounded separately. Compliance point arithmetic (`penalty_points`, `penalty_points_assessed`) is integer arithmetic with no rounding at all, and `lateness_seconds` is a whole number of seconds obtained by rounding up, so any positive fraction of a second already counts as one second late.

### 2.8. Validation Order

When a single request violates more than one rule, the response must report the violation that comes first in the following ladder. The ladder is total and global: the first rule violated wins, and no later rule may mask an earlier one. Implementations are expected to evaluate in exactly this sequence.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` one of the seven declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorisation.** The acting role appears on the target operation's authorised-role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Request shape.** The body is valid JSON, every required input field is present and of its declared type, every enum-valued field carries a declared member, integer count fields are not negative, four-decimal score fields lie within 0.0000 to 1.0000 inclusive, and `limit` / `offset` are within their bounds — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). An absent or empty body is read as an empty JSON object, so a request that omits its payload altogether fails on the first required field that payload should have carried; a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object fails here outright. A field supplied as `null` counts as absent, so a required field explicitly set to `null` fails as missing while an optional one falls back to its stated default. A string-valued field must be a non-empty string whenever it is supplied, required or optional; an integer field must be a genuine integer, so a boolean or a fractional number is refused even when its value would otherwise be in range; and a list-valued field must be an array.
4. **Telephone number format.** Every `telephone_number`, `calling_number` and `called_number` supplied is E.164: a leading `+` followed by 11 to 15 digits — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_NUMBER_FORMAT` (422). This applies to number assignment, call signing, traceback request creation, illegal-traffic notice creation and blocking-event creation.
5. **Interval well-ordering.** Every supplied interval is strictly ordered: `not_before` earlier than `not_after`, `window_start` earlier than `window_end`, `period_start` earlier than `period_end`, and `effective_from` earlier than `effective_to` whenever `effective_to` is supplied — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (422). This applies to certificate request creation, certificate renewal (which supplies a fresh interval of its own), traffic-observation creation, effectiveness-report generation and number-assignment creation.
6. **Path existence.** Every identifier embedded in the URL path must resolve to a row, validated strictly left to right, with the 404 code naming the entity that failed: `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ATTESTATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OBSERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRACEBACK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BLOCKING_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`.
7. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign reference supplied in the body must resolve to a row, validated in body-field declaration order — `provider_id` first, then `customer_id`, then whichever of `blocking_event_id`, `certificate_id`, `identified_customer_id` or `produced_attestation_id` the payload carries. The failure codes are `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BLOCKING_EVENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ATTESTATION_NOT_FOUND`, all 404.
8. **Resource ownership.** For the two entity-backed roles, the acting identity must own the target row: a `ProviderAdmin`'s `X-User-ID` equals the target row's `provider_id`, and a `Caller`'s `X-User-ID` equals the target row's `customer_id` — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This applies to every operation whose authorised roles include `ProviderAdmin` or `Caller`.
9. **Segregation of duties** — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403), on exactly the three operations named in §2.12.
10. **Provider version freshness** — `ERR_STALE_PROVIDER_VERSION` (412), on plan filing and report generation, as specified in §2.6.
11. **State preconditions**, one code per artifact family: `ERR_CERTIFICATE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_CERTIFICATE`, `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_KYC_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ORIGINATION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_TRACEBACK_ALREADY_CLOSED`, `ERR_NOTICE_ALREADY_CLOSED`, `ERR_DISPUTE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_STATE_INVALID` and `ERR_REPORT_STATE_INVALID` — all 409, in that relative order, and all before any business rule below.
12. **Business rules**, in this exact relative order: `ERR_PRACTICES_INSUFFICIENT`, then `ERR_SIGNING_SUSPENDED`, then `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_KYC_VERIFIED`, then `ERR_ORIGINATION_NOT_ENABLED`, then `ERR_SUSPENSION_CAUSE_NOT_SELF_CLEARABLE`, then `ERR_SCORE_BELOW_BLOCKING_THRESHOLD`, then `ERR_RESPONSE_WINDOW_CLOSED`, then `ERR_WINDOW_IN_FUTURE`, then `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`, then `ERR_NUMBER_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`, then `ERR_OBSERVATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, then `ERR_DUPLICATE_DISPUTE`, then `ERR_CUSTOMER_PROVIDER_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_CALL_DETAIL_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_CALL_DETAIL_INCOMPLETE`, and finally `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`.

Two consequences of this ordering are worth calling out. Because the certificate-status check sits at step 11 and the posture check at step 12, a signing attempt against a provider that has no `ACTIVE` certificate reports `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_CERTIFICATE` (409) rather than `ERR_SIGNING_SUSPENDED`, even though the missing certificate is itself one of the reasons the posture is `SIGNING_SUSPENDED`. And because ownership sits at step 8, ahead of every state and business rule, a `ProviderAdmin` reaching for another provider's row is told it does not own the row rather than being told anything about that row's state.

### 2.9. The Authentication Posture Ladder

A provider's `auth_posture` is never written directly. It is recomputed from the provider's own compliance record every time any of its four inputs moves: `penalty_points`, `rmd_filing_status`, `overdue_traceback_count`, or `active_certificate_id`. In practice that means after every mitigation-plan decision, every traceback that goes overdue or is answered late, every notice that expires unmitigated, every certificate activation, revocation or expiry, and every compliance sweep.

The recomputation evaluates three steps in order and stops at the first match. **Step one:** if `rmd_filing_status` is `UNFILED`, or `penalty_points` is at least 10, or the provider's `active_certificate_id` is null, then `auth_posture` becomes `SIGNING_SUSPENDED`. **Step two:** otherwise, if `rmd_filing_status` is `PARTIAL`, or `overdue_traceback_count` is at least 1, then `auth_posture` becomes `GATEWAY_LIMITED`. **Step three:** otherwise `auth_posture` becomes `FULL_ATTESTATION`.

`rmd_filing_status` is itself derived, from the provider's single **operative** mitigation plan — the one plan whose `filing_state` is neither `WITHDRAWN` nor `SUPERSEDED`; should more than one plan of that provider ever qualify at once, the operative one is the most recently created of them, so the derivation is total even if the single-operative-plan invariant were ever breached. If there is no such plan, or its `filing_state` is `DRAFT`, the status is `UNFILED`. If its `filing_state` is `SUBMITTED` or `DEFICIENT`, the status is `PARTIAL`. If its `filing_state` is `ACCEPTED`, the status is `COMPLETE`. Withdrawn and superseded plans never contribute to this derivation, and only the provider's own counters and its own operative plan participate — no other provider's data ever influences a provider's posture.

Consider a provider with an `ACCEPTED` plan, an `ACTIVE` certificate, 9 accumulated `penalty_points` and one traceback sitting `OVERDUE`. Step one does not fire, so step two does, and the posture is `GATEWAY_LIMITED` — which caps every call it signs at attestation level `C`. One further notice expiring unmitigated adds 5 points, taking the total to 14, and step one now fires: the posture becomes `SIGNING_SUSPENDED` and the provider cannot sign at all. Answering the overdue traceback clears `overdue_traceback_count` but does **not** clear `penalty_points`, so posture recovers to `FULL_ATTESTATION` only while points remain below 10.

A newly registered provider starts with `rmd_filing_status` of `UNFILED`, `auth_posture` of `SIGNING_SUSPENDED`, `active_certificate_id` of null and `penalty_points` of 0 — it can neither sign nor claim compliance until it has both an accepted plan and an active certificate.

### 2.10. Derived Provider Counters and the Conservation Invariants

A provider row is largely a projection of the rows that reference it. Every counter listed here is a **full recompute over the qualifying set**, never an incremental delta, which is what makes reversals exact: an operation that removes a row from a set restores the counter to precisely the value it held before the row entered. The recompute is also indivisible: whenever any one of these quantities is refreshed the service refreshes the whole derived set on that row in one pass — every counter below together with `rmd_filing_status`, `active_certificate_id` and `auth_posture` — and that single refresh moves the row's `version` by exactly 1, or leaves it alone entirely when the refresh finds every derived value already equal to what it held. The compliance sweep of §3.8 is the one exception: it advances the provider row on every run whether or not any derived value moved.

`penalty_points` is the sum of three contributions over the provider's own rows: 2 points for each deficiency decision ever recorded across the provider's mitigation plans (that is, 2 multiplied by the total of `deficiency_count` over those plans), plus 3 points for each traceback request whose `state` is either `OVERDUE` or `RESPONDED_LATE`, plus the total of `penalty_points_assessed` over the provider's illegal-traffic notices, where a notice in `EXPIRED_UNMITIGATED` contributes 5 and every other notice contributes 0. Note that the traceback contribution counts `OVERDUE` and `RESPONDED_LATE` alike: answering an overdue request late moves the row from one of those states to the other and therefore leaves `penalty_points` untouched.

`overdue_traceback_count` is the count of the provider's traceback requests whose `state` is exactly `OVERDUE`. A late answer removes the row from this set while leaving it in the penalty set — the two counters move independently over the same rows, and that asymmetry is deliberate.

`open_notice_count` is the count of the provider's illegal-traffic notices whose `state` is `NOTIFIED` or `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`.

`active_assigned_number_count` is the count of the provider's number assignments whose `status` is exactly `ACTIVE`; assignments in `SUSPENDED_BY_CUSTOMER` or `REVOKED` are excluded. This is the conserved number-inventory quantity: suspending a customer lowers it, restoring that customer raises it back to the identical value, and revoking an assignment lowers it permanently.

`outstanding_forfeiture_cents` is the sum of `total_forfeiture_cents` over the provider's enforcement actions whose `status` is `PROPOSED`, `CONTESTED` or `SUSTAINED`. Actions in `PAID` or `DISMISSED` leave that set. This is the conserved monetary quantity: contesting an action does **not** remove it from the set, sustaining a contest does not remove it either, and a dismissal restores the exact pre-assessment total.

`blocking_false_positive_rate` measures how often the provider's own analytics were wrong. Let the denominator be the count of every blocking event belonging to the provider regardless of `status`, and the numerator the count of those whose `status` is `REVERSED`. When the provider has no blocking event at all the rate is 0.0; otherwise it is the numerator divided by the denominator, rounded half up to four decimal places. Because every event sits in the denominator from the moment it is created, filing a dispute or taking one under review never moves this rate — only a reversal does, by raising the numerator.

`sweep_index` is a monotone counter that increments by 1 each time a compliance sweep is run for that provider. Two customer-level counters follow the same recompute discipline: `observation_count` is the count of traffic observations recorded for that customer, and `false_positive_count` is the count of blocking events against that customer whose `status` is `REVERSED`.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline

Several operations in this domain touch more than one row, and the propagation is specified as a chain in which each hop is caused by the previous hop rather than by the originating call. The canonical chain is four hops deep: an illegal-traffic notice expires unmitigated, which suspends the attributed customer's origination, which suspends that customer's active number assignments, which forces a recompute of the provider's `active_assigned_number_count` and — through the new `penalty_points` — of its `auth_posture`. An implementation that stops after the first hop produces a customer that is suspended while its numbers still read `ACTIVE`, and a provider whose counters disagree with its own rows.

Every row touched anywhere in such a chain has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed, including rows touched only as a cascade target and rows whose only change was a recomputed derived value. The reverse direction is symmetrical: when a reversal or restoration unwinds a chain, each hop is unwound and each affected row's `version` increments again. Reversal restores values, not version numbers.

### 2.12. Segregation of Duties

Three points in the system require two different human operators, and all three compare the acting `X-User-ID` against a user identifier the system recorded at the earlier step. In all three cases the rejection is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

The first gate is know-your-customer review: the `KycAnalyst` deciding a customer's package must not be the analyst who submitted it, so the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from that customer's `kyc_submitted_by_user_id`. The second gate is blocking-dispute resolution: the `FraudAnalyst` resolving a dispute must not be the analyst who applied the disputed block, so the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the disputed blocking event's `blocked_by_user_id`. The third gate is cross-artifact rather than within-artifact: the `ComplianceOfficer` signing off an effectiveness report must not be the officer who filed the provider's operative mitigation plan, so the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from that plan's `filed_by_user_id`.

These three operations — the know-your-customer decision, the dispute resolution, and the report sign-off — are the complete set guarded by this rule. Note that only the *deciding* half of each pair is guarded: submitting a package, applying a block, filing a plan, taking a dispute under review and filing a signed-off report carry no such restriction.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Provider Registration and the Robocall Mitigation Filing

A provider enters the system when a `Regulator` registers it with a `legal_name` and a `provider_type`, which is one of `FACILITIES_BASED`, `NON_FACILITIES_RESELLER` or `GATEWAY`. Everything else on the provider row is derived. At registration `rmd_filing_status` reads `UNFILED`, `auth_posture` reads `SIGNING_SUSPENDED`, `active_certificate_id` is null, `penalty_points`, `overdue_traceback_count`, `open_notice_count`, `active_assigned_number_count`, `outstanding_forfeiture_cents` and `sweep_index` are all 0, and `blocking_false_positive_rate` is 0.0. The provider register can be browsed by any operator role and narrowed by `provider_type`, by `auth_posture` or by `rmd_filing_status`; note that `active_certificate_id` is a computed pointer to whichever of the provider's certificates is currently `ACTIVE`, and is never something a client supplies.

The mitigation plan is the provider's written account of how it keeps illegal traffic off its network. A `ComplianceOfficer` drafts one by naming the `provider_id` it belongs to, listing the `prevention_practices` the filing describes, and carrying the `expected_provider_version` described in §2.6. Each element of `prevention_practices` is one of `KYC_ONBOARDING`, `TRAFFIC_PATTERN_MONITORING`, `CONTRACT_TERMS_PROHIBITION`, `CALL_BLOCKING_ANALYTICS` or `TRACEBACK_COOPERATION`. A newly drafted plan lands in `filing_state` `DRAFT`, with `filed_by_user_id`, `submitted_at` and `decided_at` all null, `deficiency_reasons` an empty list, and `deficiency_count` and `revision_count` both 0.

At most one plan per provider is ever outside `WITHDRAWN` and `SUPERSEDED`, and drafting enforces that invariant: creating a plan moves every other plan of the same provider whose `filing_state` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED` or `DEFICIENT` to `SUPERSEDED`, then recomputes the provider's `rmd_filing_status` and `auth_posture` from the new operative plan. A provider that had reached `COMPLETE` on an accepted plan therefore falls back to `UNFILED` — and its posture to `SIGNING_SUSPENDED` — the moment a fresh draft is filed, until that draft is itself submitted and accepted.

Submitting the plan files it with the regulator, moving `filing_state` from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED` or from `DEFICIENT` back to `SUBMITTED`. This is the point at which the practice list is audited: the plan must describe **at least three** prevention practices and the list must include both `KYC_ONBOARDING` and `TRACEBACK_COOPERATION`. A list that is shorter than three elements, or that omits either mandatory practice, is refused with `ERR_PRACTICES_INSUFFICIENT` (422) — so a three-element list of `TRAFFIC_PATTERN_MONITORING`, `CONTRACT_TERMS_PROHIBITION` and `CALL_BLOCKING_ANALYTICS` fails despite being long enough. The length test counts list elements rather than distinct practices, and the list is persisted and echoed back exactly as the client supplied it — order preserved, duplicates preserved — so a repeated member counts twice and `KYC_ONBOARDING`, `KYC_ONBOARDING` and `TRACEBACK_COOPERATION` satisfies both the length rule and the two mandatory members. Drafting itself audits nothing: a plan may be created carrying any list of declared practices, including an empty one, and only submission tests it. A successful submission stamps `submitted_at` with the current logical clock and records the submitting officer's `X-User-ID` as `filed_by_user_id`, which is the value the report sign-off gate in §2.12 will later compare against. Filing makes `rmd_filing_status` `PARTIAL`, which through the ladder in §2.9 caps the provider at `GATEWAY_LIMITED` at best. Re-submission from `DEFICIENT` does one thing more: it increments `revision_count` by 1 and empties `deficiency_reasons`, since the reasons attached to the previous rejection no longer describe the filing on the table. A submission attempted from any other `filing_state` is refused with `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID` (409).

The regulator's decision carries a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `DEFICIENT`, and may only be taken while the plan is `SUBMITTED`. An acceptance advances `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, stamps `decided_at`, and drives `rmd_filing_status` to `COMPLETE` before the posture ladder runs again. Filing completeness reads the operative plan and nothing else, exactly as §2.9 states, so an `ACCEPTED` plan yields `COMPLETE` whether or not the provider also holds an `ACTIVE` certificate; a missing certificate is caught by step one of the posture ladder rather than by the filing status, which is why a provider can read `rmd_filing_status` `COMPLETE` and `auth_posture` `SIGNING_SUSPENDED` at the same time. A deficiency decision advances `SUBMITTED → DEFICIENT`, requires `deficiency_reasons` to be supplied with at least one element, records those reason strings on the plan, stamps `decided_at`, and increments `deficiency_count` by 1. Because each deficiency is worth 2 `penalty_points`, the decision also recomputes the provider's counters and then its posture; and because the operative plan now reads `DEFICIENT` rather than `ACCEPTED`, `rmd_filing_status` becomes `PARTIAL` — not `UNFILED`, since a deficient filing is still a filing on the table — which through §2.9 caps the provider at `GATEWAY_LIMITED` at best. The revision cycle `SUBMITTED → DEFICIENT → SUBMITTED` may repeat without bound: `deficiency_count` keeps accruing across the whole life of the plan while `revision_count` tracks how many times the loop has been travelled.

Withdrawal is available while the plan is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `DEFICIENT` or `ACCEPTED`, and moves it to `WITHDRAWN`. Withdrawing the operative plan leaves the provider with no operative filing at all, so `rmd_filing_status` falls back to `UNFILED` and the posture ladder drives `auth_posture` to `SIGNING_SUSPENDED` — unless another operative plan exists, in which case the status is re-derived from that plan instead. Both `WITHDRAWN` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal: nothing further can be done to a plan in either state, and an attempt is met with `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. That same code is also what refuses a report sign-off when the reporting provider's operative plan is not `ACCEPTED`; the four operations it guards are plan submission, the regulator's plan decision, plan withdrawal, and effectiveness-report certification. Plans are discoverable by `provider_id` and by `filing_state`.

### 3.2. Certificate Acquisition, Activation and the Renewal Chain

Call signing is only meaningful if the signature can be traced to a credential a policy administrator vouched for, so certificate custody is split between two roles: the `PolicyAdministrator` controls issuance, renewal and revocation, while the provider itself controls activation. A certificate begins life when the `PolicyAdministrator` records a request naming the `provider_id` it authenticates, the `spc_token` supplied for that provider, the `public_key_fingerprint` of the signing key bound to the credential, and the validity interval `not_before` to `not_after` — both inclusive ISO 8601 UTC instants. `spc_token` and `public_key_fingerprint` are opaque non-empty strings stored verbatim: neither is parsed, matched against any grammar, nor required to be unique across certificates, and nothing prevents recording a request whose interval has already lapsed — it simply can never be issued. The new row lands in `status` `REQUESTED` with `renewal_generation` 1, `predecessor_certificate_id` null, and `issued_at`, `activated_at`, `revoked_at` and `revocation_reason` all null.

Issuance advances `REQUESTED → ISSUED` and stamps `issued_at` with the current logical clock. It is refused unless the certificate is still `REQUESTED` and its `not_after` lies strictly after the current logical clock — there is no point issuing a credential that has already lapsed. Activation is the provider's own act: a `ProviderAdmin` adopts an `ISSUED` certificate as its signing credential, which advances `ISSUED → ACTIVE`, stamps `activated_at`, and writes the certificate's `id` into the provider's `active_certificate_id`. Activation additionally requires that the current logical clock lie inside the closed validity interval — at or after `not_before` and at or before `not_after` — and that the provider hold no other certificate already in `ACTIVE`. A provider that already has an active credential must renew rather than activate a second one. Any of those preconditions failing yields `ERR_CERTIFICATE_STATE_INVALID` (409), which is also the code for issuing, renewing or revoking from a status the transition does not permit. Because activation supplies the missing `active_certificate_id`, it immediately re-runs the posture ladder of §2.9 — a provider with an `ACCEPTED` plan and no penalty points reaches `FULL_ATTESTATION` at exactly this moment.

Renewal is a create-with-supersede operation rather than an in-place edit, and it is the only certificate operation that returns a different row than the one addressed. The `PolicyAdministrator` renews an `ACTIVE` certificate by supplying a fresh `spc_token`, `public_key_fingerprint`, `not_before` and `not_after`; the service creates a **successor** certificate in `status` `ISSUED` whose `predecessor_certificate_id` points at the certificate being renewed, whose `renewal_generation` is one greater than the predecessor's, whose `issued_at` is the current logical clock, and which carries the same `provider_id`. Unlike issuance, renewal applies no lapse test of its own: the fresh interval need only be well ordered per §2.8 step 5, so a successor may be created directly in `ISSUED` with a `not_after` already behind the current logical clock — it will simply fail activation and be swept to `EXPIRED`. The predecessor moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`. Critically, the provider's `active_certificate_id` is set to null in the same operation, because the successor has not been activated yet — so the posture ladder stops at its first step and the provider reads `SIGNING_SUSPENDED` for the whole gap between renewal and activation, while `rmd_filing_status` keeps whatever the operative plan dictates. The successor is returned by the renewal call and is also discoverable afterwards by filtering the certificate collection on `predecessor_certificate_id`, which is how a client walks a renewal chain; the collection can equally be filtered by `provider_id` or by `status`.

Revocation is available from `REQUESTED`, `ISSUED` or `ACTIVE`, and requires a `revocation_reason` of `KEY_COMPROMISE`, `SPC_WITHDRAWN` or `REGULATOR_ORDER`. It moves the certificate to `REVOKED` and stamps `revoked_at`. When the certificate being revoked was the provider's active one, the operation also nulls `active_certificate_id`, and the posture ladder therefore stops at step one and drives `auth_posture` to `SIGNING_SUSPENDED` — again leaving `rmd_filing_status` untouched, because filing completeness is a plan property, not a certificate property. Revoking a certificate that was merely `REQUESTED` or `ISSUED` has no posture consequence at all, since it was never the active credential.

Expiry is not an operator action; it is observed. When a compliance sweep (§3.8) runs and finds the current logical clock strictly after a certificate's `not_after`, that certificate moves to `EXPIRED` — but only from `ACTIVE` or `ISSUED`, the two statuses in which the credential is live. A certificate still in `REQUESTED` is left exactly where it is however long its `not_after` has lapsed, so it never reaches `EXPIRED` by observation and revocation becomes its only remaining transition; the already-terminal statuses are likewise untouched. From `ACTIVE`, expiry also clears the provider's `active_certificate_id` when the pointer named this very certificate, and then recomputes both `rmd_filing_status` and `auth_posture`. From `ISSUED`, expiry is a purely local change: nothing on the provider moves, because an issued-but-never-activated credential was never the signing pointer. `SUPERSEDED`, `REVOKED` and `EXPIRED` are all terminal — no further transition is available out of any of them.

### 3.3. Customer Intake and the Know-Your-Customer Dimension

Before a provider may originate traffic on a customer's behalf it has to know who that customer is. A `ProviderAdmin` onboards an origination customer under its own `provider_id`, recording the customer's `legal_name`, a `contact_email` for correspondence, and a `kyc_evidence_level` of `BUSINESS_REGISTRY_VERIFIED`, `CONTRACT_ONLY` or `SELF_ATTESTED` describing how strong the identity evidence on file actually is. `legal_name` and `contact_email` are opaque non-empty strings stored verbatim; `contact_email` in particular is not checked against any address grammar, and neither field has to be unique across customers. A freshly onboarded customer reads `kyc_state` `PENDING` and `origination_state` `DISABLED`, with `traffic_risk_index` 0.0000, `risk_tier` `LOW`, `observation_count` 0, `false_positive_count` 0, `suspension_cause` and `suspension_source_id` null, and `kyc_submitted_by_user_id`, `kyc_decided_by_user_id` and `kyc_decided_at` all null.

A customer carries **two independent state dimensions**, and understanding that they never write each other is essential. `kyc_state` tracks identity assurance and takes the values `PENDING`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `VERIFIED` and `REJECTED`. `origination_state` tracks whether the customer may actually place calls and takes the values `DISABLED`, `ENABLED`, `SUSPENDED` and `TERMINATED`. Neither dimension has any transition that modifies the other field. The practical consequence is that inconsistent-looking pairs are legal and must be representable: a customer whose `kyc_state` has fallen to `REJECTED` may still read `origination_state` `ENABLED`, and a customer whose `kyc_state` is `VERIFIED` may sit at `DISABLED` indefinitely because granting origination capability is a separate operator decision. The pairs are reconciled not by cascade but by refusal at the point of use — a call signed for a customer that is not `VERIFIED` is rejected with `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_KYC_VERIFIED` (422) no matter what `origination_state` says.

A `KycAnalyst` opens a review by submitting the customer's know-your-customer package, which advances `kyc_state` to `UNDER_REVIEW` from `PENDING`, from `REJECTED`, or from `VERIFIED`. The submission may optionally carry a replacement `kyc_evidence_level`, which overwrites the stored value when supplied. It records the submitting analyst's `X-User-ID` as `kyc_submitted_by_user_id` and resets `kyc_decided_by_user_id` to null, so that each review round is adjudicated fresh. `kyc_decided_at` is deliberately *not* reset, and neither is any earlier `kyc_submitted_by_user_id` preserved: a customer resubmitted after an earlier decision therefore reads `UNDER_REVIEW` with a null `kyc_decided_by_user_id` alongside the previous round's `kyc_decided_at`, until the new decision overwrites that stamp. Submitting a package for a customer already `UNDER_REVIEW` is refused with `ERR_CUSTOMER_KYC_STATE_INVALID` (409); that same code refuses a decision on a customer that is not `UNDER_REVIEW`, and those two operations — package submission and package decision — are the only ones it guards.

The decision itself carries a `decision` of `VERIFY` or `REJECT` and may optionally carry a `kyc_evidence_level`, which replaces the stored evidence strength when present and leaves it unchanged when absent. It must be taken by a different analyst than the one who submitted the package, as specified in §2.12. A `VERIFY` advances `UNDER_REVIEW → VERIFIED`; a `REJECT` advances `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED`. Either outcome records the deciding analyst's `X-User-ID` as `kyc_decided_by_user_id` and stamps `kyc_decided_at` with the current logical clock. Neither outcome touches `origination_state`.

Periodic re-verification uses the same machinery in a loop: an already `VERIFIED` customer is submitted again, travelling `VERIFIED → UNDER_REVIEW → VERIFIED`. This has a real operational cost, because while the customer sits at `UNDER_REVIEW` its `kyc_state` is not `VERIFIED`, so calls cannot be signed for it even though `origination_state` still reads `ENABLED`. Similarly, a customer that was rejected can be brought back with `REJECTED → UNDER_REVIEW → VERIFIED`; `kyc_state` has no terminal value. The customer register can be filtered by `provider_id`, by `kyc_state`, by `origination_state` and by `risk_tier`, which is the usual way an analyst finds the queue of customers awaiting a decision.

### 3.4. Granting, Suspending, Restoring and Terminating Origination Capability

The origination dimension is driven through a single action-bearing operation shared by `ProviderAdmin` and `Regulator`. The request names an `action` — `ENABLE`, `SUSPEND`, `RESTORE` or `TERMINATE` — and, for a suspension, a `suspension_cause`. Attempting an action the customer's current `origination_state` does not permit is refused with `ERR_ORIGINATION_STATE_INVALID` (409), and that code guards this operation alone.

`ENABLE` is available only from `DISABLED` and only for a customer whose `kyc_state` is `VERIFIED`; an attempt on an unverified customer is refused with `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_KYC_VERIFIED` (422). It advances `DISABLED → ENABLED` and clears both `suspension_cause` and `suspension_source_id` to null. Those two fields are null in every `origination_state` other than `SUSPENDED`, without exception.

`SUSPEND` is available only from `ENABLED`. The cause must be supplied, and which value is legal depends on who is acting: a `ProviderAdmin` may only suspend with `PROVIDER_REQUEST`, and a `Regulator` may only suspend with `REGULATOR_ORDER`. A missing cause, or one that does not match the acting role, is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). A successful suspension writes the supplied value into `suspension_cause`, leaves `suspension_source_id` null because no artifact drove it, then cascades: every number assignment of that customer whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves to `SUSPENDED_BY_CUSTOMER`, and because those assignments left the active set, the provider's `active_assigned_number_count` is recomputed.

Three system events suspend a customer without any operator asking. An illegal-traffic notice that expires unmitigated suspends the customer it was attributed to with `suspension_cause` `NOTICE_EXPIRY`. A traffic observation that drives the customer's `traffic_risk_index` to 0.9000 or above suspends it with `TRAFFIC_RISK_THRESHOLD`. And a blocking event recorded with `reason` `ANALYTICS_SCORE` and an `analytics_score` of 0.9500 or above suspends it with `BLOCKING_ANALYTICS`. In each case the transition is `ENABLED → SUSPENDED`, `suspension_source_id` is set to the `id` of the artifact that caused it — the notice, the observation, or the blocking event respectively — and the same assignment cascade and provider recount follow. There is a fourth, subtler pairing: when a provider discharges a notice by blocking all of the attributed customer's traffic, the resulting suspension carries `suspension_cause` `PROVIDER_REQUEST` with `suspension_source_id` pointing at that notice, which is the only case where `PROVIDER_REQUEST` comes with a non-null source. Read together, `suspension_cause` therefore takes exactly one of `NOTICE_EXPIRY`, `BLOCKING_ANALYTICS`, `TRAFFIC_RISK_THRESHOLD`, `REGULATOR_ORDER` or `PROVIDER_REQUEST`, and `suspension_source_id` is null whenever an operator ordered the suspension directly.

`RESTORE` moves `SUSPENDED → ENABLED`, and requires the customer's `kyc_state` to still be `VERIFIED` — a customer whose verification lapsed while suspended cannot be restored until it is verified again, and the refusal is `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_KYC_VERIFIED`. Beyond that, who may restore depends on **why** the customer was suspended, and this is the asymmetry that keeps a provider from undoing its own regulatory consequences. A `ProviderAdmin` may only clear a suspension whose `suspension_cause` is `PROVIDER_REQUEST`; suspensions caused by `NOTICE_EXPIRY`, `BLOCKING_ANALYTICS`, `TRAFFIC_RISK_THRESHOLD` or `REGULATOR_ORDER` are clearable only by a `Regulator`, who may clear any cause. A `ProviderAdmin` reaching for one of the other four is refused with `ERR_SUSPENSION_CAUSE_NOT_SELF_CLEARABLE` (403), a code raised by no other operation. A successful restore nulls `suspension_cause` and `suspension_source_id`, moves every assignment of that customer whose `status` is `SUSPENDED_BY_CUSTOMER` back to `ACTIVE`, and recomputes the provider's `active_assigned_number_count` — which, because the counter is a full recompute over the active set, returns to exactly the value it held before the suspension.

There is also a system-driven restoration, described in full in §3.7: when a blocking event that caused a suspension is itself reversed by a sustained dispute, the customer travels `SUSPENDED → ENABLED` automatically, with no operator involved.

`TERMINATE` is the end of the relationship and is available from `DISABLED`, `ENABLED` or `SUSPENDED`. It may be requested by a `ProviderAdmin` or a `Regulator`, and is also driven as a system event when a provider discharges an illegal-traffic notice by terminating the attributed customer. Termination moves `origination_state` to `TERMINATED`, clears `suspension_cause` and `suspension_source_id`, and revokes number inventory rather than merely suspending it: every assignment of that customer whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_BY_CUSTOMER` moves to `REVOKED` with `revoked_at` stamped at the current logical clock, after which the provider's `active_assigned_number_count` is recomputed. `TERMINATED` is terminal; no action returns a customer from it, and the revocations it caused are permanent — a later restoration is impossible because there is nothing to restore.

### 3.5. Number Inventory and Ownership Evidence

A number assignment is the provider's claim that a particular telephone number belongs to a particular customer over a particular stretch of time, and it is the evidentiary basis for the highest attestation level. A `ProviderAdmin` creates one under its own `provider_id`, naming the `customer_id` it is assigned to, the `telephone_number` itself, the `ownership_evidence` backing the claim, and the interval `effective_from` through `effective_to`. Numbers are E.164 strings checked exactly as §2.8 step 4 describes and no more strictly: a leading `+` followed by 11 to 15 digits, with no structural constraint beyond that count. No country-code, area-code or central-office-code digit is examined, so `+11111111111` is accepted as readily as `+12125551234`, while a value carrying only ten digits after the `+`, containing a separator, or omitting the `+` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_NUMBER_FORMAT` (422). The same single rule governs every number the service accepts anywhere.

The occupation interval is **half-open**: `effective_from` is included and `effective_to` is excluded. `effective_to` is optional, and omitting it (or supplying null) means the assignment is open-ended with no upper bound. A new assignment may not overlap any existing non-`REVOKED` assignment of the same `telephone_number` under the same provider; an overlap is refused with `ERR_NUMBER_INTERVAL_OVERLAP` (409). Because the interval excludes its upper bound, **adjacency is legal**: an assignment starting at exactly the instant a previous assignment ended does not collide, and back-to-back handoffs of the same number between two customers of the same provider are therefore expressible. Two open-ended assignments of the same number, by contrast, always collide.

Assigning a number is only permitted for a customer the provider actually serves and that is actually able to originate. The referenced customer's `provider_id` must equal the `provider_id` on the request, or the assignment is refused with `ERR_CUSTOMER_PROVIDER_MISMATCH` (422). The customer's `kyc_state` must be `VERIFIED`, or the refusal is `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_KYC_VERIFIED` (422); its `origination_state` must be `ENABLED`, or the refusal is `ERR_ORIGINATION_NOT_ENABLED` (422). Those two customer-posture codes each guard exactly the operations named here and in §3.6: `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_KYC_VERIFIED` guards call signing, number assignment, and both the `ENABLE` and the `RESTORE` origination actions — the two transitions that hand origination capability back to a customer — while `ERR_ORIGINATION_NOT_ENABLED` guards call signing and number assignment alone. A successful creation lands the assignment in `status` `ACTIVE` with `revoked_at` null, and the provider's `active_assigned_number_count` is recomputed.

`ownership_evidence` is the field the attestation ladder consults, and only two of its four values count as **verified ownership**: `DIRECT_ALLOCATION`, where the number was allocated to the provider directly, and `PORTED_IN`, where the number was ported to the provider under a subscriber's authorisation. The other two — `LEASED_FROM_CARRIER` and `UNVERIFIED` — are recorded faithfully but do not establish verified ownership, and a call placed from such a number can never reach attestation level `A`.

An assignment can be revoked by a `ProviderAdmin` from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_BY_CUSTOMER`, which stamps `revoked_at` at the current logical clock, moves `status` to `REVOKED`, and recomputes the provider's `active_assigned_number_count` — the compensating half of the number-inventory invariant. `REVOKED` is terminal: an already-revoked assignment can neither be revoked again nor suspended, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409), a code that guards revocation alone. Revocation also happens as a system event — when the owning customer is terminated, and when a provider discharges an illegal-traffic notice by revoking the offending numbers.

Assignments are never suspended or reactivated by a direct request. `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED_BY_CUSTOMER` happens only as a cascade from the owning customer travelling `ENABLED → SUSPENDED`, and `SUSPENDED_BY_CUSTOMER → ACTIVE` only as a cascade from that customer travelling `SUSPENDED → ENABLED`; each hop recomputes the provider's `active_assigned_number_count`. Since no notification is emitted, the assignment collection — filterable by `provider_id`, by `customer_id`, by `telephone_number` and by `status` — is the only channel through which a client discovers which assignments a cascade created, suspended or revoked.

### 3.6. Signing Calls and Assigning the Attestation Level

Signing is the operation the whole system exists to protect. A `ProviderAdmin` submits a call for signature under its own `provider_id`, naming the `customer_id` that originated it, the `calling_number` presented as the calling party, the `called_number` dialled, and the `origination_time` — the ISO 8601 UTC instant the call was placed, and the instant at which number ownership is evaluated. Both numbers must be well-formed E.164 or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_NUMBER_FORMAT`, and the named customer must belong to the signing provider or the refusal is `ERR_CUSTOMER_PROVIDER_MISMATCH`.

Four gates then apply, in the relative order §2.8 fixes. The provider must have a non-null `active_certificate_id` — that is, exactly one certificate in `status` `ACTIVE` — or signing is refused with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_CERTIFICATE` (409). The provider's `auth_posture` must not be `SIGNING_SUSPENDED`, or the refusal is `ERR_SIGNING_SUSPENDED` (409); that code guards signing and nothing else. The customer's `kyc_state` must be `VERIFIED`, or the refusal is `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_KYC_VERIFIED` (422). And the customer's `origination_state` must be `ENABLED`, or the refusal is `ERR_ORIGINATION_NOT_ENABLED` (422).

Once the gates pass, the service resolves ownership. It looks for a number assignment belonging to the named customer whose `telephone_number` equals the `calling_number`, whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and whose half-open interval contains `origination_time` — meaning `effective_from` is at or before `origination_time` and `origination_time` is strictly before `effective_to`, with a null `effective_to` treated as unbounded into the future. The computed field `ownership_verified` is true when such an assignment exists **and** its `ownership_evidence` is `DIRECT_ALLOCATION` or `PORTED_IN`; in every other case — no covering assignment, a suspended or revoked one, or a covering assignment whose evidence is `LEASED_FROM_CARRIER` or `UNVERIFIED` — `ownership_verified` is false.

The attestation level is then assigned by walking a **seven-step ladder in exactly this order and stopping at the first step that matches**. The inputs are the signing provider's `auth_posture`, the customer's `risk_tier`, the customer's `kyc_evidence_level`, and `ownership_verified`.

1. If `auth_posture` is `GATEWAY_LIMITED`, the level is `C` with `attestation_reason_code` `GATEWAY_PASSTHROUGH`. Posture beats every other input.
2. Otherwise, if `risk_tier` is `HIGH`, the level is `C` with reason `RISK_DOWNGRADE`. Recent traffic risk beats know-your-customer strength.
3. Otherwise, if `kyc_evidence_level` is `BUSINESS_REGISTRY_VERIFIED` and `ownership_verified` is true, the level is `A` with reason `FULL_KYC_AND_OWNERSHIP`.
4. Otherwise, if `kyc_evidence_level` is `BUSINESS_REGISTRY_VERIFIED` and `ownership_verified` is false, the level is `B` with reason `KYC_ONLY_NO_OWNERSHIP`.
5. Otherwise, if `kyc_evidence_level` is `CONTRACT_ONLY` and `ownership_verified` is true, the level is `B` with reason `OWNERSHIP_WITH_PARTIAL_KYC`.
6. Otherwise, if `kyc_evidence_level` is `CONTRACT_ONLY` and `ownership_verified` is false, the level is `C` with reason `NO_VERIFIED_BASIS`.
7. Otherwise — which is to say whenever `kyc_evidence_level` is `SELF_ATTESTED` — the level is `C` with reason `NO_VERIFIED_BASIS`.

`attestation_level` is therefore always one of `A`, `B` or `C`, and `attestation_reason_code` is one of `FULL_KYC_AND_OWNERSHIP`, `OWNERSHIP_WITH_PARTIAL_KYC`, `KYC_ONLY_NO_OWNERSHIP`, `RISK_DOWNGRADE`, `POSTURE_DOWNGRADE`, `GATEWAY_PASSTHROUGH` or `NO_VERIFIED_BASIS` — though `POSTURE_DOWNGRADE` is declared for completeness only and no step of the ladder ever produces it, since a `GATEWAY_LIMITED` provider reports `GATEWAY_PASSTHROUGH` and a `SIGNING_SUSPENDED` one cannot sign at all, so every stored record carries one of the other six. The reason code is part of the observable result and is what distinguishes paths the level alone collapses: a customer with `BUSINESS_REGISTRY_VERIFIED` evidence and verified ownership signing under a `GATEWAY_LIMITED` provider is `C` with `GATEWAY_PASSTHROUGH`, while the same customer at `risk_tier` `HIGH` under a `FULL_ATTESTATION` provider is also `C` but with `RISK_DOWNGRADE`. Neither ever reaches `A`, and no `GATEWAY_LIMITED` provider and no `HIGH`-risk customer can ever produce a level above `C`.

Three further fields are computed at signing time. `risk_tier_at_signing` is a snapshot of the customer's `risk_tier` at the moment of signature and is never recomputed afterwards, even when later monitoring moves the customer to a different tier. `certificate_id` is resolved server-side from the provider's `active_certificate_id` — it is never supplied by the client. And `passport_identity_token` is the deterministic identity header material for the signature: the literal text `shaken;`, followed by the resolved `certificate_id`, followed by a semicolon, followed by the assigned `attestation_level`. Signed-call records are immutable evidence: as noted in §2.4 they carry no `version` and no `updated_at`, and no operation modifies or deletes one. They are discoverable individually and through a collection filterable by `provider_id`, by `customer_id`, by `certificate_id` and by `attestation_level`, the last of which is how a compliance officer counts level-`A` traffic for a reporting period.

### 3.7. Traffic Monitoring, Blocking Analytics and Erroneous-Blocking Disputes

Traffic monitoring is the provider's own early-warning system. A `ProviderAdmin` records one closed monitoring window at a time under its own `provider_id`, naming the monitored `customer_id`, the window bounds `window_start` and `window_end`, and four tallies drawn from that window: `total_call_count`, the calls the customer originated; `short_duration_call_count`, those lasting under six seconds; `unanswered_call_count`, those never answered; and `complaint_count`, the subscriber complaints attributed to the window. None of the four may be negative, and neither `short_duration_call_count` nor `unanswered_call_count` may exceed `total_call_count`; a violation of any of those is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Both of those cross-field comparisons are request-shape rules belonging to §2.8 step 3, so they are reported ahead of the interval ordering of step 5 and ahead of the `provider_id` and `customer_id` lookups of step 7. The acting operator's `X-User-ID` is stored as `recorded_by_user_id`.

Unlike a number assignment, a monitoring window is **closed at both ends** — `window_start` and `window_end` are both inclusive — and that changes the collision rule. A new window may not overlap any existing window of the same customer, and because both bounds are inclusive, merely *touching* a neighbour's endpoint is already a collision: a window starting at exactly the instant an earlier window ended is refused with `ERR_OBSERVATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Contrast this deliberately with the half-open assignment intervals of §3.5, where the same adjacency is allowed. Two further temporal rules apply: `window_start` must be strictly earlier than `window_end` or the refusal is `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (422), and `window_end` must be at or before the current logical clock — there are no observations about the future — or the refusal is `ERR_WINDOW_IN_FUTURE` (422). The monitored customer must also belong to the recording provider, or the refusal is `ERR_CUSTOMER_PROVIDER_MISMATCH`. No customer-posture gate applies to monitoring: a window may be recorded whatever the customer's `kyc_state` and whatever its `origination_state`, so observation history keeps accruing for a customer that is already suspended or terminated.

Each window earns a `window_subscore` computed once, at creation, and stored. When `total_call_count` is 0 the raw score is 0. Otherwise three ratios are formed against the total: the short-duration ratio is `short_duration_call_count` divided by `total_call_count`, the unanswered ratio is `unanswered_call_count` divided by `total_call_count`, and the complaint ratio is `complaint_count` divided by `total_call_count`. The raw score is then forty-five hundredths of the short-duration ratio, plus thirty-five hundredths of the unanswered ratio, plus twenty hundredths of a complaint term — that term being the complaint ratio multiplied by 50 and then capped at 1, so that one complaint per fifty calls already saturates the complaint contribution. The raw score is rounded half up to two decimal places, and that two-decimal value is what is persisted as `window_subscore`.

The customer's `traffic_risk_index` is then recomputed **from the stored two-decimal subscores, never from the unrounded raw values**. Take the customer's three observations with the greatest `window_end`, ordered by `window_end` descending, breaking any tie on `created_at` ascending and, for two windows sharing both `window_end` and `created_at`, on insertion order — never on `id`. Assign the weights fifty hundredths, thirty hundredths and twenty hundredths positionally down that ordered list. If the customer has fewer than three observations, use only the leading weights — one observation uses only the first, two use the first and second — and renormalise by dividing by the sum of the weights actually used. The index is the sum of each weight multiplied by its window's `window_subscore`, divided by the sum of the weights used, rounded half up to four decimal places. The two-stage rounding is observable and mandatory: three windows scoring 0.62, 0.38 and 1.00 under the per-window rounding yield an index of 0.7380, whereas carrying the raw unrounded values all the way through would yield 0.7390. The two-decimal stage is the specification.

Because the index depends on which windows are the three most recent by `window_end`, recording an observation whose window is *older* than existing ones can still change the index — it is recomputed on every insert regardless of where the new window falls in the ordering, and it is never recomputed at any other time, since observations are immutable and cannot be edited or deleted. The recomputation also refreshes `risk_tier`, which reads `HIGH` when `traffic_risk_index` is 0.7500 or greater, `ELEVATED` when it is 0.4000 or greater but below 0.7500, and `LOW` otherwise, and it refreshes `observation_count`. Two fields on the observation itself capture the outcome: `resulting_risk_index` is a snapshot of the customer's `traffic_risk_index` immediately after this window was folded in, and `triggered_suspension` is true exactly when this observation drove the index to 0.9000 or above while the customer's `origination_state` was `ENABLED` — in which case the customer is suspended with `suspension_cause` `TRAFFIC_RISK_THRESHOLD` and `suspension_source_id` set to this observation's `id`, with the assignment cascade of §3.4 following. The customer row absorbs the refreshed `traffic_risk_index`, `risk_tier` and `observation_count` together with the suspension where it fires as a **single** mutation, so recording one window advances that row's `version` by exactly 1 no matter how much of the chain moved. Note the two distinct thresholds: 0.7500 makes a customer `HIGH` risk and therefore caps its calls at attestation level `C`, while the higher 0.9000 stops it originating at all. Monitoring windows are readable individually and as a collection filterable by `provider_id` and by `customer_id`.

Blocking is the other half of the fraud-analytics picture, and it is where the system's own error mode lives. A `FraudAnalyst` records a block against a customer's calling number by naming the `provider_id` whose analytics fired, the `customer_id` the blocked number is assigned to, the `calling_number` itself, the `analytics_score` that drove the decision — a four-decimal value in the range 0.0000 to 1.0000 — and a `reason` of `ANALYTICS_SCORE`, `NOTICE_MANDATE` or `UNSIGNED_TRAFFIC`. When the `reason` is `ANALYTICS_SCORE` the score must be at least 0.6500, and a lower score is refused with `ERR_SCORE_BELOW_BLOCKING_THRESHOLD` (422); the threshold does not apply to the other two reasons, which are mandated rather than scored. This threshold and the suspension threshold below are both evaluated against the score **exactly as supplied**, before the four-decimal rounding of §2.7 is applied on persistence, so a supplied 0.64999 is refused even though it would have been stored as 0.6500. The customer must belong to the named provider, or the refusal is `ERR_CUSTOMER_PROVIDER_MISMATCH`, and the number must be well-formed E.164 or the refusal is `ERR_INVALID_NUMBER_FORMAT`.

A recorded block lands in `status` `BLOCKED`, stamps `blocked_at` with the current logical clock, records the acting analyst's `X-User-ID` as `blocked_by_user_id` — the value the segregation-of-duties gate will later compare against — and leaves `reversed_at` null. Its `triggered_suspension` field is true exactly when the `reason` is `ANALYTICS_SCORE` **and** the `analytics_score` is 0.9500 or above — the flag records that the analytics threshold was crossed rather than that a suspension actually followed, so unlike an observation's identically-named field it carries no further condition on the customer's `origination_state` and reads true even against a customer already `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` — and where that customer was `ENABLED` it travels `ENABLED → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_cause` `BLOCKING_ANALYTICS` and `suspension_source_id` set to this event's `id`, cascading to the customer's `ACTIVE` assignments and thence to the provider's `active_assigned_number_count`. Every recorded block, whatever its reason or score, enlarges the denominator of the provider's `blocking_false_positive_rate`, so recording a block always lowers that rate (or leaves it at 0.0). Blocking events are readable individually and as a collection filterable by `provider_id`, by `customer_id` and by `status`, and they are visible to the affected `Caller` as well as to the provider and the regulator.

A legitimate caller that has been blocked in error files a dispute. Only a `Caller` may do so, supplying the `blocking_event_id` of the block it contests and a `claim_note` stating why its traffic is legitimate; the dispute's `customer_id` is taken from the caller's own `X-User-ID` rather than from the body. Because a `Caller` reaches only its own rows, a block recorded against some other customer cannot be disputed at all: naming such a `blocking_event_id` is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403) at §2.8 step 8, ahead of the duplicate check below. A block may carry only one live dispute: if the event already has a dispute in `FILED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `UPHELD` or `REVERSED`, the filing is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DISPUTE` (409). A `WITHDRAWN` dispute is the exception — it leaves the event free to be disputed again. The new dispute lands in `state` `FILED`, stamps `filed_at` with the current logical clock, and derives `resolution_deadline_at` as exactly twenty-four hours after `filed_at`.

A `FraudAnalyst` takes the dispute under review, advancing `FILED → UNDER_REVIEW`, and then resolves it with an `outcome` of `UPHOLD` or `REVERSE` accompanied by a `resolution_note` recording the adjudicating analyst's rationale. Resolution is only available from `UNDER_REVIEW`; review only from `FILED`; withdrawal only from `FILED`. Any of those attempted from the wrong state is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_STATE_INVALID` (409), and those three operations — review, resolve and withdraw — are the complete set that code guards. Resolution also requires a different analyst than the one who applied the block, per §2.12 — and that gate sits at §2.8 step 9, **ahead** of the state precondition, so an analyst reaching for its own block is told about the segregation of duties even when the dispute is in the wrong state for resolution. Either outcome stamps `resolved_at` with the current logical clock, records the resolving analyst's `X-User-ID` as `resolved_by_user_id`, and sets `resolved_late` to true when `resolved_at` is strictly after `resolution_deadline_at` and false otherwise — resolving at exactly the deadline instant is timely.

The two outcomes then diverge sharply. An `UPHOLD` advances the dispute `UNDER_REVIEW → UPHELD` and the disputed event `BLOCKED → UPHELD`; the provider's `blocking_false_positive_rate` is recomputed, but since neither the numerator nor the denominator changed, it lands on the same value. Nothing else moves. A `REVERSE` advances the dispute `UNDER_REVIEW → REVERSED` and the disputed event `BLOCKED → REVERSED`, stamping the event's `reversed_at` at the current logical clock, and then unwinds the whole chain the block set in motion. The affected customer's `false_positive_count` is recomputed upward, and that recompute together with the restoration described next is a single mutation of the customer row, so its `version` advances by exactly 1 however much of the chain fires. If — and only if — that customer's `suspension_cause` is `BLOCKING_ANALYTICS` and its `suspension_source_id` names this very event, the customer travels `SUSPENDED → ENABLED` with both `suspension_cause` and `suspension_source_id` cleared to null; because the customer was re-enabled, every assignment of that customer whose `status` is `SUSPENDED_BY_CUSTOMER` returns to `ACTIVE`; and because those assignments re-entered the active set, the provider's `active_assigned_number_count` and `blocking_false_positive_rate` are both recomputed. The count returns to precisely the value it held before the block, while the false-positive rate rises, since the reversal added to its numerator. A customer suspended for some other cause is *not* restored by this reversal even if the reversed block was against that same customer — the source pointer is the test.

The disputing caller may instead withdraw its own dispute while it is still `FILED`, which advances `FILED → WITHDRAWN` and stamps `resolved_at` while leaving `resolution_note` and `resolved_by_user_id` null and `resolved_late` false — a withdrawal is not a resolution and records no resolver, whatever the clock reads against `resolution_deadline_at`. Withdrawal leaves the disputed event standing in `BLOCKED` and leaves every consequence of the block — including any suspension it caused — untouched; its only effect beyond the dispute row is to free the event for a later dispute. Only the caller named by the dispute's own `customer_id` may withdraw it. Disputes are readable individually and as a collection filterable by `blocking_event_id`, by `customer_id` and by `state`.

### 3.8. Traceback Response, the Downstream Blocking Obligation, and the Compliance Sweep

When the regulator traces an illegal call back through the network it serves a traceback request on the provider it believes originated it. The `Regulator` creates the request naming the `provider_id` obliged to answer, the `calling_number` and `called_number` of the traced call, its `origination_time`, and optionally an `upstream_provider_note` carrying free-text attribution the regulator already holds. Service is stamped as `received_at` at the current logical clock, and both deadlines are derived from it at that moment: `response_deadline_at` is exactly twenty-four hours after `received_at`, and `hard_cutoff_at` is exactly seventy-two hours after `received_at`. The new request lands in `state` `RECEIVED` with `responded_at`, `identified_customer_id` and `produced_attestation_id` all null, `lateness_seconds` 0 and `call_detail_complete` false, and with `upstream_provider_note` null whenever the regulator omitted it. The traced `origination_time` is recorded as supplied and compared against nothing at service time — the traced call may sit anywhere relative to the logical clock, including after it, and no interval or future-instant rule applies to a traceback request.

Answering the request means producing call detail, which a `ComplianceOfficer` does by naming the `identified_customer_id` — the originating customer the provider attributes the call to — and the `produced_attestation_id` of the signed-call record that documents it, optionally amending `upstream_provider_note` — supplying it overwrites whatever the regulator recorded, and omitting it leaves that value untouched rather than clearing it. Both identifiers are mandatory on the response. The produced record must genuinely describe the traced call: its `provider_id` must equal the traceback's `provider_id`, its `customer_id` must equal the supplied `identified_customer_id`, its `calling_number` and `called_number` must equal the traceback's, and its `origination_time` must equal the traceback's `origination_time`. Any discrepancy is refused with `ERR_CALL_DETAIL_MISMATCH` (422). Separately, the identified customer must hold at least one number assignment covering the traced `calling_number` at `origination_time` — covering in the same half-open sense as §3.6, `effective_from` at or before `origination_time` and `origination_time` strictly before a non-null `effective_to`, a null `effective_to` reaching indefinitely forward — but in **any `status` whatever**. Unlike the ownership resolution of §3.6, which insists on an `ACTIVE` assignment, this coverage test ignores `status` entirely: an assignment now `SUSPENDED_BY_CUSTOMER`, or even one already `REVOKED`, still satisfies it, because the question the traceback asks is who held the number when the call was placed and not who holds it now. A response that omits either identifier, or whose identified customer has no such assignment, is refused with `ERR_CALL_DETAIL_INCOMPLETE` (422). Both codes are raised by this operation and no other, and a mismatch is reported ahead of an incompleteness.

Timeliness is then evaluated against the two derived deadlines, with both boundaries inclusive of the deadline instant. If the current logical clock is strictly after `hard_cutoff_at`, the response is refused outright with `ERR_RESPONSE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409) and the request keeps whatever state it was in — a provider that has let three days pass cannot buy its way back into compliance with a late filing. If the clock is at or before `response_deadline_at`, the request advances to `RESPONDED` with `lateness_seconds` 0. Otherwise — the clock is after `response_deadline_at` but at or before `hard_cutoff_at` — the request advances to `RESPONDED_LATE` and `lateness_seconds` is the number of seconds between `response_deadline_at` and `responded_at`, rounded up to a whole second, so any positive fraction of a second already counts as one. Either outcome stamps `responded_at` at the current logical clock and sets `call_detail_complete` to true. As a worked timeline: with `received_at` at `2026-08-05T00:00:00Z`, answering at `2026-08-06T00:00:00Z` is `RESPONDED` with `lateness_seconds` 0; answering one second later is `RESPONDED_LATE` with `lateness_seconds` 1; answering at exactly `2026-08-08T00:00:00Z` is accepted as `RESPONDED_LATE` with `lateness_seconds` 172800; and one second after that the response is refused.

A late answer costs 3 `penalty_points`, which forces a recompute of the provider's counters and then of `auth_posture` — the first late answer alone will not suspend signing, but it accumulates. A traceback the provider simply ignores is caught by the compliance sweep: when the sweep observes the current logical clock strictly after `response_deadline_at` on a request still in `RECEIVED`, the request moves `RECEIVED → OVERDUE`, the provider's `overdue_traceback_count` rises by one and its `penalty_points` by three, and the posture ladder runs — which is what takes a `FULL_ATTESTATION` provider down to `GATEWAY_LIMITED` at the very first overdue traceback, and to `SIGNING_SUSPENDED` once accumulated points reach 10. An `OVERDUE` request can still be answered while the clock is at or before `hard_cutoff_at`, which moves it `OVERDUE → RESPONDED_LATE` with `lateness_seconds` computed the same way and `call_detail_complete` set true. That answer lowers `overdue_traceback_count` by one but leaves `penalty_points` exactly where they were, because the penalty formula counts `OVERDUE` and `RESPONDED_LATE` alike; the posture ladder then runs again and can lift the provider from `GATEWAY_LIMITED` back to `FULL_ATTESTATION`, but only if no other traceback remains overdue and accumulated points are still below 10. `RESPONDED` and `RESPONDED_LATE` are terminal, and any attempt to answer a request already in either is refused with `ERR_TRACEBACK_ALREADY_CLOSED` (409). Traceback requests are readable individually and as a collection filterable by `provider_id`, by `identified_customer_id`, by `produced_attestation_id` and by `state`.

The downstream blocking obligation works on a shorter clock. A `Regulator` notifies a provider that it is carrying illegal traffic by naming the `provider_id`, the `calling_number` the regulator identified, and a `description` of the traffic pattern. The notice is stamped `notified_at` at the current logical clock, its `mitigation_deadline_at` is derived as exactly forty-eight hours after `notified_at`, and it lands in `state` `NOTIFIED` with `customer_id`, `mitigation_action` and `resolved_at` null and `penalty_points_assessed` 0. Creation recomputes the provider's `open_notice_count`. A response landing exactly on `mitigation_deadline_at` is timely; only a strictly later clock reading expires the notice.

The provider's first obligation is attribution. A `ComplianceOfficer` investigates the notice by supplying the `customer_id` it attributes the traffic to, which advances `NOTIFIED → UNDER_INVESTIGATION` and records that attribution on the notice. The named customer must belong to the notice's own provider, or the attribution is refused with `ERR_CUSTOMER_PROVIDER_MISMATCH` (422). Investigation is available only from `NOTIFIED`, and mitigation only from `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` with a non-null attribution already recorded; either attempted from the wrong state is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_ALREADY_CLOSED` (409), and those two operations are the complete set that code guards.

Mitigation discharges the obligation, and the `mitigation_action` supplied decides both the terminal state and the cascade. All three actions stamp `resolved_at` at the current logical clock and recompute the provider's `open_notice_count` downward. `CUSTOMER_TERMINATED` and `NUMBERS_REVOKED` both land the notice in `MITIGATED`; `TRAFFIC_BLOCKED` lands it in `ALL_TRAFFIC_BLOCKED`. With `CUSTOMER_TERMINATED`, the attributed customer's `origination_state` moves to `TERMINATED`, which revokes every one of that customer's assignments in `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_BY_CUSTOMER` and then recomputes the provider's `active_assigned_number_count`. With `NUMBERS_REVOKED`, the blast radius is narrower and precise: every assignment of the attributed customer whose `telephone_number` equals this notice's `calling_number` and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_BY_CUSTOMER` moves to `REVOKED`, and the provider's `active_assigned_number_count` is recomputed — the customer itself keeps originating on any other numbers it holds. With `TRAFFIC_BLOCKED`, the customer is not terminated but silenced: its `origination_state` moves `ENABLED → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_cause` `PROVIDER_REQUEST` and `suspension_source_id` set to this notice's `id`, which suspends its `ACTIVE` assignments to `SUSPENDED_BY_CUSTOMER` and recomputes `active_assigned_number_count`. A `TRAFFIC_BLOCKED` mitigation against a customer that is not `ENABLED` — one already suspended for some other cause, or terminated — still discharges the notice into `ALL_TRAFFIC_BLOCKED`, but leaves that customer's `origination_state`, `suspension_cause` and `suspension_source_id` exactly as they stand, so the pointer is never overwritten. That is the one case in which a `PROVIDER_REQUEST` suspension carries a source pointer, and it is also the one case in which the provider can later restore the customer itself, since a `ProviderAdmin` may clear exactly the `PROVIDER_REQUEST` cause.

Ignoring a notice is the expensive path. When the sweep observes the current logical clock strictly after `mitigation_deadline_at` on a notice still in `NOTIFIED` or `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, the notice moves to `EXPIRED_UNMITIGATED`, its `penalty_points_assessed` is set to 5, and the provider's `penalty_points` and `open_notice_count` are recomputed followed by `auth_posture` — five points being half the distance to outright signing suspension. If the notice had already been attributed to a customer and that customer's `origination_state` is `ENABLED`, expiry additionally drives that customer `ENABLED → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_cause` `NOTICE_EXPIRY` and `suspension_source_id` set to this notice's `id`; because the customer was suspended, every one of its `ACTIVE` assignments moves to `SUSPENDED_BY_CUSTOMER`; and because those assignments left the active set, the provider's `active_assigned_number_count` is recomputed alongside the posture recomputed from the new `penalty_points`. That is the four-hop chain of §2.11 in full, and each hop is caused by the hop before it rather than by the sweep call itself. A notice expiring from `NOTIFIED` has no attributed customer, so the chain stops after the provider-level recompute. `MITIGATED`, `ALL_TRAFFIC_BLOCKED` and `EXPIRED_UNMITIGATED` are all terminal. Notices are readable individually and as a collection filterable by `provider_id`, by the attributed `customer_id`, and by `state`.

The sweep itself is a `Regulator`-only capability that takes a single `provider_id` and returns the refreshed provider row. It performs, for that one provider, every deadline-driven transition described above, and in exactly this order: certificates whose `not_after` has passed are expired — only those in `ACTIVE` or `ISSUED`, a certificate still in `REQUESTED` being left untouched however long its `not_after` has lapsed — then traceback requests past their response deadline are marked overdue, then notices past their mitigation deadline are expired, and every downstream cascade those transitions cause is applied. Within each of those three families the provider's own rows are visited in `created_at` order. Nothing else is swept: dispute resolution deadlines and report periods drive no transition here, and no row outside the named provider's own graph is touched. It then recomputes the provider's derived counters and finally its posture, in that order, so that the posture reflects every counter movement the sweep produced rather than a stale intermediate reading. Each run increments the provider's `sweep_index` by 1 and, like any mutation, its `version` — by exactly 1, because the `sweep_index` bump and the counter-and-posture refresh that follows it are a single mutation of the provider row, and that mutation is recorded even on a run where no counter and no posture moved. The sweep is idempotent with respect to already-transitioned rows: running it twice with the clock unchanged transitions nothing the second time, though `sweep_index` still advances.

### 3.9. Effectiveness Reporting and Enforcement Exposure

Providers must report periodically on how well their mitigation actually works, and that reported score is the one lever that reduces a forfeiture. A `ComplianceOfficer` generates a report for its `provider_id` over a reporting period running from `period_start` to `period_end`, choosing a `variant` of either `PROVISIONAL` or `CERTIFIED`, and carrying the `expected_provider_version` required by §2.6. The period is **half-open**: `period_start` is included and `period_end` excluded. `period_start` must be strictly earlier than `period_end`, or the refusal is `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`, and `period_end` must be at or before the current logical clock so that the reported period has fully elapsed — a period extending into the future is refused with `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED` (422), a code raised by report generation alone.

Nine counts are gathered, all restricted to the reporting provider's own rows and all bounded by the half-open period. `signed_call_count` counts signed-call records whose `origination_time` falls in the period, and `attestation_a_count` counts those among them whose `attestation_level` is `A`. `traceback_total_count` counts traceback requests whose `received_at` falls in the period; `traceback_on_time_count` counts those among them in `state` `RESPONDED`; and `traceback_pending_count` counts those in `state` `RECEIVED` whose `response_deadline_at` is at or after the current logical clock — that is, still answerable and not yet overdue. `notice_total_count` counts illegal-traffic notices whose `notified_at` falls in the period; `notice_mitigated_count` counts those in `state` `MITIGATED` or `ALL_TRAFFIC_BLOCKED`; and `notice_open_count` counts those in `state` `NOTIFIED` or `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` whose `mitigation_deadline_at` is at or after the current logical clock.

Three component scores follow, each rounded half up to four decimal places. `attestation_component` measures signing quality: when `signed_call_count` is 0 the component is 0.0000 and `data_sufficiency` is `INSUFFICIENT`; otherwise it is `attestation_a_count` divided by `signed_call_count`, and `data_sufficiency` is `SUFFICIENT`. Note the asymmetry — an absence of signed calls is scored as zero rather than excused. `traceback_component` measures cooperation: when `traceback_total_count` is 0 the component is 1.0000 outright; otherwise it is a compliant numerator divided by `traceback_total_count`, where the numerator is `traceback_on_time_count` plus, **only under the `PROVISIONAL` variant**, `traceback_pending_count`. `notice_component` measures the blocking obligation the same way: when `notice_total_count` is 0 the component is 1.0000; otherwise it is `notice_mitigated_count` plus, **only under `PROVISIONAL`**, `notice_open_count`, all divided by `notice_total_count`. The `effectiveness_score` is then forty hundredths of `attestation_component`, plus thirty-five hundredths of `traceback_component`, plus twenty-five hundredths of `notice_component`, rounded half up to four decimal places **from the already-rounded components**, not from unrounded ratios. Its `score_band` reads `HIGH` at 0.8000 or above, `MID` at 0.6000 or above but below 0.8000, and `LOW` otherwise.

The two variants are the same algorithm with a different attitude toward work still in flight. `PROVISIONAL` presumes that a traceback still inside its response window and a notice still inside its mitigation deadline will land compliant, so both count toward the numerator. `CERTIFIED` counts only finished work: pending tracebacks and open notices are excluded from the numerator while remaining in the denominator, so certifying a period can only lower the score or leave it unchanged. The divergence is large enough to matter. Consider a provider with two signed calls of which one is level `A`, two tracebacks of which one is `RESPONDED` and one still `RECEIVED` inside its window, and two notices of which one is `MITIGATED` and one still `NOTIFIED` inside its deadline. Under `PROVISIONAL` the components are 0.5000, 1.0000 and 1.0000, giving 0.8000 and band `HIGH`. Under `CERTIFIED` they are 0.5000, 0.5000 and 0.5000, giving 0.5000 and band `LOW` — and as the forfeiture rules below show, that single difference moves the credit on a mid-tier assessment from 25 percent to 5 percent.

A new report lands in `state` `GENERATED` with `signed_off_by_user_id`, `signed_off_at` and `filed_at` null. Generation is also a create-with-supersede operation: any report of the same provider in `state` `GENERATED` or `SIGNED_OFF` whose period overlaps the new report's period moves to `SUPERSEDED`. Overlap is tested half-open on both sides, matching the periods themselves: two periods collide only when the new `period_start` is **strictly** before the other's `period_end` **and** the other's `period_start` is strictly before the new `period_end`. Back-to-back periods that merely touch — one ending at exactly the instant the next begins — therefore do not overlap, and neither supersedes the other; contrast the closed monitoring windows of §3.7, where that same adjacency is already a collision. The direction of the two periods is irrelevant, so generating a report over an earlier period supersedes a later overlapping one just as readily. Certification is where the report becomes authoritative. A `ComplianceOfficer` other than the one who filed the operative mitigation plan — per the third gate of §2.12 — signs off a `GENERATED` report, which requires that the provider's operative plan (the plan whose `filing_state` is neither `WITHDRAWN` nor `SUPERSEDED`) be `ACCEPTED`; if it is not, the refusal is `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID` (409). The three conditions are evaluated in the order §2.8 fixes rather than the order this paragraph narrates them: the segregation-of-duties comparison against the operative plan's `filed_by_user_id` comes **first** (step 9), then the plan-accepted requirement, then the report's own `GENERATED` precondition — so the officer who filed the operative plan is told about the segregation of duties even while that plan is still merely `SUBMITTED`, and even when the report has already been signed off. Where the provider has no operative plan at all there is no `filed_by_user_id` to compare against, so that first gate cannot fire and the refusal is `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. Sign-off advances `GENERATED → SIGNED_OFF`, records the signing officer's `X-User-ID` as `signed_off_by_user_id`, stamps `signed_off_at`, **overwrites `variant` with `CERTIFIED`**, and recomputes every count, every component, the `effectiveness_score`, the `score_band` and `data_sufficiency` in place under that variant. A `PROVISIONAL` report therefore loses exactly its pending-work numerators at the moment it is certified. That recomputation reads the logical clock as it stands at sign-off rather than as it stood at generation, so a traceback that has since passed its `response_deadline_at` drops out of `traceback_pending_count` even though it was inside its window when the report was first generated. Filing then advances `SIGNED_OFF → FILED` and stamps `filed_at`, recomputing nothing: every count, every component, the score and the band all stay exactly as sign-off left them. Certification is available only from `GENERATED` and filing only from `SIGNED_OFF`; either attempted otherwise is refused with `ERR_REPORT_STATE_INVALID` (409), and a `SUPERSEDED` report accepts neither. `FILED` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal. When a report that was the provider's score source is superseded, it stops being the score source, and the band that any **subsequent** enforcement assessment reads falls back to the next most recent report in `SIGNED_OFF` or `FILED`, or to `NONE` if none remains; enforcement actions already raised keep the band they were assessed under. Reports are readable individually and as a collection filterable by `provider_id`, by `state` and by `variant`.

Enforcement closes the loop. A `Regulator` assesses a forfeiture against a `provider_id`, stating the statutory `basis` — one of `LATE_TRACEBACK`, `UNMITIGATED_NOTICE`, `FALSE_ATTESTATION` or `UNFILED_PLAN` — together with a `violation_count` of at least 1, a `violation_days` of at least 1, and an `assessment_basis` of either `PER_VIOLATION` or `CONTINUING_DAILY` recording which assessment variant the regulator has elected. Both minimums are request-shape rules, so a counter supplied as 0 or negative is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) at §2.8 step 3 — including the counter the elected basis will go on to ignore, which must still be present and still be at least 1. Both counters are always supplied, but only one of them is charged, and which one is charged changes the amount, the tier and therefore the credit percentage.

The computation runs in four stages. First, `base_assessment_cents`: under `PER_VIOLATION` it is `violation_count` multiplied by one million cents — ten thousand dollars per discrete violation — and `violation_days` is ignored entirely; under `CONTINUING_DAILY` it is `violation_days` multiplied by two hundred and fifty thousand cents — two thousand five hundred dollars for each day the violation continued — and `violation_count` is ignored entirely. Second, the statutory cap: `base_capped_cents` is the lesser of `base_assessment_cents` and one hundred million cents, which is one million dollars. Third, `assessment_tier`, which is keyed on `base_capped_cents` — that is, **after the cap and before any credit**: `TIER_1` when `base_capped_cents` is at most two million cents, `TIER_2` when it is at most twenty million cents, and `TIER_3` above that. Fourth, the good-faith credit.

The credit rests on `score_band`, which the assessment reads from the provider's most recent effectiveness report in `state` `SIGNED_OFF` or `FILED`, ranked by `period_end` descending and then by `created_at` ascending. Reports still in `GENERATED`, and reports that have been `SUPERSEDED`, are ignored — which is why a provider that generated a flattering report but never certified it is treated as having no report at all, and its `score_band` on the action reads `NONE` with zero credit. The same reading covers a provider that has never generated any report at all: with no report in `SIGNED_OFF` or `FILED` to read, the action's own `score_band` is written as the literal `NONE`, a band value no report row can itself ever carry since a report's band is always `HIGH`, `MID` or `LOW`. The credit percentage depends on both the tier and the band. In `TIER_1` a `HIGH` band earns 30 percent, `MID` earns 15 percent, `LOW` earns 5 percent and `NONE` earns nothing. In `TIER_2` a `HIGH` band earns 25 percent, `MID` earns 10 percent, `LOW` earns 5 percent and `NONE` nothing. In `TIER_3` a `HIGH` band earns 15 percent, `MID` earns 5 percent, and both `LOW` and `NONE` earn nothing at all. `mitigation_credit_cents` is `base_capped_cents` multiplied by that percentage, rounded half up to a whole number of cents, and `total_forfeiture_cents` is `base_capped_cents` minus `mitigation_credit_cents`. The credit is the only rounding step in the whole computation.

Three worked cases fix the mechanics. With `violation_count` 3, `violation_days` 10 and band `HIGH`, an election of `PER_VIOLATION` gives a base of 3,000,000 cents, which is `TIER_2`, a credit of 25 percent or 750,000 cents, and a total of 2,250,000 cents — while `CONTINUING_DAILY` on the very same body gives a base of 2,500,000 cents, still `TIER_2`, a credit of 625,000 cents, and a total of 1,875,000 cents. That the tier is keyed pre-credit is visible in the second case: 8 violations under `PER_VIOLATION` with band `HIGH` give a base of 8,000,000 cents in `TIER_2`, a credit of 2,000,000 and a total of 6,000,000, whereas 8 days under `CONTINUING_DAILY` with the same band give a base of exactly 2,000,000 cents — precisely the `TIER_1` ceiling, and therefore `TIER_1` — earning 30 percent or 600,000 cents and leaving 1,400,000. The third case shows the cap biting first: 150 violations under `PER_VIOLATION` produce 150,000,000 cents, capped to 100,000,000, which is `TIER_3`; a `HIGH` band earns 15 percent or 15,000,000 cents, leaving a total of 85,000,000.

A new action lands in `status` `PROPOSED` with `contested_at`, `adjudicated_at`, `paid_at` and `paid_amount_cents` all null, and the provider's `outstanding_forfeiture_cents` is recomputed to include it. The whole breakdown — `base_assessment_cents`, `base_capped_cents`, `assessment_tier`, `score_band`, `mitigation_credit_cents` and `total_forfeiture_cents` — is computed once, at assessment, and then frozen on the row: certifying, filing or superseding a report afterwards never re-derives the band or the credit of an action already raised, and no later transition of the action recomputes them either. The provider's `ComplianceOfficer` may contest a `PROPOSED` action, which advances `PROPOSED → CONTESTED` and stamps `contested_at`; the outstanding total does **not** change, because a contested amount is still owed unless and until it is dismissed. The `Regulator` then adjudicates the contest with an `outcome` of `SUSTAIN` or `DISMISS`, stamping `adjudicated_at` either way. A `SUSTAIN` advances `CONTESTED → SUSTAINED` and again leaves `outstanding_forfeiture_cents` unchanged, since a sustained amount remains in the outstanding set. A `DISMISS` advances `CONTESTED → DISMISSED` and recomputes `outstanding_forfeiture_cents`, which restores the exact pre-assessment total — the compensating half of the monetary invariant of §2.10.

Payment is the provider's own act: a `ProviderAdmin` pays an action in `PROPOSED` or `SUSTAINED` by tendering an `amount_cents` that must equal the action's `total_forfeiture_cents` exactly. Neither a partial payment nor an overpayment is accepted, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422), a code raised by payment alone. A successful payment advances the action to `PAID`, stamps `paid_at` at the current logical clock, records the tendered figure as `paid_amount_cents`, and recomputes `outstanding_forfeiture_cents` with the action removed from the set. `PAID` and `DISMISSED` are terminal. Contesting anything other than a `PROPOSED` action, adjudicating anything other than a `CONTESTED` one, or paying anything other than a `PROPOSED` or `SUSTAINED` one is refused with `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_STATE_INVALID` (409). Enforcement actions are readable individually — with `base_assessment_cents`, `base_capped_cents`, `assessment_tier`, `score_band`, `mitigation_credit_cents` and `total_forfeiture_cents` all exposed so the arithmetic can be audited — and as a collection filterable by `provider_id` and by `status`.
