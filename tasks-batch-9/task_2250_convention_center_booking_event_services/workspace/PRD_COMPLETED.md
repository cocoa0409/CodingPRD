# Convention Center Booking & Event Services API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A publicly owned convention center sells the same square footage many times over before it ever sells it once. A national association wants the main hall for a citywide convention four years out; a regional trade group wants the same hall for three days in the same week two years out; a consumer show promoter wants the shoulder dates. The house cannot simply take the first caller, because the city funds the building to fill hotel rooms, which means citywide conventions must be allowed to outrank local events and consumer shows inside published booking windows — but the outranking has to be procedural, auditable, and bounded, not a matter of a sales manager's discretion. This API is the system of record for that procedure.

The service governs the full commercial lifecycle of an event: the booking policy windows that decide when each class of event may even ask for dates; the tentative hold hierarchy on each rentable space, with first-option ranking, challenge procedures and release deadlines; the license agreement that pins space, dates, rental rate, deposit and the move-in/move-out periods, and the amendment negotiation that follows it; the event services ordered against utilities, rigging, cleaning and security vendors under exclusive and preferred vendor rules; the show-floor labor called from union locals under their jurisdictional and work-rule constraints; the in-house caterer's food and beverage exclusivity and the narrow exceptions to it; the post-event settlement priced on either contracted or actually attested usage, together with its dispute and arbitration path; and the facility maintenance windows and capital projects that must be threaded through the booking calendar without silently stranding a licensee.

Two technical constraints shape the whole specification. First, every deadline, window and timing rule is evaluated against a **server logical clock** that the test harness controls explicitly — there is no reliance on wall-clock time. Second, every monetary amount is an integer number of cents and every formula has a pinned evaluation and rounding order, because a settlement that two parties compute differently is a settlement that gets disputed. All resources described here are mounted under the `/convention-center` prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that apply across every endpoint. Where §3 narrates a workflow, this section fixes the shapes, precedences and arithmetic that workflow relies on.

### 2.1. Booking Authority and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six roles below — the comparison being exact and case-sensitive, so `salesmanager` is not `SalesManager` — the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). `X-User-ID` is otherwise accepted as any non-empty string and is never checked for shape. If the headers are well formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorized for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The six recognized roles are `Licensee`, `SalesManager`, `EventServicesManager`, `SettlementAnalyst`, `FacilitiesPlanner`, and `VenueAdmin`.

- A `Licensee` is the licensee organization itself, acting on its own bookings, licenses and settlements. When `X-User-Role` is `Licensee`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Client`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Client` compare the resource's client reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, for a license or a settlement the server walks from the target resource to its booking and compares that booking's `client_id` to `X-User-ID`; for a deposit it compares the `Client` named in the path to `X-User-ID`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This ownership check is enforced on exactly five operations: countersigning an issued license, accepting an open license amendment, disputing an issued settlement, recording a deposit payment on a client, and reading a single booking by its identifier. It is not applied to the collection list endpoints, which a `Licensee` may call with filters.
- A `SalesManager` books space, manages the tentative hold hierarchy and negotiates the license agreement. When `X-User-Role` is `SalesManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- An `EventServicesManager` runs event services ordering, union labor coordination, catering and show-floor attestation. When `X-User-Role` is `EventServicesManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. It is recorded as `recorded_by_user_id` on each `SpaceUsageRecord` this operator attests, but it is never compared to any entity's `id`.
- A `SettlementAnalyst` assembles, issues, escalates and finalizes event settlements. When `X-User-Role` is `SettlementAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. It is recorded as `issued_by_user_id` and `finalized_by_user_id` on `EventSettlement`, and those two recorded values are compared to each other (see §2.12), but neither is ever compared to any entity's `id`.
- A `FacilitiesPlanner` schedules maintenance windows and capital projects around the booking calendar. When `X-User-Role` is `FacilitiesPlanner`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, recorded at most for audit and never compared to any entity's `id`.
- A `VenueAdmin` is the venue administrator, holding the configuration, override and epoch-advance powers. When `X-User-Role` is `VenueAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared to any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` and is followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one timestamp in the system taken from the host wall clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.5, so it does not move when the clock is set.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints, and only for list endpoints. Two successful operations return a plain computed object rather than a resource in `data`: the hold sweep returns its four counters and the clock advance returns `current_utc`.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Filtering

Every collection endpoint accepts two pagination query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip before the first returned record and defaults to 0. A supplied value that is not parseable as an integer, a `limit` outside 1 through 100, or a negative `offset` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); an `offset` beyond the end of the collection is legal and simply yields an empty `data` array with the true `total_count`. Both appear in the response `meta` alongside `total_count` (the number of records matching the filters, before pagination) and `has_more`, which is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of records actually returned is still less than `total_count`.

Unless a specific collection says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with insertion order breaking ties between records sharing a timestamp — which is common, because `created_at` is stamped from the logical clock and many records are therefore written at the same instant. Two collections are ordered by their domain key instead: the attested usage days of a single booking — meaning the nested `GET /convention-center/bookings/{id}/usage-records` and not the flat `GET /convention-center/usage-records`, which keeps the default `created_at` ordering — are returned in `usage_date` ascending order, and the charge lines of a single settlement are returned in append order, which is `created_at` ascending.

Collections also accept domain filters, all of them optional and all of them combinable with the pagination parameters. Every filter is an exact string comparison against the record's stored field, so a syntactically well-formed value that matches nothing simply yields an empty page rather than an error, and a query parameter the collection does not declare is ignored rather than rejected. A field holding null matches only the literal filter values `null`, `None` or the empty string, and a boolean field is compared case-insensitively against `true` and `false`. Licensee organizations may be filtered by `organization_type` and `booking_standing`. Spaces may be filtered by `parent_space_id` — which selects the children of one parent space — and by `space_class`. Booking policy windows may be filtered by `event_class`. Vendors may be filtered by `service_category` and `vendor_tier`. Union locals may be filtered by `covered_category`. Bookings may be filtered by `client_id`, `space_id`, `policy_window_id` and `status`. Holds may be filtered by `booking_id`, `challenged_by_hold_id` and `status`. Licenses may be filtered by `booking_id` and `status`. Service orders may be filtered by `booking_id`, `vendor_id`, `approval_status` and `billing_status`. Labor calls may be filtered by `booking_id`, `jurisdiction_id` and `status`. Catering arrangements may be filtered by `booking_id`, `vendor_id` and `status`. Usage records may be filtered by `booking_id`. Settlements may be filtered by `booking_id` and `status`. Settlement charge lines may be filtered by `settlement_id` and `line_kind`. Maintenance windows may be filtered by `space_id` and `status`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Almost every resource in this system carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID version 4 in canonical hyphenated form. It carries no type prefix and is never supplied by the client.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recording the **logical clock** instant of §2.5 at which the resource was first persisted, not the host wall clock. Two resources created while the clock sits at the same instant therefore carry identical `created_at` values.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, likewise read from the logical clock, refreshed on every mutation of the resource.
- `version` (integer): a counter that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade. It increments at most once per resource per request: a cascade that touches the same record several times while serving one request refreshes `updated_at` each time but advances `version` only once.

Two resources are exceptions because they are immutable append-only records. A `SpaceUsageRecord` — the attestation of how a space was actually used on one calendar day — carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has no `updated_at` and no `version`. A `SettlementLineItem` — one charge line on a settlement — likewise carries only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version`. Neither may be edited or removed once written.

The `version` counter matters beyond auditing: two write operations use it as a stale-write guard, described in §2.8. Because cascades mutate resources the client did not name in the request, a cascade that touches a parent, a sibling booking or a client record refreshes that resource's `updated_at` and increments its `version` just as a direct write would.

### 2.5. The Server Logical Clock

Every deadline, booking window, order-timing decision and date comparison in this specification reads a single server-held logical clock, never the host's wall clock. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. It never falls back to real time.

A `VenueAdmin` moves the clock with `POST /convention-center/_clock/set_current_utc`, supplying the target instant as `to_iso8601`. That field is a required string; a value that is absent, empty, or not parseable as an ISO 8601 instant is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while a parseable value carrying no zone offset is read as UTC. The clock may be moved backwards as freely as forwards. The response reports the new clock as `current_utc`, rendered with three fractional digits. Setting the clock mutates no entity and performs no sweep of its own: nothing is released, nothing is recomputed, nothing transitions. Time-driven consequences are realized only when a subsequent request evaluates a deadline, or when an administrator explicitly runs the hold sweep described in §3.3.

Where a rule compares against a *date* rather than an instant (the booking window, order timing, move-in and move-out gates, and vendor insurance expiry), the comparison uses the calendar date of the logical clock in UTC. Where a rule compares against an *instant* (the challenge deadline, the hold release deadline, the settlement dispute deadline), the comparison uses the full logical-clock instant. A deadline stamped as "midnight UTC of a date" means the instant at 00:00:00.000Z on that date.

### 2.6. Money, Crew-Hours and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integer numbers of cents. Every field whose name ends in `_cents` is an integer and is returned as a JSON number, never a string, never with a fractional part. Wherever a formula multiplies a cent amount by a fraction, the product is rounded to a whole number of cents using the **round half up** method: a residue of exactly half a cent rounds away from zero.

Rounding is not applied opportunistically. Each calculation in §3 states exactly where rounding happens and in what order, and those orders are binding. Two of them are load-bearing enough to restate here: the actual-usage rental basis rounds each attested day to whole cents *before* summing the days and *before* applying the class discount, and the actualized labor charge rounds each of its three hour bands to whole cents *before* adding the three bands together. Deferring the rounding to the end of either calculation produces a different integer and is wrong.

Crew-hours are the one quantity that is not an integer. `min_call_hours`, `overtime_multiplier`, `max_crew_hours_per_day`, `billed_hours`, the scheduled and actual hour-of-day fields, and `ordered_quantity` and `actual_quantity` are all fractional numbers. Crew-hour quantities are carried to one decimal place. Ratios and multipliers — discount fractions, vendor multipliers, timing factors, usage factors — are fractions carried at their input precision and are never rounded before they are applied.

### 2.7. The Space Tree and Interval Arithmetic

Rentable space is a tree. A `SpaceUnit` with a null `parent_space_id` is a top-level space; any other `SpaceUnit` names its parent through `parent_space_id`. A child physically occupies its ancestors when it is booked, and conversely booking an ancestor consumes its descendants. Throughout this specification a **related space** of some space S means S itself, any ancestor of S reached by following `parent_space_id` upward any number of times, or any descendant of S reached by following the same links downward any number of times. Sibling spaces that share a parent are *not* related to each other; the relation is strictly along one ancestral line.

Dates are ISO 8601 calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. Every date range in this system is a **closed interval**: both endpoints belong to the range. The number of days from an earlier date to a later date is the plain difference in calendar days, so an inclusive day count over a closed interval is that difference plus one — a booking whose move-in starts on the 3rd and whose move-out ends on the 7th spans five days.

Two closed intervals **intersect** when the first begins on or before the second ends and the second begins on or before the first ends. A single shared endpoint therefore counts as an intersection: a range ending on the 10th intersects a range starting on the 10th.

Maintenance windows demand separation rather than mere non-intersection. Given a maintenance window running from `window_start_date` through `window_end_date` and a separation of `buffer_days` days, the **buffered window** is that window extended backward by `buffer_days` days from its start and forward by `buffer_days` days from its end. A candidate interval **clears** the maintenance window only when it does not intersect the buffered window — equivalently, only when the candidate ends more than `buffer_days` days before `window_start_date`, or begins more than `buffer_days` days after `window_end_date`. Separation of exactly `buffer_days` days is not enough; the requirement is strictly more than that many days, on both sides.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Guards on Negotiated Records

Two write operations advance a negotiation baseline that other writers may have already moved. Both therefore require the client to declare which version of the parent record it last observed, and both refuse the write when that declaration is out of date.

Opening an amendment round on a license agreement — `POST /convention-center/licenses/{id}/amendments` — requires the field `expected_license_version` in the request body. It is not optional. Its value must equal the target `LicenseAgreement`'s current `version`. If any writer has advanced that license's `version` since the client last read it, the amendment is refused with `ERR_STALE_LICENSE_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and the client must re-read the license before it may open the next round. A new amendment legitimately supersedes the negotiation baseline, so a writer holding an outdated view of the license must not be allowed to author it. When the amendment is accepted, the license's `version` increments by 1, which is exactly what invalidates any other writer's stale expectation.

Placing a tentative hold on a booking — `POST /convention-center/bookings/{id}/holds` — requires the field `expected_booking_version` in the request body. It is not optional. Its value must equal the target `EventBooking`'s current `version`. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_BOOKING_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Each accepted hold re-derives `hold_count` on the booking and re-derives the hold hierarchy on the space, so a writer whose view of the booking predates that derivation is reasoning from a superseded hold ladder. An accepted hold increments the booking's `version` by 1.

No other endpoint takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. Requests to endpoints outside these two must not be rejected for omitting one.

### 2.9. Conserved Counters and Cascade Discipline

Several fields are **conserved counters**: their value is defined as an aggregate over child rows, and every operation that changes the child population must move the counter in step, in both directions. Three of these matter across the whole system.

`SpaceUnit.committed_space_days` is the sum of `space_days_committed` over the bookings placed on that space or on any of its descendant spaces whose `status` is one of `TENTATIVE`, `DEFINITE`, `IN_HOUSE`, `CLOSED` or `SETTLED`. It rises when a booking is created and falls by the same amount when that booking leaves the set — that is, when it reaches `RELEASED` or `CANCELLED`. Because the counter aggregates descendants, every movement is applied to the booking's own space *and* to each of that space's ancestors, each of which has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by the adjustment; sibling and descendant spaces are untouched.

`UnionJurisdiction.committed_crew_hours` is the sum of `billed_hours` over the labor calls against that local whose `status` is one of `SCHEDULED`, `DISPATCHED` or `ACTUALIZED`. Cancelled calls are excluded, and cancelling a call returns its hours to the local.

Deposit money is conserved between two accounts. A licensee's `credit_balance_cents` holds deposit money paid in but not yet escrowed against any license; a license's `deposit_held_cents` holds money escrowed against that specific license. Money moves between the two but is never created or destroyed by a state transition: issuing a license moves the deposit out of the client's credit balance and into escrow, voiding or terminating a license moves it back, and finalizing a settlement moves it out of escrow and onto the settlement as `deposit_applied_cents`.

Cascades run to completion within the request that triggers them, and their depth is not bounded by one hop. Approving a maintenance window can release a hold, which can release a booking, which can void a license and return its escrowed deposit. Finalizing a settlement can settle a booking, which can raise a client's outstanding balance, which can restrict that client's standing, which can lock the order posture on that client's *other* bookings. Every entity a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented; the client is not required to have named those entities in the request.

### 2.10. Order Posture: the Gate over Event Services

Each `EventBooking` carries a derived gate called `order_posture`, with the values `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` and `LOCKED`. It is not set directly by any client. It is recomputed whenever the booking's `status` changes and whenever the owning client's `booking_standing` changes, according to this derivation, evaluated top to bottom with the first matching rule winning:

1. `LOCKED` when the booking's `status` is `RELEASED`, `CANCELLED` or `SETTLED`, or when the owning client's `booking_standing` is `HOLD_RESTRICTED`.
2. `RESTRICTED` when the booking's `status` is `CLOSED`.
3. `OPEN` otherwise — that is, when the booking is `TENTATIVE`, `DEFINITE` or `IN_HOUSE` and its client stands `GOOD`.

What the posture gates: creating a service order, a labor call or a catering arrangement under the booking, and approving a service order or a catering arrangement, all require `OPEN`. Reconciling a service order is the one operation that a `RESTRICTED` posture still permits, though reconciliation carries a booking-state guard of its own that narrows this in practice (see §3.5). A `LOCKED` posture permits none of the six. A posture that is not permissive enough for the requested operation yields `ERR_ORDER_POSTURE_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409). Each gate applies the derivation above to the booking's current `status` and its client's current `booking_standing` at the moment of the request rather than trusting the stored field, so the two can never disagree; the stored `order_posture` is refreshed, with `updated_at` and `version`, only when the derivation actually yields a different value from the one on the row.

### 2.11. Validation Precedence and Left-to-Right Path Resolution

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is determined by a fixed precedence ladder rather than by the order in which an implementation happens to evaluate its checks. Checks are evaluated in this order, and the first failure short-circuits the request:

1. **Authentication.** Headers present and `X-User-Role` recognized, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role authorization.** The acting role appears in the called endpoint's authorized list, else `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. **Licensee ownership.** On the five owner-scoped operations named in §2.1, `X-User-ID` matches the client reached from the target resource, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
4. **Path existence.** Every templated identifier in the URL resolves, validated strictly left to right. The first unresolvable segment decides the error, and the request is refused before any deeper segment is examined. Each entity has its own code: `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SPACE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HOLD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LABOR_CALL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CATERING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BLACKOUT_NOT_FOUND` — all HTTP 404.
5. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign identifier carried in the request body resolves, validated in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, using the same 404 codes. This applies to booking creation, to service-order, labor-call and catering-arrangement creation under a booking, to challenging a hold, to appending a settlement charge line, and to proposing a maintenance window. A reference that is *shaped* wrongly rather than merely unresolvable — absent, null, not a string, or an empty string, where the endpoint requires it — is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) at this step rather than a 404. The one optional reference in the system, a space's `parent_space_id`, is skipped when absent or null and resolved as a 404 otherwise.
6. **Booking policy window published.** On booking creation only, a policy window must exist for the submitted `event_class`, else `ERR_POLICY_WINDOW_MISSING` (HTTP 422). This check deliberately precedes generic body validation: a booking that both names an unpublished event class and carries malformed dates reports the missing policy window.
7. **Request body validity.** Types, enum membership, positive quantities, and the non-decreasing date ordering of a booking's four dates, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
8. **Citywide qualification.** On booking creation only, else `ERR_CITYWIDE_QUALIFICATION_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422).
9. **State validity.** The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, else the state-invalid code of the entity whose state fails the guard: `ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_HOLD_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_LICENSE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_SERVICE_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_LABOR_CALL_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_CATERING_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_BLACKOUT_STATE_INVALID` — all HTTP 409. Where a transition's guard concerns a *related* entity's state rather than the target's own, the rejection carries that related entity's state-invalid code; §3 names the specific pairing for each such guard.
10. **Order posture.** As described in §2.10, else `ERR_ORDER_POSTURE_NOT_OPEN`.
11. **Stale-version guards.** As described in §2.8, else `ERR_STALE_LICENSE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_BOOKING_VERSION`.
12. **Uniqueness.** At most one policy window per event class (`ERR_POLICY_WINDOW_EXISTS`), at most one live license per booking (`ERR_LICENSE_ALREADY_EXISTS`), at most one settlement per booking (`ERR_SETTLEMENT_ALREADY_EXISTS`), at most one live catering arrangement per booking (`ERR_CATERING_ALREADY_EXISTS`), at most one usage record per booking per day (`ERR_USAGE_RECORD_DUPLICATE`) — all HTTP 409.
13. **Segregation of duties** on settlement finalization, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).
14. **Vendor and union eligibility**, in this order: vendor insurance currency (`ERR_VENDOR_COI_EXPIRED`), union lockout (`ERR_UNION_LOCKOUT`), rigging capability of the space (`ERR_SPACE_NOT_RIGGING_CAPABLE`), union jurisdiction match (`ERR_UNION_JURISDICTION_MISMATCH`), vendor category match (`ERR_VENDOR_CATEGORY_MISMATCH`), exclusive vendor requirement (`ERR_EXCLUSIVE_VENDOR_REQUIRED`), catering exclusivity (`ERR_CATERING_EXCLUSIVITY_VIOLATION`), outside-catering reason (`ERR_OUTSIDE_CATERING_REASON_REQUIRED`) — all HTTP 422.
15. **Temporal windows**, in this order: booking window (`ERR_BOOKING_WINDOW_CLOSED`), maintenance blackout clearance (`ERR_MAINTENANCE_BLACKOUT_CONFLICT`), confirmed-hold date conflict (`ERR_SPACE_DATE_CONFLICT`), hold release deadline (`ERR_RELEASE_DEADLINE_PASSED`), challenge deadline (`ERR_CHALLENGE_DEADLINE_PASSED`), move-in reached (`ERR_MOVE_IN_NOT_REACHED`), event ended (`ERR_EVENT_NOT_ENDED`), dispute window closed (`ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED`), dispute window still open (`ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_OPEN`), labor work date inside the event span (`ERR_WORK_DATE_OUTSIDE_EVENT`).
16. **Capacity and round limits**, in this order: hold ceiling (`ERR_HOLD_LIMIT_REACHED`), challenge permissibility (`ERR_CHALLENGE_NOT_PERMITTED`), challenge round ceiling (`ERR_CHALLENGE_LIMIT_REACHED`), amendment round ceiling (`ERR_AMENDMENT_LIMIT_REACHED`), settlement revision ceiling (`ERR_SETTLEMENT_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`), first-option requirement (`ERR_HOLD_NOT_FIRST_OPTION`), daily crew-hour ceiling (`ERR_UNION_CREW_OVERCOMMITTED`).
17. **Settlement composition**, in this order: usage records present for an actual-usage basis (`ERR_NO_USAGE_RECORDS`), rental line agreement (`ERR_RENTAL_LINE_MISMATCH`), child obligations discharged before close (`ERR_SERVICE_ORDERS_NOT_RECONCILED`).
18. **Fiscal gates**, in this order: deposit credit sufficiency (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_DEPOSIT_CREDIT`, HTTP 402), client standing (`ERR_CLIENT_HOLD_RESTRICTED`, HTTP 409), charge-line sign rules (`ERR_LINE_AMOUNT_INVALID`, HTTP 422).

Nested paths obey the same left-to-right discipline for reads as for writes. A request for the usage records of a non-existent booking reports `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND` rather than an empty list, and a request for the charge lines of a non-existent settlement reports `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_FOUND`.

Three framework-level rejections sit outside the ladder because no endpoint has been reached. A path that matches no route at all answers HTTP 404 with `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`; a path that matches a route under a method it does not declare answers HTTP 405 with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; and a request body that is present but is not parseable as a JSON object answers HTTP 400 with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An absent body is treated as the empty object, so an endpoint with no required fields accepts a request that carries no body at all.

### 2.12. Segregation of Duties on Settlement

Public money is involved, so the analyst who issues a settlement may not be the analyst who declares it final. Each `EventSettlement` records `issued_by_user_id` — the `X-User-ID` of the analyst who most recently issued or re-issued it, null while the settlement is still `DRAFT` — and `finalized_by_user_id`, the `X-User-ID` of the analyst who finalized it, null until then. On a finalization attempt the acting `X-User-ID` is compared to `issued_by_user_id`; if they are equal, the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). This check sits above the dispute-window check in the precedence ladder, so an analyst attempting to finalize their own issuance while the dispute window is still open sees the segregation error, not the window error. The arbitration exit from a settlement is deliberately exempt: a `VenueAdmin` finalizing out of arbitration is subject to neither the segregation check nor the dispute-window check.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Venue Registry: Licensees, Spaces, Vendors, Union Locals and Booking Policy

Nothing can be booked until the registry that constrains booking exists. Five kinds of reference record make up that registry, and four of the six roles contribute to it.

A licensee organization is registered as a **Client** by a `VenueAdmin` or a `SalesManager`, who supplies the organization's registered `legal_name`, a primary billing `contact_email`, and an `organization_type` of `NATIONAL_ASSOCIATION`, `LOCAL_ORGANIZATION` or `PROMOTER`. The classification is not cosmetic: it is the qualification gate for the highest-priority event class. Four money and posture fields are server-derived and all start at their floor: `total_deposits_paid_cents`, `credit_balance_cents` and `outstanding_balance_cents` all initialize to 0, and `booking_standing` initializes to `GOOD`. Recording a deposit payment against a client — an operation a `VenueAdmin`, a `SettlementAnalyst` or the `Licensee` itself may perform on its own record, supplying `amount_cents`, which must be an integer of at least 1, so that a zero or negative payment is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — adds that amount to both `total_deposits_paid_cents`, which is a lifetime total that never decreases, and `credit_balance_cents`, which is spendable escrow credit, and increments the client's `version` by 1. `total_deposits_paid_cents` is therefore a historical fact while `credit_balance_cents` is a live balance; the two diverge the moment a license is issued.

The `booking_standing` field is a derived posture with only two values, `GOOD` and `HOLD_RESTRICTED`, and it is recomputed on every change to `outstanding_balance_cents`. `outstanding_balance_cents` is the sum of `grand_total_cents` over every settlement reachable from this client through its bookings whose `status` is `FINAL`; settlements sitting in `DRAFT`, `ISSUED`, `DISPUTED` or `ARBITRATION` contribute nothing. It is re-derived as that whole aggregate rather than adjusted by a delta, and it may therefore fall as well as rise; a re-derivation that lands on the values already stored is a no-op that neither refreshes `updated_at` nor increments `version` nor disturbs any booking's `order_posture`. Standing becomes `HOLD_RESTRICTED` when that outstanding total is strictly greater than 100,000 dollars — 10,000,000 cents — and is `GOOD` otherwise. The comparison is strict: an outstanding balance of exactly 10,000,000 cents leaves the client `GOOD`. A `HOLD_RESTRICTED` client may not have a new booking created, may not have a new hold placed, and may not have a booking confirmed as definite; all three are refused with `ERR_CLIENT_HOLD_RESTRICTED`.

Rentable space is registered as a **SpaceUnit** by a `VenueAdmin` or a `FacilitiesPlanner`, who supplies the signage `name`, the physical `space_class` (`HALL`, `BALLROOM` or `MEETING_ROOM`), the billable `rentable_sqft` floor area, and the published rack rate `daily_base_rate_cents` charged per billable show day before any class discount. Both figures are required non-negative integers, so a fractional or negative area or rate is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Two optional inputs shape the space's behavior: `parent_space_id` nests the space under an existing one and defaults to null for a top-level space, and `rigging_capable` declares whether the ceiling grid will carry rigging work, defaulting to false. Both derived counters, `committed_space_days` and `active_blackout_count`, initialize to 0; the first is the conserved space-day counter of §2.9, and the second simply counts the maintenance windows on this space that have reached `APPROVED`.

An event services or catering **Vendor** is registered by a `VenueAdmin` or an `EventServicesManager` with the vendor's `legal_name`, the single `service_category` it may be ordered for (`UTILITIES`, `RIGGING`, `CLEANING`, `SECURITY` or `CATERING`), its contract standing `vendor_tier` (`EXCLUSIVE`, `PREFERRED` or `OPEN`), and its certificate-of-insurance expiry `coi_expiry_date`. The optional `is_in_house_caterer` flag, false by default, marks the single exclusive food and beverage provider. A vendor's insurance is current only while `coi_expiry_date` is strictly after the logical clock date; a certificate expiring exactly today is expired, and an order naming that vendor is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_COI_EXPIRED`.

Show-floor labor is supplied by **UnionJurisdiction** records, which only a `VenueAdmin` may register. Each names a `craft_name` and the single `covered_category` whose show-floor work the local claims — one of `UTILITIES`, `RIGGING`, `CLEANING` or `SECURITY`, with catering deliberately absent from the list — together with the per-crew-hour `straight_time_rate_cents` and the aggregate daily capacity `max_crew_hours_per_day`. Four work-rule inputs carry defaults: `overtime_multiplier`, the factor applied to hours outside the straight-time window, defaults to 1.5; `min_call_hours`, the minimum billable hours per crew member per call, defaults to 4; and the straight-time window runs from `st_window_start_hour`, inclusive and defaulting to 8, to `st_window_end_hour`, exclusive and defaulting to 17. The two window edges are integers between 0 and 24 inclusive and are not checked against each other; `straight_time_rate_cents` is a required non-negative integer, while `max_crew_hours_per_day`, `overtime_multiplier` and `min_call_hours` are non-negative numbers that may be fractional. A fifth optional input, `lockout_active`, defaults to false. The derived `committed_crew_hours` counter initializes to 0. A `VenueAdmin` may later revise a local, submitting `lockout_active` to declare or lift a lockout and `max_crew_hours_per_day` to adjust the daily capacity; both fields are optional on that revision, a field left out leaves its stored value alone, and each such revision increments the local's `version` by 1 even when it supplies neither field. Lowering the capacity below the hours already committed is accepted and never retroactively refuses a call already written; it only constrains the next one. While `lockout_active` is true no new call may be scheduled against the local and no already-scheduled call may be dispatched — both are refused with `ERR_UNION_LOCKOUT`.

Finally, only a `VenueAdmin` publishes the **BookingPolicyWindow** rows that make the booking calendar a policy instrument rather than a queue. There is at most one window per `event_class`, and a second attempt at the same class is refused with `ERR_POLICY_WINDOW_EXISTS`. Each window fixes eight parameters for its class. `priority_rank` orders the classes against one another, and a *lower* number outranks a higher one when a hold challenge is evaluated — so the citywide class is expected to carry the smallest rank. `open_months_before` and `close_months_before` bracket the interval in whole months before the show start inside which a booking of this class may be created. `max_holds_per_booking` caps how many live holds one booking may accumulate. `challenge_response_hours` is the number of hours a challenged holder is given to respond before the sweep takes the space away. `rental_discount_pct` is the fraction subtracted from gross rental for this class; it is a number at least zero and strictly less than one, and a value outside that half-open range is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `max_challenge_rounds` caps how many times a single hold may be challenged before further challenges are refused. The six numeric parameters other than the discount are required integers: `priority_rank`, `open_months_before` and `close_months_before` are unbounded and may be negative, while `max_holds_per_booking`, `challenge_response_hours` and `max_challenge_rounds` must be at least zero. Because body validity precedes the uniqueness check, a second window for an already-published class that also carries a malformed parameter reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_POLICY_WINDOW_EXISTS`.

### 3.2. From Enquiry to Tentative Booking

A `SalesManager` or a `VenueAdmin` creates an **EventBooking** by naming the licensee through `client_id`, the space through `space_id`, the public `event_name`, the booking priority `event_class` — `CITYWIDE_CONVENTION`, `LOCAL_EVENT` or `CONSUMER_SHOW` — and the four calendar dates that define the licensed period: `move_in_start_date`, `show_start_date`, `show_end_date` and `move_out_end_date`. An optional `projected_room_nights` records the hotel room nights the client commits to, must be a non-negative integer when supplied, and defaults to 0. `event_name` is a required non-empty string. The four dates must be non-decreasing in exactly that order, so move-in may begin on the same day the show opens but never after it, the show may be a single day, and move-out may end on the last show day but never before it; any other ordering is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The booking is created in `status` `TENTATIVE` with `order_posture` `OPEN`, and the server resolves `policy_window_id` by looking up the published policy window whose `event_class` equals the submitted one — a lookup that must succeed, or the request fails with `ERR_POLICY_WINDOW_MISSING` before the dates are even inspected.

Three gates stand between the request and the booking. The **citywide qualification** gate applies only when `event_class` is `CITYWIDE_CONVENTION`: the named client's `organization_type` must be `NATIONAL_ASSOCIATION` and `projected_room_nights` must be at least 2,500. Either failure is `ERR_CITYWIDE_QUALIFICATION_NOT_MET`. The **booking window** gate counts whole months from the logical clock date forward to `show_start_date`; that count must be no greater than the class's `open_months_before` and no less than its `close_months_before`. The count is computed as the difference in calendar months between the two dates — twelve times the year difference plus the month difference — reduced by one when the show start's day of month is smaller than the clock date's day of month, and it goes negative for a show start already in the past. A citywide window that opens 48 months out and closes 6 months out therefore accepts a show 47 months away and one exactly 6 months away, but refuses one 49 months away as too early and one 5 months away as too late — both with `ERR_BOOKING_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The **maintenance clearance** gate requires the booking's closed interval from `move_in_start_date` through `move_out_end_date` to clear every `APPROVED` maintenance window on the named space and on every related space, by the buffered-window rule of §2.7; a collision is `ERR_MAINTENANCE_BLACKOUT_CONFLICT`. And as noted in §3.1, a client standing `HOLD_RESTRICTED` cannot be booked at all.

Creation also prices the license. **Rental pricing** works from billable units rather than raw days, because move-in and move-out days are licensed at half rate. Count the show days as the inclusive number of days from `show_start_date` through `show_end_date`. Count the move-in days as the number of calendar days from `move_in_start_date` forward to `show_start_date`, and the move-out days as the number of calendar days from `show_end_date` forward to `move_out_end_date`; both of these are exclusive counts, so a move-in beginning two days before the show opens contributes two move-in days. The billable units are the show days plus one half of the sum of the move-in and move-out days. `gross_rental_cents` is the space's `daily_base_rate_cents` multiplied by those billable units, rounded half up to whole cents. `contracted_rental_cents` is `gross_rental_cents` multiplied by one minus the class's `rental_discount_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. `deposit_required_cents` is `contracted_rental_cents` multiplied by one quarter, rounded half up to whole cents. The three roundings happen in that sequence and each operates on the already-rounded integer produced by the previous step; collapsing them into a single rounded expression is wrong. All three fields default to 0 and are written by this calculation. The same calculation re-runs on any accepted license amendment that changes the booking dates.

Creation also charges the space calendar. `space_days_committed` is the inclusive number of days from `move_in_start_date` through `move_out_end_date` — the difference in calendar days plus one — and the named space's `committed_space_days`, together with that of every one of its ancestors, rises by exactly that figure per §2.9. The counter falls by the same figure when the booking later leaves the committed set by reaching `RELEASED` or `CANCELLED`, as described in §2.9. A separate derived field, `hold_count`, counts the holds on this booking whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `CHALLENGED` or `CONFIRMED`, and starts at 0.

The booking's `status` moves through seven values — `TENTATIVE`, `DEFINITE`, `IN_HOUSE`, `CLOSED`, `SETTLED`, `RELEASED` and `CANCELLED` — of which the last three, `SETTLED`, `RELEASED` and `CANCELLED`, are terminal. Two of the exits are available early. **Releasing** a booking, which a `SalesManager` and a `VenueAdmin` may each do from either `TENTATIVE` or `DEFINITE` — the two roles are interchangeable here and neither is confined to one of the two source states — drives `TENTATIVE → RELEASED` or `DEFINITE → RELEASED`; both require that no settlement yet exists for the booking, and an attempt on a booking that already has one is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_ALREADY_EXISTS`. **Cancelling** a booking is reserved to a `VenueAdmin` and drives `TENTATIVE → CANCELLED` or `DEFINITE → CANCELLED`; it requires that the booking hold no space at all, meaning `hold_count` must be exactly 0, and an attempt while any hold is still live is refused with `ERR_HOLD_STATE_INVALID`. Cancellation does exactly two things beyond setting the status: it decreases the space's `committed_space_days` by this booking's `space_days_committed`, and it recomputes `order_posture` to `LOCKED`. Notably it does *not* void a license or cancel service orders — the requirement that no hold survive is what makes that safe.

Release, by contrast, runs a broad cascade, and because several other operations reach `RELEASED` indirectly it is worth naming once. Whenever a booking reaches `RELEASED`, from whatever cause, the **release cascade** performs all of the following: every hold on the booking whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `CHALLENGED` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `RELEASED`, driving `hold_count` down to 0, and each such release re-ranks the surviving holds on the same space by the ranking rule in §3.3; the booking's license agreement, if its `status` is `DRAFT` or `ISSUED`, moves to `VOIDED`, its `deposit_held_cents` returning to the client's `credit_balance_cents` and then being set to 0; every service order on the booking whose `approval_status` is `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `APPROVED` moves to `CANCELLED` with its `billing_status` moved to `VOID` and, where that `billing_status` was `ESTIMATED`, its `estimated_charge_cents` reset to 0; the space's `committed_space_days` decreases by this booking's `space_days_committed`; and `order_posture` is recomputed to `LOCKED`. Every entity touched has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented.

### 3.3. The Tentative Hold Hierarchy: Ranking, Challenges and Release Deadlines

Space is not allocated to whoever asks first; it is queued. A `SalesManager` places a **SpaceHold** on a booking, and the request body carries nothing but the stale-write guard `expected_booking_version` described in §2.8 — every other attribute of the hold is derived. That guard is a required integer, and a body that omits it or carries a non-integer is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before any version comparison happens. Placement is not gated on the booking's `status` or on its `order_posture`; only the guards named below apply. The hold records its `booking_id`, copies the booking's `move_in_start_date` into `interval_start_date` and its `move_out_end_date` into `interval_end_date` as the two edges of its closed occupation interval, starts in `status` `ACTIVE` with `challenge_round` 0, `challenged_by_hold_id` null and `challenge_deadline_utc` null, and receives a `release_deadline_utc` stamped at midnight UTC of `show_start_date` less three whole months — a month shift that clamps the day of month to the last day of the resulting month, so a show starting on the 31st yields a deadline on the 28th, 29th or 30th as that month allows. The booking's `hold_count` rises by 1 and its `version` rises by 1; the hold itself is created at `version` 1, its derived `hold_rank` being written as part of creation rather than as a subsequent mutation.

Three gates guard the placement, beyond the version guard. The booking's `hold_count` must be strictly less than the class's `max_holds_per_booking`, so a booking already carrying its ceiling of live holds is refused with `ERR_HOLD_LIMIT_REACHED`. No hold whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` may exist on the same space or on any related space with a closed interval that intersects the new hold's — a confirmed hold is a committed hold, and a collision is refused with `ERR_SPACE_DATE_CONFLICT`. And the new hold's interval must clear every `APPROVED` maintenance window on the space and on every related space by the buffered-window rule, or the placement is refused with `ERR_MAINTENANCE_BLACKOUT_CONFLICT`. A `HOLD_RESTRICTED` client cannot place holds at all.

**Hold ranking** is what makes the queue meaningful. `hold_rank` is the hold's position in the tentative hierarchy on its space, with 1 meaning first option. For a hold being inserted, assemble its competitor set: every *other* hold whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `CHALLENGED` or `CONFIRMED`, sitting on the same space or on any related space, whose closed interval intersects the new hold's closed interval — recalling from §2.7 that a shared endpoint counts as an intersection. The new hold's `hold_rank` is one greater than the number of holds in that set, so a hold with no competitors ranks 1 and the third overlapping hold on a hall ranks 3. When a hold leaves the hierarchy by moving to `RELEASED`, the surviving members of *that* hold's competitor set are re-ranked: order them by their existing `hold_rank` ascending and break ties by `created_at` ascending and then by insertion order, which decides the many cases where two holds share a `created_at` because the logical clock never moved between them — never by `id` — and reassign ranks 1, 2, 3 and so on down the list. A release therefore promotes everyone below it by exactly one position and never renumbers holds on unrelated dates or unrelated spaces. The released hold keeps whatever `hold_rank` it last held rather than being renumbered or nulled, and a hold released while `CHALLENGED` keeps its `challenged_by_hold_id` and `challenge_deadline_utc` as a record of why it fell; only answering a challenge clears those two fields.

**Challenging** is how a higher-priority event takes dates away from a lower-priority one. A `SalesManager` challenges a hold by naming the challenging hold as `challenger_hold_id` in the request, driving the target `ACTIVE → CHALLENGED`. That field is required and must be a non-empty string, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and it must resolve to a hold, else `ERR_HOLD_NOT_FOUND`. Both holds must currently be `ACTIVE` — the target and the named challenger alike — and either one standing in any other state, including a `CONFIRMED` challenger or a target already `CHALLENGED`, is refused with `ERR_HOLD_STATE_INVALID`. Given two `ACTIVE` holds, the challenger's closed interval must intersect the target's on the same space or on a related space, and the `priority_rank` of the policy window governing the challenger's booking must be strictly less than the `priority_rank` of the policy window governing the target's booking — a lower rank number is the better claim, and equal ranks do not permit a challenge. Either failure is `ERR_CHALLENGE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Finally the target's `challenge_round` must be strictly less than its class's `max_challenge_rounds`, else `ERR_CHALLENGE_LIMIT_REACHED`. On success the target's `challenged_by_hold_id` is set to the challenger's identifier, its `challenge_round` increments by 1, and its `challenge_deadline_utc` is stamped at the logical clock instant plus the class's `challenge_response_hours` hours.

The challenged holder may **answer the challenge** and keep the space, driving `CHALLENGED → ACTIVE`. The hold must be `CHALLENGED`, and the logical clock instant must be at or before `challenge_deadline_utc`; answering exactly at the deadline instant still counts, while answering after it is refused with `ERR_CHALLENGE_DEADLINE_PASSED`. Answering is not free for the challenger: the hold named by `challenged_by_hold_id` moves to `RELEASED`, which decrements `hold_count` on the *challenger's* booking, and if that decrement brings the challenger's booking to `hold_count` 0 while its `status` is `TENTATIVE`, that booking runs the release cascade of §3.2. The surviving holds on the space are re-ranked, and the answering hold's `challenged_by_hold_id` and `challenge_deadline_utc` are both cleared back to null. This is a genuine cycle: a hold may be challenged, survive, and be challenged again, incrementing `challenge_round` each time, until `max_challenge_rounds` closes the door.

Letting the deadline lapse has the opposite outcome, and it is realized by the **hold sweep** — an administrative epoch-advance capability that only a `VenueAdmin` may run. The sweep does two things, in two separate passes and in this order: first, every hold whose `status` is `CHALLENGED` and whose `challenge_deadline_utc` has strictly passed moves `CHALLENGED → RELEASED`; then every hold whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `release_deadline_utc` has strictly passed likewise moves to `RELEASED`. Each pass walks the holds in `created_at` ascending order and reads the state as the earlier releases have left it, so a hold promoted out of the first pass but past its own release deadline is still swept in the second. A hold missing the relevant deadline is skipped rather than released. The sweep takes no request body and is idempotent: run twice at the same clock instant, the second run finds nothing to do and reports four zeroes. Each release decrements the owning booking's `hold_count` by 1, and a booking whose `hold_count` reaches 0 while its `status` is `TENTATIVE` moves to `RELEASED` and runs the full release cascade — which voids a `DRAFT` or `ISSUED` license, returns its `deposit_held_cents` to the client's `credit_balance_cents`, and cancels every service order in `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `APPROVED` with `billing_status` `VOID`. For a lapsed challenge specifically, the hold named by `challenged_by_hold_id` is promoted by the ranking rule and every hold below it on the space shifts up one position. Surviving ranks are re-derived after every release. The sweep reports four counters, each counting exactly this: `expired_challenge_count`, the lapsed challenges found in the first pass; `released_hold_count`, the holds the two passes moved to `RELEASED` themselves, which does not include the sibling holds a cascading booking release swept up along the way; `promoted_hold_count`, those lapsed challenges whose challenger was still `ACTIVE`, `CHALLENGED` or `CONFIRMED` at the moment its target was released; and `released_booking_count`, the bookings the sweep drove to `RELEASED`. The response body is that four-key object rather than a resource.

Two more exits complete the hold lifecycle, whose four states are `ACTIVE`, `CHALLENGED`, `CONFIRMED` and the terminal `RELEASED`. **Confirming first option** drives `ACTIVE → CONFIRMED` and is the step that converts a queue position into a commitment; a `SalesManager` may take it only on a hold that is `ACTIVE`, whose `hold_rank` is exactly 1 — a hold at rank 2 or worse is refused with `ERR_HOLD_NOT_FIRST_OPTION` — and only while the logical clock instant is at or before `release_deadline_utc`, with a lapsed deadline refused as `ERR_RELEASE_DEADLINE_PASSED`. The release-deadline check is evaluated before the first-option check, so a rank-3 hold past its deadline reports the deadline. A `CONFIRMED` hold no longer participates in challenge evaluation at all: it can neither be challenged nor challenge. **Releasing a hold** voluntarily drives `ACTIVE → RELEASED`; a `SalesManager` or a `VenueAdmin` may do it on any `ACTIVE` hold — a `CHALLENGED` or `CONFIRMED` hold is refused with `ERR_HOLD_STATE_INVALID` — decrementing the booking's `hold_count` by 1 and re-ranking the survivors. Unlike the sweep and the blackout approval, this voluntary release never cascades to the booking: driving `hold_count` to 0 this way leaves the booking exactly where it stood.

### 3.4. The License Agreement and the Road to DEFINITE

The **LicenseAgreement** is the contract instrument, and it exists in exactly one live copy per booking. A `SalesManager` drafts it against a booking, and at that moment three values are pinned so that later edits to the space's published rates cannot silently reprice a signed deal: `rental_rate_cents` copies the space's current `daily_base_rate_cents`, `contracted_rental_cents` copies the booking's `contracted_rental_cents`, and `deposit_required_cents` copies the booking's `deposit_required_cents`. The license opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `deposit_held_cents` 0, `amendment_round` 0, `pending_amendment_note` null, and both `issued_at_utc` and `executed_at_utc` null. If the booking already carries a license whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `ISSUED`, `EXECUTED` or `AMENDMENT_PENDING`, the request is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_ALREADY_EXISTS`; a booking whose only prior license was `VOIDED` or `TERMINATED` may be drafted afresh. Drafting carries no request body and no guard on the booking's own `status` or `order_posture`, so a license may be drafted against a booking that has already been released, cancelled or settled; it is the *issue* step that insists the booking still be `TENTATIVE`.

The license's six states are `DRAFT`, `ISSUED`, `EXECUTED`, `AMENDMENT_PENDING`, and the terminal `VOIDED` and `TERMINATED`. **Issuing** the license drives `DRAFT → ISSUED` and is where deposit money first moves. A `SalesManager` may issue only while the associated booking is still `TENTATIVE` — a booking that has moved on is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID` — and only when the client's `credit_balance_cents` is at least this license's `deposit_required_cents`. A short balance is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_DEPOSIT_CREDIT` and HTTP 402, which is why §3.1's deposit-recording operation exists. On success `deposit_held_cents` is set to `deposit_required_cents`, the client's `credit_balance_cents` is decreased by exactly that amount, and `issued_at_utc` is stamped with the logical clock instant. **Countersigning** drives `ISSUED → EXECUTED` and is reserved to the `Licensee` itself: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `client_id` of the license's booking, or the request is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Countersigning stamps `executed_at_utc` with the logical clock instant, and that stamp is written only on the *first* arrival at `EXECUTED` — later returns from an amendment round leave it untouched.

**Amendment** is a bounded negotiation cycle between the house and the licensee. A `SalesManager` opens a round on an `EXECUTED` license, driving `EXECUTED → AMENDMENT_PENDING`, and the request carries two fields, both required: the stale-write guard `expected_license_version`, which must be an integer, and `amendment_note`, which must be a non-empty string and whose text is stored on the license as `pending_amendment_note`. A body failing either shape is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before any of the guards below is reached. Three guards apply. The license must be `EXECUTED`, else `ERR_LICENSE_STATE_INVALID`. `expected_license_version` must match the license's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_LICENSE_VERSION` with HTTP 412. And `amendment_round` must be strictly less than three, so a license that has already absorbed three accepted amendments refuses the fourth with `ERR_AMENDMENT_LIMIT_REACHED`. The booking behind the license must also still be live, with a `status` of `TENTATIVE`, `DEFINITE` or `IN_HOUSE`; a `CLOSED`, `SETTLED`, `RELEASED` or `CANCELLED` booking cannot be renegotiated and the attempt is `ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID`, a check that runs with the license's own state check and therefore ahead of the version and round guards. Opening a round increments the license's `version` by 1, which is exactly what makes a second writer's cached expectation stale. The `Licensee` closes the round by accepting it, driving `AMENDMENT_PENDING → EXECUTED`, again subject to the ownership check on `X-User-ID`; acceptance takes no request body, increments `amendment_round` by 1, clears `pending_amendment_note` back to null and leaves `executed_at_utc` at its original stamp. If the accepted amendment changed the booking's dates, the rental pricing calculation of §3.2 re-runs over the booking — but no endpoint in this API revises the four dates of an existing booking, so in practice acceptance never reprices anything and the booking's `gross_rental_cents`, `contracted_rental_cents`, `deposit_required_cents` and `space_days_committed` all stand as created.

A license may also die. It reaches `VOIDED` only as a cascade, never by direct request: when its booking reaches `RELEASED` the license moves `DRAFT → VOIDED` or `ISSUED → VOIDED` according to where it stood, `deposit_held_cents` is set to 0, and the prior escrowed amount returns to the client's `credit_balance_cents`. The service orders on that booking are cancelled too, each moving to `CANCELLED` with `billing_status` `VOID` — but that is the booking release doing it, not the voiding, so it happens identically whether the license stood `DRAFT` or `ISSUED` and even when the booking carried no license at all; voiding itself touches only the license's own status and the deposit. **Termination** is the deliberate counterpart, reserved to a `VenueAdmin`, driving `EXECUTED → TERMINATED`. It requires that no settlement exists for the booking, else `ERR_SETTLEMENT_ALREADY_EXISTS`. Terminating sets `deposit_held_cents` to 0 and returns the prior amount to the client's `credit_balance_cents`, restoring the monetary invariant, and then drives the booking to `RELEASED` — running the whole release cascade of §3.2, which decrements the space's `committed_space_days` by the booking's `space_days_committed`, releases every hold on the booking whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `CHALLENGED` or `CONFIRMED`, restoring the capacity invariant, cancels and voids every live service order, and locks the order posture. Because that booking move is only declared out of `TENTATIVE` and `DEFINITE`, terminating a license whose booking has already reached `IN_HOUSE` or beyond is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID`.

The whole license track exists to serve one booking transition. **Confirming a booking as definite** drives `TENTATIVE → DEFINITE` and belongs to the `SalesManager`. It carries three cross-entity guards. At least one hold on the booking must have `status` `CONFIRMED`, else `ERR_HOLD_STATE_INVALID`. The license whose booking is this booking must have `status` `EXECUTED`, else `ERR_LICENSE_STATE_INVALID`. And the owning client's `booking_standing` must be `GOOD`, else `ERR_CLIENT_HOLD_RESTRICTED`. On success the booking's `order_posture` is recomputed — which, for a `TENTATIVE`-to-`DEFINITE` move by a client in good standing, yields `OPEN` — and its `updated_at` is refreshed with its `version` incremented by 1.

### 3.5. Ordering Event Services Across Utilities, Rigging, Cleaning and Security

An `EventServicesManager` orders event services against the booking, naming the `vendor_id`, the `service_category` being ordered — `UTILITIES`, `RIGGING`, `CLEANING` or `SECURITY`, with catering handled separately in §3.7 — and the billable `ordered_quantity`, which must be a number strictly greater than zero and may be fractional. The unit of that quantity depends on the category: amps for utilities, rigging points for rigging, square feet for cleaning, and guard hours for security. The order records its `booking_id` and opens with two independent status dimensions on the same row: `approval_status` `PENDING_APPROVAL` and `billing_status` `UNBILLED`, with `estimated_charge_cents` 0, `actual_quantity` null and `actual_charge_cents` null.

Creation also freezes `order_timing`, a derived field with the two values `ADVANCE` and `LATE`. It is `ADVANCE` when the logical clock date falls more than 30 days before the booking's `show_start_date`, and `LATE` otherwise — so an order placed exactly 30 days out is `LATE`, and one placed 31 days out is `ADVANCE`. The distinction costs money.

Five eligibility gates guard creation, and they are evaluated in this order. The booking's `order_posture` must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_ORDER_POSTURE_NOT_OPEN`. The vendor's `coi_expiry_date` must be strictly after the logical clock date, else `ERR_VENDOR_COI_EXPIRED`. A `RIGGING` order requires the booking's space to have `rigging_capable` true, else `ERR_SPACE_NOT_RIGGING_CAPABLE`. The vendor's own `service_category` must equal the submitted `service_category`, else `ERR_VENDOR_CATEGORY_MISMATCH`. And the exclusive-vendor rule: if any vendor registered for the submitted `service_category` carries `vendor_tier` `EXCLUSIVE`, then the named vendor must be that exclusive vendor — or, should more than one exclusive vendor have been registered for the category, any one of them — and naming anyone else is refused with `ERR_EXCLUSIVE_VENDOR_REQUIRED`. Where no exclusive vendor exists for a category, any correctly categorized vendor may be named. Exclusivity is judged against the vendor register as it stands at the moment of the order, and it is not re-judged at approval, where only the insurance date is re-read.

**Service order pricing** is a tiered calculation applied twice over the life of an order — once to `ordered_quantity` when the order is approved, and once to `actual_quantity` when it is reconciled — with the same formula and the same constants both times. Start with the base units charge, which depends on the category. Utilities alone are marginally tiered: the first 100 amps bill at 1,200 cents each, amps from the 101st through the 400th bill at 900 cents each, and every amp beyond the 400th bills at 700 cents each; an order for 250 amps therefore bills 100 amps at the first rate plus 150 amps at the second, and an order for 80 amps bills all 80 at the first rate with the higher tiers contributing nothing. The other three categories are flat: rigging bills 25,000 cents per rigging point, cleaning bills 6 cents per square foot, and security bills 4,200 cents per guard hour. Next compute two factors. The timing factor is 1.35 when `order_timing` is `LATE` and 1.00 when it is `ADVANCE`. The vendor multiplier is 1.00 for an `EXCLUSIVE` vendor, 1.10 for a `PREFERRED` vendor, and 1.25 for an `OPEN` vendor. The combined factor is the product of those two, capped at 1.40 — so a late order against an open vendor, whose raw product is 1.6875, is charged at 1.40. Multiply the base units charge by the combined factor and round half up to whole cents, then take the greater of that figure and a floor of 15,000 cents. The floor and the rounding are applied in that order: round first, then floor. A cleaning order of 400 square feet at 6 cents, advance-timed against an exclusive vendor, prices at 2,400 cents before the floor and is therefore charged 15,000 cents.

The two status dimensions advance independently, which is the point of having two. On the approval side, `approval_status` runs from `PENDING_APPROVAL` to `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` or `CANCELLED`, the latter two being terminal. **Approving** an order drives `PENDING_APPROVAL → APPROVED` and requires that the booking's `order_posture` be `OPEN` and that the vendor's `coi_expiry_date` still be strictly after the logical clock date — insurance is re-checked at approval, because time may have moved since the order was placed, and a lapse is again `ERR_VENDOR_COI_EXPIRED`. Approval simultaneously drives the billing dimension `UNBILLED → ESTIMATED` on the same row and writes `estimated_charge_cents` from the pricing calculation over `ordered_quantity`. **Rejecting** an order drives `PENDING_APPROVAL → REJECTED`, is permitted only while `approval_status` is `PENDING_APPROVAL`, and drives the billing dimension `UNBILLED → VOID`, leaving `estimated_charge_cents` at 0 and `actual_charge_cents` at null. **Cancelling** drives `PENDING_APPROVAL → CANCELLED`, where it likewise drives billing `UNBILLED → VOID`, or `APPROVED → CANCELLED`, where it drives billing `ESTIMATED → VOID` and resets `estimated_charge_cents` to 0 — but a cancel from `APPROVED` is refused when the row's `billing_status` has already reached `RECONCILED`, since money has by then been metered and the two dimensions must not contradict each other. That refusal is `ERR_SERVICE_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`.

On the billing side, `billing_status` runs `UNBILLED` to `ESTIMATED` to `RECONCILED`, with `VOID` reachable from either `UNBILLED` or `ESTIMATED`; `RECONCILED` and `VOID` are terminal. Only one billing move is client-driven. **Reconciling** an order drives `ESTIMATED → RECONCILED`: the `EventServicesManager` submits the metered `actual_quantity` — a required number strictly greater than zero, possibly fractional, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — which is stored on the row, and `actual_charge_cents` is written by re-running the pricing calculation over that actual quantity with the same tiers, timing factor (the `order_timing` frozen at creation, never re-derived), vendor multiplier, cap and floor. Reconciliation requires `approval_status` to be `APPROVED` and `billing_status` to still be `ESTIMATED`, both refused with `ERR_SERVICE_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`, requires the booking to be `IN_HOUSE`, else `ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID`, and is the single operation that a `RESTRICTED` `order_posture` still permits alongside `OPEN` — a `LOCKED` posture refuses it with `ERR_ORDER_POSTURE_NOT_OPEN`. Those two requirements interact: the booking-state guard is evaluated first and admits only `IN_HOUSE`, so although the posture gate would accept `RESTRICTED`, a booking that has actually reached `CLOSED` — the only way to hold a `RESTRICTED` posture — is turned away with `ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID` before the posture is consulted. Reconciliation must therefore be finished while the show is still in house. Every other billing move is a cascade of an approval-side move, as described above.

### 3.6. Show-Floor Labor Under Union Jurisdiction

Show-floor work is called crew by crew. An `EventServicesManager` creates a **LaborCall** against the booking, naming the union local through `jurisdiction_id`, the `work_category` being called, the `work_date`, the `scheduled_start_hour` and `scheduled_end_hour` bracketing the call as hours of the day, and the `crew_size`. Hours of the day are fractional numbers: `scheduled_start_hour` is at least 0 and strictly less than 24, `scheduled_end_hour` must be strictly greater than `scheduled_start_hour` and no greater than 24, and `crew_size` must be an integer of at least 1, a fractional crew being rejected outright. Violations are `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The call records its `booking_id`, opens in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `actual_start_hour` and `actual_end_hour` null and `actual_charge_cents` null, and immediately receives both a crew-hour commitment and a price.

Four gates guard the call, evaluated in this order after the booking's `order_posture` has been confirmed `OPEN`. The named local's `lockout_active` must be false, else `ERR_UNION_LOCKOUT`. The local's `covered_category` must equal the submitted `work_category`, since each local claims exactly one category of show-floor work, else `ERR_UNION_JURISDICTION_MISMATCH`. The `work_date` must fall inside the booking's closed interval from `move_in_start_date` through `move_out_end_date` — the first move-in day and the last move-out day both qualify — else `ERR_WORK_DATE_OUTSIDE_EVENT`. And the local's daily capacity must hold.

**Crew-hour commitment** is what the capacity gate measures. A call's `billed_hours` is its `crew_size` multiplied by the greater of the local's `min_call_hours` and the call's own span — the scheduled span while the call is `SCHEDULED` or `DISPATCHED`, and the actual span once it is `ACTUALIZED`. A crew of six called for two hours against a local with a four-hour minimum therefore commits 24 crew-hours, not 12. The local's `committed_crew_hours` is the sum of `billed_hours` over its calls whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `DISPATCHED` or `ACTUALIZED`, and cancelled calls contribute nothing. The capacity gate is evaluated per calendar day, not against the running total: for the submitted `work_date`, the sum of `billed_hours` over that local's calls on that date whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `DISPATCHED` or `ACTUALIZED`, including the call now being written, must be no greater than the local's `max_crew_hours_per_day`. Each call's `billed_hours` is quantized to one decimal place, rounding half up, before it is stored, and the day's running total is likewise quantized to one decimal before the comparison. The comparison is inclusive, so filling a day exactly to capacity is accepted and only exceeding it is refused, with `ERR_UNION_CREW_OVERCOMMITTED`. The same gate is re-evaluated on actualization, because actual hours can exceed scheduled ones.

**Labor pricing** has two variants that deliberately disagree, and the difference between them is the house's exposure. The estimate, written at creation into `estimated_charge_cents`, is generous to the licensee: take the hours per crew member as the greater of the local's `min_call_hours` and the scheduled span, multiply by `crew_size` and by the local's `straight_time_rate_cents`, and round half up to whole cents. The estimate prices every hour at straight time and never applies the overtime multiplier, no matter where the scheduled hours fall relative to the straight-time window.

The actualized charge, written when the call is actualized into `actual_charge_cents`, splits the worked span at the window edges. Let the span be the actual end hour less the actual start hour. The straight-time hours are the overlap between the worked span and the local's straight-time window: take the lesser of `actual_end_hour` and `st_window_end_hour`, subtract the greater of `actual_start_hour` and `st_window_start_hour`, and treat any negative result as zero. The overtime hours are the span less the straight-time hours. The uplift hours are the shortfall against the minimum call — the local's `min_call_hours` less the span, or zero when the span already meets or exceeds the minimum. Then price three bands separately and round each to whole cents on its own: the straight-time band is `crew_size` times the straight-time hours times `straight_time_rate_cents`; the overtime band is `crew_size` times the overtime hours times `straight_time_rate_cents` times the local's `overtime_multiplier`; the uplift band is `crew_size` times the uplift hours times `straight_time_rate_cents`, at straight time. `actual_charge_cents` is the sum of those three rounded integers. Rounding each band before adding is binding; a single rounding of the summed real number is wrong. The consequence is that two calls of identical total length cost different amounts: a crew starting at hour 6 spends two hours outside the window at the overtime multiplier, while the same crew starting at hour 9 spends none.

The call's four states are `SCHEDULED`, `DISPATCHED`, and the terminal `ACTUALIZED` and `CANCELLED`. **Dispatching** drives `SCHEDULED → DISPATCHED` and requires the booking to stand exactly at `IN_HOUSE` — not merely to have passed through it, so a `CLOSED` booking refuses the dispatch — else `ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID`, and the local's `lockout_active` to still be false, else `ERR_UNION_LOCKOUT`. Dispatch moves no money at all: `estimated_charge_cents` was already written at creation. **Actualizing** drives `DISPATCHED → ACTUALIZED`; the `EventServicesManager` submits `actual_start_hour` and `actual_end_hour`, both required numbers, which must satisfy the actual start being strictly less than the actual end, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. That ordering is the only constraint on them: unlike the scheduled pair they are not confined to the 0-through-24 range, so a crew recorded as working from hour 22 to hour 27 is accepted and priced as a five-hour span. Actualization writes `actual_charge_cents` from the actualized variant, recomputes `billed_hours` from the actual span, and adjusts the local's `committed_crew_hours` by the difference between the new and the old `billed_hours` — up when the crew ran long, down when it finished early. Its capacity re-check compares the new `billed_hours` plus the hours of that local's *other* live calls on the same `work_date` against `max_crew_hours_per_day`, excluding the call being actualized from the running total so its own scheduled hours are not double-counted; a work date outside the event span is not re-checked here. **Cancelling** drives `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` or `DISPATCHED → CANCELLED`, and in both cases it decreases the local's `committed_crew_hours` by the call's current `billed_hours` and then sets `billed_hours` to 0, restoring the capacity invariant exactly.

### 3.7. Food and Beverage Exclusivity and Outside Catering Exceptions

The house caterer holds food and beverage exclusivity, and the whole point of the **CateringArrangement** record is to make each departure from that exclusivity a named, priced exception. An `EventServicesManager` proposes an arrangement against a booking, naming the `vendor_id`, the `arrangement_type` — `IN_HOUSE` or `OUTSIDE_EXCEPTION` — the forecast `estimated_fnb_cents`, which must be an integer of at least zero, and, when the type is an exception, the `exception_reason`, one of `ETHNIC_CUISINE`, `SPONSOR_PRODUCT_SAMPLING` or `RELIGIOUS_DIETARY`. The arrangement records its `booking_id`, opens in `status` `PROPOSED` with `buyout_fee_cents` 0 and `actual_fnb_cents` null.

A booking may carry only one live arrangement: if one already exists whose `status` is `PROPOSED`, `APPROVED` or `CLOSED`, the proposal is refused with `ERR_CATERING_ALREADY_EXISTS`, while a prior `DENIED` arrangement leaves the way clear. Beyond the `OPEN` `order_posture` requirement and the uniqueness check, four gates apply in this order. The named vendor's `coi_expiry_date` must be strictly after the logical clock date, else `ERR_VENDOR_COI_EXPIRED`. The vendor's `service_category` must be `CATERING`, else `ERR_VENDOR_CATEGORY_MISMATCH`. The exclusivity pairing must hold: an `IN_HOUSE` arrangement requires the named vendor's `is_in_house_caterer` to be true, and an `OUTSIDE_EXCEPTION` arrangement requires it to be false — naming the house caterer as an outside exception is as much a violation as naming an outside firm as the house caterer, and both are `ERR_CATERING_EXCLUSIVITY_VIOLATION`. And the reason pairing must hold in both directions: `exception_reason` must be non-null when `arrangement_type` is `OUTSIDE_EXCEPTION` and must be null when it is `IN_HOUSE`, else `ERR_OUTSIDE_CATERING_REASON_REQUIRED`.

The arrangement's four states are `PROPOSED`, `APPROVED`, and the terminal `DENIED` and `CLOSED`. **Approving** drives `PROPOSED → APPROVED` and requires the booking's `order_posture` to be `OPEN`. It also re-checks the exception pairing on the way through: for an `OUTSIDE_EXCEPTION` arrangement, `exception_reason` must still be non-null — otherwise `ERR_OUTSIDE_CATERING_REASON_REQUIRED` — and the named vendor's `is_in_house_caterer` must still be false, otherwise `ERR_CATERING_EXCLUSIVITY_VIOLATION`. Approval prices the buyout fee. **Denying** drives `PROPOSED → DENIED` from a `PROPOSED` arrangement and sets `buyout_fee_cents` to 0.

**The buyout fee** is what the house charges for surrendering exclusivity, and it is 0 for as long as `arrangement_type` is `IN_HOUSE` — the in-house caterer buys nothing out. For an `OUTSIDE_EXCEPTION`, take the basis food and beverage spend, multiply it by 22 hundredths, round half up to whole cents, and then take the greater of that figure and a floor of 250,000 cents. The rounding happens before the floor comparison. Which basis is used is the only difference between the fee's two variants: the estimated variant, computed at approval, keys on `estimated_fnb_cents` as forecast by the client at proposal time, while the actual variant, computed when the booking closes, keys on `actual_fnb_cents` as recorded after the event. The floor can therefore bind for one variant and not the other — a forecast of 800,000 cents yields a fee of 250,000 cents because 22 percent of it is only 176,000, while a settled spend of 2,000,000 cents yields 440,000 cents outright.

`actual_fnb_cents` is supplied separately, before the booking closes, by an `EventServicesManager` recording the settled food and beverage spend on the arrangement; the field is a required integer of at least zero on that request, and the revision increments the arrangement's `version` by 1. The revision carries no state guard of its own — it is accepted whatever the arrangement's `status` and whatever the booking's `status` or `order_posture`, and it may be repeated, each write overwriting the last — but it does not itself reprice anything. It should be recorded, because the final transition is a cascade with a precondition: when the booking moves from `IN_HOUSE` to `CLOSED`, an arrangement in `APPROVED` moves `APPROVED → CLOSED` and its `buyout_fee_cents` is recomputed under the actual variant, and that recomputation requires `actual_fnb_cents` to be non-null. Failing that precondition does not fail the close: an `APPROVED` arrangement whose `actual_fnb_cents` is still null is simply skipped, so the booking closes normally while that arrangement stays `APPROVED` with the estimated fee it was approved at. Arrangements in `PROPOSED`, `DENIED` or `CLOSED` are likewise untouched by the close.

### 3.8. In-House Operations: Opening the Show, Attesting Usage and Closing the Event

**Opening the show floor** drives the booking `DEFINITE → IN_HOUSE` and belongs to the `EventServicesManager`. Three guards apply, in this order. The booking must be `DEFINITE`, else `ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID`. The booking's live license must be `EXECUTED` — a booking carrying no live license at all fails here too — else `ERR_LICENSE_STATE_INVALID`. And the logical clock date must have reached `move_in_start_date`, meaning it is that date or later, an earlier attempt being refused with `ERR_MOVE_IN_NOT_REACHED`; per §2.11 the license check therefore wins over the date check when both fail. Opening recomputes `order_posture` to `OPEN`.

While the show is in house, the `EventServicesManager` attests how the space was actually used, one calendar day at a time, by writing a **SpaceUsageRecord**. Each record names its `booking_id`, the `usage_date` being attested, and the `usage_kind` — `MOVE_IN`, `SHOW`, `DARK`, `MOVE_OUT` or `HOLDOVER` — and the server stamps `recorded_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`. A booking may carry at most one attestation per calendar day, and a second attempt on the same `usage_date` is refused with `ERR_USAGE_RECORD_DUPLICATE`. Attestation is deliberately ungated beyond that uniqueness rule: it checks neither the booking's `status` nor its `order_posture`, so days may be attested before the show opens or after it closes, and the attested `usage_date` need not fall inside the booking's licensed interval. These records are append-only and immutable: they carry only `id` and `created_at`, cannot be edited, and cannot be withdrawn. They exist because a settlement may be priced on what actually happened rather than on what was contracted, and each `usage_kind` carries its own per-day factor (see §3.9).

**Closing the event** drives `IN_HOUSE → CLOSED`, again by the `EventServicesManager`, and it is the checkpoint where every open child obligation must be discharged. Three guards apply in this order. The booking must be `IN_HOUSE`, else `ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID`. The logical clock date must be strictly past `move_out_end_date` — closing on the last move-out day itself is refused with `ERR_EVENT_NOT_ENDED`. And every service order on the booking must have `billing_status` `RECONCILED` or `VOID`, and every labor call must have `status` `ACTUALIZED` or `CANCELLED`; a service order still `UNBILLED` or `ESTIMATED`, or a labor call still `SCHEDULED` or `DISPATCHED`, refuses the close with `ERR_SERVICE_ORDERS_NOT_RECONCILED`. Closing recomputes `order_posture` to `RESTRICTED`, which shuts down new ordering while leaving reconciliation open, and cascades to the catering arrangement as described at the end of §3.7.

### 3.9. Event Settlement, Dispute Resolution and Arbitration

A `SettlementAnalyst` opens the **EventSettlement** for a booking, choosing the rental `basis` — `CONTRACTED` or `ACTUAL_USAGE`, a required enum whose absence or misspelling is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — which fixes how the rental charge will be computed and cannot be changed thereafter. Exactly one settlement may exist per booking whatever that settlement's own status, so even a `FINAL` one blocks a successor, and a second attempt is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_ALREADY_EXISTS`. The settlement opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `revision_round` 0 and every money field at 0: `line_subtotal_cents`, `facility_service_fee_cents`, `deposit_applied_cents` and `grand_total_cents`. `dispute_reason`, `issued_by_user_id`, `issued_at_utc`, `dispute_deadline_utc` and `finalized_by_user_id` are all null. The analyst may open the record as soon as the booking exists and carries no settlement; the requirement that the booking has actually reached `CLOSED` is enforced when the settlement is first issued, not when it is opened.

The analyst then appends **SettlementLineItem** rows, each naming its `settlement_id`, its `line_kind` — `RENTAL`, `SERVICE`, `LABOR`, `CATERING` or `ADJUSTMENT` — and its signed `amount_cents`. `settlement_id` is a body reference resolved as `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `line_kind` is a required enum and `amount_cents` a required integer of either sign — a fractional amount is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, not a sign violation. Two optional fields travel with each line: `source_ref_id`, an opaque identifier of the originating service order, labor call or catering arrangement, copied for audit only, never dereferenced by any rule and not required to resolve to anything, and `memo`, a human-readable description; both must be strings when supplied and both default to null. Lines are append-only and immutable, carrying only `id` and `created_at`, and appending one does not recompute any settlement total — totals move only when the settlement is issued. Appending carries no state guard of its own: a line may be appended whatever the settlement's `status`, so an append against a settlement already `ISSUED`, `DISPUTED`, in `ARBITRATION` or `FINAL` is accepted and simply leaves that settlement's stored totals as they stand. The sign rule is enforced at append time: every line whose `line_kind` is not `ADJUSTMENT` must carry a strictly positive `amount_cents`, an `ADJUSTMENT` line may be negative, but no append may drive the settlement's subtotal to zero or below — and the subtotal that rule reads is the running sum of `amount_cents` over the lines already appended to this settlement plus the line now being appended, not the stored `line_subtotal_cents`, which moves only at issue. Violations are `ERR_LINE_AMOUNT_INVALID`.

**The rental basis** is the one line whose amount the house computes rather than accepts. Under the `CONTRACTED` variant the rental basis is simply the booking's `contracted_rental_cents`, and every attested usage record is ignored: the licensee is billed the rental exactly as contracted. Under the `ACTUAL_USAGE` variant the settlement re-prices each attested day at its own factor. Walk the booking's usage records in `usage_date` ascending order; for each one, multiply the space's `daily_base_rate_cents` by the factor for that record's `usage_kind` and round half up to whole cents — a move-in day and a move-out day each bill at one half of the daily rate, a show day bills at the full rate, a dark day bills at one eighth of the rate, and a holdover day bills at one and one half times the rate. Sum those rounded per-day integers, then multiply the sum by one minus the class's `rental_discount_pct` and round half up once more. The order is pinned: round each usage day first, then sum, then apply the class discount and round again. Summing the unrounded day products and rounding once at the end yields a different total and is wrong. Because usage records are immutable and append-only, every record for the booking participates with no status filter.

The settlement's five states are `DRAFT`, `ISSUED`, `DISPUTED`, `ARBITRATION` and the terminal `FINAL`. **Issuing** drives `DRAFT → ISSUED` on a first issue and `DISPUTED → ISSUED` on a re-issue, and both are the `SettlementAnalyst`'s work. On a first issue the booking must have reached `CLOSED`, else `ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID`. When the `basis` is `ACTUAL_USAGE`, at least one usage record must exist for the booking, else `ERR_NO_USAGE_RECORDS`. Exactly one line whose `line_kind` is `RENTAL` must exist, and its `amount_cents` must equal the rental basis computed for the settlement's own `basis`; no rental line, more than one, or a mismatched amount is refused with `ERR_RENTAL_LINE_MISMATCH` — which is also what a settlement carrying no lines at all reports, since it necessarily has no rental line. A re-issue out of `DISPUTED` additionally requires `revision_round` to be strictly less than two, else `ERR_SETTLEMENT_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`, and it increments `revision_round` by 1 and clears `dispute_reason` back to null. On either path the totals are recomputed, `issued_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`, `issued_at_utc` is stamped with the logical clock instant, and `dispute_deadline_utc` is set to that instant plus 240 hours — ten days. A re-issue restamps both timestamps, so each round of dispute resolution grants a fresh ten-day window over the recomputed figures, which may reflect lines appended while the settlement sat `DISPUTED`.

**Totalling** a settlement is a five-step calculation whose order is pinned, because the facility service fee is keyed on the subtotal while the grand total includes the fee — a dependency that only looks circular. Step one: `line_subtotal_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over every line whose `settlement_id` is this settlement, processed in `created_at` ascending order, with `ADJUSTMENT` lines added according to their sign. Step two: select the fee percentage from the subtotal. A subtotal strictly below 50,000 dollars — 5,000,000 cents — takes 9 percent; a subtotal strictly below 200,000 dollars — 20,000,000 cents — takes 7 percent; anything else takes 5.5 percent. Both breakpoints are strict less-than comparisons, so a subtotal of exactly 5,000,000 cents falls into the middle band at 7 percent, and a subtotal of exactly 20,000,000 cents falls into the top band at 5.5 percent. Step three: `facility_service_fee_cents` is the subtotal multiplied by that percentage, rounded half up to whole cents, once. Step four: `deposit_applied_cents` is the license's `deposit_held_cents` at finalization, and zero at every earlier point. Step five: `grand_total_cents` is the subtotal plus the facility service fee less the deposit applied. The fee percentage is never keyed on `grand_total_cents`; recomputing the band from the total is wrong.

**Disputing** drives `ISSUED → DISPUTED` and belongs to the `Licensee` alone, subject to the ownership check comparing `X-User-ID` to the booking's `client_id`. The logical clock instant must be at or before `dispute_deadline_utc`; disputing exactly at the deadline instant still counts, while disputing after it is `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The submitted `dispute_reason` is a required non-empty string, validated ahead of the status and deadline checks, and is stored on the settlement. From `DISPUTED` the analyst has two exits, and which one is available depends on how many rounds have already run. Re-issuing is the exit while `revision_round` is below two, as described above. **Escalating** drives `DISPUTED → ARBITRATION` and is available only once `revision_round` has reached two or more; escalating too early is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. Escalation retains `dispute_reason` as the arbitration statement rather than clearing it. The cycle from `ISSUED` to `DISPUTED` and back to `ISSUED` is therefore bounded at two revisions, after which the only way forward is arbitration.

**Finalizing** ends the matter, and there are two doors. The ordinary door drives `ISSUED → FINAL` and belongs to the `SettlementAnalyst`: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `issued_by_user_id` per §2.12, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and the logical clock instant must be strictly past `dispute_deadline_utc`, else `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_OPEN` — the licensee's window must have run out before the house may close the book. The arbitration door drives `ARBITRATION → FINAL` and belongs to the `VenueAdmin`; it requires only that the settlement be in `ARBITRATION`, and it applies neither the dispute-window check nor the segregation check.

Both doors perform identical effects, and the cascade runs four levels deep. `deposit_applied_cents` is set to the license's `deposit_held_cents` for this booking, and that field is then set to 0, moving the escrowed money onto the settlement and preserving the monetary invariant of §2.9. The license consulted is the booking's live one — the single license standing in `DRAFT`, `ISSUED`, `EXECUTED` or `AMENDMENT_PENDING` — falling back, when the booking has no live license left, to the most recently created license that still holds a deposit and then to the most recently created license of any status; a booking that never carried a license applies a deposit of 0. `line_subtotal_cents` and `facility_service_fee_cents` are *not* recomputed here — they stand as the last issue left them, so a line appended after the final issue does not reach the total — while `grand_total_cents` is recomputed as `line_subtotal_cents` plus `facility_service_fee_cents` less `deposit_applied_cents`. `finalized_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`. Then the booking moves `CLOSED → SETTLED`, which increases the client's `outstanding_balance_cents` by this settlement's `grand_total_cents`; that booking move and everything downstream of it fire only when the booking actually stands `CLOSED`, so finalizing a settlement whose booking has moved elsewhere still settles the settlement and still draws down the escrow but leaves the booking, the client's balance and every posture alone. That increase re-runs the standing calculation of §3.1, which may move the client's `booking_standing` from `GOOD` to `HOLD_RESTRICTED`. And a `HOLD_RESTRICTED` standing in turn recomputes `order_posture` to `LOCKED` on every *other* booking of the same client whose `status` is `TENTATIVE`, `DEFINITE`, `IN_HOUSE` or `CLOSED` — so one unpaid settlement freezes event services across that licensee's entire forward calendar. The settled booking's own `order_posture` also becomes `LOCKED`. Every entity touched along that chain has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented.

### 3.10. Maintenance Windows and Capital Project Scheduling

A `FacilitiesPlanner` proposes a **MaintenanceBlackout** against a space, supplying `space_id`, the `window_start_date` and `window_end_date` bracketing the unavailability, and the `project_kind` — `ROUTINE_MAINTENANCE` for ordinary facility work or `CAPITAL_PROJECT` for scheduled capital work. The window end must be on or after the window start, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The optional `buffer_days` declares the separation the window demands on **both** sides, must be a non-negative integer when supplied — a `buffer_days` of 0 is legal and reduces clearance to plain non-intersection — and defaults to 2. The blackout opens in `status` `PROPOSED`, and its three states are `PROPOSED` and the terminal `APPROVED` and `DEFERRED`.

Proposing costs nothing and blocks nothing: a `PROPOSED` window is invisible to the booking and hold clearance checks, which consult only `APPROVED` windows. The consequential step is **approving** it, which drives `PROPOSED → APPROVED` and is also the planner's. Approval is guarded by the booking calendar it would disturb: no booking on the blackout's space or on any related space whose `status` is `DEFINITE` or `IN_HOUSE` may have an interval that fails to clear the buffered window. A committed event standing in the way refuses the approval with `ERR_MAINTENANCE_BLACKOUT_CONFLICT` — the planner must defer the window or wait. Bookings still merely `TENTATIVE` are not protected by this guard, which is precisely the asymmetry that makes a tentative hold tentative.

Approval then takes the space. The space's `active_blackout_count` increments by 1 — only on the space the window names, never on its ancestors or descendants, since this counter is a plain tally rather than an aggregate. Every hold whose interval fails to clear the buffered window on that space or on any descendant space moves to `RELEASED`, whatever its prior `status` — `ACTIVE → RELEASED`, `CHALLENGED → RELEASED` and even `CONFIRMED → RELEASED` all fire here, and this approval is the only trigger in the system that can take a confirmed hold away. Each such release decrements the owning booking's `hold_count` by 1, and a booking whose `hold_count` reaches 0 while its `status` is `TENTATIVE` **or** `DEFINITE` moves to `RELEASED` and runs the release cascade of §3.2 — note that this cascade reaches one state further than the hold sweep's, which releases only `TENTATIVE` bookings. That booking release moves its license, if `DRAFT` or `ISSUED`, to `VOIDED`, returning `deposit_held_cents` to the client's `credit_balance_cents`, and reverses the space's `committed_space_days`.

**Deferring** a proposed window drives `PROPOSED → DEFERRED` and is the planner's escape hatch. It changes no space and no booking: no counter moves, no hold is released, and the booking calendar is untouched. Approving or deferring a window that is not `PROPOSED` is refused with `ERR_BLACKOUT_STATE_INVALID`.
