# Label Distribution Recoupment API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An independent record label lives or dies by the accuracy of its recoupment arithmetic. Money arrives from a distribution partner as a quarterly digital revenue report and as wholesale proceeds on physical product; money leaves as advances, recording costs, marketing commitments and royalty payments to artists. Between the two sits a chain of contractual promises — a distribution agreement with its own fee structure and advance, a set of releases each carrying a slice of that advance, and one royalty agreement per artist per release, each with its own escalation trigger and its own set of recoupable costs. Today those five sub-ledgers (the label's advance against the distributor, the artist's advance, recoupable recording costs, recoupable marketing, and the physical returns reserve) are reconciled in spreadsheets that nobody can audit and that silently disagree with one another.

This API replaces that spreadsheet estate with a single accounting system of record. It governs the whole arc of a distribution relationship: drafting and executing an agreement with a fee structure, a term and a licensed territory list; registering releases and allocating the advance across them; contracting artists at a base royalty rate that escalates once the label's advance is recouped; approving marketing spend and classifying how much of it the artist absorbs; ingesting per-stream revenue at rates that vary by service, territory and subscription tier; manufacturing and shipping physical product against a returns reserve that grows as returns come in; and finally issuing auditable royalty statements that the artist can object to and audit. When the term ends — by natural expiry or by for-cause termination — the system runs the post-term collection window and then reverts the catalog to the artists.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. The first is **conservation**: every ledger figure this system derives has a stated inverse, so that disputing an accepted revenue report, voiding a reserve liquidation, or reopening a finalized statement after an audit restores the books to exactly the state they held before, with no drift. The second is **determinism**: every deadline, window and accounting-period boundary is measured against a server-side logical clock rather than wall-clock time, every rounding decision has a pinned order, and every rejection has one and only one machine-readable identifier. The API is mounted flat — each collection is a root-level path, and relationships between records are carried by identifier fields in request payloads rather than by nesting one collection inside another's URL.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the rules that hold across every endpoint: who may call, what a response looks like, how lists are paged, how money and percentages are rounded, how concurrent writers to a shared ledger are serialized, and in what order a request's possible rejections are evaluated.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. Both must be present and non-blank; a header supplied as an empty string, or containing nothing but whitespace, counts as absent and the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). There is no session, token or login step — the two headers are the entire authentication surface.

Five roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `LabelAdmin`, `DistributionPartner`, `RoyaltyAnalyst`, `BusinessAffairs`, and `Artist`. A value of `X-User-Role` that is not one of these five, or that is one of the five but is not among the roles authorized for the endpoint being called, is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). Section 4 lists the authorized roles for each endpoint.

Three of the roles are label-internal operators whose identity the system records but never matches against a stored record. When `X-User-Role` is `LabelAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the label-operations desk sets up counterparties, artists, deals, releases, agreements, marketing requests, rate cards and manufacturing runs, and drives the deal lifecycle. When `X-User-Role` is `RoyaltyAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier; the analyst accepts or disputes revenue reports, liquidates returns reserves, opens statements and prepares them. When `X-User-Role` is `BusinessAffairs`, `X-User-ID` is again an opaque operator identifier; business affairs decides marketing spends, resolves agreement amendments, issues and finalizes statements, resolves objections and concludes audits. For these three roles the server may record the identifier on a record — for example as `requested_by_user_id` or `decided_by_user_id` on a marketing spend, or as `prepared_by_user_id` or `issued_by_user_id` on a statement, where the corresponding transition writes it — but it never compares that identifier to any entity's `id`.

The remaining two roles are entity-backed, and for them `X-User-ID` is a resource identifier that ownership checks compare against a foreign key. When `X-User-Role` is `DistributionPartner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Distributor`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Distributor` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — a revenue report is owned by the partner whose `id` equals the `distributor_id` of the report's deal, and a manufacturing run is owned by the partner whose `id` equals the run's own `distributor_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Artist`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Artist`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Artist` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — a statement is owned by the artist whose `id` equals the `artist_id` of the statement's artist agreement. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

Ownership is enforced on exactly seven operations: appending a revenue line to a report, submitting a report, shipping a manufacturing run, recording returns against a run, filing an objection to a statement, requesting an audit of a statement, and reading a single statement. On every other endpoint the role check of the preceding paragraphs is the only access control applied — a `DistributionPartner` listing releases sees all of them, and an `Artist` listing agreements is not narrowed to its own.

Separately from role and ownership, two operations pairs enforce **segregation of duties** by comparing `X-User-ID` against a stored actor identifier: the operator who submitted a marketing spend may not decide it, and the operator who prepared a statement may not issue it, resolve an objection on it, finalize it, or conclude its audit. Because all three internal roles carry opaque identifiers, one human holding two roles is caught by this comparison. A violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection-listing endpoint is paged with two query parameters. `limit` controls the page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls how many records are skipped before the page begins: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest record first — on every list endpoint, including the two sub-resource listings (a report's revenue lines, and a release's revenue lines across all reports). That ordering is not merely cosmetic: several calculations in this specification resolve ties by earliest creation, and the list order is the reader's window onto that sequence.

A list response reports its paging state in the envelope's `meta` object through four fields: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values used for the query, `total_count` reports how many records match the filter before paging is applied, and `has_more` is true when records remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Record Versions

Every persisted record carries a server-minted `id` — a UUID, with no type prefix and no embedded meaning. Clients never supply an `id` on creation; a client-supplied value is ignored. Records also carry `created_at`, the instant the record was written, formatted as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits.

Nine of the eleven record types are mutable and therefore also carry `updated_at` and `version`. `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical-clock instant on every mutation, in the same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits. `version` is an integer that begins at 1 when the record is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record — including mutations a record suffers as a side effect of an operation aimed at some other record. A cascade that moves five releases and three agreements bumps eight `version` counters, not one. These nine types are the distributor, the artist, the distribution deal, the release, the artist agreement, the marketing spend, the DSP revenue report, the physical run and the artist statement.

Two record types are immutable once written and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at`: the stream rate card and the DSP revenue line. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from those two payloads entirely. A revenue line's computed money fields are written by the report-level operations that price and allocate it, but the line itself is never re-created, re-parented or edited by a client, and it exposes no version counter for a client to echo.

### 2.5. The Logical Accounting Clock

Nothing in this system reads the host's wall clock. Objection deadlines, audit-rights windows, term-end comparisons, post-term collection cut-offs, and the timestamps written into `executed_at`, `approved_at`, `accepted_at`, `issued_at`, `objection_filed_at` and `finalized_at` are all measured against a server-side logical clock. A `LabelAdmin` may reposition that clock to an arbitrary UTC instant by supplying `to_iso8601`; the call reports HTTP 200 with an empty data object and every subsequent request reads the new instant. Before the first such call the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` — that fixed startup default, and not real time, is what an unset clock returns.

Where a rule compares a calendar date rather than an instant, the comparison uses the logical clock's UTC calendar date. Where a rule compares an instant, the comparison is exact to the millisecond.

### 2.6. Money, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in whole cents. There are no fractional-cent balances anywhere in the system, and no field stores money as a decimal string.

All contractual percentages — distribution fees, profit shares, service fees, royalty rates, recoupable fractions, marketing shares, returns reserve percentages and the liquidation fraction — are decimal fractions between 0 and 1 carrying **exactly four decimal places**. A value of 0.15 is not an acceptable substitute for 0.1500: the request-validation rank rejects a percentage that is not written with four decimals, or that falls outside the range declared for that field, with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Whenever a monetary amount is derived by multiplying by a percentage or dividing by a scale factor, the result is rounded **half up** to whole cents — a raw value of 400000.5 becomes 400001, and a raw value of 400000.4 becomes 400000. Where a derived percentage is stored rather than a derived amount — the returns rate on a manufacturing run — it too is rounded half up, to four decimal places. The only rounding operator that is not half-up is the inventory-obligation floor described in Section 3.7, which rounds a unit count **up** to the next whole unit. Per-stream rates are quoted in micros, where 10,000 micros make one cent; that conversion is applied per revenue line, as Section 3.5 specifies in detail, and the order in which rounding and summation are applied is pinned rather than left to the implementer.

### 2.7. Version Echo on Ledger-Mutating Writes

Four operations mutate a shared ledger that other operations are simultaneously entitled to mutate, and all four therefore require the caller to echo the `version` of the parent record it last observed. The echoed value is a **required** field on those four requests, not an optional guard.

Accepting a DSP revenue report reads and writes the parent deal's `net_receipts_to_date_cents`, `advance_recouped_cents` and `unrecouped_advance_cents`. The request must carry `expected_deal_version` matching the current `version` of the report's `DistributionDeal`, as last read from that deal's single-record endpoint. If any other writer — another report's acceptance, a dispute, or a marketing settlement — has advanced the deal's `version` since the caller read it, the acceptance is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEAL_VERSION` (HTTP 412), because the waterfall the caller intended would have been computed from a superseded advance balance. Re-reading the deal and retrying with the fresh `version` succeeds; the rule rejects only a write derived from a stale read, never a legitimate second acceptance.

Disputing a DSP revenue report reverses that report's contribution to the same three deal fields and therefore carries the same guard: the request must supply `expected_deal_version` matching the deal's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEAL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Disputing report A remains legitimate whether or not report B has since been accepted, so this can never be reported as a state violation — the caller must simply refresh the deal and retry, which succeeds.

Liquidating part of a returns reserve is a read-modify-write on the run's `reserve_held_cents` and `reserve_liquidated_cents`, and successive partial liquidations are both legitimate. The request must therefore carry `expected_run_version` matching the current `version` of the `PhysicalRun`, as last read from that run's single-record endpoint; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RUN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Without this guard, a liquidation computed against a held balance that a returns recording or an earlier partial liquidation had already moved would over-release the reserve while still appearing, to its caller, to sit inside the available balance.

Voiding a liquidation moves liquidated cents back into the held balance and carries the same guard: `expected_run_version` must match the run's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RUN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Voiding is itself a repeatable, legitimate operation on a run whose reserve dimension is `PARTIALLY_LIQUIDATED` or `LIQUIDATED`, so the protection is a version echo rather than a state precondition; re-reading the run and retrying succeeds.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field. A create, a submit, a decide, a settle, an execute, a terminate, a prepare, an issue, a finalize or an audit call that supplies one is simply not reading a documented field, and callers must not expect any version guard on those operations.

### 2.8. The Order in Which a Request Can Fail

A request frequently violates more than one rule at once. The evaluation order is fixed, and the **first** violated rule decides the response: no later rule is evaluated, and no later rule's identifier can appear. Implementers must therefore treat the following ladder as normative, because a correct rejection with the wrong identifier is still a wrong answer.

1. **Authentication.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present and non-blank, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorization.** The role is one of the five named roles and appears in the called endpoint's authorized list, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
3. **Path existence.** Every identifier embedded in the URL path resolves to an existing record, validated left to right, so that an outer segment's absence is reported before an inner segment is examined at all. The identifier is the one matching the missing record type: `ERR_DEAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RELEASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MARKETING_SPEND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVENUE_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PHYSICAL_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ARTIST_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_FOUND` (all 404). This rank applies to every endpoint with a templated path segment.
4. **Ownership.** For the two entity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` equals the resource's owning foreign key, on the seven operations enumerated in Section 2.1 — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
5. **Request shape.** On every creating or acting request, the payload's own shape: required fields present, types correct, enum members recognised, percentages carrying exactly four decimals and sitting inside their declared range, money fields integral and not negative, dates written as `YYYY-MM-DD` with a period's end not before its start and a term's end strictly after its start, `upc` between 12 and 14 ASCII digits, `territory_codes` a non-empty array of distinct ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 codes, and the deal-type conditional fee fields obeying their required-or-null pairing. Any violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
6. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to an existing record — `distributor_id`, `artist_id`, `deal_id`, `primary_artist_id`, `release_id`, `artist_agreement_id` and `supersedes_agreement_id` — otherwise the matching one of `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ARTIST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RELEASE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND` (404). This rank applies to the eight creating operations that accept a foreign key: creating a deal, a release, an artist agreement, a marketing spend, a revenue report, a revenue line, a physical run, or an artist statement.
7. **Version echo.** On the four operations of Section 2.7, the supplied `expected_deal_version` or `expected_run_version` equals the parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_DEAL_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_RUN_VERSION` (412).
8. **State gates.** The target record's own status — and, for a physical run or an artist statement, the relevant one of its two independent dimensions — is a declared source state of the requested transition. The identifier names the record type: `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_RELEASE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_AGREEMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_SPEND_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_STATEMENT_STATE` (all 409). Four related gates sit at this same rank rather than after it, so any of them may be the one that fires: a revenue report may be accepted only while its deal is still collecting, otherwise `ERR_DEAL_NOT_COLLECTING` (409); a statement may be opened or prepared only against an agreement that can still be accounted, otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_STATEMENTABLE` (409); a marketing spend may be created, submitted or approved only against a promotable release, otherwise `ERR_RELEASE_NOT_PROMOTABLE` (409); and a manufacturing run may be planned only for a release that is physically distributable and not already reverting, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_RELEASE_STATE` (409). Comparisons that read the stored row to validate a supplied quantity — the shipped count against the recorded manufactured count, the settled amount against the approved amount — are reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` but belong to their transition's own precondition list, and are therefore evaluated after that transition's state gate rather than at rank 5.
9. **Segregation of duties.** The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the stored counterpart actor, on the five operations named in Section 2.1 — deciding a marketing spend, and issuing, resolving an objection on, finalizing or concluding the audit of a statement — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
10. **Temporal windows.** Deadlines and period-containment rules, all 422: `ERR_OBJECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_OBJECTION_WINDOW_OPEN`, `ERR_AUDIT_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, `ERR_TERM_NOT_ENDED`, `ERR_POST_TERM_COLLECTION_ACTIVE` and `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_TERM`. Each is described alongside the workflow it guards.
11. **Referential and uniqueness rules.** `ERR_RATE_CARD_OVERLAP` (409), `ERR_STATEMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409), `ERR_DUPLICATE_UPC` (409), `ERR_OPEN_OBJECTIONS_BLOCK_CLOSE` (409), `ERR_STATEMENT_PERIOD_NOT_CONTIGUOUS` (422), `ERR_RATE_CARD_NOT_FOUND` (422) and `ERR_PROVISIONAL_NOT_ISSUABLE` (422).
12. **Fiscal ceilings.** Last of all, the money limits: `ERR_MARKETING_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_ADVANCE_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_INVENTORY_OBLIGATION_BREACH`, `ERR_RETURNS_EXCEED_SHIPPED`, `ERR_DISPUTED_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_NET` and `ERR_LIQUIDATION_EXCEEDS_RESERVE` (all 422).

Two consequences of this ladder deserve emphasis. A caller who echoes a stale parent version *and* targets a record in the wrong state receives the 412, not the 409, because the version echo sits at rank 7 and the state gate at rank 8. Conversely, a caller who prepares a statement whose period falls outside the term *and* whose period overlaps a sibling statement receives `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_TERM`, because temporal containment at rank 10 precedes the referential overlap check at rank 11.

### 2.9. Conserved Quantities and Compensating Actions

Three arithmetic identities hold at all times and are the tightest specification of what "the books balance" means here. On a distribution deal, the advance paid by the distributor always equals the part recouped plus the part still outstanding: `advance_amount_cents` is `advance_recouped_cents` plus `unrecouped_advance_cents`, with no drift, after every acceptance and after every dispute. On a manufacturing run, the sized reserve always equals the part still withheld plus the part released: `reserve_initial_cents` is `reserve_held_cents` plus `reserve_liquidated_cents`, after every liquidation and after every void. On a release, the marketing the artist has actually absorbed accumulates in `recoupable_marketing_applied_cents` and is reduced by exactly the amount a reopened statement had charged.

Because those identities must survive reversal, three operations in this system are **compensating actions** rather than forward transitions: disputing an already-accepted revenue report, voiding a reserve liquidation, and concluding an audit whose finding is material. Each is specified as the exact inverse of the operation it reverses — the same amounts, subtracted from the same fields — and each is documented in Section 3 next to the forward operation it undoes. A compensating action still bumps the `version` of every record it touches; reversal restores balances, not counters.

### 2.10. Flat Topology, Reference Resolution and Discovery

Eleven record types sit as peers, each with its own root-level collection. No collection is nested inside another's path, and the only two sub-resource listings that exist — a report's own revenue lines, and the revenue lines attributed to one release across every report — exist because a revenue line has no root-level collection of its own and would otherwise be undiscoverable.

Relationships are therefore carried by identifier fields in request payloads: a release names its `deal_id` and its `primary_artist_id`, an artist agreement names its `artist_id` and `release_id` and optionally the `supersedes_agreement_id` it will replace, a marketing spend names its `release_id`, a revenue report names its `deal_id`, a revenue line names its `report_id` and `release_id`, a physical run names its `release_id` and its `distributor_id`, and an artist statement names its `artist_agreement_id`. Reverse navigation is served by the list endpoints, each of which accepts the relevant foreign key as an optional filter alongside `status` and the paging parameters — deals filter by `distributor_id`; releases by `deal_id` and `primary_artist_id`; agreements by `artist_id`, `release_id` and `supersedes_agreement_id`; marketing spends by `release_id` and `category`; rate cards by `dsp_code`, `territory_code` and `tier`; revenue reports by `deal_id` and `dsp_code`; physical runs by `release_id`, `distributor_id` and additionally by `reserve_status`, since a run's two dimensions are independently interesting; and statements by `artist_agreement_id` and `basis_variant`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The nine subsections that follow walk the label's year: setting up the counterparty and the deal, putting releases into the catalog, contracting artists, committing marketing money, ingesting digital and physical revenue, recouping the advance, accounting the artist, and finally winding the term down. Rules that belong to more than one stage are stated where they first bite, and cross-references name the state transition rather than the URL.

### 3.1. Counterparties, Artists and Deal Formation

Before anything can be accounted, the two outside parties must exist as records. Registering a distribution counterparty captures its trading `name` (1 to 120 characters, not blank) and the registered `legal_entity` name that appears on the agreement (1 to 160 characters, not blank). A third input, `default_returns_reserve_pct`, is optional and sets the baseline physical returns reserve that manufacturing runs inherit when they do not state their own; it is a four-decimal fraction from 0.0000 through 0.5000 and, when omitted, defaults to 0.2000 — twenty percent of wholesale value held back against returns. The new distributor's `active_deal_count` starts at 0. That counter is derived, never supplied: it is the number of distribution deals naming this distributor whose status is `EXECUTED`, `TERMINATION_NOTICE` or `POST_TERM_COLLECTION`, and it is recomputed whenever a deal enters or leaves that set. A distributor's `id` is the value a `DistributionPartner` presents as `X-User-ID`.

Registering a recording artist captures the `legal_name` that appears on artist agreements (1 to 120 characters, not blank) and a contact `email` that must be shaped as a valid address and must be unique across all artists when compared case-insensitively; a second registration reusing an existing address, in any letter case, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Two derived totals start at 0 and are maintained thereafter by the statement workflow. `lifetime_paid_cents` is the sum of `net_payable_cents` across every `FINALIZED` statement belonging to any agreement of this artist. `unrecouped_advance_total_cents` is the sum of `unrecouped_artist_advance_cents` across this artist's agreements whose status is `ACTIVE`, `AMENDMENT_PENDING` or `POST_TERM`. An artist's `id` is the value an `Artist` presents as `X-User-ID`.

A deal is drafted before it is signed. Creating one names the counterparty through `distributor_id` and selects the agreement structure through `deal_type`, which is one of `DISTRIBUTION_FEE`, `PROFIT_SHARE` or `LABEL_SERVICES`. The structure decides which fee fields the payload must carry and which it must leave out, and the pairing is strict in both directions. A `DISTRIBUTION_FEE` deal requires `distribution_fee_pct`, a four-decimal fraction from 0.0100 through 0.3000, and both of the other structures' fee fields must be null. A `PROFIT_SHARE` deal requires `profit_share_label_pct`, the label's share of the profit base, a four-decimal fraction from 0.3000 through 0.9000. A `LABEL_SERVICES` deal requires both of its two components: `services_fee_pct`, a four-decimal fraction from 0.0100 through 0.2500, and `services_monthly_fee_cents`, a fixed monthly amount that may be zero but not negative. Supplying a fee field that the chosen structure does not use, or omitting one it does, is a request-shape violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

The remainder of the draft payload sets the commercial frame. `term_start_date` and `term_end_date` are calendar dates, the latter strictly after the former, and together they bound every accountable period. `territory_codes` is a non-empty array of distinct ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 codes naming the licensed territories; a revenue line reporting consumption in a territory absent from this list is refused, as Section 3.5 describes. `cross_collateralized` is a boolean that decides, once and for all, whether the advance recoups against a single deal-wide pool or release by release. `advance_amount_cents` is the advance the distributor pays the label on execution, recoupable from net receipts and permitted to be zero. `marketing_budget_cents` is the ceiling on committed marketing across the deal's releases. Two windows are optional: `objection_window_days`, the number of days after a statement is issued during which the artist may object, defaults to 30 and must lie between 1 and 180; and `post_term_collection_days`, the number of days after the term ends during which revenue may still be collected before the catalog reverts, defaults to 180 and must lie between 0 and 1095.

A freshly created deal has status `DRAFT`. Its `advance_payment_reference`, `executed_at`, `termination_effective_date` and `post_term_collection_end_date` are all null, its `recoupment_status` reads `UNRECOUPED`, and every derived counter — `advance_recouped_cents`, `unrecouped_advance_cents`, `net_receipts_to_date_cents`, `post_recoupment_net_receipts_cents` and `marketing_committed_cents` — sits at 0.

Execution is the moment the deal becomes real, and it moves the deal `DRAFT → EXECUTED`. The `LabelAdmin` executing it supplies `advance_payment_reference`, the wire reference for the advance payment, which is stored on the deal. Two preconditions apply: the deal must still be `DRAFT`, and it must already carry at least one release whose status is `PLANNED` or `RELEASED` — executing an empty deal is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STATE` (409), so releases are registered against the draft before it is signed. Execution stamps `executed_at` from the logical clock, sets `advance_recouped_cents` to 0 and `unrecouped_advance_cents` to the full `advance_amount_cents`, and then sets `recoupment_status`: a deal whose advance is zero is `RECOUPED` from the outset, and any positive advance leaves it `UNRECOUPED`. The distributor's `active_deal_count` is recomputed, and the deal's own `version` increases by 1.

### 3.2. Releases and the Allocation of the Advance

A release is the unit of catalog, of advance allocation and of reversion. Registering one names its parent agreement through `deal_id` and its featured artist through `primary_artist_id`, and carries a `upc` — a Universal Product Code of between 12 and 14 ASCII digits — that must be unique across every release in the system; a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_UPC` (409). The `title` is 1 to 200 characters and may not be blank. `planned_release_date` is the scheduled street date and must fall on or between the deal's `term_start_date` and `term_end_date`; a planned date outside the term is a request-shape violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `formats` is a non-empty array of distinct values drawn from `DIGITAL`, `VINYL`, `CD` and `CASSETTE`, and it is what decides whether physical product can ever be manufactured for this release.

The advance is carved up at registration time. `advance_share_cents` is this release's slice of the deal advance; it is optional, defaults to 0, and may not be negative. Across the deal's releases whose status is anything other than `REVERTED`, the shares must never sum to more than the deal's `advance_amount_cents` — a registration that would push the total over the advance is rejected with `ERR_ADVANCE_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDED` (422). The significance of the share depends on the deal's `cross_collateralized` flag. When that flag is false, each release recoups strictly against its own `advance_share_cents`, so a strong release stops recouping once its own slice is paid off even while the deal as a whole remains unrecouped. When the flag is true, the advance recoups against one deal-level pool and strong releases pay down weak ones; the per-release shares then drive only the comparative projection described in Section 3.6.

A new release has status `PLANNED`, a null `actual_release_date`, a null `reversion_eligible_date`, and four derived counters at 0: `net_receipts_to_date_cents` (its own share of digital and physical net receipts), `release_advance_recouped_cents` (the advance attributed to it by the waterfall), `unrecouped_advance_share_cents` (its `advance_share_cents` less the advance already attributed, never below zero), and `recoupable_marketing_applied_cents` (the marketing recoupment finalized statements have charged the artist on this release).

Taking a release to market advances it `PLANNED → RELEASED` and records the street date supplied as `actual_release_date`. The parent deal must be `EXECUTED` at that moment — a release cannot go to market under a draft, terminated, expired or closed agreement, and an attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STATE` (409). The supplied `actual_release_date` must itself fall on or between the deal's `term_start_date` and `term_end_date`; a street date outside the term is rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_TERM` (422). The release's `version` increases by 1. Beyond `RELEASED`, a release moves only under the deal's wind-down cascades, into `REVERSION_PENDING` and ultimately `REVERTED`, as Section 3.9 sets out; `REVERTED` is terminal.

### 3.3. Artist Agreements, Royalty Rates and Amendments

One artist agreement governs one artist's royalties on one release, and it carries the artist-side half of the recoupment machinery. Drafting one names the contracting artist through `artist_id` and the governed release through `release_id`, and fixes six commercial terms. `royalty_rate_pct` is the base royalty rate, a four-decimal fraction from 0.0100 through 0.5000, and it is the rate that applies while the release's deal has not yet recouped its advance. `escalated_royalty_rate_pct` is the rate that applies afterwards; it must be at least the base rate and no more than 0.5000, so an agreement may escalate or hold flat but never step down. `advance_amount_cents` is the artist advance, recouped first out of royalties. `recording_costs_cents` is the total recording cost booked against the release, and `recording_recoupable_pct` — a four-decimal fraction from 0.0000 through 1.0000 — is the portion of it the artist bears. `marketing_recoup_share_pct`, also a four-decimal fraction from 0.0000 through 1.0000, is the artist's share of the recoupable marketing pool computed in Section 3.8. An optional `supersedes_agreement_id` chains this draft to the agreement it will replace on activation; it is null for an original agreement.

A drafted agreement has status `DRAFT`, a null `active_royalty_rate_pct`, null `proposed_royalty_rate_pct` and `proposed_advance_amount_cents`, a null `post_term_collection_end_date`, and both recoupment balances — `unrecouped_artist_advance_cents` and `unrecouped_recording_cents` — sitting at 0. Nothing is recoupable until the agreement is activated.

Activation moves the agreement `DRAFT → ACTIVE` and is the moment the artist-side ledger is seeded. Two preconditions guard it. The governed release must be `PLANNED` or `RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_RELEASE_STATE` (409) — an agreement cannot be activated onto a release that is already reverting or reverted. And when `supersedes_agreement_id` is not null, the agreement it points at must itself be `ACTIVE` and must govern the same `release_id`; a predecessor in any other state, or one attached to a different release, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_AGREEMENT_STATE` (409). On success, `unrecouped_artist_advance_cents` is seeded from `advance_amount_cents`, and `unrecouped_recording_cents` is seeded from the recoupable portion of the recording spend — `recording_costs_cents` multiplied by `recording_recoupable_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. The `active_royalty_rate_pct` is then selected by the rule stated below, and the artist's `unrecouped_advance_total_cents` is recomputed. The agreement's `version` increases by 1.

Supersession, when it applies, is deliberately not a debt amnesty. The predecessor moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` — a terminal state — and its two outstanding balances are carried forward onto the successor: whatever remained of the predecessor's `unrecouped_artist_advance_cents` is added to the successor's freshly seeded advance balance, and whatever remained of its `unrecouped_recording_cents` is added to the successor's freshly seeded recording balance. The predecessor's own two balances are then zeroed, so the artist's total outstanding position is unchanged by the paperwork except for whatever new advance the successor itself carries. The predecessor's `version` increases by 1 as well. Finally, any statement of the predecessor still sitting in `DRAFT` or `PREPARED` is voided, because unissued working papers computed against a superseded agreement can no longer be published.

Terms can be renegotiated mid-flight through a two-step amendment that forms a cycle the agreement may traverse without bound. A `LabelAdmin` proposing an amendment moves the agreement `ACTIVE → AMENDMENT_PENDING` and parks two values, `proposed_royalty_rate_pct` and `proposed_advance_amount_cents`, without touching the live terms or any balance. The release's deal must be `EXECUTED` or `TERMINATION_NOTICE` at that moment — an amendment cannot be proposed under a deal that has expired, entered post-term collection or closed — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STATE` (409).

Resolution is a `BusinessAffairs` decision and returns the agreement to `ACTIVE` either way, which is what closes the cycle; the two arms are distinguished entirely by their side effects, and the divergence is observable on `royalty_rate_pct`. With `decision` set to `ACCEPT`, the parked values are copied into the live terms — `proposed_royalty_rate_pct` into `royalty_rate_pct` and `proposed_advance_amount_cents` into `advance_amount_cents` — and `unrecouped_artist_advance_cents` is increased by the amount by which the new advance exceeds the old one, with a negative difference treated as zero so that a reduced advance never claws back a balance already recouped. The `active_royalty_rate_pct` is then re-selected, both parked fields are cleared to null, and the artist's `unrecouped_advance_total_cents` is recomputed. With `decision` set to `REJECT`, `royalty_rate_pct`, `advance_amount_cents` and every ledger balance are left exactly as they were; only the two parked fields are cleared to null. Both arms increase the agreement's `version` by 1, and the agreement may be amended again immediately afterwards.

Rate selection itself is a single, sharply defined rule that runs in five places: on activation, on amendment resolution, on the acceptance cascade of a revenue report, on the compensation cascade of a dispute, and at the moment a statement is prepared. If the release's deal has `recoupment_status` equal to `RECOUPED`, then `active_royalty_rate_pct` takes the value of `escalated_royalty_rate_pct`; otherwise it takes the value of `royalty_rate_pct`. The value is copied verbatim as a four-decimal fraction — no arithmetic is performed, so no rounding arises. Only agreements whose status is `ACTIVE`, `AMENDMENT_PENDING` or `POST_TERM` participate: an agreement in `SUPERSEDED` or `TERMINATED` is never re-rated, whatever happens to the deal afterwards. When a statement is prepared, the agreement's current `active_royalty_rate_pct` is frozen onto that statement as `applied_royalty_rate_pct`, so a later escalation does not retroactively re-price a statement already prepared.

The two remaining destinations of an agreement are reached only by cascade. A deal's for-cause termination moves live agreements to `POST_TERM` and stamps `post_term_collection_end_date` onto them; a deal's closure moves agreements to `TERMINATED`, which is terminal. Section 3.9 specifies both.

### 3.4. Marketing Commitments, Approval and Recoupability

Marketing money is committed one spend at a time, and each spend records how much of itself the artist will eventually absorb. Creating a spend names the promoted release through `release_id`, the spend `category`, the requested `amount_cents` (which must be strictly positive) and the `vendor` or agency the money is committed to (1 to 120 characters, not blank). The release must be `PLANNED` or `RELEASED`; a release in `REVERSION_PENDING` or `REVERTED` may not accrue new marketing and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_RELEASE_NOT_PROMOTABLE` (409).

The `category` is one of `DIGITAL_ADS`, `RADIO_PROMO`, `VIDEO_PRODUCTION`, `TOUR_SUPPORT` or `PUBLICITY`, and it determines the spend's `recoupability` at creation without the requester choosing it: `DIGITAL_ADS` and `RADIO_PROMO` produce `FULLY_RECOUPABLE`, `VIDEO_PRODUCTION` and `TOUR_SUPPORT` produce `HALF_RECOUPABLE`, and `PUBLICITY` produces `NON_RECOUPABLE`. A new spend has status `DRAFT`, with `requested_by_user_id`, `decided_by_user_id`, `approved_at`, `settled_amount_cents` and `revision_note` all null.

Submitting a spend for decision moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, and the same action also moves a spend back `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, which is what closes the revision cycle — a spend may bounce between `SUBMITTED` and `REVISION_REQUESTED` without bound. Either way the spend's release must still be `PLANNED` or `RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_RELEASE_NOT_PROMOTABLE` (409), and the acting operator's `X-User-ID` is stored as `requested_by_user_id`. The submitter may optionally supply a fresh `amount_cents`, a fresh `category`, or both, in the same call; when either is supplied it replaces the stored value and `recoupability` is re-derived from the resulting `category` by the mapping above. The one asymmetry between the two source states is the reviewer's note: on the `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` edge, `revision_note` is cleared back to null, because the note that prompted the revision has now been answered, whereas on the `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` edge there is no note to clear. Both edges increase the spend's `version` by 1. Submitting a spend that is neither `DRAFT` nor `REVISION_REQUESTED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_SPEND_STATE` (409).

Deciding a submitted spend is reserved to `BusinessAffairs` and takes a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `REJECT` or `REQUEST_REVISION`. All three arms require the spend to be `SUBMITTED` and all three enforce segregation of duties: an acting `X-User-ID` equal to the spend's stored `requested_by_user_id` is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403), so the operator who asked for the money cannot be the one who grants it. All three arms store `decided_by_user_id` and increase `version` by 1.

The `APPROVE` arm moves the spend `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and carries three further checks. The release's deal must be `EXECUTED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DEAL_STATE` (409); the release itself must be `PLANNED` or `RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_RELEASE_NOT_PROMOTABLE` (409); and the deal's already-committed marketing plus this spend's `amount_cents` must not exceed the deal's `marketing_budget_cents`, otherwise `ERR_MARKETING_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (422). On success the logical-clock instant is stamped into `approved_at`, which is what decides the accounting period the spend falls into, and an optional `recoupability_override` in the payload replaces the category-derived `recoupability` — the only way a spend's recoupability ever departs from its category mapping. The deal's `marketing_committed_cents` is then recomputed.

The `REJECT` arm moves the spend `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, a terminal state, and does nothing beyond recording the decider. The `REQUEST_REVISION` arm moves the spend `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED` and additionally requires a `revision_note` in the payload that is present and not blank — a revision request without a note is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — storing that note for the requester to answer. Neither of these two arms touches the deal's budget, because neither creates a commitment.

Settlement records what the vendor actually invoiced and moves the spend `APPROVED → SETTLED`, a terminal state. The supplied `settled_amount_cents` must not exceed the approved `amount_cents`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; and the deal's committed total, recomputed with this spend valued at its settled figure instead of its approved figure, must still sit inside `marketing_budget_cents`, otherwise `ERR_MARKETING_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (422). Both the approval ceiling and the settlement ceiling read the same committed total, which is defined as follows: over every marketing spend attached to any release of the deal, a spend in `SETTLED` contributes its `settled_amount_cents` and a spend in `APPROVED` contributes its `amount_cents`, while spends in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `VOIDED` contribute nothing at all.

The last destination, `VOIDED`, is never reached by a client call. Entering post-term collection voids the deal's uncommitted marketing surface, and closing the deal voids approved commitments too; Section 3.9 gives the exact sets. `VOIDED` is terminal, as are `REJECTED` and `SETTLED`.

### 3.5. Rate Cards and Digital Revenue Reporting

Digital revenue is not reported in money; it is reported in streams, and the label prices those streams itself from a published rate book. Each rate card fixes one per-stream rate for one combination of service, territory and subscription tier over one effective window. `dsp_code` names the digital service provider — 2 to 24 characters, uppercase letters A through Z and the underscore only. `territory_code` is an ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code. `tier` is one of `PREMIUM`, `AD_SUPPORTED` or `FREE_TRIAL`. `rate_micros_per_stream` is the rate in micros and must be strictly positive; ten thousand micros make one cent, so a rate of 4,000 micros pays four tenths of a cent per stream. `effective_from` is the inclusive first day of the window, and `effective_to` is its **exclusive** end — a card whose `effective_to` is null is open-ended, and a card that states one must state a date strictly after its `effective_from`.

Because the window is half-open, two cards for the same combination may sit back to back: a card whose `effective_from` falls exactly on another's `effective_to` is adjacent, not overlapping, and is accepted. Any genuine intersection of windows on the same combination of `dsp_code`, `territory_code` and `tier` is rejected with `ERR_RATE_CARD_OVERLAP` (409). Rate cards are immutable once published — there is no edit and no withdrawal, which is why they carry neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

A revenue report is opened by the distribution partner for one deal and one accounting period, naming the deal through `deal_id`, the reporting service through `dsp_code`, and the period through `period_start` and `period_end` (the end not before the start). It begins in `DRAFT` with a null `submitted_by_user_id`, a null `accepted_at`, a null `dispute_reason`, and `gross_receipts_cents`, `distributor_share_cents`, `net_receipts_cents` and `advance_applied_cents` all at 0.

Stream counts are appended to the report one line at a time. Each line names the release the streams were attributed to through `release_id`, the `territory_code` of consumption, the `tier` of consumption, and a `stream_count` that may be zero but not negative; the report the line joins is identified by the `report_id` it is filed under. Lines may only be appended while the report is `DRAFT` or `DISPUTED` — appending to a `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` report is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_STATE` (409) — and only by the partner that owns the report, meaning the `X-User-ID` must equal the `distributor_id` of the report's deal, failing which `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). The line's `territory_code` must appear in the deal's `territory_codes`; consumption in an unlicensed territory is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A newly appended line has `applied_rate_micros_per_stream`, `resolved_revenue_cents` and `net_receipts_cents` all null: a line is priced when its report is submitted and allocated when its report is accepted, never at creation. Lines are immutable thereafter, and a line's contribution to one release is discoverable through the per-release revenue-line listing, which is the only way to reach lines by release since lines are created inside a report.

Submitting the report is the pricing event. It moves the report `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, and the same action moves a report back `DISPUTED → SUBMITTED`, closing the report cycle. Either way the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `distributor_id` of the report's deal, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403), and the report must carry at least one line — submitting an empty report is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_STATE` (409). The acting identifier is stored as `submitted_by_user_id`, and on the `DISPUTED → SUBMITTED` edge the previous `dispute_reason` is cleared back to null and every figure is recomputed from scratch against the report's current lines and the current rate book, so a re-submission after a dispute is a genuine re-pricing and not a replay of the old numbers. The report's `version` increases by 1.

**Pricing the lines.** Rate resolution runs per line, with the lines iterated in ascending order of their creation time. For a given line, the candidate rate cards are those whose `dsp_code` equals the report's `dsp_code`, whose `territory_code` equals the line's `territory_code`, whose `tier` equals the line's `tier`, whose `effective_from` falls on or before the report's `period_end`, and whose `effective_to` is either null or falls strictly after the report's `period_end`. The candidates are ranked by `effective_from` descending — the most recently effective card wins — and ties on `effective_from` are broken by taking the card created earliest. The chosen card's `rate_micros_per_stream` is written onto the line as `applied_rate_micros_per_stream`. If a line has no candidate at all, the entire submission is rejected with `ERR_RATE_CARD_NOT_FOUND` (422); no line is priced and no report figure changes.

Each line's own revenue is then computed and rounded before anything is added together: `resolved_revenue_cents` is the line's `stream_count` multiplied by its `applied_rate_micros_per_stream`, divided by the ten thousand micros that make a cent, and rounded half up to a whole number of cents. Only then is the report's `gross_receipts_cents` formed, as the plain sum of those already-rounded per-line figures. This order is pinned and it matters: rounding is applied once per line and never to the sum, so an implementation that sums raw fractional values and rounds once at the end will report a different gross figure and will be wrong.

**Pricing the distributor's cut.** Immediately after the lines are priced, the report's `distributor_share_cents` and `net_receipts_cents` are derived from the report's gross figure, and which formula applies depends entirely on the deal's `deal_type`.

On a `DISTRIBUTION_FEE` deal the raw share is the gross receipts multiplied by the deal's `distribution_fee_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents.

On a `PROFIT_SHARE` deal the label and the distributor split a profit base rather than the gross. The recoverable costs of the period are the sum of two amounts: first, the `settled_amount_cents` of every marketing spend on any release of this deal whose status is `SETTLED` and whose `approved_at` falls strictly before the midnight that opens the report's `period_start`; and second, the `manufacturing_cost_cents` of every physical run on any release of this deal whose status is `SHIPPED` or `RECONCILED` and whose `window_end` falls on or before the report's `period_end`. The profit base is the gross receipts less those recoverable costs, floored at zero. The label's share is the profit base multiplied by the deal's `profit_share_label_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents, and the raw distributor share is the gross receipts less that label share. Note the deliberate exclusion in the first term: marketing approved *inside* the period being reported never reduces the distributor's share for that same period, which is what keeps the marketing-cap chain of Section 3.8 acyclic.

On a `LABEL_SERVICES` deal the fee has a percentage component and a monthly component. The number of months in the period is computed from the calendar rather than from day counts: it is twelve times the difference between the year of `period_end` and the year of `period_start`, plus the difference between the month of `period_end` and the month of `period_start`, plus one — so a period from March to May of the same year counts as three months, and a period wholly inside one month counts as one. The raw distributor share is the gross receipts multiplied by the deal's `services_fee_pct` and rounded half up to whole cents, plus the deal's `services_monthly_fee_cents` multiplied by that month count.

Whichever branch produced it, the raw share is then clamped: `distributor_share_cents` is the raw share floored at zero and capped at the gross receipts, so the distributor can never take more than came in and can never owe the label money on this line of the calculation. Finally `net_receipts_cents` is the gross receipts less `distributor_share_cents`. Both figures live on the report and are what the label's advance recoups against.

### 3.6. Acceptance, the Advance Waterfall and Compensating Disputes

A submitted report is a claim; acceptance is what puts it on the books, and it is the single busiest operation in this system. Acceptance is a `RoyaltyAnalyst` action, moves the report `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, and must carry `expected_deal_version` as described in Section 2.7. Three further preconditions apply. The report must be `SUBMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_STATE` (409). The deal must still be collecting — its status must be `EXECUTED`, `TERMINATION_NOTICE` or `POST_TERM_COLLECTION`, since a `DRAFT`, `EXPIRED` or `CLOSED` deal cannot absorb receipts — otherwise `ERR_DEAL_NOT_COLLECTING` (409). And the reported period must lie inside the deal's accountable span, which runs from `term_start_date` to the deal's `post_term_collection_end_date` when that date has been set and to `term_end_date` when it has not; a period reaching outside that span is rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_TERM` (422).

Acceptance stamps `accepted_at` from the logical clock and then runs a five-hop cascade, each hop caused by the one before it.

The first hop allocates the distributor's cut down to the individual lines, because a statement to an artist is computed from lines, not from report totals. The allocation is a largest-remainder split of `distributor_share_cents` across the report's lines, weighted by each line's `resolved_revenue_cents`. If those weights sum to zero, every line's `net_receipts_cents` is set to 0. Otherwise each line's exact share is `distributor_share_cents` multiplied by that line's weight and divided by the total of the weights; each line provisionally takes the whole-cent part of its exact share; the leftover is `distributor_share_cents` less the sum of those whole-cent parts; and that leftover is distributed one cent at a time to the lines ranked by the fractional part of their exact share descending, with ties broken by earliest line creation, until it is exhausted. Each line's `net_receipts_cents` is then its own `resolved_revenue_cents` less the share allocated to it.

The second hop rolls those line figures up. Each release referenced by the report's lines has its `net_receipts_to_date_cents` increased by the sum of its own lines' `net_receipts_cents`, and the deal's `net_receipts_to_date_cents` is increased by the report's `net_receipts_cents`.

The third hop is the advance recoupment waterfall, and it is where the deal's `cross_collateralized` flag finally bites. The pool available for recoupment is the report's `net_receipts_cents`, and each release's contribution to that pool is the sum of its own lines' `net_receipts_cents`, with releases iterated in ascending order of their creation time. Only releases whose status is `PLANNED`, `RELEASED` or `REVERSION_PENDING` receive further attribution; a `REVERTED` release keeps the balances it has recorded but is attributed nothing more. Reports in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `DISPUTED` contribute nothing to any of this.

Under the **cross-collateralized** variant, which applies when `cross_collateralized` is true, there is one deal-level bucket: the total applied is the smaller of the period's pool and the deal's `unrecouped_advance_cents`, so strong releases pay down weak releases' advance and the whole period's net receipts are available to that single bucket. That total is then split back across the releases by the same largest-remainder method used for the lines — weighted by each release's contribution to the pool, leftover cents going to the largest fractional parts first and ties broken by earliest release creation — purely so that each release's attributed figure can be recorded.

Under the **siloed** variant, which applies when `cross_collateralized` is false, each release has its own bucket capped at its own slice of the advance. For each release, taken in ascending order of creation time, the release's own unrecouped share is its `advance_share_cents` less its `release_advance_recouped_cents`, floored at zero, and the amount applied to that release is the smaller of its contribution to the pool and that unrecouped share. The total applied is the sum of the per-release amounts. A release whose pool exceeds its own share therefore stops recouping and its surplus becomes post-recoupment excess even while the deal as a whole is still unrecouped — which is precisely the difference the two variants exist to express.

Both variants then settle identically. The report's `advance_applied_cents` records the total applied. The deal's `advance_recouped_cents` is increased by that total, and its `unrecouped_advance_cents` is recomputed as `advance_amount_cents` less `advance_recouped_cents`, which is what keeps the conservation identity of Section 2.9 exact. The deal's `post_recoupment_net_receipts_cents` is increased by the part of the pool that was *not* applied to the advance — the pool less the total applied. Each release has its `release_advance_recouped_cents` increased by its own applied amount, and its `unrecouped_advance_share_cents` recomputed as `advance_share_cents` less `release_advance_recouped_cents`, floored at zero. No percentage is applied anywhere in this hop, so the only rounding that arises is the largest-remainder split.

The fourth hop is the escalation trigger: when the recomputation leaves `unrecouped_advance_cents` at exactly 0, the deal's `recoupment_status` flips `UNRECOUPED → RECOUPED`. The fifth hop follows from it — every artist agreement whose status is `ACTIVE`, `AMENDMENT_PENDING` or `POST_TERM` on any release of this deal has its `active_royalty_rate_pct` recomputed, which under a `RECOUPED` deal means every one of those agreements now pays `escalated_royalty_rate_pct`. Acceptance increases the report's `version` by 1 and the deal's `version` by 1, and each cascaded release and agreement bumps its own `version` as well.

**Disputing a report** is the reverse channel, also a `RoyaltyAnalyst` action, and it must carry `expected_deal_version` together with a `dispute_reason` that is present and not blank. It has two source states with sharply different consequences. From `SUBMITTED`, the dispute moves the report `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED` and does nothing but store `dispute_reason` — nothing had been posted, so nothing needs unwinding. From `ACCEPTED`, the same action moves the report `ACCEPTED → DISPUTED` and runs a four-hop compensation that is the exact inverse of the acceptance cascade. Before any of it runs, one additional guard applies on this arm only: if any statement in `FINALIZED` reads a line of this report, the dispute is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATEMENT_STATE` (409), because a finalized statement's figures may not be pulled out from underneath it.

The compensation's first hop clears `net_receipts_cents` back to null on every line of the report. Its second hop, caused by the first, subtracts each release's share back out of that release's `net_receipts_to_date_cents` and subtracts the report's `net_receipts_cents` from the deal's `net_receipts_to_date_cents`. Its third hop, caused by the second, subtracts the report's `advance_applied_cents` from the deal's `advance_recouped_cents` and from each release's `release_advance_recouped_cents` pro rata, restoring the deal's `unrecouped_advance_cents` and `post_recoupment_net_receipts_cents` to their pre-acceptance values, and then zeroes the report's own `advance_applied_cents`. Its fourth hop, caused by the third, is the mirror of the escalation trigger: when `unrecouped_advance_cents` becomes positive again, the deal's `recoupment_status` flips `RECOUPED → UNRECOUPED` and every affected agreement's `active_royalty_rate_pct` reverts to its `royalty_rate_pct`. The `dispute_reason` is stored, the report's `version` and the deal's `version` each increase by 1, and the identity that the advance equals the recouped part plus the outstanding part holds again with zero drift. A disputed report can be re-priced and re-submitted, so the report cycle `ACCEPTED → DISPUTED → SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED` may repeat without bound; the report type has no terminal state.

**Comparing the two variants without committing to either.** Because the choice between cross-collateralization and siloed recoupment is worth tens of thousands of cents on the same underlying data, the deal exposes a computed, read-only recoupment projection. It accepts an optional `variant` of `CROSS_COLLATERALIZED` or `SILOED`; omitting it evaluates the deal's own `cross_collateralized` setting, and supplying one forces that arm so that the two can be compared on identical stored data. Nothing is persisted by this read. Critically, the projection is always recomputed from a **zero baseline**: its pool is the sum of `net_receipts_cents` over every report of this deal whose status is `ACCEPTED`, every release bucket starts at zero, and the persisted `advance_recouped_cents` and `release_advance_recouped_cents` balances are never read — without that pinned baseline the siloed arm would be contaminated by the balances the cross-collateralized arm had already written. The projection reports the `variant` it evaluated, the `pool_net_receipts_cents` it worked from, the deal's `advance_amount_cents`, the `advance_applied_cents` the variant would apply, the resulting `unrecouped_advance_cents` and `post_recoupment_net_receipts_cents`, the `recoupment_status` that would follow, and a `release_breakdown` array carrying the per-release attribution.

### 3.7. Physical Manufacturing, Shipping and Returns Reserves

Physical product introduces an obligation digital never has: records shipped to retail can come back. The label therefore withholds part of the physical proceeds from the artist until the returns picture is known, and a manufacturing run tracks that withholding on a dimension of its own.

A run is planned by the label against one release and one shipping partner, naming them through `release_id` and `distributor_id`, with `units_planned` and `unit_manufacturing_cost_cents` both strictly positive and a manufacturing window given by `window_start` and `window_end`, the end not before the start. The target release must actually have something to press: its `formats` must contain at least one of `VINYL`, `CD` or `CASSETTE`, and planning a run against a purely digital release is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RELEASE_STATE` (409). The run's `returns_reserve_pct` may be supplied — between 0.0000 and 0.5000 inclusive, at exactly four decimal places — and when it is omitted it is copied from the shipping partner's `default_returns_reserve_pct`, so every run has a baseline reserve percentage from the moment it exists. A new run opens with `status` at `PLANNED` and `reserve_status` at `HELD`.

Those two enum fields are **independent dimensions on the same row**, and they must be read as such. `status` is the fulfilment axis — `PLANNED`, `MANUFACTURED`, `SHIPPED`, `RECONCILED` — and it describes where the physical goods are. `reserve_status` is the money axis — `HELD`, `PARTIALLY_LIQUIDATED`, `LIQUIDATED` — and it describes how much of the withheld reserve has been released for payment. Recording returns advances neither axis: it is a self-loop that leaves the run in `SHIPPED` and leaves the reserve axis alone. The two axes touch at exactly one point, described at the end of this subsection.

Manufacturing is a label action and books the actual production quantity: `units_manufactured` must be strictly positive, and the run's release must be `PLANNED` or `RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_RELEASE_STATE` (409). The run moves `PLANNED → MANUFACTURED` and `manufacturing_cost_cents` is set to `units_manufactured` multiplied by `unit_manufacturing_cost_cents` — the planned per-unit cost applied to the actual quantity, not to `units_planned`.

Shipping is the partner's action and is where both the money and the reserve appear. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the run's `distributor_id`, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). The `units_shipped` reported may not exceed `units_manufactured`; a larger figure is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Beyond that, the label holds the partner to an inventory obligation: the stock left behind, `units_manufactured` less `units_shipped`, must be at least five percent of `units_shipped` rounded **up** to a whole unit, so shipping 1,000 units out of a 1,040-unit pressing breaches the obligation (40 retained against a 50-unit floor) while 1,050 satisfies it exactly. A breach is refused with `ERR_INVENTORY_OBLIGATION_BREACH` (422). On success the run moves `MANUFACTURED → SHIPPED`, storing `units_shipped` and `wholesale_unit_price_cents`, sizing the reserve as described below with `units_returned` still at zero, and raising both the release's and the deal's `net_receipts_to_date_cents` by the run's `physical_net_receipts_cents`.

Returns are recorded incrementally by the same partner, with `returned_units` added to the run's cumulative `units_returned` on each call. The run must be `SHIPPED`, the cumulative total may never exceed `units_shipped` — a larger cumulative figure is refused with `ERR_RETURNS_EXCEED_SHIPPED` (422) — and the reserve axis must still be `HELD`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE` (409): once any part of the reserve has been paid out, the reserve may not be silently re-sized underneath it. Each recording re-runs the reserve sizing from scratch and re-derives `physical_net_receipts_cents`, adjusting the release's and the deal's `net_receipts_to_date_cents` by the **delta** between the new figure and the old one rather than by the new figure itself.

**Sizing the reserve.** The baseline percentage is the run's own `returns_reserve_pct` when it is set and the shipping partner's `default_returns_reserve_pct` when it is null. The `returns_rate` is `units_returned` divided by `units_shipped`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and is 0.0000 while `units_shipped` is zero. Two escalation tiers then apply, both tested with "at least", not "more than": a `returns_rate` of 0.2500 or above lifts the baseline by 0.1500, otherwise a `returns_rate` of 0.1000 or above lifts it by 0.0500, otherwise the baseline stands unchanged. Whatever the tier produces is capped at 0.5000 and stored as `effective_reserve_pct`. A run with a 0.2000 baseline that has taken back 250 units of 1,000 therefore sits exactly on the second tier and reserves at 0.3500, while 249 returns of 1,000 gives a rate of 0.2490, falls to the first tier, and reserves at 0.2500.

The gross shipped value is `units_shipped` multiplied by `wholesale_unit_price_cents`, and `reserve_initial_cents` is that value multiplied by `effective_reserve_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. `reserve_held_cents` is then adjusted by the difference between the new `reserve_initial_cents` and the sum of the currently held and currently liquidated cents, floored at zero — which means already-liquidated cents are never clawed back by a re-tiering, and the identity that `reserve_initial_cents` equals `reserve_held_cents` plus `reserve_liquidated_cents` continues to hold. Finally `physical_net_receipts_cents` is the units that stayed sold, `units_shipped` less `units_returned`, multiplied by `wholesale_unit_price_cents`, less `manufacturing_cost_cents`, floored at zero — a run that cost more to press than it earned contributes nothing rather than a negative amount.

**Liquidating and un-liquidating.** Releasing reserve is an analyst action, carries `expected_run_version` as described in Section 2.7, and takes a `liquidation_pct` strictly above 0.0000 and at most 1.0000 (anything outside that half-open range is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`). The run's `status` must be `SHIPPED` or `RECONCILED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE` (409). The amount moved is `reserve_initial_cents` multiplied by `liquidation_pct`, rounded half up, and it may not exceed the currently held `reserve_held_cents`; a request that would over-draw is refused with `ERR_LIQUIDATION_EXCEEDS_RESERVE` (422). Note that the percentage always applies to `reserve_initial_cents`, never to the diminishing held balance, so two successive liquidations at 0.6000 over-draw and the second is refused.

Where the reserve axis lands depends on how much is left. A partial release from `HELD` with a `liquidation_pct` below 1.0000 moves the axis `HELD → PARTIALLY_LIQUIDATED`, transferring the cents from `reserve_held_cents` into `reserve_liquidated_cents`. A subsequent release whose cumulative liquidation reaches `reserve_initial_cents` moves the axis `PARTIALLY_LIQUIDATED → LIQUIDATED`, setting `reserve_held_cents` to 0 and `reserve_liquidated_cents` to the full `reserve_initial_cents`. A single release at exactly 1.0000 skips the middle state and moves the axis `HELD → LIQUIDATED` directly with the same end figures. **This is the one coupling between the two dimensions**: whenever the reserve axis reaches `LIQUIDATED` in a request, the fulfilment axis moves `SHIPPED → RECONCILED` in that same request, closing the run. Nothing else on the fulfilment axis is driven by the reserve axis, and nothing on the reserve axis is driven by fulfilment.

Liquidation has a compensating action. Voiding is available from either `PARTIALLY_LIQUIDATED` or `LIQUIDATED`, also carries `expected_run_version`, and pulls every liquidated cent back: `reserve_liquidated_cents` returns to `reserve_held_cents` and is set to 0, the axis returns to `HELD`, and `reserve_initial_cents` is unchanged — the conserved total survives the round trip exactly. From `LIQUIDATED` the void also reverses the coupling, moving the fulfilment axis `RECONCILED → SHIPPED` so that a reopened reserve reopens the run. One guard protects downstream work: if any statement in `FINALIZED` has already read this run's `reserve_held_cents`, the void is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATEMENT_STATE` (409), for the same reason a report that a finalized statement read cannot be disputed.

### 3.8. Artist Statements and the Deduction Waterfall

An artist statement accounts one agreement for one period. It is the point where every other sub-ledger in the system is read at once, and it carries two independent dimensions of its own: `status`, the accounting axis (`DRAFT`, `PREPARED`, `ISSUED`, `OBJECTED`, `REVISED`, `FINALIZED`, `VOIDED`), and `audit_status`, the audit axis (`NONE`, `AUDIT_REQUESTED`, `AUDIT_COMPLETED`, `AUDIT_DENIED`), which is the subject of Section 3.9.

A statement is opened by an analyst against an `artist_agreement_id` for a period given by `period_start` and `period_end`, and it must declare its `basis_variant` — `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL` — at creation. That single choice governs every figure the statement will ever carry. The agreement must be accountable at that moment, meaning its status is `ACTIVE`, `AMENDMENT_PENDING` or `POST_TERM`; opening a statement against a `SUPERSEDED` or `TERMINATED` agreement is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_STATEMENTABLE` (409), and the same test is applied again at preparation because an agreement can be superseded in between. A new statement is `DRAFT` with `audit_status` at `NONE` and every money field zeroed.

Preparing the statement computes it. Preparation is an analyst action available from `DRAFT` and again from `REVISED`, and it is the only place in the system where the marketing cap and the net-payable waterfall run. The agreement being accounted must be `ACTIVE`, `AMENDMENT_PENDING` or `POST_TERM`; a `SUPERSEDED` or `TERMINATED` agreement cannot be stated and is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_STATEMENTABLE` (409). On the `DRAFT` entry the statement's period must lie inside the deal's accountable span — `term_start_date` through `post_term_collection_end_date` when set and `term_end_date` otherwise — failing which `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_TERM` (422). Also on that entry, and only for a `FINAL` statement, the period is checked against the agreement's existing non-`VOIDED` `FINAL` statements: it may not intersect one of their periods, not even by a single day, which is `ERR_STATEMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409); and it must begin on the day immediately after the latest such period ends, which is `ERR_STATEMENT_PERIOD_NOT_CONTIGUOUS` (422). `FINAL` statements therefore tile the agreement's history with no gaps and no double counting, while `PROVISIONAL` statements are free to overlap anything, since they are interim views.

**The revenue bases.** `digital_royalty_base_cents` is the sum of `net_receipts_cents` over the revenue lines whose `release_id` is the agreement's release, counting only lines on reports whose status is `ACCEPTED` and whose own `period_start` through `period_end` is **fully contained** in the statement's period — a report straddling the statement boundary contributes nothing at all rather than a pro-rated part. `physical_royalty_base_cents` is the sum of `physical_net_receipts_cents` over the runs on that release whose status is `SHIPPED` or `RECONCILED` and whose `window_end` falls inside the statement's period. Preparation also freezes the rate: `applied_royalty_rate_pct` is copied from the agreement's `active_royalty_rate_pct` at that moment, so a later recoupment escalation does not retroactively re-rate an already-prepared statement. `gross_royalty_cents` is the sum of the two bases multiplied by `applied_royalty_rate_pct`, rounded half up.

**Sizing the artist's marketing charge.** Before the waterfall runs, the artist's absorbable share of marketing is sized, and the ordering here is deliberate because the underlying dependency looks circular: marketing feeds the distributor's share, the distributor's share determines net receipts, net receipts cap the artist's marketing charge, and that charge is marketing again. The cut that breaks the cycle was made in Section 3.5 — the profit-share branch of the distributor's share may only deduct settled marketing approved strictly *before* the reported period began, so marketing approved inside a period never influences that same period's net receipts. Given that, the evaluation order is pinned and no fixed-point iteration is performed.

The period's net receipts figure is the sum of `digital_royalty_base_cents` and `physical_royalty_base_cents`, the already-net bases computed above. The cap, `marketing_cap_cents`, is thirty percent of that figure, rounded half up. The raw pool is then formed by taking each qualifying marketing spend on the agreement's release, multiplying its basis amount by the recoupability factor of its `recoupability` — 1.0000 for `FULLY_RECOUPABLE`, 0.5000 for `HALF_RECOUPABLE`, 0.0000 for `NON_RECOUPABLE` — rounding half up per spend row, and summing those per-row results. The recoupable pool is the smaller of that raw pool and the cap. The artist's share is that pool multiplied by the agreement's `marketing_recoup_share_pct`, rounded half up, less the release's `recoupable_marketing_applied_cents`, floored at zero — the subtraction of the cumulative applied figure is what stops the same marketing being charged to the artist twice across successive statements.

Which spends qualify, and at what value, is the first place `basis_variant` diverges. A `PROVISIONAL` statement takes spends whose status is `APPROVED` or `SETTLED` and values each at its `amount_cents` — the commitment, not the invoice. A `FINAL` statement takes only spends whose status is `SETTLED` and values each at its `settled_amount_cents` — the invoice actually paid. Spends in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `VOIDED` enter neither basis.

**The deduction waterfall.** With the gross royalty and the artist's marketing share in hand, three buckets are filled strictly in order, each taking the smaller of what remains and its own ceiling. `advance_applied_cents` takes the lesser of the gross royalty and the agreement's `unrecouped_artist_advance_cents`. What remains after that flows to `recording_applied_cents`, which takes the lesser of the remainder and the agreement's `unrecouped_recording_cents`. What remains after that flows to `marketing_applied_cents`, which takes the lesser of the remainder and the artist's marketing share. The order is fixed: advance first, then recording costs, then marketing. Reordering the buckets changes every figure on the statement whenever the gross royalty is too small to fill all three.

The reserve is withheld last and sits outside the waterfall. `reserve_withheld_cents` is a reserve basis multiplied by `applied_royalty_rate_pct`, rounded half up — the artist is only withheld their own royalty share of the reserve, not the whole reserve. This is the second place `basis_variant` diverges, and it is the sharper of the two: a `PROVISIONAL` statement uses the **full** reserve, the sum of `reserve_initial_cents` over the release's runs in `SHIPPED` or `RECONCILED`, while a `FINAL` statement uses only the **unliquidated** reserve, the sum of `reserve_held_cents` over those same runs. Liquidating a reserve therefore raises the `FINAL` net payable without moving the `PROVISIONAL` figure by a cent. `net_payable_cents` is what is left after the three buckets and the reserve, floored at zero.

The two variants, run against byte-identical stored data, produce materially different statements. On a period with an `applied_royalty_rate_pct` of 0.1800, a digital base of 595,000 cents and a physical base of 510,000 cents, the gross royalty is 198,900 cents; after an advance bucket of 50,000 and a recording bucket of 50,000 there are 98,900 cents left in both variants. The `PROVISIONAL` statement then charges 40,000 cents of marketing and withholds 24,300 cents against the full 135,000-cent reserve, netting 34,600 cents payable. The `FINAL` statement, after forty percent of that reserve has been liquidated, charges 22,500 cents of marketing and withholds 14,580 cents against the 81,000 cents still held, netting 61,820 cents. The divergence is 27,220 cents on the same period and the same underlying rows, which is why the variant is fixed at creation and never inferred.

Preparation records the acting `X-User-ID` as `prepared_by_user_id`. On the `REVISED → PREPARED` re-entry, every figure is recomputed against current data — overwriting the previous run rather than adjusting it — the statement's `objection_adjustment_cents` (treated as zero when null) is added back into `net_payable_cents` after the floor is applied, and `revision_count` increases by 1. That counter is the audit trail of how many times a statement has been reworked, and it is incremented on the `REVISED` entry only, never on the first preparation from `DRAFT`.

Nothing in the artist's ledgers moves at preparation. `PREPARED` figures are advisory: the debits happen only at finalization, described in Section 3.9. Statements that were never issued are also disposable — an unissued statement in `DRAFT` or `PREPARED` is moved to `VOIDED` when its deal is terminated or when its agreement is superseded by a successor, and `VOIDED` statements are never recomputed. Statements already in `ISSUED`, `OBJECTED`, `REVISED` or `FINALIZED` are never voided by any cascade; published work is only ever revised, never discarded.

**Issuing.** Publication is a business-affairs action, available from `PREPARED` and again from `OBJECTED` when an objection has been denied. Two guards apply. First, segregation of duties: the acting `X-User-ID` must not equal the statement's `prepared_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403) — whoever computed the statement may not publish it. Second, only a `FINAL` statement may be published at all; issuing a `PROVISIONAL` statement is refused with `ERR_PROVISIONAL_NOT_ISSUABLE` (422), which is the structural reason a `PROVISIONAL` statement can never be finalized, objected to or audited. Issuing stamps `issued_at` from the logical clock, records `issued_by_user_id`, and sets `objection_deadline` to `issued_at` plus the deal's `objection_window_days`, computed to the millisecond.

### 3.9. Objections, Audit Rights, Finalization and Catalog Wind-Down

An issued statement is a published claim against the artist, and the artist is given two successive rights over it: a short objection window immediately after issue, and a long audit-rights window after finalization. Both are exercised by the artist and both are answered by business affairs, and the accounting axis of a statement is genuinely cyclic — `ISSUED → OBJECTED → REVISED → PREPARED → ISSUED` may repeat without bound, and even a finalized statement can be pulled back into the cycle by an audit.

**Objecting.** An objection may be filed only by the artist the statement belongs to: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `artist_id` of the statement's agreement, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). The statement must be `ISSUED`, and the logical clock must not have passed `objection_deadline` — an objection filed at exactly that instant is accepted, and one filed strictly after it is refused with `ERR_OBJECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422). The artist states a categorised `objection_reason_code` of `RATE_DISPUTE`, `MISSING_REVENUE`, `COST_DISPUTE` or `RESERVE_DISPUTE`, an `objection_disputed_amount_cents` and a free-text `objection_narrative`. The disputed amount is bounded by what is actually in play on the statement: it may not exceed `net_payable_cents` plus `marketing_applied_cents` plus `reserve_withheld_cents` — the sum of what the artist was paid and what was deducted from them for marketing and reserve — and a larger claim is refused with `ERR_DISPUTED_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_NET` (422). Note that the advance and recording buckets are deliberately excluded from that ceiling: those deductions repay the artist's own borrowings and are not disputable through this channel. A filed objection moves the statement `ISSUED → OBJECTED` and stamps `objection_filed_at` from the clock alongside the three supplied fields.

**Resolving an objection.** Resolution is a business-affairs action from `OBJECTED`, and the operator resolving it must not be the analyst who prepared it — the acting `X-User-ID` may not equal `prepared_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). The supplied `resolution` decides which of two very different destinations the statement reaches. `DENIED` returns the statement `OBJECTED → ISSUED`, stores `objection_resolution` as `DENIED`, leaves `objection_adjustment_cents` null, and — importantly — leaves `objection_deadline` exactly as it was, so the objection window does not reopen and the artist cannot object a second time to the same figures. `UPHELD` instead moves the statement `OBJECTED → REVISED`, stores `objection_resolution` as `UPHELD` together with the granted `objection_adjustment_cents`, and clears `issued_at`, `issued_by_user_id` and `objection_deadline` back to null, un-publishing the statement so that it must be re-prepared and re-issued. The granted adjustment is not applied at resolution time; it is added back into `net_payable_cents` on the next preparation, as Section 3.8 describes. `objection_adjustment_cents` is optional on the request and is only meaningful on the `UPHELD` arm.

**Finalizing.** Finalization is the only operation in the entire system that debits the artist-side recoupment ledgers, and it has two source states whose preconditions are disjoint. In both cases the acting operator is business affairs and must not be the statement's preparer, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).

The ordinary path runs `ISSUED → FINALIZED`. It requires `objection_resolution` to be either null or `DENIED` — an upheld objection has already moved the statement off `ISSUED`, so this simply records that no live dispute survives. It requires the logical clock to be strictly past `objection_deadline`; finalizing at or before the deadline is refused with `ERR_OBJECTION_WINDOW_OPEN` (422), because the artist's window must actually elapse. And it requires `audit_status` not to be `AUDIT_REQUESTED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATEMENT_STATE` (409) — this is the audit axis gating the accounting axis.

The second path runs `PREPARED → FINALIZED` and exists solely for post-term collection accounting. It requires the statement's `basis_variant` to be `FINAL` and the governing agreement's status to be `POST_TERM`, which is the only configuration in which a statement is finalized without ever being published to the artist. On this path `objection_deadline` is null, so no objection-window test applies at all — which is precisely why the two source states cannot be collapsed into one rule.

Both paths perform the same debits. The agreement's `unrecouped_artist_advance_cents` is reduced by the statement's `advance_applied_cents` and its `unrecouped_recording_cents` by `recording_applied_cents`, while the release's `recoupable_marketing_applied_cents` is increased by `marketing_applied_cents` — the cumulative figure that stops the same marketing being charged again on the next statement. The artist's roll-ups, `lifetime_paid_cents` and `unrecouped_advance_total_cents`, are recomputed. `finalized_at` is stamped from the logical clock, which is what starts the audit-rights window. Both the statement's `version` and the agreement's `version` increase by 1.

**Requesting an audit.** Audit rights are exercised by the artist against a `FINALIZED` statement, and again the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `artist_id` of the statement's agreement, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). The request must arrive no later than 365 days after `finalized_at`; the window is inclusive of that instant, and a request beyond it is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (422). The statement's `audit_status` must be `NONE`, so audit rights are exercisable exactly once per statement — a second request, or a request against a statement whose audit has already been concluded or denied, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATEMENT_STATE` (409). The request moves the audit axis `NONE → AUDIT_REQUESTED` and stores the scope the artist wants examined, `audit_scope_period_start` and `audit_scope_period_end`, plus the `auditor_name` of the independent auditor nominated.

**Concluding an audit.** Conclusion is a business-affairs action from `AUDIT_REQUESTED`, subject to the same segregation-of-duties test, and it supplies `findings_adjustment_cents`, a signed amount that may be negative. Two mutually exclusive edges leave `AUDIT_REQUESTED`, and which one is taken depends on nothing but whether the stored scope is contained in the statement's own accounting period.

If `audit_scope_period_start` falls on or after the statement's `period_start` **and** `audit_scope_period_end` falls on or before its `period_end`, the scope is answerable from this statement's own figures and the audit axis moves `AUDIT_REQUESTED → AUDIT_COMPLETED`. The supplied amount is stored as `audit_findings_adjustment_cents` and `audit_cost_bearer` is decided by materiality: it is `LABEL` when the absolute value of the finding is at least five percent of `net_payable_cents` — that threshold being the statement's `net_payable_cents` multiplied by 0.05 and rounded half up — and `ARTIST` otherwise, so the label pays for an audit that found something and the artist pays for one that did not. Materiality has a second, larger consequence. A material finding also moves the **accounting** axis `FINALIZED → REVISED`, reversing the finalize debit exactly: `unrecouped_artist_advance_cents` is increased by `advance_applied_cents`, `unrecouped_recording_cents` by `recording_applied_cents`, and the release's `recoupable_marketing_applied_cents` is decreased by `marketing_applied_cents`, restoring the ledgers cent for cent. `issued_at`, `objection_deadline` and `finalized_at` are all cleared to null, the statement re-enters the cycle to be prepared again, and both the statement's and the agreement's `version` increase by 1. An immaterial finding leaves the accounting axis untouched at `FINALIZED`; only the audit axis and `audit_cost_bearer` move.

If instead `audit_scope_period_start` falls before the statement's `period_start` **or** `audit_scope_period_end` falls after its `period_end`, the artist has asked about figures this statement does not account for, and the audit axis moves `AUDIT_REQUESTED → AUDIT_DENIED`, which is terminal. On this edge `audit_findings_adjustment_cents` stays null whatever was supplied, `audit_cost_bearer` is set to `ARTIST`, and the accounting axis does not move — an out-of-scope audit never reopens a finalized statement. The two edges share one trigger and are separated purely by that containment test, so an implementation that evaluates scope loosely will reopen statements it should have refused.

**Winding the deal down.** A deal leaves `EXECUTED` by one of two forks, and the forks are not interchangeable — the whole point of having both is that they do different things to the catalog underneath.

The **for-cause** fork moves the deal `EXECUTED → TERMINATION_NOTICE`. It sets `termination_effective_date` to the logical clock's calendar date and `post_term_collection_end_date` to that date plus the deal's `post_term_collection_days`. It then runs a three-hop cascade. Hop one splits the catalog by whether it ever reached the market: every release in `RELEASED` moves to `REVERSION_PENDING` and receives `reversion_eligible_date` equal to the deal's `post_term_collection_end_date`, while every release still in `PLANNED` — one that never went to market — moves straight to `REVERTED`. Hop two, caused by hop one, moves every agreement governing one of those releases whose status is `ACTIVE` or `AMENDMENT_PENDING` to `POST_TERM` and stamps the deal's `post_term_collection_end_date` onto each. Hop three, caused by hop two, voids the unissued working papers: every statement of one of those agreements sitting in `DRAFT` or `PREPARED` moves to `VOIDED`. The distributor's `active_deal_count` is recomputed and the deal's `version` increases by 1.

The **natural-expiry** fork moves the deal `EXECUTED → EXPIRED` and may only be taken once the term has actually run out: the logical clock's calendar date must be strictly after `term_end_date`, otherwise `ERR_TERM_NOT_ENDED` (422). It sets `post_term_collection_end_date` to `term_end_date` plus `post_term_collection_days` and leaves `termination_effective_date` null. Its treatment of the catalog is deliberately the opposite of the for-cause fork: every release in `PLANNED` or `RELEASED` receives `reversion_eligible_date`, but **no release changes state**, no agreement moves to `POST_TERM`, and consequently no statement is voided. Statements in flight when a deal expires naturally survive and can still be prepared, issued and finalized; statements in flight when a deal is terminated for cause are discarded unless they had already been published. The distributor's `active_deal_count` is recomputed and the deal's `version` increases by 1.

Both forks join at the same next state. Entering post-term collection moves the deal `TERMINATION_NOTICE → POST_TERM_COLLECTION` or `EXPIRED → POST_TERM_COLLECTION` with identical preconditions and identical side effects from either side: the marketing surface is frozen, meaning every marketing spend on the deal's releases sitting in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` moves to `VOIDED`, and the deal's `version` increases by 1. Spends already `APPROVED` or `SETTLED` are untouched — money already committed still settles and still recoups. Throughout `POST_TERM_COLLECTION` the deal keeps collecting: revenue reports may still be accepted, which is why that status appears in the collecting set of Section 3.6, and post-term statements may still be prepared and finalized against `POST_TERM` agreements.

Closure is the terminal event and moves the deal `POST_TERM_COLLECTION → CLOSED`. Two guards protect it. The logical clock's calendar date must be strictly after `post_term_collection_end_date`, otherwise `ERR_POST_TERM_COLLECTION_ACTIVE` (422) — the collection tail must be over. And no statement on any of the deal's agreements may be sitting in `OBJECTED` with a null `objection_resolution`; an unanswered artist objection blocks closure with `ERR_OPEN_OBJECTIONS_BLOCK_CLOSE` (409). Closure then reverts the catalog through its own three-hop cascade. Hop one moves every release in `PLANNED`, `RELEASED` or `REVERSION_PENDING` to `REVERTED` — rights return to the artist, and the fact that `RELEASED` appears here as well as `REVERSION_PENDING` is what lets the expiry fork, where no release ever passed through `REVERSION_PENDING`, still revert its catalog. Hop two, caused by hop one, moves every agreement on those releases whose status is `ACTIVE`, `AMENDMENT_PENDING` or `POST_TERM` to `TERMINATED`. Hop three, caused by hop two, voids every remaining marketing spend on those releases in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` — note that `APPROVED` is voidable at closure though not at the entry to post-term collection, since an approved commitment that was never settled by the end of the deal will never be settled. The distributor's `active_deal_count` is recomputed a final time and the deal's `version` increases by 1. `CLOSED` is terminal, as are `REVERTED` for a release, `SUPERSEDED` and `TERMINATED` for an agreement, `REJECTED`, `SETTLED` and `VOIDED` for a marketing spend, `RECONCILED` on a run's fulfilment axis, and `AUDIT_DENIED` on a statement's audit axis.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is mounted at the API root and speaks JSON in the envelope of Section 2.2. Creating a new resource returns 201 with the created record; every other successful call — reads, lifecycle transitions, computed projections and the clock control — returns 200 with the affected or requested record, and list endpoints return 200 with an array plus the pagination `meta` block. Requests are authenticated by the `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` headers described in Section 2.1, and the Authorized Role(s) column is exhaustive: a role absent from a row is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
