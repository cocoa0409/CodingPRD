# Marketing Mix Incrementality API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Brand marketing teams live with two irreconcilable numbers. The first comes out of an econometric marketing-mix model built on aggregate spend, sales, price and distribution series; the second comes out of the advertising platforms themselves, each claiming credit for conversions it observed. Neither number is trustworthy on its own: the model is a regression over correlated media plans and cannot separate a channel's true effect from a seasonal tailwind, while platform-reported attribution is systematically self-favouring. The only arbiter is a controlled experiment — a geographic holdout or an audience split that withholds media from part of the population and measures what did not happen. This service exists to make that arbitration a repeatable, auditable operation rather than a quarterly spreadsheet exercise.

The platform is organised around a single analytical asset: the **model run**. A run is specified against a sealed window of observations, fitted with per-channel adstock and saturation curves, backtested against a trailing holdout, calibrated against accepted experiment readouts, and finally published as the market's single source of truth for budget planning. Around that spine sit four supporting concerns — data collection with restatement handling, experiment design with statistical power gating and holdout-share ceilings, budget optimization under diminishing returns and contractual spend bounds, and stakeholder reconciliation that quantifies the gap between model attribution and platform attribution. Every one of these concerns can invalidate work downstream of it, and the system's central design commitment is that such invalidation is explicit and immediate rather than discovered months later.

Because analytical outputs are used to move real money, the service enforces strict separation of duties, optimistic concurrency on the two aggregates that multiple analysts genuinely contend over, and a market-level posture gate that refuses to let planning proceed while the underlying data is in an acknowledged state of repair. The API is mounted under `/mmm`, with a single auxiliary test-driving surface for the logical clock, and every collection is a flat peer resource — parentage is expressed through foreign keys carried in request bodies, never through nested URLs.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capability set that operator is acting under. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs; a header whose value consists only of whitespace counts as empty for this purpose, and surrounding whitespace is trimmed from both headers before the role is matched against the declared set. If `X-User-Role` is a string that is not one of the six declared role names, or is a valid role that is not permitted to invoke the requested operation, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `DataEngineer`, `Modeler`, `ExperimentDesigner`, `AnalyticsLead`, `BrandPlanner` and `Auditor`.

A `DataEngineer` owns the data-collection surface: registering markets, declaring media channels and revising their curve parameters, adding geographic cells, opening training windows, sealing and reopening them, and filing spend and sales observations. A `DataEngineer` cannot fit a model, publish anything, design an experiment, optimize a budget, or approve any decision. A `Modeler` specifies model runs, fits them, validates them against their holdout, files calibration records and applies those records through recalibration; a `Modeler` cannot publish a run — publication is a decision, not a modelling step — and cannot touch source data. An `ExperimentDesigner` drafts experiments, locks their designs, enlists geographic cells into arms, starts them in market, posts their read-outs, releases their cells, and runs the market washout sweep; an `ExperimentDesigner` cannot decide the outcome of a readout. An `AnalyticsLead` is the sole decision authority: publishing model runs, accepting or rejecting experiment readouts, approving budget scenarios, and filing reconciliation reports. It is also the only role permitted to publish through a stability breach. A `BrandPlanner` builds budget scenarios, enrols channels into them with baseline spend, runs the optimizer and reverts an optimization, but can never approve a scenario — not even one another planner created. An `Auditor` is strictly read-only: it may read every collection, including superseded observations and stale reports, and may perform no mutation whatsoever, not even advancing the logical clock.

All six roles may issue every read operation under `/mmm`. The five non-`Auditor` roles may also set the logical clock.

Identity in this service is deliberately opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `DataEngineer`, `Modeler`, `ExperimentDesigner`, `AnalyticsLead`, `BrandPlanner` or `Auditor`, the accompanying `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it on rows where an audit trail matters — as `fitted_by_user_id` and `published_by_user_id` on a model run, as `designed_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on an experiment, and as `created_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a budget scenario, and as `created_by_user_id` on a calibration record — but it never compares `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`, and no resource is "owned" by an operator for access-control purposes. The only comparisons the system makes against `X-User-ID` are the two separation-of-duties checks described in §2.11, and both compare it against a previously stored opaque identifier rather than against a row's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Deterministic List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position within the ordered result set: its default is 0 and it may not be negative. A value outside either range is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, as is a value that is not a base-ten integer; a parameter that is omitted, or supplied as an empty or whitespace-only string, falls back to its default instead. Every other query parameter named in this document is a plain equality filter on the field it names: its value is never validated against that field's declared enum, so an unrecognised value simply matches no rows and yields an empty page, and an empty-string filter value is ignored altogether. When a list endpoint responds, the `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the size of the full filtered result set before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

List results are always ordered by `created_at` ascending. Because analytical writes frequently land inside the same millisecond, each collection also declares a deterministic tie-break that applies when two rows share `created_at` exactly. Markets tie-break on ascending `market_code`; media channels on ascending `channel_code`; geographic cells on ascending `cell_code`. Spend observations tie-break on ascending `period_start_date` and then on the ascending `channel_code` of the referenced channel; sales observations, which name no channel at all, tie-break on ascending `period_start_date` alone. Channel contributions and budget allocations tie-break on the ascending `channel_code` of the channel they describe. Data windows tie-break on ascending `window_start_date`, experiments on ascending `start_date`, budget scenarios on ascending `planning_start_date`, and model runs, calibration records and reconciliation reports on ascending `created_at`. A row's `id` is never a sort key, never a tie-break, and never an input to any allocation or evaluation order anywhere in this service.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every persisted resource in this service carries four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and is never supplied by a client. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 timestamps in UTC with exactly three fractional-second digits; `created_at` is fixed at insertion and `updated_at` is refreshed on every mutation of the row. Both are read from real server time rather than from the logical clock of §2.9, so advancing the clock never moves either of them. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by one on every mutation of that row, including mutations caused indirectly by a cascade fired from another resource. This applies uniformly to markets, media channels, geographic cells, data windows, spend observations, sales observations, model runs, channel contributions, experiments, calibration records, budget scenarios, budget allocations and reconciliation reports; there is no entity in this domain that omits `updated_at` or `version`.

The `version` counter is not cosmetic. It is the serialization point for the two optimistic-concurrency rules in §2.7, and because cascades bump it, a client that read a model run before an unrelated calibration record was filed against that run will hold a stale number and will be told so rather than silently overwriting work it never saw.

Creating a resource returns HTTP 201. Every other successful operation — reads, revisions and the action endpoints that drive state machines — returns HTTP 200. Operations that create or modify a resource return the created or updated resource in `data`.

### 2.5. Nested Path Resolution and Body-Level Referential Integrity

The URL topology of this service is flat. Every business path is either `/mmm/<collection>`, or `/mmm/<collection>/{id}`, or `/mmm/<collection>/{id}/<action>`, so at most one path parameter is ever present and it is always spelled `{id}`. Parent-to-child navigation is performed by filtering a list endpoint, never by walking a nested URL: a market's channels are found by filtering the media-channel list on `market_id`, a run's contributions by filtering the channel-contribution list on `model_run_id`, and an experiment's enlisted cells by filtering the geographic-cell list on `assigned_experiment_id`. Channel contributions in particular are only ever produced as a side effect of fitting a run, so the filtered list is their sole discovery channel.

Path parameters are resolved before any body is examined, left to right, and an unresolvable identifier yields the 404 belonging to that collection: `ERR_MARKET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEDIA_CHANNEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GEO_CELL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DATA_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SPEND_OBSERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SALES_OBSERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MODEL_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHANNEL_CONTRIBUTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXPERIMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CALIBRATION_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUDGET_SCENARIO_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUDGET_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_RECONCILIATION_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`. Foreign keys carried in a request body are resolved after the body's shape has been validated, and an unresolvable body reference yields the same collection-specific 404 — a `market_id` that names nothing gives `ERR_MARKET_NOT_FOUND`, a `channel_id`, an element of `included_channel_ids` or a channel named inside a platform line gives `ERR_MEDIA_CHANNEL_NOT_FOUND`, an element of `treatment_cell_ids` or `control_cell_ids` gives `ERR_GEO_CELL_NOT_FOUND`, a `data_window_id` gives `ERR_DATA_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, a `model_run_id` gives `ERR_MODEL_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, an `experiment_id` gives `ERR_EXPERIMENT_NOT_FOUND`, and a `budget_scenario_id` gives `ERR_BUDGET_SCENARIO_NOT_FOUND`.

Unknown fields in a request body are ignored rather than rejected. A body that fails to parse, omits a required field, carries an enum value outside its declared set, carries a numeric value outside its declared range, or carries a date that is not an ISO 8601 UTC-midnight instant is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). A field present with an explicit JSON `null` is treated exactly as if it had been omitted: a required field so supplied is reported as missing, and an optional one falls back to its declared default. An absent body — and a body that is literally JSON `null` — is treated as an empty object, while a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object is itself `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A date-valued field accepts either a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` date or a full ISO 8601 instant at UTC midnight; an instant carrying a non-zero time-of-day component, or any UTC offset other than zero, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and whichever of those wire shapes the client supplied is the one echoed back verbatim on every later read of the row. A request naming a path this document does not define, or using a method that path does not support, is likewise answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, but carrying HTTP 404 or HTTP 405 respectively rather than 400.

### 2.6. Decimal Discipline and Rounding

All arithmetic in this service is carried in exact decimal arithmetic. A value is rounded only at the points a rule explicitly calls for rounding, and every such rounding is half-up — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero. Square roots are evaluated to twelve decimal places before being consumed by any further step. Intermediate quantities are never truncated to the precision of the field they eventually feed.

Because the domain mixes money, unit counts, percentages and ratios, the precision of each published quantity is fixed per field rather than globally. Monetary amounts are integer cents throughout; there is no fractional cent anywhere in the system, and every cent-valued computed field is rounded half-up to a whole cent. Contribution and projection quantities expressed in sales units carry four decimal places. Percentages — holdout share, lag completeness, holdout error, measured lift and its interval, per-line and weighted variance — carry two decimal places. Average return on investment, calibration multipliers, stability scores and the experiment power quantities carry four decimal places. Marginal return on investment carries six decimal places, and the synthetic-control weight carries six decimal places. Every one of these quantities is carried on the wire as a JSON number rather than as a quoted string, so its declared scale governs the rounding applied before serialisation rather than the literal digits a JSON encoder prints.

Two rounding *orders* are load-bearing and are stated explicitly where they arise, because a plausible alternative order produces a different published number: the per-period saturation terms that make up a channel's contribution are summed exactly and rounded once at the end, whereas the per-period terms that make up a holdout prediction are each rounded to four decimals before being summed; and per-line reconciliation variance percentages are rounded to two decimals before being revenue-weighted, never weighted first and rounded once.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Contended Aggregates

Two write operations in this service are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because in both cases a second writer working from the same read would silently supersede the first writer's row without either party noticing.

Filing a spend observation requires `expected_data_window_version` in the request body. This field is not optional. It must carry the `version` of the parent data window as the client last observed it, expressed as an integer of zero or more; a value that is missing, non-integer or negative is a shape failure answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than a version mismatch. If that value does not equal the window's current `version`, the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DATA_WINDOW_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is persisted. The requirement holds on every spend-observation create, including the very first observation filed for a channel-period where no incumbent exists to supersede. Every accepted spend-observation write increments the parent window's `version`, and the response echoes the post-write value in the observation's `data_window_version_after`, so a client filing a series of observations can chain its writes without re-reading the window between them.

Filing a calibration record requires `expected_model_run_version` in the request body, likewise non-optional and likewise an integer of zero or more, carrying the `version` of the parent model run as the client last observed it. A value that does not equal the run's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MODEL_RUN_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no record is created. Every accepted calibration record increments the run's `version`, and the response echoes the post-write value in the record's `model_run_version_after` for the same chaining reason. Because a calibration record also drives the run's calibration dimension forward, the version bump that this rule observes is the same bump the calibration cascade produces.

No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field. The action endpoints that drive state machines are guarded by state preconditions rather than by version matching, and a client that invokes one from the wrong state receives that entity's invalid-state error instead.

### 2.8. Market Data Posture

Each market carries a computed `data_posture` that gates the analytical operations of everything beneath it. It takes one of three values — `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` — and it is recomputed on every write that touches one of the market's data windows or model runs: sealing a window, reopening a window, fitting a run, publishing a run, recalibrating a run, filing a calibration record, and accepting an experiment readout.

The three tests are evaluated in a fixed order and the first one that matches determines the posture. First: if any data window belonging to the market is in `REOPENED` status, the posture is `FROZEN`. Second: if the market has no model run whose `publication_status` is `PUBLISHED`, or that published run's `calibration_status` is `STALE`, or the market has no data window in `SEALED` status, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. A newly registered market has no windows and no runs and therefore lands on `RESTRICTED`, which is why `RESTRICTED` is the create-time default rather than `OPEN`.

Four operations consult the posture. Locking an experiment design, publishing a model run and filing a reconciliation report all require that the posture is not `FROZEN`; running the budget optimizer requires the stricter condition that the posture is exactly `OPEN`. In all four cases the rejection is `ERR_MARKET_POSTURE_BLOCKED` (HTTP 409). A market also publishes two convenience projections alongside its posture: `published_model_run_id`, which resolves to the `id` of the single run of that market whose `publication_status` is `PUBLISHED` and is null when no such run exists, and the population aggregates described in §3.1.

### 2.9. The Logical Clock

Every temporal rule in this service — whether a window has elapsed, whether an experiment may start or read out, whether a washout quarantine has expired — reads a server-side logical clock rather than wall-clock time. At startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must not fall back to real wall-clock time before any client has set it.

The clock is advanced through a dedicated test-driving endpoint, `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, whose body carries a single required field `current_utc` holding an ISO 8601 instant at zero UTC offset — a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` date is read as that day's midnight, while a missing field or a non-zero offset is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and whose successful response carries that one field alone in `data`, echoing the accepted instant exactly as it was supplied. The clock only moves forward: a request naming an instant earlier than the clock's current value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND` (HTTP 422). Advancing the clock changes no stored row and fires no cascade — it simply changes the answer that temporal comparisons give from that point on. All roles except `Auditor` may advance the clock; an `Auditor` attempting it receives `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

### 2.10. Cascading Invalidation, Recomputation and Conservation

Analytical artefacts in this system form a dependency chain — window, run, contributions, scenario, report — and when a link near the head of that chain changes, everything downstream of it is marked as no longer trustworthy in the same request. Cascades are immediate and synchronous; there is no deferred sweep and no eventual consistency. Every row a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, exactly as if it had been mutated directly.

Two cascade heads exist on the data side. Reopening a sealed window is the deeper of the two: it walks from the window to the runs trained on it, from those runs to their contribution rows, from those contributions to the budget scenarios that consumed them, and then — separately, as a consequence of the market's posture changing — to the remaining optimized scenarios of the whole market. Publishing a run is the other: it walks from the newly published run to the incumbent it displaces, from that incumbent to its contribution rows and its current reconciliation reports, and from those contributions to the scenarios built on them. On the experiment side, accepting a readout walks from the accepted experiment to the market's published run, from that run to its current reconciliation reports and the market posture, and from the posture to the market's optimized scenarios. Each of these chains is spelled out step by step in §3, including which rows are deliberately left untouched.

Two conserved quantities are maintained across the whole system, each with an operation that breaks it and an operation that restores it. The first is fiscal: for every budget scenario, the sum of `allocated_total_cents`, `reallocation_fee_cents` and `unallocated_cents` equals `total_budget_cents` at every observable moment from the scenario's first allocation row onwards — a freshly opened scenario reports all three at 0 against a positive budget, since nothing has been enrolled yet. The optimizer is what redistributes those three components, and reverting an optimization restores them to their pre-optimization values with no residual drift. The second is population: a market's `held_out_population` always equals the summed `population` of its geographic cells whose `assignment_state` is `TREATMENT`, `CONTROL` or `WASHOUT`, and it returns to zero only once every experiment of the market has been closed *and* the market has been swept. Enlisting cells into an experiment is what breaks the quantity away from zero; releasing an experiment's cells alone does not restore it, because a released cell sits in `WASHOUT` and is still held out. Only the market washout sweep gives the population back.

### 2.11. Separation of Duties

Two decisions in this service may not be made by the operator who set them up, and both checks compare opaque identifiers rather than roles. Accepting or rejecting an experiment readout requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the `designed_by_user_id` recorded when the experiment was drafted. Approving a budget scenario requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the `created_by_user_id` recorded when the scenario was opened. In either case a match yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

Because the comparison is on the stored opaque identifier, the same human acting under a different role is still blocked, and a different human acting under the same role is allowed. The roles that may perform these decisions are legitimately able to perform both halves of the workflow — an `AnalyticsLead` can publish and approve, an `ExperimentDesigner` can design any experiment — so the constraint has to be per-operator rather than per-role to have any force at all.

### 2.12. Validation Precedence

When a single request violates more than one rule, the response carries the error belonging to the highest-precedence rule that the request violates. The precedence ladder is fixed for the whole service and runs as follows.

Authentication comes first: missing or empty headers give `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, then an unknown or unpermitted role gives `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Existence comes next, in three steps: path identifiers are resolved left to right, then the request body's shape, required fields, enum membership, numeric ranges and date formats are checked, then the foreign keys carried in the body are resolved. After existence comes the separation-of-duties comparison, then optimistic-concurrency version matching, then the target resource's own state precondition, then cross-entity state preconditions that reach into a related row.

Below the state layer sit the safety gates — the market posture check first, then the experiment power gate — followed by referential-coherence checks (whether a referenced channel or cell belongs to the right market, whether a design type matches the action, whether a required set of child rows exists, whether the supplied platform lines match the run's contributions), then aggregate and boundary constraints (holdout share, conversion counts against arm populations, lift significance, redesign limits, scoped uniqueness, non-negative baseline, calibration-multiplier range, model stability), then temporal constraints (channel-window occupancy, window overlap, elapsed windows, experiment start and end, period alignment, clock monotonicity), then completeness constraints (window completeness, lag identifiability, spend floors against budget), and finally the fiscal checks (market budget ceiling, then baseline-versus-budget reconciliation).

Three specific collisions are worth stating outright because each is reachable by a single well-formed request that breaks both rules at once. Opening a budget scenario against a run that is not published *and* with a budget above the market's ceiling returns `ERR_MODEL_RUN_NOT_PUBLISHED`, not `ERR_BUDGET_CEILING_EXCEEDED` — the run-state check outranks the fiscal ceiling. Locking an experiment design that is both underpowered *and* over the holdout-share ceiling returns `ERR_EXPERIMENT_UNDERPOWERED`, not `ERR_HOLDOUT_SHARE_EXCEEDED`. Fitting a run where an included channel has no usable spend observations *and* whose window is too short for the declared lag structure returns `ERR_NO_SPEND_OBSERVATIONS`, not `ERR_LAG_WINDOW_INCOMPLETE`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Market Foundations, Media Channels and the Geographic Cell Pool

A `DataEngineer` begins by registering a **brand market** — the unit of analysis for everything that follows. Registration supplies a `market_code`, which must be an uppercase snake-case identifier of three to thirty-two characters beginning with a letter and composed thereafter of uppercase letters, digits and underscores, and which must be unique across every market in the system; a `name` between one and one hundred twenty characters; a `total_population` of at least one thousand, which serves as the denominator of every holdout-share rule; an `annual_budget_ceiling_cents` of zero or more, which is the hard fiscal ceiling no single budget scenario may exceed; and optionally a `currency_code`, three uppercase letters, defaulting to `USD`. The currency code is the reporting currency for every cent-denominated field on every row belonging to that market. A second market claiming an existing `market_code` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. A freshly registered market reports `celled_population` of 0, `held_out_population` of 0, `held_out_population_pct` of 0.00 and a `data_posture` of `RESTRICTED`, since it has neither a sealed window nor a published run.

Two population aggregates ride on the market row and are maintained by the cell lifecycle. `celled_population` is the summed `population` of every geographic cell belonging to the market, regardless of assignment. `held_out_population` is the summed `population` of only those cells whose `assignment_state` is `TREATMENT`, `CONTROL` or `WASHOUT`. Alongside it, `held_out_population_pct` is reported as one hundred multiplied by `held_out_population`, divided by `total_population`, rounded half-up to two decimal places; this percentage is for human reporting only and is never the quantity a holdout-share rule compares against. Markets can be listed and filtered by `market_code` or `data_posture`.

Each market then declares its **media channels**. A channel carries the `market_id` it belongs to and a `channel_code` following the same uppercase pattern as a market code — three to thirty-two characters, letter-initial — but required to be unique only within its market; a duplicate within one market is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`, while the same code in a different market is perfectly legal. The `channel_type` records the media family and must be one of `SEARCH`, `SOCIAL`, `VIDEO`, `DISPLAY`, `OOH`, `TV` or `RETAIL_MEDIA`; no behaviour anywhere in the system keys off this value, and it exists purely for documentation and filtering.

The remainder of a channel's declaration is the response curve the model will fit through. `adstock_decay_rate` is the geometric carryover weight applied per lag period; it must be at least 0.00 and at most 0.95 and must carry at most two decimal places — a value with fewer places, an integer `0` included, is accepted, while a third decimal place is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `adstock_max_lag_periods` is the highest lag included in the carryover sum, at least 0 and at most 8 — a channel declaring 8 admits lags zero through eight inclusive. `saturation_half_point_cents` must be strictly greater than zero and is the per-period adstocked spend level at which the channel's response reaches half of its ceiling. `response_ceiling_units` is the per-period asymptotic incremental unit response and must be zero or more. Two contractual bounds complete the row: `min_spend_cents`, the per-planning-horizon spend floor the optimizer must honour, at least zero; and `max_spend_cents`, the per-planning-horizon ceiling, which must be at least as large as `min_spend_cents`. Finally, `status` defaults to `ACTIVE` and must be one of `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED` or `RETIRED`.

Channel `status` carries three distinct meanings that surface in three different places. An `ACTIVE` channel may participate in a model fit and may absorb budget. A `PAUSED` channel keeps all of its history and may still participate in a fit, but the budget optimizer skips it. A `RETIRED` channel does neither: a fit whose inclusion list contains one is refused outright with `ERR_CHANNEL_RETIRED`, and a retired channel may not hold a budget allocation row at all. A `DataEngineer` may revise a channel later, supplying any subset of `status`, `adstock_decay_rate`, `adstock_max_lag_periods`, `saturation_half_point_cents`, `response_ceiling_units`, `min_spend_cents` and `max_spend_cents`. Whichever subset arrives, the resulting row must still satisfy `max_spend_cents` at least `min_spend_cents`; the check is applied to the merged values, so raising a floor above an unchanged ceiling is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Such a revision is never retroactive — it bumps the channel's `version` and changes what future fits and future optimizations read, but no model run that has already been fitted is altered by it. The revision is unconditionally a mutation: a body that changes nothing, or that supplies no revisable field at all, still refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version`. Channels can be listed filtered by `market_id`, by `channel_code` or by `status`.

Geographic experimentation needs a pool of divisible geography, so a `DataEngineer` also populates the market with **geographic cells**. A cell carries its `market_id`, a `cell_code` of two to sixteen characters beginning with an uppercase letter and continuing with uppercase letters, digits or underscores, unique within the market, and a `population` strictly greater than zero. A cell's population is immutable once created — the whole holdout-share arithmetic depends on it being a stable denominator — and a repeated `cell_code` within a market is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. Adding a cell adds its population to the market's `celled_population` and bumps the market's `version`. A new cell starts with `assignment_state` `UNASSIGNED`, a null `assigned_experiment_id` and a null `washout_until_date`. Cells can be listed filtered by `market_id`, by `assignment_state`, by `cell_code`, or by `assigned_experiment_id` — the last of which is the only way to discover which cells a given experiment consumed, since cell assignment happens as a cascade rather than through a per-cell write.

The cell lifecycle is cyclic: `UNASSIGNED` cells are enlisted into `TREATMENT` or `CONTROL` when an experiment assigns its arms, both of those states move to `WASHOUT` when the experiment releases its cells, and `WASHOUT` returns to `UNASSIGNED` only through the **market washout sweep**. That sweep is an operational checkpoint run by an `ExperimentDesigner` against a whole market. It examines every cell of the market in `WASHOUT` and, for each one whose quarantine has elapsed — that is, whose `washout_until_date` is at or before the current logical clock — sets `assignment_state` back to `UNASSIGNED`, clears `assigned_experiment_id` to null, clears `washout_until_date` to null, and subtracts that cell's `population` from the market's `held_out_population`. Cells whose quarantine has not yet elapsed are skipped silently: they are left exactly as they were and their presence is not an error, so one sweep call routinely applies to some cells and not others. Afterwards the market's `held_out_population` and `held_out_population_pct` are recomputed and the market's `version` is incremented; when the last held cell clears, the two aggregates return to 0 and 0.00 respectively. A swept cell is fully available again and may be enlisted by a later experiment.

### 3.2. Assembling a Training Window: Observations, Restatements and Completeness

Modelling starts with a **data window** — a bounded stretch of calendar time, chopped into equal periods, into which spend and sales are filed. A `DataEngineer` opens a window by naming its `market_id`, its `window_start_date` and `window_end_date` (both ISO 8601 dates at UTC midnight, with the end strictly after the start), and its `granularity`, which must be either `WEEKLY` or `DAILY`. The date range is half-open: the start date is inside the window and the end date is not. From that range and the granularity the system derives `expected_period_count` as the number of days between the two dates divided by seven when the granularity is `WEEKLY`, or by one when it is `DAILY`. That division must come out exact — a window spanning a number of days that is not a whole multiple of its period length is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, because a partial trailing period would silently distort every per-period average downstream. Both cross-field tests — the end strictly after the start, and the exact division — count as body-shape checks and therefore run before `market_id` is resolved, so a request that breaks one of them while also naming a market that does not exist answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_MARKET_NOT_FOUND`.

Windows of the same market must not collide, and the collision test is deliberately stricter than the window's own semantics. For conflict detection only, a window occupies the **closed** interval running from `window_start_date` through `window_end_date` inclusive — the exclusive period boundary is still treated as occupied — and no buffer is added on either side. The consequence is that two back-to-back windows whose endpoints merely touch do conflict: a window ending 2026-08-31 blocks a new window starting 2026-08-31, and the new window must start 2026-09-01 or later. Granularity is part of the conflict key rather than a tie-break, so two windows of the same market with different granularities may overlap as freely as the analyst likes — a weekly window and a daily window over the same quarter are both legitimate views of the same period. A genuine collision is `ERR_DATA_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, and the incumbent's `status` plays no part in the test: an `OPEN`, a `SEALED` and a `REOPENED` window all occupy their intervals alike. A new window opens in `OPEN` status with `sales_period_count`, `spend_observation_count` and `lag_completeness_pct` all at zero, `reopen_count` at 0 and `reopen_reason` null. Windows may be listed filtered by `market_id`, by `status` or by `granularity`.

An `OPEN` window accepts observation writes, and so does a `REOPENED` one; a `SEALED` window accepts neither a new observation nor a restatement, and an attempt to file into one is `ERR_DATA_WINDOW_SEALED`. Both kinds of observation must land on a legal period boundary. The supplied `period_start_date` has to fall inside the **half-open** range beginning at `window_start_date` and ending strictly before `window_end_date`, and it has to sit on the window's period grid: for a `WEEKLY` window that means an exact whole multiple of seven days after `window_start_date`, and for a `DAILY` window any day inside the range qualifies. A date outside the half-open range or off the grid is `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ALIGNED`. Note the deliberate asymmetry with the previous paragraph — period placement uses the half-open range, while window-versus-window conflict detection uses the closed range.

**Media spend** is filed one channel-period at a time. The body carries the `data_window_id` and the `channel_id` that spent the money, the `period_start_date` of the period being reported, a `spend_cents` amount of zero or more, and, optionally, `impressions` of zero or more, a `price_index` and a `distribution_index`. The two indices are the external control variables the specification may declare: `price_index` is the period's own-price index relative to a base of 100.00, and `distribution_index` is the period's relative distribution and availability index. Neither index is range-bounded or precision-bounded — any finite decimal is accepted, negatives included — and both default to null when omitted. Delivered `impressions` are recorded for reporting and enter no calculation anywhere.

Restatement is a first-class operation rather than an edit. There is no uniqueness constraint on the combination of window, channel and period; instead, when a live observation already exists for that same combination, filing a new one **supersedes** the incumbent. The incumbent's `superseded_by_id` is set to the new row's identifier, and the new row's `is_restatement` becomes true. When no incumbent existed, the new row's `is_restatement` is false and its `superseded_by_id` stays null. A superseded row is never deleted — it remains readable individually, and appears in list results only when `include_superseded` is requested, which is otherwise excluded by default. Every accepted spend write recomputes the parent window's `spend_observation_count` over its non-superseded rows only, increments the window's `version`, and stamps the observation's `data_window_version_after` with the post-write value. Spend observations may be listed filtered by `data_window_id`, by `channel_id`, by `superseded_by_id` — which walks the supersession chain forward from an incumbent — and by `include_superseded`. That boolean accepts `true`, `1` or `yes` for true and `false`, `0` or `no` for false, case-insensitively, and any other value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; and because a row carrying a non-null `superseded_by_id` is by definition superseded, the `superseded_by_id` filter returns anything only when `include_superseded` is also true.

**Sales** are filed at market level, one row per period, and here uniqueness *is* enforced: a period of a window may carry at most one sales row, and a second attempt is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The body carries the `data_window_id`, the `period_start_date`, a `units_sold` count strictly greater than zero — it is the denominator of every holdout-error term, so zero would be undefined — and a `revenue_cents` amount of zero or more, optionally accompanied by a `distribution_pct` between 0.00 and 100.00 and a `macro_index` expressing the external macroeconomic control relative to a base of 100.00; `macro_index` itself carries no range or precision constraint, and both optional fields are stored as null when omitted. On acceptance the row's `avg_price_cents` is computed as `revenue_cents` divided by `units_sold`, rounded half-up to a whole cent; it is reporting only. The parent window then increases its `sales_period_count` by one and recomputes `lag_completeness_pct` as one hundred multiplied by `sales_period_count`, divided by `expected_period_count`, rounded half-up to two decimal places — which, because each period admits at most one sales row, is exactly the share of the window's expected periods that carry a sales observation. The window's `version` is incremented. Sales observations may be listed filtered by `data_window_id`.

### 3.3. Sealing, Reopening, and the Four-Hop Staleness Cascade

A window has to be **sealed** before it can train anything, and sealing is where data-collection discipline is enforced. A `DataEngineer` may seal a window that is in `OPEN` or `REOPENED` status; sealing one that is already `SEALED` is `ERR_DATA_WINDOW_INVALID_STATE`. Three conditions must hold. The window must have elapsed — the current logical clock must be at or after `window_end_date`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_DATA_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`. The window must carry at least four sales periods, that is a `sales_period_count` of four or more. And it must be complete: `lag_completeness_pct` must be exactly 100.00, meaning every expected period of the window has its sales row. A failure of either of the last two conditions is `ERR_DATA_WINDOW_INCOMPLETE`. Both the `OPEN → SEALED` and the `REOPENED → SEALED` transitions apply exactly these three checks.

Sealing sets `status` to `SEALED`, bumps the window's `version`, and recomputes the market's posture. That recomputation is why sealing matters beyond the window itself: a market's first `SEALED` window can be what lifts the posture from `RESTRICTED` towards `OPEN`, and sealing the *last* `REOPENED` window of a market is what lifts the posture out of `FROZEN`. When the posture returns to `OPEN` as a result, every budget scenario of that market sitting in `FROZEN` thaws back to `DRAFT`, with each of its allocation rows' `allocated_cents` restored to its `baseline_spend_cents` and the scenario's `reallocation_fee_cents` reset to 0.

**Reopening** is the acknowledged data-integrity breach, and it is only available from `SEALED` — reopening an `OPEN` or already-`REOPENED` window is `ERR_DATA_WINDOW_INVALID_STATE`. The request carries a single field, `reason`, which must be present as a string but is not required to be non-empty, and whose text is stored on the window as `reopen_reason`, replacing any justification recorded by a previous reopen. The transition sets `status` to `REOPENED`, increases `reopen_count` by one, and bumps `version`. From there a four-hop cascade unwinds everything the window's data supported.

The first hop reaches the runs: every model run whose training window is this one and whose `fit_status` is `FITTED` moves to `fit_status` `STALE_FIT` and simultaneously has its `validation_status` reset to `UNVALIDATED`, because a backtest against retracted data proves nothing. The second hop, caused by the first, reaches those runs' decomposition rows: every `ChannelContribution` row belonging to each affected run has `is_current` set to false. The third hop, caused by the second, reaches planning: every budget scenario whose model run is one of those runs and whose status is `OPTIMIZED` or `APPROVED` moves to `INVALIDATED`. Separately, because a `REOPENED` window now exists, the market's posture is recomputed to `FROZEN`, and the fourth hop follows from that posture change: every remaining budget scenario of the *whole market* whose status is `OPTIMIZED` moves to `FROZEN`. Scenarios in `DRAFT` are deliberately untouched by that fourth hop — a draft holds no optimizer output to unwind, so it stays writable, and it is the scenario's own later optimize attempt that surfaces `ERR_MARKET_POSTURE_BLOCKED`.

### 3.4. Specifying and Fitting a Model Run

A `Modeler` specifies a **model run** before fitting anything; creation is pure declaration and performs no computation. The specification names the `market_id` being modelled and the `data_window_id` that will train it, and the window must belong to the same market as the run, or the create is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. That coherence test, the `holdout_periods` comparison described below and the channel-membership test are all evaluated only after the three foreign keys have resolved — so a request that also names a market or a window that does not exist answers that collection's 404 first — and among themselves they fire in that order, the window-versus-market test first and the channel check last. It carries `included_channel_ids`, an array of between one and eight distinct media-channel identifiers, every one of which must reference a channel whose market matches the run's market — a channel from another market is `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_IN_MARKET`. The order of that array is irrelevant to everything downstream: the fit always processes channels in ascending `channel_code`, never in array order and never in identifier order.

Three declarative fields describe how the specification treats the world outside media. `control_variables` is an optional array drawn from `PRICE_INDEX`, `DISTRIBUTION_INDEX`, `MACRO_INDEX` and `SEASONALITY_WEEK_OF_YEAR`, recorded as an empty array when omitted; `seasonality_mode` is one of `NONE`, `WEEK_OF_YEAR` or `MONTH_OF_YEAR` and defaults to `WEEK_OF_YEAR`. Both are recorded as part of the specification and echoed back verbatim on every read of the run, and neither alters the contribution arithmetic — they document the analyst's intent so that two runs over the same window are distinguishable in the audit trail. The fourth field is not declarative at all: `carryover_attribution_mode` selects which of two genuinely different decompositions the fit will use, must be stated explicitly on creation, and must be either `EFFECT_PERIOD` or `SPEND_PERIOD`; it carries no default at all, so a specification that omits it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Finally, `holdout_periods` is optional, defaults to 1, must be at least 1, and must be strictly less than the window's `expected_period_count` — a run cannot hold out its entire training set.

A newly created run reports `fit_status` `PENDING`, `validation_status` `UNVALIDATED`, `calibration_status` `NOT_REQUIRED` and `publication_status` `UNPUBLISHED`, with `refresh_generation` already sitting at 1 and `unit_price_cents`, `modeled_sales_units`, `baseline_sales_units`, `observation_period_count`, `holdout_mape_pct` and `stability_score` all null until a fit or a publication supplies them. Those four dimensions evolve independently on different triggers, and no movement in one implies a movement in another except where this document says so. `previous_published_run_id` stays null until the run itself publishes. Runs may be listed filtered by `market_id`, by `data_window_id`, by `previous_published_run_id` — which walks the publication succession chain — and by any of the four status dimensions: `fit_status`, `validation_status`, `calibration_status` and `publication_status`. Filtering on `publication_status` is also how a client resolves a market's current published run.

Fitting is the `Modeler`'s core operation and it moves `fit_status` from `PENDING` to `FITTED`, or from `STALE_FIT` back to `FITTED`. Invoking it on a run that is already `FITTED` is `ERR_MODEL_RUN_INVALID_STATE`. Five preconditions guard it. The training window must be in `SEALED` status; a run whose window is `OPEN` or `REOPENED` is refused with `ERR_DATA_WINDOW_NOT_SEALED`. Every included channel must have a `status` of either `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED`; a `RETIRED` channel in the inclusion list is `ERR_CHANNEL_RETIRED`, and it is rejected rather than skipped. Every included channel must still belong to the run's market, or `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_IN_MARKET`. The window's `expected_period_count` must be strictly greater than the largest `adstock_max_lag_periods` among the included channels — otherwise the declared lag structure cannot be identified from the data available — and a failure here is `ERR_LAG_WINDOW_INCOMPLETE`. And every included channel must have at least one non-superseded spend observation inside the window, or `ERR_NO_SPEND_OBSERVATIONS`. When a single request violates both of the last two, the missing-spend failure is the one reported.

Before touching any channel, the fit computes three run-level aggregates over **all** sales observations of the training window, holdout periods included. `observation_period_count` is the number of sales observations found. `modeled_sales_units` is the sum of their `units_sold`. `unit_price_cents` is the sum of their `revenue_cents` divided by the sum of their `units_sold`, rounded half-up to a whole cent. The holdout split affects only the backtest described in §3.5; it never narrows the population used for the price or the sales total.

#### Adstock: turning raw spend into carried-over spend

For each included channel the fit first builds an **adstocked spend series** over the window's periods, taken in ascending `period_start_date` order. The raw spend for a period is the sum of `spend_cents` across that channel's spend observations whose `period_start_date` equals that period's start and whose `superseded_by_id` is null; superseded restatements are excluded entirely, and a period in which the channel filed nothing contributes raw spend of zero rather than being skipped. Two symbols matter: the channel's `adstock_decay_rate`, the per-lag carryover weight, and its `adstock_max_lag_periods`, the highest lag admitted.

Under `EFFECT_PERIOD` the carryover lands on *later* periods. The adstocked value for a period is that period's own raw spend plus, for each lag from one up to the smaller of the channel's maximum lag and the number of periods that precede this one, the raw spend of the period that many positions earlier multiplied by the decay rate raised to the power of that lag; the whole sum is then rounded half-up to a whole cent. Because no spend from before the window is imputed, the earliest periods of the series are truncated — they simply have fewer lags available to draw on — and the series total therefore comes out smaller than the un-truncated geometric total would be.

Under `SPEND_PERIOD` the entire carryover tail is credited back to the period that spent the money. A single geometric gain is formed once for the channel as the sum, over every lag from zero through the channel's maximum lag inclusive, of the decay rate raised to that lag. Each period's adstocked value is then that period's own raw spend multiplied by this gain, rounded half-up to a whole cent. No period is truncated, so for identical spend the `SPEND_PERIOD` series total is at least as large as the `EFFECT_PERIOD` one, and strictly larger whenever the channel's decay rate and maximum lag are both non-zero.

Whichever variant applies, the resulting integer-cent series is stored on the channel's decomposition row as `adstocked_spend_series`, in ascending period order, and `mean_adstocked_spend_cents` is stored as the sum of that series divided by the window's period count, rounded half-up to a whole cent. The variant actually used is recorded on the row as `attribution_mode_used`, copied from the run's `carryover_attribution_mode` as it stood when the row was produced, so a row remains interpretable even after the run's specification is superseded by a later one.

#### Saturation, contribution and the baseline identity

Adstocked spend is converted into units through a saturating response curve applied period by period. For a period, the channel's contribution in units is its `response_ceiling_units` — which is a *per-period* asymptote — multiplied by that period's adstocked spend, divided by the sum of that period's adstocked spend and the channel's `saturation_half_point_cents`; a period whose adstocked spend is zero contributes zero units. The channel's `contribution_units` is then the exact sum of those per-period values across the whole window, rounded half-up to four decimal places **once**, over the exact sum. The per-period terms are deliberately not pre-rounded here; rounding each period first and summing afterwards is a different number and is not what this rule specifies.

Once every included channel has a decomposition row, the run closes its accounting identity. `baseline_sales_units` is `modeled_sales_units` minus the sum of every included channel's `contribution_units`, rounded half-up to four decimal places, so that the channel contributions plus the baseline reconstruct the modelled sales total exactly. If that subtraction would come out negative — the channels between them claiming more units than the market actually sold — the fit is rejected with `ERR_MODEL_BASELINE_NEGATIVE` and **no** row is written: neither the contribution rows — an incumbent row set left over from a `STALE_FIT` run included, which is deleted only once the identity has closed — nor the run's three aggregates and its `baseline_sales_units`, nor any change to the run's four status dimensions, survives the attempt.

#### Return on investment and the confidence interval

Each channel's decomposition row also carries its economics. `attributed_spend_cents` is the sum of `spend_cents` over that channel's non-superseded spend observations inside the training window — raw spend, never the adstocked figure. `contribution_revenue_cents` is `contribution_units` multiplied by the run's `unit_price_cents`, rounded half-up to a whole cent. `average_roi` is `contribution_revenue_cents` divided by `attributed_spend_cents`, rounded half-up to four decimal places, and is reported as exactly 0.0000 when `attributed_spend_cents` is zero rather than being left undefined.

`marginal_roi` answers a different question — what the next cent of spend buys — and is evaluated at the mean of the adstocked series. It is the run's `unit_price_cents` multiplied by the channel's `response_ceiling_units`, multiplied by the channel's `saturation_half_point_cents`, divided by the square of the quantity formed by adding `mean_adstocked_spend_cents` to `saturation_half_point_cents`, rounded half-up to six decimal places. Because the raw spend denominator behind `average_roi` is identical under both attribution modes while the numerator moves, the two variants' returns remain directly comparable and their divergence is attributable entirely to the decomposition.

Finally, each row carries an interval whose width depends only on how many periods the run observed. `ci_half_width_pct` is 40.0 divided by the square root of the run's `observation_period_count`, rounded half-up to two decimal places; it is therefore identical for every channel of a run. `ci_low_units` is `contribution_units` multiplied by one minus one-hundredth of `ci_half_width_pct`, rounded half-up to four decimals, and `ci_high_units` is `contribution_units` multiplied by one plus one-hundredth of `ci_half_width_pct`, rounded half-up to four decimals.

A successful fit writes one decomposition row per included channel, created in ascending `channel_code` order, each with `is_current` true and with `calibrated_contribution_units` and `calibrated_contribution_revenue_cents` initially equal to their uncalibrated counterparts — that equality holds for as long as the run's `calibration_status` remains `NOT_REQUIRED` or `PENDING_CALIBRATION`. The run then sets `fit_status` to `FITTED`, resets `validation_status` to `UNVALIDATED`, clears `holdout_mape_pct` to null, records the acting operator in `fitted_by_user_id`, and increments its `version`. Re-fitting a run that had gone `STALE_FIT` deletes the previous decomposition row set outright and replaces it with a freshly computed set whose `is_current` is true. A row's `is_current` becomes false whenever its producing run's `fit_status` leaves `FITTED` or its `publication_status` becomes `SUPERSEDED`. Decomposition rows are readable individually and listable filtered by `model_run_id`, by `channel_id`, by `is_current` — which reads `true`, `1` or `yes`, case-insensitively and after trimming, as true and treats every other non-empty value as false rather than rejecting it — and by `attribution_mode_used`.

### 3.5. Holdout Validation, Publication and Refresh Cadence

A fitted run is not yet trustworthy. Validation is a separate `Modeler` operation over the `validation_status` dimension, available only while `fit_status` is `FITTED`; attempting it on a run that is still `PENDING` or has gone `STALE_FIT` is `ERR_MODEL_RUN_INVALID_STATE`. It always succeeds as an operation and always moves `validation_status` out of `UNVALIDATED`, and it may be invoked again on a run whose validation has already branched — the figure is simply recomputed in place, and neither the run's `publication_status` nor its `calibration_status` is consulted — but it branches: the same request reaches `HOLDOUT_PASSED` or `HOLDOUT_FAILED` depending on the measured error, and only a run in `HOLDOUT_PASSED` may ever be published.

The backtest scores the run over the trailing periods of its window — specifically the last `holdout_periods` periods when the window's periods are taken in ascending `period_start_date` order. First, a flat per-period baseline is formed as `baseline_sales_units` divided by `observation_period_count`, rounded half-up to four decimal places — the baseline as the run currently reports it, so a recalibration that has already re-closed the identity moves the backtest with it. Then, for each holdout period, a prediction is formed as that per-period baseline plus the sum, across every included channel, of that channel's saturated per-period unit contribution for that period — re-derived from the run's `is_current` decomposition rows, out of the `adstocked_spend_series` and the curve parameters the fit itself recorded, so a channel revised after the fit cannot move the backtest — **each rounded half-up to four decimal places before being added**, with the resulting total rounded half-up to four decimal places. The actual figure for the period is the `units_sold` on its sales observation. `holdout_mape_pct` is then one hundred multiplied by the sum, over the holdout periods, of the absolute difference between prediction and actual divided by that period's actual, all divided by the number of holdout periods, rounded half-up to two decimal places.

Note the deliberate contrast with §3.4: the contribution rule sums exact per-period terms and rounds once at the end, whereas the backtest rounds each per-period term to four decimals before summing. The two rules use different rounding points on purpose, and using one rule's rounding point in the other produces the wrong published figure. Note also that the run was fitted on every period including the holdout, so the backtest is knowingly in-sample-contaminated — which is precisely why the acceptance ceiling is set as tight as it is. `validation_status` becomes `HOLDOUT_PASSED` when `holdout_mape_pct` is at most 12.00 and `HOLDOUT_FAILED` when it is above 12.00. Either way `holdout_mape_pct` is stored and the run's `version` increments.

Publication is an `AnalyticsLead` decision and the only writer of the `publication_status` dimension. It requires the run's `fit_status` to be `FITTED`, or `ERR_MODEL_RUN_INVALID_STATE` — which is also the answer when the run is already `PUBLISHED` or has been `SUPERSEDED`. It requires `validation_status` to be exactly `HOLDOUT_PASSED`; an `UNVALIDATED` or `HOLDOUT_FAILED` run is refused with `ERR_HOLDOUT_NOT_PASSED`. It requires the market's `data_posture` not to be `FROZEN`, or `ERR_MARKET_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. And it requires the run to be stable relative to the incumbent it would displace.

Stability is measured on the ROI vector rather than on the fit statistics, because what stakeholders actually consume from a refresh is the channel ranking. Before writing the publication status, the system locates the market's current run with `publication_status` `PUBLISHED` — there is at most one. When there is none, this is the market's first publication: `stability_score` is recorded as exactly 1.0000, `refresh_generation` is 1, and the stability gate is skipped entirely. When an incumbent exists, the comparison runs over the **union** of the two runs' channels, taken in ascending `channel_code` order, using only decomposition rows whose `is_current` is true, and a channel that appears in one run but not the other contributes an `average_roi` of 0 for the run it is missing from. A drift total is formed as the sum, across that union, of the absolute difference between the candidate's `average_roi` and the incumbent's `average_roi` for that channel. A base total is formed as the sum of the incumbent's `average_roi` values across the incumbent's own channels. When that base total is zero the division is not attempted at all: `stability_score` is exactly 1.0000 if the drift total is also zero and exactly 0.0000 otherwise. Otherwise `stability_score` is one minus the drift total divided by the base total, floored at zero so it never goes negative, rounded half-up to four decimal places; and `refresh_generation` is the incumbent's `refresh_generation` plus one.

If `stability_score` falls below 0.6000 the publication is refused with `ERR_MODEL_UNSTABLE`, unless the request body carries `override_stability` set to the boolean `true` — the single privilege that lets an `AnalyticsLead` publish through a stability breach, and the only route past that error. That field is optional and boolean-typed: any non-boolean value there is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, raised on the body's shape before any state, posture or stability check runs, while `false` or an omitted field leaves the gate armed. The union rule has a deliberate consequence worth internalising: dropping a channel from the specification is penalised far harder than changing the attribution mode, because a dropped channel's ROI falls all the way to zero inside the union while a re-decomposed channel's ROI merely moves.

A successful publication records `previous_published_run_id` as the identifier of the market's outgoing published run — null when there was none, and null forever for the market's first publication — sets `publication_status` to `PUBLISHED`, records the deciding operator in `published_by_user_id`, and increments `version`. Then the succession cascade fires. Its first hop moves the market's previously published run to `publication_status` `SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal: no operation returns a run from `SUPERSEDED`. Its second hop, caused by the first, sets `is_current` to false on every decomposition row of the superseded run and moves that run's reconciliation reports whose `status` is `CURRENT` to `STALE`. Its third hop, caused by the second, moves every budget scenario built on the superseded run whose status is `OPTIMIZED` or `APPROVED` to `INVALIDATED`. Finally the market's `data_posture` and `published_model_run_id` are recomputed.

Both terminal states reached by this cascade preserve evidence rather than erasing it. A reconciliation report that has gone `STALE` freezes every computed variance figure at the value it was filed with and cannot be refreshed in place — a fresh report must be filed against the new run. A budget scenario that has gone `INVALIDATED` keeps its computed allocation and projection figures exactly as the optimizer left them, deliberately not reset, so that the plan that was almost approved stays auditable; optimizing, reverting and approving all answer `ERR_BUDGET_SCENARIO_INVALID_STATE` from there.

### 3.6. The Incrementality Experiment Lifecycle

An **experiment** is the ground truth against which the model is corrected, and it moves through a long, partly cyclic lifecycle: `DRAFT → DESIGN_LOCKED → CELLS_ASSIGNED → RUNNING → READ_OUT`, then a fork to either `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED`, and from those two a single further step that reaches `CLOSED` from the accept arm and loops all the way back to `DRAFT` from the reject arm. `CLOSED` is terminal; every action invoked against a closed experiment answers `ERR_EXPERIMENT_INVALID_STATE`, as does any action invoked from a state that has no such transition.

An `ExperimentDesigner` drafts an experiment against exactly one channel of one market. The body carries the `market_id`, the `channel_id` whose incrementality is being measured — which must belong to that market, or `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_IN_MARKET` — and a `design_type` of either `GEO_HOLDOUT`, which consumes real geographic cell population, or `AUDIENCE_SPLIT`, which declares its arm sizes directly and consumes no cells at all. The `readout_method` fixes the estimator up front and must be either `DIFF_IN_DIFF` or `SYNTHETIC_CONTROL`. The in-market period runs from an inclusive `start_date` to an exclusive `end_date` strictly after it — that comparison is a body-shape check, so a draft that breaks it while also naming a market or channel that does not exist answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than either 404 — both UTC-midnight dates, and a required `washout_days` between 0 and 60 inclusive appends an adstock-carryover quarantine to the tail of that period. Three statistical parameters complete the draft: `minimum_detectable_effect_pct`, the relative lift the design must be able to detect, strictly greater than 0.0 and at most 100.0 with one decimal place; `baseline_conversion_rate`, the pre-test per-person conversion rate to four decimal places and strictly inside the open interval between 0.0000 and 1.0000; and `planned_treatment_population` and `planned_control_population`, both strictly positive. The draft is created in `DRAFT` with `redesign_count` 0, with `power_index`, `computed_power`, `synthetic_control_weight`, the four conversion counts and the four lift fields all null, with `treatment_population` and `control_population` at 0, and with the acting operator captured in `designed_by_user_id` — the comparand every later decision on this experiment is measured against. Experiments may be listed filtered by `market_id`, `channel_id`, `status`, `design_type` and `readout_method`.

#### Locking the design

Locking freezes the design and is where three independent gates fire in a fixed order. The experiment must be in `DRAFT`, or `ERR_EXPERIMENT_INVALID_STATE`, and the market's `data_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, or `ERR_MARKET_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. Then the **power gate** runs, and it runs before the holdout-share ceiling — an experiment that is simultaneously underpowered and over the share ceiling answers `ERR_EXPERIMENT_UNDERPOWERED`, never `ERR_HOLDOUT_SHARE_EXCEEDED`. Last of the three comes the channel-window occupancy test.

Power is computed in two steps. A standardised effect size, `power_index`, is formed as one-hundredth of `minimum_detectable_effect_pct`, multiplied by the square root of the following quantity: the smaller of `planned_treatment_population` and `planned_control_population`, multiplied by `baseline_conversion_rate`, divided by twice the difference between one and `baseline_conversion_rate`. The result is rounded half-up to four decimal places. That index is then read against a step table to produce `computed_power`, rounded to four decimal places, where each bound is **strictly** less-than and the first matching row wins: a `power_index` below 1.0000 gives power 0.2000; below 1.6000 gives 0.4500; below 2.0000 gives 0.6500; below 2.5000 gives 0.7500; below 2.8000 gives 0.8000; below 3.2400 gives 0.9000; and anything at or above 3.2400 gives 0.9500. The strictness of those bounds is observable at the acceptance threshold: a `power_index` of exactly 2.5000 lands on the 0.8000 row and passes, while 2.4999 lands on 0.7500 and fails. `computed_power` must be at least 0.80, or the lock is refused with `ERR_EXPERIMENT_UNDERPOWERED` (HTTP 422).

The **holdout-share ceiling** protects the market from measuring itself into irrelevance. For a `GEO_HOLDOUT` design, the sum of the market's current `held_out_population`, the `planned_treatment_population` and the `planned_control_population`, multiplied by one hundred, must be at most twenty multiplied by the market's `total_population` — in other words the market may never plan to hold out more than twenty percent of its addressable people. The comparison is carried out in exact integers on both sides and never through the two-decimal `held_out_population_pct`, which exists only for reporting. A breach is `ERR_HOLDOUT_SHARE_EXCEEDED`.

The **occupancy test** prevents two live tests from contaminating each other on the same channel. No other experiment on the same market and channel whose status is `DESIGN_LOCKED`, `CELLS_ASSIGNED`, `RUNNING`, `READ_OUT` or `ACCEPTED` may occupy an overlapping interval. A `DRAFT` sibling reserves nothing — it may never lock — and a `REJECTED` or `CLOSED` one has already given the channel back, so only locked-or-later experiments occupy time. The test is deliberately asymmetric: the candidate occupies the half-open interval from its own `start_date` up to but not including its `end_date`, while each incumbent occupies the half-open interval from its `start_date` up to but not including its `end_date` advanced by its own `washout_days` — the quarantine buffer is appended to the incumbent's tail only, never to the candidate's. Touching is therefore allowed: a candidate whose `start_date` equals an incumbent's `end_date` plus that incumbent's `washout_days` is fine, while one day earlier is a conflict and answers `ERR_EXPERIMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

A successful lock freezes `power_index` and `computed_power` onto the row, sets `status` to `DESIGN_LOCKED` and increments `version`. For an `AUDIENCE_SPLIT` design it also copies `planned_treatment_population` into `treatment_population` and `planned_control_population` into `control_population`, because that design has nothing further to enlist. A `GEO_HOLDOUT` design leaves both realised populations at 0 until it consumes actual cells.

#### Enlisting cells and going in market

Cell assignment applies only to `GEO_HOLDOUT` designs and is the operation that breaks the market's population conservation away from zero. The body carries two arrays, `treatment_cell_ids` and `control_cell_ids`, both of which must be present — each may legitimately be empty, and neither is checked for repeated identifiers. Every listed cell must resolve, or `ERR_GEO_CELL_NOT_FOUND`, and that resolution runs first: only then must the experiment be in `DESIGN_LOCKED`, or `ERR_EXPERIMENT_INVALID_STATE`. Every listed cell must currently be `UNASSIGNED`; a cell already sitting in `TREATMENT`, `CONTROL` or `WASHOUT` is `ERR_GEO_CELL_UNAVAILABLE`, and that availability check outranks both the design-type check and the market-membership check, so a request that names an occupied cell on an `AUDIENCE_SPLIT` experiment answers `ERR_GEO_CELL_UNAVAILABLE`. Below it, the design must actually be `GEO_HOLDOUT` or the answer is `ERR_DESIGN_TYPE_MISMATCH`, and every listed cell must belong to the experiment's market or the answer is `ERR_GEO_CELL_NOT_IN_MARKET`. Finally the holdout-share ceiling is re-evaluated with real numbers: the market's current `held_out_population` plus the summed `population` of the *distinct* cells named in this request, multiplied by one hundred, must be at most twenty multiplied by `total_population`, again compared as exact integers, or `ERR_HOLDOUT_SHARE_EXCEEDED`.

On success, each cell named in `treatment_cell_ids` moves to `assignment_state` `TREATMENT` and each cell named in `control_cell_ids` moves to `CONTROL`, both recording the experiment in their `assigned_experiment_id`; each such cell adds its `population` to the market's `held_out_population`. The experiment's `treatment_population` becomes the summed population of its treatment cells and its `control_population` the summed population of its control cells, each array summed exactly as it was supplied — so an identifier repeated inside one array counts twice towards that arm, and a cell named in both arrays counts towards both arms while the control assignment is the one that sticks on the cell itself. The market's `held_out_population` and `held_out_population_pct` are recomputed, the experiment moves to `CELLS_ASSIGNED`, and its `version` increments.

Going in market is a branching join. A `GEO_HOLDOUT` experiment reaches `RUNNING` from `CELLS_ASSIGNED`; attempting it directly from `DESIGN_LOCKED` is refused with `ERR_CELLS_NOT_ASSIGNED`, because the arms do not exist yet. An `AUDIENCE_SPLIT` experiment reaches `RUNNING` straight from `DESIGN_LOCKED`, and that step (re)asserts `treatment_population` from `planned_treatment_population` and `control_population` from `planned_control_population`. Either way the current logical clock must have reached `start_date`; earlier than that is `ERR_EXPERIMENT_NOT_STARTED`. Success sets `status` to `RUNNING` and increments `version`.

#### Reading out the lift

Reading out is available only from `RUNNING`, or `ERR_EXPERIMENT_INVALID_STATE`, and only once the logical clock has reached `end_date`; earlier than that is `ERR_EXPERIMENT_NOT_ELAPSED`. The body carries four observed counts: `treatment_conversions` and `control_conversions` from the in-test period, and `pre_treatment_conversions` and `pre_control_conversions` from the pre-period. `treatment_conversions` and `pre_treatment_conversions` must be zero or more; `control_conversions` and `pre_control_conversions` must be strictly greater than zero, since each of them ends up in a denominator. Any of the four exceeding its arm's realised population is `ERR_CONVERSIONS_EXCEED_POPULATION`, and that boundary check outranks the elapsed test, so a premature read-out that also carries an oversized count answers `ERR_CONVERSIONS_EXCEED_POPULATION` rather than `ERR_EXPERIMENT_NOT_ELAPSED`.

Four rates are formed from the counts and the realised arm populations: the post-period treatment rate is `treatment_conversions` divided by `treatment_population`; the post-period control rate is `control_conversions` divided by `control_population`; the pre-period treatment rate is `pre_treatment_conversions` divided by `treatment_population`; and the pre-period control rate is `pre_control_conversions` divided by `control_population`.

Under `DIFF_IN_DIFF`, the difference-in-differences quantity is the change in the treatment rate from pre-period to post-period, minus the change in the control rate over the same two periods. `measured_lift_pct` is one hundred multiplied by that quantity, divided by the **observed** post-period control rate, rounded half-up to two decimal places. This estimator removes the control arm's own pre-to-post drift, and `synthetic_control_weight` is not derived at all under it — the field stays null.

Under `SYNTHETIC_CONTROL`, the two pre-period counts are consumed only to derive a weight. `synthetic_control_weight` is the pre-period treatment rate divided by the pre-period control rate, rounded half-up to six decimal places. The control arm's post-period rate is then rescaled by that weight — the published six-decimal figure, never the unrounded quotient — and `measured_lift_pct` is one hundred multiplied by the difference between the treatment post-period rate and the rescaled control rate, divided by the **rescaled** control rate, rounded half-up to two decimal places. This estimator never forms a pre-to-post difference at all. `synthetic_control_weight` is never accepted from a request body under either method; supplying it is an unknown field and is ignored.

The interval half-width is identical under both estimators, because the observed post-period control rate is its denominator in both cases. A pooled conversion rate is formed as the sum of `treatment_conversions` and `control_conversions`, divided by the sum of `treatment_population` and `control_population`. A standard error is formed as the square root of the following product: the pooled rate, multiplied by one minus the pooled rate, multiplied by the sum of the reciprocal of `treatment_population` and the reciprocal of `control_population`; that square root is evaluated to twelve decimal places. `lift_ci_half_width_pct` is one hundred multiplied by 1.96, multiplied by that standard error, divided by the observed post-period control rate, rounded half-up to two decimal places. `lift_ci_low_pct` is `measured_lift_pct` minus `lift_ci_half_width_pct`, rounded half-up to two decimals, and `lift_ci_high_pct` is `measured_lift_pct` plus `lift_ci_half_width_pct`, rounded half-up to two decimals. Because the two estimators share the half-width exactly, any divergence between them is attributable to the estimator rather than to the interval. The four counts are stored, the four lift fields are set, `status` becomes `READ_OUT` and `version` increments.

#### The decision fork and giving the population back

An `AnalyticsLead` decides a readout, and the two arms of that fork are deliberately asymmetric in their consequences. Both arms require the experiment to be in `READ_OUT`, and both require that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the experiment's `designed_by_user_id` — a match is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and because the duty check outranks the state check, an operator who both designed the experiment and invokes a decision from the wrong state is told about the duty conflict rather than about the state.

Accepting additionally requires that the market has a model run with `publication_status` `PUBLISHED` to calibrate — there is nothing to correct otherwise, and the absence answers `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_MODEL_RUN`. It also requires that the measured effect is significantly positive: `lift_ci_low_pct` must be strictly greater than 0.00, and a readout whose interval straddles or touches zero is refused with `ERR_LIFT_NOT_SIGNIFICANT`. On success the experiment moves to `ACCEPTED`, records the deciding operator in `decided_by_user_id`, and increments `version` — and then a three-hop cascade reprices the market's whole analytical posture. Its first hop moves the market's published run, if its `calibration_status` is `CALIBRATED`, to `calibration_status` `STALE`: newly accepted experimental truth invalidates the correction that was derived from older truth. Its second hop, caused by the first, moves that run's reconciliation reports whose `status` is `CURRENT` to `STALE` and recomputes the market's `data_posture`, which falls from `OPEN` to `RESTRICTED` because the published run's calibration is now stale. Its third hop, caused by the posture leaving `OPEN`, moves every budget scenario of the market whose status is `OPTIMIZED` to `FROZEN`, resetting each of those scenarios' allocation rows to `allocated_cents` equal to `baseline_spend_cents`, `shifted_cents` 0, `constraint_binding` `NONE` and `projected_contribution_units` 0.0000, zeroing the scenario's `reallocation_fee_cents`, `shifted_total_cents` and both projection totals, and recomputing `allocated_total_cents` as the restored baseline sum with `unallocated_cents` as whatever of `total_budget_cents` that sum leaves over. Scenarios in `DRAFT` are untouched by that hop.

Rejecting requires a `rejection_reason` in the body, present and non-empty — and unlike a window's reopen `reason`, a whitespace-only string counts as empty here — or `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. On success the experiment moves to `REJECTED`, stores the text in `rejection_reason`, records `decided_by_user_id`, and increments `version` — and fires **no** cascade whatsoever. The published run keeps its `calibration_status`, the market keeps its `data_posture`, every reconciliation report stays `CURRENT` and every budget scenario stays exactly where it was. That contrast with the accept arm is intentional and is the difference a reader should be able to predict from the two states alone.

Releasing the cells is the last step of the lifecycle, and it is a single operation whose destination is chosen by the **source state**, not by anything in the request. It is available from `ACCEPTED` and from `REJECTED`; from anywhere else it is `ERR_EXPERIMENT_INVALID_STATE`. In both cases every geographic cell whose `assigned_experiment_id` is this experiment and whose `assignment_state` is `TREATMENT` or `CONTROL` moves to `WASHOUT`, with `washout_until_date` set to the experiment's `end_date` advanced by its `washout_days` days and rendered in the same wire shape the experiment's `end_date` was supplied in. The market's `held_out_population` is explicitly **unchanged** by this step: a cell in `WASHOUT` is still held out, and the conserved population only returns to the market through the washout sweep described in §3.1.

From `ACCEPTED` the experiment lands on `CLOSED` and its `version` increments; that is the end of its life. From `REJECTED` it loops back to `DRAFT` for a redesign, but only while `redesign_count` is still below two — a third loop is refused with `ERR_REDESIGN_LIMIT_REACHED`, and that limit is tested before any cell is moved, so a refused loop leaves the cells, the market and the experiment exactly as they were. The loop increments `redesign_count`, clears `computed_power` and `power_index` back to null, clears all four conversion counts and all four lift fields — `measured_lift_pct`, `lift_ci_half_width_pct`, `lift_ci_low_pct` and `lift_ci_high_pct` — back to null, resets `treatment_population` and `control_population` to 0, and increments `version`. The design parameters supplied at draft time survive the loop, and so do `synthetic_control_weight`, `rejection_reason` and `decided_by_user_id`, which are deliberately left standing as filed; everything else the lifecycle computed does not.

### 3.7. Calibrating Model Attribution Against Experiment Truth

The point of running experiments is to correct the model, and the bridge between the two is the **calibration record** — a cross-aggregate row that binds one model run, one media channel and one experiment readout together. A `Modeler` files one by naming the `model_run_id`, the `channel_id` and the `experiment_id`, together with `expected_model_run_version` as described in §2.7. The channel must appear in the run's `included_channel_ids` and must be the same channel the experiment measured; a failure of either test is `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_IN_MARKET`, because a record cannot correct a channel the experiment never tested. The run must also still carry a decomposition row for that channel whose `is_current` is true — a run that has never been fitted, or whose rows have stopped being current, is `ERR_MODEL_RUN_INVALID_STATE`. The experiment supplies the ground truth, so it must have been read out — a record is filed against a readout that has reached `READ_OUT` or `ACCEPTED`, and any other experiment status is `ERR_EXPERIMENT_INVALID_STATE`, which is checked immediately after the version match and therefore outranks all three of the checks just described.

Filing the record derives the correction immediately. `experiment_lift_pct` is copied from the experiment's `measured_lift_pct` at the instant of filing and is never recomputed afterwards, so a later redesign of the experiment cannot retroactively move a record that has already been filed. The model's own view of the same quantity, `model_implied_lift_pct`, is computed from the run's current decomposition row for that channel — the one whose `is_current` is true — as one hundred multiplied by that row's `contribution_units`, divided by the difference between the run's `modeled_sales_units` and that same `contribution_units`, rounded half-up to four decimal places. In words: the model's implied lift is the channel's incremental units expressed as a percentage of everything the channel did *not* drive.

`calibration_multiplier` is then `experiment_lift_pct` divided by `model_implied_lift_pct`, rounded half-up to four decimal places, and constrained to lie within the closed range from 0.25 to 4.00. A raw quotient falling outside that range is not silently clamped into it: the record is refused with `ERR_CALIBRATION_MULTIPLIER_OUT_OF_RANGE` and nothing is created, on the reasoning that a discrepancy of more than fourfold in either direction indicates a broken specification rather than an attribution bias worth correcting. The same error answers the two degenerate cases in which the quotient cannot be formed at all: a run whose `modeled_sales_units` equals that row's `contribution_units`, leaving no residual to express the lift against, and a `model_implied_lift_pct` that comes out to exactly 0.0000. From the multiplier the record derives `bias_direction`: `OVER_ATTRIBUTED` when the multiplier is strictly below 0.95, `UNDER_ATTRIBUTED` when it is strictly above 1.05, and `ALIGNED` when it sits anywhere in the closed band between those two bounds inclusive. The alignment band is five hundredths on either side of one.

Filing a record has consequences beyond its own row. Any prior live record for the same run-and-channel pair has its `superseded` flag set to true, and superseded records are excluded from every downstream read — the recalibration step and every report see only the live record for a pair. The parent run's `calibration_status` moves to `PENDING_CALIBRATION`, which it does from `NOT_REQUIRED`, from `CALIBRATED`, from `STALE`, and from `PENDING_CALIBRATION` itself when a second channel's record arrives. The run's `version` increments — and this is exactly the bump the optimistic-concurrency rule for calibration records observes, which is why two records filed for the same pair from the same read cannot both succeed. The post-write value is echoed on the record as `model_run_version_after`, the acting operator is captured in `created_by_user_id`, and the market's `data_posture` is recomputed, since a published run whose calibration has moved may change what the market permits. Calibration records may be listed filtered by `model_run_id`, by `channel_id`, by `experiment_id`, by `superseded` — which takes the same `true`/`1`/`yes` and `false`/`0`/`no` vocabulary as `include_superseded`, any other value being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and by `bias_direction`.

**Recalibration** applies the live records to the run's decomposition rows and is a `Modeler` operation. It requires `fit_status` to be `FITTED`, or `ERR_MODEL_RUN_INVALID_STATE`, and requires `calibration_status` to be exactly `PENDING_CALIBRATION`; a run whose calibration is `NOT_REQUIRED`, already `CALIBRATED`, or `STALE` is refused with `ERR_CALIBRATION_NOT_PENDING`. The step walks the run's live records joined to its `is_current` decomposition rows in ascending `channel_code` order. For each channel that has a non-superseded record, `calibrated_contribution_units` becomes that channel's `contribution_units` multiplied by the record's `calibration_multiplier`, rounded half-up to four decimal places, and `calibrated_contribution_revenue_cents` becomes those calibrated units multiplied by the run's `unit_price_cents`, rounded half-up to a whole cent. For each channel that has no live record, both calibrated figures pass through unchanged: `calibrated_contribution_units` equals `contribution_units` and `calibrated_contribution_revenue_cents` equals `contribution_revenue_cents`. A decomposition row whose two calibrated figures both come out identical to the values it already carried is not counted as mutated at all — its `updated_at` and `version` stay where they were — so a pass-through channel, or one whose multiplier is exactly 1.0000, is left untouched while the rows that actually move are bumped.

The run then re-closes its accounting identity against the calibrated figures: `baseline_sales_units` becomes `modeled_sales_units` minus the sum, across every channel, of `calibrated_contribution_units`, rounded half-up to four decimal places. If that would be negative the whole recalibration is refused with `ERR_MODEL_BASELINE_NEGATIVE` and nothing at all is written. The consequence of the identity is worth stating plainly: units that the model over-credited to a channel are pushed **back into the baseline**, never discarded, so a downward correction on a channel raises the baseline by exactly the amount the channel loses. On success `calibration_status` becomes `CALIBRATED`, the run's `version` increments, and the market's `data_posture` is recomputed — a published run that has just become `CALIBRATED` is often what restores a market from `RESTRICTED` to `OPEN`.

The calibration dimension is cyclic. A `CALIBRATED` published run falls to `STALE` when a new experiment readout is accepted in its market, as described in §3.6, and a `STALE` run returns to `PENDING_CALIBRATION` as soon as the next record is filed against it, from where recalibration can run again. `NOT_REQUIRED` is simply the state of a run that no experiment has ever spoken about.

### 3.8. Budget Scenarios: Enrolment, the Two-Pass Waterfall and Approval

A **budget scenario** is where the model's response curves are turned into a spending plan. A `BrandPlanner` opens one by naming the `market_id` being planned and the `model_run_id` whose curves will drive the optimizer — the run must belong to the same market, or the create is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — together with a `total_budget_cents` strictly greater than zero, a `planning_start_date` and an exclusive `planning_end_date` strictly after it, and optionally a `reallocation_fee_pct` between 0.00 and 5.00 inclusive with two decimal places, defaulting to 0.00 when it is omitted. The referenced run must already be the market's published, fitted model: a run that is not both `PUBLISHED` and `FITTED` is refused with `ERR_MODEL_RUN_NOT_PUBLISHED`. Separately, `total_budget_cents` may not exceed the market's `annual_budget_ceiling_cents`, or `ERR_BUDGET_CEILING_EXCEEDED`. Those two checks collide on a single request often enough to be worth restating: the run-state check outranks the fiscal ceiling, so a request that breaks both is answered with `ERR_MODEL_RUN_NOT_PUBLISHED`.

The planning horizon is measured in the same periods the model was trained on. `planning_period_count` is derived as the length of the horizon divided by the period length of the referenced run's data window — seven days per period for a `WEEKLY` window, one day per period for a `DAILY` one — and that division must come out exact, or the create is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Both that exactness test and the same-market test above it are evaluated before the run-state and ceiling gates, so a request that breaks either of them while also naming a run that is not published answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_MODEL_RUN_NOT_PUBLISHED`. A newly created scenario is in `DRAFT` with `reallocation_fee_cents`, `allocated_total_cents`, `unallocated_cents` and `shifted_total_cents` all at 0, `projected_contribution_units` at 0.0000 and `projected_incremental_revenue_cents` at 0, and with the acting operator captured in `created_by_user_id` as the approval comparand. Because a draft has no allocation rows yet, those three cent totals are trivially zero at that moment; the conservation identity of §2.10 becomes meaningful as soon as rows exist and holds in every response thereafter. Scenarios may be listed filtered by `market_id`, by `model_run_id` and by `status`.

Channels are enrolled one at a time as **budget allocations**. Each carries the `budget_scenario_id`, the `channel_id`, and a `baseline_spend_cents` of zero or more representing the current plan for that channel over the horizon. A channel whose `status` is `RETIRED` may not hold an allocation row at all and is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_RETIRED`, a check that outranks both membership tests and the duplicate test below it; a channel that does not belong to the scenario's market, or that is absent from the referenced run's `included_channel_ids`, is `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_IN_MARKET`; and a second row for the same scenario-and-channel pair is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. Enrolment is not gated on the scenario's own status: a row may be added to an `OPTIMIZED`, `APPROVED`, `FROZEN` or `INVALIDATED` scenario exactly as it may to a `DRAFT` one. A new row starts with `allocated_cents` equal to its `baseline_spend_cents`, `shifted_cents` 0, `constraint_binding` `NONE` and `projected_contribution_units` 0.0000 — that is, an un-optimized scenario reports the plan it was handed. Enrolling a row is also a write to the scenario it joins: the scenario's `allocated_total_cents` is recomputed as the sum of its rows' `allocated_cents`, and its `unallocated_cents` as `total_budget_cents` minus `reallocation_fee_cents` minus that sum, so the conservation identity of §2.10 holds from the first row onwards and not only once the optimizer has run; `shifted_total_cents` and the two projection totals are left untouched by the enrolment. That recomputation is a mutation of the scenario row — refreshing its `updated_at` and incrementing its `version` — whenever it changes either figure, and leaves the row alone when it changes neither, as a row whose `baseline_spend_cents` is 0 does on any enrolment after the first — the very first enrolment always moves `unallocated_cents` off its create-time 0 and therefore always bumps the scenario. Allocation rows may be listed filtered by `budget_scenario_id`, by `channel_id` and by `constraint_binding`.

#### The optimizer

Optimizing is available only from `DRAFT`, or `ERR_BUDGET_SCENARIO_INVALID_STATE`. It further requires that the referenced run is still `PUBLISHED` and `FITTED`, or `ERR_MODEL_RUN_NOT_PUBLISHED`; that the market's `data_posture` is exactly `OPEN` — the strictest posture requirement anywhere in the service — or `ERR_MARKET_POSTURE_BLOCKED`; that at least one allocation row exists, or `ERR_NO_ALLOCATIONS`; that the sum of the enrolled channels' `min_spend_cents` is at most `total_budget_cents`, or `ERR_FLOORS_EXCEED_BUDGET`; and that the sum of the rows' `baseline_spend_cents` equals `total_budget_cents` exactly, or `ERR_BASELINE_BUDGET_MISMATCH`. Those five sit at different rungs of the precedence ladder, so a request breaking several of them is answered in that order: run state first, then posture, then the missing-rows check, then the floors check, then the baseline reconciliation. A refusal at any of those rungs persists nothing: the scenario and its allocation rows — cent buckets, `status`, `updated_at` and `version` alike — read afterwards exactly as they read before the request, and in particular an un-optimized scenario still reports its baseline plan rather than zeros.

The projection function the optimizer maximises is the same one it publishes. For a channel and a candidate horizon-level allocation, the per-period allocation is the candidate amount divided by `planning_period_count`; the projected units for the horizon are `planning_period_count` multiplied by the channel's `response_ceiling_units`, multiplied by that per-period allocation, divided by the sum of that per-period allocation and the channel's `saturation_half_point_cents`. The function is concave, which is what makes a greedy marginal walk correct.

The greedy walk itself takes a budget and proceeds as follows. Each channel is seeded at its own `min_spend_cents`. The remaining budget is the supplied budget minus the sum of those floors; if that remainder is negative the optimization is refused with `ERR_FLOORS_EXCEED_BUDGET`. Then, while the remaining budget is at least one hundred thousand cents — a one-thousand-unit-of-currency marginal step — the walk forms a candidate set of the channels for which adding one more step would keep the allocation at or below the channel's `max_spend_cents`. If that candidate set is empty the walk stops early and the remainder stays unspent. Otherwise it selects the candidate for which the projected-units gain from adding one step is largest — that gain is compared exactly, not at the four decimals the published projection carries — breaking ties by ascending `channel_code` and never by identifier or creation time, awards that channel one step, and reduces the remaining budget by one step. A `PAUSED` channel keeps its row and its history and is still seeded at its `min_spend_cents`, but is skipped by the optimizer's candidate selection; `PAUSED` is the only status the walk skips, so a channel retired after it was enrolled is walked exactly like an `ACTIVE` one.

The optimizer runs that walk **twice**, because the agency fee for churning a media plan has to be priced out of the same budget it churns. Pass one runs the greedy walk over the full `total_budget_cents`. The pass-one churn is the sum, across channels, of the absolute difference between each channel's pass-one allocation and its `baseline_spend_cents`, and `reallocation_fee_cents` is that churn multiplied by `reallocation_fee_pct`, divided by one hundred, rounded half-up to a whole cent. Pass two then runs the greedy walk again over `total_budget_cents` reduced by that fee; because the fee shrinks the budget the second walk seeds against, a floors total that fits `total_budget_cents` but not the fee-reduced figure surfaces `ERR_FLOORS_EXCEED_BUDGET` from that second pass, with nothing written. Critically, the fee is **pinned at its pass-one value** and is not recomputed from pass two's smaller churn: the fee prices the plan the optimizer first proposed, and recomputing it would make the two passes chase each other without converging.

Pass two's result is what gets published, and every enrolled allocation row is written — its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented — even when the waterfall leaves its figures exactly where they already stood. Each allocation row's `allocated_cents` becomes its pass-two figure; its `shifted_cents` becomes the absolute difference between that figure and its `baseline_spend_cents`; its `constraint_binding` becomes `FLOOR` when `allocated_cents` equals the channel's `min_spend_cents`, `CEILING` when it equals the channel's `max_spend_cents`, and `NONE` otherwise, with `FLOOR` winning when a channel's floor and ceiling are the same number; and its `projected_contribution_units` becomes the projection function evaluated at `allocated_cents`, rounded half-up to four decimal places. A `FLOOR` binding means only that the waterfall never chose that channel, not that a ceiling stopped it; a `CEILING` binding means the channel was removed from the candidate set and the surplus flowed to the next-best marginal gain, or — when every channel is capped — stayed unspent.

On the scenario, `allocated_total_cents` is the sum of the rows' `allocated_cents`; `shifted_total_cents` is the sum of the rows' `shifted_cents`, which is the pass-two churn and is reporting only; `unallocated_cents` is `total_budget_cents` minus `reallocation_fee_cents` minus `allocated_total_cents`; `projected_contribution_units` is the sum of the rows' `projected_contribution_units`; and `projected_incremental_revenue_cents` is that total multiplied by the referenced run's `unit_price_cents`, rounded half-up to a whole cent. The referenced run contributes nothing else to the optimizer — only its unit price. The scenario moves to `OPTIMIZED` and its `version` increments, and the identity that `allocated_total_cents` plus `reallocation_fee_cents` plus `unallocated_cents` equals `total_budget_cents` is directly checkable off the response.

#### Reverting, approving, freezing and invalidating

Reverting an optimization is the exact compensating action and is available only from `OPTIMIZED`, or `ERR_BUDGET_SCENARIO_INVALID_STATE`. It restores every allocation row — unconditionally, so each row's `version` increments even when its figures were already at baseline — to `allocated_cents` equal to `baseline_spend_cents`, `shifted_cents` 0, `constraint_binding` `NONE` and `projected_contribution_units` 0.0000; on the scenario it sets `reallocation_fee_cents`, `shifted_total_cents`, `unallocated_cents` and `projected_incremental_revenue_cents` to 0, `projected_contribution_units` to 0.0000, and `allocated_total_cents` to `total_budget_cents`; and it returns the scenario to `DRAFT` with its `version` incremented. Every side effect of the optimizer is undone with no residual drift, and the conservation identity still holds afterwards. The `DRAFT` and `OPTIMIZED` states therefore form a cycle a planner can walk as many times as it likes.

Approval is an `AnalyticsLead` decision available only from `OPTIMIZED`, or `ERR_BUDGET_SCENARIO_INVALID_STATE`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the scenario's `created_by_user_id`, or `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and exactly as on the experiment fork that duty comparison outranks the state check, so the scenario's own author invoking approval from `DRAFT` is told about the duty conflict rather than about the state. The referenced run must still be `PUBLISHED`, or `ERR_MODEL_RUN_NOT_PUBLISHED` — a plan cannot be approved against a model that has been displaced since the optimizer ran. And the run's `calibration_status` must be either `NOT_REQUIRED` or `CALIBRATED`; a run sitting in `PENDING_CALIBRATION` or `STALE` blocks approval with `ERR_CALIBRATION_STALE`, because a correction that has been filed but not applied means the published contributions are known to be wrong. On success the scenario becomes `APPROVED`, records `approved_by_user_id`, and increments `version`.

Two further transitions are driven entirely by the market rather than by a planner. When the market's `data_posture` leaves `OPEN`, every scenario of that market in `OPTIMIZED` moves to `FROZEN`, with its allocations reset to baseline exactly as a revert would have done; a `DRAFT` scenario is deliberately not frozen, since it holds no optimizer output to unwind. When the posture returns to `OPEN`, every `FROZEN` scenario of the market thaws back to `DRAFT`, its rows reset to baseline once more; both transitions rewrite every allocation row of the scenario they touch, so each row's `version` increments even when its figures do not move. And when the decomposition rows the scenario was built on stop being current — because the training window was reopened or the run was superseded — a scenario in `OPTIMIZED` or `APPROVED` moves to `INVALIDATED`, which is terminal.

### 3.9. Reconciling Model Attribution with Platform Reporting

The last obligation in the cycle is to face the stakeholders with both numbers side by side. An `AnalyticsLead` files a **reconciliation report** against a model run of the market — in practice its published one, though the run's `publication_status` is never actually inspected, so a fitted but still `UNPUBLISHED` run is an acceptable target — naming the `model_run_id`, an inclusive `reporting_period_start` and an exclusive `reporting_period_end` strictly after it, and a `platform_lines` array carrying at least one entry. Each entry of that array is an object carrying a `channel_id` and a `platform_revenue_cents` figure — what the platform itself claims it drove, a whole number of cents of either sign. An entry missing either key, an empty or non-array `platform_lines`, an entry that is not an object, and a `platform_revenue_cents` that is not a whole number are all `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and an entry naming a channel that does not exist is `ERR_MEDIA_CHANNEL_NOT_FOUND`.

The array must match the run's decomposition exactly: there must be precisely one line per decomposition row of the run whose `is_current` is true, and no line for any other channel. A missing channel, a duplicated channel, or a channel that has no current contribution row on that run all yield `ERR_PLATFORM_LINE_MISMATCH`. Filing is additionally gated on the run's `calibration_status` being `NOT_REQUIRED` or `CALIBRATED` — a report against a run whose calibration is `PENDING_CALIBRATION` or `STALE` is refused with `ERR_CALIBRATION_STALE` — and on the market's `data_posture` not being `FROZEN`, or `ERR_MARKET_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. Those two fire in that order, the calibration state outranking the posture gate, and both outrank the platform-line match. Only one report may exist per run and `reporting_period_start` pair; a second is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`, and that uniqueness check is the last of the four, so a request that both duplicates a period and mismatches the lines answers `ERR_PLATFORM_LINE_MISMATCH`.

Which model figure the report compares against is recorded on the row as `basis`. When the run's `calibration_status` is `CALIBRATED` the `basis` is `CALIBRATED` and each channel's model figure is its `calibrated_contribution_revenue_cents`; in every other admissible case the `basis` is `RAW` and the model figure is its `contribution_revenue_cents`. Recording the basis on the report is what makes two reports on the same run comparable after a calibration has landed.

The lines are processed in ascending `channel_code` order of the channel they name. For each line, the model figure described above becomes that line's `model_cents`; `variance_cents` is the line's `platform_revenue_cents` minus its `model_cents`, so a platform claiming more than the model gives a positive variance; and `variance_pct` is one hundred multiplied by `variance_cents`, divided by `model_cents`, **rounded half-up to two decimal places at this point** — or reported as exactly 0.00 when that line's `model_cents` is zero, rather than dividing by zero. The resulting objects are stored on the report as `variance_lines`, each carrying `channel_id`, `model_cents`, `platform_revenue_cents`, `variance_cents` and `variance_pct`, in that ascending channel order, while the submitted entries are echoed back on the row as `platform_lines`, in the order they were supplied and reduced to their `channel_id` and `platform_revenue_cents` keys.

Three totals are then summed across the lines: `platform_reported_revenue_cents` from the platform figures, `model_attributed_revenue_cents` from the model figures, and `total_variance_cents` from the per-line variances. All three are exact integer cent arithmetic with no rounding step at all.

The headline number, `weighted_variance_pct`, is a revenue-weighted mean of the **already-rounded** per-line percentages: the sum, across lines, of each line's `variance_pct` multiplied by that line's `model_cents`, divided by `model_attributed_revenue_cents`, rounded half-up to two decimal places; when that denominator is zero the quotient is not formed and `weighted_variance_pct` is 0.00. This order is mandated and the alternative — weighting the unrounded percentages and rounding once at the end — is explicitly rejected. The two orders are not merely stylistically different; they land on different sides of the materiality threshold on realistic figures, and the flag is what stakeholders read. That flag, `attribution_gap_flag`, is `MATERIAL` when the absolute value of `weighted_variance_pct` is at least 15.00 and `IMMATERIAL` when it is strictly below 15.00.

A new report is created in `status` `CURRENT`. It flips to `STALE` when its run becomes `SUPERSEDED` or when its run's `calibration_status` becomes `STALE`, and `STALE` is terminal — every computed variance field freezes at its filed value and a fresh report must be created against the new run rather than refreshing the old one in place. Reports may be listed filtered by `model_run_id`, by `status`, by `attribution_gap_flag` and by `basis`, and each one is readable individually with its full per-line variance breakdown.
