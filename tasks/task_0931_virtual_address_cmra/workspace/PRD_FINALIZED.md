# Virtual-Address & Mail-Scanning (CMRA) API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A Commercial Mail Receiving Agency (CMRA) rents mailbox addresses to people and companies who cannot, or do not wish to, publish a residential address. Behind that simple product sits an unusually dense compliance surface: the United States Postal Service requires a signed and notarized Form 1583 plus two classes of identification for every named recipient before a single piece of mail may be handed over; state registered-agent statutes impose their own multi-year retention on service-of-process documents; and the commercial side of the business — scanning, forwarding, shredding, check deposit, plan tiers and overage — runs on top of the same physical pieces of paper that compliance governs. The result is a system in which one clerical act (verifying a driver's licence, opening a compliance review, letting a clock tick past midnight) can legitimately move a dozen rows across five entities at once.

This document specifies the backend API for that business. It is written for an engineering team building the service from scratch, and it is deliberately complete on the points where a reasonable implementer would otherwise guess: every constant carries its value, every threshold states whether its boundary is inclusive or exclusive, every rounding rule states the order in which it is applied, every state transition names its full side-effect list, and the request-validation ladder is given rung by rung in the exact order the server must evaluate it. Where the underlying business rules genuinely admit two readings, this document says so rather than inventing a third.

Three structural ideas run through everything below. First, **parallel status axes**: a piece of mail simultaneously carries a custody axis, a storage-economics axis and a metering axis, and each advances on its own schedule. Second, **global write gates**: a suspended mailbox and an incomplete Form 1583 each block a set of operations, and the two sets overlap without being identical, so the order in which they are checked is part of the contract. Third, **a logical clock**: nothing in this system reads wall-clock time; time advances only when an administrator moves it, which makes every expiry, every storage day and every notification deadline exactly reproducible.

The API is mounted at the server root; every path in this document is absolute.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — without exception, including reads — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the six role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Test material sometimes refers to this situation as an "Anonymous" caller; there is no such role, and any request that would be described that way lands on `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. If the headers are well-formed but the acting role is not among the roles the target endpoint permits, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `CmraOperator`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `Notary`, `BoxHolder` and `Recipient`, spelled exactly so.

A `PlatformAdmin` is the platform operator. It configures the plan catalogue and the carrier acceptance rules, runs billing-cycle closes, attaches and deactivates the registered-agent add-on, and drives the logical clock. A `CmraOperator` is counter staff at the facility: it opens mailboxes, manages the roster and identification documents, logs and matches inbound mail, runs the scanning line, executes dispositions, handles checks and finalizes cancellations. A `ComplianceOfficer` is the USPS compliance reviewer: it accepts and rejects Form 1583 applications and opens, suspends and resolves compliance reviews. A `Notary` is a commissioned notary who witnesses the Form 1583 signature and holds no other authority whatsoever. A `BoxHolder` is the mailbox account holder, and a `Recipient` is a roster member acting for itself.

Identity binding differs by role, and the difference is load-bearing:

- When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `CmraOperator`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `Notary`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition says so — as `opened_by_user_id` and `resolved_by_user_id` on a compliance review, and as `notary_user_id` and `accepted_by_user_id` on a Form 1583 — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `BoxHolder`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting mailbox. Ownership checks compare the mailbox the request targets — directly through a `/boxes/{id}/…` path, or indirectly through the `box_id` of the resource named in the path — to `X-User-ID`. A mismatch is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `Recipient`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting roster member. A `Recipient` may read only pieces whose `matched_recipient_id` equals its own id; anything else is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Segregation of duties is enforced on two operations. A `ComplianceOfficer` whose `X-User-ID` equals the review's `opened_by_user_id` may not resolve that review, and a `ComplianceOfficer` whose `X-User-ID` equals a form's `notary_user_id` may not accept that form. Both violations are `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or an `offset` below 0, is a validation failure and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The one endpoint that states otherwise is the scan-job listing for a piece, which is returned in ascending `split_index` order. Because scan jobs of one split group are created in ascending `split_index` order, the two orderings coincide for a single group; across groups, `created_at` ascending is the outer ordering and `split_index` ascending the inner one. Where two rows share the same `created_at`, the tie is broken by `id` ascending so that paging is stable.

The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before `limit` and `offset` are applied; `has_more` is true when the sum of `offset` and the number of rows actually returned is less than `total_count`, and false otherwise.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Version Ledger

Every persisted resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix, and a `created_at`. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` and `version`. Three resource types deliberately do not: a plan tier, a carrier rule and a usage-ledger entry carry only `id` and `created_at`, and their JSON bodies contain neither `updated_at` nor `version`. All other resources — mailboxes, roster members, Form 1583 applications, identification documents, mail items, scan jobs, disposition requests, forward shipments, check deposits, deposit batches, registered-agent services, compliance reviews, billing cycles and cancellation requests — carry all four.

`version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created. **It is bumped exactly once per row per request that mutates that row**, never once per side effect. A single request that touches a mailbox three times over — say, a compliance suspension that changes `service_status`, `compliance_status` and `suspended_reason` — advances `Box.version` by exactly 1, not by 3. A request that mutates several different rows bumps each of those rows once. The response body of a mutating operation reflects the **post-bump** value: after a successful write, the `version` in the returned payload is the value a subsequent optimistic-concurrency check must be given. `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock on any request that bumps `version`, and is equal to `created_at` on a freshly created row.

**Timestamp rendering.** Every server-minted instant — `created_at`, `updated_at`, `activated_at`, `notarized_at`, `verified_at`, `received_at`, `accepted_at`, `released_at`, `notified_at`, `notification_due_at`, `executed_at`, `shipped_at`, `closed_at`, `resolved_at`, `deactivated_at`, `form_1583_accepted_at` and the envelope's `api_request_timestamp` — is rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`, for example `2026-05-01T00:00:00.000Z`. This rendering is used unconditionally and does not vary with the format in which an instant was supplied to the clock endpoint: an administrator who sets the clock with the string `2026-06-15T09:00:00Z` will still see every instant minted afterwards rendered with three fractional digits and a `Z`. Caller-supplied **calendar dates** are a different matter: fields such as `business_date` and an identification document's `expires_on` are plain `YYYY-MM-DD` strings and are stored and echoed back byte-for-byte exactly as supplied. Server-computed calendar dates — `cycle_anchor_date`, `accepted_on`, a form's `expires_on`, `period_start`, `period_end`, `requested_on`, `notice_end_date`, `final_forward_deadline`, `abandonment_date` and `destruction_eligible_on` — are likewise plain `YYYY-MM-DD` strings with no time component. Nullable instants and nullable dates are rendered as JSON `null`, never as an empty string.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system consults wall-clock time. The server holds a single logical clock which, before any administrator has moved it, reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`. Every instant the server mints, every date comparison, every expiry evaluation and every storage-day count is taken from that clock. It never falls back to real time, and it may be moved backwards as freely as forwards.

Moving the clock is a `PlatformAdmin`-only capability, and its single body field is `to_iso8601`, the instant the clock is to be set to. The operation returns HTTP 200 with an empty `data` object; it does not echo the supplied string back.

Moving the clock is not a passive act. Advancing it re-derives, in this order: the effective status of every identification document, monotonically, so that a document past its expiry becomes `EXPIRED`; the status of every accepted Form 1583, so that a form past its `expires_on` becomes `EXPIRED`; the derived release eligibility of every roster member, together with the mail-item cascade that moves pieces between `HELD` and `AWAITING_CLEARANCE` in both directions; the storage axis of every piece, which may run `FREE_WINDOW → ACCRUING` and append storage usage; the notification obligation of every accepted package or service-of-process piece, which may move `PENDING → BREACHED`; and finally the cancellation lifecycle, which may run `REQUESTED → FINAL_FORWARD`.

Date arithmetic throughout this document is calendar-day arithmetic on the clock's UTC date, and hour comparisons are on the clock's UTC hour. "Whole days of custody" means the number of complete 86,400-second intervals that have elapsed, computed by taking the elapsed seconds and discarding any remainder.

### 2.6. Money, Rounding and Pinned Evaluation Order

All money in this system is expressed in **whole cents** as an integer; there are no fractional-cent fields and no floating-point money in any response. Wherever a monetary amount is produced by a multiplication or a division, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent — a value ending in exactly one half cent rounds away from zero, so 2537.5 becomes 2538.

Three calculations pin an evaluation **order** on top of that rounding rule, and in each the order is load-bearing because the two orders produce different integers:

1. **Forward pricing rounds per line item, then sums.** Each piece on a consolidation is priced, rounded to a whole cent, and only then added into the shipment's `base_price_cents`. Summing the unrounded per-item products and rounding once at the end is a different and incorrect answer: three `PRIORITY` pieces in the 1750-cent bracket give 2538 cents each and 7614 cents in total under the pinned order, against 7613 cents under sum-then-round.
2. **Proration multiplies the whole monthly fee first, then divides, then rounds once.** The monthly fee is multiplied by the number of remaining days, that product is divided by 30, and the quotient is rounded half up exactly once. Deriving a rounded daily rate first and multiplying it by the remaining days is a different and incorrect answer: a 6,100-cent tier with 11 days remaining yields 2237 cents under the pinned order and 2233 cents under the rounded-daily-rate order.
3. **The loyalty discount is keyed on the pre-discount subtotal.** The discount percentage is selected by comparing the subtotal computed before any discount, and the discount is then subtracted. Re-keying the percentage on the post-discount total would be circular and would demote a subtotal sitting exactly on a tier boundary out of its own tier.

Counts, page tallies, whole days and whole pounds are integers and are never rounded half up: where the rules below call for a ceiling ("rounded up to the next whole unit") or a floor ("any remainder discarded"), that is what is meant, and it is stated explicitly at each site.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Parents

Four write operations mutate a parent whose state the client must have observed before it may safely write. Each of those four requires a version field in the request body; the field is **not optional**, and omitting it is `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400) at rung 9 of the ladder. If the value supplied does not equal the parent row's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written.

- `POST /mail-items/{id}/dispositions` requires `expected_mail_item_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target mail item. Another writer may already have lodged a terminal disposition on the piece which, under the precedence rule, would legitimately supersede the one being lodged now — so a client working from a stale view of the piece must be told to re-read it.
- `POST /boxes/{id}/plan-change` requires `expected_box_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the mailbox. Proration is computed against the tier and the cycle position the client believed were in force, so a stale view would book the wrong credit and the wrong charge.
- `POST /boxes/{id}/forward-shipments` requires `expected_box_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the mailbox. Consolidation pricing and the eligible-item set are derived from the box state the client observed, and another writer may already have consolidated some of those pieces.
- `POST /deposit-batches/{id}/checks` requires `expected_deposit_batch_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the deposit batch. The per-batch amount and count caps are evaluated against the batch totals the client observed, and another writer may already have consumed the remaining headroom.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and no other endpoint may reject a request with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

### 2.8. The Two Global Write Gates and Their Precedence

Two global gates sit across the write surface of this API. They overlap but they do not block the same operations, so the precedence between them is part of the contract. **They are evaluated in the order given below, and the first gate whose condition holds wins; the loser's error is never returned for that request.** Concretely, the suspension gate is evaluated at rung 11 of the validation ladder and the Form 1583 gate at rungs 17, 18 and 19, so a request that trips both returns the suspension gate's error and never the Form 1583 gate's.

**Gate A — the suspension gate.** Its condition is that the target mailbox has `service_status` equal to `SUSPENDED`. When it holds and the operation is on the list below, the request is rejected with `ERR_BOX_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 409).

Gate A blocks exactly these eight operations:

| Operation blocked by Gate A |
|---|
| `POST /mail-items/{id}/scan-jobs` |
| `POST /mail-items/{id}/dispositions` |
| `POST /dispositions/{id}/execute` |
| `POST /boxes/{id}/forward-shipments` |
| `POST /forward-shipments/{id}/ship` |
| `POST /deposit-batches/{id}/checks` |
| `POST /boxes/{id}/recipients` |
| `POST /boxes/{id}/plan-change` |

Gate A explicitly **permits** these six operations on a suspended mailbox — mail must still be receivable and the holder must still be able to give notice:

| Operation exempt from Gate A |
|---|
| `POST /boxes/{id}/mail-items` |
| `POST /mail-items/{id}/match` |
| `POST /mail-items/{id}/refuse` |
| `POST /mail-items/{id}/notify` |
| `POST /mail-items/{id}/package-acceptance` |
| `POST /boxes/{id}/cancellation` |

Gate A carries one carve-out. When the target piece has `item_class` equal to `SOP` and the mailbox has a registered-agent add-on with `active` true, a scan request at `scan_tier` `COVER` or `INTERIOR` is exempt from Gate A even though scan requests are otherwise blocked. The carve-out does not extend to `OCR`, and it does not exempt service-of-process pieces from the Form 1583 gate below.

**Gate B — the lifecycle gate.** Its condition is that the target mailbox has `service_status` equal to `PENDING_SETUP` or `CLOSED`. It is evaluated at rung 12, immediately after Gate A, and blocks every write under a `/boxes/{id}/` path and every write on a resource whose owning box is in one of those two states, with two exceptions: `POST /boxes/{id}/activate` and `POST /boxes/{id}/recipients` remain permitted. The rejection is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). A mailbox therefore projects a derived `release_gate_state`: `GATED_SUSPENDED` when `service_status` is `SUSPENDED`, `GATED_LIFECYCLE` when `service_status` is `PENDING_SETUP` or `CLOSED`, and `OPEN` in every other case, which is to say when `service_status` is `ACTIVE` or `CLOSING`. That derivation is evaluated fresh on every read of the mailbox and on every gated write.

**Gate C — the Form 1583 gate.** Its condition is that the roster member matched to the piece does **not** have `release_eligibility` equal to `RELEASABLE`. It is decomposed into three ordered rungs so that the caller learns *which* clause failed: rung 17 fires when the matched member holds no Form 1583 in status `ACCEPTED` and returns `ERR_FORM_1583_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409); rung 18 fires when an accepted form exists but the member has never had a document of `doc_class` `PHOTO`, or has never had one of `doc_class` `ADDRESS`, reach `VERIFIED`, and returns `ERR_ID_DOCUMENT_SET_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409); rung 19 fires when both classes have been verified at some point but the currently governing document of at least one class has since become `EXPIRED`, and returns `ERR_ID_DOCUMENT_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409).

Gate C blocks exactly these five operations:

| Operation blocked by Gate C |
|---|
| `POST /mail-items/{id}/scan-jobs`, but only at `scan_tier` `INTERIOR` or `OCR` |
| `POST /mail-items/{id}/dispositions` |
| `POST /dispositions/{id}/execute` |
| `POST /boxes/{id}/forward-shipments` |
| `POST /deposit-batches/{id}/checks` |

Gate C explicitly **permits** these four operations regardless of release eligibility:

| Operation exempt from Gate C |
|---|
| `POST /boxes/{id}/mail-items` |
| `POST /mail-items/{id}/match` |
| `POST /mail-items/{id}/notify` |
| `POST /mail-items/{id}/scan-jobs`, at `scan_tier` `COVER` only |

Gate C has **no** service-of-process carve-out. A service-of-process piece may be cover-scanned and notified with no Form 1583 on file at all, but it may not be forwarded, opened for interior imaging, deposited or shredded until its matched roster member is `RELEASABLE`.

> **Un-adjudicated point.** Gate C is phrased in terms of "the matched Recipient", which is a single determined row for the three operations that address one piece. Two of the five gated operations — creating a forward shipment for a mailbox, and adding a check to a deposit batch — are not addressed to one piece: the first names an array of pieces which may have different matched members (and the consolidation-eligibility rule separately drops pieces whose member is not `RELEASABLE`), and the second identifies its member only by running payee matching inside the same request. This specification does not state, for those two operations, whether the gate is evaluated over every referenced member, over any one of them, or not at all. Neither reading is adopted here.

### 2.9. The Request Validation Ladder

Every request is evaluated against the following 36 rungs **in strictly ascending numeric order**, with a single declared exception stated at rung 33 and applying to one endpoint only, and the first rung that fails determines the response. A rung that does not apply to the endpoint under evaluation is skipped without effect. Text order is not a stylistic choice here: it is the reason a mid-flight suspension surfaces as `ERR_BOX_SUSPENDED` rather than being masked by a later state check, and the reason a caller with the wrong role never learns whether the resource it named exists.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are present and `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorization.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's permitted-role list. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Primary resource existence.** The first templated resource named in the path exists. The error is the one belonging to that resource type: `ERR_BOX_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECIPIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FORM_1583_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ID_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MAIL_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCAN_JOB_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FORWARD_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEPOSIT_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHECK_DEPOSIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPLIANCE_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_BILLING_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, each at HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint with a templated path segment.
4. **Sub-collection existence.** A sub-collection addressed under the primary resource resolves — deactivating the registered-agent add-on requires an existing add-on, withdrawing or finalizing a cancellation requires an existing cancellation request, and closing a billing cycle requires an existing `OPEN` cycle. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to `POST /boxes/{id}/registered-agent/deactivate`, `POST /boxes/{id}/cancellation/withdraw`, `POST /boxes/{id}/cancellation/finalize` and `POST /boxes/{id}/billing-cycles/close`.
5. **Plan tier reference.** A `plan_tier_id` or `new_plan_tier_id` supplied in the body resolves to a plan tier. Otherwise `ERR_PLAN_TIER_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to `POST /boxes` and `POST /boxes/{id}/plan-change`.
6. **Carrier rule reference.** A `carrier_rule_id` supplied in the body resolves to a carrier rule. Otherwise `ERR_CARRIER_RULE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to `POST /boxes/{id}/mail-items`.
7. **Body reference resolution.** A `recipient_id`, a `mail_item_id` or a `guarantor_recipient_id` supplied in the body resolves, and every id inside `mail_item_ids` resolves to a mail item. The rung is declared with `ERR_RECIPIENT_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to `POST /mail-items/{id}/match`, `POST /boxes/{id}/recipients`, `POST /deposit-batches/{id}/checks` and `POST /boxes/{id}/forward-shipments`.
8. **Scope ownership.** A `BoxHolder`'s `X-User-ID` equals the mailbox the request targets, directly or through the target resource's `box_id`; a `Recipient`'s `X-User-ID` equals the target piece's `matched_recipient_id`. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint whose permitted-role list includes `BoxHolder` or `Recipient`.
9. **Field presence, type and range.** Every required body field is present, carries the declared type, and sits inside its declared numeric range. This rung covers, among others: `requested_pages` at least 1 and at most 200; `amount_cents` at least 1; `jurisdiction_code` exactly two uppercase letters; a plan change whose new tier has the same `rank` as the tier currently in force; `carrier_rule_id` present when `item_class` is `PACKAGE` or `SOP` and absent when it is `LETTER` or `FLAT`; and a missing optimistic-concurrency version field. Otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). Applies to every `POST`, `PUT` and `PATCH`.
10. **Enum membership.** Every enum-valued body field carries a declared member. This rung applies to **exactly** these fields and no others: a plan tier's `tier_code`, a carrier rule's `carrier_code`, a roster member's `recipient_kind`, a Form 1583's `rejection_reason`, an identification document's `doc_class` and `doc_type`, a mail item's `item_class` and `refusal_reason`, a scan job's `scan_tier`, a disposition request's `disposition_type`, a forward shipment's `service_level`, a compliance review's `reason` and `resolution`, and every enum-valued query filter (`handling_status`, `storage_status`, `roster_status`, `release_eligibility`, `status`, `unit_type`, `tranche`, `doc_class`, `item_class`, `service_status`). Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (400). No other field routes here; a non-enum field carrying a bad value routes to `ERR_VALIDATION` at rung 9.
11. **Gate A, the suspension gate.** The target mailbox is `SUSPENDED`, the operation is on Gate A's blocked list, and the service-of-process carve-out does not cover it. Otherwise `ERR_BOX_SUSPENDED` (409). Applies to Gate A's eight blocked operations.
12. **Gate B, the lifecycle gate.** The target mailbox is `PENDING_SETUP` or `CLOSED` and the operation requires an in-service mailbox. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to every write under a `/boxes/{id}/` path and every write on a resource whose mailbox is `PENDING_SETUP` or `CLOSED`, except `POST /boxes/{id}/activate` and `POST /boxes/{id}/recipients`.
13. **Optimistic concurrency.** The supplied `expected_mail_item_version`, `expected_box_version` or `expected_deposit_batch_version` equals the parent's current `version`. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /mail-items/{id}/dispositions`, `POST /boxes/{id}/plan-change`, `POST /boxes/{id}/forward-shipments` and `POST /deposit-batches/{id}/checks`.
14. **Declared transition.** The target resource sits in a state from which the requested transition is declared. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to every action endpoint that drives a state machine.
15. **Segregation of duties.** The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the review's `opened_by_user_id` when resolving, and from the form's `notary_user_id` when accepting. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to `POST /compliance-reviews/{id}/resolve` and `POST /form-1583/{id}/accept`.
16. **Roster match.** The roster member supplied to a match belongs to the piece's mailbox, has a `roster_status` other than `REMOVED`, and the normalized `addressee_name` equals either the member's normalized `legal_name` or one of its normalized `dba_aliases`. Otherwise `ERR_RECIPIENT_NOT_ON_ROSTER` (409). Applies to `POST /mail-items/{id}/match`.
17. **Gate C, clause one.** The matched roster member holds no Form 1583 in status `ACCEPTED`. Then `ERR_FORM_1583_INCOMPLETE` (409). Applies to Gate C's five blocked operations.
18. **Gate C, clause two.** The matched roster member holds an accepted form, but the governing `PHOTO` document or the governing `ADDRESS` document does not exist — that class has never reached `VERIFIED`. Then `ERR_ID_DOCUMENT_SET_INCOMPLETE` (409). Applies to Gate C's five blocked operations.
19. **Gate C, clause three.** Both governing documents exist but at least one carries status `EXPIRED`. Then `ERR_ID_DOCUMENT_EXPIRED` (409). Applies to Gate C's five blocked operations.
20. **Registered-agent requirement.** A service-of-process piece requires an add-on with `active` true on its mailbox before it may be accepted, scanned or notified. Otherwise `ERR_RA_SERVICE_REQUIRED` (403). Applies to `POST /mail-items/{id}/package-acceptance`, `POST /mail-items/{id}/scan-jobs` and `POST /mail-items/{id}/notify`, in each case only when the piece's `item_class` is `SOP`.
21. **Interior scan consent.** An `INTERIOR` or `OCR` scan requires the piece's `interior_scan_consent` to be true. Otherwise `ERR_SCAN_CONSENT_REQUIRED` (403). Applies to `POST /mail-items/{id}/scan-jobs`.
22. **Signature authorization.** A piece whose `signature_required` is true requires its matched roster member to carry `signature_authorization_on_file` true. Otherwise `ERR_SIGNATURE_AUTHORIZATION_MISSING` (403). Applies to `POST /mail-items/{id}/package-acceptance`.
23. **Carrier signature policy.** A piece whose `signature_required` is true, arriving from a carrier whose rule has `accepts_signature_required` false, cannot be accepted. Otherwise `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_ACCEPTED` (422). Applies to `POST /mail-items/{id}/package-acceptance`.
24. **Oversize.** The oversize check does not trip. Otherwise `ERR_PACKAGE_OVERSIZE` (422). Applies to `POST /mail-items/{id}/package-acceptance`.
25. **Guarantor pairing.** A roster member of `recipient_kind` `MINOR` carries a `guarantor_recipient_id` pointing at a non-`MINOR` member of the same mailbox, and a non-`MINOR` member carries none. Otherwise `ERR_GUARANTOR_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to `POST /boxes/{id}/recipients`.
26. **Roster capacity.** The roster-capacity check does not trip. Otherwise `ERR_ROSTER_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to `POST /boxes/{id}/recipients`.
27. **Disposition precedence.** The incoming request's `precedence_rank` is strictly lower than every pending rank already on the piece. Otherwise `ERR_DISPOSITION_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to `POST /mail-items/{id}/dispositions`.
28. **Retry budget.** A retry is refused once the job has been abandoned. Otherwise `ERR_SCAN_RETRY_EXHAUSTED` (409). Applies to `POST /scan-jobs/{id}/retry`.
29. **Deposit cutoff.** The logical clock's UTC hour is strictly less than 21. Otherwise `ERR_BATCH_CUTOFF_PASSED` (409). Applies to `POST /deposit-batches/{id}/checks`.
30. **Rematch resolution.** Payee matching resolves a roster member. Otherwise `ERR_PAYEE_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to `POST /check-deposits/{id}/rematch`.
31. **Deposit caps.** The per-check amount, the per-batch total and the per-batch count all stay inside their caps. Otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to `POST /deposit-batches/{id}/checks`.
32. **Consolidation feasibility.** At least one supplied `mail_item_ids` member passes consolidation eligibility. Otherwise `ERR_ITEM_NOT_CONSOLIDATABLE` (422). Applies to `POST /boxes/{id}/forward-shipments`.
33. **Single pending cancellation.** No cancellation request on this mailbox has status `REQUESTED` or `FINAL_FORWARD`. Otherwise `ERR_CANCELLATION_ALREADY_PENDING` (409). Applies to `POST /boxes/{id}/cancellation`. This is the ladder's one ordering exception: on that endpoint — the only endpoint this rung applies to — this rung is evaluated **ahead of rung 14**, so a mailbox that already carries a request in `REQUESTED` or `FINAL_FORWARD` is refused with `ERR_CANCELLATION_ALREADY_PENDING` and not with rung 14's `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Every earlier rung, rung 12's lifecycle gate included, still runs before this one; rung 14 still fires on that endpoint whenever the mailbox's `service_status` is anything other than `ACTIVE` and no such request is outstanding; and no other rung is reordered on this or any other endpoint.
34. **Statutory retention.** The service-of-process retention deadline has passed for every such piece still in custody. Otherwise `ERR_RA_RETENTION_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to `POST /boxes/{id}/cancellation/finalize` and to `POST /dispositions/{id}/execute` when the disposition type is `SHRED`.
35. **Retention floor.** The clock date is on or after the cancellation's `destruction_eligible_on`. Otherwise `ERR_RETENTION_FLOOR_NOT_REACHED` (409). Applies to `POST /boxes/{id}/cancellation/finalize`.
36. **Quota ceiling.** Overage metering does not need to write an `OVERAGE` tranche against a plan tier whose `allows_overage` is false. Otherwise `ERR_QUOTA_EXHAUSTED` (402), and nothing at all is written. Applies to `POST /scan-jobs/{id}/complete`, `POST /forward-shipments/{id}/ship` and `POST /form-1583/{id}/accept`.

> **Un-adjudicated point.** Rung 7 is declared with `ERR_RECIPIENT_NOT_FOUND`, yet its check also covers a `mail_item_id` supplied in a body and every member of `mail_item_ids`; separately, `ERR_MAIL_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` is catalogued as covering "a mail item id in the path **or body**". This specification does not state which of the two error identifiers an unresolvable body-supplied mail item id returns. Neither reading is adopted here.

### 2.10. Nested Path Resolution

Paths that name more than one resource are resolved left to right, and the leftmost unresolved segment decides the error. Reading the scan jobs of a piece resolves the piece first: an unknown piece id yields `ERR_MAIL_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` (404) even if the caller also got the sub-path wrong. A resource addressed under a parent must genuinely belong to that parent; a scan job that exists but hangs off a different piece is treated as not found under the piece named in the path.

### 2.11. Conservation Invariants

Five invariants hold after every request, and they are the fastest way to check an implementation against itself.

**Quota tranche partition.** Within one billing cycle and one unit type, the quantities on `IN_TIER` ledger entries plus the quantities on `OVERAGE` ledger entries sum exactly to that cycle's consumption counter for the unit type. Furthermore, the `IN_TIER` sum equals the smaller of the cycle's consumption for that unit type and the plan allowance for that unit type as defined by overage metering. Because the allowance for storage days is zero by construction, the `IN_TIER` sum for storage days is always zero and `storage_days_consumed` counts billable days only. This holds after every metering event and after a cycle close.

**Deposit money partition.** For one deposit batch, `received_amount_cents` equals `deposited_amount_cents` plus `returned_amount_cents` plus `held_amount_cents`, at all times.

**Mail custody cardinality.** For one mailbox, the number of pieces whose `storage_status` is `FREE_WINDOW` or `ACCRUING`, plus the number whose `storage_status` is `RELEASED`, plus the number whose `storage_status` is `DESTROYED`, equals the total number of pieces on that mailbox. Every piece sits in exactly one custody bucket; no piece is ever in two, and none is ever in none.

**Refund waterfall conservation.** For one cancellation request, `refund_total_cents` plus `applied_to_overage_cents` plus `forfeited_cents` equals `prepaid_at_request_cents` plus `deposit_held_cents`, exactly, in every branch.

**Storage-day time partition.** For one piece, `free_storage_days_used` plus `billable_storage_days` equals the piece's total custody days, where total custody days is the elapsed time from `received_at` to the end of custody divided by 86,400 seconds with any remainder discarded, and the end of custody is `released_at` when `storage_status` is `RELEASED` or `DESTROYED` and the current logical clock otherwise.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Catalogue and Opening a Mailbox

Before a single mailbox exists, a `PlatformAdmin` populates two reference catalogues. A **plan tier** is created with a `tier_code` drawn from `BASIC`, `PLUS`, `BUSINESS` and `ENTERPRISE` — each code may exist only once — a human-readable `name`, and a `rank` between 1 and 4 inclusive which must also be unique across the catalogue; a higher `rank` means a richer tier, and the comparison of ranks is the only thing that decides whether a later plan change counts as an upgrade or a downgrade. The commercial body of a tier is `monthly_fee_cents`, which must be zero or more and is charged at the start of each 30-day cycle; four inclusion allowances, namely `included_recipients` (at least 1), `included_scan_pages`, `included_forwards` and `included_storage_days` (each zero or more); and four overage rates, namely `overage_scan_page_cents`, `overage_forward_cents`, `overage_storage_day_cents` and `overage_recipient_cents` (each zero or more). A final flag, `allows_overage`, defaults to true; when it is set false, any request that would consume a unit beyond the included quota is rejected outright with `ERR_QUOTA_EXHAUSTED` instead of being metered. Note that `included_storage_days` is a *per-piece* free window measured in whole days from the moment a piece is logged, not a per-cycle pool.

A **carrier rule** governs what the facility will take from one delivering carrier. Its `carrier_code` is one of `USPS`, `UPS`, `FEDEX`, `DHL` and `AMAZON`, unique across the catalogue. `dim_divisor` is at least 1 and converts cubic inches into dimensional pounds. `max_length_plus_girth_in` and `max_actual_weight_lb` are both strictly positive and set the refusal thresholds. `accepts_signature_required` defaults to true and says whether the facility may sign for that carrier's signature-demanding parcels at all. `acceptance_fee_cents` is the flat handling fee and `base_rate_cents_per_lb` the per-billable-pound rate, both zero or more. Neither plan tiers nor carrier rules carry `updated_at` or `version`; they are created and read, never amended. Plan tiers may be listed by anyone holding any of the six roles, filtered by `tier_code`; carrier rules may be listed by platform staff, counter staff, compliance and holders, filtered by `carrier_code`.

Opening a mailbox is a `CmraOperator` act. The payload carries a `box_number`, which must be non-empty and unique across all mailboxes, the `plan_tier_id` of the tier the holder signed up on, a `holder_name` — the legal name of the account holder who signs the agreement — and a `holder_email`, which is the address used for package and service-of-process notifications. Three optional money-and-slot fields all default to 0: `security_deposit_cents`, a refundable deposit that later enters the cancellation refund waterfall; `extra_recipient_slots`, purchased roster capacity stacked on top of the tier's `included_recipients`; and `prepaid_balance_cents`, credit on the account which cycle charges draw down. All three must be zero or more. A newly opened mailbox lands in `service_status` `PENDING_SETUP`, `compliance_status` `COMPLIANT`, `suspended_reason` `NONE` and, by the derivation in §2.8, `release_gate_state` `GATED_LIFECYCLE`. Its `pending_plan_tier_id` is null.

Putting the mailbox into service — the transition `PENDING_SETUP → ACTIVE`, permitted only from `PENDING_SETUP` — is likewise a `CmraOperator` act and does four things at once. It stamps `activated_at` with the current logical-clock instant. It stamps `cycle_anchor_date` with the calendar date of that same instant. It creates the first billing cycle, with `cycle_index` 1, status `OPEN`, `period_start` equal to `cycle_anchor_date` and `period_end` equal to `period_start` plus 30 days — `period_start` is the inclusive first date of the cycle and `period_end` the exclusive last, so every subsequent cycle's `period_start` is `cycle_anchor_date` plus a whole multiple of 30 days. And it bumps `Box.version` by one.

A roll-up view of a mailbox is available to platform staff, counter staff, compliance and the holder. It reports `box_id`, `service_status`, `compliance_status`, `release_gate_state`, `roster_authorized_count`, `open_billing_cycle_id`, the four quota counters of the open cycle (`scan_pages_consumed`, `forwards_consumed`, `storage_days_consumed`, `recipients_consumed`), `outstanding_overage_cents`, and the three custody buckets `items_in_custody`, `items_released` and `items_destroyed`. `roster_authorized_count` is the number of roster members on the mailbox whose `roster_status` is `AUTHORIZED`. `outstanding_overage_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over every usage-ledger entry of this mailbox whose `tranche` is `OVERAGE` and whose owning billing cycle is still `OPEN`. `items_in_custody` counts pieces whose `storage_status` is `FREE_WINDOW` or `ACCRUING`, `items_released` counts those at `RELEASED`, and `items_destroyed` counts those at `DESTROYED`; by the custody invariant of §2.11 the three sum to the mailbox's total piece count. Mailboxes may also be listed and filtered by `plan_tier_id`, by `pending_plan_tier_id` and by `service_status`.

### 3.2. Building the Roster

A mailbox's roster is the set of names that may lawfully receive mail there, and adding a member is a `CmraOperator` act. The payload carries `legal_name`, which must be non-empty and is the name exactly as it appears on that member's Form 1583, and `recipient_kind`, one of `INDIVIDUAL`, `BUSINESS` and `MINOR`. Three optional fields round it out: `dba_aliases`, an array of additional trade names that mail and checks may be addressed to, defaulting to the empty array; `guarantor_recipient_id`, pointing at the adult guarantor of a minor; and `signature_authorization_on_file`, defaulting to false, which records whether the member has authorized the facility to sign for signature-demanding parcels on their behalf. The member's `box_id` is taken from the mailbox named in the path, never from the body.

The guarantor rule is symmetric and strict: a member of `recipient_kind` `MINOR` **must** carry a `guarantor_recipient_id` pointing at a member of the same mailbox whose `recipient_kind` is not `MINOR`, and a member of any other kind **must not** carry one. Either violation is `ERR_GUARANTOR_REQUIRED`. The pointer is self-referential — a roster member referring to another roster member on the same roster.

Capacity is checked next. The effective cap is the smaller of two numbers: the tier's `included_recipients` plus the mailbox's `extra_recipient_slots` on one side, and the USPS statutory ceiling of 12 members per mailbox on the other. Occupancy is the count of members on the mailbox whose `roster_status` is `PENDING`, `AUTHORIZED` or `SUSPENDED`; members at `REMOVED` do not occupy a slot and their removal frees it for reuse. The create is rejected with `ERR_ROSTER_CAP_EXCEEDED` when occupancy is **greater than or equal to** the effective cap — so with an effective cap of 3 the third create succeeds and the fourth is rejected.

A new member starts at `roster_status` `PENDING`, `release_eligibility` `BLOCKED` and `blocked_reason` `NO_ACCEPTED_1583`. Counter staff may later amend `dba_aliases` or `signature_authorization_on_file` on an existing member; that amendment bumps `Recipient.version` by one and touches nothing else. Adding an alias is the ordinary way a check that was held for a payee mismatch becomes depositable.

Removal drives `roster_status` to `REMOVED`, which is terminal, and is declared from all three of `PENDING`, `AUTHORIZED` and `SUSPENDED`. All three share one precondition: no other member of the mailbox may name this member as its `guarantor_recipient_id` while still holding a `roster_status` other than `REMOVED` — a guarantor cannot walk out from under a minor. Removal from `AUTHORIZED` carries one further precondition: no piece on the mailbox whose `matched_recipient_id` is this member may sit in `handling_status` `AWAITING_CLEARANCE`, `HELD` or `PENDING_DISPOSITION`; live custody must be cleared first. Removal from `AUTHORIZED` also has a side effect the other two do not: `release_eligibility` is set to `BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` `NO_ACCEPTED_1583`. Each of the three removals bumps `Recipient.version` by one. Roster members may be listed per mailbox filtered by `roster_status`, and across mailboxes filtered by `box_id`, `guarantor_recipient_id`, `roster_status` and `release_eligibility`.

### 3.3. Clearing a Member: Form 1583, Identification and Release Eligibility

Nothing may be released to a roster member until USPS paperwork is complete, and "complete" is a derived, five-clause condition that is re-evaluated constantly rather than a flag someone sets.

**The form.** A `CmraOperator` files a Form 1583 application against a roster member; the only body field is `home_address_line`, the applicant's residence address as declared on the form, which must be non-empty. The application is created in status `DRAFT`. A `Notary` — and only a `Notary` — witnesses the signature, moving the form `DRAFT → NOTARIZED`, which stamps `notarized_at` with the current logical-clock instant, records the acting `X-User-ID` as `notary_user_id`, and bumps `Form1583.version` by one.

Acceptance is a `ComplianceOfficer` act on a form in `NOTARIZED`, and it carries three preconditions: the form is in `NOTARIZED`; the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the form's `notary_user_id`, on pain of `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`; and the member already holds at least one identification document of `doc_class` `PHOTO` in status `VERIFIED`. Notice what acceptance does *not* require: an `ADDRESS` document. A member can therefore be fully accepted and still be blocked from release, which is exactly the situation the second clause of the Form 1583 gate exists to report. Acceptance sets `accepted_on` to the clock's calendar date, `accepted_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, and `expires_on` to `accepted_on` plus 1,825 days — five years, the USPS validity period. It also drives the roster axis `PENDING → AUTHORIZED`, meters one `RECIPIENT` unit, re-derives the member's own `release_eligibility`, and then — after that own re-derivation has settled — re-derives `release_eligibility` for every member whose `guarantor_recipient_id` points at this member. `Form1583.version` is bumped by one. Rejection, also a `ComplianceOfficer` act on a `NOTARIZED` form, requires a `rejection_reason` drawn from `ADDRESS_MISMATCH`, `ILLEGIBLE`, `NAME_MISMATCH` and `INCOMPLETE_ID`, moves the form to `REJECTED`, and bumps its version by one. `REJECTED` and `EXPIRED` are both terminal for a form.

An accepted form ages out. Whenever the logical clock advances, any form in `ACCEPTED` whose `expires_on` is **strictly less than** the new clock date moves `ACCEPTED → EXPIRED`; a form whose `expires_on` is exactly the clock date is still accepted. Expiry cascades: the owning member re-derives to `BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` `NO_ACCEPTED_1583`, and `Form1583.version` is bumped by one.

**The documents.** A `CmraOperator` files an identification document against a member with `doc_class` — one of `PHOTO` and `ADDRESS`, the two classes USPS requires — `doc_type`, one of `DRIVERS_LICENSE`, `PASSPORT`, `STATE_ID`, `UTILITY_BILL`, `LEASE`, `VOTER_CARD` and `VEHICLE_REGISTRATION`, and `expires_on`, the calendar date after which the document is no longer acceptable. The document is created in `PENDING_REVIEW`. Verifying it is also a `CmraOperator` act and requires two things: the document is in `PENDING_REVIEW`, and the clock date is **less than or equal to** `expires_on` — a document may be verified on its expiry date but not the day after. Verification stamps `verified_at` with the current clock instant, moves every *other* document of the same member and the same `doc_class` that was in `VERIFIED` to `SUPERSEDED` (each such document's own `version` bumped by one), re-derives the owning member's `release_eligibility`, and bumps the verified document's `version` by one.

Document status is monotone. Whenever the clock advances, a document in `VERIFIED` or in `PENDING_REVIEW` whose `expires_on` is **strictly less than** the clock date — the expiry grace period is zero days, so there is no slack at all — moves to `EXPIRED` and bumps its `version` by one; expiry from `VERIFIED` additionally re-derives the owning member's eligibility, while expiry from `PENDING_REVIEW` has no such cascade because an unverified document never contributed to eligibility. Once a document is `EXPIRED` or `SUPERSEDED`, that status is final and never changes again, not even if the clock is subsequently set backwards: a document expiring on 2026-06-30 is still `VERIFIED` on 2026-06-30, is `EXPIRED` on 2026-07-01, and remains `EXPIRED` after the clock is moved back to 2026-06-15.

**Governing documents.** For a member and a class `D`, the *governing* document of class `D` is chosen among that member's documents of `doc_class` `D` whose status is `VERIFIED` **or** `EXPIRED` — deliberately including expired rows — taking the one with the greatest `verified_at`, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending. If no such document exists, the governing document of that class is null. Documents in `PENDING_REVIEW` and `SUPERSEDED` never govern.

**The derivation.** `release_eligibility` is a computed axis with two values, `BLOCKED` and `RELEASABLE`, and it is re-derived at the end of accepting a form, verifying a document, advancing the logical clock, resolving a compliance review and suspending a mailbox under review. The five clauses are evaluated **in this order, stopping at the first failure**, and `blocked_reason` reports the *first* failing clause, never the last:

1. The member holds a Form 1583 in status `ACCEPTED`. Otherwise `BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` `NO_ACCEPTED_1583`.
2. The governing `PHOTO` document is not null and the governing `ADDRESS` document is not null. Otherwise `BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` `ID_SET_INCOMPLETE`.
3. The governing `PHOTO` document has status `VERIFIED` and the governing `ADDRESS` document has status `VERIFIED`. Otherwise `BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` `ID_EXPIRED`.
4. The member's `recipient_kind` is not `MINOR`, or else the guarantor member named by `guarantor_recipient_id` itself has `release_eligibility` `RELEASABLE`. Otherwise `BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` `GUARANTOR_BLOCKED`.
5. The member's `roster_status` is `AUTHORIZED`. Otherwise `BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` `ROSTER_NOT_AUTHORIZED`.

When all five pass, the member is `RELEASABLE` and `blocked_reason` is `NONE`. Clauses 2 and 3 are separately reachable precisely because the governing document may be an expired row: clause 2 fails only when a class has never been verified at all — which is the ordinary state of a member whose form was accepted on the strength of a photo document alone, with no address document ever filed — while clause 3 fails when both classes were verified at some point and the governing row of one of them has since aged past its `expires_on`.

Crossing `BLOCKED → RELEASABLE` sets `blocked_reason` to `NONE`, re-derives eligibility for every member whose `guarantor_recipient_id` points at this member, cascades every piece whose `matched_recipient_id` is this member and whose `handling_status` is `AWAITING_CLEARANCE` into `HELD`, and bumps `Recipient.version` by one. Crossing `RELEASABLE → BLOCKED` — which happens when any clause stops holding, whether through document expiry on a clock advance, form expiry, roster suspension or a guarantor becoming blocked — sets `blocked_reason` to the first failing clause, cascades every piece whose `matched_recipient_id` is this member and whose `handling_status` is `HELD` back into `AWAITING_CLEARANCE`, and bumps `Recipient.version` by one. The axis is re-entrant in both directions: verifying a fresh address document after the previous one expired takes a member from `BLOCKED` to `RELEASABLE` again, dragging its pieces from `AWAITING_CLEARANCE` back to `HELD` with them.

A member's `form_1583_accepted_at` records the logical-clock instant of the most recent transition of one of its forms into `ACCEPTED`, and is null while no form has ever been accepted. A member's Form 1583 applications may be listed filtered by `status`, and its identification documents filtered by `doc_class` and `status`, in both cases with the derived status of each row as of the current clock.

The clearance cascade into `HELD` deliberately leaves `notification_status` alone. That axis is owned solely by package acceptance and by the notification record; a piece that clears compliance while sitting in `AWAITING_CLEARANCE` moves to `HELD` with its notification obligation exactly as it was.

**The clearance chain.** Accepting a notarized form therefore runs a five-hop chain: the form moves `NOTARIZED → ACCEPTED`; because the form was accepted, the member's roster axis moves `PENDING → AUTHORIZED` and one `RECIPIENT` unit is metered; because the roster axis reached `AUTHORIZED`, the member's `release_eligibility` is re-derived and — if all five clauses now pass — moves `BLOCKED → RELEASABLE`; because the member became releasable, eligibility is re-derived for every member it guarantees; and because the member became releasable, every piece of theirs sitting in `AWAITING_CLEARANCE` moves to `HELD`. Each hop fires only if the one before it did: a form accepted for a member whose address document has never been filed stops after the second hop, leaving the member `AUTHORIZED` but `BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` `ID_SET_INCOMPLETE`, and its pieces stay in `AWAITING_CLEARANCE`.

### 3.4. Inbound Mail: Intake, Matching and Refusal

Every physical piece that arrives at the counter is logged by a `CmraOperator` against the mailbox it was addressed to. Logging is one of the few operations a suspended mailbox still permits — the post does not stop arriving because compliance opened a file. The payload names `addressee_name`, the name printed on the piece before any roster matching has happened, which must be non-empty, and `item_class`, one of `LETTER`, `FLAT`, `PACKAGE` and `SOP`, where `SOP` is a service-of-process document delivered to the facility in its capacity as registered agent. `carrier_rule_id` is **required** when `item_class` is `PACKAGE` or `SOP`, because both arrive through a carrier, and **forbidden** for `LETTER` and `FLAT`; getting that pairing wrong is `ERR_VALIDATION`. `signature_required` defaults to false and records whether the carrier demands a signature at handover. Four measurements are always supplied and must each be zero or more: `actual_weight_lb`, the scale weight in pounds, and `length_in`, `width_in` and `height_in`, the three dimensions in inches, with `length_in` being the longest. `interior_scan_consent` defaults to false and is the standing consent to open the envelope for interior imaging.

Intake stamps `received_at` from the logical clock and sets all four status axes to their initial values: `handling_status` `RECEIVED`, `storage_status` `FREE_WINDOW`, `billing_status` `UNBILLED` and `notification_status` `NOT_REQUIRED`. It also sets `billable_weight_lb` to `actual_weight_lb` for every piece regardless of class; for packages and service-of-process pieces that value is recomputed later at acceptance. `accepted_at` and `released_at` are null, `matched_recipient_id` and `forward_shipment_id` are null, and `notification_due_at` and `notified_at` are null.

Matching attaches a piece to a roster member, and it too is permitted on a suspended mailbox and regardless of the Form 1583 gate — the facility must be able to say whose mail this is before it can say what may be done with it. The body carries `recipient_id`. The piece must be in `handling_status` `RECEIVED`; the named member must belong to the same mailbox and hold a `roster_status` of `PENDING`, `AUTHORIZED` or `SUSPENDED` (which is to say, anything but `REMOVED`); and the piece's normalized `addressee_name` must equal either the member's normalized `legal_name` or one of its normalized `dba_aliases`. Any of those three failing is `ERR_RECIPIENT_NOT_ON_ROSTER`. **Normalization** — used identically here and in payee matching — uppercases the string, strips leading and trailing spaces, collapses every internal run of spaces to a single space, and deletes the four characters full stop, comma, apostrophe and hyphen. No similarity scoring of any kind is used; equality after normalization is exact or it is nothing.

A successful match sets `matched_recipient_id`, which is never subsequently cleared, and bumps `MailItem.version` by one. Where the piece lands depends entirely on the member: when the matched member's `release_eligibility` is `RELEASABLE`, the piece goes `RECEIVED → HELD` and is available for disposition; when it is `BLOCKED`, the piece goes `RECEIVED → AWAITING_CLEARANCE` and waits there until the member clears, at which point the clearance cascade of §3.3 pulls it into `HELD`.

Refusal is the other exit from `RECEIVED`. A `CmraOperator` refuses a piece with a `refusal_reason` drawn from `NOT_ON_ROSTER`, `OVERSIZE`, `CARRIER_NOT_ACCEPTED` and `HOLDER_REQUEST`; the field is required on refusal and null otherwise. Refusal is a three-axis event: `handling_status` goes `RECEIVED → REFUSED`, which is terminal; `storage_status` moves to `RELEASED` with `released_at` stamped from the clock; and `billing_status`, if it was still `UNBILLED`, moves to `WRITTEN_OFF`. The whole refusal bumps `MailItem.version` by exactly one. Refusal, like intake, is permitted while the mailbox is suspended.

Pieces may be listed per mailbox filtered by `handling_status` and `storage_status`, and across mailboxes filtered by `box_id`, `matched_recipient_id`, `carrier_rule_id`, `forward_shipment_id`, `handling_status` and `item_class`. The usage-ledger entries attributable to one piece may also be listed, filtered by `unit_type`.

### 3.5. Package Acceptance, Pricing and the Notification Clock

A `PACKAGE` or `SOP` piece is not merely logged; it is *accepted* from the carrier by a `CmraOperator`, and acceptance is where measurement, money and a statutory deadline all land at once. The operation takes no body at all — everything it needs is already on the piece and on its carrier rule.

Four guards run before any arithmetic, in the ladder order of §2.9. A service-of-process piece requires the mailbox to carry a registered-agent add-on with `active` true, failing which the request is `ERR_RA_SERVICE_REQUIRED`. A piece whose `signature_required` is true requires its matched member's `signature_authorization_on_file` to be true, failing which the request is `ERR_SIGNATURE_AUTHORIZATION_MISSING`. A piece whose `signature_required` is true arriving from a carrier whose rule has `accepts_signature_required` false cannot be taken at all, and is `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_ACCEPTED`. Finally the oversize check: girth is twice the sum of `width_in` and `height_in`, and length-plus-girth is `length_in` added to that girth. The piece is oversize when length-plus-girth is **strictly greater than** the rule's `max_length_plus_girth_in`, or when `actual_weight_lb` is **strictly greater than** the rule's `max_actual_weight_lb`. Both comparisons are strict, so a piece sitting exactly on either threshold is accepted and a piece one tenth above it is `ERR_PACKAGE_OVERSIZE`. Inches and pounds are carried to one decimal place throughout.

Weight comes next, and it is a two-branch calculation. Dimensional weight is the product of `length_in`, `width_in` and `height_in` divided by the carrier rule's `dim_divisor` and then **rounded up to the next whole pound**. `billable_weight_lb` is then the greater of `actual_weight_lb` and that dimensional weight, carried to one decimal place. When the scale weight is greater than or equal to the dimensional weight, `billable_weight_lb` is simply the scale weight and the cubic dimensions never enter the price; when the dimensional weight is strictly greater, `billable_weight_lb` is the dimensional weight and a light bulky parcel prices above what it weighs. The value that results drives both the acceptance fee below and the forwarding weight bracket later.

The fee branches on class. For a service-of-process piece, `package_acceptance_fee_cents` is zero — statutory documents are handled free as part of the registered-agent add-on. For every other class it is the carrier rule's `acceptance_fee_cents` plus the product of `billable_weight_lb` and the rule's `base_rate_cents_per_lb`, with that whole sum rounded half up to whole cents. Acceptance stamps `accepted_at` with the current logical-clock instant — a field that stays null forever on `LETTER` and `FLAT` pieces — writes `billable_weight_lb` and `package_acceptance_fee_cents`, and bumps `MailItem.version` by one.

Acceptance also starts the notification obligation. `notification_status` moves from `NOT_REQUIRED` to `PENDING` and `notification_due_at` is computed from `accepted_at`: for a service-of-process piece on a mailbox carrying an add-on with `active` true, the deadline is `accepted_at` plus **4 hours**, the same-business-day statutory clock; for every other case — an ordinary package, or a service-of-process piece with no active add-on — the deadline is `accepted_at` plus **24 hours**. The same acceptance instant therefore produces a service-of-process deadline twenty hours earlier than a package's. Whenever the logical clock advances past that deadline — strictly greater than `notification_due_at`, while `notified_at` is still null — `notification_status` moves `PENDING → BREACHED`. A piece whose `notification_status` is `NOT_REQUIRED` has a null `notification_due_at`.

Recording the notification is a `CmraOperator` act and is permitted on a suspended mailbox and regardless of the Form 1583 gate. It moves `notification_status` `PENDING → SENT` and stamps `notified_at` from the clock, bumping `MailItem.version` by one. A piece that has already breached cannot be retro-notified: attempting to move `BREACHED → SENT` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A service-of-process piece may only be notified while its mailbox carries an active add-on, on pain of `ERR_RA_SERVICE_REQUIRED`.

### 3.6. Imaging: Splitting, Retries and Page Charges

Imaging is requested by the `BoxHolder`, not by staff. The request names a `scan_tier` — `COVER` images the exterior only, `INTERIOR` opens the piece, and `OCR` opens it and adds a searchable text layer — and `requested_pages`, the total number of pages wanted across the whole request, which must be at least 1 and at most 200. Anything outside that range is `ERR_VALIDATION`.

Three guards apply. An `INTERIOR` or `OCR` request requires the piece's `interior_scan_consent` to be true, failing which the request is `ERR_SCAN_CONSENT_REQUIRED`. A service-of-process piece requires an active registered-agent add-on on its mailbox, failing which it is `ERR_RA_SERVICE_REQUIRED`. And the Form 1583 gate applies at `INTERIOR` and `OCR` but not at `COVER`: the exterior of an envelope may be photographed for a member with no paperwork at all, while opening it may not.

A request larger than a single job is split. The number of jobs is `requested_pages` divided by 25 and **rounded up to the next whole number**. Every job but the last carries exactly 25 pages in its `page_count`; the last carries `requested_pages` less 25 times one fewer than the job count. Each job records `split_index`, its 1-based position in the group, and `split_group_size`, the number of jobs the request was split into; jobs are created in ascending `split_index` order and each stores the full `requested_pages` of the originating request. The boundary is inclusive: 25 requested pages produce exactly one job of 25 pages, and 26 produce two jobs of 25 and 1. Every job is created in `QUEUED`, with `retry_count` 0 and `billed_pages_in_tier`, `billed_pages_overage` and `charge_cents` unset until completion. The created resource returned to the caller is the job with `split_index` 1.

Counter staff drive each job through its lifecycle. Starting moves it `QUEUED → IN_PROGRESS`. Completing moves it `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` and is where the money happens. Failing moves it out of `IN_PROGRESS` along one of two branches decided by the retry budget, which is capped at 2 retries: while `retry_count` is strictly less than 2 the job goes `IN_PROGRESS → FAILED`, and once `retry_count` has reached 2 the very same operation instead goes `IN_PROGRESS → ABANDONED`. An abandoned job is terminal and consumes no quota whatsoever: no usage-ledger entry is appended for it and no counter moves. Retrying a `FAILED` job moves it `FAILED → QUEUED` and increments `retry_count` by one, which makes `QUEUED → IN_PROGRESS → FAILED → QUEUED` a genuine cycle, traversable at most twice. Retrying a job that has been abandoned is `ERR_SCAN_RETRY_EXHAUSTED`. Each of these five transitions bumps `ScanJob.version` by one. A scan job has exactly two terminal states, `COMPLETED` and `ABANDONED`; `QUEUED`, `IN_PROGRESS` and `FAILED` are all live. The jobs of a piece may be listed — by platform staff, counter staff, the holder and the matched member — filtered by `status` and by `scan_tier`.

**Page charging at completion.** A service-of-process piece bypasses the scan quota entirely: `billed_pages_in_tier`, `billed_pages_overage` and `charge_cents` are all zero, no usage-ledger entry is appended, and `scan_pages_consumed` on the cycle does not move. For every other class, the remaining allowance is the tier's `included_scan_pages` less the cycle's `scan_pages_consumed`, floored at zero. `billed_pages_in_tier` is the smaller of the job's `page_count` and that remaining allowance, and `billed_pages_overage` is `page_count` less `billed_pages_in_tier`. The tier multiplier is 1 for `COVER`, 1 for `INTERIOR` and 2 for `OCR`. `charge_cents` is `billed_pages_overage` multiplied by the plan's `overage_scan_page_cents` multiplied by that tier multiplier. The plan consulted is always the one currently in force through `plan_tier_id`, never a deferred `pending_plan_tier_id`. A usage-ledger entry with `tranche` `IN_TIER` is appended when `billed_pages_in_tier` is greater than zero, and a second with `tranche` `OVERAGE` when `billed_pages_overage` is greater than zero; a job that sits wholly inside the allowance produces one entry and no charge, while a job that straddles the allowance produces two. Completion then increments the cycle's `scan_pages_consumed` by the job's `page_count`, moves the piece's `billing_status` `UNBILLED → METERED` if it was still unbilled, and bumps `ScanJob.version` by one.

> **Un-adjudicated point.** `charge_cents` on the job is explicitly the overage pages times the plan rate times the tier multiplier, while a usage-ledger entry's `amount_cents` is explicitly its `quantity` times its `unit_rate_cents`, and `unit_rate_cents` on an overage entry is described as "the overage rate" for the unit type. For an `OCR` job those two definitions coincide only if the tier multiplier is folded into `unit_rate_cents`. This specification does not state whether the `OCR` multiplier is reflected in the ledger entry — and therefore in the cycle's `overage_total_cents` — or lives only on the job's `charge_cents`. Neither reading is adopted here.

### 3.7. Dispositions and the Precedence Contest

Once a piece is `HELD`, its holder decides what finally happens to it by lodging a disposition request. The body names a `disposition_type` — `FORWARD`, `SHRED`, `DEPOSIT` or `RETURN` — and the optimistic-concurrency field `expected_mail_item_version` described in §2.7. Every type carries a fixed `precedence_rank`: `DEPOSIT` is 1, `FORWARD` is 2, `RETURN` is 3 and `SHRED` is 4, and a **lower** rank wins. The intuition is that a cheque should be banked before the envelope is forwarded, and anything at all is better than shredding.

The contest resolves as follows. If the piece carries no request in status `PENDING`, the incoming request is simply created `PENDING`. If it does, and the lowest `precedence_rank` among those pending requests is **strictly greater** than the incoming request's rank, then every pending request moves to `SUPERSEDED` with `resolution_reason` `LOST_PRECEDENCE` and each has its own `version` bumped by one, and the incoming request is created `PENDING`. Otherwise the incoming request is rejected with `ERR_DISPOSITION_CONFLICT`. Equality is a rejection, not an idempotent no-op: a shred lodged over a pending shred is a conflict, while a deposit lodged over a pending shred supersedes it.

Lodging the first request drives the custody axis `HELD → PENDING_DISPOSITION` and bumps `MailItem.version` by one. It also **suspends evaluation of the storage axis**: while `handling_status` is `PENDING_DISPOSITION` the transition `FREE_WINDOW → ACCRUING` is not evaluated at all, even though elapsed custody time keeps accumulating in the background. A request is created with `status` `PENDING`, `resolution_reason` `NONE` and a null `executed_at`.

> **Un-adjudicated point.** The custody axis declares `HELD → PENDING_DISPOSITION` as the transition that lodging a request drives, and declares no transition out of `PENDING_DISPOSITION` back into itself; yet the precedence contest above describes superseding requests that are already `PENDING`, a situation that can only arise while the piece is already in `PENDING_DISPOSITION`. This specification does not state whether a superseding request lodged against a piece already in `PENDING_DISPOSITION` is admitted by the precedence rule or refused by the declared-transition rung. Neither reading is adopted here.

Withdrawing a pending request is the holder's privilege. It moves the request `PENDING → CANCELLED` with `resolution_reason` `HOLDER_CANCELLED` and bumps `DispositionRequest.version` by one. When — and only when — no request in status `PENDING` remains on the piece after that write, the custody axis returns `PENDING_DISPOSITION → HELD`, which **resumes** evaluation of the storage axis: if total custody days is by then strictly greater than the tier's `included_storage_days`, the storage axis immediately runs `FREE_WINDOW → ACCRUING`, appending a storage-day usage entry. That resumption bumps `MailItem.version` by one.

Carrying a disposition out is `CmraOperator` work, and only two of the four types are executed directly: `SHRED` and `RETURN`. A `FORWARD` is executed by shipping the consolidation it was placed on, and a `DEPOSIT` is executed by closing the deposit batch its cheque was logged into. Direct execution requires the request to be `PENDING`, the piece to be in `PENDING_DISPOSITION`, the request's type to match, and the request to be the winner on its piece — no other pending request on the same piece may hold a strictly lower `precedence_rank`. Execution sets `status` `EXECUTED`, `resolution_reason` `EXECUTED` and `executed_at` from the clock, and bumps `DispositionRequest.version` by one. A `SHRED` drives the piece `PENDING_DISPOSITION → SHREDDED` with `storage_status` moving to `DESTROYED` and `released_at` stamped; it additionally requires either that the piece is not a service-of-process document or that the mailbox has no active registered-agent add-on whose retention period still covers the piece, failing which it is `ERR_RA_RETENTION_ACTIVE`. A `RETURN` drives the piece `PENDING_DISPOSITION → RETURNED` with `storage_status` moving to `RELEASED` and `released_at` stamped. Both terminal moves bump `MailItem.version` by one. Both lodging and executing a disposition are blocked by the suspension gate and by the Form 1583 gate. Disposition requests may be listed per piece filtered by `status`.

### 3.8. Consolidated Forwarding

Forwarding is done in batches, because postage is cheaper per piece when several pieces travel in one box. A `BoxHolder` creates a shipment naming a `service_level` of `ECONOMY`, `PRIORITY` or `EXPRESS`, a free-text `destination_address` that must be non-empty, an array `mail_item_ids` of the pieces to consolidate, and `expected_box_version`. The shipment is priced at creation and is created in status `PRICED`.

**Eligibility is per item and partial.** A supplied id is eligible only when every one of the following holds: the piece belongs to the mailbox named in the path; its `handling_status` is `PENDING_DISPOSITION`; its winning disposition request has `disposition_type` `FORWARD` and status `PENDING`; its `storage_status` is `FREE_WINDOW` or `ACCRUING`; its `forward_shipment_id` is null, so it is not already on another consolidation; its `item_class` is not `SOP`; and its matched roster member has `release_eligibility` `RELEASABLE`. Eligible pieces are attached to the shipment and have their `forward_shipment_id` set to it; ineligible ones are silently left off. `item_count` is the number attached and `excluded_item_count` the number of supplied ids that failed. An ineligible piece does **not** abort the shipment. Only when *every* supplied id fails is the whole request rejected, with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_CONSOLIDATABLE`.

**Pricing walks the eligible pieces in ascending `created_at` order.** Each piece falls into a weight bracket keyed on its `billable_weight_lb`, and each bracket's upper bound is **inclusive**: at most 1.0 lb costs 850 cents, above that and at most 5.0 lb costs 1,750 cents, above that and at most 20.0 lb costs 3,900 cents, and anything heavier costs 7,200 cents. Exactly 5.0 lb therefore sits in the 1,750-cent bracket and 5.1 lb in the 3,900-cent one. The service multiplier is 1.00 for `ECONOMY`, 1.45 for `PRIORITY` and 2.20 for `EXPRESS`. Each piece's price is its bracket amount multiplied by that multiplier and **rounded half up to whole cents at that point**, and `base_price_cents` is the sum of those already-rounded per-item prices; the rounding order is the pinned one of §2.6 and summing first would give a different total. `total_weight_lb` is the sum of `billable_weight_lb` over the pieces actually attached.

The consolidation discount rewards volume. When `item_count` is **greater than or equal to** 3, `consolidation_discount_cents` is 15 percent of `base_price_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents; otherwise it is zero. Exactly three eligible pieces earn the discount; two eligible pieces plus one excluded piece do not, because excluded pieces never count toward the threshold. `total_price_cents` is `base_price_cents` less `consolidation_discount_cents`. Creating the shipment bumps `Box.version` by one.

Dispatch is `CmraOperator` work and requires the shipment to be `PRICED` with `item_count` of at least 1. It moves the shipment `PRICED → SHIPPED`, stamps `shipped_at` from the clock, drives every attached piece `PENDING_DISPOSITION → FORWARDED` with the piece's winning `FORWARD` request moving to `EXECUTED` with `resolution_reason` `EXECUTED`, moves each piece's `storage_status` to `RELEASED` with `released_at` stamped, appends exactly one usage-ledger entry of `unit_type` `FORWARD` and `quantity` 1 through the metering rule of §3.12, increments the cycle's `forwards_consumed` by 1, and bumps `ForwardShipment.version` by one. Note that one shipment consumes one forward unit no matter how many pieces it carries — that is the whole point of consolidating.

A shipment has exactly two terminal states, `SHIPPED` and `CANCELLED`, and only `PRICED` is live. Cancelling a priced shipment is the holder's privilege and moves it `PRICED → CANCELLED`, clears `forward_shipment_id` on every attached piece so they may be consolidated again later, sets `item_count` to 0, and bumps `ForwardShipment.version` by one; the pieces themselves stay in `PENDING_DISPOSITION` with their forward requests still `PENDING`. Creating a shipment is blocked by both the suspension gate and the Form 1583 gate; dispatching one is blocked by the suspension gate. Shipments may be listed per mailbox filtered by `status`.

### 3.9. Cheques and Deposit Batches

Holders who receive payments by post can have the facility bank them. A `CmraOperator` opens a deposit batch for a mailbox against a `business_date`, a plain calendar date the batch is booked to. A new batch has `closed` false, a null `closed_at`, derived `status` `OPEN`, `check_count` 0, and all four money counters — `received_amount_cents`, `deposited_amount_cents`, `returned_amount_cents` and `held_amount_cents` — at 0. The derived `status` is `OPEN` while `closed` is false and `CLOSED` once it is true.

Logging a cheque into the batch names the `mail_item_id` of the piece it arrived in, `payee_name_on_check` exactly as printed on the cheque and non-empty, `amount_cents` of at least 1, an optional `endorsed` flag defaulting to false which records whether the cheque bears the payee's endorsement on the reverse, and `expected_deposit_batch_version`. The cheque's `deposit_batch_id` is taken from the batch in the path, is set at creation, and is never cleared.

Two guards fire before matching, in ladder order. First the cutoff: cheques may only be logged while the logical clock's UTC hour is **strictly less than 21**, so a request at exactly 21:00:00Z is already too late and is `ERR_BATCH_CUTOFF_PASSED`. Then the caps, all of which produce `ERR_DEPOSIT_CAP_EXCEEDED`: a single cheque's `amount_cents` may not be **strictly greater than** 500,000 cents, so a cheque of exactly 500,000 is accepted; the batch's `received_amount_cents` plus the incoming `amount_cents` may not be **strictly greater than** 2,500,000 cents; and the batch's `check_count` must be **strictly less than** 25, so a batch already holding exactly 25 cheques rejects the next one.

**Payee matching** decides where the cheque lands. Candidates are the roster members of the batch's mailbox whose `roster_status` is `PENDING`, `AUTHORIZED` or `SUSPENDED`; removed members are excluded. Using the same normalization as roster matching in §3.4 — uppercase, trim, collapse internal space runs, delete full stop, comma, apostrophe and hyphen — the exact set is the candidates whose normalized `legal_name` equals the normalized payee, and the alias set is the candidates any of whose normalized `dba_aliases` entries equals it. If the exact set is non-empty, the match is its first member in ascending `created_at` order and `match_mode` is `EXACT_LEGAL`; the alias set is then **never consulted**, even if an alias on a different member would also have matched. If the exact set is empty but the alias set is not, the match is the alias set's first member in ascending `created_at` order and `match_mode` is `ALIAS`. If both are empty, `matched_recipient_id` is null and `match_mode` is `NONE`. There is no similarity scoring.

A logged cheque therefore goes one of two ways. It goes `RECEIVED → MATCHED` when payee matching resolves a member on the same mailbox **and** `endorsed` is true, which sets `matched_recipient_id` and `match_mode`. It goes `RECEIVED → HELD` when matching resolves nobody, or when `endorsed` is false, which sets `hold_reason` to `PAYEE_MISMATCH` or `NOT_ENDORSED` respectively. Either way `received_amount_cents` and `held_amount_cents` on the batch both increase by the cheque's `amount_cents`, and `DepositBatch.version` is bumped by one.

A held cheque is not lost. Re-running the match — a `CmraOperator` act, typically after the roster gained the alias the payee line used — takes it `HELD → MATCHED` when matching now resolves a member and `endorsed` is true, setting `matched_recipient_id`, `match_mode` and `hold_reason` `NONE`, and bumping `CheckDeposit.version` by one. If matching still resolves nobody, the request is `ERR_PAYEE_MISMATCH`. Alternatively a held cheque may be returned to sender, moving it `HELD → RETURNED`, moving its `amount_cents` out of `held_amount_cents` and into `returned_amount_cents`, and bumping its `version` by one. Both `DEPOSITED` and `RETURNED` are terminal for a cheque.

Closing the batch banks everything matched. Every cheque in `MATCHED` moves to `DEPOSITED`, its `amount_cents` moves out of `held_amount_cents` and into `deposited_amount_cents`, and its `version` is bumped by one. Closing also carries the piece-level consequence: a deposited cheque drives its piece `PENDING_DISPOSITION → DEPOSITED`, with the piece's winning `DEPOSIT` request moving to `EXECUTED` and its `storage_status` moving to `RELEASED` with `released_at` stamped, **only** when that piece is genuinely in `PENDING_DISPOSITION` carrying a `PENDING` request of type `DEPOSIT`; in every other case the piece's axes are left entirely untouched. Closing sets `closed` true, stamps `closed_at` from the clock, moves derived `status` to `CLOSED`, and bumps `DepositBatch.version` by one. Throughout, `held_amount_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the batch's cheques in `RECEIVED`, `MATCHED` or `HELD` — that is, everything neither banked nor returned — which is what makes the money partition of §2.11 hold at every instant. Batches may be listed per mailbox filtered by `status`; cheques may be listed across the system filtered by `mail_item_id`, `deposit_batch_id`, `matched_recipient_id` and `status`. Logging a cheque is blocked by both the suspension gate and the Form 1583 gate.

### 3.10. The Registered-Agent Add-On and Service-of-Process Handling

A mailbox may carry a registered-agent add-on, attached by a `PlatformAdmin`. It names a `jurisdiction_code`, exactly two uppercase letters identifying the state whose statute governs service-of-process retention, and an optional `retention_days` which defaults to 1,095 days — three years — and must be at least 1. The add-on is created with `active` true and a null `deactivated_at`, and reports `sop_count`, the number of pieces on the mailbox whose `item_class` is `SOP`. Deactivating it sets `active` false, stamps `deactivated_at` from the clock, and bumps `RegisteredAgentService.version` by one. Deactivation is forward-looking only: statutory retention already running on service-of-process pieces the facility has accepted survives it. Add-ons may be listed per mailbox filtered by `active`.

Service-of-process pieces are treated differently from ordinary mail in six specific ways, and each is stated in its own place above: they may only be accepted, scanned or notified while an active add-on exists (`ERR_RA_SERVICE_REQUIRED`); their acceptance fee is zero; their notification deadline is 4 hours rather than 24; they are exempt from storage accrual, so their `free_storage_days_used` equals their total custody days and their `billable_storage_days` is always zero no matter how long custody runs, and their storage axis never leaves `FREE_WINDOW` for `ACCRUING`; they bypass the scan-page quota entirely; and they are never eligible for consolidation onto a forward shipment. They also enjoy the suspension-gate carve-out of §2.8 for `COVER` and `INTERIOR` scans, and they enjoy no carve-out at all from the Form 1583 gate.

**Statutory retention** governs destruction. For a service-of-process piece on a mailbox with an active add-on, the retention deadline is the calendar date of the piece's `received_at` plus the add-on's `retention_days`. For every other piece — any other class, or a service-of-process piece on a mailbox whose add-on is not active — the retention deadline is the cancellation request's `destruction_eligible_on`. Destruction is refused while the clock date is **strictly less than** that deadline, which is to say destruction becomes permissible on the deadline date itself. The refusal is `ERR_RA_RETENTION_ACTIVE`, and it is the reason a statutory period can outlive the customer relationship: an active add-on's retention window blocks finalization of a cancellation even after `destruction_eligible_on` has already passed.

> **Un-adjudicated point.** Nothing constrains a mailbox to a single registered-agent add-on: the add-on collection is created and listed per mailbox, with an `active` filter that presupposes more than one row may exist. Every rule that consults the add-on is phrased over "an add-on with `active` true" or "the mailbox's add-on", singular. This specification does not state which add-on's `jurisdiction_code` and `retention_days` govern when a mailbox carries more than one active add-on. Neither reading is adopted here.

### 3.11. Compliance Review and the Seven-Hop Suspension Cascade

A `ComplianceOfficer` opens a review against a mailbox with a `reason` drawn from `ID_EXPIRY`, `USPS_AUDIT`, `ROSTER_ANOMALY` and `PAYMENT`. The review is created in status `OPEN` with `opened_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, a null `resolved_by_user_id`, `resolution` `NONE` and a null `resolved_at`. Opening drives the mailbox's compliance axis. From `COMPLIANT` the axis moves to `UNDER_REVIEW`, which requires the mailbox's `service_status` to be `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSING`. From `NON_COMPLIANT` the axis also moves to `UNDER_REVIEW` — a loop edge that re-enters review without ever passing back through `COMPLIANT` — which instead requires that no review of this mailbox currently sits in `OPEN` or `SUSPENDED`, and which creates a second review row. Either way `Box.version` is bumped by one. Reviews may be listed per mailbox filtered by `status`.

**Suspension is the head of a seven-hop chain.** It requires the review to be `OPEN` and the mailbox's `service_status` to be `ACTIVE`, and it is where the deepest cascade in this system runs. The seven hops execute **in exactly this order**, and each hop is *caused* by the one before it, which means each hop fires only when its predecessor actually fired:

- **Hop 1.** The review moves `OPEN → SUSPENDED`, and `ComplianceReview.version` is bumped by one. This hop does not fire when the review is not in `OPEN`; the request is then rejected outright at rung 14 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` and no hop runs.
- **Hop 2.** Because the review suspended, the mailbox's `service_status` moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` set to `COMPLIANCE_REVIEW` and `release_gate_state` set to `GATED_SUSPENDED`, and its `compliance_status` moves `UNDER_REVIEW → NON_COMPLIANT`. `Box.version` is bumped by one for the whole request. This hop does not fire when the mailbox's `service_status` is not `ACTIVE`; the request is rejected at rung 14 before hop 1 commits.
- **Hop 3.** Because the mailbox is now suspended, every disposition request whose piece belongs to this mailbox and whose status is `PENDING` moves to `CANCELLED` with `resolution_reason` `GATE_SUSPENSION`, each bumping its own `version` by one. This hop does not fire when the mailbox has no pending disposition requests at all.
- **Hop 4.** Because no pending request remains on them, each of those pieces moves `handling_status` `PENDING_DISPOSITION → HELD`, bumping its `version` by one. This hop does not fire for a piece that still carries a pending request after hop 3, nor for pieces that were never in `PENDING_DISPOSITION`.
- **Hop 5.** Because those pieces re-entered `HELD`, their storage axis is re-evaluated, having been suspended while they sat in `PENDING_DISPOSITION`. Any piece whose total custody days is **strictly greater than** the tier's `included_storage_days` runs `FREE_WINDOW → ACCRUING`, appending a usage-ledger entry of `unit_type` `STORAGE_DAY` into the mailbox's `OPEN` billing cycle and incrementing that cycle's `storage_days_consumed`. This hop does not fire for a piece whose total custody days is still less than or equal to `included_storage_days`, for a piece whose `storage_status` has already left `FREE_WINDOW`, or for a service-of-process piece, which is storage-exempt.
- **Hop 6.** Because the mailbox is suspended, every roster member with `roster_status` `AUTHORIZED` moves to `SUSPENDED`, each bumping its own `version` by one. A member of `recipient_kind` `MINOR` whose guarantor is also being suspended re-derives to `BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` `GUARANTOR_BLOCKED` rather than `ROSTER_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, because the guarantor clause is evaluated before the roster clause. This hop does not fire for members already at `PENDING`, `SUSPENDED` or `REMOVED`.
- **Hop 7.** Because those members left `AUTHORIZED`, their `release_eligibility` re-derives `RELEASABLE → BLOCKED`, which moves every piece of theirs whose `handling_status` is `HELD` back to `AWAITING_CLEARANCE`, each such piece bumping its `version` by one. This hop does not fire for a member who was already `BLOCKED` before hop 6, and it moves no piece for a member holding none in `HELD`.

**Resolution** closes a review and is where segregation of duties bites: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the review's `opened_by_user_id`, on pain of `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. The body carries `resolution`, which is required and is either `CLEARED` or `ESCALATED_CLOSURE`. Resolution always sets `resolved_by_user_id`, `resolution` and `resolved_at`, moves the review to `RESOLVED` — a terminal state — and bumps `ComplianceReview.version` by one.

Two shapes of resolution exist, and they are declared from different review states. A review still in `OPEN` — one that never suspended the mailbox — may be resolved only as `CLEARED`, which moves the mailbox's compliance axis `UNDER_REVIEW → COMPLIANT` and leaves the service axis alone. A review in `SUSPENDED` may be resolved either way. Resolved as `CLEARED`, it moves the mailbox `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` on the service axis, sets `suspended_reason` back to `NONE`, recomputes `release_gate_state`, moves the compliance axis `NON_COMPLIANT → COMPLIANT`, and returns every roster member of the mailbox whose `roster_status` is `SUSPENDED` to `AUTHORIZED` — each of which re-derives its own `release_eligibility` and bumps its `version` by one, dragging pieces from `AWAITING_CLEARANCE` back into `HELD` wherever all five clauses now pass. Resolved as `ESCALATED_CLOSURE`, it instead moves the mailbox `SUSPENDED → CLOSING`, sets `suspended_reason` to `USPS_ORDER`, and creates a cancellation request in status `REQUESTED` with `forwarding_address` set to the literal string `ADDRESS_ON_FILE`, `requested_on` set to the clock's calendar date, and `prepaid_at_request_cents` and `deposit_held_cents` snapshotted from the mailbox; that branch additionally requires that no cancellation request on this mailbox already sits in `REQUESTED` or `FINAL_FORWARD`. Either branch bumps `Box.version` by one. Re-entry into `ACTIVE` is what makes the service axis cyclic: a mailbox may be suspended, cleared, suspended again and cleared again without limit.

### 3.12. Storage Economics, Metering and the Billing Cycle

**Storage days.** Every piece accrues custody time from the instant it was logged. The end of custody is `released_at` once `storage_status` is `RELEASED` or `DESTROYED`, and the current logical clock otherwise. Total custody days is the elapsed time between `received_at` and that end of custody, divided by 86,400 seconds with any remainder discarded — whole days only, always rounded down. `free_storage_days_used` is the smaller of total custody days and the tier's `included_storage_days`, and `billable_storage_days` is total custody days less `free_storage_days_used`. For a service-of-process piece the split is different and unconditional: `free_storage_days_used` equals total custody days and `billable_storage_days` is zero, however long custody runs.

The storage axis leaves `FREE_WINDOW` for `ACCRUING` only when four conditions hold together: the piece is still in `FREE_WINDOW`; its `handling_status` is `RECEIVED`, `AWAITING_CLEARANCE` or `HELD` — never `PENDING_DISPOSITION`, which is precisely the suspension described in §3.7; its total custody days is **strictly greater than** the tier's `included_storage_days`, so a piece sitting at exactly the included figure is still free; and its `item_class` is not `SOP`. The transition appends a usage-ledger entry of `unit_type` `STORAGE_DAY` into the mailbox's `OPEN` billing cycle, increments that cycle's `storage_days_consumed`, moves the piece's `billing_status` `UNBILLED → METERED` if it was still unbilled, and bumps `MailItem.version` by one. Thereafter the accrual repeats once per additional whole day: the quantity metered on any occasion is the piece's `billable_storage_days` now less its `billable_storage_days` at the previous metering, and the money is that difference multiplied by the tier's `overage_storage_day_cents`.

Custody ends in one of two ways. Any custody-axis move into `FORWARDED`, `DEPOSITED`, `RETURNED` or `REFUSED` drives the storage axis from `FREE_WINDOW` or from `ACCRUING` into `RELEASED`, which stamps `released_at` and **freezes** `free_storage_days_used` and `billable_storage_days` at their values as of that instant. A move into `SHREDDED` drives the storage axis from `FREE_WINDOW` or from `ACCRUING` into `DESTROYED`, which likewise stamps `released_at`. Each of those four transitions bumps `MailItem.version` by one. `RELEASED` and `DESTROYED` are both terminal.

**The metering axis.** A piece's `billing_status` runs on its own. It moves `UNBILLED → METERED` on the first usage-ledger entry that names the piece. It moves `METERED → BILLED` when a billing cycle closes, provided every usage-ledger entry for that piece belongs to the cycle being closed. It moves `METERED → WRITTEN_OFF` when a cancellation is finalized and that cancellation was created by an escalated-closure resolution, and it moves `UNBILLED → WRITTEN_OFF` when the piece is refused. `BILLED` and `WRITTEN_OFF` are both terminal, and each of the four transitions bumps `MailItem.version` by one.

**The usage ledger** is append-only and carries no `updated_at` and no `version`. Each entry records the `billing_cycle_id` of the open cycle the consumption was metered into, a nullable `mail_item_id` naming the piece the consumption is attributable to, a `unit_type` of `SCAN_PAGE`, `FORWARD`, `STORAGE_DAY` or `RECIPIENT`, a `tranche` of `IN_TIER` or `OVERAGE`, a `quantity` of at least 1, a `unit_rate_cents` which is always 0 when the tranche is `IN_TIER`, an `amount_cents` equal to `quantity` multiplied by `unit_rate_cents`, and a `source_event` of `SCAN_COMPLETED`, `FORWARD_SHIPPED`, `STORAGE_ACCRUAL`, `RECIPIENT_AUTHORIZED` or `PLAN_CHANGE`. Ledger entries may be listed per cycle filtered by `unit_type` and `tranche`.

**Overage metering** runs on every consumption event: a scan job completing, a shipment dispatching, a storage day accruing and a roster member being authorized. The allowance for a unit type is `included_scan_pages` for scan pages, `included_forwards` for forwards, `included_recipients` plus the mailbox's `extra_recipient_slots` for recipients, and **zero** for storage days. That zero is deliberate rather than an omission: `included_storage_days` is a per-piece free window which the storage-day calculation has already subtracted, so every storage day that reaches the ledger is by construction already outside the allowance, and applying the free window a second time here would grant it twice. Consumption is the cycle's counter for that unit type as it stood before this event. The remaining allowance is the allowance less the consumption, floored at zero. The in-tier quantity is the smaller of the event's quantity and that remainder, and the overage quantity is the event's quantity less the in-tier quantity. If the overage quantity is greater than zero and the tier's `allows_overage` is false, the **whole request** is rejected with `ERR_QUOTA_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 402) and nothing at all is written. Otherwise an `IN_TIER` entry at `unit_rate_cents` 0 is appended when the in-tier quantity is greater than zero, an `OVERAGE` entry at the tier's overage rate for that unit type is appended when the overage quantity is greater than zero, and only then is the cycle's counter for that unit type incremented by the event's full quantity.

> **Un-adjudicated point.** Two rules describe how a newly authorized roster member is tranched. The roster transition itself says the entry is `IN_TIER` when the mailbox's `AUTHORIZED` count *after* the transition is less than or equal to `included_recipients` plus `extra_recipient_slots`, and `OVERAGE` at `overage_recipient_cents` otherwise — a rule keyed on the live roster. Overage metering says the tranche is decided by the cycle's `recipients_consumed` against the same allowance — a rule keyed on cycle-scoped consumption, which resets to zero at every close. The two disagree for a member authorized in a later cycle while earlier members remain authorized. This specification does not state which rule governs. Neither reading is adopted here.

**Closing a cycle** is a `PlatformAdmin` act and requires the mailbox to have an `OPEN` cycle whose `period_end` the clock date has reached — the clock date must be **greater than or equal to** `period_end`. Closing runs as two transitions inside one request. First `OPEN → CLOSING`, which freezes `plan_tier_id_at_close` with the mailbox's `plan_tier_id` as of that moment and `base_fee_cents` with that tier's `monthly_fee_cents`, and runs the metering and monthly-charge calculations. Then, immediately afterwards and inside the same request, `CLOSING → CLOSED`, which writes `total_charge_cents`, debits the mailbox's `prepaid_balance_cents` by the smaller of `total_charge_cents` and the current `prepaid_balance_cents`, moves every piece of the mailbox whose `billing_status` is `METERED` to `BILLED`, and creates the next cycle with `cycle_index` one greater, status `OPEN`, `period_start` equal to this cycle's `period_end`, and all four quota counters — `scan_pages_consumed`, `forwards_consumed`, `storage_days_consumed` and `recipients_consumed` — reset to 0. `plan_tier_id_at_close` is null while a cycle is `OPEN`. `CLOSED` is terminal.

> **Un-adjudicated point.** §2.4 states that a row's `version` is bumped exactly once per request that mutates it, never once per side effect. The cycle close declares a `version` increment on each of its two transitions, `OPEN → CLOSING` and `CLOSING → CLOSED`, and both run inside a single request against the same cycle row. This specification does not state whether the closing cycle's `version` therefore advances by one or by two. Neither reading is adopted here.

**The monthly charge** is computed in three pinned steps. The subtotal is `base_fee_cents` plus `overage_total_cents` plus `proration_charge_cents` less `proration_credit_cents`, where `overage_total_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over this cycle's usage-ledger entries whose `tranche` is `OVERAGE`. The loyalty percentage is then selected **from that subtotal**: 10 percent when the subtotal is greater than or equal to 40,000 cents, otherwise 5 percent when it is greater than or equal to 15,000 cents, otherwise zero. Both boundaries are inclusive, so a subtotal of exactly 40,000 earns 10 percent and 39,999 earns 5 percent. `loyalty_discount_cents` is the subtotal multiplied by that percentage, rounded half up to whole cents. `total_charge_cents` is the subtotal less `loyalty_discount_cents`. The percentage is never re-keyed on the post-discount total.

**Deferred downgrades land at the close.** When the mailbox carries a non-null `pending_plan_tier_id` at the `CLOSING → CLOSED` transition, `plan_tier_id` is set to it and `pending_plan_tier_id` is set to null. The new cap is the newly effective tier's `included_recipients` plus the mailbox's `extra_recipient_slots`, and the excess is the mailbox's `roster_authorized_count` less that cap, floored at zero. When the excess is greater than zero, one usage-ledger entry is appended **into the newly opened cycle**, not the closing one, with `unit_type` `RECIPIENT`, `tranche` `OVERAGE`, `quantity` equal to the excess, `unit_rate_cents` equal to the newly effective tier's `overage_recipient_cents`, and `source_event` `PLAN_CHANGE`.

**Changing plan** is the holder's act, carrying `new_plan_tier_id` and `expected_box_version`. The old tier is the one named by `plan_tier_id`, the new tier the one named in the body, and the cycle is the mailbox's `OPEN` one. Days elapsed is the whole-day difference between the clock date and the cycle's `period_start`, and is never negative; days remaining is 30 less days elapsed, clamped at zero from below. When the new tier's `rank` is **strictly greater** than the old tier's — an upgrade — `proration_credit_cents` is the old tier's `monthly_fee_cents` multiplied by days remaining, divided by 30, rounded half up exactly once, and `proration_charge_cents` is the same computation on the new tier's `monthly_fee_cents`; `plan_tier_id` is set to the new tier immediately and `pending_plan_tier_id` is set to null. When the new tier's `rank` is **strictly lower** — a downgrade — both proration amounts are zero, `plan_tier_id` is left exactly as it was, and `pending_plan_tier_id` is set to the new tier so that the change lands at the next close. An identical request body therefore produces two entirely different outcomes depending only on the rank comparison. When the two ranks are **equal**, the request is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Either accepted branch writes `proration_charge_cents` and `proration_credit_cents` onto the open cycle and bumps `Box.version` by one. Cycles may be listed per mailbox filtered by `status`.

### 3.13. Cancellation, the Closure Timeline and the Refund Waterfall

A `BoxHolder` gives notice by lodging a cancellation request with a non-empty `forwarding_address`, the address to which the final forward will be sent. The mailbox must be `ACTIVE`, and no cancellation request on it may already sit in `REQUESTED` or `FINAL_FORWARD`, failing which the request is `ERR_CANCELLATION_ALREADY_PENDING`. Of those two conditions the pending-cancellation one is checked first, per the ordering exception at rung 33: a mailbox that already carries an outstanding request is refused with `ERR_CANCELLATION_ALREADY_PENDING` even though its `service_status` is by then no longer `ACTIVE`, while a mailbox with no outstanding request whose `service_status` is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Lodging stamps `requested_on` with the clock's calendar date, snapshots `prepaid_at_request_cents` from the mailbox's `prepaid_balance_cents` and `deposit_held_cents` from its `security_deposit_cents` as of that instant, creates the request in status `REQUESTED`, drives the mailbox `ACTIVE → CLOSING`, and bumps `Box.version` by one. `applied_to_overage_cents`, `refund_total_cents` and `forfeited_cents` all start at 0.

Four dates are computed at that moment and never recomputed. `notice_end_date` is `requested_on` plus 30 days, and service ends at the start of that date. `final_forward_deadline` is `notice_end_date` plus 15 days, and is the last date on which a final forward may ship. `abandonment_date` is `final_forward_deadline` plus 10 days, and on or after that date unclaimed pieces other than service-of-process documents are returned to sender. `destruction_eligible_on` is `abandonment_date` plus 30 days, and is the earliest date on which remaining non-service-of-process pieces may be destroyed. Every comparison against these dates is **inclusive on the lower edge**. A request lodged on 2026-05-02 therefore carries `notice_end_date` 2026-06-01, `final_forward_deadline` 2026-06-16, `abandonment_date` 2026-06-26 and `destruction_eligible_on` 2026-07-26. The same four dates are computed identically for a cancellation created by an escalated-closure resolution.

The request advances on its own. Whenever the logical clock reaches or passes `notice_end_date`, a request in `REQUESTED` moves to `FINAL_FORWARD` and bumps its `version` by one. Withdrawal is available from both live states and is the holder's privilege: from `REQUESTED` unconditionally, and from `FINAL_FORWARD` only while the clock date is **less than or equal to** `final_forward_deadline`. Either withdrawal moves the request to `WITHDRAWN`, drives the mailbox `CLOSING → ACTIVE`, and bumps both the request's and the mailbox's `version` by one. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal, and this is the second of the two re-entry edges into `ACTIVE`.

Finalization is a `CmraOperator` act and is the end of the mailbox. It requires the mailbox to be `CLOSING`, its cancellation request to be in `FINAL_FORWARD`, the clock date to be **greater than or equal to** `destruction_eligible_on` — failing which the request is `ERR_RETENTION_FLOOR_NOT_REACHED` — and no service-of-process piece on the mailbox to be still inside its statutory retention period while an active add-on covers it, failing which the request is `ERR_RA_RETENTION_ACTIVE`. Finalization then, in order: drives every remaining piece whose `item_class` is not `SOP` and whose `storage_status` is `FREE_WINDOW` or `ACCRUING` to `storage_status` `DESTROYED` and `handling_status` `SHREDDED`; runs the refund waterfall and writes `applied_to_overage_cents`, `refund_total_cents` and `forfeited_cents`; moves the request `FINAL_FORWARD → FINALIZED`; closes the mailbox's `OPEN` billing cycle; drives the mailbox `CLOSING → CLOSED`; and bumps `Box.version` by one. `CLOSED` is terminal for a mailbox and `FINALIZED` for a request.

**The refund waterfall** has four steps and conserves every cent. The pool is `prepaid_at_request_cents` plus `deposit_held_cents`. Step one sets `applied_to_overage_cents` to the smaller of the pool and the mailbox's `outstanding_overage_cents` at the moment of finalization. Step two takes the remainder as the pool less `applied_to_overage_cents`. Step three sets `forfeited_cents` to that whole remainder when the mailbox carries a compliance review whose `resolution` is `ESCALATED_CLOSURE`, and to zero otherwise — money is only forfeited when USPS ordered the closure. Step four sets `refund_total_cents` to the remainder less `forfeited_cents`. In every branch the three outputs sum exactly to the pool. Cancellation requests may be listed per mailbox filtered by `status`.
