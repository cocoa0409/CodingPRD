# Sidewalk Delivery Robot Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Autonomous sidewalk delivery is a business that lives or dies on the quality of its map and the discipline of its paperwork. Our robots share the footway with pedestrians, wheelchair users and strollers; they cross streets under traffic-control regimes that differ block by block; and they operate only where a municipality has issued a sidewalk permit with its own clearance minimum, its own ground-speed ceiling and its own daily operating window. The operations team that runs this fleet is therefore doing three jobs at once — surveying and approving a sidewalk graph, dispatching robots across it against merchant orders, and answering to municipal authorities and insurers whenever something goes wrong. Today those three jobs live in disconnected spreadsheets, which is why a segment closed for construction can still appear on a live route, and why a refund can be paid out of a zone budget that was already exhausted.

This document specifies the API that replaces those spreadsheets. It is a single service that owns the sidewalk network, the permit lifecycle of each service zone, the merchant integration surface, the robot fleet with its depot charging and maintenance bays, the delivery order and its mission, the route plans that carry a mission across the graph, the remote-takeover procedure that a teleoperator runs when onboard autonomy loses confidence, the customer remedy policy that pays out when a delivery fails, and the incident-reporting workflow that files with the municipality and, above a damage threshold, with the insurer. Every collection sits at the root of the URL space; entities are peers joined by explicit references rather than nested inside one another, and the sidewalk graph itself is a self-referential adjacency structure over segments.

Two properties matter more than any individual feature. The first is determinism: every temporal rule in the system reads a logical clock that never advances on its own, so a curfew violation, a late handoff or an overdue municipal filing can be reproduced exactly. The second is conservation: reserved depot minutes and quoted merchant fees are conserved quantities that move between named buckets and never leak, so the depot's committed capacity and the merchant's escrow always reconcile against the live rows that justify them. The reader should treat both as invariants that the implementation is expected to preserve through every cascade described in §3.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts and cross-cutting principles that hold for every endpoint in the API. Where §3 describes a workflow, it does so on top of the rules stated here.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — including the logical-clock endpoint — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, which identifies the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, which declares the capacity in which that principal is acting. If `X-User-Role` is absent or empty the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); this is the very first check the server performs, ahead of every other rule in the system. If `X-User-Role` names something that is not a recognised role, or names a role that the target endpoint does not admit, the request is refused with `ERR_ROLE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403). The same code covers the per-action role restrictions that the mission, intervention and incident transition endpoints impose: those endpoints admit several roles, but each individual action inside them belongs to exactly one role, and a role submitting another role's action is refused with `ERR_ROLE_FORBIDDEN`.

Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows.

- `PlatformAdmin` — the municipal-compliance and configuration authority. It owns the mapping and permitting surface: service zones and their permit lifecycle, sidewalk segments and their survey/approval lifecycle, segment closures, merchant onboarding and integration, depot bay provisioning and robot registration. It is also the only role that may reject a `MINOR` incident report, and it may act on behalf of any merchant for order intake, order cancellation and container loading without being subject to the merchant ownership restriction.
- `FleetDispatcher` — runs the fleet. It owns dispatch, route planning and plan lifecycle, depot bay reservations and their lifecycle, and the duty dimension of every robot, plus the `DEPART`, `ARRIVE`, `COMPLETE` and `ABORT` mission actions and the zone economics read. It may not touch the health dimension of a robot, may not file or progress incident reports, and may not issue customer remedies.
- `RemoteOperator` — occupies the teleoperation seat. It owns the `REPORT_BLOCKAGE` and `RESUME` mission actions and the `ACKNOWLEDGE`, `ASSUME` and `ESCALATE` takeover actions, may open intervention requests, may ingest telemetry frames, and may file incident drafts — but may never approve its own submissions, cannot dispatch and cannot plan routes.
- `SafetyOfficer` — owns robot health and the municipal reporting workflow: the health dimension of every robot, the `SUBMIT_MUNICIPAL`, `NOTIFY_INSURER`, `CLOSE` and `RETURN_FOR_DETAIL` incident actions, the `DEESCALATE` takeover action, and the release of a takeover that is sitting in `ESCALATED`. It may also file incident drafts.
- `CustomerSupport` — faces the customer after a failed or late delivery. Issuing the customer remedy is the only state-mutating operation it may perform anywhere in the API; otherwise it is limited to zone, merchant, order and incident reads.
- `MerchantOperator` — a merchant's own staff. It creates and cancels its own orders and performs the physical container handoff, and every read and write it makes is scoped to its own merchant.

Five of the six roles carry an opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `FleetDispatcher`, `RemoteOperator`, `SafetyOfficer` or `CustomerSupport`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it — as `operator_user_id` on an intervention event when a `RemoteOperator` assumes control, and as `filed_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` on an incident report — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. `MerchantOperator` is the one exception: when `X-User-Role` is `MerchantOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Merchant` row, and ownership checks on any resource that references a merchant compare that reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `MerchantOperator` request is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403) whenever `X-User-ID` does not equal the `Merchant` `id` that owns the target row — the merchant row itself, the `merchant_id` of a target order, or the `merchant_id` of the order behind a target mission. That ownership rule guards order intake, order cancellation, container loading, the single-merchant read and the order list; on the order list and the merchant list a `MerchantOperator` sees only its own rows regardless of any filter it supplies.

Three further identity checks live in the same authorisation band and are evaluated in a fixed order relative to one another. Merchant ownership is checked first. Then comes segregation of duties on incident submission: a municipal submission requested with `X-User-ID` equal to the report's `filed_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), and because this check runs ahead of every state check, a same-user submission against an already-submitted report reports the duties failure rather than a state failure. Then comes the reviewer check: returning a submitted report for more detail is permitted only to the user recorded in `reviewed_by_user_id`, and any other caller is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Last in the band comes the takeover-control check: escalating a takeover, or releasing one as a `RemoteOperator`, requires `X-User-ID` to equal `operator_user_id`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; a `SafetyOfficer` releasing an escalated takeover is exempt from that comparison.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip before the window begins; when omitted it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. Both are echoed back to the caller together with the size of the unpaginated result set and a boolean indicating whether more rows follow: the `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`.

Unless a specific list declares otherwise, rows are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Three lists declare otherwise, and each says so in its own description in §4: the service-zone list is newest-first, ordered by `created_at` descending and tie-broken by `id` ascending; the route-plan list is ordered by `revision_index` ascending, which reproduces the revision history of a mission in the order the plans were written; and the robot list, when it is asked for the eligible pool only, is ordered by `rolling_autonomy_index` descending, then `battery_pct` descending, then `created_at` ascending.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every persisted entity in this system — `ServiceZone`, `SidewalkSegment`, `SegmentClosure`, `Merchant`, `Robot`, `DepotBay`, `BayReservation`, `DeliveryOrder`, `DeliveryMission`, `RoutePlan`, `InterventionEvent` and `IncidentReport` — carries the same four system-managed properties, and no entity in this system omits any of them.

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no domain-flavoured formatting.
- `created_at` (string): the instant the row was created, as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`, for example `2026-08-03T09:00:00.000Z`.
- `updated_at` (string): the instant the row was last mutated, in the same format.
- `version` (integer): 1 immediately after creation, and increased by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. This includes mutations applied by a cascade the caller never addressed directly — when a hard closure blocks a segment, that segment's `version` advances even though the caller only touched the closure. A read never changes a `version`, and a mutation always refreshes `updated_at` alongside it.

All temporal reasoning reads a logical clock rather than wall-clock time. The clock's startup default is `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, it never advances on its own, and it moves only when a caller sets it explicitly by supplying a `current_utc` instant; that operation touches no entity row and increments no `version`. Every rule in this document that speaks of "the current instant" — the operating-window containment check, the urgency window on an order, the merchant handoff deadline, closure activation windows, reservation activation windows, blocked-minute accumulation, control-second accumulation and the municipal filing deadline — reads that clock. Any client-supplied datetime field must itself be an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`; anything else is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.5. Optimistic Concurrency: Pinning the Parent Version

Three creation endpoints in this API write a child row whose meaning depends on the current state of a parent row, and each of them requires the client to pin the parent version it reasoned about. The pinned field is a non-optional part of the request body; a request that omits it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), and a request that supplies a value which does not equal the parent's current `version` is refused with a stale-version error carrying HTTP 412. No endpoint outside this list accepts a version field of any kind.

- `POST /route-plans` requires `expected_delivery_mission_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `DeliveryMission`. A newly activated plan legitimately supersedes the mission's previous active plan, so a create carrying a stale mission version would silently displace a plan the client never saw. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MISSION_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- `POST /bay-reservations` requires `expected_depot_bay_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `DepotBay`. A non-overlapping window on the same bay is legitimate on its own merits, so pinning the version is the only way to guarantee that the client reasoned about the bay's real committed-minutes ledger. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BAY_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- `POST /delivery-missions` requires `expected_delivery_order_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `DeliveryOrder`. A redelivery mission legitimately supersedes an aborted predecessor, so a create carrying a stale order version can double-dispatch an order whose state the client has not observed. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

These three checks sit in their own band of the rejection ladder described in §2.10: they run after every existence check has resolved and before any state check is evaluated, in the order mission version, then bay version, then order version. A caller that reads a mission, then watches another writer activate a plan on it, and then submits a route plan against the version it originally read receives the stale-mission rejection rather than a geometry or contiguity finding.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding

Money in this system is always an integer number of cents; there are no fractional-cent fields anywhere. The remaining numeric outputs have precision declared per calculation, and the rounding method is always round half up.

- Delivery fees (`quoted_fee_cents`, `settled_fee_cents`) are integer cents with round half up applied once, at the single final step of the fee computation. No intermediate term of that computation is rounded.
- The customer remedy amount (`remedy_amount_cents`) is integer cents, but its rounding is deliberately different: each of its three buckets is rounded half up on its own and the already-rounded buckets are then added. Summing the unrounded buckets and rounding the total once is not the same number and is not the specified behaviour.
- `crossing_risk_score` carries one decimal place, rounded half up, and is clamped into the range 0.0 through 100.0 before that rounding is applied.
- `clearance_margin_cm` carries one decimal place, rounded half up. `total_length_m` carries one decimal place. `planned_duration_min` is a whole number of minutes and is always rounded up, never to nearest.
- `rolling_autonomy_index` and `lifetime_autonomy_index` are integers, rounded half up, and clamped into the range 40 through 100 after an accumulation that itself performs no rounding.
- On the zone economics read, `utilization_pct` carries one decimal place and `throughput_per_robot_hour` carries two decimal places; `allocated_overhead_cents`, `variable_cost_cents`, `cost_per_delivery_cents` and `contribution_per_delivery_cents` are integer cents. Each rounding happens at the step boundary named in §3.9, and the rounded value is what the following step reads.
- `window_minutes` on a reservation is an exact whole number of minutes obtained by discarding any partial minute, and `blocked_minutes`, `active_minutes` and `control_seconds` are likewise whole units obtained by discarding the partial remainder.

### 2.7. The Ordinance and Accessibility Compliance Envelope

A service zone is first and foremost a container for the terms of a municipal permit, and those terms are the outer envelope inside which every other rule operates. When a zone is provisioned it is given a human-readable `name` of 1 to 120 characters, the `municipality_code` of the authority that issued the permit as a string of 2 to 12 characters, and the numeric terms of the permit itself: `ordinance_min_clearance_cm`, the minimum passable sidewalk clearance the ordinance demands, which must be greater than zero; `ordinance_max_speed_cm_s`, the maximum robot ground speed the ordinance allows, a whole number greater than zero; and `max_concurrent_robots`, the largest number of robots the zone may have out on missions at any one moment, which must be at least 1. The daily permitted operating window is given as `operating_window_start_utc_hhmm` and `operating_window_end_utc_hhmm`, each a four-digit UTC "HHMM" string with no separator whose first two digits fall between 00 and 23 and whose last two digits fall between 00 and 59 — for example `"0700"` and `"2100"`. Both endpoints of the window are inclusive, and the end must be strictly greater than the start when the two four-digit strings are compared as strings.

Three further zone terms shape safety and autonomy decisions. `max_crossing_risk` is the crossing-safety ceiling, a value between 0.0 and 100.0 that defaults to 45.0 when the caller does not supply it; no segment may be approved while its computed risk sits above that ceiling. `takeover_confidence_threshold_pct` is the autonomy-confidence breakpoint at or below which a remote takeover becomes mandatory, a whole number between 0 and 100 that defaults to 35. `insurer_notify_threshold_cents` is the estimated-damage level at or above which an incident must be escalated to the insurer; it must be zero or greater and defaults to 25000. Alongside these, the zone carries the two planning assumptions the forward-looking fee quote consumes — `planning_haul_m`, which must be greater than zero, and `planning_crossings`, which must be zero or greater — and `remedy_budget_cents`, the total customer-remedy budget authorised for the zone, which must be zero or greater.

Accessibility is enforced with a fixed platform buffer: a robot is only considered to fit through a sidewalk element when the element's measured clearance leaves at least 12 centimetres of slack beyond the robot's own chassis width. That buffer is not configurable per zone; the zone-level `ordinance_min_clearance_cm` governs whether a segment may be approved at all, and the 12-centimetre buffer governs whether a particular robot may be routed over it. Four distinct compliance findings can therefore arise, each with HTTP 422: `ERR_CLEARANCE_INSUFFICIENT` when accessibility clearance is short, `ERR_CROSSING_RISK_EXCEEDED` when a crossing-risk score sits above the zone ceiling, `ERR_ORDINANCE_SPEED_EXCEEDED` when a robot is faster than the ordinance permits, and `ERR_CURFEW_WINDOW_VIOLATION` when a planned run would fall outside the daily operating window.

### 2.8. Conserved Quantities, Denormalized Mirrors and Cascade Discipline

Two quantities in this system are conserved, meaning that the stored aggregate must at all times equal the sum over the live rows that justify it.

The first is depot capacity, measured in whole minutes. Each reservation carries an immutable `window_minutes`, computed once when the reservation is created and never recomputed. The invariant is twofold: a bay's `committed_minutes` equals the sum of `window_minutes` over that bay's reservations whose `state` is `HELD` or `ACTIVE`, and a zone's `reserved_bay_minutes` equals the sum of `committed_minutes` over the zone's bays, which is equivalently the sum of `window_minutes` over every live reservation of the zone. Creating a reservation adds its `window_minutes` to both ledgers; moving a reservation from `HELD` to `ACTIVE` changes neither ledger, because both of those states occupy the interval; and releasing or cancelling a reservation subtracts exactly `window_minutes` from both. A create-then-cancel round trip therefore restores both ledgers to their exact pre-create values with no drift, and so does a create-activate-release round trip. Because `RELEASED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal, the subtraction can never be applied twice.

The second is merchant money, measured in integer cents. Every cent a merchant's orders were ever quoted sits in exactly one of three buckets at all times: `escrowed_fee_cents` for orders still in flight, `recognized_fee_cents` for orders that reached delivery, and `released_fee_cents` for orders that were cancelled or ended in exception. The invariant is that the three buckets together equal the sum of `quoted_fee_cents` over every delivery order the merchant ever created. Accepting an order moves its quote into escrow; delivery moves the same amount from escrow to recognised; an exception or a cancellation moves it from escrow to released; and a redelivery remedy, which sends the order back to the start of its lifecycle, moves the amount from released back into escrow, which is the compensating direction of the same conserved quantity. Separately from the invariant, `settlement_variance_cents` accumulates the signed difference between the settled fee and the quoted fee on each delivery: a reroute that lengthened the haul pushes it positive, and a blockage credit can push it negative. That variance is deliberately outside the three-bucket reconciliation.

A third pair of zone-level quantities is arithmetically derived rather than conserved. `remedy_spent_cents` is the sum of `remedy_amount_cents` over every one of the zone's orders whose remedy has been issued, and `remedy_budget_remaining_cents` is `remedy_budget_cents` less `remedy_spent_cents`; a zone begins life with the remaining budget equal to the authorised budget and every ledger counter at zero. The zone also maintains `open_incident_count`, the number of its incident reports whose `report_state` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED_MUNICIPAL` or `INSURER_NOTIFIED`; `on_mission_robot_count`, the number of its robots whose `duty_state` is `ON_MISSION`; and `lid_code_sequence`, a zone-scoped counter that only ever increases.

Several fields in this system are denormalized mirrors rather than owning references, and the distinction matters because a mirror is maintained by cascade and may be cleared without the caller ever addressing the row that carries it. A robot's `active_mission_id` mirrors the mission that currently occupies it and is null whenever its `duty_state` is not `ON_MISSION`; its `current_bay_reservation_id` mirrors the reservation authorising a charging or maintenance occupation and is null otherwise. A mission's `active_route_plan_id` mirrors whichever of its plans is currently active, and its `blocking_segment_id` mirrors the segment that caused a blockage. A segment's `blocked_by_closure_id` mirrors the hard closure that forced it out of service. A plan's `supersedes_plan_id` mirrors the plan it displaced when it became active, and is null for the first active plan of a mission.

Cascades follow two discipline rules everywhere they appear. Whenever a cascade touches a set of rows rather than a single row, that set is processed in `created_at` ascending order, so the observable sequence of effects is deterministic. And whenever a cascade mutates a row, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is increased by exactly 1, exactly as if the caller had addressed it directly — which is why a single closure activation can advance the `version` of a segment, several route plans, several missions, several orders, several merchants and the zone itself.

### 2.9. Path Identifiers, Reference Resolution and Reverse Lookups

Every collection in this API sits at the root of the URL space; no entity is nested inside another entity's path. The only paths with more than one level are action sub-paths on a single resource and the computed economics read on a zone. Path variables are named after the entity they address: `zone_id`, `segment_id`, `closure_id`, `merchant_id`, `robot_id`, `bay_id`, `reservation_id`, `order_id`, `mission_id`, `plan_id`, `event_id` and `report_id`. When a request carries several identifiers — whether in the path or in the body — they are resolved strictly left to right, path before body, and the first identifier that resolves to no row decides the rejection. Each entity has its own not-found code, all of them HTTP 404: `ERR_ZONE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLOSURE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MERCHANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROBOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BAY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MISSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INTERVENTION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`. Segment resolution is exhaustive rather than shallow: every element of a submitted route sequence and every element of a submitted adjacency array must resolve, and an unresolvable element yields the segment not-found code.

Because relationships are expressed as plain references, every reference is navigable in reverse through a filter on the referenced entity's child collection. Zones expose their sidewalk network, their merchants, their fleet and their depot through a `zone_id` filter on the segment, merchant, robot and depot-bay lists; incident reports are additionally filterable by `zone_id`, `mission_id` and `robot_id`. Segments expose their closures through a `segment_id` filter. Bays and robots expose their reservations through `bay_id` and `robot_id`. Merchants and dropoff segments expose their orders through `merchant_id` and `dropoff_segment_id`. Orders and robots expose their missions through `order_id` and `robot_id`. Missions expose their plan history through `mission_id` on the route-plan list and their takeovers through `mission_id` on the intervention list. State-valued filters are available alongside these: `permit_state` and `municipality_code` on zones; `status` and `segment_type` on segments; `state`, `closure_kind` and `cause` on closures; `integration_state` and `merchant_grade` on merchants; `duty_state`, `health_state` and `eligible_only` on robots; `bay_type` and `state` on depot bays; `state` and `reservation_type` on reservations; `order_state` and `exception_reason` on orders; `mission_state` on missions; `plan_state` on plans; `takeover_state`, `trigger_reason` and `mandatory` on interventions; and `report_state`, `severity` and `category` on incident reports.

### 2.10. The Rejection Ladder

When a single request violates more than one rule, the outcome is decided by a fixed ladder rather than by the order in which an implementation happens to evaluate its checks. The ladder is walked from the top and stops at the first violated rule, and its bands are, in order: authentication and authorisation; request-payload syntax; existence of referenced rows; parent-version pinning; state of the target row and of the rows it depends on; safety and ordinance compliance; structural consistency of the submitted graph; and finally capacity, caps and budgets.

Within the authorisation band the order is: missing or empty role header, then role-not-permitted, then merchant ownership, then incident segregation of duties, then the recorded-reviewer check on a return-for-detail, then the takeover-control check on an escalate or operator release.

Within the payload band, all of whose findings carry `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at HTTP 400, the order is: required fields present; enum values recognised; numeric ranges respected; the payload-volume upper bound; datetime format; interval endpoint ordering; the action being one the endpoint declares; the fields that a particular action requires; the parent-version field being present at all; and the well-formedness of a submitted segment sequence or adjacency array.

The existence band resolves references in the order zone, segment, closure, merchant, robot, bay, reservation, order, mission, route plan, intervention event, incident report. The version-pinning band follows, in the order described in §2.5. The state band then evaluates, in order: whether the requested action is a declared transition out of the target row's current state; whether the zone permits operations; whether the merchant's integration is active; whether the named robot's duty state admits dispatch; whether its health state admits dispatch; whether every segment of a plan is approved; whether any segment of a plan is blocked; whether the mission is already terminal; whether a reservation is live enough for the requested transition; whether an open takeover blocks a handoff; whether a remedy has already been issued; and whether the order has a latched exception at all.

The safety band evaluates route clearance, then crossing risk, then robot speed against the ordinance, then operating-window containment, then a segment's measured clearance against the ordinance minimum at approval time. Accessibility and ordinance findings are deliberately reported ahead of the structural band, so a plan that is both too tight for its robot and mis-sequenced reports the clearance failure. The structural band then evaluates route contiguity, then whether the route terminates at the order's dropoff segment, then the four cross-zone consistency checks — plan segments against the mission robot's zone, an order's dropoff segment against its merchant's zone, a mission's robot against the order's zone, and a reservation's bay against the robot's zone — and finally whether a submitted adjacency array stays inside one zone.

The last band covers capacity and money, in the order: payload against container capacity; the dispatch eligibility filter and the empty-pool case; the lid access code; the zone's concurrent-robot cap; bay window exclusivity; hard closure overlap; the zone remedy budget; the outstanding-incident condition on clearing a quarantine; reservation type against bay type; and finally whether the bay is in service at all.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Mapping the Sidewalk Network and Permitting a Service Zone

Nothing can happen in a zone until somebody has walked its sidewalks. A `PlatformAdmin` provisions the zone first, with the ordinance terms and planning assumptions described in §2.7; the zone lands in `permit_state` `PROVISIONED`, every derived ledger at zero, `remedy_budget_remaining_cents` equal to the authorised `remedy_budget_cents`, `lid_code_sequence` at zero and `restriction_reason` null.

Mapping then proceeds one sidewalk element at a time. Each element is recorded against its `zone_id` with a survey code in `segment_code`, which must be unique among the segments of that zone, and a `segment_type` of `WALKWAY`, `CROSSING` or `RAMP`. Its physical description comprises `length_m`, the centreline length used later by route distance and duration arithmetic, which must be greater than zero; `measured_clearance_cm`, the narrowest passable clearance on the element, also greater than zero; and `surface_grade_pct`, the steepest running grade as a percentage, between 0.0 and 25.0. A `crossing_control` value describes the traffic-control regime — `NONE`, `SIGNALIZED`, `UNSIGNALIZED` or `MIDBLOCK` — and is stored on every element even though it is only meaningful on a crossing; it defaults to `NONE`. Three observation fields may be recorded at mapping time or deferred to the survey: `sightline_m` and `pedestrian_flow_per_min`, both defaulting to 0.0 and both required to be zero or greater, and the accessibility flag `curb_ramp_present`, defaulting to false. A newly mapped element sits in `status` `MAPPED` with `crossing_risk_score` still null and `blocked_by_closure_id` null.

The sidewalk graph itself is expressed through `connected_segment_ids`, the set of segments reachable from this one in a single hop. It defaults to an empty set, and the server enforces symmetric closure: naming a neighbour when a segment is mapped also adds the new segment's own id to that neighbour's own `connected_segment_ids`, and each such neighbour is a mutation in its own right, so each of their versions advances by one. Three well-formedness rules apply to the array. It may not contain the segment's own id, which is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; every element must resolve to a real segment, and an unresolvable element is refused with `ERR_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`; and every named neighbour must sit in the same zone as the segment being mapped, with a cross-zone neighbour refused as `ERR_ZONE_MISMATCH`.

Surveying is the act that turns a mapped element into a scored one, moving `status` from `MAPPED` to `SURVEYED`. The surveyor may override any of `sightline_m`, `pedestrian_flow_per_min`, `curb_ramp_present` and `measured_clearance_cm`; each of the four is optional, and an omitted field keeps whatever value was recorded when the element was mapped, so a survey that supplies no overrides simply scores the mapped values. From the resulting values the server computes and freezes `crossing_risk_score`, and the element's `version` advances by one.

That score is the sum of five terms. The sightline term is a step function of `sightline_m`: it contributes 0 when the sightline is at least 30 metres, 8 when it is at least 20 but less than 30, 18 when it is at least 10 but less than 20, and 30 when it is below 10 — so a sightline of 30.0 and a sightline of 39.9 both contribute 0 while 29.9 contributes 8. The pedestrian-flow term is `pedestrian_flow_per_min` multiplied by 1.4, but never more than 28; whichever of the two is smaller is the term. The control term is read from `crossing_control`: `NONE` contributes 0, `SIGNALIZED` contributes negative 12, `UNSIGNALIZED` contributes positive 10 and `MIDBLOCK` contributes positive 22. The grade term is the amount by which `surface_grade_pct` exceeds 5.0 percent, multiplied by 3.0, and it is exactly 0 for any grade up to and including 5.0 percent because the excess is treated as zero when the grade does not exceed the hinge. The ramp term is negative 6 when `curb_ramp_present` is true and 0 otherwise. Those five terms are added together, the total is then clamped into the range 0.0 through 100.0, and only after clamping is it rounded half up to one decimal place — which is why a raw total of negative 6.0 stores as 0.0 rather than as a negative score.

Approval is the gate that admits a segment to routing, moving `status` from `SURVEYED` to `APPROVED`, and it enforces exactly two compliance findings. If the element's `measured_clearance_cm` is below the zone's `ordinance_min_clearance_cm` the approval is refused with `ERR_CLEARANCE_INSUFFICIENT`; the comparison is inclusive, so a clearance exactly equal to the ordinance minimum passes. If the frozen `crossing_risk_score` is above the zone's `max_crossing_risk` the approval is refused with `ERR_CROSSING_RISK_EXCEEDED`; that comparison is inclusive too, so a score exactly equal to the ceiling passes. Both findings carry HTTP 422.

Retirement removes an element from service permanently and is available from either `SURVEYED` or `APPROVED`. It is refused while any route plan whose `plan_state` is `ACTIVE` still names the segment in its sequence. When it succeeds, `status` becomes the terminal `RETIRED` and the graph heals around the gap: the retired segment's id is removed from the `connected_segment_ids` of every neighbour that carried it, those neighbours are processed in `created_at` ascending order, and each of their versions advances by one alongside the retired segment's own.

Permitting is the zone's own lifecycle and it is entirely a `PlatformAdmin` concern, driven by four action names: `ACTIVATE_PERMIT`, `RESTRICT`, `LIFT_RESTRICTION` and `REVOKE`. The `ACTIVATE_PERMIT` step carries the zone from `PROVISIONED` to `PERMITTED`, and it requires that at least one segment in the zone has already reached `APPROVED` — a zone whose network has been mapped but never approved cannot be permitted, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Activation also clears `restriction_reason` back to null. From `PERMITTED`, a restriction step requires a `reason`, moves the zone to `RESTRICTED` and records that text in `restriction_reason`; critically, it has no effect whatsoever on missions already in flight, so a mission that is already en route or blocked continues, and only new dispatch is refused, with `ERR_ZONE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Lifting the restriction returns the zone from `RESTRICTED` to `PERMITTED` and clears `restriction_reason` to null again, closing the `PERMITTED → RESTRICTED → PERMITTED` cycle. Each of these three steps advances the zone's `version` by one.

Revocation is the destructive step, and it is available from either `PERMITTED` or `RESTRICTED`. It requires a `reason`, sets `permit_state` to the terminal `REVOKED`, records the reason in `restriction_reason`, and then fans out across the zone. Every mission whose robot is homed to the zone and whose `mission_state` is `PLANNED`, `LOADED`, `EN_ROUTE` or `BLOCKED` is driven to `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` `ZONE_REVOKED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. For each such mission the delivery order moves to `EXCEPTION` with `exception_reason` `BLOCKED_ROUTE`, the order's quoted amount leaves the merchant's `escrowed_fee_cents` and enters its `released_fee_cents`, and the merchant's `exception_count` advances by one. Each affected robot then follows its ordinary route out of `ON_MISSION` to `AVAILABLE` because its mission reached a terminal state, its `active_mission_id` is cleared to null, and the zone's `on_mission_robot_count` is decremented once per robot. The zone's own `version` advances by one.

### 3.2. Closures, Blockages and the Five-Link Reroute Cascade

Construction crews, storms, street festivals and vandals all take sidewalk out of service, and a `PlatformAdmin` records each of those events as a closure over a single segment. A closure names its `segment_id`, a `closure_kind` of either `HARD` or `ADVISORY`, a `cause` of `CONSTRUCTION`, `WEATHER`, `EVENT` or `VANDALISM`, and the fully closed interval `starts_at_utc` through `ends_at_utc`, both instants inclusive. The end must be strictly after the start; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A new closure lands in `state` `SCHEDULED` with `reroute_triggered_count` at zero. The distinction between the two kinds is total: a `HARD` closure blocks the segment and forces reroutes, while an `ADVISORY` closure is purely informational and never changes a segment's status.

Hard closures of the same segment may not overlap one another. When a hard closure is scheduled, its interval is compared against the interval of every existing closure of the same segment that is also `HARD` and whose `state` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE`; a conflict exists when the new closure's start is at or before the existing closure's end and the new closure's end is at or after the existing closure's start. Because both interval endpoints are inclusive and no buffer is applied, two hard closures that merely touch at a shared instant do conflict, and the request is refused with `ERR_HARD_CLOSURE_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Advisory closures are outside the scope of this test entirely: an advisory closure never conflicts with anything, in either direction.

Activation is only permitted while the closure is still `SCHEDULED` and only while the logical clock sits inside the closed interval — that is, the current instant must be at or after `starts_at_utc` and at or before `ends_at_utc`. Activation is also the head of the longest cascade in this system, and its five links fire in a fixed order.

The first link sets the closure's own `state` to `ACTIVE`. The second link fires only when `closure_kind` is `HARD`: the segment moves from `APPROVED` to `BLOCKED` and its `blocked_by_closure_id` is pointed at the activating closure. An advisory activation stops after the first link, leaving the segment's `status` untouched and no further link firing at all. The third link is caused by the second: every route plan whose `plan_state` is `ACTIVE` and whose sequence contains the newly blocked segment is driven to `SUPERSEDED` with `rejection_reason` `SEGMENT_BLOCKED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, and the closure's `reroute_triggered_count` advances by one for each plan it superseded. The fourth link is caused by the third: each superseded plan's mission, if its `mission_state` is `EN_ROUTE`, moves to `BLOCKED`, records the closed segment in `blocking_segment_id`, stamps `blocked_at_utc` with the current instant, and has its `active_route_plan_id` cleared to null. The fifth link is caused by the fourth: each newly blocked mission's delivery order, if its `order_state` is `IN_TRANSIT`, moves to `EXCEPTION` with `exception_reason` derived from the closure's `cause` — a `WEATHER` cause latches `WEATHER`, a `VANDALISM` cause latches `VANDALISM`, and both `CONSTRUCTION` and `EVENT` latch `BLOCKED_ROUTE`. For each such order the quoted amount leaves the merchant's `escrowed_fee_cents` and enters its `released_fee_cents`, and the merchant's `exception_count` advances by one. Finally the closure's own `version` advances by one, as does the `version` of every row the cascade touched on its way through.

Lifting a closure is the reverse operation, and it behaves differently depending on where the closure had got to. Lifting an `ACTIVE` closure sets `state` to the terminal `LIFTED`; then, when the lifted closure was `HARD` and no other closure on the same segment is both `HARD` and `ACTIVE`, the segment returns from `BLOCKED` to `APPROVED` and its `blocked_by_closure_id` is cleared to null. A segment held down by two overlapping-in-effect hard closures therefore stays `BLOCKED` until the last of them is lifted, which closes the `APPROVED → BLOCKED → APPROVED` cycle. Lifting a closure that is still `SCHEDULED` also sets `state` to `LIFTED` but produces no segment and no route side effects at all, because the closure never became active. Either way the closure's `version` advances by one.

Two deliberate non-effects deserve emphasis, because they are the most common source of wrong assumptions. Lifting a closure never resumes a mission: a mission that was driven to `BLOCKED` stays `BLOCKED` until a replacement route plan has been created and activated and an operator explicitly resumes it. And lifting a closure never restores an order: an order that the fifth link pushed into `EXCEPTION` stays there, and the only path back into the delivery pipeline is a redelivery remedy.

### 3.3. Merchant Integration, Order Intake and the Fee Quote

A merchant is onboarded into exactly one zone. A `PlatformAdmin` supplies the merchant's `zone_id` and trading `name` of 1 to 120 characters, and may supply two commercial terms: `handoff_sla_seconds`, the number of seconds allowed between mission creation and the container handoff before the handoff counts as late, which must be zero or greater and defaults to 300; and `merchant_grade`, one of `STANDARD`, `PREFERRED` or `ANCHOR`, defaulting to `STANDARD`. A new merchant lands in `integration_state` `PENDING` with `escrowed_fee_cents`, `recognized_fee_cents`, `released_fee_cents`, `settlement_variance_cents` and `exception_count` all at zero, and it cannot receive orders in that state.

Integration has three transitions, all driven by a `PlatformAdmin`. Activation carries the merchant from `PENDING` to `ACTIVE` and requires the merchant's zone to be in `permit_state` `PERMITTED`; a merchant in a `PROVISIONED`, `RESTRICTED` or `REVOKED` zone cannot be activated and the attempt is refused with `ERR_ZONE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Suspension carries the merchant from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`; it has no effect on orders that already exist, and only new order intake is refused, with `ERR_MERCHANT_NOT_ACTIVE`. Reinstatement carries the merchant from `SUSPENDED` back to `ACTIVE` and, like activation, requires the zone to be `PERMITTED`. Each transition advances the merchant's `version` by one. `Merchant` has no terminal state — a suspended merchant can always be brought back.

Order intake belongs to the merchant's own staff, and a `PlatformAdmin` may also perform it on any merchant's behalf without being subject to the ownership restriction. The payload names the `merchant_id` that originated the order, the `dropoff_segment_id` on which the customer will take delivery, a `customer_ref` of 1 to 64 characters used later for remedy correspondence, the declared cargo volume `payload_liters`, which must be greater than zero, the customer-declared cargo value `declared_value_cents`, which must be zero or greater, and `promised_by_utc`, the promised delivery instant, which must be strictly after the current logical-clock instant. A volume above 45.0 litres has no payload tier and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The merchant must be `ACTIVE`, or the intake is refused with `ERR_MERCHANT_NOT_ACTIVE`; and the dropoff segment must sit in the merchant's own zone, or the intake is refused with `ERR_ZONE_MISMATCH`.

Three values are frozen at this moment and never recomputed on the order. `urgency_flag` is true when the interval from the order's `created_at` to its `promised_by_utc` is at most 45 minutes, and false otherwise. `payload_tier` is `SMALL` when `payload_liters` is at most 15.0 litres, `MEDIUM` when it is above 15.0 but at most 30.0, and `LARGE` when it is above 30.0 but at most 45.0. And `quoted_fee_cents` is the forward-looking evaluation of the delivery fee. The order itself lands in `order_state` `RECEIVED`, with `settled_fee_cents`, `exception_reason`, `remedy_kind`, `remedy_reason_code` and `remedy_amount_cents` all null and `dispatch_attempt_count` at zero. Immediately afterwards the quoted amount is escrowed: the merchant's `escrowed_fee_cents` increases by `quoted_fee_cents` and the merchant's `version` advances by one.

The delivery fee is one formula evaluated twice in an order's life from deliberately different input sources. It is assembled as follows. The distance term first converts distance into whole 100-metre units, rounding any partial unit up, and charges 22 cents per unit. To that is added a base fee of 349 cents; the payload-tier supplement, which is 0 cents for `SMALL`, 120 cents for `MEDIUM` and 260 cents for `LARGE`; an urgency surcharge of 180 cents when `urgency_flag` is true and nothing when it is false; 35 cents for each crossing traversed; and 95 cents for each remote intervention counted against the delivery. That total is the gross fee. A blockage credit is then computed as 8 cents for each whole minute the delivery spent blocked, but the credit is capped at 40 percent of the gross fee with any fraction of a cent in that cap discarded; whichever of the two amounts is smaller is the credit that applies. The credit is subtracted from the gross fee, and the merchant grade discount is applied exactly once to that whole remaining basis — nothing for `STANDARD`, 5 percent off for `PREFERRED` and 12 percent off for `ANCHOR` — never per component. The result is rounded half up to whole cents at that single final step, with no intermediate rounding anywhere in the chain.

At intake there is no mission and no route plan, so the quote is built entirely from the zone's planning assumptions: the distance is the `planning_haul_m` of the zone that owns the dropoff segment, the crossing count is that zone's `planning_crossings`, and both the intervention count and the blocked minutes are treated as zero. The second evaluation, described in §3.7, replaces all four of those inputs with what the mission actually accumulated.

A merchant may cancel an order, but only while it is still `RECEIVED`, and the request must carry a `reason`. Cancellation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` from any other state, and a `MerchantOperator` cancelling another merchant's order is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. On success `order_state` becomes the terminal `CANCELLED`, the quoted amount leaves `escrowed_fee_cents` and enters `released_fee_cents` — the compensating half of the merchant fee invariant, after which escrow has returned to its pre-intake value while the three buckets together still equal the sum of `quoted_fee_cents` over every order the merchant ever created — and both the merchant's and the order's versions advance by one.

### 3.4. Fleet Registration, the Duty Cycle and Depot Bay Capacity

A robot is registered into exactly one zone by a `PlatformAdmin`, who supplies the homing `zone_id`, the fleet `asset_tag`, which must be unique across the whole fleet and not merely within the zone, the widest chassis dimension `width_cm`, the usable container volume `container_capacity_liters` and the maximum commanded ground speed `max_speed_cm_s`. The three physical measurements must each be greater than zero. Registration is also the first of two places where the zone ordinance is enforced against the machine: a robot whose `max_speed_cm_s` exceeds the zone's `ordinance_max_speed_cm_s` cannot be registered at all and the attempt is refused with `ERR_ORDINANCE_SPEED_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). The comparison is inclusive in the robot's favour, so a robot built for exactly the posted limit is acceptable. A newly registered robot has `duty_state` `OFFLINE`, `health_state` `NOMINAL`, `battery_pct` 100.0, `odometer_m` 0.0, `yield_event_count` 0, `tamper_flag` false, `lifetime_mission_count`, `lifetime_intervention_count`, `lifetime_escalation_count` and `clean_mission_count` all zero, both `rolling_autonomy_index` and `lifetime_autonomy_index` at 100, and `active_mission_id` and `current_bay_reservation_id` both null.

Every robot carries two independent state dimensions on the same row, and they advance in parallel rather than in sequence. `duty_state` records what the robot is doing and is driven by a `FleetDispatcher`; `health_state` records whether the robot is fit for service and is driven by a `SafetyOfficer`, as described in §3.5. Neither dimension is a stage of the other: a robot can be `DEGRADED` while `ON_MISSION`, or `NOMINAL` while `RETIRED`. Only one interaction crosses the two dimensions, the quarantine cascade, and it runs in one direction only — a health transition can force a duty transition, while a duty transition never changes `health_state`.

The duty cycle begins when a dispatcher stages an `OFFLINE` robot with the `STAGE` action, which carries it to `STAGING`. Three preconditions apply: the robot's zone must be in `permit_state` `PERMITTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ZONE_NOT_PERMITTED`; the robot's `max_speed_cm_s` must still be within the zone's `ordinance_max_speed_cm_s`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ORDINANCE_SPEED_EXCEEDED`; and the robot must not be `QUARANTINED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ROBOT_HEALTH_BLOCKED`. Note that staging tolerates `DEGRADED` and even `IMPAIRED` health — only quarantine blocks it. The `MAKE_AVAILABLE` action then carries the robot from `STAGING` to `AVAILABLE`, and here the health gate tightens: `health_state` must be `NOMINAL` or `DEGRADED`, so an `IMPAIRED` robot may be staged but may not be made available, and `battery_pct` must be at least 35.0 percent, inclusive at that boundary. Entry into `ON_MISSION` and exit from it are never requested directly by anyone: dispatch puts the robot there and mission termination brings it back, both described in §3.6 and §3.7.

Depot occupation is the other half of the duty cycle. The `BEGIN_CHARGE` action carries an `AVAILABLE` robot to `CHARGING` and requires a `bay_reservation_id` in the request body naming a reservation that exists, is in `state` `ACTIVE`, has `reservation_type` `CHARGE` and has a `robot_id` equal to this robot; any of those four conditions failing is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). On success the robot's `current_bay_reservation_id` is pointed at that reservation. `BEGIN_MAINTENANCE` is the exactly symmetric action for `MAINTENANCE`, differing in only one respect: the named reservation's `reservation_type` must be `SERVICE` rather than `CHARGE`. Coming back out of either state uses the same `MAKE_AVAILABLE` action with the same two gates — `health_state` in `NOMINAL` or `DEGRADED` and `battery_pct` at least 35.0 percent — and releases the depot resource: the reservation named by `current_bay_reservation_id` moves to `RELEASED`, the bay's `committed_minutes` and the zone's `reserved_bay_minutes` each decrease by that reservation's `window_minutes`, and `current_bay_reservation_id` is cleared to null. The `MAINTENANCE` exit differs from the `CHARGING` exit in one detail that matters: it performs the release only when `current_bay_reservation_id` is not null, because a robot parked in `MAINTENANCE` by the quarantine cascade never held a reservation in the first place.

`RETIRE` is the only way into the terminal `RETIRED` duty state and is permitted from `OFFLINE`, `STAGING`, `AVAILABLE`, `CHARGING` or `MAINTENANCE` — that is, from every duty state except `ON_MISSION`, since retiring a robot mid-delivery is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Retirement also sweeps the robot's depot footprint: every reservation of that robot whose `state` is `HELD` or `ACTIVE` moves to `CANCELLED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, and each one returns its own `window_minutes` to both the bay's `committed_minutes` and the zone's `reserved_bay_minutes`.

Depot bays are provisioned separately by a `PlatformAdmin`, who supplies the `zone_id` of the zone whose fleet the bay serves, a `bay_code` that must be unique within that zone, the `bay_type` of `CHARGE`, `STAGE` or `SERVICE`, an optional charger output `power_kw` that must be zero or greater and defaults to 0.0, and an optional `state` of `OPEN` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE` defaulting to `OPEN`. A bay's `state` is fixed at provisioning and there is no transition endpoint for it; an out-of-service bay simply refuses every reservation for its whole life. A new bay starts with `committed_minutes` at zero.

Reserving a bay window is a dispatcher action and is the one write in this system whose optimistic concurrency token names the parent rather than the row being changed: the request must carry `bay_id`, `robot_id`, `reservation_type`, `window_start_utc`, `window_end_utc` and `expected_depot_bay_version`, and that expected version must equal the current `version` of the named `DepotBay` or the write is refused with `ERR_STALE_BAY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Three consistency gates then apply in order of the rejection ladder: the bay's `state` must be `OPEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_BAY_OUT_OF_SERVICE` (HTTP 409); the requested `reservation_type` must be exactly equal to the bay's `bay_type`, so a `CHARGE` reservation on a `SERVICE` bay is refused with `ERR_BAY_TYPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422); and the bay's `zone_id` must equal the robot's `zone_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ZONE_MISMATCH`, because a robot may never be parked in another zone's depot. The window itself must have `window_end_utc` strictly after `window_start_utc`, and the interval is half-open: the start instant is inside the occupation and the end instant is not. From that window the server derives `window_minutes` as the number of whole minutes from start to end, discarding any partial minute, and that value is frozen for the life of the reservation even though the window fields themselves are also immutable.

Bay windows are exclusive, and the exclusivity test is not a bare interval overlap: each existing reservation of the same bay whose `state` is `HELD` or `ACTIVE` is first inflated by a turnaround buffer of 15 minutes on both sides, to allow a robot to vacate and the next one to arrive. A conflict therefore exists when the requested `window_start_utc` is earlier than the existing reservation's `window_end_utc` advanced by 15 minutes, and at the same time the requested `window_end_utc` advanced by 15 minutes is later than the existing reservation's `window_start_utc`. A conflict is refused with `ERR_BAY_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Because the comparisons are strict at both ends, a window that starts exactly 15 minutes after an existing window ends is accepted, while one that starts 14 minutes after it ends is rejected. Reservations already in `RELEASED` or `CANCELLED` are invisible to this test, so a cancelled booking frees its slot immediately.

Creating a reservation books its minutes into two denormalized ledgers at once: the bay's `committed_minutes` increases by `window_minutes` and the zone's `reserved_bay_minutes` increases by the same amount, with the `version` of the bay, the zone and the new reservation each advancing by one. Those two ledgers are conserved quantities. At every instant a bay's `committed_minutes` equals the sum of `window_minutes` over that bay's reservations whose `state` is `HELD` or `ACTIVE`, and a zone's `reserved_bay_minutes` equals the sum of `committed_minutes` over that zone's bays, which is the same as the sum of `window_minutes` over every live reservation anywhere in the zone.

The reservation lifecycle has three requested actions. `ACTIVATE` carries a reservation from `HELD` to `ACTIVE` and requires the logical clock to be inside the half-open window — the current instant at or after `window_start_utc` and strictly before `window_end_utc` — and the bay's `state` to still be `OPEN`, or the activation is refused with `ERR_BAY_OUT_OF_SERVICE`. Activation deliberately changes neither ledger, because `HELD` and `ACTIVE` are both live occupation states and the minutes were already booked at create time. `RELEASE` carries a reservation from `ACTIVE` to the terminal `RELEASED`, and `CANCEL` carries a reservation from `HELD` to the terminal `CANCELLED`; each of those two subtracts exactly `window_minutes` from both the bay's `committed_minutes` and the zone's `reserved_bay_minutes`, advances the bay's and the zone's `version` by one each, and clears the robot's `current_bay_reservation_id` when it was pointing at that reservation. A `RELEASE` aimed at a reservation that is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_ACTIVE`. Because `RELEASED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal, the subtraction can never be applied twice, which is what makes both round trips exact: create-then-cancel and create-activate-release each return both ledgers to their precise pre-create values with no drift.

The fleet is read back through the robot, bay and reservation list endpoints. Robots may be filtered by `zone_id`, which is the reverse lookup from a zone to its fleet, by `duty_state`, by `health_state` and by the `eligible_only` flag described in §3.6. Bays may be filtered by `zone_id`, `bay_type` and `state`, and reservations by `bay_id`, `robot_id`, `state` and `reservation_type`, `bay_id` and `robot_id` being the two reverse lookups into a reservation's parents.

### 3.5. Robot Health, Telemetry and the Autonomy Index

Robots report their own condition through a telemetry frame, which either a `FleetDispatcher` or a `RemoteOperator` may ingest. Every field of a frame is optional: `battery_pct` replaces the stored state of charge outright, `odometer_delta_m` is added to the stored `odometer_m`, `yield_event_delta` is added to the stored `yield_event_count`, and `tamper_flag` reports physical interference. A frame is the only writer of `battery_pct`, `odometer_m` and `yield_event_count`; no other endpoint touches them. Ingestion advances the robot's `version` by one.

Two health consequences follow automatically from a frame, and each is exactly the same transition a `SafetyOfficer` could have requested by hand. A frame that leaves `battery_pct` at 20.0 percent or below downgrades a `NOMINAL` robot to `DEGRADED`; the threshold is inclusive, and because the transition is defined only from `NOMINAL`, a robot already `DEGRADED`, `IMPAIRED` or `QUARANTINED` is left where it is. A frame reporting `tamper_flag` true drives the robot to `QUARANTINED` from whatever health state it was in and latches `tamper_flag` true on the row, with the full cascade below. Both consequences can fire from a single frame.

Health is driven explicitly by a `SafetyOfficer` supplying an `action` and an optional `reason`. `DOWNGRADE` carries `NOMINAL` to `DEGRADED`. `IMPAIR` carries `DEGRADED` to `IMPAIRED`; an impaired robot is no longer eligible for dispatch, but any mission it is already running continues untouched, which is the clearest illustration of the two dimensions being independent. `QUARANTINE` carries `NOMINAL`, `DEGRADED` or `IMPAIRED` to `QUARANTINED`. `CLEAR` is the single action name that serves two different recoveries: from `IMPAIRED` it steps back only as far as `DEGRADED`, while from `QUARANTINED` it returns all the way to `NOMINAL` and also resets `tamper_flag` to false. `health_state` has no terminal state, so a robot can be quarantined and cleared any number of times. Every health transition advances the robot's `version` by one and, apart from the quarantine cascade, leaves `duty_state` exactly as it was.

Quarantine is the head of a four-link cascade. The first link sets `health_state` to `QUARANTINED` and, when the trigger was a telemetry frame, sets `tamper_flag` to true. The second link fires only when the robot's `duty_state` is `ON_MISSION`: the robot moves from `ON_MISSION` to `MAINTENANCE` rather than to `AVAILABLE`, its `active_mission_id` is cleared to null, the mission that pointer named is driven to the terminal `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` `ROBOT_QUARANTINED`, and the zone's `on_mission_robot_count` is decremented by one. The third link is caused by the second and acts on that mission's delivery order: `order_state` becomes `EXCEPTION`, `exception_reason` becomes `VANDALISM` when the robot's `tamper_flag` now stands true and `ROBOT_FAULT` when it stands false, the order's quoted amount moves out of the merchant's `escrowed_fee_cents` and into its `released_fee_cents`, and the merchant's `exception_count` advances by one. The same link also files an incident automatically: a new `IncidentReport` is created in `report_state` `DRAFT` with `category` `VANDALISM`, `severity` `SERIOUS`, `filed_by_user_id` set to the literal string `SYSTEM`, `zone_id` copied from the robot, `mission_id` set to the aborted mission, `robot_id` set to this robot and `estimated_damage_cents` at 0. The fourth link is caused by the third and advances the zone's `open_incident_count` by one along with the zone's `version`. A robot quarantined while `OFFLINE`, `STAGING`, `AVAILABLE`, `CHARGING` or `MAINTENANCE` stops after the first link: with no mission to abort, links two through four have nothing to act on.

Clearing a quarantine is deliberately harder than imposing one. Beyond requiring `health_state` to be `QUARANTINED`, the `CLEAR` action requires that the robot is not `ON_MISSION` and that no `IncidentReport` naming this robot is still sitting in `report_state` `DRAFT`; an unfiled report blocks the clear with `ERR_MANDATORY_INCIDENT_UNFILED` (HTTP 409). Since the quarantine cascade itself files a `DRAFT` report, the normal recovery path is necessarily two-sided: a `SafetyOfficer` must submit the incident before the same officer can return the robot to `NOMINAL`. Clearing does not restore duty either — a robot the cascade parked in `MAINTENANCE` stays in `MAINTENANCE` until a dispatcher explicitly makes it available again.

Fleet quality is tracked by an autonomy index in the closed range 40 to 100, of which every robot carries two variants that are both recomputed whenever one of that robot's missions reaches a terminal state. The per-mission arithmetic is shared. Each terminal mission contributes a penalty of 6 points for every remote intervention it recorded plus a further 4 points for every one of those interventions that was escalated, so a mission with one plain intervention costs 6 points while a mission with one escalated intervention costs 10. Each terminal mission that recorded no intervention at all instead contributes a credit of 2 points; a mission with interventions earns no credit. Both variants start from a base of 100, apply their contributions, then clamp the result into the range 40 to 100 and round half up to a whole number, with the clamp and the rounding applied only once at the end.

The two variants differ in which missions they consider and how heavily each one counts. The rolling variant, stored in `rolling_autonomy_index`, is a recency-weighted window: the robot's terminal missions are ordered by `completed_at_utc` descending with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, the newest five are kept and the rest ignored, and the mission in position zero of that order counts at full weight while each subsequent position counts at 0.85 times the weight of the position before it. Concretely the weights are 1, then 0.85, then 0.85 multiplied by itself, and so on for the five positions, and each mission's credit minus its penalty is multiplied by its own weight before the sum is added to the base of 100. The curve restarts from the newest mission on every recomputation, so a fresh clean mission immediately dilutes an older intervention rather than merely adding to it. The lifetime variant, stored in `lifetime_autonomy_index`, weights every terminal mission the robot has ever run at exactly 1 and is expressed through the stored counters: from the base of 100, subtract 6 points for each unit of `lifetime_intervention_count` and 4 more for each unit of `lifetime_escalation_count`, then add 2 points for each unit of `clean_mission_count`. Order is irrelevant to the lifetime variant, which is precisely why the two variants diverge for any robot whose history is not uniform. Only `rolling_autonomy_index` gates dispatch; `lifetime_autonomy_index` is reported by the fleet read endpoints and never used as a gate.

### 3.6. Dispatch and Route Planning

Dispatch is a `FleetDispatcher` action that binds a specific robot to a specific order and creates the mission that will carry it. The request names `order_id`, `robot_id` and `expected_delivery_order_version`; the optimistic concurrency token is on the parent order, so the expected version must equal the order's current `version` or the dispatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The order must be in `order_state` `RECEIVED`, which is true both of a freshly created order and of an order returned to `RECEIVED` by a redelivery remedy; any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

The robot is never chosen by the server. The dispatcher supplies it explicitly, and the named robot must satisfy every clause of one eligibility filter, whose seven conditions are each answered by their own error so a rejection tells the dispatcher which clause failed. The robot's `zone_id` must equal the `zone_id` of the order's dropoff segment, or the dispatch is refused with `ERR_ZONE_MISMATCH`. Its `duty_state` must be `AVAILABLE`, or the dispatch is refused with `ERR_ROBOT_NOT_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 409) — a robot that is `OFFLINE`, `STAGING`, already `ON_MISSION`, `CHARGING`, in `MAINTENANCE` or `RETIRED` is out. Its `health_state` must be `NOMINAL` or `DEGRADED`, or the dispatch is refused with `ERR_ROBOT_HEALTH_BLOCKED` (HTTP 409). Its `container_capacity_liters` must be at least the order's `payload_liters`, or the dispatch is refused with `ERR_PAYLOAD_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Its `max_speed_cm_s` must not exceed the zone's `ordinance_max_speed_cm_s`, or the dispatch is refused with `ERR_ORDINANCE_SPEED_EXCEEDED`. The two remaining clauses share one error: `battery_pct` must be at least 35.0 percent and `rolling_autonomy_index` must be at least 70, and a failure of either, or a zone in which no robot at all satisfies the whole filter, is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ROBOT` (HTTP 422). Every comparison in the filter is inclusive at its boundary, so a battery of exactly 35.0 and an index of exactly 70 are both eligible.

The same filter has a read-side face. The robot list endpoint accepts `eligible_only`, and when that flag is true the list is narrowed to the eligible pool and ordered by `rolling_autonomy_index` descending, then `battery_pct` descending, then `created_at` ascending; the `id` is never consulted for ordering, even as a last-resort tie-break. The read side is deliberately gentler than the write side about emptiness: an empty eligible pool is reported as an empty list with a 200 status, and only the dispatch write treats it as `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ROBOT`.

One further condition is not about the robot at all but about the zone's collective load. When a robot is taken onto a mission, the zone's `on_mission_robot_count` must be strictly below its `max_concurrent_robots`; a zone already at its cap refuses the dispatch with `ERR_ZONE_ROBOT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). This is the one rule where each individual dispatch is perfectly legal and only the aggregate fails, and it is checked at exactly the point where the counter would be incremented.

A successful dispatch writes four rows. The new mission is created in `mission_state` `PLANNED` with `traveled_distance_m`, `traversed_crossing_count`, `blocked_minutes`, `intervention_count`, `escalation_count` and `active_minutes` all at zero and `lid_access_code`, `actual_payload_liters`, `active_route_plan_id`, `blocking_segment_id`, `departed_at_utc`, `blocked_at_utc`, `completed_at_utc` and `abort_reason` all null, with `handoff_late` false. The order moves from `RECEIVED` to `DISPATCHED` and its `dispatch_attempt_count` advances by one, which is why a redelivered order that is dispatched a second time shows a count of two. The robot moves from `AVAILABLE` to `ON_MISSION` and its `active_mission_id` is pointed at the new mission. The zone's `on_mission_robot_count` advances by one. All four rows advance their `version` by one.

A mission in `PLANNED` has no route yet. A route plan is submitted by a `FleetDispatcher` naming `mission_id`, the ordered `segment_sequence` of segment identifiers the robot is to traverse, and `expected_delivery_mission_version`; here the optimistic concurrency token is on the parent mission, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_MISSION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The sequence must be well formed before anything else is examined: it may not be empty and it may not name the same segment twice, and either fault is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while an element that resolves to no row is refused with `ERR_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`. Creation is where all the geometry is derived and all the geometry gates run; the plan then lands in `plan_state` `DRAFT` and must still be validated and activated before the robot may move.

Five values are derived from the sequence at creation. `total_length_m` is the sum of `length_m` over the sequence in the given order, carried to one decimal place. `crossing_count` is the number of elements whose `segment_type` is `CROSSING`. `clearance_margin_cm` is the accessibility slack of the tightest segment for this particular robot: for each segment in the sequence, subtract the robot's `width_cm` and then a further ordinance buffer of 12 centimetres from that segment's `measured_clearance_cm`, and take the smallest of those results, rounded to one decimal place. `planned_duration_min` converts the haul into whole minutes at a derated speed: the robot's `max_speed_cm_s` multiplied by 0.6 gives an effective travel rate in metres per minute, since a rate expressed in centimetres per second multiplied by 0.6 is the same rate expressed in metres per minute, and `total_length_m` divided by that rate is rounded up to the next whole minute — always up, even when the division is exact to a fraction of a minute. `revision_index` is the one-based ordinal of this plan among the plans of the same mission ordered by `created_at` ascending, so a mission's first plan carries `revision_index` 1 and its first replacement carries 2.

Six gates then run in the order fixed by the rejection ladder. The clearance gate is inclusive: a `clearance_margin_cm` of exactly 0.0 passes and any negative margin is refused with `ERR_CLEARANCE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). Because the margin is computed against the mission's own robot, one identical segment sequence can be perfectly legal for a narrow robot and illegal for a wider one. The crossing-risk gate compares the largest `crossing_risk_score` among the submitted segments against the zone's `max_crossing_risk` and refuses anything above it with `ERR_CROSSING_RISK_EXCEEDED`. The operating-window gate consumes `planned_duration_min`: the interval that begins at the current logical-clock instant and ends that many minutes later must lie entirely inside the zone's daily window from `operating_window_start_utc_hhmm` to `operating_window_end_utc_hhmm` on the current UTC date, inclusive at both ends, and a route that would run past curfew is refused with `ERR_CURFEW_WINDOW_VIOLATION`. The contiguity gate walks each consecutive pair of the sequence and requires the successor's identifier to appear in the predecessor's `connected_segment_ids`, refusing a gap with `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_CONTIGUOUS`. The termination gate requires the last element of the sequence to be exactly the order's `dropoff_segment_id`, refusing anything else with `ERR_ROUTE_DROPOFF_MISMATCH`. The zone gate requires every segment in the sequence to share the zone of the mission's robot, refusing a stray with `ERR_ZONE_MISMATCH`.

The plan lifecycle has three requested actions and two system-driven exits. `VALIDATE` carries a plan from `DRAFT` to `VALIDATED` and re-examines segment status rather than geometry: every segment in the sequence must have `status` `APPROVED` or `BLOCKED`, so a `MAPPED`, `SURVEYED` or `RETIRED` segment is refused with `ERR_SEGMENT_NOT_APPROVED`, and then no segment may actually be `BLOCKED`, which is refused with `ERR_SEGMENT_BLOCKED`. The two-step shape matters: an unapproved segment and a blocked segment are different failures with different error identifiers. The geometry gates are deliberately not re-evaluated at validation, having already run at creation. `ACTIVATE` carries a plan from `VALIDATED` to `ACTIVE` and requires the mission to be in `PLANNED`, `LOADED` or `BLOCKED` — never `EN_ROUTE`, so a route cannot be swapped under a moving robot — and re-checks that no segment in the sequence is `BLOCKED`, since a closure may have activated between validation and activation. On success the mission's previously `ACTIVE` plan, if there was one, moves to `SUPERSEDED` with `rejection_reason` `REPLACED_BY_NEWER`, the new plan's `supersedes_plan_id` is pointed at that older plan, and the mission's `active_route_plan_id` is repointed at the new plan. `REJECT` carries a plan from `DRAFT` or `VALIDATED` to the terminal `REJECTED` and requires a `rejection_reason` in the request. The two system exits are the ones described elsewhere: a plan is driven to `SUPERSEDED` with `SEGMENT_BLOCKED` when a hard closure covers one of its segments or when an operator reports a blockage, and a plan of any non-terminal state is driven to `REJECTED` with `OPERATOR_REJECTED` when its mission is aborted. Both `SUPERSEDED` and `REJECTED` are terminal, and route plan lists are returned in `revision_index` ascending order so a mission's replanning history reads chronologically.

### 3.7. The Delivery Run: Handoff, Takeover and Completion

The merchant's own staff perform the physical handoff, and a `PlatformAdmin` may do it on their behalf. The request carries `actual_payload_liters`, the volume actually loaded, and `lid_seal_confirmed`, which must be true — a handoff submitted without a confirmed seal is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The caller must be the merchant that owns the mission's order, or the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, and `actual_payload_liters` must not exceed the robot's `container_capacity_liters`, or it is refused with `ERR_PAYLOAD_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Note that the loaded volume is checked independently of the ordered volume, so a merchant who overfills the container is caught here even though the order passed the same check at dispatch.

A successful handoff carries the mission from `PLANNED` to `LOADED`, stores `actual_payload_liters` and mints the lid access code from a per-zone monotonic counter: the zone's `lid_code_sequence` advances by one, and `lid_access_code` becomes the six-digit zero-padded decimal representation of 100000 plus the new value of that sequence, so the first code minted in a zone is 100001. The counter is never reused and never rolls back, which means a zone's codes are unique for the life of the zone and the sequence value also reveals how many handoffs that zone has seen. The handoff is also timed against the merchant's promise: `handoff_late` is set to true when the elapsed time from the mission's `created_at` to the current instant strictly exceeds the merchant's `handoff_sla_seconds`, and false otherwise, so a handoff at exactly the SLA boundary is on time. When it is true the merchant's `exception_count` advances by one — the one place a merchant is charged an exception for something other than a failed delivery.

Departure is a `FleetDispatcher` action carrying the mission from `LOADED` to `EN_ROUTE`. It requires `active_route_plan_id` to be non-null and that plan to be in `plan_state` `ACTIVE`, the zone to still be in `permit_state` `PERMITTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ZONE_NOT_PERMITTED`, and the robot's `health_state` to be `NOMINAL` or `DEGRADED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ROBOT_HEALTH_BLOCKED` — a robot quarantined between load and departure never leaves. Departure stamps `departed_at_utc` with the current instant, adds the active plan's `total_length_m` to the mission's `traveled_distance_m` and its `crossing_count` to `traversed_crossing_count`, and carries the order from `DISPATCHED` to `IN_TRANSIT`.

An operator who sees the robot stopped by something the map did not know about reports a blockage. This is a `RemoteOperator` action carrying the mission from `EN_ROUTE` to `BLOCKED`, and it requires a `blocking_segment_id` that is actually an element of the active plan's `segment_sequence`; a segment outside the route is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The reported segment is stored, `blocked_at_utc` is stamped, the active plan moves to `SUPERSEDED` with `rejection_reason` `SEGMENT_BLOCKED` and `active_route_plan_id` is cleared to null. The order deliberately stays in `IN_TRANSIT`: an operator-reported blockage is a rerouting event, not a delivery failure, which is the sharpest contrast with the closure cascade of §3.2, where the identical mission transition does push the order into `EXCEPTION`.

Resuming is the same operator's action in the other direction, carrying the mission from `BLOCKED` back to `EN_ROUTE`, and it cannot be taken until a replacement route exists: `active_route_plan_id` must be non-null and name an `ACTIVE` plan, which in practice means a new plan has been submitted, validated and activated while the mission sat blocked. No segment of that new plan may be `BLOCKED`, or the resume is refused with `ERR_SEGMENT_BLOCKED`. On success `blocking_segment_id` is cleared, the blocked time is banked — the whole minutes elapsed from `blocked_at_utc` to the current instant, discarding any partial minute, are added to `blocked_minutes` — and `blocked_at_utc` is cleared to null. The replacement plan's `total_length_m` and `crossing_count` are then added to the mission's accumulators on top of what the superseded plan already contributed, because the robot really did travel both. That is the whole point of the accumulators being chronological rather than derived from the final plan, and it closes the cycle from `EN_ROUTE` through `BLOCKED` and back.

Interventions run alongside the mission on their own row. Either a `RemoteOperator` or a `FleetDispatcher` opens one against a live mission, supplying `mission_id`, a `trigger_reason` of `LOW_CONFIDENCE`, `PEDESTRIAN_CONFLICT`, `OBSTACLE`, `CROSSING_ASSIST` or `VANDALISM_ALERT`, and the robot's `autonomy_confidence_pct` at the moment of the request. The event is created in `takeover_state` `REQUESTED`, and one value is frozen at that instant: `mandatory` is true when `autonomy_confidence_pct` is at or below the zone's `takeover_confidence_threshold_pct` and false otherwise. The comparison is inclusive, so against the default threshold of 35 a confidence of 35 is mandatory while a confidence of 36 is not, and because the zone is reached through the mission's robot, two events with identical confidence in differently configured zones classify differently. Once frozen, `mandatory` never changes even if the zone's threshold is later retuned.

The takeover procedure walks `REQUESTED` to `ACKNOWLEDGED` on the `ACKNOWLEDGE` action and then to `ACTIVE` on the `ASSUME` action, which additionally requires the mission to be in `EN_ROUTE` or `BLOCKED` and which stamps `assumed_at_utc` and latches `operator_user_id` to the calling operator. From `ACTIVE` the `ESCALATE` action moves the event to `ESCALATED` with a stored `escalation_reason` and advances the mission's `escalation_count` by one, and may only be taken by the operator who assumed control; another operator is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. A `SafetyOfficer` may then take the `DEESCALATE` action to return the event to `ACTIVE`, and here two omissions are deliberate: `escalation_reason` is retained rather than cleared, and the mission's `escalation_count` is not decremented, because the escalation did happen and continues to charge the robot's autonomy index. That closes the cycle from `ACTIVE` through `ESCALATED` and back, and it means the count can exceed the number of events currently sitting escalated.

Releasing control is the only route into the terminal `RELEASED` state and has three distinct forms, which differ in who may act and what is recorded. From `ACTIVE` the operator named in `operator_user_id` releases; from `ESCALATED` a `SafetyOfficer` may release regardless of who assumed control, while a `RemoteOperator` still needs to be that named operator. Both of those forms set `control_seconds` to the whole seconds elapsed from `assumed_at_utc` to the current instant. The third form is a release straight out of `REQUESTED` by a `FleetDispatcher`, for a low-severity request the robot recovered from on its own; it is permitted only when `mandatory` is false, since waiving a mandatory takeover without an operator ever assuming control is refused with `ERR_TAKEOVER_REQUIRED`, and it records `control_seconds` as 0 and leaves `assumed_at_utc` null. All three forms advance the mission's `intervention_count` by one, so even a waived request counts against the robot's autonomy index.

Arrival is a `FleetDispatcher` action carrying the mission from `EN_ROUTE` to `HANDOFF_READY`, and it is gated on the intervention set: no event of this mission may still be in `REQUESTED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `ACTIVE` or `ESCALATED`, or the arrival is refused with `ERR_TAKEOVER_REQUIRED`. Completion is the same dispatcher's action carrying the mission from `HANDOFF_READY` to the terminal `COMPLETED`; it repeats the same open-takeover gate and additionally requires a `lid_access_code` in the request that exactly equals the code minted at handoff, refusing a mismatch with `ERR_LID_CODE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).

Completion is the widest fan-out in the system. The mission stamps `completed_at_utc` and computes `active_minutes` as the whole minutes elapsed from `departed_at_utc` to `completed_at_utc`, discarding any partial minute. The order moves from `IN_TRANSIT` to the terminal `DELIVERED` and `settled_fee_cents` is written from the settlement evaluation of the delivery fee — the same formula as the quote of §3.3, but with all four future-dependent inputs replaced by what actually happened: the distance is the mission's `traveled_distance_m`, the crossing count is its `traversed_crossing_count`, the intervention count is its `intervention_count` and the blocked minutes are its `blocked_minutes`. The frozen `urgency_flag` and `payload_tier` from intake are reused unchanged, and the merchant grade discount is read from the merchant as it stands now. When the mission's `completed_at_utc` is strictly later than the order's `promised_by_utc`, `exception_reason` is latched to `LATE` on the delivered order without changing `order_state` — a late delivery is still a delivery, but it becomes eligible for a remedy. The merchant's money then moves from escrow into revenue: `escrowed_fee_cents` decreases by `quoted_fee_cents`, `recognized_fee_cents` increases by the same `quoted_fee_cents`, and `settlement_variance_cents` accumulates the signed difference of `settled_fee_cents` minus `quoted_fee_cents`, so a reroute that lengthened the haul pushes the variance positive while a large blockage credit can push it negative. Note carefully that the two money buckets move by the quote, never by the settlement; the variance field is the only place the difference is recorded. Finally the robot returns from `ON_MISSION` to `AVAILABLE`, its `active_mission_id` is cleared, the zone's `on_mission_robot_count` is decremented, and the robot's history absorbs the mission: `lifetime_mission_count` advances by one, `lifetime_intervention_count` by the mission's `intervention_count`, `lifetime_escalation_count` by its `escalation_count`, and `clean_mission_count` by one only when the mission recorded no interventions at all, after which both autonomy index variants of §3.5 are recomputed.

Aborting is the other way out. A `FleetDispatcher` may abort from `PLANNED`, `LOADED`, `EN_ROUTE` or `BLOCKED` with a `reason_code` of either `DISPATCH_CANCELLED` or `UNRECOVERABLE_BLOCKAGE`; an already terminal mission is refused with `ERR_MISSION_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409). The mission moves to the terminal `ABORTED`, `abort_reason` records the supplied code, `completed_at_utc` is stamped, and `active_minutes` is computed the same way as on completion except that it is 0 when `departed_at_utc` is null, which is the case for a mission aborted before it ever departed. Every route plan of that mission whose `plan_state` is `DRAFT`, `VALIDATED` or `ACTIVE` moves to `REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` `OPERATOR_REJECTED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. The order moves to `EXCEPTION` with the `exception_reason` given by the pinned abort mapping, the merchant's quoted amount moves from `escrowed_fee_cents` to `released_fee_cents`, and the merchant's `exception_count` advances by one. The robot returns from `ON_MISSION` to `AVAILABLE` with exactly the same counter and index recomputation as on completion, because an aborted mission is a terminal mission and counts against the fleet just as a completed one does. The two system-driven aborts described in §3.1 and §3.5 take this same path with `abort_reason` `ZONE_REVOKED` or `ROBOT_QUARANTINED` respectively, the only difference being that a quarantine parks the robot in `MAINTENANCE` instead of returning it to `AVAILABLE`.

The abort mapping from cause to `exception_reason` is pinned and total, so the enum has no unreachable value. An abort with `ROBOT_QUARANTINED` maps to `VANDALISM` when the robot's `tamper_flag` stands true after the quarantine and to `ROBOT_FAULT` when it stands false. `ZONE_REVOKED` maps to `BLOCKED_ROUTE`, `DISPATCH_CANCELLED` maps to `ROBOT_FAULT`, and `UNRECOVERABLE_BLOCKAGE` maps to `BLOCKED_ROUTE`. Together with the closure-cause mapping of §3.2 and the `LATE` latch above, those are the only producers of the field.

The mission transition endpoint is also the clearest illustration of the role split running inside a single endpoint rather than between endpoints. Departure, arrival, completion and abort are `FleetDispatcher` actions; reporting a blockage and resuming are `RemoteOperator` actions. A dispatcher submitting a resume, or an operator submitting a departure, is refused with `ERR_ROLE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403) even though both roles are permitted to call the endpoint at all.

### 3.8. Delivery Exceptions and the Customer Remedy Waterfall

When a delivery fails or arrives late, the customer is made whole by a remedy, which only `CustomerSupport` may issue. The request carries a single field, `remedy_kind`, and everything else is derived from the order as it already stands. Exactly one remedy may ever be issued against an order: a second attempt, whatever kind it names, is refused with `ERR_REMEDY_ALREADY_ISSUED` (HTTP 409) because `remedy_kind` is no longer null. The order must be in `order_state` `EXCEPTION` or `DELIVERED`, and its `exception_reason` must not be null, since a null reason selects no policy row and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; that is why a delivered order qualifies only when the `LATE` latch of §3.7 was applied to it. An order in `RECEIVED`, `DISPATCHED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `CANCELLED` or already `REFUNDED` cannot be remedied.

The policy row is selected from the order's latched `exception_reason` and never from anything in the request, which is what makes the remedy amount a function of what actually went wrong rather than of what was asked for. Each of the five reasons carries three policy numbers: the fraction of the delivery fee refunded, the fraction of the capped declared value compensated, and a flat goodwill amount in cents. A `WEATHER` failure refunds the full fee, compensates none of the declared value and adds 200 cents of goodwill. A `VANDALISM` failure refunds the full fee, compensates 60 percent of the capped declared value and adds 500 cents. A `BLOCKED_ROUTE` failure refunds half the fee, compensates none of the declared value and adds 150 cents. A `ROBOT_FAULT` failure refunds 35 percent of the fee, compensates 60 percent of the capped declared value and adds 250 cents. A `LATE` delivery refunds 25 percent of the fee, compensates none of the declared value and adds 150 cents.

The amount is assembled as a three-bucket waterfall, and the order of rounding is normative. The first bucket is the fee refund: the fee basis is the order's `settled_fee_cents` when that field is not null and its `quoted_fee_cents` otherwise, since a mission that never completed has no settlement, and the basis multiplied by the policy's fee fraction is rounded half up to whole cents. The second bucket is the declared-value compensation: the order's `declared_value_cents` is first capped at 15000 cents, whichever of the two is smaller being the compensable value, that value is multiplied by the policy's declared-value fraction, and the product is rounded half up to whole cents on its own. The third bucket is the policy's flat goodwill amount, which is already an exact integer and never rounds. The remedy amount is the sum of the three rounded buckets. Each bucket is filled and rounded before the next is considered, and the buckets are reported in that order; summing the three unrounded buckets and rounding the total once is a different and incorrect answer whenever two buckets each round down and their discarded fractions together reach half a cent.

Which buckets are actually charged depends on `remedy_kind`, and each kind drives a different order transition. A `REFUND` charges all three buckets and carries the order to the terminal `REFUNDED`, from either `EXCEPTION` or `DELIVERED`. A `GOODWILL_CREDIT` charges only the third bucket, so its amount is exactly the policy's flat goodwill figure, and it leaves `order_state` completely untouched — a delivered order stays delivered and an excepted order stays excepted. A `REDELIVER` charges nothing at all, records a `remedy_amount_cents` of 0, and is the only kind that puts the order back into the pipeline: it is permitted only from `EXCEPTION` and carries the order back to `RECEIVED`, clearing `exception_reason` to null so a later failure of the same order may latch a different reason. It also reverses the money movement of the original exception, moving `quoted_fee_cents` out of the merchant's `released_fee_cents` and back into its `escrowed_fee_cents`, which is the compensating direction of the merchant fee invariant, and it consumes no zone budget whatsoever. That closes the redelivery cycle from `EXCEPTION` through `RECEIVED` to `DISPATCHED` again.

Every remedy records three fields on the order regardless of kind: `remedy_kind` as requested, `remedy_reason_code` set to the `exception_reason` that selected the policy — captured before a redelivery clears it, so the reason survives on the order even after the field it came from is null — and `remedy_amount_cents` as computed. Money that leaves the platform is charged against the zone reached through the order's merchant. For a `REFUND` and for a `GOODWILL_CREDIT`, the computed amount must be no greater than the zone's `remedy_budget_remaining_cents`, and an amount above it is refused with `ERR_REMEDY_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402). The gate is inclusive, so an amount exactly equal to the remaining budget is allowed and drives the remaining budget to zero. On success the zone's `remedy_spent_cents` increases by the amount and `remedy_budget_remaining_cents` is recomputed as `remedy_budget_cents` minus `remedy_spent_cents`, with the zone's `version` advancing by one. A `REDELIVER` touches neither `remedy_spent_cents` nor the remaining budget and therefore can never be refused for budget reasons.

Orders are read back with filters on `merchant_id`, `dropoff_segment_id`, `order_state` and `exception_reason`, the last of which is how support staff sweep for every delivery that failed for one particular cause.

### 3.9. Incident Reporting and Zone Unit Economics

A physical incident is filed by either a `RemoteOperator` or a `SafetyOfficer`, who supplies the `zone_id` it occurred in, the `robot_id` involved, a `category` of `PEDESTRIAN_CONTACT`, `RIGHT_OF_WAY_VIOLATION`, `VANDALISM`, `OBSTRUCTION` or `CLEARANCE_BREACH`, a `severity` of `MINOR`, `SERIOUS` or `CRITICAL`, a `narrative` of 1 to 2000 characters for the municipal file, and optionally the `mission_id` it happened on and an `estimated_damage_cents` figure that must be zero or greater and defaults to 0. The report is created in `report_state` `DRAFT`, `filed_by_user_id` latches the calling user so the later submission can enforce separation of duties, and the zone's `open_incident_count` advances by one along with the zone's `version`.

Two fields are computed at creation and then frozen. `insurer_notification_required` is true when the `severity` is `CRITICAL`, or when `estimated_damage_cents` is at or above the zone's `insurer_notify_threshold_cents`; the damage comparison is inclusive, so with the default threshold of 25000 cents a damage estimate of exactly 25000 does require the insurer branch. Either condition alone is enough, so a `CRITICAL` incident with no damage at all still requires notification. `municipal_due_by_utc` is the filing deadline and applies only to reports that the municipality must see: for a `SERIOUS` or `CRITICAL` report it is the report's `created_at` advanced by the fixed platform constant of 72 filing hours, and for a `MINOR` report it is null. The deadline is informational — it is recorded and reported but does not by itself block any transition.

Submission is a `SafetyOfficer` action carrying the report from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED_MUNICIPAL`. It requires a non-empty `municipal_case_ref`, which is stored, and it enforces separation of duties: the submitting user must not be the user recorded in `filed_by_user_id`, and a self-submission is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). A draft created automatically by the quarantine cascade carries the literal `SYSTEM` in `filed_by_user_id` and therefore never trips that check, which is what allows any officer to file a machine-generated report. Submission also latches `reviewed_by_user_id` to the submitting user.

From `SUBMITTED_MUNICIPAL` the workflow forks, and the two branches are deliberately asymmetric. When `insurer_notification_required` is true, the officer must take the `NOTIFY_INSURER` action, which requires a non-empty `insurer_claim_ref` and carries the report to `INSURER_NOTIFIED`; that action on a report whose flag is false is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, because a report below the insurer gate has no such branch. The `CLOSE` action then converges both branches on the terminal `CLOSED`: it is always available from `INSURER_NOTIFIED`, and it is available from `SUBMITTED_MUNICIPAL` only when `insurer_notification_required` is false, so closing a notifiable report before the insurer has been told is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Closing decrements the zone's `open_incident_count` by one and advances the zone's `version`.

Two other exits exist. `RETURN_FOR_DETAIL` carries a report from `SUBMITTED_MUNICIPAL` back to `DRAFT` and may only be taken by the user recorded in `reviewed_by_user_id`; another officer is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. It clears both `municipal_case_ref` and `reviewed_by_user_id` back to null, which means the same officer may afterwards resubmit the report — the separation-of-duties check compares against `filed_by_user_id`, not against the cleared reviewer. It deliberately leaves the zone's `open_incident_count` alone, because a returned report was never closed, and that asymmetry against `CLOSE` is what keeps the counter honest across the `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED_MUNICIPAL` to `DRAFT` cycle. `REJECT` is the only `PlatformAdmin` action in the incident workflow: it carries a report from `DRAFT` to the terminal `REJECTED` and is allowed only when `severity` is `MINOR`, since a `SERIOUS` or `CRITICAL` incident is municipally mandatory and its rejection is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Rejection decrements the zone's `open_incident_count` in the same way a close does. Reports are read back with filters on `zone_id`, `mission_id`, `robot_id`, `report_state`, `severity` and `category`, and interventions with filters on `mission_id`, `takeover_state`, `trigger_reason` and `mandatory`.

Finally, a zone's unit economics are computed on demand and nothing about them is stored. The result is an eight-step pipeline, and the step order is normative because several steps consume the rounded output of an earlier step; keeping unrounded intermediates or inlining a step yields a different answer. The first step sums `active_minutes` over the zone's terminal missions to give the robot active minutes, a mission belonging to the zone when its robot's `zone_id` matches. The second step multiplies the number of the zone's robots whose `duty_state` is not `RETIRED` by a nominal shift of 600 minutes each to give the fleet capacity minutes. The third step computes utilization as a percentage: active minutes divided by capacity minutes, multiplied by 100, capped at 100.0, and rounded half up to one decimal place — and it is exactly 0.0 when the capacity is zero, since a zone with no unretired robot has no denominator. The fourth step allocates overhead as a base of 40000 cents plus 150 cents for every whole utilization point, the point count being the third step's already-rounded percentage with its fractional part discarded; that consumption of a rounded value is precisely why the third step cannot be folded into this one. The fifth step computes variable cost as 9 cents for each whole 100-metre unit of the total distance travelled, rounding any partial unit up, plus 140 cents for each intervention counted, both totalled over the same terminal-mission set as the first step, plus the zone's `remedy_spent_cents` exactly as it stands. The sixth step counts the zone's delivered orders — those whose `order_state` is `DELIVERED` or `REFUNDED` — and divides the sum of allocated overhead and variable cost by that count, rounded half up to whole cents, to give the cost per delivery; when the count is zero the cost per delivery is null rather than zero. The seventh step computes contribution per delivery as the recognised revenue divided by the same delivered count, rounded half up to whole cents, minus the already-rounded cost per delivery, the subtraction therefore happening between two integers; recognised revenue is the sum of `settled_fee_cents` over those orders less the sum of `remedy_amount_cents` over them, and contribution per delivery is null whenever the cost per delivery is null because both quotients share the same denominator. The eighth step computes throughput as the delivered count divided by the fleet capacity expressed in hours, that is capacity minutes divided by 60, rounded half up to two decimal places, and it is 0.00 when the capacity is zero. Missions in `PLANNED`, `LOADED`, `EN_ROUTE` or `BLOCKED` and orders in `RECEIVED`, `DISPATCHED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `EXCEPTION` or `CANCELLED` are excluded from every step of the pipeline.

The economics read reports each of those steps as its own field so an operator can audit the pipeline rather than only its conclusion: `zone_id`, `robot_active_minutes` from step one, `fleet_capacity_minutes` from step two, `utilization_pct` from step three, `allocated_overhead_cents` from step four, `variable_cost_cents` from step five, `delivered_count` and `cost_per_delivery_cents` from step six, `contribution_per_delivery_cents` from step seven and `throughput_per_robot_hour` from step eight. `utilization_pct` carries one decimal place and `throughput_per_robot_hour` two; every cents field and every count is an integer; `cost_per_delivery_cents` and `contribution_per_delivery_cents` are the only two that may be null.
