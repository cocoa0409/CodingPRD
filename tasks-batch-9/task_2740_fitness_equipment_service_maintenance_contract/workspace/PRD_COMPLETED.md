# FleetCare Service Contracts API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

FleetCare is the back-office platform of a fitness-equipment service company. Its customers are gyms and health clubs that own treadmills, ellipticals, bikes and strength stations, and that buy multi-year maintenance coverage instead of paying per repair. The commercial promise inside those agreements is unusually operational: a guaranteed arrival window when a machine breaks, a fixed number of preventive-maintenance visits per year, a defined share of spare parts included in the fee, and a monetary credit whenever a covered unit is unavailable for longer than the agreement tolerates. Every one of those promises has to be measured against what actually happened in the field, and that measurement is what this API exists to produce.

Three pressures shape the design. The first is **reconciliation**: downtime credit is estimated the moment a field technician closes a call, using raw wall-clock unavailability, and then re-derived at month end using only the minutes the club was actually open — two different numbers for the same outage, both of which must survive on the record for audit. The second is **safety**: a member injury, a rejected inspection document or a failed safety-critical checklist item locks a machine out of service, and a lockout is cleared only by documented clearance work signed by somebody other than the technician who performed the work. The third is **obsolescence**: fitness equipment outlives its spare-parts supply, so the platform must track a part's slide from active stock into refurbished-only availability and finally into unsupported status, propagate that verdict onto every affected machine, and let it show up in the renewal price.

This document specifies the complete HTTP contract. All business resources are mounted under the `/fleetcare` prefix, and a single administrative endpoint outside that prefix drives the server's logical clock so that time-dependent behaviour — SLA windows, quote expiry, the trailing-365-day failure lookback, month-end period keys — is deterministic and testable. Every entity in the system is addressed as a peer collection; parentage between a club, a contract, a machine, a visit, a call, an order and a downtime event is expressed through identifier fields carried in request payloads, never through nested URL trees, because a single downtime event legitimately belongs to a contract, a machine and a service call at the same time.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — including reads and including the clock endpoint — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, or present but blank or entirely whitespace, or if `X-User-Role` is not exactly one of the six role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Surrounding whitespace on either value is trimmed before the value is used, so ` ClubManager ` names the same role as `ClubManager`, and the trimmed `X-User-ID` is what the ownership checks below compare. If both headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the target endpoint (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `ContractAdministrator`, `ServiceCoordinator`, `PartsPlanner`, `FieldTechnician`, `SafetyInspector` and `ClubManager`.

A `ContractAdministrator` owns the commercial side of the relationship: registering clubs, drafting and activating contracts, suspending and reinstating them, adjusting the parts allowance, reconciling downtime credit for a period, issuing renewal quotes and waiving downtime events as a goodwill or data-quality correction. A `ServiceCoordinator` runs the dispatch board and the preventive schedule: booking visits, dispatching technicians to calls, cancelling or marking visits missed, and releasing calendar reservations. A `PartsPlanner` owns the spare-parts catalogue and its obsolescence lifecycle: stock adjustments, end-of-support declarations and the refurbished-sourcing policy that follows from them. A `FieldTechnician` performs the work — starting and completing visits, recording checklist results, filing safety documentation, arriving at and resolving calls, and installing allocated parts. A `SafetyInspector` is a technician holding the inspector credential who tags machines out of service and certifies or rejects visit safety documentation. A `ClubManager` is the club site itself: it reports faults, cancels its own calls, and accepts or declines renewal quotes.

Identity binding differs by role, and it is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership:

- When `X-User-Role` is `ContractAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may log it but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ServiceCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier, not compared to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `PartsPlanner`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier, not compared to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `FieldTechnician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Technician` row; ownership checks on a service call, a preventive visit or a part order compare the work item's assigned-technician identifier to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `SafetyInspector`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Technician` row; the server additionally requires that row's `is_safety_inspector` flag to be `true`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ClubManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Club` row; every club-scoped operation is confined to resources belonging to that club.

Three ownership rules follow from those bindings, and each carries its own rejection code. A `ClubManager` acting on a resource that belongs to a different club — resolved through the target's `club_id`, or through the referenced machine's or contract's `club_id` — is rejected with `ERR_CLUB_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403); this applies to every `ClubManager` operation, namely opening a service call, cancelling a call, accepting or declining a renewal quote, and every club-scoped read of a *single* resource. Collection reads sit outside that confinement: a list endpoint is neither narrowed to the caller's club nor refused, so a `ClubManager` listing clubs, machines, contracts, visits, calls, orders, events or quotes sees every row in the collection and must supply the `club_id` or `contract_id` filter itself if it wants only its own. A `FieldTechnician` acting on a call, visit or part order that is not assigned to them is rejected with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_ASSIGNED` (HTTP 403); this guards call arrival, parts hold, resume and resolve, visit start, checklist recording and completion, safety-document submission, and part-order installation. A principal presenting `SafetyInspector` whose `Technician` row has `is_safety_inspector` equal to `false` is rejected with `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 403); this guards safety-document certification and rejection and equipment tag-out. A principal presenting `SafetyInspector` whose `X-User-ID` resolves to no `Technician` row at all is answered with that same code, since a principal the pool does not know cannot be shown to hold the credential.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from real time, so every response issued while the clock stands at one instant carries that same value.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- This envelope answers every failure, including the ones decided before any business handler runs. A request to a path no endpoint serves, and a request whose method that path does not accept, both answer `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` carried under the transport's own status — 404 and 405 respectively — rather than under the 400 the catalogue of §5 lists for that identifier; an unexpected server-side defect answers `ERR_INTERNAL` under 500. Neither situation is reachable through the documented contract, and no failure ever answers an HTML error page.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection read accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A value outside those bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), and so is a value that is not a whole number at all, such as `abc` or `2.5`. A parameter supplied with an empty value is treated as absent and falls back to its default. `offset` has no upper bound: an offset past the end of the matching set is legal and answers an empty `data` array with `has_more` `false`.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest row first. Because the logical clock of §2.5 only moves when it is told to, rows routinely carry an identical `created_at`; such ties are broken by insertion sequence, so the effective order is always the order in which the rows were created. The `meta` object of every list response carries `limit` (the page size actually applied), `offset` (the starting position actually applied), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination) and `has_more` (`true` when rows matching the filters remain beyond the current page).

Collection reads also accept domain filters, listed per endpoint in §4. Filters combine conjunctively: a request naming both `club_id` and `lifecycle_status` returns only rows matching both. Each filter is matched by exact, case-sensitive equality against the stored field, and a filter supplied with an empty value is treated as absent. A value no row carries — including a string outside an enum's declared set — is not a malformed request: it simply matches nothing and answers an empty page with `total_count` `0`. The two boolean filters, `is_safety_inspector` and `is_critical_spare`, accept `true`, `false`, `1` and `0` in any letter case and reject anything else with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Query parameters an endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and Record Versioning

Twelve resource types are persisted: `Club`, `EquipmentModel`, `Equipment`, `ServiceContract`, `Technician`, `TechnicianAssignment`, `PreventiveVisit`, `ServiceCall`, `PartCatalogEntry`, `PartOrder`, `DowntimeEvent` and `RenewalQuote`. Every one of them carries the same four common properties in addition to its domain fields.

`id` is a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no client-supplied component; a client-provided identifier in a creation payload is ignored. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the row was inserted, rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z`. `updated_at` uses the same format and is refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations reached indirectly through a cascade rather than directly through the endpoint the caller invoked. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, again including cascaded mutations. All four properties appear in every response body that returns the resource.

A write that would leave every field of a row exactly as it already stands is not a mutation of that row: `updated_at` and `version` both hold still. Re-asserting a technician's current `duty_status`, re-posting a usage reading identical to the stored one, and a derived recount that lands on the numbers already recorded therefore all answer `200` with the `version` unchanged. Four writes are deliberate exceptions and always advance `version`, because each is a ledger event in its own right even when the values it writes are unchanged: a parts-allowance adjustment (including one of exactly zero cents), a credit reconciliation, a renewal-quote issuance, and the recording of a checklist result over an identical earlier entry.

The distinction between a direct write and a cascaded write matters for `version` in one place in particular. Several operations that address a service call, a preventive visit, a part order or a downtime event also mutate the covering `ServiceContract` — its credit counters, its allowance balance, its compliance percentage, its risk classification. Those contract mutations refresh the contract's `updated_at` and advance its `version` exactly as a direct contract write would, which is precisely why the concurrency token described in §2.8 is anchored on the contract.

### 2.5. The Logical Service Clock

Nothing in FleetCare reads the host machine's wall clock. Instead the server maintains a single logical UTC instant, and every timestamp it stamps, every elapsed-minute computation, every window comparison, every expiry check and the trailing-365-day failure lookback all read that one value. On startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it holds that value until it is moved; it never falls back to real time.

An administrative endpoint outside the business prefix repositions the clock. It takes a single field, `to_iso8601`, holding the ISO 8601 UTC instant the clock should adopt, and it answers with `current_utc` set to the newly adopted instant. All six roles may drive it, because it exists so that time-dependent behaviour can be exercised deterministically. Moving the clock backwards is permitted and does not retroactively alter any timestamp already stamped on a row. The adopted instant is held at full precision for every comparison, but every timestamp the server *renders* — `current_utc` in this endpoint's own answer, `created_at`, `updated_at` and every domain instant — is truncated to three fractional digits rather than rounded, so a clock set to `2026-08-01T00:00:00.123900Z` renders everywhere as `2026-08-01T00:00:00.123Z`.

### 2.6. Peer Addressing and Left-to-Right Identifier Resolution

Resources are addressed as flat peer collections. A machine is not reached through its club, and a downtime event is not reached through its service call; each lives at its own collection path and names its relatives through identifier fields. This is deliberate: a `DowntimeEvent` simultaneously belongs to a contract, a machine and a service call, and a `TechnicianAssignment` belongs to *either* a service call or a preventive visit, so no single nesting tree could express the graph. A trailing slash on any of these paths is tolerated and addresses exactly the same resource or collection.

Path variables are resolved strictly left to right, and the first unresolvable variable decides the response. `{clubId}` that matches no club yields `ERR_CLUB_NOT_FOUND`; `{modelId}` yields `ERR_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`; `{equipmentId}` yields `ERR_EQUIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`; `{contractId}` yields `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`; `{technicianId}` yields `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_FOUND`; `{visitId}` yields `ERR_VISIT_NOT_FOUND`; `{callId}` yields `ERR_CALL_NOT_FOUND`; `{assignmentId}` yields `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`; `{partId}` yields `ERR_PART_NOT_FOUND`; `{orderId}` yields `ERR_PART_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`; `{eventId}` yields `ERR_DOWNTIME_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`; and `{quoteId}` yields `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND`. All twelve carry HTTP 404.

Identifiers carried in a request *body* are resolved after the request shape has been validated, and they use the same catalogue of not-found codes: `club_id` resolves against clubs, `model_id` against equipment models, `equipment_id` against machines, `contract_id` against contracts, `technician_id` and `safety_clearance_technician_id` against technicians, and `part_id` against the parts catalogue. An unresolvable body identifier produces the not-found code of the entity type it was supposed to name, with HTTP 404. This applies to machine registration, contract drafting, visit scheduling, call opening, call dispatch, parts holds, call resolution and part registration.

### 2.7. Money, Minutes, Scores and Rounding

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in cents. There are no fractional-cent values anywhere in the system, and no amount is ever stored as a floating-point number or as a decimal string. A monetary field in a request body must accordingly arrive as a JSON integer: `1000.0`, `"1000"` and `1000.5` are each `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Wherever a monetary amount is produced by multiplying by a rate, a factor or a percentage, the result is rounded to whole cents using round-half-up (a value exactly at the half-cent rounds away from zero, upward). Where a calculation proceeds in several priced steps — the renewal quote is the extreme case — rounding is applied at each step named in §3, and in particular per line item *before* the lines are summed, never once at the end.

Elapsed durations are integers denominated in whole minutes. A duration derived from two instants is computed as the difference in seconds and then floored to whole minutes, so a span of 119 seconds is 1 minute and a span of 3,599 seconds is 59 minutes. Two derived percentages and two derived scores carry two decimal places with round-half-up: `pm_compliance_pct`, `health_score`, `fleet_age_index` and the usage figures recorded against a machine. The rounding fixes the value, not its printed width: each of those is emitted as a JSON number, so `48.00` serialises as `48.0` and `100.00` as `100.0`, and a reader must compare them numerically rather than by string form. Ratios used only for classification — the replacement-candidate ratio behind `fleet_renewal_risk` — are compared at full precision without intermediate rounding.

Boundary inclusivity is specified explicitly at every threshold in §3 and is never left to the reader's discretion. As a general rule the system favours the customer at exact boundaries: an arrival exactly at the SLA target counts as met, a technician landing exactly on their minute ceiling is accepted, and a gap of exactly the travel buffer between two reservations is legal.

### 2.8. The Contract Version as an Optimistic-Concurrency Token

Two operations rewrite the contract's commercial ledger in a way that cannot be replayed safely against a stale view, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control keyed on `ServiceContract.version`. In each case the request body must carry a non-optional field named `expected_contract_version`, holding the `version` value the client last observed on the contract it is about to write. The token must be an integer of zero or more; a missing, fractional, string-typed, boolean or negative token is a request-shape failure answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) at rung 18, before the value is ever compared with the contract's live `version`. Endpoints not named here take no version field at all, and supplying one to them has no effect.

The first guarded operation is renewal quoting — the `ContractAdministrator` action that issues a `RenewalQuote` against a contract. If `expected_contract_version` does not equal the contract's live `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no quote is created. Re-quoting is a legitimate product operation that supersedes the previously issued quote, so the guard cannot be expressed as a state precondition: without it, a client holding an out-of-date view of the contract would silently discard a quote it had never seen, along with the fleet and service-history changes that produced that quote's numbers.

The second guarded operation is period credit reconciliation — the `ContractAdministrator` action that converts a month's closed downtime events into posted credit. If `expected_contract_version` does not equal the contract's live `version`, the request is rejected with the same `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no event is reconciled. Reconciliation is not idempotent on the ledger: a client working from a stale contract view would post credit against a cap value and a set of closed events that no longer reflect the contract, and the annual cap could be consumed twice over.

Because both quoting and reconciliation themselves advance `ServiceContract.version`, a client that performs one and then the other must re-read the contract in between. So must a client whose intervening actions touched anything that cascades onto the contract — a resolved call, an installed loaner, a completed or missed visit, an allowance adjustment, a waived event.

### 2.9. The Rejection Ladder

FleetCare evaluates a request against a single, strictly ordered ladder of checks, and the **first** check that fails decides the response. No later check may mask an earlier one, and no check is evaluated in parallel with another. This matters constantly in practice: a request that is simultaneously mis-scoped, aimed at a non-existent machine and commercially impossible must answer with the authorisation failure, not with the commercial one. The order below is normative; the business meaning of each check and the state it inspects are developed in §3.

**Rungs 1–5 — authentication and authorisation.** (1) Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and the role is one of the six declared names, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). (2) The acting role appears in the target endpoint's authorised-role list, otherwise `ERR_ROLE_FORBIDDEN` (403). (3) A `ClubManager` is confined to its own club, otherwise `ERR_CLUB_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN` (403). This rung outranks existence only where the club is itself the path variable, as on the club read; on every other club-scoped operation the target row has to be found before its owning club can be known at all, so a `ClubManager` naming a row that does not exist is answered with that row's not-found code from rungs 6–17 rather than with the scope failure. (4) A `FieldTechnician` is confined to work items assigned to them — call arrive, parts hold, resume and resolve; visit start, checklist results and complete; visit safety-document submission; part-order install — otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_ASSIGNED` (403). (5) A `SafetyInspector` must hold the credential on safety-document certify and reject and on equipment tag-out, otherwise `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_CERTIFIED` (403).

**Rungs 6–17 — path identifier existence, resolved left to right.** In ascending order: (6) `{clubId}` on the club read, else `ERR_CLUB_NOT_FOUND`; (7) `{modelId}` on the equipment-model read, else `ERR_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`; (8) `{equipmentId}` on the machine read, usage readings and tag-out, else `ERR_EQUIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`; (9) `{contractId}` on every contract sub-resource operation, else `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`; (10) `{technicianId}` on the technician read and duty-status change, else `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_FOUND`; (11) `{visitId}` on every preventive-visit operation, else `ERR_VISIT_NOT_FOUND`; (12) `{callId}` on every service-call operation, else `ERR_CALL_NOT_FOUND`; (13) `{assignmentId}` on the assignment read and release, else `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`; (14) `{partId}` on the part read, inventory adjustments and end-of-support declaration, else `ERR_PART_NOT_FOUND`; (15) `{orderId}` on the part-order read, install and cancel, else `ERR_PART_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`; (16) `{eventId}` on the downtime-event read and waive, else `ERR_DOWNTIME_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`; (17) `{quoteId}` on the quote read, accept and decline, else `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND`. All twelve answer HTTP 404.

**Rung 18 — request shape.** On every write endpoint, all required body fields must be present, every enum-typed field must hold one of its declared values, every field that is a count, an amount or a quantity must be non-negative where negativity is meaningless, `qty` must be at least 1, and every timestamp must parse as an ISO 8601 UTC instant. A failure here is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Four conventions make this rung precise. A body that is absent entirely reads as an empty object, so every required field is reported missing; a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object is itself a failure at this rung. A field supplied as JSON `null` is treated exactly as if it had been omitted — it takes its declared default where one exists and is a missing-field failure where the field is required. Types are checked strictly, with no coercion: an integer-typed field rejects `1.0`, `"1"` and `true` alike, a number-typed field rejects numeric strings, and a boolean-typed field rejects `0` and `"true"`. And a timestamp may be written with a trailing `Z`, with an explicit numeric UTC offset, or with no zone designator at all — the last read as UTC — and is normalised to UTC before it is stored or compared.

**Rung 19 — body-referenced existence.** Every identifier carried in the body must resolve, using the code catalogue of §2.6, with HTTP 404. This applies to machine registration, contract drafting, visit scheduling, call opening, call dispatch, parts holds, call resolution and part registration.

**Rung 20 — optimistic concurrency.** On renewal quoting and on credit reconciliation, `expected_contract_version` must equal the live `ServiceContract.version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This rung sits deliberately above every state and business check: a client writing from a stale contract view is turned away before the server reasons about whether the write would have been legal.

**Rungs 21–30 — state preconditions.** (21) A contract lifecycle action must match the contract's `status`: activation requires `DRAFT`, suspension requires `ACTIVE`, reinstatement requires `SUSPENDED`, and renewal quoting requires `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_QUOTED`; otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_INVALID_STATE` (409). (22) Operational work requires the covering contract to be `ACTIVE` — call opening, call dispatch, visit scheduling, parts-allowance adjustment and credit reconciliation — otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). (23) A service-call action must match the call's `status`: dispatch requires `OPEN`, arrival requires `DISPATCHED`, parts hold and resolve require `ON_SITE`, resume requires `PARTS_HOLD`, and cancellation requires `OPEN` or `DISPATCHED`; otherwise `ERR_CALL_INVALID_STATE` (409). (24) A visit action must match `visit_status`: start requires `SCHEDULED`, checklist recording and completion require `IN_PROGRESS`, cancellation requires `SCHEDULED`, and marking missed requires `SCHEDULED` together with a logical clock at or after `window_end`; otherwise `ERR_VISIT_INVALID_STATE` (409). (25) A safety-document action must match `safety_doc_status`: submission requires `PENDING` or `REJECTED` with `visit_status` of `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED`, while certification and rejection require `SUBMITTED`; otherwise `ERR_SAFETY_DOC_INVALID_STATE` (409). (26) Part-order install and cancel require the order to be `ALLOCATED`, and install additionally requires the owning call to be in `PARTS_HOLD`; otherwise `ERR_PART_ORDER_INVALID_STATE` (409). (27) A downtime event may be waived from `OPEN`, `CLOSED` or `RECONCILED`, but an already `WAIVED` event cannot be waived again, which answers `ERR_DOWNTIME_INVALID_STATE` (409). (28) An assignment may be released only from `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED`; a `RELEASED` or `COMPLETED` assignment answers `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID_STATE` (409). (29) Quote acceptance and decline require the quote to be `ISSUED` and its contract to be `RENEWAL_QUOTED`, otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_ISSUED` (409). (30) Quote acceptance and decline require the logical clock to be strictly before `expires_at`; at or after that instant the answer is `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED` (409).

**Rungs 31–36 — safety and compliance, which outrank every commercial rule.** (31) Preventive work may not start on a machine whose `lifecycle_status` is `TAGGED_OUT`, and a contract may not be reinstated while its club's `open_safety_lockout_count` is greater than zero; either situation answers `ERR_SAFETY_LOCKOUT_OPEN` (409). (32) When a call's `safety_review_required` is `true`, resolution must carry `safety_clearance_technician_id` naming a technician whose `is_safety_inspector` is `true`, otherwise `ERR_SAFETY_CLEARANCE_REQUIRED` (422). (33) When the target machine is `TAGGED_OUT` and a dispatch names its technician explicitly, that technician must have `is_safety_inspector` equal to `true`, otherwise `ERR_SAFETY_CERTIFICATION_REQUIRED` (422); this code belongs to the named-technician path alone, because automatic routing instead drops non-inspectors from the eligible pool and reports an empty pool as `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TECHNICIAN` (rung 57). (34) The same person may not stand on both sides of a safety control: the certifier or rejecter of a visit's documentation must differ from that visit's `assigned_technician_id`, and the clearance signer named on a resolve must differ from that call's `assigned_technician_id`; a violation on safety-document certify, safety-document reject or call resolve answers `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409). (35) A call reporting a member injury must be filed at `CRITICAL` severity, otherwise `ERR_INJURY_SEVERITY_MISMATCH` (422). (36) A visit may not be completed while any safety-critical checklist element carries a `FAIL` result, which answers `ERR_SAFETY_CRITICAL_FAIL_BLOCKS_COMPLETION` (409).

**Rungs 37–41 — referential coherence between entities.** (37) On call opening and visit scheduling, the referenced machine's `club_id` must equal the referenced contract's `club_id`, otherwise `ERR_EQUIPMENT_CLUB_MISMATCH` (422). (38) On a parts hold, the requested catalogue line's `model_id` must equal the serviced machine's `model_id`, otherwise `ERR_PART_MODEL_MISMATCH` (422). (39) On call dispatch and visit scheduling, the chosen technician's `region_code` must equal the club's `region_code`, otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_REGION_MISMATCH` (422). (40) On the same two operations, the serviced machine's model `category` must appear in the chosen technician's `skill_categories`, otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_SKILL_MISMATCH` (422). (41) Again on the same two operations, the chosen technician's `duty_status` must be `ON_DUTY`, otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_OFF_DUTY` (409).

**Rungs 42–45 — temporal and calendar conflicts.** (42) On visit scheduling, `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`, the span must be at most 480 minutes, and the whole window must lie inside the club's daily operating window; otherwise `ERR_VISIT_WINDOW_INVALID` (422). (43) On visit scheduling, the club's concurrent-visit count including the candidate must not exceed `max_concurrent_visits`, otherwise `ERR_CLUB_VISIT_CAPACITY` (409). (44) On call dispatch and visit scheduling, the candidate interval must not collide with any `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED` assignment of the chosen technician under the symmetric travel buffer, otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). (45) On the same two operations, the technician's `committed_minutes` plus the candidate's `planned_minutes` must not exceed their `max_committed_minutes`, otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).

**Rungs 46–60 — domain constraints.** (46) A contract may be activated only when its club has no other contract in `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `RENEWAL_QUOTED`, otherwise `ERR_ACTIVE_CONTRACT_EXISTS` (409). (47) A machine's `serial_number` must be unique across all machines, otherwise `ERR_SERIAL_DUPLICATE` (409). (48) The requested `parts_inclusion` must be offered at the requested `coverage_tier`, otherwise `ERR_TIER_PARTS_MISMATCH` (422). (49) `pm_visits_per_year` must meet the tier minimum, otherwise `ERR_PM_FREQUENCY_BELOW_TIER` (422). (50) A usage reading must be at least the machine's stored `usage_hours`, otherwise `ERR_USAGE_REGRESSION` (422). (51) A submitted `item_code` must match an element of this visit's `checklist`, otherwise `ERR_CHECKLIST_ITEM_UNKNOWN` (422). (52) Completion requires `checklist_resolved_count` to equal `checklist_total_count`, otherwise `ERR_CHECKLIST_INCOMPLETE` (409). (53) A machine may not have a second open call — one already in `OPEN`, `DISPATCHED`, `ON_SITE` or `PARTS_HOLD` answers `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_CALL` (409). (54) A call may enter the parts cycle at most three times; a fourth attempt answers `ERR_PARTS_HOLD_LIMIT` (409). (55) The requested source bucket must hold at least `qty` units, otherwise `ERR_PART_OUT_OF_STOCK` (409). (56) The requested `source` must be permitted for the part's `lifecycle_status`, otherwise `ERR_PART_SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE` (422). (57) When `technician_id` is omitted on dispatch or visit scheduling, automatic routing must find at least one eligible technician, otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TECHNICIAN` (422). (58) A call may resume from parts hold only when no part order on it is still `ALLOCATED`, otherwise `ERR_UNRESOLVED_PART_ORDER` (409). (59) A renewal quote requires the contract's club to own at least one machine, otherwise `ERR_FLEET_EMPTY` (422). (60) A reconciliation's `period_key` must differ from the contract's `last_reconciled_period_key` and at least one `CLOSED` event must exist in that period, otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_ALREADY_RECONCILED` (409).

**Rungs 61–63 — fiscal checks, deliberately last.** (61) When the requested part is covered by the contract's `parts_inclusion`, `parts_allowance_remaining_cents` must be greater than zero, otherwise `ERR_PARTS_ALLOWANCE_EXHAUSTED` (409). (62) A parts-allowance adjustment must not drive `parts_allowance_remaining_cents` or `parts_allowance_total_cents` below zero, otherwise `ERR_ALLOWANCE_ADJUSTMENT_INVALID` (422). (63) An inventory adjustment must not drive `on_hand_qty` or `refurb_on_hand_qty` below zero, otherwise `ERR_INVENTORY_ADJUSTMENT_INVALID` (422).

The placement of the fiscal band at the bottom is a product decision, not an accident. A technician standing next to a broken treadmill should be told that the part does not fit the machine, or that the stock room is empty, before being told that the club has exhausted its parts allowance — the physical impossibility outranks the commercial one.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Derived Counters

A great deal of FleetCare's value sits in fields nobody writes directly. Club-level counters, machine condition, contract-level compliance and credit totals and the renewal-risk classification are all *derived*, and they are refreshed synchronously inside the same request that changed their inputs. There is no deferred job and no eventual consistency: by the time a response is returned, every counter the request touched already reflects the new reality, and any row that moved carries a refreshed `updated_at` and an advanced `version`.

Two counters hang off the club. `covered_unit_count` is the number of machines whose `club_id` names that club, counted across every `lifecycle_status` — a machine that is `DOWN` or `TAGGED_OUT` still counts as covered. `open_safety_lockout_count` is the number of those machines whose `lifecycle_status` is exactly `TAGGED_OUT`. Both are recomputed whenever a machine is registered, whenever a lockout is applied and whenever a lockout is cleared.

The longest cascade in the system runs from a resolved service call all the way to the renewal-risk classification, and it is worth stating as a chain because each hop is caused by the previous one rather than by the request itself. Closing a downtime event increases the machine's `downtime_minutes_ytd` and the contract's `credit_provisional_cents`; because `downtime_minutes_ytd` and the resolved-call history changed, the machine's `health_score` and `health_score_variant` are recomputed; because `health_score` changed, `replacement_recommended` is re-evaluated against its threshold; because `replacement_recommended` may have flipped, the contract's `replacement_candidate_count` is recounted; and because that count changed, `fleet_renewal_risk` is re-derived from the new ratio. The same chain runs in reverse when a downtime event is waived, and a shorter version of it — starting at `support_status` instead of at `downtime_minutes_ytd` — runs when a critical spare is declared end-of-support.

Reversal is symmetric and exact. Every operation that undoes an earlier effect restores the affected counters to the values they held before that effect, rather than recomputing them from an approximation: cancelling a part order returns its allowance draw to the contract and its units to the bucket they came from; waiving a closed downtime event subtracts exactly the `provisional_credit_cents` and the `downtime_minutes` that its closure added; waiving a reconciled event additionally subtracts exactly the `posted_credit_cents` it contributed and the forgone amount it recorded. After any reversal the derived condition and risk fields are recomputed from the restored inputs.

### 2.11. Three Standing Invariants

Three identities must hold after every request that could disturb them. They are not endpoints; they are consistency conditions on the data, and a request whose effects would break one of them is a defect rather than a permitted state.

**The parts-allowance identity.** A contract's `parts_allowance_total_cents` always equals its `parts_allowance_remaining_cents` plus the sum of `allowance_draw_cents` over every part order that belongs to one of that contract's service calls and whose `status` is `ALLOCATED` or `INSTALLED`. Orders in `CANCELLED` are excluded, because cancellation zeroes the order's draw and returns the money to the remaining balance. The identity is checked in exact integer cents after every parts hold, after every part-order cancellation and after every parts-allowance adjustment, including the awkward case of a cancellation that follows a draw which was only partially covered.

**The technician-capacity identity.** A technician's `committed_minutes` always equals the sum of `planned_minutes` over that technician's assignments whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED`. Assignments in `RELEASED` or `COMPLETED` contribute nothing, which is why releasing a reservation frees its slot immediately. The identity is maintained in exact whole minutes after every assignment creation, release and completion, and every teardown path must return the minutes it once took: visit cancellation, marking a visit missed, call cancellation, contract suspension, contract expiry on a declined quote, and explicit release.

**The parts-inventory identity.** A catalogue line's `allocated_qty` always equals the sum of `qty` over the part orders on that line whose `status` is `ALLOCATED`. Bucket movement is prescribed exactly: allocating from `NEW` decreases `on_hand_qty` by the quantity and increases `allocated_qty` by the same amount; allocating from `REFURBISHED` or from `LOANER_SWAP` decreases `refurb_on_hand_qty` and increases `allocated_qty`; installation decreases `allocated_qty` without restoring any bucket, because the unit was physically consumed; and cancellation decreases `allocated_qty` and credits back **the same bucket that was debited**, so cancelling a refurbished or loaner allocation must never inflate `on_hand_qty`. No bucket may hold a negative number of units. This identity is checked after every parts hold, part-order install, part-order cancel and inventory adjustment.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Clubs, Equipment Models and the Installed Fleet

Everything in FleetCare hangs off a **club** — one physical gym site. A `ContractAdministrator` registers a club with a `name` between 1 and 120 characters and a `region_code` of two to four uppercase letters. The region code is not decoration: technician eligibility is filtered on an exact match against it, so a club in `NW` can only be served by technicians whose own region reads `NW`.

A club also declares when it is open, because the operating window is what separates the two downtime-credit numbers described in §3.8. `operating_open_minute` and `operating_close_minute` are integers counting minutes after UTC midnight. Opening defaults to 300 — five in the morning UTC — and accepts any value from 0 through 1439. Closing defaults to 1380 — eleven at night UTC — accepts any value from 1 through 1440, and must be strictly greater than the opening minute. Finally, `max_concurrent_visits` declares how many preventive visits may overlap on this site at one instant; it defaults to 1 and must be at least 1. The two derived counters `covered_unit_count` and `open_safety_lockout_count` both start at zero on a newly registered club. Clubs may be listed and filtered by `region_code`.

An **equipment model** is the catalogue-level description shared by every physical unit of the same product, and it may be registered by a `ContractAdministrator` or a `PartsPlanner`. A model carries a `manufacturer` (1 to 80 characters), a `model_code` (1 to 40 characters; the code is *not* required to be unique, so two models may legitimately share a manufacturer and a model code and are distinguished only by their `id`), a `category` drawn from `TREADMILL`, `ELLIPTICAL`, `BIKE` and `STRENGTH`, and a `model_year` between 1990 and 2100. Three numbers on the model drive downstream policy: `pm_interval_hours` is the number of usage hours the manufacturer allows between preventive visits and must be at least 1; `expected_service_life_hours` is the design life in usage hours, also at least 1, and serves as the denominator of the usage-based condition score; and `base_annual_service_cents` is the per-unit annual service list price in cents, which becomes the starting point of each renewal-quote line, and which may be zero but never negative.

The model also owns the safety work itself, in the form of a `checklist_template`. This is an ordered array of at most 20 elements, each of which is an object carrying a `code` of 1 to 40 characters, an `is_safety_critical` boolean marking the element as a safety gate, and a `consumable` boolean marking it as an item whose replacement should be recorded. Codes are unique within a template. The template is optional at registration and defaults to an empty array — a model with no checklist is entirely legal, and it has a specific consequence at visit completion that §3.4 spells out. Models may be listed and filtered by `category` and `manufacturer`.

A physical unit — an **Equipment** row — is added to a club's fleet by a `ContractAdministrator` or a `ServiceCoordinator`. The creation payload names the owning club through `club_id` and the product through `model_id`, records the manufacturer's `serial_number` (1 to 40 characters), records the calendar date the unit entered service as `install_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form (a well-shaped but impossible day such as `2026-02-30` is refused as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while a date in the future is accepted), and may supply `initial_usage_hours` for a machine that arrives with hours already on the meter. Serial numbers must be unique across the entire installed base, compared as exact case-sensitive strings and irrespective of club or model; a duplicate is refused with `ERR_SERIAL_DUPLICATE`, checked after `club_id` and `model_id` have both resolved. On registration, `usage_hours` and `usage_hours_at_last_pm` are both seeded to `initial_usage_hours` (zero when the field is omitted), `pm_status` reads `OK`, `lifecycle_status` reads `IN_SERVICE`, `support_status` reads `SUPPORTED`, `downtime_minutes_ytd` is zero, `loaner_active` is `false`, and `eos_notified_at`, `last_safety_clearance_at` and `safety_lockout_reason` are all null. The condition score is computed immediately in its usage-based form, which sets `health_score`, `health_score_variant` and `replacement_recommended`; the club's `covered_unit_count` then increases, and the covering contract's `replacement_candidate_count` and `fleet_renewal_risk` are re-derived.

The **usage meter** is the input that keeps preventive maintenance honest. A `ContractAdministrator`, a `ServiceCoordinator` or a `FieldTechnician` may post a meter reading carrying a single field, `usage_hours`. Readings are monotonic: a value below the machine's stored `usage_hours` is refused with `ERR_USAGE_REGRESSION`, while a value equal to the stored figure is accepted and simply confirms the meter. Hours are non-negative and are held to two decimal places, rounded half up. That rounding is applied *before* the monotonicity comparison, so a reading that rounds to exactly the stored figure — `1000.004` against a stored `1000.00` — is accepted as a confirmation rather than refused as a regression, and it is the rounded figure that is stored. Accepting a reading overwrites `usage_hours`, then recomputes `pm_status`, then recomputes the condition score, then re-evaluates `replacement_recommended`, and finally re-derives the covering contract's `replacement_candidate_count` and `fleet_renewal_risk`.

`pm_status` is a three-way verdict derived from how far the machine has run since its last completed preventive visit. Take the difference between `usage_hours` and `usage_hours_at_last_pm` — call it the hours since the last service — and compare it against the model's `pm_interval_hours`. When the hours since the last service are strictly less than the interval, the status is `OK`. When they are at least the interval but strictly less than the interval multiplied by 1.25, the status is `DUE`; the boundary is inclusive on the interval itself, so a machine that has run exactly its interval reads `DUE` rather than `OK`. At one and a quarter times the interval or beyond, the status is `OVERDUE`. Only a *completed* preventive visit resets `usage_hours_at_last_pm` to the current `usage_hours`; a service call, a checklist entry and a meter reading never move that baseline. Fleet reads may be filtered by `club_id`, `model_id`, `lifecycle_status`, `pm_status` and `support_status`, and a single-unit read exposes `health_score`, `health_score_variant`, `replacement_recommended` and `downtime_minutes_ytd` alongside everything above.

### 3.2. The Coverage Agreement: Drafting, Activation, Suspension and Reinstatement

A **service contract** is the commercial instrument covering one club's whole fleet, and it is drafted by a `ContractAdministrator`. The payload names the site through `club_id`, selects a `coverage_tier` from `BRONZE`, `SILVER` and `GOLD`, selects a `parts_inclusion` from `NONE`, `WEAR_ONLY` and `FULL`, commits to a `pm_visits_per_year` count, states the `annual_base_cents` value of the term, grants an opening `initial_parts_allowance_cents`, and fixes the term with `term_start_date` and `term_end_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, where the end date must fall strictly after the start date. That term-ordering rule is a request-shape check rather than a business rule: an end date on or before the start date answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at rung 18, and is therefore reported before `club_id` is resolved against the club collection. The annual base must be at least 1 cent; the opening allowance may be zero.

Two commercial rules bind the tier to the rest of the payload. The parts terms must be offered at the chosen tier: `BRONZE` may be sold only with `NONE` or `WEAR_ONLY`, while `SILVER` and `GOLD` may each be sold with any of `NONE`, `WEAR_ONLY` and `FULL`. `FULL` therefore requires `SILVER` or `GOLD`, but the implication runs one way only: a `GOLD` agreement is *not* obliged to take `FULL` and may be drafted with `NONE` or `WEAR_ONLY` just as freely. `BRONZE` paired with `FULL` is the single combination the table refuses, and it is refused with `ERR_TIER_PARTS_MISMATCH`. And the promised visit frequency must meet the tier floor — at least one visit a year on `BRONZE`, at least two on `SILVER`, at least four on `GOLD` — with a shortfall refused as `ERR_PM_FREQUENCY_BELOW_TIER`.

Drafting pins several derived values that later tier changes can never re-read. `sla_response_minutes`, the contractual arrival window, is taken from the tier at this moment: eight hours (480 minutes) on `BRONZE`, four hours (240 minutes) on `SILVER`, two hours (120 minutes) on `GOLD`. `parts_allowance_total_cents` and `parts_allowance_remaining_cents` are both set to `initial_parts_allowance_cents`. `credit_cap_cents`, the hard ceiling on credit the club can earn across the whole term, is fifteen percent of `annual_base_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents. Every credit counter — `credit_provisional_cents`, `credit_accrued_cents` and `credit_forgone_cents` — starts at zero, as do `pm_visits_completed_count` and `pm_visits_missed_count`; `pm_compliance_pct` starts at exactly `100.00`; `last_reconciled_period_key` and `suspension_reason` start null; and `obsolete_unit_count`, `replacement_candidate_count` and `fleet_renewal_risk` are derived from whatever fleet the club already has. A new contract's `status` is `DRAFT`.

The contract's `status` traverses `DRAFT` → `ACTIVE`, then optionally cycles `ACTIVE` → `SUSPENDED` → `ACTIVE` any number of times, then advances `ACTIVE` → `RENEWAL_QUOTED`, may re-enter `RENEWAL_QUOTED` from itself on a re-quote, and finally forks to either `RENEWED` or `EXPIRED`. Both `RENEWED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal. Contracts may be listed and filtered by `club_id`, `status` and `coverage_tier`, and a single-contract read is where a client picks up the `version` value it will later present as `expected_contract_version`.

Activation by a `ContractAdministrator` moves `DRAFT` → `ACTIVE` and requires two things: the contract must currently be `DRAFT`, and the club must have no other contract sitting in `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `RENEWAL_QUOTED` — a second live agreement on the same site is refused with `ERR_ACTIVE_CONTRACT_EXISTS`. Note the deliberate gap in that set: a club whose previous contract has reached `RENEWED` or `EXPIRED`, or which still has other contracts in `DRAFT`, may activate freely. Activation advances the contract's `version`.

Suspension moves `ACTIVE` → `SUSPENDED`, records the supplied `reason` into `suspension_reason`, and tears down the forward schedule in three causal hops. First, every preventive visit on this contract whose `visit_status` is `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED` with its `cancellation_reason` set to the literal string `CONTRACT_SUSPENDED`; visits already `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED`, `MISSED` or `CANCELLED` are left untouched. Second, because those visits became `CANCELLED`, each of their technician assignments still in `RESERVED` moves to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to the literal string `CONTRACT_SUSPENDED`. Third, because those assignments were released, each affected technician's `committed_minutes` is reduced by that assignment's `planned_minutes`. Open service calls are deliberately left alone — safety work continues while commercial terms are frozen — and the contract's `version` advances.

Reinstatement moves `SUSPENDED` → `ACTIVE`, clears `suspension_reason` back to null and advances `version`, but it is gated on the club's safety state rather than on anything commercial: the club's `open_safety_lockout_count` must be zero, meaning no covered machine may be sitting in `lifecycle_status` `TAGGED_OUT`. A single locked-out treadmill anywhere on the site blocks reinstatement with `ERR_SAFETY_LOCKOUT_OPEN`, and the only way through is the documented clearance work described in §3.5. Note that reinstatement does *not* resurrect the visits that suspension cancelled; the coordinator must book them again.

The parts allowance can be topped up or clawed back at any time by a `ContractAdministrator`, who supplies a signed `amount_cents` and an audit `memo` of at least one character. The `memo` is required for the request to be accepted, but it is not persisted as a field on the contract and nothing in the response echoes it. A positive amount credits the allowance and a negative amount debits it; both `parts_allowance_total_cents` and `parts_allowance_remaining_cents` move by exactly that amount. An adjustment that would drive either figure below zero is refused with `ERR_ALLOWANCE_ADJUSTMENT_INVALID`, and the allowance identity of §2.11 must still hold afterwards. Allowance adjustment is operational work and therefore requires the contract to be `ACTIVE`; attempting it on a draft, suspended, quoted, renewed or expired agreement answers `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

### 3.3. The Technician Pool, Calendar Reservations and the Routing Selector

A **technician** is registered by a `ContractAdministrator` or a `ServiceCoordinator` with a `full_name` of 1 to 80 characters, a `region_code` of two to four uppercase letters, and a `skill_categories` array holding between one and four distinct values drawn from `TREADMILL`, `ELLIPTICAL`, `BIKE` and `STRENGTH`. Two optional fields shape what the technician is allowed to take on: `is_safety_inspector`, which defaults to `false` and marks the holder of the safety-inspector credential, and `max_committed_minutes`, which defaults to 480 — a standard eight-hour day — must be at least 1, and caps how much reserved work the technician may carry at once. A newly registered technician starts `ON_DUTY` with `committed_minutes` at zero.

Duty status is switched by a `ContractAdministrator` or a `ServiceCoordinator` supplying `duty_status` as either `ON_DUTY` or `OFF_DUTY`. Going off duty removes the technician from automatic routing and causes any dispatch or visit that names them explicitly to be refused with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_OFF_DUTY`, but it does **not** disturb work already booked: existing assignments in `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED` remain exactly as they are and continue to occupy the technician's calendar and their `committed_minutes`. Technicians may be listed and filtered by `region_code`, `duty_status` and `is_safety_inspector`, and a single-technician read always shows the live `committed_minutes`.

A **technician assignment** is the calendar reservation itself. Assignments are never created directly by a client; each one appears as a side effect of dispatching a service call or scheduling a preventive visit, and its provenance is recorded in exactly one of two fields. An assignment born from a dispatch carries `service_call_id` naming the call and leaves `preventive_visit_id` null; an assignment born from a visit carries `preventive_visit_id` and leaves `service_call_id` null. Both kinds carry `technician_id`, the interval bounds `start_at` and `end_at`, and `planned_minutes`, which is the difference between those two instants expressed in seconds and floored to whole minutes. Every assignment begins in `RESERVED`, and its `planned_minutes` are added to the technician's `committed_minutes` at that moment.

From `RESERVED` an assignment advances to `CONFIRMED` when the technician actually turns up — arriving at the service call, or starting the preventive visit — and confirmation leaves `committed_minutes` unchanged, because a reserved and a confirmed interval occupy the calendar identically. From `CONFIRMED` it advances to `COMPLETED` when the work finishes — the call is resolved, or the visit is completed — and completion returns the `planned_minutes` to the technician. Both `COMPLETED` and `RELEASED` are terminal. The `RELEASED` state is reachable from `RESERVED` and from `CONFIRMED`, always returns the `planned_minutes`, and always records why in `release_reason`.

Release has one explicit operator path and five automatic ones. A `ServiceCoordinator` or a `ContractAdministrator` may release a `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED` slot directly, supplying a `reason` that is stored verbatim in `release_reason`; a slot already `RELEASED` or `COMPLETED` answers `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_INVALID_STATE`. Releasing a `RESERVED` assignment that belonged to a still-`SCHEDULED` visit through this operator path leaves the visit's `visit_status` alone and retains its `assigned_technician_id` for audit, so the visit remains on the books with a technician of record but no live reservation. The automatic paths write fixed reason strings instead of an operator's words: `VISIT_CANCELLED` when the visit is cancelled, `VISIT_MISSED` when the visit is marked missed, `CALL_CANCELLED` when a dispatched call is cancelled, and `CONTRACT_SUSPENDED` when the covering contract is suspended; contract expiry on a declined quote takes the same teardown route. Assignments may be listed and filtered by `technician_id`, `service_call_id`, `preventive_visit_id` and `status`.

Two independent gates protect a technician's day, and both are evaluated against assignments in `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED` only — `RELEASED` and `COMPLETED` rows never block anything, which is exactly why releasing a slot frees it immediately.

The first gate is the **travel buffer**. Assignment intervals are half-open — they run from `start_at` up to but not including `end_at` — so a new interval may in principle begin at the very instant an old one ends. On top of that, the system reserves 45 minutes of travel time symmetrically around every existing interval: a candidate interval collides with an existing one when the candidate's start is strictly earlier than 45 minutes after the existing interval's end, *and* the existing interval's start is strictly earlier than 45 minutes after the candidate's end. A gap of exactly 45 minutes between two pieces of work is therefore legal, while a gap of 44 minutes is a conflict, and the collision is refused with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

The second gate is the **minute ceiling**. A candidate reservation is accepted only when the technician's current `committed_minutes` plus the candidate's `planned_minutes` is at most their `max_committed_minutes`. The boundary is inclusive: landing exactly on the ceiling is allowed, and one minute beyond it is refused with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

When a dispatch or a visit-scheduling request names `technician_id` explicitly, that named technician is checked against every eligibility condition individually and each failure surfaces its own specific code — `ERR_TECHNICIAN_REGION_MISMATCH` when their `region_code` differs from the club's, `ERR_TECHNICIAN_SKILL_MISMATCH` when the machine's model `category` is absent from their `skill_categories`, `ERR_TECHNICIAN_OFF_DUTY` when they are `OFF_DUTY`, and the two calendar codes above.

When `technician_id` is **omitted**, the **routing selector** chooses instead. The eligible pool is every technician who is `ON_DUTY`, whose `region_code` equals the club's `region_code`, whose `skill_categories` contains the serviced machine's model `category`, who has no calendar collision with the candidate interval under the 45-minute buffer rule, whose `committed_minutes` plus the candidate's `planned_minutes` is at most their `max_committed_minutes`, and who — if the target machine is `TAGGED_OUT` — additionally has `is_safety_inspector` equal to `true`. If that pool is empty the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TECHNICIAN`; this code belongs to automatic routing only and never stands in for the specific rejection of an explicitly named technician. Otherwise the pool is ordered by `committed_minutes` ascending, ties broken by `created_at` ascending so that the earliest-registered technician wins, and the first row is the winner. Selection never consults the technician's `id`.

### 3.4. Preventive Maintenance: Scheduling, Checklist Execution and Compliance

Preventive maintenance is booked by a `ServiceCoordinator`, who supplies the covering `contract_id`, the target `equipment_id`, the window bounds `window_start` and `window_end`, and optionally a `technician_id`. The three body identifiers are resolved in that written order, so a payload naming a missing contract *and* a missing machine answers `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, and `technician_id` — when it is supplied at all — is resolved last. Booking is operational work, so the contract must be `ACTIVE` or the request answers `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and the machine must belong to the contract's club — the referenced machine's `club_id` must equal the referenced contract's `club_id`, or the request answers `ERR_EQUIPMENT_CLUB_MISMATCH`.

The window itself is constrained three ways: `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`, the span between them must be at most 480 minutes (eight hours), and the whole window must lie inside the club's daily operating window as defined by `operating_open_minute` and `operating_close_minute`. Both operating-window boundaries are inclusive: a window beginning exactly at `operating_open_minute` or ending exactly at `operating_close_minute` is accepted, and only a start earlier than the open minute or an end later than the close minute is refused. The two offsets are measured in minutes from the UTC midnight that opens `window_start`'s own calendar day, with the closing offset taken as the opening offset plus the span rather than re-derived from `window_end`'s day, so a window that runs past midnight UTC can never satisfy the rule. Any of those three answers `ERR_VISIT_WINDOW_INVALID`.

The club's own capacity is checked next, and it uses a deliberately different interval convention from the technician calendar. **Visit windows are closed intervals**: two visits that merely touch at an endpoint *do* collide, which is the opposite of the half-open technician rule in §3.3. A candidate overlaps an existing visit when the candidate's `window_start` is at or before the existing visit's `window_end` and the existing visit's `window_start` is at or before the candidate's `window_end`. The system counts the overlapping visits on **any** machine of the same club whose `visit_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` — regardless of which contract or technician they belong to — and refuses the booking with `ERR_CLUB_VISIT_CAPACITY` when that count plus the candidate exceeds `max_concurrent_visits`. Visits already `COMPLETED`, `MISSED` or `CANCELLED` free their slot. A club configured with a `max_concurrent_visits` of 1 therefore accepts its first visit and refuses any later window that so much as touches it.

A successful booking produces a visit whose `visit_status` is `SCHEDULED` and whose `safety_doc_status` is `PENDING` — two dimensions that advance on entirely separate triggers and are read together in exactly one place, described at the end of this subsection. `planned_minutes` is the window span in whole minutes, `assigned_technician_id` is the named technician or the routing winner, and the `checklist` array is copied element by element from the model's `checklist_template`. Each copied element carries the template's `code`, `is_safety_critical` and `consumable` values plus three new ones — `result`, seeded to `PENDING`, `note`, seeded to null, and `consumable_replaced`, seeded to `false`. `checklist_total_count` is set to the number of elements copied (zero when the template was empty), while `checklist_resolved_count`, `checklist_failed_count`, `safety_critical_failed_count` and `consumables_replaced_count` all start at zero. `started_at`, `completed_at`, `usage_hours_at_visit`, `inspection_summary`, `safety_doc_certified_by_id`, `safety_doc_rejection_reason` and `cancellation_reason` are all null, and `pm_credit_earned` is `false`. A `RESERVED` technician assignment is created spanning the window and the technician's `committed_minutes` rises accordingly. The contract's `pm_visits_per_year` figure is a commitment used in reporting and renewal discussion; the system does not refuse a booking merely because the contracted count has already been reached.

The assigned technician then starts the visit, advancing `visit_status` from `SCHEDULED` to `IN_PROGRESS`, stamping `started_at` with the logical clock and moving the visit's assignment from `RESERVED` to `CONFIRMED`. Only the assigned technician may do this. One cross-entity condition guards the start: the machine's `lifecycle_status` must not be `TAGGED_OUT`. Nobody performs routine maintenance on a machine that is locked out for safety, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SAFETY_LOCKOUT_OPEN`. Note the asymmetry with scheduling: the lockout is checked at the start and nowhere else, so a visit may be *booked* against a machine that is already `TAGGED_OUT`, and when the booking names a `technician_id` explicitly no inspector credential is demanded of them — `ERR_SAFETY_CERTIFICATION_REQUIRED` belongs to call dispatch alone (§3.6). Automatic routing is the one place the credential still bites on a booking, because the eligible pool of §3.3 drops every non-inspector whenever the target machine is `TAGGED_OUT`.

Checklist execution happens one element at a time. The assigned technician records a result by supplying `item_code`, the `result` itself — `PASS` or `FAIL` — and optionally `consumable_replaced` to flag that the consumable part behind that element was swapped — it defaults to `false` when omitted — and `note` for a free-text observation, which defaults to null when omitted and may also be sent as the empty string. Because both defaults are re-applied on every recording, a later submission for the same element that omits them resets that element's `note` to null and its `consumable_replaced` to `false`. The visit must be `IN_PROGRESS`, and the `item_code` must match the `code` of an element on this visit's `checklist`; an unrecognised code answers `ERR_CHECKLIST_ITEM_UNKNOWN`. Recording is idempotent-by-overwrite rather than append-only: submitting the same `item_code` twice replaces the earlier `result`, `note` and `consumable_replaced` with the new ones. After every recording the four counters are recomputed from the array — `checklist_resolved_count` counts elements whose `result` is anything other than `PENDING`, `checklist_failed_count` counts elements whose `result` is `FAIL`, `safety_critical_failed_count` counts elements that are both `is_safety_critical` and `FAIL`, and `consumables_replaced_count` counts elements whose `consumable_replaced` is `true`.

A failure on a safety-critical element is not merely bookkeeping. The moment such an element is recorded as `FAIL`, the machine's `lifecycle_status` becomes `TAGGED_OUT` with `safety_lockout_reason` set to `SAFETY_CRITICAL_FAIL` — applied whatever the machine's `lifecycle_status` happened to be at that moment, overwriting any reason an earlier lockout had recorded — and the club's `open_safety_lockout_count` is recomputed under the recount rule of §3.5, which leaves it exactly where it stood when the machine was already `TAGGED_OUT`. No service call already open against the machine has its `safety_review_required` raised by this event. Because the same code can be re-recorded, a technician who mis-keyed a failure can correct it to `PASS`, which brings `safety_critical_failed_count` back down; the lockout that the failure created, however, is a safety state and is cleared only through the clearance path of §3.5.

Completion is where the visit pays off, and it is guarded by two counting rules that are evaluated in the ladder's order, safety before bookkeeping. First, `safety_critical_failed_count` must be zero; an outstanding safety-critical failure answers `ERR_SAFETY_CRITICAL_FAIL_BLOCKS_COMPLETION` (rung 36). Only then is `checklist_resolved_count` required to equal `checklist_total_count`, so that every element carries a recorded result; a shortfall answers `ERR_CHECKLIST_INCOMPLETE` (rung 52). A visit that is simultaneously short of results and carrying a safety-critical failure therefore reports the safety code, never the incompleteness one. A visit copied from an empty template satisfies both rules vacuously — no element can have failed, and zero resolved equals zero total — so it can be completed immediately after starting. Completion sets `visit_status` to `COMPLETED`, stamps `completed_at`, captures the machine's current `usage_hours` into `usage_hours_at_visit` and writes the same figure back into the machine's `usage_hours_at_last_pm`, which recomputes `pm_status` to `OK`. The visit's assignment moves from `CONFIRMED` to `COMPLETED` and the technician's `committed_minutes` are returned. The contract's `pm_visits_completed_count` rises by one and `pm_compliance_pct` is recomputed. Completion stops there: it does not re-derive the machine's `health_score`, `health_score_variant` or `replacement_recommended`, and it does not touch the club's counters or the contract's `replacement_candidate_count` and `fleet_renewal_risk`, none of which take a preventive visit as an input (§2.10).

Two coordinator actions dispose of a visit that never happened, and the difference between them is the whole point of tracking compliance. **Cancelling** a `SCHEDULED` visit records the supplied `reason` into `cancellation_reason`, moves `visit_status` to `CANCELLED`, releases the `RESERVED` assignment with `release_reason` `VISIT_CANCELLED` and returns the technician's minutes — and touches neither `pm_visits_completed_count` nor `pm_visits_missed_count`, so `pm_compliance_pct` does not move at all. **Marking missed** requires the same `SCHEDULED` starting point *and* a logical clock at or after `window_end`; an attempt before the window has closed answers `ERR_VISIT_INVALID_STATE`. It moves `visit_status` to `MISSED`, releases the assignment with `release_reason` `VISIT_MISSED`, returns the minutes, raises `pm_visits_missed_count` by one and recomputes `pm_compliance_pct`. Cancelling a visit therefore cannot damage compliance, while missing one can. Neither disposal is gated on the covering contract's status: a visit whose contract has since been suspended, renewed or expired may still be cancelled or marked missed. Cancellation requires a `reason` of at least one character, whereas marking missed takes no request body at all.

`pm_compliance_pct` itself is straightforward but has one sharp edge. Its denominator is `pm_visits_completed_count` plus `pm_visits_missed_count`. When that denominator is zero — a contract with no visit history at all — the value is exactly `100.00`, not zero and not null. Otherwise it is one hundred multiplied by `pm_visits_completed_count`, divided by the denominator, rounded half up to two decimal places. Visits that are `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` are not yet history and enter neither figure; `CANCELLED` visits are excluded from both permanently.

Finally, `pm_credit_earned` is the single field on a visit that reads both status dimensions at once: it is `true` exactly when `visit_status` is `COMPLETED` **and** `safety_doc_status` is `CERTIFIED`. Because the two dimensions advance independently, the flag can be set by either event arriving second — completion sets it when the documentation was already certified, and certification sets it when the visit was already completed. Visits may be listed and filtered by `contract_id`, `equipment_id`, `assigned_technician_id`, `visit_status` and `safety_doc_status`.

### 3.5. Safety Documentation, Lockouts and Documented Clearance

Alongside the execution dimension of a visit runs a **documentation dimension**, tracked in `safety_doc_status` and driven by its own triggers. It begins at `PENDING`, moves to `SUBMITTED` when the technician files the inspection paperwork, and from there forks to `CERTIFIED` or `REJECTED`. `REJECTED` is not terminal: the documentation dimension has its own cycle, and a rejected document may be resubmitted, returning `REJECTED` → `SUBMITTED`. Because this dimension is independent of `visit_status`, paperwork may be filed while the visit is still running or after it has been completed, and certification may land before or after completion.

The assigned technician files the document, supplying `inspection_summary` — the narrative of what was inspected. Filing is permitted when `safety_doc_status` is `PENDING` or `REJECTED` and `visit_status` is `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED`; filing against a visit that has not yet started, or that was cancelled or marked missed, answers `ERR_SAFETY_DOC_INVALID_STATE`. A successful filing sets `safety_doc_status` to `SUBMITTED`, stores the `inspection_summary`, and clears any `safety_doc_rejection_reason` from a previous round back to null.

Certification and rejection both belong to a `SafetyInspector`, both require `safety_doc_status` to be `SUBMITTED`, and both are subject to two credentials rather than one. The acting technician's `is_safety_inspector` must be `true`, or the answer is `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_CERTIFIED`. And the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the visit's `assigned_technician_id` — nobody signs off their own work — or the answer is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Certification sets `safety_doc_status` to `CERTIFIED` and records the acting inspector's identifier in `safety_doc_certified_by_id`, a value that by construction never equals `assigned_technician_id`. If `visit_status` was already `COMPLETED`, certification is the second of the two conditions and flips `pm_credit_earned` to `true`.

Rejection is heavier. It sets `safety_doc_status` to `REJECTED`, stores the supplied `rejection_reason` into `safety_doc_rejection_reason`, and forces `pm_credit_earned` back to `false` even if the visit had already completed and previously earned it. It then cascades: the serviced machine's `lifecycle_status` becomes `TAGGED_OUT` with `safety_lockout_reason` set to `SAFETY_DOC_REJECTED` — applied whatever the machine's `lifecycle_status` already was, so a machine locked out a moment earlier for another reason simply has its reason overwritten — and the club's `open_safety_lockout_count` is recomputed on the recount rule stated below. Rejection does not raise `safety_review_required` on any service call already open against the machine. That second hop has a commercial consequence one step further out — because the club now carries an open lockout, any contract on that club sitting in `SUSPENDED` can no longer be reinstated until a clearance is signed. That consequence is enforced live as the reinstatement precondition of §3.2 rather than stored as a flag anywhere.

**Machine lifecycle.** A machine's `lifecycle_status` starts at `IN_SERVICE` and has no terminal state. It moves `IN_SERVICE` → `DOWN` when a non-injury service call is opened against it, and returns `DOWN` → `IN_SERVICE` when that call is resolved or cancelled. It moves `IN_SERVICE` → `TAGGED_OUT`, `DOWN` → `TAGGED_OUT`, or `TAGGED_OUT` → `TAGGED_OUT` as a re-assertion, on any of four events: an inspector's direct tag-out, a rejected visit safety document, a failed safety-critical checklist element, or the opening of a service call reporting a member injury. `safety_lockout_reason` records which of the four it was, holding `INSPECTOR_TAG_OUT`, `SAFETY_DOC_REJECTED`, `SAFETY_CRITICAL_FAIL` or `MEMBER_INJURY` while the lockout stands and null otherwise. None of the four events carries a precondition on the machine's current `lifecycle_status`: each may be applied to a machine that is `IN_SERVICE`, that is `DOWN` or that is already `TAGGED_OUT`, and when the machine was already locked out the event merely overwrites `safety_lockout_reason` with its own value, so the most recent event wins. `open_safety_lockout_count` is accordingly not an incremented tally but a **full recount** of the club's machines whose `lifecycle_status` is exactly `TAGGED_OUT`, taken after every lockout and every clearance. A first entry into `TAGGED_OUT` therefore raises it by one, while an event landing on a machine that was already `TAGGED_OUT` leaves it exactly where it stood. Only the direct inspector tag-out reaches sideways into the machine's open service calls; the three cascade-driven lockouts leave every open call's `safety_review_required` untouched.

A `SafetyInspector` tags a machine out directly by supplying a `reason`, which must be one of the four declared lockout reasons and is stored into `safety_lockout_reason` **verbatim**. The endpoint does not substitute `INSPECTOR_TAG_OUT` for whatever the inspector wrote: `INSPECTOR_TAG_OUT` is simply the value an inspector normally sends, and an inspector who sends `MEMBER_INJURY`, `SAFETY_DOC_REJECTED` or `SAFETY_CRITICAL_FAIL` instead has that value stored on the machine. Tag-out carries no state precondition whatsoever — it is accepted from `IN_SERVICE`, from `DOWN` and from `TAGGED_OUT` alike, and there is no invalid-state code for it. Re-tagging a machine that is already `TAGGED_OUT` therefore answers `200` with `lifecycle_status` unchanged, leaves the club's `open_safety_lockout_count` where it already stood, and advances the machine's `version` only when the newly supplied `reason` actually differs from the stored one. Beyond recomputing the club counter, it reaches sideways into any service call already open on that machine — any call whose `status` is `OPEN`, `DISPATCHED`, `ON_SITE` or `PARTS_HOLD` has its `safety_review_required` set to `true`, so a call that began as an ordinary repair now demands a clearance signature before it can be resolved.

**Clearance is the only exit.** A machine leaves `TAGGED_OUT` in exactly one way: a service call on that machine is resolved carrying a `safety_clearance_technician_id` that resolves to a technician other than the call's own `assigned_technician_id`. The inspector credential on that signer is demanded by the resolve endpoint only when the call's `safety_review_required` is `true` — which is the situation the clearance was designed for, and the one that arises whenever the machine was already locked out when the call was filed or was tagged out directly by an inspector afterwards. On the narrower path where the lockout arrived after filing through a cascade that does not raise the flag — a rejected visit document, or a failed safety-critical element on a concurrent visit — any other resolvable technician named in `safety_clearance_technician_id` is enough to lift it. Omitting the field never lifts a lockout. That resolution sets `lifecycle_status` back to `IN_SERVICE`, clears `safety_lockout_reason` to null, stamps `last_safety_clearance_at` with the logical clock, and lowers the club's `open_safety_lockout_count` by one. There is no bare status write that unlocks a machine, and cancelling a call never unlocks one — a `TAGGED_OUT` machine whose call is cancelled stays `TAGGED_OUT`. In practice, clearing a lockout that arose from a rejected document or a failed checklist item therefore means opening a fresh service call on that machine, dispatching it to a technician who holds the inspector credential (see §3.6), and resolving it with a second inspector's signature.

Three segregation-of-duties boundaries all share `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The inspector certifying a visit's documentation must differ from that visit's `assigned_technician_id`. The inspector rejecting it must likewise differ. And the `safety_clearance_technician_id` named on a call resolution must differ from that call's `assigned_technician_id`. The company's position is that the person who did the work is never the person who attests that the work was safe.

### 3.6. Reactive Service Calls: Response Time, the Parts Cycle and Resolution

When a machine breaks between preventive visits, a `ClubManager` or a `ServiceCoordinator` opens a **service call**. A `ClubManager` may file only inside its own club, and because no club appears in the path the scope of §2.9 rung 3 is resolved from the payload itself: from `contract_id` when that names an existing contract, otherwise from `equipment_id` when that names an existing machine; a payload naming neither resolvably falls through to the ordinary shape and body-existence checks instead of answering `ERR_CLUB_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN`. The payload names the `contract_id` and `equipment_id`, a `severity` of `CRITICAL`, `MAJOR` or `MINOR`, a free-text `symptom_code`, and optionally `injury_reported`. The two body references are resolved contract first and machine second, so a payload in which neither resolves answers `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`. The contract must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`) and the machine must belong to that contract's club (`ERR_EQUIPMENT_CLUB_MISMATCH`). A machine may carry only one live call at a time: if any other call on the same `equipment_id` sits in `OPEN`, `DISPATCHED`, `ON_SITE` or `PARTS_HOLD`, the request is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_CALL`. And a call that reports a member injury must be filed at the top severity — `injury_reported` `true` with any `severity` other than `CRITICAL` is `ERR_INJURY_SEVERITY_MISMATCH`.

Creation pins the promise the company will be judged against. `reported_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `status` is `OPEN`, `parts_hold_count` starts at `0`, and `sla_target_minutes` is frozen onto the call as `round_half_up(contract.sla_response_minutes × SEVERITY_SLA_FACTOR[severity])`, where the severity factor is `0.5` for `CRITICAL`, `1.0` for `MAJOR` and `2.0` for `MINOR`. The contract's own `sla_response_minutes` was itself derived from its `coverage_tier` at drafting — `480` for `BRONZE`, `240` for `SILVER`, `120` for `GOLD`. A critical call on a gold contract therefore carries a sixty-minute target while a minor call on a bronze contract carries sixteen hours. Because the target is stored on the call, a later change of tier or a later renewal never retroactively moves the bar for a call already open.

Creation also has three consequences beyond the call itself. `safety_review_required` is set to `true` when `injury_reported` is `true` **or** when the machine was already `TAGGED_OUT` at the moment of filing, and `false` otherwise. The machine's `lifecycle_status` moves to `TAGGED_OUT` with `safety_lockout_reason` `MEMBER_INJURY` when an injury was reported; otherwise it moves from `IN_SERVICE` to `DOWN`, and a machine that was already `TAGGED_OUT` stays `TAGGED_OUT`. Finally, one `DowntimeEvent` is created in status `OPEN` with `started_at` equal to `reported_at` and with `contract_id`, `equipment_id` and `service_call_id` copied from the call — the downtime meter starts running the instant the fault is reported, not when a technician is dispatched. Apart from `status` and the common properties of §2.4, those four are the only fields a newborn event carries a value in: `ended_at`, `mitigated_at`, `effective_end_at`, `downtime_minutes`, `operating_downtime_minutes`, `period_key`, `provisional_credit_cents`, `final_credit_cents`, `posted_credit_cents` and `waive_reason` are all present on the row and all read null while the event is `OPEN`, each acquiring its value only from the later step that computes it — `mitigated_at` from a loaner installation (§3.7); `ended_at`, `effective_end_at`, `downtime_minutes`, `operating_downtime_minutes`, `period_key` and `provisional_credit_cents` from closure; `final_credit_cents` and `posted_credit_cents` from reconciliation; and `waive_reason` from a waiver (§3.8). In particular `period_key` is acquired at closure and nowhere else, so a still-`OPEN` event's `period_key` reads null rather than the `YYYY-MM` of its `started_at`. The club's `open_safety_lockout_count` is recomputed.

**Dispatch.** A `ServiceCoordinator` dispatches the call for a window beginning at `start_at`, optionally naming a `technician_id`; when it is omitted the routing selector of §3.3 chooses. The call must be `OPEN` (`ERR_CALL_INVALID_STATE`) and the contract must still be `ACTIVE` — a contract that has been suspended or has expired since the call was filed cannot dispatch, and answers `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The chosen or routed technician runs the full eligibility gauntlet: `ERR_TECHNICIAN_OFF_DUTY`, `ERR_TECHNICIAN_REGION_MISMATCH`, `ERR_TECHNICIAN_SKILL_MISMATCH`, `ERR_TECHNICIAN_WINDOW_CONFLICT` and `ERR_TECHNICIAN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` for a named technician, or `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TECHNICIAN` when automatic routing finds nobody. One extra gate applies here that does not apply to preventive scheduling: if the machine is `TAGGED_OUT`, the technician who takes the call must have `is_safety_inspector` set to `true`. `ERR_SAFETY_CERTIFICATION_REQUIRED` belongs to the named-technician path alone, and is raised there ahead of the region, skill, duty, calendar and capacity tests; automatic routing instead silently drops non-inspectors from the eligible pool and reports an empty pool as `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TECHNICIAN`. This is what makes a locked-out machine recoverable — the only person who may be sent to a locked-out machine is somebody qualified to inspect it.

Dispatch sets `assigned_technician_id`, stamps `dispatched_at` from the clock, moves `status` to `DISPATCHED`, and creates one `TechnicianAssignment` in status `RESERVED` whose `start_at` is the requested value, whose `end_at` is `start_at` plus `SEVERITY_PLANNED_MINUTES[severity]` — `180` minutes for `CRITICAL`, `120` for `MAJOR`, `60` for `MINOR` — and whose `planned_minutes` is that same span. Its `service_call_id` names the call and its `preventive_visit_id` is null. The technician's `committed_minutes` rises by `planned_minutes`.

**Arrival.** The assigned technician records arrival on a `DISPATCHED` call; anyone else is `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_ASSIGNED` and any other status is `ERR_CALL_INVALID_STATE`. Arrival stamps `arrived_at` from the clock, computes `response_minutes` as `floor((arrived_at − reported_at) / 60 seconds)`, and freezes the verdict: `sla_met` is `true` when `response_minutes` is less than **or equal to** `sla_target_minutes`, so landing exactly on the target counts as met. `status` becomes `ON_SITE` and the call's assignment moves `RESERVED` → `CONFIRMED`, which does not change `committed_minutes` — the minutes were already committed at dispatch and remain committed while the work is in progress.

**The parts cycle.** From `ON_SITE` the assigned technician may put the call on hold for a part, which is the one transition in this system that can repeat. Each hold moves `status` to `PARTS_HOLD`, raises `parts_hold_count` by one, and allocates stock as described in §3.7. A hold is permitted only while `parts_hold_count` is strictly below three, so a call may enter the cycle a first, second and third time; the fourth attempt is refused with `ERR_PARTS_HOLD_LIMIT`. Resuming returns `PARTS_HOLD` → `ON_SITE`, and is permitted only when no `PartOrder` on the call is still in status `ALLOCATED` — every ordered part must have been installed or cancelled first, or the answer is `ERR_UNRESOLVED_PART_ORDER`. Resuming deliberately does **not** touch `arrived_at`, `response_minutes` or `sla_met`. The response clock is a measure of how fast the company reached the member's machine and is measured exactly once, on first arrival; a call that cycles through parts three times still reports the response time it earned at its original arrival.

**Resolution.** The assigned technician resolves an `ON_SITE` call, supplying a `resolution_code` and, where required, a `safety_clearance_technician_id`. If `safety_review_required` is `true`, that clearance must be present and must reference a technician whose `is_safety_inspector` is `true`; a missing or uncertified signature is `ERR_SAFETY_CLEARANCE_REQUIRED`. The segregation test is not conditioned on the flag: wherever a `safety_clearance_technician_id` is named at all it must differ from the call's own `assigned_technician_id`, and a self-signature is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On a call that was never flagged for review a named clearance is optional and faces no credential test — a technician without the inspector credential is accepted — but it is still resolved as a body reference (`ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_FOUND`), still stored on the row, and still counts as a signed clearance for the lifecycle branch of hop two below. Resolution sets `status` to `RESOLVED`, stamps `resolved_at`, and stores both the `resolution_code` and the `safety_clearance_technician_id`. The call's assignment moves `CONFIRMED` → `COMPLETED` and the technician's `committed_minutes` falls by that assignment's `planned_minutes`, freeing capacity for the next job.

Resolution then triggers the deepest cascade in the system, five hops long, each hop caused by the hop before it. **Hop one:** the call's `DowntimeEvent` moves `OPEN` → `CLOSED`, and in doing so acquires `ended_at` from the clock plus a computed `effective_end_at`, `downtime_minutes`, `operating_downtime_minutes`, `period_key` and `provisional_credit_cents` (§3.8). **Hop two:** because that event closed, the machine's `downtime_minutes_ytd` rises by the event's `downtime_minutes`, the contract's `credit_provisional_cents` rises by the event's `provisional_credit_cents`, and the machine's `lifecycle_status` returns to `IN_SERVICE` — always from `DOWN`, but from `TAGGED_OUT` only when a clearance was signed, in which case `last_safety_clearance_at` is stamped and `safety_lockout_reason` cleared. **Hop three:** because `downtime_minutes_ytd` and the resolved-call history both changed, `health_score` and `health_score_variant` are recomputed. **Hop four:** because the score changed, `replacement_recommended` is re-evaluated against its threshold. **Hop five:** because the flag may have flipped, the contract's `replacement_candidate_count` is recomputed and `fleet_renewal_risk` re-derived from the new ratio (§3.9). The club's `open_safety_lockout_count` is recomputed last. Where the call has no `OPEN` downtime event left to close — because it was already waived under §3.8 — hop one does nothing and hop two's ledger arithmetic is skipped entirely, leaving `downtime_minutes_ytd` and `credit_provisional_cents` where they stood; hop two's lifecycle return and hops three, four and five still run exactly as described, so the machine still comes back to `IN_SERVICE` and the condition and risk fields are still re-derived.

**Cancellation.** A `ServiceCoordinator` or the `ClubManager` may cancel a call from `OPEN` or from `DISPATCHED`, supplying a `reason` stored as `cancellation_reason`; from any other status the answer is `ERR_CALL_INVALID_STATE`. Both branches set `status` to `CANCELLED`, move the call's `OPEN` downtime event to `WAIVED` with `waive_reason` `CALL_CANCELLED` and no credit computed at all, and return the machine to `IN_SERVICE` when it was `DOWN` — a `TAGGED_OUT` machine stays locked out. The branches differ in exactly one respect, which is the reservation. Cancelling from `OPEN` has no assignment to unwind, because none was ever created. Cancelling from `DISPATCHED` moves the call's `RESERVED` assignment to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` `CALL_CANCELLED` and reduces the technician's `committed_minutes` by that assignment's `planned_minutes`. Calls may be read individually — the single-call read is the natural place to inspect `sla_target_minutes`, `response_minutes` and `sla_met` together — and listed with filters on `contract_id`, `equipment_id`, `assigned_technician_id`, `status` and `severity`. The two reads differ in one respect: the single-call read is club-scoped, so a `ClubManager` reaching a call outside its own club is answered `ERR_CLUB_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN` once the call has been found, whereas the collection read applies no club confinement at all and returns matching calls from every club to every role. Cancellation is club-scoped in the same way as the single read.

### 3.7. Spare Parts: Sourcing, the Allowance Waterfall, Loaners and Obsolescence

A `PartsPlanner` or a `ContractAdministrator` registers a spare part against an equipment model, supplying `model_id`, `part_number`, a `category` of `CONSUMABLE`, `WEAR`, `STRUCTURAL` or `ELECTRONIC`, a `unit_price_cents` list price, and optionally `is_critical_spare`, `initial_on_hand_qty` and `initial_refurb_qty`. An unknown model is `ERR_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`. Registration sets `lifecycle_status` to `ACTIVE`, `allocated_qty` to `0`, and the two stock buckets `on_hand_qty` and `refurb_on_hand_qty` from the supplied opening quantities, each defaulting to `0`. Parts may be read individually — the single-part read is where all three quantity buckets and the obsolescence state appear together — and listed with filters on `model_id`, `category`, `lifecycle_status` and `is_critical_spare`.

A `PartsPlanner` also moves stock directly, supplying a signed `new_qty_delta` and `refurb_qty_delta`. Each delta is added to its bucket, and neither bucket may be driven below zero: an adjustment that would do so is refused whole with `ERR_INVENTORY_ADJUSTMENT_INVALID`, leaving both buckets untouched.

**The sourcing waterfall.** When a technician places a call on parts hold (§3.6) they supply a `part_id`, a `qty`, and a `source` of `NEW`, `REFURBISHED` or `LOANER_SWAP`. Four gates apply in ladder order. The part must belong to the same model as the machine under repair, or the answer is `ERR_PART_MODEL_MISMATCH`. The requested bucket must hold at least `qty` units — `NEW` draws on `on_hand_qty` while both `REFURBISHED` and `LOANER_SWAP` draw on `refurb_on_hand_qty` — or the answer is `ERR_PART_OUT_OF_STOCK`. The source must be permitted for the part's `lifecycle_status`: once a part is `OBSOLETE`, `NEW` sourcing is refused with `ERR_PART_SOURCE_UNAVAILABLE`, and refurbished or loaner sourcing must be used instead. That is the only combination this gate refuses: an `ACTIVE` part and an `END_OF_SUPPORT_ANNOUNCED` part both still permit all three sources. And when the requested part falls inside the contract's `parts_inclusion` coverage, `parts_allowance_remaining_cents` must be strictly greater than zero, or the answer is `ERR_PARTS_ALLOWANCE_EXHAUSTED`. This last gate reads coverage alone and never the `source`, so a covered `LOANER_SWAP` is refused against an exhausted allowance even though the order it would have created is priced at zero.

The money on the resulting `PartOrder` is computed in a fixed order, and the order of operations matters. First the effective unit price: `unit_cents = round_half_up(part.unit_price_cents × SOURCE_PRICE_FACTOR[source])`, where the source factor is `1.00` for `NEW`, `0.60` for `REFURBISHED` and `0.00` for `LOANER_SWAP`. The per-unit figure is rounded to whole cents **before** it is multiplied by quantity, so `list_total_cents = unit_cents × qty`. Rounding once per unit and then multiplying is not the same as multiplying and then rounding once, and this specification requires the former. A loaner swap therefore always produces a `list_total_cents` of exactly `0`.

Next, coverage. `covered_cents` is the whole of `list_total_cents` when `parts_inclusion` is `FULL`; it is the whole of `list_total_cents` when `parts_inclusion` is `WEAR_ONLY` **and** the part's `category` is exactly `WEAR`; and it is `0` in every other case — under `NONE`, or for a `CONSUMABLE`, `STRUCTURAL` or `ELECTRONIC` part under `WEAR_ONLY`. Then the waterfall: `allowance_draw_cents = min(covered_cents, contract.parts_allowance_remaining_cents)`, and `club_billable_cents = list_total_cents − allowance_draw_cents`. The allowance bucket fills first and the club is billed only what the allowance could not absorb, which means a partly exhausted allowance splits one order across both buckets — a covered order of 5,000 cents against a remaining allowance of 1,800 cents draws 1,800 and bills the club 3,200. The two buckets always sum to `list_total_cents`. The contract's `parts_allowance_remaining_cents` then falls by `allowance_draw_cents`.

Allocation also moves stock: the source bucket falls by `qty` and `allocated_qty` rises by `qty`. The order is created in status `ALLOCATED`.

**Installation and cancellation.** The call's assigned technician installs an `ALLOCATED` order while the owning call is in `PARTS_HOLD`; another technician is `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_ASSIGNED`, and both remaining preconditions share one code: a non-allocated order **and** an owning call outside `PARTS_HOLD` each answer `ERR_PART_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`, the order's own status being tested first. Install never reports `ERR_CALL_INVALID_STATE`, because the state being asserted belongs to the order in the path rather than to the call. Installation sets `status` to `INSTALLED`, stamps `installed_at`, and reduces `allocated_qty` by `qty` **without** restoring any stock bucket — the unit was physically consumed. An installed order whose `source` is `LOANER_SWAP` does one thing more: it sets the machine's `loaner_active` to `true` and stamps `mitigated_at` on the call's open `DowntimeEvent` from the logical clock. That stamp caps both credit variants at the moment the loaner went in, on the reasoning that a member who has a working substitute machine is no longer being deprived of service even though the original unit is still under repair.

A `ServiceCoordinator` or a `PartsPlanner` cancels an `ALLOCATED` order with a `reason`, and cancellation is a true compensation rather than a simple status write. `status` becomes `CANCELLED` and `cancellation_reason` is stored. The contract's `parts_allowance_remaining_cents` is credited back the order's original `allowance_draw_cents`, and only then are `allowance_draw_cents` and `club_billable_cents` both zeroed — the money must be returned before the record of it is erased. The stock movement is reversed into **the same bucket it came from**: `on_hand_qty` for a `NEW` order, `refurb_on_hand_qty` for a `REFURBISHED` or `LOANER_SWAP` order, and `allocated_qty` falls by `qty`. Restoring a refurbished unit into the new-stock bucket would silently manufacture inventory and is incorrect. After every parts operation both standing identities of §2.11 must hold: `allocated_qty` equals the summed `qty` of the part's still-`ALLOCATED` orders, and the allowance total equals remaining plus the summed draws of `ALLOCATED` and `INSTALLED` orders — cancelled orders drop out of both sums because their draw was returned and zeroed. Orders may be read individually and listed with filters on `service_call_id`, `part_id`, `status` and `source`. Neither read is club-scoped: unlike the single-call and single-event reads, a `ClubManager` may read any part order and any catalogue line, because the catalogue and its orders are treated as company-wide inventory records rather than club property.

**Obsolescence.** A part's `lifecycle_status` begins `ACTIVE` and ends, terminally, at `OBSOLETE`. A `PartsPlanner` declares manufacturer end-of-support, and where that declaration lands depends on whether refurbished stock survives it. With `refurb_on_hand_qty` greater than zero the part becomes `END_OF_SUPPORT_ANNOUNCED`; with `refurb_on_hand_qty` at zero it goes straight to `OBSOLETE`. Either way `eos_declared_at` is stamped from the clock. A part already announced can also slide the rest of the way: an inventory adjustment whose resulting `refurb_on_hand_qty` is exactly `0` moves `END_OF_SUPPORT_ANNOUNCED` → `OBSOLETE`. The test is on the resulting balance, not on the movement, so an adjustment of `0` and `0` against an announced part whose refurbished bucket is already empty completes the slide just as a draining adjustment would — and, conversely, a parts hold that consumes the last refurbished unit does **not** trigger the slide, because allocation moves stock without consulting the part lifecycle at all. Those are the only three transitions the part lifecycle has, and the declaration endpoint has no state precondition of its own: it may be re-invoked at any time and never answers a state error. Re-declaring on an `END_OF_SUPPORT_ANNOUNCED` part with refurbished stock still on hand holds the status there; re-declaring on one whose refurbished bucket has since emptied moves it to `OBSOLETE`; and re-declaring on an already `OBSOLETE` part leaves the terminal status where it is. Every one of those re-declarations still re-stamps `eos_declared_at` — which is a change, so the part's `version` advances — and still re-runs the cascade below, re-stamping `eos_notified_at` on every matching machine.

Each of them may cascade outward, but **only when `is_critical_spare` is `true`**. A part with `is_critical_spare` set to `false` changes nothing but its own row, however dramatic its declaration — the company's position is that a fleet is only stranded by the loss of a part it cannot run without. When the part is a critical spare, the cascade runs in a fixed order. Hop one: every machine whose `model_id` matches the part's takes a new `support_status` — `END_OF_SUPPORT_ANNOUNCED` when the part became announced, `UNSUPPORTED` when the part became obsolete — and has `eos_notified_at` stamped from the clock. Hop two: because `support_status` changed, each touched machine re-evaluates `replacement_recommended`. Here the two branches genuinely diverge. An `UNSUPPORTED` machine is judged against the relaxed threshold of `60.00`, so any unit whose `health_score` is at or below `60.00` flips to recommended immediately; an `END_OF_SUPPORT_ANNOUNCED` machine keeps the ordinary `40.00` threshold and is very often left untouched. Hop three: because those flags changed, every covering contract recomputes `obsolete_unit_count` and `replacement_candidate_count` and re-derives `fleet_renewal_risk` from the new ratio, in that order. A single declaration on one critical spare can therefore move a contract from `LOW` to `HIGH` renewal risk without anybody touching the contract.

### 3.8. Downtime, Contractual Credits and Period Reconciliation

Every service call opens exactly one `DowntimeEvent`, and that event is the unit of the credit ledger. Its `status` begins at `OPEN` and can reach `CLOSED`, `RECONCILED` or `WAIVED`; only `WAIVED` is terminal, because a closed event still awaits reconciliation and a reconciled event may still be waived as a correction. Events are never created or deleted directly — they are born with their call and driven by what happens to it. They may be read individually, and the single-event read is deliberately the one place where both credit variants sit side by side for comparison. That read is club-scoped through the event's contract, so a `ClubManager` reaching an event outside its own club is answered `ERR_CLUB_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN` once the event has been found. They may be listed with filters on `contract_id`, `equipment_id`, `service_call_id`, `status` and `period_key`; the collection read, like every other listing in this specification, applies no club confinement.

**Closing an event.** When the owning call is resolved, its `OPEN` event moves to `CLOSED`. `ended_at` is stamped from the clock. Then `effective_end_at` is derived: it is `mitigated_at` when a loaner swap set that field (§3.7), and `ended_at` otherwise. Every subsequent number is measured against `effective_end_at`, not `ended_at`, so a loaner installed at hour three of a ten-hour repair truncates the billable outage to three hours.

Two spans are then measured. `downtime_minutes` is the raw wall-clock span, `floor((effective_end_at − started_at) / 60 seconds)`. `operating_downtime_minutes` is the part of that span that fell inside the club's trading hours: intersect the half-open interval `[started_at, effective_end_at)` with the club's daily window `[operating_open_minute, operating_close_minute)` — both expressed as minutes past UTC midnight — and sum the overlap day by day across every UTC calendar day the outage touched. Overnight and pre-opening minutes are excluded from the second figure and included in the first. `period_key` is the `YYYY-MM` of `effective_end_at`, which is the batch key the reconciliation will later match on.

**Two credit numbers from one event.** The same tier-based grace allowance and hourly rate are applied to both spans, producing two genuinely different money figures that both live on the row. Grace is `720` minutes for `BRONZE`, `360` for `SILVER` and `180` for `GOLD`; the rate is `400` cents per hour for `BRONZE`, `900` for `SILVER` and `1600` for `GOLD` — a richer tier both forgives less delay and pays more for it.

The *provisional wall-clock* variant is computed at closure, by the field technician's own action. It takes `billable = max(0, downtime_minutes − grace)` and sets `provisional_credit_cents = round_half_up(billable / 60 × rate)`. It bills every wall-clock minute the machine was unavailable, on the reasoning that a field crew has no authority to decide which of a club's hours count. The *final operating-hours* variant is computed at reconciliation, by a contract administrator. It takes `billable = max(0, operating_downtime_minutes − grace)` and sets `final_credit_cents = round_half_up(billable / 60 × rate)`. Because it counts only trading minutes, an outage that spanned a closed period is never worth more finally than provisionally, and is worth strictly less whenever its wall-clock span exceeded the tier's grace allowance — while an outage short enough that even the wall-clock span sits inside grace is worth exactly `0` in both variants. An outage entirely outside trading hours can carry a substantial provisional credit and a final credit of exactly `0`. Reconciliation posts the final figure and leaves `provisional_credit_cents` on the row untouched, as the audit record of what was first estimated. The grace allowance is applied **per event**, never once per period, and each event is rounded to whole cents before any period-level sum is taken.

**Period reconciliation.** A `ContractAdministrator` reconciles one period on one contract, supplying a `period_key` and the `expected_contract_version` token of §2.8. The candidate set is narrow: `DowntimeEvent` rows on that contract whose `status` is exactly `CLOSED` and whose `period_key` equals the requested key. `OPEN` events are excluded because their call is still unresolved, `WAIVED` events are excluded because they were forgiven, and `RECONCILED` events are excluded because they were already settled. A period whose key equals the contract's `last_reconciled_period_key` cannot be run a second time and answers `ERR_PERIOD_ALREADY_RECONCILED`. A period in which the candidate set comes out empty — no `CLOSED` event on this contract carries that `period_key`, whether because none was ever closed there or because every one has already been reconciled or waived — is refused with that same `ERR_PERIOD_ALREADY_RECONCILED` rather than with a distinct code and rather than settling zero events successfully; the two triggers are deliberately indistinguishable to the caller, and no ledger field, `last_reconciled_period_key` included, moves on either.

The candidates are processed in `created_at` ascending order — chronologically, never by identifier and never by magnitude — and the annual cap is consumed in that order. `credit_cap_cents` was pinned at contract creation as `round_half_up(annual_base_cents × 0.15)`. For each event in turn: `final_credit_cents` is computed with the operating-hours variant; `remaining_cap` is `credit_cap_cents − credit_accrued_cents` as it stands at that moment; `posted_credit_cents` is `min(final_credit_cents, max(0, remaining_cap))`; the shortfall `final_credit_cents − posted_credit_cents` is forgone; `status` becomes `RECONCILED`; and the contract's `credit_accrued_cents` and `credit_forgone_cents` rise by the posted and forgone amounts respectively before the next event is considered. Because the cap is consumed sequentially, **which** event gets clipped is a function of creation order alone: an early small event is paid in full and a later large one is truncated, even though reversing their order would have produced the same total posted and a completely different per-event breakdown. Once the loop finishes, `last_reconciled_period_key` is set to the requested key and the contract's `version` is incremented by one.

The reconciliation answers with a computed summary rather than a stored row: the `period_key` just settled, the `reconciled_event_count`, the `final_credit_total_cents` summed across those events, the `posted_credit_cents` and `forgone_credit_cents` totals for the run, the contract's resulting `contract_credit_accrued_cents`, and the new `contract_version` — which the client will need as the `expected_contract_version` of its next write. A separate computed read exposes the standing ledger for a contract to a `ContractAdministrator`, `ClubManager` or `ServiceCoordinator`: `contract_id`, `credit_provisional_cents`, `credit_accrued_cents`, `credit_forgone_cents`, `credit_cap_cents`, and the `open_event_count`, `closed_event_count` and `reconciled_event_count` behind them. Note that provisional and accrued are reported side by side and are not expected to agree; the gap between them is the difference between the two variants plus whatever the cap refused.

**Waiving.** A `ContractAdministrator` waives an event with a `waive_reason`, as a goodwill gesture or a data-quality correction. Waiving is permitted from `OPEN`, from `CLOSED` and from `RECONCILED`; a `WAIVED` event cannot be waived again and answers `ERR_DOWNTIME_INVALID_STATE`. What the waiver has to undo depends entirely on how far the event had got, and the three branches are deliberately unequal. From `OPEN` there is nothing to reverse: no credit fields were ever populated, so the event simply becomes `WAIVED` with its reason. This is also the branch a service-call cancellation takes automatically, recording `CALL_CANCELLED` as the reason. From `CLOSED`, the closure's arithmetic must be unwound: the contract's `credit_provisional_cents` falls by the event's `provisional_credit_cents` and the machine's `downtime_minutes_ytd` falls by the event's `downtime_minutes`, restoring both to their pre-closure values, after which `health_score`, `health_score_variant` and `replacement_recommended` are recomputed and the contract's `replacement_candidate_count` and `fleet_renewal_risk` re-derived. From `RECONCILED`, all of that happens **and** the settled ledger is unwound too: `credit_accrued_cents` falls by `posted_credit_cents`, `credit_forgone_cents` falls by `final_credit_cents − posted_credit_cents`, and `credit_provisional_cents` falls by `provisional_credit_cents`. Returning accrued credit to the contract also returns cap headroom, so a later period reconciled after such a waiver has more room than it would otherwise have had. The event's own computed figures are left on the row in every branch; the reversal is applied to the aggregates, not by erasing history.

### 3.9. Fleet Condition, Replacement Recommendations and Renewal Pricing

**Health scoring has two variants, and the row itself decides which one applies.** Whenever a machine's condition is re-derived — on service-call resolution, on the waiver of a `CLOSED` or `RECONCILED` downtime event, on a usage reading, on an end-of-support declaration touching its model, or on the inventory adjustment that completes an announced critical spare's slide into obsolescence — the system first counts the machine's `RESOLVED` service calls whose `resolved_at` falls at or after the logical clock minus 365 days. `CANCELLED`, `OPEN`, `DISPATCHED`, `ON_SITE` and `PARTS_HOLD` calls never count toward that census; only calls that were actually worked to a conclusion do. With fewer than three such calls the variant is `USAGE_BASED`; with three or more it is `FAILURE_BASED`. The chosen variant is recorded on the row in `health_score_variant`, so a reader can always tell which model produced the number.

The `USAGE_BASED` variant scores wear and age and ignores failures entirely. It takes `usage_ratio = usage_hours / model.expected_service_life_hours` and `age_years = floor((clock date − install_date) / 365 days)`, then `raw = 100 − (100 × usage_ratio) − (2 × age_years)`. `age_years` never goes negative: an `install_date` later than the clock's own date yields exactly `0`, and the same clamp applies wherever an age in whole years is derived from a calendar day, including the renewal-quote lines of the same subsection. A machine at 40% of its service life and six years old therefore scores `100 − 40 − 12 = 48.00`, and neither its downtime nor its repair history moves that figure at all.

The `FAILURE_BASED` variant is the mirror image: it ignores `usage_hours`, `install_date` and `expected_service_life_hours` completely, and scores from severity-weighted failures plus accumulated outage. It takes `raw = 100 − (8 × critical count) − (4 × major count) − (2 × minor count) − (1 × (downtime_minutes_ytd / 60))`, where the three counts are over the same trailing-365-day `RESOLVED` set that selected the variant, and the downtime term charges one point per hour of year-to-date downtime. Because the two formulas share no inputs beyond the row's identity, the same machine can score materially higher or lower the moment it crosses the three-call boundary — a lightly used but repeatedly repaired machine's score can fall sharply on its third resolution, and a heavily worn machine's score can rise. This is intended: past three failures in a year, the company's position is that observed unreliability tells you more than the hour meter.

Either way, `health_score` is `round_half_up(raw, 2)` clamped into `[0.00, 100.00]`. In the same hop, `replacement_recommended` is re-evaluated as `health_score <= threshold`, where the threshold is `60.00` when `support_status` is `UNSUPPORTED` and `40.00` otherwise. The boundary is inclusive, so a score of exactly `40.00` on a supported machine recommends replacement, as does exactly `60.00` on an unsupported one. Note that `END_OF_SUPPORT_ANNOUNCED` does **not** relax the threshold; only full `UNSUPPORTED` does.

**Fleet risk.** Three counters roll up from the fleet to the covering contract whenever `replacement_recommended` or `support_status` changes on any machine, and whenever a machine is added to a club. `Club.covered_unit_count` is the number of machines at the club; `contract.replacement_candidate_count` is how many of them carry `replacement_recommended` `true`; `contract.obsolete_unit_count` is how many carry `support_status` `UNSUPPORTED`. The contract-side roll-up reaches *every* contract whose `club_id` names that club, whatever that contract's own `status`: a `DRAFT`, `SUSPENDED`, `RENEWED` or `EXPIRED` contract has all three fields refreshed alongside the live one, in `created_at` order, and each such contract whose numbers actually moved advances its own `version`. Every machine of the club counts regardless of `lifecycle_status` — a `DOWN` or `TAGGED_OUT` machine is still part of the fleet and still part of the ratio. The ratio is `replacement_candidate_count / covered_unit_count`, treated as `0` when the fleet is empty. `fleet_renewal_risk` is `HIGH` when that ratio is at or above `0.30`, `MODERATE` when it is at or above `0.15`, and `LOW` otherwise; both boundaries are inclusive, so exactly `0.30` is `HIGH` and exactly `0.15` is `MODERATE`.

**Issuing a renewal quote.** A `ContractAdministrator` issues a quote on a contract that is `ACTIVE` or already `RENEWAL_QUOTED`, presenting the `expected_contract_version` token. The club's fleet must be non-empty — a contract covering no machines cannot be priced and answers `ERR_FLEET_EMPTY`. Pricing then runs in seven ordered steps, and the order of the roundings is part of the specification.

*Step one, per-unit lines.* Every machine of the contract's club is priced, in `created_at` ascending order. For each: `age_years = floor((issue date − install_date) / 365 days)`; `age_multiplier` comes from the step table `1.00` for 0–2 years, `1.15` for 3–5, `1.35` for 6–8 and `1.60` for 9 or more, with the lower year of each band inclusive, so a machine exactly six years old prices at `1.35`; and `condition_multiplier` starts at `1.00` and takes `+0.25` when `replacement_recommended` is `true`, `+0.20` when `support_status` is `UNSUPPORTED`, and `+0.10` when `support_status` is `END_OF_SUPPORT_ANNOUNCED`. The adders stack, so a replacement-recommended unsupported machine carries `1.45`. The line is `line_cents = round_half_up(model.base_annual_service_cents × age_multiplier × condition_multiplier)`, and one entry recording `equipment_id`, `age_years`, `age_multiplier`, `condition_multiplier` and `line_cents` is appended to `line_items`. `quoted_base_cents` is the sum of the lines — each line is rounded to whole cents **before** the summation, never after.

*Step two, parts surcharge.* `parts_surcharge_cents = round_half_up(quoted_base_cents × PARTS_SURCHARGE_PCT[parts_inclusion])`, where the rate is `0.00` for `NONE`, `0.08` for `WEAR_ONLY` and `0.18` for `FULL`.

*Step three, service-history load.* `sla_breach_count` counts every service call on the contract whose `sla_met` is `false`. `critical_call_count` counts every service call on the contract whose `severity` is `CRITICAL` and whose `status` is `RESOLVED`, filed with `reported_at` at or after `term_start_date` at `00:00:00Z` and strictly before the quote's `issued_at`; the breach count is scoped the same way. Then `history_load_pct = min(0.25, 0.03 × sla_breach_count + 0.015 × critical_call_count)`, and `history_surcharge_cents = round_half_up(quoted_base_cents × history_load_pct)`. The cap means a catastrophically bad service year loads the base by at most a quarter, however many breaches it contained.

*Step four, subtotal.* `subtotal_cents = quoted_base_cents + parts_surcharge_cents + history_surcharge_cents`.

*Step five, compliance discount.* `pm_compliance_pct_snapshot` is copied from the contract's live `pm_compliance_pct`, freezing it onto the quote. `compliance_discount_pct` is `0.05` when that snapshot is at or above `90.00`, `0.02` when it is at or above `75.00`, and `0.00` otherwise; `compliance_discount_cents = round_half_up(subtotal_cents × compliance_discount_pct)`. A club that let the company complete its preventive visits is rewarded for it.

*Step six, the collar.* `quoted = subtotal_cents − compliance_discount_cents` is then collared against the expiring term's `annual_base_cents`: `floor = round_half_up(annual_base_cents × 0.90)` and `cap = round_half_up(annual_base_cents × 1.35)`. **The floor is applied first and the cap second.** If `quoted` is below the floor it is raised to the floor and `floor_applied` is set `true`; then, separately, if `quoted` is above the cap it is lowered to the cap and `cap_applied` is set `true`. The result is `quoted_annual_cents`. Applying these in the reverse order can produce a different answer, so the stated order is mandatory.

*Step seven, index and validity.* `fleet_age_index = round_half_up(sum of age_multiplier over line_items / count of line_items, 2)`. `issued_at` is the logical clock and `expires_at` is exactly 30 days later.

Every intermediate figure is persisted on the quote, not merely used and discarded: `quoted_base_cents`, `parts_surcharge_cents`, `history_load_pct`, `history_surcharge_cents`, `subtotal_cents`, `compliance_discount_pct`, `compliance_discount_cents`, `quoted_annual_cents`, `fleet_age_index`, `sla_breach_count`, `critical_call_count`, `pm_compliance_pct_snapshot`, `floor_applied` and `cap_applied` are all readable on the row afterwards, so the whole derivation can be re-checked without recomputing it.

The quote is created in status `ISSUED`. Any quote already `ISSUED` on the same contract moves to `SUPERSEDED`, its numbers frozen for audit — this legitimate re-quote is the reason the endpoint is guarded by the version token of §2.8 rather than by a state precondition, since `RENEWAL_QUOTED` → `RENEWAL_QUOTED` is a permitted cycle. The contract's `status` becomes (or remains) `RENEWAL_QUOTED` and its `version` advances. Because every line is pinned into `line_items`, a later fleet change, a later resolution or a later end-of-support declaration never mutates an already-issued quote. A newborn quote also carries `decline_reason` as a present field reading null; only a decline ever gives it a value. Quotes may be read individually — the single-quote read is where the pinned lines and the whole pricing breakdown appear together — and listed with filters on `contract_id` and `status`. The single-quote read is club-scoped through the quote's contract, answering `ERR_CLUB_SCOPE_FORBIDDEN` to a `ClubManager` from another club once the quote has been found; the listing is not.

**Accepting and declining.** The `ClubManager` of the club decides. Both actions require the quote to be `ISSUED` and the contract to be `RENEWAL_QUOTED` (`ERR_QUOTE_NOT_ISSUED`), and both require the logical clock to be strictly before `expires_at` (`ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`); a decision landing exactly on `expires_at` is too late. The two branches then diverge sharply, and the asymmetry is deliberate. Acceptance sets the quote's `status` to `ACCEPTED`, moves the contract to `RENEWED`, and advances `version` — and does nothing else. In particular this endpoint creates no contract: the renewed term is transacted separately, by drafting a fresh contract in the ordinary way of §3.2, so nothing on the expiring row's forward schedule needs disturbing here and its `SCHEDULED` preventive visits and their reservations are left exactly as they stand. Decline sets the quote's `status` to `DECLINED`, records `decline_reason` from the supplied `reason`, moves the contract to `EXPIRED`, and advances `version` — and then tears the forward schedule down: every `PreventiveVisit` on the contract still in `visit_status` `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` set to the literal string `CONTRACT_EXPIRED`, each such visit's `RESERVED` `TechnicianAssignment` moves to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to that same literal `CONTRACT_EXPIRED`, and every affected technician's `committed_minutes` falls by that assignment's `planned_minutes`. Visits already `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED`, `MISSED` or `CANCELLED` are left untouched, exactly as under suspension, and open service calls on the contract are likewise left alone. `RENEWED` and `EXPIRED` are both terminal: there is no further transition out of either, and no operational work of any kind is permitted against a contract in either state.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is served under the `/fleetcare` prefix, with the single exception of the logical-clock control described in §2.5. Endpoints that bring a new resource into existence answer `201`; every other successful call answers `200`, including the state-transition endpoints that mutate an existing resource and the two computed reads. Note the two places where creation and transition coincide: placing a service call on parts hold answers `201` because it creates a `PartOrder`, and issuing a renewal quote answers `201` because it creates a `RenewalQuote`, even though both also advance the status of the resource named in the path. Unless stated otherwise, a successful call returns the affected resource in `data`; the two computed reads and the reconciliation return the computed object described in §3.8 instead. All list endpoints accept `limit` and `offset` and behave as described in §2.3.
