# Concierge Pediatrics & Newborn Care API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A concierge pediatric practice sells two things at once, and they are billed on entirely different logic. The first is a **membership**: a household pays a monthly retainer per enrolled child and receives unhurried well-child care, direct round-the-clock access to a named physician, lactation and sleep consulting, and school or camp paperwork turned round inside a promised service-level window. The second is ordinary **clinical care** — a sick visit, an after-hours call, a newborn weight check — which may still be a claim against the family's insurance carrier. The practice's operational pain is that a single clinical event can land on either side of that line depending on what kind of visit it was, what tier the family bought, and whether the household happens to hold an active policy on the day the charge is written.

This document specifies the backend service that runs such a practice. It is mounted under `/peds` and exposes a flat set of peer collections: plan tiers, panel physicians, households, children, insurance policies, memberships, panel waitlist entries, benefit pools, consult bookings, well visits, immunization doses, encounters, service lines, invoices and form requests. Nothing is nested in the URL space. Relationships are carried by identifier fields on the rows themselves, which means the service — never the client — owns the job of keeping related records consistent when any one of them moves. And they move constantly: enrolling a second child re-prices the first, cancelling a membership frees panel weight that promotes somebody off a waiting list, invalidating a single vaccine dose can retroactively shift an entire series and block a camp form that was queued yesterday.

Three commitments run through everything below. First, **the ladder is always evaluated after the mutation, never before** — sibling discounts, panel occupancy and vaccine series positions are all recomputed from the post-mutation world, so an implementation that computes them from a pre-mutation snapshot will disagree with this specification on nearly every multi-child household. Second, **every compensating action returns exactly what its forward action took**: cancelling a consult booking returns its unit to the same benefit pool, and voiding an adjudicated service line gives the policy back precisely the deductible dollars that line consumed. Third, **clinical truth and financial truth advance on separate axes**: an encounter can be clinically finished while its charges are still unrouted, or fully billed and closed while the physician has not yet written a note, and the service models both facts simultaneously rather than collapsing them into one status.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, naming the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity the caller acts in. If either header is missing, or if `X-User-Role` carries a value outside the five declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). The five roles are `PracticeAdmin`, `Physician`, `CareCoordinator`, `BillingSpecialist` and `Parent`. A caller whose role is recognised but is not listed among the authorised roles for the endpoint it is calling is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).

Two of the five roles are entity-backed, meaning `X-User-ID` names a row that already exists in the system.

- When `X-User-Role` is `Physician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Physician row; ownership checks on any resource that references a physician compare the resource's physician identifier to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, on the four clinical writes named below, the membership's `physician_id` must equal `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Parent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Family row; ownership checks on any resource that reaches a household compare that household's identifier to `X-User-ID`.

The other three are opaque identifiers not tied to any persisted row. When `X-User-Role` is `PracticeAdmin`, `X-User-ID` identifies a practice operator; the server may record it — for instance as the `submitted_by_user_id` or `adjudicated_by_user_id` stamped on a service line — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds when `X-User-Role` is `CareCoordinator`, the front-office scheduler who registers children, enrols and cancels memberships, manages the waitlist and books consults and well visits, and when `X-User-Role` is `BillingSpecialist`, the revenue-cycle operator who attaches policies, submits encounter charges, adjudicates and settles lines, and drives invoices.

**Ownership enforcement.** A `Parent` may only reach resources belonging to their own household. The household is reached from the target resource by whichever path exists: a child's `family_id`, a membership's `family_id`, or — for a consult booking, encounter, form request, invoice, service line or panel waitlist entry — the household of the membership (or, for an invoice, the household named directly on the row) that the resource belongs to. If the household so reached is not the caller's own `X-User-ID`, the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). A `Physician` is subject to the same rung on four clinical writes only — completing a well visit, completing the clinical axis of an encounter, cancelling an encounter, and issuing a form request — where the targeted membership's `physician_id` must equal `X-User-ID`, failing which the rejection is again `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Operations that target a Child rather than a Membership — recording an immunization dose and invalidating one — never apply this rung at all, because a dose belongs to a child and not to a panel.

**Segregation of duties.** One rule binds every role, including `PracticeAdmin`: the caller who adjudicates a service line may not be the same caller who submitted it. This is expanded in §3.7 and is enforced with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read — like every other instant this service emits — from the logical clock of §2.4 and never from the host's wall clock, so it repeats verbatim across requests taken at the same clock setting.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints, and are absent from every single-object response, whose `meta` carries `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp` and nothing else.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Ordering and List Filters

Every list endpoint accepts two pagination query parameters. `limit` caps how many rows the page returns; it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` skips that many rows before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (400); so is either parameter supplied as something that is not a base-10 integer. Either parameter omitted, or present as an empty string, falls back to its default.

List results are ordered by the row's creation timestamp, ascending, in every collection. A secondary tie-break is genuinely needed and is pinned here: because `created_at` is stamped from the logical clock of §2.4 rather than from a real-time source, every row a single request mints carries an identical `created_at`, so ties are broken by **write order** — the server keeps one hidden, store-wide, strictly increasing creation counter for exactly this purpose — and never by `id`. Wherever this document orders rows by `created_at` ascending or descending, that same write-order tie-break decides: the sibling ladder of §3.2, the invoice line array of §3.3, the promotion selector and the cascade fan-out of §3.4, the dose series of §3.5, the service lines of an encounter and the policy candidate set of §3.7. Pagination is applied after sorting. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the envelope's `meta` object, where `total_count` counts every row matching the supplied filters — not the rows on the returned page — and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

Filters are optional, combine conjunctively, and restrict results to rows whose corresponding field matches the supplied value. A filter that is absent, or present as an empty string, imposes no restriction at all. Except for the two typed cases noted at the end of this paragraph, every filter value is compared as an exact string against the row's field and is never resolved, parsed or validated first — an identifier naming nothing that exists simply matches no row and returns an empty page with `total_count` 0 rather than a 404 or a 400, and `cycle_start_date` is compared against the invoice's cycle start rendered in the same bare `YYYY-MM-DD` form, so a malformed value likewise just matches nothing. Insurance policies filter on `family_id` and `status`. Children filter on `family_id`. Memberships filter on `family_id`, `child_id`, `plan_id`, `physician_id` and `status`. Panel waitlist entries filter on `physician_id`, `child_id`, `requested_plan_id`, `promoted_membership_id` and `status`. Entitlement grants filter on `membership_id`, `benefit_type`, `membership_year_index` and `status`. Consult bookings filter on `membership_id`, `grant_id`, `consultant_physician_id` and `status`. Well visits filter on `membership_id`, `child_id`, `milestone_code` and `status`. Immunization doses filter on `child_id`, `vaccine_code`, `validity` and `prior_valid_dose_id`. Encounters filter on `membership_id`, `child_id`, `clinical_status` and `billing_status` — the two axes are filtered independently because they move independently. Service lines filter on `encounter_id`, `consult_booking_id`, `well_visit_id`, `membership_id`, `policy_id`, `route` and `status`. Invoices filter on `family_id`, `status` and `cycle_start_date`. Form requests filter on `membership_id`, `child_id` and `status`. An enum-typed filter carrying a value outside its declared member set is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (400) — that is the first typed case, and it binds every enum-typed filter named above whether or not §2.7 lists that particular field by name. The second is `membership_year_index` on the entitlement-grant list, the one numeric filter in the service, which must parse as an integer and is otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (400).

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads the host's wall clock. Every window test, expiry comparison, age calculation and elapsed-day interval is measured against a single server-held **logical clock**. Before any client has moved it, the clock reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `PracticeAdmin` moves it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response's `data` object reports the new setting as `current_utc` and carries no other field — it is a computed answer, not a resource, so none of the four common properties of §2.5 appear on it. The clock may only move forward or stand still: a `to_iso8601` value earlier than the clock's present value is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE` (400).

Wherever this document says "the current date" or "today", it means the UTC calendar date of the logical clock; "the current instant" means the clock's full value. Day counts between two calendar dates are whole-day differences with no fractional component. Moving the clock also runs two sweeps, described where they belong: overdue well visits are swept to `MISSED` (§3.5) and stale panel waitlist entries are swept to `EXPIRED` (§3.4), in that order. Both sweeps run on every accepted call, including a call that merely sets the clock to the value it already held.

Every datetime the API emits — `created_at`, `updated_at`, `started_at`, `ends_at`, `scheduled_start`, `scheduled_end`, `requested_at`, `due_at`, `issued_at` and the clock's own `current_utc` — is rendered as ISO 8601 UTC with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z`, for example `2026-06-18T00:00:00.000Z`. Inbound datetime values are accepted with or without fractional digits — anywhere from one to nine of them, truncated rather than rounded to milliseconds — with either `T` or `t` separating date from time and with `Z`, `z`, `+00:00`, `+0000`, `-00:00` or `-0000` as the zone, and are normalised to that emitted form before persisting. A caller who supplies `2026-03-01T00:00:00Z` therefore reads it back as `2026-03-01T00:00:00.000Z`, byte-identical to a server stamp; a value carrying any other zone offset, or no zone at all, does not parse. Fields typed as a calendar date — `date_of_birth`, `start_date`, `cycle_start_date`, `cycle_end_date`, `administered_date`, `completed_date`, `scheduled_date`, `needed_by_date`, `target_date`, `window_open_date`, `window_close_date`, `cancellation_effective_date`, `issued_date`, `due_date` and `paid_date` — are rendered as a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` string with no time part. A date field that does not parse as `YYYY-MM-DD`, or a datetime field that does not parse as ISO 8601 UTC, is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (400).

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Every resource in the system — membership plans, physicians, families, insurance policies, children, memberships, panel waitlist entries, entitlement grants, consult bookings, well visits, immunization doses, encounters, service lines, invoices and form requests — carries the same four system-managed fields. A resource's payload is exactly these four plus the fields the narrative of §3 names for that entity, whether the read is a single-object read, a list row or the object returned by a create or a state transition: no endpoint hides a field the entity carries and none adds one it does not, and a field whose value is not yet set is emitted as JSON `null` rather than omitted. The four fields are:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and are never supplied by the client.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written, taken from the logical clock.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations the server performs as a side effect of an operation aimed at some other resource.
- `version` (integer): a counter that begins at 1 and increments by 1 on every mutation of the row — but **at most once per row per HTTP request**, however many separate code paths of that one request happen to write to it. A row the request itself created is exempt outright: it is born at `version` 1 and answers at `version` 1 even when a later step of the same request rewrites its fields, which is why a newly enrolled membership reads 1 after the sibling ladder has re-priced it, a newly created entitlement grant reads 1 after a booking has taken a unit out of it, and a newly opened draft invoice reads 1 after it has been computed. `updated_at` follows the looser rule and is refreshed from the logical clock on every write, including writes that leave `version` exactly where it stood.

Because the cascades in this system routinely reach several rows deep, it bears repeating: when the service suspends a membership because an invoice went past due, expires a benefit pool because a membership was cancelled, or rewrites a draft invoice because a sibling enrolled, each touched row has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented. There is no such thing as a silent side effect here. Exactly two deliberate exceptions to the once-per-request ceiling exist, and both are described where they belong. An insurance policy's `version` moves once per **adjudicated line voided** when an encounter is cancelled (§3.6), so cancelling an encounter that carries two adjudicated lines against the same policy advances that policy by 2 inside the single request. And the sibling ladder of §3.2 writes to a membership only when re-running it actually changes that row's `enrollment_sequence`, `discount_pct` or `monthly_rate_cents`, so a sibling whose rung did not move keeps both its `version` and its `updated_at`. A `DRAFT` invoice rebuild is the counter-case: it is unconditional and advances the invoice's `version` even when the recomputed lines come out identical to the ones already there.

### 2.6. Identifier Resolution and Not-Found Routing

Identifiers that appear in a URL path are resolved before any body validation, ownership check or business rule runs, and they are resolved left to right — the first segment that fails to resolve determines the 404 returned. The path variables are `{plan_id}`, `{physician_id}`, `{family_id}`, `{policy_id}`, `{child_id}`, `{membership_id}`, `{waitlist_entry_id}`, `{grant_id}`, `{booking_id}`, `{well_visit_id}`, `{dose_id}`, `{encounter_id}`, `{service_line_id}`, `{invoice_id}` and `{form_request_id}`. Identifiers supplied in a request *body* are resolved later — after body-shape and date validation — but are routed to the same family of error codes.

Which 404 comes back depends on the collection the missing identifier belongs to. A household that does not resolve yields `ERR_FAMILY_NOT_FOUND`. A child yields `ERR_CHILD_NOT_FOUND`. A physician yields `ERR_PHYSICIAN_NOT_FOUND` — this covers `physician_id` and `consultant_physician_id` alike. A plan tier yields `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, covering both `plan_id` and `requested_plan_id`. A membership yields `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_FOUND`. Everything else — an insurance policy, panel waitlist entry, entitlement grant, consult booking, well visit, immunization dose, encounter, service line, invoice or form request — yields the generic `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. All six carry HTTP status 404. No route in this service carries more than one path identifier, so the left-to-right rule never has to choose between two unresolvable segments; where two identifiers could both fail on one request they are a path identifier and a body identifier, and the path one is always resolved first. A request aimed at a path matching no route at all, or at a known path with a method that path does not support, is answered with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404) as well.

### 2.7. The Order in Which a Request Is Screened

Rules are evaluated in a strict ascending order and the **first** rule that fails determines the response. No later rule runs and no partial mutation is persisted. The ladder has seventeen rungs; wherever two rules could both fire on the same request, this ordering is normative.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the five declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorisation.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised-role list in §4 — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path existence.** Every identifier in the URL path resolves, left to right, routed to the six 404 codes of §2.6: `{family_id}` to `ERR_FAMILY_NOT_FOUND`, `{child_id}` to `ERR_CHILD_NOT_FOUND`, `{physician_id}` to `ERR_PHYSICIAN_NOT_FOUND` (on both the physician read and the panel summary read), `{membership_id}` on every membership route to `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_FOUND`, `{plan_id}` to `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, and every other collection's identifier to `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`.
4. **Request schema.** On every POST endpoint, each field the endpoint declares as required must be present and carry its declared JSON type, failing which the response is `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). A field declared as a string must be a non-empty string; a field declared as an integer must be a JSON integer and a boolean is never accepted as one; a field declared as an array must be a non-empty array whose members all carry the declared element type — concretely `licensed_state_codes` must be a non-empty array each of whose members is exactly two upper-case characters, and a violation there is `ERR_VALIDATION` too. Fields the endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected, and a request whose body is absent, empty or not well-formed JSON is screened exactly as though it were `{}`, so it fails on the first field the endpoint requires. Each enum-typed body field must carry a declared member value — this covers `tier`, `form_type`, `consult_type`, `modality`, `encounter_type`, `vaccine_code`, `benefit_type`, `status`, `route`, `validity`, `clinical_status`, `billing_status` and `milestone_code`, whether supplied in a body or as a list filter — failing which the response is `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (400). Numeric ranges must hold: `panel_slot_cap` strictly greater than 0, `monthly_rate_cents` at least 0, `coinsurance_rate` from 0 through 1 inclusive, `included_lactation_consults` and `included_sleep_consults` at least 0, `overage_consult_fee_cents` at least 0, `form_sla_hours` strictly greater than 0, `deductible_cents` at least 0, `limit` from 1 through 100 inclusive, `offset` at least 0, and `allowed_amount_cents` from 0 through the target line's `billed_amount_cents` inclusive. A range violation is `ERR_VALIDATION` (400) and the rung guards physician creation, plan creation, insurance-policy creation, service-line adjudication and every list endpoint.
5. **Date format and ordering.** Every date field must parse as `YYYY-MM-DD` and every datetime field as ISO 8601 UTC, failing which the response is `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). Then ordering is checked, failing which the response is `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE` (400): `scheduled_end` must be strictly after `scheduled_start` on consult-booking creation and reschedule; `cycle_start_date` must be the 1st day of a calendar month on invoice creation; a membership `start_date` must satisfy today at or before `start_date`, and `start_date` at or before the last day of the calendar month containing today; a cancellation `effective_date` must be on or after the membership's `start_date` and must itself lie between today and the last day of the current calendar month, both bounds inclusive; and `to_iso8601` on the clock endpoint must not be earlier than the clock's current value.
6. **Body reference existence.** Identifiers carried in the request body must resolve. `family_id` is checked on child creation, insurance-policy creation and invoice creation (`ERR_FAMILY_NOT_FOUND`). `child_id` is checked on membership creation, waitlist-entry creation and immunization-dose creation (`ERR_CHILD_NOT_FOUND`). `plan_id` on membership creation and `requested_plan_id` on waitlist-entry creation are checked against the plan collection (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`). `physician_id` on membership creation and waitlist-entry creation, and `consultant_physician_id` on consult-booking creation, are checked against the physician collection (`ERR_PHYSICIAN_NOT_FOUND`). `membership_id` is checked on consult-booking creation, encounter creation and form-request creation (`ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_FOUND`). All four codes carry 404. Where one endpoint carries several body identifiers the resolution order inside this rung is pinned: membership creation resolves `child_id`, then `plan_id`, then `physician_id`, and only then the household reached through the child; waitlist-entry creation resolves `child_id`, then `requested_plan_id`, then `physician_id`; and consult-booking creation resolves `consultant_physician_id` **before** `membership_id`, so a request naming neither answers `ERR_PHYSICIAN_NOT_FOUND`. Every other endpoint carries a single body identifier and the question does not arise.
7. **Resource ownership.** The `Parent` and `Physician` rules of §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). The rung applies to every endpoint whose authorised-role list includes `Parent` or `Physician` and that reads or writes a household-scoped resource; it never applies to immunization-dose recording or invalidation, and it never applies to a membership-plan or physician read, both of which are household-agnostic catalogue lookups any authorised role may make. On a **list** endpoint the rung does not reject at all: a `Parent` caller's result set is silently narrowed to the rows of their own household before filtering and paging, and `total_count` counts only those rows, so a `Parent` listing children, memberships, waitlist entries, entitlement grants, consult bookings, well visits, immunization doses, encounters, service lines, invoices or form requests sees their own household and nothing else rather than a 403. The caller's own `X-User-ID` is never itself resolved against the Family collection either — a `Parent` naming a household that does not exist matches no row and is refused ownership on a single-resource read, never answered with a 404.
8. **Segregation of duties.** On service-line adjudication, `X-User-ID` must differ from that line's `submitted_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
9. **Optimistic concurrency.** The version expectations of §2.8 — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). The rung guards exactly three writes: membership creation, membership cancellation and service-line adjudication. On adjudication the comparison is skipped outright when the target line names no policy — a line routed `COVERED_BY_MEMBERSHIP` or `PATIENT_RESPONSIBILITY` carries a null `policy_id`, so there is no version to compare against — which means such a line can never answer `ERR_STALE_VERSION` however wrong the supplied `expected_policy_version` is, and falls through to rung 12 instead. The field is still required by rung 4 on every adjudication, insurance-routed or not.
10. **Target state preconditions.** The target row's current state must permit the requested transition, including refusal of every transition attempted out of a terminal state — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). This rung guards every action endpoint on memberships, panel waitlist entries, consult bookings, well visits, immunization doses, encounters, service lines, invoices and form requests. Two state rules sit on the same rung with their own codes. Where an operation additionally demands that the governing membership currently read `ACTIVE` — creating a consult booking, rescheduling a consult booking, creating an encounter and completing a well visit — a membership in `PENDING_ACTIVATION`, `SUSPENDED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). (Form issuance expresses the same requirement differently: rather than erroring, it routes a non-active membership through the gate ladder of §3.9 and returns a `BLOCKED` row.) And where an operation depends on the household's invoice standing — reinstating requires the family to hold zero invoices in `ISSUED` whose `due_date` is strictly before today, while suspending requires at least one such invoice — a violation is `ERR_ENROLLMENT_INVOICE_UNPAID` (409). Where both a status test and one of those two extra tests could fire on the same request, the target row's own status is always evaluated first: suspending a membership that is not `ACTIVE` answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` even when the household also has no past-due invoice, and reinstating a membership that is not `SUSPENDED` answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` even when a past-due invoice is outstanding.
11. **Cross-entity policy state.** On service-line adjudication, the insurance policy the line names must currently read `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_POLICY_INACTIVE` (409).
12. **Route eligibility.** On service-line adjudication, the line's `route` must be `BILLED_TO_INSURANCE`; a line routed `COVERED_BY_MEMBERSHIP` or `PATIENT_RESPONSIBILITY` can never be adjudicated and is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_ADJUDICABLE` (422). This is the one rung evaluated **out of numeric order — immediately after rung 9 and therefore ahead of rungs 10 and 11** — and deliberately so: a line on either of those two routes is born `SETTLED` and names no policy, so leaving the route test at position 12 would let rung 10 answer `ERR_INVALID_STATE` first and make `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_ADJUDICABLE` unreachable in practice. For a line that *is* routed `BILLED_TO_INSURANCE` the route test passes silently and rungs 10 and 11 then run in their declared order, so an insurance line that is already `ADJUDICATED`, `SETTLED` or `VOID` still answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` and an insurance line on a `TERMINATED` policy still answers `ERR_POLICY_INACTIVE`.
13. **Clinical and compliance safety.** When `modality` is `TELEHEALTH`, the supplied `patient_state_code` must be a member of the responsible physician's `licensed_state_codes` — the responsible physician being the membership's `physician_id` for an encounter and the `consultant_physician_id` for a consult booking — otherwise `ERR_LICENSURE_NOT_COVERED` (422), guarding encounter creation and consult-booking creation. On encounter creation, an `encounter_type` of `NEWBORN_WEIGHT_CHECK` may never be delivered with `modality` `TELEHEALTH`, and such a request is refused with `ERR_MODALITY_NOT_ALLOWED` (422). Clinically dated fields may not lie in the future: `date_of_birth` on child creation, `administered_date` on dose creation and `completed_date` on well-visit completion may not be after today, and `started_at` on encounter creation may not be after the current instant — otherwise `ERR_FUTURE_DATED` (422).
14. **Temporal overlap.** Consult-booking creation and reschedule run the buffered calendar test of §3.8 against the consultant's other `SCHEDULED` bookings, and a collision is `ERR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` (409). Encounter creation runs the closed-interval test of §3.6 against the child's other `SCHEDULED` encounters, and a collision is `ERR_ENCOUNTER_OVERLAP` (409).
15. **Uniqueness.** On membership creation, the child must hold no other membership whose status is `PENDING_ACTIVATION`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_MEMBERSHIP` (409). Three further uniqueness constraints share the code `ERR_DUPLICATE_WAITLIST_ENTRY` (409): no other panel waitlist entry with the same `physician_id` and `child_id` may currently be `WAITING`; no membership plan may already carry the requested `tier`; and no invoice may already exist for the same `family_id` and `cycle_start_date`.
16. **Panel capacity.** On membership creation, the physician's `weighted_occupancy` plus the candidate child's `current_slot_weight` must be less than or equal to the physician's `panel_slot_cap`; equality is accepted. Exceeding it is `ERR_PANEL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
17. **Fiscal and limit checks.** A consult booking's `reschedule_count` must be strictly less than 2 before a reschedule is applied — otherwise `ERR_RESCHEDULE_LIMIT_REACHED` (409). On adjudication, a supplied `patient_responsibility_override_cents` must be less than or equal to the sum of the `deductible_applied_cents` and `coinsurance_cents` derived from the submitted `allowed_amount_cents`; anything larger is an attempt to collect the write-off from the household and is refused with `ERR_BALANCE_BILLING_BLOCKED` (422). A form request whose status is `BLOCKED` may not be issued and must first be recovered through a recheck; attempting issuance is `ERR_FORM_GATE_NOT_MET` (409). Finally, settlement is exact: `amount_paid_cents` must equal the invoice's `total_cents` on invoice payment and `payment_received_cents` must equal the line's `patient_responsibility_cents` on line settlement, and any other amount — short or over — is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` (400).

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on the Household and on the Policy

Three write operations in this system read a parent row, make a decision that depends on what they read, and then write something that legitimately supersedes the previous view. All three require the caller to prove it saw the parent as it currently stands. All three reject with the same identifier, `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, at HTTP status 412.

**Enrolling a child.** A `POST /peds/memberships` request must carry `expected_family_version`, a non-optional integer holding the client's last observed `version` of the Family reached through the named child. If that value does not equal the family's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). This matters because enrolment rewrites the household's entire pricing ladder: every sibling membership is re-sequenced, re-discounted and re-prorated, and the family's draft invoice is rebuilt. A caller holding an older version priced the enrolment against a ladder that has already moved.

**Cancelling a membership.** A `POST /peds/memberships/{membership_id}/cancel` request must carry `expected_family_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of that membership's Family. A mismatch with the family's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). Cancellation shifts every remaining sibling one rung up the discount ladder and rewrites the family's draft invoice, so a caller working from a stale ladder must re-read before acting.

**Adjudicating a service line.** A `POST /peds/service-lines/{service_line_id}/adjudicate` request must carry `expected_policy_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the InsurancePolicy the line names. A mismatch with the policy's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). Every adjudication consumes part of the policy's remaining deductible and supersedes the previous view of `deductible_met_cents`; a caller working from an older version computed its deductible split against a balance that no longer exists.

No other endpoint accepts a version expectation field. Endpoints not named above take no version input and perform no concurrency check.

### 2.9. Money, Weights and Rounding

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in whole cents. There are no fractional-cent values anywhere in a request or a response and no currency strings. Wherever a monetary computation produces a fraction — a sibling discount, a prorated slice of a billing cycle, a coinsurance share — the result is rounded to the nearest whole cent using the round-half-up convention, meaning a value exactly halfway between two cents rounds away from zero to the larger cent.

Two rounding *orders* are pinned and must not be rearranged, because rearranging them changes the answer:

- In invoice construction, the discount is applied first, then the proration, and each line is rounded to whole cents **before** any summation. The subtotal is the sum of already-rounded line values; the sum is never rounded a second time.
- In the insurance split, the round-half-up is applied exactly once, to the coinsurance product, and never to the intermediate deductible or plan-paid figures, which are exact integer subtractions.

Panel slot weights are the one non-monetary quantity carrying a precision rule: they are expressed to exactly one decimal place, and every capacity comparison is performed on the values multiplied by ten and rounded to the nearest integer, so a comparison of 4.5 against 4.5 is exact rather than subject to binary floating-point drift. Discount percentages are exact decimal fractions — `0.00`, `0.20` and `0.35` — and are never rounded themselves; only the money they produce is. Unit counts, day counts and dose indices are integers throughout and are never rounded because they are never fractional.

### 2.10. Conservation Guarantees

Three quantities are conserved. Each has a forward action that moves a quantity from one bucket to another and a compensating action that moves it back, and each identity must hold after **every** request, not merely at the end of a workflow.

**Benefit-pool conservation.** For every entitlement grant, `available_count` plus `booked_count` plus `consumed_count` always equals `granted_count`. Booking a consult against a pool with units left moves one unit from available to booked; cancelling that booking moves exactly the same unit from booked back to available; completing it moves the unit from booked to consumed, which is permanent. `released_count` and `overage_count` sit deliberately **outside** this identity — they are audit counters, not terms of it. A create followed by a cancel therefore restores `available_count` to its exact pre-create value, leaves `booked_count` and `consumed_count` unchanged, and advances `released_count` by one.

**Deductible conservation.** For every insurance policy, `deductible_met_cents` always equals the sum of `deductible_applied_cents` across that policy's service lines currently in `ADJUDICATED` or `SETTLED`. Adjudicating a line adds that line's `deductible_applied_cents` to the policy's running total; voiding an already-adjudicated line — which happens when its parent encounter is cancelled — subtracts exactly the same amount back out. Voiding a line that was merely `ROUTED` touches nothing, because nothing had been consumed yet. Voiding is not applied to lines already in `SETTLED`, so their contribution to the total stands.

**Invoice conservation.** For every invoice, `total_cents` always equals `subtotal_cents` minus `cap_adjustment_cents`, and that same figure always equals the sum of `line_total_cents` across the invoice's lines. The family-cap waterfall of §3.3 distributes the adjustment across the lines in a pinned order precisely so that these two ways of arriving at the total can never disagree.

### 2.11. Status Codes

Operations that bring a new resource into existence return `201 Created`. Every other successful operation — state transitions, recomputations, clock movements and reads — returns `200 OK`. Two operations return a success status even though the business outcome was a refusal: creating a form request whose gate fails returns 201 with a `BLOCKED` row, and issuing a form request whose gate has since failed returns 200 with a `BLOCKED` row. Error responses carry the HTTP status listed against their identifier in §5.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Setting Up the Practice: Tiers, Panels, Households and Charts

A `PracticeAdmin` seeds the practice, and the first thing they create is the **plan catalogue**. Each plan row declares a `tier` of `ESSENTIAL`, `PLUS` or `PREMIER`, and no two plan rows may carry the same tier — a second attempt at an existing tier is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_WAITLIST_ENTRY`. The plan carries a `monthly_rate_cents`, the undiscounted full-cycle retainer for one child, which must be at least 0. It carries `included_lactation_consults` and `included_sleep_consults`, both at least 0, which decide how many of each benefit a membership on this tier receives per membership year. It carries `form_sla_hours`, strictly greater than 0, promising how quickly a school or camp form on this tier will be turned round. It carries a boolean `after_hours_surcharge_waived`, which decides whether an after-hours contact on this tier costs the household anything. And it carries `overage_consult_fee_cents`, at least 0, the amount billed for a consult booked once the benefit pool has run dry. In the practice's own reference catalogue the `ESSENTIAL` tier bills 25 000 cents a cycle with one lactation consult, no sleep consults, a five-day (120-hour) form turnaround, no surcharge waiver and a 18 000-cent overage fee; `PLUS` bills 40 000 cents with three lactation and two sleep consults, a three-day (72-hour) turnaround and a 15 000-cent overage fee; `PREMIER` bills 60 000 cents with six lactation and four sleep consults, a same-day (24-hour) turnaround, the surcharge waived outright and a 12 000-cent overage fee. Plan rows may be read individually.

Panel **physicians** are registered next. A physician row carries a `full_name`, a `panel_slot_cap` strictly greater than 0 and expressed to exactly one decimal place giving the maximum weighted panel occupancy that clinician may carry, and a `licensed_state_codes` array — a non-empty list of two-character upper-case state codes naming where the clinician may practise telehealth. Two further figures are computed on every read rather than stored: `weighted_occupancy`, the live sum of the slot weights of every membership on that physician whose status is `PENDING_ACTIVATION`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, evaluated against today's date; and `available_panel_weight`, which is the `panel_slot_cap` minus the `weighted_occupancy` and is floored at 0 so it never reports a negative figure. A cancelled membership contributes nothing. A separate panel-summary capability returns the same physician's `physician_id`, `panel_slot_cap`, `weighted_occupancy` and `available_panel_weight` alongside two counts: `occupying_membership_count`, how many memberships currently occupy the panel in those three statuses, and `waiting_entry_count`, how many panel waitlist entries for that physician are still `WAITING`. That summary is a computed projection rather than a resource: it carries those six fields and none of the four common properties of §2.5. The `panel_slot_cap` is echoed back exactly as it was supplied, and `weighted_occupancy` and `available_panel_weight` are rendered to one decimal place on both the physician read and the panel summary.

Occupancy is deliberately **age-weighted and recomputed live**. A newborn takes far more of a concierge physician's year than a school-age child, so a child under one year of age — that is, whose age in whole days is strictly less than 365 — consumes 2.0 units of panel weight; a child of at least 365 days but strictly under 1095 days consumes 1.5; and a child of 1095 days or more consumes 1.0. Because the weight is a function of the child's age **at today's date** rather than at enrolment, a physician's occupancy falls on its own as the panel ages, and a panel that could not accept a new baby last month may be able to accept one this month without anybody having left.

**Households** are created as families. A family row carries a `household_name` and a `family_monthly_cap_cents` of at least 0, the ceiling that any single invoice for that household may reach once the sibling ladder has been applied. It also exposes a computed `active_membership_count`: how many of its memberships currently hold status `PENDING_ACTIVATION`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`. The family's `version` is the concurrency token described in §2.8, and the household read is the natural place for a client to fetch it before enrolling or cancelling.

**Children** are registered by a `PracticeAdmin` or a `CareCoordinator` into an existing household, naming the `family_id`, the child's `first_name` and a `date_of_birth`. A birth date after today is refused with `ERR_FUTURE_DATED`. Three values are computed on every read of a child and never stored: `age_days`, the whole-day difference between today and `date_of_birth`; `current_slot_weight`, the panel weight the child consumes today under the 2.0 / 1.5 / 1.0 bands just described; and `immunization_complete_for_age`, the boolean gate described in §3.5.

**Insurance policies** attach to a household. A `PracticeAdmin` or `BillingSpecialist` creates one naming the `family_id`, the `carrier_name`, a `deductible_cents` of at least 0, and a `coinsurance_rate` from 0 through 1 inclusive giving the household's share of the allowed amount once the deductible is satisfied. The optional `status` defaults to `ACTIVE` and may be supplied as `TERMINATED` at creation for a policy that is being recorded historically. The running counter `deductible_met_cents` starts at 0 and thereafter tracks the deductible conservation identity of §2.10. Terminating a policy moves it `ACTIVE → TERMINATED`, increments the policy's `version` by 1, and is permitted exactly once — a terminate aimed at a policy already in `TERMINATED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Termination is deliberately *not* retroactive: service lines already routed to that policy keep their `policy_id` and their current status, but they can no longer be adjudicated, and `deductible_met_cents` is left exactly where it stood rather than being reset. What does change is the future — because the candidate set of active policies for that household is now empty, subsequent sick-visit charges for the family fall through to the household itself, as §3.7 explains.

### 3.2. Joining a Panel: Enrolment, Weighted Capacity and the Sibling Ladder

Enrolling a child is the single most consequential write in the system, and a `PracticeAdmin` or `CareCoordinator` performs it by naming the `child_id`, the `plan_id` of the tier the family is buying, the `physician_id` of the panel they are joining, a `start_date` giving the first covered day, and the `expected_family_version` token of §2.8. The `start_date` may not be earlier than today and may not be later than the last calendar day of the month containing today; anything outside that window is `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. A child who already holds a membership in `PENDING_ACTIVATION`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` may not hold a second — the attempt is `ERR_DUPLICATE_MEMBERSHIP`. Note that a `CANCELLED` membership does not block re-enrolment, which is what allows a family to leave and come back.

The **admission test** on panel capacity is inclusive at equality. The physician's current `weighted_occupancy` plus the candidate child's `current_slot_weight` must be less than or equal to that physician's `panel_slot_cap`; landing exactly on the cap is an acceptance, not a rejection. Exceeding it is `ERR_PANEL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Both sides of the comparison are taken to one decimal place and compared as values multiplied by ten and rounded to the nearest integer, so equality is exact. Consider a physician with a `panel_slot_cap` of 4.5 carrying two children born 2025-07-01 and 2026-04-01. Read on 2026-06-01 their ages are 335 and 61 days, both weighted 2.0, giving an occupancy of 4.0; a third child born 2022-01-01 would weigh 1.0 and push the total to 5.0, so the enrolment is refused. Read a month later on 2026-07-01 the elder child's age is exactly 365 days, its weight drops to 1.5, occupancy falls to 3.5, and the identical enrolment now lands on exactly 4.5 and is accepted. Nothing changed except the calendar.

A membership is born in `PENDING_ACTIVATION`. Its `family_id` is denormalised from the child at creation and never changes afterwards. Its `cancellation_effective_date` and `suspension_reason` both start null. Its `slot_weight` mirrors the child's `current_slot_weight` evaluated against today, and it becomes 0.0 the moment the membership reaches `CANCELLED`.

**The sibling ladder** is what makes enrolment expensive. Three membership fields — `enrollment_sequence`, `discount_pct` and `monthly_rate_cents` — look circularly dependent, because the rate depends on the discount, the discount depends on the sequence, and the sequence depends on the very set of memberships the request is mutating. The evaluation order is therefore pinned and must be followed exactly. First, build the household's occupancy set **after** the mutation has been applied: every membership of that family whose status is `PENDING_ACTIVATION`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, with a membership that has just become `CANCELLED` excluded. Second, order that set by `start_date` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending, and assign `enrollment_sequence` as the 1-based position in that order — the ordering key is enrolment, not the children's ages, so a younger child enrolled first outranks an older child enrolled later. Third, derive `discount_pct` from the sequence: the first-ranked membership takes 0.00, the second takes 0.20, and every membership ranked third or beyond takes 0.35. Fourth, set each membership's `monthly_rate_cents` to its plan's `monthly_rate_cents` multiplied by one minus its `discount_pct`, rounded half-up to whole cents. Only after every membership in the set carries its own rate may the household cap be considered, and the cap — which lives in invoice construction, §3.3 — never feeds back into `discount_pct`. A membership that has been cancelled keeps the `enrollment_sequence`, `discount_pct` and `monthly_rate_cents` it held at cancellation, frozen for audit. Two mechanical details of the rerun are pinned. The `created_at` half of the ordering key resolves ties by write order under §2.3, because every membership of a household enrolled at the same clock setting shares one `created_at`. And a rerun writes to a sibling only when it genuinely changes one of that sibling's three ladder fields: a membership whose rung did not move is left entirely untouched and keeps its `version` and `updated_at`, so enrolling a third child advances only the siblings whose sequence, discount or rate actually shifted.

Enrolment fans out. Beyond writing the new membership and re-running the ladder across every sibling, the operation increments the household's `version` by 1; it creates one entitlement grant per benefit type — the `LACTATION_CONSULT` grant written first and the `SLEEP_CONSULT` grant second — at `membership_year_index` 0, as §3.8 details; it generates the child's well-visit schedule in the milestone order listed in §3.5; and it touches the household's billing. Those legs run in that written order — ladder, grants, well visits, billing — which is the order the write-order tie-break of §2.3 will later report them in. Specifically, if the family has no invoice at all for the calendar month containing `start_date`, a `DRAFT` invoice for that cycle is opened automatically; then every `DRAFT` invoice of the family is recomputed from scratch. If an invoice for that cycle already exists but has reached `ISSUED` or `PAID`, it is left completely untouched and **no replacement invoice is created** — the newly enrolled child is simply unbilled for that already-closed cycle.

**When the panel is full**, a `PracticeAdmin`, `CareCoordinator` or `Parent` queues the child instead, naming the `physician_id`, the `child_id` and the `requested_plan_id` the eventual membership should be created on. The entry opens in `WAITING` with `promoted_membership_id` null, and exposes a live `candidate_slot_weight` — the child's `current_slot_weight` today, which is the weight a promotion would consume. A household may not stack duplicates: a second `WAITING` entry for the same physician and child is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_WAITLIST_ENTRY`. An entry may be withdrawn at any time while it is `WAITING`, moving it `WAITING → WITHDRAWN` and removing it from every future promotion selector; withdrawal aimed at an entry in any other status is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Waiting entries also age out: whenever the logical clock moves, every entry still in `WAITING` whose creation date plus 90 days falls strictly before the new current date is swept `WAITING → EXPIRED`. An entry created on 2026-05-01 therefore survives a clock reading of 2026-07-30 (90 days later) and expires only once the clock passes to 2026-07-31 or beyond. `PROMOTED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `EXPIRED` are all terminal.

### 3.3. The Monthly Cycle: Building, Capping, Issuing and Paying the Household Invoice

Households are billed one calendar month at a time. A `BillingSpecialist` or `PracticeAdmin` opens an invoice by naming the `family_id` and a `cycle_start_date`, which must be the 1st day of a calendar month — any other day is `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE` — and which must not already have an invoice for that household, a duplicate being `ERR_DUPLICATE_WAITLIST_ENTRY`. From that single input the service derives `cycle_end_date`, the last calendar day of the month containing `cycle_start_date`, and `days_in_cycle`, the number of calendar days in that month. The invoice opens in `DRAFT` with `issued_date`, `due_date` and `paid_date` all null and `amount_paid_cents` at 0. Enrolment opens a draft invoice for the current cycle on its own when none exists, so most invoices in practice are born as a side effect of somebody joining the panel rather than through a deliberate create.

The invoice's `lines` array carries one object per membership billed in that cycle. Each object exposes the `membership_id` and `child_id` it belongs to, the `enrollment_sequence` and `discount_pct` that priced it, the `base_rate_cents` taken straight from the plan before any discount, the discounted `monthly_rate_cents`, the `covered_days` the membership was live for, the `prorated_cents` those days cost, a per-line `cap_adjustment_cents` and the resulting `line_total_cents`. The array is ordered by whether the membership is cancelled ascending — a membership in any status other than `CANCELLED` sorts before one that is — then by `enrollment_sequence` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending. Lines for memberships still occupying the panel therefore come first and lines for memberships cancelled mid-cycle come last. Cancellation status is the primary sort key rather than the sequence, because a cancelled membership keeps the sequence it held at cancellation, frozen for audit, and that frozen number would otherwise interleave it back among the living.

**Day counting is inclusive on both endpoints.** For each membership, the counted window opens on the later of its `start_date` and the invoice's `cycle_start_date`. It closes on the invoice's `cycle_end_date` when the membership has no `cancellation_effective_date`, and otherwise on the earlier of the `cancellation_effective_date` and the `cycle_end_date`. The `covered_days` figure is the whole-day difference between those two dates plus one, and a negative result is clamped to 0. A membership whose `start_date` lies after the `cycle_end_date`, or whose `cancellation_effective_date` lies before the `cycle_start_date`, therefore contributes 0 covered days and is omitted from `lines` entirely rather than appearing as a zero-value row. Membership rows are considered for the cycle when their status is `PENDING_ACTIVATION`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, and also when their status is `CANCELLED` with a `cancellation_effective_date` falling on or after the `cycle_start_date`.

Two proration shapes exist and they are not symmetric. Under the **enrolment** shape — a membership with no `cancellation_effective_date` whose `start_date` falls strictly after the `cycle_start_date` — the counted window runs from the `start_date` through the `cycle_end_date`, both inclusive; the leading edge of the month is cut and the trailing edge is billed. Under the **cancellation** shape — a membership whose `cancellation_effective_date` falls on or between the `cycle_start_date` and the `cycle_end_date` — the counted window runs from the later of `start_date` and `cycle_start_date` through the `cancellation_effective_date`, both inclusive; the leading edge is billed and the trailing edge is cut. For one and the same date the two shapes select disjoint day sets and therefore produce different money: a membership starting 2026-06-11 in a 30-day cycle bills 20 days under the enrolment shape, while the same membership cancelled effective 2026-06-11 bills 11 days under the cancellation shape.

Building the invoice total follows a pinned four-step order, and **rounding per line before summing is the whole difficulty** — rounding the sum instead produces a different, wrong answer. Step one: for each membership in the cycle, taken in `enrollment_sequence` ascending order, record `base_rate_cents` as the plan's undiscounted rate, take `discount_pct` from the membership, set `monthly_rate_cents` to the base rate multiplied by one minus the discount rounded half-up to whole cents, compute `covered_days` as above, and set `prorated_cents` to the discounted monthly rate multiplied by `covered_days` and divided by `days_in_cycle`, rounded half-up to whole cents right here, before anything is added to anything. Step two: `subtotal_cents` is the sum of those already-rounded `prorated_cents` values. Step three: `cap_adjustment_cents` is the amount by which the subtotal exceeds the household's `family_monthly_cap_cents`, or 0 when the subtotal is at or below the cap. Step four: that adjustment is waterfalled against the lines in the **exact reverse** of the line order defined above — so the cancelled-mid-cycle line absorbs the cap first and the sequence-1 line absorbs it last. Walking that reversed order, each line gives up the smaller of the remaining adjustment and its own `prorated_cents`; that amount becomes the line's `cap_adjustment_cents`, its `line_total_cents` is its `prorated_cents` minus what it gave up, and the remaining adjustment shrinks by the same amount. Finally `total_cents` is `subtotal_cents` minus `cap_adjustment_cents`, which by construction equals the sum of every `line_total_cents`.

Worked through: June 2026 has 30 days; three siblings on the `PLUS` tier with a base rate of 40 000 cents start on the 1st, the 11th and the 14th; the household cap is 100 000 cents. The first sequence takes no discount and 30 of 30 days, giving 40 000. The second takes the 0.20 discount for a rate of 32 000 and 20 of 30 days, giving 21 333.33 which rounds to 21 333. The third takes the 0.35 discount for a rate of 26 000 and 17 of 30 days, giving 14 733.33 which rounds to 14 733. Rounding each line then summing yields a subtotal of 76 066 — the correct answer; summing first and rounding once would have yielded 76 067 and is wrong. With the cap set to 60 000 instead of 100 000, the adjustment is 16 066: the third line surrenders all 14 733, the second surrenders the remaining 1 333 and drops from 21 333 to 20 000, the first is untouched, and `total_cents` lands on exactly 60 000.

Only `DRAFT` invoices are ever recomputed. **Issuing** an invoice moves it `DRAFT → ISSUED`, and requires that its `lines` array be non-empty — an issue aimed at an invoice with nothing on it, or at one already `ISSUED` or `PAID`, is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Issuance stamps `issued_date` with today's date and sets `due_date` to fourteen days after that. From that moment the invoice is frozen: later enrolments and cancellations in the same household no longer rewrite its lines, no matter how much the ladder moves underneath it.

**Paying** an invoice moves it `ISSUED → PAID` and is exact-settlement only. The supplied `amount_paid_cents` must equal the invoice's `total_cents` precisely; a partial payment, or an overpayment, is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. On success the paid amount is recorded in `amount_paid_cents` and `paid_date` is stamped with today's date. Payment is also the **activation gate for the whole practice**: as a side effect, every membership of that household sitting in `PENDING_ACTIVATION` whose `start_date` falls between the invoice's `cycle_start_date` and `cycle_end_date`, both bounds inclusive, transitions `PENDING_ACTIVATION → ACTIVE` and has its own `version` incremented by 1. No panel arithmetic changes at that moment — the membership was already occupying its slot weight while pending — but a great deal of downstream capability unlocks, because consult booking, encounter creation and well-visit completion all require an `ACTIVE` membership. There is no other route to activation: a membership whose family never pays its cycle invoice simply never activates. `PAID` is terminal.

**Suspension and reinstatement** form a genuine two-node loop that a membership may traverse any number of times. A `PracticeAdmin` or `BillingSpecialist` suspends an `ACTIVE` membership for non-payment, supplying a `reason` that is recorded in `suspension_reason`. The operation requires the household to hold at least one invoice in `ISSUED` whose `due_date` is strictly before today; with no such past-due invoice the suspension is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_INVOICE_UNPAID`. Suspension is financially inert on both sides: the membership keeps its panel slot weight, so the physician's `weighted_occupancy` is unchanged, and it keeps its invoice line, so no draft invoice is recomputed. Reinstatement runs the mirror test — the household must hold **zero** invoices in `ISSUED` whose `due_date` is strictly before today, and any remaining past-due invoice refuses the reinstatement with the same `ERR_ENROLLMENT_INVOICE_UNPAID` — and on success moves the membership `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` and clears `suspension_reason` back to null. Suspending something that is not `ACTIVE`, or reinstating something that is not `SUSPENDED`, is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.4. Leaving the Panel: Cancellation and the Promotion Chain

Cancellation is available to a `PracticeAdmin` or a `CareCoordinator` and is reachable from all three living states — `PENDING_ACTIVATION → CANCELLED`, `ACTIVE → CANCELLED` and `SUSPENDED → CANCELLED` — with byte-for-byte identical consequences on every one of those three paths. A membership already in `CANCELLED` refuses the operation with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The request carries an `effective_date`, the membership's last covered day, and the `expected_family_version` token of §2.8. The effective date must be on or after the membership's own `start_date`, and must itself fall between today and the last calendar day of the month containing today, both bounds inclusive; a date outside that window is `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`.

The direct write sets the membership's status to `CANCELLED`, records the supplied `effective_date` in `cancellation_effective_date`, drives its `slot_weight` to 0.0, and increments the household's `version` by 1. Six further consequences fan out from that write directly, and they are not conditional on one another:

- **The pricing ladder is rerun.** Every remaining membership of the household is re-sequenced, re-discounted and re-priced under §3.2's pinned order, with the just-cancelled membership excluded from the occupancy set. Every household with more than one child therefore sees its `enrollment_sequence`, `discount_pct` and `monthly_rate_cents` shift the moment a sibling leaves.
- **Draft invoices are rebuilt.** Every invoice of that household still in `DRAFT` is recomputed under §3.3, using the cancellation proration shape for the departing membership's own line. Invoices already in `ISSUED` or `PAID` are not touched.
- **Benefit pools expire.** Every entitlement grant of that membership currently in `OPEN` or `EXHAUSTED` transitions to `EXPIRED`. Units already burned are not given back: `consumed_count` is preserved exactly as it stood, while `booked_count` is driven to 0 as the held bookings cancel underneath it. The two legs are **ordered**, and the order is what makes that sentence true: the pools expire *before* the bookings release, so the release described in §3.8 can never flip an `EXPIRED` pool back to `OPEN`. Each release still decrements `booked_count` and still advances `released_count` — an overage-route booking still decrements `overage_count` — but the grant's status stays `EXPIRED`, and `available_count` rises exactly as the arithmetic dictates without reopening anything.
- **Scheduled consults are cancelled.** Every consult booking of that membership still in `SCHEDULED` transitions to `CANCELLED`, which releases its entitlement hold in the ordinary way described in §3.8.
- **Open well visits are cancelled.** Every well visit of that membership in `DUE` or `SCHEDULED` transitions to `CANCELLED`. Well visits already in `COMPLETED` or `MISSED` are left exactly as they are — history is not rewritten.
- **Pending forms block.** Every form request of that membership in `PENDING` transitions `PENDING → BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` set to `MEMBERSHIP_NOT_ACTIVE`. Form requests already in `ISSUED` are not touched and no issued form is ever revoked.
- **Open encounters are voided.** Every encounter of that membership whose `clinical_status` is `SCHEDULED` moves to `clinical_status` `CANCELLED` and `billing_status` `BILLING_VOID`. Encounters that have already reached `CLINICALLY_COMPLETE` are **not** touched, and their service lines are **not** voided — the clinical work happened and the charges stand. For the encounters the leg does reach, the void carries all the way down to their charges under the rules of §3.6: every service line of such an encounter still in `ROUTED` or `ADJUDICATED` moves to `VOID`, lines already `SETTLED` are left alone, and each voided line that had reached `ADJUDICATED` on the insurance route reverses its `deductible_applied_cents` out of the policy and advances that policy's `version`. Because the leg keys off the clinical axis alone, `billing_status` is driven to `BILLING_VOID` from whatever it currently reads — `BILLING_PENDING`, `BILLING_ROUTED` or even `BILLING_CLOSED` — rather than being refused the way a direct encounter cancel refuses a billing axis that has already closed.

Beyond that fan-out runs a genuine four-hop causal chain, where each hop exists only because the previous one did.

**Hop one — the panel loosens.** Because the membership has left the occupancy set, the physician's `weighted_occupancy` is recomputed and falls by exactly the weight that membership used to carry, and `available_panel_weight` rises by the same amount.

**Hop two — the promotion selector runs.** Because free weight appeared, the service examines the panel waitlist entries for that physician whose status is `WAITING`, ordered by `created_at` ascending, and promotes the **first** entry whose `candidate_slot_weight` is less than or equal to the physician's newly recomputed `available_panel_weight`. Entries earlier in that order whose weight does not fit are simply skipped — they stay `WAITING`, are not reordered and are not penalised. Exactly one entry is promoted per cancellation, even when the freed weight would comfortably have accommodated two. If no waiting entry fits, no promotion occurs, every waiting entry keeps its status, and hops three and four do not fire at all; this is a perfectly ordinary outcome and not an error. The promoted entry moves `WAITING → PROMOTED`.

**Hop three — a membership is created.** Because an entry was promoted, a new membership is created for that entry's `child_id` on the `requested_plan_id` the entry recorded and on the same `physician_id`, with `start_date` set to today's date and status `PENDING_ACTIVATION`. The entry's `promoted_membership_id` is set to the new membership's `id`, and the promoted child's own household has its `version` incremented by 1. Note that this second household is ordinarily a different family from the one that cancelled, and both families' versions move as a result of the one request; in the corner case where the promoted child belongs to the very household that cancelled, the once-per-request ceiling of §2.5 governs and that single family's `version` advances by exactly 1 for the whole request rather than by 2. Hop three is a server action, not a client enrolment, and it therefore skips the three rungs that guard the client route: no `expected_family_version` is demanded of anybody, the duplicate-membership test of rung 15 is not run, and the panel-capacity test of rung 16 is not re-run because hop two already proved the fit.

**Hop four — the new membership's scaffolding appears.** Because a membership was created, its two entitlement grants are generated — one per benefit type at `membership_year_index` 0 — and its well-visit rows are generated under the periodicity rules of §3.5. The promoted child's household then gains a `DRAFT` invoice for the current cycle if it had none, or has its existing draft recomputed if it did, so that the new enrolment-prorated line appears on it.

### 3.5. Preventive Care: The Periodicity Schedule and the Immunization Record

#### The well-visit schedule

Enrolment projects the child's whole preventive-care calendar in one go. The practice follows an eleven-milestone periodicity schedule, each milestone identified by a `milestone_code` and defined by three day-offsets from the child's birth date: a target age, a window-open offset and a window-close offset. `NEWBORN` targets day 5 with a window running from day 3 to day 7. `M1` targets day 30, window 23 to 44. `M2` targets day 61, window 53 to 75. `M4` targets day 122, window 114 to 136. `M6` targets day 183, window 175 to 197. `M9` targets day 274, window 259 to 289. `M12` targets day 365, window 350 to 380. `M24` targets day 730, window 700 to 760. `M36` targets day 1095, window 1065 to 1125. `M48` targets day 1460, window 1430 to 1490. `M60` targets day 1825, window 1795 to 1855.

For each milestone in that listed order the service computes `target_date` as the child's `date_of_birth` plus the target age in days, `window_open_date` as the birth date plus the window-open offset, and `window_close_date` as the birth date plus the window-close offset. A well-visit row is generated only when that `window_close_date` is on or after the membership's `start_date` — the test is inclusive, so a milestone whose window closes exactly on the start date **is** generated. Milestones whose window closed strictly before the start date are never generated at all, which is why a child enrolled at four months of age carries no `NEWBORN` and no `M1` row. Each generated row records the `membership_id` that produced it, the `child_id` denormalised from that membership, its `milestone_code`, the three computed dates, a null `scheduled_date`, a null `completed_date`, a null `punctuality`, and status `DUE`.

A `CareCoordinator` or `PracticeAdmin` books a due visit by supplying a `scheduled_date`, moving it `DUE → SCHEDULED`. Booking requires the owning membership to be in `PENDING_ACTIVATION` or `ACTIVE` — notably a not-yet-activated membership may still book, because families schedule their newborn visit before the first invoice clears — and requires the `scheduled_date` not to be earlier than today. A booking aimed at a visit in any status other than `DUE` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and the visit's own status is tested before the membership's. A membership in `SUSPENDED` or `CANCELLED` is likewise refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than with `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_ACTIVE`, because scheduling asks for a wider set of statuses than the ordinary active-membership rung does. A `scheduled_date` earlier than today is `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE` (400).

Completion is a `Physician` or `PracticeAdmin` action and is reachable from **both** `DUE → COMPLETED` and `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`, with identical consequences on either path — a family that simply walks in and is seen never needed the scheduling step. Completion requires the owning membership to be `ACTIVE`, refusing anything else with `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_ACTIVE`, and requires the supplied `completed_date` not to be after today, refusing a future date with `ERR_FUTURE_DATED`. On success the visit records its `completed_date`, classifies `punctuality`, and produces a service line: `service_code` `WELL_VISIT`, route `COVERED_BY_MEMBERSHIP`, `billed_amount_cents` 22 000, `membership_covered_cents` 22 000, `patient_responsibility_cents` 0, carrying the `well_visit_id` of the visit and created directly in status `SETTLED`. Completion also refreshes the recency rung of the form gate described in §3.9, which is often the real reason a family is asked to come in before camp season.

**Punctuality is a three-way classification and both window boundaries belong to the middle band.** A `completed_date` strictly before the `window_open_date` is `EARLY`. A `completed_date` on or after the open date and on or before the `window_close_date` is `ON_SCHEDULE`. A `completed_date` strictly after the close date is `DELAYED`. For a child born 2026-04-01 the `M2` milestone opens 2026-05-24 and closes 2026-06-15, so a completion on 2026-05-23 is `EARLY`, one on 2026-05-24 is `ON_SCHEDULE`, one on 2026-06-15 is still `ON_SCHEDULE`, and one on 2026-06-16 is `DELAYED`.

Visits that are never kept eventually lapse, but not the instant the window shuts. Whenever the logical clock moves, every well visit in `DUE` or `SCHEDULED` whose `window_close_date` plus thirty days falls strictly before the new current date is swept to `MISSED`. That thirty-day tail is deliberate: inside it a late completion is still recordable and is classified `DELAYED`. `COMPLETED`, `MISSED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal; a visit reaches `CANCELLED` only through the membership cancellation of §3.4.

#### The immunization record

A `Physician` or `PracticeAdmin` records an administered dose by naming the `child_id`, a `vaccine_code` of either `HEPB` or `DTAP`, and the `administered_date`, which may not be after today. Two series are tracked. `HEPB` runs to three doses: position 1 has a minimum age of 0 days and no interval requirement; position 2 has a minimum age of 28 days and a minimum interval of 28 days from the prior valid dose; position 3 has a minimum age of 168 days and a minimum interval of 56 days. `DTAP` runs to five doses: position 1 at minimum age 42 days with no interval requirement; position 2 at minimum age 70 days with a 28-day interval; position 3 at minimum age 98 days with a 28-day interval; position 4 at minimum age 365 days with a 168-day interval; position 5 at minimum age 1460 days with a 168-day interval.

Validity is decided at the moment the dose is recorded, and it is decided **by position in the valid series rather than by insertion order**. A dose's `effective_series_index` is one plus the number of that child's doses of the same `vaccine_code` currently carrying `validity` `VALID` whose `administered_date` is strictly earlier than this dose's, or equal to it with a strictly earlier `created_at`. Its `prior_valid_dose_id` names the valid dose of the same child and vaccine whose `administered_date` is the greatest value strictly less than this dose's, with ties on the date broken by taking the later `created_at`; that field is null when the dose sits at effective position 1. Its `age_days_at_administration` is the whole-day difference between the `administered_date` and the child's `date_of_birth`. Its `interval_days_since_prior` is the whole-day difference between this dose's `administered_date` and the prior valid dose's, and is null at effective position 1. One corner of that pair is pinned separately: a dose can sit beyond effective position 1 and still have a null `prior_valid_dose_id`, which happens when every earlier valid dose of the series shares its own `administered_date` and none is therefore *strictly* earlier. In that case the interval is measured against the last of those same-date valid doses and comes out as 0, while `prior_valid_dose_id` stays null.

A newly recorded dose carries `manually_invalidated` false and `invalidation_reason_note` null; only an explicit invalidation ever changes either.

Recording a dose is not a purely additive act. The write re-runs the whole verdict across **every** dose of that child and that `vaccine_code`, walked in `administered_date` ascending and then write order, exactly as a retrospective invalidation does — so a back-dated insertion pushes the doses recorded after it down the series and can re-classify them in either direction. The dose being created is exempt from the version rule of §2.5 like any other row minted inside its own request and answers at `version` 1 however much the re-evaluation writes to it; each sibling dose the re-evaluation actually changes advances by 1, and a sibling dose whose computed fields come out identical is not touched at all.

The verdict then runs as an ordered ladder. If the `effective_series_index` exceeds the series' total dose count — more than 3 for `HEPB`, more than 5 for `DTAP` — the dose is `INVALID` with `invalid_reason` `SERIES_ALREADY_COMPLETE` and nothing further is evaluated. Otherwise the **age rung** is tested first: the practice allows a four-day grace, so the dose passes when its `age_days_at_administration` plus four is greater than or equal to the position's minimum age. A dose given exactly four days early therefore passes and one given five days early fails. If the age rung fails, the dose is `INVALID` with `invalid_reason` `MIN_AGE_NOT_MET`. Only if the age rung passes is the **interval rung** tested, and it is skipped entirely at effective position 1. The same four-day grace applies: the dose passes when its `interval_days_since_prior` plus four is greater than or equal to the position's minimum interval. A failure here is `INVALID` with `invalid_reason` `MIN_INTERVAL_NOT_MET`. A dose clearing both rungs is `VALID` with `invalid_reason` null. **The rung order is pinned**: when a dose fails both rungs, the reported reason is always `MIN_AGE_NOT_MET` and never `MIN_INTERVAL_NOT_MET`.

The two failure modes are genuinely different problems with different remedies, and neither one implies the other. An age failure means the child is simply too young and the fix is to wait — for a child born 2026-04-01, a third `HEPB` dose administered 2026-09-11 sits at 163 days of age, and 163 plus the four-day grace is 167, short of the 168-day minimum, so the dose is invalid for age even though the interval since the second dose is enormous. An interval failure means the dose came too soon after the *prior valid dose* and the child's age is beside the point — for the same birth date, a first `DTAP` dose on 2026-05-26 sits at 55 days and is valid, while a second on 2026-06-18 gives an interval of 23 days, and 23 plus the grace is 27, short of the 28-day minimum, so it is invalid for interval even though its age of 78 days clears the 70-day age floor comfortably.

**Retrospective invalidation is where the record starts to move underneath people.** A `Physician` or `PracticeAdmin` may invalidate any dose currently `VALID`, supplying a `reason` that is stored in `invalidation_reason_note`. The dose's `validity` becomes `INVALID`, its `invalid_reason` becomes `MANUALLY_INVALIDATED`, and its `manually_invalidated` flag is set to true — a flag that is permanent, because a manually invalidated dose can never return to `VALID` no matter what happens to the series around it. An invalidate aimed at a dose already `INVALID` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Three consequences chain off that write. First, because the dose has left the valid set, every dose of the same child and the same `vaccine_code` with a strictly later `administered_date` is re-evaluated in `administered_date` ascending then `created_at` ascending order, recomputing each one's `effective_series_index`, `prior_valid_dose_id`, `interval_days_since_prior`, `validity` and `invalid_reason` from scratch. This re-evaluation runs **in both directions**: a dose may flip `VALID → INVALID` because the series shifted underneath it, and equally a dose may flip `INVALID → VALID` because removing an earlier dose moved it down to a position whose age and interval requirements it now satisfies — the sole exception being that a `manually_invalidated` dose stays `INVALID` forever. A dose that stays `INVALID` across a re-evaluation may still change its `invalid_reason` among `MIN_AGE_NOT_MET`, `MIN_INTERVAL_NOT_MET` and `SERIES_ALREADY_COMPLETE`, while a reason of `MANUALLY_INVALIDATED` never changes. Second, because the child's valid-dose counts have changed, `immunization_complete_for_age` is recomputed. Third, because that flag may have gone false, every form request of that child still in `PENDING` transitions `PENDING → BLOCKED` with `blocked_reason` set to `IMMUNIZATION_INCOMPLETE`; form requests already `ISSUED` are not touched, and no already-issued form is ever revoked. That third leg is deliberately **unconditional**: it selects on the dose's `child_id`, so it reaches every `PENDING` request of that child across every membership the child holds, and it does not re-run the gate of §3.9 before acting. A `PENDING` request therefore blocks with `IMMUNIZATION_INCOMPLETE` even in the case where `immunization_complete_for_age` happens to still read true after the invalidation — the block is a consequence of the invalidation itself, not of a fresh gate verdict.

**Completeness for age** is what the forms gate actually reads. For each of the two vaccine series, the required dose count is the number of positions in that series whose minimum age plus a thirty-day lag is less than or equal to the child's `age_days` today — the comparison is inclusive, so a position becomes required on the exact day the child's age reaches that sum. The child's holding for that series is the count of their doses with `validity` `VALID`, and doses with `validity` `INVALID` are excluded regardless of why they are invalid. If any series' holding is less than its requirement, `immunization_complete_for_age` is false; if every series clears, it is true. This produces an important empty-set boundary: for a newborn every requirement is 0, so a child with no recorded doses at all is vacuously complete. A child born 2026-05-20 read on 2026-06-15 is 26 days old, needs nothing of either series, and reads complete; read four days later on 2026-06-19 the same child is 30 days old, the first `HEPB` position has become required, and the same child now reads incomplete.

### 3.6. Round-the-Clock Access: Encounters, the After-Hours Verdict and Two Independent Axes

Direct access is what families are actually buying, and every instance of it — a sick child at two in the afternoon, a panicked call at eleven at night, a routine question over video, a newborn brought back for a weight check — is recorded as an **encounter**. A `CareCoordinator`, `PracticeAdmin`, `Physician` or `Parent` opens one by naming the `membership_id` it is billed under, an `encounter_type` of `SICK_VISIT`, `AFTER_HOURS_CONTACT`, `ROUTINE_QUESTION` or `NEWBORN_WEIGHT_CHECK`, a `modality` of `IN_PERSON` or `TELEHEALTH`, the `patient_state_code` naming the two-letter state the patient is physically in, and a `started_at` instant. The `child_id` is denormalised from the membership. The membership must be `ACTIVE`, failing which the response is `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_ACTIVE`, and the `started_at` may not be after the current instant, failing which the response is `ERR_FUTURE_DATED`.

Two safety gates apply and their relative order matters. Licensure is checked first: when `modality` is `TELEHEALTH`, the `patient_state_code` must appear in the `licensed_state_codes` of the physician the membership names, and if it does not the request is refused with `ERR_LICENSURE_NOT_COVERED`. Modality suitability is checked second: a `NEWBORN_WEIGHT_CHECK` requires a physical scale and may never be delivered over video regardless of licensure, so pairing that `encounter_type` with `TELEHEALTH` is refused with `ERR_MODALITY_NOT_ALLOWED`. In person, the same encounter type is entirely ordinary.

The service then derives `ends_at` as the `started_at` plus thirty minutes and applies a **closed-interval collision test** on the child. A child may carry at most one open encounter at a time. The new encounter's interval runs from `started_at` through `ends_at` with **both endpoints included**, and it is compared against the same closed interval of every other encounter for that same child whose `clinical_status` is currently `SCHEDULED`; encounters in any other clinical status are invisible to the test. Because both intervals are closed, touching endpoints **do** collide — a contact beginning exactly thirty minutes after another began is a collision, not a clean handoff. A collision is refused with `ERR_ENCOUNTER_OVERLAP`.

**The after-hours verdict** is computed at creation and is a pinned four-rung ladder, decided against the UTC calendar date and the UTC minute-of-day of `started_at` and against nothing else — the `encounter_type` is never consulted, so an `AFTER_HOURS_CONTACT` opened at 14:00 on an ordinary Tuesday reads `after_hours` false with a null `after_hours_reason` and `surcharge_cents` 0, exactly as a `SICK_VISIT` at that same instant would. The first rung that matches wins and no later rung is consulted. If the weekday of that date is `SATURDAY` or `SUNDAY`, `after_hours` is true and `after_hours_reason` is `WEEKEND`. Otherwise, if the date is one of the practice's declared holidays — 2026-05-25, 2026-07-03, 2026-09-07, 2026-11-26, 2026-12-25 and 2027-01-01 — `after_hours` is true and the reason is `HOLIDAY`. Otherwise, if the minute of day is at or after 18:00 UTC, `after_hours` is true and the reason is `WEEKDAY_EVENING`. Otherwise, if the minute of day is strictly before 08:00 UTC, `after_hours` is true and the reason is `WEEKDAY_EARLY_MORNING`. Failing all four, `after_hours` is false and `after_hours_reason` is null. Both evening and morning boundaries are exact: a contact starting at 18:00:00 **is** after-hours while one starting at 17:59:00 is not, and one starting at 07:59:00 **is** after-hours while one starting at 08:00:00 is not.

The two shapes of that ladder behave very differently. On an ordinary Monday-to-Friday date that is not a declared holiday, the verdict is a two-sided window on the clock alone — after-hours only when the minute of day is at or after 18:00 or strictly before 08:00 — so a contact at 2026-06-15T14:00:00Z, a Monday, is not after-hours and carries `surcharge_cents` 0. On a Saturday, a Sunday or a declared holiday the minute of day is never consulted at all and the entire calendar day is after-hours; that same 14:00:00Z contact moved to 2026-06-13, a Saturday, or to 2026-07-03, a declared Friday holiday, **is** after-hours and carries `surcharge_cents` 7 500 on a tier that does not waive it. The `surcharge_cents` figure itself is 0 whenever `after_hours` is false or the membership's plan sets `after_hours_surcharge_waived` to true, and 7 500 otherwise.

A newly opened encounter carries `clinical_status` `SCHEDULED` and `billing_status` `BILLING_PENDING`, with `clinical_note` and `cancel_reason` both null. **The two axes advance independently and neither reads the other**, which is the point: a physician may close the chart days before the billing office touches it, and the billing office may route charges on a contact whose note has not yet been written.

On the clinical axis, a `Physician` or `PracticeAdmin` completes the encounter by supplying a `clinical_note`, moving it `SCHEDULED → CLINICALLY_COMPLETE`. The `billing_status` is neither read nor changed by this call — the billing axis may already read `BILLING_ROUTED` or may still read `BILLING_PENDING`, and both are perfectly legal. One side effect matters operationally: the encounter leaves the child's open-encounter overlap set, so the next contact for that child may occupy the same half-hour.

On the billing axis, a `BillingSpecialist` or `PracticeAdmin` submits the charges, moving the encounter `BILLING_PENDING → BILLING_ROUTED`. The `clinical_status` is **not** read here either; billing may be submitted before or after clinical completion. The submission stamps the acting `X-User-ID` onto every line it creates as `submitted_by_user_id`, which is the value the segregation-of-duties rule of §3.7 will later compare against. A primary service line is created whose `service_code` follows a fixed mapping from the encounter type: a `SICK_VISIT` encounter and an `AFTER_HOURS_CONTACT` encounter both map to service code `SICK_VISIT`, a `ROUTINE_QUESTION` maps to `TELEHEALTH_VISIT`, and a `NEWBORN_WEIGHT_CHECK` maps to `WELL_VISIT`. The line's `billed_amount_cents` comes from the practice's charge master: a `WELL_VISIT` line bills 22 000 cents, a `SICK_VISIT` line 18 000, a `TELEHEALTH_VISIT` line 12 000 and an `AFTER_HOURS_SURCHARGE` line 7 500. When the encounter's `after_hours` flag is true, a **second** line with `service_code` `AFTER_HOURS_SURCHARGE` is created alongside the primary one: routed `COVERED_BY_MEMBERSHIP` with `billed_amount_cents` 0 when the plan's `after_hours_surcharge_waived` is true, and routed `PATIENT_RESPONSIBILITY` with `billed_amount_cents` 7 500 when it is not. The primary line is always written before the surcharge line, which fixes their order in the encounter's line set for every later read and for the order in which a cancellation walks them.

The billing axis then **bubbles up from its children**. At the end of any request that leaves every service line of the encounter in a terminal status, and provided the encounter has at least one line and every one of them reads `SETTLED` or `VOID`, the encounter advances `BILLING_ROUTED → BILLING_CLOSED`. In practice this fires in two places. It fires inside the submission itself when every line created was born `SETTLED` — a membership-covered or patient-responsibility encounter therefore reads `BILLING_CLOSED` in the very response of the submission that routed it. And it fires inside line settlement when the last insurance-routed line settles, which is why an insurance-routed encounter stops at `BILLING_ROUTED` and waits.

**Cancelling an encounter collapses both axes at once.** A `CareCoordinator`, `PracticeAdmin` or `Physician` supplies a `reason`, which is stored in `cancel_reason`. The clinical axis moves to `CANCELLED` from either `SCHEDULED` or `CLINICALLY_COMPLETE` — both paths are permitted and both produce identical consequences — and the billing axis moves to `BILLING_VOID` from either `BILLING_PENDING` or `BILLING_ROUTED`. A cancel aimed at an encounter whose clinical axis already reads `CANCELLED`, or whose billing axis already reads `BILLING_CLOSED` or `BILLING_VOID`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. When the billing axis was still `BILLING_PENDING` no service line exists yet and nothing is voided. When it had reached `BILLING_ROUTED`, every service line of that encounter whose status is `ROUTED` or `ADJUDICATED` transitions to `VOID`. Lines already in `SETTLED` are **not** voided. And for each voided line that was routed `BILLED_TO_INSURANCE` and had reached `ADJUDICATED`, the policy's `deductible_met_cents` is decreased by exactly that line's `deductible_applied_cents` and the policy's `version` is incremented by 1 — the compensating action that keeps the deductible identity of §2.10 true. A line voided straight out of `ROUTED` needs no such reversal because it had consumed nothing.

### 3.7. Splitting the Bill: Routing, Adjudication and Settlement

Every charge in the practice is a **service line**, and no client ever creates one directly. Lines appear from exactly three sources: encounter billing submission, which sets the line's `encounter_id`; consult completion, which sets its `consult_booking_id`; and well-visit completion, which sets its `well_visit_id`. Whichever source produced it, the line records the `membership_id` it belongs to, and the two identifier fields belonging to the other two sources stay null. A `policy_id` is set only on the insurance route and is null otherwise. Whichever of the three operations produced the line, its `submitted_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID` of that operation — the billing operator on an encounter submission, the completing caller on a consult completion, the completing `Physician` or `PracticeAdmin` on a well-visit completion — and that is the value the segregation-of-duties rule later compares against.

**Routing decides everything downstream, and exactly one route is assigned.** The branches are mutually exclusive and are evaluated top to bottom. A line whose `service_code` is `WELL_VISIT` or `TELEHEALTH_VISIT` is routed `COVERED_BY_MEMBERSHIP` — these are the two service codes the concierge retainer exists to absorb, and the household's insurance is never consulted for them. A line whose `service_code` is `AFTER_HOURS_SURCHARGE` is routed `COVERED_BY_MEMBERSHIP` when the membership's plan sets `after_hours_surcharge_waived` to true and `PATIENT_RESPONSIBILITY` when it does not. A line whose `service_code` is `LACTATION_CONSULT` or `SLEEP_CONSULT` is routed `COVERED_BY_MEMBERSHIP` when the originating booking's `coverage_route` was `ENTITLEMENT` and `PATIENT_RESPONSIBILITY` when it was `OVERAGE`. And a line whose `service_code` is `SICK_VISIT` — the only insurance-eligible code in the catalogue — goes to the household's policy: the candidate set is every insurance policy whose `family_id` matches the membership's household and whose `status` is currently `ACTIVE`, ranked by `created_at` descending so the most recently created active policy wins, with the write-order tie-break of §2.3 deciding between two policies stamped at the same logical instant — the later-written of the two takes it. If that set has a member, the line is routed `BILLED_TO_INSURANCE` with `policy_id` set to that policy. If the set is empty — the household never had a policy, or the only one it had has been terminated — the line falls back to `PATIENT_RESPONSIBILITY` with `policy_id` null. This fallback is a normal outcome, not an error.

Lines routed `COVERED_BY_MEMBERSHIP` or `PATIENT_RESPONSIBILITY` are created directly in status `SETTLED`, with no adjudication step and no cash movement required. Lines routed `BILLED_TO_INSURANCE` are created in status `ROUTED` with their `policy_id` populated, and wait for a carrier answer. On that insurance route the line's `allowed_amount_cents` is null until adjudication supplies it, while on the other two routes it equals the `billed_amount_cents` from the moment the line exists. Every line is born with `adjudicated_by_user_id` null and `payment_received_cents` at 0, and with `write_off_cents`, `deductible_applied_cents`, `coinsurance_cents` and `plan_paid_cents` all at 0 until the waterfall writes them.

**The financial split has three shapes, and their divergence on identical clinical work is the flagship behaviour of this system.** On the membership route the `allowed_amount_cents` equals the `billed_amount_cents`, `write_off_cents` is 0, `deductible_applied_cents`, `coinsurance_cents` and `plan_paid_cents` are all 0, `patient_responsibility_cents` is 0, and the entire billed amount lands in `membership_covered_cents`. No policy is read and `deductible_met_cents` is not touched. On the patient route the `allowed_amount_cents` again equals the `billed_amount_cents` and `write_off_cents` is again 0, but the whole billed amount lands in `patient_responsibility_cents` while `membership_covered_cents` stays 0 and the deductible and coinsurance figures stay 0. This is the shape taken by an after-hours surcharge on a non-waiving tier, an overage consult, and a sick visit for a household with no active policy.

On the insurance route the split is a waterfall and it runs at adjudication rather than at creation. The `allowed_amount_cents` is supplied by the adjudicating operator and must be at least 0 and no greater than the line's `billed_amount_cents`. The policy's remaining deductible is its `deductible_cents` minus its `deductible_met_cents`, floored at 0. The `deductible_applied_cents` is the smaller of the allowed amount and that remaining balance. What survives the deductible is the allowed amount minus the applied deductible; the `coinsurance_cents` is that surviving figure multiplied by the policy's `coinsurance_rate` and rounded half-up to whole cents — this is the single rounding in the entire split. The `plan_paid_cents` is the surviving figure minus the coinsurance, an exact subtraction. The `patient_responsibility_cents` is the applied deductible plus the coinsurance. The `write_off_cents` is the `billed_amount_cents` minus the `allowed_amount_cents`, is booked to the practice, and is never collectable from the household. `membership_covered_cents` stays 0. Finally the policy's `deductible_met_cents` increases by the applied deductible and the policy's `version` increments by 1.

**Balance billing is structurally impossible** because `patient_responsibility_cents` is derived only from the `allowed_amount_cents` and never from the `billed_amount_cents`. The adjudication call accepts an optional `patient_responsibility_override_cents` as a declared collection figure, and it is checked against a hard ceiling: it must be less than or equal to the sum of the `deductible_applied_cents` and `coinsurance_cents` the waterfall derived from the supplied `allowed_amount_cents`. Anything larger is an attempt to pass the write-off to the household and is refused with `ERR_BALANCE_BILLING_BLOCKED`. The field is a ceiling check only — a line's `patient_responsibility_cents` is always the computed sum, never the supplied override. The supplied figure is consumed by that one comparison and is neither persisted on the line nor echoed anywhere in its payload; a line carries no `patient_responsibility_override_cents` field of its own. Omitting the field entirely skips the ceiling check.

Three examples pin the waterfall. Take a policy with a `deductible_cents` of 50 000 and a `coinsurance_rate` of 0.20, adjudicating sick-visit lines billed at 18 000 and allowed at 14 000 each time. On the first call the policy has met nothing, so the whole 14 000 is applied to the deductible, nothing survives it, the coinsurance and plan-paid figures are both 0, the household owes 14 000, and `deductible_met_cents` rises to 14 000. On the second call the same thing happens again and `deductible_met_cents` rises to 28 000. On a call made when `deductible_met_cents` already stands at 50 000, the remaining deductible is 0, so nothing is applied, the whole 14 000 survives, the coinsurance is 2 800, the plan pays 11 200, the household owes 2 800, and `deductible_met_cents` does not move. For the rounding boundary, take a `deductible_cents` of 9 770 and a `coinsurance_rate` of 0.15 against the same 18 000 billed and 14 000 allowed: 9 770 is applied to the deductible, 4 230 survives, the coinsurance product is 634.5 which rounds half-up to 635, the plan pays 3 595, the household owes 10 405 and the practice writes off 4 000.

Adjudication is performed by a `BillingSpecialist` or `PracticeAdmin` and is fenced by five conditions beyond the schema checks, evaluated in the ladder order of §2.7 with the one documented deviation that the route condition is lifted ahead of the status and policy-state conditions. The order actually walked is: segregation of duties, then the version expectation, then the route, then the line's status, then the policy's status. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the line's `submitted_by_user_id`, and the same operator submitting and adjudicating their own charge is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — this rule binds `PracticeAdmin` exactly as tightly as it binds `BillingSpecialist`. The supplied `expected_policy_version` must match the policy's current `version`, failing which the response is `ERR_STALE_VERSION` — a comparison that is skipped whenever the line names no policy, as §2.7's ninth rung sets out. The line must currently be in status `ROUTED`, failing which the response is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The policy the line names must currently be `ACTIVE`, failing which the response is `ERR_POLICY_INACTIVE` — this is how a terminated policy freezes its outstanding lines. And the line's `route` must be `BILLED_TO_INSURANCE` — tested straight after the version expectation and therefore **before** the two status conditions just described — so that a membership-covered or patient-responsibility line, which is born `SETTLED` and has no carrier behind it, is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_ADJUDICABLE` rather than with the `ERR_INVALID_STATE` its terminal status would otherwise have drawn. On success the line moves `ROUTED → ADJUDICATED` and records the acting caller in `adjudicated_by_user_id`.

**Settlement** closes the line. A `BillingSpecialist` or `PracticeAdmin` supplies a `payment_received_cents` that must equal the line's `patient_responsibility_cents` exactly; any other figure is `ERR_VALIDATION`. The line moves `ADJUDICATED → SETTLED` and records the cash in `payment_received_cents`. A settle aimed at a line that is not `ADJUDICATED` — one still `ROUTED`, one already `SETTLED`, or one `VOID` — is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. When that settlement leaves every service line of the parent encounter in `SETTLED` or `VOID`, the encounter's billing axis bubbles `BILLING_ROUTED → BILLING_CLOSED` as described in §3.6. `SETTLED` and `VOID` are terminal line statuses.

### 3.8. Lactation and Sleep Support: Benefit Pools, Bookings and Overage

Every membership receives a fixed allowance of lactation and sleep consulting per membership year, and the accounting for that allowance lives in **entitlement grants**. A grant is never created by a client; the service creates them. Each grant records the `membership_id` it belongs to, a `benefit_type` of either `LACTATION_CONSULT` or `SLEEP_CONSULT`, a zero-based `membership_year_index` naming which membership year it covers, and a `granted_count` copied from the plan at grant creation — `included_lactation_consults` for the lactation pool and `included_sleep_consults` for the sleep pool — and frozen thereafter, so a later plan edit never retroactively rewrites an allowance already granted. Enrolment creates the first pair of grants, one per benefit type, at `membership_year_index` 0. Grants may be listed and read but never written directly.

The membership year is computed by taking the whole-day difference between the date in question and the membership's `start_date`, dividing by 365 and discarding the remainder. Because a membership's `start_date` is never earlier than the day it was created, this index can never go negative. When a booking needs the grant for its `consult_type` and for the membership year containing its `scheduled_start`, and no such grant row exists yet, the service creates one on the spot with `granted_count` taken from the plan for that benefit type — which is how the second and third membership years come into being.

Five counters ride on every grant, and four of them — `booked_count`, `consumed_count`, `released_count` and `overage_count` — start at 0 when the grant is created. `booked_count` holds units currently reserved by bookings that are `SCHEDULED` and carry `coverage_route` `ENTITLEMENT`. `consumed_count` holds units burned by bookings that reached `COMPLETED` on that same route. `available_count` is the `granted_count` minus the booked and consumed counts and is never negative, so a freshly created grant reads `available_count` equal to its `granted_count`. `released_count` is a cumulative audit counter of units handed back by cancellation, and `overage_count` counts bookings created against the pool while nothing was available. A grant opens in status `OPEN` unless its `granted_count` is 0. As §2.10 insists, the first three are the terms of the conservation identity and the last two sit outside it.

**Booking a consult** is done by a `CareCoordinator`, `PracticeAdmin` or `Parent`, naming the `membership_id`, a `consult_type` of `LACTATION_CONSULT` or `SLEEP_CONSULT`, the `consultant_physician_id` whose calendar the appointment will occupy, a `scheduled_start` and a `scheduled_end` that must be strictly after it, a `modality` of `IN_PERSON` or `TELEHEALTH`, and the `patient_state_code` where the patient will be located. The membership must be `ACTIVE`, refusing anything else with `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_ACTIVE`. When the `modality` is `TELEHEALTH`, the `patient_state_code` must appear in the **consultant's** `licensed_state_codes` — not the membership's own physician's — failing which the response is `ERR_LICENSURE_NOT_COVERED`.

The consultant's calendar test uses a **half-open interval padded on the trailing edge**. A booking occupies from its `scheduled_start` inclusive up to but not including its `scheduled_end`, and that occupancy is then extended by a fifteen-minute turnaround on the trailing edge only. The new interval is tested against every other booking of the same `consultant_physician_id` currently in `SCHEDULED`; bookings in any other status are invisible to the test. A booking that begins exactly at a prior booking's `scheduled_end` plus fifteen minutes does **not** collide — the buffer is satisfied at equality. Anything closer does collide, and a collision is refused with `ERR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. The booking opens in `SCHEDULED` with `reschedule_count` at 0, its `grant_id` pointing at the selected pool.

The pool decides how the booking is paid for. If the selected grant's `available_count` is strictly greater than 0 at the moment of creation, the booking takes `coverage_route` `ENTITLEMENT` and the grant's `booked_count` rises by 1. Otherwise the booking takes `coverage_route` `OVERAGE` and the grant's `overage_count` rises by 1; an overage booking never touches `booked_count` or `consumed_count` at all. Whenever a counter update leaves `available_count` at 0, the grant transitions `OPEN → EXHAUSTED`, and subsequent bookings against it are created on the overage route. A grant whose `granted_count` is 0 — the sleep pool on the `ESSENTIAL` tier, for instance — is `EXHAUSTED` from its very first read, because that edge fires inside the request that created it.

**Rescheduling** is a capped self-loop: the booking stays in `SCHEDULED` and simply moves. A `CareCoordinator`, `PracticeAdmin` or `Parent` supplies a fresh `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end`, the owning membership must still be `ACTIVE`, and the new interval must clear the consultant's calendar under the same buffered test. The booking's `reschedule_count` must be strictly less than 2 before the move is applied, so a booking may be moved twice and the third attempt is refused with `ERR_RESCHEDULE_LIMIT_REACHED`. On success the interval is overwritten and `reschedule_count` rises by 1. **No entitlement counter moves on a reschedule** — the hold the booking placed at creation is exactly the hold it still has afterwards.

**Completion** is performed by a `CareCoordinator`, `PracticeAdmin` or `Physician` and requires that the booking's `scheduled_end` is not after the current instant — a consult cannot be marked delivered before it has finished; a `scheduled_end` still in the future is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409), the same code a booking that is not `SCHEDULED` draws, and the booking's own status is tested first. Completion is also the one booking operation that does **not** demand an `ACTIVE` membership: a consult already on the calendar may be closed out after the household's membership has been suspended. The booking moves `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`. On the entitlement route the grant's `booked_count` falls by 1 and its `consumed_count` rises by 1, which leaves `available_count` exactly where it was, so an `EXHAUSTED` grant stays `EXHAUSTED` through a completion. On the overage route no grant counter moves at all — and the grant row is not written either, so neither its `version` nor its `updated_at` moves. Either way a service line is produced, carrying the `consult_booking_id` and a `service_code` equal to the booking's `consult_type`: on the entitlement route it is routed `COVERED_BY_MEMBERSHIP` with `billed_amount_cents` 0, and on the overage route it is routed `PATIENT_RESPONSIBILITY` with `billed_amount_cents` equal to the plan's `overage_consult_fee_cents`. In both cases the line is created directly in status `SETTLED`.

**Cancellation** is the compensating action, available to a `CareCoordinator`, `PracticeAdmin` or `Parent` on a booking still in `SCHEDULED`. Two consequences chain off it. First, because the booking released its hold, an entitlement-route booking decrements the grant's `booked_count` by 1, increments its `released_count` by 1 and thereby raises its `available_count` by 1, while an overage-route booking decrements the grant's `overage_count` by 1 and moves no other counter. Second, because `available_count` has become strictly positive, a grant sitting in `EXHAUSTED` transitions back to `OPEN` — closing a loop the pool may traverse any number of times. **No service line is created by a cancellation**; nothing was delivered, so nothing is charged. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal booking statuses and an operation aimed at a booking in either is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Walked through on the `PLUS` tier, whose lactation pool grants three units: the first three bookings all take the entitlement route, driving `available_count` to 0 and flipping the grant to `EXHAUSTED`; a fourth booking takes the overage route and lifts `overage_count` to 1; cancelling the second booking returns `available_count` to 1, lifts `released_count` to 1, and flips the grant back to `OPEN`. Grants also end: when the owning membership is cancelled, every grant of that membership in `OPEN` or `EXHAUSTED` moves to `EXPIRED`, its `booked_count` driven to 0 as the held bookings cancel underneath it while `consumed_count` is preserved. `EXPIRED` is terminal.

### 3.9. School, Camp and Sports Paperwork: The Issuance Gate and the Turnaround Promise

Forms are the small favour families judge a concierge practice on, and the practice sells a turnaround promise rather than a form. A `CareCoordinator`, `PracticeAdmin` or `Parent` opens a request naming the `membership_id`, a `form_type` of `SCHOOL`, `CAMP` or `SPORTS`, and the `needed_by_date` the household would like it in hand. The `child_id` is denormalised from the membership. The service stamps `requested_at` with the current instant and computes `due_at` once, as `requested_at` plus the plan's `form_sla_hours` — five days on `ESSENTIAL`, three on `PLUS`, one on `PREMIER` — and never recomputes it afterwards, so a mid-flight plan change does not move a promise already made. The row opens with `issued_at` null, `sla_met` null and `blocked_reason` null.

**The gate is an ordered three-rung ladder drawing on three different entities, and the first failing rung supplies `blocked_reason` while later rungs are not evaluated at all.** Rung one: the owning membership's status must be `ACTIVE`; otherwise the reason is `MEMBERSHIP_NOT_ACTIVE`. Rung two: the child's `immunization_complete_for_age` must be true under the counting rules of §3.5; otherwise the reason is `IMMUNIZATION_INCOMPLETE`. Rung three: the membership must have at least one well visit in status `COMPLETED` whose `completed_date` falls within the last 365 days — precisely, on or after today minus 365 days and on or before today, **both bounds inclusive**, so a visit completed exactly 365 days ago still satisfies the rung while one completed 366 days ago does not; otherwise the reason is `WELL_VISIT_STALE`.

Creation evaluates that gate immediately. If every rung passes the request rests in `PENDING`. If any rung fails, the request is written straight to `BLOCKED` with the first failing rung's reason recorded in `blocked_reason` — and the call still returns 201 with the blocked row. A failing gate is a piece of information for the household, not an error condition.

**Issuance** is a `Physician` or `PracticeAdmin` action and re-evaluates the gate from scratch, because a great deal can have changed since the request was filed. Three outcomes are possible. If the request is in `PENDING` and the gate now passes on every rung, it moves `PENDING → ISSUED`, `issued_at` is stamped with the current instant, and `sla_met` is set to true when the `issued_at` is at or before the `due_at` and false when it is later — the comparison is inclusive at equality, so issuing at the exact due instant still counts as met. No service line is created; school and camp forms are always absorbed by the membership. If the request is in `PENDING` but the gate now fails, it moves `PENDING → BLOCKED` with the first failing rung recorded, no form is issued, and the call returns 200 carrying the blocked row rather than an error. If the request is already in `BLOCKED`, issuance is refused outright with `ERR_FORM_GATE_NOT_MET` — a blocked request must first be recovered through a recheck. Issuance aimed at a request already in `ISSUED`, which is terminal, is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

**Rechecking** is the recovery path and closes a loop a request may traverse any number of times. A `CareCoordinator`, `PracticeAdmin` or `Physician` re-runs the gate against a `BLOCKED` request. If every rung now passes, the request moves `BLOCKED → PENDING` and its `blocked_reason` is cleared to null, putting it back in the issuance queue. If a rung still fails, the request stays `BLOCKED` but its `blocked_reason` is overwritten with the first failing rung of this fresh evaluation, which may well differ from the previous value — a household that cleared its overdue invoice but still has a stale well visit will watch the reason move from `MEMBERSHIP_NOT_ACTIVE` to `WELL_VISIT_STALE`. Either outcome counts as a write: a recheck that leaves the request `BLOCKED` on the very same reason it already carried still refreshes `updated_at` and advances `version` by 1. A recheck aimed at a request that is not `BLOCKED` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Requests are also blocked from the outside by two cascades described elsewhere. Cancelling a membership moves every `PENDING` request of that membership to `BLOCKED` with reason `MEMBERSHIP_NOT_ACTIVE`, and invalidating an immunization dose moves every `PENDING` request of that child to `BLOCKED` with reason `IMMUNIZATION_INCOMPLETE`. In both cases requests already in `ISSUED` are untouched — a signed form in a family's hands is never withdrawn.
