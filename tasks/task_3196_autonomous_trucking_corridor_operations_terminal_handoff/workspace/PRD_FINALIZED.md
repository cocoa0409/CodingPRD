# Autonomous Trucking Corridor Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Freight that moves itself is only as good as the handoffs at either end of the highway. Autonomous trucking operators have settled on a hybrid operating model: a human driver covers the first mile out of a shipper's dock and the last mile into the receiving dock, while the autonomous system runs the long, boring, high-lane-mile stretch in between. The place where those two worlds meet is the transfer hub terminal, and the thing that links two hubs together is a corridor — a mapped highway segment with a named weather profile and a human-readable identity such as Sun Belt I-10. This document specifies the API that an Autonomous Trucking Corridor Operations platform must expose to plan those corridors, commission and staff their terminals, evidence their launch readiness, dispatch runs over them, watch them in flight, and account for what happens when something goes wrong.

The domain is unusually hostile to premature automation. A corridor cannot open until sensor calibration has actually passed, the mapped route has actually been validated against current conditions, and a secured load has actually been weighed. A single critical incident anywhere on the corridor must be able to freeze every downstream operation within the same transaction that filed the report — aborting tendered run plans and displacing apron windows that were already reserved. Meanwhile the commercial side of the business runs on tight, deliberately asymmetric arithmetic: carrier rates are floored by shipper contracts, discounted by safety tier, inflated by weather exposure, and settled with a platform commission that makes the invoiced figure strictly larger than the quote the dispatcher saw. Every one of those figures has to reconcile exactly, because carriers will invoice against them.

The platform therefore treats three things as first-class: a derived **posture** on each corridor that gates every child operation beneath it, a logical **ledger epoch** that all validity windows, review windows and hour budgets are measured against, and a set of **evidence records** — readiness checks, safety cases, inspection programmes, incident reports, security records — whose state changes propagate upward into corridor posture, carrier standing and terminal staffing without a client ever asking for the propagation. The remainder of this document defines the shared rules first, then walks the workflows, then fixes the HTTP surface.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the rules that hold across the whole API: who may act, what every response looks like, how lists are paged, how corridor posture gates its children, how the ledger epoch works, what evidence the launch gate demands, how apron and monitoring geometry is measured, how money and time are rounded, how stale writes and duplicate submissions are handled, and what every resource carries by default.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting operator and `X-User-Role` names the operational capacity they are acting in. A request that arrives without a usable identity pair — a missing header, an empty value, or a role string that does not name one of the roles declared below — is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and an HTTP 401. A request whose role is well-formed but not permitted for the operation it targets is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and an HTTP 403. These two checks are the highest-priority rules in the system: authentication is decided before authorisation, and authorisation is decided before any resource is looked up.

The recognised role names, spelled exactly as they must appear in the `X-User-Role` header, are: `Any`, `PlatformAdmin`, `CorridorOperationsManager`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `CarrierDispatcher`, `TerminalManager`, `RemoteSupportSupervisor`, `RemoteSupportOperator`, `IncidentReviewer`, `SafetyCaseAuthor`, `RegulatoryLiaison`, `FleetMaintenanceTech`, `DriverOutreachCoordinator`, `ShipperPartnershipManager`, `BookingClerk`, `TerminalOperator`, and `ExchangeParticipant`. `Any` is not a login identity but the shared read surface: every operational role may read the collections and single resources that `Any` covers.

When `X-User-Role` is `RemoteSupportOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `DriverProspect`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `DriverProspect` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is one of `Any`, `PlatformAdmin`, `CorridorOperationsManager`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `CarrierDispatcher`, `TerminalManager`, `RemoteSupportSupervisor`, `IncidentReviewer`, `SafetyCaseAuthor`, `RegulatoryLiaison`, `FleetMaintenanceTech`, `DriverOutreachCoordinator`, `ShipperPartnershipManager`, `BookingClerk`, `TerminalOperator`, or `ExchangeParticipant`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The platform may record it where a transition writes an operator field, but it is never compared to any entity's `id`.

The role model is a segregation of duties: no single role owns an entire lifecycle end to end. `CorridorOperationsManager` opens and validates corridors and drives sensor calibration and route validation; `ComplianceOfficer` closes incidents, changes carrier standing, retires and suspends corridors, and administers shipping instructions and security records; `CarrierDispatcher` tenders and dispatches run plans; `TerminalManager` commissions hubs and approves apron windows; `RemoteSupportSupervisor` schedules monitoring rosters while `RemoteSupportOperator` — the only entity-backed role — takes them and executes safe stops; `IncidentReviewer` files, investigates and discloses incidents; `SafetyCaseAuthor` drafts and submits safety cases while `RegulatoryLiaison` files them and runs state engagements; `FleetMaintenanceTech` owns inspection programmes and fleet-unit hour posting; `DriverOutreachCoordinator` owns workforce transition enrolment; `ShipperPartnershipManager` owns capacity commitments and pricing floors; `BookingClerk` owns multi-day holds and container bookings; `TerminalOperator` owns in-hub container moves; `ExchangeParticipant` owns the hours-of-service agreement codes; and `PlatformAdmin` alone controls the logical clock and the ledger epoch.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint in this API is paged with two query parameters. `limit` caps how many items come back and defaults to 10, with a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` says how many items to skip and defaults to 0. Unless a subsection says otherwise, list results are ordered by creation timestamp ascending, which for this domain means the order in which records were filed, commissioned or posted — an incident's `report_sequence_number`, for example, follows exactly that order.

List responses carry four pagination fields in the `meta` object of the envelope: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`. Collections that support filtering accept the filter parameters named in §4; filters are applied before the count is taken, so `total_count` always describes the filtered result set rather than the underlying table.

### 2.4. Corridor Posture: The Gate on Every Child Operation

Every corridor carries a derived `posture` whose value is one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED`, or `FROZEN`, and that single field decides whether the corridor's children may do anything at all. The posture is never set directly by a client. It is recomputed from three inputs: the corridor's `check_pass_count`, its `has_blocking_incident` flag, and its `weather_risk_score`. A freshly registered corridor is `FROZEN` — registration itself reports `posture` as `FROZEN` until launch readiness is formally opened, because no calibration evidence exists yet. Opening the launch readiness review moves the posture to `RESTRICTED`, since `check_pass_count` is still 0. Passing the launch gate recomputes the posture again and lands on `OPEN` when the corridor's `weather_risk_score` is below 40, and on `RESTRICTED` otherwise. A corridor that drops from `ACTIVE` to `LIMITED` recomputes to `RESTRICTED`; a corridor that is suspended, failed or retired recomputes to `FROZEN`, which is terminal for a retired corridor.

The children inherit those three readings as three distinct rejection codes. An operation that must be pinned to live weather conditions — starting the highway segment — demands the posture be `OPEN`, and a `RESTRICTED` corridor rejects it with `ERR_CORRIDOR_RESTRICTED` (409). An operation that is merely long-lived — creating a run plan, tendering one, starting the highway segment, executing a safe stop, or activating a carrier — demands the posture be `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED`, and a `FROZEN` corridor rejects any of them with `ERR_CORRIDOR_FROZEN` (409). Finally, an operation that targets a row whose own state does not admit the requested transition is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409), independently of posture. These three checks read as a cascade: posture first, then the row's own state.

Two derived counters feed the posture and are themselves recomputed upward from the corridor's children whenever a child mutates. `check_pass_count` counts the corridor's readiness-check rows that have reached `CALIBRATION_PASSED` or later, while `check_count` counts the rows in any non-terminal status; `is_test_payload` rows are excluded from both. `has_blocking_incident` is true when at least one incident report on the corridor sits in status `INVESTIGATING` or `SUBMITTED` **and** carries severity `CRITICAL`; a critical report that has been closed stops blocking immediately, which is precisely how a `LIMITED` corridor earns its way back to `ACTIVE`. The corridor also carries a read-only `readiness_score` derived from its included checks, defined in §2.6.

The propagation is deliberately one-directional and automatic. Nothing in the platform asks a client to reconcile a parent: filing a critical incident, voiding an inspection programme, letting a calibration window lapse, or rolling the ledger epoch will all reach upward and move corridor posture, corridor status, carrier standing, terminal staffing and apron reservations in the same operation. Two timestamps mark the corridor's own readiness history: `route_validation_calculated_at` records the server moment of the most recent mapped-route validation sweep, and `launch_authorized_at` stays null until the corridor first reaches `ACTIVE` and is cleared back to null whenever the corridor returns to `READINESS_REVIEW`.

### 2.5. The Ledger Epoch, the Logical Clock and Autonomous-Hour Budgets

Every temporal rule in this API is measured against a logical clock, never against wall-clock time. The server's startup default clock is exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`. When a rule says "the ledger clock", "now", "the current epoch day", or "the filing epoch", it means this logical instant. The clock is advanced only through a platform-administration capability that takes a `current_utc` instant; advancing it mutates no business field on any entity, but it will immediately change the outcome of every staleness, validity and review-window rule that is evaluated afterwards.

Time is divided into ledger epochs. A `LedgerCheckpoint` is opened with a free-form `checkpoint_code`, an `epoch_start_at` and an `epoch_end_at` that must be strictly after the start, and an `hours_per_epoch` budget that defaults to 24. A checkpoint's `checkpoint_status` begins at `OPEN`, moves to `ADOPTED` when the platform adopts it as the active epoch — `adopted_at` recording the server moment, and the earlier of two same-epoch checkpoints winning the debits — and finally to `ROLLED_OVER`, which stamps `rolled_over_at`.

Rolling an epoch over is the single largest reconciliation in the system, and it has consequences far beyond the checkpoint row itself. Every carrier's `previous_epoch_incidents` is overwritten with that carrier's `recordable_incidents_last_epoch`, and `recordable_incidents_last_epoch` is then reset to 0; each carrier's `incident_band` is recomputed from the newly frozen `previous_epoch_incidents`. Every state authority's `hours_used` resets to 0 and its `remaining_autonomous_hours` recomputes. Every carrier's `rate_settlement_cents` recomputes. Every readiness check in `CALIBRATION_PASSED` or `VALIDATED` whose valid window has elapsed moves to `EXPIRED_FAILED`, as does every check still sitting in `INITIALIZED` whose window has elapsed, while checks in `CALIBRATION_PASSED` whose corridor did receive a launch authorisation advance to `VALIDATED`. The `check_pass_count` of every corridor holding a mutated check recomputes. Any carrier whose `carrier_status` is `ACTIVE` and whose recomputed `incident_band` reaches `SUSPENDED` is moved to `SUSPENDED`, and every run plan on that carrier sitting in `DRAFT` or `READINESS_PENDING` is aborted, displacing the apron windows that were merely `REQUESTED` on those runs.

Autonomous operating hours are budgeted per state authority, per epoch. Each authority declares an `autonomous_hours_allowed` ceiling (default 24) and tracks `hours_used`, with `remaining_autonomous_hours` reading as the allowance minus the usage. `hours_used` accumulates only from engagements whose `state_status` is `SUBMITTED` and whose `engaged_at` falls inside the closed-open epoch window `[epoch_start_at, epoch_end_at)`; a `DRAFT` or `REJECTED` engagement never debits the budget, and an `ACCEPTED` engagement counts exactly once no matter how many later submissions follow it. An engagement whose `engaged_at` falls outside the current epoch window is refused with `ERR_OUTSIDE_EPOCH_WINDOW` (422), and a request for more hours than remain is refused with `ERR_HOURS_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (409).
### 2.6. Launch Evidence: Calibration, Route Validation, Load Securement and Readiness Scoring

Launching a corridor is an evidence-gathering exercise before it is an operational one. `LaunchReadinessCheck` rows are the unit of that evidence, and each one declares a `check_type` of `SENSOR_CALIBRATION`, `ROUTE_VALIDATION` or `LOAD_SECUREMENT`, together with a client-declared `check_result` of `PASS`, `FAIL` or `PENDING`. Every check also carries the GPS coordinates of the run that produced it — `suite_latitude` and `suite_longitude`, both defaulting to 0 and both rounded to 3 decimals on write — and a `valid_window_days` (default 180) measured from the `valid_window_start_at` epoch start that the server stamps at creation. The check's own `check_status` proceeds through `INITIALIZED → CALIBRATION_PASSED → VALIDATED → EXPIRED_FAILED`, and its `check_contribution` is the per-check readiness contribution described below.

Sensor calibration is judged by a derived quantity rather than by the raw coordinates. `calibration_variance_ms` is the floor, to 3 decimal places, of 2.4 times `suite_latitude` plus 1.6 times `suite_longitude`. The tolerance for that variance is 150 milliseconds: a check whose variance exceeds 150 ms cannot be treated as calibrated, and any readiness check on the corridor carrying `check_result` `FAIL` fails the corridor gate outright. Both conditions surface as `ERR_CALIBRATION_FAILED` (409). Load securement is a separate and non-negotiable claim: the corridor must carry at least one `LOAD_SECUREMENT` check whose `load_payload_lbs` reaches 40000 pounds and whose `check_result` is `PASS`; without it the gate refuses with `ERR_LOAD_SECUREMENT_FAILED` (409). Route validation is provenance-driven — `corridor.route_validation_calculated_at` must be current within the check's `valid_window_days`, and the route must have been validated at all — and a stale or unvalidated route is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_VALIDATED` (409).

The corridor's `readiness_score` aggregates the included checks, and the same arithmetic has a second variant surfaced on individual checks as `check_contribution`. Per check, a `PASS` result is worth 60 points, a `PENDING` result 20, and a `FAIL` result 0; the calibration variance in milliseconds is added to that weight and the sum is rounded half up to two decimal places to give the check's base score. That base is then capped at 40 whenever the check's variance is within the 150 ms tolerance and the base would otherwise exceed 100 — so two checks whose bases are 100 and 100 score 40 and 40 while two checks at variance 140 and 160 contribute 40 and 100 respectively, because only the first of those is within tolerance. The corridor-level figure sums the per-check scores of the included rows and divides by the number of included rows, rounded half up to two decimal places. A row is included only when its `check_status` is `CALIBRATION_PASSED` or `VALIDATED` and its `is_test_payload` flag is false; `INITIALIZED` and `EXPIRED_FAILED` rows are excluded, and rows carrying `is_test_payload` true are excluded from the numerator and the denominator alike. When no row is included, the corridor's `readiness_score` is 0. Note the deliberate asymmetry between the two variants: a per-row base above 100 is pulled down by the shared 40-point cap, while the corridor average is not separately capped. A check marked `is_test_payload` is a synthetic fixture — it never blocks a corridor and never counts toward `check_pass_count`.

Readiness checks are also the system's one idempotency surface. `SENSOR_CALIBRATION` and `ROUTE_VALIDATION` rows are deduplicated on the triple of `corridor_id`, `correlation_key` and `epoch_start_at`: when a second request arrives carrying the same `correlation_key` within the same epoch, the server returns the original row with an HTTP 200 rather than recording a second check. Every check carries the `epoch_start_at` stamped by the ledger checkpoint at creation precisely so that this comparison has a stable epoch component.

### 2.7. Apron Geometry and Remote Support Screening

Transfer-hub operations are governed by interval arithmetic, and the domain is explicit about the geometry. A `TerminalServiceWindow` occupies the half-open interval that begins at `window_start_minute` and ends at `window_end_minute`, with `buffer_minutes` appended to the end as a yard re-positioning allowance. The window must satisfy `window_start_minute` strictly less than `window_end_minute`, and its `service_type` is one of `HANDOFF_INBOUND`, `HANDOFF_OUTBOUND`, `YARD_TRANSFER` or `MAINTENANCE_STAGING`. The window's read-only `buffer_seconds` is computed from the same interval by the shared buffer rounder: for a `HANDOFF_INBOUND` window it is the floor of the window length plus 0.49, and for every other service type it is the floor of the window length plus 0.51, rendered as whole seconds. A window begins its life as `REQUESTED`, and its later states are `APPROVED`, `IN_SERVICE`, `COMPLETED`, `DISPLACED` and `CANCELLED`.

Screening against other approved windows follows the adjacency rule: touching windows do not occupy. A new window whose `window_start_minute` equals an existing window's effective end — that is, its end minute plus its buffer — is permitted, but any window that would share even a single minute with an existing approved or in-service window is refused with `ERR_APRON_OVERLAP` (409). The screening set is the terminal's windows in `APPROVED` or `IN_SERVICE`; `DISPLACED` and `CANCELLED` windows have already released their apron and are ignored. The same geometry is reused by remote support monitoring, with one twist driven by the assignment's `geofence_type`. Under `GEOFENCE_ONLY` the collision test runs on the exact intervals after the apron buffer has been added. Under `GEOFENCE_PLUS_BUFFER` the interval is first expanded multiplicatively by 1.5 before screening, so a monitoring window that clears under the exact test can still be rejected under the buffered one. The reading belongs to the window being placed: its own `geofence_type` decides whether it is expanded, while a window already on file is measured on its exact interval regardless of the type it declared. A monitoring window that overlaps another active window held by the same operator — same operator, same corridor, either seat, either direction — is refused with `ERR_MONITOR_OVERLAP` (409).

Apron occupancy is itself a derived quantity. `Terminal.reserved_apron_units` is the sum, over the terminal's `APPROVED` service windows, of the window length in minutes divided by 60 and rounded half up to two decimal places, and it is compared against the terminal's `apron_capacity`: an approval that would push occupancy past capacity is refused with `ERR_APRON_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Two further terminal readings ride on the same set of rows: `total_handoff_minutes` sums `handoff_duration_minutes` across the approved windows, and `approved_window_count` counts them. The terminal's `transfer_turnaround_minutes` is the round-half-up, to three decimal places, of `total_handoff_minutes` multiplied by a round-trip factor of 0.5 and divided by the approved-window count — with one precise edge: when no window is approved the quotient is undefined and the reading collapses to 0 rather than to a stale prior average.

### 2.8. Money: Precision, Rounding and the Rate Family

All monetary quantities in this API are decimal amounts, and every derived monetary field is rounded half up to exactly 2 decimal places at the moment it is computed. Where a calculation produces several additive terms, the rounding is applied per term before the terms are summed unless the calculation says otherwise; the run-plan exposure calculation in §3.9 is the worked example of that distinction, and its two variants differ by exactly one cent on the same inputs. Rates quoted in cents per mile — `posted_per_mile_cents`, `per_mile_rate_cents`, `committed_rate_cents`, `scenario_rate_cents` and `run_window_rate_cents` — follow the same 2-decimal rule even though they are expressed in cents. Percentage-style quantities such as the platform commission are applied as basis points over ten thousand and then rounded with the result.

Four derived rates hang off every run plan and they are not interchangeable. `posted_per_mile_cents` is the carrier's client-supplied all-in rate. `per_mile_rate_cents` is the carrier rate the platform computes from it. `committed_rate_cents` is the rate the shipper contract will actually be billed at after the carrier's safety-tier discount. `scenario_rate_cents` is the worst-case planning figure that folds in corridor weather exposure, and `run_window_rate_cents` is a chassis holding figure cascaded up from the run plan's chassis record. A fifth figure, `safety_score` on the run plan, is a 0–100 reading initialised to 50 at creation and banded through `LOW`, `MEDIUM`, `HIGH` and `CRITICAL`. The exact arithmetic for each of these lives with the workflow that produces it, in §3.2 and §3.9.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two transitions in this API are protected by optimistic concurrency control, and both of them guard a decision that would otherwise be made against state the caller has not seen. Validating a corridor's launch readiness requires the request to carry `expected_corridor_version`, the client's last observed value of that corridor's `version`; promoting a run plan to dispatch ready requires `expected_run_plan_version`, the client's last observed value of that run plan's `version`. In each case the server compares the supplied value to the parent row's current `version`, and a mismatch — because a maintenance programme, an incident report, a readiness check, an apron-window approval or a rate recalculation has already advanced the parent since the client read it — is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412. The rejection is a pure equality test against the current version; the platform does not require the writes to have overlapped in time, only that the caller's observation is no longer current. Every other endpoint in §4 takes no version field at all, and supplying one where the endpoint does not declare it is treated as an unknown field and refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422).

### 2.10. Common Resource Properties

Every entity in this system is addressable by an `id` that the server mints as a plain UUID — there is no type prefix, no entity name embedded in the identifier, and no client-supplied natural key standing in for it. Natural keys such as `terminal_code`, `dot_number`, `state_code`, `unit_id`, `security_code` and `container_number` are business identifiers and are validated for uniqueness where the entity says so, but they are always carried alongside the `id` and never replace it.

Resources also carry `created_at` and, where the entity is mutable, `updated_at` and `version`. Both timestamps are ISO 8601 UTC with exactly three fractional digits, the read form being `2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z`. `created_at` records when the row was first written and never changes. `updated_at` records the most recent mutation of the row and is refreshed whenever anything on it changes, including changes the server makes on its own behalf: when a derived field recomputes because a child moved, the parent's `updated_at` refreshes and its `version` increments. `version` is a plain integer starting at 1 and incrementing by exactly 1 on every mutation; it is the field the two optimistic-concurrency checks above read, and it is the field operators pass back as `expected_corridor_version`, `expected_run_plan_version`, `expected_check_version`, `expected_instruction_version`, `expected_fleet_unit_version` and `expected_security_record_version`.

Every entity in this scaffold is declared with the full common-field set, so none of them omits `updated_at` or `version`; the read-only derived readings that hang off them — `reserved_apron_units`, `check_pass_count`, `remaining_slots`, `hours_posted_total` and the rest — are ordinary computed fields rather than separate resources, and recomputing one is itself a mutation that touches the parent's `updated_at` and `version`.

### 2.11. Validation Order and Identifier Resolution

Rules do not fire in the order a reader happens to encounter them in §3. When more than one rule could reject a request, the platform evaluates them in a fixed priority order, and the error a caller sees is the one belonging to the first rule that fails. Authentication is decided first, then authorisation, then the existence of the resource named in the path, then the resolution of every identifier supplied outside the path, then corridor posture, then the target row's own state, and only then the domain-specific safety, commercial and temporal rules. A concrete consequence of that ordering is worth stating plainly: a request whose path names a corridor that does not exist is answered with `ERR_CORRIDOR_NOT_FOUND` (404) even if the request would also have failed a weather or capacity rule, and a request whose body references a `hauler_team_id` that resolves to no `DriverProspect` is answered with `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` (422) before any commercial rule is consulted.

Identifier resolution covers nine body-supplied references by name: `origin_terminal_id`, `destination_terminal_id`, `carrier_id`, `shipper_commitment_id`, `hauler_team_id`, `state_authority_id`, `run_plan_id`, `prospect_id` and `terminal_id`. Each must resolve to a row of its declared entity, and any that does not yields `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`. Path identifiers get their own, more specific codes: a missing corridor is `ERR_CORRIDOR_NOT_FOUND`, a missing run plan `ERR_RUN_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, a missing terminal `ERR_TERMINAL_NOT_FOUND`, a missing carrier `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_FOUND`, a missing shipper commitment `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND` and a missing incident report `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`. When a path nests several identifiers — a state authority beneath a corridor, a service window beneath a terminal, a chassis beneath a run plan — they are validated left to right and the request stops at the first one that does not resolve.

Malformed requests are caught by `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422): a required body field is missing, a field arrives with the wrong type, or a field is supplied that the endpoint does not declare. The remaining domain rules — safety, commercial, temporal and referential — are catalogued city by city in §3 and listed without their triggers in §5.

### 2.12. Idempotency and Deduplication

One surface in this API is explicitly idempotent: readiness-check creation. As described in §2.6, a `SENSOR_CALIBRATION` or `ROUTE_VALIDATION` check is deduplicated on `(corridor_id, correlation_key, epoch_start_at)`, and a repeat within the same epoch returns the original row with HTTP 200 instead of creating a twin. Outside that surface the API is not idempotent by design: tendering a run plan twice, filing two incident reports for the same event, or inserting the same apron window twice are all distinct business acts and are recorded as such. Where a repeated act would corrupt a derived balance, the platform relies on state and capacity checks rather than on replay detection — the `remaining_slots` check on a commitment and the `hold_status` check on an apron window are the two examples that carry the most weight.
## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Corridor Planning and the Launch Gate

A `CorridorOperationsManager` opens a corridor by naming it — a `corridor_name` such as Sun Belt I-10 is enough for humans — and declaring its two hubs through `origin_terminal_id` and `destination_terminal_id`, the terminal where the autonomous highway segment is loaded inbound and the terminal where it is unloaded outbound. The registration also carries `highway_segment_miles`, the length of the autonomous stretch exclusive of the terminal aprons, a `mapped_route` descriptor, and a `weather_profile`. The route descriptor is a nested object whose parts are validated later, during launch readiness: `route_code`, `waypoint_count`, `has_hov_lane` and `requires_tunnel_clearance`. The weather profile is one of `CLEAR_DEFAULT`, `HIGH_WIND`, `HEAVY_PRECIPITATION`, `LOW_VISIBILITY` or `WINTER_SURFACE`; `HIGH_WIND` and `LOW_VISIBILITY` are the two high-risk classes. An optional `weather_risk_score` gives the corridor a 0–100 weight for that profile and defaults to 0. Two rules guard registration itself: the two terminal identifiers must be different terminals, and naming the same one for both is a round trip and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ROUTE` (422); every terminal identifier supplied in the body must resolve, or the request stops with `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` (422). A newly registered corridor is `PLANNED`, its derived `check_pass_count` and `check_count` are both 0, and its `posture` reads `FROZEN` — a corridor with no calibration evidence is inert by construction.

Both hubs must exist and be in service before the corridor can be reviewed. A `TerminalManager` commissions a transfer hub with a `terminal_code` such as PHX-WEST, a `terminal_name`, the `state_code` of the state whose apron it sits on, and an `apron_capacity`. Commissioning leaves the hub in `COMMISSIONING` with `reserved_apron_units` and `transfer_turnaround_minutes` both at 0. Moving the hub into service transitions `terminal_state` to `OPERATIONAL`, which is the state the launch gate demands of both of a corridor's terminals; the terminal state can later move to `DEGRADED` or to the terminal `CLOSED`. The terminal also carries a derived `staffing_status` of `STAFFED` or `UNDERSTAFFED`, which is reconciled at finalization time against the transition enrolments staffing the hub — a signal this section only registers, because the staffing workflow itself belongs to §3.8.

Opening launch readiness review moves the corridor from `PLANNED` to `READINESS_REVIEW`. Three preconditions bind it: the corridor must actually be in `PLANNED` (anything else is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, 409), both terminals must be `OPERATIONAL`, and `highway_segment_miles` must be greater than zero. Opening the review recomputes `posture` to `RESTRICTED`, refreshes `check_pass_count`, and clears `launch_authorized_at` back to null; a corridor that has never been authorised simply keeps it null. The same action is the recovery path from suspension: a `SUSPENDED` corridor may re-enter `READINESS_REVIEW`, but only once no incident report on it remains in `INVESTIGATING` or `SUBMITTED` — otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_BLOCKED_BY_INCIDENT` (409). Re-entering review is deliberately destructive: every readiness check on the corridor that has not yet reached a terminal result moves to `EXPIRED_FAILED` and `check_pass_count` resets to 0, forcing the whole evidence base to be rebuilt.

Evidence is then recorded as `LaunchReadinessCheck` rows. Each check is declared with a `check_type` of `SENSOR_CALIBRATION`, `ROUTE_VALIDATION` or `LOAD_SECUREMENT`, a client-declared `check_result` of `PASS`, `FAIL` or `PENDING`, and the coordinates of the run that produced it through `suite_latitude` and `suite_longitude` (both defaulting to 0 and both rounded to 3 decimals on write). A `LOAD_SECUREMENT` check additionally declares `load_payload_lbs`, the secured load weight in pounds. A check may be given a `prior_run_plan_id` naming a locally stored last-mile run plan; a corridor may store at most one such plan, so a second check claiming a prior plan for the same corridor is rejected. The check's validity period is `valid_window_days`, defaulting to 180 days and measured from the epoch start the server stamps as `valid_window_start_at`. Two flags complete the record: `is_test_payload`, which marks a synthetic fixture that never blocks a corridor and never counts toward `check_pass_count`, and `correlation_key`, the idempotency key described in §2.6. Recording a check leaves it in `INITIALIZED`, stamps `epoch_start_at` from the active ledger epoch, derives `calibration_variance_ms`, and recomputes the corridor's `check_count` and `readiness_score`.

Three follow-on actions advance a check through `CALIBRATION_PASSED`, `VALIDATED` and `EXPIRED_FAILED`. Driving the sensor calibration step moves the check to `CALIBRATION_PASSED` and recomputes `calibration_variance_ms`, `check_pass_count`, the corridor's `posture` and the readiness score. Validating the mapped-route window moves the check to `VALIDATED`, requires the client's `expected_check_version` to match the check's current `version`, and refreshes the corridor's `route_validation_calculated_at`. Expiring a check whose validity window has elapsed — a `ComplianceOfficer` action — moves it to `EXPIRED_FAILED` and recomputes `check_pass_count`, posture and score downward. A corridor whose only passing check lapses therefore restricts itself without anyone filing anything.

Passing the launch gate is where the evidence is weighed, and the checks are evaluated in a fixed order. First the corridor must clear its own readiness arithmetic: `check_pass_count` must be at least `check_count` with `check_count` at least 1, and no check on the corridor may carry `check_result` `FAIL`; either failure is `ERR_READINESS_CHECK_FAILED` (409). Second comes calibration integrity — every check in `CALIBRATION_PASSED` or `VALIDATED` must sit at or under the 150 millisecond variance tolerance, failing which the gate answers `ERR_CALIBRATION_FAILED` (409). Third comes load securement: the corridor must carry at least one `LOAD_SECUREMENT` check whose `load_payload_lbs` reaches 40000 pounds and whose `check_result` is `PASS`, or the gate answers `ERR_LOAD_SECUREMENT_FAILED` (409). Fourth comes the safety case, and fifth the weather ceiling of `weather_risk_score` at or below 80, which refuses with `ERR_WEATHER_RESTRICTED` (409). Sixth is the presence of an unresolved blocking incident, which refuses with `ERR_BLOCKED_BY_INCIDENT` (409). Seventh is route validation, which refuses with `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_VALIDATED` (409) when the mapped route has not been validated or its validation has aged past the check's validity window, and requires either `has_hov_lane` to be true or `requires_tunnel_clearance` to be false. Eighth is maintenance standing, which refuses with `ERR_MAINTENANCE_OVERDUE` (409) when any fleet unit assigned to the corridor carries an inspection programme in `OVERDUE` status. A coordinated-dispatch readiness claim made with empty fleet logs is refused with `ERR_MISSING_LOGS` (422), and any body reference that does not resolve stops the request earlier still with `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`.

A successful pass is a guarded write. The request must carry `expected_corridor_version`; a corridor whose `version` has advanced since the client last read it — because a maintenance programme, an incident report or a readiness check has already moved its derived posture — is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412, exactly as §2.9 describes. On success the corridor increments its `version`, moves `corridor_status` to `ACTIVE`, stamps `launch_authorized_at` from the ledger clock, and recomputes `posture`, landing on `OPEN` when `weather_risk_score` is below 40 and on `RESTRICTED` otherwise. Every readiness check on the corridor sitting in `CALIBRATION_PASSED` advances to `VALIDATED`, and the corridor's `readiness_score` recomputes. The same gate is the recovery route from `LIMITED` back to `ACTIVE`: there the corridor must carry no inspection programme in `OVERDUE` status, hold `weather_risk_score` below 80, and have `has_blocking_incident` false, and the pass recomputes posture to `OPEN` when the weather weight is below 40. One asymmetry is worth registering: apron windows that were displaced while the corridor was limited are not restored automatically — operators must request them again.

Corridors also come to an end. A `ComplianceOfficer` may retire a corridor from `READINESS_REVIEW` or from `ACTIVE`, and both routes require that no run plan on the corridor is `IN_TRANSIT`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_RUN_PLAN_IN_TRANSIT` (409). Retirement is terminal and drives `posture` to `FROZEN`, aborts every non-terminal run plan on the corridor, and cancels every apron window on those runs that was `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`. Retiring an `ACTIVE` corridor carries one further consequence: the carriers referenced by its runs move `carrier_status` to `OFFBOARDED` when the carrier has no other `ACTIVE` corridor left to serve. Forced suspension is the other exit: a `ComplianceOfficer` may fail a corridor that carries a blocking incident — the precondition is exactly that `has_blocking_incident` is true, and the request is refused with `ERR_BLOCKED_BY_INCIDENT` (409) otherwise — which recomputes `posture` to `FROZEN`, aborts every run plan on the corridor still in `DRAFT`, `READINESS_PENDING` or `DISPATCH_READY`, sets `filing_required_next` to true on the corridor's referenced state authorities, and leaves the corridor's safety case reading as `STALE`. Forced suspension is also the automatic consequence of a critical incident that requires regulatory notice, as §3.5 explains.
### 3.2. Tendering a Run Plan and Pricing It Against the Shipper Contract

A run plan is how a carrier partnership turns a corridor into a scheduled movement. A `CarrierDispatcher` creates one by naming the `corridor_id`, the supplying `carrier_id`, the `origin_terminal_id` and `destination_terminal_id` it loads and unloads at, the `shipper_commitment_id` whose capacity it draws against, the inclusive `window_start_epoch_day` and exclusive `window_end_epoch_day` of its scheduling window, the `trailer_count` and the `posted_per_mile_cents` the carrier is asking. It also names a `hauler_team_id` — the driver workforce transition unit that will handle the first and last mile at both hubs — and may declare `sacra_loadboard_slot_held`, which says whether the run claims a loadboard slot and defaults to false. The window must satisfy `window_end_epoch_day` strictly greater than `window_start_epoch_day`, the corridor must not be `FROZEN` (otherwise `ERR_CORRIDOR_FROZEN`, 409), the two terminals must differ or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ROUTE` (422), and `trailer_count` must be exactly 1 or exactly 2 — every other value is `ERR_INVALID_TRAILER_COUNT` (422). Creating a run plan leaves its `run_status` at `DRAFT` with a `safety_score` of 50 and a `safety_band` of `MEDIUM`, and it immediately computes the four derived rates this section and §3.11 refer to.

The first of those is the carrier rate. `per_mile_rate_cents` is the client-supplied `posted_per_mile_cents` plus a heavy-trailer surcharge, taken as the trailer count divided by two and rounded up to a whole number, multiplied by the heavy-trailer surcharge rate — applied for any `trailer_count` of at least 1, so a single-trailer run adds one surcharge unit and a two-trailer run adds one as well. This field is recomputed whenever the run plan is repriced. The second is the commitment rate, `committed_rate_cents`, and it is where the shipper contract and the carrier's safety standing meet. The platform takes the larger of `per_mile_rate_cents` and the commitment's `shipper_floor_per_mile_cents` as the pre-discount floor, then subtracts the carrier's `committed_discount_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places. That discount is selected from the carrier's `safety_tier` band, and the band boundaries are inclusive at their lower edges: a carrier whose `safety_score` is at least 800 is `HIGH` and takes a discount of 0; a carrier whose `safety_score` is between 500 and 799 inclusive at the lower edge is `MEDIUM` and takes a discount of 15; a carrier below 500 is `LOW` and takes a discount of 40. The two readings of the same arithmetic are worth keeping apart — when the shipper floor is the larger input the commitment floor is dominant and the committed rate tracks the shipper contract rather than the carrier, and when `per_mile_rate_cents` is the larger input the carrier rate is dominant and the same discount band yields a strictly larger committed rate. One post-condition is asserted for every pricing: `committed_rate_cents` is never below `shipper_floor_per_mile_cents` minus 40.

The pricing rules also gate creation. The requested `posted_per_mile_cents` must be at least `shipper_floor_per_mile_cents` minus the maximum band discount of 40, or the request is refused with `ERR_RATE_BELOW_COMMITMENT_FLOOR` (422). The named commitment must cover the run window — its reserved capacity interval runs from `reserved_window_start_epoch_day`, inclusive, to `reserved_window_end_epoch_day`, exclusive — and a run window that no commitment covers is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_COMMITMENT` (409). Should the pricing rules later be re-evaluated after a carrier re-price, the `committed_rate_cents` derivation above is the version that travels with the run.

Commitments themselves are registered upstream of all of this by a `ShipperPartnershipManager`, who supplies the partnership's `shipper_name`, the `shipper_floor_per_mile_cents` the carrier may not price below, the inclusive `reserved_window_start_epoch_day` and exclusive `reserved_window_end_epoch_day` of the reserved capacity window, and the `committed_slots` count. The commitment registers with `commitment_status` `DRAFT`, `consumed_slots` of 0 and `remaining_slots` equal to `committed_slots`; it may hold no fewer than 1 and no more than 50 slots. A draft commitment can be confirmed outright, which moves its `commitment_status` to `CONFIRMED` without waiting for a run to be tendered against it. Two further readings ride on the commitment and both are touched by the driver workforce workflow in §3.8 and the completion step in §3.4: `verified_ride_along_count` counts the transition enrolments on the commitment's runs that reached `RIDE_ALONG_VERIFIED`, and `ride_along_credit_cents` reads as the floor, to two decimal places, of a per-verified-ride-along credit of 1200.00 dollars multiplied by that count and divided by two. The companion `billed_ride_along_cents` is a reversal ledger: revoking a commitment that is `CONFIRMED` or `SHIPPED` moves its `commitment_status` to `REVOKED`, moves the whole of `ride_along_credit_cents` into `billed_ride_along_cents` and recomputes `ride_along_credit_cents` to 0, so the sum of the two readings is conserved across the revocation rather than lost.

Tendering the run is the next step, and it happens when the dispatcher submits a `DRAFT` run plan. Before the run moves to `READINESS_PENDING`, four things must hold. The corridor must not be `FROZEN`, or the submit is refused with `ERR_CORRIDOR_FROZEN` (409). The `trailer_count` must again be 1 or 2, or `ERR_INVALID_TRAILER_COUNT` (422). The commitment must have at least one unconsumed loadboard slot, or the submit is refused with `ERR_COMMITMENT_CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED` (409) — the commitment may reserve no fewer than one and no more than 50 slots in total. Every readiness check on the corridor sitting in `CALIBRATION_PASSED` or `VALIDATED` must sit at or under the 150 millisecond calibration variance tolerance, and no check may carry a `FAIL` result; the two conditions surface as `ERR_LOAD_SECUREMENT_FAILED` and `ERR_CALIBRATION_FAILED` respectively. Finally, a corridor whose posture is `RESTRICTED` will not accept a tendered run while its safety case exists only in `DRAFT`; that combination is refused with `ERR_SAFETY_CASE_STALE` (409). Tendering also enforces the corridor's coverage exclusivity: the corridor may not already carry a covered run plan in `IN_TRANSIT` when a second plan for the same coverage window is tendered, and the collision is refused with `ERR_RUN_PLAN_OVERLAP` (409).

A successful tender consumes capacity. The owning commitment's `consumed_slots` increases by exactly one and its `remaining_slots`, which reads as `committed_slots` minus `consumed_slots`, recomputes; a commitment whose `commitment_status` is still `DRAFT` flips to `CONFIRMED`; and the corridor's `check_pass_count` recomputes. The commitment's `consumed_slots` counts the run plans claiming its loadboard slot whose `run_status` is `READINESS_PENDING`, `DISPATCH_READY`, `IN_TRANSIT` or `COMPLETED`; a run that is aborted releases its slot, and a run that merely requeues still holds one — only a plan that never left `DRAFT` costs the commitment nothing. A slot claim made against a commitment whose `remaining_slots` is exactly zero is refused, which is the same `ERR_COMMITMENT_CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED` code described above.

### 3.3. Dispatch Readiness, Requeue and Abort

Promoting a tendered run to `DISPATCH_READY` is the point at which terminal capacity is actually committed, and it is one of the two guarded writes in this API. The request must carry `expected_run_plan_version`; a run plan whose `version` has advanced since the dispatcher last read it — because an apron window approval or a rate recalculation has already touched the plan — is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412, as §2.9 states. Before the promotion proceeds, three substantive conditions hold. Every `ROUTE_VALIDATION` check on the corridor must carry `check_result` `PASS`. The corridor's mapped-route validation must be current, meaning the corridor's `route_validation_calculated_at` falls within the check's `valid_window_days`. And the run's `per_mile_rate_cents` must be the current carrier price, i.e. the carrier re-price has already recomputed it. A dispatch request into a window already committed on the same corridor is refused with `ERR_DISPATCH_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).

Promotion carries a cascade that reaches three different parents. Any `VALIDATED` readiness check on the corridor whose validity window has elapsed is moved to `EXPIRED_FAILED`. Every apron window that was merely `REQUESTED` for this run is re-evaluated on the spot: a window declaring `yield_to_transit` true is `DISPLACED`, and every other requested window is `APPROVED`. The owning terminal's `reserved_apron_units`, `approved_window_count` and `transfer_turnaround_minutes` all recompute, and the corridor's readiness score recomputes as well. The run itself increments its `version` and enters `DISPATCH_READY`.

There is exactly one step back. A `DISPATCH_READY` run can be requeued to `READINESS_PENDING`, and the step is only available while the corridor posture is `RESTRICTED` — an `OPEN` or `FROZEN` corridor refuses the requeue with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) and a `FROZEN` corridor answers with the more specific `ERR_CORRIDOR_FROZEN` (409) instead. Requeueing returns every `APPROVED` apron window on the run to `REQUESTED`, recomputes the owning terminal's `reserved_apron_units` downward, and recomputes the commitment's `consumed_slots` — which, as §3.2 noted, stays where it was, because a requeued run still holds its loadboard slot. The terminal's turnover reading has a sharp edge here: when the reverted windows were the terminal's last approved ones, `transfer_turnaround_minutes` collapses to the empty default of 0 rather than retaining the previous average.

Abort is the run plan's other terminal state, and a `ComplianceOfficer` owns it. A run plan may be aborted from `DRAFT`, `READINESS_PENDING` or `DISPATCH_READY` — a plan in any other state answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409), and a plan that has already reached `COMPLETED` or `ABORTED` answers the more specific `ERR_RUN_PLAN_TERMINAL` (409). Aborting cancels every apron window on the run that was `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`, decrements the owning commitment's `consumed_slots` by exactly one so the aborted run releases its slot, recomputes the commitment's `remaining_slots`, and recomputes the corridor's `check_pass_count`. Aborting also touches the carrier: its `recordable_incidents_last_epoch` recomputes, and its `safety_tier` and `committed_discount_cents` recompute from the carrier's current `safety_score`. Every terminal run plan in the system — `COMPLETED` and `ABORTED` alike — rejects all further transitions with `ERR_RUN_PLAN_TERMINAL`.

### 3.4. The Highway Segment, Safe Stop and Completion

Handing a dispatched run to the autonomous system is the transition into `IN_TRANSIT`, and it is the strictest gate in the run lifecycle because it is the moment the platform stops being able to intervene directly. The corridor posture must be exactly `OPEN`; a `RESTRICTED` corridor refuses with `ERR_CORRIDOR_RESTRICTED` (409) and a `FROZEN` corridor with `ERR_CORRIDOR_FROZEN` (409). At least one apron window on the run must already be `APPROVED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_NO_APPROVED_HANDOFF_WINDOW` (409) — the human handoff must be physically reserved before the truck rolls. No remote support assignment on the run may be sitting in `SCHEDULED` or `MONITORING` while declaring the `STANDBY_TAKEOVER` intervention protocol; such an assignment blocks the start. The corridor's `weather_risk_score` ceiling of 80 and the maintenance-standing rule both apply here as well, refusing with `ERR_WEATHER_RESTRICTED` (409) and `ERR_MAINTENANCE_OVERDUE` (409) respectively. On success every approved window on the run moves to `IN_SERVICE` — which releases nothing, because an in-service window still occupies its apron — and the terminal's `reserved_apron_units` and `transfer_turnaround_minutes` recompute.

Safe stop is the remote operator's emergency capability, and the `RemoteSupportOperator` who executes it is the only entity-backed actor in the system: `X-User-ID` identifies that operator's `DriverProspect`. The procedure requires a monitoring assignment on the run to be in `MONITORING` status; if none is, the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Executing it moves the run from `IN_TRANSIT` back to `READINESS_PENDING`, sets `safe_stop_required` true on the monitoring assignment and moves that assignment to `EXPIRED`, recomputes the assignment's `safe_stop_fee_cents`, and cancels every apron window on the run. The fee that lands on the assignment is banded against the run's exposure, and the exposure figure is assembled from four inputs: the corridor's `highway_segment_miles` multiplied by the corridor's `weather_risk_score`, multiplied by the run's `trailer_count`, multiplied by a health factor taken from the owning carrier's `incident_band` — 1.0 when the carrier is `CLEAN`, 1.5 when it is `WATCH` and 2.0 when it is `SUSPENDED`. That product is rounded half up to two decimal places to give the run exposure, and the fee is then read from three bands: strictly below 1000 yields 0, at or above 1000 but strictly below 5000 yields 25000, and at or above 5000 yields 60000. The boundaries are exact — a run at exactly 1000 falls in the 25000 band while a run at 999.99 stays at 0. A near-variant of the same reading substitutes mileage-scaled transit minutes for the weighted exposure term, computed as the corridor's `highway_segment_miles` multiplied by 1.6 and rounded half up to two decimal places, and applies the same three bands, so a short high-wind run can cross the 5000 band under the exposure reading while staying below it under the transit-minutes reading. Executing a safe stop also recomputes the owning carrier's `recordable_incidents_last_epoch` and the corridor's `has_blocking_incident`, and the corridor may bubble back up from `LIMITED` to `ACTIVE` if that flag clears.

Completion closes the commercial side of the run. A `CarrierDispatcher` completes a run from `IN_TRANSIT`, moving it to `COMPLETED` and settling the commitment: every driver transition attached to the run that has reached `RIDE_ALONG_VERIFIED` increments the commitment's `verified_ride_along_count`, whose credit ledger works out as described in §3.8; the commitment's `ride_along_credit_cents` and `billed_ride_along_cents` recompute; and the commitment flips to `SHIPPED` with `shipped_at` stamped. Completion also recomputes the owning carrier's `safety_score` from its run plans and, in consequence, its `safety_tier` and `committed_discount_cents`.

### 3.5. Incidents, Regulatory Notice and the Blocking Cascade

An `IncidentReviewer` files an incident report against a corridor run by naming the `corridor_id`, the `run_plan_id` the event occurred on, and a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MODERATE` or `CRITICAL`. The filing may also carry `recordable_incidents`, the count the regulator will record against the owning carrier (default 0), a `corrective_action_count` (default 0), a `review_days_allowed` (default 0), a `reproduction_case_kind` and a `logs_populated` flag. A filed report begins in `FILED`, is assigned a monotonic one-based `report_sequence_number` per corridor in `created_at` order, resolves its `incident_tier` — `TIER_1`, `TIER_2` or `TIER_3`, as banded by the corridor's weather profile and the applicable criteria table — and recomputes its `regulatory_notice_required` flag. That flag is true when the `incident_tier` is `TIER_1` or `TIER_2`, or when the `severity` is `CRITICAL`; it in turn drives `review_days_allowed`, which is 3 business days when regulatory notice is required and 5 business days otherwise. Filing also recomputes the owning carrier's `recordable_incidents_last_epoch`.

Investigating a filed report opens the corridor's exposure. The transition moves the report's `report_status` to `INVESTIGATING` and recomputes the corridor's `has_blocking_incident`, which is what turns a `CRITICAL` report into a blocking one. The corridor's `posture` recomputes, and a critical report on an `ACTIVE` corridor forces it to `LIMITED`; a third critical report inside the review window forces `SUSPENDED`. That suspension is the automatic corridor-escalation path described in §3.1: a corridor that comes to `SUSPENDED` has its run plans in `DRAFT`, `READINESS_PENDING` or `DISPATCH_READY` aborted, its referenced state authorities flagged `filing_required_next` true, and a `LIMITED` corridor's route to the same place requires `has_blocking_incident` to be true.

Recording corrective action moves the report to `CORRECTIVE_ACTION`, and it will not proceed without at least one corrective action on file: a `corrective_action_count` below 1 is refused with `ERR_CORRECTIVE_ACTION_REQUIRED` (409). That same transaction recomputes `regulatory_notice_required` from the tier and recomputes `review_days_allowed` to 3 when notice is required and 5 when it is not, then recomputes the corridor's `has_blocking_incident` — which can lift a `SUSPENDED` corridor back to `LIMITED`. One further rule guards the corrective-action step: the reproduction case for a compensatory formula run must be labelled a reproduction, not a replication, and a case presented as a replication rather than a reproduction is refused with `ERR_REPRO_NOT_REPLICATION` (422).

Closing is the exit that restores corridor capacity. A `ComplianceOfficer` closes a report from `CORRECTIVE_ACTION`, again requiring `corrective_action_count` of at least 1 and again refusing with `ERR_CORRECTIVE_ACTION_REQUIRED` (409). Closing moves the report to `CLOSED`, recomputes the corridor's `has_blocking_incident` — which is exactly how a `LIMITED` corridor recovers to `ACTIVE` — and recomputes the owning carrier's `recordable_incidents_last_epoch` and `incident_band`, whose own consequences for carrier standing are set out in §3.6.

Two routes move a report out of `INVESTIGATING` without closing it. Submitting a report to the state authority requires `regulatory_notice_required` to be true — otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) — and requires the ledger clock to fall within `review_days_allowed` business days of the filing epoch, measured inclusively, or the request is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_WINDOW_LAPSED` (409). A successful submission moves the report to `SUBMITTED`, recomputes the corridor's `has_blocking_incident`, and recomputes the carrier's `recordable_incidents_last_epoch` and `incident_band`; a `LIMITED` corridor whose last blocking report has just been submitted recovers to `ACTIVE`, while an `ACTIVE` corridor that gains a blocking submission moves down to `LIMITED`. The reopen route is available from `CORRECTIVE_ACTION` and from `SUBMITTED` — a report in any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) — and both variants demand the incident still be within `review_days_allowed` of its filing epoch, refusing with `ERR_REVIEW_WINDOW_LAPSED` (409) otherwise. Reopening returns the report to `INVESTIGATING` and recomputes the corridor's blocking flag and posture — a reopen from `CORRECTIVE_ACTION` can push a `LIMITED` corridor to `SUSPENDED`, and a reopen from `SUBMITTED` can push an `ACTIVE` corridor down to `LIMITED`.
### 3.6. Carrier Standing, Safety Bands and the Backup Chain

A carrier partnership is onboarded by a `ComplianceOfficer` with a `carrier_name` and a `dot_number`, the USDOT number that acts as the partnership's natural key and must be unique among carriers; it may also name a `backup_carrier_id`, a self-referential pointer to the fallback carrier consulted when the primary cannot dispatch. Onboarding leaves the carrier in `ONBOARDING` with a `safety_score` of 500, a `safety_tier` of `MEDIUM` and a `committed_discount_cents` of 15. Activation moves the carrier to `ACTIVE`, and it requires two things: at least one safety case anywhere on the platform must be in `FILED` status, and the `dot_number` must still be unique. Activation recomputes the carrier's safety score from its frozen incident count as the larger of 900 minus 25 times `previous_epoch_incidents` and 0, and then recomputes `safety_tier` and `committed_discount_cents` from that score.

Carrier safety is banded in two places at once, and the two bandings must not be confused. The carrier's own `safety_score` runs from 0 to 1000, starting at 900 and debited 25 points for each recordable incident counted in `previous_epoch_incidents`, never falling below 0. Its `safety_tier` reads `HIGH` at or above 800, `MEDIUM` from 500 through 799, and `LOW` below 500 — the same three bands that select `committed_discount_cents` at 0, 15 and 40 respectively, exactly as §3.2 used them. Separately, the carrier's `incident_band` reads `CLEAN`, `WATCH` or `SUSPENDED` and is driven by the current epoch's recordable count: `SUSPENDED` once `recordable_incidents_last_epoch` reaches 5, `WATCH` once it reaches 2, and `CLEAN` below 2. A carrier whose incident band recomputes to `SUSPENDED` while it is `ACTIVE` moves automatically to `SUSPENDED` — no client action is required — and that suspension sets `committed_discount_cents` to 40, aborts every run plan on the carrier sitting in `DRAFT` or `READINESS_PENDING`, and expires every monitoring assignment on those runs that was still `SCHEDULED`.

Reinstatement is the counter-move and it is epoch-driven. Once a ledger rollover has frozen the carrier's `previous_epoch_incidents` at a value below 5, a `ComplianceOfficer` may reinstate the carrier to `ACTIVE`, which recomputes `safety_score` from that frozen count, recomputes `incident_band`, and recomputes `committed_discount_cents` from the resulting tier. Offboarding is the terminal carrier state: a carrier with no run plan in `READINESS_PENDING`, `DISPATCH_READY` or `IN_TRANSIT` may be offboarded to `OFFBOARDED`, while a carrier still holding such a run is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_HAS_LIVE_RUN_PLANS` (409). Offboarding freezes the `consumed_slots` of every commitment the carrier dispatches against, and recomputes the `backup_carrier_score` of any carrier naming this one as its fallback down to 0.

That backup pointer deserves one explicit statement, because it is the only self-referential relationship in the model. `backup_carrier_id` names another `Carrier`, defaults to null, and is never compared to `id` for ownership purposes; the read-only `backup_carrier_score` mirrors the named carrier's `safety_score` and reads 0 whenever `backup_carrier_id` is null. Carriers that name a given carrier as their fallback are themselves listable, and the reverse read is how the platform audits the fallback chain.

### 3.7. Safety Cases, Inspection Programmes and State Authorities

Safety case documentation is the corridor's contract with the state that supervises it. A `SafetyCaseAuthor` opens a revision with a `corridor_id`, a client-supplied `safety_case_version`, a `hazard_count` and an optional `open_hazard_count` defaulting to 0. The supplied version must exceed the corridor's current highest safety case version, and a version that does not is refused with `ERR_INVALID_VERSION` (422); the revision opens in `DRAFT` with `filed_date` null. Submitting the revision moves its `case_status` to `SUBMITTED`. Filing is a `RegulatoryLiaison` action that supplies the `filed_date`, moves the `case_status` to `FILED`, recomputes its `residual_risk_score`, supersedes any earlier `FILED` case on the same corridor, and recomputes the corridor's `case_status_summary`. Two conditions guard filing: `hazard_count` must be at least 1, and `open_hazard_count` must be 0 before the case can be filed. The read-only `residual_risk_score` is the floor, to two decimal places, of one plus `hazard_count`, multiplied by one plus the floor, to three decimal places, of the corridor's `highway_segment_miles` divided by 100 — evaluated at filing time.

Each corridor's safety case reading is summarised in `case_status_summary`, whose values are `NOT_FILED`, `CURRENT`, `STALE` and `SUPERSEDED`. It reads `NOT_FILED` when the corridor has no filed case at all, and `STALE` when the filed case's `filed_date` is strictly more than the supervising state's `engagement_window_days` before the ledger clock. That staleness reading is also what the launch gate consults in its safety-case step: a corridor whose filed case has aged past the engagement window — measured inclusively, so exactly `engagement_window_days` is still valid while one day more fails — is refused with `ERR_SAFETY_CASE_STALE` (409), as is a corridor with no filed case at all. A corridor that is suspended has its safety case reading forced to `STALE` regardless of the underlying dates, which is the third face of the suspension cascade described in §3.1.

State authorities are the per-state regulatory surface. A `RegulatoryLiaison` registers one with a `state_code`, an `authority_name`, an optional `autonomous_hours_allowed` defaulting to 24, an optional `requires_safety_case_filed` defaulting to true, and an optional `engagement_window_days` defaulting to 30. Registration zeroes `hours_used` and clears `filing_required_next` to false; the flag is set true by a corridor-suspension transition and cleared again by a reinstatement transition. When `requires_safety_case_filed` is true, a corridor touching that state cannot pass launch readiness without a `FILED` safety case — the rule §3.1 evaluates. The authority's `hours_used` and `remaining_autonomous_hours` are the per-epoch budget described in §2.5.

Autonomous hardware inspection is a parallel programme to standard vehicle inspection, and it runs on the fleet unit. A `FleetMaintenanceTech` opens a `MaintenanceProgram` with a `program_name`, the `fleet_unit_id` it inspects, the `state_code` where the unit is registered, an `inspection_interval_days`, an optional `inspection_odometer_miles` defaulting to 0, and an `inspection_anchor_at`. The interval has an unusual contract: 0 selects the default programme length of 3 days, the values 1 through 365 are accepted verbatim, and every value below 1 other than 0, or above 365, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_INTERVAL` (422). The programme opens in `DRAFT`, and its read-only `standard_inspection_due_date` computes as exactly `inspection_interval_days` after the scheduled inspection anchor, rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC datetime at midnight. Its `inspection_programs_status` reads `PENDING`, `COMPLIANT` or `OVERDUE`, with `UNFILLED` reserved for a unit that has no programme at all; the `OVERDUE` reading is taken when the anchor plus the interval falls strictly before the ledger clock.

Scheduling a draft programme moves it to `SCHEDULED` and requires the resolved interval to be 0 or in 1 through 365 and the computed due date to parse as a valid date on or after the ledger clock; scheduling recomputes `inspection_programs_status` to `PENDING` and recomputes the owning carrier's `safety_score` when the unit's overdue status clears. Recording an inspection moves the programme's `program_status` to `INSPECTED`, requires `inspection_odometer_miles` of at least 0 and requires the ledger clock to be on or after the scheduled anchor, stamps `last_inspection_at` from the ledger clock, recomputes `inspection_programs_status` to `COMPLIANT`, and — this is the bubble-up that matters — moves any corridor whose gate was being held down by this unit's overdue status from `LIMITED` back to `ACTIVE`. Rescheduling an inspected programme returns it to `SCHEDULED`, rolls `standard_inspection_due_date` forward by the interval, retains the previous `last_inspection_at` in the inspection history, and recomputes the status reading.

Voiding and closing are the two exits. A `FleetMaintenanceTech` may void a scheduled programme only when its `inspection_programs_status` is not `COMPLIANT` for the current epoch; a compliant programme is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Voiding recomputes the status to `UNFILLED`, moves the programme's `program_status` to `VOIDED`, and moves every corridor carrying that unit's programme from `ACTIVE` to `LIMITED` as a direct cross-entity consequence — which is the same restricted corridor that later recovers under the recording-inspection bubble-up above. A `ComplianceOfficer` closes an inspected programme, moving its `program_status` to the terminal `CLOSED` reading and recomputing `inspection_programs_status` to `UNFILLED` because the programme is closed.
### 3.8. The Driver Workforce Transition and Terminal Staffing

The humans who used to drive the highway segment are the platform's most carefully tracked operational asset, because they are being redeployed rather than replaced. A `DriverOutreachCoordinator` registers a `DriverProspect` with a `prospect_name`, a `home_terminal_id` naming the hub they belong to, and a `pathway`. The pathway is one of `FIRST_LAST_MILE`, `REMOTE_SUPPORT`, `FLEET_TECHNICIAN`, `TRANSFER_HUB` or `RIDE_ALONG_EVALUATOR`, and it describes which of the new roles the prospect is preparing for. A prospect starts with `hours_worked` of 0, `enrollments_on_file` of 0 and a `safety_reliability_score` of 1000. The `home_terminal_id` must resolve to an existing terminal, or registration stops with `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` (422).

Enrolling the prospect is a `DriverTransition`, and it is created against a `prospect_id` and a `terminal_id` — the hub the transition is staffing — with its own `pathway` declaration, an inclusive `transition_start_epoch_day`, a `transition_days_count` of at least 1, and an optional `hours_recorded` defaulting to 0. A newly enrolled transition sits in `ENROLLED`, computes its read-only `transition_end_epoch_day` as the start day plus the day count minus one, adds `hours_recorded` onto the prospect's `hours_worked`, and recomputes the terminal's `staffing_status`. That derived status reads `STAFFED` or `UNDERSTAFFED` and is reconciled at finalization time against the number of transition enrolments staffing the hub relative to the managed fleet unit count staged there. The prospect's `enrollments_on_file` counts the transitions that have not been cancelled or rejected, so an enrolment that ends early stops counting toward the prospect's roster size while a live one continues to.

Attaching a supervised evaluation run is the step that turns an enrolment into a demonstrably qualified one. A ride-along action on a transition takes a `run_plan_id` and creates a `RunPlanHauler` evaluation plan attached to the transition, moving its `transition_status` to `RIDE_ALONG_VERIFIED` and recomputing the owning commitment's `verified_ride_along_count`. The `RunPlanHauler` record is the join between a run plan and the transition supplying its human labour: it names the `run_plan_id`, the `driver_transition_id` that supplies the unit, and a `hauler_role` of `FIRST_MILE_DRIVER`, `LAST_MILE_DRIVER` or `YARD_SHUTTLE` — of which the ride-along action declares no role of its own and always records `FIRST_MILE_DRIVER` — and carries a `hauler_status` that begins at `TRUCK_HOLDER_BLENDED` and proceeds to `HAULER_ON_SITE` and finally to either `RECEIVED_ON_SITE` or `OFF_TARIFF`; no operation declared in §4 advances that status past its starting reading.

Credential lapse is the failure mode this whole apparatus exists to catch. A `CorridorOperationsManager` records a credential-lapse event on a transition, which moves the transition to `CREDENTIAL_LAPSED`, sets its `credential_event_count` to 1, and debits exactly 50 points from the owning prospect's `safety_reliability_score`, floored at 0. The penalty is capped at one application per transition row, and this is a deliberate design point rather than an accident: a transition whose `credential_event_count` somehow exceeds 1 still debits exactly 50 once, and the excess is not carried forward — an excess event is simply not a figure included in the count. The lapse also triggers the driver-risk recomputation described below. Cancelling an enrolment is the other terminal route: a `DriverOutreachCoordinator` cancels a transition, moving it to `CANCELLED`, and the optional `hours_recorded` supplied with the cancellation releases those hours, so the prospect's `hours_worked` recomputes downward by the released amount. `REJECTED` is the remaining terminal reading on a transition, reached when an enrolment is refused.

Driver risk is a corridor-scoped aggregate that the transition state feeds. The platform gathers the run plans on a corridor that share one `hauler_team_id` and are in `READINESS_PENDING` or `IN_TRANSIT` — `DRAFT` and `DISPATCH_READY` runs are excluded because they have not yet been tendered — and takes the maximum `safety_score` across that group, treating an empty group as a maximum of 0. The risk reading is then the larger of two quantities and 0 — the group maximum multiplied by the driver weight of 0.5, and that same product reduced by 50 for each credential event on the transition row — with the result rounded half up to two decimal places. A fleet-scoped variant of the same reading maximises `safety_score` across every non-terminal run plan instead of just those on the named corridor, so a high-scoring run elsewhere in the fleet raises the fleet figure but leaves the corridor figure untouched.

### 3.9. Transfer Hubs, Apron Windows and Remote Support Monitoring

The transfer hub is where the autonomous segment begins and ends, and its capacity is measured in apron units. A `TerminalManager` requests a service window against a terminal by naming the `run_plan_id` it serves, a `service_type` of `HANDOFF_INBOUND`, `HANDOFF_OUTBOUND`, `YARD_TRANSFER` or `MAINTENANCE_STAGING`, an inclusive `window_start_minute` and an exclusive `window_end_minute`, and optionally a `handoff_duration_minutes`, a `buffer_minutes` and a `yield_to_transit` flag, all of which default to 0 or false. The window must satisfy `window_start_minute` strictly less than `window_end_minute`. A requested window sits in `REQUESTED` and computes its read-only `buffer_seconds`, which for a `HANDOFF_INBOUND` window is the floor of the window length plus 0.49 and for every other service type is the floor of the window length plus 0.51, rendered as whole seconds. The apron geometry itself — the half-open interval, the adjacency rule that touching windows do not occupy, and the 1.5-times multiplicative expansion used under `GEOFENCE_PLUS_BUFFER` — is fixed by §2.7 and is exactly what the approval step screens against.

Approving a requested window is a `TerminalManager` decision with four conditions. The referenced run must be `DISPATCH_READY` or `IN_TRANSIT`, or the approval is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409); the window's minute range must be well formed; the terminal's `reserved_apron_units` plus the new window's length with its buffer must not exceed the terminal's `apron_capacity`, or the approval is refused with `ERR_APRON_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409); and the occupation must not share a single minute with an already approved block on the same terminal, which is refused with `ERR_APRON_OVERLAP` (409). Approval moves the window's `window_status` to `APPROVED` and recomputes four readings on the terminal: `total_handoff_minutes`, `approved_window_count`, `transfer_turnaround_minutes` and `reserved_apron_units`. A window may also be displaced without anyone approving it — a `REQUESTED` window whose run has just become `DISPATCH_READY` and whose `yield_to_transit` flag is true is moved to `DISPLACED` in the same transaction, which recomputes the terminal's occupancy, count and turnover readings. A window that was approved and whose run then requeues reverts to `REQUESTED`, and one whose run is aborted or retired is `CANCELLED`; both recompute the same terminal readings downward, and a cancelled window must have been `APPROVED` to begin with or the cancellation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). When the human driver actually takes the load, a `TerminalManager` completes the handoff: the window may complete from `IN_SERVICE`, provided the referenced run is `IN_TRANSIT` or is `READINESS_PENDING` after a safe stop, and completion recomputes the terminal's `total_handoff_minutes` and `transfer_turnaround_minutes`. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are the two terminal readings on a window.

Remote support monitoring is the human oversight layer that rides alongside the autonomous system. A `RemoteSupportSupervisor` schedules a monitoring window by naming the `corridor_id` and the `run_plan_id` being monitored, an inclusive `monitoring_start_at` and an exclusive `monitoring_end_at` that must be strictly after the start, the `remote_operator_id` holding the window, and a `safety_support_operator_id` jointly staffed on it. Both operator identifiers name `DriverProspect` rows, and the same person may not fill both seats: a window whose two operators are the same person is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPERATOR` (422). The window also declares a `geofence_type` of `GEOFENCE_ONLY` or `GEOFENCE_PLUS_BUFFER` and an `intervention_protocol` of `MONITOR_ONLY`, `VOICE_ADVISORY`, `STANDBY_TAKEOVER` or `REMOTE_DISENGAGE`. A scheduled window begins with its `assignment_status` at `SCHEDULED`, `intervention_count` of 0 and `safe_stop_required` false, and its `closure_source` stays null until the window closes, at which point it reads `REPOSITIONED_ACKNOWLEDGED` on acknowledgement, `SAFE_STOP_EXPIRED` on a safe stop or `RELEASED` on release. The overlap screen described in §2.7 runs against the same operator's other active windows on the same corridor in either seat and in either direction, refusing with `ERR_MONITOR_OVERLAP` (409); it is evaluated both when a window is scheduled and when it is acknowledged.

A `RemoteSupportOperator` takes a scheduled window by acknowledging it, which moves it to `MONITORING` and sets `closure_source` to `REPOSITIONED_ACKNOWLEDGED`; the same operator logs interventions against the window, each of which increments `intervention_count` and refreshes `intervention_protocol`, and the same operator is the only role authorised to execute the safe-stop procedure described in §3.4. A `RemoteSupportSupervisor` releases a window, moving it to `RELEASED` with `closure_source` reading `RELEASED`; a window whose run is safe-stopped expires automatically to `EXPIRED`. The read-only `safe_stop_fee_cents` on the assignment is computed by the banded exposure arithmetic set out in §3.4, and a window that has never been involved in a stop carries 0. A run may not start its highway segment while one of its monitoring windows declares `STANDBY_TAKEOVER` and is still `SCHEDULED` or `MONITORING`, which is the third precondition §3.4 listed for entering `IN_TRANSIT`.
### 3.10. Container Booking, Multi-Day Holds and Intermodal Handoff

The container side of the platform is where a `BookingClerk` books the physical freight that rides a highway run and where a `TerminalOperator` moves it through the hub. A booking opens as a `ShippingInstruction` created against a `run_plan_id` — the highway run the booked containers will travel on — with the `container_unit_ids` to be attached. The instruction begins in `BOOKED` and each named unit is attached to it. A `ContainerUnit` carries its own identity and geometry: an `instruction_id` that is null while the container is unallocated, an ISO 6346 `container_number`, a `length_feet` of 20, 40 or 45, a `gross_weight_kg`, and a `verified_gross_mass_kg` defaulting to 0. Its read-only `unit_rate_cents` is the floor, to two decimal places, of a slot base of 20000.00 dollars multiplied by `length_feet` and divided by 40, and it is recomputed from `gross_weight_kg` whenever the container moves. Its `unit_status` walks the chain `PENDING → INGATED → LOADED → ON_VESSEL → DISCHARGED → OFFLOADED → GATED_OUT`, and each step along that chain increments the unit's `version` by 1.

Placing holds is the first instruction transition. A `BookingClerk` places holds on the named containers by supplying the `container_unit_ids` and the instruction's `expected_instruction_version`; the instruction must be in `BOOKED` or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409), and every named container must actually belong to this instruction. Placing holds increments the instruction's `version`, moves the `instruction_status` to `HOLD_PLACED`, moves each named container from `PENDING` to `INGATED` while incrementing that container's `version`, and recomputes the instruction's `booked_vgm_kg_sum`. That sum is the total `gross_weight_kg` across the attached containers whose status is `INGATED`, `LOADED`, `ON_VESSEL` or `DISCHARGED` — containers still `PENDING` and containers already `OFFLOADED` or `GATED_OUT` contribute nothing to it. Cancelling holds is the reverse move: it returns the instruction to `BOOKED`, moves each named container from `INGATED` back to `PENDING` while incrementing its `version`, and recomputes `booked_vgm_kg_sum` downward.

Verifying the mass is the step the code vetoes hardest. A `BookingClerk` verifies the mass on every held container by supplying `expected_instruction_version`; each unit's `verified_gross_mass_kg` must reach at least 100 kilograms, and a container below that is refused with `ERR_VGM_VERIFICATION_FAILED` (409). No container on the instruction may already be `OFFLOADED` when verification runs. Success moves the instruction to `VGM_VERIFIED` and recomputes three derived figures: `booked_vgm_kg_sum`, `booked_line_total_cents` and `vgm_override_fee_cents`. The line total is the per-row charge rounded per row and then summed, where each row is priced from its own per-line freight rate and the length tier of the highest attached container. The override fee is the ceiling, rounded to two decimal places, of an override base amount divided by `booked_vgm_kg_sum`, so a heavier instruction carries a smaller per-kilogram verification charge. The instruction also holds a `container_discount_cents`, a discount applied to carried line totals and keyed on the length tier of the highest attached container.

Completing carriage closes the instruction content-wise. A `ComplianceOfficer` completes an instruction from `VGM_VERIFIED` provided no container on it is in `OFFLOADED` or `PENDING`, and the transition moves the instruction to `CARRIED` and recomputes `booked_line_total_cents` from the carried units only. Rejection is the other instruction exit: a `ComplianceOfficer` rejects an instruction after a hold on one of its units has been declined, passing the `declined_container_unit_id`; an instruction whose holds are not in that condition is refused with `ERR_HOLD_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Rejection moves the instruction to `REJECTED`, returns each container in `INGATED` or `LOADED` to `PENDING` with its `version` incremented, and recomputes `booked_vgm_kg_sum` to 0.

The container moves themselves each carry their own preconditions and cascades. Gating a container in moves it to `INGATED`, increments its `version`, and recomputes `unit_rate_cents`, `booked_vgm_kg_sum` and `booked_line_total_cents`. Loading a container requires the owning instruction to be in `HOLD_PLACED` or `VGM_VERIFIED` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) — moves the unit to `LOADED`, increments its `version`, recomputes the owning line's totals, and recomputes the owning carrier's `booking_count`. A loaded container reaches `ON_VESSEL` automatically once its instruction is `VGM_VERIFIED` and the vessel departs. Discharging moves a container from `ON_VESSEL` to `DISCHARGED`, increments its `version`, recomputes `booked_line_total_cents` and `container_discount_cents` on the line, and recomputes the owning carrier's `booking_rebate_cents` and `booking_count`. Offloading moves a container from `DISCHARGED` to `OFFLOADED`, requires the owning instruction not to be `CARRIED` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, 409), increments the `version`, recomputes `booked_vgm_kg_sum` downward, and recomputes the carrier's `refund_from_carrier_cents` and `booking_rebate_cents`. An offloaded container can be loaded again — the `OFFLOADED` to `LOADED` move recomputes `booked_vgm_kg_sum` upward and recomputes `refund_from_carrier_cents` — and a discharged container can be gated out, which moves it to its terminal `GATED_OUT` reading, increments the `version`, recomputes `booked_vgm_kg_sum` downward and recomputes the carrier's `booking_count`.

Multi-day holds are the coverage-date mechanism for terminal and regulatory reservations, and they are the one place the platform exposes an onward chain. A `BookingClerk` requests a hold with an inclusive `hold_start_epoch_day` and an exclusive `hold_end_epoch_day`, a `single_day_hold` flag and a `hold_type` of `WINDOW_BLOCK`, `MAINTENANCE` or `REGULATORY_INSPECTION`. The hold is a half-open interval, and the window must run forward: a hold whose end does not strictly follow its start is refused with `ERR_HOLD_WINDOW_INVERTED` (422). The `coverage_date` is system-filled rather than client-supplied in the ordinary case — when the client sends a null or blank `coverage_date` the server fills it from the chain's start date and the local consecutive-run count — and a chain day that is requested without a coverage date, where the request does not carry every day of the chain, is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_DATE_REQUIRED` (422). A hold declaring `single_day_hold` true must be booked one day at a time, and a multi-day request against such a row is refused with `ERR_MULTI_DAY_NOT_ALLOWED` (422). Requesting a hold leaves it in `REQUESTED` and assigns its `sequence_in_chain`, the one-based position it occupies inside its chain in `created_at` order. Bulk insertion takes the `hold_ids` of a chain and moves each from `REQUESTED` to `INSERTED`, recomputing `sequence_in_chain` across the whole chain. Removing a hold moves it to `REMOVED`, recomputes `sequence_in_chain`, and carries the `coverage_date` forward to the next chain day; declining a hold moves it to `DECLINED`, which is terminal alongside `REMOVED`.

### 3.11. Fleet Units, Operational Hours and Carrier Settlement

Fleet units are the physical autonomous assets, and their hour accounting is the platform's maintenance early-warning system. A `FleetMaintenanceTech` registers a unit with a `unit_id` — an uppercase fleet prefix and number such as AUR-2214, unique across the fleet — a `fleet_name`, a `home_terminal_id`, the `curated_at` moment it entered the curated roster, an `inspection_anchor_at` from which the standard inspection cadence is measured, a `weather_clearance_hours` allowance of at least 1, and an `is_watch_squad` flag defaulting to false. Registration leaves the unit `ACTIVE` with `maint_charges` of 0 and `remain_hours` equal to `weather_clearance_hours`. Three derived readings hang off the unit. `hours_posted_total` accumulates the autonomous hours posted against it in the current clearance cycle. `remain_hours` is `weather_clearance_hours` minus `hours_posted_total`, floored at 0. The route-driven reading `route_driven_hours_cents` is the floor, to two decimal places, of the sum of a per-day auto-hours quota, a mileage-hours quota and a route-load-hours quota multiplied by 100, with one twist: the route-load term is doubled for a watch-squad unit before the sum is taken.

The unit's maintenance arithmetic is deliberately pinned to an evaluation order, because the two derived money figures depend on each other. Evaluated first, `maint_charges` is the floor, to two decimal places, of `hours_posted_total` converted to decimal and multiplied by the maintenance unit rate. Evaluated second, `maint_projection_cents` is the floor, to two decimal places, of `maint_charges` converted to decimal and multiplied by a weather-condition discount that is 1.08 for a corridor whose weather profile is `HIGH_WIND` or `LOW_VISIBILITY` and 1.0 otherwise. Posting operational hours against a unit supplies an `hours_logged` figure and the unit's `expected_fleet_unit_version`; the request must match the unit's current `version` or it is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412, and on success the unit increments its `version`, adds `hours_logged` to `hours_posted_total`, and recomputes `maint_projection_cents`, `maint_charges` and `remain_hours`. Posting route-driven hours names a `corridor_id` and recomputes `route_driven_hours_cents` from that corridor's mileage together with `maint_projection_cents`, `maint_charges` and `remain_hours`, recording the double-shift reading for a watch-squad unit. Clearing the watch-squad flag sets `is_watch_squad` false and recomputes `remain_hours`. A unit's `unit_status` runs `ACTIVE → MAINTENANCE_HOLD → RETIRED`.

Chassis staging is the run plan's trailer-holding arrangement. A `CarrierDispatcher` attaches a `RunPlanChassis` to a run by supplying a `chassis_code` and a `coverage_days` of at least 1; the record's `chassis_status` begins at `HELD`, and its read-only `holding_rate_cents` is the larger of a minimum chassis rate and the run's `per_mile_rate_cents`, multiplied by `coverage_days`. That figure cascades into the owning run plan's `run_window_rate_cents`, which reads as the larger of the minimum chassis rate and `per_mile_rate_cents`, multiplied by the coverage days, so the run-level reading and the chassis-level reading are the same quantity viewed from two records. A re-price requires the chassis record's holding rate to be recomputed, which in turn recomputes the owning run's `run_window_rate_cents`; the chassis then closes at `RELEASED`.

Carrier invoicing is the last money workflow, and it is where the provisional quote and the settled rate finally separate. The `carrier_rate_reconciliation` reading is built on a band discount taken from the carrier's `safety_tier`: a `HIGH` carrier takes a discount of 0, a `MEDIUM` carrier 15 and a `LOW` carrier 40. The provisional quote is `posted_per_mile_cents` minus that discount, rounded half up to two decimal places, and it is the binding figure the dispatcher sees at tendering. The settled net rate adds the platform commission on top of the provisional quote — the commission being 125 basis points, i.e. the provisional quote multiplied by 125 and divided by 10000 — and rounds half up to two decimal places. Because the commission is strictly positive, the settled reading is strictly larger than the provisional quote for any positive quote. `rate_settlement_cents` on the carrier is the settled figure, and it recomputes at every ledger rollover. The two readings are both correct and they differ for identical inputs; the platform surfaces each in its own context rather than picking one.

Run-plan exposure is the carrier-liability attribution that ties the whole pricing family together. `applicable_cents` is `committed_rate_cents` minus `committed_discount_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `late_fee_cents` applies a late rate of 0.0105 to `committed_rate_cents` multiplied by a `late_count`, rounded half up to two decimals, where `late_count` is the number of days the run's `window_start_epoch_day` trails the current ledger epoch day, floored at 0. `weather_fee_cents` multiplies the corridor's `weather_risk_score` by a weather attribution rate of 8.037 cents. The three terms sum to the run's exposure, and the rounding convention is the crux: when the fees are attributed line by line for carrier invoicing each term is rounded to two decimals before summing, whereas the rolled-up attribution total sums the unrounded terms and rounds once. On the same inputs the two readings differ by exactly one cent — terms of 310, 10.2375 and 442.035 give 762.28 line by line but 762.27 aggregated — and both are legitimate readings of the same arithmetic rather than an error in either.

One more rate belongs to the pricing family and is the only forward-looking one among them. `scenario_rate_cents` is the worst-case planning figure built from a one-month coverage period. Take the larger of `per_mile_rate_cents` and the commitment's `shipper_floor_per_mile_cents` as the monthly base, and convert it to a decimal and round half up to two decimal places. Separately, multiply the corridor's `weather_risk_score` by a weather adjustment of 8 cents and round that effort figure half up to two decimal places. The scenario rate is the monthly base plus the weather effort, rounded half up to two decimal places once more. It is triggered at run-plan creation and again on every weather-profile or rollout-effort change on the corridor, and because it is a projection it excludes no terminal run plan from its inputs. A near-variant of the same reading — the one-month-only projection — drops the weather effort term entirely and returns just the rounded one-month base, which is what the pricing engine surfaces when it is asked for a near-term figure rather than a worst case.
### 3.12. Regulatory Engagement, Security Records and Agreement Codes

State-by-state regulation is a running negotiation, and the platform models it as an engagement ledger rather than a one-off filing. A `RegulatoryLiaison` opens a `RegulatoryEngagement` against a `corridor_id` and a `state_authority_id` with an `engagement_type` of `SAFETY_BRIEFING`, `OPERATIONAL_NOTICE`, `INCIDENT_DISCLOSURE` or `HOURS_ALLOCATION_REQUEST`, an `autonomous_hours_requested` figure, an optional `autonomous_hours_logged` defaulting to 0, and the `engaged_at` moment of the engagement. The engagement opens in `DRAFT`, and both `state_status` and `hours_charge_allowed` recompute at once. The `state_status` reads `FORMAL`, `INFORMAL` or `EXEMPT`: it is `FORMAL` for a `SAFETY_BRIEFING` or an `INCIDENT_DISCLOSURE`, `INFORMAL` for an `OPERATIONAL_NOTICE` when the corridor's weather profile is `CLEAR_DEFAULT`, and `EXEMPT` in every other case. The charge permission is narrower still: `hours_charge_allowed` is true only when the engagement's `state_status` is `FORMAL` or `INFORMAL` **and** an `ACCEPTED` prior engagement already exists in the same epoch.

Two temporal rules guard the opening of an engagement. Its `engaged_at` must fall inside the current epoch's closed-open window — from `epoch_start_at` inclusive to `epoch_end_at` exclusive — and an engagement outside that window is refused with `ERR_OUTSIDE_EPOCH_WINDOW` (422). The `autonomous_hours_requested` must not exceed the state authority's `remaining_autonomous_hours` for the epoch, and a request that overshoots is refused with `ERR_HOURS_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (409). Opening an engagement recomputes the owning authority's `remaining_autonomous_hours`.

Submitting an engagement moves its `engagement_status` to `SUBMITTED` and debits the authority's hour budget — `hours_used` accumulates the engagement's `autonomous_hours_logged`, and `remaining_autonomous_hours` recomputes. Submission is also where the charge-permission rule becomes a hard gate: an engagement whose `state_status` is not `FORMAL` or `INFORMAL`, or which has no accepted prior engagement in the same epoch, is refused with `ERR_CHARGE_NOT_ALLOWED` (422). The budget arithmetic itself has two readings. The cumulative reading reports the running whole-hour balance after every submitted engagement in the epoch, floored at zero, so a negative running total reads as 0 rather than as a deficit; the immediate reading adjudicates a single submission and charges only that engagement, ignoring earlier submissions in the same epoch that are still in `DRAFT`. An authority's `hours_used` counts only engagements whose `state_status` is `SUBMITTED` within the current epoch window, so `DRAFT` and `REJECTED` rows never debit and an `ACCEPTED` row counts exactly once however many later submissions follow it.

Accepting an engagement records that the state agreed, moves it to `ACCEPTED`, and recomputes `hours_charge_allowed` on later engagements in the same epoch — which is precisely how the charge-permission precondition above is satisfied for the next submission. Rejecting an engagement moves it to `REJECTED` and recomputes the owning authority's `hours_used` downward, releasing the hours it had debited; `ACCEPTED` and `REJECTED` are the two terminal readings on an engagement. The authority's `filing_required_next` flag is driven separately by corridor suspension and reinstatement, as §3.1 and §3.7 described, and is not a client-settable field.

International road transport security is a parallel compliance surface with its own status clamping. A `ComplianceOfficer` creates a `SecurityRecord` with a `security_code`, an `origin_terminal_id`, an optional `destination_terminal_id`, a `security_classification` of `NORMAL`, `SENSITIVE` or `HIGH_CONSEQUENCE`, a `clearance_required` flag, a `high_consequence_clearance` flag, a `convention_status` and a `consignor_code`. The record begins in `PENDING`, and its `record_status` later walks `PENDING → ACTIVE → REPORTING → CLOSED`. The `convention_status` field is the one place the record applies a clamp rather than a free assignment: it is clamped to `NORMAL` while the record is in transit, and clamped to `HIGH_CONSEQUENCE` once the high-consequence clearance lands. Rolling the consignor copies the record's own `security_code` into the `consignor_code` field and recomputes `record_status`. Routing the record assigns its `destination_terminal_id` from the supplied terminal and requires the client's `expected_security_record_version` to match the record's current `version` — a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412 — then increments the `version` and recomputes `record_status`. Clamping the record sets `convention_status` to `HIGH_CONSEQUENCE` and recomputes `record_status`. Rolling the record onto the outbound movement assigns the supplied `outbound_lane_code` — a field that is null until the record is routed — and recomputes `record_status`. Marking the record as reporting accepts a new `convention_status` reading and recomputes `record_status`.

The last regulatory surface is the hours-of-service agreement code, owned by an `ExchangeParticipant`. An agreement is created from a `code_value` and a `display_value`; it begins in `ACTIVE`. Executing the agreement supplies a fresh `code_value`, sanitizes it, and moves the code to its terminal `EXECUTED` reading. The agreement carries two read-only readings surfaced in the execution response: a `code_status` that reads `ACTIVE` or `EXECUTED`, and an `exec_sequence` sequence number. It also carries an `order_sentinel` that is fixed at the exact string `AA` for every execution — this is a deterministic ranking token and it is ranked by that literal string, never by a random identifier — and the `display_value` is kept verbatim even when the code value itself is sanitized.

### 3.13. Arbitration Clause Variants and the Reproduction Doctrine

Arbitration clause variants are the one place the platform manipulates a string structurally, and the derivation is worth stating exactly. A `ComplianceOfficer` publishes a variant from a `clause_stem` and a `wildcard` flag. The read-only `variant_code` is derived rather than supplied: when `wildcard` is true, the code is the clause stem with its wildcard segment — the first digit-to-digit segment in the stem — removed, and when `wildcard` is false the code is the clause stem verbatim. The variant begins in `PENDING`. Electing the wildcard reading sets `wildcard` true, recomputes `variant_status` to `ELECTED`, and recomputes `variant_status_summary`. Declining the variant records a `decline_reason`, moves `variant_status` to `DECLINED` and recomputes `variant_status_summary`. The variant carries two distinct status fields that must not be conflated: the raw `variant_status` reads `PENDING`, `ELECTED` or `DECLINED`, while `variant_status_summary` — the reading used in the clause register — reads `NOT_ELECTED`, `ELECTED` or `DECLINED_PENDING`.

A second doctrine runs across the domain rather than living in one workflow, and it is worth isolating because two separate rules enforce it. Incident investigation distinguishes a reproduction from a replication of a failure case: a compensatory formula run's case must be labelled a reproduction, and a corrective action that claims replication rather than reproduction of the incident case is refused with `ERR_REPRO_NOT_REPLICATION` (422). The same discipline appears in the readiness gate, where a coordinated-dispatch claim made with empty fleet logs is refused with `ERR_MISSING_LOGS` (422). Both rules exist because the platform's evidence standards treat a re-derivation of a case and a rerun of the original as different claims, and neither may be substituted for the other.

One further cross-cutting convention applies to every record in the domain. Instants are always ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits; day-count windows always count from the ledger epoch start rather than from any client-supplied anchor; and every window expressed as a range is half-open at the end and closed at the start unless the text says otherwise, so an inclusive start day and an exclusive end day define a range that contains its first day and excludes its last.

The platform also exposes three test-driving capabilities that exist purely to exercise a plan under test. Each takes a `plan_code` naming the plan being exercised and a `lift_minutes` figure describing the routing lift to apply: one lifts a terminal's vehicle-routing plan for a named `terminal_id`, and one lifts a corridor's routing plan for a named `corridor_id`. The third exercises ring maintenance on a `unit_id` for a named `ring_code` and takes no lift figure at all. None of the three persists a business field — they apply their effect and return without mutating any entity — and they are reachable only by the compliance and incident-review roles named in §4.
