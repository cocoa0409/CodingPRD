# Competitive Price Intelligence API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Retailers who compete on shelf price carry a structural disadvantage: the competitor's price is a fact about the world, while the retailer's own price is a decision that has to be defended to merchants, to brands, to store operations and to the shopper standing at the register. This document specifies the Competitive Price Intelligence API, the service that closes that loop. It owns the competitor set for each market, the raw price observations collected online and by in-store audit, the product-to-competitor matches that make those observations comparable, the index reporting that scores a market against its declared strategic positioning, the automated repricing rules that move own prices inside margin and brand constraints, and the price-match programme that lets a store honour a competitor's price at the register without leaking margin.

Three properties drive the design. First, **nothing is repriced on unverified data**: a competitor price only becomes a benchmark after a human-confirmed match, a fresh in-stock observation, and a confidence score above an explicit floor. Second, **every price move is reversible and attributable**: change orders are authored, decided and executed by different hands, override approvals are written to an append-only ledger, and honoured claims can be reversed with every side effect compensated. Third, **the market can be taken off the field**: when a published index report shows the market has drifted too far from its strategic target, the market's posture escalates and the service progressively refuses price-change and price-match writes rather than allowing operators to compound the error.

The service is mounted under `/pricing`. All monetary amounts are integer minor currency units, all index and confidence figures are integer basis points, and all temporal reasoning is performed against a server-side logical clock rather than wall-clock time so that TTL, claim-window, frequency-limit and per-day-ledger behaviour is deterministically testable.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the rules that hold for every endpoint in the service, regardless of which business process is being driven.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. A request that omits either header, presents either as empty — a value consisting only of whitespace counts as empty — or names a role the service does not recognise, role names being matched exactly and case-sensitively, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing takes place. Once the caller is authenticated, the acting role must appear in the authorised-role list for the requested endpoint as given in §4; otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403).

Seven roles exist, and their spellings are exact:

- `PlatformAdmin` — sets up reference data: markets, competitor sets, catalog products and stores. A `PlatformAdmin` never authors, approves or executes a price change.
- `PricingAnalyst` — builds the intelligence layer: proposes product matches, revises match evidence, computes index reports and drafts change orders. A `PricingAnalyst` cannot approve any order, including its own.
- `PricingManager` — holds pricing authority: confirms matches, publishes index reports, administers repricing rules, and approves and executes change orders.
- `ComplianceOfficer` — an independent control function: suspends or retires competitors, administers price-match policy, adjudicates claim verification and reverses honoured claims. A `ComplianceOfficer` holds no repricing authority.
- `DataCollector` — the online scraper or in-store price auditor. Submits and voids raw price observations only, with no matching, indexing or repricing authority.
- `StoreAssociate` — the front-line register user of one store. Files price-match claims and honours those within its own override-authority tier.
- `StoreManager` — the manager of one store. Adds `MANAGER`-tier honour authority, claim verification, claim reversal and shelf-label batch scheduling on top of everything a `StoreAssociate` may do.

Identity semantics differ between the operator roles and the store roles, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `PricingAnalyst`, `PricingManager`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `DataCollector`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `authored_by_user_id` or `decided_by_user_id` on a price change order, or as `approved_by_user_id` on an override ledger row — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `StoreAssociate` or `StoreManager`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Store`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Store` compare that resource's store-referencing field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `StoreAssociate` or `StoreManager` acting on a price-match claim, on a shelf-label batch, or on a store itself must present an `X-User-ID` equal to the `store_id` recorded on the targeted claim or batch (or the `id` of the targeted store); the test is an exact string comparison of the header value exactly as supplied, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STORE_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` and continues with exactly twenty further characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`. It alone is read from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.5, so moving the logical clock does not move it.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` that is not an integer in the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or an `offset` that is not an integer of at least 0, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 422). Each is read from the query string as a decimal integer with surrounding whitespace trimmed, and an empty or non-numeric value is rejected as malformed. The two parameters are examined in two phases — integer form for `limit` and then for `offset`, and only afterwards the range of `limit` followed by the range of `offset` — so a `limit` of 500 supplied alongside an `offset` of `abc` surfaces the malformed `offset`.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending; because the logical clock only moves when it is explicitly set, rows routinely share an identical `created_at`, and every such tie is broken by ascending creation sequence — the order in which the rows were written. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective starting position), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page, that is when `offset` plus the number of rows actually returned is still less than `total_count`). Filter parameters on list endpoints are conjunctive: every supplied filter must match. A filter is an exact comparison of the supplied string against the string form of the row's stored field, a filter value that matches nothing yields an empty page rather than an error, and query parameters an endpoint does not declare are ignored.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Each persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Client-supplied identifiers are never honoured as `id` values. Each resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written; that instant is read from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from wall-clock time, so rows written without moving the clock in between carry the same `created_at`. In every serialised resource the common fields `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version` are emitted after the resource-specific fields.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits and likewise stamped from the logical clock — it equals `created_at` exactly until the row is first mutated — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource — including mutations caused indirectly by a cascade rather than by a request that names the resource directly. This applies to `Market`, `Competitor`, `Product`, `Store`, `CompetitorProductLink`, `PriceObservation`, `PriceIndexReport`, `RepricingRule`, `PriceChangeOrder`, `PriceMatchPolicy`, `PriceMatchClaim` and `LabelPrintBatch`.

The override ledger row is the one exception. An `OverrideLog` is an append-only audit record: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version`, because it is never modified after it is written — a correction is expressed by appending a second, compensating row rather than by editing the first.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The service maintains a logical current UTC instant rather than reading wall-clock time. On every fresh service start this clock is initialised to the mandatory default `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. An operator of any of the seven roles may move it by submitting a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting capability described in §4; the response echoes the new instant as `{ "current_utc": ... }` in the `data` block of a standard `object` envelope, rendered in the same ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits and a trailing `Z` that every other timestamp the service returns uses. `current_utc` is required and must be a parseable ISO 8601 instant; a missing, non-string or unparseable value is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). Every ISO 8601 instant a client supplies anywhere in this service, this one included, is normalised to UTC before it is stored or compared: an instant carrying a non-UTC offset is converted, and an instant carrying no offset at all is read as already being UTC. Moving the clock recomputes no business state — no report is republished, no posture is re-evaluated, no ledger is re-swept — it simply changes the instant against which subsequent requests evaluate temporal rules.

Every temporal rule in this specification reads that logical clock: the observation freshness window, the observation proximity window, the price-match claim window, the automated-change frequency limit, a change order's effective window, a print batch's occupation window, and the per-UTC-day boundaries of both the store label-slot ledger and the store override-budget ledger.

### 2.6. Currency, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

Every field whose name ends in `_cents` is an integer count of minor currency units in the currency named by the owning market's `currency_code`, which defaults to `USD`. No monetary value is ever stored or returned as a decimal fraction.

Comparative pricing figures are expressed in integer basis points, where 10000 basis points means exact parity with the reference value; 9800 basis points therefore means two percent below the reference. This convention governs `strategic_index_target_bp`, `index_tolerance_bp`, `observed_index_bp`, `positioning_gap_bp`, `index_bp`, `perception_survey_index_bp`, `target_position_bp`, `index_weight_bp`, `confidence_bp`, `gap_bp` and `stale_share_bp`.

Unless a calculation explicitly states another operator, every rounding step in this specification rounds half up to the nearest whole unit — whole cents for money, whole basis points for indices and confidence. Two calculations deliberately depart from that default and say so where they are defined: the margin-derived price floor rounds **up** to the next whole cent, and an automated rule's maximum single-step size rounds **down** to a whole cent. Fractional inputs supplied by clients are limited to the three ratio fields `margin_floor_pct`, `max_change_pct` and `max_match_discount_pct`, which are fractions of a price rather than percentages; each accepts a JSON integer as well as a JSON fraction, so a `margin_floor_pct` of `0` is a well-formed ratio.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two creation endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency against a parent resource's `version`, and no other endpoint in this service takes a version field of any kind.

A `POST /pricing/change-orders` request must carry `expected_product_version` as a required, non-optional field, holding the client's last observed `version` of the `Product` named by `product_id`. If that value does not equal the product's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PRODUCT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason is substantive rather than defensive: an earlier writer that created or executed an order on the same product has already advanced the product's `version`, so the caller's view of `own_price_cents`, `effective_floor_cents` and `last_price_change_at` is stale and any price it would propose was derived from figures that no longer hold. A successful create itself increments the product's `version` by 1, so a client that wants to create two orders against one product must re-read the product between them.

A `POST /pricing/index-reports` request must likewise carry `expected_market_version` as a required, non-optional field, holding the client's last observed `version` of the `Market` named by `market_id`. A value that does not equal the market's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MARKET_VERSION` (HTTP 412), because an earlier report creation or publication has already moved `observed_index_bp`, `positioning_gap_bp` or `pricing_posture`, and the strategic-positioning comparison the caller intended would be computed against a superseded market baseline. Creating a report increments the market's `version` by 1, and publishing one increments it again.

### 2.8. Market Pricing Posture as a System-Wide Gate

Each market carries a derived `pricing_posture` with three values — `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` and `FROZEN` — which acts as a gate over price-change and price-match writes in that market. A newly defined market starts at `OPEN`, and the value is thereafter recomputed only when an index report is published in that market (§3.5).

The gate reads as follows. Submitting a change order for approval, and approving one, require the market to be `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED`. Executing an approved order requires the market to be `OPEN` — `RESTRICTED` is enough to get an order approved but not enough to move a shelf price, and this holds for every `origin`. Honouring a price-match claim requires the market to be anything other than `FROZEN`. Creating a change order in a market whose posture is `FROZEN` is refused as well. Every one of these refusals carries `ERR_MARKET_POSTURE_BLOCKED` (HTTP 409).

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties and Store Scoping

Two independent separation rules run through the service. The first governs price authority: the `X-User-ID` that decides a price change order must differ from the `authored_by_user_id` recorded on that order when it was created. An operator who tries to decide their own order is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), and this holds whether the decision is an approval or a rejection.

The second governs register authority: a claim whose computed `required_approval_tier` is `MANAGER` may only be honoured by a caller whose `X-User-Role` is `StoreManager`. A `StoreAssociate` attempting it is refused with `ERR_OVERRIDE_AUTHORITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 403). Both store roles are additionally confined to their own store by the `ERR_STORE_SCOPE_VIOLATION` rule of §2.1, which applies to filing a claim, starting or restarting claim verification, deciding a claim, honouring a claim, reversing a claim, reserving a label print batch, advancing a batch and cancelling a batch.

### 2.10. Path Resolution and Referential Coherence

Identifiers embedded in a URL path are resolved left to right, and the first one that fails to name a live resource determines the error. The resolution errors are `ERR_MARKET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPETITOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OBSERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, each carrying HTTP 404.

Identifiers supplied in a request body are resolved after the path and in a fixed declaration order — `policy_id`, then `product_id`, then `competitor_id`, then `store_id`, then `link_id`, then `market_id`, then `supersedes_order_id` — and the first that fails to resolve determines which of `ERR_MARKET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPETITOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` is returned, again with HTTP 404. A body identifier that is absent or explicitly null is skipped by this pass — whether the field was required at all is the rank-5 concern of §2.11 — while an identifier supplied as anything other than a string can name no row and so fails this pass with that field's own 404.

Resolution alone is not coherence. Rows that resolve individually may still belong to different markets, and the service refuses those combinations with `ERR_MARKET_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) in three places: when a proposed match names a product and a competitor whose `market_id` values differ; when a price-match claim's store, product, competitor and policy do not all share one `market_id`; and when a change order's `signage_store_id` names a store outside the product's market.

### 2.11. Order of Evaluation

When several rules could reject the same request, the service evaluates them in the following fixed order and returns the first failure it meets. This ordering is normative: a request that violates two rules always surfaces the earlier one.

1. Header authentication — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, on every endpoint.
2. Endpoint role authorisation — `ERR_ROLE_FORBIDDEN`, on every endpoint.
3. Path identifier existence, left to right — the 404 family of §2.10, on every endpoint carrying path identifiers.
4. Body foreign-key existence, in the declaration order of §2.10 — the 404 family, on every creating or patching endpoint that carries foreign keys.
5. Required-field presence, declared type, and numeric constraints (strictly positive prices, non-negative counts, scores within 0 through 100 inclusive, ratios at least 0 and strictly below 1) — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, on every creating or patching endpoint. A request body that is absent, unparseable or not a JSON object is treated as an empty object, so its declared fields simply read as missing; body fields an endpoint does not declare are ignored; an optional field supplied as null is treated as absent and takes its default; a JSON boolean is never accepted where an integer or a ratio is declared; and every string field named in §3 must be non-empty.
6. Enum membership for every enum-typed body field — `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`, on every creating or patching endpoint that accepts an enum. This rank is reached only once the field has cleared the rank-5 shape check, so an enum-typed field whose value is not a string at all fails at rank 5 with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and only a well-formed string that is not a member of the enum yields `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`.
7. Pagination bounds — `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`, on every list endpoint.
8. Store scoping for `StoreAssociate` and `StoreManager` — `ERR_STORE_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, on the eight store-scoped operations enumerated in §2.9.
9. Optimistic concurrency — `ERR_STALE_PRODUCT_VERSION` on change-order creation and `ERR_STALE_MARKET_VERSION` on index-report creation.
10. State admissibility: the target row must currently be in a state from which the requested transition is declared, and the transition's own timing preconditions must hold — a batch may not advance to printing before its `window_start` is reached, and an approved order may not execute before its `effective_from` is reached. Failures carry `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). This governs every action endpoint: match review, match revision, observation voiding, report publication, order submission, order decision, order execution, claim verification, claim decision, claim honouring, claim reversal, batch advancement, batch cancellation, and both status patches.
11. Market posture — `ERR_MARKET_POSTURE_BLOCKED`, on order submission, order decision, order execution and claim honouring, exactly as described in §2.8.
12. Minimum advertised price on claim intake — `ERR_MAP_FLOOR_VIOLATION`.
13. Brand repricing restriction on change-order creation — `ERR_BRAND_REPRICING_RESTRICTED`.
14. Segregation of duties on an order decision — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
15. Associate override authority on claim honouring — `ERR_OVERRIDE_AUTHORITY_EXCEEDED`.
16. Competitor eligibility, on claim intake and on a claim decision — `ERR_COMPETITOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.
17. Category exclusion on claim intake — `ERR_CATEGORY_EXCLUDED`.
18. Confirmed-match availability when a claim decision marks the claim verified — `ERR_NO_CONFIRMED_MATCH`.
19. Market coherence, on match creation, claim intake and change-order creation — `ERR_MARKET_MISMATCH`.
20. Applicable repricing rule on change-order creation — `ERR_NO_APPLICABLE_REPRICING_RULE`.
21. Competitor benchmark on change-order creation — `ERR_NO_COMPETITOR_BENCHMARK`.
22. Uniqueness — `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`, on market, competitor, product, store, match and policy creation.
23. Loop ceilings, which share this rank because they never apply to the same endpoint: `ERR_MAX_REVIEW_ROUNDS` on match review and match revision, and `ERR_MAX_EVIDENCE_ROUNDS` on claim verification and on a claim decision that sends the claim back for more evidence.
24. Confirmation confidence threshold on a confirming match review — `ERR_CONFIDENCE_BELOW_CONFIRM_THRESHOLD`.
25. Index data sufficiency on report publication — `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_INDEX_DATA`.
26. Automated-change frequency limit on order execution — `ERR_CHANGE_FREQUENCY_LIMIT`.
27. Effective-window overlap on change-order creation — `ERR_EFFECTIVE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
28. Printer window clearance on batch creation — `ERR_PRINT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.
29. Daily printer capacity on batch creation — `ERR_PRINT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
30. Observation proximity on observation creation — `ERR_DUPLICATE_OBSERVATION_WINDOW`.
31. Forward-dated instants: `ERR_OBSERVATION_IN_FUTURE` on observation creation and on claim intake, and `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_EXPIRED` on claim intake.
32. Claim savings on claim intake — `ERR_CLAIM_NO_SAVINGS`.
33. Claim discount cap on claim intake — `ERR_MATCH_DISCOUNT_CAP_EXCEEDED`.
34. Store daily override budget on claim honouring — `ERR_OVERRIDE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`.
35. Requested-price shape on change-order creation — `ERR_REQUESTED_PRICE_MISMATCH`.

### 2.12. Conserved Ledger Quantities

Two per-store daily quantities are maintained as derived aggregates rather than as incrementally patched counters, and each satisfies a conservation invariant that must hold after every operation that touches it.

For shelf-label printing, a store's `label_printer_capacity_per_day` always equals `label_slots_used_today` plus `label_slots_remaining_today`. For price-match overrides, the governing policy's `daily_override_budget_cents` always equals `override_budget_used_cents` plus `override_budget_remaining_cents`. Both are recomputed from their underlying rows whenever any contributing row changes, so an implementation that patches the counters directly and an implementation that recomputes them from scratch must agree on the result at every observable point.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Market: Positioning, Competitor Set, Catalog and Stores

Everything in this service hangs off a **market** — a trading area with one currency, one declared competitive strategy and one competitor set. A `PlatformAdmin` defines a market by supplying a `market_code` (a short identifier such as `US-WEST`, between 1 and 32 characters long and globally unique across all markets), a human-readable `name`, a `strategic_positioning` drawn from `PRICE_LEADER`, `AT_PARITY` and `PREMIUM`, and a `strategic_index_target_bp` between 5000 and 15000 inclusive that expresses in basis points where the market intends to sit against its competitor set. An optional `currency_code` names the ISO 4217 currency of every monetary field in the market and defaults to `USD` — any non-empty string is accepted and stored verbatim, the service not checking the value against the ISO 4217 register; an optional `index_tolerance_bp`, which must be at least 0, states how far the market may drift from its target before it is considered off target and defaults to 150. Reusing a `market_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` (HTTP 409).

A market's four derived fields start out empty or benign: `observed_index_bp` and `positioning_gap_bp` are both null because no report has been published yet, `insufficient_data_report_count` is 0, and `pricing_posture` is `OPEN`. All four are recomputed only when an index report is published (§3.5), and markets can be listed filtered by `strategic_positioning` or by `pricing_posture` when an operator wants to see which trading areas are currently gated.

Competitors are added to a market's set one at a time, again by a `PlatformAdmin`, with a `market_id` naming the market, a `competitor_code` that must be unique within that market, the competitor's banner `name`, a `collection_method` of `ONLINE`, `STORE_AUDIT` or `HYBRID` describing how its prices are gathered, and a `tier` of either `PRIMARY` or `SECONDARY`. Only `PRIMARY` competitors set the index benchmark; `SECONDARY` competitors are collected and matched but never enter a benchmark selection. The optional `price_match_eligible` flag, which defaults to false, decides whether a shopper may cite this banner in a price-match claim. A new competitor's `status` is `ACTIVE`, its `suspension_reason` is null, and its three drift counters — `confirmed_match_count`, `stale_match_count` and `stale_share_bp` — all start at 0. Reusing a `competitor_code` inside one market is another `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. Competitor listings can be narrowed by `market_id`, by `status`, by `tier` and by `collection_method`.

Catalog rows are per-market: a `Product` carries the `market_id` it belongs to, a `sku` that must be unique within that market, a `category_code` used by index reporting and by repricing rules, a `brand` name, the current shelf and online price `own_price_cents`, and the landed cost `unit_cost_cents`. Both prices must be strictly greater than 0. Four optional fields tune how aggressively the product may be repriced: `margin_floor_pct` is the minimum gross margin as a fraction of price, at least 0 and strictly below 1, defaulting to 0.20; `map_floor_cents` is the brand's minimum advertised price in cents, at least 0, defaulting to 0 to mean the brand imposes none; `brand_repricing_restricted` defaults to false and, when true, means the brand forbids automated repricing of this product; and `index_weight_bp`, which must be strictly greater than 0 and defaults to 10000, is the product's basket weight inside its category index. An optional `status` of `ACTIVE` or `DISCONTINUED` is accepted at creation and defaults to `ACTIVE`; a `DISCONTINUED` product is excluded from every index basket. A duplicate `sku` within a market is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`, and product listings accept `market_id`, `category_code`, `brand` and `status` filters.

Three product fields are derived rather than supplied. `last_price_change_at` is the logical-clock instant of the most recent executed change order on the product and is null until the product is first repriced; `price_change_count` counts the change orders on this product that reached execution and starts at 0. The third, `effective_floor_cents`, is the hard price floor that both repricing and price matching must respect: take `unit_cost_cents`, divide it by the quantity one minus `margin_floor_pct`, and round that quotient **up** to the next whole cent; the floor is the larger of that margin-derived figure and `map_floor_cents`. It is recomputed whenever any of its three inputs changes, and `own_price_cents` itself is mutated by exactly one event in the whole system — the execution of an approved price change order (§3.7).

A store is where price matching physically happens, and a `PlatformAdmin` creates one with its `market_id`, a `store_code` unique within that market, an `override_authority_cents` of at least 0 — the largest per-claim discount one of its associates may honour without manager involvement — and a `label_printer_capacity_per_day` strictly greater than 0, counting the shelf-label reprint slots the store's printer can serve in a single UTC calendar day. A duplicate `store_code` within a market is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. On creation `label_slots_used_today` is 0, `label_slots_remaining_today` equals `label_printer_capacity_per_day`, and `override_budget_used_cents` is 0; `override_budget_remaining_cents` is the governing price-match policy's daily budget less the used amount, and is null while the store's market has no `ACTIVE` policy. Both ledgers are described in detail in §3.9 and §3.10. Stores can be listed filtered by `market_id`.

### 3.2. Matching Products to Competitors: Evidence, Confidence and the Review Cycle

A competitor's price is only comparable once someone has asserted that the competitor's item and the retailer's item are the same thing. A `PricingAnalyst` proposes that assertion as a **match**, naming a `product_id` and a `competitor_id`, declaring which evidence channel produced the candidate through `match_method` — `IDENTIFIER`, `ATTRIBUTE` or `IMAGE` — and supplying all three agreement scores, `identifier_score`, `attribute_score` and `image_score`, each an integer from 0 through 100 inclusive. When the channel is `IDENTIFIER`, the competitor-side GTIN, UPC or MPN is carried in `competitor_identifier`; for the other two channels it is conventionally null. The field is nonetheless optional on every channel and is never cross-validated against `match_method`: any non-empty string supplied is stored verbatim whatever the channel, an omitted or explicitly null value is stored as null even under `IDENTIFIER`, and an empty string is refused by the rank-5 shape check with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The competitor must belong to the same market as the product, or the proposal is refused with `ERR_MARKET_MISMATCH`, and at most one non-rejected match may exist for any one product-and-competitor pair — a second attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`.

Confidence is scored the moment the match is proposed, and again every time its evidence is revised. The starting point is a weighted blend of the three channels: multiply `identifier_score` by 0.55, `attribute_score` by 0.30 and `image_score` by 0.15, add the three products together, multiply the sum by 100 to convert it to basis points, and round half up. Because all three scores are whole numbers from 0 through 100, this blend is exact and equals 55 times `identifier_score` plus 30 times `attribute_score` plus 15 times `image_score`.

From there the scoring forks into two variants, and which one applies is recorded on the match as `confidence_variant`. The **`IDENTIFIER_ANCHORED`** variant applies when `match_method` is `IDENTIFIER` **and** `identifier_score` is at least 90. It computes an anchored figure by multiplying `identifier_score` by 100 and adding a 500-basis-point anchor bonus, takes whichever is larger of that anchored figure and the blend, and caps the result at 10000 basis points. Under this variant the attribute and image channels can only raise the outcome, never lower it, and the low-image ceiling described next does not apply at all. The **`ATTRIBUTE_BLEND`** variant applies in every other case — when `match_method` is `ATTRIBUTE` or `IMAGE`, or when it is `IDENTIFIER` but `identifier_score` is below 90. It uses the plain blend, and it applies one penalty: when `image_score` is below 50, the result is capped at 6000 basis points; otherwise the result is simply capped at 10000. The difference is not cosmetic — scores of 92 on identifier, 40 on attribute and 30 on image yield 9700 basis points under `IDENTIFIER_ANCHORED` and 6000 basis points under `ATTRIBUTE_BLEND`. Whichever branch ran, the outcome is written to `confidence_bp` as an integer basis-point figure clamped into the range 0 through 10000 inclusive.

The confidence figure is then graded into `match_grade`: `EXACT` when `confidence_bp` is at least 8500, `CLOSE` when it is at least 6000 but below 8500, and `WEAK` when it is below 6000. A newly proposed match has `status` `PROPOSED`, `review_round` 0 and `data_status` `INSUFFICIENT`, since no competitor price has been collected against it yet; `latest_effective_price_cents`, `selected_observation_id` and `gap_bp` are all null and `gap_stale` is false, and no effective-price election is run at proposal time. Matches can be listed filtered by `product_id`, by `competitor_id`, by `status`, by `match_grade` and by `min_confidence_bp`, the last of which keeps only matches whose `confidence_bp` is at least the supplied figure. `min_confidence_bp` is read from the query string as a decimal integer with surrounding whitespace trimmed and is not bounds-checked; a value that does not parse as an integer is silently ignored — the filter simply does not apply and the request still succeeds — rather than being rejected, and a negative value keeps every row.

A `PricingManager` adjudicates a proposed match, choosing a `decision` of `OPEN_REVIEW`, `CONFIRM` or `REJECT` and optionally attaching a free-text `note`. The `note` is shape-checked whenever it is present — it must be a non-empty string, an empty one failing with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — but it is not persisted on the match and appears in no response field, on any of the three decisions. Choosing `OPEN_REVIEW` moves the match `PROPOSED → UNDER_REVIEW` and changes nothing else: `confidence_bp`, `match_grade` and `review_round` are all left exactly as they were, and only the match's `updated_at` and `version` move. A match under review is handed back to a `PricingAnalyst`, who revises it: the revision accepts any of `identifier_score`, `attribute_score`, `image_score`, `match_method` and `competitor_identifier`, overwrites each field that is supplied, moves the match `UNDER_REVIEW → PROPOSED`, increases `review_round` by 1, and recomputes `confidence_bp`, `confidence_variant` and `match_grade` from the revised evidence exactly as at proposal time. Every one of the five is optional and a field supplied as null counts as absent, leaving the stored value untouched — a revision can overwrite `competitor_identifier` with a different string but can never clear it back to null. All five are shape-checked, and `match_method` enum-checked, before the `UNDER_REVIEW` state test and the round ceiling are consulted. A revision runs no effective-price election and touches none of `data_status`, `latest_effective_price_cents`, `selected_observation_id`, `gap_bp` or `gap_stale`, which keep whatever the last election left on them.

That loop is bounded. Both opening a review and revising an under-review match require `review_round` to be strictly less than 3, so a match may go around the cycle three times; the fourth attempt to enter it is refused with `ERR_MAX_REVIEW_ROUNDS` (HTTP 409).

Confirmation is where the review loop pays for itself, because the two confirming edges carry different confidence bars. Confirming a match that sits in `PROPOSED` — `PROPOSED → CONFIRMED` — demands `EXACT` grade, meaning `confidence_bp` of at least 8500. Confirming a match that sits in `UNDER_REVIEW` — `UNDER_REVIEW → CONFIRMED` — demands only `confidence_bp` of at least 6000, because a human has looked at it: a reviewed `CLOSE` match may be confirmed by hand. A confirmation attempt whose `confidence_bp` falls below whichever bar applies to the match's current state is refused with `ERR_CONFIDENCE_BELOW_CONFIRM_THRESHOLD` (HTTP 422). Both edges additionally require the underlying product's `status` to be `ACTIVE` and the competitor's `status` to be `ACTIVE`; both of those are state-admissibility failures carrying `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) rather than a coherence error, the product is tested before the competitor, and both are tested before the confidence bar — so a `WEAK` match on a `DISCONTINUED` product surfaces `ERR_INVALID_STATE` and not `ERR_CONFIDENCE_BELOW_CONFIRM_THRESHOLD`. Both edges have identical consequences: the competitor's `confirmed_match_count` increases by 1 and its `stale_share_bp` is recomputed, and the match re-runs the full effective-price election of §3.3 — the in-stock and 72-hour freshness filters included, not merely a scan of `ACTIVE` rows — recomputing `latest_effective_price_cents`, `selected_observation_id`, `data_status`, `gap_bp` and `gap_stale` from the observations it already holds. Because a competitor's three drift counters are derived at read time rather than stored, a confirmation mutates only the match: the competitor row's own `updated_at` and `version` do not move, and the match's `version` advances by exactly 1 however far the election moves its fields.

Rejection is available from either state. `PROPOSED → REJECTED` and `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED` both require only that the match currently sits in the state being left, and both have the same side effect: every `ACTIVE` price observation on the match moves to `VOIDED` with its `void_reason` set to `LINK_REJECTED`. Because that void empties the match's live observation pool, the match then re-runs the effective-price election of §3.3 over whatever the void left behind, so it lands on `data_status` `INSUFFICIENT` with `latest_effective_price_cents`, `selected_observation_id` and `gap_bp` all null and `gap_stale` false. The whole rejection counts as a single mutation of the match: its `version` advances by exactly 1, the void and the re-election that follow adding no further increment, while each observation the void touched advances its own `version` by 1. `REJECTED` is terminal — a rejected match never returns, and because only non-rejected matches occupy the uniqueness slot for a product-and-competitor pair, rejecting one frees that pair for a fresh proposal.

### 3.3. Collecting Competitor Prices and Choosing the Effective Price

Raw prices are submitted by a `DataCollector`, one observation at a time, against the match they were collected for. An observation carries the `link_id` of that match, the `observed_price_cents` as collected (strictly greater than 0), a `collection_method` of either `ONLINE` or `STORE_AUDIT`, and the `observed_at` instant at which the price was seen. Intake requires only that `link_id` resolve to a live match; the match's own `status` is never consulted, so a price may be recorded against a match that is still `PROPOSED`, that is `UNDER_REVIEW`, or that has already been `REJECTED`. The `collection_method` must be compatible with the competitor's own declared method: a competitor collected `ONLINE` yields `ONLINE` observations, one collected by `STORE_AUDIT` yields `STORE_AUDIT` observations, and a `HYBRID` competitor accepts either; an incompatible pairing fails the request-shape check with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Two optional flags complete the row: `in_stock`, which defaults to true, and `promo_flag`, which defaults to false and marks a temporary promotional price. A new observation's `status` is `ACTIVE` and its `void_reason` is null. Observations may be listed filtered by `link_id`, by `status` and by `collection_method`.

Two temporal rules police intake. An `observed_at` later than the logical clock's current instant is refused with `ERR_OBSERVATION_IN_FUTURE` (HTTP 422) — the service will not accept a price that has not been seen yet. And to stop a scraper double-posting, no other `ACTIVE` observation on the same match with the same `collection_method` may lie strictly within 60 minutes of the new `observed_at`; a separation of exactly 60 minutes is accepted, while anything closer is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OBSERVATION_WINDOW` (HTTP 409). Note that the proximity test is scoped to the pairing of match and collection method, so an online reading and a store audit taken minutes apart on the same match coexist happily.

Out of the observations a match accumulates, exactly one is elected the **effective competitor price**, and the election is re-run every time the pool changes — on a new observation, on a void, when a match is confirmed, and inside the competitor suspension and retirement cascades. An observation qualifies for the election only if all four of these hold: its `status` is `ACTIVE`; its `in_stock` flag is true, because an out-of-stock price is not a price a shopper can buy at; its `observed_at` is not later than the logical clock's current instant; and its `observed_at` is not earlier than 72 hours before that instant, so an observation exactly 72 hours old still qualifies and one a minute older does not. Qualifying observations are then ranked by `observed_price_cents` ascending — the cheapest competitor price wins, which is what price-leader benchmarking requires — with ties broken first by the most recent `observed_at`, then by the earliest `created_at`, and finally by ascending creation sequence. The election never consults the match's own `status`, so a match that is still `PROPOSED` and holds a qualifying observation does reach `data_status` `SUFFICIENT` and does carry a `gap_bp`.

When at least one observation qualifies, the winner's price becomes `latest_effective_price_cents`, the winner's own identifier is recorded in `selected_observation_id` (a bookkeeping trace only, never an input to any later decision), and `data_status` becomes `SUFFICIENT`. The match's price gap is then computed: multiply the product's `own_price_cents` by 10000, divide by `latest_effective_price_cents`, and round half up to a whole basis point; the result is `gap_bp`, so 10000 means the retailer is exactly level with the competitor and 10800 means it is eight percent dearer. Every election that lands on a qualifying observation writes `gap_stale` false, even when the winner is the very observation that was already selected, so any observation activity on a match clears a drift flag the execution cascade of §3.7 had raised. A re-run whose outcome is identical to the state it found — the same `latest_effective_price_cents`, the same `selected_observation_id`, `data_status`, `gap_bp` and `gap_stale` — is not a mutation at all: the match's `updated_at` and `version` are left where they were, and only a re-run that actually moves one of those five fields advances them by one. When no observation qualifies, the match falls to `data_status` `INSUFFICIENT`, with `latest_effective_price_cents`, `selected_observation_id` and `gap_bp` all null and `gap_stale` false.

A `DataCollector` or a `PricingAnalyst` may void a mis-scraped or superseded observation, supplying a mandatory `void_reason` that is recorded on the row as the observation moves `ACTIVE → VOIDED`. Voiding re-runs the election on the parent match; if it leaves the match with no qualifying observation the match drops to `data_status` `INSUFFICIENT` with a null `latest_effective_price_cents` and a null `gap_bp`, and the match's competitor recomputes `stale_match_count` and `stale_share_bp`. `VOIDED` is terminal for an observation, and a void attempt on an already-voided row is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Drift is the mechanism by which a competitor's data quality is judged. Whenever a product's own price moves, every confirmed match on that product recomputes `gap_bp`, and any match whose gap sits more than 1200 basis points away from parity in either direction — that is, whose absolute difference between `gap_bp` and 10000 exceeds 1200 — has `gap_stale` set to true, marking it as data that no longer reflects a comparable position. Each competitor then counts those: `stale_match_count` is the number of its confirmed matches currently flagged stale, `confirmed_match_count` is the number of its confirmed matches altogether, and `stale_share_bp` is `stale_match_count` multiplied by 10000, divided by `confirmed_match_count`, rounded half up — defined as 0 whenever `confirmed_match_count` is 0. A competitor whose stale share reaches half of its confirmed matches, meaning `stale_share_bp` of at least 5000, has lost the right to benchmark anything, and §3.4 describes what happens to it.

### 3.4. Competitor Lifecycle: Suspension, Reinstatement, Retirement and Drift

A competitor's participation in the intelligence layer is controlled by a status change that either a `PlatformAdmin` or a `ComplianceOfficer` may request, supplying a `to_status` of `SUSPENDED`, `ACTIVE` or `RETIRED` and optionally a free-text `reason`. The three destinations behave very differently, and only `RETIRED` is terminal.

**Suspension** — `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` — is available only while the competitor is `ACTIVE`. It records the supplied `reason` as `suspension_reason`, leaving that field null when no reason was given. It then tears down the competitor's collected intelligence in one pass: every match on this competitor whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` moves `CONFIRMED → UNDER_REVIEW` and has its `review_round` increased by 1; every `ACTIVE` observation on those matches moves `ACTIVE → VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to `COMPETITOR_SUSPENDED`; each affected match re-runs the effective-price election, so a match left with no qualifying observation drops to `data_status` `INSUFFICIENT` with a null `latest_effective_price_cents`, a null `selected_observation_id` and a null `gap_bp`; and the competitor's `confirmed_match_count`, `stale_match_count` and `stale_share_bp` are recomputed, with `confirmed_match_count` falling by 1 for each match that left `CONFIRMED`.

The same suspension edge is also taken automatically, by the system rather than by an operator, as the **drift auto-suspend** described at the end of §3.3. It fires from inside the price-execution cascade of §3.7, and it fires only when all three of these hold: the competitor's `status` is `ACTIVE`, its `confirmed_match_count` is at least 1, and its `stale_share_bp` is at least 5000. When it fires, the cascade body is exactly the operator-driven one above, with one difference: `suspension_reason` is written as the fixed string `MATCH_DRIFT_AUTO_SUSPEND` rather than taken from a request. Its affected matches likewise fall to `data_status` `INSUFFICIENT` with `latest_effective_price_cents`, `selected_observation_id` and `gap_bp` all null and `gap_stale` reset to false.

**Reinstatement** — `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` — is available only from `SUSPENDED`. It clears `suspension_reason` back to null, and it deliberately does nothing else: the matches that were pushed to `UNDER_REVIEW` stay there. Reinstating a banner does not silently re-confirm its matches; an analyst must revise them and a manager must confirm them again, and the `review_round` those matches already accumulated still counts against the three-round ceiling.

**Retirement** is reachable from both live states, and both edges reject the competitor's entire match population. From `ACTIVE → RETIRED`, every match on the competitor whose `status` is `PROPOSED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `REJECTED`, and every `ACTIVE` observation on those matches moves to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to `COMPETITOR_RETIRED`; each of those matches then re-runs the effective-price election over what the void left behind, so it drops to `data_status` `INSUFFICIENT` with `latest_effective_price_cents`, `selected_observation_id` and `gap_bp` all null and `gap_stale` false. From `SUSPENDED → RETIRED`, every match whose `status` is `PROPOSED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `CONFIRMED` likewise moves to `REJECTED`; no further observation voiding is needed on this edge because the suspension that preceded it already voided the competitor's `ACTIVE` observations, and no election is re-run on this edge either, so a match rejected here keeps whatever elected figures it was already holding. Requesting a status change from a state that does not declare the requested edge — retiring an already-retired banner, or suspending a suspended one — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).

### 3.5. Index Reporting, Perception Research and the Market Posture Gate

A **price index report** scores one market and one merchandise category against its strategic target. A `PricingAnalyst` requests one by supplying the `market_id`, the `category_code` to draw the basket from — where the literal value `*` means every category in the market — the `basis` under which to compute it, and the `expected_market_version` demanded by §2.7. A `perception_survey_index_bp` may optionally accompany the request, carrying the shopper price-perception index for this market and category taken from price-perception research; it must be an integer of at least 0 and carries no upper bound, and it is null when no study accompanies the report. Reports are listed filtered by `market_id`, `category_code`, `basis`, `status` and `data_sufficiency`.

The basket universe is every product in the report's market whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `category_code` matches the report's category (or every active product in the market when the report's category is `*`). The size of that universe is recorded as `candidate_product_count`. Each candidate is then put through a per-product benchmark selection: consider the matches on that product whose `status` is `CONFIRMED`, whose `data_status` is `SUFFICIENT`, whose `confidence_bp` is at least 7000, and whose competitor is both `ACTIVE` and of `PRIMARY` tier; rank the survivors by `latest_effective_price_cents` ascending, break ties by the earliest match `created_at` and settle any remaining tie by ascending match creation sequence; the winner's `latest_effective_price_cents` is that product's benchmark. A product with no surviving match has no benchmark. The selector reads each match's `data_status` and `latest_effective_price_cents` exactly as the last effective-price election of §3.3 left them and never re-tests the elected observation against the logical clock, so the clock moving past that observation's 72-hour freshness window does not by itself withdraw the match from the selector; those stored figures move only when an election trigger of §3.3 re-runs on the match. Discontinued products, `SECONDARY` competitors, matches below the 7000-basis-point confidence floor and matches whose data is `INSUFFICIENT` are therefore all absent from both the numerator and the weight denominator of the index.

For each candidate that does have a benchmark, the service computes a price ratio: multiply the product's `own_price_cents` by 10000, divide by its benchmark, and round half up to a whole basis point. The count of such products is `matched_product_count`. **The rounding order here is pinned and must not be rearranged:** each product's ratio is rounded to a whole basis point first, and the weighted average of those already-rounded ratios is rounded second. Averaging unrounded ratios and rounding once at the end is a different algorithm and is not what this service computes.

When `matched_product_count` is 0 the report cannot be computed: `index_bp` is null, `data_sufficiency` is `INSUFFICIENT`, `imputed_product_count` is 0 and `excluded_product_count` equals `candidate_product_count`. Otherwise `data_sufficiency` is `SUFFICIENT` and the two `basis` variants diverge.

Under **`MATCHED_BASKET`**, the basket is exactly the matched products and `imputed_product_count` is 0; unmatched candidates are counted in `excluded_product_count` and have no effect whatsoever on `index_bp`. Under **`IMPUTED_FULL_CATALOG`**, the unmatched candidates are pulled into the basket at an imputed ratio so that their weights do enter the denominator. That imputed ratio is the median of the matched ratios, rounded half up: order the matched ratios ascending, breaking ties by the owning product's `created_at` ascending, and take the middle value when there is an odd number of them, or round half up the mean of the two middle values when there is an even number. Every unmatched candidate then enters the basket carrying that single imputed ratio, and `imputed_product_count` is `candidate_product_count` less `matched_product_count`.

With the basket fixed, the index itself is a weight-weighted average: sum, over every product in the basket, its ratio multiplied by its `index_weight_bp`; divide that sum by the total of the `index_weight_bp` values of the basket's products; and round half up to a whole basis point. The result is `index_bp`. `excluded_product_count` is then `candidate_product_count` less `matched_product_count` less `imputed_product_count` — which is 0 under `IMPUTED_FULL_CATALOG`, since nothing is left out. The two variants genuinely differ on identical data: one fixture yields 9743 basis points under `MATCHED_BASKET` and 9760 basis points under `IMPUTED_FULL_CATALOG`.

The report then judges itself against the market. `positioning_gap_bp` is `index_bp` less the market's `strategic_index_target_bp`, and is null whenever `index_bp` is null. `positioning_verdict` is `INDETERMINATE` when `index_bp` is null; otherwise it is `ON_TARGET` when the absolute value of `positioning_gap_bp` is less than or equal to the market's `index_tolerance_bp`, `ABOVE_TARGET` when `positioning_gap_bp` is strictly greater than 0, and `BELOW_TARGET` in the remaining case.

Price-perception research is validated in the same breath, immediately after the index is computed. `perception_validation` is `INDETERMINATE` when either `perception_survey_index_bp` or `index_bp` is null. Otherwise, when the absolute difference between the survey figure and the computed `index_bp` is at most 200 basis points, shoppers' perception agrees with the arithmetic and `perception_validation` is `CONFIRMS`; a difference strictly greater than 200 basis points means the market is priced one way and perceived another, and `perception_validation` is `CONTRADICTS`. The comparison is inclusive at exactly 200 basis points. A freshly computed report has `status` `DRAFT` and a null `published_at`, and computing it increases the market's `version` by 1.

Publication is a `PricingManager` act, and it is what makes a report govern. A report may be published only from `DRAFT`, and only when its `data_sufficiency` is `SUFFICIENT`; publishing an insufficient report is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_INDEX_DATA` (HTTP 422), and publishing one that is not in `DRAFT` — a `SUPERSEDED` report, say — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On success the report moves `DRAFT → PUBLISHED`, its `published_at` is set to the logical clock's current instant, and any other report in the same market with the same `category_code` and the same `basis` that was `PUBLISHED` moves `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED` and stops governing anything. Publication never recomputes the report: `index_bp`, the four basket counts, `data_sufficiency`, `positioning_gap_bp`, `positioning_verdict` and `perception_validation` all stand exactly as the create left them, however far the market's products, matches and observations have moved in between.

Publication then re-evaluates the market's posture, and this is the single most consequential cascade in the service. First, the governing report is selected: among all reports for this market whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` — of any category and either basis — rank by `published_at` descending, break ties by the earliest `created_at` and settle any remaining tie by ascending creation sequence; the first is the governing report. The market's `observed_index_bp` becomes that report's `index_bp`, and the market's own `positioning_gap_bp` becomes `observed_index_bp` less `strategic_index_target_bp`. When no published report exists at all, both are null. Second, `insufficient_data_report_count` is recounted as the number of reports for this market whose `data_sufficiency` is `INSUFFICIENT` and whose `status` is either `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED`; superseded reports are excluded from both the governing selection and this count. Third, the drift is taken as the absolute value of the market's `positioning_gap_bp`, or as 0 when that gap is null, and the posture follows: `FROZEN` when the drift is strictly greater than twice the market's `index_tolerance_bp`; otherwise `RESTRICTED` when the drift is strictly greater than the `index_tolerance_bp` itself **or** `insufficient_data_report_count` is at least 1; and `OPEN` in the remaining case. Both comparisons are strict, so a drift exactly equal to the tolerance leaves the market `OPEN`, and a drift exactly equal to twice the tolerance leaves it `RESTRICTED`. The market's `version` increases by 1.

When that recomputation lands on `FROZEN`, the market is taken off the field. Every price change order in the market whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `APPROVED` moves to `SUSPENDED`, and every price-match policy in the market whose `status` is `ACTIVE` moves to `SUSPENDED`. Orders suspended from `DRAFT` or `PENDING_APPROVAL` keep their `signage_status` untouched at `NOT_REQUIRED`, because nothing had been queued for them. An order suspended from `APPROVED` whose `signage_status` is `QUEUED` gives its printer slot back: `signage_status` moves `QUEUED → RELEASED`, `label_batch_id` is cleared, the order's identifier is removed from its batch's `queued_order_ids` so that the batch's `item_count` falls by 1, and every store that lost a queued order recomputes `label_slots_used_today` and `label_slots_remaining_today`.

Freezing is reversible. Whenever a publication in that market recomputes the posture to `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED`, every order sitting in `SUSPENDED` in the market returns `SUSPENDED → DRAFT` so it can be resubmitted. The thaw is not conditional on the market having been `FROZEN` beforehand — it runs on every recomputation that does not land on `FROZEN` — and it does not ask why an order was suspended, so an order suspended by the claim reversal of §3.9 is returned to `DRAFT` by it just as a frozen-market casualty is. Its `signage_status` is left exactly as the suspension left it, `RELEASED` for an order whose queued label was taken away and `NOT_REQUIRED` for one that never had a label; the shelf label is re-queued only when the order is approved again. The thaw covers orders only: a price-match policy driven to `SUSPENDED` by a freeze stays there permanently, because the service declares no edge back to `ACTIVE` for a policy, and a market that wants its programme back must create a fresh one.

### 3.6. Automated Repricing Rules and the Proposal of a Price Change

A **repricing rule** encodes how aggressively a category may be moved, and a `PricingManager` owns them. Creating one requires the `market_id` it governs, the `category_code` it applies to — again with the literal `*` matching every category — a `target_position_bp` between 5000 and 15000 inclusive stating where own price should sit relative to the competitor benchmark (9700 meaning three percent below it), a `max_change_pct` strictly greater than 0 and at most 1 capping the size of a single step as a fraction of the current own price, a `change_frequency_limit_days` of at least 0 giving the minimum number of whole days between automated changes on one product, and a `priority` of at least 0 used to rank competing rules, where a higher number wins. An optional `brand_exclusions` array lists brand names the rule refuses to touch and defaults to empty; an optional `status` of `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED` is accepted at creation and defaults to `ACTIVE`. A new rule's `applied_order_count`, which counts the orders selecting this rule that reached execution, starts at 0. A `PricingManager` may later flip a rule between `ACTIVE` and `PAUSED` by supplying a `to_status`, which accepts those two values and no others — a rule has no third destination and nothing else clears the enum check — and which must differ from the rule's current `status`: re-asserting the status a rule already holds is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) rather than treated as a successful no-op. A `PAUSED` rule is simply absent from the candidate set the next time a rule is selected. Rules are listed filtered by `market_id`, `category_code` and `status`.

A **price change order** is the unit of pricing action, and it is created by either a `PricingAnalyst` or a `PricingManager`. The request names the `product_id` being repriced, an `origin`, an `effective_from` instant, and the `expected_product_version` demanded by §2.7. `origin` accepts `AUTOMATED_RULE` or `MANUAL` only: the third value, `PRICE_MATCH`, is producible exclusively by the claim-honour cascade of §3.9 and is refused as a direct request through the enum check. Four optional fields complete the payload: `requested_price_cents` (strictly greater than 0) carries the operator's hand-set price, `effective_until` closes the order's price-effective window, `supersedes_order_id` names an existing non-terminal order on the same product that this one replaces, and `signage_store_id` names the store whose shelf label must be reprinted when the order is approved.

The shape of the payload must match the `origin`. A `MANUAL` order must carry `requested_price_cents`; an `AUTOMATED_RULE` order must not carry one, and neither may a `PRICE_MATCH` order. Either violation is refused with `ERR_REQUESTED_PRICE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). An `AUTOMATED_RULE` order is additionally refused outright, with `ERR_BRAND_REPRICING_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 422), when the target product's `brand_repricing_restricted` flag is true — the brand has withheld consent for machine repricing of that item. When `signage_store_id` is supplied it must resolve to a live store — it occupies the `store_id` slot of the body declaration order of §2.10, so an unresolvable value fails with `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND` after `product_id` has resolved and before `supersedes_order_id` is looked at — and it must name a store inside the product's market, or the create fails with `ERR_MARKET_MISMATCH`; when it is omitted the order needs no shelf label at all, and its `signage_status` stays `NOT_REQUIRED` for the order's entire life with no batch ever selected for it.

For an `AUTOMATED_RULE` order the service must first find a rule, then find a benchmark. **Rule selection** considers every repricing rule in the product's market whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `category_code` either equals the product's `category_code` or is the wildcard `*`, and whose `brand_exclusions` does not contain the product's `brand`. Survivors are ranked by `priority` descending, then by specificity descending — a rule naming the category exactly outranks a wildcard rule at the same priority, specificity being that binary distinction and nothing finer — then by the earliest `created_at`, and finally by ascending creation sequence. The brand-exclusion filter is exact, case-sensitive membership of the product's `brand` in the rule's `brand_exclusions` array. The winner's identifier is recorded on the order as `rule_id`. When no rule survives the filter, whether because none matches the market and category or because every candidate excludes the product's brand, the create is refused with `ERR_NO_APPLICABLE_REPRICING_RULE` (HTTP 422). **Benchmark selection** then applies exactly the same eligibility, ranking and tie-break as the per-product benchmark selector inside index reporting (§3.5): confirmed matches on this product with `data_status` `SUFFICIENT` and `confidence_bp` of at least 7000, on `ACTIVE` competitors of `PRIMARY` tier, ranked by `latest_effective_price_cents` ascending and tie-broken by the earliest match `created_at`. The winner's `latest_effective_price_cents` is stored on the order as `benchmark_price_cents`. Sharing the selector is deliberate: an automated order and an index report can never disagree about which competitor price is the benchmark. When no match survives, the create is refused with `ERR_NO_COMPETITOR_BENCHMARK` (HTTP 422). For `MANUAL` and `PRICE_MATCH` origins, `rule_id` and `benchmark_price_cents` are both null.

The proposed price is then derived in three steps, and the order of the last two is pinned. Before anything else, the order snapshots the product's hard floor into its own `effective_floor_cents`: `unit_cost_cents` divided by the quantity one minus `margin_floor_pct`, rounded up to the next whole cent, or `map_floor_cents` if that is larger. **Step one** produces `target_price_cents`. Under the **`AUTOMATED_RULE`** variant the target is `benchmark_price_cents` multiplied by the selected rule's `target_position_bp`, divided by 10000, rounded half up; and a finite change band is established around the product's current price by taking `own_price_cents` multiplied by the rule's `max_change_pct`, rounded **down** to a whole cent, as the maximum step, then setting the band's low end to `own_price_cents` less that step and its high end to `own_price_cents` plus that step. Under the **`HAND_SET`** variant, which covers both `MANUAL` and `PRICE_MATCH` origins, the target is simply the price handed in — `requested_price_cents` for a manual order, the claim's honoured price for a price-match order — and there is no change band at all: the low end is 0 and the high end unbounded, because a change-frequency band has no business constraining a hand-set price. **Step two** clamps the target into the band, producing `banded_price_cents`: it is the target, raised to the band's low end if it fell below and lowered to the band's high end if it rose above. **Step three, which runs last, clamps upward to the hard floor**, producing `proposed_price_cents`: whichever is larger of `banded_price_cents` and `effective_floor_cents`.

That ordering is not commutative and the service depends on the pinned order. With an own price of 24999 cents, a unit cost of 22000 cents, a `margin_floor_pct` of 0.20, a `map_floor_cents` of 22999, a benchmark of 22499 cents, a `target_position_bp` of 9700 and a `max_change_pct` of 0.10, the pinned band-then-floor sequence yields a `proposed_price_cents` of 27500, whereas clamping to the floor before clamping into the band would yield 27498. Margin compliance and minimum advertised price outrank the change band by construction, and the two boolean fields on the order record when that happened: `floor_binding` is true exactly when `banded_price_cents` fell below `effective_floor_cents`, meaning the floor clamp had to lift the price and `proposed_price_cents` equals the floor; `band_breach` is true when `proposed_price_cents` ends up outside the band's low or high end, which can only happen because the floor clamp ran after the band clamp — and therefore only under the `AUTOMATED_RULE` variant, since the `HAND_SET` band running from 0 to unbounded can never be breached and leaves `band_breach` false on every `MANUAL` and `PRICE_MATCH` order.

Two more things are settled at creation. The order copies the product's `market_id` onto itself, records the calling `X-User-ID` as `authored_by_user_id`, leaves `decided_by_user_id`, `label_batch_id` and `source_claim_id` null, and starts life with `status` `DRAFT` and `signage_status` `NOT_REQUIRED`. Creating it increases the product's `version` by 1. And the order's effective window must not collide with any other live order on the same product. That window runs from `effective_from` inclusive to `effective_until` exclusive, and a null `effective_until` means the order occupies everything from `effective_from` onward. The new window must not intersect the window of any other order on the same product whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `APPROVED` or `EXECUTED`. Because the intervals are half-open, exactly touching endpoints do not collide — an earlier order ending precisely where the new one begins is fine — but any genuine overlap is refused with `ERR_EFFECTIVE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). The test runs before the order named by `supersedes_order_id` is moved out of the way and grants that order no exemption: a replacement whose window overlaps the very order it supersedes is refused, unless that order is `SUSPENDED`, which is not one of the four statuses the test treats as live.

Superseding is how an operator replaces a price decision that has not yet taken effect. When `supersedes_order_id` is supplied, the named order must reference the same `product_id` and must currently sit in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED`; either violation — a different product, or a status outside that set — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) at the state-admissibility rank of §2.11, after the `expected_product_version` check and before the market posture gate. On success it moves to `SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal. What the superseded order gives up depends on where it was. From `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED` and from `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED`, an order whose `signage_status` is `QUEUED` returns its printer slot exactly as a frozen-market suspension does: `signage_status` moves `QUEUED → RELEASED`, `label_batch_id` is cleared, the order's identifier leaves the batch's `queued_order_ids` so the batch's `item_count` falls by 1, and the store recomputes `label_slots_used_today` and `label_slots_remaining_today`. From `PENDING_APPROVAL → SUPERSEDED` there is no signage change, because an order awaiting a decision has never had a label queued. `SUSPENDED → SUPERSEDED` runs the same release step, which is a no-op in practice because the suspension that got the order there had already released its label. The rule underneath all four edges is that the release fires exactly when the superseded order's `signage_status` reads `QUEUED` at that instant and is skipped silently otherwise. Change orders can be listed filtered by `product_id`, `market_id`, `rule_id`, `source_claim_id`, `supersedes_order_id`, `label_batch_id`, `signage_store_id`, `status`, `signage_status` and `origin` — the `label_batch_id` filter being the documented way to read a batch's queued orders back out in the reverse direction.

### 3.7. Approving and Executing a Price Change, and the Drift Cascade It Sets Off

A drafted order is put in front of a decision-maker by either a `PricingAnalyst` or a `PricingManager`, moving it `DRAFT → PENDING_APPROVAL`. Submission requires the market's `pricing_posture` to be `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` and recomputes nothing at all: `proposed_price_cents` was frozen when the order was created and submission does not revisit it, no matter how much the product or its competitors have moved since.

Only a `PricingManager` decides, choosing a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and optionally attaching a `note`, which must be a non-empty string when it is supplied but is not persisted anywhere — no field on the order records it and no response echoes it back. Both decisions record the deciding `X-User-ID` as `decided_by_user_id`, and both are barred to the operator whose identifier already sits in `authored_by_user_id` — the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.9, refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. `APPROVE` moves the order `PENDING_APPROVAL → APPROVED` and additionally requires the market to be `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED`. It is also the moment the shelf label is queued: when the order carries a non-null `signage_store_id`, its `signage_status` moves `NOT_REQUIRED → QUEUED` and a print batch is selected as described in §3.10; when `signage_store_id` is null, `signage_status` stays `NOT_REQUIRED` and no batch is selected. `REJECT`, by contrast, sends the order back rather than killing it: it is not posture-gated at all — a rejection succeeds even while the market is `FROZEN`, the gate of §2.8 applying to the approving decision only — and it moves `PENDING_APPROVAL → DRAFT` so the order can be reworked and resubmitted, and `signage_status` stays `NOT_REQUIRED` because nothing was ever queued for a rejected order. An order that has already been through approval and been sent back — one whose `signage_status` is `RELEASED` while its `status` is `PENDING_APPROVAL` — moves `RELEASED → QUEUED` on re-approval, re-running batch selection and consuming a slot again.

Execution is the only event in the entire service that mutates a product's `own_price_cents`, and it belongs to a `PricingManager`. An order may execute only from `APPROVED`; only while the market's `pricing_posture` is `OPEN`, since `RESTRICTED` is enough to get an order approved but not enough to move a shelf price; and only once the logical clock has reached the order's `effective_from`, an attempt before that instant being refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. For an `AUTOMATED_RULE` order there is one further gate: either the product's `last_price_change_at` is null, meaning it has never been repriced, or the number of whole days between `last_price_change_at` and the logical clock's current instant is at least the selected rule's `change_frequency_limit_days`. Executing sooner than that is refused with `ERR_CHANGE_FREQUENCY_LIMIT` (HTTP 409).

What follows is a five-level cascade in which each level is caused by the level above it rather than by the execute request itself, and every level must be applied in order.

At **level one**, the order moves `APPROVED → EXECUTED` — a terminal state — and the product is repriced: `own_price_cents` becomes the order's `proposed_price_cents`, `last_price_change_at` becomes the logical clock's current instant, `price_change_count` increases by 1, `effective_floor_cents` is recomputed, and the product's `version` increases by 1. Separately, when the order carries a non-null `rule_id`, that rule's `applied_order_count` increases by 1.

At **level two**, caused by the own-price move, every match on this product whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` recomputes `gap_bp` against its current `latest_effective_price_cents`, and any such match whose absolute difference between the new `gap_bp` and 10000 exceeds 1200 basis points has `gap_stale` set to true. A `CONFIRMED` match whose `latest_effective_price_cents` is null is passed over entirely — there is nothing to measure a gap against, so its `gap_bp` stays null, its `gap_stale` keeps whatever it held and its `version` does not move — while every match that is recomputed has its `version` increased by 1 whether or not its flag changed. This level only ever raises the flag: a match whose recomputed gap falls back inside 1200 basis points keeps a `gap_stale` of true, because nothing clears that flag except a re-run of the effective-price election of §3.3.

At **level three**, caused by those flags being raised, every competitor owning a match that level two found stale recomputes `stale_match_count` and `stale_share_bp`. Staleness here is read off the recomputed gap rather than off a transition of the flag, so a match that was already carrying `gap_stale` true and still exceeds the threshold enrols its competitor exactly as a freshly flagged match does. Each such competitor is considered once, in the order in which its stale matches were met at level two — that is, by match `created_at` ascending and then by ascending match creation sequence — and a competitor whose recomputed `stale_share_bp` is at least 5000 while its `status` is still `ACTIVE` and its `confirmed_match_count` is at least 1 takes the drift auto-suspend edge to `SUSPENDED`, with `suspension_reason` written as `MATCH_DRIFT_AUTO_SUSPEND`.

At **level four**, caused by those auto-suspensions, every `CONFIRMED` match on each newly suspended competitor moves `CONFIRMED → UNDER_REVIEW` with its `review_round` increased by 1, and every `ACTIVE` observation on those matches moves `ACTIVE → VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to `COMPETITOR_SUSPENDED`.

At **level five**, caused by that voiding, each of those matches that is left with no qualifying observation falls to `data_status` `INSUFFICIENT`, with `latest_effective_price_cents` null, `selected_observation_id` null, `gap_bp` null and `gap_stale` reset to false.

The net effect is that a single aggressive price move can silently withdraw a competitor from benchmarking and strand the matches that depended on it — which is precisely the serialization point the design intends, since the next automated order on any of those products will find no benchmark and be refused with `ERR_NO_COMPETITOR_BENCHMARK` rather than repricing against data the service no longer trusts.

### 3.8. Price Match Policy, Claim Intake and the Verification Workflow

The price-match programme for a market is described by a **policy**, which either a `PricingManager` or a `ComplianceOfficer` may administer. Creating one requires the `market_id`, an `eligible_competitor_tiers` array naming which competitor tiers a shopper may cite — a non-empty subset of `PRIMARY` and `SECONDARY`, most commonly just `PRIMARY` — and a `daily_override_budget_cents` of at least 0 setting the per-store, per-UTC-day ceiling on honoured discounts. Five optional fields tune the programme: `excluded_category_codes` lists categories the programme does not cover and defaults to empty; `verification_required` defaults to true; `max_match_discount_pct`, strictly greater than 0 and at most 1, caps a single honoured discount as a fraction of the product's own price and defaults to 0.25; `claim_window_hours`, strictly greater than 0, states how old a claimed sighting may be and defaults to 168 hours, that is one full week; and `status`, accepted as `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` at creation, defaults to `ACTIVE`. A new policy's `honored_claim_count` starts at 0. A market may hold at most one `ACTIVE` policy, so a second one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`; that check runs only when the policy being created would itself be `ACTIVE`, so a policy created as `SUSPENDED` is always accepted and a market may accumulate any number of suspended policies alongside its single active one. Neither array field is normalised: `eligible_competitor_tiers` and `excluded_category_codes` are stored and echoed exactly as supplied, in the order given and with duplicate entries neither rejected nor collapsed. Policies are listed filtered by `market_id` and `status`. Recall from §3.5 that a market falling to `FROZEN` posture drives every `ACTIVE` policy in that market to `SUSPENDED`, and that `verification_required` records whether the programme demands the verification pass at all — since the honour edge is declared only out of `VERIFIED`, a claim reaches the register only by way of verification.

A claim is filed at the register by a `StoreAssociate` or a `StoreManager` acting for their own store. The payload names the `policy_id` in force, the `product_id` the shopper wants matched, the `competitor_id` whose price they are citing, the `store_id` they are standing in — which must equal the caller's `X-User-ID` — the `claimed_price_cents` they say the competitor charges (strictly greater than 0), and the `claimed_observed_at` instant at which they saw it. An optional `competitor_evidence_ref` carries a receipt or screenshot reference and is null when nothing was supplied. Store, product, competitor and policy must all belong to one market, or the claim is refused with `ERR_MARKET_MISMATCH`. Intake does not test the policy's own `status`: a claim naming a `SUSPENDED` policy is filed successfully and is stopped only later, when verification is started.

Four eligibility checks guard intake, and each has its own code. The cited competitor must have `status` `ACTIVE`, must carry `price_match_eligible` true, and must have a `tier` that appears in the policy's `eligible_competitor_tiers`; any of those failing yields `ERR_COMPETITOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). The product's `category_code` must not appear in the policy's `excluded_category_codes`, or the claim is refused with `ERR_CATEGORY_EXCLUDED` (HTTP 422). The `claimed_price_cents` must not sit below the product's `map_floor_cents`, because honouring such a claim would breach the brand's minimum advertised price; a claim below that floor is refused with `ERR_MAP_FLOOR_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422). And the sighting must be timely in both directions: a `claimed_observed_at` later than the logical clock's current instant is refused with `ERR_OBSERVATION_IN_FUTURE` (HTTP 422), while a sighting whose age — the logical clock's current instant less `claimed_observed_at` — exceeds the policy's `claim_window_hours` is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422).

At creation the claim resolves `verification_link_id` exactly once: it is the identifier of the `CONFIRMED` match for the claim's product-and-competitor pair, or null when no such match exists at that instant. This resolution is never repeated. Confirming the match afterwards does not rescue a claim that was filed without one, which is why a claim carrying a null `verification_link_id` fails at decision time rather than at intake.

The honoured price is computed at creation and then frozen for the claim's whole life. It starts from a **beat-the-price bonus** whose tier is chosen from `claimed_price_cents`: a claimed price strictly below 2000 cents earns no bonus at all, a claimed price of at least 2000 cents but strictly below 10000 cents earns five percent, and a claimed price of 10000 cents or more earns ten percent. Both breakpoints are strict, so a claim of exactly 10000 cents lands in the ten-percent tier. The bonus is `claimed_price_cents` multiplied by that tier fraction, rounded half up, and then capped at 500 cents; the result is stored as `beat_bonus_cents`. **The tier is selected from the claimed price and is never re-derived from the outcome.** That ordering is pinned not merely because re-deriving would give a different answer but because it would be ill-defined: a claim of 2050 cents on a product whose `effective_floor_cents` is 1500 yields a bonus of 103 cents and an honoured price of 1947 cents under the pinned order, whereas keying the tier on the honoured price has no fixed point — the five-percent tier produces 1947, which sits in the no-bonus tier, and the no-bonus tier produces 2050, which sits in the five-percent tier.

Subtracting `beat_bonus_cents` from `claimed_price_cents` gives a pre-floor figure, and `honored_price_cents` is whichever is larger of that figure and the product's `effective_floor_cents` — the same hard floor that governs repricing, so a price match can no more breach margin or minimum advertised price than an automated reprice can. `discount_cents` is the product's `own_price_cents` less `honored_price_cents`. `required_approval_tier` is then `ASSOCIATE` when `discount_cents` is less than or equal to the store's `override_authority_cents`, and `MANAGER` otherwise. All four of these figures, along with the store's override authority they were measured against, are read at creation and frozen thereafter.

Two fiscal checks close out intake. The claim must actually save the shopper money, meaning the product's `own_price_cents` must be strictly greater than the computed `honored_price_cents` so that `discount_cents` is at least 1; a claim that saves nothing is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NO_SAVINGS` (HTTP 422). And the discount must fit under the policy's cap: `discount_cents` must be no greater than the product's `own_price_cents` multiplied by the policy's `max_match_discount_pct`, rounded half up. Exceeding that figure is refused with `ERR_MATCH_DISCOUNT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). A surviving claim is stored with `status` `SUBMITTED`, `evidence_round` 0, and both `rejection_reason` and `reversal_reason` null. Claims are listed filtered by `policy_id`, `product_id`, `competitor_id`, `store_id`, `verification_link_id`, `status` and `required_approval_tier`.

Verification is adjudicated by a `StoreManager` — confined to its own store — or by a `ComplianceOfficer`, who is not. Starting verification moves the claim `SUBMITTED → VERIFYING` and requires the governing policy's `status` to be `ACTIVE`, a `SUSPENDED` policy being refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409); if the request supplies a `competitor_evidence_ref` it overwrites whatever the claim already held, and supplying none on this edge is perfectly acceptable. The same capability also restarts a parked claim, moving it `NEEDS_EVIDENCE → VERIFYING`, and on that edge a non-empty `competitor_evidence_ref` is mandatory — fresh evidence is the entire point of the round trip — a request without one being refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Because that requirement belongs to the edge rather than to the payload, it is evaluated only once the claim's state has selected the edge: a claim in a state that declares no verification edge at all still fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` even when no reference was supplied. On the restart the claim's `evidence_round` increases by 1 and the supplied reference overwrites the old one.

Closing verification is a separate act, taking a `decision` of `VERIFIED`, `REJECTED` or `NEEDS_EVIDENCE` and an optional `reason`. `NEEDS_EVIDENCE` parks the claim, moving it `VERIFYING → NEEDS_EVIDENCE` with no ledger movement, with `honored_price_cents` still frozen at its creation value and with `evidence_round` untouched — only the restart out of `NEEDS_EVIDENCE` advances that counter. `REJECTED` moves the claim `VERIFYING → REJECTED`, a terminal state, and records the supplied reason as `rejection_reason`, leaving that field null when the request carried no `reason` at all; a `reason` supplied alongside either of the other two decisions is validated as a non-empty string and then discarded, since neither edge has a field to keep it in. `VERIFIED` moves the claim `VERIFYING → VERIFIED` and is the strictest edge: the cited competitor must still have `status` `ACTIVE` and `price_match_eligible` true, its `tier` must still appear in the policy's `eligible_competitor_tiers` — any of those failing yields `ERR_COMPETITOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, exactly as at intake, since a banner suspended between filing and adjudication must not be matched — and the claim's `verification_link_id` must be non-null, so that the claim can be checked against collected data at all. A claim whose `verification_link_id` is null is refused at this edge with `ERR_NO_CONFIRMED_MATCH` (HTTP 422). The governing policy's `status`, by contrast, is not re-tested here: a policy suspended between the start of verification and its close does not stand in the way of a `VERIFIED` decision, the `ACTIVE` requirement belonging to the entry into verification alone. No ledger movement occurs on verification.

The evidence loop, like the match review loop, is bounded. Both moving a claim into `NEEDS_EVIDENCE` and restarting verification out of it require `evidence_round` to be strictly less than 2, so a claim may go around twice; the third attempt to enter the loop is refused with `ERR_MAX_EVIDENCE_ROUNDS` (HTTP 409).

### 3.9. Honouring a Claim, the Store Override Ledger, and Reversal

Honouring is what the shopper experiences, and either a `StoreAssociate` or a `StoreManager` performs it — both confined by `X-User-ID` to the claim's own `store_id`. A claim may be honoured only from `VERIFIED`; only while the market's `pricing_posture` is anything other than `FROZEN`; only by a `StoreManager` when the claim's `required_approval_tier` is `MANAGER`, an associate attempting it being refused with `ERR_OVERRIDE_AUTHORITY_EXCEEDED`; and only while the store's daily override budget can absorb it, meaning the store's current `override_budget_used_cents` plus the claim's `discount_cents` is less than or equal to the governing policy's `daily_override_budget_cents` — where the governing policy on this gate is the one the claim itself names in `policy_id`, read whatever its `status` has since become, and not whichever policy happens to be `ACTIVE` in the market. Equality is allowed — a claim that exactly exhausts the budget is honoured — and anything beyond it is refused with `ERR_OVERRIDE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).

A successful honour moves the claim `VERIFIED → HONORED` and sets off a three-level cascade. At **level one** the authorisation is written to the append-only ledger: a new override log row is appended carrying the `claim_id`, a `direction` of `DEBIT`, an `approval_tier` equal to the claim's `required_approval_tier`, the caller's `X-User-Role` as `approved_by_role` and their `X-User-ID` as `approved_by_user_id`, an `approved_discount_cents` equal to the claim's `discount_cents`, and a snapshot of the claim's `honored_price_cents`. The store then recomputes `override_budget_used_cents` and `override_budget_remaining_cents`, and the policy's `honored_claim_count` increases by 1. Neither the store row nor the policy row is itself mutated by that recomputation — all three figures are derived when they are read — so their `version` and `updated_at` values do not move; the only row whose `version` the honour advances is the claim's own.

At **level two**, caused by that ledger row existing, the service synthesizes a price change order so the shelf price and the register agree. Its `origin` is `PRICE_MATCH`, its `source_claim_id` is the claim, its `product_id` is the claim's product, its `proposed_price_cents` is the claim's `honored_price_cents`, its `signage_store_id` is the claim's `store_id`, its `effective_from` is the logical clock's current instant, its `effective_until` is null, its `rule_id`, `benchmark_price_cents`, `requested_price_cents` and `supersedes_order_id` are all null, its `authored_by_user_id` is the honouring caller's `X-User-ID` while `decided_by_user_id` stays null because no separate decision was ever taken, and it is born already in `status` `APPROVED` with `signage_status` `QUEUED` — it skips drafting, submission and decision entirely, because the verification workflow has already served as its approval. Its `target_price_cents`, `banded_price_cents` and `effective_floor_cents` are derived under the `HAND_SET` variant of §3.6 over the claim's honoured price, but `proposed_price_cents` is that frozen honoured price verbatim and is not lifted by the floor clamp: if the product's `effective_floor_cents` has risen since the claim was created, the synthesized order still proposes the honoured price and merely reports `floor_binding` true. The synthesized order is also exempt from the effective-window overlap test of §3.6, so it may overlap a live order on the same product, and no `expected_product_version` is involved — though creating it increases the product's `version` by 1. At **level three**, caused by that order becoming approved, the order runs print-batch selection exactly as an ordinary approval does: on a hit its identifier is appended to the batch's `queued_order_ids`, the batch's `item_count` is recomputed, the order's `label_batch_id` is set, and the store recomputes `label_slots_used_today` and `label_slots_remaining_today`.

The **override budget ledger** itself is an aggregate, not a counter. Take the UTC calendar date of the logical clock's current instant. Consider every override log row whose claim belongs to this store and whose `created_at` falls on that date; rows from earlier dates are excluded entirely, which is how the budget resets at midnight UTC. `override_budget_used_cents` is the sum of `approved_discount_cents` over the `DEBIT` rows minus the sum of `approved_discount_cents` over the `CREDIT` rows. `override_budget_remaining_cents` is the governing policy's `daily_override_budget_cents` less that used figure — and the policy governing those two store-level figures is the single `ACTIVE` policy of the store's own market, so when the market holds no `ACTIVE` policy at all `override_budget_remaining_cents` is null and the §2.12 identity is vacuous for that store. Both figures are signed and nothing clamps them: a claim honoured on one UTC date and reversed on the next leaves only the compensating credit inside the window, so `override_budget_used_cents` can read negative and `override_budget_remaining_cents` can then exceed `daily_override_budget_cents`. The ledger is append-only: a reversal never edits the debit row, it writes a compensating credit row. The conservation invariant of §2.12 must hold after every honour and every reversal.

Reversal exists for fraudulent evidence and returned goods, and it is performed by a `StoreManager` — confined to the claim's own store — or by a `ComplianceOfficer`. It requires a `reversal_reason`, which is recorded on the claim as it moves `HONORED → REVERSED`, a terminal state. Every side effect of the honour is compensated. A second override log row is appended with `direction` `CREDIT`, an `approved_discount_cents` equal to the same `discount_cents`, an `approval_tier` and an `honored_price_cents` snapshot copied off the claim exactly as the debit row took them, and an `approved_by_role` and `approved_by_user_id` naming the reversing caller rather than the operator who honoured the claim, so the store's `override_budget_used_cents` returns to its pre-honour value and `override_budget_remaining_cents` with it; the policy's `honored_claim_count` decreases by 1; and the synthesized order whose `source_claim_id` names this claim moves `APPROVED → SUSPENDED` — only an order still sitting in `APPROVED` is touched, so a synthesized order that has already been executed stays `EXECUTED` and the product keeps the matched `own_price_cents`, a reversal never un-executing a price move — which releases its queued shelf label — `signage_status` moves `QUEUED → RELEASED`, `label_batch_id` is cleared, the order leaves its batch's `queued_order_ids` so the batch's `item_count` falls by 1, and the store's slot counters are recomputed.

The ledger rows written for a single claim are readable in `created_at` ascending order and can be filtered by `direction`, which is how an auditor reconstructs the sequence of a debit and its compensating credit.

### 3.10. Shelf Labels: Print Batches, Printer Capacity and the Signage Dimension

Changing a price in the system is only half the job; the shelf label has to be reprinted, and a store's label printer is a finite resource. A `StoreManager` acting for its own store, or a `PricingManager` acting for any store, reserves printer time as a **label print batch**, supplying the `store_id`, an inclusive `window_start` and an inclusive `window_end` that must be strictly later than the start — a `window_end` at or before it is refused at rank 5 with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and optionally a `reserved_item_count` of at least 0 covering labels planned up front, which defaults to 0 when omitted. A new batch has `status` `SCHEDULED`, an empty `queued_order_ids` list, a null `cancel_reason`, and an `item_count` equal to `reserved_item_count` plus the number of entries in `queued_order_ids` — so 0 plus the reservation at the start. Batches are listed filtered by `store_id` and by `status`.

Two capacity rules guard the reservation. The first is a **buffered window rule**: for every other batch on the same store whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `PRINTING` or `COMPLETED`, the new batch must either start at least 45 minutes after that batch's `window_end` or end at least 45 minutes before that batch's `window_start`. A gap of exactly 45 minutes is accepted; anything tighter — including windows that merely touch, and windows that do not overlap at all but sit closer than the buffer — is refused with `ERR_PRINT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409), because the printer needs changeover time between runs. The second is an **aggregate daily rule**: the store's current `label_slots_used_today` plus the new batch's `reserved_item_count` must not exceed its `label_printer_capacity_per_day`, or the reservation is refused with `ERR_PRINT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). That comparison is always made against today's usage as the logical clock reads it, even when the batch being reserved opens on some other date; and symmetrically, once created, a batch whose `window_start` does not fall on the clock's current date contributes nothing to `label_slots_used_today`. A reservation for a future date is therefore charged against today's capacity at the moment it is made and never counted again.

The **slot ledger** those rules read is, like the override budget, an aggregate over the logical clock's current UTC date. Consider every batch on this store whose `window_start` falls on that date and whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `PRINTING` or `COMPLETED`; `label_slots_used_today` is the sum of their `item_count` values and `label_slots_remaining_today` is `label_printer_capacity_per_day` less that sum. A `CANCELLED` batch contributes nothing, which is exactly how cancelling a batch returns its slots. The conservation invariant of §2.12 must hold after every batch operation and every signage move.

**Batch selection** is what an approval performs, and it never fails with an error. When the order being approved has a null `signage_store_id`, no label is needed: its `label_batch_id` stays null and its `signage_status` stays `NOT_REQUIRED`. Otherwise the candidates are the batches belonging to the store named by `signage_store_id` whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, whose `window_end` is strictly later than the logical clock's current instant, and only while that store's `label_slots_remaining_today` is at least 1. Candidates are ranked by the earliest `window_start`, tie-broken by the earliest `created_at` and then by ascending batch creation sequence, and the first is chosen: the order's identifier is appended to that batch's `queued_order_ids`, the batch's `item_count` is recomputed as `reserved_item_count` plus the number of queued entries, the order's `label_batch_id` is set to the batch, and `signage_status` becomes `QUEUED`. When the candidate pool is empty — no scheduled batch, every window already closed, or the day's slots exhausted — the order's `label_batch_id` simply stays null and its `signage_status` still becomes `QUEUED`: the label is queued but unbatched, awaiting a batch, and no error is raised. The `queued_order_ids` list is held in queue-insertion order, which is the `created_at` ascending order of the orders in it; it is not a foreign-key array, and the supported way to read the relationship in reverse is the change-order listing filtered by `label_batch_id`.

`signage_status` is a **second, independent dimension** on a change order, advancing without touching the order's `status`. It begins at `NOT_REQUIRED`, becomes `QUEUED` on approval when a store was named, becomes `PRINTED` when the batch holding it completes, and falls back to `RELEASED` whenever the order is suspended, superseded, or its batch is cancelled — from where a later re-approval can return it to `QUEUED`. The two dimensions really are independent: an order can be `EXECUTED` while its `signage_status` is still `QUEUED`, and a batch completing changes signage without moving the order's `status` at all.

A batch is driven forward by supplying a `to_status`, which accepts `PRINTING` and `COMPLETED` and nothing else: cancellation is a separate capability and `SCHEDULED` is no destination at all, so either of those values fails the enum check of §2.11 rank 6 with `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`. Moving `SCHEDULED → PRINTING` requires the batch to be `SCHEDULED` and the logical clock to have reached its `window_start`; an attempt before that instant is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Orders already queued keep `signage_status` `QUEUED` through this move. Moving `PRINTING → COMPLETED` requires the batch to be `PRINTING` and carries no timing precondition of its own — a printing batch may be completed at any instant, before or after the logical clock passes its `window_end` — and it is what marks the labels as physically produced: every order in the batch's `queued_order_ids` whose `signage_status` is `QUEUED` moves `QUEUED → PRINTED`, while those orders' own `status` values are untouched. The batch keeps its `queued_order_ids` and each printed order keeps its `label_batch_id`, so a completed batch still names the labels it produced. A completed batch keeps occupying its slots, so `label_slots_used_today` does not change on completion. `COMPLETED` is terminal.

Cancellation is available from `SCHEDULED` and from `PRINTING`, and both edges behave identically. The supplied `cancel_reason` is recorded, every order in `queued_order_ids` moves `signage_status` `QUEUED → RELEASED` and has its `label_batch_id` cleared, `queued_order_ids` becomes empty and `item_count` becomes 0 — the stored `reserved_item_count` is left exactly as it was, `item_count` reading 0 only because a `CANCELLED` batch is defined to contribute nothing — and the store recomputes `label_slots_used_today` and `label_slots_remaining_today` — a `CANCELLED` batch contributes nothing to either. `CANCELLED` is terminal, and advancing or cancelling a batch that has already reached `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. In every one of these operations a `StoreManager` must be acting for the batch's own store, while a `PricingManager` is not so confined.
