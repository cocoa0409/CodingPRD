# Fuel Tax and Registration Apportionment API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Interstate motor carriers live with a peculiar accounting burden: they buy fuel in one jurisdiction and burn it in another, and they register a single power unit against a dozen tax authorities at once. The International Fuel Tax Agreement settles the first problem by having the carrier report where its wheels actually turned and where its receipts were actually issued, then netting the two into a payment or a refund per jurisdiction. The International Registration Plan settles the second by apportioning one annual plate fee across jurisdictions in proportion to reported distance. Both regimes share one weakness — they are self-reported — and both compensate with the same instrument: records, retained for years, subject to audit, and penalised when the carrier substitutes reconstruction for evidence.

This document specifies the Fuel Tax and Registration Apportionment API, the compliance backbone that a jurisdiction's motor carrier tax desk and its licensees share. The system ingests telematics trips and detects the odometer gaps that betray missing data, admits analyst reconstruction of those gaps only inside a bounded window and only against a cited document, collects fuel purchase records and admits into tax-paid gallons only those that are both receipted and pump-taxed, and then settles a quarter on the resulting mileage and fuel bases — twice, if an auditor later disagrees with the carrier's own figures. In parallel it runs the registration side: distance schedules on either an actual or an estimated basis, apportionment percentages frozen at invoicing, mid-year additions, deletions and transfers priced by proration against those frozen percentages, and the decals, cab cards and licence copies that must reach a named driver before a truck can legally roll.

Three design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **segregation of duties is enforced by user identity, not by role**: the analyst who reconstructed a trip may not accept it, and the preparer who computed a return may not file it, even though both actors hold a role that nominally permits the second step. Second, **conserved quantities must reconcile exactly** — a disallowed purchase returns the carrier's running gallons to the value they held before the purchase existed, and a reversed fleet supplement returns the application's fee total to the value it held before the approval. Third, **every date and window is measured against a server-controlled logical clock**, never against wall-clock time, so that retention expiry, reconstruction windows, quarter boundaries, proration months and credential validity are all deterministically reproducible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section applies across the whole surface. Where a later business-logic narrative appears to contradict a rule stated here, the rule stated here is the one that binds, unless the narrative names an explicit exception.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user acts. Four roles exist and their spellings are exact: `CarrierAdmin`, `MileageAnalyst`, `TaxPreparer`, and `ComplianceOfficer`. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` carries any string other than one of those four, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A header whose value is nothing but whitespace counts as empty for this purpose, and surrounding whitespace is trimmed from both header values before the role is matched and before `X-User-ID` is recorded or compared. If both headers are well-formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The two headers mean different things depending on the role. When `X-User-Role` is `CarrierAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Carrier`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Carrier` compare the resource's carrier reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `CarrierAdmin` request is confined to its own fleet: the target vehicle's `carrier_id`, the parent application's `carrier_id`, or, for a credential handover, the `carrier_id` of the vehicle the credential was printed for, must all equal `X-User-ID`. A violation is rejected with `ERR_CARRIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This scope check governs mid-year supplement filing, credential distribution, driver enrolment, vehicle enrolment, trip capture, and fuel purchase recording — and nothing else. In particular no read is ever scope-checked: a `CarrierAdmin` may read any resource of any carrier, and no filter is silently applied to narrow a collection read to the caller's own fleet.

When `X-User-Role` is `MileageAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it — as `reconstructed_by_user_id` on a `TripRecord` when a reconstruction is written — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `TaxPreparer`: `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier, recorded as `prepared_by_user_id` on a `FuelTaxReturn` or a `RegistrationApplication`, and never resolved against an entity row. And the same again for `ComplianceOfficer`: an opaque operator identifier, recorded as `filed_by_user_id` on a filed return, `reviewed_by_user_id` on a decided application or a completed retention review, and `decided_by_user_id` on a decided fleet change, but never treated as an entity `id`. Because these three identifiers are opaque, no carrier-scope check applies to them; a `MileageAnalyst`, `TaxPreparer` or `ComplianceOfficer` may act across carriers.

The four roles divide the work as follows. A `CarrierAdmin` is the registrant's own back office: it enrols drivers and power units, uploads telematics trips and fuel receipts, requests mid-year supplements, and hands credentials to drivers. A `MileageAnalyst` staffs the mileage and fuel evidence desk: it reconstructs gap-flagged trips from supporting documents and disallows unsupported fuel purchases, and it may also capture trips and record purchases. A `TaxPreparer` opens and computes quarterly returns and opens, schedules and submits registration applications, and may file mid-year supplements; it can neither file a return nor invoice an application. A `ComplianceOfficer` is the jurisdiction-side authority: it publishes reference data and registers carriers, reviews trips, files returns, invoices applications, issues credentials, decides and reverses fleet changes, runs audit selection and retention review, and drives the logical clock. All four roles may call every read endpoint.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`; the prefix is followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the upper-case letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`, generated afresh for every request, error responses included.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`. It is read from real server time, not from the logical clock of §2.5, so moving the clock never moves it.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints and appear nowhere else: the `meta` of a single-object success response carries only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx and 5xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

Three failures arise from the transport rather than from any business rule and are decided by routing, before the header checks of §2.1 run, so they answer identically whether or not the caller supplied credentials. A request whose path matches no declared endpoint answers `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404); a request whose path exists but not for that HTTP method answers `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 405); and an unexpected server fault answers `ERR_INTERNAL` (HTTP 500). All three carry the error envelope above. A trailing slash on any path is tolerated and routes to the same endpoint as the slashless form.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection read accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. Both are reported back in `meta` alongside `total_count` — the number of rows matching the filters before the window is applied — and `has_more`, a boolean that is true exactly when rows remain beyond the returned window. A value of `limit` outside 1 through 100, a negative `offset`, or a value of either that is not a base-10 integer, is a request-shape error and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

The default ordering for a collection is `created_at` ascending. Several collections carry a domain-natural ordering that supersedes the default, and in each case `created_at` ascending remains the final tiebreaker: declared jurisdictions sort by `jurisdiction_code` ascending; the fee chart sorts by `jurisdiction_code` ascending then `weight_group_code` ascending; trip records sort by `trip_start_utc` ascending; mileage legs sort by `leg_sequence` ascending; fuel purchases sort by `purchase_date` ascending; quarterly returns sort by `filing_year` ascending then `filing_quarter` ascending; return jurisdiction lines sort by `jurisdiction_code` ascending; registration applications sort by `registration_year` ascending; distance schedule lines sort by `jurisdiction_code` ascending; fleet change requests sort by `effective_date` ascending; and a carrier's audit selections sort by `selection_year` ascending then `selection_quarter` ascending. Carriers, drivers, vehicles and credentials use the plain `created_at` ascending default. A sort key holding a date or an instant is compared as the stored string, which for the canonical forms this API stores is the same as chronological order. The one exception is `trip_start_utc`: trip records are ordered on that instant parsed and normalised to UTC rather than on the string as written, so two trips recorded with different UTC offsets still sort chronologically.

Filters offered on a collection narrow the row set before ordering and windowing. They are always optional, and supplying two filters conjoins them. A filter matches by exact, case-sensitive equality against the stored value, with no trimming. Unlike `limit` and `offset`, a filter value that cannot be read in its field's own type is **not** a request-shape error: it simply matches no row, and the endpoint answers 200 with an empty `data` array and `total_count` 0. That covers a non-integer offered where an integer field is filtered, and anything other than `true`, `1`, `yes`, `false`, `0` or `no` — compared case-insensitively — offered where a boolean field is filtered. An unrecognised query parameter name is ignored rather than rejected.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource is addressed by `id`, a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no client-supplied component — a random version-4 UUID rendered in the canonical 36-character lower-case hyphenated form. Clients never propose an `id`.

Most entities carry the full common property set: `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, and `version`. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`. Both are read from real server time, not from the logical clock of §2.5 — the logical clock governs only the business instants and dates enumerated there — and that real-time source is strictly increasing, so no two rows ever share a `created_at` and `created_at` ascending is therefore a total order. At creation `updated_at` is set to exactly the same instant as `created_at`. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations that arrive as the side effect of a cascade rather than as a direct write. `updated_at` is refreshed on the same occasions. The entities carrying all four are `Carrier`, `Vehicle`, `Driver`, `TripRecord`, `FuelPurchase`, `FuelTaxReturn`, `RegistrationApplication`, `FleetChangeRequest`, `Credential`, and `AuditSelection`.

The three-fractional-digit rendering applies to server-stamped instants only — `created_at`, `updated_at`, `api_request_timestamp`, `filed_at`, `apportion_frozen_at` and `distributed_at`. A date or instant supplied by the client — `trip_start_utc`, `trip_end_utc`, `purchase_date`, `effective_date`, `valid_from_date`, `valid_to_date` — is validated for parseability and then stored and echoed back byte-for-byte as the client wrote it, without normalisation or reformatting; a plain calendar date must be written as `YYYY-MM-DD`, while an instant may be written with any UTC offset, or with none, in which case UTC is assumed for the server's own comparisons.

Five entities are deliberately lighter. `Jurisdiction`, `WeightGroupFee`, `JurisdictionMileageLeg`, `ReturnJurisdictionLine`, and `DistanceScheduleLine` carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on them, and neither field appears in their serialised representation. This is not an oversight: none of the three line-shaped entities is ever versioned. When a computation is superseded, the existing rows are marked as voided and a fresh set is inserted rather than being rewritten, so there is nothing on a line to version — the single in-place fill is the one described in §3.7, where invoicing writes a `DistanceScheduleLine`'s computed percentage and fee onto the same `ACTIVE` row that already carries the reported distance.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The server keeps a **logical clock** and every temporal rule in this specification reads it: record-retention expiry, the manual-reconstruction window, quarter-window membership, proration month derivation, credential validity assignment, `activated_on_date`, `apportion_frozen_at`, `filed_at` and `distributed_at`. Before the clock is ever moved it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. It must not fall back to real wall-clock time under any circumstance.

A `ComplianceOfficer` may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting capability. The move takes effect immediately for all subsequent requests and is not validated against the clock's previous value — time may be advanced or rewound freely, because this exists so that a multi-year compliance narrative can be exercised deterministically. `to_iso8601` is required and must parse as an ISO 8601 instant; absent, empty or unparseable, it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). The instant is normalised to UTC before being stored, and the move answers 200 with a `data` object of exactly one field, `current_utc`, holding the new clock reading in the same three-fractional-digit UTC form used elsewhere. The clock is process state, not a persisted resource: it carries no `id`, no timestamps and no `version`, and it returns to `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` whenever the server restarts.

### 2.6. Jurisdiction Coupling by Natural Code

Jurisdictions are the one thing in this system that is not referenced by `id`. A `Jurisdiction` row is keyed for business purposes by `jurisdiction_code`, exactly two uppercase letters, unique across all declared jurisdictions; a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_JURISDICTION` (HTTP 409). The two-letter shape is enforced only where a jurisdiction is declared, and a malformed code there is a request-shape error rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than a jurisdiction-declaration error. Wherever the declaration check below applies, a supplied code is matched against the declared rows without any shape check of its own, so a code of any shape that matches nothing is reported as undeclared rather than as malformed. Mileage legs, fuel purchases, weight group fee cells, distance schedule lines, return jurisdiction lines and a carrier's `base_jurisdiction_code` all couple to a jurisdiction by that two-letter code rather than by a foreign key. Whenever a request names a `jurisdiction_code` — directly in its body, or nested inside a submitted legs list — the code must resolve to a declared `Jurisdiction`; if any one of them does not, the request is rejected with `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_DECLARED` (HTTP 422). This check applies uniformly to all five operations that admit a jurisdiction code from the client: declaring a weight group fee cell, capturing a telematics trip together with its mileage legs, reconstructing a trip's legs manually, recording a fuel purchase, and submitting a registration application's distance schedule.

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right before any body content is examined. A nested read such as a trip's mileage legs, a return's jurisdiction lines, a carrier's drivers, a carrier's audit selections, or an application's distance schedule first resolves the parent id and answers with that parent's not-found error if the parent is absent; only then does it apply the child filters. A request whose parent is missing therefore never reports a child-level problem.

### 2.7. Numeric Precision and Pinned Evaluation Order

All rounding in this system is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero. Scale is fixed per quantity kind and never varies by context: distances and mileage in miles carry 1 decimal place; gallons carry 3 decimal places; monetary amounts, tax rates aside, carry 2 decimal places; jurisdiction tax and surcharge rates per gallon carry 4 decimal places; compliance and deficiency ratios carry 4 decimal places; apportionment percentages carry 6 decimal places; and risk scores are integers. A client-supplied quantity is itself quantised to its kind's scale under the same round-half-up rule at the moment it is stored, so a value offered at finer precision is stored and echoed at the pinned scale; the field's own bound, however, is checked against the value exactly as supplied, before that quantisation. Every such quantity is serialised as a JSON number rather than a string, and JSON numbers do not preserve trailing zeros — a monetary amount of 100.00 appears as `100.0`, a gallons figure of 12.500 as `12.5`, and a zero of any scale as `0.0`. A quantity that is null is serialised as `null`.

Three evaluation orders are pinned because they are observable in stored values and a different order would produce different numbers:

- **Fleet MPG is rounded before it is used as a divisor.** The quarter's fleet miles per gallon is rounded to 2 decimal places first, and that rounded figure — not the raw quotient — is what each jurisdiction's taxable miles are divided by.
- **Totals sum already-rounded line amounts.** Where a total is described as the sum over a line set, each line's amount is rounded to its own scale first and the total is the sum of those rounded amounts. The total is never computed by rounding a full-precision sum.
- **Risk scores are rounded once, at the end.** The weighted components of a risk score are combined at full precision and the single resulting sum is rounded to a whole number. Rounding each weighted component separately produces a different score and is wrong.

Apportionment percentages are **not** re-normalised to sum to exactly 1.000000. Each is rounded independently to 6 decimal places, and any residue left by that rounding stays in the schedule.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Baselines

Two write operations sit on top of a parent record whose contents form the baseline of the write's own arithmetic. In both cases the write requires the client to declare which version of that parent it computed against, and the write is refused if the parent has moved on. Neither field is optional, and omitting it is a request-shape error rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

A `POST /apportionment/fleet-change-requests` request must carry `expected_registration_application_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `RegistrationApplication`. If that value does not equal the application's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_APPLICATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Nothing in the state machine blocks a second supplement — a carrier may legitimately file any number of mid-year supplements against one invoiced application, and the second create would succeed on its own terms. It is refused solely because the application's fee basis, `total_apportioned_fee` and `apportioned_vehicle_count`, has advanced past the snapshot the requester priced its supplement against.

A `POST /apportionment/fuel-tax-returns/{id}/prepare` request must carry `expected_carrier_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the return's `Carrier`. If that value does not equal the carrier's current `version`, the prepare is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CARRIER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Re-preparation is an ordinary product operation here — a `PREPARED` return may be recomputed and legitimately supersede its prior jurisdiction line set, so the state machine permits it and a second sequential prepare would otherwise succeed. It is refused because the carrier record backing the settlement — `ifta_license_status`, `latest_prepared_fleet_mpg`, `unsubstantiated_mileage_ratio`, `audit_flag` — has advanced past the version the preparer observed.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. In particular, deciding or reversing a fleet change, reviewing a return, submitting or reviewing an application, issuing credentials, distributing a credential, reconstructing or reviewing a trip, disallowing a purchase and running a retention review all take no expected-version input.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Three review steps are barred to the user who performed the step being reviewed, and the bar is on `X-User-ID`, not on role. Reviewing a trip is refused when the caller's `X-User-ID` equals the trip's `reconstructed_by_user_id`. Reviewing a quarterly return is refused when the caller's `X-User-ID` equals the return's `prepared_by_user_id`; the same identity is also what `filed_by_user_id` must differ from once stamped. Reviewing a registration application is refused when the caller's `X-User-ID` equals the application's `prepared_by_user_id`. In all three cases the rejection is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Holding the `ComplianceOfficer` role is necessary for these reviews but never sufficient.

The bar applies only where an upstream identity was actually recorded. A trip that reached its reviewable state without any reconstruction has a null `reconstructed_by_user_id`, and no segregation comparison is possible or performed.

### 2.10. Cascades, Void-and-Replace, and Derived State

Computed line sets in this system are never edited. When a computation is superseded — a return is re-prepared, a return is sent back for correction, a filed return is re-settled on the audit basis, an application's schedule is voided, a trip's legs are replaced by a reconstruction — the existing rows have their status field moved from `ACTIVE` to `VOIDED` and, where a replacement exists, a fresh set is inserted with status `ACTIVE`. Voided rows remain readable through the corresponding line-listing endpoint, which is the only channel through which cascade results are observable. Nothing is ever hard-deleted.

Cascades in this system run transitively and are immediate: every hop completes inside the request that triggered the first hop, and the response reflects the settled end state. When a cascade hop mutates an entity that carries `version`, that entity's `version` increments by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed, exactly as a direct write would do. A cascade that touches one entity twice within one request increments its `version` once per mutation.

Two entities carry two independent state dimensions on a single row. A `Vehicle` carries `vehicle_status`, its fleet dimension, and `credential_status`, its credential dimension; they move on entirely different triggers and neither is derivable from the other. A `Carrier` carries `ifta_license_status`, its quarterly-filing dimension, and `irp_account_status`, its apportioned-registration dimension; an IFTA suspension deliberately leaves the IRP account alone, so registration work continues while filing is frozen, and only a revocation couples the two.

`Vehicle.credential_status` is **derived**, never written directly. It is recomputed from the vehicle's `Credential` rows every time one of those rows changes status, under exactly this rule: `NONE` when the vehicle has no `Credential` whose `credential_status` is `ISSUED` or `DISTRIBUTED`; `DISTRIBUTED` when at least one such credential exists and every one of them is `DISTRIBUTED`; and `ISSUED` in every other case. That rule admits five observable movements — `NONE` to `ISSUED` when the first credentials are printed, `ISSUED` to `DISTRIBUTED` when the last outstanding credential reaches a driver, `DISTRIBUTED` back to `ISSUED` when a fresh credential is printed for a vehicle whose earlier credentials were all handed out, and `DISTRIBUTED` to `NONE` or `ISSUED` to `NONE` once no credential of the vehicle remains in `ISSUED` or `DISTRIBUTED`. Likewise, `Jurisdiction.is_surcharge_jurisdiction` is derived and true exactly when that jurisdiction's `surcharge_rate_per_gallon` is greater than 0.0000.

### 2.11. Validation Precedence

Checks are evaluated in a fixed order and the **first** violated check decides the response; no later check runs. Implementations must not report a lower-priority problem when a higher-priority one is also present. The order is:

1. Header presence and role membership — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorisation for the endpoint — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. Existence of the path resource, in this sequence by resource kind: carrier (`ERR_CARRIER_NOT_FOUND`), vehicle (`ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`), trip record (`ERR_TRIP_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`), fuel purchase (`ERR_FUEL_PURCHASE_NOT_FOUND`), fuel tax return (`ERR_FUEL_TAX_RETURN_NOT_FOUND`), registration application (`ERR_REGISTRATION_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`), fleet change request (`ERR_FLEET_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`), credential (`ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_FOUND`), audit selection (`ERR_AUDIT_SELECTION_NOT_FOUND`). The two reference tables are read by `id` as well, and an unknown id there answers `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_WEIGHT_GROUP_FEE_NOT_FOUND`, both HTTP 404.
4. Existence of every foreign key supplied in the request body, evaluated in this body-field order: `carrier_id`, `vehicle_id`, `replaced_vehicle_id`, `registration_application_id`, `distributed_to_driver_id` — answering with `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REGISTRATION_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate, all HTTP 404.
5. Request shape — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
6. Jurisdiction declaration for every referenced `jurisdiction_code` — `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_DECLARED`.
7. Carrier scope for a `CarrierAdmin` caller — `ERR_CARRIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
8. Optimistic-concurrency version match — `ERR_STALE_APPLICATION_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_CARRIER_VERSION`.
9. State compatibility of the target resource with the requested transition — `ERR_INVALID_TRIP_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PURCHASE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_RETURN_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`, `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_ISSUED` or `ERR_AUDIT_SELECTION_NOT_SELECTED`, all HTTP 409.
10. IFTA licence liveness — `ERR_CARRIER_LICENSE_INACTIVE`.
11. IRP account liveness — `ERR_CARRIER_IRP_INACTIVE`.
12. Vehicle-state compatibility with a fleet change's `change_type` — `ERR_VEHICLE_STATE_INCOMPATIBLE`.
13. Transfer irreversibility — `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_REVERSIBLE`.
14. Unresolved in-flight trip review inside a quarter being prepared — `ERR_TRIP_GAP_UNRESOLVED`.
15. Reconstruction paper trail — `ERR_RECONSTRUCTION_SUPPORT_MISSING`.
16. Record retention period — `ERR_RETENTION_PERIOD_EXPIRED`.
17. Manual reconstruction window — `ERR_RECONSTRUCTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
18. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
19. Natural-key uniqueness — `ERR_DUPLICATE_JURISDICTION`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_WEIGHT_GROUP_FEE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_USDOT_NUMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_UNIT_NUMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_RETURN_PERIOD`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_APPLICATION_YEAR`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_AUDIT_PERIOD`.
20. One active distance schedule line per jurisdiction — `ERR_DUPLICATE_JURISDICTION_LINE`.
21. Driver–carrier match and driver liveness on a handover — `ERR_DRIVER_CARRIER_MISMATCH`.
22. Filed-return lock on a purchase disallowance — `ERR_PURCHASE_LOCKED_BY_FILED_RETURN`.
23. Fleet change effective date inside the registration year — `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_OUT_OF_YEAR`.
24. Trip interval overlap on the same vehicle — `ERR_TRIP_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`.
25. Driver credential window conflict — `ERR_DRIVER_CREDENTIAL_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.
26. Reconstructed leg mileage reconciliation — `ERR_LEG_MILEAGE_MISMATCH`.
27. Weight group fee chart completeness — `ERR_WEIGHT_GROUP_FEE_MISSING`.
28. Existence of exactly one filed return for an audited period — `ERR_NO_FILED_RETURN_FOR_PERIOD`.
29. Bond coverage at filing — `ERR_BOND_CEILING_EXCEEDED`.
30. Presence of accepted tax-paid gallons in the quarter — `ERR_NO_TAX_PAID_GALLONS`.
31. Presence of accepted mileage in the quarter — `ERR_NO_ACCEPTED_MILEAGE`.

Two consequences of this ordering are worth stating explicitly. Because state compatibility outranks every domain rule below it, a stale-state request never surfaces a fiscal or temporal complaint. And because carrier scope outranks optimistic concurrency, a `CarrierAdmin` reaching outside its own fleet is told so even when it also supplied a stale version.

Request-shape validation at step 5 covers: presence of every required field, correct types, membership of every enum value in its declared set, rejection of unknown fields, `gallons` strictly greater than 0.000, `start_odometer` and `end_odometer` each no smaller than 0.0, `end_odometer` strictly greater than `start_odometer`, `start_odometer` no smaller than the previous trip's `end_odometer` for the same vehicle (an odometer rollback is a shape error, not a gap), `trip_end_utc` strictly later than `trip_start_utc`, `reported_distance_miles` no smaller than 0.0, every `leg_miles` strictly greater than 0.0, a non-empty legs list, `expected_records_count` at least 1, `records_missing_count` between 0 and `expected_records_count` inclusive, `filing_quarter` one of 1, 2, 3 or 4, `declared_weight_lbs` strictly greater than 0, `late_filing_count` no smaller than 0, and `retention_deficiency_count` no smaller than 0.

Types are checked strictly. A value must be a JSON value of the kind the field declares: a numeric string is not a number, a boolean is never accepted where a number or an integer is expected, and a number carrying a fractional part is not an integer. A string field must hold a JSON string, and a string of nothing but whitespace counts as empty. An optional string field is no more tolerant on that point than a required one: supplied as an empty or whitespace-only string it is itself a shape error rather than being read as absent. The only two string fields exempt from that rule are `supporting_document_reference` and `receipt_reference`, whose emptiness is judged by the domain rules of §3.3 and §3.4 instead. An explicit `null` is treated exactly as an omitted field — it takes the field's documented default where one exists, and is reported as a missing required field where none does. A request that carries no body at all is read as an empty JSON object, so an endpoint all of whose fields are optional accepts an empty request; a body that is present but is not a JSON object, including one whose content type prevents it from being parsed as JSON at all, is itself a shape error rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Unknown-field rejection is exhaustive on every write endpoint, including those that take no fields at all, where any supplied field is unknown.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Reference Data, Licensee Registration and Fleet Enrolment

Nothing in this system can be computed until the jurisdiction desk has published two reference tables. The server holds no seeded or preloaded data of any kind: a freshly started process begins with every collection empty, and every row that exists was created by a client through the endpoints below. The first declares the member jurisdictions themselves. A `ComplianceOfficer` declares a jurisdiction with its two-letter `jurisdiction_code`, a display `name`, and a `fuel_tax_rate_per_gallon` — the rate, carried to 4 decimal places and never negative, that will be applied to net taxable gallons. Some jurisdictions additionally levy a surcharge that is not collected at the pump, and for those the declaration carries a `surcharge_rate_per_gallon`, likewise 4 decimal places and never negative, defaulting to 0.0000 when omitted. Whether a jurisdiction is a surcharge jurisdiction is not a matter of operator opinion: `is_surcharge_jurisdiction` is derived, and it is true exactly when `surcharge_rate_per_gallon` is greater than 0.0000. A client may include `is_surcharge_jurisdiction` in the declaration payload, but the derived rule governs the stored value. Declaring a code that already exists is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_JURISDICTION`.

The second reference table is the apportioned-fee chart. Each cell answers one question — what full annual registration fee does one jurisdiction charge for one weight group — and a `ComplianceOfficer` publishes it by supplying `jurisdiction_code`, a `weight_group_code` label such as the eighty-thousand-pound group, the `declared_weight_lbs` ceiling of that group in pounds (a whole number strictly greater than 0, stored and returned as an integer rather than a scaled decimal), and the `annual_fee_amount`, strictly greater than 0.00 and carried to 2 decimal places. The pair of `jurisdiction_code` and `weight_group_code` is unique across the chart; republishing an existing pair is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_WEIGHT_GROUP_FEE`. A cell whose `jurisdiction_code` has not been declared is refused with `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_DECLARED`.

Registering a motor carrier makes it simultaneously an IFTA licensee and an IRP account holder. A `ComplianceOfficer` supplies the `legal_name` exactly as it appears on the IFTA licence, the carrier's `usdot_number`, and the `base_jurisdiction_code` — the code of the jurisdiction where the licence is issued. Carrier registration is deliberately absent from §2.6's exhaustive list of the five operations that check a client-supplied jurisdiction code, so `base_jurisdiction_code` is required to be a non-empty string and is then stored verbatim: it is checked neither against the declared jurisdictions nor against the two-letter shape, and a carrier may therefore be registered before its base jurisdiction has been declared. A `usdot_number` already on file is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_USDOT_NUMBER`. The registration may also declare a `bond_ceiling_amount`, the largest quarterly amount the carrier is permitted to file without posting a bond rider; it is carried to 2 decimal places, may not be negative, and defaults to 5000.00 when omitted. Every other carrier attribute is system-owned and starts at a known value: `ifta_license_status` at `ACTIVE`, `irp_account_status` at `ACTIVE`, `tax_paid_gallons_ytd` at 0.000, `unsubstantiated_mileage_ratio` at 0.0000, `audit_flag` at false, `suspension_count` at 0, `latest_prepared_fleet_mpg` at null until the first return of that carrier is successfully prepared, and `version` at 1.

Drivers and power units are enrolled beneath a carrier by either the carrier's own back office or the jurisdiction desk. A driver enrolment carries the `carrier_id` of the employing carrier, the `full_name` that will be printed on the distribution log, and the `cdl_number`. Unlike a vehicle's `unit_number`, `cdl_number` carries no uniqueness rule: it is stored as supplied, no duplicate check runs against it, and the catalogue of §5 accordingly holds no driver-duplicate code. Every new driver starts with `driver_status` at `ACTIVE`, and the only other value that field ever takes is `INACTIVE`.

A power unit enrolment carries the `carrier_id`, the carrier-assigned `unit_number`, the `vin`, the `weight_group_code` under which its fee will be looked up, the `factory_price_amount` used by jurisdictions that assess value-based fees — carried to 2 decimal places and never negative, zero included — and the `registration_year` whose apportioned fleet the unit belongs to, a whole number of at least 1. All six of those fields are required. Neither `weight_group_code` nor `registration_year` is validated against the fee chart at enrolment; the chart is consulted only when an application naming that weight group is invoiced. The combination of `carrier_id`, `unit_number` and `registration_year` must be unique; a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_UNIT_NUMBER`. A newly enrolled unit is not yet plated: `vehicle_status` starts at `PENDING`, the derived `credential_status` reads `NONE`, `replaced_vehicle_id` is null, and `activated_on_date` is null. The unit becomes `ACTIVE` only when the registration application covering its year is invoiced, or when a mid-year addition or transfer supplement naming it is approved. Beyond `PENDING` and `ACTIVE`, `vehicle_status` also admits `DELETED` and the terminal `TRANSFERRED`.

### 3.2. Telematics Mileage Capture and Gap Detection

Distance enters the system one trip at a time. Either a `CarrierAdmin` — confined to its own fleet — or a `MileageAnalyst` captures a trip by supplying the `carrier_id` whose fleet distance the trip contributes to, the `vehicle_id` of the power unit that ran it, the `trip_start_utc` and `trip_end_utc` instants bounding it, the `start_odometer` and `end_odometer` readings, and a `legs` array decomposing the run into per-jurisdiction distances. Each element of `legs` is a JSON object naming a `jurisdiction_code` and a `leg_miles` figure strictly greater than 0.0 and carried to 1 decimal place, and carrying nothing else — an element that is not an object, or that offers a field other than those two, is a shape error exactly as an unknown top-level field would be; the array may not be empty, and every code in it must be a declared jurisdiction. Two elements may name the same jurisdiction, and nothing requires the named `vehicle_id` to belong to the named `carrier_id`: the trip's own `carrier_id` is the reference §2.1's carrier-scope check compares, and a unit enrolled under some other carrier is captured against without complaint. Each element becomes one `JurisdictionMileageLeg` row whose `trip_record_id` points back at the trip, whose `leg_status` starts at `ACTIVE`, and whose `leg_sequence` is assigned by the server from the element's 1-based position in the submitted array. `leg_sequence` is never supplied by the client, is at least 1, is unique among the trip's `ACTIVE` legs, and is the sort key whenever legs are processed in order. Legs are the child of a trip; the leg-listing read is the only channel through which reconstruction-replaced and cascade-voided legs become observable, and it accepts a `leg_status` filter for exactly that purpose.

A vehicle occupies **half-open** intervals in time. The new trip's interval runs from `trip_start_utc` inclusive to `trip_end_utc` exclusive, and it may not overlap the interval of any existing trip of the same `vehicle_id` whose `trip_status` is anything other than `REJECTED`. There is no buffer: a trip that starts at exactly the instant the previous one ended is adjacent, not overlapping, and is accepted. Any true overlap is refused with `ERR_TRIP_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`. (Contrast this deliberately with the closed, buffered geometry used for credential validity windows in §3.8.)

Gap detection then runs, before the trip's initial status is decided, and it is where telematics dropouts are caught. The trip's own distance is fixed first: `odometer_delta_miles` is the end odometer reading minus the start odometer reading, rounded to 1 decimal place. That subtraction is performed on the two readings exactly as the client supplied them and only its result is rounded, whereas each reading is separately quantised to 1 decimal place for storage, so on finer-grained input the stored `odometer_delta_miles` need not equal the difference of the two stored readings. The gap below, by contrast, is computed from the stored, already-quantised readings. The predecessor is then selected **chronologically, never by id**: among all trips of the same `vehicle_id` whose `trip_status` is not `REJECTED` and whose `trip_start_utc` is strictly earlier than the new trip's `trip_start_utc`, sort by `trip_start_utc` ascending with `created_at` ascending as tiebreaker and take the last element. If no such trip exists, `gap_miles_from_previous` is 0.0. Otherwise `gap_miles_from_previous` is the new trip's `start_odometer` minus the predecessor's `end_odometer`, rounded to 1 decimal place. Because rejected trips are invisible to predecessor selection, rejecting a trip re-links the odometer chain across it, and a later capture will compare against the trip before the rejected one.

If `gap_miles_from_previous` is **strictly greater** than the five-mile gap tolerance, the trip is born `GAP_FLAGGED`: unaccounted distance exists between the vehicle's last known position and this trip's start, and someone must account for it. A gap of exactly 5.0 miles is tolerated; 5.1 is flagged. Otherwise the trip is born `CAPTURED`, which is not the same thing as settled — a `CAPTURED` trip still requires an explicit review decision before its miles may enter any settlement. In both cases `mileage_source` starts at `TELEMATICS`, `accepted_leg_miles_total` is set to the sum of `leg_miles` over the trip's `ACTIVE` legs rounded to 1 decimal place, and `reconstructed_by_user_id` and `supporting_document_reference` are null.

Every capture also refreshes the carrier's compliance posture, because the whole point of gap detection is to make unsubstantiated distance visible. The carrier's `unsubstantiated_mileage_ratio` is recomputed as follows: the numerator is the sum of `odometer_delta_miles` over all of that carrier's trips whose `trip_status` is `GAP_FLAGGED` or `RECONSTRUCTED`; the denominator is the sum of `odometer_delta_miles` over all of that carrier's trips whose `trip_status` is anything other than `REJECTED`. If the denominator is 0.0 the ratio is 0.0000; otherwise the ratio is the numerator divided by the denominator, rounded to 4 decimal places. The carrier's `audit_flag` is then re-derived and is true exactly when `unsubstantiated_mileage_ratio` is **strictly greater** than fifteen hundredths — that is, greater than 0.1500. The carrier's `version` increments by 1. This recomputation is not confined to capture: it runs after every change to any trip's `trip_status`, and it runs again inside the quarterly settlement.

Note which statuses sit where. A trip that has been reconstructed but not yet accepted is still in the numerator, because a reconstruction is a claim and not yet evidence. An `ACCEPTED` reconstruction has been substantiated by a reviewer and leaves the numerator entirely. A `REJECTED` trip leaves both numerator and denominator, so rejecting a gap-flagged trip lowers the ratio by removing its miles from the fleet total rather than by substantiating them.

### 3.3. Manual Reconstruction and Trip Review

A `GAP_FLAGGED` trip has two possible futures. The carrier may leave it flagged, accepting that its miles stay out of every settlement and instead weigh on the carrier's `unsubstantiated_mileage_ratio` — this is a legitimate, settled outcome, not an unfinished one. Or a `MileageAnalyst` may reconstruct it from paper, replacing the trip's leg decomposition with one derived from fuel receipts, dispatch logs or toll statements.

Reconstruction is exclusive to the `MileageAnalyst` role and moves the trip `GAP_FLAGGED → RECONSTRUCTED`. Attempting it on a trip in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRIP_STATE`. The analyst supplies a `reconstructed_legs` array — each element carrying a `jurisdiction_code` and a `leg_miles` figure — together with a `supporting_document_reference` naming the retained document that substantiates the claim. If `supporting_document_reference` is absent, null, empty or nothing but whitespace, or if `reconstructed_legs` is absent, null or empty, the attempt is refused with `ERR_RECONSTRUCTION_SUPPORT_MISSING`. None of those omissions is treated as a request-shape error, so each answers 422 rather than 400 and each is reported only after the trip's status has been checked. A `reconstructed_legs` value that is present but otherwise malformed — not an array, an element that is not an object, an unrecognised field inside an element, or a `leg_miles` that is not a number strictly greater than 0.0 — remains an ordinary shape error, and a `supporting_document_reference` that is not a JSON string likewise. A reconstruction without a paper trail is not a reconstruction.

Two temporal gates then apply, in this order, and both are measured backwards from the logical clock to the trip's `trip_end_utc`. The outer gate is record retention: if more than 1460 days — four years of retained records — have elapsed since `trip_end_utc`, the source documents are no longer required to exist at all, so the trip cannot be reconstructed under any circumstance and the attempt is refused with `ERR_RETENTION_PERIOD_EXPIRED`. A trip exactly 1460 days old is still inside retention. The inner gate is the reconstruction window itself: if more than 120 days have elapsed since `trip_end_utc`, the window has closed and the attempt is refused with `ERR_RECONSTRUCTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`; a trip exactly 120 days old is still reconstructible. Because retention is checked first, a very old trip reports expiry rather than a closed window even though both conditions hold.

The reconstruction must finally reconcile to the odometer evidence. The sum of `leg_miles` across the submitted `reconstructed_legs` is compared against the trip's `odometer_delta_miles` plus its `gap_miles_from_previous` — the analyst is accounting for the trip's own distance *and* the unexplained distance that preceded it, which is the whole reason the trip was flagged. The absolute difference between those two figures may be at most half a mile: a deviation of exactly 0.5 miles is accepted and 0.6 miles is refused with `ERR_LEG_MILEAGE_MISMATCH`. Note that this reconciliation tolerance is **inclusive**, in deliberate contrast to the strictly-greater-than comparison that decides gap flagging.

A successful reconstruction rewrites the trip's mileage basis in one step. Every one of the trip's `ACTIVE` legs becomes `VOIDED`, and the submitted legs are inserted as `ACTIVE` with `leg_sequence` assigned from their 1-based position in `reconstructed_legs`. `mileage_source` flips from `TELEMATICS` to `MANUAL_RECONSTRUCTION` and never flips back. `reconstructed_by_user_id` is stamped with the calling analyst's `X-User-ID` and `supporting_document_reference` is stored. `accepted_leg_miles_total` is recomputed from the new `ACTIVE` legs. The carrier's `unsubstantiated_mileage_ratio` and `audit_flag` are recomputed and the carrier's `version` increments by 1.

Every trip that is to contribute miles to a settlement must pass an explicit review by a `ComplianceOfficer`, who supplies a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` and may attach an optional `review_note`. Five review outcomes exist, and which one applies is determined by the trip's current status together with the decision; every other pairing — accepting a `GAP_FLAGGED` trip, or either decision aimed at a trip already `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED` — corresponds to no transition and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRIP_STATE`. The `review_note`, where supplied, is validated but neither persisted on the trip nor echoed in any response.

Accepting a `CAPTURED` trip moves it `CAPTURED → ACCEPTED`. This is permitted only while `gap_miles_from_previous` is no greater than the five-mile tolerance, and only while the trip's vehicle has a `vehicle_status` other than the terminal `TRANSFERRED` — a unit that has left the fleet on a transfer cannot have new distance admitted against it, and an attempt to do so is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRIP_STATE`. On acceptance, `accepted_leg_miles_total` is recomputed from the trip's `ACTIVE` legs, the carrier's `unsubstantiated_mileage_ratio` and `audit_flag` are recomputed, and the carrier's `version` increments by 1.

Accepting a `RECONSTRUCTED` trip moves it `RECONSTRUCTED → ACCEPTED`, and this is where segregation of duties bites: the reviewer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the trip's `reconstructed_by_user_id`, or the review is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The trip's `supporting_document_reference` must also still be present and non-null, on pain of `ERR_RECONSTRUCTION_SUPPORT_MISSING`. Unlike the `CAPTURED` path, this one is not gated on the vehicle at all: a reconstruction may be accepted even for a unit that has since reached the terminal `TRANSFERRED`. On acceptance, the carrier's `unsubstantiated_mileage_ratio` and `audit_flag` are recomputed and the carrier's `version` increments by 1; because an accepted reconstruction has now been substantiated, its miles leave the unsubstantiated numerator.

Rejecting a `RECONSTRUCTED` trip is the revision loop, and it is symmetrically constrained: the reviewer's `X-User-ID` must likewise differ from `reconstructed_by_user_id`, on pain of `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The trip moves `RECONSTRUCTED → GAP_FLAGGED` — back to precisely the state it came from, so the analyst may try again. The trip's `ACTIVE` legs become `VOIDED`, `accepted_leg_miles_total` resets to 0.0, and both `supporting_document_reference` and `reconstructed_by_user_id` are cleared to null so that the next attempt starts from a clean paper trail and any analyst, including the original one, may make it. `mileage_source` deliberately **stays** `MANUAL_RECONSTRUCTION`: the trip's history is not rewritten by a rejection. The carrier's `unsubstantiated_mileage_ratio` and `audit_flag` are recomputed and the carrier's `version` increments by 1.

Rejecting a `GAP_FLAGGED` trip, and rejecting a `CAPTURED` trip, both move it to the terminal `REJECTED`. In both cases the trip's `ACTIVE` legs become `VOIDED`, `accepted_leg_miles_total` resets to 0.0, and the carrier's `unsubstantiated_mileage_ratio` and `audit_flag` are recomputed with the carrier's `version` incrementing by 1. A rejected trip is gone from the mileage picture in both directions — it leaves the unsubstantiated numerator and the fleet-total denominator alike, and it is invisible to gap detection, so the odometer chain closes over it.

There is one further way a trip can leave `ACCEPTED`, and it is not a reviewer's doing: the correction cascade described in §3.6 pushes accepted manual reconstructions back to `GAP_FLAGGED`.

### 3.4. Fuel Purchase Evidence and Tax-Paid Gallons

Fuel evidence is collected by either a `CarrierAdmin`, within its own fleet, or a `MileageAnalyst`. A purchase record carries the `carrier_id` claiming the gallons, the `vehicle_id` of the unit fuelled, the `jurisdiction_code` where the fuel was bought and pump tax was paid, the `purchase_date` as a plain calendar date in year-month-day form, the `gallons` figure — strictly greater than 0.000, carried to 3 decimal places — and the `tax_paid_flag`, which defaults to true and is set false for bulk untaxed fuel drawn from the carrier's own tank. The record may also carry a `receipt_reference` identifying the retained receipt; it is optional at the API boundary but decisive in what follows. A `receipt_reference` supplied as an empty or whitespace-only string is stored verbatim rather than rejected as a shape error, and simply counts as no receipt in the acceptance test below. As with a trip capture, the named `vehicle_id` is not required to belong to the named `carrier_id`, and §2.1's carrier-scope check is applied to the submitted `carrier_id`.

Acceptance is automatic and immediate, not a separate approval step. A purchase moves `RECORDED → ACCEPTED` at creation time when **both** conditions hold: `receipt_reference` is present and holds at least one non-whitespace character, and `tax_paid_flag` is true. Only `ACCEPTED` purchases ever feed tax-paid gallons. A purchase missing its receipt, or one flagged as untaxed bulk fuel, stays `RECORDED` and contributes nothing to any figure — it is retained as a record, not as a credit. When a purchase does become `ACCEPTED`, the carrier's running `tax_paid_gallons_ytd` becomes its prior value plus the purchase's `gallons`, rounded to 3 decimal places, and the carrier's `version` increments by 1. A purchase that stays `RECORDED` leaves both untouched.

That running total is a **conserved quantity**, maintained incrementally and exactly reversible, and its definition is the sum of `gallons` over the carrier's purchases whose `purchase_status` is `ACCEPTED` and whose `tax_paid_flag` is true, held to 3 decimal places.

A `MileageAnalyst` — and only a `MileageAnalyst` — may disallow a purchase found unsupported, supplying a `disallow_reason_code` that is one of `MISSING_RECEIPT`, `BULK_UNTAXED`, `DUPLICATE` or `AUDIT_ADJUSTMENT`. Disallowance is reachable from `ACCEPTED` and from `RECORDED` alike and lands on the terminal `DISALLOWED`; a purchase that is already `DISALLOWED` cannot be disallowed again, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PURCHASE_STATE`.

The two disallowance paths differ in exactly one respect, and the difference is the conservation property. Disallowing an `ACCEPTED` purchase stamps `disallow_reason_code` and reduces the carrier's `tax_paid_gallons_ytd` to its prior value minus the purchase's `gallons`, rounded to 3 decimal places — restoring, when that purchase was the only change, exactly the value the field held before the purchase existed. Disallowing a `RECORDED` purchase stamps `disallow_reason_code` and leaves `tax_paid_gallons_ytd` untouched, because a `RECORDED` purchase's gallons were never counted in the first place. Both paths increment the carrier's `version` by 1.

Once a quarter has been filed, its fuel basis is frozen. If any quarterly return of the same carrier is in `FILED` or `AUDIT_ADJUSTED` status and its quarter window contains the purchase's `purchase_date`, the disallowance is refused with `ERR_PURCHASE_LOCKED_BY_FILED_RETURN`. The lock is per-period and per-carrier: a purchase whose date falls in an unfiled quarter remains disallowable even while a neighbouring quarter is closed.

### 3.5. The Quarterly Settlement

A `TaxPreparer` opens a quarterly return by naming the `carrier_id` of the licensee, the `filing_year` of the reporting quarter — a whole number of at least 1 — and the `filing_quarter`, which must be 1, 2, 3 or 4. A carrier may hold only one return per period: a second attempt at the same combination of `carrier_id`, `filing_year` and `filing_quarter` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RETURN_PERIOD`. The new return opens in `DRAFT` with every computed figure at its zero value — `total_fleet_miles` at 0.0, `total_tax_paid_gallons` at 0.000, `reconstructed_mileage_ratio` at 0.0000, `total_net_tax_due`, `reconstruction_penalty_amount`, `total_amount_due` and `audit_assessment_delta` all at 0.00 — with `settlement_basis` at `PREPARER_BASIS`, `fleet_mpg` at null, and `prepared_by_user_id`, `filed_by_user_id` and `filed_at` all null. Beyond `DRAFT`, `return_status` admits `PREPARED`, `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION`, `FILED`, and the terminal `AUDIT_ADJUSTED`.

**The quarter window.** Every membership test in the settlement uses a half-open window in UTC that opens at midnight on the first day of the quarter's first month and closes at midnight on the first day of the month after the quarter's last month. First quarter runs from January 1 up to but excluding April 1; second quarter from April 1 up to but excluding July 1; third quarter from July 1 up to but excluding October 1; and fourth quarter from October 1 of `filing_year` up to but excluding January 1 of the following year. A trip belongs to the quarter when its `trip_start_utc` falls inside that window — the trip's end instant is irrelevant, so a trip that straddles a quarter boundary settles entirely in the quarter it began. A purchase belongs to the quarter when its `purchase_date` falls inside the same window on the same half-open basis.

**Preparation.** A `TaxPreparer` computes the settlement, and may recompute it as new mileage or fuel evidence arrives. Preparation is reachable from `DRAFT`, from `PREPARED` — a return may legitimately be re-prepared, superseding its own prior line set — and from `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION`. From any other status the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RETURN_STATE`. Four further conditions guard it. The carrier's `ifta_license_status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_LICENSE_INACTIVE`. The supplied `expected_carrier_version` must match the carrier's current `version`, as described in §2.8. No trip of the carrier whose `trip_start_utc` falls inside the quarter window may still be sitting in `CAPTURED` or `RECONSTRUCTED` — either is review work still in flight, and its presence is refused with `ERR_TRIP_GAP_UNRESOLVED`. A `GAP_FLAGGED` trip is explicitly *not* in-flight: the carrier has chosen not to reconstruct it, so its miles stay out of the settlement and instead raise the carrier's `unsubstantiated_mileage_ratio`. Finally the quarter must have substance in both bases: if the quarter's accepted tax-paid gallons total is not strictly greater than 0.000, fleet MPG would be undefined and the attempt is refused with `ERR_NO_TAX_PAID_GALLONS`; and if the quarter's total effective mileage is not strictly greater than 0.0, the attempt is refused with `ERR_NO_ACCEPTED_MILEAGE`. The gallons check is evaluated before the mileage check.

**Step one — the mileage basis.** The trips that count are those of the return's carrier whose `trip_status` is `ACCEPTED` and whose `trip_start_utc` falls inside the quarter window. `GAP_FLAGGED`, `RECONSTRUCTED`, `CAPTURED` and `REJECTED` trips contribute nothing. Each counting trip carries an **uplift factor**: on the preparer basis every trip's factor is 1.000, and on the audit basis a trip whose `mileage_source` is `MANUAL_RECONSTRUCTION` carries a factor of one and one tenth — 1.100 — while a telematics trip still carries 1.000. For each `ACTIVE` leg of each counting trip, the leg's effective miles are its `leg_miles` multiplied by its trip's uplift factor, rounded to 1 decimal place. Each jurisdiction's `taxable_miles` is then the sum of the effective miles of that jurisdiction's legs, rounded to 1 decimal place, and the return's `total_fleet_miles` is the sum of those per-jurisdiction figures, again rounded to 1 decimal place.

**Step two — the fuel basis.** For each jurisdiction, `tax_paid_gallons` on the line is the sum of `gallons` over the carrier's purchases whose `jurisdiction_code` is that jurisdiction, whose `purchase_status` is `ACCEPTED`, and whose `purchase_date` falls inside the quarter window, rounded to 3 decimal places. `RECORDED` and `DISALLOWED` purchases contribute zero. The return's `total_tax_paid_gallons` is the sum of the per-jurisdiction figures, rounded to 3 decimal places.

**Step three — fleet MPG, rounded before use.** The return's `fleet_mpg` is `total_fleet_miles` divided by `total_tax_paid_gallons`, rounded to 2 decimal places. That rounded value — and not the raw quotient — is what every jurisdiction's taxable miles are subsequently divided by. This ordering is observable in the stored line figures and must not be reordered. Should the rounding drive `fleet_mpg` to exactly 0.00 — a quarter whose accepted mileage is negligible beside its accepted gallons — the division is not attempted and every line's `taxable_gallons` is 0.000 instead; nothing is refused on that account.

**Step four — the per-jurisdiction lines.** One `ReturnJurisdictionLine` is written for every jurisdiction that appears either in the quarter's mileage or in the quarter's accepted purchases, iterated in `jurisdiction_code` ascending order, each carrying the `fuel_tax_return_id` of its parent return, its `jurisdiction_code`, and `line_status` set to `ACTIVE`. For each line, in order:

- `taxable_gallons` is the line's `taxable_miles` divided by the return's rounded `fleet_mpg`, rounded to 3 decimal places.
- `net_taxable_gallons` is `taxable_gallons` minus `tax_paid_gallons`, rounded to 3 decimal places. A negative result is legitimate and means the carrier bought more taxed fuel in that jurisdiction than it burned there — a credit position.
- `base_tax_amount` is `net_taxable_gallons` multiplied by the jurisdiction's `fuel_tax_rate_per_gallon`, rounded to 2 decimal places. A credit position therefore yields a negative base tax.
- `surcharge_amount` is, for a jurisdiction whose `is_surcharge_jurisdiction` is true, the line's **full** `taxable_gallons` multiplied by that jurisdiction's `surcharge_rate_per_gallon`, rounded to 2 decimal places. For every other jurisdiction it is 0.00. The surcharge is assessed on full taxable gallons and is **never** offset by `tax_paid_gallons`, because surcharge fuel tax is not collected at the pump — the carrier owes it in full on every gallon it burned in that jurisdiction regardless of where it bought fuel.
- `net_tax_amount` is `base_tax_amount` plus `surcharge_amount`, rounded to 2 decimal places.

**Step five — the reconstructed-mileage penalty.** Carriers that lean on reconstruction rather than telematics pay for the privilege. The quarter's reconstructed miles are the portion of the quarter's taxable miles contributed by trips whose `mileage_source` is `MANUAL_RECONSTRUCTION`, measured on the same effective (uplifted, where the audit basis applies) miles the settlement used. If `total_fleet_miles` is 0.0 the return's `reconstructed_mileage_ratio` is 0.0000; otherwise it is the reconstructed miles divided by `total_fleet_miles`, rounded to 4 decimal places. When that ratio is **strictly greater** than one tenth — greater than 0.1000 — the `reconstruction_penalty_amount` is the larger of a hundred-dollar floor and one tenth of the quarter's net tax due: specifically, take `total_net_tax_due` or 0.00, whichever is larger, multiply by 0.10, round to 2 decimal places, and then take whichever is larger of that figure and 100.00. When the ratio is 0.1000 or below there is no penalty and `reconstruction_penalty_amount` is 0.00. Because the comparison is strict, a ratio of exactly 0.1000 attracts nothing while 0.1001 attracts at least the full floor. Clamping the net tax at zero before applying the rate is what keeps a refund quarter from generating a negative penalty.

**Step six — the totals.** `total_net_tax_due` is the sum of the `net_tax_amount` values of the return's `ACTIVE` lines — each already rounded to 2 decimal places, summed as-is, never re-derived from a full-precision aggregate. A negative `total_net_tax_due` means the quarter is a net refund. `total_amount_due` is `total_net_tax_due` plus `reconstruction_penalty_amount`, rounded to 2 decimal places.

**What preparation writes.** Every `ReturnJurisdictionLine` of the return whose `line_status` is `ACTIVE` becomes `VOIDED`, and the freshly computed set is inserted as `ACTIVE`; the voided set stays readable through the jurisdiction-line read, which accepts a `line_status` filter. `settlement_basis` is set to `PREPARER_BASIS`. `total_fleet_miles`, `total_tax_paid_gallons`, `fleet_mpg`, `reconstructed_mileage_ratio`, `total_net_tax_due`, `reconstruction_penalty_amount` and `total_amount_due` are all written. `prepared_by_user_id` is stamped with the caller's `X-User-ID`. `return_status` becomes `PREPARED`, whichever of the three permitted statuses it started from. And one field outside the return changes: the carrier's `latest_prepared_fleet_mpg` is set to the newly computed `fleet_mpg`, and the carrier's `version` increments by 1 — which is precisely why a second prepare needs a freshly read `expected_carrier_version`.

### 3.6. Filing, Correction and the Reopening Cascade

A `PREPARED` return is decided by a `ComplianceOfficer` supplying a `decision` of either `FILE` or `RETURN_FOR_CORRECTION`, with an optional `review_note` that, as on a trip review, is validated but neither persisted nor echoed. Either decision requires that the return be in `PREPARED`; from any other status the review is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RETURN_STATE`. Both decisions require that the reviewer's `X-User-ID` differ from the return's `prepared_by_user_id`, failing which the review is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Both also require the carrier's `ifta_license_status` to be `ACTIVE`, on pain of `ERR_CARRIER_LICENSE_INACTIVE`.

Filing carries one additional, fiscal condition. The return's `total_amount_due` must be no greater than the carrier's `bond_ceiling_amount`; a return whose amount due exceeds the ceiling is not covered by the carrier's bond and is refused with `ERR_BOND_CEILING_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402). An amount exactly equal to the ceiling is covered and files successfully. On a successful filing the return moves `PREPARED → FILED`, `filed_by_user_id` is stamped with the filing officer's `X-User-ID` — necessarily a different identity from `prepared_by_user_id` — and `filed_at` is stamped with the logical clock. Filing also locks the quarter's evidence: the accepted trips and accepted purchases the return settled become frozen, and any later attempt to disallow a purchase whose `purchase_date` falls inside the filed quarter's window is refused with `ERR_PURCHASE_LOCKED_BY_FILED_RETURN`. Filing writes nothing at all outside the return row: no carrier field moves and the carrier's `version` does not advance, and no trip, purchase or jurisdiction line is rewritten — the freeze is enforced entirely by that later guard rather than by any status change on the frozen evidence.

Returning a return for correction is the more consequential decision, because it unwinds four levels of dependent state inside the one request. The return moves `PREPARED → RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION`, and then:

- **First hop — the return itself.** All `ACTIVE` `ReturnJurisdictionLine` rows of the return become `VOIDED`. `total_net_tax_due`, `reconstruction_penalty_amount` and `total_amount_due` reset to 0.00; `total_fleet_miles` resets to 0.0; `fleet_mpg` resets to null; `reconstructed_mileage_ratio` resets to 0.0000. `total_tax_paid_gallons`, `settlement_basis` and `prepared_by_user_id` are deliberately left exactly as the superseded preparation left them.
- **Second hop — the mileage basis.** Because the settled line set has been voided, the reconstructions it rested on must be re-substantiated. Every trip of the return's carrier whose `trip_status` is `ACCEPTED`, whose `mileage_source` is `MANUAL_RECONSTRUCTION`, and whose `trip_start_utc` falls inside the return's quarter window transitions `ACCEPTED → GAP_FLAGGED`. For each such trip, its `ACTIVE` legs become `VOIDED`, its `accepted_leg_miles_total` resets to 0.0, and its `supporting_document_reference` is cleared. `reconstructed_by_user_id` is **not** cleared, unlike on a reviewer's rejection in §3.3: a reopening leaves the analyst's identity on the trip, so that analyst stays barred from the trip's next review decision, and `mileage_source` likewise stays `MANUAL_RECONSTRUCTION`. Telematics-sourced accepted trips are untouched — only manual reconstructions are reopened.
- **Third hop — the carrier's compliance posture.** Because those trips have left `ACCEPTED` and returned to `GAP_FLAGGED`, they re-enter the unsubstantiated numerator, so the carrier's `unsubstantiated_mileage_ratio` is recomputed and its `audit_flag` is re-derived against the fifteen-hundredths threshold. The carrier's `version` increments by 1. This is the mechanism by which reopening a quarter raises a carrier's audit exposure rather than merely resetting some numbers.
- **Fourth hop — audit re-selection.** This hop runs on every correction, whatever the third hop left `audit_flag` reading: every `AuditSelection` of that carrier whose `selection_year` equals the return's `filing_year` and whose `selection_status` is `NOT_SELECTED` is re-scored by re-evaluating the whole four-factor formula of §3.9 — against the carrier's freshly recomputed `unsubstantiated_mileage_ratio`, the carrier's current `latest_prepared_fleet_mpg`, and the `late_filing_count` and `retention_deficiency_count` already stored on the selection — and the recomputed `risk_score` is written back to the record even when it leaves that record `NOT_SELECTED`. Where the recomputed `risk_score` reaches the selection threshold of 70, the selection transitions `NOT_SELECTED → SELECTED` and the carrier's `audit_flag` is set true again even if the third hop had just cleared it. A selection whose recomputed `risk_score` equals the score already stored, and which is therefore not promoted, is left exactly as it stood, with no `version` increment and no `updated_at` refresh; the carrier's own `version` advances once for the whole cascade, in the third hop, however many selections this hop rewrites. A period the carrier had already escaped can therefore be pulled back into audit by a correction on a later-reviewed return of the same year.

A corrected return is not dead. A `TaxPreparer` may prepare it again, moving it `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION → PREPARED`, but only once the reopened reconstructions have been re-reconstructed and re-accepted — until then the quarter contains no `ACCEPTED` mileage and the attempt fails on the accepted-mileage condition, and while any of them sits in `RECONSTRUCTED` awaiting review the attempt fails on the in-flight condition instead. The loop between `PREPARED` and `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION` may be traversed any number of times.

### 3.7. Apportioned Registration: Distance Schedules, Fee Apportionment and Credential Issuance

The registration side of the system runs on a calendar year rather than a quarter. A `TaxPreparer` opens an apportioned registration application by naming the `carrier_id` of the registrant, the `registration_year` being applied for — a whole number of at least 1, exactly as on a power unit — the `weight_group_code` declared for the whole apportioned fleet — which is what drives the fee chart lookup — and the `distance_basis`, which is either `ACTUAL_DISTANCE_BASIS` or `ESTIMATED_DISTANCE_BASIS`. A carrier may hold only one live application per year: if it already has an application for the same `registration_year` whose `application_status` is anything other than `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION`, the new application is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_APPLICATION_YEAR`. A corrected application does not block a replacement, which is what makes the correction loop workable.

The new application opens in `DRAFT` with `prepared_by_user_id` stamped from the creating preparer's `X-User-ID`, `reviewed_by_user_id` null, `fleet_total_distance_miles` at 0.0, `base_fee_total` and `total_apportioned_fee` at 0.00, `apportion_frozen_at` null, and `apportioned_vehicle_count` computed immediately from the carrier's power units for that `registration_year` whose `vehicle_status` is either `PENDING` or `ACTIVE`. Beyond `DRAFT`, `application_status` admits `SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION`, `INVOICED`, and the terminal `ISSUED`.

The preparer then builds the distance schedule one jurisdiction at a time, each addition carrying a `jurisdiction_code` and the `reported_distance_miles` the registrant claims for that jurisdiction, carried to 1 decimal place and never negative. Each addition creates a `DistanceScheduleLine` whose `registration_application_id` points at the application and whose `line_status` starts `ACTIVE`; its `effective_distance_miles`, `apportion_percent` and `jurisdiction_fee_amount` all remain at zero until the application is invoiced. Schedule lines may be added only while the application is `DRAFT` or `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION`; at any other status the addition is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`. An application may hold at most one `ACTIVE` line per jurisdiction, and a second `ACTIVE` line for the same `jurisdiction_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_JURISDICTION_LINE` — note that a `VOIDED` line from an earlier correction round does not block a fresh one for the same jurisdiction. A line naming an undeclared jurisdiction is refused with `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_DECLARED`. The schedule read is the only channel through which computed percentages and per-jurisdiction fees become observable, and it accepts a `line_status` filter so that voided rounds remain inspectable.

Submitting the application moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, or `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION → SUBMITTED` on a later round; from any other status the submission is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`. The submission takes no request body. Three substantive conditions must hold, and a failure of any of them is likewise refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`: at least one `ACTIVE` `DistanceScheduleLine` must exist (on a correction round the prior set was voided, so new lines must have been added before resubmission); one of those `ACTIVE` lines must name a `jurisdiction_code` equal to the carrier's `base_jurisdiction_code`, because a registrant must always report distance in the jurisdiction that licenses it; and `apportioned_vehicle_count` must be at least 1. Separately, the carrier's `irp_account_status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_IRP_INACTIVE`. On success `apportioned_vehicle_count` is recomputed from the carrier's `PENDING` and `ACTIVE` units for the year, and the application's `version` increments by 1.

A `ComplianceOfficer` then decides the submitted application with a `decision` of `INVOICE` or `RETURN_FOR_CORRECTION` and an optional `review_note`. Either decision requires `SUBMITTED` status, on pain of `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`, and requires that the reviewer's `X-User-ID` differ from the application's `prepared_by_user_id`, on pain of `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Both decisions stamp `reviewed_by_user_id` with the deciding officer's `X-User-ID` and increment the application's `version` by 1.

Invoicing additionally requires the fee chart to be complete: for every `ACTIVE` line's `jurisdiction_code`, a `WeightGroupFee` cell must exist paired with the application's `weight_group_code`. A missing cell is refused with `ERR_WEIGHT_GROUP_FEE_MISSING` — the jurisdiction cannot be billed for a weight group it has not published a price for.

**The apportionment computation** then runs across the application's `ACTIVE` lines, iterated in `jurisdiction_code` ascending order. First each line's `effective_distance_miles` is derived from its `reported_distance_miles` according to the application's `distance_basis`. Under `ACTUAL_DISTANCE_BASIS` — the basis for a carrier that genuinely operated in every listed jurisdiction during the reporting period — `effective_distance_miles` is `reported_distance_miles` verbatim, and the resulting percentage mix is the carrier's true operational mix. Under `ESTIMATED_DISTANCE_BASIS` — the basis for a new or newly expanded fleet with no actual history in a jurisdiction — `effective_distance_miles` is whichever is larger of `reported_distance_miles` and a twenty-thousand-mile chart floor, that is 20000.0 miles. Because the floor lifts only the below-floor jurisdictions, it changes the denominator *and* redistributes the mix: on identical reported distances, every line's `apportion_percent` and `jurisdiction_fee_amount` differ from the actual-basis result, and so does `base_fee_total`.

Then `fleet_total_distance_miles` is the sum of `effective_distance_miles` over the `ACTIVE` lines, rounded to 1 decimal place — this is the denominator of every percentage. For each line, `apportion_percent` is that line's `effective_distance_miles` divided by `fleet_total_distance_miles`, rounded to 6 decimal places; the percentages are deliberately not re-normalised, so their sum may differ from exactly 1.000000 by the residue of that rounding. Should `fleet_total_distance_miles` itself come out at 0.0 — every active line reported zero distance on the actual basis — the division is not attempted, every line's `apportion_percent` is 0.000000, and every `jurisdiction_fee_amount` and `base_fee_total` is 0.00; nothing is refused on that account. Each line's `jurisdiction_fee_amount` is its `apportion_percent` multiplied by the `annual_fee_amount` of the `WeightGroupFee` cell for that line's `jurisdiction_code` paired with the application's `weight_group_code`, multiplied again by the application's `apportioned_vehicle_count` — read as the count already recorded on the application at submission, never recomputed here, so a unit enrolled between submission and invoicing is activated by the cascade below without being priced into the fee — rounded to 2 decimal places. The application's `base_fee_total` is the sum of those already-rounded per-line fees, and at invoicing time — before any supplement exists — `total_apportioned_fee` equals `base_fee_total`.

Invoicing has two further effects. `apportion_frozen_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and that stamp is load-bearing: the percentages captured at this moment are **frozen** and are never recomputed by any later fleet change, so a mid-year addition is priced on exactly the same jurisdictional mix as the original credentials. And a cascade runs across the fleet: every power unit of the carrier for the application's `registration_year` whose `vehicle_status` is `PENDING` transitions `PENDING → ACTIVE`, with `activated_on_date` stamped from the logical-clock date as a plain `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date. The application itself moves `SUBMITTED → INVOICED`.

Returning an application for correction moves it `SUBMITTED → RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION` and unwinds the frozen schedule: all `DistanceScheduleLine` rows with `line_status` `ACTIVE` become `VOIDED`, `fleet_total_distance_miles` resets to 0.0, `base_fee_total` and `total_apportioned_fee` reset to 0.00, and `apportion_frozen_at` resets to null, while `apportioned_vehicle_count` is left exactly as it stood. Vehicle statuses are not disturbed by a correction. The application may bounce between `SUBMITTED` and `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION` any number of times.

**Credential issuance** closes the application lifecycle. A `ComplianceOfficer` issues credentials against an `INVOICED` application, moving it `INVOICED → ISSUED`; from any other status the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`. The carrier's `ifta_license_status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_LICENSE_INACTIVE` — a suspended licensee gets no new paper. At least one power unit of the carrier for the application's `registration_year` must have `vehicle_status` `ACTIVE`, without which there is nothing to print and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`; that fleet condition is evaluated only after the licence check, so a suspended licensee that also has no active unit is told about its licence rather than about its fleet. The caller may supply a `valid_from_date` and a `valid_to_date`; each is optional, and where omitted the defaults apply as described below.

For every power unit of the carrier for that `registration_year` whose `vehicle_status` is `ACTIVE`, exactly three `Credential` rows are created — one of `credential_type` `DECAL_SET`, one of `CAB_CARD`, and one of `IFTA_LICENSE_COPY`, written in exactly that order and unit by unit in the order the units were enrolled, so the credential list read in its default `created_at` ascending order reproduces that sequence — each carrying the `vehicle_id` of the unit it is printed for, the `registration_application_id` of the issuing application, and a `registration_year` copied from that application. Each opens with `credential_status` `ISSUED`, with `distributed_to_driver_id`, `distributed_at` and `voided_reason_code` all null. Each credential's validity interval is a **closed** interval of whole calendar days: `valid_from_date` is the supplied value or, when omitted, the logical-clock date at issuance, and `valid_to_date` is the supplied value or, when omitted, December 31 of the credential's `registration_year`. Neither endpoint is ever checked against the other, so a window that runs backwards — whether supplied that way or produced by defaulting `valid_from_date` to a clock date past the year's end — is accepted exactly as given. As a consequence of the credentials appearing, each of those vehicles' derived `credential_status` moves `NONE → ISSUED`. The application's `version` increments by 1, and the response carries the updated application rather than the credentials, which are observable only through their own reads.

### 3.8. Mid-Year Fleet Supplements and the Life of a Credential

Fleets change during a registration year, and the apportionment system prices those changes by proration rather than by re-apportionment. A supplement is filed by either a `CarrierAdmin`, confined to its own fleet, or a `TaxPreparer`. The request names the `registration_application_id` of the application being supplemented, the `vehicle_id` of the unit being added, deleted or brought in, the `change_type` — one of `ADDITION`, `DELETION` or `TRANSFER` — and the `effective_date` on which the change takes effect, expressed as a calendar date in year-month-day form. On a `TRANSFER` the request must also name `replaced_vehicle_id`, the outgoing unit being swapped out; that field is required exactly when `change_type` is `TRANSFER` and must be null for `ADDITION` and `DELETION`. The request must further carry `expected_registration_application_version` holding the `version` of the application the requester priced the supplement against; a mismatch against the application's current `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_APPLICATION_VERSION`, as set out in §2.8. Neither the incoming unit nor the outgoing one is required to belong to the application's carrier or to carry the application's `registration_year`; the only conditions the named units must satisfy are the `vehicle_status` conditions checked at approval.

A newly filed supplement opens with `change_status` `REQUESTED`, `fee_delta` at 0.00, `prorate_months` at 0, `decided_by_user_id` and `reversal_reason_code` null, and `requested_by_user_id` stamped from the caller's `X-User-ID` — which, for a `CarrierAdmin`, is the requesting carrier's own `id`. Filing a supplement itself increments the parent application's `version` by 1, which is why two supplements filed back to back require the second requester to re-read the application. Beyond `REQUESTED`, `change_status` admits `APPROVED` and the terminal `REJECTED` and `REVERSED`.

A `ComplianceOfficer` decides the supplement with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and an optional `decision_note`. Either decision requires `change_status` to be `REQUESTED`; a decision against a request in any other status is refused with `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_PENDING`. Rejection is the simple path: `change_status` becomes `REJECTED`, `decided_by_user_id` is stamped, `fee_delta` stays 0.00 and `prorate_months` stays 0, no vehicle and no credential is touched, and the parent application's `version` increments by 1.

Approval carries four further conditions. The parent application's `application_status` must be `INVOICED` or `ISSUED`, because a supplement can only be priced against percentages that have already been frozen; otherwise the approval is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`. The carrier's `irp_account_status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the approval is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_IRP_INACTIVE`. The `effective_date` must fall inside the application's `registration_year` — January 1 through December 31 of that year, both inclusive — and a date outside it is refused with `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_OUT_OF_YEAR`. And the referenced units must be in the states their `change_type` requires: for `ADDITION`, the target unit's `vehicle_status` must be `PENDING`; for `DELETION`, it must be `ACTIVE`; for `TRANSFER`, the incoming unit named by `vehicle_id` must be `PENDING` **and** the outgoing unit named by `replaced_vehicle_id` must be `ACTIVE`. Any mismatch is refused with `ERR_VEHICLE_STATE_INCOMPATIBLE`.

**Pricing the supplement.** The per-vehicle full-year cost is read entirely from the application's **frozen** `ACTIVE` distance schedule — the set that was fixed at invoicing and stamped in `apportion_frozen_at` — and is never recomputed for a supplement. For each of those frozen `ACTIVE` lines, multiply the line's `apportion_percent` by the `annual_fee_amount` of the `WeightGroupFee` cell for that line's `jurisdiction_code` paired with the application's `weight_group_code`; sum those products across the lines and round the sum to 2 decimal places. Call that the per-vehicle full-year fee; it is rounded **once**, here, before any proration.

Proration then keys off the month number of `effective_date`, counting January as 1 through December as 12. For an `ADDITION`, the month of entry is charged, so `prorate_months` is thirteen minus the month number — a January addition is charged all twelve months and a December addition is charged one — and `fee_delta` is the per-vehicle full-year fee multiplied by `prorate_months`, divided by twelve, rounded to 2 decimal places. For a `DELETION`, the month of exit is **not** credited, so `prorate_months` is twelve minus the month number — a January deletion is credited eleven months and a December deletion is credited none — and `fee_delta` is the negative of the per-vehicle full-year fee multiplied by `prorate_months`, divided by twelve, rounded to 2 decimal places. `fee_delta` is signed, and it is negative for a `DELETION`.

A `TRANSFER` is an addition and a deletion settled on one credential swap, and its two legs are each rounded before being combined. The addition leg uses thirteen minus the month number as its month count; the credit leg uses twelve minus the month number. Compute the per-vehicle full-year fee multiplied by the addition month count divided by twelve and round that to 2 decimal places; compute the per-vehicle full-year fee multiplied by the credit month count divided by twelve and round that to 2 decimal places; subtract the second rounded figure from the first; add a flat transfer administration charge of 15.00; and round the result to 2 decimal places. The request's `prorate_months` records the addition month count. Because each leg is rounded independently, a transfer's `fee_delta` is not in general equal to a bare addition's delta minus a bare deletion's delta computed at full precision.

**What approval writes on the application.** `fee_delta` and `prorate_months` are stored on the request and `decided_by_user_id` is stamped. The application's `total_apportioned_fee` becomes its `base_fee_total` plus the sum of `fee_delta` over all of that application's supplements whose `change_status` is `APPROVED`, rounded to 2 decimal places — a conserved quantity recomputed from the whole approved set rather than nudged incrementally. `apportioned_vehicle_count` is adjusted by plus one for an `ADDITION`, minus one for a `DELETION`, and zero for a `TRANSFER`, since a transfer swaps one unit for another. The application's `version` increments by 1. Note that the frozen `apportion_percent` values and `base_fee_total` are untouched — only `total_apportioned_fee` and the vehicle count move.

**What approval cascades onto the fleet and its paper.** An approved `ADDITION` moves the target unit `PENDING → ACTIVE` and stamps `activated_on_date` with the supplement's `effective_date` — not with the logical clock, which is the rule that applies to activation at invoicing instead. An approved `DELETION` moves the target unit `ACTIVE → DELETED`, and then a further hop reaches its paper: every `Credential` of that unit whose `credential_status` is `ISSUED` or `DISTRIBUTED` becomes `VOIDED` with `voided_reason_code` set to `FLEET_DELETION`. Where such a credential had already been handed out, its `distributed_to_driver_id` is deliberately retained so that the driver handover trail survives the void. Because no credential of the unit remains in `ISSUED` or `DISTRIBUTED`, the unit's derived `credential_status` falls to `NONE`. Both dimensions of that unit move inside a single mutation of its row, so the unit's `version` advances by exactly 1 however many credentials the hop voids.

An approved `TRANSFER` moves two units at once. The incoming unit named by `vehicle_id` moves `PENDING → ACTIVE` with `activated_on_date` stamped from the supplement's `effective_date`, and its `replaced_vehicle_id` is set to the `id` of the outgoing unit — this is the self-referential link that lets a transfer chain be walked, and the vehicle list read exposes a `replaced_vehicle_id` filter for exactly that purpose. The outgoing unit named by the supplement's `replaced_vehicle_id` moves `ACTIVE → TRANSFERRED`, which is terminal, and every `Credential` of that outgoing unit whose `credential_status` is `ISSUED` or `DISTRIBUTED` becomes `REPLACED` with `voided_reason_code` set to `TRANSFER_REPLACEMENT`. The outgoing unit's derived `credential_status` is re-derived and falls to `NONE`, and — importantly for the handover rules below — the validity window those replaced credentials occupied on their driver is freed and becomes available for a later distribution. As on a deletion, each of the two units carries all of its own movements inside a single mutation, so each unit's `version` advances by exactly 1.

**Reversal.** An approved supplement may be undone by a `ComplianceOfficer`, who supplies a `reversal_reason_code` that is one of `CLERICAL_ERROR`, `VEHICLE_NOT_DELIVERED` or `JURISDICTION_REJECTION`. Reversal requires `change_status` to be `APPROVED`, and a reversal aimed at a request in any other status is refused with `ERR_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_PENDING`. It requires the parent application's `application_status` to still be `INVOICED` or `ISSUED`, without which it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`. And it requires `change_type` to be `ADDITION` or `DELETION`: an approved `TRANSFER` is **not** reversible, because the outgoing unit has already reached the terminal `TRANSFERRED` state and its credentials have already been `REPLACED`, so an attempt is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_REVERSIBLE`.

A reversal moves the request `APPROVED → REVERSED`, stores `reversal_reason_code`, and re-stamps `decided_by_user_id` with the reversing officer's `X-User-ID`, overwriting the approver's identity that field previously held. The row's `fee_delta` is **retained** as an audit trail but stops counting as approved and is never re-added, so the application's `total_apportioned_fee` is recomputed as `base_fee_total` plus the sum of `fee_delta` over the remaining `APPROVED` supplements, rounded to 2 decimal places, returning to exactly the value it held before this approval; `apportioned_vehicle_count` is likewise restored to its pre-approval value; and the application's `version` increments by 1. The vehicle cascade is undone in the direction it came from: a reversed `ADDITION` moves its unit `ACTIVE → PENDING` and clears `activated_on_date` back to null, and a reversed `DELETION` moves its unit `DELETED → ACTIVE`. Credentials do **not** come back. Credentials voided with `FLEET_DELETION` stay `VOIDED`, so a restored unit's derived `credential_status` reads `NONE` until fresh credentials are issued for it.

**Handing credentials to drivers.** A credential is only operationally useful once it is in a driver's cab, and distribution is performed by either a `CarrierAdmin`, scope-checked against the `carrier_id` of the credential's vehicle, or a `ComplianceOfficer`. The caller names the `distributed_to_driver_id` of the receiving driver. The credential's `credential_status` must be `ISSUED`; a credential that is already `DISTRIBUTED`, or that has been `VOIDED` or `REPLACED`, cannot be handed over and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_ISSUED`. The receiving driver must belong to the same carrier as the credential's vehicle and must have `driver_status` `ACTIVE`; a driver of another carrier, or an `INACTIVE` driver, is refused with `ERR_DRIVER_CARRIER_MISMATCH`.

The last condition is the handover geometry, and it is deliberately unlike the trip-interval geometry of §3.2. A credential's validity interval from `valid_from_date` to `valid_to_date` is **closed** — both endpoints belong to the interval — and the conflict test expands **both** windows by a two-day handover buffer on each side before comparing them. The distribution is refused when, for any other `Credential` of the **same** `credential_type` already `DISTRIBUTED` to that same driver, the new credential's `valid_from_date` minus two days is on or before the other credential's `valid_to_date` plus two days, **and** the new credential's `valid_to_date` plus two days is on or after the other credential's `valid_from_date` minus two days. Because both windows are expanded and both endpoints are inclusive, two windows handed to one driver for one credential type need **more than four clear days** between them; a handover gap of exactly two clear days, or of exactly four, still conflicts. The rejection is `ERR_DRIVER_CREDENTIAL_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Only credentials in `DISTRIBUTED` status are considered: a `VOIDED` or `REPLACED` credential frees its window again, and a credential of a different `credential_type` never conflicts, so the same driver may hold a decal set and a cab card covering the same dates.

On a successful handover the credential moves `ISSUED → DISTRIBUTED`, `distributed_to_driver_id` is stored and `distributed_at` is stamped with the logical clock. The owning unit's derived `credential_status` is then re-derived: it moves `ISSUED → DISTRIBUTED` once every one of that unit's credentials in `ISSUED` or `DISTRIBUTED` has reached `DISTRIBUTED`, and it stays at `ISSUED` while any of them is still outstanding. The converse also occurs: when fresh credentials are printed for a unit whose earlier credentials had all been handed out, that unit's derived `credential_status` moves `DISTRIBUTED → ISSUED`, because at least one credential is now `ISSUED` while at least one remains `DISTRIBUTED`. Distribution moves only the credential dimension; it never touches `vehicle_status`.

### 3.9. Audit Selection, Retention Review and Licence Standing

Audit selection is a scoring exercise the jurisdiction's audit desk runs against one filing period at a time. A `ComplianceOfficer` scores a carrier by naming the `carrier_id`, the `selection_year` — a whole number of at least 1 — and the `selection_quarter` (1 through 4) the selection covers — together those two also identify the quarterly return the selection will later be measured against. The officer may supply `late_filing_count`, the number of late filings the desk observed for the carrier in the selection year, and `retention_deficiency_count`, the number of record-retention deficiencies observed in the records request; each defaults to 0 when omitted and neither may be negative. A carrier may be scored only once per period: a repeat of the same `carrier_id`, `selection_year` and `selection_quarter` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_AUDIT_PERIOD`.

**The risk score.** `risk_score` is an integer between 0 and 100 assembled from four weighted factors, all combined at full precision and rounded **once** at the end. The unsubstantiated-mileage factor contributes up to 40 points: take the carrier's `unsubstantiated_mileage_ratio`, divide it by a saturation ratio of 0.25, cap the quotient at 1, and multiply by 40 — so a carrier at or above a quarter unsubstantiated distance saturates this factor. The fuel-efficiency factor contributes up to 25 points and is computed only where the carrier has a `latest_prepared_fleet_mpg`; if that field is null, because no return of the carrier has ever been prepared, this factor contributes exactly 0 rather than saturating. Where it is present, take the absolute difference between `latest_prepared_fleet_mpg` and an industry baseline of 6.50 miles per gallon, divide by that same 6.50 baseline to get a relative variance, divide the variance by a tolerated band of 0.20, cap the quotient at 1, and multiply by 25. The late-filing factor contributes 5 points per late filing, counting at most three of them, for up to 15 points. The retention factor contributes up to 20 points: divide `retention_deficiency_count` by a saturation count of 4, cap the quotient at 1, and multiply by 20. Add the four contributions, round the sum to a whole number, and cap the result at 100. Rounding the individual weighted contributions separately produces a different figure and is incorrect.

The score decides the selection immediately. When `risk_score` reaches the selection threshold of 70 — the comparison is inclusive, so exactly 70 selects — the record is born `SELECTED` rather than `SCORED`, and the carrier's `audit_flag` is set to true with the carrier's `version` incrementing by 1. When `risk_score` falls below 70 the record is born `NOT_SELECTED` and nothing outside the record changes. Both outcomes leave `expected_records_count`, `records_missing_count`, `retention_deficiency_ratio` and `post_review_risk_score` null until a retention review runs, and `reviewed_by_user_id` null. `selection_status` also admits the terminal `RETENTION_REVIEWED`; a `NOT_SELECTED` record can still be pulled to `SELECTED` later by the correction cascade of §3.6.

**The retention review** is where the audit actually lands, and it is the deepest cascade in the system. A `ComplianceOfficer` records the outcome of a records request by supplying `expected_records_count`, the number of source records the desk asked for, which must be at least 1, and `records_missing_count`, the number the carrier could not produce, which must be between 0 and `expected_records_count` inclusive. The selection must be in `SELECTED`; a review aimed at a record in any other status is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_SELECTION_NOT_SELECTED`. And exactly one quarterly return of the carrier with `filing_year` equal to `selection_year` and `filing_quarter` equal to `selection_quarter` must be in `FILED` status; if none is, the review is refused with `ERR_NO_FILED_RETURN_FOR_PERIOD`, because there is no filed basis to audit against.

The review writes its own findings first. `selection_status` moves `SELECTED → RETENTION_REVIEWED`, `expected_records_count` and `records_missing_count` are stored, `retention_deficiency_ratio` is set to `records_missing_count` divided by `expected_records_count` rounded to 4 decimal places, and `reviewed_by_user_id` is stamped with the reviewing officer's `X-User-ID`. `post_review_risk_score` is then the record's existing `risk_score` plus a retention penalty of thirty points scaled by the measured deficiency — multiply `retention_deficiency_ratio` by 30, round that product to a whole number, add it to `risk_score`, and cap the total at 100. The response to the review is the updated `AuditSelection` record itself; the re-settled return and the carrier's new licence standing produced by the cascade below are observable only through their own reads.

**First cascade hop — the filed return is re-settled on the audit basis.** The matching `FILED` return is recomputed by exactly the settlement machinery of §3.5, but on the audit basis: legs belonging to trips whose `mileage_source` is `MANUAL_RECONSTRUCTION` are uplifted by the factor of 1.100 before aggregation, while telematics legs are unchanged. All of the return's `ACTIVE` jurisdiction lines become `VOIDED` and a freshly computed set is inserted as `ACTIVE`. `settlement_basis` becomes `AUDIT_BASIS`, and `total_fleet_miles`, `total_tax_paid_gallons`, `fleet_mpg`, `reconstructed_mileage_ratio`, `total_net_tax_due`, `reconstruction_penalty_amount` and `total_amount_due` are all rewritten from the fresh computation — the fuel basis included, even though a filed quarter's purchases are locked against disallowance and the recomputed gallons therefore normally reproduce the filed figure — while `prepared_by_user_id`, `filed_by_user_id` and `filed_at` are left exactly as the filing left them. `audit_assessment_delta` is written as the audit-basis `total_amount_due` minus the preparer-basis `total_amount_due` that was actually filed, rounded to 2 decimal places. The return moves `FILED → AUDIT_ADJUSTED`, which is terminal.

The two bases do not agree, and the reason is worth stating because it is easy to assume they cancel. Uplifting reconstructed legs raises `total_fleet_miles`, which raises `fleet_mpg`, which lowers every jurisdiction's `taxable_gallons` per taxable mile; but it simultaneously raises the taxable miles of precisely those jurisdictions the reconstructed trips ran in. The two effects apply to different jurisdictions in different proportions, so each line's `net_tax_amount`, and therefore `total_net_tax_due` and `total_amount_due`, diverge from the preparer-basis result on identical underlying data. The uplift also feeds the penalty ratio, which is computed on effective miles, so the audit basis can cross the one-tenth penalty threshold on a quarter the preparer basis kept below it.

**Second cascade hop — the carrier's licence reacts.** The reaction is evaluated on `post_review_risk_score` against a suspension threshold of 90, inclusive, so exactly 90 triggers it. Below 90 the licence is untouched. An already-`REVOKED` licence is likewise untouched whatever the score, revocation being terminal: nothing further moves, `suspension_count` does not advance, no credential is voided, `irp_account_status` does not move and the carrier's `version` does not increment. At or above 90 the outcome depends on the carrier's current `ifta_license_status` and on how complete the record failure was:

- An `ACTIVE` licence with `retention_deficiency_ratio` below 1.0000 moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`. `suspension_count` increments by 1 and the carrier's `version` increments by 1. As a further hop, every `Credential` belonging to that carrier's vehicles whose `credential_type` is `IFTA_LICENSE_COPY` and whose `credential_status` is `ISSUED` or `DISTRIBUTED` becomes `VOIDED` with `voided_reason_code` set to `LICENSE_SUSPENSION`, and each affected unit's derived `credential_status` is re-derived accordingly. Decal sets and cab cards are untouched — the suspension withdraws the fuel licence, not the plate. Critically, `irp_account_status` stays `ACTIVE`: apportioned registration work continues while quarterly filing is frozen.
- An `ACTIVE` licence with `retention_deficiency_ratio` exactly 1.0000 — no record of the period survived retention at all — skips suspension and moves `ACTIVE → REVOKED`, which is terminal. `suspension_count` increments by 1 and the carrier's `version` increments by 1. The `IFTA_LICENSE_COPY` credentials are `VOIDED` with `LICENSE_SUSPENSION` exactly as above, and then revocation couples the second dimension: `irp_account_status` moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`.
- An already-`SUSPENDED` licence moves `SUSPENDED → REVOKED` on a later review that again reaches 90, regardless of that review's deficiency ratio. `suspension_count` increments by 1, the carrier's `version` increments by 1, and `irp_account_status` moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`.

**Final hop — drivers.** Whenever `irp_account_status` moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, every driver of that carrier whose `driver_status` is `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → INACTIVE`. No credential moves in this hop — the handover trail is not rewritten, and even the `IFTA_LICENSE_COPY` credentials voided a hop earlier keep the `distributed_to_driver_id` and `distributed_at` they were handed over with — and only future distributions are blocked, since an `INACTIVE` driver can no longer receive a credential.

The downstream consequences of a suspended or revoked licence are enforced by the ordinary guards described earlier: preparation and review of quarterly returns and issuance of credentials are all refused with `ERR_CARRIER_LICENSE_INACTIVE` once `ifta_license_status` is `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED`, and application submission and fleet-change approval are refused with `ERR_CARRIER_IRP_INACTIVE` once `irp_account_status` is `SUSPENDED`. Reads are never blocked by licence standing.
