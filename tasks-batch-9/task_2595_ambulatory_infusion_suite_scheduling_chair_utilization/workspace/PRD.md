# Infusion Suite Scheduling API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An ambulatory infusion suite is one of the most capital-dense rooms in a health system: a handful of chairs, a thin nursing roster, and a pharmacy whose product costs more per vial than the chair costs per day. Every hour of that estate is spent three times over — once on pre-medication, once on the drug push itself, and once on the post-infusion observation that safety protocols demand — and each of those legs has to be reserved before the patient arrives. This document specifies the API for a scheduling and chair-utilization platform that treats those minutes as the scarce resource they are: it templates each visit from the drug's own administration profile, staggers starts onto a fixed grid so that nursing acuity never spikes, and refuses to seat a patient whose payer authorization, laboratory work, or compounded dose is not in place.

The operational pain the platform is built around is waste in three currencies. Chair-minutes are wasted when a no-show leaves a staggered slot empty and the turnover buffer has already been paid; drug units are wasted when a compounded dose outlives its stability window because a visit cancelled after the pharmacist mixed it; and clinician attention is wasted when a reaction extends an observation window and nobody notices that the next patient on that chair no longer fits. Accordingly, the system maintains a conserved unit ledger on every drug lot, prices late cancellations against the waste they actually caused, tracks a recency-weighted no-show risk score that drives how aggressively a patient is reminded, and escalates chronically non-adherent or expensive patients into a site-of-care steering review rather than continuing to hold suite capacity for them.

Six operational roles share the surface without any one of them dominating: schedulers own the booking estate, infusion nurses own the bedside, pharmacists own the catalogue and the compounding bench, authorization specialists own the payer conversation, medical directors own clinical governance and analytics, and patients hold a narrow self-service window onto their own visits. Because nearly every rule in the domain is a deadline, a freshness window, or a grace period, the server keeps its own logical clock and every temporal comparison in this specification is made against that clock rather than against wall-clock time.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every collection in this API hangs directly off the root — there is no nesting prefix and no parent path segment. A visit is addressed at `/appointments/{id}`, its chair at `/chairs/{id}`, and its compounded dose at `/drug-preparations/{id}`; where an action sub-path exists it is a verb on one resource, not a statement of ownership. Parent scoping is expressed through filter query parameters on the flat collection reads.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the actor, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role the actor is acting as. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is any string other than one of the six declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The six roles are `Scheduler`, `InfusionNurse`, `Pharmacist`, `AuthorizationSpecialist`, `MedicalDirector`, and `Patient`. When the headers are well formed but the acting role does not appear in the authorized-role list of the addressed endpoint, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The two headers interact differently for each role, and the distinction matters because ownership checks read `X-User-ID` directly:

- When `X-User-Role` is `InfusionNurse`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Nurse` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Nurse` compare that resource's nurse foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, the five chair-side actions on a visit — checking in, starting the infusion, completing the infusion, discharging, and recording an adverse event — require `X-User-ID` to equal the visit's `nurse_id`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NURSE_NOT_ASSIGNED` (HTTP 403). The same header value is what the server stores as `reported_by_nurse_id` on a reaction record.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Patient`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Patient` row. A patient may only address rows that belong to them: cancelling a visit, answering a reminder contact, reading a patient record, reading that patient's no-show risk figure, and reading a single appointment all compare the addressed row's `patient_id` (or, for the patient record itself, its `id`) to `X-User-ID`, and any mismatch is rejected with `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_OWNER` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `Scheduler`, `Pharmacist`, `AuthorizationSpecialist`, or `MedicalDirector`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record or log it, but it never compares it to any entity's `id`, and no ownership check is performed for these four roles.

Role ownership is peer-shaped rather than hierarchical: no role is a superset of another. A `Scheduler` owns the booking estate but cannot compound a dose, decide a payer authorization, act at the bedside, or close a clinical review. An `InfusionNurse` owns the infusion and the bedside reaction record but cannot book, confirm, cancel, or reschedule. A `Pharmacist` owns the drug catalogue, physical inventory, and compounding, and cannot start an infusion. An `AuthorizationSpecialist` owns submission, resubmission after a pend, and recording the payer's decision, and nothing else that mutates. A `MedicalDirector` owns adverse-event review, the decision to resume a suspended regimen, and the utilization and billing analytics, and cannot compound, infuse, or book. A `Patient` may cancel their own visit and answer their own reminder, and may read their own record, regimens, visits, reminder contacts, and risk score.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two pagination query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` outside its permitted range, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window. Unless a collection declares its own domain ordering (see §2.10), rows are returned sorted by `created_at` ascending, so the oldest row appears first.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and the Minute-of-Day Coordinate System

The server maintains a single logical clock, and every deadline, expiry, freshness check, cutoff, and grace period specified in this document is evaluated against it. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it never falls back to real wall-clock time. An operator in any of the five staff roles may move the clock to any UTC instant by supplying a `current_utc` value; the response reports both the new `current_utc` and the `previous_utc` it replaced. Moving the clock does not by itself recompute any entity's stored state — it changes the answer that every subsequent temporal comparison gives.

Scheduling geometry is expressed in **minutes of day**, counted from midnight UTC of the visit's own `service_date`. A center that opens at 08:00Z and closes at 19:00Z carries an `open_minute` of 480 and a `close_minute` of 1140. `service_date` is always an ISO 8601 calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and every minute-of-day field on a request must be an integer from 0 through 1439 inclusive; a value outside that band, a malformed `service_date`, a missing required field, a mistyped field, a numeric field outside its declared range, or an enum-valued field carrying an undeclared member is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

All chair and nurse occupancy arithmetic uses **half-open intervals**: an occupancy that runs from its `start_minute` up to but not including its `end_minute`. Two half-open intervals overlap exactly when the first begins strictly before the second ends and the second begins strictly before the first ends; an occupancy that ends precisely where another begins does not overlap it.

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and Immutable Records

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`, formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp expressed as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Thirteen of the fourteen resource kinds — infusion centers, chairs, nurses, drugs, drug lots, patients, treatment plans, appointment templates, appointments, authorization requests, drug preparations, adverse events, and reminder contacts — additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation. A recomputation of a derived counter is a mutation for this purpose: when a booking pushes a chair's committed minutes up, that chair's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` advances.

Laboratory results are the single exception. A `LabResult` row carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, because a filed draw is immutable. A corrected value is never patched onto an existing draw; it is filed as a new, later draw and the gate simply consults the most recent one.

### 2.6. Precision and Rounding

Three numeric conventions govern the whole domain, and they are not interchangeable.

**Minutes and units are integers.** Every duration, minute-of-day, acuity point, dispensing unit, and sweep counter in the system is a whole number. Where the specification says a value is rounded up to a whole number, the rounding is a ceiling — the smallest whole number that is not less than the exact value.

**Percentages and scores carry two decimal places, rounded half up.** Both the chair-utilization percentage and the no-show risk score are computed as exact ratios and then rounded to two decimal places using round-half-up, so an exact 12.345 becomes 12.35. A patient's persisted `no_show_risk_score` obeys the same rule.

**Money is in integer cents.** Drug acquisition cost, waste cost, cancellation charges, patient liability, and the day's billable revenue are all whole numbers of cents, and no monetary field is ever expressed in fractional cents. Exactly one rounding step occurs on the money path: when a patient's percentage share of a wasted dose is applied, the resulting share is rounded half up to a whole number of cents before it is added to the base charge. The weight-based dose figure that drives tier selection is the only value rounded to one decimal place, again half up.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Superseding Writes

Three write operations in this domain legitimately supersede the row they address, which means no state precondition can distinguish a deliberate second supersede from one derived from a view of the row that has since gone stale. Those three writes therefore each require an optimistic-concurrency field as a non-optional part of the request body, carrying the `version` value the client last observed on the parent row. If the supplied value does not equal the parent row's current `version`, the write is refused and nothing is mutated.

- Rescheduling a visit requires `expected_appointment_version` to equal the addressed `Appointment` row's current `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_APPOINTMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Because a reschedule cancels the original visit at zero charge and mints a replacement row that records `rescheduled_from_appointment_id`, this check is the only thing that stops a client from superseding a visit it has not actually seen.
- Booking an inventory adjustment against a lot requires `expected_drug_lot_version` to equal the addressed `DrugLot` row's current `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Successive adjustments are each individually legal, so the stale-version check is what makes a superseded physical count observable rather than silently overwritten.
- Filing a dose revision against a regimen requires `expected_treatment_plan_version` to equal the addressed `TreatmentPlan` row's current `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A revision derived from a stale dose view would otherwise silently discard an intervening dose change.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field. Every other mutation is accepted on the strength of its state preconditions alone.

### 2.8. The Validation Precedence Ladder

When a single request would fail several checks, the response must carry the error of the **highest-precedence** failing check, and the ladder below is that precedence in strict order. This ordering is a contract, not a suggestion: a request that is simultaneously unauthenticated and malformed reports the authentication failure, and a booking that would simultaneously breach a clinical safety bar and a capacity bar reports the safety failure, because in a hospital setting clinical checks precede fiscal checks and fiscal checks precede capacity checks.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` naming one of the six roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorization.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Nurse assignment ownership.** On the five chair-side visit actions, `X-User-ID` equals the visit's `nurse_id` — otherwise `ERR_NURSE_NOT_ASSIGNED`.
4. **Patient ownership.** When the acting role is `Patient`, `X-User-ID` equals the `patient_id` of the addressed visit, reminder contact, or patient row — otherwise `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_OWNER`.
5. **Request shape.** Every required input field present, correctly typed, within its declared numeric range, with declared enum members only, minute-of-day fields integral in 0 through 1439, and `service_date` a valid calendar date — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
6. **Path existence.** Each identifier in the path resolves to an existing row of the addressed collection, checked left to right — otherwise the 404 belonging to that collection.
7. **Body foreign-key existence.** Every foreign-key field in the request body resolves to an existing row, validated in the order the endpoint's input list declares those fields — otherwise the 404 belonging to the missing row's collection.
8. **Optimistic concurrency.** The three stale-version checks of §2.7, in the order appointment, then lot, then plan (only one applies to any given endpoint).
9. **State preconditions on the addressed row.** The status-and-transition checks described throughout §3: `ERR_INVALID_SCHEDULE_STATE`, then `ERR_APPOINTMENT_TERMINAL`, then `ERR_INVALID_AUTH_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PREPARATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ADVERSE_EVENT_STATE`, then the regimen bars `ERR_PLAN_SUSPENDED`, `ERR_PLAN_COMPLETED`, `ERR_PLAN_SUPERSEDED`, then `ERR_TEMPLATE_RETIRED`, then `ERR_CHAIR_OUT_OF_SERVICE`, then `ERR_LOT_NOT_AVAILABLE`, then `ERR_REMINDER_ALREADY_ANSWERED`.
10. **Referential coherence between the named rows.** In order: `ERR_PLAN_PATIENT_MISMATCH`, `ERR_TEMPLATE_DRUG_MISMATCH`, `ERR_TEMPLATE_CENTER_MISMATCH`, `ERR_TEMPLATE_TIER_MISMATCH`, `ERR_TEMPLATE_VERSION_STALE`, `ERR_CYCLE_OUT_OF_RANGE`.
11. **Clinical safety.** In order: `ERR_ADVERSE_EVENT_OPEN`, `ERR_LAB_RESULT_MISSING`, `ERR_LAB_RESULT_STALE`, `ERR_LAB_GATE_NOT_MET`, `ERR_PREP_STABILITY_SHORT`, `ERR_PATIENT_STEERING_HOLD`.
12. **Authorization and fiscal gates.** In order: `ERR_AUTH_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_AUTH_EXPIRED`, `ERR_AUTH_RESUBMIT_LIMIT`.
13. **Temporal windows.** In order: `ERR_SERVICE_DATE_IN_PAST`, `ERR_SAME_DAY_REQUIRES_ADDON`, `ERR_ADDON_DATE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_ADDON_CUTOFF_PASSED`, `ERR_OUTSIDE_OPERATING_HOURS`, `ERR_CHECK_IN_WRONG_DAY`, `ERR_PREP_TOO_EARLY`, `ERR_PATIENT_DOUBLE_BOOKED`.
14. **Downstream readiness.** `ERR_PREPARATION_NOT_READY`, the check that a compounded dose is actually standing by before an infusion begins.
15. **Capacity and quota.** Evaluated last, in order: `ERR_URGENT_RESERVE_LIMIT`, `ERR_NO_CHAIR_CAPACITY`, `ERR_URGENT_RESERVE_UNAVAILABLE`, `ERR_NO_NURSE_ON_SHIFT`, `ERR_NURSE_ACUITY_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_REMINDER_CADENCE_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_LOT`, `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_DRUG_UNITS`.
16. **Uniqueness.** `ERR_DUPLICATE_MRN`, the last check applied when a patient is registered.

Note two consequences of the ladder that are easy to get wrong. First, the terminal-visit bar sits above every clinical and capacity check, so an action against a visit that has already reached `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED`, or `NO_SHOW` always reports `ERR_APPOINTMENT_TERMINAL` rather than whatever else was also wrong. Second, capacity refusals sit at the very bottom, so a booking that names a past `service_date` and would also find no free chair reports `ERR_SERVICE_DATE_IN_PAST`.

### 2.9. Derived Counters, Cascades and Conserved Quantities

A great deal of this system's state is derived rather than supplied, and derived values are recomputed eagerly — the platform is immediately consistent, never eventually consistent. Two derived quantities in particular are **conserved**, meaning that a compensating action must restore them exactly.

The first is a chair's `committed_minutes`, the sum of the `chair_minutes` of every visit that holds that chair across all service dates while its `schedule_status` is one of `TENTATIVE`, `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `INFUSING`, or `OBSERVING`. Visits in `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED`, or `NO_SHOW` sit outside that committed set and therefore contribute nothing; a booking raises the figure and any exit from the committed set lowers it by exactly what it added. A nurse's `assigned_acuity_points` behaves identically over the same five statuses, summing the `acuity_points` of the visits assigned to that nurse.

The second is a lot's `units_available`, defined as `units_received` less `units_committed`, less `units_administered`, less `units_wasted`. The separate `units_returned` counter is a cumulative audit figure only and is deliberately **not** part of that balance, because returned units have already been added back into `units_available`. Requesting a dose and then returning it therefore leaves `units_available` exactly where it started.

Cascades in this domain run to completion within the triggering request, and each hop is a real mutation that refreshes `updated_at` and advances `version` on the row it touches. Where a single operation moves more than one entity, §3 names every hop; the important structural pattern is that a patient-level steering flip suspends that patient's live regimens, each regimen suspension cancels that regimen's still-schedulable visits at zero charge, and each of those cancellations releases chair minutes, nurse acuity, and drug commitments. Nothing in that chain is optional or deferred.

### 2.10. Collection Reads, Path Resolution and Filters

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the error. Each collection has its own 404: `ERR_CENTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHAIR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NURSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DRUG_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEMPLATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PREPARATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ADVERSE_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LAB_RESULT_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_REMINDER_NOT_FOUND`. The same catalogue answers a foreign key in a request body that does not resolve — for instance, a booking naming a `treatment_plan_id` that does not exist reports `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, and registering a biosimilar whose `reference_drug_id` does not resolve reports `ERR_DRUG_NOT_FOUND`.

Because the topology is flat, every relationship declared in this specification is reverse-navigable through a filter on the relevant collection read rather than through a nested path. Chairs may be filtered by `center_id`, by `status`, and by `reserved_for_urgent`, and are returned ordered by `label` ascending. Nurses may be filtered by `center_id` and `status`. Drugs may be filtered by `name`, by `is_biosimilar`, and by `reference_drug_id` — the last of which returns the biosimilar family of one originator product and is how site-of-care steering candidates are discovered. Drug lots may be filtered by `drug_id` and `status` and are ordered by `expires_on` ascending then `created_at` ascending, which is exactly the order the lot selector consults them in. Patients may be filtered by `mrn`, `reminder_tier`, and `steering_status`. Regimens may be filtered by `patient_id`, `drug_id`, `status`, and `supersedes_plan_id`, the latter walking a revision chain forward from a superseded regimen to its replacement. Templates may be filtered by `center_id`, `drug_id`, `dose_tier`, and `status`. Infusion centers may be filtered by `name`.

Visits accept the widest filter set — any combination of `center_id`, `patient_id`, `treatment_plan_id`, `template_id`, `chair_id`, `nurse_id`, `rescheduled_from_appointment_id`, `service_date`, `schedule_status`, `readiness_status`, and `booking_class` — and are ordered by `service_date` ascending, then `start_minute` ascending, then `created_at` ascending. Authorization requests may be filtered by `appointment_id`, `treatment_plan_id`, `payer_name`, and `status`. Compounded doses may be filtered by `appointment_id`, `drug_lot_id`, and `status`. Reaction records may be filtered by `appointment_id`, `reported_by_nurse_id`, `grade`, and `status`. Laboratory draws may be filtered by `patient_id` and `status` and are ordered by `collected_at` descending then `created_at` descending, the order in which the lab gate considers them. Reminder contacts may be filtered by `appointment_id`, `patient_id`, `channel`, and `outcome`, and are ordered by `sequence_number` ascending.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Suite: The Chair Estate and the Nursing Roster

A `Scheduler` brings a suite online by registering an **infusion center**, supplying a `name` of 1 to 120 non-blank characters together with the two boundaries of its service day: `open_minute`, the first bookable minute counted from midnight UTC, and `close_minute`, the exclusive end of the day. The two must satisfy an ordering — `open_minute` is zero or greater, strictly less than `close_minute`, and `close_minute` is at most 1440 — and every chair occupancy the center ever accepts must end at or before `close_minute`. Six further policy dials may be supplied and each has a default when omitted: `turnover_buffer_minutes`, the cleaning time appended to the **end** of every chair occupancy before the next may begin, defaults to a quarter of an hour and may be anywhere from zero to a full hour; `urgent_reserve_chair_count`, the ceiling on how many of the center's chairs may simultaneously be flagged as urgent reserve, defaults to one and may be anywhere from zero to ten; `addon_cutoff_minute`, the latest minute of the day at which a same-day add-on may still be accepted, defaults to 900 — that is, 15:00Z — and must itself lie between `open_minute` and `close_minute` inclusive; `lab_validity_hours`, the maximum age a laboratory draw may have and still satisfy a drug's lab gate, defaults to three full days and may be anywhere from one hour to a full week; `stagger_grid_minutes`, the granularity of legal start minutes, defaults to a quarter of an hour and must be one of 5, 10, 15, 20, or 30; and `acuity_slot_minutes`, the width of the nurse-acuity accounting slot, defaults to half an hour and must be 15, 30, or 60.

Two figures on the center are derived and are reported as zero the moment the center is registered, before any chair exists. `available_chair_count` is the number of that center's chairs whose `status` is `AVAILABLE`, and chairs flagged for urgent use **are** counted in it. `reserved_chair_count` is the narrower number of that center's chairs that are both `AVAILABLE` and carry `reserved_for_urgent` set to true.

Chairs are added one at a time. Each names its owning centre through `center_id` and carries a `label` of 1 to 16 characters that must be unique among that center's chairs; the label doubles as the deterministic tie-break key the chair selector falls back on, compared byte-wise in ascending order. A chair's physical class is given by `chair_type`, one of `STANDARD`, `BARIATRIC`, or `ISOLATION`, defaulting to `STANDARD`. Its serviceability is `status`, either `AVAILABLE` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, defaulting to `AVAILABLE`. The `reserved_for_urgent` flag defaults to false; when true, the chair is withheld from `ROUTINE` and `SAME_DAY_ADDON` bookings entirely and is offered **first** to `URGENT` ones. A newly added chair starts with `committed_minutes` at zero, and adding it immediately re-derives its center's `available_chair_count` and `reserved_chair_count`.

One combination is forbidden at all times: no chair may be left simultaneously `OUT_OF_SERVICE` and flagged `reserved_for_urgent`. Neither the creation of a chair nor a later edit to one may produce that pairing — an `OUT_OF_SERVICE` chair cannot be nominated as the urgent reserve, and a chair already carrying `reserved_for_urgent` cannot be taken `OUT_OF_SERVICE` by the same request that sets the flag. Either attempt is refused with `ERR_CHAIR_OUT_OF_SERVICE` (HTTP 409).

Editing a chair's serviceability is more consequential than it looks. A `Scheduler` may change a chair's `status`, its `reserved_for_urgent` flag, or both; the write refreshes the owning center's two derived counts and advances the chair's own `version` by one. When the edit moves the chair to `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, the chair not only leaves every future selector run — it also releases the visits already holding it. Each of that chair's visits still sitting in `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED` is demoted along the bump path described in §3.5: `schedule_status` becomes `TENTATIVE` (a `CONFIRMED` visit is demoted, an already-`TENTATIVE` one stays put), both `chair_id` and `nurse_id` are cleared to null, and the released chair's `committed_minutes` and the released nurse's `assigned_acuity_points` are both recomputed. Visits already at or past `CHECKED_IN` are left strictly alone — the patient is in the chair.

The nursing roster is configured the same way. A nurse names their `center_id` and `full_name`, declares `acuity_capacity_points` — the maximum sum of visit acuity the nurse may carry in any single acuity slot, from 1 through 20 — and declares a shift as `shift_start_minute` and `shift_end_minute`, where the start is zero or greater, strictly before the end, and the end is at most 1440. A nurse's `status` is `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE` and defaults to `ACTIVE`; only `ACTIVE` nurses are selectable. `assigned_acuity_points` begins at zero. A `Scheduler` may later adjust `acuity_capacity_points`, `shift_start_minute`, `shift_end_minute`, or `status`, and any such edit advances the nurse's `version` by one. Moving a nurse to `INACTIVE` triggers the same bump path a chair going out of service does: that nurse's `TENTATIVE` and `CONFIRMED` visits are demoted to `TENTATIVE` with `chair_id` and `nurse_id` cleared and the affected chairs' and nurses' derived load figures recomputed.

### 3.2. The Drug Catalogue, Physical Lots and the Unit Ledger

A `Pharmacist` owns the product catalogue. A drug is registered with a `name` of 1 to 120 characters that must be unique across the catalogue, and with a timing profile that later becomes the raw material of every appointment template. `base_administration_minutes` is the baseline push time before any dose-tier escalation, from 5 through 480; `min_administration_minutes` is the clinical floor below which the administration leg never falls, likewise from 5 through 480. `premedication_minutes`, defaulting to zero and capped at 120, is the pre-medication window that occupies the chair **before** the administration leg begins. Two observation windows are declared rather than one: `observation_minutes_first_dose` applies to a patient's first dose on a regimen and `observation_minutes_subsequent` applies once a dose on that regimen has already completed, each from zero through 240. `acuity_points`, from 1 through 10, is the baseline nursing acuity cost of one administration. `prep_stability_minutes`, from 15 through 1440, is how long a compounded dose of this product stays usable after it is mixed. `unit_cost_cents` is the acquisition cost in cents of one dispensing unit — one vial — and must not be negative.

Biosimilar lineage is expressed on the product itself. `is_biosimilar` defaults to false; `reference_drug_id` points at the originator product a biosimilar references and must be null whenever `is_biosimilar` is false. Originator products carry no reference. This self-referential family is what makes site-of-care and product steering measurable later.

Laboratory gating is also a product property. `requires_lab_gate` defaults to false. When it is true, the drug must also carry `lab_min_anc`, the minimum absolute neutrophil count in units of ten to the ninth per litre permitting administration, and `lab_min_platelets`, the minimum platelet count in the same units; both default to null and, when present, must be zero or greater.

Physical inventory arrives as **lots**. A lot names its `drug_id` and a `lot_number` of 1 to 32 characters unique among that drug's lots, declares `units_received` (zero or greater) and an `expires_on` instant, and is born with `units_available` equal to `units_received`, every other counter at zero, and `status` `AVAILABLE`. Five counters then track the lot's whole life. `units_committed` is the sum of `units_requested` across that lot's compounded doses still in `REQUESTED` or `PREPARED`. `units_administered` sums those that reached `ADMINISTERED`. `units_wasted` sums those that reached `WASTED`. `units_returned` sums those that reached `RETURNED` and is, as §2.9 states, a pure audit figure outside the conservation balance. `units_available` is `units_received` reduced by the committed, administered, and wasted counters — the returned counter is deliberately excluded because returned units are already back in the available pool.

A lot's `status` is derived, not supplied. It reads `AVAILABLE` while `units_available` is above zero and `expires_on` is still later than the logical clock. It becomes `DEPLETED` the moment any recomputation drives `units_available` to exactly zero — whether that recomputation came from a new dose request or from a negative inventory adjustment — and a `DEPLETED` lot is out of the selection pool. The reverse edge exists too: a `DEPLETED` lot returns to `AVAILABLE` when a recomputation lifts `units_available` above zero again, which happens when a compounded or merely-requested dose is returned or when a positive inventory adjustment is booked, and only while `expires_on` remains later than the clock. Expiry is terminal: once the daily ops checkpoint observes that `expires_on` has reached or passed the clock, a lot in either `AVAILABLE` or `DEPLETED` moves to `EXPIRED` and never leaves that state, though preparations already committed against it are untouched by that step.

Pharmacy counts do not always match the receipt. A `Pharmacist` may book an inventory adjustment against a lot — a receipt correction or a physical-count shrinkage — by supplying a signed `units_delta`, a free-text `reason`, and the `expected_drug_lot_version` described in §2.7. The adjustment adds `units_delta` to `units_received` and re-derives both `units_available` and `status`, then advances the lot's `version` by one. Two bars guard it: an adjustment that would drive `units_available` below zero is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_DRUG_UNITS` (HTTP 422), and an adjustment against a lot that is no longer `AVAILABLE` — because it has expired or been depleted — is refused with `ERR_LOT_NOT_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 409).

### 3.3. Patients, Regimens and Dose Revisions

A patient record is thin by design: an `mrn` of 1 to 32 characters that must be unique across the entire system, a `full_name` of 1 to 120 non-blank characters, and a `weight_kg` dosing weight from 1 through 400 that is later multiplied by the regimen's per-kilogram dose to derive the milligram dose. Registering a second patient with an `mrn` another patient already holds is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_MRN` (HTTP 409). Everything else on the record is derived, and a freshly registered patient reads `no_show_strikes` zero, `no_show_risk_score` 0.00, `reminder_tier` `STANDARD`, `waste_liability_cents` zero, and `steering_status` `SUITE`.

Those derived figures are the patient's adherence and cost profile. `completed_appointment_count` counts that patient's visits whose `schedule_status` is `COMPLETED`. `no_show_strikes` counts that patient's visits in `NO_SHOW` whose `service_date` falls within the 180 calendar days ending on the logical clock's own date, that end date included. `no_show_risk_score` is the persisted recency-weighted figure specified in §3.9, carried to two decimal places. `reminder_tier` is read straight off that score: `STANDARD` while the score is below 20, `ELEVATED` while the score is at least 20 and still below 45, and `INTENSIVE` once the score reaches 45 or more. `waste_liability_cents` is the running sum of `cancellation_charge_cents` over every one of that patient's visits sitting in `CANCELLED`. Finally, `steering_status` is `SUITE` for an ordinary patient and flips to `HOME_INFUSION_REVIEW` as soon as either `no_show_strikes` reaches 3 or more or `waste_liability_cents` reaches 50,000 cents — five hundred dollars of accumulated liability — and once flipped it never returns to `SUITE` on its own. Only a deliberate clinical decision restores in-suite eligibility.

A **treatment plan** is the regimen a patient is on. Opening one requires the named `patient_id` and `drug_id` both to exist, and declares `dose_mg_per_kg` (greater than zero and at most 100), `total_cycles` (from 1 through 52), and optionally `premed_waived`, which defaults to false and, when true, drops the pre-medication leg from the chair window of every visit on the regimen. A new regimen is `ACTIVE`, with `completed_cycles` zero, `first_dose_completed` false, and `supersedes_plan_id` null. Both derived cycle figures follow the visits: `completed_cycles` counts that regimen's `COMPLETED` visits and `first_dose_completed` is true exactly when `completed_cycles` is 1 or more, which is what selects the subsequent-dose observation window over the first-dose one.

A regimen's `status` walks four values. It is `ACTIVE` from creation. It becomes `SUSPENDED` when a patient's steering flip or an adverse-event review disposition pulls it out of service, and a suspension is not quiet: every visit on that regimen still sitting in `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED` is cancelled with `cancellation_charge_cents` set to zero, and each of those cancellations in turn recomputes the released chair's `committed_minutes` and the released nurse's `assigned_acuity_points` and returns or wastes that visit's compounded doses exactly as §3.7 describes. It becomes `COMPLETED` by bubble-up: when a discharge recomputes `completed_cycles` and finds it has reached or passed `total_cycles`, the regimen leaves `ACTIVE` for `COMPLETED`. And it becomes `SUPERSEDED` when a revision replaces it.

A suspension can be lifted. A `MedicalDirector` may resume a `SUSPENDED` regimen with a `reason`, returning its `status` to `ACTIVE` and advancing its `version` by one, but only while the patient's `steering_status` still reads `SUITE` — resuming a regimen for a patient under a steering review is not permitted. Resumption never resurrects the visits the suspension already cancelled; the regimen simply becomes bookable again. The `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` to `ACTIVE` round trip is a legal cycle and may repeat.

Dose revisions are the other way a regimen ends. A `Scheduler` or a `MedicalDirector` files a revision by supplying a new `dose_mg_per_kg`, a new `total_cycles`, optionally a new `premed_waived` flag, and the `expected_treatment_plan_version` guard of §2.7. On success the addressed regimen moves to `SUPERSEDED` and its `version` advances by one; a brand-new regimen is minted in `ACTIVE` carrying `supersedes_plan_id` pointing back at the one it replaced and copying that regimen's `patient_id` and `drug_id`; and every visit on the superseded regimen still in `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED` is cancelled with `cancellation_charge_cents` zero, releasing its chair minutes, its nurse acuity, and its drug commitments. The state model declares this supersede edge out of both `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED` with an identical side-effect chain in either case.

Three status bars guard the regimen surface, and they are the same three that guard booking. A regimen that is `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_PLAN_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 409); one that is `COMPLETED` is refused with `ERR_PLAN_COMPLETED` (HTTP 409); one that is `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_PLAN_SUPERSEDED` (HTTP 409). All three apply when a visit is booked, when a visit is rescheduled, and when a revision is filed; the completed and superseded bars additionally apply when a resumption is attempted. Filing a revision also re-checks that the regimen's `patient_id` is the patient the request is about — a mismatch is `ERR_PLAN_PATIENT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).

### 3.4. Timing Templates and Sizing the Chair Window

Nothing in this system is booked directly against a drug's timing profile. Bookings are made against an **appointment template**, a center-scoped, dose-tier-specific snapshot of that profile, and the indirection is deliberate: a pharmacist who corrects a product's push time next month must not retro-change windows that were already booked and staffed this month.

A `Scheduler` or a `Pharmacist` builds a template by naming a `center_id`, a `drug_id`, and a `dose_tier` of `LOW`, `MID`, or `HIGH`. Among templates whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, the combination of centre, drug, and tier must be unique. Creation reads exactly one drug row and one center row — no patient and no visit participate — and snapshots five timing figures across without alteration: `base_admin_minutes` takes the drug's `base_administration_minutes`, `min_admin_minutes` takes its `min_administration_minutes`, `premed_minutes` takes its `premedication_minutes`, `observation_minutes_first` takes its `observation_minutes_first_dose`, and `observation_minutes_subsequent` takes its `observation_minutes_subsequent`. Two further figures are derived at the same moment. The template's own `acuity_points` is the drug's `acuity_points` for a `LOW` or `MID` template, and the drug's `acuity_points` plus one surcharge point for a `HIGH` template, because the top dose band is heavier work at the chair. And `stagger_offset_minutes` is the center's `stagger_grid_minutes` doubled when the template's derived `acuity_points` is 4 or more, and the plain `stagger_grid_minutes` otherwise — high-acuity products are spread twice as widely across the grid so that nurses are not asked to start two heavy pushes back to back. A template is born `ACTIVE`.

Templates have exactly two levers after creation, and both advance the template's `version` by one. Setting `status` to `RETIRED` takes the template out of service: a `RETIRED` template can no longer back a new visit, and a booking or reschedule that names one is refused with `ERR_TEMPLATE_RETIRED` (HTTP 409). Setting `resnapshot` to true re-reads every one of the five timing fields from the current drug profile and re-derives both `acuity_points` and `stagger_offset_minutes` from the values it just read. Those are the only two levers: `status` and `resnapshot` are the whole editable surface of a template, and the snapshotted minutes can never be written directly. Either lever has a downstream consequence worth stating plainly: because the version advances, every visit that pinned the template's older version becomes stale and can no longer be confirmed.

Version pinning works in two directions. A booking request may optionally supply `template_version`, asserting the template revision the requester believes they are booking against; when supplied and unequal to the template's current `version`, the booking is refused with `ERR_TEMPLATE_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 409). Independently, every successful booking records `template_version_pinned` — the template's `version` as observed at scheduling time — and confirmation later compares that pinned value against the template's current `version`, refusing with the same `ERR_TEMPLATE_VERSION_STALE` when the template has moved on in the interim.

**Sizing the window.** Every visit's chair occupancy is built in a fixed sequence from the regimen, the patient, and the pinned template. The regimen must be `ACTIVE` and the template must be `ACTIVE` for the build to run at all.

First, the milligram dose is the regimen's `dose_mg_per_kg` multiplied by the patient's `weight_kg`, rounded half up to one decimal place. Second, that dose selects a tier with both upper edges inclusive: the tier is `LOW` when the dose is 500 milligrams or less, `MID` when the dose is above 500 and at most 1000, and `HIGH` when the dose is above 1000. Each tier carries an escalation added to the push time — nothing for `LOW`, half an hour for `MID`, and a full hour for `HIGH`. Third, the pinned template's own `dose_tier` must equal the tier just derived; if it does not, the booking is refused with `ERR_TEMPLATE_TIER_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422), because a template built for one dose band cannot time a visit in another.

Fourth, the visit's `admin_minutes` is the larger of two quantities: the template's `min_admin_minutes` on the one hand, and the template's `base_admin_minutes` plus the tier escalation on the other. The floor is applied **over** the escalated value, not before it, so a short-floored product can still be escalated past its floor. Fifth, the two conditional legs are resolved. The visit's `premed_minutes` is zero when the regimen carries `premed_waived` set to true, and otherwise the template's `premed_minutes`. The base observation leg is the template's `observation_minutes_first` when the regimen's `first_dose_completed` is false, and the template's `observation_minutes_subsequent` when it is true; the visit's `observation_minutes` is that base leg plus the visit's `observation_extension_minutes`, which is zero at creation and only ever grows through the reaction path of §3.8.

Sixth and last, occupancy and acuity fall out. The visit's `chair_minutes` is its `premed_minutes` plus its `admin_minutes` plus its `observation_minutes` — the pre-medication window, the push, and the observation, in that physical order. The visit's `acuity_points` is the template's `acuity_points` plus one surcharge point when the regimen's `first_dose_completed` is false, and the template's `acuity_points` unchanged once a dose on that regimen has completed; a patient's first exposure to a product is watched more closely than their fifth.

### 3.5. Booking a Visit: Grid Search, Booking Classes and Capacity Refusals

A `Scheduler` books a visit by naming the `center_id` it will occupy, the `patient_id` being infused, the `treatment_plan_id` whose cycle it administers, the `template_id` that will time it, a `service_date`, a `requested_start_minute`, and a `cycle_number`. Optionally the request carries a `booking_class` — defaulting to `ROUTINE` — and the `template_version` assertion described above. A visit is born already holding a chair and a nurse; there is no unassigned intake state and no queue.

Before the selector runs, the named rows must cohere. The regimen's `patient_id` must be the patient in the request, or the booking fails with `ERR_PLAN_PATIENT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The template's `drug_id` must equal the regimen's `drug_id`, or the booking fails with `ERR_TEMPLATE_DRUG_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The template's `center_id` must equal the requested `center_id`, or the booking fails with `ERR_TEMPLATE_CENTER_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The requested `cycle_number` must lie between 1 and the regimen's `total_cycles` inclusive **and** must not already be held by another visit of the same regimen that is either still live or already `COMPLETED`; a violation of either half is `ERR_CYCLE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422). The `requested_start_minute` must be at or after the center's `open_minute` and strictly before its `close_minute`. The patient's `steering_status` must read `SUITE`: a patient under `HOME_INFUSION_REVIEW` cannot take new in-suite capacity, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_PATIENT_STEERING_HOLD` (HTTP 409). Clinical safety is also checked here rather than only at the bedside — if any reaction record belonging to the patient's prior visits is still sitting in `REPORTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` when a new visit for the same drug is booked, the booking is refused with `ERR_ADVERSE_EVENT_OPEN` (HTTP 409). Finally, the patient must not already hold another non-terminal visit on the same `service_date` whose half-open window from `start_minute` up to `end_minute` overlaps the window being built — measured **without** the turnover buffer — or the booking is refused with `ERR_PATIENT_DOUBLE_BOOKED` (HTTP 409).

**The three booking classes** differ in how far into the future they reach and how hard they push. `ROUTINE` books a future service day and lets the selector search forward across the grid. `SAME_DAY_ADDON` books the logical clock's own date and only before the center's add-on cutoff. `URGENT` also books the clock's own date, but takes a single fixed start minute and may claim a chair that ordinary bookings cannot touch. Four temporal rules police the combinations, and they are evaluated in the order given. A `service_date` earlier than the logical clock's own UTC date is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_DATE_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422). A booking that names the clock's own UTC date while declaring `booking_class` `ROUTINE` is refused with `ERR_SAME_DAY_REQUIRES_ADDON` (HTTP 422) — same-day work must be labelled as such. Conversely, a `SAME_DAY_ADDON` or `URGENT` booking whose `service_date` is anything other than the clock's own UTC date is refused with `ERR_ADDON_DATE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). A `SAME_DAY_ADDON` request must arrive at or before the center's `addon_cutoff_minute` measured on the clock's own day — arriving exactly at the cutoff minute is accepted, arriving after it is refused with `ERR_ADDON_CUTOFF_PASSED` (HTTP 422) — while `URGENT` is exempt from the cutoff entirely, since urgency is precisely the case the cutoff would otherwise block. Separately, once the grid-snapped start is known, both that start and the full chair window must fit inside the center's operating window from `open_minute` through `close_minute`; a window that spills outside it is refused with `ERR_OUTSIDE_OPERATING_HOURS` (HTTP 422).

**The staggered-start selector** then chooses a start minute, a chair, and a nurse together, and it is fully deterministic. It works from two center dials — the grid width `stagger_grid_minutes` and the turnover width `turnover_buffer_minutes` — and from the `chair_minutes` the window build produced.

The first candidate start is the `requested_start_minute` snapped **up** onto the grid: the smallest multiple of `stagger_grid_minutes` that is not less than the requested minute. For an `URGENT` booking that snapped minute is the *only* candidate, because urgency forbids pushing the patient later. For `ROUTINE` and `SAME_DAY_ADDON` the candidate list is nine minutes long in ascending order: the snapped minute, and then eight successive additions of one further grid width. Candidates are then discarded unless the candidate start is at or after `open_minute` and the candidate start plus `chair_minutes` is at or before `close_minute`.

A chair is eligible at a candidate start when four conditions hold together. It belongs to the visit's center. Its `status` is `AVAILABLE`. Either its `reserved_for_urgent` flag is false, or the booking's `booking_class` is `URGENT` — reserved chairs are invisible to `ROUTINE` and `SAME_DAY_ADDON` work. And it is free of buffered collisions: for **every** other visit already holding that chair on the same `service_date` whose `schedule_status` is one of `TENTATIVE`, `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `INFUSING`, or `OBSERVING`, it must not be the case that the candidate start falls before that visit's `end_minute` plus the turnover buffer while that visit's `start_minute` falls before the candidate start plus `chair_minutes` plus the turnover buffer. Read plainly: the turnover buffer is appended to the **end** of both occupancies, so a candidate start landing exactly at an earlier visit's `end_minute` plus the buffer is legal, while one minute earlier is not. Visits in `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED`, or `NO_SHOW` hold no chair time and free their slot.

A nurse passes the shift filter at a candidate start when they belong to the visit's center, their `status` is `ACTIVE`, their `shift_start_minute` is at or before the candidate start, and the candidate start plus `chair_minutes` is at or before their `shift_end_minute` — the shift must cover the whole occupancy, not merely its beginning. A nurse then passes the acuity filter when, for every acuity slot the occupancy touches, that nurse's existing load in the slot plus this visit's `acuity_points` is at most their `acuity_capacity_points`; a sum landing exactly on capacity is accepted. Slots partition the service day starting at `open_minute`, each `acuity_slot_minutes` wide, so slot zero runs from `open_minute` for one slot width, slot one begins where slot zero ends, and so on. A nurse's load in one slot on one service date is the sum of the `acuity_points` of that nurse's visits on that date whose `schedule_status` is one of the same five committed values and whose half-open occupancy intersects the slot. The intersection test is strict in both directions: the visit must begin before the slot ends and the slot must begin before the visit ends, so a visit that ends exactly where a slot begins puts no load on that slot at all. That per-slot sum is also what a nurse's `assigned_acuity_points` figure aggregates.

Candidate starts are tried in ascending order and the **first** one that can be satisfied wins — the selector never looks for a better fit later in the list. At a satisfying start, the chair is chosen by ordering the eligible chairs by `reserved_for_urgent` descending first when the booking is `URGENT` (so a reserved chair is genuinely offered first, and for non-urgent bookings reserved chairs have already been filtered out), then by `committed_minutes` ascending so the least-loaded chair absorbs the work, then by `label` ascending byte-wise as the final tie-break; the first row in that order is taken. The nurse is chosen by ordering the acuity-eligible nurses by `assigned_acuity_points` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending, taking the first row. The visit records the chosen chair as `chair_id`, the chosen nurse as `nurse_id`, the winning candidate as `start_minute`, and `end_minute` as `start_minute` plus `chair_minutes`.

When no candidate start can be satisfied, the refusal names the *first* resource that ran out, evaluated in exactly this order. If no candidate start had a single eligible chair, the answer depends on the class: an `URGENT` booking is refused with `ERR_URGENT_RESERVE_UNAVAILABLE` (HTTP 409) and any other class with `ERR_NO_CHAIR_CAPACITY` (HTTP 409). Otherwise, if some candidate start had an eligible chair but no candidate start had a single shift-covering `ACTIVE` nurse, the refusal is `ERR_NO_NURSE_ON_SHIFT` (HTTP 409). Otherwise a chair and an on-shift nurse were both available at some start and only the per-slot acuity ceiling stood in the way, and the refusal is `ERR_NURSE_ACUITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). One further quota is checked ahead of all three: the number of `URGENT` visits already holding a reserved chair at that center on that `service_date` must be strictly below the number of that center's chairs carrying `reserved_for_urgent`; when the reserve is already fully claimed the booking is refused with `ERR_URGENT_RESERVE_LIMIT` (HTTP 409).

A successful booking lands the visit in `schedule_status` `TENTATIVE`. Beyond the window and assignment fields, it pins `template_version_pinned`, derives `reminder_cadence_code` from the patient's current `reminder_tier`, raises the chosen chair's `committed_minutes` by the visit's `chair_minutes`, raises the chosen nurse's `assigned_acuity_points` by the visit's `acuity_points`, and derives the visit's initial `readiness_status`: `PENDING` when the regimen's drug carries `requires_lab_gate` set to true and no qualifying laboratory draw is yet on file, and `LAB_CLEARED` when the laboratory condition is already satisfied — either because the drug has no gate at all or because a qualifying draw already exists.

**Confirmation** moves a visit from `TENTATIVE` to `CONFIRMED` and is the scheduler's commitment that the visit will happen. It requires the visit to actually be in `TENTATIVE`, to still hold both a `chair_id` and a `nurse_id`, to have a `template_version_pinned` equal to the template's current `version`, to sit on a regimen whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and to be covered by an authorization request in `APPROVED` whose `expires_at` is later than the logical clock. A visit with no `APPROVED` authorization is refused with `ERR_AUTH_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 409). Confirmation re-derives `readiness_status` and advances the visit's `version` by one. Attempting it from any `schedule_status` other than `TENTATIVE` is `ERR_INVALID_SCHEDULE_STATE` (HTTP 409), and attempting it against a visit already in `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED`, or `NO_SHOW` is `ERR_APPOINTMENT_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409).

**The bump path** is the only movement backwards through `schedule_status` anywhere in this system. A visit in `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED` is bumped when its current combination of `chair_id`, `start_minute`, and `end_minute` no longer satisfies the buffered chair-occupancy rule — which happens when an earlier visit on the same chair has its observation window extended by a reaction — or when its chair leaves `AVAILABLE`, or when its nurse leaves `ACTIVE`. A bumped visit's `schedule_status` becomes `TENTATIVE` (demoting a `CONFIRMED` visit, leaving an already-`TENTATIVE` one where it is), both `chair_id` and `nurse_id` are cleared to null, the released chair's `committed_minutes` and the released nurse's `assigned_acuity_points` are recomputed, and the visit's `version` advances by one. The visit is not cancelled — it is un-seated, and a scheduler must find it a new slot.

### 3.6. Payer Authorization, Laboratory Gating and the Readiness Dimension

A visit carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**, and this is the single most important structural fact about the appointment resource. `schedule_status` tracks where the visit is in the operational day and walks the values `TENTATIVE`, `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `INFUSING`, `OBSERVING`, and then one of the three terminal values `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED`, or `NO_SHOW`. `readiness_status` tracks clinical clearance and walks `PENDING`, `AUTH_CLEARED`, `LAB_CLEARED`, `FULLY_CLEARED`, and `BLOCKED`. Neither dimension is a substate of the other; each evolves from its own triggers, and `readiness_status` never blocks a `schedule_status` movement except where a transition's preconditions explicitly name it.

**Authorization.** An `AuthorizationSpecialist` or a `Scheduler` files a payer authorization request against a visit, supplying only the `payer_name` (1 to 120 non-blank characters). The visit must not already be in one of the three terminal `schedule_status` values, or the filing is refused with `ERR_APPOINTMENT_TERMINAL`. The new request is created in `DRAFT` with `submission_count` at zero, copies the visit's `treatment_plan_id` onto itself so that authorization history is queryable per regimen, and triggers a re-derivation of the visit's `readiness_status`.

Submission is where the cycle lives. An `AuthorizationSpecialist` may submit a request that is in `DRAFT` **or** in `PENDED`, which is what allows a pended request to be re-filed with more clinical justification; either way the `status` becomes `SUBMITTED`, `submission_count` increases by one, the visit's `readiness_status` is re-derived, and the request's `version` advances by one. Submitting a request in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AUTH_STATE` (HTTP 409). The loop is not infinite: `submission_count` must be below 3 for a submission to be accepted, so a fourth attempt against a request that has already been submitted three times is refused with `ERR_AUTH_RESUBMIT_LIMIT` (HTTP 409).

Recording the payer's answer requires the request to be in `SUBMITTED` — any other status is again `ERR_INVALID_AUTH_STATE` — and carries a `decision` naming one of three dispositions, optionally with `approved_units` and a free-text `decision_reason`. On `APPROVED`, `approved_units` must be present and at least 1; it is stored, `decided_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `expires_at` is set to `decided_at` plus 30 days. On `PENDED`, the `decision_reason` is stored, `decided_at` is stamped, and `expires_at` is left null; the request is back in the resubmission loop. On `DENIED`, the `decision_reason` is stored and `decided_at` is stamped, and the request is terminal — `DENIED` requests never move again. All three dispositions re-derive the covered visit's `readiness_status` and advance the request's `version` by one. Separately, the daily ops checkpoint moves an `APPROVED` request whose `expires_at` has reached or passed the logical clock into `EXPIRED`, which is also terminal, and re-derives the covered visit's readiness on the way out.

**Laboratory draws.** A `Pharmacist` or an `InfusionNurse` files a draw for a patient, naming the `patient_id`, the `collected_at` instant at which the specimen was actually drawn, and optionally `anc_value` (from 0 through 100 when present) and `platelet_value` (from 0 through 2000 when present). A draw carrying **both** values is created with `status` `RESULTED`; a draw missing either value is created `PENDING` and is invisible to the gate. Filing a draw re-derives the `readiness_status` of that patient's non-terminal visits. Freshness is always measured from `collected_at`, never from the row's `created_at`, and as §2.5 notes a draw is immutable — corrections are filed as new, later draws.

**The lab gate** is evaluated whenever an infusion is about to start and also as one term of the readiness derivation. When the regimen's drug carries `requires_lab_gate` set to false, the gate passes unconditionally. Otherwise the gate consults **exactly one** draw: of that patient's `RESULTED` draws, ordered by `collected_at` descending and then `created_at` descending, it takes the first — the single most recent resulted draw. If the patient has no `RESULTED` draw at all, the gate fails with `ERR_LAB_RESULT_MISSING` (HTTP 422). If the chosen draw's age, measured in whole and fractional hours from its `collected_at` to the logical clock, is greater than the center's `lab_validity_hours`, the gate fails with `ERR_LAB_RESULT_STALE` (HTTP 422); an age exactly equal to the limit passes. If the chosen draw's `anc_value` is below the drug's `lab_min_anc`, or its `platelet_value` is below the drug's `lab_min_platelets`, the gate fails with `ERR_LAB_GATE_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422); a value exactly equal to the minimum passes. Because only the latest resulted draw is consulted, an older passing draw cannot rescue a newer failing one — filing a bad count deliberately closes the gate.

**Deriving readiness.** `readiness_status` is a pure derivation, recomputed after every event that touches authorization, laboratory work, or reactions for the visit, and it is evaluated in this precedence. If any reaction record on the visit has a `grade` of 3 or higher while its `status` is `REPORTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`, the readiness is `BLOCKED`, whatever else holds. Otherwise, define the authorization condition as *there exists an authorization request for this visit whose `status` is `APPROVED`* — note carefully that `expires_at` is **not** part of this condition, so a lapsed but still-`APPROVED` request keeps clearing the readiness gate until the sweep observes its expiry and moves it to `EXPIRED`. Define the laboratory condition as *the drug carries `requires_lab_gate` false, or the lab gate above returns a pass*. Then the readiness is `FULLY_CLEARED` when both conditions hold, `AUTH_CLEARED` when the authorization condition holds and the laboratory one does not, `LAB_CLEARED` when the laboratory condition holds and the authorization one does not, and `PENDING` when neither holds.

That derivation produces a specific set of observable movements, each of which — except where noted — advances the visit's `version` by one. An `APPROVED` decision moves a visit from `PENDING` to `AUTH_CLEARED` when the drug has a lab gate and no qualifying draw exists yet, and from `PENDING` straight to `FULLY_CLEARED` when the laboratory condition already holds; it also moves a visit from `LAB_CLEARED` to `FULLY_CLEARED`. A newly filed qualifying draw moves a visit from `PENDING` to `LAB_CLEARED` when the authorization condition does not hold, and from `AUTH_CLEARED` to `FULLY_CLEARED` when it does. A reaction of `grade` 3 or higher moves a visit from `FULLY_CLEARED` to `BLOCKED`, and closing that review with a disposition of `RESUME_SAME_DRUG` moves it from `BLOCKED` back to `FULLY_CLEARED` provided no other reaction on the visit still carries a `grade` of 3 or higher in `REPORTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` and both the authorization and laboratory conditions still hold. That `FULLY_CLEARED` to `BLOCKED` to `FULLY_CLEARED` round trip is a legal cycle — a second severe reaction re-blocks the same row. Two expiry-driven movements complete the picture: when the ops checkpoint moves the covering authorization from `APPROVED` to `EXPIRED` while the laboratory condition still holds, the visit moves from `FULLY_CLEARED` to `LAB_CLEARED` and its `version` advances; and when the logical clock is simply moved forward far enough that every `RESULTED` draw of the patient is older than the center's `lab_validity_hours` while the authorization condition still holds, the visit reads `AUTH_CLEARED` on its next read without any `version` bump at all, because a clock move alone mutates nothing.

### 3.7. Compounding, Stability and Dose Disposition

A compounded dose is requested by a `Pharmacist` against a specific visit, and the request carries only `units_requested`, a whole number of dispensing units from 1 through 100. The visit must be in `TENTATIVE`, `CONFIRMED`, or `CHECKED_IN`; any other `schedule_status` is `ERR_INVALID_SCHEDULE_STATE`, and a terminal visit is `ERR_APPOINTMENT_TERMINAL`.

**Lot selection is first-expiry-first-out.** The candidate pool is the set of lots of the regimen's drug whose `status` is `AVAILABLE`, whose `expires_on` is strictly later than the logical clock, and whose `units_available` is at least the `units_requested` being asked for. Partial fills are not permitted: one lot must cover the whole request by itself, so two lots holding four units each cannot jointly satisfy a request for six. The candidates are ordered by `expires_on` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending, and the first row is taken — the stock closest to expiry is always consumed first. When the candidate pool is empty the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_LOT` (HTTP 409). On success the dose is created in `REQUESTED` with `drug_lot_id` set to the chosen lot, that lot's `units_committed` rises by `units_requested` and its `units_available` falls by the same amount, and a lot whose `units_available` thereby reaches zero moves from `AVAILABLE` to `DEPLETED`.

**Compounding** — the physical act of mixing — moves a dose from `REQUESTED` to `PREPARED` and is deliberately hemmed in on both sides, because a dose mixed too early is a dose likely to be wasted. The dose must be in `REQUESTED`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_PREPARATION_STATE` (HTTP 409). The backing lot must still be `AVAILABLE` at the moment of mixing — it may have expired or been depleted since the dose was requested — and if it is not, the attempt is refused with `ERR_LOT_NOT_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 409). On the early side, the logical clock must be at or after the visit's start instant minus one hour, where the start instant is midnight UTC of the visit's `service_date` plus its `start_minute`; mixing more than 60 minutes ahead of the visit is refused with `ERR_PREP_TOO_EARLY` (HTTP 422). On the late side, the dose must still be usable when the drug actually goes up: the logical clock plus the drug's `prep_stability_minutes` must be at or after the visit's start instant plus its `premed_minutes`, because the administration leg does not begin until the pre-medication window has run. A stability window too short to survive until then is refused with `ERR_PREP_STABILITY_SHORT` (HTTP 422). On success the dose's `status` becomes `PREPARED`, `prepared_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `usable_until` is set to `prepared_at` plus the drug's `prep_stability_minutes`, the lot's counters are left untouched (the units are still committed, merely in a different physical form), and the visit's `readiness_status` is re-derived.

From `PREPARED` the dose forks three ways, and the three branches differ in what happens to the lot ledger.

**Administered.** When the visit is discharged, every dose of that visit still in `PREPARED` moves to `ADMINISTERED`. The lot's `units_committed` falls by `units_requested` and its `units_administered` rises by the same amount; `units_available` does not change, because those units were already out of the available pool and have now simply been consumed.

**Wasted.** A dose in `PREPARED` moves to `WASTED` in four situations: the visit is cancelled by a scheduler or a patient, the visit is rescheduled, a regimen cascade cancels the visit, or the daily ops checkpoint observes that `usable_until` has reached or passed the logical clock. Waste is priced: `waste_cost_cents` is written as `units_requested` multiplied by the drug's `unit_cost_cents`, and it is written only on this transition. The lot's `units_committed` falls by `units_requested` and its `units_wasted` rises by the same amount, while `units_available` does not change — the units are physically gone. When the trigger was a patient- or scheduler-initiated cancellation, that `waste_cost_cents` is the figure the cancellation pricing of §3.9 consumes; when the trigger was the ops checkpoint, no patient is billed at all, because no visit was cancelled.

**Returned.** A dose still in `REQUESTED` was never mixed, so nothing is physically lost and it may be returned unconditionally — by an explicit pharmacist return, by a visit cancellation, by a reschedule, or by a regimen cascade. A dose already in `PREPARED` may also be returned by a `Pharmacist`, but only while the logical clock is still strictly before its `usable_until`; a dose past its stability window cannot go back to stock. An explicit return carries a free-text `reason`. Either origin has the same ledger effect, and it is the compensating action that makes the unit ledger conserved: the lot's `units_committed` falls by `units_requested`, its `units_returned` rises by the same amount, and its `units_available` rises by the same amount, so a request followed by a return leaves `units_available` exactly where it started. A `DEPLETED` lot whose `units_available` climbs back above zero returns to `AVAILABLE`. A return attempted from any status other than `REQUESTED` or `PREPARED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PREPARATION_STATE`.

### 3.8. The Chair-Side Day: Check-in through Discharge, Reactions and Review

On the service day itself the visit walks four forward transitions, and every one of them is a bedside action.

**Checking in** advances `schedule_status` from `CONFIRMED` to `CHECKED_IN`. Either a `Scheduler` or an `InfusionNurse` may perform it, and when the actor is an `InfusionNurse` the ownership rule of §2.1 applies — `X-User-ID` must equal the visit's `nurse_id`. Two further bars are specific to this moment. The logical clock's UTC calendar date must equal the visit's `service_date`; checking a patient in a day early or a day late is refused with `ERR_CHECK_IN_WRONG_DAY` (HTTP 409). And the payer coverage must be live in the strict sense: the visit needs an authorization request in `APPROVED` (absence is `ERR_AUTH_NOT_APPROVED`), and that request's `expires_at` must still be later than the logical clock (a lapsed one is `ERR_AUTH_EXPIRED`, HTTP 409). This is where the deliberate asymmetry in the readiness derivation pays off: a lapsed-but-`APPROVED` authorization still reads as cleared on the readiness dimension until the sweep catches it, yet it can never seat a patient, because check-in compares `expires_at` against the clock directly. Check-in advances the visit's `version` by one.

**Starting the infusion** advances `CHECKED_IN` to `INFUSING` and belongs to the assigned `InfusionNurse` alone. The visit's `readiness_status` must not be `BLOCKED`, and any reaction record of the patient still sitting in `REPORTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` bars the start with `ERR_ADVERSE_EVENT_OPEN` (HTTP 409). When the regimen's drug carries `requires_lab_gate`, the lab gate of §3.6 must return a pass, so this is the moment at which `ERR_LAB_RESULT_MISSING`, `ERR_LAB_RESULT_STALE`, and `ERR_LAB_GATE_NOT_MET` surface. At least one compounded dose of the visit must be standing by in `PREPARED`; if none is, the start is refused with `ERR_PREPARATION_NOT_READY` (HTTP 409). On success the visit stamps `actual_start_minute` as the minute of day of the logical clock and advances its `version` by one.

**Completing the infusion** advances `INFUSING` to `OBSERVING`, again by the assigned nurse, and advances `version` by one. It is a small transition with a large meaning: the drug is in, and what remains is the observation window the template sized.

**Discharging** advances `OBSERVING` to `COMPLETED` and is the settlement point of the whole visit. The assigned nurse performs it, and it requires that no reaction record on the visit carries a `grade` of 3 or higher while still in `REPORTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` — equivalently, that `readiness_status` is not `BLOCKED`. A patient with an unreviewed severe reaction is not sent home. The cascade that follows is long and every hop is mandatory. The visit stamps `actual_end_minute` from the logical clock's minute of day and computes `billable_chair_units` as the realised chair time — `actual_end_minute` less `actual_start_minute` — divided by 15 and rounded **up** to a whole number, so a 46-minute occupancy bills four quarter-hour units. Every compounded dose of the visit still in `PREPARED` moves to `ADMINISTERED`, and each of those movements recomputes its lot's `units_committed`, `units_administered`, and `units_available`, with a lot whose `units_available` reaches zero moving from `AVAILABLE` to `DEPLETED`. The chair's `committed_minutes` falls by the visit's `chair_minutes`, because `COMPLETED` sits outside the committed set, and the nurse's `assigned_acuity_points` falls by the visit's `acuity_points`. The regimen's `completed_cycles` and `first_dose_completed` are recomputed, and when `completed_cycles` has reached or passed `total_cycles` the regimen itself moves from `ACTIVE` to `COMPLETED`. The patient's `completed_appointment_count` and `no_show_risk_score` are recomputed and `reminder_tier` re-derived. Finally the visit's `version` advances by one.

**Reactions at the chair.** The assigned `InfusionNurse` records a reaction while the visit is in `INFUSING` or `OBSERVING` — no earlier and no later — supplying a `grade` from 1 through 4, an `action` of `SLOW_RATE`, `HOLD`, or `ABORT`, and optionally a narrative `note`. The record is created in `status` `REPORTED` with `reported_by_nurse_id` taken from the acting `X-User-ID`.

The observation consequence is computed in two stages. First, the grade alone implies an extension: no extra minutes for grade 1, half an hour for grade 2, a full hour for grade 3, and two full hours for grade 4. Then the bedside action can raise but never lower that figure: when the `action` was `ABORT`, the record's `observation_extension_minutes` is the larger of the grade-implied extension and one hour, so even an aborted grade-1 reaction earns a full hour of watching; for `SLOW_RATE` and `HOLD` the grade-implied extension stands unchanged. Second — and this is the part most easily got wrong — the visit's own `observation_extension_minutes` is the **maximum** across all of that visit's reaction records, not their sum, and every record counts regardless of its `status`, so a `CLOSED` reaction keeps its extension because a resolved reaction does not shorten the observation the patient still needs. The visit is then adjusted by the *change* in that maximum: the difference between the new maximum and the previous one is added to the visit's `observation_minutes` and to its `chair_minutes`, and `end_minute` is recomputed as `start_minute` plus the new `chair_minutes`. When the difference is zero — a second, milder reaction on the same visit, for instance — the window and every downstream chair are left completely untouched.

A lengthened occupancy has a downstream cost. The extended window is immediately re-evaluated against the buffered chair-occupancy rule for later visits on the same `chair_id` and `service_date`, and every visit that now collides is bumped along the path described in §3.5 — demoted to `TENTATIVE` with `chair_id` and `nurse_id` cleared — which in turn recomputes those chairs' `committed_minutes` and those nurses' `assigned_acuity_points`. A reaction on the 09:00 patient can therefore un-seat the 13:00 patient, and that is the intended behaviour: the suite would rather re-slot a later visit than double-book a chair. Additionally, a reaction of `grade` 3 or higher drives the visit's `readiness_status` to `BLOCKED`, which bars the infusion from starting (and the patient from being discharged) until the review is closed.

**Review.** A `MedicalDirector` opens a review, moving the record from `REPORTED` to `UNDER_REVIEW` and advancing its `version` by one; readiness stays `BLOCKED` throughout for a severe reaction. Attempting to open a review on a record that is not `REPORTED`, or to close one that is not `UNDER_REVIEW`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ADVERSE_EVENT_STATE` (HTTP 409). Closing requires a `review_outcome` and accepts an optional `note`; the record moves to `CLOSED` with the outcome stored and its `version` advanced by one. The three dispositions diverge sharply. `RESUME_SAME_DRUG` recomputes the visit's `readiness_status`, lifting it from `BLOCKED` back to `FULLY_CLEARED` when nothing else blocks. `SWITCH_BIOSIMILAR` and `DISCONTINUE` both move the visit's regimen from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`, which cancels that regimen's visits still in `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED` with `cancellation_charge_cents` of zero, which in turn releases those visits' chair minutes, nurse acuity, and drug commitments. Under every disposition the observation extension already applied to the visit is retained.

### 3.9. Attrition: Cancellation Pricing, Reschedules, Reminders and No-Show Risk

**Cancelling a visit** is available to a `Scheduler` and, for their own visit, to a `Patient`; an optional `reason` may be recorded. It is legal while `schedule_status` is `TENTATIVE`, `CONFIRMED`, or `CHECKED_IN`, and when the acting role is `Patient` the visit's `patient_id` must equal `X-User-ID`. The visit's `chair_id` and `nurse_id` are deliberately **not** cleared — they remain as an audit trail of who was scheduled to do the work — but because `CANCELLED` sits outside the committed set, the chair's `committed_minutes` and the nurse's `assigned_acuity_points` are recomputed and therefore release this visit's contribution anyway. Every compounded dose of the visit still in `REQUESTED` is returned and every dose in `PREPARED` is wasted, with the lot counter chain of §3.7 applied in each case. The visit's `version` advances by one.

**Pricing a cancellation** runs in a pinned sequence of five steps, and the sequence matters because the patient's share is keyed on an intermediate figure rather than on the final one.

1. *Lateness and base charge.* The visit's start instant is midnight UTC of its `service_date` plus its `start_minute` minutes. The visit's `late_cancellation` flag is true when the logical clock is at or after that start instant minus 24 hours, and false when the cancellation happened earlier than that (it is also false while the visit remains open). The base charge is nothing at all when `late_cancellation` is false. When `late_cancellation` is true it is 2,500 cents for a `ROUTINE` visit, and 4,000 cents for a `SAME_DAY_ADDON` or `URGENT` visit — priority work forfeits more because the slot cannot be refilled.
2. *Waste actually incurred.* The waste figure is the sum of `waste_cost_cents` across exactly those compounded doses of this visit that **this** cancellation moved from `PREPARED` to `WASTED`, where each dose's `waste_cost_cents` is its `units_requested` multiplied by the drug's `unit_cost_cents`. Doses that went from `REQUESTED` to `RETURNED` contribute nothing, since nothing was mixed. Doses wasted earlier by the ops checkpoint are not re-charged here, because their visit was not cancelled.
3. *Provisional total.* The provisional total is the base charge plus that waste figure.
4. *Share tier, keyed on the provisional total.* Both upper edges are inclusive. When the provisional total is 5,000 cents or less, the patient's share of the waste is nothing. When it is above 5,000 and at most 25,000 cents, the share is 20 percent. When it is above 25,000 cents, the share is 35 percent. The tier is looked up on the provisional total — never on the final charge, and never on the waste figure alone.
5. *Final charge.* `cancellation_charge_cents` is the base charge plus the waste figure multiplied by the share percentage, that product rounded half up to a whole number of cents. This is the only rounding on the money path.

The charge then propagates: the patient's `waste_liability_cents` is recomputed as the sum of `cancellation_charge_cents` across every one of that patient's `CANCELLED` visits, and when it reaches 50,000 cents the patient's `steering_status` flips from `SUITE` to `HOME_INFUSION_REVIEW`. That flip is not cosmetic — it suspends every `ACTIVE` regimen of that patient, each suspension cancels that regimen's visits still in `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED` at zero charge, and each of those cancellations releases its chair minutes and nurse acuity. The patient's `no_show_risk_score` and `reminder_tier` are refreshed on the same request. System-initiated cancellations — a regimen suspension, a regimen supersede, or the retirement half of a reschedule — bypass this calculation entirely and write `cancellation_charge_cents` of zero with `late_cancellation` false. The system never bills a patient for a decision the system made.

**Rescheduling** is a supersede rather than an edit. A `Scheduler` supplies a new `service_date`, a new `requested_start_minute`, optionally a new `booking_class`, and the `expected_appointment_version` guard of §2.7, and the visit must be in `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED`. The original visit moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_charge_cents` zero and `late_cancellation` false, its `REQUESTED` doses returned and its `PREPARED` doses wasted with the full lot counter chain, its chair and nurse released, and its `version` advanced by one. A brand-new visit is then created in `TENTATIVE` carrying `rescheduled_from_appointment_id` pointing at the original and copying the original's `patient_id`, `treatment_plan_id`, `template_id`, `center_id`, `cycle_number`, and `booking_class`, then running the window build and the staggered-start selector from scratch against the newly supplied date and minute. Because the original's chair and nurse are released **before** the selector runs for the replacement, a reschedule to a nearby minute on the same day can legitimately land on the very same chair. The temporal rules of §3.5 — the past-date bar, the operating-hours fit, the patient double-booking bar — and the capacity refusals all apply to the replacement exactly as they do to a fresh booking, as do the regimen status bars and the referential checks on template drug, template centre, template tier, and cycle number.

**Reminder cadence.** A `Scheduler` records an outreach contact against a visit by naming a `channel` of `SMS`, `VOICE`, or `PORTAL`. The contact is stamped with `sequence_number` equal to the count of contacts already recorded for that visit plus one, with `sent_at` taken from the logical clock, with `outcome` `NO_RESPONSE`, and with `patient_id` copied from the visit so that outreach history is patient-queryable. How many contacts a visit accepts depends on the patient's adherence profile: a `STANDARD` patient accepts one contact for the visit, an `ELEVATED` patient three, and an `INTENSIVE` patient five. Each visit also carries `reminder_cadence_code` — `C_STANDARD`, `C_ELEVATED`, or `C_INTENSIVE` — mirroring the patient's `reminder_tier` at read time and expressing that cap on the visit itself. An attempt to record a contact once the cap implied by the patient's `reminder_tier` has been reached is refused with `ERR_REMINDER_CADENCE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).

A patient may answer their own contact, and a `Scheduler` may record an answer on their behalf, by supplying an `outcome`. The contact must still be sitting at `NO_RESPONSE`; a contact that already carries an answer is refused with `ERR_REMINDER_ALREADY_ANSWERED` (HTTP 409), and when the actor is a `Patient` the contact's `patient_id` must equal `X-User-ID`. Recording `CONFIRM_ATTENDANCE` leaves the visit untouched. Recording `CANCEL_REQUEST` sets the contact's `outcome` accordingly and then runs the **full** cancellation path on the referenced visit — waste, pricing, ledger propagation, and chair release included — provided that visit's `schedule_status` is `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED`. Either way the contact's `version` advances by one.

**No-show risk.** A patient's risk figure is computed from their terminal visit history: every visit of theirs whose `schedule_status` is `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED`, or `NO_SHOW`. Each such visit carries an event value — a full 1.00 for a `NO_SHOW`, 0.50 for a `CANCELLED` visit whose `late_cancellation` is true, 0.25 for a `CANCELLED` visit whose `late_cancellation` is false, and 0.00 for a `COMPLETED` one. Two variants read that history differently, and only one of them is persisted.

The **`ROLLING_6`** variant is the persisted score and the default reading. The history is ordered by `service_date` descending and then by `created_at` descending as a pinned tie-break, and the window is the first six rows — or all of them when the patient has fewer than six. Rows in the window are weighted by recency: the most recent row carries weight 100 and each successive row carries 15 less, giving 100, 85, 70, 55, 40, and 25. The score is the sum of each windowed row's weight multiplied by its event value, divided by the total of the weights of the rows actually present, multiplied by 100, and rounded half up to two decimal places; an empty window scores 0.00. Because a recent no-show carries weight 100 against a small weight total, one recent miss dominates the figure, which is exactly the intent. Calendar position is irrelevant to this variant.

The **`QUARTER_TO_DATE`** variant is the payer-facing adherence figure and is never persisted. The relevant quarter is the calendar quarter containing the logical clock's UTC date — January through March, April through June, July through September, or October through December — and the scoped history is those terminal visits whose `service_date` falls inside that quarter, inclusive of both quarter boundaries. The score is the count of `NO_SHOW` visits in that scope divided by the total count of visits in that scope, multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to two decimal places; an empty scope scores 0.00. This variant is unweighted, so a visit six weeks ago counts exactly as much as yesterday's, and it drops to 0.00 the instant the quarter rolls over. For the same history the two variants agree only by coincidence.

`reminder_tier` is always derived from the `ROLLING_6` score and never from the quarter figure: `STANDARD` below 20, `ELEVATED` from 20 up to but not including 45, and `INTENSIVE` at 45 and above. The risk figure is also readable on demand for one patient, where an optional `variant` selects between the two readings and defaults to `ROLLING_6`; that read reports the `patient_id`, the `variant` used, the `score`, the resulting `reminder_tier`, and a `considered_appointment_count` giving the number of history rows the chosen variant actually took into account.

### 3.10. Reading the Suite Back: Utilization, Billing, the Daily Checkpoint and Test-Driving

The whole point of pinning windows, chairs and cancellations so tightly is that the suite can then be measured. Four read-only reports and two operational capabilities close the system.

**Chair utilization** is reported for one centre on one `service_date`, with an optional `variant` that defaults to `SCHEDULED`. A `Scheduler` or a `MedicalDirector` may read it, and it reports the `center_id`, the `service_date`, the `variant` used, the `available_chair_minutes`, the `occupied_chair_minutes`, the `utilization_pct`, the `appointment_count`, and the `biosimilar_savings_cents` described below.

The denominator is the same under both variants: `available_chair_minutes` is the number of that centre's chairs whose `status` is `AVAILABLE` multiplied by the centre's daily open span, that span being `close_minute` less `open_minute`. Chairs whose `status` is `OUT_OF_SERVICE` are excluded from the denominator entirely, so taking a chair out of service raises the reported utilization rather than lowering it.

The numerator is where the two variants part company. The **`SCHEDULED`** variant sums the *planned* `chair_minutes` of every visit at that centre on that date whose `schedule_status` is `TENTATIVE`, `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `INFUSING`, `OBSERVING`, or `COMPLETED`; note that a `COMPLETED` visit still contributes its planned length under this variant, not the length it actually ran. `CANCELLED` and `NO_SHOW` visits contribute nothing. The **`ACTUAL`** variant instead asks how long each chair was really occupied: a `COMPLETED` visit contributes `actual_end_minute` less `actual_start_minute`; a visit currently `INFUSING` or `OBSERVING` contributes the logical clock's minute of day less its `actual_start_minute`, so a mid-day read is a live figure that grows as the clock advances; and a visit in `TENTATIVE`, `CONFIRMED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `CANCELLED`, or `NO_SHOW` contributes nothing, because no one has sat down yet or no one ever will. A booked-but-unstarted day therefore reads high under `SCHEDULED` and zero under `ACTUAL`.

`utilization_pct` is the occupied minutes divided by the available minutes, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places — and when `available_chair_minutes` is zero, because every chair at the centre is `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, the percentage is reported as exactly 0.00 rather than as an arithmetic failure. Note that the percentage can legitimately exceed 100.00 when scheduled work overruns the nominal open span; the figure is not clamped. The `appointment_count` accompanying the report is the number of that day's visits at that centre to which the chosen variant attributed a length.

**Biosimilar savings** ride along with the utilization report as `biosimilar_savings_cents`, answering what the day would have saved had every eligible reference-product dose been given as the cheapest interchangeable biosimilar. It is computed over the visits at that centre on that date whose `schedule_status` is `INFUSING`, `OBSERVING`, or `COMPLETED` — that is, over drug actually going into patients. For each such visit, take the drug of its regimen. A drug that is already itself a biosimilar contributes nothing. Otherwise gather the candidate alternatives: drugs whose `reference_drug_id` points at that drug, whose `is_biosimilar` is true, and whose `unit_cost_cents` is strictly lower than the reference drug's; if there are none, the visit contributes nothing. Order the candidates by `unit_cost_cents` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending as a pinned tie-break, and take the first as the chosen alternative. The visit's contribution is the sum of `units_requested` across that visit's compounded doses whose `status` is `PREPARED` or `ADMINISTERED`, multiplied by the reference drug's `unit_cost_cents` less the chosen alternative's `unit_cost_cents`. Doses that were wasted or returned contribute nothing, since no clinical benefit was purchased with them. The savings figure is a whole number of cents throughout, so no rounding arises.

**The chair billing summary** is reported for one centre on one `service_date` and is readable by a `Scheduler` or a `MedicalDirector`. It reports the `center_id`, the `service_date`, the `completed_appointment_count`, the `day_billable_units`, and the `day_billable_cents`. `day_billable_units` is the sum of `billable_chair_units` across every `COMPLETED` visit at that centre on that date, where each visit's unit figure was already rounded **up** to a whole quarter-hour at discharge. The order of operations is pinned and must not be rearranged: round each visit up first, then sum the rounded figures. Summing raw minutes across the day and rounding once at the end would systematically under-bill, since each visit's partial quarter-hour is billable on its own. `day_billable_cents` is `day_billable_units` multiplied by 9,000 cents — ninety dollars per quarter-hour of chair time. Visits that were cancelled, no-showed, or are still in progress contribute nothing to either figure and are not counted in `completed_appointment_count`.

**The daily checkpoint.** A `Scheduler` or `MedicalDirector` runs the end-of-day sweep for one centre against one `service_date`. It executes four steps in a fixed order, and within each step the affected rows are processed in `created_at` ascending order so that the outcome is deterministic. The first three steps are global in scope — they are not limited to the named centre — while the fourth is scoped to the named centre and the supplied `service_date`.

1. *Expire drug lots.* Every lot whose `status` is `AVAILABLE` or `DEPLETED` and whose `expires_on` is at or before the logical clock moves to `EXPIRED`, advancing its `version` by one. Lots already `EXPIRED` are untouched, and doses already committed against an expiring lot are left exactly as they are.
2. *Expire authorizations.* Every authorization request whose `status` is `APPROVED` and whose `expires_at` is at or before the logical clock moves to `EXPIRED`, advancing its `version` by one, and each such expiry drives the affected visit's `readiness_status` down from `FULLY_CLEARED` or `AUTH_CLEARED` as the derivation of §3.6 requires.
3. *Waste stale preparations.* Every compounded dose whose `status` is `PREPARED` and whose `usable_until` is at or before the logical clock moves to `WASTED`, with its `waste_cost_cents` computed and its lot's `units_committed`, `units_wasted`, and `units_available` recomputed, and its `version` advanced by one. No cancellation charge arises from this path, because no patient cancelled anything.
4. *Mark no-shows.* Every visit at the named centre whose `service_date` equals the supplied date, whose `schedule_status` is `TENTATIVE` or `CONFIRMED`, and whose grace period has elapsed — that is, the logical clock is at or after midnight UTC of the `service_date` plus the visit's `start_minute` plus 30 minutes — moves to `NO_SHOW`. Each such movement releases the chair's `committed_minutes` and the nurse's `assigned_acuity_points`, returns that visit's `REQUESTED` doses and wastes its `PREPARED` doses with the usual lot counter chain, recomputes the patient's `no_show_strikes`, recomputes the patient's `no_show_risk_score` and re-derives `reminder_tier`, and flips the patient's `steering_status` to `HOME_INFUSION_REVIEW` once `no_show_strikes` has reached 3 — with the regimen-suspension and zero-charge cancellation cascade that flip always carries. A visit exactly at the 30-minute mark is a no-show; one minute earlier it is not.

The report returns four counters describing what the run did: `expired_lot_count`, `expired_authorization_count`, `wasted_preparation_count`, and `no_show_count`. Running the checkpoint a second time without advancing the clock is safe and returns all four counters as zero, because every step selects only rows still in the pre-transition status. The steps are also ordered for a reason: expiring authorizations before marking no-shows means a visit can be recorded as a no-show on the same run whose earlier step withdrew its coverage.

**Driving the clock.** The logical clock introduced in §2.4 is settable: any of the five staff roles — `Scheduler`, `Pharmacist`, `InfusionNurse`, `AuthorizationSpecialist`, or `MedicalDirector` — may set it by supplying a `current_utc` instant, and the operation reports back both the new `current_utc` and the `previous_utc` it replaced, so a caller can always see what it moved. Every derivation in this document — lateness, stability, authorization expiry, the no-show grace period, the quarter-to-date window, live occupancy — reads that value and nothing else.

**Injecting visit history.** Because risk scores, billing summaries and utilization reports are all functions of history that would otherwise take months to accumulate, the system exposes a `Scheduler`-only capability that writes a visit already in one of the three terminal `schedule_status` values — `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED`, or `NO_SHOW` — directly into the record, without walking the booking, authorization, check-in and discharge chain. It requires a `center_id`, a `patient_id`, a `treatment_plan_id`, a `template_id`, a `service_date`, a `schedule_status`, and a `chair_minutes` figure, and optionally accepts a `start_minute`, an `actual_start_minute`, an `actual_end_minute`, a `late_cancellation` flag, a `booking_class`, and a `cycle_number`. The visit is created immediately in the supplied terminal `schedule_status` with `chair_id` and `nurse_id` left null — no chair or nurse is retroactively consumed, so injected history never affects present-day capacity — and with `billable_chair_units` computed from the supplied actual minutes when both were provided. The injection then recomputes the patient's `no_show_strikes`, `completed_appointment_count`, `ROLLING_6` `no_show_risk_score`, `reminder_tier`, and `steering_status`, and when that recomputation flips `steering_status` to `HOME_INFUSION_REVIEW` every `ACTIVE` regimen of that patient moves to `SUSPENDED` with the usual zero-charge cancellation cascade, and recomputes the regimen's `completed_cycles` and `first_dose_completed` when the injected visit is `COMPLETED`. Injected visits count in the utilization report and in the chair billing summary exactly as organically created ones do, which is precisely what makes them useful.
