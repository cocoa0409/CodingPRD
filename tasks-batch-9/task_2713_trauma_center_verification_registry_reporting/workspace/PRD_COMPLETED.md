# Trauma Verification Registry API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A verified trauma center lives under two simultaneous audits. One is national: a verification body sends surveyors on a three-year cycle, reads the center's registry, counts its criteria deficiencies and either verifies the center, verifies it under contingency, or denies it outright. The other is the state: a designation authority runs a parallel review over the same evidence, on its own calendar, with its own outreach-hour expectations and its own vocabulary for the outcome. Program staff today reconcile these two reviews by hand, in spreadsheets that cannot tell them whether closing one deficiency has just unblocked a submission, whether last week's under-triage rate has crossed the alert line, or whether the surgeon who arrived forty-one minutes after the page has already been counted against a performance-improvement loop that nobody has closed.

The Trauma Verification Registry API replaces that spreadsheet with a single system of record. It owns the trauma registry itself — case inclusion decisions, abstraction submission and adjudication against a data dictionary, and the over/under-triage arithmetic that both audits demand. It owns the performance improvement and patient safety (PIPS) program, including the loop-closure documentation that surveyors read first. It owns verification-cycle preparation, from criteria deficiency remediation through surveyor scheduling to the decision itself. And it owns the peripheral obligations that are easy to forget and expensive to miss: outreach and injury-prevention credit hours, required specialty response times, and the referral center agreements that make a transfer-out legitimate rather than a documentation gap.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **every derived number is a consequence, never an assertion**: a compliance score, a triage rate, a readiness score and a center's overall readiness posture are all recomputed from primary records whenever a primary record moves, and a compensating action must return them exactly to where they were. Second, **the system refuses to let a center walk into a site visit blind**: when the evidence degrades past a defined line, the affected verification cycle blocks itself, and when it degrades further, the center's writes freeze until the underlying deficiency is remediated.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract every endpoint honours: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, common properties, arithmetic conventions, the center-wide posture gate, cascade semantics, concurrency control, separation of duties, and the order in which rejections are decided.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is absent or present but empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not — matched case-sensitively and exactly — one of the seven roles below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When the headers are well-formed but the declared role is not authorised for the operation being attempted, the rejection is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The seven recognised roles are `SystemAdmin`, `TraumaProgramManager`, `RegistryAbstractor`, `RegistryValidator`, `TraumaMedicalDirector`, `VerificationReviewer` and `StateDesignationOfficer`.

`SystemAdmin` is the platform operator: it registers trauma centers, declares the immutable registry data-dictionary rules, and drives the logical clock. `TraumaProgramManager` runs a single center's trauma program and is scoped to that center on every write. `RegistryAbstractor` abstracts the registry record and may never adjudicate its own submission. `RegistryValidator` adjudicates submitted abstractions for data-dictionary compliance. `TraumaMedicalDirector` owns triage adjudication, the PIPS loop and outreach verification. `VerificationReviewer` is the national verification authority. `StateDesignationOfficer` is the state designation authority, working the same review surface but deciding only its own kind of cycle.

Identity binding differs by role, and it matters:

- When `X-User-Role` is `TraumaProgramManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `TraumaCenter`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `TraumaCenter` compare the resource's center reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, the center in scope is the `center_id` supplied in the body when the body carries one, the owning center of the registry case for a case-scoped write, the owning center of the verification cycle for a cycle-scoped write, the cycle's center for a write against a criteria deficiency, the outreach activity's center for an outreach write, `referring_center_id` when a referral agreement is drafted, `receiving_center_id` when a referral agreement is activated, and either party when a referral agreement is terminated. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_CENTER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `SystemAdmin`, `RegistryAbstractor`, `RegistryValidator`, `TraumaMedicalDirector`, `VerificationReviewer` or `StateDesignationOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands attribution — as `submitted_by_user_id` on an abstraction, as `validated_by_user_id` when an abstraction is adjudicated, as `reviewed_by_user_id` on an outreach activity, as `verified_by_user_id` on a corrective action — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`.

One further authority rule cuts across the review surface. The deciding authority must match the kind of cycle being reviewed: a cycle whose `cycle_type` is `NATIONAL_VERIFICATION` may only be decided, deficiency-recorded, deficiency-closed, surveyor-booked and moved into deliberation by a `VerificationReviewer`, while a cycle whose `cycle_type` is `STATE_DESIGNATION` may only be handled on those same five operations by a `StateDesignationOfficer`. Crossing the line yields `ERR_DESIGNATION_AUTHORITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`. It is minted freshly for every response, error responses included, and is never persisted or replayed on a later call.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits. It is the one timestamp in this API read from the host's real wall clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.4, so it advances between two requests even when the logical clock has not moved, and it bears no relation to the `*_at` fields of the resource being returned.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Transport-level failures use this same envelope. A request against a path no endpoint serves is `ERR_REFERENCED_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404); a method the path does not serve is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` carried on **HTTP 405**, the single case in which that error id does not travel with HTTP 400; a body that is unparseable JSON, is not a JSON object, or arrives with an unsupported media type is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400); and an unexpected server fault is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 500. A trailing slash on any path is accepted as equivalent to the path without one.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, and an explicitly supplied value must be at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip and defaults to 0; an explicitly supplied value must be zero or greater. A `limit` outside the 1-to-100 band or a negative `offset` is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), and so is a `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer literal. Either parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as though it had been omitted, and its default applies.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest record first — with records sharing an identical `created_at` broken by the order in which the server created them, so the ordering is always total and stable. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` counts every record matching the filters (not merely the page) and `has_more` indicates whether records remain beyond the current page. Filters are applied before pagination, so `total_count` is the filtered population.

Every filter query parameter documented in §3 is matched by exact string equality against the stored field and is *not* enum-validated: a filter value that is not a member of the field's enumeration simply matches no record and returns an empty page with `total_count` 0 rather than an error, and a filter supplied as an empty string is treated as though it had been omitted.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every resource the API mints carries a server-generated `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix and no embedded semantics. Clients never supply an `id`. Every property this document names for a resource is always present in that resource's representation: a property that has no value yet — an unstamped `*_at`, an unwritten note or reason, an unresolved optional reference, a bound a dictionary rule's kind does not use — is returned as JSON `null` rather than omitted, and no representation ever exposes an internal bookkeeping key.

Alongside it, most resources carry three system-managed audit properties. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits, recording when the record was first written. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, rewritten on every mutation of the record — including mutations that touch only derived fields such as a recomputed score or counter. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation, whether that mutation was requested directly by a client or applied by a cascade the client never asked for. These two audit timestamps are the deliberate exception to the logical clock described below: both are stamped from the host's real wall clock, so neither reads nor respects a clock a `SystemAdmin` has moved, and every record that a single request mutates receives the same `updated_at` instant.

There is one deliberate exception. A `DataDictionaryRule` is immutable configuration: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version`, and no operation in this API modifies one after creation.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, not the host's wall clock. On startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it continues to read that value until it is moved; the server never falls back to real wall-clock time. A `SystemAdmin` may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, and the response echoes the new value as `current_utc`. Every deadline comparison, every system-stamped `*_at` field — `opened_at`, `closed_at`, `completed_at`, `verified_at`, `locked_at`, `decided_at`, `activated_at`, `terminated_at` — and every "is this overdue?" evaluation reads the logical clock at the moment the request is processed. That list is exhaustive: `created_at`, `updated_at` and the envelope's `api_request_timestamp` are the wall-clock fields, and everything else the server stamps is logical.

Timestamps a client supplies — the clock instant itself and every `*_at`, `*_date` or interval endpoint in a payload — are read as ISO 8601, and three spellings are all accepted: a trailing `Z`, an explicit UTC offset (normalised to UTC), and no zone designator at all (taken as UTC). A date-only value such as `2026-08-01` is accepted and read as midnight UTC of that day; anything that cannot be parsed this way is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Fields whose names read as calendar dates — `verification_cycle_start_date`, `site_visit_date`, `event_date` and `decision_effective_date` — are nevertheless stored and returned as full instants in the same three-fractional-digit form as every other timestamp, never as bare dates. All state is process-local and held in memory: a freshly started server holds no records at all, with its logical clock at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`.

### 2.5. Nested Path Resolution and Reference Existence

When a path addresses a nested or referenced resource, identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve determines the rejection. The trauma center in a path or body is checked first and reported as `ERR_CENTER_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404); this covers every center-scoped path such as `/trauma-centers/{id}/activation-tiers` as well as the body-supplied center of a new registry case, a new PI event, a new verification cycle and a new referral agreement. A registry case that does not resolve is `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and that applies both to every case-scoped path and to a PI event created with a `case_id`. A verification cycle that does not resolve is `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) on every cycle-scoped path. A PI event that does not resolve is `ERR_PI_EVENT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) on every PI-event-scoped path.

Every other unresolved reference collapses into one code, `ERR_REFERENCED_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404): an abstraction addressed for adjudication, a corrective action addressed for completion or verification, a criteria deficiency addressed for remediation, closure or escalation, an outreach activity addressed for verification or rejection, a referral agreement addressed for activation or termination, the `activation_tier_id` supplied in a new case body, and the receiving center supplied in a transfer-out body.

Because existence is settled before state, a request that names a resource that does not exist never surfaces a state or business error — the 404 wins.

### 2.6. Arithmetic, Precision and Quantization

Unless a rule says otherwise, every stored decimal is a number (never a string) carried to exactly **2 decimal places**, and every rounding step uses **round half up**. This governs `registry_compliance_score`, `under_triage_rate_pct`, `over_triage_rate_pct`, `outreach_credit_hours_ytd`, `readiness_score`, `applied_penalty_points` and `response_minutes`. "Two decimal places" describes the *value*, not its printed form: these fields travel as ordinary JSON numbers, so a value whose hundredths are zero legitimately appears on the wire as `100.0` or `0.0` rather than as `100.00`.

Three conventions deserve explicit statement because they are easy to get subtly wrong:

- **Scores and rates floor at zero.** `registry_compliance_score` and `readiness_score` are clamped so they never fall below 0.00, and a rate whose denominator is zero is 0.00 rather than an error.
- **Rates are percentages out of one hundred**, computed as one hundred multiplied by the numerator, divided by the denominator, then rounded half up to two decimals.
- **Outreach credit hours quantize before they sum.** Each activity's weighted contribution is snapped to the nearest quarter hour before it enters the center's ledger, and the ledger total is then rounded half up to two decimals. Snapping to the nearest quarter hour means multiplying by four, rounding half up to a whole number, and dividing by four.

Counters (`open_pi_event_count`, `closed_pi_event_count`, `open_critical_deficiency_count`, `escalated_critical_deficiency_count`, `registry_included_case_count`, `registry_excluded_case_count`, `open_type_i_count`, `open_type_ii_count`, `open_type_iii_count`, `corrective_action_count`, `verified_action_count`, `dictionary_error_count`, `review_round`) are exact integers compared exactly. `injury_severity_score`, `iss_abstracted`, `gcs_arrival`, `effective_iss` and `iss_threshold` are integers compared exactly. `los_hours` is compared at two decimals. Timestamp comparisons are evaluated on the whole stored instant, with no truncation to seconds — a deadline missed by a fraction of a second is missed — even though rendering carries only three fractional digits. `credit_hours` is accepted with up to three decimals as raw input and must be greater than zero, and it is returned rounded half up to three decimals rather than to the usual two; `audience_count` must be zero or greater. `band_multiplier_used` is returned as the plain JSON number `1.0`, `1.5` or `2.0`.

### 2.7. Readiness Posture: the Center-Wide Gate

Every `TraumaCenter` carries a derived `readiness_posture`, and it is the single most consequential field in the system because it can freeze writes. A newly registered center starts at `READY`. The posture is recomputed whenever any of `open_critical_deficiency_count`, `escalated_critical_deficiency_count`, `open_pi_event_count`, `under_triage_rate_pct`, `over_triage_rate_pct` or `national_verification_status` changes, and additionally whenever a verification cycle decision is rendered. Only center-scoped counters and rates feed it; no other center's data ever influences a center's posture. The value a center's representation reports is additionally evaluated at response time from that center's persisted counters, rates and `national_verification_status` together with the current logical clock, so a posture that has degraded only because the clock passed an open loop's `loop_closure_due_at` is visible on the very next read without any refresh of the center's `updated_at` or `version`. The `readiness_posture` filter on the center list is matched against that same response-time evaluation rather than against the persisted field, so the filter and a center's own representation can never disagree.

The evaluation is a first-match-wins ladder, and the ordering is normative. Before the ladder runs, the system counts the center's **overdue loops**: PI events belonging to the center whose `status` is `IDENTIFIED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `ACTION_PLANNED` or `REOPENED` and whose `loop_closure_due_at` is strictly earlier than the current logical-clock instant.

1. If the center's `national_verification_status` is `SUSPENDED`, or `escalated_critical_deficiency_count` is at least 2, the posture is `SUSPENDED`.
2. Otherwise, if `open_critical_deficiency_count` is at least 1, or `under_triage_rate_pct` is at least 5.00, or there is at least one overdue loop, the posture is `AT_RISK`.
3. Otherwise, if `open_pi_event_count` is at least 3, or `over_triage_rate_pct` is at least 35.00, the posture is `CONDITIONAL`.
4. Otherwise the posture is `READY`.

A `SUSPENDED` posture is a hard gate on exactly two operations: locking a registry case for registry submission, and submitting a prepared verification cycle for review. Both are refused with `ERR_POSTURE_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 409). Note the asymmetry with the other three postures — `AT_RISK` and `CONDITIONAL` are reporting signals only and gate nothing.

The center's other two status fields are independent of the posture ladder in one direction only. `national_verification_status` — one of `NOT_VERIFIED` (the value at registration), `VERIFIED`, `CONTINGENCY` or `SUSPENDED` — feeds branch 1 of the ladder. `state_designation_status` — one of `NOT_DESIGNATED` (the value at registration), `DESIGNATED`, `PROVISIONAL` or `WITHDRAWN` — feeds nothing at all. A state authority can withdraw a designation without changing the posture; a national authority cannot deny verification without suspending the center.

### 2.8. Cascade Propagation and Counter Conservation

Most writes in this system are not confined to the resource named in the request. A single call routinely rewrites a parent center's counters, re-derives one or more scores, and can even move a verification cycle's state without any client asking it to. Three principles govern this.

**Cascades are transitive and immediate.** There is no deferred or batched reconciliation. If a write changes a counter, every score that reads that counter is recomputed inside the same request, and if a recomputed score crosses a state threshold, the state transition happens inside the same request too. A cascade may therefore run several hops deep: a triage adjudication rewrites a rate, the rate opens a PI event, the new PI event raises a counter, the counter re-derives a posture *and* re-derives every one of the center's verification-cycle readiness scores — meaning every cycle of that center which has not yet been decided, which is every cycle whose `status` is `PREP`, `PREP_BLOCKED`, `SUBMITTED`, `SITE_VISIT_SCHEDULED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `CONTINGENCY`, since only `VERIFIED` and `DENIED` count as decided and are skipped — and a cycle whose score fell below the submission line blocks itself.

**Every touched record is a mutation.** Any resource whose fields change during a cascade — including a center whose counter merely moved, or a cycle whose `readiness_score` was refreshed — has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1. Derived-field-only changes are mutations like any other. The bump is once per request per record, not once per touch: a record that one request rewrites directly and then rewrites again several cascade hops later still advances by exactly 1, and a record *created* by the request ends that request at `version` 1 with `updated_at` equal to `created_at`, however many later hops of the same request touch it. A `DataDictionaryRule`, carrying neither field, is outside this bookkeeping entirely.

**Counters are conserved, and compensating actions restore them exactly.** Several pairs of operations exist precisely so that a mistake can be undone without drift. Closing a PI loop moves one unit from `open_pi_event_count` to `closed_pi_event_count`; reopening it moves that unit back, restoring the pre-closure partition exactly. Recording a transfer-out can move a case from the included cohort to the excluded cohort; rescinding it returns the case, and both `registry_included_case_count` and `registry_excluded_case_count` land back on their pre-transfer values. Verifying an outreach activity adds its weighted hours to the ledger; rejecting it recomputes the ledger over the remaining verified activities and must land exactly on the pre-verification value, and repeated crossings of the verified boundary must not drift the total. Rejecting an abstraction restores to `registry_compliance_score` exactly the `applied_penalty_points` that the submission had deducted.

Two verification-cycle transitions are *purely* system-driven, with no endpoint of their own. A cycle counts as **blocked** when its recomputed reviewer readiness score is below 70.00 **or** its `open_type_i_count` is at least 1 — an open `TYPE_I_CRITICAL` finding of its own blocks the cycle on its own, however healthy the score. A cycle sitting in `PREP` that is blocked moves itself to `PREP_BLOCKED` and can no longer be submitted. A cycle sitting in `PREP_BLOCKED` that is no longer blocked — its reviewer readiness score back at 70.00 or above *and* its `open_type_i_count` down to 0 — moves itself back to `PREP` and becomes submittable again. Both are evaluated on every recomputation that touches the cycle, and in both the cycle's `readiness_score` is rewritten.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Parent Versions

Two writes in this API are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a **parent** resource's `version`. In both cases the version field is a required, non-optional input; omitting it is a malformed request.

Submitting an abstraction against a registry case — the write served by `POST /registry-cases/{id}/abstractions` — must carry `expected_case_version` holding the `version` of the parent `RegistryCase` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the case's current `version`, the submission is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard exists because resubmission is a legitimate product operation — a newer abstraction supersedes the prior submitted record — so no state precondition can protect the abstractor from overwriting an adjudication or a supersede they never observed.

Submitting a remediation packet for a criteria deficiency — the write served by `POST /criteria-deficiencies/{id}/remediation` — must carry `expected_cycle_version` holding the `version` of the parent `VerificationCycle` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the cycle's current `version`, the submission is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Remediation resubmission on the same deficiency is legal and supersedes the prior note, so again no state precondition covers it; the version check catches a program manager whose view of the cycle predates another deficiency's closure, another deficiency's escalation, or the cycle decision itself.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. In particular, adjudication, locking, triage review, transfer-out, loop closure, cycle submission and cycle decision carry no expected-version input at all.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

The program is auditable only if no single person can both perform and bless the same step. Four operations enforce this, all with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409):

- Validating an abstraction requires `X-User-ID` to differ from that abstraction's `submitted_by_user_id`.
- Rejecting an abstraction requires the same: `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_user_id`.
- Verifying a corrective action requires `X-User-ID` to differ from that action's `owner_user_id`.
- Documenting loop closure on a PI event requires `X-User-ID` to be the `owner_user_id` of none of the event's corrective actions.

### 2.11. Validation Precedence

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is not a matter of implementation convenience. Checks are evaluated in the fixed order below, and the **first** violated check determines the response. Later checks are never reached; a violation of a later check is invisible when an earlier one also fails.

**Band A — identity and authorisation (evaluated on every request, in this order):** the identity headers and role enumeration (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`); the acting role's presence in the operation's authorised set (`ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`); center scope for every `TraumaProgramManager` write (`ERR_CENTER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`); and the designation-authority match on the five cycle-review operations — rendering a decision, recording a criteria deficiency, closing a criteria deficiency, booking a surveyor window, and opening deliberation after the site visit (`ERR_DESIGNATION_AUTHORITY_MISMATCH`).

**Band B — existence, in this order:** trauma center, then registry case, then verification cycle, then PI event, then any other reference — `ERR_CENTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PI_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERENCED_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, as scoped in §2.5.

**Band C — payload shape, in this order:** every required body field present and correctly typed, plus the pagination bounds of §2.3 (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, HTTP 400); then every enum-typed body field carrying a declared member of its enumeration (`ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`, HTTP 422). The enum check covers `triage_review_status`, `decision`, `deficiency_class`, `activity_kind`, `responder_role`, `event_type`, `severity`, `tier_code`, `rule_kind`, `cycle_type` and `variant` — `variant` being supplied as a query parameter rather than in the body, and an out-of-enumeration `variant` being `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` just the same, while the ordinary list filters of §2.3 are never enum-checked.

Presence in Band C is judged by value, not by key. An optional field supplied explicitly as `null` is treated exactly as though it had been omitted, so the field's documented default (or `null`) applies; a **required** field that is absent, `null`, or of the wrong JSON type is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A required string must additionally be non-empty and not made only of whitespace, a JSON boolean is never accepted where an integer or a number is required, and an integer field rejects a fractional number outright. The body is parsed as JSON whatever `Content-Type` the client declared, and an absent or empty body is read as an empty object — so such a request fails on the first required field it lacks, not on the missing body. A numeric or integer field whose value falls outside an inclusive band this document declares for it — a tier's 1-to-240 response limits, a 0-to-75 `injury_severity_score` or `iss_threshold`, a non-negative `los_hours` or `audience_count`, a strictly positive `credit_hours`, a 1-to-720 `guaranteed_acceptance_minutes` — is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` in this same band, never a business error and never `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`.

**Band D — optimistic concurrency:** `expected_case_version` on abstraction submission (`ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION`), then `expected_cycle_version` on remediation submission (`ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION`). These precede every state check, so a stale version is reported even when the target is also in the wrong state.

**Band E — state legality, in this order:** the case is abstractable (`ERR_CASE_NOT_ABSTRACTABLE`); the case is not already locked, which guards locking, transfer-out and transfer-out rescission (`ERR_CASE_ALREADY_LOCKED`); the case's triage dimension is still unreviewed (`ERR_TRIAGE_ALREADY_REVIEWED`); the abstraction has not already been adjudicated, which guards both validation and rejection (`ERR_ABSTRACTION_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`); the verification cycle is in the required from-state, which guards submission, surveyor booking, site-visit scheduling, opening deliberation, deciding, and deficiency recording (`ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`); the PI event is in the required from-state, guarding review start, corrective-action attachment, loop closure and reopening (`ERR_INVALID_PI_STATE`); the criteria deficiency is in a legal from-state for remediation, closure or escalation (`ERR_INVALID_DEFICIENCY_STATE`); the corrective action is in a legal from-state for completion or verification (`ERR_INVALID_ACTION_STATE`); the referral agreement is in a legal from-state for activation or termination (`ERR_INVALID_AGREEMENT_STATE`); and the outreach activity is in a legal from-state for a verify or reject decision (`ERR_INVALID_OUTREACH_STATE`).

**Band F — business safety, in this order:** the center's posture is not `SUSPENDED`, guarding case locking and cycle submission (`ERR_POSTURE_SUSPENDED`); the abstraction submission violates no `CRITICAL` data-dictionary rule (`ERR_DICTIONARY_CRITICAL_VIOLATION`); the case's latest abstraction has been validated before the case may be locked (`ERR_ABSTRACTION_NOT_VALIDATED`); the PI event has at least one corrective action and all of them are verified before loop closure (`ERR_OPEN_ACTIONS_BLOCK_LOOP_CLOSURE`); and no critical deficiency remains open before a `VERIFIED` decision (`ERR_CRITICAL_DEFICIENCY_BLOCKS_VERIFICATION`).

**Band G — referential, temporal and constraint checks, in this order:** an active referral agreement covers the transfer-out (`ERR_NO_ACTIVE_REFERRAL_AGREEMENT`); the abstraction window is still open (`ERR_ABSTRACTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`); interval endpoints are ordered and the remediation due date is in the future, guarding deficiency recording, surveyor booking and agreement drafting (`ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL_DATES`); segregation of duties on abstraction validation, abstraction rejection, corrective-action verification and loop closure (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`); MRN uniqueness within a center (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CASE_MRN`); configuration uniqueness for activation tiers and dictionary rules (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CONFIGURATION`); interval exclusivity on the directed center pair at agreement activation (`ERR_AGREEMENT_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`); surveyor window exclusivity once the travel buffer is applied (`ERR_SURVEYOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`); and finally the reviewer readiness threshold at cycle submission (`ERR_READINESS_BELOW_THRESHOLD`).

Three consequences of this ordering are worth spelling out because they are counter-intuitive. An abstraction that both violates a `CRITICAL` dictionary rule *and* arrives after its 60-day window closes is reported as `ERR_DICTIONARY_CRITICAL_VIOLATION`, because the dictionary gate sits in Band F and the window gate in Band G. Locking a case reports `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_LOCKED` before `ERR_POSTURE_SUSPENDED`, and `ERR_POSTURE_SUSPENDED` before `ERR_ABSTRACTION_NOT_VALIDATED`. And loop closure reports `ERR_OPEN_ACTIONS_BLOCK_LOOP_CLOSURE` before `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, so an action owner attempting to close a loop with unverified actions sees the unverified-actions error, not the duties error.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Center and Its Activation Criteria Tiers

Everything begins with a registered trauma center. A `SystemAdmin` registers one by supplying its legal `name`, its `state_code` — exactly two uppercase letters `A`–`Z` identifying the state whose designation program owns it, any other spelling (wrong length, lowercase, or containing a non-letter) being rejected as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` in Band C — its `designation_level`, which is one of `LEVEL_I`, `LEVEL_II` or `LEVEL_III`, and its `verification_cycle_start_date`, the first day of the current three-year verification cycle. A freshly registered center is deliberately clean: `readiness_posture` is `READY`, `national_verification_status` is `NOT_VERIFIED`, `state_designation_status` is `NOT_DESIGNATED`, `registry_compliance_score` is 100.00, `outreach_credit_hours_ytd`, `under_triage_rate_pct` and `over_triage_rate_pct` are all 0.00, every one of `open_critical_deficiency_count`, `escalated_critical_deficiency_count`, `open_pi_event_count`, `closed_pi_event_count`, `registry_included_case_count` and `registry_excluded_case_count` is 0, and `version` is 1.

Before any patient can be registered, the center's **activation criteria tiers** must be configured — one row per tier, created by a `SystemAdmin` or by the center's own `TraumaProgramManager`. A tier is identified by its `tier_code`, one of `TIER_1_FULL`, `TIER_2_MODIFIED` or `TIER_3_CONSULT`, and each code may exist at most once per center; a second attempt at the same code for the same center is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CONFIGURATION`. Each tier declares two response expectations and one triage expectation. `surgeon_response_limit_minutes` is the maximum documented surgeon arrival interval for that tier, and `specialty_response_limit_minutes` the maximum for every non-surgeon specialty; both are integers from 1 through 240 inclusive. `iss_threshold` is the Injury Severity Score at or above which that tier is the criteria-correct activation, an integer from 0 through 75 inclusive. Tiers may be listed back from their owning center, optionally narrowed by `tier_code`.

The registry data dictionary is configured separately and globally. A `SystemAdmin` declares each `DataDictionaryRule` against one abstraction field — `field_name` must be one of `iss_abstracted`, `gcs_arrival`, `mechanism_code` or `ed_departure_at` — with a `rule_kind` of `REQUIRED`, `RANGE` or `CONFORMANCE` and a `severity` of `CRITICAL`, `MAJOR` or `MINOR`. `field_name` is deliberately *not* one of the enum-checked fields listed in §2.11, so an unrecognised `field_name` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`. A `RANGE` rule carries an inclusive `min_value` and an inclusive `max_value`, and both are required of it: a `RANGE` rule missing either bound, or carrying one that is not a JSON number, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A `CONFORMANCE` rule carries `allowed_codes`, the array of permitted string codes, which must be a non-empty array every element of which is a string, and is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` otherwise. A `REQUIRED` rule needs neither. These bound-and-code shape checks are evaluated before the `rule_kind` and `severity` enum checks, and a bound or code array that the declared `rule_kind` does not use is discarded rather than stored, whatever the client supplied. Each field-and-kind combination is unique across the whole system, and a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CONFIGURATION`. The rule's `error_weight` is not supplied by the client — it is derived from `severity`: 4 for `CRITICAL`, 2 for `MAJOR` and 1 for `MINOR`. Dictionary rules are global configuration, not center-scoped: every rule in the database is evaluated against every abstraction, whatever center submitted it. They may be listed with optional `severity` and `rule_kind` filters.

### 3.2. Opening a Registry Case and Deciding Registry Inclusion

A `RegistryAbstractor` or the center's `TraumaProgramManager` opens a trauma registry case against exactly one configured tier. The payload names the receiving center as `center_id` and the tier actually activated at arrival as `activation_tier_id`, carries the patient's `mrn` (medical record number), the ED `arrival_at` instant that anchors every downstream deadline, the arrival-recorded `injury_severity_score` as an integer from 0 through 75 inclusive, and `los_hours` — length of stay in hours, zero or greater, carried at two decimals. Two inputs are optional: `ed_disposition`, which is one of `ADMIT`, `TRANSFER_OUT`, `DISCHARGE` or `MORGUE` and defaults to `ADMIT` when omitted, and `transfer_in_from_center_id`, which names the referring center for a transfer-in case and is null when the patient arrived directly; a `transfer_in_from_center_id` that resolves to no center is `ERR_CENTER_NOT_FOUND`, evaluated immediately after `center_id` and before `activation_tier_id`. The named tier must exist but is *not* required to belong to `center_id`: a tier configured for some other center is accepted, and its `tier_code` and response limits then govern this case. The pair of center and MRN must be unique across the registry; a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CASE_MRN`.

A registry case carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**, and this independence is deliberate. The abstraction dimension, `abstraction_status`, begins at `PENDING` and moves through `ABSTRACTED`, `VALIDATED` or `REJECTED` to the terminal `LOCKED`. The triage dimension, `triage_review_status`, begins at `UNREVIEWED` and settles into `UNDER_TRIAGE_CONFIRMED`, `OVER_TRIAGE_CONFIRMED` or `APPROPRIATE`. Neither dimension gates the other: a case still `PENDING` on the abstraction dimension may be triage-reviewed, and a case already `VALIDATED` may still be `UNREVIEWED`. Only `LOCKED` freezes anything, and it freezes only the abstraction dimension. `locked_at` is null until the case is locked.

At creation the case's `effective_iss` equals the arrival-recorded `injury_severity_score`. `effective_iss` is the audit-grade severity: once the case's abstraction dimension has reached `VALIDATED` or `LOCKED`, `effective_iss` instead takes the `iss_abstracted` value of the most recently created abstraction record of that case whose adjudication reached validated status — the latest such record by `created_at`, newest first. If that latest validated record's own `iss_abstracted` is null, the arrival-recorded `injury_severity_score` stands instead; the system does not fall further back to an older validated record that did carry a value. Until then, and for any case whose abstraction dimension is `PENDING`, `ABSTRACTED` or `REJECTED`, the arrival-recorded value stands.

**Registry inclusion** is then decided by a three-branch classification evaluated top to bottom, first match winning, and it is re-decided from scratch every time the case is created, every time an abstraction of the case is validated, and every time a transfer-out is recorded or rescinded:

1. If the case records a receiving center for a transfer-out, and its `los_hours` is strictly less than 24.00, and its `effective_iss` is strictly less than 9, then `inclusion_status` is `EXCLUDED` and `inclusion_reason` is `TRANSFER_OUT_MINOR_UNDER_24H`.
2. Otherwise, if `effective_iss` is strictly less than 4 and the case's activated tier has `tier_code` `TIER_3_CONSULT`, then `inclusion_status` is `EXCLUDED` and `inclusion_reason` is `BELOW_INJURY_THRESHOLD`.
3. Otherwise `inclusion_status` is `INCLUDED` and `inclusion_reason` is `MEETS_INCLUSION_CRITERIA`.

Note what branch 2 does *not* say: a minor injury that was activated at `TIER_1_FULL` or `TIER_2_MODIFIED` stays in the registry. Only a consult-tier activation with a severity below 4 falls out on injury grounds.

Each evaluation also rewrites the owning center's cohort partition from the ground up: `registry_included_case_count` becomes the number of that center's cases whose `inclusion_status` is `INCLUDED`, and `registry_excluded_case_count` the number whose `inclusion_status` is `EXCLUDED`. Every case of the center participates regardless of its abstraction or triage dimension, and no time window is applied. Because the counters are rewritten rather than adjusted, the two must always sum to the center's total case count. Opening a case rewrites this partition and re-derives the center's posture, but it does **not** recompute the center's persisted `under_triage_rate_pct` and `over_triage_rate_pct` and does **not** refresh any verification cycle's `readiness_score`: the stored rates keep the values they already held — stale denominator and all — until one of the operations §3.5 names actually recomputes them.

Cases may be read individually, and listed with filters for `center_id`, `activation_tier_id`, `transfer_in_from_center_id`, `transfer_out_to_center_id`, `abstraction_status`, `inclusion_status` and `triage_review_status` — the last two of which make the list endpoint the natural reverse lookup for both cohort partitions and both transfer directions.

### 3.3. Abstraction Submission, the Data Dictionary Scan, and Adjudication

Abstraction is where the registry earns or loses its compliance score. A `RegistryAbstractor` — and only a `RegistryAbstractor` — submits an abstraction against a case, providing `expected_case_version` (see §2.9) together with up to four abstracted values: `iss_abstracted`, the audit-grade Injury Severity Score; `gcs_arrival`, the abstracted arrival Glasgow Coma Score — neither of these two integers carries a range constraint of its own at submission, so any JSON integer is accepted and only a `RANGE` dictionary rule can object to it; `mechanism_code`, the abstracted injury mechanism code; and `ed_departure_at`, the abstracted ED departure timestamp. All four are nullable on the wire, precisely so that a `REQUIRED` dictionary rule has something to catch. The new record's `case_id` is taken from the path segment, its `submitted_by_user_id` is set from `X-User-ID`, and its `validation_status` starts at `SUBMITTED` with `validated_by_user_id` and `rejection_reason` both null until it is adjudicated. The three scan results below — `dictionary_error_count`, `band_multiplier_used` and `applied_penalty_points` — are computed once and persisted on the abstraction record itself, so the pricing of a submission is auditable long after the center's score has moved on.

Submission is legal while the case's abstraction dimension is `PENDING`, `ABSTRACTED` or `REJECTED`, and illegal once it is `VALIDATED` or `LOCKED`, where the refusal is `ERR_CASE_NOT_ABSTRACTABLE`. Resubmission is therefore a first-class operation rather than an error: an `ABSTRACTED` case may be abstracted again, and a `REJECTED` case is re-opened for exactly this purpose. When a new record is created, **any prior abstraction record of the same case still sitting at `SUBMITTED` is set to `REJECTED` with its `rejection_reason` written as the literal string `SUPERSEDED_BY_RESUBMISSION`**. A supersede is bookkeeping, not an adjudication: the superseded record's `validated_by_user_id` stays null, and none of its `applied_penalty_points` are returned to the center's `registry_compliance_score` — only the explicit rejection endpoint described later in this section performs that restoration. The case's abstraction dimension lands on `ABSTRACTED` whichever from-state it started in — `PENDING → ABSTRACTED` and `REJECTED → ABSTRACTED` share the same side-effect set — and the case's `version` increments by 1.

Timeliness is enforced against the logical clock: a submission is accepted only while the clock is at or before the case's `arrival_at` plus a 60-day abstraction window. Once that instant has passed, submission fails with `ERR_ABSTRACTION_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422).

Every accepted submission is scanned against the whole data dictionary, and the evaluation order of that scan is pinned:

- **Step one — pick the band.** Read the center's `registry_compliance_score` *as persisted at the start of this request*, before this submission's penalty is applied. If that score is 95.00 or higher, `band_multiplier_used` is 1.0; otherwise if it is 90.00 or higher, `band_multiplier_used` is 1.5; otherwise `band_multiplier_used` is 2.0. Recomputing the band after subtracting the penalty is a different and wrong answer.
- **Step two — find the violations.** Evaluate every active dictionary rule in ascending order of severity rank and then of `field_name`, where `CRITICAL` ranks 1, `MAJOR` ranks 2 and `MINOR` ranks 3. A `REQUIRED` rule is violated when the submitted value of its `field_name` is null. A `RANGE` rule is violated when the submitted value is not null and is either below `min_value` or above `max_value` — both bounds are inclusive, so a value exactly equal to either bound satisfies the rule. A `CONFORMANCE` rule is violated when the submitted value is not null and is not one of its `allowed_codes`, membership being tested after string coercion so that a numeric value satisfies a code spelling the same number. The scan reads the payload exactly as it arrived, which has three consequences: a value supplied as the empty string counts as *supplied*, so it satisfies a `REQUIRED` rule while remaining testable by the other two kinds; `ed_departure_at` is scanned as the literal timestamp text the client sent rather than as a normalised instant, so a `CONFORMANCE` rule on that field matches against that text; and a `RANGE` rule is never violated by a value that is not a JSON number, so a `RANGE` rule declared against a string-valued field never fires. `dictionary_error_count` is the number of violated rules, and the raw penalty is the sum of `error_weight` over the violated rules — 4 per critical violation, 2 per major, 1 per minor.
- **Step three — apply the band.** `applied_penalty_points` is the raw penalty multiplied by `band_multiplier_used`, rounded half up to two decimals.
- **Step four — rewrite the score.** The center's `registry_compliance_score` becomes the score read in step one minus `applied_penalty_points`, floored at 0.00, rounded half up to two decimals.

If the scan finds **any** violation of a rule whose `severity` is `CRITICAL`, the submission is refused outright with `ERR_DICTIONARY_CRITICAL_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422) before step three ever runs: no penalty is written, the center's score is untouched, and no abstraction record is created at all. Major and minor violations, by contrast, are recorded and priced rather than blocking.

Accepting the submission also refreshes readiness downstream: the reviewer readiness score is recomputed for every verification cycle of the center whose `status` is `PREP`, `PREP_BLOCKED`, `SUBMITTED`, `SITE_VISIT_SCHEDULED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`, the center's posture is re-derived, and the center's `version` is bumped.

**Adjudication** belongs to a `RegistryValidator`, who must be a different person from the submitter (§2.10). Acceptance sets the record's `validation_status` to `VALIDATED`, writes `validated_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, and drives the case's abstraction dimension `ABSTRACTED → VALIDATED`. That in turn recomputes the case's `effective_iss` from `iss_abstracted`, re-runs the inclusion classification of §3.2 — which rewrites both of the center's cohort counters — recomputes the center's provisional-monitoring triage rates, and increments the case's `version` by 1. Rejection sets `validation_status` to `REJECTED`, stores the supplied `rejection_reason`, drives the case's abstraction dimension `ABSTRACTED → REJECTED` so the case re-opens for resubmission, **restores to the center's `registry_compliance_score` exactly the `applied_penalty_points` this record had deducted** — added back in full even when the floor at 0.00 had clipped part of the original deduction, with the restored score then capped at 100.00 — re-derives the center's posture, and increments the case's `version` by 1. Either way the abstraction is now terminal: a second adjudication attempt on the same record is refused with `ERR_ABSTRACTION_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`. Abstraction records of a case may be listed back from the case, optionally narrowed by `validation_status`.

**Locking** a case is the program manager's act of freezing the registry submission. It is permitted only while the case's abstraction dimension is `VALIDATED`, only while the case's latest abstraction record is itself validated — otherwise `ERR_ABSTRACTION_NOT_VALIDATED` — and only while the center's posture is not `SUSPENDED`. Locking stamps `locked_at` with the current logical-clock instant, moves the abstraction dimension `VALIDATED → LOCKED`, and increments the case's `version` by 1. The lock has one further consequence that surfaces in §3.5: a locked case joins the verification-audit cohort, so the audit-variant triage rates change even though nothing about the patient did.

### 3.4. Documenting Required Specialty Response and Surgeon Presence

Surveyors read response-time documentation before they read anything else, so each activation carries a set of response logs — one per required responder. A `RegistryAbstractor` or the center's `TraumaProgramManager` documents one by naming the `responder_role`, which is one of `SURGEON`, `ANESTHESIA`, `NEUROSURGERY`, `ORTHOPEDICS` or `RADIOLOGY`; the `responder_user_id`, an opaque identifier of the documented responder; the `notified_at` instant at which the activation page went out; and optionally the `arrived_at` instant of bedside arrival. The log's `case_id` comes from the path segment. A null `arrived_at` is meaningful rather than missing data: it records that presence was never documented at all.

Compliance is derived, never supplied. `applicable_limit_minutes` is copied from the case's activated tier according to the responding service: a `SURGEON` log takes the tier's `surgeon_response_limit_minutes`, and every other `responder_role` takes the tier's `specialty_response_limit_minutes`. Then, if `arrived_at` is null, `response_minutes` is null and `compliance_status` is `UNDOCUMENTED`. Otherwise `response_minutes` is the interval from `notified_at` to `arrived_at` expressed in minutes and rounded half up to two decimals — no ordering constraint is imposed between the two instants, so an `arrived_at` earlier than `notified_at` is accepted and recorded as a negative `response_minutes`, which being under the limit resolves to `COMPLIANT` — and `compliance_status` is `COMPLIANT` when `response_minutes` is less than or equal to `applicable_limit_minutes` and `LATE` when it is greater. The limit comparison is inclusive: a response landing exactly on the limit is `COMPLIANT`, not `LATE`.

Surgeon presence carries a consequence the other services do not. A `SURGEON` log that resolves to `LATE`, or to `UNDOCUMENTED`, automatically opens a performance-improvement event for the center with `origin` `SYSTEM_AUTO`, `event_type` `DELAYED_SURGEON` and `severity` `HIGH`. That event's `case_id` points at the triggering case and its `source_deficiency_id` is null. There is no de-duplication here, unlike the under-triage cascade of §3.5: response logs are not unique per responder, so every further `SURGEON` log of the same case that resolves to `LATE` or `UNDOCUMENTED` opens another event of its own, even while an earlier `DELAYED_SURGEON` loop is still open. That raises the center's `open_pi_event_count` by 1, which re-derives the center's posture and recomputes the readiness score of every one of the center's verification cycles, and the center's `version` is bumped. A late anaesthesia or radiology arrival is recorded and reportable but opens no loop by itself.

Response logs of a case can be listed back from the case, optionally narrowed by `responder_role` or by `compliance_status`, which is how a program manager assembles the site-visit packet on required specialty response times.

### 3.5. Triage Adjudication and Over/Under-Triage Rate Monitoring

Over-triage and under-triage rates are the two numbers a verification body will not accept hand-waving about, and this system computes them **two different ways from the same data** — a distinction that must not be collapsed.

A `TraumaMedicalDirector` adjudicates a case's triage decision, supplying the resulting `triage_review_status` and optionally a `review_note` for the record. The adjudication is permitted only while the triage dimension is still `UNREVIEWED` — a second attempt is refused with `ERR_TRIAGE_ALREADY_REVIEWED` — and only while the case's `inclusion_status` is `INCLUDED`, because an excluded case is not part of the denominator that the rates are computed over — and an `EXCLUDED` case is refused with that very same `ERR_TRIAGE_ALREADY_REVIEWED` (HTTP 409) rather than with an error of its own, the still-`UNREVIEWED` test being the one evaluated first. Three outcomes are available, and each moves the triage dimension out of `UNREVIEWED`: `UNREVIEWED → APPROPRIATE` when the activation matched the injury, `UNREVIEWED → OVER_TRIAGE_CONFIRMED` when the center over-activated, and `UNREVIEWED → UNDER_TRIAGE_CONFIRMED` when it under-activated. All three recompute the center's provisional-monitoring rates, additionally refresh the `readiness_score` of every not-yet-decided verification cycle of the center — unconditionally, whether or not the alert cascade below opens a PI event — and increment the case's `version` by 1; what differs is what happens after.

The **provisional-monitoring** variant is the one persisted onto the center as `under_triage_rate_pct` and `over_triage_rate_pct`, and it is the variant every threshold and posture check reads. Its cohort is every registry case of the center whose `inclusion_status` is `INCLUDED`, at any abstraction status, with no time window. The denominator is the size of that cohort. The under-triage numerator counts cohort cases whose `triage_review_status` is `UNDER_TRIAGE_CONFIRMED`; the over-triage numerator counts those whose `triage_review_status` is `OVER_TRIAGE_CONFIRMED`. Each rate is one hundred multiplied by its numerator, divided by the denominator, rounded half up to two decimals — and when the denominator is zero, both rates are 0.00 rather than an error. An `APPROPRIATE` adjudication therefore leaves both numerators and the denominator unchanged and still triggers a recomputation, which is the correct behaviour, not a redundant one.

The **verification-audit** variant is never persisted; it exists to answer the surveyor's question "what do the rates look like if we recompute them from the injury data rather than from your own review outcomes?" Its cohort is narrower: only cases whose `inclusion_status` is `INCLUDED` **and** whose abstraction dimension has reached `VALIDATED` or `LOCKED`. Its numerators ignore the director's adjudications entirely. The under-triage numerator counts cohort cases whose `effective_iss` is at least 16 — the Cribari severity line — and whose activated tier has `tier_code` `TIER_3_CONSULT`, that is, a badly injured patient handled as a consult. The over-triage numerator counts cohort cases whose `effective_iss` is strictly below 9 and whose activated tier has `tier_code` `TIER_1_FULL`, that is, a full activation for a minor injury. The same rounding, zero-denominator and percentage conventions apply. Because the cohort and the numerator predicates both differ, identical underlying data legitimately produces two different pairs of rates, and this is the reason locking a case (§3.3) changes an audit-variant rate without changing the persisted one.

A computed read is available on the center for both variants. The `variant` query parameter takes the literal value `PROVISIONAL_MONITORING` for the provisional-monitoring variant or `VERIFICATION_AUDIT` for the verification-audit variant, and defaults to `PROVISIONAL_MONITORING` when omitted or supplied as an empty string; any other value is `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` per §2.11. Requesting the audit variant never persists its result onto the center, and neither variant of this read rewrites the center's persisted rates or touches its `updated_at` or `version`. The response reports exactly six properties and nothing else — the `variant` that was applied, the `denominator_case_count`, the `under_triage_numerator`, the `over_triage_numerator`, and the resulting `under_triage_rate_pct` and `over_triage_rate_pct` — the two rates as numbers at two decimals and the three counts as exact integers. Being a computed projection rather than a resource, the payload carries no `id` and none of the audit properties of §2.4.

**The under-triage alert cascade.** Immediately after a provisional-monitoring recomputation is persisted, the system tests one threshold, and confirming an under-triage case is the canonical way to trip it. If the persisted `under_triage_rate_pct` is at or above 5.00, and no PI event of that center already has `event_type` `UNDER_TRIAGE` with a `status` of `IDENTIFIED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `ACTION_PLANNED` or `REOPENED`, the system opens one. Trace the full four-hop consequence of a single `UNREVIEWED → UNDER_TRIAGE_CONFIRMED` adjudication that crosses the line:

1. The provisional-monitoring rates are recomputed and `under_triage_rate_pct` is written onto the center.
2. Because the rate is now at or above 5.00 and no under-triage loop is already open, a PI event is created with `origin` `SYSTEM_AUTO`, `event_type` `UNDER_TRIAGE`, `severity` `HIGH`, `status` `IDENTIFIED` and `loop_closure_due_at` set to the current logical-clock instant plus 60 days. That raises the center's `open_pi_event_count` by 1.
3. Because `open_pi_event_count` changed, the posture ladder of §2.7 re-runs. A center whose `under_triage_rate_pct` has reached 5.00 lands on `AT_RISK`.
4. Because `open_pi_event_count` changed, the reviewer readiness score of every verification cycle of the center is recomputed, and each cycle sitting in `PREP` whose recomputed score falls below 70.00 transitions `PREP → PREP_BLOCKED`.

The de-duplication condition in hop 2 is strict: only an *open* under-triage loop suppresses a new one. If the center's previous under-triage event has already reached `LOOP_CLOSED`, the next crossing opens a fresh event.

An `UNREVIEWED → OVER_TRIAGE_CONFIRMED` adjudication runs the mirrored but shorter path: the center's `over_triage_rate_pct` is recomputed and persisted, and the posture ladder re-runs, yielding `CONDITIONAL` once `over_triage_rate_pct` is at or above 35.00 and no higher-priority branch applies. No PI event is opened for over-triage — over-activation is a resource-utilisation concern, not a patient-safety one.

### 3.6. Referral Center Agreements and Transfer-In / Transfer-Out Patterns

A transfer-out is only legitimate if a referral agreement was in force when the patient arrived, and the system enforces exactly that. Agreements are directed: a `TraumaProgramManager` drafts one by naming the `referring_center_id` — the center that sends patients out, which must be the manager's own center at draft time — and the `receiving_center_id`, the center that guarantees acceptance. Both must resolve to registered centers, reported as `ERR_CENTER_NOT_FOUND` with the referring side checked first, but the two are not required to differ: a self-directed agreement naming the same center on both sides is accepted. The draft also carries the coverage interval as `effective_from` and `effective_to`, and `guaranteed_acceptance_minutes`, the contracted acceptance-decision window, an integer from 1 through 720 inclusive. A drafted agreement has `agreement_status` `DRAFT`, with `activated_at` and `terminated_at` both null. `effective_to` must be strictly later than `effective_from`, or the draft is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL_DATES`.

The coverage interval is **half-open**: `effective_from` is inclusive and `effective_to` is exclusive. Only the guaranteeing party may bring an agreement into force — activation requires `X-User-ID` to equal the agreement's `receiving_center_id`, and any other program manager attempting it is refused with `ERR_CENTER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Activation moves `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, stamps `activated_at` with the current logical-clock instant, and opens transfer-out coverage for the interval. It is also where interval exclusivity is enforced: among agreements on the *identical ordered pair* of referring and receiving center whose `agreement_status` is `ACTIVE`, no existing interval may intersect the requested one. Two half-open intervals intersect when the new interval's `effective_from` is strictly earlier than the existing interval's `effective_to` **and** the existing interval's `effective_from` is strictly earlier than the new interval's `effective_to`. Because both comparisons are strict and the intervals are half-open, adjacency is legal: an interval that starts exactly when another ends does not intersect it. Candidates are examined in ascending order of `effective_from` and then of `created_at`, and the first intersection found is reported as `ERR_AGREEMENT_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`. Drafted and terminated agreements are ignored by this check, and the reversed pair is a different resource entirely — an agreement from A to B never conflicts with an agreement from B to A.

Either party may terminate an agreement that is `ACTIVE`: termination requires `X-User-ID` to be either the `referring_center_id` or the `receiving_center_id`, carries a `termination_reason` for the record, moves `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` so that `agreement_status` becomes `TERMINATED`, stamps `terminated_at`, and frees the interval so a fresh agreement on the same directed pair can be activated over it. Activating anything that is not `DRAFT`, or terminating anything that is not `ACTIVE`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AGREEMENT_STATE`. Agreements can be listed from a center with a `direction` filter that selects whether the center is being treated as the referring or the receiving party, and an `agreement_status` filter. `direction` is read case-insensitively after trimming surrounding whitespace: `REFERRING`, `REFERRING_CENTER`, `OUTBOUND`, `OUT` and `SENDING` all select the referring side, while `RECEIVING`, `RECEIVING_CENTER`, `INBOUND` and `IN` all select the receiving side. Omitting `direction`, supplying it empty, or supplying any value outside those two sets is not an error — the list then returns every agreement on which the center is either party, which is also the unfiltered default.

**Recording a transfer-out** is done by the center's `TraumaProgramManager` or by a `RegistryAbstractor`, supplying `transfer_out_to_center_id`. Two preconditions apply beyond the shared checks: the case must not already record a receiving center — a second transfer-out against the same case is refused with `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_LOCKED` (HTTP 409), the same code the lock guard uses, rather than with an error of its own — and there must exist an agreement whose `referring_center_id` is the case's own center, whose `receiving_center_id` is the supplied `transfer_out_to_center_id`, whose `agreement_status` is `ACTIVE`, and whose half-open interval contains the case's `arrival_at` — that is, `effective_from` is at or before `arrival_at` and `arrival_at` is strictly before `effective_to`. With no such agreement the request fails with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_REFERRAL_AGREEMENT`. A locked case may not be transferred out at all (`ERR_CASE_ALREADY_LOCKED`).

Recording the transfer writes `transfer_out_to_center_id` onto the case and sets its `ed_disposition` to `TRANSFER_OUT`, then re-runs the inclusion classification of §3.2 — so a case whose `los_hours` is under 24.00 with an `effective_iss` below 9 flips `INCLUDED → EXCLUDED` with `inclusion_reason` `TRANSFER_OUT_MINOR_UNDER_24H` — rewrites both of the center's cohort counters, recomputes the provisional-monitoring triage rates (and, if the recomputed `under_triage_rate_pct` has reached 5.00, opens the same `UNDER_TRIAGE` loop described in §3.5), re-derives the center's posture, refreshes the `readiness_score` of every not-yet-decided verification cycle of the center, and increments the case's `version` by 1.

**Rescinding a transfer-out** is the compensating action and belongs to the `TraumaProgramManager` alone. It requires that the case currently records a receiving center — a rescission against a case that records none is refused with that same `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_LOCKED` — and that the case is not `LOCKED`, and it carries a `rescind_reason`, which is retained on the record for the audit trail but is not one of the properties the case representation returns. Rescission clears `transfer_out_to_center_id` back to null, returns `ed_disposition` to `ADMIT`, re-runs the inclusion classification so an `EXCLUDED` / `TRANSFER_OUT_MINOR_UNDER_24H` case returns to `INCLUDED` / `MEETS_INCLUSION_CRITERIA`, restores both cohort counters to their pre-transfer values, recomputes the triage rates and the posture, refreshes the `readiness_score` of every not-yet-decided verification cycle of the center, and increments the case's `version` by 1. The cohort partition after a rescind must be byte-for-byte the partition before the transfer-out.

The inbound direction needs no agreement and no workflow: `transfer_in_from_center_id` is simply recorded at case creation, and the case list's filter on it is what produces transfer-in pattern reports. Together with the filter on `transfer_out_to_center_id`, the two directions make the case list the referral-pattern report for both sides of every agreement.

### 3.7. The Performance Improvement and Patient Safety Loop

The PIPS program is where surveyors look for evidence that a center notices its own problems and closes them. A loop in this system is a **cyclic** process, not a one-way pipeline: `IDENTIFIED → UNDER_REVIEW → ACTION_PLANNED → LOOP_CLOSED`, and then, if a reviewer finds the closure unsupported, `LOOP_CLOSED → REOPENED → UNDER_REVIEW → ACTION_PLANNED → LOOP_CLOSED` again, for as many rounds as it takes. `LOOP_CLOSED` is emphatically **not** a terminal state.

A `TraumaMedicalDirector` opens an event manually by naming the `center_id` whose PIPS program owns the loop, the `event_type` and the `severity`, which is one of `HIGH`, `MEDIUM` or `LOW`. Optionally the payload names the `case_id` of the registry case that triggered the event, and a `source_deficiency_id`. Available `event_type` values are `UNDER_TRIAGE`, `DELAYED_SURGEON`, `DEFICIENCY_ESCALATION`, `MORTALITY_REVIEW` and `TRANSFER_DELAY`; of these, `DEFICIENCY_ESCALATION` is by convention the one the system generates for itself, but the manual enumeration is not narrowed to exclude it — a director who names `DEFICIENCY_ESCALATION` explicitly gets an accepted event carrying `origin` `MANUAL`, because the enum check of §2.11 admits all five values on this endpoint. The optional `case_id` must resolve to a registry case or the request is `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, whereas `source_deficiency_id` is a free-form back-reference that is *not* checked for existence and is stored exactly as supplied. A manually opened event has `status` `IDENTIFIED`, `origin` `MANUAL`, `opened_at` at the current logical-clock instant, `loop_closure_due_at` at `opened_at` plus 60 days, `review_round` 1, and `closed_at` null. Creation raises the center's `open_pi_event_count` by 1, re-derives the center's posture, recomputes the readiness score of every not-yet-decided verification cycle of the center — applying the automatic `PREP ↔ PREP_BLOCKED` gate of §2.8 to each — and bumps the center's `version`.

System-generated events set `origin` to `SYSTEM_AUTO` and are created by three distinct triggers, already described or described below: an under-triage rate crossing (§3.5), a late or undocumented surgeon response (§3.4), and a critical criteria-deficiency escalation (§3.9). The first two carry a 60-day loop-closure deadline from the moment of creation, matching a manual event; a `DEFICIENCY_ESCALATION` event carries only 30 days, reflecting the compressed remediation clock that escalation imposes. Events can be listed with filters for `center_id`, `case_id`, `source_deficiency_id`, `status`, `event_type` and `origin` — `source_deficiency_id` being the reverse lookup from a deficiency to the loop it spawned, and `origin` being how a program manager separates the loops it opened itself from the loops the system opened for it.

Moving an event into multidisciplinary review is the director's act, and it is legal from either `IDENTIFIED` or `REOPENED` — both transition to `UNDER_REVIEW`. Neither changes any counter: the event was open before and remains open, so `open_pi_event_count` is untouched. Starting review on an event that is already `UNDER_REVIEW`, or on one that is `ACTION_PLANNED` or `LOOP_CLOSED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PI_STATE`.

Corrective actions are attached to an event under review, by either a `TraumaMedicalDirector` or the center's `TraumaProgramManager`. The payload carries the `action_type` — one of `EDUCATION`, `PROTOCOL_CHANGE`, `CREDENTIAL_ACTION` or `EQUIPMENT` — the `owner_user_id` of the accountable owner — an opaque identifier that is not checked for existence — and the committed `due_at` completion date, on which no ordering constraint at all is imposed, so a `due_at` already behind the logical clock is accepted; the action's `pi_event_id` comes from the path segment. Attaching an action requires the event to be `UNDER_REVIEW` and advances it `UNDER_REVIEW → ACTION_PLANNED`. The new action starts with `completion_status` `OPEN`, `completed_at` null, `verified_at` null, `verified_by_user_id` null and `completion_note` null — the note being a property the action representation always reports, null until a completion supplies one — and the event's `corrective_action_count` and `verified_action_count` are recomputed, with the event's `version` bumped. Attaching an action bumps no counter on the center and does not touch the center at all. Actions of an event may be listed back from it, optionally narrowed by `completion_status`.

An action's own lifecycle is `OPEN → COMPLETED → VERIFIED`, with `VERIFIED` terminal. Completion may be recorded by a `TraumaProgramManager` — who must be the program manager of the parent event's own center, or the request is `ERR_CENTER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` — or by a `TraumaMedicalDirector`, optionally with a `completion_note`, and it requires two things: the action must still be `OPEN`, and its parent event's `status` must be either `ACTION_PLANNED` or `REOPENED`. Of those two the **parent event's state is tested first**, per the Band E ordering of §2.11, so an attempt that violates both is reported as `ERR_INVALID_PI_STATE`. Completion stamps `completed_at` with the current logical-clock instant and neither recomputes the event's action counters nor touches the center. Verification is then performed by a `TraumaMedicalDirector` or a `VerificationReviewer` on an action that is `COMPLETED`, by someone who is not that action's `owner_user_id` (§2.10); it stamps `verified_at`, writes `verified_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, and recomputes the parent event's `verified_action_count` — which is precisely what unblocks loop closure — bumping the event's `version`. Verification imposes **no** precondition on the parent event's own `status`, and it too leaves the center untouched. Verifying an action that is still `OPEN`, or completing one that is already `VERIFIED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ACTION_STATE` — subject to the precedence just stated, so completing an already-`VERIFIED` action whose event has meanwhile reached `LOOP_CLOSED` reports `ERR_INVALID_PI_STATE` instead.

**Loop closure** is the documented moment a director asserts the problem is fixed. It requires the event to be `ACTION_PLANNED`; it requires the event to have at least one corrective action and every one of them to be `VERIFIED`, failing which the refusal is `ERR_OPEN_ACTIONS_BLOCK_LOOP_CLOSURE` — an event with no corrective actions at all is refused by the same rule; and it requires the closing director to be the `owner_user_id` of none of the event's actions. The payload carries a `loop_closure_note`, which is retained on the event and is one of the properties the event representation reports (null on an event that has never been closed). Closure moves `ACTION_PLANNED → LOOP_CLOSED`, stamps `closed_at`, and executes the conserved PI accounting identity: `open_pi_event_count` decreases by 1 while `closed_pi_event_count` increases by 1. The center's posture is re-derived, the readiness score of every not-yet-decided verification cycle of the center is recomputed, and any cycle sitting in `PREP_BLOCKED` that is thereby no longer blocked in the full sense of §2.8 — recomputed reviewer score back at 70.00 or above **and** `open_type_i_count` down to 0 — transitions `PREP_BLOCKED → PREP`; a cycle still carrying an open `TYPE_I_CRITICAL` deficiency of its own stays blocked however far the score recovered. The center's `version` is bumped.

**Reopening** is the compensating action, available to a `TraumaMedicalDirector` or a `VerificationReviewer` on an event that is `LOOP_CLOSED`, and carrying a `reopen_reason`, which is retained on the event and reported as one of its properties (null until a first reopen). It moves `LOOP_CLOSED → REOPENED`, increments `review_round` by 1, clears `closed_at` back to null, and resets `loop_closure_due_at` to the current logical-clock instant plus 30 days — a reopened loop gets a shorter leash than the original 60 days; the earlier `loop_closure_note` is left in place rather than cleared. Every corrective action of the event that had reached `VERIFIED` is returned to `OPEN`, with its `completed_at`, `verified_at` and `verified_by_user_id` all cleared back to null while its `completion_note` is retained, and the event's `verified_action_count` resets to 0, so the next round must be verified afresh; actions that were merely `OPEN` or `COMPLETED` are left exactly as they were, a `COMPLETED` action keeping its `completed_at`. The counter move exactly undoes closure: `open_pi_event_count` increases by 1 and `closed_pi_event_count` decreases by 1, restoring the pre-closure partition. Posture and the readiness score of every not-yet-decided cycle of the center are recomputed, and the center's `version` is bumped. Reopening anything that is not `LOOP_CLOSED` is `ERR_INVALID_PI_STATE`.

One deadline consequence is easy to overlook. An open loop — one whose `status` is `IDENTIFIED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `ACTION_PLANNED` or `REOPENED` — whose `loop_closure_due_at` has fallen strictly behind the logical clock is an **overdue loop**, and overdue loops are read in two places: they push the center's posture to `AT_RISK` (§2.7), and they carry extra weight in a national cycle's readiness penalty (§3.9). Simply advancing the clock past a due date therefore degrades a center's posture and its readiness with no write against the center at all.

### 3.8. Outreach, Injury Prevention and Education Credit

Both audits require the center to show community and professional engagement, measured in weighted credit hours. The center's `TraumaProgramManager` logs each delivered activity against its own center, supplying the `activity_kind` — one of `INJURY_PREVENTION`, `PROFESSIONAL_EDUCATION` or `COMMUNITY_OUTREACH` — the `event_date` on which it was delivered, the raw `credit_hours` claimed (greater than zero, accepted with up to three decimals) and the `audience_count` of participants reached (zero or greater). A newly logged activity has `verified_status` `LOGGED` and `reviewed_by_user_id` null, and it contributes **nothing** to the center's ledger until it is verified; the center's `outreach_credit_hours_ytd` is unchanged by logging.

Each activity carries its own weighted contribution, `weighted_credit_hours`, computed from the raw hours and the kind of activity. Injury-prevention work counts at one and a half times its contact hours, professional education at exactly its contact hours, and community outreach at half its contact hours. That product is then snapped to the nearest quarter hour — multiplied by four, rounded half up to a whole number, and divided by four — so `weighted_credit_hours` is always an exact multiple of 0.25.

**The rounding order is pinned and it changes the answer.** Each activity is quantized to the nearest quarter hour *first*, and the quantized values are then summed; the center's `outreach_credit_hours_ytd` is that sum rounded half up to two decimals. Summing raw weighted hours and quantizing the total once is a different and wrong result. Consider three activities whose weighted products are 1.65, 1.15 and 0.375 hours: quantizing each first gives 1.75, 1.25 and 0.50, which sum to 3.50, whereas summing first gives 3.175, which quantizes to 3.25.

Only activities whose `verified_status` is `VERIFIED` enter the ledger — `LOGGED` and `REJECTED` rows contribute nothing — and no date window is applied, so the ledger spans every verified activity of the center.

Adjudication belongs to the `TraumaMedicalDirector`. Verification is legal from either `LOGGED` or `REJECTED`, so `LOGGED → VERIFIED` and `REJECTED → VERIFIED` are both supported; rejection is legal from either `LOGGED` or `VERIFIED`, so `VERIFIED → REJECTED` and a direct `LOGGED → REJECTED` are both supported. A verify against an already-verified activity, or a reject against an already-rejected one, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_OUTREACH_STATE`. Either decision writes `reviewed_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`; rejection additionally carries a `rejection_reason`. A `REJECTED → VERIFIED` transition leaves the earlier `rejection_reason` in place rather than clearing it, so a verified activity may legitimately still carry the reason for which it was once rejected. Either decision recomputes the ledger from scratch over the center's remaining verified activities and recomputes the readiness score of every verification cycle of the center that has not yet been decided.

Because the ledger is recomputed rather than incremented, the review dimension is **cyclic without drift**: verifying an activity raises `outreach_credit_hours_ytd` by that activity's `weighted_credit_hours`, rejecting it must land the ledger exactly back on its pre-verification value, and an activity that crosses the verified boundary repeatedly must never accumulate a rounding residue. Activities of a center can be listed back from it with `verified_status` and `activity_kind` filters.

### 3.9. Verification Cycle Preparation, the Site Visit, and the Decision

A verification cycle is the container for one audit. The center's `TraumaProgramManager` opens one by naming its own `center_id`, the `cycle_type` — `NATIONAL_VERIFICATION` or `STATE_DESIGNATION` — and the planned `site_visit_date`. A new cycle has `status` `PREP`, each of `open_type_i_count`, `open_type_ii_count` and `open_type_iii_count` at 0, `decided_at`, `decision_effective_date` and `decision` all null — `decision` being a property the cycle representation always reports, null until a decision is rendered and thereafter equal to the cycle's own `status` — and a `readiness_score` computed immediately under the reviewer variant. Creation is the one recomputation that stores the score **without** applying the `PREP ↔ PREP_BLOCKED` gate of §2.8, so a cycle opened at a center whose outreach, PI or registry penalties already drag the score below 70.00 is nevertheless created in `PREP`, and only the next cascade that touches it moves it to `PREP_BLOCKED`. Creation touches no counter of the center and does not bump the center's `version`. Cycles may be read individually, listed across centers with `center_id`, `cycle_type` and `status` filters, and listed as a reverse lookup from their owning center with `cycle_type` and `status` filters.

**The readiness score** is the number that decides whether a cycle can be submitted, and it is assembled from four independent, individually capped penalties subtracted from a base of 100.00. It is recomputed whenever a deficiency is recorded, remediated, closed or escalated; whenever a PI event is created, closed or reopened; whenever a case's triage decision is adjudicated, whenever a transfer-out is recorded and whenever a transfer-out is rescinded (§3.5, §3.6) — unconditionally in all three, whether or not the alert cascade opens a PI event; whenever a late or undocumented surgeon response opens a `DELAYED_SURGEON` loop (§3.4); whenever an outreach activity is verified or rejected; whenever an abstraction is submitted; on a `CONTINGENCY` decision; at cycle submission; and on demand through the cycle's computed readiness read. Recording a deficiency, submitting its remediation and closing it refresh **only the deficiency's own cycle**; every other trigger in this list refreshes every not-yet-decided cycle of the center as defined in §2.8, except that an abstraction submission skips cycles sitting in `CONTINGENCY` and refreshes only those in `PREP`, `PREP_BLOCKED`, `SUBMITTED`, `SITE_VISIT_SCHEDULED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`.

- **Deficiency penalty.** Count this cycle's own criteria deficiencies that are still considered open, split by class: let the first count be its `TYPE_I_CRITICAL` deficiencies, the second its `TYPE_II_MAJOR` deficiencies and the third its `TYPE_III_MINOR` deficiencies. The penalty is 15 points per critical deficiency plus 6 points per major plus 2 points per minor, capped at 60 points.
- **Outreach penalty.** A national cycle requires 12.00 weighted credit hours; a state cycle requires 8.00. The gap is the required hours minus the center's `outreach_credit_hours_ytd`, floored at 0.00 so a center in surplus is not rewarded. The penalty is that gap multiplied by 1.5, rounded half up to two decimals, capped at 20.00 points.
- **PI penalty**, and this input branches on cycle type. For a national cycle the penalty is 4 points per open PI event of the center plus 8 points per overdue loop, capped at 20 points. For a state cycle it is 2 points per open PI event of the center, with overdue loops carrying no extra weight, capped at 10 points. "Open PI event" means the center's `open_pi_event_count`; "overdue loop" carries the meaning fixed in §3.7.
- **Registry penalty.** The center's shortfall from a perfect data-dictionary record — 100.00 minus its `registry_compliance_score` — multiplied by 0.25, rounded half up to two decimals, floored at 0.00.

`readiness_score` is 100.00 less all four penalties, floored at 0.00 and rounded half up to two decimals. Deficiency inputs are scoped to this cycle alone; the outreach, PI and registry inputs are center-wide and identical for both variants.

The score has **two variants that differ in exactly one respect**: which deficiency statuses count as open. The **reviewer** variant treats `OPEN`, `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` and `ESCALATED` as open, so a center's own remediation packets do not improve its score until the reviewing authority actually closes them. The **self-assessment** variant treats only `OPEN` and `ESCALATED` as open, crediting `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` as resolved, which is what makes it useful for the center's own pre-review gap analysis and also what makes it systematically more generous than the reviewer variant on identical data. The reviewer variant is the one persisted onto the cycle as `readiness_score`, the one the submission gate reads, and the one that drives the automatic `PREP ↔ PREP_BLOCKED` transitions of §2.8. The cycle's computed readiness read accepts a `variant` query parameter whose literal values are `REVIEWER_ASSESSMENT` for the reviewer variant and `SELF_ASSESSMENT` for the self-assessment variant; it defaults to `REVIEWER_ASSESSMENT` when the parameter is omitted or supplied as an empty string, and any other value is `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` per §2.11. The self-assessment variant is never persisted onto the cycle — and neither, in fact, is the read itself persisting anything under either variant: the read never rewrites `readiness_score`, never re-evaluates the `PREP ↔ PREP_BLOCKED` gate, and never touches the cycle's `updated_at` or `version`. That read returns exactly seven properties and nothing else — the `variant` applied, the `base_score` (always 100.00), the `deficiency_penalty`, the `outreach_penalty`, the `pi_penalty`, the resulting `readiness_score`, and a `submit_eligible` boolean that is true exactly when *that requested variant's* `readiness_score` is at or above 70.00, so a `SELF_ASSESSMENT` read can report `submit_eligible` true on a cycle whose actual submission would still be refused. The registry penalty of the fourth bullet is an input to the score but is deliberately **not** reported as a property of this read. Being a computed projection rather than a resource, the payload carries no `id` and none of the audit properties of §2.4.

**Criteria deficiencies** are recorded by the reviewing authority whose role matches the cycle's type — the cycle is resolved before that match is tested, so a mismatched authority naming a `cycle_id` that does not exist is reported as `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND` rather than `ERR_DESIGNATION_AUTHORITY_MISMATCH` — and only while the cycle's `status` is `PREP`, `PREP_BLOCKED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `CONTINGENCY`; anywhere else the refusal is `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`. The payload carries the `criterion_code` — the verification criterion identifier, for instance `CD-5-13`, which also serves as the ordering key for escalation cascades — the `deficiency_class`, one of `TYPE_I_CRITICAL`, `TYPE_II_MAJOR` or `TYPE_III_MINOR`, and the `remediation_due_at` deadline, which must be strictly later than the current logical-clock instant or the request fails with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL_DATES`. The deficiency's `verification_cycle_id` comes from the path segment; it starts with `status` `OPEN`, `remediation_note` null, `closed_at` null and `escalation_reason` null — the escalation reason being a property the deficiency representation always reports, null until an escalation supplies one. `criterion_code` is free-form text and is not matched against any registered catalogue, and a cycle may carry several deficiencies on the identical `criterion_code`. Recording it increments the cycle's open count for its class, recomputes the cycle's `readiness_score` — the deficiency's own cycle only, never the center's other cycles — and, for a `TYPE_I_CRITICAL` deficiency, moves the cycle to `PREP_BLOCKED` *if and only if it was sitting in `PREP`* (a cycle recording deficiencies from `UNDER_REVIEW` or `CONTINGENCY` keeps that status) and increments the center's `open_critical_deficiency_count`, which in turn re-derives the center's posture to at least `AT_RISK`. Both the cycle's and the center's `version` are bumped, and the center's bump is unconditional: recording even a `TYPE_III_MINOR` deficiency, which moves no counter of the center at all, still advances the center's `version` by 1. Deficiencies of a cycle can be listed back from it with `deficiency_class` and `status` filters.

Note how the cycle's three per-class counters are defined: each counts this cycle's deficiencies of that class whose `status` is `OPEN`, `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` or `ESCALATED`. `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` still counts as open for these counters, which is why submitting remediation does not move them and does not improve the reviewer readiness score. The center's `open_critical_deficiency_count`, by contrast, counts every `TYPE_I_CRITICAL` deficiency of that center whose `status` is anything other than `CLOSED`, and `escalated_critical_deficiency_count` counts only those whose `status` is exactly `ESCALATED`.

**Remediating a deficiency** is the program manager's move, and it is legal from `OPEN`, from `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` (resubmission supersedes the previous packet) and from `ESCALATED`; anywhere else the refusal is `ERR_INVALID_DEFICIENCY_STATE`. The payload carries `expected_cycle_version` (§2.9) and the `remediation_note` describing the evidence, which is stored as the most recent remediation submission. The deficiency lands on `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` — `OPEN → REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` and `ESCALATED → REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` are both supported — the cycle's three per-class open counters are left unchanged, the cycle's readiness score is recomputed (improving under the self-assessment variant while standing still under the reviewer variant), and the cycle's `version` is incremented by 1. The escalated path carries one extra consequence: coming out of `ESCALATED`, a `TYPE_I_CRITICAL` deficiency decrements the center's `escalated_critical_deficiency_count` by 1 and the center's posture is re-derived, which is how a center climbs back out of `SUSPENDED`.

**Escalating a deficiency** is available to the reviewing authority when remediation has not arrived in time. It is legal from `OPEN` and from `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`, carries an `escalation_reason`, and moves the deficiency to `ESCALATED` while rewriting its `remediation_due_at` to the current logical-clock instant plus 30 days. Escalation opens one PI event with `origin` `SYSTEM_AUTO`, `event_type` `DEFICIENCY_ESCALATION` and its `source_deficiency_id` pointing back at the escalated deficiency, which raises the center's `open_pi_event_count` by 1; for a `TYPE_I_CRITICAL` deficiency it also raises the center's `escalated_critical_deficiency_count` by 1. Unlike deficiency recording, closure and remediation, escalation is *not* one of the operations that tests the designation-authority match, so either reviewing authority may escalate a deficiency of either cycle type. The center's posture is re-derived and the center's `version` is bumped; and because the new PI event moved `open_pi_event_count`, the escalated deficiency's own cycle has its `readiness_score` recomputed and its `version` bumped, and so does every other not-yet-decided cycle of the center. Escalating a closed or already-escalated deficiency is `ERR_INVALID_DEFICIENCY_STATE`.

**Closing a deficiency** belongs to the reviewing authority whose role matches the cycle type, and it is legal from `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` and from `ESCALATED` — never from `OPEN`, which is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DEFICIENCY_STATE`. Closure takes no payload. It stamps `closed_at` with the current logical-clock instant, decrements the cycle's open count for that deficiency's class, decrements the center's `open_critical_deficiency_count` when the class is `TYPE_I_CRITICAL`, re-derives the center's posture, recomputes that cycle's readiness score and no other cycle's — a cycle sitting in `PREP_BLOCKED` moves back to `PREP` only when it is no longer blocked in the full sense of §2.8, that is when its recomputed reviewer score has returned to 70.00 or above **and** its `open_type_i_count` has fallen to 0, so closing a `TYPE_III_MINOR` deficiency while a `TYPE_I_CRITICAL` of the same cycle is still open leaves the cycle in `PREP_BLOCKED` however healthy the score — and increments the cycle's `version` by 1. Unlike deficiency recording, closure does not force a `version` bump on the center: the center advances only if closing this deficiency actually moved one of its counters.

**Surveyor bookings** protect a surveyor's calendar rather than a cycle's. The reviewing authority books a window by supplying the `surveyor_user_id`, an opaque identifier of the site-visit surveyor, the inclusive `window_start` and the inclusive `window_end` of the on-site window, and optionally `travel_buffer_hours`, an integer of 0 or greater — a negative value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` in Band C, and the field defaults to 24 when omitted or supplied as `null`; the booking's `verification_cycle_id` comes from the path segment. `window_end` must be strictly later than `window_start`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL_DATES`. Bookings are status-neutral with respect to the cycle's own lifecycle and may be added while the cycle is `SUBMITTED` or `SITE_VISIT_SCHEDULED`; attempting one from any other cycle state is `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`. A booking mutates nothing outside itself: the cycle's and the center's `version` are untouched.

The window itself is **closed** at both ends and is padded symmetrically by the travel buffer, and adjacency is *not* enough to avoid a conflict. The buffer applied is the **requested** booking's own `travel_buffer_hours` — the default 24 when the field was omitted, otherwise whatever integer was supplied — and it is applied to both sides of the comparison; each existing booking's own stored `travel_buffer_hours` is ignored entirely, so a request carrying `travel_buffer_hours` 0 collides only on genuine overlap even against a neighbour booked with a 48-hour buffer, and a request carrying 72 is padded by 72 against every candidate whatever buffer that candidate recorded. A requested window conflicts with an existing one when the requested `window_start` is strictly earlier than the existing `window_end` plus the requested buffer **and** the existing `window_start` is strictly earlier than the requested `window_end` plus the requested buffer. The candidate set is every existing booking of that same `surveyor_user_id`, across every cycle and every center — a surveyor cannot be in two places at once regardless of who is paying for the visit. Candidates are examined in ascending order of `window_start` and then of `created_at`, and the first conflict found is reported as `ERR_SURVEYOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Because both comparisons are strict, a gap of exactly the requested buffer — exactly 24 hours at the default — between two windows is accepted. Bookings of a cycle may be listed back from it, optionally narrowed by `surveyor_user_id`.

**Submitting a cycle** for review is the program manager's act and requires three things at once: the cycle is in `PREP` (a cycle in `PREP_BLOCKED` is exactly the case this gate exists to catch, and it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`), the center's posture is not `SUSPENDED`, and the cycle's reviewer readiness score is at or above 70.00. The posture consulted is the response-time evaluation of §2.7, so a center whose posture degraded only because the clock passed an open loop's due date is judged on that live value. Submission refreshes `readiness_score` at submission time — freezing the value the reviewers will see — rewrites the three per-class open counters from the cycle's own deficiencies, moves `PREP → SUBMITTED`, and increments the cycle's `version` by 1. It touches neither the center's counters nor the center's `version`.

From there the reviewing authority drives the visit — though only *opening deliberation* is authority-matched among these two: scheduling the site visit is deliberately not one of the five operations of §2.11 that test the designation-authority match, so either reviewing authority may confirm a visit date on either cycle type, while opening deliberation from the wrong authority is `ERR_DESIGNATION_AUTHORITY_MISMATCH`. Scheduling the site visit requires the cycle to be `SUBMITTED` and requires at least one surveyor booking to already exist for the cycle, created while the cycle was `SUBMITTED` — a booking added later, while the cycle was already `SITE_VISIT_SCHEDULED`, does not satisfy this requirement, and its absence is reported as `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE` too; it overwrites the cycle's `site_visit_date` with the newly supplied `site_visit_date`, moves `SUBMITTED → SITE_VISIT_SCHEDULED`, and increments the cycle's `version` by 1. Opening deliberation after the visit requires the cycle to be `SITE_VISIT_SCHEDULED` and again requires at least one surveyor booking to exist; it moves `SITE_VISIT_SCHEDULED → UNDER_REVIEW`, making the cycle eligible to record the deficiencies found on site, and increments the cycle's `version` by 1.

**The decision** is rendered from `UNDER_REVIEW` only, by the authority whose role matches `cycle_type`, supplying the `decision` and the `decision_effective_date` that the decision carries. All three outcomes stamp `decided_at` with the current logical-clock instant, write the supplied `decision_effective_date` onto the cycle verbatim with no ordering constraint against `decided_at`, record the `decision` property, move the cycle's own `status` to that same literal — `VERIFIED`, `CONTINGENCY` or `DENIED` — re-derive the center's posture and bump the center's `version`; but their remaining side-effect sets diverge sharply, and the national and state branches are not symmetric.

`UNDER_REVIEW → VERIFIED` is permitted only when the cycle's `open_type_i_count` is exactly 0; any `TYPE_I_CRITICAL` deficiency still `OPEN`, `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` or `ESCALATED` blocks it with `ERR_CRITICAL_DEFICIENCY_BLOCKS_VERIFICATION`. It writes `decision_effective_date` from the payload. A `NATIONAL_VERIFICATION` cycle sets the center's `national_verification_status` to `VERIFIED`; a `STATE_DESIGNATION` cycle sets the center's `state_designation_status` to `DESIGNATED` and leaves `national_verification_status` untouched. The center's posture is then re-derived.

`UNDER_REVIEW → CONTINGENCY` has no deficiency precondition at all — it is the outcome that *exists* for cycles with unresolved critical findings — and it drives the deepest cascade in the system. Follow all four hops:

1. `decided_at` is stamped and the decision is written to the center: a `NATIONAL_VERIFICATION` cycle sets `national_verification_status` to `CONTINGENCY`, while a `STATE_DESIGNATION` cycle sets `state_designation_status` to `PROVISIONAL`.
2. Because the cycle entered `CONTINGENCY`, every criteria deficiency of that cycle whose `deficiency_class` is `TYPE_I_CRITICAL` and whose `status` is `OPEN` or `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` is set to `ESCALATED`, with its `remediation_due_at` rewritten to the current logical-clock instant plus 30 days. These are processed in ascending `criterion_code` order, ties broken by ascending `created_at`. Unlike the explicit escalation endpoint, this cascade supplies no reason: an `escalation_reason` that was null stays null on every deficiency escalated this way.
3. Because each of those deficiencies became `ESCALATED`, one PI event is created per escalated deficiency, with `origin` `SYSTEM_AUTO`, `event_type` `DEFICIENCY_ESCALATION`, `severity` `HIGH`, `status` `IDENTIFIED`, a null `case_id`, its `source_deficiency_id` pointing at the escalated deficiency, and `loop_closure_due_at` at the current logical-clock instant plus 30 days. The center's `open_pi_event_count` rises by the number of escalations.
4. Because `escalated_critical_deficiency_count` and `open_pi_event_count` have both changed, the posture ladder re-runs, and it yields `SUSPENDED` as soon as `escalated_critical_deficiency_count` reaches 2 — meaning a contingency decision over two or more unresolved critical findings freezes the center's case locking and cycle submission outright.

The cycle's `readiness_score` is refreshed as part of the same decision — together with its three per-class open counters, and without the `PREP ↔ PREP_BLOCKED` gate being evaluated, since a `CONTINGENCY` cycle is in neither of those two states. This refresh is peculiar to the `CONTINGENCY` branch: a `VERIFIED` or a `DENIED` decision leaves the cycle's `readiness_score` exactly as submission froze it. And because the escalations of hop 3 raised the center's `open_pi_event_count`, when at least one deficiency was escalated the readiness score of every *other* not-yet-decided cycle of the center is refreshed too, gate included.

`UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED` likewise has no deficiency precondition, and this is where the two authorities diverge most. A `NATIONAL_VERIFICATION` denial writes `national_verification_status` `SUSPENDED`, which by branch 1 of the posture ladder immediately makes the center's `readiness_posture` `SUSPENDED` and gates both case locking and cycle submission with `ERR_POSTURE_SUSPENDED`. A `STATE_DESIGNATION` denial writes `state_designation_status` `WITHDRAWN` and nothing else: `national_verification_status` is not touched, and the center's `readiness_posture` is therefore unchanged. Losing a state designation is a reporting event; losing national verification is an operational freeze.

Both `VERIFIED` and `DENIED` are terminal for the cycle: every further operation addressed at the cycle itself — submitting it, booking a surveyor window on it, scheduling its site visit, opening deliberation, deciding it again, or recording a further deficiency against it — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`. Terminality does **not** extend to the cycle's existing deficiencies, whose three operations are guarded by the deficiency's own `status` and by nothing about the cycle: remediating, closing or escalating a still-open deficiency of a decided cycle is legal, and it does recompute and rewrite that decided cycle's `readiness_score` even though the value was supposed to be frozen. The `PREP ↔ PREP_BLOCKED` gate remains inert throughout, the cycle being in neither state. A `CONTINGENCY` cycle is not terminal at all — it remains eligible to record further deficiencies so that the remediation trail can be documented.
