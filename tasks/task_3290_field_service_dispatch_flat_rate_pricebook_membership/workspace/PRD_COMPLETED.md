# Field Service Dispatch, Flat-Rate Pricebook & Membership API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A home-services contractor lives or dies on three intertwined numbers: how many technician-hours the day can absorb, what the homeowner is quoted before the van rolls, and how much of that quote the technician is allowed to change once they are standing in the basement. This document specifies the API that a service business uses to run that loop end to end — intake and attribution, booking, dispatch, quoting from a flat-rate pricebook, override review, completion with revenue accrual, membership sales with deferred revenue, and the marketing and coaching reporting built on top of the resulting history.

The system is deliberately built on a small number of high-leverage invariants. Dispatch capacity is a conserved quantity: every hour a branch commits to a job is returned, without drift, when that job finishes or is cancelled. Technician revenue is a ledger, not a running guess: completion accrues and cancellation of a completed job reverses through a compensating entry, so the technician's total lands exactly where it started. Deferred membership revenue is allocated across the visits the plan owes, and the allocation is a waterfall that sums back to the annual price. Each of these invariants is observable through the API and is asserted as a contract rather than left to convention.

Pricing is the second axis of complexity. A task's price is not a property of the job but of a *revision* of a pricebook entry, and revisions supersede their predecessors; a quote committed to a homeowner therefore has to name the revision it was drawn from, be re-derivable when the pricebook moves, and be frozen against further silent change once the technician is dispatched. When a technician wants to sell above the flat rate, the system lets them ask, bounds the answer by a computed ceiling, and holds the request behind optimistic concurrency so a manager never approves a price against an appointment snapshot the system has since revised. Sales conduct is measured as well as permitted: repeated rejected overrides accumulate strikes that move a technician through a parallel performance dimension, and incentive pay is computed from a pinned rounding order so the statement is reproducible cent for cent.

Time in this system is logical, not wall-clock. The server begins with its clock fixed at 2026-09-01T00:00:00Z and advances only when an operator sets it, which makes every service window, maintenance interval, overdue sweep and rolling revenue window deterministic and testable.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Requests carry two headers: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. A request is authenticated only when `X-User-Role` names a role declared in the RBAC matrix of this document *and* `X-User-ID` is present and non-empty; anything else — a missing `X-User-Role`, a missing `X-User-ID`, an empty `X-User-ID`, or a role string that this document does not declare — is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 401). Authenticated requests whose acting role is not permitted on the targeted endpoint are rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403).

Five roles exist, and their names are spelled exactly as follows: `Dispatcher`, `Technician`, `Manager`, `CustomerService`, and `PlatformAdmin`.

- A `Dispatcher` runs the dispatch board, books jobs, assigns technicians, and owns catalog operations.
- A `Technician` is a field technician who executes jobs, sells pricebook options, and files price override requests.
- A `Manager` approves or rejects price overrides, cancels agreements and appointments, and owns coaching.
- A `CustomerService` representative is a call-center operator who records calls and captures the marketing attribution of each intake.
- A `PlatformAdmin` is an operator who owns tenant-level configuration: branches, plans, campaigns, and the logical clock.

Two roles are backed by a persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `Dispatcher`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Technician`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Technician` compare the resource's `technician_id` field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Technician`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Technician`, and the same comparison applies — a technician acting as themselves is referenced by `technician_id` on the appointment they are completing.

The remaining three roles are not entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `Manager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `decided_by_user_id` on a `PricebookOverrideRequest` and as `coached_by_user_id` on a `CallRecord`, but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `CustomerService`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `cs_user_id` on a `CallRecord`, but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, recorded nowhere and never compared to any entity's `id`.

Role assignment is a segregation of duties, not a hierarchy. Endpoints restricted to a single role are exclusive to that role: catalog and pricebook creation, appointment booking, line addition, repricing, dispatching, membership payments and visit scheduling, and visit rescheduling belong to `Dispatcher`; appointment completion, override filing, and membership sales belong to `Technician`; override decisions, agreement cancellation, and coaching belong to `Manager`; intake call registration belongs to `CustomerService`; branch, plan, campaign, technician-hire, and clock operations belong to `PlatformAdmin`. Read endpoints are shared more liberally and the exact permitted set for each route is given in §4. No role inherits another role's exclusive operations.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` is the maximum number of rows returned and defaults to 10; its permitted range is 1 to 100 inclusive, so a `limit` below 1 or above 100 is not a valid request. `offset` is the number of rows skipped from the start of the result set and defaults to 0. When a list endpoint is called without either parameter, the caller receives the first ten rows of the result set.

List endpoints report their pagination state in the `meta` object using four fields: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`. `total_count` is the size of the full filtered result set before the page is cut, and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset + limit` is strictly less than `total_count`. The `data` field of a list response is an array and its `response_type` is `"array"`.

Unless a route states otherwise, list endpoints order their rows by `created_at` ascending. Two list endpoints in this system carry a different natural key and say so where they are described: the membership-visits collection on an agreement is ordered by `sequence_number` ascending and then by creation order, and the attribution history used for campaign arithmetic is ordered chronologically as described in §3.
### 2.4 Resource Identity, Logical Time and Mutable-State Bookkeeping

Every resource in this API carries a server-minted `id`. The identifier is a UUID and carries no type prefix — an appointment's id, a technician's id and a pricebook task revision's id are indistinguishable in form, and nothing in the contract depends on parsing one out of the string. Every resource also carries a `created_at` stamp recording when the row was first persisted, and `updated_at` recording the instant of its most recent mutation. Both timestamps are ISO 8601 UTC instants written with three fractional digits (for example `2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z`), and both are server-generated: a client never supplies them.

Mutation is versioned. Every resource exposes an integer `version` that begins at 1 when the row is created and is incremented by exactly 1 on each write that changes the row, including state transitions, side-effect-driven field updates, and recomputations. Two entity types are deliberately exception to this: an `AppointmentLine` and a `RevenueLedgerEntry` are append-mostly rows that carry only `id` and `created_at` — neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on them, and they are never used as the parent of an optimistic-concurrency check. All other entity types — `Branch`, `MembershipPlan`, `Campaign`, `PricebookTask`, `Technician`, `Appointment`, `PricebookOverrideRequest`, `MembershipAgreement`, `MembershipVisit` and `CallRecord` — carry the full quartet of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

Time is logical, and only logical. The server starts with its logical clock fixed at `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z` and never falls back to wall-clock time, so every service window, maintenance interval, overdue determination, deferred-allocation ordering and rolling revenue window is deterministic until an operator moves the clock. Only `PlatformAdmin` may move it, and moving it forward is not a passive read: the sweep rules described in §3 run as a consequence.

Computed fields are derived, not stored by hand. Fields reported as computed — branch capacity consumption, campaign attribution counters, technician hour rollups, ledger totals, appointment quote figures, deferred-revenue buckets, visit counts — are recalculated from their source rows whenever the source changes, and a reader of a resource always sees the reconciled value rather than a lagging copy.

### 2.5 Resource Hierarchy and Path Resolution

The URL space mirrors the ownership graph of the domain. Branch-scoped resources are addressed beneath their branch: a technician roster is reached through a branch, and an appointment row names the branch that owns the dispatch commitment through its `branch_id`. Appointment-scoped resources are addressed beneath their appointment: the pricebook lines of a job and the price override requests filed against it are both sub-resources of a single appointment. Membership is a two-level hierarchy: an agreement hangs off its plan in addressability but is owned by the branch that services it, and the maintenance visits of an agreement are addressed beneath that agreement. Intake calls are addressed beneath the campaign that owns their attribution and also listed under the campaign's call-records collection.

Path parameters are validated left to right, and the first failure short-circuits the request. When a request targets a nested resource, the outermost identifier is resolved first; an unparseable or unknown parent id is reported before any child id is even examined, so a request against a nonexistent appointment reports a missing appointment rather than a missing line. The rejection for any unresolved path parameter is `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

Some collections are deliberately reachable only through a parent rather than by a dedicated list route. A branch's technician roster is one such collection. A membership visit's `branch_id` is reachable through the branch's technician roster rather than through a dedicated list. Where an appointment references its technician, its campaign, its originating call or its agreement, those relationships are established by the identifier fields carried on the appointment payload and are resolved at request time rather than by a separate lookup route. Alongside the routed reads of §4, four further inert read endpoints exist and are open to any role that authenticates successfully: the pricebook revision lineage is readable as a whole at `GET /api/pricebook/tasks` and by revision id at `GET /api/pricebook/tasks/{id}`, a single membership agreement is readable at `GET /api/membership-agreements/{id}` (permitted to `Dispatcher`, `Manager` and `PlatformAdmin`, the same set as the agreement listing), and an appointment's settlement read model is readable at `GET /api/appointments/{id}/settlement`.

### 2.6 The Dispatch Day: Capacity as a Conserved Quantity

A service day is a budget of technician-hours, and the system treats the budget as an accounting identity rather than a soft limit. Each `Branch` declares a `daily_dispatch_capacity_hours`, defaulting to 80, which is the number of technician-hours that branch may consume in one dispatch day; it must be greater than zero. Each `Technician` declares a `daily_capacity_hours` ceiling on the estimated hours they personally may be dispatched in a dispatch day, and again the value must be greater than zero. A new technician is hired into a branch with `skill_codes`, at least one `shift_segments` element, a `postal_code` of exactly five digits, and a `status` defaulting to `AVAILABLE`.

Consumption is tracked on two live rollups. `Branch.capacity_consumed_hours` is the sum of the `estimated_hours` of that branch's appointments whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `DISPATCHED`. `Technician.scheduled_hours` is the sum of `estimated_hours` over that technician's appointments whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, and `Technician.dispatched_hours` is the sum of `estimated_hours` over that technician's appointments whose `dispatch_status` is `DISPATCHED`. Note that the branch rollup and the technician rollup key off different dimensions, so an appointment that is dispatched inflates `dispatched_hours` while an appointment merely booked inflates `scheduled_hours`, and the branch figure counts both.

The identity that must hold is this: committing hours adds to the rollups and releasing them returns the rollups to precisely their pre-commitment value. When a job is dispatched, the technician's dispatched hours rise by the appointment's `estimated_hours` and the branch's consumed hours rise by the same amount. When that job completes, or when a dispatched job is cancelled, both figures fall by exactly that amount, so after a dispatch and its paired completion or cancellation the branch's consumed hours and the technician's dispatched hours equal what they were immediately before the dispatch. No rounding is permitted to accumulate drift across the pair: hours are held at two decimal places, rounded half up.

### 2.7 Optimistic Concurrency on Repricing and Override Decisions

Two write operations are protected against stale client views by an expected-version contract, and only these two. Everything else in the API operates without a version precondition.

The first is repricing an appointment, addressed at `POST /appointments/{id}/reprice`. That request must carry `expected_pricebook_task_version` as a non-optional body field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `PricebookTask` revision the appointment's lines were quoted from. If the supplied value differs from the current `version` of any `PricebookTask` revision referenced by this appointment's lines — the check compares against every revision the lines are anchored to, so a mismatch on any one of them refuses the reprice — because the pricebook row has since advanced, the reprice is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), since the client's view of the revision's `flat_rate_cents` is stale and any recomputed quote would be anchored to a price the pricebook no longer publishes.

The second is deciding a price override request, addressed at `POST /override-requests/{id}/decide`. That request must carry `expected_appointment_version` as a non-optional body field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `Appointment`. If the supplied value differs from the appointment's current `version` — because a reprice or a line sale has since recomputed the appointment's `labor_base_cents` and its approvable ceiling — the decision is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), since the approval would no longer be bounded by the current ceiling.

These are staleness checks against a persisted version, not a mechanism that depends on requests overlapping in time. A client that has not observed the latest version of the parent is refused regardless of when it sends its request. No other endpoint in this API accepts an expected-version field.

Because the contract is a persisted-version comparison rather than a live lock, no request in this API is required to take a mutual-exclusion lock, and a reader is never asked to observe a `409` raised for a concurrent write alone; every rejection this document specifies is decided on the state of the rows at the moment the request is handled.

### 2.8 Money, Hours and Percentages: Precision and Rounding

Money is carried in integer cents throughout. `annual_price_cents`, `flat_rate_cents`, `sold_price_cents`, `quoted_price_cents`, `labor_base_cents`, `requested_price_cents`, `max_approved_price_cents`, `original_quote_cents`, `spend_cents`, `amount_cents` and the ledger amounts are all integers, and no monetary field is ever returned as a fractional number. Wherever a calculation divides money, the division is rounded once, half up, at the point the money value is materialised, and the rounded integer is what subsequent arithmetic consumes.

Hours are decimals held at two decimal places, rounded half up. `estimated_hours`, `daily_capacity_hours`, `daily_dispatch_capacity_hours`, `scheduled_hours`, `dispatched_hours` and `capacity_consumed_hours` are compared and summed at that precision.

Percentages are expressed either as integer basis-of-one fractions or as bounded decimals, according to the field. `included_labor_discount_pct` on a membership plan is a fraction bounded between 0 and 1 inclusive. The conversion rate reported for a campaign is a decimal held to three decimal places and rounded half *down* — that is, when the true ratio's fourth decimal digit is exactly five, the value is truncated downward rather than rounded upward. Percentages used inside threshold comparisons are compared exactly, without an intermediate rounding step, unless the rule naming them says otherwise.

### 2.9 Enumeration Vocabulary

The following value sets are exhaustive and their spellings are part of the contract. A membership plan's `status`, and a pricebook task revision's `status`, are each one of `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED` or `RETIRED`; a plan is `DRAFT` until published. A pricebook task's `category` is one of `HVAC`, `PLUMBING`, `ELECTRICAL`, `APPLIANCE` or `GENERAL`. A campaign's `channel` is one of `SEARCH`, `YARD_SIGN`, `REFERRAL`, `DIRECT_MAIL` or `ORGANIC`.

A technician carries two independent enumerations. The availability dimension `status` is one of `AVAILABLE`, `ON_SHIFT`, `ON_JOB` or `OFF_SHIFT`, defaulting to `AVAILABLE`. The parallel performance dimension `performance_status` is one of `CLEAR`, `COACHING` or `PROBATION`, defaulting to `CLEAR`, and the grade produced by the performance review is one of `DOES_NOT_MEET`, `MEETS`, `EXCEEDS` or `OUTSTANDING`, defaulting to `DOES_NOT_MEET`. The two dimensions never interact: an availability change leaves the performance dimension untouched and a strike-driven performance change leaves availability untouched.

An appointment carries three parallel dimensions. The lifecycle dimension `status` is one of `SCHEDULED`, `DISPATCHED`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`, defaulting to `SCHEDULED`. The capacity dimension `dispatch_status` is one of `UNASSIGNED`, `DISPATCHED`, `IN_SERVICE` or `RELEASED`, defaulting to `UNASSIGNED`. The pricing dimension `pricing_status` is one of `NOT_QUOTED`, `AUTO_QUOTED`, `OVERRIDE_PENDING`, `QUOTE_APPROVED` or `QUOTE_REJECTED`, defaulting to `NOT_QUOTED`.

An appointment line's `status` is one of `PROPOSED`, `SOLD` or `DECLINED`, defaulting to `PROPOSED`. Its `option_tier` is one of `GOOD`, `BETTER` or `BEST`, or null when the task is sold without a tier. A price override request's `status` is one of `PENDING`, `APPROVED` or `REJECTED`, defaulting to `PENDING`. A membership agreement's `status` is one of `PENDING_PAYMENT`, `ACTIVE`, `OVERDUE` or `CANCELLED`, defaulting to `PENDING_PAYMENT`, and its `term_months` is either 12 or 24, defaulting to 12. A membership visit's `status` is one of `SCHEDULED`, `COMPLETED` or `MISSED`, defaulting to `SCHEDULED`. A call record's `coaching_status` is one of `UNREVIEWED`, `COACHING_ASSIGNED` or `REVIEWED`, defaulting to `UNREVIEWED`. A revenue ledger entry's `entry_type` is either `ACCRUAL` or `REVERSAL`.

### 2.10 Request Validation Precedence

Rules do not fire in the order they happen to be described in this document; they fire in a fixed precedence, and when two rules could both reject the same request the lower-numbered one is the one the caller sees. Understanding this ordering matters because several rules guard the same endpoint and a caller that fixes only the reported problem may still be rejected by the next rule in line.

Authentication holds first. A request whose role header is absent, whose user header is absent or empty, or whose role is not declared in the RBAC matrix is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 401) before anything else, and a request from an authenticated but unpermitted role is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403) before any domain rule runs. Resource existence comes next: an endpoint carrying a path parameter resolves it before evaluating anything else, reporting `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) for an unknown resource, and — as §2.5 sets out — resolving outer identifiers before inner ones.

State legality follows existence, and it is where the ordering becomes domain-specific. A request that asks for a transition the target entity's current state does not declare is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) ahead of any business rule. Only after the state is known to be legal do the business guards run, and they run in this order: the appointment-lines-present guard on dispatching (`ERR_NO_APPOINTMENT_LINES`, HTTP 409), then the already-pending override guard (`ERR_OVERRIDE_ALREADY_PENDING`, HTTP 409), then the branch window collision guard (`ERR_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, HTTP 409), then the window-ordering guard (`ERR_WINDOW_INVALID`, HTTP 400), then the maintenance-interval overlap guard on rescheduling (`ERR_VISIT_OVERLAP`, HTTP 409).

Referential and constraint guards close out the middle band, again in a fixed order: technician skill availability (`ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TECHNICIAN`, HTTP 422) precedes the appointment's membership-visit sequence guard (`ERR_MEMBER_VISIT_SEQUENCE_INVALID`, HTTP 422), which precedes the published-revision guard on adding a line (`ERR_PRICEBOOK_REVISION_NOT_PUBLISHED`, HTTP 422), which precedes the technician capacity guard (`ERR_TECHNICIAN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 422), which precedes the duplicate visit sequence guard (`ERR_DUPLICATE_VISIT_SEQUENCE`, HTTP 409), which precedes the term entitlement guard (`ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_TERM_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 422), which precedes the override price ceiling (`ERR_OVERRIDE_EXCEEDS_LIMIT`, HTTP 422), which precedes the discount floor (`ERR_DISCOUNT_FLOOR_REACHED`, HTTP 422), which precedes the over-collection guard on payments (`ERR_OVER_COLLECTION`, HTTP 422), which precedes the membership cancellation guard (`ERR_MEMBERSHIP_CANCEL_BLOCKED`, HTTP 409), which precedes the linked-agreement status guard (`ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, HTTP 409), which precedes the completion-description quality gate (`ERR_DESCRIPTION_TOO_SHORT`, HTTP 422), which precedes the call reviewability guard (`ERR_CALL_NOT_REVIEWABLE`, HTTP 409).

Two exceptions sit outside this ordering because they are enforced on different grounds. The repricing stale-version check (`ERR_STALE_VERSION`, HTTP 412) is evaluated whenever a reprice is attempted against lines whose revision has moved, and the override-decision stale-version check (`ERR_STALE_VERSION`, HTTP 412) is evaluated whenever a decision is attempted against an appointment that has since advanced. Both are enforced as a precondition of their own operation rather than as a rank in the business-guard sequence. For the override decision specifically, that precondition is examined ahead of the request's own state legality: the appointment's current version, and the request's frozen original quote, are both compared before the request's `PENDING` status is tested, so a decision carrying a stale version or a superseded quote reports `ERR_STALE_VERSION` rather than `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Finally, the segregation-of-duties check on voiding a completed appointment's revenue (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, HTTP 403) is an authorization decision and is enforced on that basis. A request with a missing or unparseable required body field is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) when its field set cannot be understood at all.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

This section is organised by business process rather than by entity: intake and attribution, then the pricebook lineage, then the dispatch day, then money — quoting, overrides, settlement, membership and marketing. Each subsection states the rules a reader needs to implement that process, including the guards it shares with neighbouring processes. Rules are deliberately not collected into a single validation block; a guard appears with the workflow it protects, and §2.10 supplies the precedence that orders them when more than one could apply.

### 3.1 Standing Up the Business: Branches, Technicians, Plans and Campaigns

Everything in this system is anchored to a branch. A `PlatformAdmin` creates one by supplying a `name`, which must be non-empty, and a `postal_code` that must be exactly five digits — the branch's postal code is not decoration, it is the geographic anchor used by technician location matching and by the agreement-territory rule in §3.8. An optional `daily_dispatch_capacity_hours`, defaulting to 80, sets the technician-hours the branch may consume in one dispatch day and must be greater than zero. A branch is born with its `capacity_consumed_hours` and its `membership_count_active` both at 0, and it carries the standard identity quartet described in §2.4.

Technicians are hired into a branch by a `PlatformAdmin`, who supplies a `name`, a five-digit `postal_code` describing where the technician works, a `daily_capacity_hours` ceiling above zero, at least one `shift_segments` element, and the `skill_codes` the technician holds as an optional list defaulting to an empty array. Each shift segment is a pair of a `start_utc` and an `end_utc`, and the end must be strictly later than the start; a segment with an end at or before its start is not a valid shift. Skill codes are uppercase strings. The technician also carries an explicit `home_branch_id` naming the branch they report to, and an optional `status` defaulting to `AVAILABLE`. A new hire starts with `scheduled_hours` and `dispatched_hours` at 0, `sales_coaching_strike_count` at 0, `performance_status` at `CLEAR`, and `technician_revenue_ledger_cents` at 0. The roster of a branch is reachable through the branch itself rather than by a top-level list, as §2.5 notes.

Membership plans are catalogue entries created by a `PlatformAdmin` before any agreement can be sold. A plan takes a `name`, an `annual_price_cents` for a twelve-month agreement that must be greater than zero, an `included_service_visit_count` of maintenance visits owed per agreement year that must be at least 1, a `maintenance_visit_interval_days` of calendar days between consecutive visits that must be at least 1, and an `included_labor_discount_pct` expressed as a fraction bounded between 0 and 1 inclusive that discounts labour on appointments booked under a plan of this type. An optional `status` defaults to `DRAFT`, and a plan moves between `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED` and `RETIRED`; only a `PUBLISHED` plan can be sold (§3.8). A plan's `maintenance_visit_interval_days` and `included_service_visit_count` are not merely descriptive — they define the shape of the visit obligation and the bucket count of the deferred-revenue waterfall in §3.8.

Marketing campaigns are created by a `PlatformAdmin`, capturing a `name` and a `channel` drawn from `SEARCH`, `YARD_SIGN`, `REFERRAL`, `DIRECT_MAIL` or `ORGANIC`. A `spend_cents` figure is optional and defaults to 0; it may not be negative. Everything the campaign will later be judged by is derived rather than supplied, so a freshly created campaign reports `attributed_job_count` of 0, `attributed_revenue_cents` of 0 and a `conversion_rate` of null, all three of which are recomputed from attribution history and intake calls as described in §3.3 and §3.12.

Tenant-level configuration also includes the logical clock itself. A `PlatformAdmin` sets it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, and the operation is not a passive assignment: moving the clock forward triggers the overdue sweep whose three cascading effects are specified in §3.9. This is the only way time advances in the system, and its determinism is what makes windows, intervals and rolling windows reproducible.

### 3.2 The Pricebook: Revisions that Supersede

A flat-rate pricebook is not a list of prices but a lineage of revisions. A `Dispatcher` creates a task revision by supplying a `code`, a `category` from `HVAC`, `PLUMBING`, `ELECTRICAL`, `APPLIANCE` or `GENERAL`, a homeowner-facing `display_name`, a `required_skill_code`, an `estimated_hours` greater than zero, a `flat_rate_cents` of at least zero, an optional `options` array defaulting to an empty list, and an optional `status` defaulting to `DRAFT`. The `code` and the `required_skill_code` must be uppercase strings drawn from A–Z, 0–9 and dashes — the code is the shared identity of a logical task across all of its revisions, and the required skill code is what the dispatch skill clause in §3.5 tests against.

A revision's `revision` number is assigned by the server, never supplied. The first row to carry a given `code` receives revision 1, and every later row carrying the same code receives one greater than the highest revision already recorded for that code. That makes the lineage monotone: revisions never reuse a number and never skip backwards, so "the current revision of a code" is always a comparison of integers. A revision's `published_at_utc` is a computed stamp recording the logical clock reading at which this revision first became `PUBLISHED`; while the revision is `DRAFT` the field is null, and it is not retroactively backfilled when a different revision of the same code is published later.

Option tiers are the presentation layer of a task and are carried inline rather than in a table of their own. The `options` array holds one element per tier the task offers, and each element carries a `tier`, a `label`, a `price_cents`, an `included_materials_cents` and a `warranty_months`. The `tier` must be one of `GOOD`, `BETTER` or `BEST`, and a task may carry at most one element per tier — two elements claiming the same tier on the same revision is not a valid task definition. A task whose options array is empty is simply sold at its base rate with no tier choice.

The selection of which revision a quote is drawn from is the `pricebook_revision_selection` calculation, a fixed ranking, and it is the same rule both when a line is added and when an appointment is repriced. The candidate pool is every `PricebookTask` row whose `code` equals the requested code and whose `status` is `PUBLISHED`; rows that are `DRAFT` or `RETIRED` never enter the pool, however recent they are. The pool is ranked by `revision` descending first, then by `published_at_utc` ascending, then by creation order ascending, and the first row in that ranking is the one selected. In practice the descending-revision key dominates and the second key only matters between rows that share a revision number, but all three keys are part of the contract. When the pool is empty — the code has never been published, or every row carrying it has been retired — the request is refused with `ERR_PRICEBOOK_REVISION_NOT_PUBLISHED` (HTTP 422).

### 3.3 Intake and Booking: Attribution Captured at the Source

A booking is where a homeowner's problem becomes a dispatchable commitment, and it is also where the system freezes where that job came from. A `Dispatcher` books an appointment by supplying the owning `branch_id`, the required `customer_name`, a five-digit `postal_code` describing the service location, and the committed service window as a `window_start_utc` together with a `window_end_utc` that must be strictly later than the start. The window is treated as half-open — from its start inclusive to its end exclusive — and that convention is what the collision rules below test.

Four optional identifiers travel with the booking. A `campaign_id` attributes the job to a marketing source. A `call_record_id` names the intake call that produced it. A `membership_agreement_id` links the job to a membership agreement the homeowner holds. And a `membership_visit_sequence_number`, defaulting to null, names which maintenance visit of that agreement the job fulfils. When a call record is named without an explicit `campaign_id`, the appointment inherits the call's campaign as its own attribution source, and the referenced call record's `attributed_appointment_id` is set to the new appointment's id, so the booking is what ties a recorded call to the work it generated. The appointment's attribution source is immutable after creation: `campaign_id` is captured at booking and is not modified by any later operation, which is what makes the campaign arithmetic in §3.12 a stable history rather than a moving target.

Two temporal guards run at booking, and the collision test is evaluated ahead of the ordering test: when a request would violate both, the booking is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409) rather than `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID`. The collision test reads a reversed window as the span between its two endpoints, so a window whose end is not strictly later than its start is still checked for a collision before the ordering rule is consulted. When the supplied `window_end_utc` is not strictly later than `window_start_utc` and no such collision exists, the booking is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_INVALID` (HTTP 400). When the new appointment's half-open interval collides with the identically shaped interval of any other appointment on the same branch whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `DISPATCHED`, the booking is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409); appointments that are `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` no longer occupy the branch's calendar, so a slot they vacated is free again. The comparison is of intervals on the same branch — two branches may book the same wall-clock slot without conflict.

Two referential guards run alongside them. When a `membership_agreement_id` is supplied, the referenced agreement must belong to the same branch as the appointment — its `branch_id` must equal the appointment's `branch_id` — and its `status` must be `PENDING_PAYMENT` or `ACTIVE`; anything else, including an `OVERDUE` or `CANCELLED` agreement, is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). And when a `membership_visit_sequence_number` is supplied, it must name an actual `MembershipVisit` on the linked agreement, and that visit's `status` must not already be `COMPLETED`; a sequence number that matches no visit, or one whose visit is already finished, is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_VISIT_SEQUENCE_INVALID` (HTTP 422).

A successful booking establishes the appointment's three parallel starting values and nothing else. Its `status` begins `SCHEDULED`, its `dispatch_status` begins `UNASSIGNED` and its `pricing_status` begins `NOT_QUOTED`. Its `technician_id` is null because dispatch has not happened, its `current_pricebook_version_id` is null and its `repriced_at` is null because no lines exist, its `quoted_price_cents` is 0 and its `authorization_required` is false. Its `estimated_hours` is 0 for the same reason. `CANCELLED` and `COMPLETED` are terminal for the lifecycle dimension; a terminal appointment occupies no branch calendar and carries no technician commitment. The branch's `capacity_consumed_hours` is recomputed, and the appointment's version is incremented.

Booked appointments are read back through a single listing that accepts an optional `branch_id`, `technician_id`, `status`, `campaign_id`, `membership_agreement_id` or `call_record_id` filter alongside the standard `limit` and `offset` paging parameters, and through a single-appointment read that reports all three of the appointment's parallel state dimensions at once. The line collection of an appointment is separately listable with `limit` and `offset`, and an appointment's override requests are listable as one of its own collections.

### 3.4 Quoting the Job: Lines, the Household Load Trace and Repricing

Before a job can go anywhere it has to carry work. A `Dispatcher` adds a line to an appointment by supplying the pricebook `pricebook_task_code`, an optional `option_tier` and an optional `status` defaulting to `PROPOSED`. The line is quoted against the highest published revision of the requested code, selected by the ranking rule in §3.2; if no published revision of that code exists the addition is refused with `ERR_PRICEBOOK_REVISION_NOT_PUBLISHED` (HTTP 422). The line records which revision it was drawn from as its `pricebook_task_id`, capturing that revision's `flat_rate_cents` as its own `flat_rate_cents` at add time. Its `sold_price_cents` is derived: when `option_tier` is set it takes the `price_cents` of the matching option element on the referenced revision, and when no tier is set it takes the line's own `flat_rate_cents`. The line belongs to the appointment named in `appointment_id`.

Every line addition is a load event on the household, and the system traces the accumulating load hop by hop. The `household_load_propagation` calculation produces the appointment's `load_units_trace`, a sequence with one entry per contributing line, recomputed from scratch whenever a line is added so that the trace always reflects the current set of published lines. Only lines whose referenced revision is `PUBLISHED` contribute; a line whose revision is `DRAFT` or `RETIRED` is skipped entirely, contributing nothing to the trace and nothing to `estimated_hours`. Contributing lines are taken in order of the sequence in which the appointment's payload carried them, and ties are broken by the task's `code` ascending.

The propagation itself has a pinned evaluation order, and the trace makes that order observable. The running load starts at zero. For each hop in turn, the increment size is chosen from a bucket keyed on the running total *as it stood before this hop was added*: while that pre-hop total is below 500 the increment is 300, while it is at least 500 but below 1500 the increment is 600, and once it is 1500 or more the increment is 900. Then the hop's own task category scales the increment: an `APPLIANCE` task contributes one hundred and fifteen percent of the bucket increment, and every other category contributes one hundred percent of it. The scaled increment is added to the running total, and the new total is recorded as this hop's trace entry. Because the bucket is read from the pre-hop total, the trace does not advance a bucket on the hop that crosses the boundary; it advances on the following hop. A five-hop appointment made entirely of non-appliance tasks therefore traces 300, 600, 1200, 1800 and 2700. Selecting the bucket after adding the hop's own increment would instead trace 300, 900, 2400, 4200 and 8100 — an implementation that produces that sequence has inverted the evaluation order and is wrong. Every hop's increment is an exact integer, because each bucket increment is divisible by the percent denominators used, so no rounding arises anywhere in the trace.

Two appointment-level rollups follow from the lines. `estimated_hours` is the sum of the referenced revisions' `estimated_hours` over the appointment's lines whose revision is `PUBLISHED`, and is 0 while no such line exists. `labor_base_cents` is the sum of the lines' `flat_rate_cents` over lines whose `status` is `SOLD`. Adding a line also refreshes the appointment's `current_pricebook_version_id` to the id of the highest-revision task referenced by any of its lines, null while no lines exist, and increments the appointment's `version`. Later a line becomes `SOLD` when it is added with that status or when it is re-added under a `SOLD` status; no separate sale operation exists, and a reprice is the only transition away from `SOLD` that this document specifies.

A committed quote can be re-drawn when the pricebook moves underneath it. Repricing is an explicit `Dispatcher` action against an appointment that is still `SCHEDULED`, and it exists precisely because a revision may have been superseded since the quote was frozen. The `SCHEDULED` requirement is tested first: a reprice attempted against an appointment whose `status` is not `SCHEDULED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) on that ground, ahead of the stale-version check, so a request that is both out of state and carrying a stale version reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The request must carry `expected_pricebook_task_version`, the client's last observed version of the revision the lines were quoted from (§2.7), and may optionally name a `pricebook_task_id`; a supplied value that differs from the current version of a revision referenced by the appointment's lines is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) before any recalculation happens. The operation also only does something when the pricebook has genuinely moved: at least one of the appointment's lines must reference a task whose `code` now carries another row at a strictly higher revision whose `status` is `PUBLISHED`.

When a reprice succeeds, every line's `flat_rate_cents` is reset to the `flat_rate_cents` of the highest published revision of its code, and every line that carries an option has its `sold_price_cents` recomputed from that revision's option price and its `status` reset to `PROPOSED` — a re-quote invalidates a prior sale of a superseded price. The appointment's `quoted_price_cents` is recomputed from the new labour base under the same discount rule that governs the automatic quote in §3.5, and `authorization_required` is recomputed against the same ceiling. The appointment's `repriced_at` is stamped with the logical clock reading, and `current_pricebook_version_id` is set to the highest-revision task's id. Repricing bumps the version of the `PricebookTask` revision as well as the appointment's own version, because the revision — or rather the code's head — has been touched. It is the revision each line is re-anchored to that is advanced, not merely the single highest-revision task: when a reprice re-quotes lines spanning more than one code, every head revision those lines are re-pointed at has its version bumped.

### 3.5 The Dispatch Board: Eligibility, Matching and the Automatic Quote

Dispatching commits a technician to a job, and the system chooses that technician rather than letting the caller name one. A `Dispatcher` dispatches an appointment by naming only the `appointment_id`; everything else — the technician, the hours, the quote — follows from rules. The appointment must be `SCHEDULED` and its `dispatch_status` must be `UNASSIGNED` or `RELEASED`, and it must carry at least one line; an appointment with no lines has `estimated_hours` of 0 and is not dispatchable, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_NO_APPOINTMENT_LINES` (HTTP 409).

The `technician_eligibility_filter` calculation narrows the roster in two stages. First it builds the candidate pool from every `Technician` whose availability `status` is `AVAILABLE` or `ON_SHIFT`, whose `scheduled_hours` is strictly below their `daily_capacity_hours`, and for whom the addition of this appointment's `estimated_hours` to their `scheduled_hours` would still be at or below their `daily_capacity_hours`. The availability test admits a technician already `ON_SHIFT` on another job — it is not a filter that keeps only idle technicians, and the load-levelling order below is what spreads the day's work across busy ones — while a technician who is `ON_JOB` or `OFF_SHIFT` is not a candidate. A technician who is already at or over their personal ceiling is not merely deprioritised; they are not a candidate. Second, it applies four clauses to each candidate, and a candidate must satisfy all four:

- the shift clause: at least one of the technician's `shift_segments` must genuinely overlap the appointment window, meaning the segment's end is strictly later than `window_start_utc` and the segment's start is strictly before `window_end_utc`. The overlap test is strict, so a shift that ends exactly at the appointment's window start does not overlap and the technician is ineligible.
- the skill clause: the technician's `skill_codes` must contain every `required_skill_code` demanded by the appointment's lines whose referenced revision is `PUBLISHED`. One missing code disqualifies the candidate outright — this is a conjunction, not a scoring average.
- the location clause: the first three characters of the technician's `postal_code` must equal the first three characters of the appointment's `postal_code`. Matching is by the leading three-character prefix, which is the system's proxy for a compatible service territory.
- the branch clause: the appointment's `branch_id` must equal the technician's `home_branch_id`.

When no candidate at all holds every required skill code of the appointment's published task revisions, the dispatch is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TECHNICIAN` (HTTP 422). When candidates pass the skill test but the technician that would otherwise be selected already has `scheduled_hours` at or above their `daily_capacity_hours`, the dispatch is refused with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). A technician who was released from this appointment is excluded from its later dispatches, so a returned job is not simply re-offered to the person who was pulled off it.

Among the eligible technicians the selection is deterministic, and the ordering is by the technician's current `scheduled_hours` ascending, then their `dispatched_hours` ascending, then their `postal_code` ascending, then their creation order ascending; the first in that order is selected. The effect is deliberate load-levelling: the least-booked eligible technician takes the job, and among equally booked technicians the one with the fewest hours on the road takes it. Hours are compared at two decimal places, rounded half up.

A successful dispatch writes a single coherent commitment. The appointment's `technician_id` becomes the selected technician's id, its `status` becomes `DISPATCHED` and its `dispatch_status` becomes `DISPATCHED`. Its `estimated_hours` is recomputed as the sum of the referenced revisions' `estimated_hours` over its lines whose revision is `PUBLISHED`. The pricing dimension moves from `NOT_QUOTED` to `AUTO_QUOTED` — the dispatch assigns `AUTO_QUOTED` regardless of the dimension's prior value, so a job dispatched while an override request was still pending leaves that pricing state behind — and the automatic labour quote is committed to the homeowner.

That quote — `quoted_price_cents` — is the appointment's `labor_base_cents` multiplied by one minus a discount rate, with the product rounded half up to a whole number of cents. The discount rate is normally zero, but when the appointment names a `membership_agreement_id` whose agreement's `status` is `ACTIVE`, the rate becomes half of that agreement's plan's `included_labor_discount_pct`. Half, not the whole figure, and the halving applies at quote time to the money, not to the stored percentage. Alongside it, `authorization_required` is set true exactly when the committed quote exceeds one and twenty hundredths of the appointment's `labor_base_cents`; the quote is frozen after dispatch and does not silently follow later pricebook changes.

The dispatch also moves the capacity ledger. The selected technician's `status` becomes `ON_SHIFT` and their `dispatched_hours` rises by the appointment's `estimated_hours`; the branch's `capacity_consumed_hours` rises by the same amount. Both figures must return exactly to their pre-dispatch values when this job later completes or is cancelled (see §3.7 and §2.6). Finally, the dispatch bumps the version of the `PricebookTask` revision and the appointment's own version, reflecting that both rows have been touched by the operation. As with repricing, the revisions advanced are the ones the appointment's lines are actually anchored to — the distinct revisions referenced by its lines, not merely a single highest-revision task.

### 3.6 Selling Above the Flat Rate: Override Requests and Manager Decisions

A technician standing in a basement sometimes needs to sell above the flat rate, and the system permits it only through a bounded, reviewable channel. A `Technician` files a `PricebookOverrideRequest` against an appointment by supplying `requested_price_cents`, which must be strictly greater than zero. The appointment must be in `SCHEDULED` or `DISPATCHED` status. Only one unresolved request may exist per appointment: filing while another request on the same appointment still has `status` of `PENDING` is refused with `ERR_OVERRIDE_ALREADY_PENDING` (HTTP 409). This guard is what makes re-filing after a rejection possible while a re-filing over a live request is impossible.

The filing captures a snapshot of what the technician is competing against. The request's `original_quote_cents` records the appointment's `quoted_price_cents` at submission. Its `max_approved_price_cents` is the appointment's `labor_base_cents` multiplied by one and twenty-five hundredths and then by one and ten hundredths — a twenty-five percent technician markup on top of a further ten percent of headroom — with the product truncated to an integer number of cents. Truncated, not rounded: a fraction of a cent is discarded downward. The new request carries `status` of `PENDING` and a `decided_by_user_id` of null, since no manager has ruled on it, and the appointment's `pricing_status` moves to `OVERRIDE_PENDING`.

Filing is legal from more than one pricing state, and the system treats re-filing uniformly. A request may be filed when the appointment is `AUTO_QUOTED`, moving it to `OVERRIDE_PENDING`; it may be filed while the appointment already sits at `OVERRIDE_PENDING`, in which case the second filing is a self-transition that leaves the dimension where it was and is permitted only when no request is outstanding — the guard is the pending-request rule above, not a state rejection. And a request may be filed when the appointment is `QUOTE_REJECTED`, moving it back to `OVERRIDE_PENDING`: a refused quote is not a dead end and may be re-submitted. In every case the appointment's version is incremented.

A `Manager` decides the request by supplying a `decision` and `expected_appointment_version`, and the decision is what makes the ceiling enforceable. Requests whose `status` is not `PENDING` have no legal transition to decide from and are refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The supplied `expected_appointment_version` carries the manager's last observed version of the parent appointment; if it differs from the appointment's current version because a reprice or a line sale has since recomputed the appointment's `labor_base_cents` and its approvable ceiling, the decision is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) — an approval computed against a superseded snapshot would not be bounded by the current ceiling (§2.7). The appointment also must not have been repriced since the request was filed: the appointment's current `quoted_price_cents` must still equal the request's `original_quote_cents`, and a mismatch is likewise refused as a stale decision.

On approval the ceiling binds first. If the request's `requested_price_cents` exceeds its computed `max_approved_price_cents`, the approval is refused with `ERR_OVERRIDE_EXCEEDS_LIMIT` (HTTP 422). Otherwise the request's `status` becomes `APPROVED` and its `decided_by_user_id` becomes the acting `X-User-ID`; the appointment's `quoted_price_cents` is replaced by the request's `requested_price_cents`; the appointment's `pricing_status` becomes `QUOTE_APPROVED`; `authorization_required` is recomputed against the one-point-two ceiling; and the appointment's version is incremented.

On rejection the consequence falls on the technician, not the price. The request's `status` becomes `REJECTED` and its `decided_by_user_id` again records the acting `X-User-ID`, the appointment's `pricing_status` becomes `QUOTE_REJECTED`, and the acting technician's `sales_coaching_strike_count` is incremented by one. That counter drives the parallel performance dimension: the technician's `performance_status` becomes `COACHING` once the strike count reaches two, and becomes `PROBATION` once it reaches three. Because the count is cumulative over all rejected decisions on this technician's appointments, the strikes persist after the appointment that earned them is long finished, and the performance dimension is entirely independent of the technician's availability `status`.

### 3.7 Closing the Job: Two-Call Completion, Accrual and the Description Gate

Completion is deliberately a two-call flow, and the intermediate state exists so that "the van is on site" is observable. A `Technician` completes an appointment by supplying a `completion_description`. The first call against a `DISPATCHED` appointment moves only the capacity dimension: the appointment's `dispatch_status` becomes `IN_SERVICE` and the technician's `status` becomes `ON_JOB`, while the job's `status` remains `DISPATCHED`. The second call against the same appointment finishes the work, because the appointment's `status` is still `DISPATCHED` and its `dispatch_status` is still `DISPATCHED` for the purposes of the completion transition; the first call's move to `IN_SERVICE` does not itself complete anything. A call against an appointment whose status or dispatch status is not in a state from which the transition is declared is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The transition is declared from exactly two positions: a `DISPATCHED` appointment whose `dispatch_status` is `DISPATCHED` (the first call) or whose `dispatch_status` is `IN_SERVICE` (the second call).

The second call must carry a substantive `completion_description`, and the bar is computed from the job's size rather than fixed. The `appointment_description_quality` calculation fixes the required word count as the larger of ten words and five percent of the appointment's `estimated_hours` expressed in minutes, taken as an integer ceiling — that is, the number of minutes the job was estimated to take, multiplied by five hundredths and rounded up to a whole number of words, with a floor of ten. A `completion_description` that is absent counts as zero supplied words. If the supplied word count is below that requirement the completion is refused with `ERR_DESCRIPTION_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 422). The gate is inclusive at the boundary: a description with exactly the required number of words passes.

The `discount_floor_check` calculation is evaluated on the same operation, and it protects margin rather than thoroughness. The check forms a denominator from the sum of `flat_rate_cents` over the appointment's lines whose `status` is `SOLD`, and a numerator from the appointment's `quoted_price_cents`. The effective discount is the denominator minus the numerator, divided by the denominator. When that fraction is strictly greater than twenty hundredths — a discount of more than twenty percent — the completion is refused with `ERR_DISCOUNT_FLOOR_REACHED` (HTTP 422). A discount of exactly twenty percent passes, and an appointment whose denominator is zero leaves the effective discount at zero and passes the check. The comparison is made exactly, with no rounding of the fraction beforehand.

When both gates pass, the second call settles the job comprehensively. The appointment's `status` becomes `COMPLETED` and its `dispatch_status` becomes `RELEASED`. The technician's `status` returns to `AVAILABLE` and their `dispatched_hours` falls by the appointment's `estimated_hours`; the branch's `capacity_consumed_hours` falls by the same amount, so the dispatch-day ledger returns to its pre-dispatch position (§2.6). For every line on the appointment whose `status` is `SOLD`, a `RevenueLedgerEntry` is appended: its `entry_type` is `ACCRUAL`, its `amount_cents` is that line's `sold_price_cents` as a positive addition, its `technician_id` is the completing technician, its `appointment_id` is this appointment, and its `recorded_at_utc` is the logical clock reading. Its `running_total_cents` is the technician's `technician_revenue_ledger_cents` immediately after the entry is appended, and that ledger total advances by the same amount. The appointment's version is incremented.

If the appointment carries a `membership_visit_sequence_number`, the completion also fulfils a maintenance visit. The `MembershipVisit` on the linked agreement whose `sequence_number` equals the appointment's `membership_visit_sequence_number` and whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` transitions to `COMPLETED`, taking this appointment's id as its `completed_appointment_id` and stamping `completed_at_utc` with the logical clock reading. The agreement's `visits_fulfilled_count` rises by one, its `revenue_recognized_cents` is recomputed by the `membership_revenue_recognition` calculation described in §3.8, and its `unearned_revenue_balance_cents` is reset to its `annual_price_cents` minus that recognised figure.

### 3.8 Membership: Sales, Down Payments, Visit Entitlement and the Revenue Waterfall

A membership agreement is sold by a `Technician`, who supplies `membership_plan_id`, `branch_id`, `homeowner_name`, `homeowner_postal_code` and an optional `term_months` defaulting to 12. Two conditions gate the sale. The referenced plan must have `status` of `PUBLISHED` — a `DRAFT` or `RETIRED` plan is not saleable — and the referenced branch must exist and its `postal_code` must equal the supplied `homeowner_postal_code` exactly, since the agreement's service territory is pinned to its branch. A homeowner postal code that does not match the branch's is not a soft mismatch; it is a refusal.

The sale is a snapshot. The agreement's `annual_price_cents` captures the plan's `annual_price_cents` at that moment and is frozen for the life of the agreement, so a later plan price change does not re-price existing agreements. The agreement's `homeowner_name` is required and its `homeowner_postal_code` must be exactly five digits. Its `collected_amount_cents`, `revenue_recognized_cents` and `unearned_revenue_balance_cents` all begin at zero, its `visits_fulfilled_count` begins at zero, and its `status` begins at `PENDING_PAYMENT` — the sale is not the activation. The branch's `membership_count_active` is recomputed, and the agreement's version is incremented.

Activation is driven by money. A `Dispatcher` records a payment by supplying `amount_cents`, which must be strictly greater than zero, and the agreement's `collected_amount_cents` is incremented by it. The agreement only becomes `ACTIVE` once `collected_amount_cents` has reached thirty percent of its `annual_price_cents`, that threshold being the plan's thirty-percent down payment computed with the product rounded half up to a whole number of cents; a first payment below it leaves the agreement at `PENDING_PAYMENT` and simply accumulates. When the threshold is met, the agreement's `status` becomes `ACTIVE` and its `agreement_start_utc` is stamped with the logical clock reading — that instant anchors the alive window used by visit intervals. Any payment that would push `collected_amount_cents` beyond the agreement's `annual_price_cents` is refused with `ERR_OVER_COLLECTION` (HTTP 422); the collected total may equal the annual price but never exceed it. Activation also triggers the deferred-revenue waterfall described below, and the branch's `membership_count_active` is recomputed.

Maintenance visits are the plan's obligations. A `Dispatcher` schedules one by supplying a `sequence_number` and a `scheduled_date`. The sequence number is a 1-based position in the agreement's service sequence and must be at least 1; it must not already be present on the agreement, and a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VISIT_SEQUENCE` (HTTP 409). It must not exceed the agreement's total visit entitlement, which is the plan's `included_service_visit_count` multiplied by the agreement's `term_months` and divided by twelve; a sequence number beyond that is refused with `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_TERM_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). The parent agreement may be `PENDING_PAYMENT`, `ACTIVE` or `OVERDUE` when a visit is scheduled. A new visit begins at `SCHEDULED`. The agreement's version is incremented and its buckets are redistributed.

Deferred revenue is allocated across visits by the `deferred_revenue_recognition_waterfall` calculation, and the allocation must sum back to the annual price. The number of buckets is the plan's `included_service_visit_count` multiplied by the agreement's `term_months` and divided by twelve. Each bucket receives the agreement's `annual_price_cents` divided by that bucket count using whole-number division, and the remainder of that division is distributed one cent at a time to the first buckets in order. The ordering that defines "first" is by the visit's `sequence_number` ascending and then by creation order ascending; the bucket index is a visit's *position* in that ordering, not its sequence number, so a gap in numbering does not shift the distribution. Concretely: for the visit at position i in the ordering, its `deferred_revenue_cents` is the base share plus one cent when i is less than the remainder and the base share alone otherwise. The invariant the allocation must satisfy is that once the agreement carries exactly as many visits as there are buckets, their `deferred_revenue_cents` sum exactly to the agreement's `annual_price_cents`, with no cent lost and none invented. Visit buckets are recomputed when the agreement is activated, when a visit is rescheduled, and whenever the set of visits changes, because a change in the bucket count changes the per-bucket share.

Revenue is recognised forward-looking against the visits the plan owes. The `membership_revenue_recognition` calculation draws its input set from the agreement's `MembershipVisit` rows whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `MISSED` or `COMPLETED`; rows in any other status are excluded from both the numerator and every denominator. The numerator is the count of those rows whose `status` is `COMPLETED`. The calculation has two denominators, and which one applies is the variant in force. The base denominator counts every row in the input set. The variant denominator counts only those rows whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` — it drops `MISSED` visits. The base variant is the one in force wherever the recognition is applied by the system — fulfilling a visit on completion, reopening one on cancellation, and the refund evaluation used by the agreement-cancellation guard all recognise under the base denominator — and the variant denominator is honoured only where a surface exposes a variant selector and the caller asks for it. Under the base variant, the agreement's `revenue_recognized_cents` is its `annual_price_cents` multiplied by the completed count and divided by the base denominator, with the single half-up rounding applied to the money at the end of the division. Under the variant, the same product is divided by the narrower denominator instead. In both cases the agreement's `unearned_revenue_balance_cents` is its `annual_price_cents` minus the recognised figure. The practical difference is large: an agreement worth 100000 cents with two of four owed visits completed recognises 50000 cents under the base variant, because the missed visits inflate the denominator without contributing to the numerator; under the variant that drops missed visits from the denominator the same agreement recognises 66667 cents and carries an unearned balance of 33333 rather than 50000. The forward-looking character is the point — revenue is recognised against what the plan still owes, and a completed visit is what moves the numerator.

### 3.9 The Overdue Sweep, Visit Rescheduling and Agreement Cancellation

Nothing in this system goes overdue by the passage of wall-clock time. The only way a visit becomes late is that the logical clock is advanced, and advancing it is an active operation with consequences. When an operator sets the clock to a new reading, three cascading effects follow. Every `MembershipVisit` whose `scheduled_date` is strictly earlier than the new reading and whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` transitions to `MISSED`. Every `MembershipAgreement` whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and which now carries a `MISSED` visit transitions to `OVERDUE`. And the `membership_count_active` of every affected branch is recomputed. The sweep is the first hop of a cascading chain: a visit moves `SCHEDULED` to `MISSED`, the parent agreement moves `ACTIVE` to `OVERDUE`, and the branch's active-member count is refreshed behind it. The agreement-side hop is evaluated by the same sweep whenever a visit on that agreement turns `MISSED`.

Rescheduling is how the chain is unwound. A `Dispatcher` reschedules a visit by supplying a new `scheduled_date`, which must be strictly later than the logical clock reading. The visit's new interval is the half-open window from the new `scheduled_date` to `scheduled_date` plus the plan's `maintenance_visit_interval_days`. That interval must not overlap the identically shaped interval of any other visit on the same agreement whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`, and both intervals are additionally clipped to the agreement's alive window, which starts at its `agreement_start_utc`. An overlap is refused with `ERR_VISIT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). When the reschedule succeeds the visit's `status` returns to `SCHEDULED` and its `scheduled_date` is replaced, and the parent agreement returns to `ACTIVE` when it was `OVERDUE` and no other visit on it remains `MISSED`. The agreement's buckets are recomputed. Two guards also apply to this operation: the linked agreement's status must be `PENDING_PAYMENT` or `ACTIVE` or the request is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409), and the visit must be in a state from which the transition is declared.

Cancellation of an agreement is a `Manager` operation and is gated on a surviving-recognition test, because cancelling an agreement whose revenue has already been fully recognised would strand the liability. An agreement still at `PENDING_PAYMENT` may be cancelled outright, and an agreement already at `OVERDUE` may also be cancelled outright. An `ACTIVE` agreement is the guarded case: it may be cancelled only when either its `visits_fulfilled_count` is zero, or the refund formula evaluated with this agreement's `annual_price_cents` as the value of its variable leaves an amount strictly less than that annual price. When the guard fails — the agreement has fulfilled visits and the refund formula does not leave an amount strictly below the annual price — the cancellation is refused with `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_CANCEL_BLOCKED` (HTTP 409); there is no bespoke error identifier for the blocked case, so both the unresolved-`MISSED` refusal and the surviving-recognition refusal report `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_CANCEL_BLOCKED`. Within the guarded `ACTIVE` case the unresolved-`MISSED` arm is evaluated ahead of the fulfilled-visit arm, so an `ACTIVE` agreement that still carries a `MISSED` visit is refused on that ground before its fulfilled-visit count or refund figure is ever consulted. An `ACTIVE` agreement may not be cancelled while any of its `MISSED` visits has not yet been rescheduled, so the overdue chain must be unwound before the agreement can be retired. A successful cancellation of an `ACTIVE` agreement sets its `unearned_revenue_balance_cents` to zero and its `revenue_recognized_cents` to its full `annual_price_cents`, discharging the remaining deferred liability in a single step. A cancellation from `PENDING_PAYMENT` or `OVERDUE` leaves the recognised and unearned figures where they stood. In every case the agreement's `status` becomes `CANCELLED`, the branch's `membership_count_active` is recomputed, and the agreement's version is incremented. `CANCELLED` is terminal: no transition leaves it.

### 3.10 Voiding Work: Cancellation of Appointments and the Compensating Ledger

Cancelling an appointment is available from three different states and the consequences differ sharply by case, which is exactly why the operation is specified per state rather than as one rule. A `Dispatcher` cancels a `SCHEDULED` or a `DISPATCHED` appointment; a `Manager` cancels a `COMPLETED` one, because voiding settled revenue is a management act rather than a scheduling one.

A `SCHEDULED` appointment is cancelled if its `dispatch_status` is `UNASSIGNED` or `RELEASED` — the guard stops a request from voiding a job that a technician has already been committed to. The cancellation sets `status` to `CANCELLED` and bumps the appointment's version. No capacity figures move, because a merely-booked appointment never contributed to the dispatched-hours ledger; its hours were carried in the branch's consumed-capacity rollup, which is recomputed from the surviving appointments. Any attempt to cancel from a state the machine does not declare is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).

A `DISPATCHED` appointment is cancelled only when it has been dispatched and no accrual exists for it: no `RevenueLedgerEntry` with `entry_type` of `ACCRUAL` may name this appointment. Where that holds, the cancellation sets `status` to `CANCELLED`, sets `dispatch_status` to `RELEASED`, and clears `technician_id` back to null, releasing the technician from the job. The technician's `status` returns to `AVAILABLE` and their `dispatched_hours` falls by the appointment's `estimated_hours`; the branch's `capacity_consumed_hours` falls by the same amount. The membership visit named by the appointment's `membership_visit_sequence_number`, if its `status` is `SCHEDULED`, is detached: its `completed_appointment_id` stays null and its status remains `SCHEDULED`, so the visit stays owed. The appointment's version is incremented. The released technician is excluded from this appointment's later dispatches.

A `COMPLETED` appointment is the delicate case, and it is the reason the technician revenue ledger exists in the first place. Cancelling one is legal only when an `ACCRUAL` `RevenueLedgerEntry` names it, and it is subject to a segregation-of-duties check: the acting `X-User-ID` must not equal the `technician_id` recorded on that appointment's `ACCRUAL` ledger entry, so the same user may not both earn and void the same creditable revenue. A violation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Where the guard passes, the cancellation sets the appointment's `status` to `CANCELLED` and appends a compensating `RevenueLedgerEntry`: its `entry_type` is `REVERSAL`, its `amount_cents` is the negated appointment `sold_price_cents`, its `technician_id` and `appointment_id` identify the same technician and appointment as the original accrual, and its `recorded_at_utc` is the logical clock reading. The technician's `technician_revenue_ledger_cents` is reduced by the appointment's `sold_price_cents` in the same step. The `MembershipVisit` named by the appointment's `membership_visit_sequence_number`, whose `status` is `COMPLETED`, transitions back to `SCHEDULED` and its `completed_appointment_id` is cleared, and the agreement's `visits_fulfilled_count` falls by one with its `revenue_recognized_cents` recomputed afterwards. The appointment's version is incremented.

The `technician_revenue_ledger_total` calculation defines the total as the sum of the technician's entries with accruals positive and reversals negative. Its core identity is that after an accrual and its paired reversal, the technician's revenue ledger total equals its value immediately before the accrual was appended. Every entry contributes with a sign — `ACCRUAL` rows positively, `REVERSAL` rows negatively — and the technician's `technician_revenue_ledger_cents` is read as the amount of the latest entry, which is the running total after all entries have been applied. The ledger is append-only in normal operation: nothing edits a prior entry, and a correction is always a new compensating entry. The same conservation principle governs booked hours, which the `technician_booked_hours` calculation defines as the sum of `estimated_hours` over a technician's appointments whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `DISPATCHED`: dispatching adds an appointment's `estimated_hours` to the technician's dispatched hours and to the branch's consumed capacity, and completing or cancelling the job subtracts exactly the same figure, so the pair nets to zero. Rounding is excluded from both ledgers deliberately, since hours are held at two decimal places and ledger amounts are integers.

### 3.11 Performance, Incentives and the Rolling Review

Sales conduct is measured as well as policed. The strike counter described in §3.6 is read by two reporting capabilities: the incentive statement, which prices the technician's month, and the performance review, which grades the technician's quarter. Both are read-only and neither mutates anything.

The `technician_monthly_incentive_pay` calculation prices the month from the technician's completed work, and its evaluation order is pinned because the order is observable in the result. The statement covers a `period_year` and a `period_month` supplied as inputs. Its first step walks the technician's `COMPLETED` appointments and, for each, takes that appointment's `sold_price_cents` as a base, applies a rate that depends on the base, and rounds the product half up: while the base is below 20000 cents the rate is two percent, while the base is at least 20000 but below 50000 cents the rate is four percent, and once the base is 50000 cents or more the rate is six percent. Each appointment's component is rounded individually, before any summation. The gross is the sum of those individually rounded components — not the rounding of the raw total, which is the whole point of pinning the order, since rounding per appointment can leave the gross on a different side of a later boundary than rounding once would.

The second step clamps the already-summed gross at 150000 cents: the clamped figure is the gross or 150000, whichever is smaller. The clamp applies to the sum, not to the components and not after the charges. The third step subtracts the coaching-strike charge *after* that clamp, at 1500 cents per strike, multiplying the technician's `sales_coaching_strike_count` by 1500 and subtracting the product from the clamped figure. The clamped figure is deliberately not rounded at this stage. The fourth step maps the resulting figure onto a pay rate, then rounds: a figure below zero yields a rate of zero, a figure of at most 25000 cents yields a rate of two hundredths, a figure of at most 75000 cents yields a rate of three and a half hundredths, and a larger figure yields a rate of five hundredths. Note the boundaries are inclusive at the top — a figure of exactly 25000 takes the second rate and a figure of exactly 75000 takes the third. The incentive is the figure multiplied by its rate, rounded half up once at the end. Every interim value is an integer number of cents.

The statement reports four amounts: `gross_component_cents` is the unclamped sum of the per-appointment components, `clamped_component_cents` is that sum after the 150000 cap, `charge_cents` is the total strike charge deducted, and `incentive_cents` is the final rounded payout. Reporting the pre-clamp and post-clamp figures separately is what makes the clamp auditable, so an implementation that folds the clamp into the components will disagree on `gross_component_cents`.

The `technician_performance_review` calculation grades the technician over a rolling ninety-day window measured against the logical clock. The window starts ninety days before the current reading, exclusively — an entry recorded exactly at the window start is not included — and ends at the current reading, inclusively. The window revenue is the sum of `amount_cents` over the technician's `RevenueLedgerEntry` rows whose `recorded_at_utc` lies strictly after the window start and at or before the window end; `ACCRUAL` rows contribute positively and `REVERSAL` rows negatively, so a single reversal can pull the sum below a grade boundary. The grade follows from that sum: a window revenue below 400000 cents is `DOES_NOT_MEET`, a revenue of at least 400000 but below 600000 cents is `MEETS`, a revenue of at least 600000 and at most 900000 cents is `EXCEEDS`, and a revenue above 900000 cents is `OUTSTANDING`. The boundaries are inclusive exactly as stated — 600000 is `MEETS` and 900000 is `EXCEEDS`, and an implementation that puts either boundary one cent higher is wrong.

The review reports `window_start_utc`, `window_end_utc`, `window_revenue_cents` and `grade`. The technician resource itself exposes `revenue_window_cents` — the same ninety-day sum — and `performance_grade`, the same grade, both of which default to a `DOES_NOT_MEET` grade when the ledger is empty; `technician_revenue_ledger_cents` remains the all-time running total and is a different figure from the window sum, since entries older than ninety days fall out of the window while staying in the total.

### 3.12 Campaign Arithmetic: Attributed Jobs, Revenue and Conversion

Marketing reporting answers one question with one arithmetic: what did this campaign buy? The `campaign_attribution_revenue` calculation builds a booking history from every `Appointment` whose `campaign_id` equals the campaign's id, ordered by the appointment's creation instant and then by its `window_start_utc` ascending. Out of that history, `attributed_job_count` counts the bookings and `attributed_revenue_cents` sums the `sold_price_cents` of the lines whose `status` is `SOLD` on those appointments whose `status` is `COMPLETED` — a booking that never completed contributes no revenue, and a line that was never sold contributes nothing even on a completed job.

The report is rendered only when it is asked for: the campaign read accepts an optional `include_attribution` flag, and when it is true the response carries the attributed counters and a `conversion_rate`. Sampling cost is reported alongside, as `cac_cents`, the campaign's `spend_cents` divided by `attributed_job_count`, rounded half up to an integer number of cents, and set to 0 when there are no attributed jobs rather than being left undefined. Because a campaign created without a spend figure defaults `spend_cents` to 0, its `attributed_job_count` and `attributed_revenue_cents` both begin at 0 and its `conversion_rate` begins as null.

The calculation has two variants and the caller selects one through an optional `attribution_variant` input; when the input is absent or names neither variant, the chronological variant is the one in force. Under the chronological variant the job count is the count of the whole attributed booking history, whether or not the underlying visit completed. Under the visit-completion-waterfall variant the history is walked in reverse — later bookings first, still ordered by creation instant within each bucket — and the count narrows to only those appointments that either carry no agreement at all or whose named membership visit is itself `COMPLETED`. Concretely, an appointment survives the narrower count when its `membership_agreement_id` is absent, or when some `MembershipVisit` exists on that agreement at its `sequence_number` whose `status` is `COMPLETED`. The revenue sum is then taken over that narrower set of bookings. The effect is that whenever any attributed appointment maps to an incomplete membership visit, the waterfall variant's job count, its revenue and its cost per acquisition are all strictly smaller than the chronological variant's — the two variants cannot agree while an incomplete visit sits in the history.

The conversion rate comes from the `marketing_attribution_denominator` calculation, whose numerator is the `attributed_job_count` produced by whichever variant was selected and whose denominator is the count of *distinct* campaigns referenced across the whole database as an `Appointment.campaign_id` or a `CallRecord.campaign_id` — the denominator is a property of the tenant's campaign population, not of this campaign alone, so adding an unrelated campaign with attributed traffic changes this campaign's rate. The rate is 0.000 when the numerator is zero, and 1.000 when the numerator equals the denominator. Otherwise it is the numerator divided by the denominator, rounded to three decimal places, and the rounding at the third place is *half down* — a true ratio whose fourth decimal digit is exactly five rounds toward zero rather than away from it. This is the one place in the API where half-up is deliberately not the rule.

### 3.13 Recording and Coaching Calls

Intake is captured as evidence. A `CustomerService` representative registers a recorded call against the campaign it belongs to, supplying the owning `campaign_id`, their own opaque `cs_user_id`, the call's `duration_seconds` — which must be zero or a positive number — and a required non-empty `recording_uri` pointing at the stored audio. The call's `started_at_utc` is optional and defaults to the logical clock reading when it is omitted. A newly registered call begins `UNREVIEWED`, with `coached_by_user_id` null and `attributed_appointment_id` null, and its version begins its life at creation.

The link between a call and the job it produced is established at booking, not at intake. When an appointment is booked with a `call_record_id` and no explicit `campaign_id`, the appointment inherits that call's campaign as its own attribution source, and the referenced `CallRecord`'s `attributed_appointment_id` is set to the new appointment's id. A call that produced no booking simply keeps a null `attributed_appointment_id` and remains unattributed — which is exactly the state that blocks its review, below. A call is also readable through its campaign's call-records collection, which accepts an optional `coaching_status` filter and the standard `limit` and `offset` paging parameters.

Coaching is a `Manager` operation with two distinct decisions, and it is the one place where the system refuses to close a review loop early. When the manager assigns coaching by supplying a `decision` of `ASSIGN_COACHING`, the call's `coaching_status` must currently be `UNREVIEWED` — the assignment is not legal from an already-assigned or already-reviewed call, and such an attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). On success the call's `coaching_status` becomes `COACHING_ASSIGNED` and its `coached_by_user_id` becomes the acting `X-User-ID`. The call's version is incremented.

Closing the review is subject to a cross-entity guard, and the guard is the same whichever path the call takes to it. A manager closes the review by supplying a decision of `COMPLETE_REVIEW`, and the call must have an `attributed_appointment_id` whose appointment's `status` is `COMPLETED`. A call with no attributed appointment, or whose attributed appointment is not yet complete, cannot be closed: the attempt is refused with `ERR_CALL_NOT_REVIEWABLE` (HTTP 409). Note that this is not the same failure as an illegal state — a call may be perfectly reviewable in principle and still blocked by the guard. Two paths lead to `REVIEWED`: a call already at `COACHING_ASSIGNED` may be closed, and a call that never needed coaching may be closed directly from `UNREVIEWED`. Both are legal from their respective starting states, but both carry the identical attribution guard, so the fast path is not a way past it. On closure the call's `coaching_status` becomes `REVIEWED`, which is terminal — no transition leaves it — and its version is incremented.

### 3.14 The Round Trip: Reads, Filters and What Actually Fires a Rule

Every guard in this document fires on a specific *kind* of interaction, and knowing which requests are inert is as important as knowing which are checked. Creation — creating a branch, hiring a technician, creating a plan, creating a campaign, creating a pricebook revision, registering a call, booking an appointment, adding a line, filing an override, selling an agreement, scheduling a visit — runs the full validation ladder of §2.10 in its stated precedence. Modification — dispatching, completing, cancelling, repricing, deciding, rescheduling, assigning coaching, recording a payment — runs the ladder as well as any state precondition belonging to the transition. But every read is inert. Reading a campaign, an appointment, an appointment's lines, an appointment's override requests, an agreement, an agreement's visits, a branch, a technician, a campaign's call records, an incentive statement or a performance review changes nothing: no window is re-evaluated, no sweep is triggered, no ledger entry is written, and no counter moves. The one read that appears to compute something — a campaign read with `include_attribution` set true — computes the attributed counters and the conversion rate for the response only; the campaign's stored fields are not permanently rewritten by the read, and the same campaign read again without the flag reports its baseline figures.

A large class of rejections is simply the resource not being there. Any request that carries an identifier in its path resolves that identifier before anything else, so a request against a nonexistent branch, technician, appointment, pricebook revision, override request, membership plan, agreement, visit, campaign or call record is refused with `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). Because path parameters resolve left to right (§2.5), a request against a child of an absent parent reports the parent's absence rather than the child's: naming an appointment that does not exist yields a not-found for the appointment, never for the line or the override that hangs beneath it. This is one guard for every tucked-away identifier in the API, and it is why an unresolvable parent is a different failure from an unresolvable child.

The listing endpoints each accept their own filter set on top of the common paging parameters, and the filters are part of the contract rather than conveniences. The appointment listing filters by `branch_id`, `technician_id`, `status`, `campaign_id`, `membership_agreement_id` and `call_record_id`. The agreement listing filters by `branch_id`, `membership_plan_id` and `status`. The agreement's visit listing filters by `status`, and pages with `limit` and `offset`. The pricebook-task listing filters by `code` and `status` alongside its paging parameters. The appointment's line listing and its override-request listing page with `limit` and `offset`. The campaign's call-record listing filters by `coaching_status` and pages with `limit` and `offset`. An unfiltered listing returns the whole set, paged at the default page size, ordered by `created_at` ascending except where §2.3 records the explicit exceptions.

Some write operations accept an optional explanatory field and some accept a required one, and the distinction matters to a caller building a request. Cancelling an appointment and cancelling a membership agreement each accept an optional `reason` — a cancellation without one is perfectly legal and the field is simply absent. Completing an appointment accepts a required `completion_description` whose length is gated as §3.7 describes, so its absence is a rejection rather than a default. Dispatching accepts only an `appointment_id` and nothing else, because the technician and the quote are chosen by the system rather than named by the caller. Recording a payment accepts only `amount_cents`. Filing an override accepts only `requested_price_cents`. Deciding an override accepts a `decision` together with `expected_appointment_version`; the decision tokens are `APPROVE` and `REJECT`, matched case-insensitively, with `APPROVED` and `REJECTED` accepted as aliases. Repricing accepts `expected_pricebook_task_version` and an optional `pricebook_task_id`. Assigning coaching accepts only a `decision`. Setting the clock accepts only `to_iso8601`. A request missing a required field entirely, or carrying one that cannot be parsed at all, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Two cross-entity read models close the loop between what a member owes and what a job is worth, and the two disagree by design. The `appointment_settlement_balance` calculation renders an appointment's settlement position, and the variant in force is selected through the read's optional `variant` query parameter. A single settlement response reports both variants at once: `appointment_outstanding_cents` carries the outstanding-balance figure and `agreement_covered_cents` carries the agreement-covered figure, alongside the appointment's `sold_price_cents` and the linked agreement's `collected_amount_cents`. Under the outstanding-balance variant — the one an invoice renders — the outstanding figure starts from the appointment's `sold_price_cents`, subtracts half of the linked plan's `included_labor_discount_pct` applied to that sold price and rounded half up to a whole number of cents, and then subtracts the linked agreement's `collected_amount_cents`. Because it nets what the member has already paid against what this discounted visit is worth, it can legitimately go negative when a member has prepaid more than the visit is worth. Under the agreement-covered variant — the one used when the appointment is a membership visit — the agreement-covered figure is 0 whenever the visit fulfilled by this appointment has reached `COMPLETED`, and is the appointment's `sold_price_cents` otherwise, regardless of any collected amounts. The two variants never disagree in sign, since neither can produce a figure below zero and above `sold_price_cents` at the same time, but they disagree in magnitude whenever the agreement carries a non-zero collected amount. When no agreement is linked, the collected amount read is 0 and the outstanding variant reduces to the discounted sold price alone.
