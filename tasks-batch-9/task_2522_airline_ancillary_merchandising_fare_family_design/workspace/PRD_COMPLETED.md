# Airline Ancillary Merchandising API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Airlines no longer sell a single undifferentiated seat. A modern retailing organisation sells a *ladder* of fare families — each one a bundle of air transport plus a curated set of ancillary attributes covering baggage, seat selection, changes, boarding priority and onboard product — and then merchandises whatever the passenger did not already buy, at every touchpoint from the initial booking through check-in and into the cabin. This API is the system of record for that retailing apparatus. It owns the catalog of ancillary products, the construction and pricing review of fare families, the segmentation and dynamic pricing of the seat map, the willingness-to-pay models behind offer sequencing, the publication of family content to indirect distribution channels, the irregular-operations waivers that credit fees back to passengers, and the append-only revenue ledger that accounts for every cent with its correct tax treatment.

Three pressures shape the design. The first is **commercial**: ancillary pricing is dynamic, so the same product carries a different price at booking than it does in flight, and a different price again through a marked-up online travel agency than through the airline's own site. The second is **regulatory**: fees that a passenger will be asked to pay must be disclosed at the point of sale, the disclosure must quote catalog list prices rather than negotiated overrides, and the passenger must acknowledge that disclosure before a ticket may be issued. If the underlying family content changes after the disclosure was issued, the disclosure is stale and the sale cannot close on it. The third is **distributional**: content parity obligations mean a fare family published to a channel must remain byte-identical to what the airline actually sells; when it drifts, the channel is in breach and sales through it stop until the publication is resynchronised.

The specification that follows is deliberately explicit about ordering, rounding and cascade scope, because these are where ancillary retailing systems diverge from one another and from their own accounting. Every monetary quantity is an integer number of cents. Every rate, multiplier and margin is expressed in basis points, where ten thousand basis points is exactly one. Every clock reading is drawn from a server-controlled logical clock rather than wall-clock time, so that offer validity windows, cool-off windows and accounting periods are reproducible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, resource properties, arithmetic discipline, concurrency, validation precedence, and the three cross-cutting invariants (content parity, fee disclosure, and the revenue ledger).

### 2.1. Retailing Roles and Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which it acts. If either header is absent, empty, or made up entirely of whitespace, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined. If the supplied role is not one of the six roles below, or is a recognised role that is not authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are:

- **`MerchandisingAnalyst`** — builds the ancillary catalog and designs fare families and their bundled attributes. Exclusively creates catalog products, creates fare families, repositions or renames a draft family, attaches attributes to a family, reprices or withdraws an attribute, and submits a family for pricing review.
- **`PricingApprover`** — reviews and publishes fare families, and is the only role that may suspend or retire one. Exclusively records review decisions and performs family status changes. A `PricingApprover` may never review a family it designed.
- **`DistributionManager`** — registers distribution channels and manages content parity of published families across them. Exclusively registers channels, creates channel publications, and resynchronises a drifted publication.
- **`OperationsController`** — owns flights, the seat map, the revenue-management demand feed and irregular-operations waivers. Exclusively creates flights, advances a flight's retailing phase, publishes a demand snapshot, creates seat-map segments, declares irregular-operations waivers, and rescinds them.
- **`RevenueAccountant`** — reads the append-only ancillary revenue ledger and its tax treatment. This is the **only** role permitted to read revenue entries, in any of the three ways the ledger is exposed.
- **`ChannelPartner`** — an indirect or direct selling channel acting on its own bookings. Exclusively creates bookings, issues and acknowledges fee disclosures, tickets, opens and settles changes, cancels, runs merchandising offer sessions, accepts and declines offers, assigns seats and releases them.

Every role may read every resource except the revenue ledger, and every role may drive the logical clock.

**Identity semantics.** When `X-User-Role` is `ChannelPartner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `DistributionChannel`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `DistributionChannel` compare the resource's `distribution_channel_id` to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `ChannelPartner` may only act on a `Booking` whose `distribution_channel_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`, and only on an `AncillaryOffer` or `SeatAssignment` whose owning booking satisfies the same condition; a violation is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `MerchandisingAnalyst`, `PricingApprover`, `DistributionManager`, `OperationsController` or `RevenueAccountant`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — a fare family stamps `design_owner_user_id` from the creating `MerchandisingAnalyst`'s `X-User-ID`, and stamps `reviewer_user_id` from the deciding `PricingApprover`'s `X-User-ID` — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`.

**Read scoping for a `ChannelPartner`.** Single-resource reads of a booking, an ancillary offer or a seat assignment belonging to another channel are *refused* with `ERR_CHANNEL_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. The corresponding list reads behave differently: they silently filter the result set down to the acting partner's own channel rather than raising an error. Both behaviours are deliberate and both are tested.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` and continues with exactly twenty characters drawn from uppercase A–Z and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside 1–100, a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list endpoint returns its rows sorted by `created_at` ascending. Because the logical clock of §2.4 makes it routine for many rows to carry the very same `created_at`, that ordering is completed by a tie-break: rows sharing a `created_at` are returned in the order the server created them, oldest first, and every list in this document — the nested ones included — uses that same total order. The revenue trail of a single booking is explicitly ordered `created_at` ascending as well, because the ledger is read as a chronological narrative.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope. `total_count` counts the rows that match the request's filters *before* the `limit`/`offset` window is applied; `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the returned window.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every stored resource carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits.

`AncillaryProduct`, `FareFamily`, `FamilyAttribute`, `Flight`, `SeatInventorySegment`, `DistributionChannel`, `ChannelPublication`, `Booking`, `SeatAssignment`, `AncillaryOffer` and `IropsWaiver` additionally carry:

- **`updated_at`** — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, refreshed on every mutation of the resource, including mutations that only touch derived fields.
- **`version`** — an integer that increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the resource. It starts at 1 when the resource is created.

`RevenueEntry` is the sole exception. Revenue entries are immutable accounting events: they carry only `id` and `created_at`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are **absent** from a revenue entry. Corrections to the ledger are made by posting compensating entries, never by editing an existing one.

**The logical clock.** The server maintains a logical UTC clock that is the sole source of "now" for this system. Before the clock is ever set it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time. A test-driving capability, available to every role, sets the clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant and answers with the resulting `current_utc` together with `expired_offer_count`, the number of presented offers the call swept into expiry. Every field described below as "the logical clock" — `valid_from`, `valid_until`, `declared_at`, `disclosure_issued_at`, `disclosure_acked_at`, `last_resync_at`, and the `accounting_period` stamped on a revenue entry — is read from this clock.

**Rendering server-minted timestamps.** Every timestamp field the server itself mints is rendered in one uniform form: an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`, the same form already stated for `created_at`. This applies to `created_at`, `updated_at`, `valid_from`, `valid_until`, `declared_at`, `disclosure_issued_at`, `disclosure_acked_at`, `last_resync_at`, the `current_utc` the clock-setting call answers with, and the envelope's `api_request_timestamp`; a field whose value is null is rendered as null. Two adjacent fields are deliberately outside this rule: `accounting_period` has its own `YYYY-MM` format fixed in §2.11, and a client-supplied instant is never re-rendered — a flight's `scheduled_departure_utc` is stored and echoed back byte for byte as the creating request supplied it, whatever ISO 8601 UTC spelling that was. Any ISO 8601 spelling is accepted from a client: a trailing `Z`, an explicit numeric offset, or no zone designator at all, which is read as UTC; only the clock-setting call's `to_iso8601` is normalised, converted to UTC before it becomes the clock reading that `current_utc` renders back.

### 2.5. Basis Points, Cents and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is an integer number of cents. There are no fractional cents and no decimal money fields anywhere in the API. All rates, multipliers, margins, uplifts and scores are integers expressed in **basis points**, where 10,000 basis points equals a factor of exactly one; a commission of 250 basis points is therefore two and a half percent.

Wherever a computation divides, the result is rounded using **round half up** — the magnitude is rounded to the nearest integer and a value exactly halfway between two integers rounds away from zero. Three properties of the rounding discipline are load-bearing and are specified per calculation rather than globally:

1. **Where** the rounding happens. Some calculations round once per row and then sum the rounded rows; others multiply the exact integer product by several factors and round exactly once at the very end. These are not interchangeable, and the correct choice is stated for each calculation in §3.
2. **How many times** it happens. The dynamic ancillary price applies three sequential roundings and then clamps; folding its three multipliers into a single product produces a different answer and is wrong.
3. **When floor is used instead.** The ticketing allocation deliberately floors rather than rounds in its first pass, because a separate remainder pass owns the residue and guarantees the allocation sums exactly to the amount being allocated.

The one non-monetary precision rule concerns fee fingerprints: they are compared by exact string equality, byte for byte, never by normalised or case-insensitive comparison.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations require the client to prove it has observed the current state of a *parent* resource before it may write against it. No other endpoint accepts or requires a version field.

**Accepting an ancillary offer.** The accept request must carry `expected_booking_version`, a non-optional integer field holding the client's last observed `version` of the offer's parent `Booking`. If it does not equal that booking's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BOOKING_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Acceptance is a heavy write: it freezes an accepted price, posts a sale into the ledger, supersedes every sibling offer for the same product on that booking, and re-derives the booking's money fields. A client holding a stale view of the booking would be settling against a revenue and settlement picture that no longer exists, so the write is refused rather than merged.

**Creating a channel publication.** The publication-create request must carry `expected_fare_family_version`, a non-optional integer field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `FareFamily`. If it does not equal that family's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FAMILY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A publication pins `published_fee_fingerprint` and `published_family_version` from the family it is created against; if an earlier write has re-fingerprinted that family since the client read it, publishing from the stale view would put content on a channel that the airline no longer sells.

Neither rule is about requests overlapping in time. Both are pure staleness checks: the supplied number either equals the parent's current `version` or it does not. Both fields are shape-checked only as integers and carry no range of their own, so zero or a negative number is a well-formed request that simply fails the equality check.

### 2.7. Validation Precedence

Checks are evaluated in a fixed order and the **first** failure determines the response. The ladder below is exhaustive and its numbering is the precedence: a lower number is evaluated earlier and wins. Where a rule names the operations it guards, that enumeration is the complete scope of the rule.

1. **`ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`** (401) — `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and non-empty. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **`ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`** (403) — the supplied role is one of the six declared roles and that role is authorised for the endpoint being called. Applies to every endpoint. This same error is also the answer when a `MerchandisingAnalyst` other than a family's `design_owner_user_id` attempts to submit that family for review.
3. **`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`** (400) — the body carries every required field of the operation's input, carries no unknown fields, and every value matches its declared type, enum member set and numeric range. This covers the `record_locator` format, the airport-code format for `origin` and `destination`, the character sets of `sku` and `code`, membership of `seat_letters` and `seat_letter` in the letters A through F, a non-empty `waiver_scope`, `delay_minutes` within 0 to 1440, `booked_load_factor_pct` within 0 to 100, `demand_index_bp` within −5000 to 5000, `boarding_priority_tier` within 1 to 4, `included_quantity` within 0 to 9, `limit` within 1 to 100 and a non-negative `offset`. Applies to every write endpoint and to the pagination and filter parameters of every list endpoint. Six further shape rules hold uniformly across those inputs. An integer field accepts only a JSON integer, so a boolean, a numeric string and a number written with a decimal point or an exponent — `5.0` included — are all refused; an id-valued field must be a non-empty string, so a blank id is a shape failure here rather than a not-found later; and neither the `row` of a seat assignment nor either `expected_*_version` field carries a range of its own. An explicit JSON null is never an accepted value for any field: `upsell_parent_family_id`, `override_price_cents` and `review_note` are optional in the sense that they may be omitted, but supplying any field as null fails at this step. An array of enum members must additionally be free of repeated elements, which covers `seat_letters`, `waiver_scope` and `supported_categories`, while `requested_product_ids` is checked only to be an array of non-empty strings and may be empty or repetitive. Fields carried as free text are constrained to be strings and nothing more — no length, no character set, and the empty string is accepted — namely a product's or family's `name`, `revenue_account_code`, `flight_no`, `channel_code`, `passenger_name`, `acknowledged_fee_fingerprint`, `review_note`, and the `reason`, `cancel_reason`, `release_reason` and `rescind_reason` strings. The body itself is read as a JSON object: an absent, empty or literal-null body counts as an empty object, so an operation that declares no input accepts no body at all, whereas a body that parses to anything other than an object, or does not parse, is refused here; no `Content-Type` header is required for any request. Query parameters follow the same discipline with one relaxation: an enum-valued filter outside its member set is refused here, an id-valued filter is matched as an opaque string so an id that resolves to nothing yields an empty page rather than a 404, and a query parameter the endpoint does not declare is ignored rather than refused.
4. **404 by path** — every id templated into the URL resolves to a stored row, validated **left to right**, so the leftmost unresolvable identifier decides the error. The mapping is fixed: a flight id yields `ERR_FLIGHT_NOT_FOUND`, a fare-family id yields `ERR_FARE_FAMILY_NOT_FOUND`, a family-attribute id yields `ERR_FAMILY_ATTRIBUTE_NOT_FOUND`, an ancillary-product id yields `ERR_ANCILLARY_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, a seat-segment id yields `ERR_SEAT_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`, a distribution-channel id yields `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_CHANNEL_NOT_FOUND`, a channel-publication id yields `ERR_CHANNEL_PUBLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, a booking id yields `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, an ancillary-offer id yields `ERR_ANCILLARY_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, a seat-assignment id yields `ERR_SEAT_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, and an irops-waiver id yields `ERR_IROPS_WAIVER_NOT_FOUND`. The ledger is the one identifier outside that mapping: a revenue-entry id that resolves to nothing answers with `ERR_REVENUE_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, which the §5 table does not list. Applies to every endpoint that has a path parameter. All of these carry HTTP 404.
5. **404 by body reference** — every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to a stored row, validated in the order the operation declares its inputs, using the same per-entity not-found error ids as the previous step. This guards fare-family creation (whose `upsell_parent_family_id`, when supplied, must resolve to a stored family or the request is refused with `ERR_FARE_FAMILY_NOT_FOUND`), attribute attachment, seat-segment creation, publication creation, booking creation, seat assignment and offer-session creation.
6. **`ERR_CHANNEL_SCOPE_VIOLATION`** (403) — when the acting role is `ChannelPartner`, `X-User-ID` equals the `distribution_channel_id` of the booking the request targets, or of the booking that owns the targeted offer or seat assignment. This guards booking creation, disclosure issuance, disclosure acknowledgement, ticketing, change-open, change-settle, cancellation, offer sessions, seat assignment, offer acceptance, offer decline and seat release; and it guards the three single-resource reads of a booking, an ancillary offer and a seat assignment. The three corresponding list reads instead filter silently, as §2.1 describes.
7. **`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`** (403) — the acting `PricingApprover`'s `X-User-ID` differs from the target family's `design_owner_user_id`. The analyst who designed a family may never record its review decision. This guards the review-decision operation only.
8. **`ERR_FAMILY_STATE_INVALID`** (409) — the family's `status` is the from-state the requested action declares: `DRAFT` to submit for review, `IN_REVIEW` to record a review decision, `DRAFT` to reposition the family on the upsell ladder, `DRAFT` to attach a new attribute, and `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED` to reprice or withdraw an existing attribute. For a status change, the family's current `status` must be one from which the requested `target_status` is reachable: `PUBLISHED` reaches `SUSPENDED` or `RETIRED`, and `SUSPENDED` reaches `PUBLISHED` or `RETIRED`. Every other pair is rejected. This guards submit, review-decision, status-change, the family reposition, attribute repricing and attribute attachment.
9. **`ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID`** (409) — the booking's `booking_status` is a legal from-state for the action: `HELD` for issuing a disclosure and for ticketing; `HELD` for acknowledging a disclosure; `TICKETED` for opening a change; `CHANGE_PENDING` for settling a change; `HELD`, `TICKETED` or `CHANGE_PENDING` for cancelling and for assigning a seat; `HELD` or `TICKETED` for running an offer session and for accepting an offer. This guards disclosure, disclosure acknowledgement, ticketing, change-open, change-settle, cancellation, seat assignment, offer sessions and offer acceptance.
10. **`ERR_DISCLOSURE_STATE_INVALID`** (409) — the booking's `disclosure_state` is `PENDING_ACK` when a disclosure is acknowledged. This guards disclosure acknowledgement only.
11. **`ERR_OFFER_STATE_INVALID`** (409) — the offer's `offer_status` is `PRESENTED` when it is accepted or declined. This guards offer acceptance and offer decline.
12. **`ERR_SEAT_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`** (409) — the assignment's `assignment_state` is either `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` when it is released; an assignment already `RELEASED` cannot be released again. This guards seat release only.
13. **`ERR_WAIVER_STATE_INVALID`** (409) — the waiver's `waiver_state` is `ACTIVE` when it is rescinded. This guards waiver rescission only.
14. **`ERR_PUBLICATION_STATE_INVALID`** (409) — the publication's `parity_state` is `OUT_OF_PARITY` when it is resynchronised. This guards publication resynchronisation only.
15. **`ERR_PHASE_TRANSITION_INVALID`** (409) — the requested `target_phase` is exactly the next phase along `PRESALE` → `CHECKIN_OPEN` → `BOARDING` → `DEPARTED`. Skipping a phase or moving backwards is rejected. This guards phase advance only.
16. **`ERR_FLIGHT_ALREADY_DEPARTED`** (409) — the flight's `sales_phase` is not `DEPARTED` for a write that touches the flight's inventory, waivers, bookings or seats. This guards seat-segment creation, demand snapshots, waiver declaration, waiver rescission, booking creation, change-open and seat release. Seat *assignment* is deliberately **not** in this scope: departure closes every segment of the flight, so an assignment attempt after departure is refused by the more specific segment-closed check at step 17 instead.
17. **`ERR_SEGMENT_CLOSED`** (409) — the target segment's `segment_state` is `OPEN` when a seat on it is assigned. This guards seat assignment only.
18. **`ERR_TOUCHPOINT_PHASE_MISMATCH`** (409) — the requested `touchpoint` matches the flight's `sales_phase` exactly: `BOOKING` requires `PRESALE`, `CHECKIN` requires `CHECKIN_OPEN`, and `INFLIGHT` requires `BOARDING`. This guards offer sessions only.
19. **`ERR_FLIGHT_SALES_PHASE_CLOSED`** (409) — the flight's `sales_phase` is `PRESALE` or `CHECKIN_OPEN` when a booking is ticketed. This guards ticketing only.
20. **`ERR_OFFER_WINDOW_CLOSED`** (409) — the logical clock is at or after `valid_from` and strictly before `valid_until`. The window is half-open, so a clock reading exactly equal to `valid_until` is closed. This guards offer acceptance and offer decline.
21. **`ERR_STALE_BOOKING_VERSION`** (412) — `expected_booking_version` equals the booking's `version`. This guards offer acceptance only.
22. **`ERR_STALE_FAMILY_VERSION`** (412) — `expected_fare_family_version` equals the family's `version`. This guards publication creation only.
23. **`ERR_FAMILY_NOT_PUBLISHED`** (409) — the family's `status` is `PUBLISHED`. This guards ticketing and publication resynchronisation.
24. **`ERR_IROPS_SALES_BLOCKED`** (409) — the flight's `irops_state` is not `IRREGULAR` when a booking on it is ticketed; no new sale may close while irregular operations are open. This guards ticketing only.
25. **`ERR_FEE_DISCLOSURE_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`** (422) — the booking's `disclosure_state` is neither `NOT_ISSUED` nor `PENDING_ACK` at ticketing; the passenger must have acknowledged the point-of-sale fee disclosure. This guards ticketing only.
26. **`ERR_FEE_DISCLOSURE_STALE`** (409) — at ticketing, the booking's `disclosure_state` is not `STALE` **and** its `disclosed_fee_fingerprint` equals the live `fee_fingerprint` of its fare family. This guards ticketing only.
27. **`ERR_DISCLOSURE_FINGERPRINT_MISMATCH`** (409) — the supplied `acknowledged_fee_fingerprint` equals the booking's `disclosed_fee_fingerprint` byte for byte. This guards disclosure acknowledgement only.
28. **`ERR_UNDISCLOSED_FEE_CATEGORY`** (422) — every attribute of the family whose `attribute_state` is `ACTIVE` and whose `inclusion` is `CHARGEABLE` references a product whose `disclosable_fee` is true. An undisclosable chargeable fee may not be sold at the point of sale. This guards disclosure issuance only.
29. **`ERR_FAMILY_NOT_PUBLISHED_TO_CHANNEL`** (409) — a publication exists for the booking's `fare_family_id` and `distribution_channel_id` pair whose `parity_state` is `IN_PARITY`. This guards ticketing only.
30. **`ERR_CHANNEL_CONTENT_PARITY_BREACH`** (409) — the channel's `content_parity_status` is `COMPLIANT`. This guards ticketing and offer acceptance.
31. **`ERR_CHANNEL_CATEGORY_UNSUPPORTED`** (422) — every category the request would merchandise or disclose is a member of the channel's `supported_categories`: the categories of the `CHARGEABLE` attributes at disclosure, the category of every product named in `requested_product_ids` at an offer session, and the offered product's category at acceptance. This guards disclosure issuance, offer sessions and offer acceptance.
32. **Natural-key uniqueness** (409) — each natural key is unique across all stored rows and carries its own error id: a duplicate product `sku` yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU`, a duplicate family `code` yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_FAMILY_CODE`, and a duplicate `channel_code` yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHANNEL_CODE`. These guard product creation, family creation and channel registration respectively.
33. **`ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD_LOCATOR`** (409) — the supplied `record_locator` is unique across all stored bookings, including bookings already `CANCELLED` or `FLOWN`. This guards booking creation only.
34. **`ERR_ATTRIBUTE_DUPLICATE_PRODUCT`** (409) — at most one attribute per fare-family-and-product pair may have `attribute_state` `ACTIVE`. This guards attribute attachment only.
35. **`ERR_DUPLICATE_PUBLICATION`** (409) — at most one publication per fare-family-and-channel pair may have a `parity_state` of `PENDING`, `IN_PARITY` or `OUT_OF_PARITY`. A `WITHDRAWN` publication does not block a new one. This guards publication creation only.
36. **`ERR_INVALID_ATTRIBUTE_QUANTITY`** (422) — `included_quantity` is at least 1 when `inclusion` is `INCLUDED`, and exactly 0 when `inclusion` is `CHARGEABLE`. The rule is enforced both at attachment and against the post-patch state of a repriced attribute. This guards attribute attachment and attribute repricing.
37. **`ERR_UPSELL_CYCLE_DETECTED`** (422) — following `upsell_parent_family_id` upward from the family being created or updated never revisits a family already on the path; the upsell ladder must stay acyclic. At creation only an unknown parent can fail, at step 5; the cycle is therefore provoked by re-parenting an existing `DRAFT` family. This guards family creation and the family reposition.
38. **`ERR_SEAT_PRODUCT_CATEGORY_INVALID`** (422) — the product a seat segment references has `category` equal to `SEAT`. This guards seat-segment creation only.
39. **`ERR_SEAT_SEGMENT_ROW_OVERLAP`** (409) — the new segment's rectangle of rows crossed with letters shares no individual seat with any existing segment on the same flight whose `segment_state` is `OPEN`. Touching row ranges with disjoint letters are allowed, and adjacent-but-disjoint row ranges are allowed. This guards seat-segment creation only.
40. **`ERR_SEGMENT_EXCEEDS_CABIN`** (422) — the sum of `total_seats` over every segment of the flight, including the one being created, is at most the flight's `cabin_seat_total`. This guards seat-segment creation only.
41. **`ERR_SEAT_ROW_OUT_OF_SEGMENT`** (422) — the requested `row` is at least the segment's `row_start` and at most its `row_end`, and the requested `seat_letter` is a member of the segment's `seat_letters`. This guards seat assignment only.
42. **`ERR_SEAT_INVENTORY_EXHAUSTED`** (409) — the segment's `available_seats` is at least 1 at the instant a seat on it is assigned. This aggregate capacity check is deliberately ordered **before** the point-occupancy check at step 47, so a request against a fully held or fully sold segment answers with the capacity error rather than with the occupancy error. This guards seat assignment only.
43. **`ERR_OFFER_COOLOFF_CONFLICT`** (409) — when the request supplies `requested_product_ids`, none of those products has a declined offer on this booking whose `updated_at` plus the cool-off window is strictly later than the logical clock. Products *not* named in `requested_product_ids` are silently filtered out of the candidate pool instead of raising this error. This guards offer sessions only.
44. **`ERR_DELAY_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`** (422) — when `event_type` is `DELAY` and `CHANGE` is a member of `waiver_scope`, `delay_minutes` is at least the three-hour irregular-operations change-waiver threshold. This guards waiver declaration only.
45. **`ERR_FAMILY_HAS_NO_ATTRIBUTES`** (422) — the family has at least one attribute whose `attribute_state` is `ACTIVE` when it is submitted for review. This guards submit only.
46. **`ERR_FAMILY_HAS_LIVE_BOOKINGS`** (409) — no booking on the family has a `booking_status` of `HELD`, `TICKETED` or `CHANGE_PENDING` when the family is retired. This guards status-change only.
47. **`ERR_SEAT_ALREADY_OCCUPIED`** (409) — no assignment with `assignment_state` `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` already occupies the same segment, `row` and `seat_letter` triple. This check is reached only once the segment still has at least one available seat per step 42. This guards seat assignment only.
48. **`ERR_OFFER_PRICE_CAP_EXCEEDED`** (422) — the offer's `offered_price_cents` is at most the channel's `max_ancillary_price_cents` at acceptance. The ceiling is deliberately **not** applied when the offer is priced, so an unsellable price can be presented and then refused. This guards offer acceptance only.
49. **`ERR_BUNDLE_DISCOUNT_FLOOR_BREACH`** (422) — the family's `bundle_price_cents` is at most its `a_la_carte_total_cents` as recomputed from the family's `ACTIVE` attributes inside the submit transition. A bundle that costs more than buying every component a la carte may not be submitted for review. This guards submit only.

### 2.8. The Fee Fingerprint and Content Parity

Content parity — the obligation that what a channel shows is what the airline sells — is enforced in this system through a single deterministic string, the **fee fingerprint**, stored on every fare family as `fee_fingerprint`. Anything that can change what a passenger will be charged changes the fingerprint; anything that cannot, does not.

The fingerprint is built as follows. Take every attribute of the family whose `attribute_state` is `ACTIVE`, ordered by the `sku` of the referenced catalog product ascending. For each one, form a part by joining four pieces with a colon between them: the product's `sku`, then the attribute's `inclusion`, then its `included_quantity` written as a decimal integer, then its `effective_unit_cents` written as a decimal integer. Then form the fingerprint by writing the family's `code`, a vertical bar, the family's `revision_no` as a decimal integer, a second vertical bar, and finally the parts joined together with a semicolon between consecutive parts. A family with no `ACTIVE` attributes therefore fingerprints as its `code`, a vertical bar, its `revision_no` and a trailing vertical bar with nothing after it. Withdrawn attributes are excluded from the fingerprint exactly as they are excluded from the valuation.

Because `revision_no` is one of the components, a rejected review always drifts the fingerprint even if not one attribute moved — a second submission is never byte-identical to the first.

Two consumers pin the fingerprint and are held against it. A `ChannelPublication` stores `published_fee_fingerprint` and `published_family_version`, captured at publication or at the last resynchronisation; parity holds for as long as the pinned string equals the live one. A `Booking` stores `disclosed_fee_fingerprint`, captured when its fee disclosure was issued; the disclosure is honest for as long as the pinned string equals the live one. Comparison in both cases is exact string equality.

At the channel level, `content_parity_status` is derived, not stored by hand: it reads `BREACHED` when the channel's `parity_required` is true and at least one of that channel's publications is `OUT_OF_PARITY`, and `COMPLIANT` otherwise. A channel whose `parity_required` is false never breaches — its drifted publications drift alone. Re-deriving the status is a mutation of the channel only when the derived value differs from the stored one: a parity loss always mutates the channel, because `parity_breach_count` moves with it, whereas a resynchronisation or a retirement that leaves `content_parity_status` exactly where it already was leaves the channel's `updated_at` and `version` untouched. Separately, `parity_breach_count` is a monotonic counter on the channel: it increases by one each time one of the channel's publications moves from `IN_PARITY` to `OUT_OF_PARITY`, and it never decreases, not even when the publication is resynchronised. The counter is an audit measure of how often parity has been lost, not a measure of how many publications are drifted right now.

### 2.9. The Point-of-Sale Fee Disclosure Obligation

A booking carries two state dimensions that advance independently of one another: `booking_status`, the sales dimension, and `disclosure_state`, the regulatory dimension. A booking can be `HELD` with `disclosure_state` `NOT_ISSUED`, `HELD` with `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `HELD` with `STALE`, and so on; the two enumerations never constrain each other except where a rule in §2.7 or §3 says so explicitly.

The disclosure dimension exists because a passenger must be told, at the point of sale, what chargeable fees the fare family will expose them to, and must acknowledge that they were told, before a ticket may be issued. Issuance snapshots four fields onto the booking: `disclosed_fee_fingerprint`, `disclosed_total_fee_cents`, `disclosed_unbundled_list_cents` and `disclosure_issued_at`. Acknowledgement stamps `disclosure_acked_at`. All five are null while `disclosure_state` is `NOT_ISSUED`.

The regulatory intent behind `disclosed_unbundled_list_cents` deserves emphasis, because it is the one place in the system where the *catalog* price is authoritative over the *negotiated* price: a disclosure must quote what the components cost at list, never what the airline privately agreed to value them at inside the bundle.

When the underlying family content drifts after a disclosure was issued, the disclosure is no longer honest and the booking's `disclosure_state` becomes `STALE`, while the four snapshotted fields **keep their stale values** so that the drift remains auditable. This staling is conditional: it applies only to bookings still in `booking_status` `HELD`. A booking already `TICKETED` is *not* staled by a later fingerprint drift, because its disclosure obligation was discharged at the point of sale and cannot be retroactively invalidated.

### 2.10. Cascade Semantics, Derived Fields and Version Propagation

Many operations in this system move more than one entity. The rule is uniform: every entity a cascade touches is itself mutated — its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments by 1 — even when the client's request named only the head of the cascade. Ticketing a booking, for example, increments the booking's `version`, increments the `version` of each seat assignment it confirms, and increments the parent flight's `version` when it re-derives that flight's counters.

Derived counters and aggregates are never client-supplied and are always recomputed from the rows they summarise, not incrementally patched. This includes a family's `included_attribute_count` and `publication_count`; a flight's `demand_tier`, `active_waiver_count` and `ticketed_booking_count`; a segment's `total_seats`, `held_seats`, `sold_seats`, `available_seats`, `zone_multiplier_bp` and `effective_price_cents`; a channel's `content_parity_status`; a booking's `ancillary_gross_cents`, `recognized_ancillary_net_cents`, `commission_cents`, `incentive_credit_cents` and `channel_settlement_total_cents`; an attribute's `effective_unit_cents` and `attr_value_cents`; and an offer's `wtp_score_bp`, `offered_price_cents` and `expected_value_cents`.

Two conservation invariants hold at all times and are the accounting backstop for the cascades. **Seat inventory conservation:** a segment's `available_seats` always equals its `total_seats` minus its `held_seats` minus its `sold_seats`. **Ancillary net revenue conservation:** a booking's `recognized_ancillary_net_cents` always equals the signed sum of `net_cents` over that booking's revenue entries whose `category` is anything other than `AIR_TRANSPORT`; correspondingly, `ancillary_gross_cents` always equals the signed sum of `gross_cents` over that booking's entries whose `entry_type` is `SALE`, `REFUND`, `WAIVER_CREDIT` or `WAIVER_REVERSAL`.

### 2.11. The Append-Only Ancillary Revenue Ledger

Every cent that moves is recorded as a `RevenueEntry`, and revenue entries are never edited or deleted. Each entry stamps `booking_id` for the booking it belongs to, a `product_sku` snapshot of the selling product, a `category`, an `entry_type`, the signed amounts `gross_cents`, `tax_cents` and `net_cents`, the snapshotted `tax_treatment`, `revenue_class` and `revenue_account_code`, an `accounting_period` written as the four-digit year and two-digit month taken from the logical clock at posting and joined by a hyphen, and a `source_ref` audit string.

The `source_ref` is not a foreign key. It is a human-readable provenance string formed as a kind prefix, a colon, and the id of the originating record: `ticket:` followed by the booking id for a ticketing allocation, `offer:` followed by the offer id for an accepted ancillary offer, `seat:` followed by the seat-assignment id for a seat sale or its reversal, `waiver:` followed by the waiver id for an irregular-operations credit or its reversal, and `change:` followed by the booking id for a confirmed change fee. A compensating entry that mirrors an existing one copies that entry's `category`, `product_sku`, `tax_treatment`, `revenue_class` and `revenue_account_code` verbatim and negates both `gross_cents` and `tax_cents`; it also copies the mirrored entry's `source_ref` unchanged — so a `REFUND` of an accepted offer's sale carries that sale's `offer:` reference and a `REFUND` of a ticketing allocation carries that allocation's `ticket:` reference — except for the two irregular-operations kinds, which instead write `waiver:` followed by the id of the waiver being declared or rescinded.

`entry_type` distinguishes five kinds of event: `ALLOCATION` is posted at ticketing when the pinned fare component is split across categories; `SALE` is posted on an ancillary purchase; `REFUND` reverses a sale; `WAIVER_CREDIT` is posted when an irregular-operations waiver credits a posted fee back to the passenger; and `WAIVER_REVERSAL` undoes such a credit when the waiver is rescinded. Sign convention is fixed: `gross_cents` is **positive** for `ALLOCATION`, `SALE` and `WAIVER_REVERSAL`, and **negative** for `REFUND` and `WAIVER_CREDIT`.

Tax is derived per entry from the entry's `tax_treatment`, at a rate of 750 basis points for `TAXABLE_TRANSPORT`, 400 basis points for `TAXABLE_SERVICE` and nothing at all for `EXEMPT`. `tax_cents` is the absolute value of `gross_cents` multiplied by that rate and divided by 10,000, rounded half up, and then given the same sign as `gross_cents`. `net_cents` is `gross_cents` minus `tax_cents`. The `category` of an entry is one of `BAGGAGE`, `SEAT`, `CHANGE`, `BOARDING`, `ONBOARD` or the synthetic `AIR_TRANSPORT` bucket that carries the air-transport component of a fare family; an `AIR_TRANSPORT` entry always uses `tax_treatment` `TAXABLE_TRANSPORT`, `revenue_class` `AIR_TRANSPORT_RELATED`, `revenue_account_code` `3000` and `product_sku` `AIR_TRANSPORT`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Merchandising Catalog and Fare Family Design

Everything the airline can sell as an ancillary begins life as a catalog product authored by a `MerchandisingAnalyst`. A product declares a `sku` — its merchandising key, between 3 and 32 characters drawn only from uppercase A–Z, the digits and the underscore, and unique across the whole catalog — a `name` shown at the point of sale, and a `category` chosen from `BAGGAGE`, `SEAT`, `CHANGE`, `BOARDING` and `ONBOARD`. The category is not decoration: it drives which channels may merchandise the product, where the product sits in offer sequencing, which irregular-operations waivers can reach it, and which revenue bucket its money lands in. A product also declares `list_price_cents`, the a-la-carte list price in cents and never negative, which is the base of every dynamic-price computation the system performs; a `tax_treatment` of `TAXABLE_TRANSPORT`, `TAXABLE_SERVICE` or `EXEMPT`; a `revenue_class` of either `AIR_TRANSPORT_RELATED` or `NON_AIR`; and a `revenue_account_code`, the general-ledger account code that is stamped onto every revenue entry the product ever generates.

Three further product flags carry heavy downstream consequences and default in ways that are easy to get wrong. `refundable` defaults to **false**, and when it is false a booking cancellation does *not* reverse that product's sale entries — the passenger has bought something non-refundable and the airline keeps the money. `disclosable_fee` defaults to **true**, and when it is true a chargeable fee for the product must appear in the point-of-sale fee disclosure; a family that exposes an active chargeable attribute pointing at a product whose `disclosable_fee` is false cannot have a disclosure issued at all, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_UNDISCLOSED_FEE_CATEGORY`. `product_state` defaults to `ACTIVE` and may also be `WITHDRAWN`; a `WITHDRAWN` product is excluded from offer eligibility but remains referenced by history, so past sales and past family attributes continue to resolve against it. Catalog reads are available to every role, both as a single product and as a paginated list filterable by `category` and by `product_state`; a reverse lookup returns every family attribute that references one product, across all fare families, so an analyst can see the blast radius before repricing a catalog item.

A **fare family** is the sellable bundle. Creating one requires a `code` — 3 to 24 characters of uppercase A–Z, digits and underscore, unique across all families — a marketing `name`, a `cabin_class` of `ECONOMY`, `PREMIUM` or `BUSINESS`, a strictly positive `base_fare_cents` representing the air-transport component alone, a strictly positive `bundle_price_cents` representing the all-in sell price, a `refundability` of `NON_REFUNDABLE`, `REFUNDABLE_WITH_FEE` or `FULLY_REFUNDABLE`, a non-negative `change_fee_cents`, and a `boarding_priority_tier` between 1 and 4 where tier 1 boards first. An `upsell_parent_family_id` may optionally name the family this one upsells from; it defaults to null for the entry-level rung of a ladder. At creation the family lands in `status` `DRAFT` with `revision_no` 1, its `design_owner_user_id` stamped from the creating analyst's `X-User-ID`, its `reviewer_user_id` null because no decision has yet been recorded, its `fee_fingerprint` computed over an empty attribute set, and every computed valuation field — `unbundled_value_cents`, `a_la_carte_total_cents`, `bundle_discount_bp`, `included_attribute_count` and `publication_count` — at 0. Families can be read singly or listed with filters on `status`, on `cabin_class`, and on `upsell_parent_family_id` so that the children of one upsell rung can be enumerated.

Attributes are what make a family a bundle. Attaching one to a `DRAFT` family names the `ancillary_product_id` being attached, an `inclusion` of either `INCLUDED` or `CHARGEABLE`, and an `included_quantity` between 0 and 9. The two enum members mean opposite commercial things. An `INCLUDED` attribute is bundled into `bundle_price_cents` and must carry a quantity of at least 1. A `CHARGEABLE` attribute is sold separately, must therefore be disclosed as a fee, and must carry a quantity of exactly 0; a violation of either half of that pairing — at attachment or after a reprice — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ATTRIBUTE_QUANTITY`. Optionally the analyst supplies `override_price_cents`, a non-negative value that replaces the referenced product's list price for valuation purposes only, and `waiver_eligible`, which defaults to false and declares that this attribute's category may be waived by an irregular-operations waiver. Each attribute records the `fare_family_id` it belongs to, an `attribute_state` that defaults to `ACTIVE` and may become `WITHDRAWN`, and two computed money fields: `effective_unit_cents`, which is `override_price_cents` when that is not null and otherwise the referenced product's `list_price_cents`; and `attr_value_cents`, the attribute's extended contribution to the bundle. At most one attribute per family-and-product pair may be `ACTIVE` at a time, and a second attempt is refused with `ERR_ATTRIBUTE_DUPLICATE_PRODUCT`. Attributes are readable singly and as a paginated list filterable by `fare_family_id`, by `ancillary_product_id` and by `attribute_state`.

**Valuing the bundle.** Whenever the attribute set of a family changes — on attachment, on a reprice, and again inside the review submission — the family's valuation is rebuilt from scratch under what this document calls the **override basis**. Take every attribute of the family whose `attribute_state` is `ACTIVE`, ordered by the referenced product's `sku` ascending. Withdrawn attributes are excluded from every *family-level* output, including the fingerprint, but not from their own: a `WITHDRAWN` attribute keeps its `effective_unit_cents` and `attr_value_cents` populated, and both are recomputed by the very formulas below on every later rebuild of the family's valuation, even though the attribute contributes nothing to `unbundled_value_cents`, to `a_la_carte_total_cents`, to `included_attribute_count`, to the fingerprint or to an allocation weight. For each attribute in turn, take its effective unit price, multiply it by the attribute's `included_quantity`, multiply that by 10,000 plus the cabin uplift in basis points for the family's `cabin_class`, divide by 10,000, and round the result half up: that single integer is the attribute's `attr_value_cents`. The cabin uplift is nothing at all for `ECONOMY`, 1,500 basis points — a fifteen percent premium — for `PREMIUM`, and 3,000 basis points — a thirty percent premium — for `BUSINESS`. The rounding order is pinned: each attribute is rounded on its own, and only the rounded per-attribute values are summed. Never sum the un-rounded products. Because a `CHARGEABLE` attribute always carries a quantity of 0, it always contributes exactly 0 to the bundle value while still contributing to the fingerprint and to the disclosed fee total.

The family's `unbundled_value_cents` is the sum of those per-attribute values. Its `a_la_carte_total_cents` is `base_fare_cents` plus `unbundled_value_cents`. Its `included_attribute_count` is the number of `ACTIVE` attributes whose `inclusion` is `INCLUDED`. Its `bundle_discount_bp` — the basis points a passenger saves by buying the bundle instead of every component separately — is 0 when `unbundled_value_cents` is 0; otherwise it is `a_la_carte_total_cents` minus `bundle_price_cents`, multiplied by 10,000, divided by `a_la_carte_total_cents`, and rounded half up. Note that this second rounding is applied on top of the per-attribute roundings, so the discount is derived from already-rounded inputs.

There is a second, narrower basis for the same valuation, used in exactly one place. Under the **list basis**, an attribute's effective unit price is always the referenced product's `list_price_cents`, ignoring `override_price_cents` entirely. Everything else about the computation — the `ACTIVE`-only input set, the `sku` ascending order, the cabin uplift, the once-per-attribute rounding, the summation — is identical. The list basis is used only to populate a booking's `disclosed_unbundled_list_cents` at fee-disclosure time, for the regulatory reason given in §2.9. Every write that persists the family's own valuation fields, every write that persists an attribute's `attr_value_cents`, and every allocation weight used at ticketing all use the override basis.

Repricing or withdrawing a single attribute is permitted while the parent family's `status` is `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED`, and may supply any of `inclusion`, `included_quantity`, `override_price_cents`, `waiver_eligible` and `attribute_state`. On a `DRAFT` family it is a quiet edit: the attribute's `effective_unit_cents` and `attr_value_cents` are recomputed, the parent family's valuation outputs and `fee_fingerprint` are rebuilt, and the family's `version` increments. On a `PUBLISHED` family the very same request is the head of a four-way cascade, because the family's content has changed underneath everything that pinned it — provided the rebuild actually moves the fingerprint, since a reprice that leaves `fee_fingerprint` byte-identical, a `waiver_eligible`-only edit for instance, still increments the attribute's and the family's `version` but drifts no publication and stales no booking. Every publication of that family whose `parity_state` is `IN_PARITY` moves to `OUT_OF_PARITY` with `drift_reason` set to `ATTRIBUTE_REPRICED`, each such channel's `parity_breach_count` increases by one, and each such channel's `content_parity_status` is re-derived. Every booking on that family that is still `HELD` and whose `disclosure_state` is `PENDING_ACK` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` moves to `disclosure_state` `STALE`. Every offer still `PRESENTED` on those bookings moves to `SUPERSEDED`, because the price basis it was computed from no longer exists. And the family's `version` increments once for the whole operation.

### 3.2. Pricing Review, Publication and the Upsell Ladder

Submission for review is the analyst's hand-off, and it is the transition `DRAFT` → `IN_REVIEW`. Four conditions gate it, evaluated in the order §2.7 fixes, which puts the ownership check first of all. The acting user's `X-User-ID` must equal the family's `design_owner_user_id` — only the owning designer may submit — and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` at step 2, ahead of every state check, so a stranger aiming a submission at a family that is not even `DRAFT` is answered with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. The family must then currently be in `DRAFT`, or the request is refused with `ERR_FAMILY_STATE_INVALID`. The family must have at least one attribute whose `attribute_state` is `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_FAMILY_HAS_NO_ATTRIBUTES`. Finally, the valuation is recomputed inside the transition and the family's `bundle_price_cents` must be less than or equal to the freshly recomputed `a_la_carte_total_cents`; a bundle priced above the sum of its parts is refused with `ERR_BUNDLE_DISCOUNT_FLOOR_BREACH`. When all four hold, the submission recomputes the override-basis valuation into `unbundled_value_cents`, `a_la_carte_total_cents`, `bundle_discount_bp` and `included_attribute_count`, rebuilds `fee_fingerprint`, and increments the family's `version`.

A `PricingApprover` then records a decision, supplying `decision` — either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` — and optionally a `review_note`. The `review_note` is a free-text string that may be omitted altogether — an explicit null is refused as a shape failure under §2.7 — and it is accepted purely as an operator courtesy: it is stored nowhere. Neither branch of the decision persists it onto the fare family, and no response — the reviewed family included — ever carries it back. Two conditions gate the decision regardless of which way it goes, and §2.7 evaluates them in this order: the approver's `X-User-ID` must differ from the family's `design_owner_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; and then the family must be `IN_REVIEW`, or the request is refused with `ERR_FAMILY_STATE_INVALID` — so an approver deciding on a family it designed itself is told `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` even when that family is not `IN_REVIEW` either. This is the segregation-of-duties spine of the whole design process: the person who built the bundle may not be the person who signs it off, and the check is a straight comparison of the two user identifiers.

A **rejection** drives `IN_REVIEW` → `DRAFT` and carries four consequences plus the version bump. The family's `revision_no` increases by 1. Its `reviewer_user_id` is set to the deciding approver's `X-User-ID`. Its `fee_fingerprint` is rebuilt — and because `revision_no` is one of the fingerprint's components, the fingerprint *always* drifts on a rejection even when no attribute moved, which is precisely why a resubmission can never be byte-identical to the first attempt. Every publication of the family whose `parity_state` is `IN_PARITY` moves to `OUT_OF_PARITY` with `drift_reason` `FAMILY_REVISION_REJECTED`, and every booking on the family that is still `HELD` and whose `disclosure_state` is `PENDING_ACK` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` moves to `STALE`. Finally the family's `version` increments.

An **approval** drives `IN_REVIEW` → `PUBLISHED` with a deliberately different and smaller set of consequences. The family's `reviewer_user_id` is set to the deciding approver's `X-User-ID`. Every publication of the family whose `parity_state` is `PENDING` is promoted to `IN_PARITY`, pinning `published_fee_fingerprint` from the family's `fee_fingerprint` and `published_family_version` from the family's `version` **as of the end of this transition** — that is, after the version bump this transition performs, not before it. The family's `publication_count` is recomputed, counting the publications of this family whose `parity_state` is `PENDING`, `IN_PARITY` or `OUT_OF_PARITY`. And the family's `version` increments. Note the asymmetry against rejection: an approval does not touch `revision_no`, does not rebuild `fee_fingerprint`, and does not stale any booking's disclosure.

Once published, a family is moved between the three post-publication states by an approver supplying a `target_status` and a `reason`. The `reason` is mandatory input but, exactly like a review note, is stored nowhere: no field of the fare family records it and no response carries it back. Four moves are legal and no others; any other pair is refused with `ERR_FAMILY_STATE_INVALID`. `PUBLISHED` → `SUSPENDED` takes the family off sale and moves every `IN_PARITY` publication of it to `OUT_OF_PARITY` with `drift_reason` `FAMILY_SUSPENDED`. `SUSPENDED` → `PUBLISHED` puts it back on sale but — and this trips implementers — **does not** restore parity: publications stay `OUT_OF_PARITY`, and the only way back to `IN_PARITY` is an explicit resynchronisation of each publication. `PUBLISHED` → `RETIRED` and `SUSPENDED` → `RETIRED` both require that no booking on the family has a `booking_status` of `HELD`, `TICKETED` or `CHANGE_PENDING`, or the retirement is refused with `ERR_FAMILY_HAS_LIVE_BOOKINGS`; both then move every publication of the family whose `parity_state` is `PENDING`, `IN_PARITY` or `OUT_OF_PARITY` to `WITHDRAWN` and recompute `publication_count`, which consequently falls to 0. `RETIRED` is terminal. All four moves increment the family's `version`.

The **upsell ladder** is the self-referential structure over families, and it is edited only while a family is in `DRAFT`. A reposition may supply a new `name`, a new `upsell_parent_family_id`, both, or neither — a request that supplies no field at all is well-formed, changes no field, and still refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version`; the parent can only be re-pointed at another family, because an explicit null is refused as a shape failure under §2.7, so a reposition never returns a family to the entry-level rung. This operation deliberately does not touch the attribute set, so `fee_fingerprint` is unchanged by it — only `version` moves. The one hard constraint is acyclicity: following `upsell_parent_family_id` upward from the family being repositioned must never revisit a family already seen on the path, and a request that would close a loop is refused with `ERR_UPSELL_CYCLE_DETECTED`. The same acyclicity rule nominally applies at family creation, but at creation an unknown parent id fails earlier as `ERR_FARE_FAMILY_NOT_FOUND`, so in practice the cycle can only be provoked by re-parenting an existing draft family.

### 3.3. Flights, Seat Map Segmentation and the Demand Feed

An `OperationsController` opens the operational side of the system by creating a flight with a marketing `flight_no`, an `origin` and a `destination` each given as exactly three uppercase letters and required to differ from one another — an `origin` equal to the `destination` is a request-shape failure refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, not a semantic one — a `scheduled_departure_utc` instant in ISO 8601 UTC, and a strictly positive `cabin_seat_total` giving the physical seat count of the cabin. A new flight starts with `sales_phase` `PRESALE`, `irops_state` `NORMAL`, `booked_load_factor_pct` at 0, `demand_index_bp` at 0, `demand_tier` `LOW`, and both `active_waiver_count` and `ticketed_booking_count` at 0. Flights are readable singly and as a paginated list filterable by `sales_phase` and by `irops_state`.

A flight carries **two independent state dimensions**. `sales_phase` walks the retailing timeline `PRESALE` → `CHECKIN_OPEN` → `BOARDING` → `DEPARTED` and is terminal at `DEPARTED`. `irops_state` moves among `NORMAL`, `IRREGULAR` and `RECOVERED` and has no terminal state. They do not constrain each other: a flight can legitimately be `CHECKIN_OPEN` and `IRREGULAR`, or `BOARDING` and `RECOVERED`, and every combination that the individual dimensions permit is reachable.

Advancing the retailing phase takes a `target_phase` and moves the flight exactly one step; anything else is refused with `ERR_PHASE_TRANSITION_INVALID`. Each step closes the touchpoint it leaves behind. `PRESALE` → `CHECKIN_OPEN` expires every offer still `PRESENTED` whose `touchpoint` is `BOOKING`, on any booking of this flight. `CHECKIN_OPEN` → `BOARDING` expires every offer still `PRESENTED` whose `touchpoint` is `CHECKIN`. `BOARDING` → `DEPARTED` is the heavy one and does five things: it expires every offer still `PRESENTED` regardless of touchpoint; it moves every segment of the flight whose `segment_state` is `OPEN` to `CLOSED`; it moves every waiver on the flight whose `waiver_state` is `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED`, posting **no** reversal entries, so fees credited before departure stay credited; it moves every booking on the flight whose `booking_status` is `TICKETED` to `FLOWN`; and it recomputes `active_waiver_count` and `ticketed_booking_count`. Bookings left in `CHANGE_PENDING` at departure stay in `CHANGE_PENDING` — only ticketed bookings fly. Every phase advance increments the flight's `version`, and each cascaded entity's own `version` increments as §2.10 requires.

The revenue-management team feeds demand into the flight by publishing a snapshot carrying `booked_load_factor_pct`, an integer percentage from 0 to 100, and `demand_index_bp`, an integer in basis points from −5000 to 5000. These two fields are settable **only** through that snapshot; nothing else in the system writes them. Publishing a snapshot re-derives the flight's `demand_tier` — `HIGH` when `booked_load_factor_pct` is at least 80, `MID` when it is at least 60 but below 80, and `LOW` otherwise — and then reprices every seat segment on the flight, including segments already `CLOSED`, since a closed segment is still repriced even though it accepts no assignments. Every segment of the flight is mutated by that repricing — its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented — even when the recomputed `effective_price_cents` is identical to the one it already carried.

**Segmenting the seat map.** A seat inventory segment is a rectangle of the cabin: a closed range of rows crossed with a set of seat letters. Creating one names the `flight_id`, an `ancillary_product_id` whose product must have `category` `SEAT` (otherwise `ERR_SEAT_PRODUCT_CATEGORY_INVALID`), a `zone` of `FWD`, `MID`, `AFT`, `EXIT_ROW` or `BULKHEAD`, a `row_start` of at least 1, a `row_end` no smaller than `row_start` — a `row_end` below `row_start` is a request-shape failure refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and it is decided before the `flight_id` and `ancillary_product_id` lookups, so it outranks either not-found — a `seat_letters` array of between 1 and 6 distinct single uppercase letters drawn from A through F, and a non-negative `base_price_cents` giving the pre-zone, pre-demand seat price. The row interval is **closed** at both ends: a segment with `row_start` 10 and `row_end` 12 covers rows 10, 11 and 12. The segment's `total_seats` is therefore `row_end` minus `row_start` plus one, multiplied by the number of entries in `seat_letters`. At creation `available_seats` equals `total_seats`, `held_seats` and `sold_seats` are 0, and `segment_state` is `OPEN`.

Two capacity rules guard segment creation. First, the new segment's rectangle must share no individual seat with any existing segment on the same flight whose `segment_state` is `OPEN`; overlap is refused with `ERR_SEAT_SEGMENT_ROW_OVERLAP`. The test is per seat, not per row range, so two segments may cover the very same rows as long as their letter sets are disjoint, and two segments with adjacent but non-overlapping row ranges never collide. A `CLOSED` segment leaves this candidate set entirely and cannot block a new segment. Second, the sum of `total_seats` across every segment of the flight, counting the one being created, may not exceed the flight's `cabin_seat_total`; exceeding it is refused with `ERR_SEGMENT_EXCEEDS_CABIN`. Segments are readable singly and as a paginated list filterable by `flight_id`, by `ancillary_product_id`, by `zone` and by `segment_state`.

**Pricing a segment.** A segment's `effective_price_cents` is derived on creation and re-derived on every demand snapshot of its parent flight, in three stages with exactly one rounding at the very end. First, a row-band adjustment is chosen from the segment's `row_start`: 1,500 cents when `row_start` is 5 or lower, 500 cents when `row_start` is between 6 and 15 inclusive, and nothing at all when `row_start` is 16 or higher. That adjustment is added to `base_price_cents` to give an exact integer zone base — no rounding happens here, because nothing has been divided yet. Second, two multipliers are selected: the zone multiplier stored on the segment as `zone_multiplier_bp`, which is 13,000 basis points for `FWD`, 10,000 for `MID`, 9,000 for `AFT`, 16,000 for `EXIT_ROW` and 14,000 for `BULKHEAD`; and the demand multiplier for the flight's current demand tier, which is 10,000 basis points at `LOW`, 11,000 at `MID` and 12,500 at `HIGH`. Third — and this is the pinned part — the exact integer zone base is multiplied by both multipliers, the product is divided by one hundred million (that is, by 10,000 twice, once for each basis-point factor), and only that final quotient is rounded half up. The zone and demand multipliers are applied to the exact rational product, never to an intermediate rounded value; rounding after the first multiplication and again after the second gives a different and incorrect answer.

### 3.4. Distribution Channels and Publishing Family Content

A `DistributionManager` registers each selling path as a distribution channel with a unique `channel_code`, a `channel_type` of `DIRECT`, `GDS`, `OTA` or `NDC`, a `commission_bp` between 0 and 3,000 basis points applied to the settlement subtotal, a `markup_bp` between 0 and 2,000 basis points applied inside the dynamic ancillary price, a strictly positive `max_ancillary_price_cents` that is the hard ceiling this channel will accept for a single accepted ancillary offer, and a non-empty `supported_categories` array listing which of `BAGGAGE`, `SEAT`, `CHANGE`, `BOARDING` and `ONBOARD` the channel can merchandise and fee-disclose. A `parity_required` flag defaults to true and decides whether a drifted publication breaches the whole channel or merely drifts on its own. A new channel starts `COMPLIANT` with a `parity_breach_count` of 0. Channels are readable singly and as a paginated list filterable by `channel_type` and by `content_parity_status`. The `id` returned when a channel is registered is exactly the value a `ChannelPartner` presents as its `X-User-ID`.

Publishing a family to a channel creates a channel publication naming the `fare_family_id`, the `distribution_channel_id` and the mandatory `expected_fare_family_version` described in §2.6. At most one publication per family-and-channel pair may be in `PENDING`, `IN_PARITY` or `OUT_OF_PARITY`; a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PUBLICATION`, though a pair whose only prior publication is `WITHDRAWN` may be published again. The new publication's initial `parity_state` depends on the family: if the family's `status` is already `PUBLISHED`, the publication is promoted to `IN_PARITY` within the same request; otherwise it waits in `PENDING` until the family is approved. Either way, `published_fee_fingerprint` and `published_family_version` are pinned from the family, the family's `publication_count` is recomputed, and the family's `version` increments. The pin is taken **after** that increment: the family's `version` moves first and `published_family_version` records the post-increment number, so it is one greater than the `expected_fare_family_version` the request had to supply.

Parity is lost — `IN_PARITY` → `OUT_OF_PARITY` — by exactly three upstream events, and the `drift_reason` recorded tells them apart: repricing or withdrawing one of the family's attributes stamps `ATTRIBUTE_REPRICED`, a rejected review decision stamps `FAMILY_REVISION_REJECTED`, and a suspension stamps `FAMILY_SUSPENDED`. The condition underneath all three is the same: the family's live `fee_fingerprint` no longer equals the publication's `published_fee_fingerprint`, or the family has left `PUBLISHED`. Losing parity has four consequences beyond the state change itself. The owning channel's `parity_breach_count` increases by 1. The owning channel's `content_parity_status` becomes `BREACHED` when that channel's `parity_required` is true. Every offer still `PRESENTED` on a booking whose `fare_family_id` and `distribution_channel_id` both match this publication moves to `SUPERSEDED`. And the publication's own `version` increments.

Recovering parity is always explicit. A resynchronisation moves the publication `OUT_OF_PARITY` → `IN_PARITY`, and requires both that the publication is currently `OUT_OF_PARITY` — otherwise `ERR_PUBLICATION_STATE_INVALID` — and that the family's `status` is `PUBLISHED` — otherwise `ERR_FAMILY_NOT_PUBLISHED`. On success `published_fee_fingerprint` and `published_family_version` are re-pinned to the family's live values, `drift_reason` is cleared to null, `last_resync_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the channel's `content_parity_status` is re-derived, returning to `COMPLIANT` once no publication of that channel remains `OUT_OF_PARITY`. `parity_breach_count` is deliberately untouched: it is monotonic and never decreases. Publications are readable singly and as a paginated list filterable by `fare_family_id`, by `distribution_channel_id` and by `parity_state`.

Retiring a family withdraws its content everywhere. Every publication of the family in `PENDING`, `IN_PARITY` or `OUT_OF_PARITY` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, `publication_count` is recomputed down to 0, and each affected channel's `content_parity_status` is re-derived — so a retired family's formerly drifted publications no longer breach their channel. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal for a publication.

### 3.5. Selling: Booking, Fee Disclosure and Ticketing

A `ChannelPartner` opens a sale by creating a booking with a `record_locator` of exactly six characters drawn from uppercase A–Z and the digits, a `flight_id`, a `fare_family_id`, a `distribution_channel_id` and a `passenger_name`. The record locator must be unique across every stored booking, including bookings that have already reached `CANCELLED` or `FLOWN`; a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECORD_LOCATOR`. The supplied `distribution_channel_id` must equal the acting partner's own `X-User-ID` — a partner cannot open a booking on somebody else's channel — and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. The flight must not yet have departed. A new booking starts with `booking_status` `HELD`, `disclosure_state` `NOT_ISSUED`, `fare_component_cents` and `pending_change_fee_cents` both null, `change_count` at 0, `change_fee_waived_cents` at 0, `waiver_applied` false, and every derived money field — `ancillary_gross_cents`, `recognized_ancillary_net_cents`, `commission_cents`, `incentive_credit_cents` and `channel_settlement_total_cents` — at 0. Bookings are readable singly and as a paginated list filterable by `flight_id`, by `fare_family_id`, by `distribution_channel_id`, by `booking_status` and by `disclosure_state`, with the channel-scoping behaviour of §2.1 applied to both.

**Issuing the disclosure.** Before a ticket can be issued, the partner must issue the regulatory point-of-sale fee disclosure, which drives `disclosure_state` from `NOT_ISSUED` to `PENDING_ACK`, and — importantly — also drives it from `STALE` back to `PENDING_ACK` when the content has drifted and must be re-disclosed. In both cases the booking must still be `HELD`, or the request is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID`, and the acting partner must own the booking's channel. The `disclosure_state` itself is not guarded: issuance is accepted from `PENDING_ACK` and from `ACKNOWLEDGED` as well, and because it always resets `disclosure_acked_at` to null and drives `disclosure_state` back to `PENDING_ACK`, re-issuing over an acknowledgement discards that acknowledgement and re-snapshots the four values against the family's content as it stands at that moment. Issuance from `NOT_ISSUED` carries two further content checks, and only from `NOT_ISSUED`: every attribute of the booking's fare family that is `ACTIVE` and `CHARGEABLE` must reference a product whose `disclosable_fee` is true, or the request is refused with `ERR_UNDISCLOSED_FEE_CATEGORY`; and every such attribute must reference a product whose `category` is a member of the channel's `supported_categories`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_CATEGORY_UNSUPPORTED`. The undisclosed-fee check is evaluated first, so an attribute that violates both is answered with `ERR_UNDISCLOSED_FEE_CATEGORY`.

Issuance snapshots four values onto the booking. `disclosed_fee_fingerprint` takes the family's live `fee_fingerprint`. `disclosed_unbundled_list_cents` takes the family's bundle valuation computed under the **list basis** of §3.1 — catalog list prices, with every `override_price_cents` ignored. `disclosed_total_fee_cents` takes the sum of `effective_unit_cents` over every attribute of the family that is `ACTIVE` and `CHARGEABLE`, plus the family's `change_fee_cents`; note that this total uses the *override-basis* effective unit price, unlike `disclosed_unbundled_list_cents`, and that the change fee is always included because a change fee is a chargeable fee the passenger may face. `disclosure_issued_at` takes the logical clock. Re-issuance from `STALE` performs exactly the same four snapshots, computed against the drifted content, and additionally resets `disclosure_acked_at` back to null, because a fresh acknowledgement is now required. Both paths increment the booking's `version`.

**Acknowledging the disclosure.** The passenger's acknowledgement carries `acknowledged_fee_fingerprint` and drives `disclosure_state` from `PENDING_ACK` to `ACKNOWLEDGED`. The booking's `disclosure_state` must be `PENDING_ACK`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_STATE_INVALID`; the acting partner must own the booking's channel; and the supplied fingerprint must equal the booking's `disclosed_fee_fingerprint` byte for byte, or the request is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_FINGERPRINT_MISMATCH`. Note the deliberate distinction: the acknowledgement is compared against the *snapshotted* fingerprint, not against the family's live one. On success `disclosure_acked_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the booking's `version` increments.

Whenever a fare family's `fee_fingerprint` changes — through a rejected review decision, through an attribute reprice or withdrawal, or through any other write that rebuilds it — every booking on that family that is still `HELD` and whose `disclosure_state` is `PENDING_ACK` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` is driven to `STALE`. The two source states behave identically: from `PENDING_ACK` the booking becomes `STALE`, and from `ACKNOWLEDGED` the booking becomes `STALE`. In both cases the `disclosed_fee_fingerprint`, `disclosed_total_fee_cents`, `disclosed_unbundled_list_cents`, `disclosure_issued_at` and `disclosure_acked_at` snapshots are preserved exactly as they were, so an auditor can see both what was disclosed and what it should have been; and in both cases the booking's `version` increments. Bookings already `TICKETED` are untouched by this cascade.

**Ticketing.** Issuing the ticket drives `booking_status` from `HELD` to `TICKETED` and is the single most heavily guarded operation in the system. Nine conditions must hold, and §2.7 fixes the order in which they are evaluated so that a booking failing several of them always reports the same error. The acting partner's `X-User-ID` must equal the booking's `distribution_channel_id` (`ERR_CHANNEL_SCOPE_VIOLATION`), and this scope check comes first of the nine, ahead of the state check. The booking must be `HELD` (`ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID`). The flight's `sales_phase` must be `PRESALE` or `CHECKIN_OPEN` (`ERR_FLIGHT_SALES_PHASE_CLOSED`). The family's `status` must be `PUBLISHED` (`ERR_FAMILY_NOT_PUBLISHED`). The flight's `irops_state` must not be `IRREGULAR` (`ERR_IROPS_SALES_BLOCKED`). The booking's `disclosure_state` must be neither `NOT_ISSUED` nor `PENDING_ACK` (`ERR_FEE_DISCLOSURE_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`, HTTP 422). The booking's `disclosure_state` must not be `STALE` and its `disclosed_fee_fingerprint` must equal the family's live `fee_fingerprint` (`ERR_FEE_DISCLOSURE_STALE`). A publication must exist for the booking's family-and-channel pair whose `parity_state` is `IN_PARITY` (`ERR_FAMILY_NOT_PUBLISHED_TO_CHANNEL`). And the channel's `content_parity_status` must be `COMPLIANT` (`ERR_CHANNEL_CONTENT_PARITY_BREACH`).

When all nine hold, ticketing performs seven things in order. It pins `fare_component_cents` from the family's `bundle_price_cents` at that instant, freezing the fare component against later family edits. It runs the ticketing allocation described below over that pinned amount, posting one `ALLOCATION` revenue entry per weighted category, each with a `source_ref` of `ticket:` followed by the booking's id. It moves every seat assignment on the booking whose `assignment_state` is `HELD` to `CONFIRMED`, with the inventory and revenue consequences described in §3.7. It recomputes the booking's `recognized_ancillary_net_cents` and `ancillary_gross_cents` from that booking's revenue entries. It runs the channel settlement calculation of §3.9 into `commission_cents`, `incentive_credit_cents` and `channel_settlement_total_cents`. It recomputes the flight's `ticketed_booking_count`, which counts the bookings on that flight whose `booking_status` is `TICKETED` or `CHANGE_PENDING`. And it increments the booking's `version`.

**The ticketing allocation.** The pinned `fare_component_cents` is split across revenue categories in three passes, and the split must sum back to the pinned amount exactly. First, the weights. Each ancillary category's weight is the sum of `attr_value_cents` over the family's attributes that are `ACTIVE` and `INCLUDED` whose product falls in that category — `CHARGEABLE` and `WITHDRAWN` attributes carry no weight at all, since they are not part of what the bundle price bought. The synthetic `AIR_TRANSPORT` category's weight is the family's `base_fare_cents`. The total weight is the family's `a_la_carte_total_cents`, which by construction equals `base_fare_cents` plus `unbundled_value_cents`. The buckets to be posted are exactly those categories whose weight is greater than zero, iterated in the fixed category priority order `BAGGAGE`, `SEAT`, `CHANGE`, `BOARDING`, `ONBOARD`, `AIR_TRANSPORT`.

Pass one prorates by **flooring**, never by rounding half up: each bucket's gross amount is the pinned `fare_component_cents` multiplied by that bucket's weight, divided by the total weight, and truncated downward to a whole number of cents. The remainder is the pinned amount minus the sum of the floored gross amounts. Pass two distributes that remainder **one cent at a time**, walking the buckets in descending order of weight and breaking ties by the bucket's index in the category priority order ascending; because the remainder is always strictly less than the number of buckets, each bucket receives at most one extra cent. Pass three taxes each bucket: the bucket's tax is its gross amount multiplied by the tax rate in basis points for the bucket's tax treatment, divided by 10,000, and rounded half up; the bucket's net is its gross minus its tax. Each bucket then becomes one revenue entry with `entry_type` `ALLOCATION`.

The metadata each allocation entry carries is snapshotted from a specific contributing attribute: for an ancillary bucket, the entry's `tax_treatment`, `revenue_class`, `revenue_account_code` and `product_sku` are taken from the contributing attribute whose product's `sku` is lexicographically **smallest** within that category. The `AIR_TRANSPORT` bucket instead uses `tax_treatment` `TAXABLE_TRANSPORT`, `revenue_class` `AIR_TRANSPORT_RELATED`, `revenue_account_code` `3000` and `product_sku` `AIR_TRANSPORT`. The governing invariant is that the sum of the buckets' gross amounts equals the pinned `fare_component_cents` exactly — which is why pass one floors and pass two owns the residue.

### 3.6. Merchandising Sessions: Candidate Selection, Dynamic Pricing and Acceptance

Merchandising happens in **sessions**. A `ChannelPartner` opens a session on one of its own bookings, declaring a `touchpoint` of `BOOKING`, `CHECKIN` or `INFLIGHT` and optionally narrowing the pool with a `requested_product_ids` array. The requested touchpoint must match the flight's `sales_phase` exactly — `BOOKING` requires `PRESALE`, `CHECKIN` requires `CHECKIN_OPEN`, and `INFLIGHT` requires `BOARDING` — and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_TOUCHPOINT_PHASE_MISMATCH`. The booking must be `HELD` or `TICKETED`. Every category named through `requested_product_ids` must be a member of the channel's `supported_categories`, or the session is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_CATEGORY_UNSUPPORTED`. And if any explicitly requested product is still inside its post-decline cool-off on this booking, the session is refused with `ERR_OFFER_COOLOFF_CONFLICT` rather than quietly dropping it — the partner asked for it by name and deserves to be told why it cannot have it.

**Building the candidate pool.** A catalog product is a candidate for this session only if all eight of the following hold. Its `product_state` is `ACTIVE`. Its `category` is a member of the channel's `supported_categories`. If the request supplied `requested_product_ids`, the product's id is a member of that list. The booking's fare family has no `ACTIVE`, `INCLUDED` attribute pointing at this product — you do not sell somebody a bag allowance they already bought inside the bundle. The booking has no offer for this product whose `offer_status` is `ACCEPTED` — you do not sell it twice. The booking has no offer for this product whose `offer_status` is `PRESENTED` — it is already on the table. The product is not inside its cool-off: that is, there is no offer for this product on this booking whose `offer_status` is `DECLINED` and whose `updated_at` plus thirty minutes is strictly later than the logical clock. And, in the two negative cases, the product's `category` is not `CHANGE` while the `touchpoint` is `BOOKING` — no change fee may be merchandised before a ticket exists — and there is no waiver on the booking's flight whose `waiver_state` is `ACTIVE` and whose `waiver_scope` contains the product's `category`, since a category currently being waived cannot simultaneously be sold.

**Pricing each candidate.** Every candidate is priced independently, and the price is built in two stages. Stage one produces a willingness-to-pay score in basis points, and this is where the calculation forks into two mutually exclusive variants, recorded on the resulting offer as `pricing_variant`.

The `RESIDUAL_INVENTORY` variant applies when — and only when — the product's `category` is `SEAT` **and** at least one seat segment on the booking's flight referencing that product has `segment_state` `OPEN`. It reads physical seat scarcity and ignores the flight's demand feed entirely. Sum `total_seats` across those open segments to get a basis total, sum `available_seats` across the same segments to get a basis available, subtract the available from the total, multiply by 100, divide by the basis total, and round half up: that is the sold percentage. The raw score is then 10,000 basis points plus 60 basis points for every percentage point by which the sold percentage exceeds 50 — equivalently, 10,000 plus 60 multiplied by the sold percentage minus 50, which goes negative when less than half the seats have gone.

The `WTP_ELASTIC` variant applies in every other case: whenever the product's `category` is anything other than `SEAT`, **or** when no seat segment on the flight for that product is `OPEN`. It reads the revenue-management feed instead. The raw score is 10,000 basis points, plus 40 basis points for every percentage point by which the flight's `booked_load_factor_pct` exceeds 50 — that is, plus 40 multiplied by `booked_load_factor_pct` minus 50 — plus a scarcity term formed by multiplying the flight's `demand_index_bp` by 60, dividing by 10,000 and rounding half up. Because the two variants read different inputs, identical flight data can and does yield a different `offered_price_cents` for a seat product depending on whether an open segment exists for it.

Whichever variant produced it, the raw score is then clamped into the band from 6,000 to 16,000 basis points and stored as `wtp_score_bp`: anything below 6,000 becomes 6,000 and anything above 16,000 becomes 16,000.

Stage two is a chain of **three sequential roundings followed by a clamp**, and the order is pinned. First, multiply the product's `list_price_cents` by `wtp_score_bp`, divide by 10,000, and round half up. Second, multiply that result by the touchpoint multiplier — 10,000 basis points at `BOOKING`, 11,000 at `CHECKIN`, and 12,500 at `INFLIGHT`, so an in-flight impulse buy carries a twenty-five percent uplift over the same product at booking — divide by 10,000, and round half up again. Third, multiply that result by 10,000 plus the channel's `markup_bp`, divide by 10,000, and round half up a third time. Folding the three multipliers into a single product and rounding once diverges from this specification and is wrong.

The chained result is then clamped between a floor and a cap derived from the product's own list price, not from the chain: the floor is `list_price_cents` multiplied by 7,000, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up — seventy percent of list — and the cap is `list_price_cents` multiplied by 18,000, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up — one hundred and eighty percent of list. The offer's `offered_price_cents` is the chained price raised to the floor if it fell below it and lowered to the cap if it rose above it. Finally, `expected_value_cents` is `offered_price_cents` multiplied by `wtp_score_bp`, divided by 10,000 and rounded half up; this is the ranking key. Note explicitly what is **not** applied here: the channel's `max_ancillary_price_cents` plays no part in pricing, so a price this channel will refuse to settle can legitimately be presented and only rejected later.

**Ranking, truncation and the empty pool.** Candidates are ranked by `expected_value_cents` descending. Ties are broken first by the index of the product's `category` in the priority order `BAGGAGE`, `SEAT`, `CHANGE`, `BOARDING`, `ONBOARD`, `AIR_TRANSPORT` ascending, then by the product's `created_at` ascending, and finally — because the logical clock makes it ordinary for a whole catalog to share a single `created_at` — by the order in which the server created the products, oldest first; never by the product's `id`. The first three entries of the ranked list, at most, are presented; each becomes an offer with `offer_status` `PRESENTED`, a `sequence_rank` of 1 through the number presented in ranked order, a `valid_from` equal to the logical clock, and a `valid_until` exactly one hour later. The validity window is half-open: the clock may equal `valid_from` but must be strictly before `valid_until`. The session responds with the `touchpoint` it ran at, a `candidate_count` giving the size of the eligible pool **before** truncation, a `presented_offer_ids` array of the ids created in ranked order, and an empty `suppression_reason`. When the candidate pool comes back empty, the session still succeeds: it answers with an empty `presented_offer_ids` array, a `candidate_count` of 0 and a `suppression_reason` of `NO_ELIGIBLE_CANDIDATES`, and creates no offer rows at all. A session mutates no pre-existing row of any kind: it only inserts offers, so the booking's own `updated_at` and `version` are exactly what they were before the session ran, and a `version` read before a session is still the one an acceptance must quote as `expected_booking_version`. Offers are readable singly and as a paginated list filterable by `booking_id`, by `ancillary_product_id`, by `touchpoint` and by `offer_status`, channel-scoped per §2.1.

**Accepting an offer.** An acceptance drives `offer_status` from `PRESENTED` to `ACCEPTED`, and carries the mandatory `expected_booking_version` of §2.6. Eight conditions gate it, in the order §2.7 fixes: the acting partner must own the owning booking's channel (`ERR_CHANNEL_SCOPE_VIOLATION`); the booking's `booking_status` must be `HELD` or `TICKETED` (`ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID`); the offer must be `PRESENTED` (`ERR_OFFER_STATE_INVALID`); the logical clock must be at or after `valid_from` and strictly before `valid_until` (`ERR_OFFER_WINDOW_CLOSED`); `expected_booking_version` must equal the booking's `version` (`ERR_STALE_BOOKING_VERSION`, HTTP 412); the channel's `content_parity_status` must be `COMPLIANT` (`ERR_CHANNEL_CONTENT_PARITY_BREACH`); the offered product's `category` must still be a member of the channel's `supported_categories` (`ERR_CHANNEL_CATEGORY_UNSUPPORTED`, HTTP 422), which matters because a channel may have narrowed its categories after the session presented the offer; and the offer's `offered_price_cents` must be at most the channel's `max_ancillary_price_cents` (`ERR_OFFER_PRICE_CAP_EXCEEDED`, HTTP 422). On success, `accepted_price_cents` is frozen from `offered_price_cents`; one `SALE` revenue entry is posted whose `category` is the product's `category`, whose `gross_cents` is the accepted price, whose tax and reporting metadata are snapshotted from the product, and whose `source_ref` is `offer:` followed by the offer's id; every *other* offer on the same booking for the same `ancillary_product_id` that is still `PRESENTED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`; the booking's `recognized_ancillary_net_cents`, `ancillary_gross_cents` and settlement figures are recomputed; and both the booking's `version` and the offer's `version` increment.

**Declining an offer.** A decline drives `offer_status` from `PRESENTED` to `DECLINED`. It requires only that the offer is `PRESENTED`, that the acting partner owns the booking's channel, and that the logical clock still lies inside the half-open validity window. Its side effect is deceptively small but functionally important: the offer's `updated_at` is set to the logical clock, and that timestamp anchors a thirty-minute cool-off during which the same product cannot be re-offered on the same booking. The offer's `version` increments. No revenue moves.

**The two ways an offer dies without a decision.** An offer becomes `EXPIRED` when its window or its touchpoint closes. Advancing the logical clock to a value at or after an offer's `valid_until` sweeps that offer to `EXPIRED` deterministically as part of the clock-setting call, so no test ever needs to wait on real time; the call reports how many offers it swept as `expired_offer_count`. Advancing a flight's retailing phase likewise expires the offers whose touchpoint the new phase closes: reaching `CHECKIN_OPEN` closes `BOOKING`, reaching `BOARDING` closes `CHECKIN`, and reaching `DEPARTED` closes every touchpoint. Expiry posts no revenue, makes the offer terminal, and frees the session slot it occupied. An offer becomes `SUPERSEDED` instead when the priced content changed underneath it: an attribute reprice or withdrawal, a rejected review decision, the declaration of an irregular-operations waiver covering its category, a parity loss on its channel publication, a sibling acceptance of the same product, or the cancellation of its booking. Superseding also posts no revenue; a fresh session is required to re-merchandise the product. The distinction matters and is tested: `EXPIRED` means the window or touchpoint closed, `SUPERSEDED` means the content moved. Every one of these transitions increments the offer's `version`, and `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED` and `SUPERSEDED` are all terminal.

### 3.7. Seat Assignment and Release

Assigning a seat names the `seat_inventory_segment_id` the seat comes from, the `row` and the `seat_letter`. Four checks guard it in the precedence §2.7 fixes: the segment's `segment_state` must be `OPEN` (`ERR_SEGMENT_CLOSED`); the requested `row` must be at least the segment's `row_start` and at most its `row_end`, and the requested `seat_letter` must be a member of the segment's `seat_letters` (`ERR_SEAT_ROW_OUT_OF_SEGMENT`); the segment's `available_seats` must be at least 1 (`ERR_SEAT_INVENTORY_EXHAUSTED`); and no assignment already `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` may occupy that same segment, row and letter (`ERR_SEAT_ALREADY_OCCUPIED`). The relative order of the last two is deliberate and observable: a request against a segment that is entirely held or entirely sold answers with the capacity error, not the occupancy error, even though both would be true. Note also that a departed flight does not surface here as a departure error — departure closes every segment, so the closed-segment check answers first. Two coarser gates run ahead of all four: the acting partner must own the booking's channel, and the booking's `booking_status` must be `HELD`, `TICKETED` or `CHANGE_PENDING`, or the request is refused with `ERR_BOOKING_STATE_INVALID` before the segment is examined at all. Nothing, however, requires the segment to sit on the booking's own flight: no check compares the two and no error id in §5 describes such a mismatch, so an assignment that crosses flights is accepted.

Pricing the seat has one exception worth stating plainly. The assignment's `price_charged_cents` is normally the segment's `effective_price_cents`, pinned at the instant the seat is taken so that a later demand snapshot cannot retroactively change what the passenger was charged. But when the booking already holds an offer with `offer_status` `ACCEPTED` for the very product the segment references, the seat is priced at **0** — the passenger has already paid for seat selection through the ancillary offer and must not be billed a second time.

The assignment's initial `assignment_state` depends on where the booking is. On a `HELD` booking the seat is taken as `HELD` and the segment's `held_seats` increases by one. On a `TICKETED` or `CHANGE_PENDING` booking the seat is auto-confirmed in the same request: `assignment_state` is `CONFIRMED`, the segment's `sold_seats` increases by one while `held_seats` is left untouched — the row is never counted as held on this path — and, when `price_charged_cents` is greater than zero, one `SALE` revenue entry with `category` `SEAT`, `gross_cents` equal to `price_charged_cents` and `source_ref` of `seat:` followed by the assignment's id is posted immediately. The rest of the auto-confirm path is **not** conditional on that price: the booking's money fields and settlement figures are recomputed and the booking's `updated_at` and `version` move whether or not a `SALE` was written, so a seat priced at 0 because the passenger prepaid through an accepted offer still bumps the booking's `version` even though no revenue entry exists. The `HELD` path is the opposite: it leaves the booking itself entirely alone, recomputing nothing and bumping no booking `version`. In both cases the segment's `available_seats` is re-derived and the segment's own `version` increments. Assignments are readable singly and as a paginated list filterable by `booking_id`, by `seat_inventory_segment_id` and by `assignment_state`, channel-scoped per §2.1.

A `HELD` assignment is also confirmed as a side effect of ticketing its booking. On that path the accounting differs from the auto-confirm path above, because the seat *was* previously counted as held: the segment's `held_seats` decreases by one and its `sold_seats` increases by one. As before, a `price_charged_cents` greater than zero posts one `SALE` entry with `category` `SEAT`, `gross_cents` equal to `price_charged_cents` and a `source_ref` of `seat:` followed by the assignment's id; the segment's `available_seats` is re-derived; and the assignment's `version` increments.

Releasing gives a seat back to inventory and requires a `release_reason` in the request. An assignment must be `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` to be released, and a release of an already-`RELEASED` assignment is refused with `ERR_SEAT_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`; releasing a `CONFIRMED` assignment additionally requires that the flight has not departed (`ERR_FLIGHT_ALREADY_DEPARTED`). The two source states have deliberately different money consequences. Releasing from `HELD` decreases the segment's `held_seats` by one and increases its `available_seats` by one, and posts and reverses **nothing**, because a held seat was never sold. Releasing from `CONFIRMED` decreases the segment's `sold_seats` by one and increases its `available_seats` by one, and — when `price_charged_cents` is greater than zero — posts one `REFUND` entry mirroring the seat sale, with `gross_cents` equal to the negated `price_charged_cents`, `tax_cents` equal to the negated tax of the original sale, and a `source_ref` of `seat:` followed by the assignment's id; the booking's money fields and settlement figures are then recomputed. Either way the assignment's `version` increments and `RELEASED` is terminal.

The `released_reason` recorded on the assignment says which of three paths gave the seat back, and it is null while the assignment is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED`. An explicit release stamps `PASSENGER_RELEASE`. A release cascaded from a booking cancellation stamps `BOOKING_CANCELLED`. A release cascaded from a diversion waiver stamps `IROPS_REACCOMMODATION`.

### 3.8. Changes, Cancellation and Irregular Operations

The change cycle is the one place where a booking's sales status is genuinely cyclic. Opening a change drives `booking_status` from `TICKETED` to `CHANGE_PENDING`; it requires that the booking is `TICKETED`, that the acting partner owns its channel, and that the flight has not departed. Its only effect beyond the state change and the version bump is to quote the fee: `pending_change_fee_cents` is set to the family's `change_fee_cents` less the booking's accumulated `change_fee_waived_cents`, floored at zero so a waiver larger than the fee never produces a credit. This is a quote, frozen at open time.

Settling the change drives `booking_status` back from `CHANGE_PENDING` to `TICKETED`, and the supplied `resolution` decides what happens to the money. Both branches require that the booking is `CHANGE_PENDING` and that the acting partner owns its channel, and both null out `pending_change_fee_cents` and increment the booking's `version`. On `CONFIRM`, and only when the quoted `pending_change_fee_cents` is greater than zero, one `SALE` revenue entry is posted with `category` `CHANGE`, `gross_cents` equal to the quoted fee, `tax_treatment` `TAXABLE_SERVICE`, `revenue_class` `NON_AIR`, `revenue_account_code` `4300`, `product_sku` `CHANGE_SETTLEMENT` and `source_ref` of `change:` followed by the booking's id; the booking's `change_count` increases by one; and `recognized_ancillary_net_cents`, `ancillary_gross_cents` and the settlement figures are all recomputed. Only the revenue entry is conditional on the quote: on `CONFIRM` the `change_count` increase and the money recomputation happen even when the quoted fee was zero and no entry was posted. On `ABANDON`, `change_count` is left alone and no revenue entry is posted at all. Because the cycle can repeat, and because `change_fee_waived_cents` may have grown between two changes, each successive quote can be lower than the last.

**Cancellation.** Cancelling takes a mandatory `cancel_reason` — required in the request body but recorded on no field of the booking and echoed back by no response — and is legal from three states — `HELD`, `TICKETED` and `CHANGE_PENDING` — with `CANCELLED` terminal. Every path releases every seat assignment of the booking that is still `HELD` or `CONFIRMED`, stamping `released_reason` `BOOKING_CANCELLED`; moves every offer on the booking still `PRESENTED` to `SUPERSEDED`; and reverses refundable ancillary revenue by posting, for every `SALE` entry of the booking whose product's `refundable` flag is true, one mirroring `REFUND` entry — the source sales being processed in `created_at` ascending order so the ledger reads chronologically. Sales of non-refundable products are simply not reversed. The flag is read from the catalog product whose `sku` matches the entry's `product_sku`, so a `SALE` matching no catalog product at all — the `CHANGE_SETTLEMENT` change fee — is never reversed either. This pass is also deliberately unguarded against the seat releases that precede it: unlike the diversion cascade below it does not skip a seat sale that the accompanying release has already mirrored, so a confirmed seat whose product is `refundable` is reversed twice, once by the release and once here. Cancelling from `HELD` releases only `HELD` assignments, since no assignment on a held booking can yet be confirmed.

Cancelling from `TICKETED` does everything above and then decides the fate of the fare component, which is where `refundability` earns its keep. When the family's `refundability` is `FULLY_REFUNDABLE`, one mirroring `REFUND` entry is posted for **every** `ALLOCATION` entry of the booking, the source allocations processed in `created_at` ascending order. When the family's `refundability` is `NON_REFUNDABLE` or `REFUNDABLE_WITH_FEE`, the `ALLOCATION` entries are **not** reversed and the airline keeps the fare component. Cancelling from `TICKETED` also recomputes the flight's `ticketed_booking_count`. Cancelling from `CHANGE_PENDING` performs exactly the same seat-release, offer-supersede, refund and recount work as the `TICKETED` path, and additionally nulls out `pending_change_fee_cents`. All three paths recompute `recognized_ancillary_net_cents`, `ancillary_gross_cents` and the settlement figures, and increment the booking's `version`.

**Declaring an irregular-operations waiver.** When a flight goes wrong, an `OperationsController` declares a waiver naming the `event_type` — `DELAY`, `CANCELLATION` or `DIVERSION` — a `delay_minutes` between 0 and 1440, and a non-empty `waiver_scope` array of the ancillary categories whose posted fees are to be credited and whose future fees are to be waived. Where `CHANGE` is in scope and the `event_type` is `DELAY`, `delay_minutes` must be at least 180 — a delay of three hours or more — or the declaration is refused with `ERR_DELAY_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`. The flight must not have departed. The waiver is stamped `declared_at` from the logical clock, starts in `waiver_state` `ACTIVE`, and drives the flight's `irops_state` to `IRREGULAR` from either `NORMAL` or `RECOVERED`, recomputing the flight's `active_waiver_count` and incrementing the flight's `version`. Note that an attribute's `waiver_eligible` flag is the design-time declaration that its category is waivable in this way.

The declaration then works through every booking on the flight whose `booking_status` is `TICKETED` or `CHANGE_PENDING`, and does four things to each. First, for every `SALE` entry of that booking whose `category` is a member of `waiver_scope`, it posts one `WAIVER_CREDIT` entry whose `gross_cents` is the negated `gross_cents` of the credited sale, whose `tax_cents` is the negated `tax_cents` of that sale, whose `net_cents` is its own gross minus its own tax, and whose `category`, `product_sku`, `tax_treatment`, `revenue_class` and `revenue_account_code` are copied from the credited sale, with a `source_ref` of `waiver:` followed by the waiver's id; the source sales are processed in `created_at` ascending order. Second, it sets the booking's `waiver_applied` to true, and — only when `CHANGE` is a member of `waiver_scope` — increases the booking's `change_fee_waived_cents` by that booking's family's `change_fee_cents`, capped so the accumulated waived amount never exceeds that `change_fee_cents`; when `CHANGE` is not in scope, `change_fee_waived_cents` is left exactly as it was. Third, it moves every offer on that booking still `PRESENTED` whose product's category is a member of `waiver_scope` to `SUPERSEDED`. Fourth, it recomputes that booking's `recognized_ancillary_net_cents`, `ancillary_gross_cents` and settlement figures. Every booking the declaration reaches — one credited or merely marked here, and equally one whose seat the diversion cascade below releases even though its `booking_status` is neither `TICKETED` nor `CHANGE_PENDING` — has its money recomputed once and its `version` incremented exactly once for the declaration as a whole, however many of these steps moved it.

A diversion is harsher. When `event_type` is `DIVERSION` and `SEAT` is a member of `waiver_scope`, every seat assignment on the flight that is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` is released with `released_reason` `IROPS_REACCOMMODATION`. This cascade deliberately does **not** post the confirmed-release `REFUND` entry for a seat sale that this same waiver has already credited with a `WAIVER_CREDIT`, so each sale is reversed at most once per waiver rather than twice.

The waiver itself keeps a running account of what it did: `credited_bookings_count` is the number of distinct bookings that received at least one `WAIVER_CREDIT` entry from it, and `credited_amount_cents` is the sum of the absolute values of the `gross_cents` of the `WAIVER_CREDIT` entries it posted. Waivers are readable singly, as a paginated global list filterable by `flight_id`, by `waiver_state` and by `event_type`, and as a per-flight list filterable by `waiver_state`. `rescind_reason` is null on an active waiver.

**Rescinding a waiver.** A rescission is designed as an exact compensation: it restores every field the declaration moved, so that a declare-then-rescind pair is net-zero on every credited booking's `recognized_ancillary_net_cents`. It requires that the waiver's `waiver_state` is `ACTIVE` (`ERR_WAIVER_STATE_INVALID`) and that the flight has not departed (`ERR_FLIGHT_ALREADY_DEPARTED`), takes a `rescind_reason` which is recorded on the waiver, and drives `waiver_state` from `ACTIVE` to `RESCINDED`. For every `WAIVER_CREDIT` entry this waiver posted, one mirroring `WAIVER_REVERSAL` entry is posted with both `gross_cents` and `tax_cents` sign-flipped, the source credits processed in `created_at` ascending order. For every booking the declaration reached — every booking it marked `waiver_applied`, plus every booking whose seats its diversion cascade released, whether or not that booking received a credit — `change_fee_waived_cents` decreases by exactly the amount this waiver added to it, and `waiver_applied` becomes false — but only when no other waiver on the flight is still `ACTIVE`. The flight's `active_waiver_count` is recomputed and, when it falls to 0, the flight's `irops_state` moves `IRREGULAR` → `RECOVERED`. Every one of those same bookings has its `recognized_ancillary_net_cents`, `ancillary_gross_cents` and settlement figures recomputed and its own `version` incremented once, and the waiver's `version` increments.

Departure ends a waiver differently. When the flight advances into `DEPARTED`, every waiver still `ACTIVE` moves to `EXPIRED`, the flight's `active_waiver_count` is recomputed, and the waiver's `version` increments — but **no** `WAIVER_REVERSAL` entries are posted. Fees credited before departure stay credited. Both `RESCINDED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal.

### 3.9. Revenue Accounting and Channel Settlement

Every booking carries four money fields that summarise its position with the selling channel, and they are all derived — never supplied. They are recomputed, from the ledger and from the booking's own `fare_component_cents`, in exactly one pinned seven-step sequence, and any implementation that reorders the steps will produce different numbers.

1. `ancillary_gross_cents` is the signed sum of `gross_cents` over the booking's revenue entries whose `entry_type` is `SALE`, `REFUND`, `WAIVER_CREDIT` or `WAIVER_REVERSAL`. `ALLOCATION` entries are excluded from this sum, because the fare component enters the settlement in the next step through `fare_component_cents`; counting it in both places would double it.
2. A settlement subtotal is formed as `fare_component_cents` plus `ancillary_gross_cents`.
3. `commission_cents` is the subtotal multiplied by the channel's `commission_bp`, divided by 10,000, rounded half up.
4. A provisional settlement total is formed as the subtotal plus `commission_cents`. Commission is what the airline owes the channel, so it adds to the amount to be settled.
5. The volume-incentive tier is chosen from the **provisional** total of step 4: 300 basis points when that total is greater than or equal to 50,000 cents, otherwise 150 basis points when it is greater than or equal to 30,000 cents, otherwise 0 basis points.
6. `incentive_credit_cents` is the provisional total multiplied by the chosen tier rate, divided by 10,000, rounded half up.
7. `channel_settlement_total_cents` is the provisional total minus `incentive_credit_cents`.

The tier boundary is keyed on the step-4 provisional total and is never re-derived from the step-7 figure, so a booking that lands just above a threshold before the incentive is deducted keeps its tier even though the final total falls below that threshold. The two thresholds are inclusive at their lower edge.

Before a booking is ticketed its `fare_component_cents` is null, and `commission_cents`, `incentive_credit_cents` and `channel_settlement_total_cents` all stay at 0 while `ancillary_gross_cents` still tracks step 1 over whatever entries already exist. Ticketing is what first populates the fare component and therefore what first produces a non-zero settlement.

The recomputation runs on every event that can move the ledger or the fare component: ticketing the booking; accepting an ancillary offer; every seat transition that posts or reverses a seat sale, meaning an auto-confirmed assignment, a held assignment confirmed by ticketing, and a confirmed assignment released; settling a change with `CONFIRM`; declaring an irregular-operations waiver and rescinding one; and cancelling the booking. Because the recomputation always reads the whole ledger rather than adjusting the previous figures, the four fields are a function of the entries alone and cannot drift.

Ledger reads belong to the `RevenueAccountant` alone; no other role, including the `ChannelPartner` who generated the entries, may read them. There are three ways in. A single entry may be fetched by its own identity. The full ledger is available as a paginated list filterable by `booking_id`, by `category`, by `entry_type` and by `accounting_period`, which makes a period close a single query. And a per-booking audit trail is available, filterable by `entry_type` and returned in `created_at` ascending order, which reproduces the economic story of one passenger's journey — allocation and sales at ticketing, seat sales as the passenger picks seats, refunds and waiver credits as things go wrong, waiver reversals when a waiver is withdrawn — in the order the events happened.

## 5. Error Codes

Every failure answers with the error envelope of §2.2 and one of the identifiers below. When more than one condition holds on a single request, the precedence ladder of §2.7 decides which one is reported.
