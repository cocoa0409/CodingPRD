# Recycled Paper Mill Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A recycled containerboard and tissue mill converts other people's waste into a saleable web of paper, and almost every economic decision in that conversion is a measurement dispute waiting to happen. A truck arrives carrying what the contract calls old corrugated containers; the scale says one thing, the moisture probe says another, and the sorting-line sample says the load is dirtier than the grade allows. Downstream, a pulper charge blends four inventory lots of differing cleanliness, a deink line burns caustic and peroxide chasing a brightness target, a paper machine has to hold basis weight and moisture inside a narrow band while a grade change eats tonnage as transition waste, and the effluent permit quietly caps how much of any of this the mill is allowed to discharge. This API is the system of record for that whole chain: it prices fiber against published market indices, adjudicates truck dispositions, tracks fiber and pulp tonnage as a strict ledger, schedules machine time, certifies reel quality, allocates finished rolls to customer orders, manifests residual solids, and dispatches steam from the cogeneration header.

The product exists because the mill's current practice — spreadsheets for fiber pricing, a whiteboard for machine sequencing, a paper logbook for discharge readings — cannot express the rules the business actually runs on. Those rules are unforgiving in ways that reward software: a supplier accumulates contamination strikes and loses the right to confirm large contracts; a pulp batch destined for a brightness-sensitive grade may not start a run unless it has actually been deinked; a sludge manifest may not be signed by the same environmental officer who prepared it or who logged any reading inside the permit's current rolling window; a run's quality index is computed twice, once provisionally from two reels and once definitively from all of them, and the two numbers must both be retained because they are used for different decisions. Every one of these is a hard rule with a specific rejection code, and every one of them is currently enforced by whoever happens to be paying attention.

Two structural themes run through the specification and are worth flagging before the detail begins. First, **segregation of duties**: several transitions record the acting operator's identifier and then forbid a second, related transition by that same identifier. Second, **dual-variant calculation**: fiber yield, run quality, and permit compliance are each computed in a cheap provisional form early and an authoritative form later, and the difference between the two is itself a retained business quantity. Implementers should read §2 as the binding contract surface and §3 as the operational narrative from which the workflows, thresholds, and rejection conditions must be assembled.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the eight roles declared below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401). When the acting role is a valid role but that role is not authorised for the endpoint being addressed, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles are `MillPlanner`, `FiberBuyer`, `QualityLab`, `MachineOperator`, `EnvironmentalOfficer`, `EnergyManager`, `Supplier`, and `Customer`. Their responsibilities divide as follows. A `MillPlanner` owns scheduling in the broad sense: paper machines and their operational status, paper grade specifications, customer master records, run windows, grade changes, order sequencing, and the discretionary decisions to divert a pulp batch, abort a run, or condemn its quality. A `FiberBuyer` owns recovered-fiber procurement: fiber grade definitions, published market index quotes, supplier records, and the purchase-order pricing lifecycle. A `QualityLab` operator owns sampling and quality judgement: recording truck samples and dispositioning them, approving pulp and recording brightness, certifying or holding run quality, and quarantining roll sets. A `MachineOperator` runs the equipment: charging the pulper, screening, cleaning, deink passes, starting and completing runs, and winding, wrapping and rewinding rolls. An `EnvironmentalOfficer` owns water treatment and residuals: permits, discharge readings and their voids, sludge manifests, and reversals in the solids ledger. An `EnergyManager` owns the steam header and cogeneration: allocation requests, curtailment outcomes, and releases.

Six of the eight roles are **opaque-identity** roles. When `X-User-Role` is `MillPlanner`, `FiberBuyer`, `QualityLab`, `MachineOperator`, `EnvironmentalOfficer`, or `EnergyManager`, the `X-User-ID` value is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never resolves it to a row and never compares it to any entity's `id`. It may, however, be *recorded* where a transition captures it — as `sampled_by_user_id` and `dispositioned_by_user_id` on a fiber receipt, as `started_by_user_id` and `certified_by_user_id` on a production run, as `logged_by_user_id` on an effluent reading, and as `prepared_by_user_id` and `signed_by_user_id` on a sludge manifest — and the segregation-of-duties rules compare those recorded values against the current `X-User-ID`.

The remaining two roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `Supplier`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Supplier` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a supplier compare that resource's `supplier_id` to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a customer compare that resource's `customer_id` to `X-User-ID`. For both of these roles, an `X-User-ID` that does not resolve to an existing row of the backing entity is rejected with `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 401). A `Supplier` that acts on a purchase order it does not own, or a `Customer` that acts on another customer's order or profile, is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Supplier reads of purchase orders and fiber receipts are restricted to rows whose `supplier_id` equals `X-User-ID`; customer reads of orders and profiles are restricted to rows whose `customer_id` equals `X-User-ID`. On collection listings that restriction is a silent narrowing rather than an error: the out-of-scope rows are dropped before `total_count` is taken and before the page is cut, so a scoped caller sees a smaller total and possibly an empty page rather than a `403`. The roll-set listing narrows the same way for a `Customer`, keeping only sets whose `allocated_order_id` names one of that customer's own orders — which leaves every unallocated set invisible to customers — and on single-resource reads no such narrowing is applied at all: a `Customer` permitted by the role table to read one roll set, one production run or the paper-grade specs receives them whole, so `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` arises only on the supplier, customer, purchase-order and customer-order rows named above.

Ownership and role checks are evaluated before any business-state reasoning: an unauthorised caller must not be able to learn a resource's state from the error it receives.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

All list endpoints accept two query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. The `limit` parameter defaults to 10 when omitted, has a minimum accepted value of 1 and a maximum accepted value of 100. The `offset` parameter defaults to 0 and must be a non-negative integer. A `limit` that is not an integer or that falls outside the range of 1 through 100 inclusive, or an `offset` that is negative or not an integer, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400) — both parameters are read as plain optionally-signed digit strings, so a value carrying a decimal point or any other non-digit character is an `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` failure rather than being truncated.

Unless a specific endpoint narrative says otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order — oldest first — so that a paged sweep over a collection is stable; because the logical clock of §2.9 stands still until it is moved, many rows share an identical `created_at`, and those ties are broken by insertion order, the row persisted first coming first. Every list response includes `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the total number of rows matching the request's filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Semantics

Every resource in this system is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID string carrying no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply `id` values; they are assigned on creation and are immutable thereafter. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits, recorded when the row is first persisted.

Instants a *client* supplies are normalised into that same rendering rather than being echoed as the submitted text. A supplied instant is parsed, read as UTC when it carries no explicit offset and converted to UTC when it carries one, and then stored and returned as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits — so a `window_start` of `2026-08-02T06:00:00Z` on a production run or a steam allocation, a `requested_ship_date` on a customer order, and the `to_iso8601` echoed back as the clock's `current_utc` all read back in the `2026-08-02T06:00:00.000Z` form. Fields that are calendar dates or months rather than instants — an effluent reading's `reading_date`, a quote's `effective_month`, a pricing request's `priced_for_month` — are stored and returned exactly as supplied.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of that row — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly one on every mutation. Version increments are the backbone of the concurrency contract described in §2.5, and they are not optional bookkeeping: any transition that changes a row's fields, including transitions triggered as a cascade from an operation on a different row, refreshes that row's `updated_at` and bumps its `version`.

Four resource kinds are deliberate exceptions: `FiberGrade`, `MarketIndexQuote`, `PaperGradeSpec`, and `RejectStream` rows carry only `id` and `created_at`. For those four entity kinds, both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the persisted row and from every response body. Fiber grades and paper grade specifications are immutable reference definitions once declared; market index quotes are append-only publications superseded rather than edited; and reject-stream rows are ledger entries that are compensated by new rows rather than amended in place. Every other entity in the system — `Customer`, `Supplier`, `FiberPurchaseOrder`, `FiberReceipt`, `FiberInventoryLot`, `PulpBatch`, `PaperMachine`, `CustomerOrder`, `ProductionRun`, `GradeChange`, `RollSet`, `EffluentPermit`, `EffluentReading`, `SludgeManifest`, and `SteamAllocation` — carries the full `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `version` quartet.

### 2.5. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Parents

Several operations in this system write into an aggregate whose parent row is routinely advanced by other actors, and each of them requires the client to prove it has observed the parent's current state. Wherever such a version field is specified below, it is a **required, non-optional** input field of the request; omitting it is a request-shape failure, not a concurrency failure. Version checks are evaluated after request-shape validation and body-reference resolution and before any business rule; relative to the state-machine from-state check, the version check runs **after** it on the five order-and-machine operations (roll-set allocation, order scheduling, run creation, grade-change planning, machine status change — the last of which has no from-state check at all), and **before** it on the two purchase-order operations, so pricing or withdrawing pricing with a stale `expected_purchase_order_version` reports `ERR_STALE_PURCHASE_ORDER_VERSION` even when the order is in the wrong state for the operation.

Allocating a roll set to a customer order must carry `expected_order_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `CustomerOrder`. If that value does not equal the order's current `version` — which happens whenever another allocation against the same order has already landed and moved the order's `allocated_tons` and `status` — the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Without this check, a client carrying a pre-allocation view of the order would be deciding how much tonnage the order still needs from numbers it can no longer trust.

Creating a production run on a paper machine must carry `expected_machine_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `PaperMachine`. A mismatch against the machine's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MACHINE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Run creation, run start, run completion and run abort all bump the machine's `version`, so a scheduler working from an older read is reasoning about an occupancy picture that has already changed.

Pricing a fiber purchase order must carry `expected_purchase_order_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of that `FiberPurchaseOrder`. A mismatch against the order's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PURCHASE_ORDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Withdrawing a price legitimately supersedes an earlier pricing run and bumps the version, so a buyer re-pricing from a stale read would be overwriting a revision it never saw.

Three companion operations carry the same fields for the same reason. Sequencing a customer order onto a run also requires `expected_order_version` to equal that order's current `version`, failing with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION`. Planning a grade change on a machine, and taking a machine down or returning it to service, both require `expected_machine_version` to equal that machine's current `version`, failing with `ERR_STALE_MACHINE_VERSION`. Withdrawing a purchase order's pricing requires `expected_purchase_order_version` to equal that order's current `version`, failing with `ERR_STALE_PURCHASE_ORDER_VERSION`.

Outside these seven operations — roll-set allocation, order scheduling, run creation, grade-change planning, machine status change, order pricing and pricing withdrawal — no endpoint takes a version field. Every other endpoint neither accepts nor requires `expected_order_version`, `expected_machine_version`, or `expected_purchase_order_version`; a body that carries any one of those three keys on an endpoint outside the seven is not merely ignored but rejected outright with `ERR_VALIDATION`, and that rejection precedes every other check the endpoint would make on the body.

### 2.6. Measurement Precision and Rounding Discipline

Rounding in a mill is not cosmetic — it decides who pays for a wet truck and whether a reel certifies. Every rounding operation in this specification uses **round half up**: a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. Three precision families apply. Money quantities — unit prices, surcharges, extended contract values, chemical costs, and cogeneration credits — are carried and returned at 2 decimal places. Tonnage quantities are carried at 3 decimal places, with the single deliberate exception of a fiber receipt's `net_tons`, which is finalised at 2 decimal places. Measured percentages (moisture, contamination) and quality indexes are carried at 2 decimal places, while blend contamination on a pulp batch and the rolling effluent loads are carried at 3 decimal places, and deink removal fractions and effectiveness factors at 4 decimal places.

Where a precision applies to an *input* measurement — stated with the field in §3, or, where the field itself states none, inherited from the families just listed — it is a storage precision rather than an acceptance filter: a submitted value carrying more decimals than that is not a shape failure. The field's declared range is tested against the value exactly as supplied, and the accepted value is then rounded half up to its precision before it is stored, used in any calculation, or echoed — so a reel submitted with a measured `basis_weight_gsm` of 124.55 is carried, scored and returned as 124.6. The only inputs outside this discipline are the deink pass's `caustic_kg`, `peroxide_kg` and `surfactant_kg`, which belong to no precision family and are carried exactly as supplied.

Where a formula involves both intermediate and final rounding, the **order of rounding is pinned** and is part of the contract. Intermediate per-item values are rounded first and the already-rounded items are then summed; summing raw unrounded intermediates and rounding once at the end produces different answers in the last decimal and is explicitly not the behaviour required here. The specific sequences appear with each calculation in §3, and implementers must follow them literally rather than substituting an algebraically equivalent arrangement.

Ratio comparisons are the exception to rounding: where a formula compares a ratio against a literal threshold, the ratio is compared unrounded. Discount percentages, decay factors and penalty coefficients are exact decimal literals, not derived values.

### 2.7. Nested Path Resolution and Existence Ordering

Several collections are addressed through a parent path segment — production runs and grade changes hang off a paper machine, roll sets hang off a production run, effluent readings hang off an effluent permit, and fiber receipts are created under a fiber purchase order. Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the error. Addressing a run under a machine that does not exist yields `ERR_MACHINE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) even when the run id is also nonsense; only once the machine resolves is the run id considered. A run id that exists but belongs to a different machine than the one named in the path is likewise `ERR_RUN_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) — parentage is part of identity, not a filter.

The existence codes are specific to the addressed collection: `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PURCHASE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECEIPT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVENTORY_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PULP_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MACHINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GRADE_CHANGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROLL_SET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_STEAM_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND` each cover a path id of the corresponding kind, while `ERR_ENV_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) covers an effluent reading, sludge manifest, or reject stream id in the path. Identifiers arriving in the request *body* rather than the path have their own codes: an unresolvable `fiber_grade_code`, `target_product_code` or `product_code` yields `ERR_GRADE_CODE_NOT_FOUND`; an unresolvable `supplier_id` or `customer_id` yields `ERR_PARTY_NOT_FOUND`; an unresolvable `pulp_batch_id` or a furnish line whose `inventory_lot_id` does not resolve yields `ERR_FURNISH_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`; an unresolvable `run_id`, `from_run_id`, `to_run_id` or `order_id`, or one that resolves to a row of the wrong type, yields `ERR_LINK_TARGET_NOT_FOUND`; and an entry of `reject_stream_ids` that does not resolve — or that resolves to a compensating reversal row — yields `ERR_REJECT_STREAM_REFERENCE_INVALID` (HTTP 404).

Request-shape failures are uniform. A missing required field, a value of the wrong type, a number outside its declared range, an enum member that is not declared, an unparseable datetime, or an array that breaches its cardinality bounds or its uniqueness key is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422). Two shape failures have their own code because they are relational rather than scalar: a time window whose `window_end` is not strictly after its `window_start`, and a rewind completion whose replacement `net_tons` exceeds the pre-rewind `net_tons`, both yield `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). Uniqueness collisions on business keys — a duplicate `grade_code`, `product_code`, machine `name`, `permit_number`, `manifest_number`, a duplicate `rank_order`, or a second non-voided reading for the same permit and `reading_date` — yield `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (HTTP 409).

### 2.8. Rule Evaluation Order

Where several rules could reject the same request, they are evaluated in a fixed order rather than whichever order is convenient, because the error a caller receives is part of the contract. The global ordering is: authentication and role checks first (missing headers, unknown role, unresolvable entity-backed identity, role not permitted, resource ownership), then path existence resolved left to right, then pagination and request-shape validation, then body-reference resolution, then state-machine from-state checks and optimistic-concurrency version checks in the relative order fixed by §2.5, then segregation-of-duties checks, then the domain's business rules — safety and environmental gates, then constraint and capacity gates, then temporal gates, then the fiscal and ledger gates. Within each family, §3 states the specific relative order wherever more than one rule can fire on the same operation.

### 2.9. Conserved Quantities and the Logical Clock

Three ledgers in this system are conserved quantities and must balance at every observable instant, not merely eventually. The mill fiber balance reports `total_received_tons`, `total_consumed_tons`, `total_available_tons`, and `imbalance_tons`, where the first three are the sums, each rounded to 3 decimal places, of `tons_received`, `tons_consumed` and `tons_available` across every fiber inventory lot regardless of lot status — quarantined and depleted lots included — and `imbalance_tons` is the total received minus the total consumed minus the total available, rounded to 3 decimal places. That imbalance must read exactly zero at every point at which it can be observed, including immediately after a pulp batch cancellation releases a reservation and immediately after an aborted run returns its drawn pulp.

The solids ledger reports `gross_solids_tons` (the sum of `dry_solids_tons` over rows that are not reversals, to 3 decimal places), `reversed_solids_tons` (the same sum over rows that *are* reversals), `net_solids_tons` (gross minus reversed, to 3 decimal places), and three disposition splits over non-reversal rows: `manifested_dry_solids_tons` for rows whose disposition is `MANIFESTED`, `unmanifested_dry_solids_tons` for rows whose disposition is `PENDING`, and `repulped_dry_solids_tons` for rows whose disposition is `REPULPED`. The invariant is that `net_solids_tons` equals the sum of `manifested_dry_solids_tons` and `unmanifested_dry_solids_tons` rounded to 3 decimal places, which holds because every repulped row is matched by exactly one reversal row of equal tonnage. Signing a manifest moves tonnage from pending to manifested and voiding it moves the tonnage back, leaving `net_solids_tons` identical before and after.

The order allocation ledger, described in detail with roll allocation in §3, is the third: for every customer order, `allocated_tons` equals the summed net tonnage of the roll sets currently pointing at it in `ALLOCATED` or `SHIPPED` status, and the quarantine cascade is the exact compensating action for allocation, so an allocate-then-hold sequence returns the order's `allocated_tons` to its pre-allocation value with no tonnage stranded.

All server-side "now" reasoning in this system reads a **logical clock** rather than wall time, so that scheduling and rolling-window behaviour is reproducible. Until the clock is explicitly set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time. An administrative test-driving capability lets a `MillPlanner`, an `EnvironmentalOfficer`, or an `EnergyManager` move the clock forward or backward: the request carries a single field `to_iso8601` holding the target instant, the response body reports the resulting instant as `current_utc`, and the call recomputes no entity state — it only changes the instant from which subsequent writes stamp their timestamps. Any timestamp derived by the server — `probation_entered_at`, `posture_changed_at`, and the instants used to reject out-of-range dates — reads that clock. A discharge reading whose `reading_date` falls after the logical clock's current date, or a steam window whose `window_start` precedes the logical clock instant, is rejected with `ERR_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The mill's work is organised as a single physical chain — fiber in, pulp, paper, rolls out — with two utility systems (water and steam) hanging off it. The subsections below follow that chain, folding each cross-cutting concern into the operation it constrains rather than isolating it.

### 3.1. Recovered Fiber: Grades, Published Indices and Contract Pricing

A counterparty must exist before it can be bought from: a `FiberBuyer` registers each **supplier** with its legal `name` of 1 to 120 characters and the `freight_per_ton` its deliveries are billed at, a figure at two decimal places that may not be negative but may be zero, and the mill assigns everything else — a new supplier opens with `qualification_status` of `QUALIFIED`, a `contamination_strike_count` of zero and a null `probation_entered_at`, none of which the request contributes — those three are server-assigned and any value offered for them is disregarded rather than honoured. Everything else in procurement is anchored on a declared **fiber grade**. A `FiberBuyer` declares each grade with its `grade_code` — a business key that must be unique and must take the shape of two to four uppercase letters, an underscore, and exactly two digits, so `OCC_11`, `DLK_37`, `SOP_37` and `ONP_58` are all well-formed — together with the `index_symbol` that prices it, which must be one of `PPI_OCC`, `PPI_DLK`, `PPI_SOP` or `PPI_ONP`. A grade also carries the commercial limits the sorting line will be judged against: `max_contamination_pct`, the combined outthrow-and-prohibitive ceiling from 0 through 25 at two decimal places, above which a truck cannot be accepted at that grade; `max_moisture_pct`, from 0 through 20 at two decimal places, being the moisture ceiling beyond which a weight deduction begins to bite; and `nominal_yield_pct`, the book yield from 40 through 95 at two decimal places used by the provisional pulp-yield projection. The boolean `deink_required` marks grades whose pulp must pass a deink stage before it can be approved. `downgrade_target_grade_code` names, by grade code, the fallback grade a truck rejected at its contracted grade may still be accepted as; leaving it null means the grade has no fallback and a dirty truck has nowhere to go. That field is optional and is stored exactly as supplied without being resolved at declaration time — it need not name an already-declared grade, and no `ERR_LINK_TARGET_NOT_FOUND` is raised here — so a fallback that never comes to exist behaves at disposition precisely as a null one does, refusing the downgrade with `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_PERMITTED` and letting rejection on the contamination route stand. Finally `rank_order`, a unique integer from 1 through 99, gives grade cascades and furnish ranking a deterministic ordering key. A duplicate `grade_code` or a duplicate `rank_order` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`.

Market prices arrive as published quotes rather than negotiated numbers. A `FiberBuyer` records a `MarketIndexQuote` naming the `index_symbol`, the `effective_month` as a four-digit year, a hyphen, and a two-digit month between `01` and `12`, and the `published_price_per_ton`, which must be greater than zero at two decimal places. Quotes are append-only: publishing a second quote for the same symbol and month does not edit the first, it **supersedes** it. On such a publication the previously current row has its `superseded` flag set to true, and the newest row is the only one for that pair carrying `superseded` as false. Exactly one non-superseded quote therefore survives per symbol and month, and that is the row pricing resolves against.

A purchase order begins life in `DRAFT`. Its creator supplies the `supplier_id` of the counterparty, the `fiber_grade_code` being bought, `contracted_tons` greater than zero and not above 5000 at two decimal places, the `index_basis` — either `PRIOR_MONTH` or `CURRENT_MONTH` — the `priced_for_month` in the same year-and-month form as a quote, and a signed `negotiated_delta_per_ton` between negative 80 and positive 80 at two decimal places. The `supplier_id` must resolve, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_PARTY_NOT_FOUND`, and the `fiber_grade_code` must name a declared grade, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_GRADE_CODE_NOT_FOUND`; both references are resolved in that order and only then is the counterparty's posture examined, creation being refused with `ERR_SUPPLIER_SUSPENDED` when the supplier's `qualification_status` already reads `SUSPENDED`. Nothing is priced yet: `quoted_unit_price_per_ton`, `fuel_surcharge_per_ton`, `extended_contract_value` and `index_quote_month_used` all read null until the order is priced, and `pricing_revision_count` starts at 0 while `received_net_tons` starts at 0.

Pricing moves the order `DRAFT → PRICED` and is the buyer's act. It is refused with `ERR_PURCHASE_ORDER_INVALID_STATE` unless the order is currently in `DRAFT`, refused with `ERR_SUPPLIER_SUSPENDED` when the counterparty's `qualification_status` is `SUSPENDED`, and refused with `ERR_INDEX_QUOTE_MISSING` (HTTP 422) when no non-superseded quote exists for the resolved symbol and month. The resolution itself has no discretion in it. The symbol is the `index_symbol` of the order's fiber grade. The target month is the order's `priced_for_month` when `index_basis` is `CURRENT_MONTH`, and the calendar month immediately preceding `priced_for_month` when `index_basis` is `PRIOR_MONTH`. The surviving quote's `published_price_per_ton` is the raw index price, and the resolved month is recorded as `index_quote_month_used`.

From there the arithmetic runs in a fixed five-step order, and the order matters more than the individual steps. First, the **base price** is the published index price plus the order's `negotiated_delta_per_ton`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Second, a **provisional extended value** is computed as the base price multiplied by `contracted_tons`, rounded half up to two decimal places; freight and fuel surcharge are deliberately excluded from this figure. Third, the **volume discount** tier is selected from that provisional extended value alone: a provisional value strictly below 100000.00 United States dollars earns a discount of exactly 0.000, a value of 100000.00 or more but strictly below 250000.00 earns exactly 0.015, and a value of 250000.00 or more earns exactly 0.030; both tier boundaries are inclusive at the lower edge. Fourth, the **fuel surcharge** is derived from the supplier's contracted `freight_per_ton` multiplied by a fuel surcharge rate of 0.185, rounded half up to two decimal places, and stored as `fuel_surcharge_per_ton`. Fifth, the `quoted_unit_price_per_ton` is the base price multiplied by one minus the selected discount, plus the supplier's `freight_per_ton`, plus the `fuel_surcharge_per_ton` just computed, the whole rounded half up to two decimal places; and `extended_contract_value` is that unit price multiplied by `contracted_tons`, rounded half up to two decimal places.

The apparent circularity in that sequence — a value selects a tier which sets a price which produces a value — is broken at the third step and only there. The discount tier is chosen from the provisional extended value and is **never** re-derived from the final `extended_contract_value`, even when adding freight and the fuel surcharge pushes the final figure across the 100000.00 or 250000.00 boundaries. Only one market quote and only the supplier reached through the order's `supplier_id` participate; no other purchase order contributes anything. Each successful pricing bumps the order's `version` by one.

A buyer who dislikes the resulting number may withdraw the pricing, taking the order `PRICED → DRAFT`. Withdrawal requires the order to be in `PRICED` and requires `pricing_revision_count` to be below 2; it increments `pricing_revision_count` by one, clears `quoted_unit_price_per_ton`, `fuel_surcharge_per_ton`, `extended_contract_value` and `index_quote_month_used` back to null, and bumps `version` by one. Because the counter starts at 0, this yields exactly two legitimate reprice loops; the third withdrawal attempt — the one made when `pricing_revision_count` already reads 2 — is rejected with `ERR_REPRICE_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409).

Confirmation is the supplier's move and the only write a `Supplier` principal may make. Taking an order `PRICED → CONFIRMED` requires the order to be in `PRICED`, requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal the order's `supplier_id` (otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`), and requires the supplier to be commercially fit: either `qualification_status` reads `QUALIFIED`, or it reads `PROBATION` and `contracted_tons` is no greater than 500.00. A supplier on probation attempting to confirm a contract above 500.00 tons is rejected with `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_QUALIFIED` (HTTP 409), and a `SUSPENDED` supplier attempting to confirm at all is rejected with that same `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_QUALIFIED` code rather than with `ERR_SUPPLIER_SUSPENDED`. Confirmation bumps the order's `version` by one.

Cancellation closes an unwanted contract and is available from `DRAFT`, `PRICED` or `CONFIRMED`, moving the order to `CANCELLED`. It is permitted only while `received_net_tons` reads exactly 0.00 — any receipt already accepted or downgraded against the order blocks it, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_TONNAGE_ALREADY_BOOKED` (HTTP 409). Cancellation also sweeps the order's open receipts: every fiber receipt of this order still sitting in `SAMPLED` is flipped to `REJECTED` with its `accepted_grade_code` cleared to null. Because this is an administrative rejection rather than a quality judgement, those sweep-rejected receipts carry no `dispositioned_by_user_id` and **no contamination strike is charged to the supplier** for them. The cancellation bumps the order's `version` by one.

Receiving tonnage drives the order forward without anyone calling for it. Whenever a receipt of the order reaches `ACCEPTED` or `DOWNGRADED`, the order recomputes `received_net_tons` as the sum of `net_tons` over its receipts in those two statuses and then re-evaluates its own status. From `CONFIRMED`, if the recomputed tonnage is below 98 percent of `contracted_tons` the order advances `CONFIRMED → PARTIALLY_RECEIVED`; if the very first disposition already brings it to 98 percent of `contracted_tons` or more, the order goes straight `CONFIRMED → CLOSED`. From `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED`, reaching 98 percent of `contracted_tons` or more advances `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED → CLOSED`. The comparison threshold is `contracted_tons` multiplied by 0.98, and the boundary is inclusive: tonnage exactly equal to that product closes the order. Each of these system-driven advances recomputes `received_net_tons` and bumps the order's `version` by one. `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

### 3.2. The Weighbridge: Sampling, Net Tonnage and Disposition

A truck or railcar arriving against a confirmed contract is recorded by a `QualityLab` operator as a **fiber receipt** under that purchase order. The operator supplies a `truck_reference` of 1 to 40 characters and a `sublots` array of between 1 and 12 entries, each entry carrying a `sublot_no` integer, `gross_tons` and `tare_tons` at three decimal places, and `moisture_pct` and `contamination_pct` at two decimal places. Sublot numbers must be unique and ascending within the array; a violation of the array's cardinality or its uniqueness key is an `ERR_VALIDATION` failure. Each `sublot_no` is an integer of 1 or more, and `gross_tons`, `tare_tons`, `moisture_pct` and `contamination_pct` are each zero or greater with no declared upper bound; nothing requires `gross_tons` to exceed `tare_tons`. Recording a receipt imposes no state gate of its own on the parent purchase order — a receipt may be created under an order in any status, and it is the acceptance branch of disposition that insists the order be `CONFIRMED` or `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED`. The receipt is created in `SAMPLED`, denormalises the parent order's `supplier_id` and contracted grade into `supplier_id` and `declared_grade_code`, and records the acting operator's identifier as `sampled_by_user_id`.

Net tonnage is computed once, at receipt creation, and is never recomputed afterwards. The moisture ceiling used is the `max_moisture_pct` of the receipt's declared grade. Working through the sublots in ascending `sublot_no` order, each sublot's **wet tonnage** is its `gross_tons` minus its `tare_tons`, rounded half up to three decimal places. Its **moisture excess** is its `moisture_pct` minus the grade's moisture ceiling, floored at zero so a dry sublot is never credited. Its **deduction** is that wet tonnage multiplied by the moisture excess and divided by 100, rounded half up to three decimal places, and its **dry tonnage** is the wet tonnage minus that deduction, again rounded half up to three decimal places. The receipt's `moisture_deduction_tons` is the sum of the per-sublot deductions, rounded to three decimal places, and its `net_tons` is the sum of the per-sublot **already-rounded** dry tonnages, rounded half up to two decimal places. This per-sublot-then-sum order is pinned: summing raw unrounded deductions and rounding once at the end changes `net_tons` in the third decimal for multi-sublot trucks, and that alternative is not used.

Two weighted averages come out of the same pass and both use the **pre-deduction wet tonnages** as weights, never the dry tonnages. `weighted_moisture_pct` is the sum over sublots of wet tonnage multiplied by that sublot's `moisture_pct`, divided by the sum of the wet tonnages, rounded half up to two decimal places. `weighted_contamination_pct` is the same construction over each sublot's `contamination_pct`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Only the sublots of this receipt participate; no other receipt or lot influences the numbers.

Disposition is where the money is decided, and it is a fork with three mutually exclusive branches — `SAMPLED → ACCEPTED`, `SAMPLED → DOWNGRADED`, and `SAMPLED → REJECTED` — driven by a `disposition` field carrying `ACCEPT`, `DOWNGRADE` or `REJECT`. All three branches require the receipt to be in `SAMPLED`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_RECEIPT_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), and all three enforce segregation of duties: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the receipt's `sampled_by_user_id`, and an operator attempting to disposition the truck they sampled is rejected with `ERR_SOD_SAME_SAMPLER` (HTTP 409). All three record the acting identifier as `dispositioned_by_user_id`. A requested disposition that contradicts the measured results is rejected with `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 409).

**Accepting** is permitted only when `weighted_contamination_pct` is no greater than the declared grade's `max_contamination_pct` — the boundary is inclusive, so a truck exactly at the limit is clean enough — and only when the parent purchase order is in `CONFIRMED` or `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED`. The contamination test is applied first, and a parent order in any other status is then rejected with `ERR_PURCHASE_ORDER_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) rather than with `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_PERMITTED`. Acceptance sets `accepted_grade_code` equal to `declared_grade_code` and creates exactly one fiber inventory lot whose `grade_code` is that accepted grade, whose `tons_received` is the receipt's `net_tons`, whose `contamination_pct` is copied from the receipt's `weighted_contamination_pct`, and whose lot `status` is `AVAILABLE`. The lot's `receipt_id` points back at the receipt, `tons_consumed` starts at 0 and `tons_available` starts equal to `tons_received`. The receipt's `net_tons` is added into the parent order's `received_net_tons`, which is what drives the order's own advance to `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED` or `CLOSED`. The supplier's `contamination_strike_count` is left untouched — a clean truck costs the supplier nothing.

**Downgrading** is permitted only when the truck is genuinely too dirty for its contracted grade but clean enough for the fallback: `weighted_contamination_pct` must be strictly greater than the declared grade's `max_contamination_pct`, the declared grade's `downgrade_target_grade_code` must not be null, and `weighted_contamination_pct` must be no greater than the fallback grade's own `max_contamination_pct`. The receipt's `accepted_grade_code` is set to the fallback grade code, and a single inventory lot is created at that fallback grade with `tons_received` equal to the receipt's `net_tons` and lot status `AVAILABLE`, its `receipt_id`, its `contamination_pct` copied from the receipt's `weighted_contamination_pct`, its `tons_consumed` of 0 and its `tons_available` equal to `tons_received` all formed exactly as on acceptance — the measured contamination travels with the fiber unchanged, it is not restated against the fallback grade's limit; the receipt's `net_tons` is added into the parent order's `received_net_tons` exactly as on acceptance, so the same order auto-transition may follow. Downgrading, however, is a strike against the supplier, and that strike sets off a four-hop cascade described below.

**Rejecting** is permitted in either of two situations. The contamination route: `weighted_contamination_pct` is strictly greater than the declared grade's `max_contamination_pct` and either the declared grade has no `downgrade_target_grade_code` at all, or `weighted_contamination_pct` is also strictly greater than the fallback grade's `max_contamination_pct`. The moisture route: `weighted_moisture_pct` exceeds the declared grade's `max_moisture_pct` by more than 5.00 percentage points — that is, the weighted moisture is strictly greater than the grade's ceiling plus 5.00. Rejection sets `accepted_grade_code` to null and — this is where the branch diverges sharply from the other two — creates **no** inventory lot and leaves the parent order's `received_net_tons` completely unchanged. Like downgrading, rejection charges the supplier a strike and triggers the identical four-hop cascade.

The cascade begins with the supplier's own posture. Hop one increments `contamination_strike_count` by one and re-derives `qualification_status` from the strike count, where the count is the number of fiber receipts of this supplier, across every purchase order, whose status is `DOWNGRADED` or `REJECTED` **and** whose `dispositioned_by_user_id` is set. Receipts flipped to `REJECTED` by a purchase-order cancellation sweep carry no dispositioning operator and are therefore excluded from the count. Four or more strikes makes the supplier `SUSPENDED`; two or three strikes makes it `PROBATION`; anything below two leaves it `QUALIFIED`. Both boundaries are inclusive on the lower side. When the value first becomes `PROBATION`, `probation_entered_at` is stamped with the current logical-clock instant and is then left alone for as long as the posture stays `PROBATION`; returning to `QUALIFIED` clears `probation_entered_at` to null.

Hop two fires only when hop one has just made the supplier `SUSPENDED`: every fiber inventory lot whose receipt chain resolves to this supplier and whose status is `AVAILABLE` flips to `QUARANTINED`, including the lot created moments earlier by a downgrade. Hop three follows from hop two: every pulp batch still in `QUEUED` that holds a furnish line pointing at any lot quarantined in hop two flips to `CANCELLED`, releasing its reservation so those lots' `tons_consumed` fall back. Hop four follows from hop three: every production run whose `run_status` is `SCHEDULED` and whose `pulp_batch_id` is one of the batches cancelled in hop three has its `run_status` set to `ABORTED` and its `quality_status` set to `CONDEMNED`, and each customer order sequenced onto such a run whose own status reads `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PRODUCTION` returns to `SUBMITTED` with `scheduled_run_id` cleared and its `deschedule_count` incremented, an order in any other status being left untouched. Every row touched by any hop of this cascade has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` bumped, so a client holding a pre-cascade read of a machine, an order, or a batch will find its version checks failing — which is the intended serialisation point.

Inventory lots are otherwise pure bookkeeping. A lot's `tons_consumed` is the sum of `tons_charged` over the furnish lines of every pulp batch that has actually reserved it — that is, every batch that has been charged out of `QUEUED` into `PULPING` or beyond and has not since been `CANCELLED`; a batch still sitting in `QUEUED` has reserved nothing and contributes nothing to the figure. Its `tons_available` is `tons_received` minus `tons_consumed`, rounded to three decimal places, and it is never negative. A lot reads `AVAILABLE` while tonnage remains and flips to `DEPLETED` the moment `tons_available` reaches exactly 0.000, and it reads `QUARANTINED` when the supplier-suspension cascade has seized it — that seizure is sticky, so neither of those two recomputations ever moves a `QUARANTINED` lot back to `AVAILABLE` or on to `DEPLETED`.

### 3.3. Pulping, Screening, Cleaning and the Deink Loop

A **pulp batch** is a furnish recipe with a destination. A `MachineOperator` opens one by naming the `pulper_line` — either `OCC_LINE` or `DIP_LINE`, where `DIP_LINE` is the only line physically able to run a deink pass — the `target_product_code` identifying the paper grade the furnish is destined for, and a `furnish_lines` array of between 1 and 6 entries, each carrying an `inventory_lot_id` and a `tons_charged` figure at three decimal places. A lot may appear at most once in a batch; a repeated `inventory_lot_id` is an `ERR_VALIDATION` failure, and a `tons_charged` of zero or less is likewise an `ERR_VALIDATION` failure. At creation the `target_product_code` is resolved first — an undeclared code is `ERR_GRADE_CODE_NOT_FOUND` — and each `inventory_lot_id` must then resolve to a real lot, an unresolvable one being `ERR_FURNISH_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`; nothing about the lots' statuses or their available tonnage is examined until the pulper is charged. The batch opens in `QUEUED`, and while it sits there it has reserved nothing.

Charging the pulper moves the batch `QUEUED → PULPING` and is the point at which fiber actually leaves inventory. Four preconditions guard it, and they are evaluated in this order. The batch must be in `QUEUED`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_PULP_BATCH_INVALID_STATE`. Every furnish line's lot must resolve — an unresolvable `inventory_lot_id` is `ERR_FURNISH_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` — and must currently read `AVAILABLE`; a `QUARANTINED` or `DEPLETED` lot is rejected with `ERR_LOT_NOT_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 409). Each line's `tons_charged` must be covered by that lot's `tons_available`, with the boundary inclusive so charging exactly the remaining tonnage is allowed; charging more is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FIBER` (HTTP 409). And the blend must be clean enough: the batch's computed `blend_contamination_pct` must be no greater than 3.500, and a dirtier blend is rejected with `ERR_FURNISH_CONTAMINATION_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). One line-routing rule joins them: when the target product's specification requires deinked pulp, the batch's `pulper_line` must be `DIP_LINE`, and a deink-required furnish charged on `OCC_LINE` is rejected with `ERR_WRONG_PULPER_LINE` (HTTP 409).

The charge itself pins three computed figures. `charged_tons` is the sum of the furnish lines' `tons_charged`, rounded half up to three decimal places. `blend_contamination_pct` is the sum over furnish lines of `tons_charged` multiplied by that lot's `contamination_pct`, divided by `charged_tons`, rounded half up to three decimal places. And `provisional_accepted_tons` is the book-yield projection: walking the furnish lines in ascending lot `created_at` order and, for lots created at the same instant, in ascending `rank_order` of the lot's grade, each line's **effective yield** is the grade's `nominal_yield_pct` reduced by 1.8 percentage points for every percentage point of the lot's `contamination_pct` — fractional percentage points included, so the contamination figure is used exactly as it reads and is never truncated to a whole number first — but never allowed below a yield floor of 45.00 percent; that is, the effective yield is the larger of 45.00 and the nominal yield minus 1.8 times the lot contamination. Each line then contributes its `tons_charged` multiplied by that effective yield and divided by 100, rounded half up to three decimal places, and `provisional_accepted_tons` is the sum of those per-line contributions rounded half up to three decimal places. Only this batch's furnish lines participate, and only book yields — no reject weight has been measured yet. As side effects of the charge, each furnish line's `tons_charged` is added into its lot's `tons_consumed`, each affected lot's `tons_available` is recomputed, and any lot whose recomputed `tons_available` lands on exactly 0.000 flips from `AVAILABLE` to `DEPLETED`.

Screening is the quiet step. Moving `PULPING → SCREENED` requires only that the batch be in `PULPING`, and its sole effect is to refresh the batch's `updated_at` (and, as with every mutation, bump its `version`). Cleaning, `SCREENED → CLEANED`, requires the batch to be in `SCREENED` and carries the operator's `measured_reject_tons` — the dry tonnage of screening and cleaning rejects weighed at the reject press, at three decimal places and never negative. A weight above 40 percent of the batch's `charged_tons` is treated as an instrument fault rather than a production result and is rejected with `ERR_REJECT_TONS_IMPLAUSIBLE` (HTTP 422); the permitted ceiling is `charged_tons` multiplied by 0.40 and the boundary is inclusive, so a weight exactly equal to that product is accepted. Cleaning stores the value and appends one row to the solids ledger with `source_kind` of `PULP_REJECT`, `source_ref_id` pointing at this batch, `dry_solids_tons` equal to `measured_reject_tons`, and `disposition` of `PENDING`; that row is appended unconditionally, including when the weighed reject is exactly 0.000.

From `CLEANED` the batch takes one of two roads, decided entirely by the target product. A product whose specification does **not** require deinked pulp goes straight to approval, `CLEANED → APPROVED`, at the hands of a `QualityLab` operator. A product that **does** require deinked pulp cannot be approved from `CLEANED` at all — the approval precondition explicitly requires the target specification's deink requirement to be false, and an approval attempted on such a batch is rejected with `ERR_PULP_BATCH_INVALID_STATE` rather than with `ERR_DEINK_PULP_REQUIRED` — and must instead enter the deink loop, `CLEANED → DEINKING`, at the hands of a `MachineOperator`, which additionally requires the batch's `pulper_line` to be `DIP_LINE`. A deink pass attempted on a batch that is not on `DIP_LINE` is rejected with `ERR_WRONG_PULPER_LINE`. That line test is the last of the entry checks rather than the first: the batch's status is judged first, and on a re-charge out of `DEINK_HOLD` the pass-limit test is judged next, so a held batch that has both exhausted its passes and somehow sits off `DIP_LINE` is refused with `ERR_DEINK_PASS_LIMIT`. Entering a deink pass carries the three chemical charges for the pass in flight: `caustic_kg` for the sodium hydroxide, `peroxide_kg` for the hydrogen peroxide, and `surfactant_kg` for the collector surfactant. All three are optional on the request and none may be negative: a charge the request omits is taken as 0, and all three default to 0 on a batch that has never run a pass.

Approval — from either `CLEANED` or `DEINKING` — is where the batch's authoritative tonnage is struck, and it uses measured weights rather than book yields. `final_accepted_tons` is the batch's `charged_tons` minus its `measured_reject_tons` minus the total deink sludge charged against the batch, rounded half up to three decimal places, where the deink sludge total is the sum of `dry_solids_tons` over every non-reversal solids-ledger row whose `source_kind` is `DEINK_SLUDGE` and whose `source_ref_id` is this batch, taken across all passes in ascending creation order. Only rows that are themselves reversals drop out of that sum — the test is on `reversal_of_id` being null and never on the row's `disposition` — so an original sludge row whose tonnage has since been reversed, and which therefore reads `REPULPED`, still deducts from the batch's yield in full. `yield_variance_tons` is `final_accepted_tons` minus `provisional_accepted_tons`, rounded to three decimal places and signed — it may legitimately be negative. `available_tons` is `final_accepted_tons` minus `consumed_tons` rounded to three decimal places while the batch is `APPROVED`, and reads 0.000 in every other status; at the instant of approval `consumed_tons` is still 0.000, so `available_tons` opens equal to `final_accepted_tons`. The two yield figures are deliberately kept side by side: the provisional number is a projection from grade book yields discounted for contamination, the final number is physical weight subtraction, they differ for identical furnish, and nothing in this system ever reconciles them back together.

Brightness closes a deink pass, and the measured value decides which way the batch goes. A `QualityLab` operator submits `brightness_iso`, a value from 0 through 100 at one decimal place measured after the pass just run; the field reads null on a batch that has never had a pass. If the measured brightness is at least the target specification's `min_brightness_iso`, the batch goes `DEINKING → APPROVED` and strikes its final tonnage as described above. If the measured brightness falls short of `min_brightness_iso`, the batch goes `DEINKING → DEINK_HOLD` instead. A target specification whose `min_brightness_iso` is null carries no threshold to miss, so the pass always passes and such a batch always takes the `APPROVED` branch whatever brightness is submitted. Both outcomes require the batch to be in `DEINKING`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_PULP_BATCH_INVALID_STATE`, and both share three side effects: `deink_pass_count` is incremented by one, the pass's chemical cost is added into `cumulative_chemical_cost`, and one solids-ledger row is appended with `source_kind` of `DEINK_SLUDGE`, `source_ref_id` pointing at this batch, `dry_solids_tons` equal to the pass's computed sludge tonnage, and `disposition` of `PENDING`. Only the approving branch additionally sets `final_accepted_tons` and `yield_variance_tons`.

The chemistry of a pass costs money and removes ink, and both are computed from the same inputs. The **pass index** is the batch's `deink_pass_count` before the increment plus one — the pass now being closed. The **raw cost** is `caustic_kg` multiplied by 0.62 dollars per kilogram, plus `peroxide_kg` multiplied by 1.15 dollars per kilogram, plus `surfactant_kg` multiplied by 3.40 dollars per kilogram, rounded half up to two decimal places. The **pass cost** equals that raw cost when the target product's `family` is `CONTAINERBOARD`, and equals the raw cost multiplied by 1.10 — a ten percent tissue premium — rounded half up to two decimal places when the family is `TISSUE`; identical chemical charges therefore cost different money depending on where the pulp is going. **Effectiveness** decays across passes: it is 0.65 raised to the power of the pass index minus one, rounded half up to four decimal places, so the first pass runs at full effectiveness, the second at 0.6500, the third at 0.4225. The **removal fraction** is the base removal of 0.35 plus 0.08 multiplied by the surfactant dose per ton of furnish — where the dose is `surfactant_kg` divided by the larger of 1.000 and `charged_tons`, guarding against a divide-by-tiny on a token batch — the sum then multiplied by the effectiveness, the product capped at a maximum removal fraction of 0.95, and the result rounded half up to four decimal places. The pass's **sludge tonnage** is `charged_tons` multiplied by an assumed ink load of 4.50 percent, divided by 100, multiplied by the removal fraction, rounded half up to three decimal places; that is the figure written into the appended `DEINK_SLUDGE` row. Finally, `cumulative_chemical_cost` becomes its previous value plus this pass's cost, rounded half up to two decimal places, so the field accumulates across every pass the batch ever runs.

A held batch may be re-charged. Moving `DEINK_HOLD → DEINKING` requires the batch to be in `DEINK_HOLD` and requires `deink_pass_count` to be below 3; it replaces `caustic_kg`, `peroxide_kg` and `surfactant_kg` with the new pass's charges. Since the counter starts at 0, this allows three passes in total, and the pass that would be the fourth — attempted when `deink_pass_count` already reads 3 — is rejected with `ERR_DEINK_PASS_LIMIT` (HTTP 409). A batch that cannot be brought to brightness is written off instead: a `MillPlanner` diverts it, `DEINK_HOLD → DIVERTED`, which requires the batch to be in `DEINK_HOLD` and sets both `final_accepted_tons` and `available_tons` to 0.000. Diversion cascades in three hops. Hop one aborts every production run whose `pulp_batch_id` is this batch and whose `run_status` is `SCHEDULED`, setting `run_status` to `ABORTED` and `quality_status` to `CONDEMNED`. Hop two returns every customer order whose `scheduled_run_id` is one of those aborted runs and whose own status reads `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PRODUCTION` to `SUBMITTED`, clearing `scheduled_run_id` and incrementing `deschedule_count` by one. Hop three recomputes each such order's `shortfall_tons` as its `ordered_tons` minus its `allocated_tons`, floored at zero and rounded to three decimal places, and releases the aborted run's window on the paper machine so the machine's occupancy interval no longer blocks new scheduling.

Cancellation of a batch differs by where it is caught. A batch cancelled while `QUEUED` — whether by a `MillPlanner` acting deliberately or by the supplier-suspension cascade seizing one of its furnish lots — makes no inventory adjustment at all, because a queued batch has consumed nothing. A batch cancelled while `PULPING` must undo its reservation: each furnish line's `tons_charged` is subtracted back out of its lot's `tons_consumed`, and any lot that consequently leaves `DEPLETED` returns to `AVAILABLE`. This is the compensating action that keeps the mill fiber balance at zero imbalance across a cancellation. `APPROVED`, `DIVERTED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal batch states; requesting a step from a state that does not offer it is rejected with `ERR_PULP_BATCH_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.4. Machine Capacity, Run Windows and Grade Transitions

A `MillPlanner` registers each paper machine with a `name` of 1 to 20 characters that must be unique across the mill, a `wire_width_mm` trimmed deckle width from 1000 through 8000 that caps how wide any roll slit on that machine may be, a nameplate `max_tons_per_hour` greater than zero at two decimal places, a `changeover_buffer_minutes` idle buffer from 0 through 240 that must separate two run windows on that machine, and a `steam_tph_per_ton` figure greater than zero at three decimal places describing how many tonnes per hour of steam the machine draws per production ton. A newly registered machine opens with `status` of `RUNNABLE`, `version` of 1, and `current_product_code` null. The planner may take a machine out of service or return it, setting `status` to `DOWN` or `RUNNABLE`; a `DOWN` machine blocks both new run scheduling and run starting, each attempt being rejected with `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). `current_product_code` always names the product of the most recently completed run on that machine, and is null while no run has completed there.

Finished paper is described by a **paper grade spec**, declared by the planner with a `product_code` business key that must be unique and must take the shape of two to four uppercase letters, an underscore, then two or three digits — `TL_140`, `MED_112`, `TIS_16`. Each spec names its `family`, either `CONTAINERBOARD` or `TISSUE`; a `basis_weight_gsm` target from 12 through 400 at one decimal place; a `target_moisture_pct` from 2 through 12 at two decimal places with a `moisture_tolerance_pct` from 0.1 through 3 at two decimal places giving the half-width of the acceptable band on either side of the target; a `min_sct_kn_m` minimum short-span compressive strength of at least zero at two decimal places; an optional `min_brightness_iso` from 0 through 100 at one decimal place, left null for grades with no brightness requirement; a `requires_deink_pulp` boolean marking grades that may only be fed by pulp that has completed at least one deink pass; and a unique `rank_order` integer from 1 through 99 used for grade-change sequencing and tie-breaks.

Scheduling a run is a planner act that creates the run in `run_status` of `SCHEDULED` and `quality_status` of `PENDING`. The request names the `product_code` being made, the `pulp_batch_id` of the approved furnish the run will draw from, the planned `window_start` and `window_end` as UTC instants, the `pulp_draw_tons` greater than zero at three decimal places to be reserved from that batch, and the `expected_machine_version`. The run's `machine_id` comes from the path rather than the body. `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`, or the request fails with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. The batch's `target_product_code` must equal the run's `product_code`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_PRODUCT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). The checks fall in a fixed order after request shape: the window relation, then the `product_code` resolving to a declared spec (`ERR_GRADE_CODE_NOT_FOUND`) and the `pulp_batch_id` resolving to a real batch (`ERR_FURNISH_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`), then `expected_machine_version`, then the machine's `DOWN` status, then the product match, and last the occupancy conflict. Scheduling imposes no status gate on the referenced batch and makes no comparison of `pulp_draw_tons` against the batch's `available_tons`: the batch is required to be `APPROVED` and to carry the tonnage only when the run is actually started.

Machine occupancy is a **closed interval, widened by the changeover buffer on both ends**. The requested window, extended earlier by `changeover_buffer_minutes` and later by the same amount, must not intersect the unwidened closed window of any other run on the same machine whose `run_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING`. Because the intervals are closed, two windows that merely touch at an endpoint do collide, and any intersection at all — including a single shared instant — is rejected with `ERR_MACHINE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). This is deliberately stricter than the steam header's geometry described in §3.7, where the same two timestamps would coexist.

A **grade change** records the cost of turning the machine from one product to another. The planner plans it against the machine, naming the `from_run_id` of the outgoing run and the `to_run_id` of the incoming run, together with `expected_machine_version`; both run ids must resolve to runs on the addressed machine, and a failure to do so is `ERR_LINK_TARGET_NOT_FOUND`; `from_run_id` is resolved before `to_run_id` and both before the `expected_machine_version` check, and neither run's own `run_status` is examined. The grade change copies the outgoing run's product into `from_product_code` and the incoming run's product into `to_product_code`, takes its `machine_id` from the path, and opens in `PLANNED`.

Transition waste is computed at planning time from the two specs and the parent machine, and nothing else — no other run participates. First, the **basis-weight delta** is the absolute difference between the incoming spec's `basis_weight_gsm` and the outgoing spec's. That delta selects a base waste tier: a delta of 10.0 grams per square metre or less costs 0.800 tons, a delta above 10.0 but no more than 40.0 costs 2.500 tons, and a delta above 40.0 costs 5.000 tons. Second, a **brightness penalty** of 1.500 tons is added when the incoming spec has a `min_brightness_iso` that is not null and either the outgoing spec has no brightness requirement at all or the incoming requirement is strictly higher than the outgoing one; otherwise the penalty is 0.000 tons. Third, a **family multiplier** of 1.00 applies when both specs share the same `family`, and 2.20 applies when the transition crosses between `CONTAINERBOARD` and `TISSUE`. Fourth, a **direction multiplier** of 1.15 applies when the incoming spec's `rank_order` is strictly lower than the outgoing spec's — running the sequence backwards costs an extra fifteen percent — and 1.00 otherwise, which is why the same pair of products produces different waste in the two directions. The sum of the base tier and the brightness penalty is then multiplied by the family multiplier and by the direction multiplier, the product is capped at a ceiling of 12.000 tons, and the result rounded half up to three decimal places becomes `transition_waste_tons`. Finally, `transition_minutes` is the waste tonnage divided by the machine's `max_tons_per_hour` and multiplied by 60, rounded **up** to the next whole minute, plus the machine's `changeover_buffer_minutes` — the ceiling is applied after the tonnage has been rounded to three decimal places, not before.

The plan must physically fit. The gap between the outgoing run's `window_end` and the incoming run's `window_start`, measured in whole minutes with any part-minute remainder discarded downward, must be at least the computed `transition_minutes`; the boundary is inclusive, so a gap exactly equal to the computed duration is acceptable, while a shorter gap is rejected with `ERR_TRANSITION_GAP_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 409). Planning a grade change bumps the machine's `version` by one.

A planned grade change is realised when the outgoing run completes: at that moment every grade change whose `from_run_id` is the completing run and whose status is `PLANNED` flips `PLANNED → EXECUTED`, and each appends a solids-ledger row with `source_kind` of `TRANSITION_WASTE`, `source_ref_id` pointing at the grade change itself, and `dry_solids_tons` equal to the frozen `transition_waste_tons`. A planner may instead abandon the plan, taking it `PLANNED → VOIDED`; voiding requires the grade change to be in `PLANNED`, is rejected with `ERR_ENV_RECORD_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) otherwise, and appends no solids row at all — because none was created at planning time, there is nothing to compensate.

### 3.5. Running the Machine and the Two Independent Status Axes

A production run carries **two status axes that advance independently**. The `run_status` axis tracks the physical run through `SCHEDULED`, `RUNNING`, `COMPLETED` and `ABORTED`, with `COMPLETED` and `ABORTED` terminal. The `quality_status` axis tracks the laboratory's verdict through `PENDING`, `PROVISIONAL`, `HELD`, `REWORK`, `CERTIFIED` and `CONDEMNED`. The two axes are checked separately, and this has a concrete consequence for error selection: a run that is `COMPLETED` on the run axis but `HELD` on the quality axis rejects a certification attempt on the **quality** axis with `ERR_QUALITY_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), not on the run axis. Conversely, a request that targets the run axis from the wrong state — starting a run that is not `SCHEDULED`, completing or aborting a run that is not `RUNNING` — is rejected with `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), which is also the code returned when the parent machine's `status` is `DOWN` at start time.

Starting a run, `SCHEDULED → RUNNING`, is the `MachineOperator`'s act and gathers four gates that fire in a fixed order. The run-axis and machine-status check comes first. Second comes the environmental gate: the mill's effluent permit must have a `compliance_posture` of `COMPLIANT` or `WATCH`, and a posture of `EXCEEDANCE` blocks every run start with `ERR_PERMIT_EXCEEDANCE_BLOCK` (HTTP 409) — this gate is evaluated **before** the pulp checks, so a run that is both environmentally blocked and short of pulp reports the permit exceedance, while a run that is not in `SCHEDULED` reports the run-state failure ahead of the permit. Third, if the run's product specification has `requires_deink_pulp` set, the drawn batch's `deink_pass_count` must be at least 1, and a batch that has never been deinked is rejected with `ERR_DEINK_PULP_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409). Fourth, the pulp must actually be there: the referenced batch must be in `APPROVED` and its `available_tons` must be at least the run's `pulp_draw_tons`, with the boundary inclusive, and a shortfall or an unapproved batch is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PULP` (HTTP 409). A run whose inbound grade change — a change whose `to_run_id` is this run — has not yet reached `EXECUTED` may not start either, and that attempt is rejected with `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE`; this gate belongs to the first, run-axis family and is evaluated immediately after the machine-status check and therefore **before** the environmental gate, any inbound change that is not `EXECUTED` — `PLANNED` and `VOIDED` alike — blocks the start, and a run with no inbound grade change at all is unaffected.

Starting a run has four side effects. The run's `pulp_draw_tons` is added into the batch's `consumed_tons` and the batch's `available_tons` is recomputed. The acting operator's identifier is stored as `started_by_user_id`. Every customer order whose `scheduled_run_id` is this run advances `SCHEDULED → IN_PRODUCTION`, each such order's `version` incrementing. And the parent machine's `version` is bumped by one.

Completion, `RUNNING → COMPLETED`, carries the run's measured output. The operator submits `reel_readings`, an array of 1 to 10 entries each holding a `reel_no`, a `reel_tons` figure at three decimal places, and the reel's measured `basis_weight_gsm` at one decimal place, `moisture_pct` at two decimal places and `sct_kn_m` at two decimal places, with `reel_no` an integer of 1 or more that is unique and ascending, `reel_tons` strictly greater than zero and the three measurements zero or greater with no upper bound; and `broke_tons`, the off-spec broke weighed at completion, optional, never negative and defaulting to 0. Completion stores both, recomputes `off_spec_reel_count`, computes the provisional quality index, and moves the quality axis `PENDING → PROVISIONAL` — the single point at which the two axes touch. It appends a solids-ledger row with `source_kind` of `MACHINE_BROKE`, `source_ref_id` pointing at this run, `dry_solids_tons` equal to `broke_tons` and `disposition` of `PENDING`, skipping that row entirely when `broke_tons` is 0.000. It sets the machine's `current_product_code` to the run's `product_code`, executes any planned grade change leaving this run as described in §3.4, and bumps the machine's `version` by one.

Both quality indexes are built from the same per-reel scoring function, and the function is worth stating once carefully. For a reel, the **basis-weight deviation** is the absolute difference between the reel's `basis_weight_gsm` and the spec's `basis_weight_gsm`, divided by the spec's `basis_weight_gsm` and multiplied by 100 to give a percentage. The **moisture deviation** is the absolute difference between the reel's `moisture_pct` and the spec's `target_moisture_pct`. The **strength ratio** is exactly 1.0000 when the spec's `min_sct_kn_m` is 0.00, and otherwise the reel's `sct_kn_m` divided by the spec's `min_sct_kn_m`. Three penalties are then assessed against a perfect score of 100.00: the basis-weight penalty is 4.0 points for each percentage point of basis-weight deviation; the moisture penalty is 12.0 points for each percentage point by which the moisture deviation exceeds the spec's `moisture_tolerance_pct`, floored at zero so a reel inside the band is not penalised; and the strength penalty is 30.0 points multiplied by the shortfall of the strength ratio below 1, floored at zero so a reel stronger than the minimum earns no bonus. The reel's score is 100.00 minus the three penalties, floored at 0.00 and **rounded half up to two decimal places before it is used for anything else**.

The **provisional quality index**, stored as `provisional_quality_index`, is the plain arithmetic mean of exactly two reel scores — the reel with the smallest `reel_no` and the reel with the largest `reel_no` — rounded half up to two decimal places, regardless of how many reels the run actually produced. A single-reel run uses that one reel twice, which simply reproduces its score. Alongside it, `off_spec_reel_count` is the number of reel readings that breach any spec limit, where a reel is off-spec when its basis-weight deviation percentage is strictly greater than 5.00, or its moisture deviation is strictly greater than the spec's `moisture_tolerance_pct`, or its `sct_kn_m` is strictly below the spec's `min_sct_kn_m`.

The **certified quality index** is the tonnage-weighted mean of **every** reel: the sum over all reel readings of `reel_tons` multiplied by that reel's already-rounded score, divided by the sum of all `reel_tons`, the quotient rounded half up to two decimal places. Reel order is irrelevant to a weighted mean, but the per-reel rounding is not — each score is rounded to two decimal places before it is weighted. The two indexes are stored side by side and are never reconciled: a run whose first and last reels are on-spec but whose middle reels are not can carry a provisional index of 85.00 or above while its certified index falls below 85.00, which is precisely the case the certification gate exists to catch.

Certification, `PROVISIONAL → CERTIFIED`, is a `QualityLab` act with four requirements evaluated in this order: the quality axis must read `PROVISIONAL`; the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the run's `started_by_user_id`, and an operator certifying their own run is rejected with `ERR_SOD_SAME_OPERATOR` (HTTP 409); `off_spec_reel_count` must be exactly 0; and the certified quality index must be at least 85.00. A run failing either of the last two conditions cannot be certified and must be held instead; the certification attempt is rejected with `ERR_QUALITY_INVALID_STATE`, and the index computed while evaluating that refused attempt is discarded rather than stored, so `certified_quality_index` stays null until either a certification or a hold actually lands. Certification stores `certified_quality_index`, records the acting identifier as `certified_by_user_id`, and makes every `WRAPPED` roll set of the run eligible for allocation.

Holding, `PROVISIONAL → HELD`, is the mirror branch: it requires the quality axis to read `PROVISIONAL` and requires that the run has at least one off-spec reel or a certified quality index below 85.00. It also computes and stores `certified_quality_index` — the number is struck whichever way the verdict goes. A hold then cascades in three hops. Hop one flips every roll set of the run whose status is `WOUND`, `WRAPPED` or `ALLOCATED` to `QUARANTINED`, setting `quarantine_reason` to `RUN_HELD` and clearing `allocated_order_id`. Hop two makes each customer order that lost rolls in hop one recompute its `allocated_tons` and `shortfall_tons`. Hop three drops each such order that had reached `ALLOCATED` and whose recomputed `allocated_tons` is now below its `ordered_tons` back to `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`, incrementing that order's `version`.

A held run may be reworked or written off, and both are planner decisions. Rework, `HELD → REWORK`, requires the quality axis to read `HELD` and flips every `QUARANTINED` roll set of the run to `REWOUND`, incrementing each set's `rewind_count` — this cascade is not gated by the two-rewind limit of §3.6 and increments the counter whatever it already reads. Once the rewinders have finished, a `QualityLab` operator resubmits quality, `REWORK → PROVISIONAL`, supplying corrected `reel_readings`; this requires the quality axis to read `REWORK` and requires that every roll set of the run now reads `WOUND` or `SCRAPPED`, so no rewind is still in flight, a set in any other status being rejected with `ERR_ROLL_SET_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Resubmission replaces the stored `reel_readings` and recomputes both `off_spec_reel_count` and the provisional quality index, putting the run back in front of the certification gate. This gives the cycle `PROVISIONAL → HELD → REWORK → PROVISIONAL`, which runs entirely on the quality axis while `run_status` stays `COMPLETED` throughout. Condemnation, `HELD → CONDEMNED`, also requires the quality axis to read `HELD`; it flips every roll set of the run in `QUARANTINED`, `REWOUND`, `WOUND` or `WRAPPED` to `SCRAPPED`, appending for each one a solids-ledger row with `source_kind` of `MACHINE_BROKE`, `source_ref_id` pointing at the run and `dry_solids_tons` equal to that set's `net_tons`, and it recomputes the run's `produced_tons` to 0.000.

Aborting a run, `RUNNING → ABORTED`, is the planner's emergency stop and is the compensating action for the pulp draw. It subtracts `pulp_draw_tons` back out of the batch's `consumed_tons` and recomputes `available_tons`, so total fiber tonnage is conserved. Every roll set of the run in `WOUND` or `WRAPPED` flips to `SCRAPPED`, each appending a `MACHINE_BROKE` solids row carrying that set's `net_tons`. The quality axis is forced to `CONDEMNED` regardless of what it read before. Every customer order whose `scheduled_run_id` is this run and whose own status reads `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PRODUCTION` returns to `SUBMITTED` with `scheduled_run_id` cleared and `deschedule_count` incremented, an order in any other status being left as it stands. And the machine's `version` is bumped by one. A run aborted by cascade while still `SCHEDULED` — from a pulp-batch diversion or a supplier-suspension chain — needs no pulp adjustment, because nothing had been drawn; it simply takes `run_status` of `ABORTED` and `quality_status` of `CONDEMNED`. A run's `produced_tons` is at all times the sum of `net_tons` over its roll sets whose status is anything other than `REWOUND`, rounded to three decimal places.

### 3.6. Customer Orders, Roll Finishing and Allocation

A customer is registered by a `MillPlanner` with its legal `name` of 1 to 120 characters, an `allocation_priority_tier` of `P1`, `P2` or `P3` defaulting to `P3` — the lower the number, the higher the customer ranks when rolls are being allocated — and the roll envelope the customer's own handling equipment can accept: `max_roll_diameter_mm` from 800 through 1800, `max_roll_width_mm` from 400 through 2800, and a `wrap_spec` of `KRAFT_ONLY`, `POLY_COATED` or `STRETCH_HOOD` defaulting to `KRAFT_ONLY`. A new customer opens with `status` of `ACTIVE`; the planner may later set that `status` to `SUSPENDED` or back to `ACTIVE`, and a `SUSPENDED` customer can neither submit new orders nor have existing ones sequenced onto runs.

Orders are submitted by the customers themselves. The submission carries the `customer_id` — which must equal the acting `X-User-ID`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — the `product_code` wanted, `ordered_tons` greater than zero and not above 2000 at two decimal places, a `requested_ship_date` UTC instant that feeds allocation ranking alongside the customer's priority tier, and the roll specification: `roll_width_mm` from 400 through 2800, `roll_diameter_mm` from 800 through 1800, and a `wrap_spec`. The requested specification must fit inside the customer's declared envelope — `roll_width_mm` no greater than the customer's `max_roll_width_mm`, `roll_diameter_mm` no greater than the customer's `max_roll_diameter_mm`, and `wrap_spec` exactly equal to the customer's `wrap_spec` — and a request outside that envelope is rejected with `ERR_CUSTOMER_SPEC_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422). Submitting an order for a `SUSPENDED` customer is rejected with `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The checks run in the order: the `customer_id` ownership comparison, then request shape, then the `product_code` resolving to a declared spec (`ERR_GRADE_CODE_NOT_FOUND`), then the envelope test, then the customer's `ACTIVE` status — so a suspended customer that also submits an out-of-envelope specification receives `ERR_CUSTOMER_SPEC_VIOLATION`. A new order opens in `SUBMITTED` with `allocated_tons` of 0.000, `shipped_tons` of 0.000, `shortfall_tons` equal to its `ordered_tons`, `deschedule_count` of 0, `scheduled_run_id` null, and `version` of 1.

Sequencing an order onto a run, `SUBMITTED → SCHEDULED`, is a planner act carrying the `run_id` and `expected_order_version`. The `run_id` is resolved before anything else is judged, an unresolvable one being `ERR_LINK_TARGET_NOT_FOUND`. It then requires the order to be in `SUBMITTED` and the customer to be `ACTIVE`, in that order and both failures reporting `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`; requires the target run's `run_status` to be `SCHEDULED`, a run in any other state being rejected with `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE`; then checks `expected_order_version`; and then requires the run's `product_code` to equal the order's `product_code`, a mismatch being rejected with `ERR_PRODUCT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). It also respects the run's booked capacity: the sum of `ordered_tons` over the orders already holding this run as their `scheduled_run_id`, plus this order's own `ordered_tons`, must not exceed the run's `pulp_draw_tons`, and an over-booking is rejected with `ERR_RUN_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409); the boundary is inclusive, so filling the run exactly is allowed. Success sets `scheduled_run_id` and bumps the order's `version` by one.

Descheduling, `SCHEDULED → SUBMITTED`, returns the order to the unscheduled pool. It requires the order to be in `SCHEDULED` and requires `deschedule_count` to be below 3; it clears `scheduled_run_id`, increments `deschedule_count` by one and bumps `version`. Three deschedules are therefore permitted, and the fourth attempt — made when `deschedule_count` already reads 3 — is rejected with `ERR_DESCHEDULE_LIMIT` (HTTP 409). Cascade-driven deschedules from a run abort or a batch diversion follow the same path and increment the same counter, but they are not themselves gated by the limit: a cascade returns an order that is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PRODUCTION` to `SUBMITTED` and increments `deschedule_count` even when the counter already reads 3 or more.

Rolls come off the winder as **roll sets**. A `MachineOperator` records a set against a run that is currently `RUNNING`, supplying `slit_width_mm` from 400 through 2800, `diameter_mm` from 800 through 1800, a `roll_count` from 1 through 40, and the set's `net_tons` greater than zero at three decimal places; the set's `production_run_id` comes from the path. The set's `slit_width_mm` may not exceed the producing machine's `wire_width_mm`, and a wider slit is rejected with `ERR_WIDTH_EXCEEDS_DECKLE` (HTTP 409) — a rule that applies equally when a set is wrapped and when a rewind is completed. A `wrap_spec` may also be supplied at winding and is stored as given, but it is optional there and reads null when omitted — the wrapping step is what normally sets it. A new set opens in `WOUND` with `rewind_count` of 0, `quarantine_reason` null, and `allocated_order_id` null, and its creation recomputes the run's `produced_tons`. Recording a set against a run that is not in the required run-axis state is rejected with `ERR_RUN_INVALID_STATE`.

Wrapping, `WOUND → WRAPPED`, stores the `wrap_spec` applied at the wrapper. It requires the set to be in `WOUND`, requires the producing run's `run_status` to be `COMPLETED` — an attempt before the run finishes is rejected with `ERR_RUN_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 409) — and re-checks the deckle limit.

Allocation, `WRAPPED → ALLOCATED`, is the planner's act of promising a wrapped set to an order, and it carries the `order_id` and `expected_order_version`. Its checks run in this order: the `order_id` must first resolve to a customer order, an unresolvable one being `ERR_LINK_TARGET_NOT_FOUND`; then the set must be in `WRAPPED`, or `ERR_ROLL_SET_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409); the producing run's `quality_status` must be `CERTIFIED`, or `ERR_RUN_NOT_CERTIFIED`; the target order's `status` must be `IN_PRODUCTION` or `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`, or `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`; the order's `product_code` must equal the producing run's `product_code`, or `ERR_PRODUCT_MISMATCH`; `expected_order_version` must match the order's current `version`, or `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412); and the geometry must fit the order exactly — the set's `slit_width_mm` must **equal** the order's `roll_width_mm`, the set's `diameter_mm` must be no greater than the order's `roll_diameter_mm`, and the set's `wrap_spec` must equal the order's `wrap_spec` — with any deviation rejected as `ERR_ROLL_SPEC_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). Success sets `allocated_order_id`, recomputes the order's `allocated_tons` and `shortfall_tons`, drives the order's own status forward, and bumps the order's `version` by one.

The order's ledger is recomputed from the roll sets rather than accumulated: `allocated_tons` is the sum of `net_tons` over roll sets whose `allocated_order_id` is this order and whose status is `ALLOCATED` or `SHIPPED`, rounded to three decimal places; `shipped_tons` is the same sum restricted to sets in `SHIPPED`; and `shortfall_tons` is `ordered_tons` minus `allocated_tons`, floored at zero and rounded to three decimal places. Sets in `QUARANTINED`, `REWOUND`, `WOUND`, `WRAPPED` or `SCRAPPED` contribute nothing. Each recomputation moves the order along: from `IN_PRODUCTION`, an allocation that leaves `allocated_tons` below `ordered_tons` advances the order `IN_PRODUCTION → PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`, while an allocation that already covers `ordered_tons` advances it `IN_PRODUCTION → ALLOCATED` directly; from `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED`, reaching `ordered_tons` or more advances `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED → ALLOCATED` and sets `shortfall_tons` to 0.000, with the boundary inclusive so `allocated_tons` exactly equal to `ordered_tons` reaches `ALLOCATED`. The reverse move exists too: when the quality-hold cascade strips rolls from a fully allocated order, that order falls back `ALLOCATED → PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` with `allocated_tons` and `shortfall_tons` recomputed. Every one of these system-driven order moves increments the order's `version`.

Shipping, `ALLOCATED → SHIPPED`, requires the order to be in `ALLOCATED`, requires that every roll set pointing at the order currently reads `ALLOCATED` — a set in any other status being rejected with `ERR_ROLL_SET_INVALID_STATE` — and requires the order's scheduled run to have `quality_status` of `CERTIFIED`, which is reported as `ERR_RUN_NOT_CERTIFIED` and is also the code returned when the order holds no `scheduled_run_id` at all. Shipping flips every roll set of the order `ALLOCATED → SHIPPED`, recomputes `shipped_tons`, and bumps the order's `version`. A planner may instead close a partially filled order short: `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED → SHORT_CLOSED` requires the order to be in `PARTIALLY_ALLOCATED` and requires `shortfall_tons` to be strictly greater than 0.000 — short-closing an order with nothing outstanding is rejected with `ERR_NO_SHORTFALL` (HTTP 409). Short-closing freezes `shortfall_tons` at its current value, flips the order's allocated roll sets `ALLOCATED → SHIPPED`, recomputes `shipped_tons` so those sets now count as shipped — `allocated_tons` is untouched by the flip, since a set counts as allocated whether it reads `ALLOCATED` or `SHIPPED`, which is exactly why the frozen shortfall equals the recomputed one — and bumps `version`. A customer may cancel its own order, `SUBMITTED → CANCELLED`, only while the order is in `SUBMITTED`, only when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the order's `customer_id`, and only when `allocated_tons` reads exactly 0.000; an order with tonnage already allocated is rejected with `ERR_TONNAGE_ALREADY_BOOKED` (HTTP 409). `SHIPPED`, `SHORT_CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

Roll sets have their own rework loop. A `QualityLab` operator may quarantine a set that is in `WOUND` or `WRAPPED`, supplying a `quarantine_reason` of `SPEC_MISMATCH`, `RUN_HELD` or `PERMIT_EXCEEDANCE`; the quarantine recomputes the producing run's `produced_tons`. The quality-hold cascade quarantines sets the same way, including sets already in `ALLOCATED`, in which case it clears `allocated_order_id`, stamps `quarantine_reason` as `RUN_HELD`, and forces the former order to recompute its `allocated_tons` — the compensating action that leaves the order exactly where it was before the allocation. A quarantined set goes to the rewinder, `QUARANTINED → REWOUND`, which requires `rewind_count` to be below 2 and increments it by one; two rewinds are therefore permitted and the third attempt, made when `rewind_count` already reads 2, is rejected with `ERR_REWIND_LIMIT` (HTTP 409). Completing the rewind, `REWOUND → WOUND`, supplies the new `slit_width_mm`, `diameter_mm`, `roll_count` and `net_tons`; the new `net_tons` may not exceed the pre-rewind `net_tons` because rewinding never creates fibre, and a larger figure is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (HTTP 422) — that tonnage comparison is made before the deckle re-check, so a submission that breaches both reports `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. Completion replaces the four geometry and tonnage fields, clears `quarantine_reason`, recomputes the run's `produced_tons`, and — when the pre-rewind `net_tons` exceeds the new `net_tons` — appends one solids-ledger row with `source_kind` of `MACHINE_BROKE`, `source_ref_id` pointing at the producing run, and `dry_solids_tons` equal to exactly that difference, so solids are conserved across the rewind; when the difference is 0.000 no row is appended. This yields the cycle `WOUND → WRAPPED → QUARANTINED → REWOUND → WOUND`. A planner may instead scrap a set that is in `QUARANTINED` or `REWOUND`, which appends a `MACHINE_BROKE` solids row carrying the set's full `net_tons` and recomputes the run's `produced_tons`. `SHIPPED` and `SCRAPPED` are the terminal roll-set states, and any transition requested from a state that does not offer it is rejected with `ERR_ROLL_SET_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.7. Discharge Permitting, Rolling Compliance and Residual Solids

The mill discharges under a single permit, registered by an `EnvironmentalOfficer` with a unique `permit_number` of 1 to 40 characters, a `bod_limit_kg_per_day` and a `tss_limit_kg_per_day` both greater than zero at two decimal places expressing rolling-average load ceilings, a `flow_limit_m3_per_day` greater than zero at two decimal places expressing a single-day hydraulic ceiling, and a `rolling_window_days` width from 7 through 30 defaulting to 30. A new permit opens with `compliance_posture` of `COMPLIANT`, `rolling_bod_kg_per_day` and `rolling_tss_kg_per_day` at 0.000, `exceedance_day_count` at 0, and `posture_changed_at` null. Nothing actually blocks a second permit row from being registered; where a rule needs "the" permit — the run-start gate of §3.5 and the signer's rolling-window check below — it reads the earliest-created permit row, while the `LAND_APPLICATION` bar fires whenever any permit row reads `EXCEEDANCE`.

Each day's discharge is logged as a reading against the permit: a `reading_date` in year-month-day form, a `flow_m3`, a `bod_kg` and a `tss_kg`, all three at two decimal places and none negative. The reading's `permit_id` comes from the path. Only one non-voided reading may exist per permit and calendar day, and a second one is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (HTTP 409). A `reading_date` later than the logical clock's current calendar date is rejected with `ERR_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422) — the mill cannot log tomorrow's discharge — and the duplicate-date test is applied before that date test, so a duplicate future date reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`. Logging records the acting officer's identifier as `logged_by_user_id`, and `voided` opens as false. A mis-recorded reading can be voided, which requires the reading's `voided` flag to be currently false — voiding an already-voided reading is rejected with `ERR_ENV_RECORD_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) — and sets `voided` to true, taking the row out of the rolling window entirely.

Both logging and voiding recompute the permit's rolling posture from scratch. If the permit has no non-voided readings at all, `rolling_bod_kg_per_day` and `rolling_tss_kg_per_day` are 0.000, `exceedance_day_count` is 0, and the posture is `COMPLIANT`. Otherwise, the window is anchored on the newest non-voided `reading_date`: it runs from that date backwards by the permit's `rolling_window_days` minus one, and **both ends are inclusive**, so a 30-day window spans exactly 30 calendar dates ending on the newest reading. The rolling loads are averages over the readings **present** in that window: `rolling_bod_kg_per_day` is the sum of `bod_kg` over the window's readings divided by the count of those readings, rounded half up to three decimal places, and `rolling_tss_kg_per_day` is the same over `tss_kg`. Days inside the window that carry no reading do not enter the denominator. `exceedance_day_count` is the number of readings inside the window whose `flow_m3` is strictly greater than the permit's `flow_limit_m3_per_day`. Because voiding the newest reading moves the anchor, a void can shrink the window and change every one of these figures.

The posture itself is derived from two ratios: the rolling BOD load divided by `bod_limit_kg_per_day`, and the rolling TSS load divided by `tss_limit_kg_per_day`, both compared unrounded. The posture is `EXCEEDANCE` when either ratio is strictly greater than 1.00, or when `exceedance_day_count` is 2 or more. Otherwise it is `WATCH` when the larger of the two ratios is at least 0.90. Otherwise it is `COMPLIANT`. The boundaries deserve care in both directions: a ratio of exactly 1.00 is **not** an exceedance, because the comparison is strict, so a permit sitting exactly at its limit lands on `WATCH`; and a `flow_m3` exactly equal to `flow_limit_m3_per_day` does not count as an exceedance day. Every posture change stamps `posture_changed_at` with the logical-clock instant.

Posture transitions in either direction. `COMPLIANT → WATCH` fires when the recomputation yields `WATCH`; nothing is blocked in `WATCH`, and a `LAND_APPLICATION` manifest destination remains permitted there. `WATCH → EXCEEDANCE` and `COMPLIANT → EXCEEDANCE` both fire when the recomputation yields `EXCEEDANCE` — a single bad reading can jump straight from `COMPLIANT` without resting in `WATCH` — and both carry the same three-hop cascade. Hop one voids every sludge manifest that is in `DRAFT` with a `destination` of `LAND_APPLICATION`, flipping it to `VOIDED`. Hop two clears `manifest_id` on every solids row whose `manifest_id` pointed at a manifest voided in hop one and returns that row's `disposition` to `PENDING`. Hop three puts those rows back into the unmanifested pool, so the mill-wide `unmanifested_dry_solids_tons` figure rises by exactly the total dry solids of the voided manifests. While the posture is `EXCEEDANCE`, run starts are blocked. Recovery runs the other way: `EXCEEDANCE → WATCH` fires when a recomputation — after voiding a reading, or once the rolling window has advanced past an offending day — yields `WATCH`, and `WATCH → COMPLIANT` fires when both ratios fall below 0.90 with `exceedance_day_count` at 0, which can also happen in a single step as `EXCEEDANCE → COMPLIANT`. Recovery does **not** restore the manifests voided on the way in; re-manifesting is a manual act.

Residual solids are tracked in a single append-only ledger. Every row records its `source_kind` — `PULP_REJECT` and `DEINK_SLUDGE` rows point at a pulp batch, `MACHINE_BROKE` rows point at a production run, and `TRANSITION_WASTE` rows point at a grade change — with the referenced identifier held in `source_ref_id` and the discriminator being `source_kind` itself. Each row carries its `dry_solids_tons` at three decimal places and never negative, a `disposition` that opens as `PENDING`, a `manifest_id` that stays null while the disposition is `PENDING`, and a `reversal_of_id` that is null on an ordinary row.

A hauler manifest claims pending tonnage. An `EnvironmentalOfficer` prepares one with a unique `manifest_number` of 1 to 40 characters, a `destination` of `LANDFILL`, `LAND_APPLICATION` or `FUEL`, and a `reject_stream_ids` array of between 1 and 20 unique solids-row identifiers. Every entry must resolve to a real row that is **not** itself a reversal; an entry that fails either test is rejected with `ERR_REJECT_STREAM_REFERENCE_INVALID` (HTTP 404). Preparing a `LAND_APPLICATION` manifest while the permit posture is `EXCEEDANCE` is rejected with `ERR_LAND_APPLICATION_BARRED` (HTTP 409), and so is signing one. Preparation resolves the claimed rows first, then applies the `LAND_APPLICATION` bar, and only then tests `manifest_number` for uniqueness, so a barred manifest carrying a duplicate number reports `ERR_LAND_APPLICATION_BARRED` rather than `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`. Preparation records the acting officer as `prepared_by_user_id`, computes `total_dry_solids_tons` as the sum of `dry_solids_tons` across the claimed rows rounded to three decimal places, opens the manifest in `DRAFT`, and leaves the claimed rows in `PENDING` — nothing moves until the manifest is signed.

Signing, `DRAFT → SIGNED`, is guarded by the strictest segregation-of-duties rule in the system. It requires the manifest to be in `DRAFT`, or `ERR_ENV_RECORD_INVALID_STATE`. It then requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the manifest's `prepared_by_user_id` **and** that the acting `X-User-ID` is not the `logged_by_user_id` of any non-voided reading inside the permit's current rolling window; a violation of either half is rejected with `ERR_SOD_SAME_ENV_OFFICER` (HTTP 409). Signing records `signed_by_user_id`, sets `manifest_id` on each claimed solids row, and moves each of those rows' `disposition` from `PENDING` to `MANIFESTED`. Voiding a manifest is available from `DRAFT` or `SIGNED`, moves it to `VOIDED`, and is the exact compensating action for signing: each claimed row has its `manifest_id` cleared and its `disposition` returned to `PENDING`, leaving `net_solids_tons` unchanged before and after.

A row weighed in error is reversed rather than edited. Reversing requires the addressed row's `disposition` to be `PENDING`, failing with `ERR_ENV_RECORD_INVALID_STATE` otherwise, and produces a **new** solids row whose `reversal_of_id` is the addressed row and whose `dry_solids_tons` equals the addressed row's tonnage; that reversal row copies the addressed row's `source_kind` and `source_ref_id`, opens with `disposition` of `PENDING` and `manifest_id` null, and — being a reversal — never itself contributes to any of the three disposition splits. That reversal row counts negatively in every solids aggregation, and the addressed row's `disposition` moves `PENDING → REPULPED`. Net solids therefore drop by exactly the reversed tonnage while the ledger invariant of §2.9 continues to hold, and because the reversal is created as a new resource the operation reports 201 rather than 200.

### 3.8. The Steam Header, Curtailment and Cogeneration Credit

Steam is a shared header with finite capacity, and the `EnergyManager` books it in windows. A request names the `consumer_kind` — `PAPER_MACHINE`, `DEINK_PLANT` or `EVAPORATOR` — the `consumer_ref_id`, which holds the identifier of the paper machine when the consumer kind is `PAPER_MACHINE` and is null for the deink plant and the evaporator, the `window_start` and `window_end` instants, a `demand_tph` greater than zero and not above 200 at three decimal places, and a `priority_rank` from 1 through 3 where 1 is served first. For a `PAPER_MACHINE` request the `consumer_ref_id` is mandatory — omitting it is an `ERR_VALIDATION` failure — and must resolve to a registered paper machine, an unresolvable identifier being `ERR_LINK_TARGET_NOT_FOUND`; for `DEINK_PLANT` and `EVAPORATOR` any value supplied is ignored and the field is stored as null. Those two consumer checks are applied before the window checks. `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`, and `window_start` may not precede the logical clock instant, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422). An allocation's `status` begins as `REQUESTED` and is resolved by the dispatch below to `GRANTED` or `CURTAILED` before the response is returned.

Header geometry is **half-open**: a window covers its `window_start` inclusively and its `window_end` exclusively. Two allocations therefore overlap only when the first begins strictly before the second ends and ends strictly after the second begins, which means back-to-back allocations sharing an endpoint never contend and consume nothing from each other. This is the deliberate opposite of the paper-machine occupancy rule in §3.4, where the interval is closed and widened by the changeover buffer — the very same pair of timestamps conflicts on a machine and coexists on the header.

The dispatch is a priority waterfall over the header's total capacity of 180.000 tonnes per hour. The candidate set is every allocation whose `status` is `GRANTED` or `CURTAILED` and whose window overlaps the requested window under the half-open test above. Those candidates are ordered by ascending `priority_rank`, then by ascending `window_start`, then by ascending `created_at`, and finally by insertion order for rows sharing a `created_at` instant, giving a fully deterministic sequence, and the **consumed** figure is the sum of their `granted_tph` values rounded half up to three decimal places. The **remaining** headroom is 180.000 minus that consumed figure, rounded half up to three decimal places. Four outcomes follow, in this order. If the remaining headroom is 0.000 or less, the request is rejected with `ERR_STEAM_HEADER_SATURATED` (HTTP 409). Otherwise, if the remaining headroom is at least the requested `demand_tph`, the request is granted in full: `granted_tph` equals `demand_tph` and `status` becomes `GRANTED`. Otherwise, if the remaining headroom is below a minimum viable draw of 5.000 tonnes per hour, the request is again rejected with `ERR_STEAM_HEADER_SATURATED` — a dribble of steam is worse than none. Otherwise the request is partially served: `granted_tph` equals the remaining headroom and `status` becomes `CURTAILED`. In every granted or curtailed case, `curtailed_tph` is `demand_tph` minus `granted_tph`, rounded half up to three decimal places.

Whatever the header does not consume is exported, and export earns a **cogeneration credit** computed for the new allocation from the same dispatch figures. The window's `duration_hours` is its length in seconds divided by 3600, rounded half up to four decimal places. The exportable rate is 180.000 minus the consumed figure minus this allocation's own `granted_tph`, floored at 0.000 and rounded half up to three decimal places. That exportable rate is then split into three marginal bands: the first band takes up to 30.000 tonnes per hour, the second takes up to a further 30.000, and the third takes everything beyond 60.000. Each band's contribution is its tonnes per hour multiplied by its rate — 4.10 dollars per tonne-per-hour-hour for the first band, 6.75 for the second, 9.20 for the third — multiplied by the duration in hours, and **each band's product is rounded half up to four decimal places before the three are summed**; `cogen_credit_usd` is that sum rounded half up to two decimal places. This per-band-then-total rounding order is pinned: summing the three unrounded products and rounding once produces a different cent on fractional-hour windows and is not the behaviour required.

Releasing an allocation frees header capacity. It requires the allocation's `status` to be `GRANTED` or `CURTAILED`, failing with `ERR_ENV_RECORD_INVALID_STATE` otherwise, and moves the status to `RELEASED` with `granted_tph` set to 0.000 and `curtailed_tph` set to the full `demand_tph`; the `cogen_credit_usd` struck when the allocation was dispatched is left exactly as it stands. The freed capacity is available to requests that arrive afterwards, but **existing allocations are not re-dispatched** — a curtailed neighbour stays curtailed until it is itself replaced.

### 3.9. Read Surfaces and Filters

Every collection in the mill is readable, and the read surface is where operators reconcile the ledgers against the floor. Fiber grades may be filtered by `grade_code` and by `index_symbol`; published quotes by `index_symbol`, `effective_month` and `superseded`; paper grade specs by `product_code` and `family`. Purchase orders may be filtered by `supplier_id`, `fiber_grade_code` and `status`, and receipts by `purchase_order_id`, `supplier_id` and `status`. Inventory lots may be filtered by `receipt_id`, `grade_code` and `status`. Pulp batches may be filtered by `status`, `target_product_code`, `pulper_line`, and by `inventory_lot_id` to find every batch that consumed a given lot. Production runs may be filtered by `machine_id`, `pulp_batch_id`, `product_code`, and independently by either axis using `run_status` and `quality_status`; the per-machine run listing accepts the same two axis filters. Grade changes may be filtered by `machine_id`, `status`, and by either linked run through `from_run_id` and `to_run_id`. Roll sets may be filtered by `production_run_id`, `allocated_order_id` and `status`. Customer orders may be filtered by `customer_id`, `scheduled_run_id`, `product_code` and `status`. Effluent readings of a permit may be filtered by `voided`. Solids-ledger rows may be filtered by `source_ref_id`, `manifest_id`, `reversal_of_id`, `source_kind` and `disposition`. Steam allocations may be filtered by `consumer_ref_id`, `consumer_kind` and `status`. Every one of these listings also accepts `limit` and `offset` under the rules of §2.3.

Two aggregate read surfaces close the loop. The mill-wide fiber balance returns `total_received_tons`, `total_consumed_tons`, `total_available_tons` and `imbalance_tons`, and the mill-wide solids summary returns `gross_solids_tons`, `reversed_solids_tons`, `net_solids_tons`, `manifested_dry_solids_tons`, `unmanifested_dry_solids_tons` and `repulped_dry_solids_tons`, both exactly as specified in §2.9. Neither takes any filter; both are recomputed on read, the fiber balance from every inventory lot and the solids summary from every ledger row including reversals.
