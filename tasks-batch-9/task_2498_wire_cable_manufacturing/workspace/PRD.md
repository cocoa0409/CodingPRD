# Wire & Cable Manufacturing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A wire and cable mill lives between two markets it does not control. On the input side it buys copper and aluminum rod whose price moves daily; on the output side it sells building wire, portable cord and medium-voltage cable against purchase orders that were negotiated weeks earlier. The gap between those two facts is closed by metal price pass-through, and getting pass-through wrong in either direction destroys margin quietly. This service is the system of record for that mechanism: it captures published settlement prices, receives rod against heat numbers, quotes orders with an explicit metal component, and then invoices each shipment either at the price locked when the order was booked or at the mean of a short float window ending on the ship date.

The same records carry the plant's compliance obligations. Insulation and jacket compounds are formulated and qualified against temperature, flame and voltage performance; finished constructions are listed with an agency that returns for follow-up factory inspections; building wire must carry an exact surface print naming the agency file, conductor size, application rating, temperature and voltage; and every reel of medium-voltage cable must clear spark, high-potential and partial-discharge testing before a quality engineer may release it. Because a compound suspension or a listing suspension invalidates everything built beneath it, the service propagates those events downward — through SKUs, through open orders, and into production lots still on the floor — rather than leaving reconciliation to a human.

Finally, the mill's working capital sits in two places that are easy to lose track of: reels out with customers under deposit, and receivables on invoiced shipments. Both feed a single credit exposure figure per account, and both are settled through the same ledgers, so the service treats reel returns and invoicing as fiscal events with the same seriousness as an order. Inventory positioning closes the loop by re-targeting finished-goods stock from construction-market demand and from how violently the relevant metal has been moving.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every endpoint in this service is mounted at the root of the API host; collections such as `/production-lots`, `/reels` and `/shipments` are all top-level paths, and only append-only children — factory inspections, cable tests and reel returns — are addressed beneath their parent.

### 2.1. Plant Personnel and Role-Based Access Control

Authentication is carried by two request headers, and both must be present and non-empty on every request. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting individual or account; `X-User-Role` names the role that individual is acting in. A request missing either header, or carrying an empty value for either, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A request whose `X-User-Role` is not among the roles listed for the endpoint being called is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Eight roles exist, spelled exactly as follows. A **`PlantAdmin`** is the plant systems administrator; the role holds every operation in the service for setup and recovery purposes, and is the only role permitted to move the logical clock. A **`ProcurementBuyer`** buys rod and publishes the daily settlement prices that drive pass-through pricing. A **`ProcessEngineer`** owns compound formulation, SKU definition, extrusion scheduling, the drawing, stranding and extrusion steps, and inventory positioning. A **`QualityEngineer`** records spark, high-potential and partial-discharge tests, dispositions nonconforming lots for rework or scrap, and releases conforming lots. A **`CertificationOfficer`** maintains listing agency files and records the agency's follow-up factory inspections, variation notices and reinstatements. A **`ShippingClerk`** manages the reel fleet, loads packaged lots, stages shipments and receives returned reels, and never prices or invoices anything. A **`BillingAnalyst`** opens accounts, books and prices orders, invoices shipments and settles reel deposits against credit, and never touches production or reels. A **`Customer`** is a distributor or contractor account with read-only reach into its own records.

Seven of these roles are plant-side operator roles: when `X-User-Role` is `PlantAdmin`, `ProcurementBuyer`, `ProcessEngineer`, `QualityEngineer`, `CertificationOfficer`, `ShippingClerk` or `BillingAnalyst`, the `X-User-ID` value is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to any record's `id`. The server does record such identifiers where a workflow demands an audit trail — `operator_user_id` on a cable test and `released_by_user_id` on a production lot are both copied verbatim from `X-User-ID` — but those stored values are audit strings, not foreign keys.

The eighth role is entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer` record; ownership checks compare the resource's account reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Customer` may read only the account whose `id` equals `X-User-ID`, only orders whose `customer_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and only shipments whose order belongs to that account. Reaching an account, order, shipment or reel that belongs to a different account is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, and this ownership check is applied with the same 403 status as the plain role check.

Segregation of duties is enforced within the `QualityEngineer` role rather than between roles. The individual releasing a production lot must not be one of the individuals who tested it; the mechanics of that check appear in the lot-release narrative.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range from 1 to 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`. `total_count` reports the number of records matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true whenever records remain beyond the current window.

Unless a collection states otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Two collections deviate deliberately. The settlement-price collection is ordered by `effective_date` descending — newest price first — because it exists to be read as a price series. The factory-inspection collection under a listing file is ordered by `inspection_date` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, so a file's inspection history reads chronologically.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Audit Fields and the Logical Plant Clock

Every resource carries an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers are bare UUIDs with no type prefix and no embedded semantics; clients must never parse them. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recorded when the record is first written and never changed afterwards.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` and `version`. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits refreshed on every mutation of the record, and `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the record is created and is incremented by exactly 1 on every mutation, including mutations the record receives as a side effect of an operation aimed at some other resource. Three record types are append-only history and therefore carry neither field: a settlement price quote, a cable test row and a reel return row each expose only `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version`. Those records are never modified after creation.

All dates, all datetimes and every window rule in this specification read a **logical UTC clock** rather than real wall-clock time. Before any client moves it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server never falls back to the host's real time. A `PlantAdmin` may set the clock to any instant, forward or backward, by supplying `current_utc`; every subsequent `created_at`, every default date, and the instants stored in `tested_at`, `shipped_at`, `returned_at` and `invoice_date` derive from the clock as it stands when the request is handled. Note that `order_date`, `ship_date`, `inspection_date`, `received_date`, `effective_date` and `returned_date` are all supplied by the caller and may legitimately be earlier than the logical clock's date; only server-stamped instants are forced to follow the clock.

### 2.5. Numeric Precision, Rounding and Unit Conventions

Every monetary amount in this service is carried to **2 decimal places and rounded half up**. Two families of intermediate figures are carried more finely: per-thousand-foot unit prices and per-pound metal prices are carried to **4 decimal places, rounded half up**, and the metal volatility index is likewise carried to 4 decimal places rounded half up. Inventory targets expressed in thousands of feet are carried to **1 decimal place, rounded half up**. Rod mass buckets in pounds and the concentricity percentage are carried to **2 decimal places, rounded half up**.

Where an intermediate value feeds a later step, the specification states explicitly whether the intermediate is rounded first. Two of those pinned orderings matter enough to repeat here: a volatility damper is computed from the already-rounded 4-decimal volatility index but is not itself rounded before it multiplies a base target, and a cut-to-length extension rounds each individual segment amount to cents *before* the segments are summed rather than summing exact segment amounts and rounding once.

Lengths mix units by design, because the plant does. Order quantities, inventory targets and shipped totals against an order are stated in thousands of feet; planned production length, packaged length, reel capacity, reel load length and individual cut segments are stated in plain feet. Wherever the two meet, feet are divided by 1000 to reach thousands of feet. Wall thickness is in mils, rod and finished diameters are in millimetres, temperature ratings are in degrees Celsius, voltages are in volts except for applied test voltage which is in kilovolts, and partial discharge is in picocoulombs.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Rod Lots and Orders

Two write operations mutate a parent record whose own state does not by itself reveal that the client's view is out of date, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control.

Scheduling a production lot requires the caller to send `expected_rod_lot_version`, carrying the `version` of the `RodPurchaseLot` the client last read. This field is mandatory, not optional. If the supplied value does not equal that rod lot's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ROD_LOT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is created. The guard cannot be replaced by a state precondition: allocating rod to a second lot is perfectly legitimate on its own, since the rod lot remains `RECEIVED` and may still hold ample uncommitted mass, so the only thing distinguishing a safe allocation from one computed against a superseded mass ledger is the version the client carries.

Staging a shipment requires the caller to send `expected_order_version`, carrying the `version` of the `CustomerOrder` the client last read. This field is likewise mandatory. If it does not equal the order's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no shipment is created. Partial shipments against one order are normal business, so without this guard a second staging request would succeed against a stale view of how much of the order has already shipped and of whether its pricing has since been placed on hold.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Operations not named above must not be sent an expected-version input and must not reject on version grounds.

### 2.7. Compliance Cascades and Deterministic Cascade Ordering

Compliance events in this domain are never local. Suspending a compound invalidates every SKU printed against it; suspending a listing file invalidates every SKU built under it; and both outcomes reach forward into commercial commitments and backward into metal already on the floor. The service therefore models these as **multi-hop cascades** executed inside the single request that triggered them, with immediate consistency: by the time the response is written, every affected record has already been moved and has already had its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed.

Cascades are deterministic. Wherever a cascade hop touches a set of sibling records, those records are processed in ascending `created_at` order, and each hop is evaluated only against records the previous hop actually moved. A hop never revisits a record it has already moved within the same request, and a record that fails a hop's own precondition is simply skipped rather than causing the request to fail.

Reinstatement is the mirror image of suspension, and it is deliberately not a perfect inverse. When a listing file returns to service, each SKU it suspended is re-evaluated against the compound versions that SKU pinned at its own release; a SKU whose pins still match the current compound versions returns to service, while a SKU whose pinned version has been left behind by a re-qualification during the suspension window is retired permanently instead. Orders and lots then follow only the SKUs that actually came back. The narrative for each of these hops appears with the workflow that triggers it.

### 2.8. Conservation of Metal Mass

Rod is a conserved physical quantity, and the service treats it as one. Every received rod lot records the mass it arrived with, and that arrival mass is thereafter split across exactly three buckets: mass still uncommitted, mass committed to production lots that have neither been scrapped nor cancelled, and mass written off as scrap. After every one of the three mutations that touch these buckets — scheduling a lot, scrapping a lot, and cancelling an order that had queued lots — the three buckets must still sum exactly to the arrival mass. No mutation is permitted to drive the uncommitted bucket below zero.

The distinction between the two ways mass leaves the committed bucket is fiscal, not cosmetic. Mass on a lot that was actually drawn and then scrapped is written off to the scrap bucket and counts against the rod lot's scrap percentage. Mass on a lot that was cancelled while still queued was never drawn at all, so it returns to the uncommitted bucket and is never recorded as scrap. Only the first kind of loss can trigger a quarantine.

### 2.9. The Reel Deposit Ledger and Credit Standing

An account's credit exposure combines two independent obligations: money owed on invoiced shipments, and deposits sitting on reels the account is holding. Both are tracked as running ledger fields on the account rather than recomputed from scratch on read, and both are refreshed inside the same request as the event that changed them.

The deposit ledger obeys a strict invariant: after every reel return, the lifetime total of deposits charged to an account must equal the sum of deposits still outstanding, deposit value credited back to the account, and deposit value forfeited. Refunds and forfeitures therefore always sum to the deposit that was charged, and a return never leaves value unaccounted for.

Credit standing is re-evaluated automatically whenever exposure changes, and the threshold comparison is strictly greater than: an account whose exposure lands exactly on its limit remains in good standing. Because credit standing is derived, an account can be placed on hold by an invoice and taken off hold by a reel return without any operator acting on the account itself.

### 2.10. Sub-Resource Addressing and Reference Resolution

Path parameters are validated strictly left to right. When a request addresses a sub-resource — the inspections under a listing file, the tests under a production lot, the returns under a reel, or the reel returns settled against a shipment — the parent `id` in the path is resolved first, and a missing parent produces the parent's own not-found error before the child segment is considered at all. Sub-resources are never nested more than one level deep.

Once path segments resolve, references carried in the request body are resolved in a fixed order: `rod_lot_id`, then `product_id`, then `order_id`, then `lot_id`, then `reel_id`, then `shipment_id`, then `customer_id`, then `insulation_compound_id`, then `jacket_compound_id`, then `listing_certification_id`. The first unresolvable reference determines which not-found error the caller receives: `ERR_ROD_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPOUND_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND` respectively, each with HTTP 404. A `certification_id` recorded on a factory inspection is taken from the listing file named in the path rather than from the body.

### 2.11. The Rejection Ladder

When a single request would violate more than one rule, the error the caller receives is fixed, not arbitrary. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the first failure ends the request with no persisted effect whatsoever:

1. Header presence — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorization, including a `Customer`'s ownership scope — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. Request shape: required fields present, types correct, enum members recognised, numeric bounds from the field definitions respected, a scheduled line window whose end is strictly later than its start, a non-empty requested cut list on a cut-to-length order, partial-discharge level present on a partial-discharge test, applied test voltage present on a high-potential test, and pagination bounds respected — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Registering a reel whose deposit does not agree with its type is the one shape-level rule that carries its own code, `ERR_REEL_DEPOSIT_MISMATCH`.
4. Existence of every referenced resource, path segments first and then body references in the order given in §2.10 — the matching not-found code.
5. Uniqueness — `ERR_DUPLICATE_METAL_QUOTE` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_PRODUCT_CODE`, both HTTP 409.
6. Optimistic concurrency — `ERR_STALE_ROD_LOT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION`, both HTTP 412.
7. State admissibility of the target resource for the requested transition — `ERR_INVALID_COMPOUND_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CERTIFICATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PRODUCT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_LOT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_REEL_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE` or `ERR_SHIPMENT_ALREADY_INVOICED`, all HTTP 409.
8. Pricing hold on the order a shipment is being staged against — `ERR_ORDER_PRICING_HELD`.
9. Cancellation of an order that already has shipments — `ERR_ORDER_HAS_SHIPMENTS`.
10. Reinstatement of a listing file with a lapsed corrective-action window — `ERR_OPEN_VARIATION_UNRESOLVED`.
11. Surface marking agreement — `ERR_MARKING_MISMATCH`.
12. Dimensional conformance at release — `ERR_DIMENSIONAL_NONCONFORMANCE`.
13. Completeness of the required test set at release — `ERR_REQUIRED_TESTS_INCOMPLETE`.
14. Presence of any failed test on the lot at release — `ERR_LOT_TEST_FAILED`.
15. Segregation of duties at release — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
16. Rework limit — `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.
17. Extrusion line occupancy — `ERR_LINE_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`.
18. Reel occupancy — `ERR_REEL_OCCUPANCY_OVERLAP`.
19. Inspection cadence — `ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.
20. Compound qualification and rating adequacy at SKU release — `ERR_COMPOUND_NOT_QUALIFIED` then `ERR_COMPOUND_RATING_INSUFFICIENT`.
21. Listing file active-status requirement — `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_ACTIVE`.
22. Reel capacity — `ERR_REEL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
23. Shipment quantity against the order and against the packaged lot — `ERR_SHIPMENT_EXCEEDS_ORDER`.
24. Rod availability — `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ROD_QUANTITY`.
25. Metal price availability — `ERR_NO_METAL_QUOTE` or `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PRICE_HISTORY`.
26. Credit — `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402).

Two consequences of this ladder deserve emphasis because they are easy to implement backwards. First, at lot release the marking check precedes the dimensional check, the dimensional check precedes the test-completeness check, test completeness precedes the failed-test check, the failed-test check precedes segregation of duties, and the listing file's active-status requirement is evaluated last of all — so a lot that is simultaneously mis-marked and under-walled reports `ERR_MARKING_MISMATCH`, and a lot that is under-walled and released by its own tester reports `ERR_DIMENSIONAL_NONCONFORMANCE`. Second, when a production lot is scheduled, the line-occupancy check runs *before* the rod-availability check, so a lot that both collides on the line and outruns the available rod reports `ERR_LINE_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Metal Procurement, the Settlement Price Series and the Rod Mass Ledger

Everything downstream of this subsection is priced from a series of published settlement prices, one per metal per day. A `ProcurementBuyer` records each publication with the `metal` it applies to — either `COPPER` or `ALUMINUM` — the `effective_date` it settled on in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, the `settlement_price_usd_per_lb` in dollars per pound carried to two decimals and strictly greater than zero, and an optional `source` naming the publication it was taken from. `source` accepts `COMEX`, `LME` or `MW_TRANSACTION` and defaults to `COMEX` when omitted. Settlement prices are immutable historical facts: there is no operation that edits or deletes one, which is why a quote row carries only its identifier and creation timestamp and has no version counter. The combination of `metal` and `effective_date` may appear only once; a second attempt at the same pair is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_METAL_QUOTE`. When the series is read back it may be narrowed by `metal`, by `effective_date_from` and by `effective_date_to`, and it is returned newest settlement date first so that a pricing analyst sees the current market at the top of the first page.

Rod arrives against a mill heat. Receiving a rod lot records its `metal`, the `supplier_code` of the rod mill, the as-received `rod_diameter_mm` in millimetres greater than zero, the mill `heat_number`, the as-received `purchase_weight_lbs` in pounds greater than zero, the `purchase_price_usd_per_lb` actually paid, the `pricing_basis` the purchase was struck on — either `SPOT` or `MONTH_AVG` — and the `received_date`. Receipt initialises the three mass buckets described in §2.8: `remaining_lbs` is set to `purchase_weight_lbs`, while `committed_lbs` and `scrapped_lbs` both start at 0.00, and `status` starts at `RECEIVED`. Rod lots may be listed and narrowed by `metal` and by `status`.

The three buckets move in lockstep, always by the same figure — the `consumed_rod_lbs` recorded on the production lot involved. Scheduling a lot subtracts that figure from `remaining_lbs` and adds it to `committed_lbs`. Scrapping a lot subtracts it from `committed_lbs` and adds it to `scrapped_lbs`. Cancelling an order returns it from `committed_lbs` to `remaining_lbs` for each of the order's still-queued lots. All three mass fields are carried to two decimals rounded half up, and each of these mutations increments the rod lot's own `version` by one.

A rod lot has three lifecycle values: `RECEIVED`, and the two terminal values `CONSUMED` and `QUARANTINED`. The move from `RECEIVED` to `CONSUMED` is automatic and is evaluated immediately after the mass ledger is updated within the same allocation request: when `remaining_lbs` lands at exactly zero, `status` becomes `CONSUMED` and `version` is incremented by one.

The move from `RECEIVED` to `QUARANTINED` is the mill's scrap alarm, and it is triggered by the mass mutation rather than by the request that caused it. After scrap has been posted, the lot's scrap percentage is computed as one hundred multiplied by `scrapped_lbs`, divided by `purchase_weight_lbs`, rounded half up to two decimals. If that percentage is strictly greater than 10.00 — that is, once more than a tenth of the heat has been written off — `status` becomes `QUARANTINED` and `version` is incremented by one. That transition then triggers a further hop: every *other* production lot drawn from the same rod lot whose quality dimension currently reads `PENDING`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, has its quality dimension set to `QUARANTINED` while its process dimension is left exactly as it was, each such lot's `version` incremented by one. A heat that has started producing scrap is treated as suspect metal, and everything still open against it is frozen pending investigation.

### 3.2. Compound Formulation, Qualification and Suspension

A `ProcessEngineer` registers each insulation or jacket formulation with its internal `compound_code` and the `layer_role` it is formulated for, either `INSULATION` or `JACKET`. Performance is declared up front: `temp_rating_c` is the continuous temperature rating in degrees Celsius and must be exactly 60, 75, 90 or 105; `flame_test` names the highest flame test the formulation is qualified against and must be `NONE`, `VW_1`, `FT4` or `CT`; `max_voltage_v` is the voltage ceiling the formulation is qualified for and must be exactly 600, 5000, 15000 or 35000. Two optional characteristics round out the formulation: `wet_rated` states whether the compound is qualified for wet locations and defaults to false, and `filler_pct` gives the mineral filler loading as a percentage, defaulting to 0 and constrained to at least 0 and at most 40. A newly registered formulation begins at `DRAFT` with `qualification_date` and `suspension_reason` both null.

Qualification is what makes a formulation usable. A `ProcessEngineer` may qualify a formulation that currently sits at either `DRAFT` or `SUSPENDED`; the operation stamps `qualification_date` with the current logical UTC date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, clears `suspension_reason` back to null, sets `status` to `QUALIFIED` and increments `version` by one. Qualifying a formulation that is already `QUALIFIED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_COMPOUND_STATE`. The cycle `DRAFT` → `QUALIFIED` → `SUSPENDED` → `QUALIFIED` is therefore legal and expected — and it has a consequence that reaches a long way, because a re-qualification bumps `version`, and any SKU that pinned the pre-suspension version is now pinned to a formulation that no longer exists.

Suspension runs the other way and may be initiated by either a `ProcessEngineer` or a `QualityEngineer`. The formulation must be `QUALIFIED`; suspending anything else is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_COMPOUND_STATE`. The request carries a mandatory `suspension_reason`, which is stored verbatim, and the formulation moves `QUALIFIED` → `SUSPENDED` with `version` incremented by one.

That single move then propagates through three hops, all inside the same request. In the first hop, every SKU that names this formulation either as its `insulation_compound_id` or as its `jacket_compound_id` and whose own status is `RELEASED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, moves `RELEASED` → `SUSPENDED` with its `version` incremented by one. In the second hop, caused by each of those SKU suspensions, every order placed against that SKU whose fulfillment dimension is `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_SHIPPED` *and* whose pricing dimension is `QUOTED` or `LOCKED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, has its pricing dimension set to `HELD` and its `hold_reason` set to the exact string `COMPOUND_SUSPENDED`, with `version` incremented by one. In the third hop, caused by each of those order holds, every production lot belonging to that order whose quality dimension is `PENDING` and whose process dimension is neither `SHIPPED` nor `SCRAP_CLOSED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, has its quality dimension set to `QUARANTINED` with `version` incremented by one. Orders and lots that do not satisfy their hop's conditions are simply left alone; no hop fails the request.

### 3.3. Listing Agency Files, Follow-Up Inspections and Reinstatement

A `CertificationOfficer` opens a listing file for each product family the mill lists. The file names the `agency` that owns it — `UL`, `CSA` or `ETL` — the agency `file_number`, which is printed verbatim on every reel of wire built under it, the `product_family_code` the agency file covers, the `initial_listing_date` on which the family was first listed, and an optional `inspection_interval_days` giving the follow-up factory inspection cadence in days. That interval defaults to 90 and may be no smaller than 30. A new file begins at `PENDING` with `open_variation_count` at 0 and both `last_inspection_date` and `next_inspection_due_date` null, because no inspector has visited yet.

Each follow-up visit is recorded as an inspection row against the file, carrying the `certification_id` of the file it was recorded under, the `inspection_date` on which the visit took place, the agency's `inspector_code`, the `findings_count` written up (which may be zero but never negative), and the inspector's `outcome`, which is one of `PASS`, `VARIATION` or `SUSPEND`. Recorded visits may be listed per file and narrowed by `outcome`.

Agencies do not tolerate inspection stuffing, so a cadence guard applies whenever the file already has a recorded visit: the new `inspection_date` must be at least 60 days later than the file's `last_inspection_date`. A visit dated strictly fewer than 60 days after the previous one is rejected with `ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`; a visit falling exactly 60 days later is accepted. For this purpose `last_inspection_date` always means the `inspection_date` of the file's latest recorded visit — the row with the greatest `inspection_date`, and among ties the one with the most recent `created_at`.

An accepted visit refreshes the file's cadence fields and stamps the row. `last_inspection_date` becomes the new `inspection_date`. `next_inspection_due_date` becomes that `inspection_date` advanced by `inspection_interval_days` whole calendar days. The row's `corrective_action_due_date` is set to its `inspection_date` advanced by 30 calendar days whenever `outcome` is anything other than `PASS`, and is left null on a `PASS`. Correspondingly, the row is born `CLOSED` on a `PASS` and born `OPEN` on a `VARIATION` or a `SUSPEND`. Finally `open_variation_count` is recomputed as the number of rows on the file whose own status is `OPEN`.

The file's own status then follows the outcome. A `PASS` recorded on a `PENDING` file is the initial listing audit: it additionally requires that `inspection_date` be no earlier than `initial_listing_date`, and it moves the file `PENDING` → `ACTIVE` with `version` incremented by one. A `VARIATION` recorded on an `ACTIVE` file moves it `ACTIVE` → `VARIATION_NOTICE`; products stay `RELEASED` under a variation notice, because only a suspension freezes the downstream chain. A `VARIATION` recorded on a file that is already at `VARIATION_NOTICE` while `open_variation_count` is at least 1 escalates the file `VARIATION_NOTICE` → `SUSPENDED`, as does a `SUSPEND` outcome recorded on a file at `VARIATION_NOTICE`. A `SUSPEND` recorded on an `ACTIVE` file moves it directly `ACTIVE` → `SUSPENDED`. A `PASS` recorded on a file that is already `ACTIVE` or at `VARIATION_NOTICE` refreshes the cadence fields and the row but leaves the file's status where it was. A `VARIATION` or `SUSPEND` outcome on a `PENDING` file, and any inspection at all recorded against a file that is already `SUSPENDED`, are rejected with `ERR_INVALID_CERTIFICATION_STATE`.

Reaching `SUSPENDED` runs the same three-hop freeze as a compound suspension, differing only in how the first hop selects its targets and in the reason it records. Hop one takes every SKU whose `listing_certification_id` is this file and whose status is `RELEASED`, in ascending `created_at` order, and moves it `RELEASED` → `SUSPENDED`. Hop two, caused by each of those SKU suspensions, takes every order on that SKU whose fulfillment dimension is `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_SHIPPED` and whose pricing dimension is `QUOTED` or `LOCKED`, in ascending `created_at` order, and sets its pricing dimension to `HELD` with `hold_reason` set to the exact string `CERTIFICATION_SUSPENDED`. Hop three, caused by each of those order holds, takes every production lot on that order whose quality dimension is `PENDING` and whose process dimension is neither `SHIPPED` nor `SCRAP_CLOSED`, in ascending `created_at` order, and sets its quality dimension to `QUARANTINED`. Every record touched by any hop has its `version` incremented by one.

Reinstatement returns a file to service and is available from either `VARIATION_NOTICE` or `SUSPENDED`; attempting it on a file that is already `ACTIVE` or that is still `PENDING` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_CERTIFICATION_STATE`. The agency's condition for reinstatement is that no corrective action has gone past its deadline: if any `OPEN` inspection row on the file carries a `corrective_action_due_date` earlier than the current logical date, the 30-day corrective action window has lapsed and the agency requires a fresh inspection rather than a reinstatement, so the request is rejected with `ERR_OPEN_VARIATION_UNRESOLVED`. When the condition holds, every `OPEN` row on the file, processed in ascending `created_at` order, moves `OPEN` → `CLOSED` with its own `version` incremented by one; `open_variation_count` is reset to 0; and the file's `status` becomes `ACTIVE` with `version` incremented by one.

Reinstatement then thaws what the suspension froze, and this is where the stale-pin rule described in §2.7 bites. In the first hop, every SKU on this file whose status is `SUSPENDED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, is re-evaluated against the compound versions it pinned when it was released: if `pinned_insulation_compound_version` still equals the current `version` of the formulation named by `insulation_compound_id`, and either `jacket_compound_id` is null or `pinned_jacket_compound_version` still equals the current `version` of the formulation named by `jacket_compound_id`, the SKU returns to `RELEASED`; otherwise it is retired to `OBSOLETE`, which is terminal. Either way its `version` is incremented by one. A compound that was re-qualified during the suspension window is exactly the case that produces `OBSOLETE`: the print the SKU was approved against is no longer the qualified formulation. In the second hop, caused by each SKU that returned to `RELEASED`, every order on that SKU whose pricing dimension is `HELD`, in ascending `created_at` order, moves `HELD` → `LOCKED` with `hold_reason` cleared to null and `version` incremented by one; orders whose SKU became `OBSOLETE` stay `HELD`. In the third hop, caused by each order that returned to `LOCKED`, every production lot on that order whose quality dimension is `QUARANTINED`, in ascending `created_at` order, moves `QUARANTINED` → `PENDING` with `version` incremented by one.

### 3.4. SKU Definition, Surface Marking, Release and Inventory Positioning

A catalog SKU is the mill's complete recipe for a construction, and a `ProcessEngineer` defines it. The definition carries a `product_code` that must be unique across the catalog — a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PRODUCT_CODE` — the `awg_size` label printed on the wire, written as the plant writes it, for instance `12` or `4/0`, and the `conductor_metal`, either `COPPER` or `ALUMINUM`, which decides which settlement price series prices the SKU. The `conductor_construction` is one of `SOLID`, `STRANDED_7`, `STRANDED_19` or `COMPRESSED_37`, and a `SOLID` conductor skips the stranding step on the floor entirely. The `application_rating` the construction is sold against is one of `THHN`, `THWN_2`, `XHHW_2`, `USE_2`, `MV_105` or `SOOW`, and the `voltage_class` is one of `LV_600`, `MV_5KV`, `MV_15KV` or `MV_35KV`. The print's claimed `temp_rating_c` must be exactly 60, 75, 90 or 105.

Dimensional tolerance and metal content complete the definition. `insulation_thickness_mils` is the nominal insulation wall in mils and must be greater than zero. `min_wall_pct` expresses the minimum acceptable wall as a percentage of that nominal thickness; it defaults to 90 and must fall between 80 and 100 inclusive. `concentricity_min_pct` is the minimum accepted concentricity percentage; it defaults to 85 and must fall between 50 and 100 inclusive. `conductor_lbs_per_kft` states the metal content in pounds per thousand feet, must be greater than zero, and does double duty as both the pass-through multiplier in pricing and the rod-consumption factor in production. The recipe also names the formulations and the listing file it is built to: `insulation_compound_id` must reference a formulation whose `layer_role` is `INSULATION`, the optional `jacket_compound_id` defaults to null and, when supplied, must reference a formulation whose `layer_role` is `JACKET`, and `listing_certification_id` names the agency file the SKU is listed under. Finally `base_target_kft` is the baseline finished-goods inventory target in thousands of feet and must be greater than zero, while the optional `demand_index` captures construction-market demand for the SKU, defaulting to 1.0 and required to be greater than zero.

Creating the SKU derives four values immediately. `voltage_rating_v` is taken from the voltage class: 600 volts for `LV_600`, 5000 volts for `MV_5KV`, 15000 volts for `MV_15KV` and 35000 volts for `MV_35KV`. `marking_required` is true exactly when the `application_rating` is one of `THHN`, `THWN_2`, `XHHW_2` or `USE_2` — the building-wire ratings — and false for `MV_105` and `SOOW`. `pd_test_required` is true exactly when the `voltage_class` is one of `MV_5KV`, `MV_15KV` or `MV_35KV`, and false for `LV_600`. And `required_marking_text` is composed as the exact surface print string every lot of this SKU must carry: the listing file's `agency`, one space, the file's `file_number`, one space, the `awg_size` label immediately followed by the literal `AWG` with no space between them, one space, the `application_rating`, one space, the `temp_rating_c` immediately followed by the literal `C`, one space, and finally the `voltage_rating_v` immediately followed by the literal `V`. A SKU listed under a UL file numbered `E12345`, sized `12`, rated `THHN`, at 90 degrees and 600 volts therefore prints as `UL E12345 12AWG THHN 90C 600V`. Exactly one space separates each token and there is no trailing space; the string is later compared for exact, case-sensitive equality.

A new SKU also starts with `volatility_index` at 0.0000, `target_inventory_kft` initialised to `base_target_kft`, both `pinned_insulation_compound_version` and `pinned_jacket_compound_version` null, and `status` at `DRAFT`. SKUs may be listed and narrowed by `insulation_compound_id`, by `jacket_compound_id`, by `listing_certification_id`, by `status` and by `application_rating`.

Releasing a SKU is the gate between engineering and commerce, and a `ProcessEngineer` performs it on a SKU that is `DRAFT` or that has been returned to `SUSPENDED`; a SKU that is already `RELEASED`, or that has been retired to `OBSOLETE`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PRODUCT_STATE`. Release then checks the formulations in two stages. First, both formulations must be qualified: the insulation formulation must be `QUALIFIED`, and when a jacket formulation is named it must be `QUALIFIED` too, otherwise release is refused with `ERR_COMPOUND_NOT_QUALIFIED`. Second, the insulation formulation must actually cover the claims on the print: its `temp_rating_c` must be at least the SKU's `temp_rating_c`, its `max_voltage_v` must be at least the SKU's `voltage_rating_v`, and when the SKU's `application_rating` is either `THHN` or `THWN_2` the formulation's `flame_test` must be exactly `VW_1`. Any of those three failing refuses the release with `ERR_COMPOUND_RATING_INSUFFICIENT`. Last of all, the SKU's listing file must be `ACTIVE`; a file still `PENDING`, sitting at `VARIATION_NOTICE`, or `SUSPENDED` refuses the release with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_ACTIVE`.

A successful release pins the formulation versions the SKU was approved against: `pinned_insulation_compound_version` takes the current `version` of the insulation formulation, and `pinned_jacket_compound_version` takes the current `version` of the jacket formulation when one is named and otherwise stays null. `status` becomes `RELEASED` and `version` is incremented by one. Those pins are the mill's stale-reference detector, and §3.3 describes what happens to them when a listing file is reinstated.

Inventory positioning is a `ProcessEngineer` capability that re-aims finished-goods stock at the market. It takes a mandatory `as_of_date` and an optional `product_ids` array; when the array is supplied the run is restricted to those SKUs, and when it is omitted the run covers the whole catalog. Only SKUs whose status is `RELEASED` are evaluated, in ascending `created_at` order. SKUs at `DRAFT`, `SUSPENDED` or `OBSOLETE` are skipped and keep their previous `volatility_index` and `target_inventory_kft` untouched. The run reports back the `as_of_date` it used, an `evaluated_count`, a `skipped_count`, and the `product_ids` it actually evaluated.

For each evaluated SKU the run first refreshes `volatility_index` from the settlement price series. The window is every quote whose `metal` equals the SKU's `conductor_metal` and whose `effective_date` is on or before `as_of_date`, ordered by `effective_date` descending with `created_at` descending as the tie-break, then truncated to the first ten rows. Quotes for the other metal never enter. If the window holds fewer than three quotes there is not enough history to characterise volatility and `volatility_index` is set to 0.0000. Otherwise `volatility_index` is the highest `settlement_price_usd_per_lb` in the window minus the lowest, divided by the arithmetic mean of the prices in the window, rounded half up to four decimal places; the mean itself is not rounded before the division.

The SKU's target is then recomputed from that refreshed index. A demand multiplier is chosen from `demand_index`: 0.30 when `demand_index` is strictly below 0.80, 1.00 when it is at least 0.80 and at most 1.20, and 1.80 when it is strictly above 1.20. A volatility damper is then one minus the smaller of 0.40 and eight tenths of `volatility_index` — so violent metal markets pull the target down, but never by more than forty percent, and the damper is left unrounded. The raw target is `base_target_kft` multiplied by the demand multiplier and then by the damper. That raw figure is clamped between a floor of `base_target_kft` multiplied by 0.25 rounded half up to one decimal and a ceiling of `base_target_kft` multiplied by 1.50 rounded half up to one decimal — the clamp is applied first — and the clamped result is then rounded half up to one decimal place and stored as `target_inventory_kft`.

### 3.5. Accounts, Order Booking and the Metal Pass-Through Quote

A `BillingAnalyst` opens each distributor or contractor account with its `account_code`, the customer's legal `name`, the `market_region` it builds in — one of `NORTHEAST`, `SOUTHEAST`, `MIDWEST` or `WEST` — and a `credit_limit_usd` ceiling that may be zero but never negative. Two commercial elections accompany the account. `pass_through_enabled` states whether metal price pass-through applies to the account at all and defaults to true; an account with pass-through switched off is quoted and invoiced on conversion price alone. `reel_program` records which reel return program the account is enrolled in, accepting `RETURNABLE` or `NON_RETURNABLE` and defaulting to `RETURNABLE`. A new account starts with every ledger field at 0.00 — `open_ar_usd`, `deposit_charged_total_usd`, `reel_deposit_balance_usd`, `credit_memo_balance_usd`, `forfeited_deposit_total_usd` and `credit_exposure_usd` — and with `status` at `ACTIVE`.

Accounts may be adjusted later, and an adjustment may carry any combination of a revised `credit_limit_usd`, a revised `pass_through_enabled` election and a revised `reel_program`. Every adjustment re-evaluates the account's credit standing immediately against the new limit. That evaluation is the same one used everywhere else: `open_ar_usd` is the sum of `invoice_amount_usd` across the account's shipments whose own status is `INVOICED`, with staged-but-uninvoiced shipments contributing nothing; `reel_deposit_balance_usd` carries the deposits still outstanding on reels the account holds, meaning reels currently at `SHIPPED` or `RETURN_PENDING`; and `credit_exposure_usd` is `open_ar_usd` plus `reel_deposit_balance_usd` minus `credit_memo_balance_usd`, rounded half up to two decimals. The account moves to `CREDIT_HOLD` when `credit_exposure_usd` is strictly greater than `credit_limit_usd` and returns to `ACTIVE` when it is at or below the limit, so an exposure landing exactly on the limit keeps the account in good standing.

Booking an order is where the metal market first meets a customer. The order names the `customer_id` placing it and the `product_id` being bought, records the customer's own `order_number`, and states `ordered_kft` — the ordered length in thousands of feet, strictly greater than zero. The SKU must be `RELEASED`; ordering against a SKU at `DRAFT`, `SUSPENDED` or `OBSOLETE` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PRODUCT_STATE`. `price_basis` selects how the metal content will eventually be invoiced and is either `ORDER_DATE_LOCK` or `SHIP_DATE_FLOAT`. `conversion_price_usd_per_kft` is the negotiated non-metal conversion price per thousand feet and must be greater than zero. `order_date` is the effective pricing date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form and selects which settlement price the quote reads; it may legitimately be earlier than the logical clock's date. The optional `cut_to_length_service` flag defaults to false, and `requested_cut_lengths_ft` is an ordered list of requested cut segment lengths in feet defaulting to an empty list; when `cut_to_length_service` is true the list is mandatory and must be non-empty, and an empty or absent list in that case is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

The quote is then computed in a fixed five-step order, and the order it is computed in matters. **Step one selects the metal price.** The candidate quotes are those whose `metal` equals the SKU's `conductor_metal` and whose `effective_date` is on or before `order_date`; no status filter applies, because settlement prices are immutable. Candidates are ranked by `effective_date` descending and then by `created_at` descending, and the first candidate's `settlement_price_usd_per_lb` becomes the order's `quoted_metal_price_usd_per_lb`. If there is no candidate at all the order is refused outright with `ERR_NO_METAL_QUOTE` and nothing is created. This price is recorded for `SHIP_DATE_FLOAT` orders as well as `ORDER_DATE_LOCK` ones, so that the quote remains comparable against whatever the float window later produces.

**Step two builds the unit price.** The pass-through component is zero whenever the account's `pass_through_enabled` election is false. Otherwise it is the SKU's `conductor_lbs_per_kft` multiplied by the difference between `quoted_metal_price_usd_per_lb` and a fixed base metal price — three dollars per pound for a `COPPER` conductor and one dollar per pound for an `ALUMINUM` conductor. Note that the difference may be negative when the market sits below the base, in which case pass-through reduces the price. `quoted_unit_price_usd_per_kft` is then `conversion_price_usd_per_kft` plus that pass-through component, rounded half up to four decimal places.

**Step three extends the unit price over the quantity**, and cut-to-length orders extend per requested segment rather than in bulk. For a cut-to-length order, `quoted_subtotal_usd` is the sum, taken over `requested_cut_lengths_ft` in list order, of each segment's own amount — the segment's length in feet divided by 1000, multiplied by `quoted_unit_price_usd_per_kft`, and rounded half up to two decimals *before* it enters the sum. Summing exact segment amounts and rounding once at the end yields a different number and is wrong. `quoted_cut_fee_usd` for such an order is a flat twelve dollars fifty per cut multiplied by the number of entries in `requested_cut_lengths_ft`, rounded half up to two decimals. For an order that is not cut to length, `quoted_subtotal_usd` is simply `quoted_unit_price_usd_per_kft` multiplied by `ordered_kft`, rounded half up to two decimals, and `quoted_cut_fee_usd` is 0.00.

**Step four applies the metal volatility surcharge**, and its tier is keyed on `quoted_subtotal_usd` alone — before the cut fee and before the surcharge itself are added. Reading the tier off the final total would be circular, so this evaluation order is fixed. A subtotal below five thousand dollars carries a surcharge of two percent; a subtotal of five thousand dollars or more but below twenty-five thousand dollars carries one and a half percent; a subtotal of twenty-five thousand dollars or more carries one percent. `quoted_surcharge_usd` is the subtotal multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to two decimals, and the tier is chosen from the already-rounded subtotal. **Step five totals the quote:** `quoted_total_usd` is `quoted_subtotal_usd` plus `quoted_cut_fee_usd` plus `quoted_surcharge_usd`, rounded half up to two decimals.

A newly booked order sits at `QUOTED` on its pricing dimension and `OPEN` on its fulfillment dimension, with `shipped_kft` at 0.000, `invoiced_total_usd` at 0.00 and `hold_reason` null. The two dimensions are genuinely independent: `pricing_status` moves among `QUOTED`, `LOCKED`, `HELD` and `INVOICED`, while `fulfillment_status` moves among `OPEN`, `PARTIALLY_SHIPPED`, `FULFILLED` and the terminal `CANCELLED`, and neither dimension constrains the other except where a rule says so explicitly. `shipped_kft` accumulates each shipment's shipped length converted from feet to thousands of feet, and `invoiced_total_usd` accumulates `invoice_amount_usd` across the order's shipments that have reached `INVOICED`. Orders may be listed and narrowed by `customer_id`, by `product_id`, by `pricing_status` and by `fulfillment_status`.

Confirming the price basis is a `BillingAnalyst` action. From `QUOTED` it moves the pricing dimension `QUOTED` → `LOCKED`, and it additionally requires the fulfillment dimension to be `OPEN` or `PARTIALLY_SHIPPED`. The same action also lifts an order sitting at `HELD` back to `LOCKED` once the blocking formulation or listing problem has been resolved, clearing `hold_reason` back to null as it does so. An order whose pricing dimension is already `LOCKED`, or that has reached `INVOICED`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. Either way `version` is incremented by one.

Cancellation is also a `BillingAnalyst` action and is deliberately narrow. The fulfillment dimension must be `OPEN`; cancelling an order that is already `CANCELLED` or that has reached `FULFILLED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. Beyond that, no shipment may reference the order at all — the moment anything has shipped, the commercial commitment is real and cancellation is refused with `ERR_ORDER_HAS_SHIPMENTS`. An optional free-text `reason` may accompany the request; it is accepted and is not persisted on the order. A successful cancellation sets the fulfillment dimension to `CANCELLED` and increments `version` by one, and deliberately leaves the pricing dimension exactly where it was.

Cancellation then runs the compensating half of the metal mass ledger. Every production lot belonging to the cancelled order whose process dimension is `QUEUED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, has its process dimension set to `SCRAP_CLOSED` while its quality dimension is left at `PENDING` — nothing was ever produced, so nothing is scrapped — and its `version` incremented by one. For each such lot, its `consumed_rod_lbs` moves out of the rod lot's `committed_lbs` and back into that rod lot's `remaining_lbs`, never into `scrapped_lbs`, because a queued lot has not been drawn; the rod lot's `version` is incremented by one for each movement. Throughout, the arrival mass still equals the sum of the three buckets, and `remaining_lbs` returns to exactly the value it held before the lot was scheduled.

### 3.6. Scheduling and Running a Production Lot

A `ProcessEngineer` schedules each production lot against a SKU and a heat of rod. The lot names the `product_id` being manufactured and the `rod_lot_id` the conductor will be drawn from, carries an optional `order_id` that is null for stock production, and records the plant's own `lot_code`. `planned_length_ft` is the planned finished length in feet and must be greater than zero. `line_code` names the extrusion line the lot will occupy and is the resource key for occupancy conflicts, while `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at` bound that occupancy as ISO 8601 UTC instants; `scheduled_end_at` must be strictly after `scheduled_start_at`, and a window that is not is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The request must also carry `expected_rod_lot_version` as described in §2.6.

The mass the lot will draw is computed first, because two later checks depend on it: `consumed_rod_lbs` is `planned_length_ft` divided by 1000, multiplied by the SKU's `conductor_lbs_per_kft`, rounded half up to two decimals.

Extrusion lines cannot be shared, and changeovers take time. The requested window must clear every *other* production lot on the same `line_code` whose process dimension is neither `SHIPPED` nor `SCRAP_CLOSED` by at least a thirty-minute changeover buffer at both ends. Concretely, the request is refused when the new lot's start is earlier than the other lot's end advanced by thirty minutes *and* the other lot's start is earlier than the new lot's end advanced by thirty minutes; when either of those two conditions fails, the windows are far enough apart and the schedule is accepted. A collision is reported as `ERR_LINE_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. Lots already at `SHIPPED` or `SCRAP_CLOSED` are off the line and are ignored by this check entirely.

Only after the line clears is the rod checked. The rod lot's `remaining_lbs` must be at least the `consumed_rod_lbs` the new lot would draw; anything less is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ROD_QUANTITY`, since the uncommitted bucket may never go negative.

A successful scheduling request moves `consumed_rod_lbs` out of the rod lot's `remaining_lbs` and into its `committed_lbs`, increments that rod lot's `version` by one, and — if the movement leaves `remaining_lbs` at exactly zero — carries the rod lot on to `CONSUMED` as described in §3.1. The lot's `traceability_code` is composed as the rod lot's `heat_number`, then a single hyphen, then the lot's own `lot_code`, so that a reel of finished wire can always be walked back to the heat it was drawn from. The new lot starts with its process dimension at `QUEUED`, its quality dimension at `PENDING`, `rework_count` at 0, `packaged_length_ft` at 0, and `measured_min_wall_mils`, `measured_max_wall_mils`, `measured_avg_od_mm`, `concentricity_pct` and `surface_marking_text` all null. Lots may be listed and narrowed by `product_id`, by `rod_lot_id`, by `order_id`, by `line_code`, by `process_state` and by `quality_state`.

The floor then reports completed operations one at a time. Each report names the `step` that finished, one of `DRAWING`, `STRANDING` or `EXTRUSION`, and a step requested from a process or quality state that does not permit it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_LOT_STATE`.

Drawing advances the lot `QUEUED` → `DRAWN` on the process dimension. It requires the quality dimension to read either `PENDING` or `REWORK` — so a lot that has just been rewound for rework may be drawn again — and it requires the lot's SKU to be `RELEASED` at that moment, which is where a suspended SKU stops work that had not yet started. Stranding advances the lot `DRAWN` → `STRANDED` and is only available when the SKU's `conductor_construction` is anything other than `SOLID`. This is the branch point of the process dimension: a `SOLID` construction goes `DRAWN` → `EXTRUDED` directly and never enters `STRANDED`, and extrusion from `DRAWN` is admissible only for a `SOLID` construction, while extrusion from `STRANDED` is admissible for all the stranded and compressed constructions.

Extrusion is the measurement step, and it carries four optional payload values that are in practice mandatory for it: `measured_min_wall_mils` and `measured_max_wall_mils` are the minimum and maximum insulation wall recorded across the run, `measured_avg_od_mm` is the average finished outside diameter, and `surface_marking_text` is the surface print that was actually applied. When the SKU's `marking_required` is true, the supplied `surface_marking_text` must equal the SKU's `required_marking_text` exactly, character for character and case for case; anything else is refused with `ERR_MARKING_MISMATCH`. Traceability marking on building wire is not negotiable, so this check runs before the measurements are even considered.

Extrusion stores all four values and derives `concentricity_pct` as one hundred multiplied by `measured_min_wall_mils`, divided by `measured_max_wall_mils`, rounded half up to two decimals. The process dimension moves to `EXTRUDED`. The in-line quality gate then applies concentricity **only**: when `concentricity_pct` is strictly less than the SKU's `concentricity_min_pct` the quality dimension moves `PENDING` → `NONCONFORMING`, and otherwise the quality dimension is left at `PENDING`. A lot sitting exactly on the concentricity limit conforms. The minimum-wall gate is deliberately *not* applied here — it is re-checked independently at release — which means a lot can be perfectly concentric yet uniformly under-walled, pass extrusion, pass testing, and only be stopped at the very end. Either way `version` is incremented by one.

### 3.7. Electrical Testing, Disposition and Lot Release

Electrical testing is a `QualityEngineer` responsibility, and every test row is recorded against a lot whose process dimension is `EXTRUDED`; recording a test on a lot in any other process state is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_LOT_STATE`. Each row identifies the `lot_id` under test, names the `test_type` performed — `SPARK`, `HIPOT` or `PARTIAL_DISCHARGE` — and records `duration_sec`, the test duration in whole seconds, which must be greater than zero. The server stamps two fields itself: `operator_user_id` is copied from `X-User-ID`, and `tested_at` is the logical-clock instant at which the row was recorded. Test rows are append-only history and therefore carry no `updated_at` and no `version`; they may be listed per lot and narrowed by `test_type` and by `result`.

Three measurement fields are supplied according to the test being run. `faults_detected` is the spark-test fault count, defaulting to 0 and never negative. `test_voltage_kv` is the applied voltage in kilovolts; it defaults to null, must be greater than zero when supplied, and is mandatory when `test_type` is `HIPOT` — a high-potential row without it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `breakdown_detected` states whether the high-potential test broke down and defaults to false. `pd_level_pc` is the partial discharge level in picocoulombs, defaults to null, and is mandatory when `test_type` is `PARTIAL_DISCHARGE`, its absence likewise producing `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

The verdict stored in `result` is derived, never supplied. A `SPARK` row passes when `faults_detected` is at most the permitted maximum of zero — that is, only a completely fault-free pass qualifies — and fails otherwise. A `HIPOT` row fails immediately when `breakdown_detected` is true; otherwise it passes when `test_voltage_kv` is at least the voltage the SKU's `voltage_class` demands, which is 2.5 kilovolts for `LV_600`, 15.0 kilovolts for `MV_5KV`, 35.0 kilovolts for `MV_15KV` and 70.0 kilovolts for `MV_35KV`, and fails when the applied voltage falls short. A `PARTIAL_DISCHARGE` row passes when `pd_level_pc` is at most the limit for the SKU's voltage class — 5.0 picocoulombs for both `MV_5KV` and `MV_15KV`, and a tighter 3.0 picocoulombs for `MV_35KV` — comparing at one decimal place, with a reading exactly on the limit passing.

Two consequences follow the moment the row is written. If `result` is `FAIL` and the lot's quality dimension currently reads `PENDING`, the quality dimension moves `PENDING` → `NONCONFORMING` and `version` is incremented by one. If `result` is `PASS` and the lot's latest `SPARK` row and latest `HIPOT` row both read `PASS`, the process dimension moves `EXTRUDED` → `TESTED` with `version` incremented by one, while the quality dimension is untouched and stays at `PENDING` until a quality engineer releases the lot. "Latest" throughout means the row with the greatest `tested_at`, and among ties the row with the most recent `created_at`.

Only spark and high-potential testing gate that promotion, and the omission is intentional. Spark and hipot are one-hundred-percent in-line line tests, whereas partial discharge is an off-line laboratory test on medium-voltage cable whose result routinely lags the line. A medium-voltage lot therefore reaches `TESTED` on the in-line pair alone, with its partial-discharge result still outstanding, and it is the release gate rather than the promotion gate that refuses to let it go further. A re-test likewise supersedes its predecessor for the promotion decision only: the superseded row is never deleted, and a lot carrying any failed row can be re-tested back to `TESTED` yet can never be released — it can only be reworked or scrapped. That asymmetry is deliberate.

Dispositioning a nonconforming lot is the `QualityEngineer`'s remedy, and it requires the quality dimension to read `NONCONFORMING`; dispositioning a lot whose quality dimension is anything else is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_LOT_STATE`. The request states the `disposition`, either `REWORK` or `SCRAPPED`. It may also carry an optional `rewind_to` declaring the step the floor intends to rewind to and an optional free-text `reason`; neither is persisted, and the actual rewind target is derived from the SKU's construction rather than from `rewind_to`.

A `REWORK` disposition is available from process state `EXTRUDED` and is refused once `rework_count` has already reached the limit of 2 — at that point the lot must be scrapped — with `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Rework clears the measurement record completely, setting `measured_min_wall_mils`, `measured_max_wall_mils`, `measured_avg_od_mm`, `concentricity_pct` and `surface_marking_text` all back to null, and increments `rework_count` by one. The process dimension rewinds to `STRANDED` for any construction other than `SOLID`, giving the rewind cycle `EXTRUDED` → `STRANDED` → `EXTRUDED`, and rewinds to `DRAWN` for a `SOLID` construction. On the quality dimension the lot passes through `REWORK` and ends the same request at `PENDING`: `REWORK` is observable only as the transient the disposition response reports, and the persisted end state is always `PENDING`. `version` is incremented by one.

A `SCRAPPED` disposition is available from process states `DRAWN`, `STRANDED`, `EXTRUDED` and `TESTED`. It sets the process dimension to `SCRAP_CLOSED` and the quality dimension to `SCRAPPED`, incrementing `version` by one, and then runs three cascade hops. First, the lot's `consumed_rod_lbs` moves out of the rod lot's `committed_lbs` and into that rod lot's `scrapped_lbs`, with the rod lot's `version` incremented by one. Second, caused by that mass mutation rather than by the disposition request itself, the rod lot auto-transitions `RECEIVED` → `QUARANTINED` when its scrap percentage — one hundred multiplied by `scrapped_lbs` divided by `purchase_weight_lbs`, rounded half up to two decimals — is strictly greater than 10.00. Third, caused by that quarantine, every other production lot drawn from the same rod lot whose quality dimension reads `PENDING`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, moves to `QUARANTINED`.

Releasing a lot to packaging is the last quality decision, and it is where the release-time gates that extrusion deferred are finally applied. Release requires the process dimension to read `TESTED` and the quality dimension to read `PENDING`; anything else is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_LOT_STATE`. The request carries `packaged_length_ft`, the length the quality engineer certifies as available to ship, and that figure is stored on the lot rather than derived.

Six gates then apply, in this order. The **marking** gate re-checks that a marking-required SKU's stored `surface_marking_text` still equals its `required_marking_text` exactly, refusing with `ERR_MARKING_MISMATCH` otherwise. The **dimensional** gate requires both that `concentricity_pct` be at least the SKU's `concentricity_min_pct` and that `measured_min_wall_mils` be at least the required minimum wall, which is the SKU's `insulation_thickness_mils` multiplied by its `min_wall_pct` and divided by one hundred, rounded half up to two decimals; either comparison failing produces `ERR_DIMENSIONAL_NONCONFORMANCE`, and both comparisons are inclusive, so a lot exactly at the limit conforms. This is the rung an under-walled but perfectly concentric lot finally hits. The **test-completeness** gate requires the latest row for every release-required test type to exist and to read `PASS`: `SPARK` and `HIPOT` always, plus `PARTIAL_DISCHARGE` whenever the SKU's `pd_test_required` is true. A missing or failing latest row for a required type produces `ERR_REQUIRED_TESTS_INCOMPLETE`, and this is exactly the rung a medium-voltage lot sitting at `TESTED` without a partial-discharge result hits. The **failed-test** gate then scans *every* test row on the lot, not merely the latest per type, and refuses release with `ERR_LOT_TEST_FAILED` if any of them reads `FAIL`. The **segregation-of-duties** gate requires the releasing `X-User-ID` to differ from every `operator_user_id` recorded on the lot's test rows; a quality engineer who ran any of the tests may not sign off the lot, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` at HTTP 403. The **listing** gate finally requires the SKU's listing file to be `ACTIVE`, refusing with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_ACTIVE` when the file is `PENDING`, at `VARIATION_NOTICE`, or `SUSPENDED`.

A lot that clears all six has `released_by_user_id` set from `X-User-ID` and `packaged_length_ft` set from the payload, and its quality dimension moves `PENDING` → `CONFORMING` while its process dimension deliberately stays at `TESTED` — packaging has been authorised, not performed. `version` is incremented by one.

### 3.8. The Reel Fleet, Packaging and Shipment Staging

Reels are capital equipment that leaves the plant and is expected back. A `ShippingClerk` registers each one with its `reel_serial`, its `reel_type` — `WOOD_RETURNABLE`, `STEEL_RETURNABLE` or `NON_RETURNABLE` — the `deposit_usd` that will be charged to the customer when the reel ships, and the `capacity_ft` giving the maximum loadable length in feet, which must be greater than zero. Deposit and type must agree: `deposit_usd` may never be negative, must be exactly 0 for a `NON_RETURNABLE` reel, and must be non-zero for either returnable type. A mismatch either way is rejected with `ERR_REEL_DEPOSIT_MISMATCH`. A registered reel begins at `AVAILABLE` with `loaded_length_ft` at 0, `cycle_count` at 0, and `current_lot_id`, `held_by_customer_id`, `occupancy_start_at`, `occupancy_end_at`, `shipped_at`, `returned_at` and `last_return_condition` all null or zero as applicable.

A reel moves around a loop: `AVAILABLE` → `LOADED` → `SHIPPED` → `RETURN_PENDING` → `AVAILABLE`, with `cycle_count` acting as the loop counter. Two states leave the loop: `DAMAGED`, which a reel enters when it comes back in poor condition, and the terminal `RETIRED`. Reels may be listed and narrowed by `status`, by `reel_type`, by `current_lot_id` and by `held_by_customer_id`.

Loading is a `ShippingClerk` action that takes a released lot and puts it on iron. The request names the `lot_id` to load, the `load_length_ft` in feet being wound on, and the `occupancy_start_at` instant at which the reel is claimed. The reel must be `AVAILABLE`; loading a reel in any other status is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REEL_STATE`. The lot must have its process dimension at `TESTED` and its quality dimension at `CONFORMING` — that is, it must have been released by a quality engineer — and any other combination is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_LOT_STATE`.

Two further checks then run in a fixed order. Reel occupancy is **half-open with no buffer**: when the reel has a `returned_at` instant recorded, the requested `occupancy_start_at` must be at or after that instant, so a load starting exactly at the previous return instant is accepted and a load starting even a moment earlier is refused with `ERR_REEL_OCCUPANCY_OVERLAP`. A reel that has never been returned has no lower bound. Only after occupancy clears is capacity checked: `loaded_length_ft` plus the requested `load_length_ft` must not exceed `capacity_ft`, and an overload is refused with `ERR_REEL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

A successful load sets `current_lot_id` to the loaded lot, increases `loaded_length_ft` by `load_length_ft`, records `occupancy_start_at` verbatim from the request, and clears `occupancy_end_at`, `shipped_at` and `returned_at` back to null so the reel's new occupancy starts clean. The reel's status becomes `LOADED` and its `version` is incremented by one. The loaded lot simultaneously advances its process dimension `TESTED` → `PACKAGED` with its own `version` incremented by one; its `packaged_length_ft` is *not* re-derived here, because that figure was fixed by the quality engineer at release.

Staging a shipment is the moment the mill's obligation becomes the customer's. A `ShippingClerk` names the `order_id` being shipped against, the `lot_id` being shipped, the `reel_id` it ships on, the `shipped_length_ft` in feet, and the effective `ship_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, which anchors the float-pricing window and may be earlier than the logical clock's date. An optional `cuts_count` records how many discrete cut segments were produced for a cut-to-length order; it defaults to 0 and may never be negative. The request must also carry `expected_order_version` as described in §2.6.

The staging checks run in the order the rejection ladder gives. Shipping against an order whose fulfillment dimension is `CANCELLED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`; a reel that is not `LOADED`, or whose `current_lot_id` does not match the `lot_id` in the payload, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REEL_STATE`; and a lot whose process dimension is not `PACKAGED` or whose quality dimension is not `CONFORMING` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_LOT_STATE`. An order whose pricing dimension currently reads `HELD` — because its formulation or its listing file was suspended — cannot be shipped at all, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_ORDER_PRICING_HELD`. Quantity is then checked on two axes at once: the order's `shipped_kft` plus this shipment's `shipped_length_ft` divided by 1000 must not exceed `ordered_kft`, and `shipped_length_ft` must not exceed the lot's `packaged_length_ft`; either overrun is refused with `ERR_SHIPMENT_EXCEEDS_ORDER`. Finally credit is checked: the shipping account's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, and its `credit_exposure_usd` plus the reel's `deposit_usd` must not exceed its `credit_limit_usd`. An account already on `CREDIT_HOLD`, or one that the new deposit would push past its limit, is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` at HTTP 402.

A staged shipment is born at `STAGED` with `deposit_charged_usd` taken from the reel's `deposit_usd`, and with `metal_price_used_usd_per_lb`, `price_variant_applied`, `unit_price_usd_per_kft`, `extended_amount_usd`, `cut_fee_usd`, `volatility_surcharge_usd`, `invoice_amount_usd` and `invoice_date` all still null — nothing is priced until it is invoiced. Shipments may be listed and narrowed by `order_id`, by `lot_id`, by `reel_id` and by `status`.

Staging then moves four other records. The reel advances `LOADED` → `SHIPPED`: `held_by_customer_id` is set to the ordering account's identifier taken from the order's `customer_id`, `shipped_at` is stamped with the logical-clock instant, `occupancy_end_at` is set to that same instant to close the planned occupancy window, and `version` is incremented by one. Note that `cycle_count` is *not* incremented when a reel ships — it counts completed ship-and-return cycles and is incremented only when the reel comes back. The holding account's `deposit_charged_total_usd` and `reel_deposit_balance_usd` are each increased by the reel's `deposit_usd`, after which `credit_exposure_usd` is recomputed and the account's standing re-evaluated. The lot advances its process dimension `PACKAGED` → `SHIPPED` with `version` incremented by one. And the order accumulates the shipment: `shipped_kft` is increased by `shipped_length_ft` divided by 1000, and the fulfillment dimension advances `OPEN` → `PARTIALLY_SHIPPED` when the resulting total is strictly below `ordered_kft`, `OPEN` → `FULFILLED` when the resulting total equals `ordered_kft` exactly, and `PARTIALLY_SHIPPED` → `FULFILLED` when the resulting total equals `ordered_kft` exactly. The order's `version` is incremented by one.

### 3.9. Invoicing, Reel Deposit Settlement and Credit Standing

Invoicing is the `BillingAnalyst`'s act and takes no payload — everything it needs is already on the shipment, its order, that order's SKU and account, and the settlement price series. The shipment must be `STAGED`; invoicing one that has already reached `INVOICED` is rejected with `ERR_SHIPMENT_ALREADY_INVOICED`. The order's pricing dimension must read either `QUOTED` or `LOCKED`.

Which metal price the invoice is struck on depends entirely on the order's `price_basis`, and the two variants produce genuinely different amounts for otherwise identical shipment rows. Under `ORDER_DATE_LOCK`, `metal_price_used_usd_per_lb` is simply the order's `quoted_metal_price_usd_per_lb`, the price pinned when the order was booked; no quote lookup happens at invoice time at all, so later settlements can never move the invoice, and `price_variant_applied` is recorded as `ORDER_DATE_LOCK`. Under `SHIP_DATE_FLOAT`, `metal_price_used_usd_per_lb` is the arithmetic mean of `settlement_price_usd_per_lb` across every quote whose `metal` equals the SKU's `conductor_metal` and whose `effective_date` falls inside an inclusive five-calendar-day window running from four days before the shipment's `ship_date` through `ship_date` itself, rounded half up to four decimal places. If fewer than three quotes fall inside that window there is not enough market to average, and the invoice is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_PRICE_HISTORY` while the shipment stays exactly where it was at `STAGED`. On success `price_variant_applied` is recorded as `SHIP_DATE_FLOAT`.

The rest of the invoice follows the same shape as the quote. The pass-through component is zero when the account's `pass_through_enabled` election is false, and otherwise is the SKU's `conductor_lbs_per_kft` multiplied by the difference between `metal_price_used_usd_per_lb` and the base metal price for the conductor — three dollars per pound for copper, one dollar per pound for aluminium. `unit_price_usd_per_kft` is the order's `conversion_price_usd_per_kft` plus that component, rounded half up to four decimal places.

The extension again respects the pinned rounding order. When the order is a cut-to-length order, `extended_amount_usd` is the sum, taken over the *first* `cuts_count` entries of the order's `requested_cut_lengths_ft` in list order, of each segment's own amount — the segment's length in feet divided by 1000, multiplied by `unit_price_usd_per_kft`, and rounded half up to two decimals before it enters the sum — and `cut_fee_usd` is twelve dollars fifty per cut multiplied by `cuts_count`, rounded half up to two decimals. When the order is not cut to length, `extended_amount_usd` is `unit_price_usd_per_kft` multiplied by `shipped_length_ft` divided by 1000, rounded half up to two decimals, and `cut_fee_usd` is 0.00. The volatility surcharge tier is then keyed on `extended_amount_usd` alone, exactly as the quote's tier was keyed on its subtotal alone: two percent when the extension is below five thousand dollars, one and a half percent when it is at least five thousand but below twenty-five thousand, and one percent at twenty-five thousand or more. `volatility_surcharge_usd` is the extension multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to two decimals. `invoice_amount_usd` is then the extension plus the cut fee plus the surcharge, rounded half up to two decimals, and `invoice_date` is the logical clock's current date.

Invoicing then settles four things. The shipment itself moves `STAGED` → `INVOICED` with `version` incremented by one. The order's `invoiced_total_usd` is increased by `invoice_amount_usd`, and its pricing dimension moves `LOCKED` → `INVOICED` — with `version` incremented by one — but only when the order's fulfillment dimension reads `FULFILLED` after this invoice *and* every shipment of the order has reached `INVOICED`. An order still sitting at `QUOTED` when its last shipment is invoiced can be invoiced perfectly well, but its pricing dimension never advances to `INVOICED`, because that move is available only from `LOCKED`. The account's `open_ar_usd` is increased by `invoice_amount_usd`, `credit_exposure_usd` is recomputed, and the account auto-transitions `ACTIVE` → `CREDIT_HOLD` when the recomputed exposure is strictly greater than `credit_limit_usd`. And the reel leaves the `SHIPPED` state: a `WOOD_RETURNABLE` or `STEEL_RETURNABLE` reel moves `SHIPPED` → `RETURN_PENDING` and is now expected back, while a `NON_RETURNABLE` reel moves `SHIPPED` → `RETIRED` and leaves the fleet permanently. Because a non-returnable reel's deposit is always zero, retiring it leaves the deposit ledger untouched. Either way the reel's `version` is incremented by one.

Receiving a reel back is a `ShippingClerk` action against the reel, naming the `shipment_id` that sent it out, the `returned_date` on which it physically came back in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and the `condition` it came back in, either `GOOD` or `DAMAGED`. The reel must be at `RETURN_PENDING`; returning a reel in any other status is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REEL_STATE`. For a `GOOD` return, `returned_date` must be on or after the date part of the reel's `shipped_at`. The computation reads only the returned reel, the shipment named in the payload and the holding account; no other reel enters it.

`days_out` is the number of whole calendar days from the date part of the reel's `shipped_at` to `returned_date`, and the refund schedule that applies is chosen from the reel's type and the reported condition. A `WOOD_RETURNABLE` reel returned in `GOOD` condition follows a flat step schedule recorded as `FLAT_STEP`: the whole `deposit_usd` is refunded when `days_out` is at most 90; half the deposit, rounded half up to two decimals, is refunded when `days_out` is greater than 90 and at most 180; and nothing is refunded once `days_out` exceeds 180. A `STEEL_RETURNABLE` reel returned in `GOOD` condition follows a daily decay schedule recorded as `DAILY_DECAY`: the refund percentage is the greater of thirty percent and one minus four thousandths multiplied by `days_out`, and `refund_usd` is `deposit_usd` multiplied by that percentage, rounded half up to two decimals; the percentage itself is never rounded before it multiplies the deposit. To make the difference concrete, at 120 days out a deposit of 450.00 refunds 234.00 under the steel schedule against 225.00 under the wood schedule. Any reel returned in `DAMAGED` condition, and any `NON_RETURNABLE` reel, follows the schedule recorded as `NONE`: `refund_usd` is 0.00 and the entire deposit is forfeited. In every case `forfeited_usd` is `deposit_usd` minus `refund_usd`, rounded half up to two decimals, and `refund_variant_applied` records which of `FLAT_STEP`, `DAILY_DECAY` or `NONE` produced the figure.

The return then rewrites the reel. `returned_at` is stamped with the logical-clock instant and becomes the exclusive lower bound for the reel's next occupancy; `last_return_condition` records the reported `condition`; `cycle_count` is incremented by one; and `current_lot_id`, `held_by_customer_id` and `loaded_length_ft` are cleared to null, null and 0 respectively. `version` is incremented by one. Where the reel lands depends on condition and wear: a `GOOD` return moves it `RETURN_PENDING` → `AVAILABLE` and back into the fleet; a `DAMAGED` return moves it `RETURN_PENDING` → `DAMAGED` while its `cycle_count` before this increment was still strictly below the fleet cycle limit of 10; and a `DAMAGED` return on a reel whose `cycle_count` has already reached 10 moves it `RETURN_PENDING` → `RETIRED` instead, applying exactly the same ledger and field effects but taking the reel out of service for good.

Two ledger hops close the loop, and their order is fixed. In the first hop the holding account's `reel_deposit_balance_usd` is decreased by the reel's `deposit_usd`, then its `credit_memo_balance_usd` is increased by `refund_usd`, then its `forfeited_deposit_total_usd` is increased by `forfeited_usd`. In the second hop, caused by that ledger mutation, `credit_exposure_usd` is recomputed and the account auto-transitions `CREDIT_HOLD` → `ACTIVE` whenever the recomputed exposure is at or below `credit_limit_usd`. This is how an account can be taken off hold by nothing more than a customer returning empty reels. After every return the invariant of §2.9 must hold exactly: `deposit_charged_total_usd` equals `reel_deposit_balance_usd` plus `credit_memo_balance_usd` plus `forfeited_deposit_total_usd`.

Return events are append-only history, exposing only their identifier and creation timestamp with no `updated_at` and no `version`. Each row records the `reel_id` returned, the `shipment_id` that sent it out, the `returned_date`, the reported `condition`, and the computed `days_out`, `refund_usd`, `forfeited_usd` and `refund_variant_applied`. They may be listed against the reel they belong to, narrowed by `condition`, and they may also be listed against the shipment they settled, which is how a billing analyst reconciles a single delivery's deposits.
