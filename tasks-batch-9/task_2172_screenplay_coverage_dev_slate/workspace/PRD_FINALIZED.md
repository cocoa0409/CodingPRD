# Screenplay Coverage & Development Slate API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A studio development department lives or dies by the quality of its paper trail. Material arrives from agencies, managers, entertainment attorneys and — occasionally, and dangerously — over the transom from writers with no representation at all. Somebody has to decide whether a signed release is needed before anyone in the building reads a page, assign the script to a story analyst whose taste actually matches the genre, hold that analyst to a coverage standard, translate the resulting recommendation into a position on the tracking board, and then, if the material is worth chasing, paper an option, stand up a project, attach writers against a milestone schedule, and eventually put the whole thing in front of a greenlight committee. Today those steps live in eight incompatible spreadsheets and a shared mailbox. Rights lapse unnoticed. Two executives option the same book. A reader who grades a full point generous distorts the board for a quarter.

This document specifies the REST API for a single system of record that carries a piece of underlying material from the moment it is logged at the gate to the moment it is greenlit, put in turnaround, or reverted to its author. The service is deliberately opinionated: the chain of title is derived from filed documents rather than asserted by a human; coverage scores are calibrated against the reader who wrote them before they are allowed to move the board; option economics recompute themselves whenever a budget is attached; writer commitments are a conserved ledger that unwinds exactly when an engagement is terminated; and the greenlight vote is a weighted fork-join with a binding veto. Segregation of duties is enforced at the API layer rather than trusted to convention — the executive who assigns coverage may not approve it, the business-affairs user who approves a milestone may not pay it, and a development exec who owns a project may not vote on it from a committee seat.

Everything below is written for the engineers who will build the service. Section 2 fixes the contracts that every endpoint shares — authentication, envelopes, pagination, versioning, rounding, the order in which failures are reported. Section 3 tells the story of the domain and, in telling it, specifies every state transition, formula, threshold and cascade the system must implement. Sections 4 and 5 are reference tables: the endpoint surface and the error catalogue. Read Section 3 as normative, not narrative; the tables in Sections 4 and 5 deliberately omit trigger conditions and payload shapes, because those are defined by the prose.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent, is present but empty, or if `X-User-Role` does not name one of the eight roles below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. If both headers are well-formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called (see the Authorized Role(s) column in Section 4), the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` and HTTP 403.

The eight roles are `SlateAdmin`, `StoryEditor`, `Submitter`, `Reader`, `BusinessAffairs`, `DevelopmentExec`, `Writer`, and `CommitteeMember`.

- A `SlateAdmin` runs slate operations: seating readers, writers and committee members, convening and chairing greenlight reviews, and driving the service's logical clock.
- A `StoryEditor` runs the story department: logging and clearing submissions, opening, sending and waiving release forms, assigning readers, recalling assignments, and requesting revisions on, approving or rejecting coverage.
- A `Submitter` is the external submitting party — agency, manager, attorney, or an unrepresented writer. Submitters log submissions, execute release forms, and withdraw their own material.
- A `Reader` is a story analyst who starts assignments, fills in coverage report bodies, and submits them.
- A `BusinessAffairs` user files chain-of-title documents, papers and administers option agreements, serves reversion notices, and approves and pays writer milestones.
- A `DevelopmentExec` owns slate projects: setting them up, proposing and attaching writers, completing and terminating engagements, and placing projects in turnaround.
- A `Writer` is an engaged screenwriter who delivers drafts and polishes against their own engagements.
- A `CommitteeMember` holds a greenlight committee seat carrying a weighted ballot and, in some cases, veto power.

Three of these roles are entity-backed, meaning `X-User-ID` names a persisted row. When `X-User-Role` is `Reader`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Reader` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a reader compare that resource's reader foreign key to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Writer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Writer` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a writer compare that resource's writer foreign key to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `CommitteeMember`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CommitteeMember` row, and a member may only cast a ballot as itself.

The remaining five roles — `SlateAdmin`, `StoryEditor`, `Submitter`, `BusinessAffairs` and `DevelopmentExec` — carry opaque identity. For these roles `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition says to: as `assigned_by_user_id` on a coverage assignment, as `approved_by_user_id` on a coverage report or a milestone payment, and as `owning_exec_user_id` on a development project. It is never compared against any entity's `id`.

An entity-backed role that acts on a resource belonging to a different principal — a reader starting, editing or submitting coverage for another reader's assignment, a writer delivering against another writer's engagement, or a committee member attempting to vote in someone else's name — is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` and HTTP 403. This ownership check guards exactly five operations: starting a coverage assignment, patching a coverage report body, submitting a coverage report, delivering against a writer engagement, and casting a ballot on a greenlight review.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` controls page size; it defaults to 10 when omitted, must be at least 1, and must not exceed 100. `offset` controls the starting position; it defaults to 0 and must be zero or greater. A `limit` outside 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE` and HTTP 422.

List responses populate four pagination fields inside `meta`: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values used for the page, `total_count` reports how many rows match the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window. Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The single exception is the milestone-payment collection, which is ordered by `sequence_index` ascending so that a writer's payment ladder reads in the order it must be settled.

Filter parameters offered by an endpoint are combined conjunctively: a request naming both `property_id` and `status` returns only rows matching both. Filters are applied before pagination, so `total_count` reflects the filtered population.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource is identified by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and are never supplied by the client. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, recorded as an ISO 8601 UTC string with exactly three fractional-second digits.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations the row suffers as a side effect of an operation aimed at some other resource. Whenever a rule in Section 3 says that a cascade touches a row, that row's `version` advances and its `updated_at` is refreshed.

Two resource types are append-only records rather than mutable state and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at`: chain-of-title documents and committee ballots. Neither exposes `updated_at` and neither exposes `version`; do not emit those fields for them.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock, Calendar Arithmetic and Interval Conventions

The service does not read wall-clock time. It maintains a logical clock which reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` until an administrator sets it, and which never falls back to real time. An administrator advances or rewinds it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; every subsequent read of the current instant returns that value. The clock's current value is also readable directly.

Two interval conventions are used, and they differ deliberately. An option agreement occupies a **half-open** window: the window runs from its start instant inclusive up to, but not including, its end instant, so an option beginning at precisely the instant a previous option ends does not collide with it. A reader's coverage window and a writer's engagement window are **closed** intervals: both endpoints belong to the interval, so windows that merely touch at an endpoint do collide.

Calendar-month arithmetic appears in two places — computing an option's end instant from its start, and computing a project's reversion-eligibility instant from the moment it entered turnaround. In both, advancing an instant by a whole number of calendar months means moving to the same day-of-month in the target month, preserving the time-of-day component exactly; when the target month has no such day-of-month, the result clamps to the last valid day of that month. No truncation to day boundaries is ever applied.

### 2.6. Request Validation and the Rejection Ladder

When a request violates more than one rule, the service reports the highest-precedence violation and stops. The ladder below is evaluated strictly top to bottom; a check never masks one above it, and a check never fires before one above it has passed.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present, non-empty, and `X-User-Role` one of the eight declared roles, or `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorisation.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised-role list, or `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path resolution.** Every resource id embedded in the URL is resolved left to right, and the first unresolvable segment decides the error: `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RELEASE_FORM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_READER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOARD_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WRITER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint carrying a path parameter.
4. **Body reference resolution.** Every foreign-key identifier supplied in a request body must resolve to an existing row — `property_id`, `option_agreement_id`, `project_id`, `writer_id`, `reader_id` and `supersedes_document_id` — failing with the corresponding not-found code (`ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WRITER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_READER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`) at HTTP 404. This check guards six operations: creating a submission, filing a rights document on a property, creating an option agreement, creating a development project, creating a writer engagement, and assigning a reader to a submission.
5. **Shape.** Every required input field is present and carries its declared JSON type, or `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Unknown fields in a request body are ignored rather than rejected, and any computed or system-maintained field a client supplies is likewise ignored. Applies to every write endpoint.
6. **Enumerations.** Every enum-typed input carries a declared member of that enumeration, or `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` (422). Applies to every write endpoint that accepts an enum input.
7. **Numeric ranges.** Numeric inputs sit inside their declared bounds, or `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422). The declared bounds are: each of the five craft scores from 1 through 10; `weekly_capacity` from 1 through 20; `vote_weight_bps` from 1 through 10000; `initial_period_months` from 1 through 36; `extension_period_months` from 0 through 24; `option_fee_cents`, `purchase_price_cents`, `total_fee_cents` and `budget_cents` each at least 1; `limit` from 1 through 100; and `offset` at least 0.
8. **Resource ownership.** The entity-backed ownership rule of Section 2.1, failing with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
9. **Stale-version rejection.** The optimistic-concurrency rule of Section 2.7, failing with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
10. **Declared transition.** The target resource sits in a state from which the requested transition is declared, or `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to every action endpoint.
11. **Admissible parent state on create.** For creation endpoints whose parent must be in a particular posture: a development project's `development_status` must not be `GREENLIT`, `TURNAROUND` or `DEAD` for a writer engagement or a greenlight review to be created against it; a submission must be in `RECEIVED` for a release form to be opened on it; and a property must have no option agreement in `EXERCISED` status for a further option to be drafted against it. Violations are `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409).
12. **Terminal state.** The target resource is not already sitting in one of its terminal states, or `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE` (409). This check guards five operations: withdrawing a submission, terminating an option agreement, terminating a writer engagement, withdrawing a greenlight review, and recalling a coverage assignment.
13. **Segregation of duties.** The rule of Section 2.9, failing with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
14. **Release-form expiry**, then **release-form requirement.** Expiry is checked first: after lazy expiry has been applied, no release form on the submission may be in `EXPIRED` status, or `ERR_RELEASE_FORM_EXPIRED` (409); this guards reader assignment only. The requirement check follows: when a submission's `release_form_required` is true, at least one release form on it must be in `EXECUTED` or `WAIVED` status, or `ERR_RELEASE_FORM_REQUIRED` (409); this guards two operations, clearing intake and assigning a reader.
15. **Rights posture.** Executing an option requires the property's `rights_chain_status` to equal `CLEAR`, or `ERR_RIGHTS_CHAIN_INCOMPLETE` (409). Assigning a reader requires the submission's property not to be in `DISPUTED`, or `ERR_PROPERTY_RIGHTS_DISPUTED` (409). Attaching a writer requires the project's linked option agreement to be in `ACTIVE`, `EXTENDED` or `EXERCISED` status, or `ERR_OPTION_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Creating a development project requires the supplied `option_agreement_id` to belong to the same property as the supplied `property_id`, or `ERR_OPTION_MISMATCH` (409). Opening greenlight voting requires the project's `rights_status` to equal `OWNED`, or `ERR_RIGHTS_NOT_OWNED` (409).
16. **Temporal admissibility.** In priority order: option-window intersection on execution, failing with `ERR_OPTION_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409); reader-window collision on assignment, failing with `ERR_READER_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409); option expiry on exercise or extension, failing with `ERR_OPTION_EXPIRED` (409); turnaround immaturity on a reversion notice, failing with `ERR_TURNAROUND_NOT_MATURE` (409); and writer over-commitment on attachment, failing with `ERR_WRITER_OVERCOMMITTED` (409).
17. **Remaining business constraints**, in this order: an empty auto-selection candidate pool, `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_READER` (409); an explicitly named reader who is unavailable or full, `ERR_READER_AT_CAPACITY` (409); an incomplete coverage report on submit, `ERR_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE` (422); an exhausted revision allowance on a revision request, `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409); a repeat ballot, `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE` (409); an undersized committee at open-voting, `ERR_COMMITTEE_TOO_SMALL` (409); a failed quorum at close, `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET` (409); and an out-of-sequence milestone approval, `ERR_MILESTONE_SEQUENCE_VIOLATION` (409).
18. **Fiscal constraints**, last of all: the project writer-budget headroom test, failing with `ERR_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (409) on both engagement creation and engagement attachment; and the guild minimum test, failing with `ERR_GUILD_MINIMUM_VIOLATION` (422) on engagement creation.

Because the ladder is strict, a request that is both stale and aimed at a terminal resource reports `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, not `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`; a request from an unauthorised role against a non-existent resource reports `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, not a not-found code.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Protection on Superseding Creates

Three creation endpoints legitimately destroy work that a client may not have seen, so all three require the client to prove it was looking at a current parent. Each takes a non-optional version field in its request body carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent row. If that value does not equal the parent's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and nothing is created or superseded.

- `POST /option-agreements` requires `expected_property_version`, matching the current `version` of the `LiteraryProperty` named by `property_id`. Drafting an option terminates every other drafted option on that property, so a client working from a stale property view would silently tear up a draft it never observed.
- `POST /writer-engagements` requires `expected_project_version`, matching the current `version` of the `DevelopmentProject` named by `project_id`. A new proposal supersedes any other proposal of the same engagement type on that project, and the project's writer-budget headroom moves as sibling engagements attach.
- `POST /greenlight-reviews` requires `expected_project_version`, matching the current `version` of the `DevelopmentProject` named by `project_id`. Convening a review withdraws any review already open or voting on that project, discarding ballots the client may never have seen.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and no other endpoint may reject a request for a version mismatch.

### 2.8. Money, Weights, Percentages and Rounding

All money is held in integer cents. Fields whose names end in `_cents` are whole-cent integers and are never fractional. Where a monetary computation would produce a fraction, this document states explicitly whether the result is floored or rounded half up; there is no default.

All voting weights and all share percentages used in fee schedules and escalation bands are held in basis points — hundredths of a percent — in fields whose names end in `_bps`. Ten thousand basis points is one hundred percent.

Scores and percentages that are exposed as decimal numbers — `composite_score`, `normalized_score`, `calibration_bias`, `heat_score`, `quorum_pct` and `approval_pct` — are carried to exactly two decimal places and rounded half up. Where a formula divides, the division is carried at full precision and the result is rounded once, at the very end; intermediate values are never rounded on the way through. Where a threshold is compared against one of these values, the comparison uses the rounded value.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Three decisions in this system require two different people, and the API enforces that rather than trusting policy. The approving story editor's `X-User-ID` must differ from the `assigned_by_user_id` recorded on the coverage assignment the report answers. The paying business-affairs user's `X-User-ID` must differ from the `approved_by_user_id` recorded on the milestone payment. And a voting committee member's `exec_user_id` must differ from the `owning_exec_user_id` recorded on the project under review — a development executive who also holds a committee seat is barred from voting on their own project. Any of these three collisions is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403. The check guards exactly three operations: approving a coverage report, paying a milestone payment, and casting a greenlight ballot.

### 2.10. Cascades, Counters and Conserved Ledgers

Several operations in this system touch far more than the resource they name. Where Section 3 describes a cascade, every link of it is mandatory, and each link's ordering matters because later links are caused by earlier ones. Every row a cascade mutates has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed, including parent rows whose only change is a recomputed counter.

Four counters in the system are **conserved quantities**, meaning that a compensating action must return them to exactly their pre-action value. A reader's `active_assignment_count` is the number of that reader's coverage assignments in `ASSIGNED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `SUBMITTED` status; assigning a reader and then recalling that same assignment must leave the count exactly where it started, and must leave `completed_report_count` untouched. A writer's `committed_fee_cents` is the sum of `gross_amount_cents` across every milestone payment on that writer's engagements whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `DUE` or `APPROVED`; attaching an engagement and then terminating it with no payment in between must leave `committed_fee_cents` exactly where it started and `paid_fee_cents` unchanged. A writer's `paid_fee_cents` is the sum of `gross_amount_cents` across that writer's milestone payments in `PAID` status, and `active_engagement_count` is the number of that writer's engagements in `ATTACHED` or `ACTIVE` status. Voided milestone rows contribute to neither money sum. All four counters are exact integer arithmetic with no rounding.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Underlying Material and the Chain of Title

Everything in the system hangs off a piece of underlying material. A story editor or a slate administrator registers one by supplying its `title`, its `material_type`, the `source_author_name` of the author of record, and its `genre`; a one-sentence `logline` may be supplied and defaults to the empty string when it is not. The `material_type` is one of `SPEC_SCRIPT`, `BOOK`, `ARTICLE`, `PLAY`, `LIFE_RIGHTS_STORY` or `ORIGINAL_PITCH`. The `genre` is one of `DRAMA`, `COMEDY`, `THRILLER`, `HORROR`, `SCIFI`, `FAMILY` or `DOCUMENTARY`, and it matters well beyond taxonomy: it is what the reader-selection policy matches a story analyst's specialty against. A freshly registered property starts with `rights_chain_status` of `UNVERIFIED`, `verified_document_count` of 0, `tracking_tier` of `NONE`, and `encumbrance_count` of 0. None of those four fields is ever written directly by a client; each is derived.

Chain of title is evidenced by documents, filed by business affairs against the property. Each document carries the `property_id` of the material it evidences, a `doc_type`, the `executed_on` calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and a `verified` boolean recording whether business affairs has actually checked it. A document may carry free-text `notes`, defaulting to the empty string, and may carry `supersedes_document_id` naming an earlier document on the same property that this one replaces; that field defaults to null and forms a self-referential edge, so the document set on a property is a graph rather than a flat list. When a document is filed naming a predecessor, the predecessor's `superseded` flag is set to true; a document's own `superseded` flag is true exactly when some other document carries `supersedes_document_id` equal to that document's `id`, and it starts false. The `doc_type` is one of `CHAIN_OF_TITLE`, `COPYRIGHT_CERT`, `WGA_REGISTRATION`, `PRIOR_ASSIGNMENT`, `LIFE_RIGHTS_RELEASE` or `CLEARANCE_OPINION`. Chain-of-title documents are append-only: once filed, they are read but never edited, which is why they carry neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

Filing a document immediately re-derives the property's rights posture from the full document set, and increments the property's `version` by one. The derivation considers only *live* documents — those whose `superseded` flag is false; a superseded document is invisible to every part of this calculation, both to the dispute scan and to the verified counts. Among the live documents, the verified ones are those whose `verified` flag is true; `verified_document_count` is simply how many there are. The `rights_chain_status` then follows a four-branch ladder, evaluated top to bottom, first match winning. First, if any live document is of type `PRIOR_ASSIGNMENT` and has not been verified, the property is `DISPUTED` — an unverified prior assignment is exactly the kind of unresolved competing grant that stops a deal. Otherwise, if the verified document types include both `CHAIN_OF_TITLE` and `COPYRIGHT_CERT`, and the verified document count is at least three, the property is `CLEAR`. Otherwise, if at least one live document is verified, the property is `PARTIAL`. Otherwise it remains `UNVERIFIED`.

The four possible values of `rights_chain_status` are therefore `UNVERIFIED`, `PARTIAL`, `CLEAR` and `DISPUTED`, and each of them gates something downstream: `DISPUTED` blocks reader assignment, anything other than `CLEAR` blocks option execution, and `CLEAR` earns a bonus on the tracking board. The `encumbrance_count` is a running count of how many option agreements on the property are currently in `ACTIVE`, `EXTENDED` or `EXERCISED` status, and `tracking_tier` mirrors the property's best live board position; both are described where they are earned. Properties themselves can be listed across the slate with filters on `tracking_tier`, on `rights_chain_status` and on `genre`, and a single property read exposes its derived rights posture, its tier and its current `version`. Documents can be listed property by property or across the whole slate, and the cross-slate listing accepts `property_id`, `supersedes_document_id` and `doc_type` filters so that a chain can be walked edge by edge. A `supersedes_document_id` that names no existing document is a `ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND` rejection, not a silently ignored value.

### 3.2. Submission Intake and the Release-Form Gate

Material reaches the department as a submission. Either the submitting party itself or a story editor logs one, naming the `property_id` of the material, the `submitting_agency` it came through, a `submitter_contact_email` for correspondence, and the `submitted_via` channel — one of `AGENCY`, `MANAGER`, `ATTORNEY`, `UNSOLICITED` or `INTERNAL`. An optional `priority_flag` of `RUSH`, `STANDARD` or `BACKLOG` defaults to `STANDARD`. The moment the submission is logged, the logical clock is stamped into `received_at`, the submission enters `RECEIVED` status, and its `decision` is set to `PENDING`.

Two values are computed at that instant and, in the case of the first, frozen for the life of the submission. **The release-form requirement.** A release form is required when the submission arrived through the `UNSOLICITED` channel, or when the submitting agency is not one of the five signatory agencies the studio has a standing submission agreement with — `CAA`, `WME`, `UTA`, `GERSH` and `APA`. The agency comparison is an exact, case-sensitive string match against those five names; "caa" is not `CAA`. Both conditions are considered, and the requirement is set when either holds. Nothing outside the submission's own `submitted_via` and `submitting_agency` values participates, and once `release_form_required` has been written it is never recomputed, even if the material is later resubmitted through a signatory agency. **The coverage turnaround budget.** The `coverage_due_at` instant is the `received_at` instant advanced by a turnaround budget selected by `priority_flag`: one full day for `RUSH`, three days for `STANDARD`, and a full week for `BACKLOG` — that is, 24, 72 and 168 hours respectively. This becomes the default deadline for whatever coverage assignment is later opened on the submission.

Submissions can be listed with filters on `property_id`, on `status` and on `submitted_via`. A submission moves through `RECEIVED`, `RELEASE_PENDING`, `INTAKE_CLEARED`, `READER_ASSIGNED` and `COVERAGE_IN_REVIEW`, and comes to rest in one of three terminal states: `PASSED`, `IN_DEVELOPMENT` or `WITHDRAWN`. Every one of those transitions increments the submission's `version` by one.

Where a release form is required, a story editor opens one, supplying the `expires_at` instant after which the release is no longer good. Opening a release form is only declared from `RECEIVED` and only when the submission's `release_form_required` is true and no release form already exists on that submission in `REQUIRED`, `SENT`, `EXECUTED` or `WAIVED` status; the new form is created in `REQUIRED` status and the submission advances `RECEIVED → RELEASE_PENDING`. The form itself moves through `REQUIRED`, `SENT`, and then one of three terminal states: `EXECUTED`, `WAIVED` or `EXPIRED`. A story editor sends the form, taking it `REQUIRED → SENT`, which is admissible only while the logical clock is at or before `expires_at`. The submitting party then executes it by supplying a `signer_name`; execution is declared only from `SENT`, only while the logical clock is at or before `expires_at`, stamps the clock into `signed_at`, and carries the parent submission `RELEASE_PENDING → INTAKE_CLEARED`. Alternatively a story editor waives the requirement outright by supplying a `waiver_reason`; waiving is admissible from either `REQUIRED` or `SENT`, and likewise carries the submission `RELEASE_PENDING → INTAKE_CLEARED`.

Release forms go stale, and they go stale *lazily*: nothing sweeps them in the background. Expiry is evaluated at the start of exactly two operations — assigning a reader to the submission, and withdrawing the submission. At those two moments, any release form on the submission whose status is `REQUIRED`, `SENT` or `EXECUTED` and whose `expires_at` is strictly before the current logical clock value is moved to `EXPIRED` first, before any other check runs. Note the third of those: an already-executed release can go stale. When that happens the submission remains `INTAKE_CLEARED`, but reader assignment is refused with `ERR_RELEASE_FORM_EXPIRED`, because the studio is no longer covered.

Not every submission takes the release-form leg. A submission from a signatory agency through a represented channel needs no release, and a story editor clears it directly, `RECEIVED → INTAKE_CLEARED`. Clearing intake is declared *only* from `RECEIVED`; attempting it on a submission already sitting in `RELEASE_PENDING` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. But an unsolicited submission that requires a release and has none in `EXECUTED` or `WAIVED` status is refused at this step with `ERR_RELEASE_FORM_REQUIRED` rather than `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — the difference matters, because it tells the editor to chase the paper rather than to look for a different state.

Withdrawal is available to the submitting party and to a story editor, requires a `reason`, and is declared from every non-terminal submission state — `RECEIVED`, `RELEASE_PENDING`, `INTAKE_CLEARED`, `READER_ASSIGNED` and `COVERAGE_IN_REVIEW`. A submission already in `PASSED`, `IN_DEVELOPMENT` or `WITHDRAWN` is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`. The `reason` is stored as `withdrawal_reason`. Withdrawal is a compensating action and cleans up after itself: any release form still in `REQUIRED` or `SENT` becomes `EXPIRED`; any coverage assignment still open becomes `RECALLED` and the reader's `active_assignment_count` is reduced by one; and that assignment's coverage report, unless it has already been approved, becomes `REJECTED`.

### 3.3. The Reader Bench and Coverage Assignment

Story analysts are seated by a slate administrator. Each carries a `display_name`, a `specialty_genre` drawn from the same seven-value genre set as the material, a `weekly_capacity` between 1 and 20 giving the maximum number of open assignments the analyst may hold at once, and an `employment_type` of either `STAFF` or `FREELANCE`. An optional `status` defaults to `ACTIVE` and may also be `ON_LEAVE` or `INACTIVE`. Three derived numbers ride along: `active_assignment_count` and `completed_report_count` both start at 0, and `calibration_bias` starts at 0.00. A slate administrator may later adjust a reader's `status` or `weekly_capacity`; the bench can be listed and filtered by `status`, by `specialty_genre` and by `employment_type`.

Assigning coverage is the story editor's most consequential routine act, and it does five things at once. A story editor names the submission and may supply a `reader_id` explicitly, may supply a `window_start` and a `window_due` bounding the reader's occupation window, and may supply `excluded_reader_ids` listing analysts to keep out of automatic selection. When `window_start` is omitted it defaults to the current logical clock value; when `window_due` is omitted it defaults to the submission's `coverage_due_at`. The operation is declared only from `INTAKE_CLEARED` and drives the submission `INTAKE_CLEARED → READER_ASSIGNED`. Before anything else happens, release-form lazy expiry runs, as described in the previous subsection. Then the property's rights posture is checked: a property in `DISPUTED` blocks the assignment with `ERR_PROPERTY_RIGHTS_DISPUTED`. Then, when the submission requires a release, at least one form on it must sit in `EXECUTED` or `WAIVED` once expiry has been applied, or the call is refused with `ERR_RELEASE_FORM_REQUIRED`; a form that expiry has just moved to `EXPIRED` yields `ERR_RELEASE_FORM_EXPIRED` instead, and that expiry check is evaluated ahead of the requirement check.

On success the system creates a coverage assignment in `ASSIGNED` status carrying the `submission_id`, the resolved `reader_id`, the two window bounds, and `assigned_by_user_id` set to the acting editor's `X-User-ID`. It sets `auto_selected` to true when the reader was chosen by the selector and false when the request named one explicitly. It creates that assignment's coverage report in `DRAFT` status, carrying the `assignment_id` of the new assignment, so the analyst never has to create the report themselves. It increments the chosen reader's `active_assignment_count` by one. And it advances the submission.

**Choosing the reader.** When the request names a `reader_id`, that analyst is used, but only if their `status` is `ACTIVE` and their `active_assignment_count` is strictly less than their `weekly_capacity`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_READER_AT_CAPACITY`. When the request omits `reader_id`, the selector policy runs. Its candidate set consists of every analyst who is `ACTIVE`, whose `active_assignment_count` is strictly less than their `weekly_capacity`, whose `specialty_genre` equals the property's `genre` *or* whose `employment_type` is `STAFF` (staff readers are eligible for anything; freelancers only for their own genre), whose `id` is not among the supplied `excluded_reader_ids`, whose `id` is not the reader of any assignment on this same submission that has been `RECALLED`, and who survives the window-overlap test described below. Candidates are then ranked on three keys evaluated left to right: first a genre penalty of 0 when the analyst's `specialty_genre` matches the property's `genre` and 1 otherwise, ascending, so specialists come before generalists; then the analyst's load ratio, their `active_assignment_count` divided by their `weekly_capacity`, ascending, compared as an exact rational with no rounding applied before comparison; then their `completed_report_count`, descending, so experience breaks a tie in favour of the busier veteran. If all three keys tie, the analyst with the earlier `created_at` wins; `id` is never consulted as a tie-breaker. If the candidate set is empty, the entire request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_READER` and neither a coverage assignment nor a coverage report is created.

**The window-overlap test.** A reader's coverage windows are closed intervals with a trailing buffer of twelve hours on the incumbent, which is to say: the incumbent assignment is treated as occupying its window plus half a day of recovery afterwards. A candidate window conflicts with an existing assignment when the candidate's `window_start` is at or before the existing assignment's `window_due` advanced by twelve hours, *and* the existing assignment's `window_start` is at or before the candidate's `window_due`. Both bounds are inclusive, so windows that merely touch at an endpoint do collide. The test is run against every assignment of the resolved reader whose status is `ASSIGNED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `SUBMITTED`; assignments already `COMPLETED` or `RECALLED` never conflict. A collision on an explicitly named reader is refused with `ERR_READER_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. The selector applies the same test as part of its eligibility filter, which is why automatic selection never hands back a reader who would then be rejected for a window collision.

An assignment lives through `ASSIGNED`, `IN_PROGRESS` and `SUBMITTED` before settling in one of its two terminal states, `COMPLETED` or `RECALLED`. The analyst who owns it marks it in progress, taking it `ASSIGNED → IN_PROGRESS`; only that analyst may do so. A story editor may pull an assignment back at any point while it is `ASSIGNED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, supplying a `reason` that is stored as `recall_reason`; the recall is the compensating action for the assignment and it takes the assignment to `RECALLED`, decrements the reader's `active_assignment_count` by one, drives the assignment's coverage report to `REJECTED` if that report is still in `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, and returns the submission `READER_ASSIGNED → INTAKE_CLEARED` so the material can be reassigned. Recalling an assignment already in `COMPLETED` or `RECALLED` is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`. Assignments can be listed and filtered by `submission_id`, by `reader_id` and by `status`.

### 3.4. Coverage: Writing, Revision, Approval and the Tracking Board

The coverage report is created for the analyst, in `DRAFT`, at the moment of assignment. The analyst fills in its body: five craft scores — `logline_score`, `structure_score`, `character_score`, `dialogue_score` and `marketability_score`, each an integer from 1 through 10 — a `recommendation` of `PASS`, `CONSIDER`, `RECOMMEND` or `STRONG_RECOMMEND`, a `synopsis_word_count`, and a list of `comparable_titles`. All of these begin unset: the five scores and the `recommendation` start null, `synopsis_word_count` starts at 0, and `comparable_titles` starts as an empty list. Scores are only ever *stored* through this body edit; nothing about the edit computes anything. Only the analyst who owns the parent assignment may edit the body.

**The coverage standard.** Submitting a report requires all five craft scores to be present integers in the 1-through-10 range, the `recommendation` to be present, and `synopsis_word_count` to be at least 250 words. A report that falls short of any part of that standard is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE`. Submission is declared from `DRAFT` and again from `REVISION_REQUESTED`, and in both cases requires the parent assignment to be in `ASSIGNED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. On success the report moves to `SUBMITTED`, the assignment moves to `SUBMITTED` from whichever of `ASSIGNED` or `IN_PROGRESS` it held, and the submission advances `READER_ASSIGNED → COVERAGE_IN_REVIEW`.

**The two scores.** Submitting computes a raw weighted score from the five craft scores, each multiplied by its departmental weight and the products added together: `logline_score` at one tenth (0.10), plus `structure_score` at three tenths (0.30), plus `character_score` at one quarter (0.25), plus `dialogue_score` at fifteen hundredths (0.15), plus `marketability_score` at one fifth (0.20). Those five weights sum to one. Two numbers are derived from that raw value and both are stored. `composite_score` is the raw value rounded half up to two decimal places, with no calibration adjustment and no clamping; it is the number that appears on the face of the coverage report and the number that later feeds the reader's own calibration. `normalized_score` is the raw value less the authoring analyst's `calibration_bias` as read at this very instant, then clamped so that it is at least 1.00 and at most 10.00, and only then rounded half up to two decimal places — the clamp is applied before the rounding, not after. `normalized_score` is the only score that feeds the tracking board, which is why a generous reader's report scores lower on the board than it does on its own face. Both numbers are recomputed from the current field values on every submission, including the second submission that closes a revision cycle; approving the report afterwards does not restate either of them.

**Revision.** A story editor who wants more may return the report, supplying a `revision_note`. The report moves `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED`, its `revision_round_count` increments by one, the note is stored, the assignment goes back to `IN_PROGRESS`, and the submission returns `COVERAGE_IN_REVIEW → READER_ASSIGNED`. This closes a cycle: the analyst revises and submits again, taking the report `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` and the submission `READER_ASSIGNED → COVERAGE_IN_REVIEW` once more. The department allows two revision rounds per report and no more. A revision request is admissible only while `revision_round_count` is strictly below 2; a third request is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

**Approval and the five-link cascade.** A story editor approves a report that is in `SUBMITTED`, and the approver's `X-User-ID` must differ from the `assigned_by_user_id` on the assignment. Approval fires an ordered cascade, each link caused by the one before it. Link one: the report moves to `APPROVED` and `approved_by_user_id` records the approving editor. Link two: the assignment moves `SUBMITTED → COMPLETED`. Link three, caused by link two: the reader's `active_assignment_count` decrements by one, their `completed_report_count` increments by one, and their `calibration_bias` is recomputed. Link four, also caused by link two: the submission's `decision` is set to the report's `recommendation`, and the submission moves `COVERAGE_IN_REVIEW → TRACKED` when the recommendation is `CONSIDER`, `RECOMMEND` or `STRONG_RECOMMEND`, or `COVERAGE_IN_REVIEW → PASSED` when the recommendation is `PASS`. Link five, caused by link four and only when the submission became `TRACKED`: a tracking board entry is created and the property's `tracking_tier` is raised if the new entry outranks it. When the recommendation was `PASS`, no board entry is created and the property's `tracking_tier` is left exactly as it was. A submission's `decision` is `PENDING` until a report is approved and thereafter mirrors that report's recommendation.

**Calibration.** A reader's `calibration_bias` measures how far their approved coverage runs from the department's anchor of 5.50. It is the mean `composite_score` across every coverage report authored by that reader whose status is `APPROVED` — including the report just approved — less 5.50, with the division carried at full precision and the single result rounded half up to two decimal places. Reports still in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `REJECTED` are excluded. A reader with no approved report at all has a `calibration_bias` of exactly 0.00. The same set gives `completed_report_count`, which is simply how many approved reports the reader has authored.

**Rejection.** A story editor may reject a report instead of approving it, supplying a `reason` stored as `rejection_reason` — but only once the revision allowance has been used up. Rejection is declared from `SUBMITTED` and only when `revision_round_count` is at 2 or above. It takes the report to `REJECTED`, drives the assignment to `RECALLED` with the reader's `active_assignment_count` decremented by one, and returns the submission `COVERAGE_IN_REVIEW → INTAKE_CLEARED`. A report can also reach `REJECTED` without an editor's rejection at all: when the parent assignment is recalled, or when the submission is withdrawn, a report still in `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` is driven to `REJECTED` as a side effect. Reports settle in one of two terminal states, `APPROVED` or `REJECTED`, and can be listed and filtered by `assignment_id`, by `status` and by `recommendation`.

**Heat and the board.** A tracking board entry records the `property_id` of the material, the `report_id` of the approved report that produced it, a `heat_score`, a `board_tier`, a `competing_submission_count`, and an `entered_at` stamped from the logical clock. Its `status` is `LIVE` while it drives the property's tier and `ARCHIVED` once it has been superseded or the project has been greenlit. The `competing_submission_count` is how many *other* submissions on the same property — excluding the one being tracked — sit in `TRACKED` or `IN_DEVELOPMENT` status at the instant the entry is created.

The `heat_score` is assembled from five terms and then rounded half up to two decimal places. Start with the approved report's `normalized_score` multiplied by ten. Add a recommendation bonus: nothing for `PASS`, 8 for `CONSIDER`, 18 for `RECOMMEND`, and 30 for `STRONG_RECOMMEND`. Add a competition bonus of 3 per competing submission, capped at 15 no matter how many rivals there are — so five rivals and fifteen rivals earn the same 15 points. Add 10 more when the property's `rights_chain_status` is `CLEAR` at the instant of approval, and nothing otherwise. Then subtract 5 for each revision round the report went through, that is, 5 multiplied by its `revision_round_count`. Round the result half up to two decimal places, and apply the tier ladder to that rounded number, top to bottom with inclusive lower bounds: a `heat_score` of 110.00 or more is `PRIORITY`; otherwise 85.00 or more is `HOT`; otherwise 60.00 or more is `WATCH`; otherwise `NONE`.

The property's `tracking_tier` mirrors the best live board entry it has. Tiers rank `NONE` below `WATCH`, `WATCH` below `HOT`, and `HOT` below `PRIORITY`. When a new entry is created, the property's `tracking_tier` is raised to the new entry's `board_tier` only when that tier ranks strictly higher than the property's current value; a lower or equal tier leaves the property untouched. A passed greenlight forces `PRIORITY` outright, as Section 3.9 describes. Board entries can be read individually and listed with filters on `property_id`, `report_id`, `board_tier` and `status`.

### 3.5. Option Agreements: Windows, Escalation and the Net Purchase Price

Business affairs papers the studio's rights position as an option agreement against a property. Drafting one requires the `property_id`, the `expected_property_version` described in Section 2.7, an `option_fee_cents` of at least one cent paid for the initial period, a `purchase_price_cents` of at least one cent representing the unescalated price on exercise, an `initial_period_months` between 1 and 36, and the `option_start_at` instant at which the exclusive window opens. Three further terms are optional: `extension_period_months` between 0 and 24, defaulting to 0; `extension_fee_cents`, defaulting to 0, payable to take that extension; and `applicable_to_purchase`, defaulting to true, which decides whether option fees paid are credited against the purchase price. The new agreement is created in `DRAFTED` status with `extension_exercised` false, `escalation_bps` 0, `applicable_credit_cents` 0, and `option_end_at`, `adjusted_purchase_price_cents` and `net_purchase_due_cents` all still null.

Drafting is a superseding create. Every other agreement on the same property that is still in `DRAFTED` status is moved to `TERMINATED` with its `termination_reason` set to `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_DRAFT`, and the property's `version` increments by one. A property that already carries an agreement in `EXERCISED` status cannot take a further draft at all; that attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

An agreement moves through `DRAFTED`, `ACTIVE` and `EXTENDED` on its way to `EXERCISED`, and comes to rest in `LAPSED`, `REVERTED` or `TERMINATED`. Business affairs executes a drafted agreement, taking it `DRAFTED → ACTIVE`, and two gates stand in the way. The property's `rights_chain_status` must equal `CLEAR`; anything else is `ERR_RIGHTS_CHAIN_INCOMPLETE`. And the agreement's window must not intersect any other agreement on the same property whose status is `ACTIVE`, `EXTENDED` or `EXERCISED`. Option windows are half-open, running from `option_start_at` inclusive to `option_end_at` exclusive: a candidate conflicts with an incumbent when the candidate's `option_start_at` is strictly before the incumbent's `option_end_at` and the incumbent's `option_start_at` is strictly before the candidate's `option_end_at`. Back-to-back windows therefore do not collide — a candidate starting at precisely the instant an incumbent ends is admissible. Capacity on the property is one: a single conflicting incumbent is enough to refuse the execution with `ERR_OPTION_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. On success `option_end_at` is set to `option_start_at` advanced by `initial_period_months` calendar months under the clamping rule of Section 2.5, the four pricing fields are computed, and the property's `encumbrance_count` increments by one.

**The pricing calculation, in four pinned steps.** The order matters, because step three keys its tier on the *escalated* price produced by step two rather than on the raw `purchase_price_cents`, and reading it the other way round gives a different and wrong answer.

*Step one, the escalation band.* If no development project is linked to this agreement, `escalation_bps` is 0. Otherwise the linked project's `budget_cents` selects a band: below twenty million dollars — that is, below 2,000,000,000 cents — the escalation is 0 basis points; from twenty million dollars up to but not including sixty million dollars — that is, from 2,000,000,000 cents up to 6,000,000,000 cents — the escalation is 1500 basis points; and at sixty million dollars or above it is 2500 basis points. A project whose `rights_status` has already gone to `REVERTED` still counts as linked for this purpose.

*Step two, the escalated price.* `adjusted_purchase_price_cents` is `purchase_price_cents` multiplied by ten thousand plus `escalation_bps`, divided by ten thousand, with the result rounded half up to whole cents.

*Step three, the credit cap.* Let the escalated price from step two select a cap in basis points: below two hundred and fifty thousand dollars — 25,000,000 cents — the cap is 10000 basis points, meaning the full price may be credited; from two hundred and fifty thousand dollars up to but not including one million dollars — 25,000,000 up to 100,000,000 cents — the cap is 7500 basis points; and at one million dollars or above it is 5000 basis points.

*Step four, the credit and the net.* Total option fees paid so far are `option_fee_cents`, plus `extension_fee_cents` if and only if `extension_exercised` is true. Under the applicable variant — that is, when `applicable_to_purchase` is true — `applicable_credit_cents` is the smaller of those total fees paid and the escalated price multiplied by the step-three cap in basis points divided by ten thousand and floored to whole cents. Under the non-applicable variant — when `applicable_to_purchase` is false — `applicable_credit_cents` is 0, so the studio pays its fees on top of the price rather than against it. Finally, `net_purchase_due_cents` is `adjusted_purchase_price_cents` less `applicable_credit_cents`.

This calculation is re-run at four moments: when the agreement is executed, when it is extended, when it is exercised, and when a development project is created against it, because the newly linked `budget_cents` can move the escalation band and therefore everything downstream of it.

**Extending.** Business affairs may take the single permitted extension while the agreement is `ACTIVE`, moving it `ACTIVE → EXTENDED`. The extension is admissible only when `extension_exercised` is still false, when `extension_period_months` is at least 1, and when the logical clock is strictly before `option_end_at`; a clock at or after `option_end_at` is `ERR_OPTION_EXPIRED`. On success `extension_exercised` becomes true, `option_end_at` advances by `extension_period_months` further calendar months, and `net_purchase_due_cents` is recomputed — the extension fee now counts among the fees paid, so an applicable-credit agreement's net due typically falls.

**Exercising.** Business affairs exercises from either `ACTIVE` or `EXTENDED`, moving the agreement to `EXERCISED`. The logical clock must be strictly before `option_end_at`, or the call is `ERR_OPTION_EXPIRED`. A development project must already exist whose `option_agreement_id` names this agreement and whose `rights_status` is still `OPTIONED`. On success `exercise_notice_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `net_purchase_due_cents` is recomputed, and the linked project's rights axis flips `OPTIONED → OWNED`.

**Lapsing.** When the window closes unexercised, business affairs records it, moving the agreement `ACTIVE → LAPSED` or `EXTENDED → LAPSED`. The lapse is admissible only when the logical clock is at or after `option_end_at`. Both paths fire the identical five-link cascade. Link one: the property's `encumbrance_count` decrements by one. Link two, caused by that loss of encumbrance: the linked project's `rights_status` becomes `LAPSED_RIGHTS`. Link three, caused by link two: the project's `development_status` becomes `DEAD`, and every engagement on that project in `PROPOSED`, `ATTACHED` or `ACTIVE` status becomes `TERMINATED`. Link four, caused by link three: every milestone payment on those terminated engagements whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `DUE` or `APPROVED` becomes `VOIDED`. Link five, caused by link four: each affected writer's `committed_fee_cents` is reduced by the summed `gross_amount_cents` of their voided rows, and their `active_engagement_count` is reduced by the number of their engagements that were terminated out of `ATTACHED` or `ACTIVE`.

**Terminating.** An agreement that was never executed can simply be torn up. Termination is declared only from `DRAFTED`, requires a `reason` that is stored as `termination_reason`, and takes the agreement to `TERMINATED`; an agreement already in one of its terminal states is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`. Agreements can be read individually — the read exposes all four computed pricing fields — and listed with filters on `property_id` and `status`.

### 3.6. The Development Slate and Its Two Independent Axes

A development executive sets up a slate project against a property and one of its option agreements, supplying `property_id`, `option_agreement_id`, an all-in `budget_cents` of at least one cent, and the `slate_year` the project is tracked against. The agreement must belong to the same property as the one named, or the request is refused with `ERR_OPTION_MISMATCH`. Notably, the agreement need not be executed yet: a project may be stood up against a still-`DRAFTED` agreement, and the consequence is felt later, when no writer may be attached until that agreement has been executed.

Creating the project records `owning_exec_user_id` from the acting executive's `X-User-ID`, starts `committed_writer_fee_cents` at 0, computes `writer_budget_cap_cents`, re-runs the linked agreement's pricing calculation because the new `budget_cents` may change its escalation band, and sweeps the property's submissions: every submission on the same property sitting in `TRACKED` moves `TRACKED → IN_DEVELOPMENT`. The writer budget cap is a fixed 12 percent of the production budget: `writer_budget_cap_cents` is `budget_cents` multiplied by 1200 basis points, divided by ten thousand, floored to whole cents.

**Two axes, evolving independently.** A project carries `rights_status` and `development_status` as parallel state machines, and neither drives the other except where explicitly stated. The rights axis starts at `OPTIONED` and may move to `OWNED` when the linked agreement is exercised, to `LAPSED_RIGHTS` when the linked agreement lapses, or from `OWNED` to `REVERTED` when a matured reversion notice is served. The development axis starts at `SETUP` and runs through `WRITING`, `DRAFT_DELIVERED`, `POLISH` and `POLISH_DELIVERED`, and can end at `GREENLIT`, `TURNAROUND` or `DEAD`. `DEAD` is the project's only terminal state.

On the development axis: attaching an engagement of type `ORIGINAL_DRAFT`, `REWRITE` or `PRODUCTION_REWRITE` takes `SETUP → WRITING`, and this is admissible while the rights axis holds either `OPTIONED` or `OWNED`. Delivering a first draft takes `WRITING → DRAFT_DELIVERED`. Attaching a further `REWRITE` engagement takes `DRAFT_DELIVERED → WRITING`, closing a rewrite cycle that can run as many times as the budget allows. Attaching a `POLISH` engagement takes `DRAFT_DELIVERED → POLISH`, and delivering that polish takes `POLISH → POLISH_DELIVERED`. A passed greenlight takes either `DRAFT_DELIVERED → GREENLIT` or `POLISH_DELIVERED → GREENLIT`, and requires the rights axis to be `OWNED`. Placing a project in turnaround is declared from `SETUP`, `WRITING`, `DRAFT_DELIVERED`, `POLISH` and `POLISH_DELIVERED` — that is, from any development state other than `GREENLIT`, `TURNAROUND` and `DEAD` — and a matured reversion notice takes `TURNAROUND → DEAD`.

The `greenlight_ready` flag is a convenience derived from both axes at once: it is true exactly when `rights_status` is `OWNED` and `development_status` is either `DRAFT_DELIVERED` or `POLISH_DELIVERED`, and false otherwise. It is recomputed whenever either axis moves, and it is explicitly driven back to false when a rewrite or polish attachment pulls the development axis off a delivered state. The `turnaround_start_at`, `turnaround_eligible_at` and `turnaround_reason` fields all stay null until the project is placed in turnaround. Projects can be read individually — the read exposes both axes and the current `version` — and listed with filters on `property_id`, `option_agreement_id`, `development_status`, `rights_status` and `slate_year`.

### 3.7. Writers, Engagements and the Milestone Ladder

A slate administrator registers a screenwriter with a `name`, a `guild_member` flag that defaults to true, and a standing `quote_cents`. An optional `status` defaults to `ACTIVE` and may otherwise be `UNAVAILABLE`. Three ledger fields start at zero: `committed_fee_cents`, `paid_fee_cents` and `active_engagement_count`, all maintained by the system as described in Section 2.10 and never written by a client. Writers can be listed with filters on `status` and `guild_member`, and a writer's own read exposes the committed and paid ledger.

**Proposing an engagement.** A development executive proposes an engagement by naming the `project_id`, the `writer_id`, an `engagement_type` of `ORIGINAL_DRAFT`, `REWRITE`, `POLISH` or `PRODUCTION_REWRITE`, a `total_fee_cents` of at least one cent covering all milestones, and a `start_date` and `end_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form bounding the engagement window, together with the `expected_project_version` described in Section 2.7. The engagement is created in `PROPOSED` status with `milestone_count` 0 and a computed `guild_minimum_cents`. Proposing is a superseding create: every other engagement on the same project that is still `PROPOSED` **and carries the same `engagement_type`** is moved to `TERMINATED` with `termination_reason` set to `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_PROPOSAL`, and the project's `version` increments by one. A project whose `development_status` is `GREENLIT`, `TURNAROUND` or `DEAD` cannot take a new proposal at all; that attempt is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Two fiscal tests run at proposal time. The guild minimum: when the writer's `guild_member` is true, `guild_minimum_cents` is the floor for that engagement type — seventy-five thousand dollars (7,500,000 cents) for an `ORIGINAL_DRAFT`, fifty thousand dollars (5,000,000 cents) for a `REWRITE`, twenty-five thousand dollars (2,500,000 cents) for a `POLISH`, and fifty thousand dollars (5,000,000 cents) for a `PRODUCTION_REWRITE` — and a `total_fee_cents` below that floor is refused with `ERR_GUILD_MINIMUM_VIOLATION`. When the writer is not a guild member, `guild_minimum_cents` is 0 and the test cannot fail. The budget headroom test: the project's `committed_writer_fee_cents` plus the proposed `total_fee_cents` must be at or below the project's `writer_budget_cap_cents` — the bound is inclusive, so a proposal landing exactly on the cap is admissible — and a proposal that would breach it is refused with `ERR_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. The project's `committed_writer_fee_cents` is the sum of `total_fee_cents` across its engagements in `ATTACHED`, `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETED` status; engagements still `PROPOSED` and engagements already `TERMINATED` consume no headroom. The headroom test is applied again at attachment, because siblings may have attached in the meantime.

**Attaching.** A development executive attaches a proposed engagement, taking it `PROPOSED → ATTACHED`. Five preconditions guard it. The writer's `status` must be `ACTIVE`; an `UNAVAILABLE` writer yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The project's linked option agreement must be in `ACTIVE`, `EXTENDED` or `EXERCISED` status, or the attachment is refused with `ERR_OPTION_NOT_ACTIVE` — this is the practical consequence of allowing a project to be stood up against a merely drafted agreement. The project's `development_status` must not be `GREENLIT`, `TURNAROUND` or `DEAD`. The writer must not be over-committed, as described next. And the budget headroom test must pass at this instant too.

**Writer concurrency.** Engagement windows are closed date intervals, and the studio allows a writer to carry at most two overlapping commitments at once. Count the writer's other engagements — excluding the candidate itself — whose status is `ATTACHED` or `ACTIVE` and whose window intersects the candidate's, where two closed windows intersect when the incumbent's `start_date` is on or before the candidate's `end_date` and the candidate's `start_date` is on or before the incumbent's `end_date`. The attachment is admissible when that count is at most one; a second overlapping incumbent — which would make three concurrent engagements — is refused with `ERR_WRITER_OVERCOMMITTED`. Engagements in `PROPOSED`, `COMPLETED` or `TERMINATED` status occupy no capacity.

**The milestone schedule.** Attachment generates the engagement's milestone payments from a template selected by `engagement_type`, sets `milestone_count` to the number of lines in that template, adds `total_fee_cents` to both the writer's `committed_fee_cents` and the project's `committed_writer_fee_cents`, increments the writer's `active_engagement_count` by one, and advances the project's development axis as Section 3.6 describes. The templates are ordered lists of a milestone step and its share of the fee in basis points. An `ORIGINAL_DRAFT` pays 3000 basis points on `COMMENCEMENT`, 4000 on `FIRST_DRAFT_DELIVERY` and 3000 on `FIRST_DRAFT_ACCEPTANCE`. A `REWRITE` pays 4000 on `COMMENCEMENT` and 6000 on `REWRITE_DELIVERY`. A `POLISH` pays 5000 on `COMMENCEMENT` and 5000 on `POLISH_DELIVERY`. A `PRODUCTION_REWRITE` pays 3334 on `COMMENCEMENT`, 3333 on `REWRITE_DELIVERY` and 3333 on `PRODUCTION_BONUS`. Each generated row records its `milestone_type`, its zero-based `sequence_index` giving its position in the template, and its `percentage_bps`.

The allocation of money across those lines is pinned, because the obvious alternative does not reconcile. First, every line's `gross_amount_cents` is computed as `total_fee_cents` multiplied by that line's `percentage_bps`, divided by ten thousand, and **floored** — never rounded. Then the remainder, being `total_fee_cents` less the sum of all the floored lines, is pushed in its entirety into exactly one line: the line with the largest `percentage_bps`. When two lines share the largest `percentage_bps`, the remainder goes to the one with the lowest `sequence_index`; neither `created_at` nor `id` is ever consulted. Rounding each line half up and summing is a different and wrong answer. The invariant this protects is that the sum of `gross_amount_cents` across an engagement's milestones equals its `total_fee_cents` exactly, always.

Each row also gets a `due_on` date, being the engagement's `start_date` advanced by a fixed offset in days chosen by milestone step: `COMMENCEMENT` is due on the start date itself, at zero days; `FIRST_DRAFT_DELIVERY` at 84 days — twelve weeks; `FIRST_DRAFT_ACCEPTANCE` at 98 days — fourteen weeks; `REWRITE_DELIVERY` at 56 days — eight weeks; `POLISH_DELIVERY` at 28 days — four weeks; and `PRODUCTION_BONUS` at 120 days. Every generated row starts in `SCHEDULED` status, except that attachment immediately drives the `sequence_index` 0 row — always the `COMMENCEMENT` line — to `DUE`.

**Delivering.** The engaged writer, and only that writer, records a delivery by naming the `milestone_type` delivered. The matching milestone on the engagement moves `SCHEDULED → DUE`, which is admissible while the engagement is `ATTACHED` or `ACTIVE`, and the project's development axis advances: a `FIRST_DRAFT_DELIVERY` takes the project `WRITING → DRAFT_DELIVERED`, and a `POLISH_DELIVERY` takes it `POLISH → POLISH_DELIVERED`. Both recompute `greenlight_ready`.

**Approving and paying.** Milestone payments move through `SCHEDULED`, `DUE` and `APPROVED` before settling in `PAID` or `VOIDED`. Business affairs approves a milestone that is `DUE`, and the approval is admissible only when every milestone on the same engagement with a lower `sequence_index` is already in `PAID` or `VOIDED` status; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_MILESTONE_SEQUENCE_VIOLATION`. Approval records `approved_by_user_id` from the acting user's `X-User-ID`. A second business-affairs user then pays it, taking it `APPROVED → PAID`; the payer's `X-User-ID` must differ from `approved_by_user_id`. Payment stamps the logical clock into `paid_at`, moves `gross_amount_cents` out of the writer's `committed_fee_cents` and into their `paid_fee_cents`, and — when the paid row is the `sequence_index` 0 line — takes the engagement `ATTACHED → ACTIVE`. Milestone rows are read individually and listed with filters on `engagement_id`, `milestone_type` and `status`.

**Completing and terminating.** A development executive closes out an engagement that is `ACTIVE`, taking it `ACTIVE → COMPLETED`; this is admissible only when every milestone on the engagement is in `PAID` or `VOIDED` status, and it decrements the writer's `active_engagement_count` by one. Termination is the compensating action for attachment and is declared from `PROPOSED`, `ATTACHED` and `ACTIVE`, requiring a `reason` stored as `termination_reason`; an engagement already `COMPLETED` or `TERMINATED` is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`. Terminating a merely proposed engagement records the reason and nothing more, because nothing has been committed. Terminating an attached or active engagement unwinds everything: every milestone on it in `SCHEDULED`, `DUE` or `APPROVED` status becomes `VOIDED`, the writer's `committed_fee_cents` is reduced by the summed `gross_amount_cents` of exactly those voided rows, the writer's `active_engagement_count` decrements by one, and the project's `committed_writer_fee_cents` is reduced by the engagement's `total_fee_cents`, returning the headroom to the slate. Engagements are listed with filters on `project_id`, `writer_id`, `status` and `engagement_type`.

### 3.8. Turnaround and Reversion

Projects die more often than they get made, and the studio's obligation when one dies is to hand the material back. A development executive places a project in turnaround with a `reason` stored as `turnaround_reason`, taking the development axis to `TURNAROUND` from any of `SETUP`, `WRITING`, `DRAFT_DELIVERED`, `POLISH` or `POLISH_DELIVERED`; a project already `GREENLIT`, in `TURNAROUND`, or `DEAD` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. This fires a three-link cascade plus a housekeeping sweep. Link one: `turnaround_start_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `turnaround_eligible_at` is that instant advanced by eighteen calendar months under the day-of-month clamping rule of Section 2.5. Link two, caused by the project entering turnaround: every engagement on the project in `PROPOSED`, `ATTACHED` or `ACTIVE` status becomes `TERMINATED`. Link three, caused by those terminations: each of their milestone payments in `SCHEDULED`, `DUE` or `APPROVED` status becomes `VOIDED`, and each affected writer's `committed_fee_cents` and `active_engagement_count` are reduced accordingly. Separately, any greenlight review on the project that is still `OPEN` or `VOTING` becomes `WITHDRAWN` with `withdrawal_reason` set to `PROJECT_IN_TURNAROUND`.

The reversion clock is eighteen months long and its lower bound is inclusive: a reversion notice is admissible when the logical clock reads `turnaround_eligible_at` exactly, as well as any time after. Business affairs serves that notice, and it is refused with `ERR_TURNAROUND_NOT_MATURE` while the clock is still short of the eligibility instant. The notice also requires the project's linked option agreement to be in `EXERCISED` status. Serving it takes the project `TURNAROUND → DEAD` on the development axis and `OWNED → REVERTED` on the rights axis, moves the option agreement `EXERCISED → REVERTED`, decrements the property's `encumbrance_count` by one, and — because a reversion clouds the chain of title until it is re-papered — drops the property's `rights_chain_status` from `CLEAR` back to `PARTIAL` when it was `CLEAR`, leaving it alone when it was anything else.

### 3.9. The Greenlight Committee

Committee seats are created by a slate administrator, each with a `display_name` and a `vote_weight_bps` between 1 and 10000. A seat may carry `veto_power`, defaulting to false, and may carry an `exec_user_id` naming the development executive that the seat-holder is the same person as; that field defaults to null and exists purely to make the segregation-of-duties check possible. A seat's `status` defaults to `ACTIVE` and may otherwise be `INACTIVE`; only `ACTIVE` seats are counted into a review's eligible weight. A slate administrator may later adjust a member's `status` or `vote_weight_bps`, but doing so never restates the `weight_bps_snapshot` on ballots already cast. Members are listed with filters on `status` and `veto_power`, and the ballots one member has cast can be listed on their own.

A slate administrator convenes a review by naming the `project_id`, the `session_date` of the committee session in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and the `expected_project_version` described in Section 2.7. The review is created in `OPEN` status. Convening is a superseding create: every other review on the same project still in `OPEN` or `VOTING` becomes `WITHDRAWN` with `withdrawal_reason` set to `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_REVIEW`, and the project's `version` increments by one. A project whose `development_status` is `GREENLIT`, `TURNAROUND` or `DEAD` cannot take a new review; that is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Opening the ballot takes the review `OPEN → VOTING` and is likewise a slate administrator's act. Three preconditions hold: the project's `rights_status` must be `OWNED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_RIGHTS_NOT_OWNED`; the project's `development_status` must be either `DRAFT_DELIVERED` or `POLISH_DELIVERED`; and at least three committee seats must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_COMMITTEE_TOO_SMALL`. Opening the ballot freezes `eligible_weight_bps` as the summed `vote_weight_bps` of the currently `ACTIVE` seats; that snapshot never moves again, no matter who is seated or unseated afterwards.

Each committee member then casts exactly one ballot, supplying a `vote_value` of `APPROVE`, `DECLINE`, `ABSTAIN` or `VETO` and, optionally, a `comment`. The ballot records the `review_id`, the `member_id` taken from the acting member's `X-User-ID`, and a `weight_bps_snapshot` frozen from that member's `vote_weight_bps` at the instant of casting. Ballots are append-only records and carry neither `updated_at` nor `version`. Three checks guard a ballot: the review must be in `VOTING`; the member must not already have a ballot on this review, or the request is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE`; the member's seat must be `ACTIVE`; and the member's `exec_user_id` must differ from the project's `owning_exec_user_id`. Casting a ballot leaves the review in `VOTING` — this is a self-loop — and refreshes the running tallies.

Those tallies are all sums over the review's ballots using each ballot's frozen `weight_bps_snapshot`, never the member's current weight. `cast_weight_bps` is the sum across all ballots regardless of value. `decisive_weight_bps` is `cast_weight_bps` less the summed weight of the `ABSTAIN` ballots. `approve_weight_bps` is the summed weight of the `APPROVE` ballots. And `veto_cast` is true when at least one ballot carries `VETO` from a member whose `veto_power` was true at the time that ballot was cast. All four are recomputed on every ballot; all four default to 0, and `veto_cast` defaults to false, before any ballot exists.

Closing the review is the chair's act and it resolves the outcome through a strict ladder. First, the two percentages are computed. `quorum_pct` is one hundred multiplied by `cast_weight_bps`, divided by the frozen `eligible_weight_bps`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `approval_pct` is one hundred multiplied by `approve_weight_bps`, divided by `decisive_weight_bps`, rounded half up to two decimal places — except that when `decisive_weight_bps` is zero it is 0.00 rather than a division by zero. Both comparisons below use the rounded values. At this point the two percentages are still working values: they are written onto the review only by a close that actually resolves it. A close refused at rung one of the ladder below persists nothing, so the review's stored `quorum_pct` and `approval_pct` are left exactly as they were — both are 0.00 from the moment the review is convened, and they stay there until a resolving close writes them.

Then the ladder is walked top to bottom, first match winning. **One:** if `quorum_pct` is below 60.00, the close is *refused* with `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET` and the review stays in `VOTING` — this is the only branch that is an error rather than an outcome, and it leaves the ballot open for more members to vote. **Two:** if `veto_cast` is true, the review resolves `VOTING → DECLINED`. **Three:** if `decisive_weight_bps` is zero — meaning every ballot cast was an `ABSTAIN` — the review resolves `VOTING → DEFERRED`. **Four:** if `approval_pct` is at or above 66.67, the bound being inclusive, the review resolves `VOTING → PASSED`. **Five:** otherwise the review resolves `VOTING → DECLINED`. An optional `decision_note` supplied at close is recorded on the review in every resolving branch.

A `DECLINED` or `DEFERRED` outcome leaves the project's two axes exactly as they were. A `PASSED` outcome does not: the project's `development_status` moves to `GREENLIT` from whichever of `DRAFT_DELIVERED` or `POLISH_DELIVERED` it held, the property's `tracking_tier` is forced to `PRIORITY` regardless of what it was before, and every tracking board entry on that property still in `LIVE` status becomes `ARCHIVED`.

A slate administrator may pull a review off the agenda at any point while it is `OPEN` or `VOTING`, supplying a `reason` stored as `withdrawal_reason`; a review already in `PASSED`, `DECLINED`, `DEFERRED` or `WITHDRAWN` is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`. Reviews are read individually — the read exposes the frozen eligible weight and the running tallies — and listed with filters on `project_id` and `status`; the ballots on one review can be listed as well.
