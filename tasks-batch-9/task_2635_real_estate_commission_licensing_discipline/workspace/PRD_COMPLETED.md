# Real Estate Commission Licensing & Discipline API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A state real estate commission carries two mandates that are constantly in tension. It must move licensees through education, examination and brokerage affiliation quickly enough that a functioning market has agents to work with, and it must be able to stop a licensee — or an entire brokerage — the moment public money held in escrow goes missing or a pattern of misrepresentation surfaces. Today those two mandates live in separate filing cabinets. Licensing clerks stamp transfers without knowing that the receiving brokerage is three days late on a trust-account remediation order; counsel opens a consent negotiation without knowing that the respondent is also the designated broker whose suspension will freeze forty other agents' advertising; the recovery fund pays a claim that a later reconsideration will have to claw back by hand.

This document specifies the API that replaces those filing cabinets with one enforced record. It governs the full arc of a licence — application, examination, issuance under a sponsoring broker, transfer, voluntary inactivation, expiry, continuing-education-gated reactivation, suspension and revocation — alongside the supervisory apparatus that constrains it: brokerage registration, escrow and security-deposit trust accounts, an append-only trust ledger, two-basis reconciliation audits with immediate remediation orders, complaint intake and screening, bounded consent negotiation, formal hearings with panel scheduling, sanction orders with fine collection and vacatur, recovery-fund payouts under a capped waterfall, education-provider and instructor approval, and team, DBA and branch advertising-name review.

Two design commitments run through every section that follows and should be read as non-negotiable. First, **consequences are immediate and transitive**: a single request that finds a shortage does not merely record a finding — it orders remediation, restricts the account, re-grades the brokerage's supervision posture and auto-rejects that brokerage's pending advertising names, all before the response is written. Second, **the commission never lets one person hold both sides of a matter**: the investigator who built a case cannot chair its hearing, the counsel who offered a settlement cannot execute it, and the chair who decided an order cannot vacate it. Both commitments are enforced on identity and version, not on good intentions. The entire surface described here is mounted under the `/commission` path prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the rules that hold everywhere: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, the shape of every stored record, the logical clock, stale-view protection, cascade discipline, numeric conventions, segregation of duties, reference resolution, and the order in which rules are evaluated when several of them could fire on the same request.

### 2.1. Actors, Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user acts. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the ten role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A header that is present but empty, or that holds nothing but whitespace, counts as absent for this purpose and draws the same `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`; `X-User-ID` is checked before `X-User-Role`, so a request missing both reports the missing user id. Surrounding whitespace is trimmed from both header values before anything else happens, and it is the trimmed `X-User-ID` that is compared in every ownership and identity check below and recorded in every `*_user_id` field. Role matching on the trimmed value is exact and case-sensitive: `licensinganalyst` is not a recognised role. If the role is recognised but is not among the roles authorised for the requested operation (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The ten recognised roles are `LicensingAnalyst`, `TrustAuditor`, `CommissionCounsel`, `HearingPanelChair`, `RecoveryFundTrustee`, `Broker`, `Salesperson`, `Respondent`, `ProviderAdmin` and `Public`.

- A `LicensingAnalyst` is commission licensing staff. Applications, examination results, affiliations and transfers, brokerage registration and activation, education-provider approval, instructor approval, advertising-name review and the compliance sweep are theirs exclusively; they additionally share the trust-account, ledger, audit-scheduling, complaint-intake, fine-payment, instructor-enrolment, course-completion, advertising-submission, status-change and clock capabilities with other roles.
- A `TrustAuditor` is the field auditor: opening fieldwork, issuing findings, verifying remediation, escalating an audit and closing it are theirs exclusively, and they also schedule audits and read accounts, ledgers, licensees, brokerages and complaints.
- A `CommissionCounsel` prosecutes: complaint screening and disposition, amendment requests, probable-cause findings, consent offers, consent revision and execution, and hearing scheduling are theirs exclusively.
- A `HearingPanelChair` presides over the formal hearing panel — convening, continuing and deciding a hearing — and is also the only role able to vacate a sanction order.
- A `RecoveryFundTrustee` administers the fund: crediting appropriations and running the award waterfall are theirs exclusively, and they may also file recovery claims and read the fund, claims, orders and licensees.
- A `Broker` is a licensee whose `person_type` is `BROKER` acting for a brokerage: opening trust accounts, appending ledger entries, submitting advertising names, changing their own licence status and reading their own records.
- A `Salesperson` is a licensee whose `person_type` is `SALESPERSON`, limited to self-service inactivation and reactivation plus reads of their own record, their brokerage, their course completions and advertising names.
- A `Respondent` is the licensee named in a disciplinary matter, acting only on that matter: accepting or countering a consent offer is theirs exclusively, and they may record fine payments and read their own complaint, agreements, hearings and orders.
- A `ProviderAdmin` administers one approved education provider: enrolling instructors and reporting course completions.
- `Public` is the unauthenticated-equivalent external actor: complaint intake, complaint amendment, recovery-claim filing and public reads.

Four of these roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` is not an opaque operator string but the primary key of a persisted row, and ownership is checked against it:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Broker`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Licensee`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Licensee` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Broker` acting on a licence status change must be the licensee named in the path.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Salesperson`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Licensee`, and the same ownership comparison applies.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Respondent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Licensee`; for a consent agreement the server compares it to the `respondent_licensee_id` of the agreement's parent complaint, and for a fine payment it compares it to the order's `respondent_licensee_id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ProviderAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the identifier recorded as `admin_user_id` on the acting `EducationProvider`; ownership checks compare that provider's `admin_user_id` to `X-User-ID` — for instructor enrolment the provider named by the path segment, and for a course completion the provider named by `education_provider_id` in the body. Because the comparison is against `admin_user_id`, a `ProviderAdmin` write against a provider whose `admin_user_id` is null is always refused.

An entity-backed role acting on a resource that is not its own is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Ownership is a **write-side** rule only, and the phrase "their own records" in the role summaries above describes the intended use of a read capability rather than a restriction the server enforces. No read endpoint in this API compares anything to `X-User-ID`: the role check is the whole of the authorisation on a `GET`, so a `Salesperson` may read any licensee's record and any brokerage's, a `Respondent` may read any complaint, agreement, hearing or order their role is admitted to at all, and a `ProviderAdmin` may read another provider's instructors and completions. The five write operations rung 6 of §2.11 names are the only places `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` can ever be raised. Filtering a listing to one's own rows is done with the query parameters of §2.3, not by the server.

The remaining six roles — `LicensingAnalyst`, `TrustAuditor`, `CommissionCounsel`, `HearingPanelChair`, `RecoveryFundTrustee` and `Public` — are **opaque**: `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. It does, however, record and later re-read those identifiers where the workflow assigns responsibility: `auditor_user_id` on a `TrustAudit`, `panel_chair_user_id` on a `Hearing`, `screener_user_id` and `investigator_user_id` on a `Complaint`, `offered_by_user_id` / `responded_by_user_id` / `executed_by_user_id` on a `ConsentAgreement`, and `reviewed_by_user_id` on an `AdvertisingName`. Two of those recorded identifiers gate later access rather than merely documenting history: only the exact user whose id equals a `TrustAudit`'s `auditor_user_id` may perform that audit's fieldwork or any of its dispositions — anyone else, including another `TrustAuditor`, is refused with `ERR_AUDITOR_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403) — and only the user whose id equals a `Hearing`'s `panel_chair_user_id` may dispose of that hearing, with `ERR_PANEL_CHAIR_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403) for anyone else.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`; concretely it is the literal prefix `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn uniformly at random from the uppercase letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`, so the whole value is always 24 characters long.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one instant in the system read from the **host's real wall clock** rather than from the logical commission clock of §2.5, so it keeps advancing between requests even when the logical clock has been pinned to a fixed instant, and it never matches the logical clock unless by coincidence.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1 and may not exceed 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and counts records to skip from the start of the ordered result set. Those bounds are enforced by **clamping, never by rejection**: a supplied `limit` below 1 is raised to 1, a `limit` above 100 is lowered to 100, a negative `offset` is raised to 0, and a value that is not parseable as an integer at all (or is supplied empty) falls back to that parameter's default of 10 or 0. A list request therefore never answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` because of its paging arguments. Both are echoed back in `meta` — as the clamped effective values actually used, not as the raw input — alongside `total_count`, the number of records matching the filter before the window is applied, and `has_more`, a boolean that is true when records remain beyond the current window, computed as `offset` plus the number of rows returned being strictly less than `total_count` (so an `offset` past the end returns an empty `data` array with `has_more` false).

Unless a list's own description says otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Because `created_at` is stamped from the logical clock and many rows are therefore created at the identical instant, the ordering is realised as a stable ascending walk of a monotonic per-process insertion sequence assigned at row creation: rows sharing a `created_at` value come back in the order they were inserted, and no secondary key such as `id` ever enters the comparison. This is not a cosmetic default: the recovery-fund waterfall consumes claims in exactly this order, the trust ledger is read in exactly this order, and the compliance sweep processes rows within each of its steps in exactly this order, so a client that reads a list and then acts on it sees the same sequence the server will.

Collection endpoints also accept domain filters, which combine conjunctively with each other and with the pagination window. Licensee listings filter on `sponsoring_brokerage_id`, `supervising_broker_id`, `license_status`, `discipline_status` and `person_type`. Brokerage listings filter on `designated_broker_id`, `status` and `supervision_posture`. Trust-account listings filter on `brokerage_id`, `status` and `account_type`; a single account's ledger listing filters on `entry_type`. Audit listings filter on `trust_account_id`, `auditor_user_id`, `status` and `basis`. Complaint listings filter on `respondent_licensee_id`, `respondent_brokerage_id`, `source_audit_id`, `status` and `allegation_type`; the per-complaint consent-agreement and hearing listings filter on `status`. Sanction-order listings filter on `complaint_id`, `respondent_licensee_id`, `consent_agreement_id`, `hearing_id`, `status` and `sanction_type`, and the per-order claim listing filters on `status`. Provider listings filter on `status`; instructor listings on `education_provider_id`, `licensee_id`, `status` and `credential_type`; course-completion listings on `licensee_id`, `instructor_id`, `education_provider_id`, `credit_status` and `course_type`; advertising-name listings on `brokerage_id`, `team_lead_licensee_id`, `status`, `kind` and `normalized_name`. Every filter is compared as an exact, case-sensitive string against the row's stored value once the supplied value has been trimmed of surrounding whitespace; a filter supplied empty or as whitespace only is **ignored altogether** rather than matched against the empty string, and a row whose filtered field is null never matches a supplied value, so a listing cannot be asked for the rows where a field is unset. The two derived filters follow the same rule on a value derived per candidate row: `supervision_posture` is matched against the posture re-derived for each brokerage, and `normalized_name` is matched after putting the *supplied* value through the §3.12 normalisation, so a query for `Elm & Oak Realty` finds the row stored as `ELMOAKREALTY`. An unrecognised query parameter is simply ignored.

### 2.4. Common Record Properties

Every resource the API persists carries a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID with no type prefix — the same opaque shape for a licensee, a hearing and a ledger entry alike. Clients never supply an `id`; they receive it in the creation response and quote it in later paths and payloads.

Most resources additionally carry a full audit triple: `created_at` and `updated_at`, both ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. `updated_at` is refreshed on the same mutations that advance `version`. The unit of "a mutation" here is **the request, not the individual field write**: `version` advances at most once per row per request, so a row that a request touches several times — directly and then again through one or more layers of the cascades of §2.8 — comes out of that request with `version` exactly one higher than it went in, never two or three higher. A row *created* by the request is likewise already accounted for by starting at 1, so a row that is created and then touched again later in the same request still answers `version` 1. `updated_at`, by contrast, is re-stamped on every touch; since every touch inside one request reads the same logical instant, the re-stamping is not separately observable. This applies to `Licensee`, `Brokerage`, `TrustAccount`, `TrustAudit`, `Complaint`, `ConsentAgreement`, `Hearing`, `SanctionOrder`, `RecoveryFund`, `RecoveryFundClaim`, `EducationProvider`, `Instructor` and `AdvertisingName`. The audit triple's two timestamps — together with the envelope's `api_request_timestamp` — are the only instants rendered with a fixed fractional part. Every other instant this API stores or returns — `license_issued_at`, `license_expires_at`, `inactive_since`, `exam_passed_at`, `suspension_ends_at`, a provider's `approved_at`, an audit's fieldwork window and `remediation_due_at`, a complaint's `filed_at` and `statute_deadline_at`, a hearing's slot, an order's `effective_at`, `fine_due_at` and `vacated_at`, a claim's `filed_at` and `paid_at`, a completion's `invalidated_at`, and the clock's own `current_utc` — is rendered in ISO 8601 UTC at whole-second precision with no fractional part at all whenever the instant falls on an exact second, exactly as §2.5's `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` default is written, and carries three fractional digits only when the instant does not fall on an exact second. The API's purely calendar-valued fields — `ce_period_start_on`, a ledger entry's `effective_date` and `cleared_date`, an audit's `as_of_date` and `cleared_through_date`, and a completion's `completed_on` — are a third rendering again: a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` date with no time, no `T` and no `Z`. Both instants and dates are always rendered as `null` when the underlying value is unset.

Free-text annotations are accepted for the record but are not part of it. A complaint's `narrative`, an amendment's `amendment_note`, an audit disposition's or complaint disposition's `note`, a brokerage activation's `attestation_note`, an instructor approval's `approval_note`, an appropriation's `appropriation_note` and a vacatur's `vacatur_reason` are validated as strings where §2.11's shape rung requires them, and are then **discarded**: none of them is persisted on any row and none of them appears in any response body. Consequently no serialized resource carries a narrative or note field of any kind.

Two record types are deliberately different because they are immutable once written. A `TrustLedgerEntry` and a `CourseCompletion` carry only `id` and `created_at`: neither has an `updated_at` and neither has a `version`, and no request ever edits one. A ledger entry is never amended — a booking error is compensated by appending a further entry, not by rewriting history. A course completion is never edited either; when an instructor loses their credential the completion's `credit_status` is flipped and `invalidated_at` is stamped by the server as part of a cascade, which is a system-authored consequence rather than a client edit, and those two fields are the only mutable content on the row.

The `RecoveryFund` is a singleton. Exactly one row exists, created by system initialization at server startup with `balance_cents`, `appropriated_cents`, `fine_credits_cents`, `awarded_cents`, `assessment_cents` and `clawed_back_cents` all at 0, and it is read through its own dedicated read endpoint rather than by id. Its cash position is an invariant, not a free-floating number: `balance_cents` must at all times equal `appropriated_cents` plus `fine_credits_cents` plus `clawed_back_cents`, less `awarded_cents` and less `assessment_cents`. Any operation that touches the fund must leave that identity true.

### 2.5. The Logical Commission Clock

Every deadline, window, expiry, accrual period and elapsed-month calculation in this specification reads a **logical UTC clock** maintained by the server, never the host's wall clock. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; that startup default is mandatory and the server must not fall back to real time. An administrative capability lets an operator move the clock to a supplied instant, expressed as `to_iso8601` in the request body, and answers with the resulting `current_utc`. Moving the clock advances or rewinds time only: no entity is mutated, no expiry is applied and no housekeeping is implied as a side effect of setting it. Housekeeping happens only when the compliance sweep is invoked explicitly (§3.9).

Consequently, phrases such as "now", "the current instant" and "clock now" throughout this document mean the logical clock's value at the moment the request is processed.

### 2.6. Reference Resolution and Nested Paths

Several resources are addressed through nested paths — a ledger append names its trust account, a claim names its sanction order, an instructor enrolment names its education provider. Path identifiers are resolved **strictly left to right**, and the first unresolvable segment decides the error. An audit disposition addressed under a non-existent audit therefore answers `ERR_TRUST_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND` and never gets far enough to complain about the payload. Each entity has its own 404: `ERR_LICENSEE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BROKERAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRUST_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRUST_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSENT_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HEARING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SANCTION_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EDUCATION_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COURSE_COMPLETION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ADVERTISING_NAME_NOT_FOUND`.

Identifiers supplied in a request **body** are resolved the same way and produce the same family of 404s. This covers `brokerage_id`, `supervising_broker_id`, `designated_broker_id`, `team_lead_licensee_id`, `licensee_id`, `instructor_id`, `education_provider_id` and the disbursement payload's `sanction_order_id`. A body reference to a missing brokerage is an `ERR_BROKERAGE_NOT_FOUND`; a body reference to a missing licensee, whether supplied as `supervising_broker_id`, `designated_broker_id`, `team_lead_licensee_id` or `licensee_id`, is an `ERR_LICENSEE_NOT_FOUND`; a missing `instructor_id` is an `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_NOT_FOUND`; a missing `education_provider_id` is an `ERR_EDUCATION_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`; and a missing `sanction_order_id` is an `ERR_SANCTION_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`. The affected operations are affiliation and transfer, licence status changes, brokerage registration, instructor enrolment, course-completion reporting, advertising-name submission and the recovery-fund disbursement run. Where one operation carries several body references, they too are resolved one at a time in the order that operation's own description lists them, and the first unresolvable one decides the error: an affiliation, transfer or reactivation resolves `brokerage_id` before `supervising_broker_id`; a course completion resolves `licensee_id`, then `instructor_id`, then `education_provider_id`; an advertising submission resolves `brokerage_id` before `team_lead_licensee_id`. Path segments are resolved before any body reference, so an instructor enrolment under a non-existent provider answers `ERR_EDUCATION_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND` even when its `licensee_id` is also unresolvable.

### 2.7. Stale-View Protection on Contended Parents

Three write operations in this system are legitimately repeatable — appending a second identical-looking disbursement under a fresh reference is a real business event, affiliating a second licensee into the same brokerage is a real business event, and disbursing against a second order is a real business event — which means idempotency keys cannot express "my view of the parent is still current". Those three therefore use optimistic concurrency control against the parent record's `version`.

- Appending a trust ledger entry requires `expected_trust_account_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `TrustAccount`. It is not optional. If the supplied value does not equal the account's current `version` — because an earlier append, or an audit cascade, has advanced it since the client read the account — the append is rejected with `ERR_TRUST_ACCOUNT_VERSION_CONFLICT` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written.
- Affiliating or transferring a licensee requires `expected_brokerage_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the **target** `Brokerage`. A mismatch with that brokerage's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_BROKERAGE_VERSION_CONFLICT` (HTTP 412). This is what pins the supervision-ratio headroom the client believed it observed: a prior affiliation, an activation or any posture-bearing cascade advances the brokerage's `version` and invalidates the stale view.
- Running a recovery-fund disbursement requires `expected_recovery_fund_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `RecoveryFund` singleton. A mismatch with the fund's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_RECOVERY_FUND_VERSION_CONFLICT` (HTTP 412). Appropriations, fine credits, clawbacks and earlier disbursement runs all advance that `version`, so the distributable balance the trustee believed it observed can only be relied on by presenting the version it came with.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Where an operation is not listed above, it takes no `expected_*_version` input and no stale-version check is performed on it.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline and Same-Request Settlement

The commission's supervisory model is one of **immediate consistency**: there are no queues, no deferred jobs and no eventual reconciliation. When a request triggers a consequence, that consequence and everything it in turn triggers are applied inside the same request, and the response reflects the settled state.

Cascades in this specification are described in layers — a primary transition, then the effects it causes, then the effects those cause. Where a chain is stated, **the order is normative**, because intermediate states are observable through the counters and postures that later layers read. Three chains in particular must be followed exactly as written: an audit finding a shortage settles through findings, remediation order, account restriction, posture re-grading and advertising-name rejection in that sequence (§3.7); a revocation against a designated broker suspends the brokerage first and only then, and only if it has no remaining affiliates, closes it (§3.2); and a vacatur unwinds fine credits, clawbacks, eligible-claim denials, licence restoration and counter recomputation in that sequence (§3.9).

Every entity a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, exactly as if it had been mutated directly — a licensee whose supervising broker changed underneath them, a brokerage whose posture was re-graded, an advertising name auto-rejected by a posture change. For a brokerage this stamping is **unconditional on the outcome**: because its counters and posture are derived rather than stored, any write that brings a brokerage into its cascade set stamps that brokerage — advancing `version` and re-stamping `updated_at` — even when the re-derived posture and all four counters come out identical to what they already were. The writes that do so are affiliation and transfer, parking and reactivation, brokerage activation, every ledger append, every audit disposition except the one that closes a zero-shortage audit outright, complaint intake and disposition, hearing scheduling and hearing decision, the issuance of a sanction order, vacatur, and the compliance sweep. The remaining writes bring no brokerage into their cascade set and therefore leave every brokerage row alone: creating an applicant, registering a brokerage, recording an examination result, opening a trust account, scheduling an audit, opening fieldwork, an `ISSUE_FINDINGS` disposition whose frozen shortage is 0, recording a fine payment, filing a claim, appropriating to or disbursing from the fund, complaint amendment, every consent-agreement step short of execution, and every education-provider, instructor, course-completion and advertising-name write. This is why stale-view protection matters: a client holding a `Brokerage` read from before a cascade holds a stale `version`.

Several computed properties are not stored so much as re-derived. `Licensee.ce_hours_required`, `Brokerage.supervision_posture`, `Complaint.priority_score` and a trust account's `book_obligation_cents` / `book_available_cents` / `shortage_cents` are recomputed at read time as well as on write, so a plain read reflects the current logical clock and the current ledger even if nothing has been written since.

### 2.9. Integers, Hours and Rounding

All money in this system is expressed in **integer cents**. There are no floating-point amounts on the wire and no fractional cents in storage. Ledger arithmetic, shortage arithmetic and fund arithmetic are exact integer arithmetic and perform no rounding at all, because every input is already an integer.

Rounding appears in exactly two places, and in both the method is **round half up** to a whole cent. The recovery fund's administrative assessment is a percentage of an awarded total and is rounded half up to whole cents. The reinstatement fee's monthly late surcharge is a percentage of a base fee and is likewise rounded half up to whole cents — critically, it is rounded **per period and only then multiplied** by the number of periods, never multiplied first and rounded once at the end. The two orders produce different totals and only the round-then-multiply order is correct.

Continuing-education requirements and priority scores are whole numbers. Where a quotient appears in a requirement or a score, it is truncated toward zero (floor) before any cap is applied, and caps are applied last.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

The commission enforces segregation of duties on the acting `X-User-ID`, not merely on `X-User-Role`. Holding the right role is never sufficient if the same human already occupies the incompatible seat in that matter. Three pairings are forbidden, and all three answer with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409):

- The `panel_chair_user_id` proposed when a hearing is scheduled must not equal the complaint's `investigator_user_id`. The investigator who built the case may not preside over it.
- The user executing a consent agreement must not equal the agreement's `offered_by_user_id`. The counsel who put the offer on the table may not be the one who converts it into an order.
- The user vacating a sanction order must not equal the `panel_chair_user_id` of the hearing that produced it. A chair may not reverse their own decision.

The auditor-identity rule of §2.1 is the mirror image of the same principle: fieldwork, findings, verification, escalation and closure are reserved to the one auditor named in `auditor_user_id`, so an audit cannot be quietly handed off mid-examination.

### 2.11. Order of Rule Evaluation

Many requests could fail several checks at once, and clients depend on getting the *same* error every time. The server therefore evaluates rules in one fixed ladder and returns the first failure it meets. The ladder, from first-evaluated to last, is:

1. **Header presence and role recognition** — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, on every endpoint.
2. **Role authorisation for the operation** — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, on every endpoint.
3. **Request-body shape** — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), on every write. Every field the operation requires must be present, every enum value must be a declared member of its enumeration, every integer must respect its own bounds (a ledger `amount_cents` must be greater than 0; `credit_hours` must be at least 1 and at most 12; `record_retention_years` must be at least 3 and at most 10; `alleged_loss_cents` must be at least 0; `prelicense_hours` must be at least 0; `claimed_loss_cents` must be greater than 0; `proposed_fine_cents` and `proposed_suspension_days` must each be at least 0; `reinstatement_fee_paid_cents` must be at least 0; a fine payment's `amount_cents` and an appropriation's `amount_cents` must each be greater than 0; `amended_alleged_loss_cents`, an audit disposition's `remediation_days` and a hearing decision's `fine_cents` and `suspension_days` must each be at least 0; `approval_years` must be at least 1 and carries **no upper bound**, so an approval term of a thousand years is a shape-valid request), and every date or datetime must parse as ISO 8601. An integer the operation does not bound is accepted at any magnitude and sign — an examination `score` in particular is unbounded, so a negative score or a score above 100 passes this rung untouched. Five conventions make that rung precise. A field supplied as JSON `null` is treated exactly as if it had been **omitted**: a required field explicitly set to null is a missing field and fails this rung, while an optional field explicitly set to null simply takes its documented default. A request whose body is absent altogether, empty, or not parseable as JSON is treated as the empty object `{}`, so a write with no required fields succeeds without a body while a write with required fields reports the first one missing; a body that parses but is not a JSON object — an array, a string, a number — is itself rejected as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Where an integer is expected, a JSON boolean is refused even though `true` is integral, while a JSON float whose value is exactly integral (`5.0`) is accepted and used as that integer; a non-integral float is refused. Where a string is expected, any non-string JSON value is refused, and a body foreign key supplied as an empty or whitespace-only string is treated as absent — refused when the reference is required, read as null when it is optional. Where an instant is expected, a trailing `Z` or `z` and an explicit numeric offset are both accepted and the value is normalised to UTC, and a value carrying no zone at all is read as already being UTC; where a bare date is expected, a full ISO timestamp is also accepted and only its calendar-date part is kept.
4. **Path identifier resolution**, left to right — the entity-specific 404 of §2.6.
5. **Body foreign-key resolution** — the entity-specific 404 of §2.6.
6. **Entity-backed ownership** — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, on licence status changes, respondent consent responses, fine payments, instructor enrolment and course-completion reporting.
7. **Assigned-auditor identity** — `ERR_AUDITOR_MISMATCH`, on audit fieldwork and audit disposition.
8. **Assigned-panel-chair identity** — `ERR_PANEL_CHAIR_MISMATCH`, on hearing disposition.
9. **From-state legality** — `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), on every action endpoint. The target row must currently be in a state from which the requested transition is declared, including action-specific origins: verifying remediation requires `REMEDIATION_ORDERED`, executing a consent agreement requires `ACCEPTED`, convening requires `SCHEDULED`, and a manual escalation additionally requires a `remediation_round` of at least 2, so escalating a first-round remediation order by hand answers with this same error. Four cases are deliberately **excluded** from this rung so that a more specific error can own them: recording an examination result against a `DENIED` applicant (rung 35 answers instead), opening either a consent negotiation or a hearing on a complaint that is already `CONSENT_PENDING` or `HEARING_SCHEDULED` (rung 39 answers instead), and paying a fine on or vacating a sanction order that is already `VACATED` (rung 12 answers instead, so an already-vacated order is never reported as a bare state error even though `VACATED` is not among either operation's declared origins).
10. **Trust account not closed** — `ERR_TRUST_ACCOUNT_CLOSED`, on ledger appends and audit scheduling.
11. **Disbursement bar on a restricted account** — `ERR_ACCOUNT_RESTRICTED_NO_DISBURSEMENT`, on ledger appends.
12. **Order not already vacated** — `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_VACATED`, on vacatur and on fine payments. Filing a recovery claim is deliberately not covered: a claim against a vacated order is accepted and auto-denied instead.
13. **Trust-account stale-version check** — `ERR_TRUST_ACCOUNT_VERSION_CONFLICT`, on ledger appends.
14. **Brokerage stale-version check** — `ERR_BROKERAGE_VERSION_CONFLICT`, on affiliation and transfer.
15. **Recovery-fund stale-version check** — `ERR_RECOVERY_FUND_VERSION_CONFLICT`, on the disbursement run.
16. **Supervision posture block** — `ERR_BROKERAGE_POSTURE_BLOCKED`, on affiliation and transfer, on reactivation, and on advertising-name approval. Supervision integrity outranks every capacity, uniqueness and fiscal rule below it.
17. **Segregation of duties** — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, on hearing scheduling, consent execution and vacatur.
18. **Discipline hold** — `ERR_DISCIPLINE_HOLD`, on transfer and on the `GO_INACTIVE` licence status change. The two guards are not the same width: a transfer is held for a respondent in either `PENDING_HEARING` or `SANCTIONED`, while parking a licence is held only for `PENDING_HEARING`, so a `SANCTIONED` licensee may still go inactive. Reactivation carries **no** discipline guard at all, so a licensee under a pending hearing or already sanctioned may be reinstated once the fee, posture, supervisor, ratio and CE rules are satisfied.
19. **Prohibited advertising term** — `ERR_PROHIBITED_ADVERTISING_TERM`, on advertising-name approval.
20. **Team name implying a brokerage** — `ERR_TEAM_NAME_IMPLIES_BROKERAGE`, on advertising-name approval.
21. **Team-lead eligibility** — `ERR_TEAM_LEAD_NOT_AFFILIATED`, on advertising-name submission and on advertising-name approval.
22. **Supervising-broker eligibility** — `ERR_SUPERVISING_BROKER_INELIGIBLE`, on affiliation and transfer and on reactivation.
23. **Designated-broker eligibility** — `ERR_DESIGNATED_BROKER_NOT_BROKER`, on brokerage registration and on brokerage activation.
24. **Instructor–provider agreement** — `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_PROVIDER_MISMATCH`, on course-completion reporting.
25. **Provider approval standing** — `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_APPROVED`, on instructor enrolment, instructor approval and course-completion reporting.
26. **Instructor approval standing** — `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_NOT_APPROVED`, on course-completion reporting.
27. **Correction target exists** — `ERR_CORRECTION_TARGET_NOT_FOUND`, on ledger appends.
28. **Escrow account prerequisite** — `ERR_TRUST_ACCOUNT_REQUIRED`, on brokerage activation.
29. **Advertising name uniqueness** — `ERR_ADVERTISING_NAME_TAKEN`, on advertising-name submission and approval.
30. **Ledger reference uniqueness** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_TRANSACTION_REFERENCE`, on ledger appends.
31. **Supervision ratio capacity** — `ERR_SUPERVISION_RATIO_EXCEEDED`, on affiliation and transfer and on reactivation.
32. **Consent round cap** — `ERR_CONSENT_ROUND_LIMIT`, on a consent revision.
33. **Continuance cap** — `ERR_CONTINUANCE_LIMIT`, on a hearing continuance.
34. **Amendment cap** — `ERR_AMENDMENT_LIMIT`, on an amendment request.
35. **Examination attempt cap** — `ERR_EXAM_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`, on recording an examination result.
36. **Pre-licence education hours** — `ERR_PRELICENSE_HOURS_INSUFFICIENT`, on recording an examination result.
37. **Continuing-education sufficiency** — `ERR_CE_HOURS_INSUFFICIENT`, on reactivation and on a transfer by an already-`ACTIVE` licensee.
38. **Probable-cause score floor** — `ERR_PROBABLE_CAUSE_SCORE_TOO_LOW`, on a probable-cause disposition.
39. **No channel already open** — `ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_OPEN`, on opening a consent negotiation and on scheduling a hearing. In practice this rule outranks rung 9, because rung 9 explicitly carves out complaints already in `CONSENT_PENDING` or `HEARING_SCHEDULED` on precisely these two operations so that an already-open channel is reported here rather than as a bare state error.
40. **Hearing-room occupancy with turnover buffer** — `ERR_HEARING_ROOM_CONFLICT`, on hearing scheduling and on a continuance.
41. **Panel-chair occupancy** — `ERR_PANEL_CHAIR_DOUBLE_BOOKED`, on hearing scheduling and on a continuance.
42. **Auditor concurrent-window capacity** — `ERR_AUDITOR_WINDOW_CAPACITY`, on audit scheduling.
43. **Statute of limitations** — `ERR_STATUTE_DEADLINE_PASSED`, on a probable-cause disposition and on a complainant amendment.
44. **Recovery window still open** — `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED`, on the disbursement run.
45. **Fieldwork window has opened** — `ERR_FIELDWORK_NOT_STARTED`, on opening fieldwork.
46. **Hearing slot has arrived** — `ERR_HEARING_NOT_STARTED`, on convening a hearing.
47. **Fund reserve floor** — `ERR_FUND_RESERVE_FLOOR`, on the disbursement run.
48. **Per-licensee recovery cap** — `ERR_LICENSEE_CAP_EXHAUSTED`, on the disbursement run.
49. **Candidate claims exist and award is positive** — `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CLAIMS`, on the disbursement run.
50. **Payment within remaining fine balance** — `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`, on a fine payment.
51. **Exact reinstatement fee** — `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_FEE_MISMATCH`, on reactivation.

The substance of each rung — what exactly is compared against what — is developed in §3 alongside the workflow it guards.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Licence Applications and the Examination Gate

An applicant enters the system when a `LicensingAnalyst` opens a licence application, supplying the class of licence applied for as `person_type` (either `SALESPERSON` or `BROKER`), the applicant's legal name as `full_name`, and the classroom pre-licence education hours claimed at application as `prelicense_hours`, which may be 0 but never negative. The server mints a `license_number` on creation: the prefix `SP-` for a `SALESPERSON` and `BR-` for a `BROKER`, followed by a six-digit zero-padded counter that is maintained **separately for each `person_type`** and incremented on every create, so the first salesperson ever registered is numbered `SP-000001` and the first broker `BR-000001` even though both exist in the same registry. Numbers are unique across the registry.

A fresh application starts with `license_status` at `APPLICANT` and `discipline_status` at `NONE` — the two dimensions of a licensee's standing, which from here on evolve independently of one another. Its `exam_attempt_count` and `exam_best_score` are both 0, `exam_passed_at` is null, and the derived counters `ce_hours_valid`, `prior_sanction_count` and `recovery_paid_cents` all read 0. `sponsoring_brokerage_id` and `supervising_broker_id` are null and remain null for as long as `license_status` is `APPLICANT`, `EXAM_PASSED`, `REVOKED` or `DENIED`; `license_issued_at`, `license_expires_at`, `inactive_since`, `ce_period_start_on` and `suspension_ends_at` are likewise null at this stage.

Examination results are recorded one attempt at a time by a `LicensingAnalyst`, who supplies the numeric `score` and the date the examination was sat as `taken_on`. The `score` is required and must be an integer, but it carries **no range check whatsoever**: a negative score or a score far above 100 is recorded as given, so a score of 500 passes the examination and a score of −20 is a failure that still raises `exam_best_score` if it happens to exceed the value already there. The `taken_on` date is required and must parse, but nothing else is done with it — it is not compared against the logical clock, not stored, and not reflected in the response. Two gates precede any state change, and their relative order matters. First the attempt cap: an applicant may sit the examination at most three times, so if `exam_attempt_count` has already reached 3 the result is refused with `ERR_EXAM_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`. Only a terminal `DENIED` licence can have an `exam_attempt_count` of 3, and this is exactly why a fourth attempt answers with the attempt-cap error rather than a bare state complaint — the from-state ladder deliberately steps aside for this case. Second the education gate: `prelicense_hours` must be at least the threshold for the applicant's class, which is 90 classroom hours for a `SALESPERSON` and 180 for a `BROKER`; a shortfall is refused with `ERR_PRELICENSE_HOURS_INSUFFICIENT`.

Once past both gates, every recorded result increments `exam_attempt_count` by one and raises `exam_best_score` to the greater of its previous value and the score just recorded. Where the result lands depends on the score and on how many attempts preceded it. A score of 75 or higher is a pass: the applicant advances `APPLICANT → EXAM_PASSED`, and `exam_passed_at` is stamped with the current logical instant — the first passing result stamps it and no later result overwrites it, because a passing candidate has left `APPLICANT` behind. A score below 75 recorded when fewer than two attempts had already been counted leaves the applicant on `APPLICANT`: the attempt and best-score updates still land, but there is no status change and the applicant may sit again. A score below 75 recorded when `exam_attempt_count` stood at exactly 2 before the call — that is, the third attempt — moves the applicant to the terminal `DENIED` state with `exam_attempt_count` settled at 3. `DENIED` is terminal: no affiliation and no further examination. A complaint may nonetheless still be *filed* against a `DENIED` licensee — intake examines nothing at all about the respondent's standing beyond resolving the id — but such a complaint can never be moved out of `SUBMITTED` into investigation, which is where the bar actually bites (§3.8).

An `EXAM_PASSED` candidate holds no licence yet. Passing is a prerequisite for issuance, not issuance itself; the transition to `ACTIVE` happens only through affiliation with a brokerage, described in §3.3.

### 3.2. Brokerage Registration, Trust Accounts and Supervision Posture

A brokerage is registered by a `LicensingAnalyst` who supplies the registered `name`, the `designated_broker_id` of the licensee who will be the broker of record, a `trust_account_required` flag stating whether the firm must maintain an escrow trust account before it can be activated (true unless the analyst says otherwise), and the declared transaction-record retention period as `record_retention_years`, which defaults to 5 and must be at least 3 and at most 10 years. The registered `name` is stored exactly as supplied and is **not** checked for uniqueness, neither literally nor after normalisation: two brokerages may carry the identical registered name, and registration never answers `ERR_ADVERTISING_NAME_TAKEN` or any other conflict on the strength of the name alone. Name governance is confined to the advertising-name register, whose normalisation and uniqueness rules are described in §3.10 and apply only to `AdvertisingName` rows. The nominated designated broker must be a licensee whose `person_type` is `BROKER` and whose `license_status` is either `EXAM_PASSED` or `ACTIVE`; anything else is refused with `ERR_DESIGNATED_BROKER_NOT_BROKER`, and the same check is re-run at activation because the broker's standing may have changed in between.

A newly registered brokerage sits in `PENDING` with `affiliated_licensee_count`, `open_shortage_cents`, `open_complaint_count` and `overdue_remediation_count` all at 0 and its `supervision_posture` computed from those zeros. Trust accounts may be opened against a `PENDING` brokerage — indeed they must be, when `trust_account_required` is true — by a `Broker` or a `LicensingAnalyst` who supplies the depository institution as `bank_name` and the `account_type`, which is `ESCROW` for escrow money or `SECURITY_DEPOSIT` for tenant security deposits. A new account opens in `OPEN` status with `book_obligation_cents`, `book_available_cents` and `shortage_cents` all 0 and `last_audited_at` null. Opening an account is guarded by **nothing beyond the role check and the resolution of the brokerage in the path**: the firm's own `status` is never consulted, so an account may equally be opened against a `PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or even terminally `CLOSED` brokerage, and its posture is never examined either, so a `FROZEN` firm may still open one. Nor is a `Broker` required to be affiliated with — let alone the designated broker of — the firm they are opening the account for; any recognised `Broker` may open an account anywhere. Opening an account brings no brokerage into a cascade, so the firm's `version` and `updated_at` are left exactly as they were.

Activation of a `PENDING` brokerage is a `LicensingAnalyst` action carrying an optional `attestation_note`, and it moves the firm `PENDING → ACTIVE`. Unlike the other discarded note fields, the `attestation_note` is not shape-checked even as a string: activation reads no field at all out of the body, so a note supplied as a number, an array or an object is accepted and ignored in silence exactly as a well-formed string would be, and the only way activation can fail rung 3 is for the body itself not to be a JSON object. Two preconditions apply. The designated broker must be `ACTIVE`, or else `EXAM_PASSED` with `person_type` `BROKER` — the ordinary bootstrap case, where a newly examined broker is registering their own firm. And when `trust_account_required` is true, at least one trust account belonging to this brokerage with `account_type` `ESCROW` and `status` `OPEN` must already exist; otherwise activation is refused with `ERR_TRUST_ACCOUNT_REQUIRED`. Activation recomputes `supervision_posture` and advances the brokerage's `version`. It is important to understand what activation does **not** do: it does not issue the designated broker's licence. An `EXAM_PASSED` broker becomes `ACTIVE` only by being affiliated, naming themselves as their own supervising broker, and that affiliation — not the activation — is what first raises `affiliated_licensee_count` above 0.

A brokerage's standing is summarised by the computed `supervision_posture`, one of `COMPLIANT`, `WATCH`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. It is re-derived on every mutation that touches the brokerage, any of its trust accounts or audits, any affiliated licensee's `license_status` or `discipline_status`, any complaint naming the firm, and on every compliance sweep — as well as on every read of the brokerage. The tiers are evaluated **strictly top to bottom and the first matching tier wins**:

- The posture is `FROZEN` when any of three conditions holds: `overdue_remediation_count` is at least 1; or the designated broker's `license_status` is `SUSPENDED`, `REVOKED` or `EXPIRED`; or the brokerage's own `status` is `SUSPENDED`.
- Otherwise the posture is `RESTRICTED` when any of three conditions holds: `open_shortage_cents` is at least 100,000 cents, that is one thousand dollars of unreconciled client money; or the designated broker's `discipline_status` is `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, `PENDING_HEARING` or `SANCTIONED`; or the designated broker's `license_status` is `INACTIVE`.
- Otherwise the posture is `WATCH` when either `open_shortage_cents` is greater than 0 — any shortage at all, however small — or `open_complaint_count` has reached 3.
- Otherwise the posture is `COMPLIANT`.

The shortage boundary is inclusive on the `RESTRICTED` side: a shortage of exactly 100,000 cents grades `RESTRICTED`, while 99,999 cents grades `WATCH`. The four driver counters have precise definitions. `affiliated_licensee_count` counts licensees whose `sponsoring_brokerage_id` is this brokerage **and** whose `license_status` is `ACTIVE` — inactive, suspended and expired affiliates do not count. `open_shortage_cents` sums `shortage_cents` across this brokerage's trust accounts whose `status` is `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED`, ignoring closed accounts. `open_complaint_count` counts complaints naming this brokerage as `respondent_brokerage_id` whose `status` is any of `SUBMITTED`, `INVESTIGATION`, `AMENDMENT_REQUESTED`, `PROBABLE_CAUSE`, `CONSENT_PENDING` or `HEARING_SCHEDULED`. `overdue_remediation_count` counts audits on this brokerage's accounts whose `status` is `REMEDIATION_ORDERED` and whose `remediation_due_at` lies strictly before the current logical instant — note that an audit that has already been escalated is no longer `REMEDIATION_ORDERED` and therefore stops feeding this counter, which is why escalation alone does not keep a firm `FROZEN`. The convenience flag `new_affiliation_blocked` is simply true whenever `supervision_posture` is `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`.

Discipline reaches the brokerage as well as the person. When a sanction order issues against a brokerage's designated broker with a `sanction_type` of `SUSPENSION` or `REVOCATION`, the firm moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, its posture is driven to `FROZEN`, and every advertising name of that brokerage still sitting in `SUBMITTED` is auto-rejected with `rejection_reason` `POSTURE_RESTRICTED`. **The order of what follows is normative.** Only after that suspension has been applied, and only when the order was a revocation *and* the firm's `affiliated_licensee_count` is 0, does the brokerage continue `SUSPENDED → CLOSED` inside the same request; when it still has affiliates, it stops at `SUSPENDED`. On closure, every trust account of the firm whose `shortage_cents` is 0 moves to `CLOSED` and accepts no further ledger entries; an account still carrying a shortage stays open so the money can be traced. A suspended firm can come back: a `LicensingAnalyst` may re-activate it `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` once its designated broker is `ACTIVE` again — merely `EXAM_PASSED` will not do on this path, unlike a first activation — and its `open_shortage_cents` has returned to 0, which is the `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` cycle in the brokerage's life. The zero-shortage requirement is weighed first, as a state error, before the broker's standing is examined. The escrow prerequisite is **not** re-applied here: re-activation succeeds even where `trust_account_required` is true and the firm's only `ESCROW` account has since been closed, because that gate belongs to the `PENDING → ACTIVE` path alone. `CLOSED`, by contrast, is terminal.

### 3.3. Affiliation, Issuance and Licence Transfer

Affiliation is the hinge of the whole licensing model: it is simultaneously how an examined candidate first receives a licence and how an existing licensee moves between firms. A `LicensingAnalyst` performs it by naming the receiving firm as `brokerage_id`, the supervising broker inside that firm as `supervising_broker_id`, and the client's last observed version of the receiving firm as `expected_brokerage_version` (§2.7). Which transition results depends on the licensee's current `license_status`: an `EXAM_PASSED` candidate is issued a licence and advances `EXAM_PASSED → ACTIVE`, while an already-`ACTIVE` licensee performs a transfer and stays `ACTIVE → ACTIVE`, a self-transition that nonetheless rewrites their supervisory chain and touches two brokerages.

Both paths share four checks. The receiving firm's `status` must be `ACTIVE`. Its `supervision_posture` must be neither `RESTRICTED` nor `FROZEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_BROKERAGE_POSTURE_BLOCKED` — supervision integrity ranks above every capacity and eligibility rule that follows it, so a firm under a remediation order cannot absorb new agents no matter how much headroom it has. Its `affiliated_licensee_count`, measured **before** this affiliation is applied, must be strictly below the supervision ratio ceiling of 25 active affiliates per firm, or the request is refused with `ERR_SUPERVISION_RATIO_EXCEEDED`; a firm sitting at exactly 25 is full. And the nominated supervising broker must be eligible.

Supervising-broker eligibility ordinarily requires three things at once: the nominated broker's `person_type` is `BROKER`, their `license_status` is `ACTIVE`, and their own `sponsoring_brokerage_id` equals the `brokerage_id` supplied in the same request. A nominee failing any of those is refused with `ERR_SUPERVISING_BROKER_INELIGIBLE`. There is exactly one exemption, and it is the only way a firm's first broker ever gets seated: when `supervising_broker_id` equals the licensee being affiliated **and** that same licensee is the receiving firm's `designated_broker_id`, the `ACTIVE` requirement and the same-brokerage requirement are both waived. The `person_type` `BROKER` requirement is never waived. This **self-supervision bootstrap** is what lets a freshly `EXAM_PASSED` broker who registered and activated their own firm supervise themselves into `ACTIVE`, at which point the firm has one affiliate and can begin taking on salespeople.

Initial issuance stamps the licence's calendar. On `EXAM_PASSED → ACTIVE` the server records `sponsoring_brokerage_id` as the receiving firm and `supervising_broker_id` as the nominated broker, sets `license_issued_at` to the current logical instant, sets `license_expires_at` to that instant plus a two-year licence term of 730 days, and opens the continuing-education accrual window by setting `ce_period_start_on` to the calendar date of that instant. It then advances the receiving firm's `version` by one and recomputes its `affiliated_licensee_count` and `supervision_posture`.

A transfer is guarded more tightly than an issuance, because a licensee under scrutiny must not be able to walk away from it. Beyond the four shared checks, the supplied `brokerage_id` must differ from the licensee's current `sponsoring_brokerage_id` — a transfer is by definition a move — and naming the firm the licensee already sits in is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. That refusal belongs to rung 9, and the three state facts that rung weighs on this operation are weighed in a fixed order: first the licensee's own `license_status` must be `EXAM_PASSED` or `ACTIVE`, then a transfer must not name the incumbent firm, then the receiving firm's `status` must be `ACTIVE`. All three therefore precede the stale-version comparison, so a request that both quotes a stale `expected_brokerage_version` and re-names the incumbent firm answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, not `ERR_BROKERAGE_VERSION_CONFLICT`. The licensee's `discipline_status` must be neither `PENDING_HEARING` nor `SANCTIONED`, or the transfer is refused with `ERR_DISCIPLINE_HOLD`. And the licensee must not be continuing-education deficient: `ce_deficient` must be false, meaning their `ce_hours_valid` is at least their `ce_hours_required`, which for an `ACTIVE` licensee is the base requirement of 12 hours. A deficient transferor is refused with `ERR_CE_HOURS_INSUFFICIENT` — the same rule and the same error that gate reactivation in §3.4.

The transfer's side effects reach further than the licensee's own row. `sponsoring_brokerage_id` and `supervising_broker_id` are repointed to the receiving firm. Those two pointers are the *only* fields of the licensee a transfer rewrites: the licence calendar is deliberately left alone, so `license_issued_at` keeps the instant of the original issuance, `license_expires_at` keeps the expiry computed from it — a transfer buys no extra term — and `ce_period_start_on` is not reopened, so the hours accrued under the previous firm carry straight across and `ce_hours_valid` is unchanged by the move. `license_status` is rewritten only on the issuance path; on a transfer it is already `ACTIVE` and stays there. Every advertising name of `kind` `TEAM` whose `team_lead_licensee_id` is this licensee and whose `status` is `APPROVED` is revoked — moved to `REVOKED` with `rejection_reason` `TEAM_LEAD_INELIGIBLE` — because a team brand cannot outlive its lead's departure from the firm that registered it. The receiving firm's `version` advances by one, and `affiliated_licensee_count` and `supervision_posture` are recomputed on **both** the firm being left and the firm being joined; forgetting the former leaves the departed firm overstating its supervisory load and, worse, mis-grading its posture. Because both of those figures are derived at read time (§2.8), that recomputation is a matter of what the two firms now report rather than of two rows being rewritten: on an affiliation or a transfer only the receiving firm's row is stamped, so the firm being left answers with its reduced `affiliated_licensee_count` and re-derived `supervision_posture` while keeping the `version` and `updated_at` it already had. (This is peculiar to affiliation and transfer. Where a licensee stops being an active affiliate by parking, expiring, being suspended or being revoked, the firm they were sponsored by *is* itself stamped as part of that cascade.)

### 3.4. Parking a Licence, Continuing Education and the Cost of Coming Back

A licensee may step away from practice and come back, and the commission charges for the privilege in both education and money. Both directions run through a single status-change capability that takes an `action` naming which direction is intended, plus — for a return — the `brokerage_id` and `supervising_broker_id` the licensee will practise under and the `reinstatement_fee_paid_cents` they are tendering. A `Salesperson` or `Broker` may invoke it for themselves only, so an entity-backed actor whose `X-User-ID` is not the licensee named in the path is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; a `LicensingAnalyst` may invoke it on anyone's behalf.

With `action` set to `GO_INACTIVE`, an `ACTIVE` licence is parked and the licensee moves `ACTIVE → INACTIVE`. Their `discipline_status` must not be `PENDING_HEARING` — a respondent awaiting a hearing may not go quiet, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_DISCIPLINE_HOLD`. A broker carries an additional duty: if their `person_type` is `BROKER`, they may not park the licence while they are still the `designated_broker_id` of any brokerage whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, since that would leave a live firm without a broker of record. Parking stamps `inactive_since` with the current logical instant, clears both `sponsoring_brokerage_id` and `supervising_broker_id`, and revokes every `APPROVED` `TEAM` advertising name the licensee fronts, per §3.12. Where the departing licensee was supervising others, every *other* licensee whose `supervising_broker_id` pointed at them is repointed to the designated broker of the brokerage they were sponsored by, so no agent is left unsupervised. Two details of that repointing are normative, because "the brokerage they were sponsored by" could mean either firm. The firm consulted is **the supervisee's own** `sponsoring_brokerage_id`, and only when that does not resolve to a brokerage row — because the supervisee's sponsorship is null, as it is for anyone parked, expired, suspended or revoked — does the rule fall back to the *departing* licensee's own prior `sponsoring_brokerage_id`. Whichever firm is settled on, the supervisee's `supervising_broker_id` becomes that firm's `designated_broker_id` verbatim, with no eligibility test whatever: the replacement need not be `ACTIVE`, need not be sponsored by that firm, and may even be the departing licensee themselves, in which case the supervisee is repointed straight back at the broker who has just gone inactive. When neither firm resolves the supervisee's `supervising_broker_id` is set to null, and when the settled firm has a null `designated_broker_id` the result is null as well — the rule guarantees a rewrite, not a supervisor. Every supervisee visited is rewritten and stamped, so their `version` advances even where the value written is the one already there; the departing licensee's own row is skipped by this loop and handled by the parking itself. The firm the licensee has left has its `affiliated_licensee_count` and `supervision_posture` recomputed.

Expiry is the involuntary form of the same thing. Licences run for a fixed two-year term, and the compliance sweep of §3.9 moves any `ACTIVE` licensee whose `license_expires_at` is at or before the current instant to `ACTIVE → EXPIRED`, stamping `inactive_since`, clearing the sponsoring and supervisory links and revoking the licensee's `APPROVED` `TEAM` names exactly as a voluntary parking does. The symmetry stops one step short, though: expiry does **not** repoint the expiring broker's supervisees. Every licensee whose `supervising_broker_id` pointed at the expired broker keeps pointing at them, so an expiry can leave a firm's agents nominally supervised by someone who no longer holds a licence, where a voluntary parking would have moved them. Nor does expiry check whether the licensee is the designated broker of a live firm — the sweep expires them regardless, which is precisely how a firm ends up `FROZEN`. The consequence for the firm can be severe: when the expiring licensee was the designated broker, the recomputed posture of that firm lands on `FROZEN`.

Coming back — `action` set to `REACTIVATE` — is available from both `INACTIVE` and `EXPIRED`, and lands the licensee back on `ACTIVE`. This is the system's principal cycle: `ACTIVE → INACTIVE → ACTIVE`, repeatable without limit. Five things are checked. The licensee's valid continuing-education hours must be at least their current requirement, or the request is refused with `ERR_CE_HOURS_INSUFFICIENT`. The tendered `reinstatement_fee_paid_cents` must equal the computed assessment **exactly** — neither an underpayment nor an overpayment is accepted, and a mismatch in either direction is refused with `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_FEE_MISMATCH`. The receiving firm's `status` must be `ACTIVE` and its `supervision_posture` must be neither `RESTRICTED` nor `FROZEN`, on pain of `ERR_BROKERAGE_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. Its `affiliated_licensee_count` must be below the ceiling of 25, on pain of `ERR_SUPERVISION_RATIO_EXCEEDED`. And the nominated `supervising_broker_id` must satisfy the same eligibility test as an ordinary affiliation, including the self-supervision bootstrap exemption, on pain of `ERR_SUPERVISING_BROKER_INELIGIBLE`. Unlike affiliation, reactivation takes no version field — the client presents no `expected_brokerage_version` and no stale-version check runs on it.

A successful reactivation moves `license_status` back to `ACTIVE`, sets `sponsoring_brokerage_id` and `supervising_broker_id` from the payload, clears `inactive_since`, re-stamps `license_expires_at` to the current instant plus 730 days, and — this is the part licensees find expensive — re-stamps `ce_period_start_on` to the current calendar date, opening a **fresh** accrual window. Because valid hours are counted only from the window's start, the hours that qualified the licensee for reinstatement do not carry forward: `ce_hours_valid` reads 0 immediately after reactivation, and the licensee begins accruing again for their next renewal. Nothing is deleted or re-flagged to achieve this — the fresh `ce_period_start_on` simply leaves the older completions outside the window — so the one completion that does survive the reset is a qualifying course whose `completed_on` falls on the reactivation date itself or later, which the inclusive boundary described below still counts. What reactivation does **not** rewrite matters as much: `license_issued_at` is left holding the instant of the licensee's *original* issuance and is never re-stamped, so the gap between it and the freshly computed `license_expires_at` is longer than the 730-day term for anyone who has been away, and a licensee's issuance date is a permanent fact of their record. `suspension_ends_at` and the sanction order it belongs to are likewise untouched, as are `exam_attempt_count`, `exam_best_score`, `exam_passed_at`, `prelicense_hours` and `discipline_status`. The receiving firm's `affiliated_licensee_count` and `supervision_posture` are recomputed, and because that firm is in the request's cascade set its `version` advances and its `updated_at` re-stamps.

**How valid hours are counted.** `ce_hours_valid` is the sum of `credit_hours` across that licensee's course completions that satisfy three conditions together: the completion's `credit_status` is `VALID`, its `course_type` is either `CE_CORE` or `CE_ELECTIVE`, and its `completed_on` date falls on or after `ce_period_start_on`. The window's opening date is inclusive — a course completed on the very day of issuance or reactivation counts. When `ce_period_start_on` is null, as it is for anyone who has never held a licence, `ce_hours_valid` is 0. Hours from `PRELICENSE` and `POST_LICENSE` courses never count toward this total.

**How the requirement is set.** `ce_hours_required` depends on the licensee's current `license_status`. While `ACTIVE`, it is the base requirement of 12 hours. While `INACTIVE` or `EXPIRED` **and** `inactive_since` is non-null, it escalates with the length of the absence: take the elapsed time from `inactive_since` to the current logical instant, treating any negative elapsed time as zero so that a logical clock moved back behind `inactive_since` yields no escalation rather than a negative one, divide it by thirty days and truncate downward to get the number of full thirty-day months of absence, divide that count by six and truncate downward again to get the number of completed six-month blocks, add 3 hours for each such block on top of the 12-hour base, and finally cap the result at 36 hours. So an absence of under six thirty-day months still requires 12 hours; six to just under twelve requires 15; twelve to just under eighteen requires 18; and once the escalation would exceed 36 hours the cap holds it there. For any other status — and for an `INACTIVE` or `EXPIRED` licensee whose `inactive_since` happens to be null — the requirement is the 12-hour base. `ce_deficient` is simply true whenever `ce_hours_valid` is less than `ce_hours_required`, and both are re-derived on every read of the licensee.

**How the reinstatement fee is assessed.** The fee has three components and one rounding rule that must be obeyed literally. The base reinstatement fee is 15,000 cents. To that is added a make-up charge of 2,500 cents for every continuing-education hour the licensee's requirement exceeds the 12-hour base — that is, 2,500 cents multiplied by the excess of `ce_hours_required` over 12, using the very same requirement figure computed above. To that is added a late surcharge: count the number of full thirty-day late periods by dividing the elapsed time since `inactive_since` by thirty days and truncating downward — the count is 0 when `inactive_since` is null and 0 again when the elapsed time is not strictly positive, so a rewound clock never yields a negative surcharge — then cap that count at 24 periods; separately compute the surcharge for **one** period as 1.75 percent of the 15,000-cent base, rounded half up to a whole cent, which is 263 cents (the unrounded figure is 262.5); then multiply 263 cents by the capped number of periods. **The rounding happens per period and only then is multiplied by the period count.** Multiplying 262.5 by the period count and rounding the product once at the end produces a different total and is wrong. The three components are summed to give the exact figure the licensee must tender.

### 3.5. Education Provider and Instructor Oversight

Continuing-education hours are only as good as the schools that award them, so the commission approves providers and their instructors and can invalidate credit retroactively when a credential collapses. A `LicensingAnalyst` registers an education provider by supplying its `name` and optionally an `admin_user_id` identifying the single `ProviderAdmin` who will own that provider's rows. The `name` is stored as supplied and, exactly as with a brokerage's registered name, is **not** checked for uniqueness: two providers may be registered under the identical name. The provider starts in `PENDING` with `suspended_instructor_count` and `invalidated_credit_hours` at 0. When `admin_user_id` is left null the provider simply has no `ProviderAdmin` owner, and only a `LicensingAnalyst` can ever write against it — as §2.1 explains, a `ProviderAdmin` request against such a provider can never satisfy the ownership comparison.

Approval moves a provider `PENDING → APPROVED`. The analyst may supply `approval_years`, which defaults to 2 when omitted or supplied as null and must be at least 1 but has no ceiling; the server stamps `approved_at` with the current logical instant and sets `approval_expires_at` to that instant plus `approval_years` multiplied by 365 days. The same capability serves as reinstatement for a provider that has fallen to `PROBATION`, moving it `PROBATION → APPROVED` and re-stamping both `approved_at` and `approval_expires_at` from scratch — but only when the provider has cleaned up: its `suspended_instructor_count` must be back to 0 **and** its `invalidated_credit_hours` must be strictly below 60. A provider still carrying 60 or more invalidated hours cannot be reinstated. Both cleanup failures are reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, the same error and the same rung 9 as an approval attempted from a status that is neither `PENDING` nor `PROBATION`, and they are weighed in that written order — the surviving suspended instructors are checked before the invalidated hours, so a provider failing both answers on the instructor count. Note also that neither gate applies on the `PENDING → APPROVED` path: a brand-new provider is approved without either figure being consulted.

Instructors are enrolled under a provider by a `ProviderAdmin` or a `LicensingAnalyst`, supplying the instructor's `full_name`, their `credential_type` — `ATTORNEY`, `LICENSED_BROKER` or `SUBJECT_EXPERT` — and, optionally, the `licensee_id` of the instructor's own real-estate licence, which is null for instructors who are not licensees. Enrolment requires the owning provider's `status` to be `APPROVED`; a `PENDING`, `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED` provider may neither enrol nor approve instructors, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_APPROVED`. New instructors start `PENDING` with `approved_at` and `suspended_reason` null. Approving one — a `LicensingAnalyst` action carrying an optional `approval_note` — moves it `PENDING → APPROVED` and stamps `approved_at`, and again requires the provider to be `APPROVED`. One credential class carries an extra condition: when `credential_type` is `LICENSED_BROKER`, the instructor's `licensee_id` must be non-null and that licensee must have `person_type` `BROKER` and `license_status` `ACTIVE`. That failure is reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — not as an eligibility error of its own — and it is the last thing approval weighs, after the instructor's own `PENDING` from-state and after the owning provider's `APPROVED` standing, so a `LICENSED_BROKER` instructor with no backing licence sitting under a provider on `PROBATION` answers `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_APPROVED`. Nothing stops such an instructor being *enrolled*: `licensee_id` is optional at enrolment whatever the credential class, and the mismatch only surfaces when approval is attempted. A subject expert or an attorney is approved without any licence linkage, and their `licensee_id` — if they happen to carry one — is not examined.

Completions are reported one row at a time by a `ProviderAdmin` or a `LicensingAnalyst`, naming the student as `licensee_id`, the instructor of record as `instructor_id`, the school as `education_provider_id`, the `course_type` (`PRELICENSE`, `POST_LICENSE`, `CE_CORE` or `CE_ELECTIVE`), the `credit_hours` awarded — at least 1 and at most 12 — and the completion date as `completed_on`. Three referential gates apply in order. The supplied `education_provider_id` must equal the instructor's own `education_provider_id`, or the report is refused with `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_PROVIDER_MISMATCH`; a school cannot claim another school's teacher. The provider's `status` must be `APPROVED` **or** `PROBATION` — this is the one operation a provider on probation may still perform, and only a `PENDING` or `SUSPENDED` provider is barred, with `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_APPROVED`. And the instructor's `status` must be `APPROVED` at the moment of reporting, or the report is refused with `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_NOT_APPROVED`.

A recorded completion starts with `credit_status` `VALID` and `invalidated_at` null. Its effect on the student depends on the course class: `CE_CORE` and `CE_ELECTIVE` hours are added to the student's `ce_hours_valid` when `completed_on` falls on or after that licensee's `ce_period_start_on`, `PRELICENSE` hours are added to the student's `prelicense_hours` — which is how an applicant who is short of the 90- or 180-hour examination threshold gets there — and `POST_LICENSE` hours count toward neither. In every case the student's `ce_deficient` flag is recomputed against their `ce_hours_required`. The two contributions are made by quite different mechanisms, and the difference is visible to a client. A `PRELICENSE` report is the only one that *writes* to the student: it adds the hours to the stored `prelicense_hours` counter and stamps the licensee row, so that licensee's `version` advances by one and its `updated_at` moves to the current instant. A `CE_CORE`, `CE_ELECTIVE` or `POST_LICENSE` report writes nothing to the licensee at all — the continuing-education figures are derived from the completion rows on every read (§2.8), so `ce_hours_valid` and `ce_deficient` answer differently the moment the completion exists while the licensee's `version` and `updated_at` stay exactly where they were. A completion also brings no brokerage into a cascade, so the student's sponsoring firm is never stamped by a completion report however much the report changes that agent's compliance.

Credit can be taken away. When a licensee who teaches is revoked, a cascade runs: every instructor row whose `licensee_id` is that licensee and whose `status` is `PENDING` or `APPROVED` moves to `SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` `LICENSE_REVOKED`; then, for each instructor so suspended, every course completion naming that instructor whose `credit_status` is `VALID` and whose `completed_on` falls on or after the current calendar date minus a 365-day look-back has its `credit_status` flipped to `INVALIDATED` with `invalidated_at` stamped at the current instant; then every student licensee referenced by an invalidated completion has `ce_hours_valid` and `ce_deficient` recomputed, which can silently push a compliant agent into deficiency. Completions older than the look-back window are left alone. An instructor who was only ever `PENDING` has no completions to invalidate, so their suspension stops at the status change. This chain is developed further in §3.9, where the revocation that starts it is issued.

Alongside that instructor cascade, and caused by the same suspensions, every education provider whose `status` is `APPROVED` and whose `suspended_instructor_count` has reached at least 1 moves `APPROVED → PROBATION`. `suspended_instructor_count` counts that provider's instructors whose `status` is `SUSPENDED`; `invalidated_credit_hours` sums `credit_hours` across that provider's completions whose `credit_status` is `INVALIDATED`. A provider on `PROBATION` may keep reporting completions but may not approve new instructors, and it returns to `APPROVED` only through the reinstatement described above — the `APPROVED → PROBATION → APPROVED` cycle. If instead the damage accumulates, the compliance sweep moves a `PROBATION` provider to the terminal `SUSPENDED` state once its `invalidated_credit_hours` reaches 60 or more; that suspension moves every one of its instructors in `PENDING` or `APPROVED` to `SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` `LICENSE_SUSPENDED`, and no further completion may ever be recorded against the provider. There is no declared path back to `APPROVED` for a suspended instructor.

### 3.6. Trust Ledger Stewardship

Client money is the commission's first concern, and the record of it is an **append-only ledger**. A `Broker` or a `LicensingAnalyst` adds one immutable entry at a time, supplying the `entry_type`, an `amount_cents` magnitude, the book date the entry hits the brokerage's own ledger as `effective_date`, optionally the bank clearing date as `cleared_date`, a brokerage-side `transaction_reference`, an optional free-text `memo`, and the `expected_trust_account_version` required by §2.7. The `memo` is the one free-text field in this API that is **not** discarded under §2.4: it is stored verbatim on the entry and returned as a `memo` field on every projection of it, reading `null` when it was omitted. Amounts are always **unsigned magnitudes greater than zero**; the direction of the money is implied entirely by `entry_type`, never by a negative number.

Five entry classes exist and each plays a distinct part in the reconciliation. A `DEPOSIT` brings cash in. A `DISBURSEMENT` takes cash out. An `OBLIGATION_OPENED` records that the brokerage now owes a client a sum out of the account, and an `OBLIGATION_RELEASED` records that the obligation has been discharged. A `CORRECTION` reverses a booking error, and it **always adds to cash** — it is the compensating entry for a mistaken disbursement, which is why a correction must name the `transaction_reference` of an existing `DISBURSEMENT` on the same account; if no such disbursement exists, the append is refused with `ERR_CORRECTION_TARGET_NOT_FOUND`. Because history is never rewritten, a correction is the only way to fix a misposting. References are unique per account **and entry class**: the combination of the owning `trust_account_id`, the `entry_type` and the `transaction_reference` must not already exist, or the append is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TRANSACTION_REFERENCE`. That composite is exactly what lets a correction reuse the reference of the disbursement it reverses while still preventing two identical disbursements from being booked twice under one reference.

Two account-state rules guard appends. An account whose `status` is `CLOSED` accepts nothing at all, and the attempt answers `ERR_TRUST_ACCOUNT_CLOSED`. An account whose `status` is `RESTRICTED` — the condition a remediation order puts it in — accepts everything **except** a `DISBURSEMENT`, which is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_RESTRICTED_NO_DISBURSEMENT`. `DEPOSIT`, `CORRECTION`, `OBLIGATION_OPENED` and `OBLIGATION_RELEASED` all continue to append normally on a restricted account, deliberately: a brokerage under order must still be able to fund the shortage it has been told to cure and to book obligations it has already incurred to clients.

Every accepted append re-derives the account's book position, advances the account's `version`, and then recomputes the owning brokerage's `open_shortage_cents` and `supervision_posture`. One asymmetry is worth stating explicitly: when a deposit cures a shortage and the recomputed posture leaves `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`, advertising names that were already auto-rejected during the earlier cascade stay rejected — the posture improving does not resurrect them.

The account's stored triple is the **book-basis** view of the ledger with no date filter at all, equivalent to reconciling as of the sentinel cut-off date `9999-12-31`. `book_obligation_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over every `OBLIGATION_OPENED` entry of the account less the sum over every `OBLIGATION_RELEASED` entry. `book_available_cents` is the sum over every `DEPOSIT`, less the sum over every `DISBURSEMENT`, plus the sum over every `CORRECTION`. And `shortage_cents` is the amount by which obligations exceed available cash, floored at zero: when available cash is greater than or equal to obligations the shortage is 0, never a negative surplus. All of this is exact integer-cent arithmetic with no rounding, since every input is already an integer.

### 3.7. Audit Reconciliation, Remediation and Escalation

An audit is scheduled against a specific account by a `TrustAuditor` or a `LicensingAnalyst`, who names the auditor who will own it as `auditor_user_id`, the reconciliation `basis` — `BOOK` or `BANK` — the reconciliation cut-off as `as_of_date`, and the on-site window as `fieldwork_start_at` and `fieldwork_end_at`. The window is half-open: the start instant is inclusive, the end instant exclusive. Unlike a hearing slot, a fieldwork window is **not** required to be well ordered: scheduling never compares the two instants against each other, so a `fieldwork_end_at` equal to or earlier than `fieldwork_start_at` is a shape-valid request and is stored exactly as supplied. Scheduling is refused on a `CLOSED` account with `ERR_TRUST_ACCOUNT_CLOSED`, and it computes a shortage figure as a convenience preview that is **not persisted** — nothing is frozen until fieldwork opens. Scheduling writes nothing to the account row either: its `status`, `last_audited_at`, `version` and `updated_at` are all left exactly as they were, so an `expected_trust_account_version` a client is holding survives an audit being booked against that account. A new audit starts in `SCHEDULED` with `remediation_round` 0 and `bank_statement_balance_cents`, `cleared_through_date`, `computed_shortage_cents`, `verified_shortage_cents` and `remediation_due_at` all null.

The `auditor_user_id` the body names is a body string, not the acting user, and it is stored **exactly as supplied** — no trimming, no case folding, and no check that it belongs to a real `TrustAuditor` or indeed to anyone. Every later identity test on it is an exact string comparison against the trimmed acting `X-User-ID` of §2.1, so an `auditor_user_id` recorded with stray surrounding whitespace or the wrong case names an auditor who can never be matched, and the audit is unworkable from that moment on: nobody can open its fieldwork or dispose of it, and it can only ever be left where it stands.

Auditor capacity is checked here, at scheduling time, and it is an **aggregate count rather than a pairwise exclusion**: count the other audits sharing the same `auditor_user_id` whose `status` is any of `SCHEDULED`, `FIELDWORK`, `FINDINGS_ISSUED`, `REMEDIATION_ORDERED` or `VERIFYING` and whose half-open fieldwork window intersects the requested window; that count must be strictly below 2. Two overlapping windows are therefore acceptable and a third overlapping window is refused with `ERR_AUDITOR_WINDOW_CAPACITY`. Windows that merely touch — one ending exactly where the next begins — do not intersect under half-open semantics and do not count.

Opening fieldwork moves the audit `SCHEDULED → FIELDWORK` and may be done only by the user whose `X-User-ID` equals the audit's `auditor_user_id`. The current instant must be at or after `fieldwork_start_at`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_FIELDWORK_NOT_STARTED`. On a `BANK`-basis audit the auditor must supply both the `bank_statement_balance_cents` read off the statement and the `cleared_through_date` the statement runs to; omitting either is refused as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and that one rung-3 failure is weighed **last** on this operation — after the assigned-auditor check of rung 7, after the `SCHEDULED` from-state check of rung 9 and after the fieldwork-window check of rung 45 — because the requirement is conditional on the audit's own `basis` and so cannot be evaluated before the audit has been read. On a `BOOK`-basis audit neither input is required, but neither is discarded either: whichever of the two the auditor supplies anyway is recorded on the audit and echoed on every later read of it, and it is only the shortage arithmetic that pays them no attention. Opening fieldwork freezes `computed_shortage_cents` under the audit's declared basis and stamps `last_audited_at` on the account, which also advances that account's `version` and re-stamps its `updated_at` — the one way an audit reaches the account row before any remediation is ordered.

**The two-basis reconciliation.** The shortage is the amount by which client obligations exceed the cash the basis lets the auditor see, and only the *cash* side of the ledger is basis-sensitive, because an obligation to a client never "clears" a bank. Obligations are gathered identically on both bases: take every `OBLIGATION_OPENED` and `OBLIGATION_RELEASED` entry of the account whose `effective_date` falls on or before `as_of_date`, sum the opened amounts and subtract the released amounts. Cash is gathered differently. On the `BOOK` basis, take every `DEPOSIT`, `DISBURSEMENT` and `CORRECTION` entry whose `effective_date` falls on or before `as_of_date`, regardless of whether it ever cleared the bank. On the `BANK` basis, take only those `DEPOSIT`, `DISBURSEMENT` and `CORRECTION` entries whose `cleared_date` is present — an entry with no `cleared_date` is uncleared and therefore invisible to a bank reconciliation — and whose `cleared_date` falls on or before **the earlier of** `as_of_date` and `cleared_through_date`. Both date comparisons are inclusive of the boundary date, and both are plain calendar-date comparisons of the stored `YYYY-MM-DD` values; no `effective_date` test is applied to cash on the `BANK` basis at all, so an entry booked after `as_of_date` but cleared on or before the bound does count. When `cleared_through_date` is null — as it necessarily is for the unpersisted preview computed at scheduling time, before fieldwork has recorded the statement — the bound is `as_of_date` alone. From whichever cash set the basis produced, sum the deposits, subtract the disbursements and add the corrections. The shortage is then the excess of obligations over that available cash, floored at zero.

The practical consequence is the point of having two bases: the same ledger yields a larger shortage on the `BANK` basis than on the `BOOK` basis whenever a deposit has been booked but not yet cleared, because the book view already lets that deposit offset the obligation while the bank view does not. An auditor choosing `BANK` is deliberately refusing to give the brokerage credit for money the bank has not confirmed.

**Findings and the remediation cycle.** With fieldwork done, the owning auditor issues a disposition, supplying an `action` and optionally `remediation_days` and a `note`. Only the audit's own auditor may act, on pain of `ERR_AUDITOR_MISMATCH`, and every action requires the audit to be in the state that action departs from, on pain of `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

With `action` set to `ISSUE_FINDINGS`, the audit moves `FIELDWORK → FINDINGS_ISSUED` and `computed_shortage_cents` must already be present. What happens next is decided inside the same request and depends on the frozen figure. When the frozen shortage is 0, the audit continues `FINDINGS_ISSUED → CLOSED` immediately: `verified_shortage_cents` is set to 0, `remediation_round` stays 0, `remediation_due_at` stays null, no remediation order exists and no posture anywhere changes. This is the one audit disposition that settles no brokerage at all: the audit row is the only row the request writes, the account is left alone, and the owning brokerage is never brought into the request's cascade set, so its `version` and `updated_at` come out of the request exactly as they went in (contrast §2.8, where every other disposition stamps the firm unconditionally). When the frozen shortage is greater than 0, the audit instead continues `FINDINGS_ISSUED → REMEDIATION_ORDERED` immediately, and a four-layer cascade settles before the response is written, **in this order**. First, `remediation_round` is set to 1 and `remediation_due_at` is set to the current instant plus the remediation period — three days by default, or the supplied `remediation_days` count — expressed as that many multiples of 86,400 seconds. Second, the account moves `OPEN → RESTRICTED`, barring further disbursements. Third, the owning brokerage's `open_shortage_cents` is recomputed and its `supervision_posture` re-graded, landing on `RESTRICTED` when the accumulated open shortage has reached 100,000 cents and otherwise on `WATCH`. Fourth, and only when the firm has **newly entered** the restricted band, every advertising name of that brokerage still in `SUBMITTED` is auto-rejected with `rejection_reason` `POSTURE_RESTRICTED` and `reviewed_by_user_id` left null, since no human reviewed it. "Newly entered" is the whole of the condition and it is measured against a snapshot of the posture taken **before** any of this request's writes landed: the layer fires when the re-graded posture is `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` *and* the pre-request posture was neither. A firm that was already `RESTRICTED` when the request arrived, and is still `RESTRICTED` or has worsened to `FROZEN`, does **not** re-run the auto-rejection, so a name submitted while a firm sits under an existing order survives in `SUBMITTED` until a human reviews it — and a name submitted between two orders against an already-restricted firm is likewise left alone. This entering rule governs every posture settlement in the specification, not just this cascade; the one auto-rejection that runs unconditionally is the direct `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` firm suspension of §3.2, which rejects the firm's submitted names as part of the suspension itself rather than as a consequence of the re-grade.

With `action` set to `VERIFY_REMEDIATION`, the audit moves `REMEDIATION_ORDERED → VERIFYING` and the shortage is recomputed from scratch into `verified_shortage_cents`, under this audit's own basis and against **the very same reconciliation bounds it was originally frozen with** — its stored `as_of_date` and, on the `BANK` basis, its stored `cleared_through_date`. The recomputation re-reads the ledger as it now stands but it does **not** advance the cut-off to the current date, so a deposit booked to cure the shortage is only seen if its `effective_date` falls on or before `as_of_date` (and, on the `BANK` basis, only if it also carries a `cleared_date` at or before the bound). A brokerage that cures the money with an entry dated after the cut-off verifies at the same shortage it started with. The outcome again settles in the same request. A verified shortage of 0 continues `VERIFYING → CLOSED`; the account returns `RESTRICTED → OPEN` provided its own `shortage_cents` is 0 and no other audit on that account is sitting in `REMEDIATION_ORDERED` or `ESCALATED`, and the brokerage's `open_shortage_cents` and `supervision_posture` are recomputed. A verified shortage still greater than 0 continues `VERIFYING → REMEDIATION_ORDERED`, increments `remediation_round` by one and re-arms `remediation_due_at` to the current instant plus another three days. This is the `REMEDIATION_ORDERED → VERIFYING → REMEDIATION_ORDERED` cycle: each failed verification restarts the clock rather than escalating automatically.

With `action` set to `ESCALATE`, the audit moves `REMEDIATION_ORDERED → ESCALATED`. By hand this is available only to the owning auditor and only once `remediation_round` has reached 2 or more, so escalating a first-round remediation order manually is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the compliance sweep escalates automatically, without any round requirement, once `remediation_due_at` has fallen strictly behind the clock. Escalation converts an accounting failure into a disciplinary matter through a three-layer cascade. A complaint is opened with `allegation_type` `TRUST_SHORTAGE` and `status` `INVESTIGATION` — skipping intake screening entirely — with `source_audit_id` pointing back at this audit, `respondent_licensee_id` set to the owning brokerage's designated broker, `respondent_brokerage_id` set to that brokerage, `alleged_loss_cents` set to `verified_shortage_cents` or, where verification never ran, to `computed_shortage_cents`, `investigator_user_id` set to the escalating auditor's `auditor_user_id` or to the literal value `SYSTEM_SWEEP` when the sweep raised it, and `filed_at` stamped at the current instant. The escalation complaint's remaining fields are fixed rather than supplied. Its `complainant_name` is the literal string `Trust Audit Escalation` — the commission is complaining of itself, so no human complainant is recorded. It draws a `case_number` from the very same global `CMP-` counter that publicly filed complaints use, so audit escalations and public filings interleave in one numbering sequence. Its `statute_deadline_at` is set to 180 days past the same instant as `filed_at`, exactly as at ordinary intake, so escalation does not shorten or lengthen the limitation period. Its `screener_user_id` is null and stays null — escalation skips screening, so no screener is ever recorded even though the case arrives already in `INVESTIGATION` — and its `amendment_count` starts at 0. And `alleged_loss_cents` falls back once more when neither shortage figure has been frozen: `verified_shortage_cents` is used when it is present *even if it is 0*, otherwise `computed_shortage_cents`, and where both are null the figure recorded is **0** rather than null, since the field never holds null. The respondent's `discipline_status` then moves from `NONE` or `CLEARED` to `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`; where the escalating audit's brokerage has no designated broker to name, `respondent_licensee_id` is recorded as null and this layer simply does nothing, and the complaint still opens. Finally the brokerage's `supervision_posture` is recomputed, and here the tiers interact in a way worth noticing: the escalated audit no longer counts as an overdue remediation, so the `FROZEN` driver disappears, but the designated broker being `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` pins the posture at `RESTRICTED` regardless.

With `action` set to `CLOSE`, an `ESCALATED` audit moves `ESCALATED → CLOSED`, but only once the complaint it spawned has itself reached `ADJUDICATED`, `DISMISSED` or `CLOSED_NO_ACTION`. Closure recomputes `verified_shortage_cents` under the audit's basis one last time, returns the account `RESTRICTED → OPEN` if its `shortage_cents` is 0, and recomputes the brokerage's posture. Separately from any audit, an `OPEN` account moves `OPEN → CLOSED` when its owning brokerage closes and its `shortage_cents` is 0, after which it accepts no further entries.

### 3.8. Complaint Intake, Screening and Case Prioritisation

Anyone may complain. A member of the `Public`, a `LicensingAnalyst` or a `CommissionCounsel` files a complaint by naming the person or entity filing as `complainant_name`, the licensee complained of as `respondent_licensee_id`, the statutory `allegation_type` — one of `MISREPRESENTATION`, `AGENCY_DISCLOSURE`, `COMMISSION_DISPUTE`, `TRUST_SHORTAGE`, `ADVERTISING` or `UNLICENSED_ACTIVITY` — the monetary loss alleged as `alleged_loss_cents`, which may be 0 but never negative, and optionally a free-text `narrative`.

Intake mints a `case_number` of the form `CMP-` followed by a six-digit zero-padded counter that is **global across all complaints** and incremented on every create, so the first complaint ever filed is `CMP-000001`. The complaint opens in `SUBMITTED` with `filed_at` stamped at the current logical instant and `statute_deadline_at` set to that instant plus a 180-day limitation period, expressed as 180 multiples of 86,400 seconds. `respondent_brokerage_id` is a **snapshot** taken at intake of the respondent's then-current `sponsoring_brokerage_id`, and it is null when the respondent was unaffiliated at that moment — later transfers do not move the complaint. `source_audit_id` is null for publicly filed complaints and non-null only for complaints raised by audit escalation. `amendment_count` starts at 0, `screener_user_id` and `investigator_user_id` are both null until a disposition is taken, and `priority_score` is computed under the intake variant. The respondent's brokerage, if any, has its `open_complaint_count` and `supervision_posture` recomputed at once, which is how a third open complaint alone can push a firm to `WATCH`.

**Priority scoring** decides which cases the commission may pursue, and the same complaint row scores differently before and after screening even though nothing about the complaint itself has changed. Four terms make up the score. The **base** is a fixed weight for the allegation class: 60 points for `UNLICENSED_ACTIVITY`, 50 for `TRUST_SHORTAGE`, 40 for `MISREPRESENTATION`, 35 for `AGENCY_DISCLOSURE`, 20 for `COMMISSION_DISPUTE` and 15 for `ADVERTISING`. The **harm** term awards one point for every full one thousand dollars of alleged loss — divide `alleged_loss_cents` by 100,000 and truncate downward — and is capped at 30 points, so a loss of $999 earns nothing, a loss of $1,000 earns one point, and any loss of $30,000 or more earns the maximum 30. The **prior** term awards 5 points for each entry in the respondent's `prior_sanction_count` and is capped at 20 points, so a fourth or later prior order adds nothing further. The **posture bump** is 15 points when the respondent's brokerage has a `supervision_posture` of `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`, 5 points when it is `WATCH`, and 0 when it is `COMPLIANT` — and also 0 when `respondent_brokerage_id` is null, since there is no firm to grade.

Which terms count depends on the complaint's `status`. While the complaint is still `SUBMITTED`, the **intake variant** applies: the score is the base plus the harm term, capped at 100 points in total, with the prior and posture terms omitted entirely because the commission has not yet verified the respondent's history. Once the complaint's `status` is anything other than `SUBMITTED`, the **investigation variant** applies: base plus harm plus prior plus posture bump, again capped at 100 in total. The cap is applied after the terms are summed, and each individual cap — 30 on harm, 20 on prior — is applied to its own term first. `prior_sanction_count` itself counts the sanction orders naming this licensee as respondent whose `status` is `ISSUED`, `IN_DEFAULT` or `SATISFIED`; a `VACATED` order is excluded, so a successful reconsideration genuinely lowers the respondent's future scores.

**Screening and investigation** are the exclusive province of a `CommissionCounsel`, who supplies a `disposition` naming the decision, optionally an `investigator_user_id`, and optionally a `note`. The body's `investigator_user_id` is validated as a string where §2.11's shape rung requires it and is then **ignored** exactly as the `note` is: where a disposition records an investigator at all it records the acting counsel's own `X-User-ID`, never the identifier the body offered, so counsel cannot delegate a case to a third party through this field. Whichever disposition is taken, the request's cascade set holds both the complaint's snapshot `respondent_brokerage_id` and the respondent's *current* `sponsoring_brokerage_id`, which are two different firms once the respondent has transferred since intake; both are therefore stamped and re-graded.

- `INVESTIGATE` moves the case `SUBMITTED → INVESTIGATION`. The respondent's `license_status` must not be `DENIED`; a complaint against an applicant who never got a licence has nowhere to go and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The server records `screener_user_id` and `investigator_user_id` as the acting counsel's `X-User-ID`, and the score silently switches to the investigation variant. The respondent's `discipline_status` moves from `NONE` or `CLEARED` to `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, and the sponsoring brokerage's `open_complaint_count` and `supervision_posture` are recomputed — with the designated broker now under investigation, that firm's posture is pinned at `RESTRICTED`.
- `DISMISS` moves the case `SUBMITTED → DISMISSED` and records `screener_user_id`. The respondent's `discipline_status` returns `UNDER_INVESTIGATION → CLEARED`, but **only when no other open complaint against them remains** — that is, when the count of other complaints naming this respondent whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `INVESTIGATION`, `AMENDMENT_REQUESTED`, `PROBABLE_CAUSE`, `CONSENT_PENDING` or `HEARING_SCHEDULED` is zero. The brokerage's counter and posture are recomputed.
- `REQUEST_AMENDMENT` moves the case `INVESTIGATION → AMENDMENT_REQUESTED`, pausing the investigation; probable cause is unreachable until the complainant responds. It is available only while `amendment_count` is below 2, and a third request is refused with `ERR_AMENDMENT_LIMIT`.
- `PROBABLE_CAUSE` moves the case `INVESTIGATION → PROBABLE_CAUSE` and opens both resolution channels. Two gates apply: the current instant must be strictly before `statute_deadline_at`, and a request at or after that instant is refused with `ERR_STATUTE_DEADLINE_PASSED`; and the investigation-variant `priority_score` must be at least 40, with anything lower refused as `ERR_PROBABLE_CAUSE_SCORE_TOO_LOW`. A score of exactly 40 passes.
- `CLOSE_NO_ACTION` moves the case `INVESTIGATION → CLOSED_NO_ACTION`, clears the respondent's `discipline_status` back to `CLEARED` on the same "no other open complaint" condition, and recomputes the brokerage's counter and posture. The clearing here is **wider** than the one a dismissal performs: closing without action clears a respondent sitting in either `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` or `PENDING_HEARING`, whereas a dismissal clears only from `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` and leaves a respondent already committed to a hearing exactly where they are. A respondent in `NONE`, `CLEARED` or `SANCTIONED` is untouched by either, since neither is a declared origin — a sanctioned licensee is cleared only by vacatur.

When an amendment has been requested, the complainant — a member of the `Public`, or a `LicensingAnalyst` acting for them — supplies it as an `amendment_note` plus, optionally, a revised loss figure as `amended_alleged_loss_cents`. This returns the case `AMENDMENT_REQUESTED → INVESTIGATION`, increments `amendment_count` by one, overwrites `alleged_loss_cents` with the amended value when one was supplied, and re-scores the case under the investigation variant — which is precisely how a complainant can lift a borderline case over the probable-cause floor. Amendments are also barred at or after `statute_deadline_at`, with the same `ERR_STATUTE_DEADLINE_PASSED`. The `INVESTIGATION → AMENDMENT_REQUESTED → INVESTIGATION` loop may run at most twice.

**The discipline dimension.** A licensee's `discipline_status` evolves entirely independently of their `license_status`, driven by the complaints and orders against them. From `NONE` or `CLEARED` it moves to `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` whenever any complaint against them reaches `INVESTIGATION`, whether by counsel's screening or by audit escalation — and the `CLEARED → UNDER_INVESTIGATION` step means the cycle can repeat indefinitely as new complaints arrive. From `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` it moves to `PENDING_HEARING` when a complaint against them reaches `HEARING_SCHEDULED`, and a licensee in `PENDING_HEARING` is barred from transferring. It moves to `SANCTIONED` when a sanction order against them issues, from `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` by the consent channel and from `PENDING_HEARING` by the hearing channel. It returns from `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` to `CLEARED` when a complaint is dismissed or closed without action and no other open complaint remains, and from `SANCTIONED` to `CLEARED` when the last non-vacated order against them is vacated — precisely, when the count of orders naming them whose `status` is `ISSUED`, `IN_DEFAULT` or `SATISFIED` reaches zero. Every one of these movements recomputes the sponsoring brokerage's `supervision_posture`, because the designated broker's discipline standing is one of that posture's drivers.

### 3.9. Two Roads From Probable Cause: Consent Negotiation and the Formal Hearing

A complaint in `PROBABLE_CAUSE` may be settled by agreement or tried before a panel, and the commission must pick one at a time. Both channels are opened by a `CommissionCounsel`, and both are guarded by the same exclusivity rule: neither may be opened while the complaint already has a consent agreement in `OFFERED`, `COUNTERED` or `ACCEPTED`, or a hearing in `SCHEDULED`, `CONVENED` or `CONTINUED`. A second attempt is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_OPEN` — and, as §2.11 explains, that exclusivity answer is preferred over the generic from-state answer even though the complaint has by then left `PROBABLE_CAUSE`. When a channel closes without an adjudication the complaint returns to `PROBABLE_CAUSE` and both roads reopen.

**The consent channel** is a bounded haggle. Counsel opens it by proposing a `proposed_sanction_type` — one of `REPRIMAND`, `FINE`, `PROBATION`, `SUSPENSION` or `REVOCATION` — with a `proposed_fine_cents` and `proposed_suspension_days`, each of which may be 0 but never negative. The agreement is created in `OFFERED` with `round_number` 1, `offered_by_user_id` stamped with the acting counsel's `X-User-ID`, and `counter_fine_cents`, `counter_suspension_days`, `responded_by_user_id` and `executed_by_user_id` all null. The complaint moves `PROBABLE_CAUSE → CONSENT_PENDING`.

Only the respondent may answer, and the server proves it by requiring the caller's `X-User-ID` to equal the owning complaint's `respondent_licensee_id`; any other licensee is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403) — the entity-backed ownership error of §2.1 and rung 6 of §2.11, since `ERR_RESPONDENT_MISMATCH` is not an error identifier this API defines. The respondent supplies an `action` of either `ACCEPT` or `COUNTER`, and a counter may carry `counter_fine_cents` and `counter_suspension_days`.

- `ACCEPT` moves the agreement `OFFERED → ACCEPTED` and records `responded_by_user_id`.
- `COUNTER` moves it `OFFERED → COUNTERED`, records `responded_by_user_id`, stores the two counter figures — writing `null` for either one the respondent omitted, so a bare `COUNTER` naming no numbers at all still lands in `COUNTERED` with both counter fields null — and **leaves `round_number` untouched**. A counter never ends the negotiation by itself. In particular, a counter offered while `round_number` is already at the ceiling of 3 still lands in `COUNTERED` and is perfectly legal; the ceiling is felt only on counsel's next move.

Counsel then supplies an `action` of `REVISE`, `WITHDRAW` or `EXECUTE`, with `proposed_fine_cents` and `proposed_suspension_days` both **required** to accompany a revision and `proposed_sanction_type` optional on it.

- `REVISE` moves the agreement `COUNTERED → OFFERED`, increments `round_number` by one, overwrites `proposed_fine_cents` and `proposed_suspension_days` with the revised figures, overwrites `proposed_sanction_type` **only when the revision actually supplies one** — omitting it leaves the sanction class of the previous round standing, so a revision can move the money without reopening the question of what the sanction is — and **resets `counter_fine_cents` and `counter_suspension_days` back to null** so the record never shows a stale counter against a fresh offer. Revision is permitted only while `round_number` is strictly below 3; once `round_number` has reached 3 the cycle is closed and a further revision is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_ROUND_LIMIT`.
- `WITHDRAW` moves the agreement `COUNTERED → REJECTED` and returns the complaint `CONSENT_PENDING → PROBABLE_CAUSE`, which reopens the hearing route. It is the only exit from a negotiation that has exhausted its rounds: at `round_number` 3 in `COUNTERED`, revision is barred and withdrawal is counsel's sole legal move.
- `EXECUTE` moves the agreement `ACCEPTED → EXECUTED` and records `executed_by_user_id`. It **may not be performed by the counsel who made the offer**: the caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from `offered_by_user_id`, and a self-execution is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` even though the caller holds exactly the right role.

Execution issues the settlement. A sanction order is created with `source` `CONSENT` and `status` `ISSUED`, its `sanction_type`, `fine_cents` and `suspension_days` copied from the agreement's three `proposed_*` fields, its `effective_at` stamped at the current logical instant, and its `fine_due_at` set to that instant plus 30 days when `fine_cents` is greater than 0 and left null otherwise. Because that order now exists, the complaint moves `CONSENT_PENDING → ADJUDICATED` and the respondent's `discipline_status` moves to `SANCTIONED`. Because the respondent is now sanctioned, the licence consequence follows: a `SUSPENSION` moves an `ACTIVE` licence to `SUSPENDED`, and a `REVOCATION` moves it to `REVOKED`, dragging behind it the full instructor-suspension and credit-invalidation chain described in §3.5. A `REPRIMAND`, `FINE` or `PROBATION` leaves `license_status` alone. Each consequence writes a precise set of fields. A suspension sets `suspension_ends_at` to the **order's own `effective_at`** — not to the instant the licence happens to be suspended — advanced by `suspension_days` multiples of 86,400 seconds, so a `suspension_days` of 0 yields a `suspension_ends_at` equal to `effective_at` and a suspension that the very next sweep may serve out; it also records, invisibly to the client, which order drove the suspension, and that is the order §3.13's restoration step later insists has reached `SATISFIED`. A revocation clears `sponsoring_brokerage_id`, `supervising_broker_id`, `suspension_ends_at` and the driving-order record all to null, so a revoked licensee carries no firm, no supervisor and no suspension end. Both consequences additionally revoke the licensee's `TEAM` advertising names exactly as a transfer does, since a suspended or revoked lead is no longer eligible to front a team. Each consequence is conditional on where the licence stands: a suspension is applied **only to an `ACTIVE` licence**, so an order against a licensee already `INACTIVE`, `EXPIRED`, `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED` leaves `license_status` and `suspension_ends_at` untouched and never re-arms an existing suspension, and a revocation is applied to a licence in any status **except** one already `REVOKED`. The firm-side cascade of §3.2 runs on either sanction regardless of whether the licence consequence itself applied, so an order against an already-inactive designated broker still suspends, and where the order was a revocation may still close, the firm they front. The brokerage's `supervision_posture` is recomputed at the end.

**The hearing channel** treats two things as bookable resources: the room and the person presiding. Counsel schedules a hearing by naming a `panel_chair_user_id`, a `hearing_room`, and a half-open slot from `scheduled_start_at` inclusive to `scheduled_end_at` exclusive, where the end must be strictly after the start. The chair may not be the complaint's `investigator_user_id` — the officer who built the case may not judge it — and such an attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

The two occupancy rules deliberately differ, and the difference matters at the boundary.

- **Rooms carry a 30-minute turnover buffer.** For every other hearing in the same `hearing_room` whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `CONVENED` or `CONTINUED`, the new slot must either begin at or after that hearing's `scheduled_end_at` plus 30 minutes, or end at least 30 minutes before that hearing's `scheduled_start_at`. Back-to-back booking with a gap of exactly zero is therefore a **conflict**, while a gap of exactly 30 minutes is permitted. A violation answers `ERR_HEARING_ROOM_CONFLICT`.
- **People carry no buffer.** For every other hearing with the same `panel_chair_user_id` whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `CONVENED` or `CONTINUED`, the half-open intervals from start inclusive to end exclusive must simply not intersect. A new hearing that begins at the exact instant the chair's previous hearing ends is therefore **permitted**. A genuine overlap answers `ERR_PANEL_CHAIR_DOUBLE_BOOKED`. The same room and chair may thus accept a slot under one rule and reject it under the other, and both checks are run against the same candidate window.

Both `hearing_room` and `panel_chair_user_id` are matched as **exact, case-sensitive strings with no trimming or normalisation of any kind**, exactly as the scheduling body supplied them. `Room A`, `room a` and `Room A ` are therefore three distinct rooms that never collide with one another, and a chair identifier recorded with stray surrounding whitespace never collides with the same chair recorded without it. Because the acting `X-User-ID` is trimmed on the way in (§2.1) while the body-supplied `panel_chair_user_id` is not, a hearing scheduled for a chair whose id carries surrounding whitespace can never be disposed of by anybody: every `CONVENE`, `CONTINUE` and `DECIDE` attempt fails the identity test with `ERR_PANEL_CHAIR_MISMATCH`, and the hearing occupies its room and blocks the untrimmed chair's calendar indefinitely.

Scheduling creates the hearing in `SCHEDULED` with `continuance_count` 0 and `convened_at`, `decided_at` and `finding` all null, moves the complaint `PROBABLE_CAUSE → HEARING_SCHEDULED`, moves the respondent's `discipline_status` `UNDER_INVESTIGATION → PENDING_HEARING` — which is what bars them from transferring brokerages — and recomputes the brokerage posture.

Thereafter only the presiding chair may act, proved by requiring the caller's `X-User-ID` to equal `panel_chair_user_id`; anyone else, including another `HearingPanelChair`, is refused with `ERR_PANEL_CHAIR_MISMATCH`. The chair supplies an `action` of `CONVENE`, `CONTINUE` or `DECIDE`.

- `CONVENE` moves the hearing `SCHEDULED → CONVENED` and stamps `convened_at` with the current logical instant. It may not be taken early: the clock must have reached `scheduled_start_at`, and an attempt before then is refused with `ERR_HEARING_NOT_STARTED`. Convening at exactly `scheduled_start_at` is allowed.
- `CONTINUE` postpones a convened hearing. The chair supplies a new window as `rescheduled_start_at` and `rescheduled_end_at` and optionally a new `hearing_room`. It is permitted only while `continuance_count` is strictly below 2, and a third continuance is refused with `ERR_CONTINUANCE_LIMIT`. The new window is re-checked against **both** occupancy rules, against every other hearing in `SCHEDULED`, `CONVENED` or `CONTINUED` — the hearing being continued is not its own competitor. On success `continuance_count` is incremented by one, `scheduled_start_at`, `scheduled_end_at` and `hearing_room` are overwritten with the new slot, and `convened_at` is reset to null. The hearing passes momentarily through `CONTINUED` and, within the same request, returns to `SCHEDULED` at its new slot, where it once again occupies the room and the chair's calendar. The observable end state of a granted continuance is therefore `SCHEDULED`, and the cycle `SCHEDULED → CONVENED → CONTINUED → SCHEDULED` may run at most twice.
- `DECIDE` moves the hearing `CONVENED → DECIDED`, stamps `decided_at` and records the `finding`, which must be supplied. When the `finding` is `VIOLATION_FOUND` a `sanction_type` must also be supplied, with optional `fine_cents` and `suspension_days`; a decision that omits the required accompaniment is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A finding of `VIOLATION_FOUND` creates a sanction order with `source` `HEARING`, `status` `ISSUED` and `effective_at` at the current instant, moves the complaint `HEARING_SCHEDULED → ADJUDICATED`, moves the respondent's `discipline_status` `PENDING_HEARING → SANCTIONED`, applies the same `license_status` consequence and the same revocation cascade as the consent channel, and recomputes the brokerage posture. A finding of `NO_VIOLATION` creates no order at all: the complaint moves `HEARING_SCHEDULED → CLOSED_NO_ACTION` and the respondent's `discipline_status` returns to `CLEARED`, subject to the same requirement that no other open complaint against them remains.

### 3.10. Sanction Orders, Fine Collection and Vacatur

A sanction order is never created directly by a client; it is always the product of an executed consent agreement or a hearing decision. It records the `complaint_id` and the `respondent_licensee_id` copied from that complaint, exactly one of `consent_agreement_id` or `hearing_id` — the other is null — a `source` of `CONSENT` or `HEARING` accordingly, the `sanction_type`, the ordered `fine_cents` and `suspension_days`, the `effective_at` instant, the derived `fine_due_at`, a `fine_paid_cents` and `fund_credited_cents` both starting at 0, a `status` of `ISSUED`, and a null `vacated_at`.

**Paying the fine.** The respondent, or a `LicensingAnalyst` acting at the counter, records a payment as an `amount_cents` — strictly positive — together with the calendar date of tender as `paid_on`. The `paid_on` date is required and must parse, but nothing else is done with it: no payment row is persisted anywhere, the date is discarded once validated, and the response to a payment is the updated `SanctionOrder` itself, whose running `fine_paid_cents` is the only record the system keeps of what was tendered. Payments are accepted against an order in `ISSUED` or `IN_DEFAULT` — an order already in `VACATED` answers `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_VACATED` rather than the generic state error, and a `SATISFIED` order answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and may never exceed the outstanding balance: an `amount_cents` greater than `fine_cents` minus `fine_paid_cents` is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`. A payment of exactly the outstanding balance is accepted. Each accepted payment increases `fine_paid_cents` and `fund_credited_cents` by the amount, and because **the whole of every fine payment is credited to the recovery fund**, it also increases the fund's `fine_credits_cents` and `balance_cents` by the same amount and bumps the fund's `version` by one. The order becomes `SATISFIED` only once `fine_paid_cents` has reached `fine_cents`; a partial payment leaves the order in whatever state it was already in, so a partially paid order in `IN_DEFAULT` stays in `IN_DEFAULT`. A late payment that completes the balance cures the default outright, giving the cycle `ISSUED → IN_DEFAULT → SATISFIED`.

**Falling into default** is never a client action. The compliance sweep of §3.13 moves an order `ISSUED → IN_DEFAULT` when its `fine_cents` is greater than 0, its `fine_paid_cents` is still short of that figure, and its `fine_due_at` lies strictly before the current instant. Default carries a licence consequence: the respondent moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with a `suspension_ends_at` set 90 days ahead of the sweep instant, the defaulted order is recorded as the driving order that §3.13's restoration step will later insist has reached `SATISFIED`, every `APPROVED` `TEAM` advertising name the respondent fronts is revoked with `rejection_reason` `TEAM_LEAD_INELIGIBLE` exactly as any other loss of `ACTIVE` standing does, and the sponsoring brokerage's posture is recomputed. The consequence stops at the person: unlike the suspension a sanction order itself imposes, a default suspension runs **no firm cascade**, so a brokerage whose designated broker the sweep has just suspended is not itself moved `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and has no submitted advertising names rejected on that ground — though its re-derived posture lands on `FROZEN` regardless, because its designated broker is now `SUSPENDED`. A respondent who is anything other than `ACTIVE` when the sweep defaults their order takes no licence consequence at all, and no brokerage enters the sweep's cascade set on their account.

**Vacatur** is the hardest single operation in the system, because it must unwind the fiscal and personal consequences the order had. A `HearingPanelChair` vacates an order in `ISSUED`, `IN_DEFAULT` or `SATISFIED` by supplying a `vacatur_reason`; an order already in `VACATED` answers `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_VACATED`, and any other state answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. For an order whose `source` is `HEARING`, the acting chair's `X-User-ID` must differ from that hearing's `panel_chair_user_id` — a chair may not vacate their own order — and a self-vacatur is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; an order whose `source` is `CONSENT` has no hearing behind it and is therefore subject to no segregation check at all, so any `HearingPanelChair` may vacate a consent order. The order moves to `VACATED`, `vacated_at` is stamped, and five compensations run **in this order**.

1. **The fine credits are reversed.** The fund's `fine_credits_cents` and `balance_cents` are each reduced by the order's `fund_credited_cents`, and then the order's `fund_credited_cents` and `fine_paid_cents` are both reset to 0. The fund's `version` is bumped.
2. **Paid claims are clawed back.** Every recovery fund claim against this order whose `status` is `PARTIALLY_PAID` or `PAID` moves to `CLAWED_BACK`; for each one the fund's `clawed_back_cents` and `balance_cents` are increased by that claim's `awarded_cents`, and the claim's `awarded_cents` is then reset to 0. Money returns to the fund through the dedicated clawback counter rather than by decrementing `awarded_cents`, so the cumulative award history remains visible. A clawed-back claim keeps the `paid_at` stamp it earned and keeps its `denial_reason` null — `CLAWED_BACK` is a reversal, not a denial, and the date it was once paid stays on the record.
3. **Queued claims are denied.** Every claim against this order still in `ELIGIBLE` moves to `DENIED` with `denial_reason` `ORDER_VACATED`.
4. **The respondent is restored.** A respondent in `SUSPENDED` returns to `ACTIVE` with `suspension_ends_at` cleared to null; a respondent in `REVOKED` moves to `INACTIVE` with `inactive_since` stamped at the vacatur instant — vacatur restores the person's standing but not their active practice rights, so a revoked licensee must still reactivate through the §3.4 path, paying the reinstatement fee that their time in limbo has accrued. The `discipline_status` returns `SANCTIONED → CLEARED` only when no other non-vacated order against them remains.
5. **The derived figures are recomputed.** The respondent's `prior_sanction_count` and `recovery_paid_cents` are recomputed — a vacated order counts toward neither, and clawed-back claims contribute nothing to `recovery_paid_cents` — and the sponsoring brokerage's `supervision_posture` is recomputed last. The firms settled here are the respondent's `sponsoring_brokerage_id` as it stood when the request arrived, together with the complaint's snapshot `respondent_brokerage_id`; for a respondent the order had revoked the former is already null, so only the complaint's firm is stamped.

What vacatur leaves standing is as fixed as what it undoes, and the five compensations above are the whole of it. The complaint the order resolved stays in `ADJUDICATED`: vacatur never reopens the case, and no new consent or hearing channel becomes available on it. A brokerage that the order's §3.2 firm cascade moved to `SUSPENDED`, or on a revocation closed outright, keeps that `status` — only its derived counters and posture are re-read — and the advertising names that cascade rejected stay `REJECTED`. The instructor rows a revocation suspended stay `SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` `LICENSE_REVOKED`, the course completions that chain invalidated stay `INVALIDATED` with their `invalidated_at` intact, and a provider demoted to `PROBATION` behind them stays on `PROBATION`, so the continuing-education hours a wrongly revoked instructor's students lost are not restored. A respondent moved from `REVOKED` to `INACTIVE` gets neither their `sponsoring_brokerage_id` nor their `supervising_broker_id` back, and the `TEAM` advertising names they lost are not reinstated.

### 3.11. The Recovery Fund: Claims, Caps and the Award Waterfall

The fund is a single row created at startup with `balance_cents`, `appropriated_cents`, `fine_credits_cents`, `awarded_cents`, `assessment_cents` and `clawed_back_cents` all at 0, and it obeys one invariant at all times: `balance_cents` equals `appropriated_cents` plus `fine_credits_cents` plus `clawed_back_cents`, minus `awarded_cents` and `assessment_cents`. A `RecoveryFundTrustee` may credit money in by recording an `amount_cents` and an optional `appropriation_note`, which raises both `balance_cents` and `appropriated_cents` and bumps `version`.

**Filing a claim.** A member of the `Public`, or a trustee filing on their behalf, names the `claimant_name` and the `claimed_loss_cents`, which must be strictly greater than zero, against a specific order. Filing stamps `filed_at`, sets `awarded_cents` to 0, and **evaluates eligibility inside the same request**, so `FILED` is never an observable resting state. The claim becomes `ELIGIBLE` when the order's `status` is `ISSUED`, `IN_DEFAULT` or `SATISFIED`, the order's `sanction_type` is `FINE`, `SUSPENSION` or `REVOCATION`, and the current instant is strictly before `effective_at` plus a 365-day claim window. Otherwise it becomes `DENIED` with a `denial_reason` naming the defect: `ORDER_NOT_DISCIPLINARY` when the `sanction_type` is `REPRIMAND` or `PROBATION`, `WINDOW_CLOSED` when the 365-day window has elapsed, and `ORDER_VACATED` when the order has already been vacated. Those three defects are tested **in exactly that written order and the first match decides the reason**: a claim against a vacated `REPRIMAND` order is denied for `ORDER_NOT_DISCIPLINARY`, a claim against a vacated `FINE` order whose window has also elapsed is denied for `WINDOW_CLOSED`, and `ORDER_VACATED` is recorded only when neither earlier defect applies. A denied claim never enters a disbursement run.

**Disbursing.** A trustee runs the waterfall over one order's claims by naming the `sanction_order_id` and supplying `expected_recovery_fund_version`. Three gates are checked before any allocation happens: the fund's `balance_cents` must exceed the 500,000-cent reserve floor, otherwise `ERR_FUND_RESERVE_FLOOR`; the respondent's `recovery_paid_cents` must be strictly below the 5,000,000-cent per-licensee cap, otherwise `ERR_LICENSEE_CAP_EXHAUSTED`; and there must be at least one candidate claim **and** the computed total award must be greater than zero, otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CLAIMS`. That last gate means a run in which every candidate turns out to have no room left is refused rather than committed as a zero-value disbursement.

The candidate set is every claim on the named order whose `status` is `ELIGIBLE` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`, ordered by `created_at` ascending. That ordering is the whole point: awards are allocated first-come, first-served, and `id` is never a sort key. Two running budgets are then established — the licensee's remaining headroom, being 5,000,000 cents minus the respondent's `recovery_paid_cents`, and the distributable cash, being the fund's `balance_cents` minus the 500,000-cent reserve floor.

The first pass walks the candidates in that order. For each claim, its own remaining need is `claimed_loss_cents` minus `awarded_cents`, and its remaining per-claim headroom is 2,000,000 cents minus `awarded_cents`. The award for that claim is the smallest of four figures — its remaining need, its remaining per-claim headroom, the licensee's remaining headroom and the remaining distributable cash — floored at zero so that an exhausted budget yields nothing rather than a negative number. Both running budgets are then reduced by the award before the next claim is considered, which is why an early large claim can starve a later one entirely. The pass total is the sum of the individual awards.

An **administrative assessment** of 2 percent is then retained out of that total, rounded half up to whole cents. Here is the circularity that gives the calculation its shape: the assessment is charged against the very balance whose floor constrains the awards, so an allocation that looked affordable can become unaffordable once its own assessment is added. The resolution is a **single re-pass, never an iteration to a fixed point**. If the balance minus the pass total minus the assessment would fall below the 500,000-cent floor, the shortfall is computed, and the **last claim in `created_at` ascending order** — never an earlier one — has its award reduced by the smaller of that shortfall and its own award. The pass total is reduced by the same amount and the assessment is recomputed **exactly once** from the reduced total. No further check is made, and no other claim is ever touched: earlier claimants keep their full first-pass awards even if the shave leaves the last claimant with nothing. Two consequences of taking the whole shortfall from that one claim and then re-checking nothing are normative. First, `shaved_claim_id` in the response names the last candidate whenever the re-pass **fired**, not merely when it moved money: where that last candidate's first-pass award was already 0 — because the distributable cash was exhausted before the walk reached it — the shave computes to 0, no award changes, and the response nonetheless reports that candidate's `id`. It is null only where the re-pass did not fire at all. Second, because the re-pass can take no more than that one award holds, a run whose shortfall exceeds it commits regardless and leaves `balance_cents` **below** the 500,000-cent floor; the floor is a precondition on starting a run, not an invariant a run guarantees on the way out.

Commitment then applies the awards. Each claim's `awarded_cents` is increased by its award; a claim whose `awarded_cents` now equals its `claimed_loss_cents` becomes `PAID`, one that received a positive but insufficient award becomes `PARTIALLY_PAID`, and `paid_at` is stamped at the run instant on the disbursement that first moves a claim out of `ELIGIBLE`. A `PARTIALLY_PAID` claim stays a candidate for later runs and is topped up to `PAID` when a subsequent run closes its gap. The fund's `awarded_cents` rises by the final total, its `assessment_cents` by the final assessment, and its `balance_cents` falls by the sum of the two; `version` is bumped once. The respondent's `recovery_paid_cents` is recomputed to reflect the new awards, permanently narrowing the headroom of any future run against that licensee. The response reports the run rather than a stored row: `total_awarded_cents`, `assessment_cents`, `balance_cents_after`, `fund_version`, `paid_claim_count`, `partially_paid_claim_count` and `shaved_claim_id`, the last of which names the claim reduced by the re-pass and is null when no shave occurred.

### 3.12. Advertising, Team and Branch Name Governance

A brokerage may advertise under names other than its legal name, and the commission polices those names. A `Broker` or a `LicensingAnalyst` registers one by naming the owning `brokerage_id`, the `kind` — `TEAM`, `DBA` or `BRANCH` — the name as the public will see it in `name_text`, and, for a team, the `team_lead_licensee_id`. For a `DBA` or a `BRANCH` the `team_lead_licensee_id` **must be null**; supplying one is refused with `ERR_TEAM_LEAD_NOT_AFFILIATED`, the same error that answers a team whose lead is missing, whose `license_status` is not `ACTIVE`, or whose `sponsoring_brokerage_id` is not the brokerage the name is registered under.

Every comparison the commission makes is on a **normalized** form, never on the text as typed. Normalization uppercases `name_text` and then discards every character that is not a letter A through Z or a digit 0 through 9, so spaces, punctuation, ampersands and hyphens all vanish: `"Elm & Oak Realty"` and `"elmoakrealty"` normalize identically. The result is stored as `normalized_name`.

Two substring tests run against that normalized form. `prohibited_term_hits` counts how many of the six prohibited terms — `REALTOR`, `MLS`, `COMMISSION`, `STATEOF`, `GUARANTEED`, `OFFICIAL` — appear anywhere inside `normalized_name`; each distinct term is counted once no matter how often it occurs. Because the test is a plain substring test on a form that has lost its word boundaries, a name is caught even when the term is buried inside a longer word. Separately, and only for a name whose `kind` is `TEAM`, a second test counts how many of the five forbidden team words — `REALTY`, `REALESTATE`, `BROKERAGE`, `COMPANY`, `AGENCY` — appear inside `normalized_name`, on the principle that a team must not sound like an independent firm.

Registration also enforces uniqueness. No two names may share a `normalized_name` while both are live, where live means a `status` of `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`; a collision answers `ERR_ADVERTISING_NAME_TAKEN`. Names in `REJECTED` or `REVOKED` release their normalized form for anyone to take, so a name lost to revocation immediately becomes available again. A successful registration starts in `SUBMITTED` with `reviewed_by_user_id` and `rejection_reason` null. Note that a name is admitted to `SUBMITTED` even when it contains prohibited terms — the term test bites at review, not at intake — and `prohibited_term_hits` is nonetheless counted and **stored on the row at intake**, so a submitted name already advertises the ground on which it will later fail. Submission is otherwise a thin operation: only the team-lead rule and the uniqueness rule guard it, in that order. The owning brokerage's own `status` is never consulted, so a name may be filed for a firm that is `PENDING`, `SUSPENDED` or even `CLOSED`; its `supervision_posture` is never consulted either, so a name may be filed for a `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` firm and simply sits in `SUBMITTED` until review refuses it; the forbidden team words are not tested at intake even for a `TEAM`; and a `Broker` filing a name need not be affiliated with, or the designated broker of, the brokerage the name is registered under.

**Review** is a `LicensingAnalyst`'s call. The analyst supplies a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `REJECT` or `REVOKE`, with an optional `rejection_reason`.

- `APPROVE` moves the name `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and records `reviewed_by_user_id`. Every gate is **re-evaluated against current state at the moment of approval**, not against the state at submission, because the world may have moved: `prohibited_term_hits` must be 0, or `ERR_PROHIBITED_ADVERTISING_TERM`; the owning brokerage's `supervision_posture` must not be `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`, or `ERR_BROKERAGE_POSTURE_BLOCKED`; no other live row may hold the same `normalized_name`, or `ERR_ADVERTISING_NAME_TAKEN`; and for a team the lead must still be `ACTIVE` and still sponsored by this brokerage, or `ERR_TEAM_LEAD_NOT_AFFILIATED`, and the name must contain none of the forbidden team words, or `ERR_TEAM_NAME_IMPLIES_BROKERAGE`. A name that was perfectly approvable when filed can therefore fail at review because its team lead transferred away or its firm slid into `RESTRICTED`. The gates are listed above by subject rather than by precedence; when a name fails several at once the answer is the earliest of them on the §2.11 ladder, which is posture first (rung 16), then the prohibited term (19), then the forbidden team word (20), then the team lead (21), and the uniqueness collision last of all (29). A team name filed for a `RESTRICTED` firm, carrying `REALTOR`, reading like a firm, fronted by a lead who has since gone inactive and duplicating a live registration therefore answers `ERR_BROKERAGE_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. Two further details are fixed. The posture consulted is the brokerage's posture re-derived at this instant, exactly as a read of that brokerage would report it. And the uniqueness test **excludes the row being reviewed**, so a name is never blocked by its own `SUBMITTED` registration; only a *different* live row collides. A `rejection_reason` sent alongside an `APPROVE` is validated as a member of its enumeration and then ignored; the approved row's own `rejection_reason` stays null.
- `REJECT` moves the name `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, records `reviewed_by_user_id`, and stores the reviewer's `rejection_reason`, which is one of `PROHIBITED_TERM`, `NAME_TAKEN`, `TEAM_LEAD_INELIGIBLE`, `POSTURE_RESTRICTED` or `IMPLIES_BROKERAGE`. That reason is **optional**: an analyst may reject a name without giving one, in which case `rejection_reason` is stored as null and the row shows a rejection with no stated ground. A value that is supplied must be a member of the five, so a free-text explanation is refused at rung 3. Nor is the reason cross-checked against the facts — an analyst may reject a perfectly clean name citing `PROHIBITED_TERM`, and the server records it as given.
- `REVOKE` moves an approved name `APPROVED → REVOKED` with the reviewer's `rejection_reason` — again optional and again stored as null when omitted — and releases its `normalized_name` for reuse. `REVOKE` is the one decision that does not require the row to be `SUBMITTED`; it requires `APPROVED` instead, and any other status answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Revocation by an analyst runs none of the approval gates: neither the firm's posture nor the name's terms nor the team lead's standing is consulted.

Two rejections are not reviews at all but consequences of events elsewhere. When a brokerage's `supervision_posture` enters `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`, every name of that brokerage still in `SUBMITTED` is rejected by the system with `rejection_reason` `POSTURE_RESTRICTED`, and `reviewed_by_user_id` stays **null** — the absence of a reviewer is the audit trail's way of showing no human made that call. When a team lead transfers away or loses `ACTIVE` standing, the team's `APPROVED` name is revoked by the system with `rejection_reason` `TEAM_LEAD_INELIGIBLE`, again with no `reviewed_by_user_id`. Since the row reached `APPROVED` through an analyst's review, that approver's identifier is **cleared back to null** by this cascade, so the revoked row shows no reviewer at all. Only the system cascade clears the field this way; an analyst's own `REVOKE` decision records their `X-User-ID` in it as described above.

### 3.13. The Compliance Sweep

Nothing in this system expires on its own. Licences do not lapse, remediation deadlines do not escalate, and unpaid fines do not default merely because the logical clock has moved past a stored instant; the stored row keeps its old `status` until a sweep observes it. A `LicensingAnalyst` runs the sweep with an empty request body, and it evaluates the whole register against the current logical instant in **five fixed steps, strictly in the following order**. Within a step, rows are processed in `created_at` ascending order.

1. **Expire licences.** Every licensee whose `license_status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `license_expires_at` is at or before the current instant moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED`. Expiry at exactly `license_expires_at` counts.
2. **Escalate stalled remediation.** Every audit in `REMEDIATION_ORDERED` whose `remediation_due_at` is strictly before the current instant escalates as described in §3.7, creating its escalation complaint and driving the respondent broker and their firm.
3. **Default unpaid fines.** Every order in `ISSUED` with a positive `fine_cents`, a `fine_paid_cents` still short of it, and a `fine_due_at` strictly before the current instant moves `ISSUED → IN_DEFAULT`, suspending the respondent for 90 days as described in §3.10.
4. **Restore served suspensions.** Every licensee in `SUSPENDED` whose `suspension_ends_at` is at or before the current instant returns `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` with `suspension_ends_at` cleared — but only when the order that drove the suspension has reached `SATISFIED`. A licensee suspended for a still-unpaid fine stays suspended however long the clock runs. Because this step follows step 3, a single sweep can default an order and suspend its respondent at step 3 without step 4 undoing it.
5. **Suspend failing providers.** Every education provider in `PROBATION` whose `invalidated_credit_hours` has reached 60 moves `PROBATION → SUSPENDED`, taking its instructors with it as described in §3.5: each of that provider's instructors in `PENDING` or `APPROVED` moves to `SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` `LICENSE_SUSPENDED`. The credits themselves are **not** touched — this step invalidates no course completion, so `invalidated_credit_hours` is exactly what it was before the suspension and the students of a suspended school keep every valid hour they had already earned. Nor does this step bring any brokerage into the sweep's cascade set.

Every brokerage touched by any step has its `overdue_remediation_count` and `supervision_posture` recomputed, and that settlement happens **once, after all five steps have run**, over the union of the firms the individual steps gathered — never step by step. Which firms those are is fixed: step 1 contributes the expiring licensee's `sponsoring_brokerage_id`, step 2 the brokerage owning the escalated audit's account, step 3 the defaulting respondent's `sponsoring_brokerage_id` but **only** where that respondent was `ACTIVE` and therefore took the licence consequence, step 4 the restored licensee's `sponsoring_brokerage_id`, and step 5 nothing at all. The "newly entered `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`" test of §3.7 that governs advertising-name auto-rejection is applied to each such firm against the posture it held **at the moment the step that gathered it first reached it**, which for a firm first seen in a later step already reflects the earlier steps' writes — not against the posture it held before the sweep began. A firm gathered twice keeps the first snapshot. The sweep reports what it did as `expired_licensee_count`, `escalated_audit_count`, `defaulted_order_count`, `restored_licensee_count` and `suspended_provider_count`. It is **idempotent with respect to the clock**: running it twice at the same logical instant produces the same register, and the second run reports all five counters as 0 because every qualifying row has already moved out of its qualifying state.

## 5. Error Code Catalogue

Every failed request answers with the error envelope of §2.2, carrying exactly one `error_id` from the table below. When a single request breaches several rules at once, the rule with the smallest priority number in the ladder of §2.11 decides which `error_id` is returned, and the remaining breaches are not reported.

Two failures happen before any rule in the ladder can be reached, are not domain errors, and therefore carry `error_id` values that are deliberately outside the table: a request addressed to a path this document does not define answers `ERR_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404, and a request using a method the addressed path does not support answers `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` with HTTP 405. Both still use the standard error envelope. Trailing slashes are not significant — `/commission/licensees` and `/commission/licensees/` address the same endpoint. Any other transport-level failure answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at that failure's own HTTP status, and an unexpected internal fault answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 400; no request ever answers with a 5xx status.
