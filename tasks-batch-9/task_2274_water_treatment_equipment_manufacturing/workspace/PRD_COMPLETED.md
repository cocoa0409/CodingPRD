# Water Treatment Equipment Manufacturing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A water and wastewater equipment manufacturer lives or dies on the distance between two numbers: the influent water quality a municipality hands over at the start of a project, and the finished-water limits that same municipality will hold the manufacturer to during a contractual performance guarantee demonstration two years later. Everything in between — process selection across biological, filtration, desalination and disinfection unit processes, wetted-material selection for corrosive service, pressure-vessel and potable-water code compliance, skid fabrication with controls integration, factory acceptance testing in a constrained shop, bidding a municipal specification with a pre-approved "or equal" product, and a decade of membrane and consumable supply afterwards — is a chain of commitments where a single silently wrong assumption compounds into liquidated damages.

This document specifies the API that carries that chain as a system of record. It is deliberately not a design-automation tool: the numbers it computes are the ones that must be reproducible and auditable, and the decisions it enforces are the ones where a human is legally accountable. Application engineering derives the treatment train from accepted laboratory data and active regulatory objectives, ranking a shared technology catalog by twenty-year evaluated cost. Independent reviewers freeze firm prices. The engineer of record decides, deviation by deviation, whether the offered equipment is genuinely equal to the specified basis of design. Quality engineers witness factory tests and certify guarantee demonstrations, and they are never the same people who designed the equipment or recorded the readings. Contracts administrators post the bid, record the award, and manage a conserved consumable reserve for the life of the service agreement.

Three properties govern the whole surface. First, the system is a **compliance and audit** record: every derived number is stamped on the row it belongs to, and re-derivation is never left to the reader. Second, **segregation of duties** is structural, not advisory — the API refuses a review, a bid, or a certification made by the wrong hand. Third, temporal behaviour is deterministic: every datetime comparison in this domain reads a logical clock that tests advance explicitly, never the wall clock.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The entire API is mounted under the `/water-treatment` prefix. The rules in this section apply to every endpoint unless a workflow in section 3 states a stricter rule for a particular operation.

### 2.1. Actors, Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming that operator's role. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the nine role names below spelled exactly as shown, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When both headers are well formed but the named role is not authorized for the addressed operation, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The nine roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `ApplicationEngineer`, `DesignReviewer`, `FabricationManager`, `QualityEngineer`, `CommissioningEngineer`, `EngineerOfRecord`, `OwnerRepresentative` and `ContractsAdministrator`.

- A `PlatformAdmin` maintains the three shared catalogs — unit-process technologies, wetted-material specifications and factory test bays — and the logical clock. It holds no project authority whatsoever: it cannot open a project, engineer a stage, or post a bid.
- An `ApplicationEngineer` owns application engineering: logging influent samples, declaring treatment objectives, running process selection, creating and revising equipment packages, and authoring the or-equal submittal package with its disclosed deviations. It never reviews its own design and never posts the bid.
- A `DesignReviewer` renders the independent design review that freezes the firm price and engineers the stage.
- A `FabricationManager` runs the shop floor: releasing approved packages, advancing the two skid build dimensions, and booking or cancelling test-bay time.
- A `QualityEngineer` is the independent quality authority: it accepts or rejects sampled influent data, witnesses factory acceptance test results, and certifies performance-guarantee demonstrations.
- A `CommissioningEngineer` provides field startup support and records the finished-water readings of each guarantee demonstration run. It never certifies its own readings.
- An `EngineerOfRecord` is the owner's design engineer: it dispositions each disclosed specification deviation and decides whether the offered equipment is pre-approved as an equal product.
- An `OwnerRepresentative` accepts or rejects the three closeout deliverables — the operations and maintenance manual, the operator training programme, and the recommended spare-parts list.
- A `ContractsAdministrator` owns the commercial record: it posts the municipal bid, records the award, opens the long-term service agreement, and posts and reverses consumable and membrane draws.

Opening a new project is shared between `ContractsAdministrator` and `ApplicationEngineer`. Every read (GET) route is available to all nine roles without exception.

**Identity semantics.** All nine roles carry an *opaque* identity: for every one of `PlatformAdmin`, `ApplicationEngineer`, `DesignReviewer`, `FabricationManager`, `QualityEngineer`, `CommissioningEngineer`, `EngineerOfRecord`, `OwnerRepresentative` and `ContractsAdministrator`, the value of `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any row's `id`. The server records it wherever a workflow attributes an action to a person — as `qa_decided_by_user_id` on an accepted or rejected influent profile, as `designed_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` on an equipment package, as `witnessed_by_user_id` on a factory test run, as `authored_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on an or-equal submittal, as `dispositioned_by_user_id` on a disclosed deviation, as `recorded_by_user_id` and `certified_by_user_id` on a performance demonstration, and as `posted_by_user_id` on a consumable draw. Those stored identifiers exist so that the segregation-of-duties comparisons described in section 2.7 can be made against the *same* opaque string later; there is no ownership lookup, and no resource is filtered by the caller's identity.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`: the literal prefix `req_` followed by exactly twenty randomly chosen characters drawn from the lowercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints. They are included *only* for paginated collection endpoints: the one non-collection array response in this surface — the set of treatment stages returned by the process-selection run of section 3.3 — carries `response_type` `"array"` with the stage objects in `data` and a `meta` holding just `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`, no pagination fields. The clock-setting endpoint of section 2.5 likewise returns `response_type` `"object"` with `data` carrying exactly one field, `now`, holding the canonical ISO 8601 rendering of the instant the clock was set to.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Collection endpoints use offset pagination driven by two query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, must be at least 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and must be at least 0. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds, or of the wrong type — anything that is not a bare optionally-signed run of digits — is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). A parameter supplied as the empty string is treated as absent and takes its default.

Every list response echoes `limit` and `offset` in `meta` alongside `total_count` — the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied — and `has_more`, which is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page.

Collections also accept optional filter parameters, all of which narrow the result set by exact match and combine conjunctively when more than one is supplied. The project list filters on `status` and `governing_influent_profile_id`. Influent profiles filter on `project_id` and `qa_status`, and the per-project influent list on `qa_status`. Treatment objectives filter on `project_id` and `status`. The process-technology catalog filters on `role` and `status`; the test-bay list filters on `status`. Treatment stages filter on `project_id`, `technology_id`, `upstream_stage_id` and `status`. Equipment packages filter on `treatment_stage_id`, `material_spec_id` and `status`. Skids filter on `equipment_package_id` and on either build dimension independently, by `mechanical_status`, `controls_status` or `fat_readiness`. Factory test runs filter on `skid_id`, `test_bay_id` and `status`. Or-equal submittals filter on `project_id` and `status`. Specification deviations filter on `or_equal_submittal_id`, `disposition` and `severity`, and the per-submittal deviation list on `disposition`. Performance demonstrations filter on `project_id`, `skid_id` and `result`. Service agreements filter on `project_id` and `status`. Consumable draws filter on `service_agreement_id`, `reverses_draw_id` and `draw_type`. An unrecognised filter value for an enumerated field is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A filter supplied as the empty string is treated as absent and narrows nothing, and a filter on a non-enumerated identifier is matched as an opaque string — an identifier that resolves to no row simply yields an empty page rather than a not-found error. Query parameters that are not named above are ignored.

Unless a specific collection states otherwise, lists are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The exceptions are pinned per collection and must be honoured: the project list is ordered by `created_at` **descending**, newest first; treatment objectives are ordered by `parameter` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; treatment stages are ordered by `stage_index` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; factory test runs are ordered by `scheduled_start_at` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; performance demonstrations are ordered by `round_no` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; the process-technology and material catalogs are ordered by `code` ascending; and the test-bay list is ordered by `bay_code` ascending.

Every collection carries one further tiebreak beneath the keys named above, and it is not optional: rows that compare equal on all of a collection's declared sort keys are returned in the order they were inserted, earliest insertion first. That insertion tiebreak is always ascending, even where a declared key above it is descending — so when several projects share one identical `created_at`, the `created_at` **descending** project list still returns the earliest-inserted of them first, and a first page of `limit=2` over three such projects returns the first and second projects created. A row's `id` is never a sort input.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and the Version Counter

Every persisted row carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`. Every row also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, fixed at insertion and never rewritten.

Most rows additionally carry `updated_at`, of the same format, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every accepted mutation of that row. `Project`, `InfluentProfile`, `TreatmentObjective`, `ProcessTechnology`, `MaterialSpec`, `TreatmentStage`, `EquipmentPackage`, `Skid`, `TestBay`, `FatRun`, `OrEqualSubmittal`, `SpecDeviation`, `PerformanceDemo` and `ServiceAgreement` all carry `updated_at` and `version`.

The single exception is the consumable draw. A draw row is an append-only accounting line: it is never mutated after insertion, and so it carries only `id` and `created_at`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are **absent** from a consumable draw — not null, absent. A correction to a draw is expressed by minting a compensating reversal row, never by editing the original.

Whenever a workflow says a derived value is recomputed on a parent row, that recomputation is a mutation of the parent: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by 1. A single request may therefore advance the `version` of several rows — an approving design review decision advances the package, its parent stage (only where that stage actually moves `SELECTED → ENGINEERED`), and the owning project. Conversely, a row that a request touches more than once still advances its `version` by exactly 1 for that request: the bump is per row per accepted request, never per internal write.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

No temporal rule in this domain reads the wall clock. A single logical clock, seeded to `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` on a fresh instance, supplies "now" for every comparison: bid windows, guarantee deadlines, cost escalation, membrane warranty proration, service-agreement expiry, and every `*_at` stamp the server writes. A `PlatformAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a `now` instant to the clock-setting capability; no domain rows are touched by that operation, and the clock may be moved backwards as freely as forwards. Every client-supplied instant anywhere on this surface is parsed permissively as ISO 8601 — a trailing `Z`, an explicit UTC offset, or no zone marker at all (read as UTC) are all accepted, and any number of fractional digits is accepted — but it is converted to UTC and re-rendered with exactly three fractional digits before it is stored or echoed, so what a row carries back is always the canonical form. A value that is not a parseable instant is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Advancing the clock never retroactively re-evaluates stored rows — expiry and escalation are evaluated when an operation next touches the affected record, as described in the relevant workflow.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding

Unless a rule says otherwise, "round" in this document means **round half up**, and every monetary value is an integer count of cents, never a decimal currency amount. The precision rules below are contractual:

- **Money.** All amounts named `*_cents` are whole-cent integers. Where a formula composes several factors, the rounding point is pinned by the formula itself; a value that is described as rounded once at the end must not be rounded at intermediate steps, and a value described as rounded per line item before summation must not be re-derived by summing unrounded products and rounding once. The two pinned per-line-then-sum ledgers are the bid roll-up (section 3.5) and the service reserve (section 3.9).
- **Flows.** Design flows in million gallons per day are carried and compared at three decimal places, rounded half up. Where a field's scale is declared this way, it also applies on the way *in*: a client-supplied `design_flow_mgd` or objective `limit_value` carrying more than three decimal places, or a `cost_index` carrying more than two, is rounded half up to the declared scale at insertion and stored in that rounded form. Fields with no declared scale — the influent chemistry, `peak_factor`, `design_pressure_psig`, `design_temp_c`, the technology coefficients and the bay dimensions — are stored exactly as supplied.
- **Compliance and margin percentages.** An or-equal compliance score and a per-deviation penalty are carried at two decimal places, rounded half up. Guarantee margins are carried at two decimal places, rounded half up, and compared finished-water values at three decimal places, rounded half up.
- **Cost variance.** The design-to-release cost variance is carried at four decimal places, rounded half up, expressed as a fraction rather than a percentage figure.
- **Footprint-minutes.** The test-bay reservation ledger is carried at two decimal places, rounded half up.
- **Truncation, where it is specified.** Some quantities are deliberately floored rather than rounded: whole elapsed 30-day months, whole elapsed days of guarantee delay, and the whole minutes of a booked test-bay window. Others are deliberately ceiled: each capital, energy and footprint-penalty cost term inside the process-selection ranking, and a skid's snapshotted footprint. Those cases are called out where they occur, and the stated direction is part of the contract.

Comparison boundaries are equally contractual. Where a threshold is inclusive the document says "at least", "at most", "at or after", or "no later than"; where it is exclusive it says "strictly greater than", "strictly before", or "more than". Those words are load-bearing.

### 2.7. Segregation of Duties

Three independence rules are enforced structurally, and each is checked against the opaque `X-User-ID` of the acting operator rather than against a role name — holding the right role is necessary but not sufficient.

An operator may not review a design it produced. When a design review decision is rendered, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `designed_by_user_id` stored on the package under review; the same comparison is applied when a factory acceptance test result is witnessed, against the `designed_by_user_id` of the package whose skid is on test. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SOD_DESIGNER_IS_REVIEWER` (HTTP 403).

An operator may not bid on a pre-approval it authored. When a project's bid is posted, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `authored_by_user_id` of the pre-approved or-equal submittal the bid rests on; a violation is `ERR_SOD_AUTHOR_IS_BIDDER` (HTTP 403).

An operator may not certify readings it recorded. When a performance demonstration is certified, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the demonstration's `recorded_by_user_id`; a violation is `ERR_SOD_RECORDER_IS_CERTIFIER` (HTTP 403).

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Three write operations mutate a parent record's derived state in a way that a client working from a stale read would silently corrupt. Each of those three requires a non-optional expected-version field in its request body — an integer of at least 0, absence or wrong type being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — carrying the `version` of the parent that the client last observed. If the supplied value does not equal the parent's current `version`, the write is refused and nothing is persisted.

- Booking a factory acceptance test run requires `expected_skid_version`, matching the target skid's current `version`. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_SKID_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The rule exists because an accepted booking legitimately supersedes the skid's outstanding scheduled run and releases that run's reserved bay footprint-minutes; a writer holding an out-of-date view of the skid would cancel a booking it never saw.
- Posting a consumable or membrane draw requires `expected_service_agreement_version`, matching the target agreement's current `version`. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Each accepted draw legitimately reduces `reserve_available_cents`, so a writer working from an out-of-date reserve balance would overdraw a reserve it believes is still full.
- Disclosing a specification deviation on an or-equal submittal requires `expected_or_equal_submittal_version`, matching the target submittal's current `version`. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_SUBMITTAL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Every accepted deviation legitimately lowers `compliance_score`, so a writer holding an out-of-date score would push the submittal under the compliance floor without having observed the penalty it is adding to.

No other endpoint takes an expected-version field. Supplying one where it is not specified is not required to be honoured, and omitting one where it *is* specified is a request-shape failure, not a concurrency failure.

### 2.9. Path Resolution and the Order of Checks

Nested paths are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the error. A request addressing a package under a stage resolves the `stage_id` before it looks at the body; a request addressing a deviation under a submittal resolves the `submittal_id` first. The path identifiers used across the surface are `project_id`, `profile_id`, `objective_id`, `stage_id`, `package_id`, `skid_id`, `run_id`, `submittal_id`, `deviation_id`, `demo_id`, `agreement_id`, `draw_id`, `technology_id`, `material_spec_id` and `bay_id`. Each has its own not-found code: `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INFLUENT_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TREATMENT_OBJECTIVE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TREATMENT_STAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EQUIPMENT_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SKID_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FAT_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBMITTAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEVIATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEMO_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_CONSUMABLE_DRAW_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404. The three catalog reads addressed by `technology_id`, `material_spec_id` and `bay_id` report an unresolvable identifier as `ERR_CATALOG_REF_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and so does any foreign key supplied in a *request body* — `material_spec_id`, `skid_id`, `test_bay_id` or `project_id` — that does not resolve to a live row.

**Validation precedence.** When more than one rule would reject a request, the response carries the error of the highest-priority rule, evaluated in exactly this order. This ladder is the contract; text order elsewhere in this document does not override it.

1. Authentication of the two headers — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorization for the addressed operation — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. Path existence, in the order the identifiers appear above: `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, then `ERR_INFLUENT_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TREATMENT_STAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EQUIPMENT_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SKID_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FAT_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBMITTAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEVIATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEMO_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSUMABLE_DRAW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TREATMENT_OBJECTIVE_NOT_FOUND`.
4. Body foreign-key existence — `ERR_CATALOG_REF_NOT_FOUND`.
5. Request-shape validation — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`: required fields present, types correct, enum members declared, every datetime pair ordered with the end strictly after its start, and every numeric field inside its declared constraint. A required field supplied as JSON `null` counts as absent and is reported as missing; an optional field supplied as `null` takes its documented default. A field described as "non-empty" must be a string carrying at least one non-whitespace character, while the optional free-text notes and reasons may be blank. Every string field is also length-capped, and exceeding the cap is a shape failure: 80 characters for catalog codes, `bay_code`, `tag_number`, `consumable_item_code` and a draw's `item_code`; 160 for `name`, `sample_label`, `spec_section`, `named_basis_of_design` and `spec_paragraph`; 200 for `owner_agency_name`; 300 for `regulatory_driver`; and 2000 for a deviation `description` and for every optional note, reason or annotation. A request body that is absent, empty, or JSON `null` is treated as an empty object; a body that is malformed JSON or a JSON value other than an object is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Note that this tier sits *below* existence: a body that is malformed against a path that does not resolve reports the not-found code.
6. Catalog code uniqueness — `ERR_DUPLICATE_CATALOG_CODE`; then equipment tag uniqueness — `ERR_DUPLICATE_TAG_NUMBER`.
7. Optimistic concurrency, in order: `ERR_STALE_SKID_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_SUBMITTAL_VERSION`.
8. The lazy service-agreement expiry sweep — `ERR_AGREEMENT_EXPIRED`. This deliberately outranks the agreement's active-state check, so an expired agreement reports expiry rather than inactivity.
9. Lifecycle-state checks, in order: `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`, `ERR_BID_ALREADY_SUBMITTED`, `ERR_PROFILE_ALREADY_DECIDED`, `ERR_PACKAGE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_SKID_MILESTONE_OUT_OF_ORDER`, `ERR_FAT_RUN_NOT_SCHEDULED`, `ERR_SUBMITTAL_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_DEVIATION_ALREADY_DISPOSITIONED`, `ERR_DEMO_ALREADY_CERTIFIED`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_DRAW_ALREADY_REVERSED`, `ERR_DELIVERABLE_ALREADY_ACCEPTED`, then `ERR_STAGE_SUPERSEDED`.
10. Segregation of duties, in order: `ERR_SOD_DESIGNER_IS_REVIEWER`, `ERR_SOD_AUTHOR_IS_BIDDER`, `ERR_SOD_RECORDER_IS_CERTIFIER`.
11. Material safety and code screening, in order: `ERR_MATERIAL_NOT_RATED_FOR_SERVICE`, `ERR_MATERIAL_PRESSURE_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_MATERIAL_NOT_CODE_STAMPABLE`.
12. Process-selection prerequisites, in order: `ERR_NO_ACCEPTED_INFLUENT_PROFILE`, `ERR_NO_TREATMENT_OBJECTIVES`, `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TECHNOLOGY`.
13. Test-bay and readiness rules, in order: `ERR_SKID_NOT_FAT_READY`, `ERR_TEST_BAY_UNAVAILABLE`, `ERR_TEST_BAY_EXCLUSIVE_CONFLICT`, `ERR_TEST_BAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, then the demonstration-window rule `ERR_DEMO_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.
14. Or-equal gates, in order: `ERR_FUNDAMENTAL_DEVIATION_OPEN`, then `ERR_COMPLIANCE_SCORE_TOO_LOW`.
15. Bid gates, in order: `ERR_OR_EQUAL_NOT_PREAPPROVED`, `ERR_BID_WINDOW_CLOSED`, then `ERR_BID_MARGIN_BELOW_FLOOR`.
16. Demonstration gates: `ERR_MAX_DEMO_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED` first, then `ERR_GUARANTEE_PREREQ_NOT_MET`. The retest cap deliberately outranks the commissioning prerequisite, because a project that has burned both demonstration rounds has already left commissioning and the exhausted-rounds diagnosis is the more specific one. After that comes `ERR_DEMO_READING_MISSING`.
17. Closeout and reserve gates, in order: `ERR_DELIVERABLES_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_INVALID_DRAW_QUANTITY`, then `ERR_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT`.

### 2.10. Derived Roll-ups and Cascade Discipline

Several values are never supplied by a client and never left for a reader to compute: they are recomputed and stamped by the server as a side effect of the operation that could have changed them. The project's `delivery_posture` is the most far-reaching of these. It folds every equipment package sitting on a non-superseded stage of the project, together with those packages' factory test runs and every performance demonstration on the project, into a single dominating value evaluated in a strict precedence order:

- If any of those packages is in `REWORK_HOLD`, the posture is `HELD`.
- Otherwise, if any of those packages has its `variance_flag` set, **or** any factory test run on their skids ended `FAILED` or `CONDITIONAL`, **or** any performance demonstration of the project ended `FAILED` or `CONDITIONAL_PASS`, the posture is `AT_RISK`.
- Otherwise the posture is `ON_TRACK`.

The posture is a single value, not a set: a project holding both a reworked package and a variance-flagged package is `HELD`, because `HELD` dominates `AT_RISK`, which in turn dominates `ON_TRACK`. It is recomputed on process selection, on the design review that approves a package, on the project's *first* package release for fabrication, on every demonstration certification, on a witnessed factory test result that is recorded `FAILED` or `CONDITIONAL` or that is the `PASS` completing fabrication for the whole project, and on the one skid milestone that closes a rework hold. Those are the only recomputation points, and they are also the only points at which these operations rewrite the project row: a plain skid milestone, a second or later package release, and a recorded `PASS` that leaves some sibling package still in fabrication all leave the project's `delivery_posture`, `updated_at` and `version` untouched. It is read back as an input by the bid contingency described in section 3.5, which is why an at-risk project bids more expensively than an on-track one.

Cascades in this domain are chains, not single hops, and each link must be applied. A rejected or-equal submittal withdraws its pending deviations, which changes their penalty weights, which re-scores the submittal, which blocks the project's bid. A failed factory test reverts the skid's mechanical dimension, which holds the package, which holds the project. A passing certification meets the guarantee, which activates the pending service agreement, which moves the project into service. Section 3 states each link at the point where it fires; none of them is optional, and each one carries the `updated_at` and `version` bump described in section 2.4 on every row it touches.

### 2.11. Conserved Ledgers

Two quantities in this system are conserved rather than merely computed, and the conservation is exact — integer-exact for money, and to two decimal places for resource-time. No drift is tolerated in either.

The first is a test bay's `reserved_footprint_minutes`. At every instant it equals the sum, over every factory test run booked on that bay whose status is still `SCHEDULED`, of that run's `footprint_sf` multiplied by its `duration_minutes`. Booking a run adds its footprint-minutes; every exit from `SCHEDULED` — a witnessed result, a cancellation, or a supersede by a newer booking on the same skid — subtracts exactly the same footprint-minutes that the booking added. Scheduling a run and then cancelling it restores the bay's ledger to its exact pre-booking value.

The second is a service agreement's reserve. From activation onwards — an agreement still in `PENDING_ACTIVATION` holds `reserve_available_cents` at 0 regardless of what it is funded for, because nothing is spendable before activation — `reserve_committed_accrual_cents` plus `reserve_available_cents` equals `reserve_funded_cents` at every instant, in whole cents, with no rounding slack. And for any forward draw followed by its reversal, both `reserve_available_cents` and `reserve_committed_accrual_cents` return to exactly the values they held before the forward draw was posted — restored exactly, not approximately.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Shared Catalogs: Unit Processes, Wetted Materials and Test Bays

Before any project can be engineered, a `PlatformAdmin` must populate three catalogs that every project draws on. These are the only rows in the system that are not owned by a project, and they are the only rows a `PlatformAdmin` may create.

A **unit-process technology** describes one buildable treatment process. It is registered with a catalog `code` that must be non-empty and unique across the technology catalog, a human-readable `name`, and a `role` naming the treatment duty it fills — one of `BIOLOGICAL`, `FILTRATION`, `DESALINATION` or `DISINFECTION`. Its feed-quality envelope is given by `max_feed_turbidity_ntu` and `max_feed_tds_mg_l`, the highest influent turbidity and total dissolved solids it tolerates, both at least 0. Its hydraulic envelope is `min_design_flow_mgd` and `max_design_flow_mgd`, both at least 0 and with the maximum at least the minimum. Three coefficients drive the ranking arithmetic: `unit_capex_cents_per_mgd`, strictly positive, the equipment capital cost in cents per MGD of stage design flow; `energy_kwh_per_mg`, at least 0, the electrical energy consumed per million gallons treated; and `footprint_sf_per_mgd`, strictly positive, the plan area the process occupies per MGD. Two compliance attributes complete the row: `requires_code_stamp`, defaulting to `NONE` and otherwise one of `AWWA`, `NSF61` or `ASME_U`, naming the pressure-vessel or potable-water code stamp the resulting equipment must carry; and `chemical_service_class`, one of `NEUTRAL`, `CHLORINATED`, `HIGH_CHLORIDE`, `CAUSTIC` or `ACIDIC`, naming the wetted chemical service the equipment will see. A technology that consumes a recurring element carries its `consumable_item_code`; technologies with no consumable carry null there. Finally, `status` defaults to `ACTIVE` and may be seeded as `DEPRECATED`, in which case the row never enters a process-selection candidate pool.

A **wetted-material specification** describes an alloy or lining approved for corrosive service. It carries a `code` such as `SS316L` or `DUPLEX_2205`, non-empty and unique across the material catalog, and a rating envelope: `max_chloride_ppm` and `max_pressure_psig` (the latter strictly positive), `max_temp_c`, and the pH band `ph_min` and `ph_max`, where the minimum is between 0 and 14 inclusive and the maximum is at least the minimum and at most 14. Three boolean capability flags, each defaulting to false, record whether the material may carry an ASME Section VIII U stamp (`asme_u_capable`), is NSF/ANSI 61 certified for potable contact (`nsf61_certified`), and satisfies the referenced AWWA standard (`awwa_compliant`). A `cost_index` of at least 1.0, carried at two decimal places, is the multiplier the firm price applies to the stage equipment base cost. And `service_classes_allowed` is a non-empty array whose members are drawn from the same five chemical service classes a technology declares.

A **test bay** is a physical shop position for factory acceptance testing. It is registered with a `bay_code`, non-empty and unique across the bay catalog, a strictly positive `max_skid_footprint_sf` naming the total skid footprint the bay can host at one time, a strictly positive `crane_capacity_tons` recording the overhead crane rating, and `changeover_buffer_minutes` — the rigging and changeover allowance, defaulting to 120 minutes (two hours) and required to be at least 0 — which section 3.7 applies symmetrically to both ends of every booked interval. A new bay starts with `reserved_footprint_minutes` at 0 and `status` `AVAILABLE`; it may be seeded `OUT_OF_SERVICE`.

Attempting to register a technology, material or bay whose code already exists in its own catalog is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CATALOG_CODE`. Uniqueness is scoped per catalog: a technology may share a code string with a material without conflict. The collision test — and the equipment tag-number test of section 3.4 with it — is exact, case-sensitive string equality with no trimming or normalisation, so `SS316L` and `ss316l` are two distinct codes.

### 3.2. Project Origination, Influent Characterisation and Laboratory QA

A municipal opportunity enters the system as a **project**, opened by either a `ContractsAdministrator` or an `ApplicationEngineer`. The request supplies the `name` of the project, non-empty and at most 160 characters; the `owner_agency_name` of the owning water or wastewater agency, non-empty; an optional `procurement_type` that defaults to `MUNICIPAL_BID` and may instead be `DESIGN_BUILD` or `SOLE_SOURCE`; the `bid_due_at` instant at which the agency closes bidding; the `guarantee_deadline_at` instant by which the contractual performance guarantee must be demonstrated, which must fall strictly after `bid_due_at`; and `footprint_allowance_sf_per_stage`, a strictly positive square-foot budget per treatment stage against which excess plan area is penalised during process selection. Both instants are ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits.

A new project begins life in `DRAFT` with `delivery_posture` `ON_TRACK`, `guarantee_status` `NOT_STARTED`, `governing_influent_profile_id` null, all three closeout deliverable statuses — `om_manual_status`, `operator_training_status` and `spare_parts_list_status` — at `PENDING`, and every money field at zero: `base_bid_amount_cents`, `bond_premium_cents`, `contingency_cents`, `evaluated_bid_price_cents`, `offered_price_cents`, `contract_amount_cents` and `liquidated_damages_cents`. Its `awarded_at` is null. Over its life the project walks the states `DRAFT`, `SCOPED`, `BID_READY`, `BID_SUBMITTED`, `BID_BLOCKED`, `AWARDED`, `IN_FABRICATION`, `COMMISSIONING`, `GUARANTEED`, `REMEDIATION`, `SERVICE_ACTIVE` and `CLOSED`; `REMEDIATION`, `SERVICE_ACTIVE` and `CLOSED` are terminal.

An `ApplicationEngineer` then logs one or more sampled **influent profiles** against the project. Each sample carries a `sample_label` identifying it in the field, non-empty; `design_flow_mgd`, the strictly positive average design flow in million gallons per day the sample represents, carried at three decimal places; an optional `peak_factor` defaulting to 1.5 and required to be at least 1.0, giving the peak-to-average hydraulic multiplier; and the water chemistry itself — `turbidity_ntu`, `tds_mg_l`, `toc_mg_l` and `chloride_mg_l`, each at least 0, plus `temperature_c` in degrees Celsius and `ph` between 0 and 14 inclusive. The chloride figure is what later drives corrosive-service material screening. Every logged profile enters `qa_status` `SUBMITTED` with `qa_decided_by_user_id` and `qa_note` null. Logging a sample reads no project lifecycle state and leaves the project row untouched, so samples may be added at any point in the project's life.

Sampled data is not design data until the laboratory says so. A `QualityEngineer` renders a QA disposition on a `SUBMITTED` profile by supplying `decision` — either `ACCEPT`, which moves the profile `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, or `REJECT`, which moves it `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` — together with an optional `qa_note`. Both outcomes store the acting operator's identifier as `qa_decided_by_user_id`, store the note, and advance the profile's `version` by 1. Both `ACCEPTED` and `REJECTED` are terminal: a second disposition on the same profile is refused with `ERR_PROFILE_ALREADY_DECIDED`.

**Choosing the governing sample.** A project may hold many accepted samples, and design proceeds against exactly one of them — the worst-case hydraulic load. The candidate set is precisely those influent profiles of the project whose `qa_status` is `ACCEPTED`; `SUBMITTED` and `REJECTED` samples are excluded outright, and there is no fallback to un-accepted data. Among the candidates, the sample with the greatest `design_flow_mgd` wins, comparing flows at three decimal places. When two or more accepted samples share the same design flow, the tie is broken in favour of the earlier `created_at` — the earliest accepted sample wins — and when they also share an identical `created_at`, in favour of the earlier-inserted row, exactly the insertion tiebreak of section 2.3. A row's `id` is never a decision input. The winner's identifier is written to the project's `governing_influent_profile_id`.

That selection runs at process selection, and it runs again on every QA disposition once the project has left `DRAFT` — that is, from `SCOPED` onwards, in whatever later state the project happens to be. While the project is still in `DRAFT` a QA disposition changes nothing but the profile itself. And even where the re-derivation runs, the project row is rewritten only when the derived identifier actually differs from the one already stored, so a disposition that leaves the same sample governing does not advance the project's `version`. On an acceptance, the governing sample is re-derived over the project's accepted profiles — a newly accepted, higher-flow sample can therefore take over as the design basis. The treatment train itself is **not** rebuilt by an acceptance; that requires a fresh process-selection run. On a rejection, the re-derivation matters only when the rejected sample was the governing one: the selection is then re-run over the remaining accepted profiles, and if none remain, `governing_influent_profile_id` is written back to null. If the candidate set is empty while the project is still in `DRAFT`, process selection cannot proceed and the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ACCEPTED_INFLUENT_PROFILE`.

### 3.3. Treatment Objectives and the Process Selection Run

Alongside the influent data, an `ApplicationEngineer` declares the finished-water **treatment objectives** the plant must meet. Each objective names a `parameter` from `TURBIDITY_NTU`, `TOC_MG_L`, `TDS_MG_L`, `CHLORIDE_MG_L`, `ECOLI_CFU_100ML`, `GIARDIA_LOG_REMOVAL`, `BOD5_MG_L` and `AMMONIA_N_MG_L`; a strictly positive `limit_value` carried at three decimal places; a `basis` of either `MAX_DAY` or `MONTHLY_AVERAGE`, naming the statistical basis the limit will be judged on during the guarantee demonstration; and a non-empty `regulatory_driver` citing the permit or rule that imposes the limit. A new objective is created with `status` `ACTIVE`. At most one objective may be `ACTIVE` for a given parameter on a given project: when an incoming objective declares a parameter that an existing `ACTIVE` objective of the same project already covers, the incumbent transitions `ACTIVE → RETIRED` and its `version` advances by 1. `RETIRED` is terminal, and retired objectives are invisible to every downstream calculation. Declaring an objective likewise reads no project lifecycle state and leaves the project row untouched; the retirement of an incumbent is the only other row this operation writes.

**The process selection run** is application engineering's central act, performed by an `ApplicationEngineer` on a project with no request body at all. It refuses to run unless the project is in `DRAFT` or `SCOPED` — any other lifecycle state yields `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE` — and unless the project holds at least one `ACCEPTED` influent profile (otherwise `ERR_NO_ACCEPTED_INFLUENT_PROFILE`) and at least one `ACTIVE` treatment objective (otherwise `ERR_NO_TREATMENT_OBJECTIVES`).

The run first fixes the governing sample as described in section 3.2, then derives which treatment duties the plant needs. Each `ACTIVE` objective maps to exactly one duty: turbidity and total organic carbon objectives both require `FILTRATION`; total dissolved solids and chloride objectives both require `DESALINATION`; E. coli and Giardia log-removal objectives both require `DISINFECTION`; and five-day BOD and ammonia-nitrogen objectives both require `BIOLOGICAL`. The distinct duties so derived are then laid out in one pinned hydraulic order — biological treatment first, then filtration, then desalination, then disinfection — and only the duties actually required appear. Each retained duty becomes one **treatment stage**, numbered from 1 upward in that order as its `stage_index`, with `role` copied from the duty and `upstream_stage_id` pointing at the stage created immediately before it; the head stage carries null there. Each stage records its owning `project_id`, and its `status` is `SELECTED`.

Stage hydraulics depend on the duty. Filtration and disinfection stages are sized on peak flow: the stage's `design_flow_mgd` is the governing sample's `design_flow_mgd` multiplied by that sample's `peak_factor`, rounded half up to three decimal places. Biological and desalination stages are sized on the average day: `design_flow_mgd` is the governing sample's `design_flow_mgd` rounded half up to three decimal places, and the peak factor is ignored entirely.

**Screening the candidate pool.** For each required duty, a technology qualifies only if it satisfies five predicates simultaneously: its `role` equals the duty; its `status` is `ACTIVE`, so `DEPRECATED` catalog rows never compete; its `max_feed_turbidity_ntu` is at least the governing sample's `turbidity_ntu`; its `max_feed_tds_mg_l` is at least the governing sample's `tds_mg_l`; and the stage design flow falls inside its hydraulic window, meaning `min_design_flow_mgd` is at most the stage flow and `max_design_flow_mgd` is at least it — both ends inclusive. If any required duty ends up with an empty pool, the whole run fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TECHNOLOGY` and no treatment stage is created for any duty, not even for the duties that did have candidates. The failure is total in the other direction too: on a re-run, the project's existing train is left exactly as it stood — no stage is superseded, `governing_influent_profile_id` is not rewritten, and the project's `version` does not move — because every duty is selected and priced before the first row is written.

**Ranking the pool by twenty-year evaluated cost.** Each surviving candidate is scored on three cost terms, each brought to whole cents before anything is added:

- *Capital.* Multiply the candidate's `unit_capex_cents_per_mgd` by the stage design flow and round the product **up** to the next whole cent. This capital figure is also what the stage stores as its `equipment_base_cost_cents`, and it is the base every equipment-package price later multiplies.
- *Energy over the plant's life.* Multiply the candidate's `energy_kwh_per_mg` by the stage design flow, then by 365 days, then by the pinned energy price of 12 cents per kilowatt-hour, and round that annual figure **up** to the next whole cent. Multiply the rounded annual figure by the pinned twenty-year evaluation life. The rounding happens once, on the annual figure, before the twenty-fold multiplication — not after it.
- *Excess footprint penalty.* Multiply the candidate's `footprint_sf_per_mgd` by the stage design flow and round half up to three decimal places to obtain the plan area the process would occupy. Subtract the project's `footprint_allowance_sf_per_stage`; if the result is negative, treat the excess as zero. Multiply the excess square footage by the pinned footprint penalty of 45,000 cents — 450 dollars — per square foot and round **up** to the next whole cent.

The candidate's evaluated cost is the sum of those three whole-cent terms, and the winner is the candidate with the smallest evaluated cost. Ties are broken first by the smaller `footprint_sf_per_mgd`, then by the earlier `created_at`, and finally — for catalog rows sharing an identical `created_at` — by the earlier-inserted row, the same insertion tiebreak section 2.3 applies to lists; a row's `id` is never a decision input. The winning technology's identifier is stored on the stage as `technology_id`, its evaluated cost as the stage's `evaluated_cost_cents`, and its capital term as the stage's `equipment_base_cost_cents`. Once every stage is created, the project moves `DRAFT → SCOPED`, its `delivery_posture` is recomputed, and its `version` advances by 1. The run answers HTTP 201 with the array of freshly created stage rows in ascending `stage_index`, in the non-paginated array envelope of section 2.2; the project row itself is not part of the response.

**Re-running selection.** Influent data and objectives change, and the run is deliberately repeatable: a project already in `SCOPED` may be re-scoped, which leaves it in `SCOPED` — a `SCOPED → SCOPED` self-loop rather than a new state. The re-run is refused, however, once fabrication has begun to depend on the train: if any equipment package on any `SELECTED` or `ENGINEERED` stage of the project is in `RELEASED_FOR_FAB`, `REWORK_HOLD`, `FAT_PASSED` or `SHIPPED`, the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE` — the same code the lifecycle gate above uses, checked immediately after it. When the re-run is permitted, every existing stage of the project in `SELECTED` or `ENGINEERED` transitions to `SUPERSEDED` — `SELECTED → SUPERSEDED` and `ENGINEERED → SUPERSEDED` respectively — each such stage advancing its own `version` by 1; a fresh set of stages is then created exactly as on the first run, with `governing_influent_profile_id` re-selected; and the project's `version` advances by 1. An `ENGINEERED` stage may only be superseded when no equipment package on it sits in `RELEASED_FOR_FAB`, `REWORK_HOLD`, `FAT_PASSED` or `SHIPPED`. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal for a stage. Equipment packages whose stage has just been superseded keep their own status untouched — nothing is deleted — but their stage precondition now blocks every further engineering step on them, reported as `ERR_STAGE_SUPERSEDED`.

### 3.4. Equipment Package Design, Material Screening and Independent Review

A selected stage is a process decision; an **equipment package** is the buildable thing that realises it. An `ApplicationEngineer` designs a package against a stage by supplying `material_spec_id`, naming the wetted material chosen for corrosive service; a `tag_number`, non-empty and unique among the packages sharing that same `treatment_stage_id` — a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_TAG_NUMBER`, and the same tag string may legitimately be reused on a different stage; a strictly positive `design_pressure_psig`; and a `design_temp_c` in degrees Celsius. The package records its own `treatment_stage_id`, copies `code_stamp_required` from the stage technology's `requires_code_stamp` at creation, and enters `status` `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0, `designed_by_user_id` set to the acting operator's identifier, `reviewed_by_user_id` null, `firm_cost_cents` at 0, `cost_variance_pct` at 0, `variance_flag` false, and both `approved_at` and `released_at` null. Package status walks `DRAFT`, `IN_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `RELEASED_FOR_FAB`, `REWORK_HOLD`, `FAT_PASSED` and `SHIPPED`, with `SHIPPED` terminal. Design reads no *project* lifecycle state at all: the only structural precondition is that the addressed stage not be `SUPERSEDED`, which is `ERR_STAGE_SUPERSEDED`, and a stage of an `AWARDED` or `IN_FABRICATION` project accepts a new package as readily as a stage of a `SCOPED` one. Any number of packages may be designed against one stage; the single-package-per-stage expectation is enforced only at bid time, by section 3.6.

A `DRAFT` package may be revised by the same role, supplying any subset of `material_spec_id`, `tag_number`, `design_pressure_psig` and `design_temp_c`; whichever fields are omitted keep their stored values, the supplied ones overwrite, `budgetary_cost_cents` is re-derived, and the package's `version` advances by 1. Revision is only accepted while the package is in `DRAFT` — any other package state is `ERR_PACKAGE_INVALID_STATE` — and only while the parent stage is `SELECTED` or `ENGINEERED`, a superseded stage refusing the revision with `ERR_STAGE_SUPERSEDED`. All three material screens below are re-applied against whichever material ends up on the row, supplied or retained. A revision that supplies nothing at all is accepted: it re-screens and re-stamps the same data and still advances the `version`.

**Corrosive service and code screening.** Three independent screens stand between a chosen material and a buildable package, and all three are re-run at package creation, at every revision, and again at submission for review, so that a material that was adequate against yesterday's governing sample is caught if the governing sample has since changed. They are evaluated in this order:

1. *Service rating.* The material must be rated for the water it will touch. Its `max_chloride_ppm` must be at least the governing influent profile's `chloride_mg_l`; its `max_temp_c` must be at least the governing profile's `temperature_c`; the governing profile's `ph` must fall within the material's band, meaning `ph_min` is at most the pH and `ph_max` is at least it; and the stage technology's `chemical_service_class` must be one of the members of the material's `service_classes_allowed`. Any of those four failing is `ERR_MATERIAL_NOT_RATED_FOR_SERVICE`. When the project holds no governing influent profile at the moment of the screen — a package designed before the first process-selection run, or after the governing sample was rejected away — the three water-chemistry comparisons have nothing to compare against and are skipped; only the chemical-service-class membership is then evaluated in this screen, and screens 2 and 3 run unchanged.
2. *Pressure rating.* The package's `design_pressure_psig` must be at most the material's `max_pressure_psig`; exceeding it is `ERR_MATERIAL_PRESSURE_EXCEEDED`.
3. *Code stampability.* The material must be capable of the stamp the stage technology demands: a `code_stamp_required` of `ASME_U` demands `asme_u_capable` be true, `NSF61` demands `nsf61_certified` be true, `AWWA` demands `awwa_compliant` be true, and `NONE` demands nothing at all. A shortfall is `ERR_MATERIAL_NOT_CODE_STAMPABLE`.

**Budgetary pricing.** A package is priced twice in its life, from the same skeleton but with different commitments. Both variants start from the same base — the parent stage's `equipment_base_cost_cents` — and apply the same two multipliers. The **pressure uplift** is one plus three percent for every hundred psig of the package's `design_pressure_psig`; the design pressure is divided by 100 and that quotient multiplied by 0.03, so a 250 psig package carries an uplift factor of 1.075 rather than a stepped one. The **code-stamp surcharge** is one plus the stamp's premium: nothing for `NONE`, four percent for `AWWA`, six percent for `NSF61`, and eleven and a half percent for `ASME_U`.

The `BUDGETARY` variant, stamped when the package is created, whenever it is revised, and again when it is submitted for review, multiplies the base by a nominal material factor of exactly 1.0 — the material is not yet committed, so the selected material's `cost_index` is deliberately ignored — then by the pressure uplift, then by the code-stamp surcharge, and rounds the product half up to whole cents exactly once, at the end. The result is `budgetary_cost_cents`. No escalation applies, because the release date is not yet committed either.

**Submitting for independent review.** An `ApplicationEngineer` submits a `DRAFT` package with an optional `submittal_note`, moving it `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW`, re-stamping `budgetary_cost_cents`, and advancing its `version` by 1. Submission requires that the parent stage be `SELECTED` or `ENGINEERED`; a superseded stage refuses the submission with `ERR_STAGE_SUPERSEDED`. All three material screens above are re-applied here.

**The review decision.** Only a `DesignReviewer` renders it, only on an `IN_REVIEW` package, and never on a package it designed itself — the segregation rule of section 2.7 applies with `ERR_SOD_DESIGNER_IS_REVIEWER`. The body carries `decision`, either `APPROVE` or `REQUEST_REVISION`, plus an optional `review_note`.

`REQUEST_REVISION` moves the package `IN_REVIEW → DRAFT`, increments `revision_round` by 1, stores the acting operator's identifier as `reviewed_by_user_id`, and advances the package's `version` by 1. This is a genuine cycle: a package may loop `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW → DRAFT` any number of times, each loop adding one to the revision round, and no cap applies.

`APPROVE` moves the package `IN_REVIEW → APPROVED`, stamps `approved_at` with the logical clock's current instant, stores `reviewed_by_user_id`, and freezes the firm price. Approval also cascades: the parent stage transitions `SELECTED → ENGINEERED` and advances its own `version` by 1, and the project's `delivery_posture` is recomputed — becoming `AT_RISK` when the newly computed variance flag is set. The package's `version` advances by 1.

**Firm pricing and the variance flag.** The `FIRM` variant is stamped at approval and differs from `BUDGETARY` in two ways. It multiplies by the *actual* selected material's `cost_index` instead of the nominal 1.0, and it applies **escalation** for the time the design has been sitting: count the whole thirty-day months elapsed between the parent stage's `created_at` and the logical clock's instant at the review decision, discarding any partial month, and multiply the price by one plus three-quarters of one percent for each of those whole months. So `firm_cost_cents` is the base multiplied by the material cost index, by the pressure uplift, by the code-stamp surcharge, and by the escalation factor, rounded half up to whole cents exactly once at the end. For identical package data, the firm price is strictly greater than the budgetary price whenever the material's cost index exceeds 1.0.

The two prices are then compared: `cost_variance_pct` is the firm price minus the budgetary price, divided by the budgetary price, rounded half up to four decimal places — a fraction, not a percentage figure, so a fifteen-percent overrun is stored as 0.15. `variance_flag` becomes true when that fraction is **strictly greater than** 0.15; a variance of exactly 0.15 leaves the flag false. A set flag is what drives the project's posture to `AT_RISK`, and through the posture it later raises the bid's contingency.

### 3.5. Or-Equal Submittals, Deviation Disposition and Pre-Approval as an Equal

Municipal specifications name a basis-of-design product, and a manufacturer that wants to supply something else must be pre-approved as an equal before it may bid. An `ApplicationEngineer` opens an **or-equal submittal** by supplying the owning `project_id` in the body, the `spec_section` the request is filed against, and the `named_basis_of_design` — the manufacturer named in the specification whose product the offered equipment claims equality with. All three are non-empty. The submittal enters `status` `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0, `compliance_score` at 100.00, `open_fundamental_count` at 0, `authored_by_user_id` set to the acting operator's identifier, `decided_by_user_id` null and `submitted_at` null. Submittal status walks `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `PRE_APPROVED` and `REJECTED`, with `PRE_APPROVED` and `REJECTED` terminal. Opening a submittal reads no project lifecycle state and leaves the project row untouched, and any number of submittals may be opened against one project.

Honesty about differences is the point of the exercise. On a `DRAFT` submittal — and only on a `DRAFT` submittal, `ERR_SUBMITTAL_INVALID_STATE` otherwise — the same engineer discloses each **specification deviation**, supplying the `spec_paragraph` the offered product departs from, a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MODERATE`, `MAJOR` or `FUNDAMENTAL`, a non-empty `description` narrating the departure, and the concurrency field `expected_or_equal_submittal_version` described in section 2.8. The deviation is created with `disposition` `PENDING`, `dispositioned_by_user_id` null, and its `weighted_penalty_points` computed immediately; the parent's `compliance_score` and `open_fundamental_count` are re-derived and the parent's `version` advances by 1. Deviation dispositions walk `PENDING` to one of `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN`, all three terminal.

**Scoring specification compliance.** Every child deviation of the submittal contributes, regardless of its disposition — withdrawn rows are *not* dropped from the sum, they simply contribute a zero multiplier. Each deviation's `weighted_penalty_points` is its severity weight multiplied by its disposition multiplier, rounded half up to two decimal places. Severity weights are pinned: 2 points for `MINOR`, 6 for `MODERATE`, 15 for `MAJOR` and 30 for `FUNDAMENTAL`. Disposition multipliers are equally pinned: a `PENDING` deviation counts at full weight, a `REJECTED` one also counts at full weight, an `ACCEPTED` one counts at one quarter of its weight, and a `WITHDRAWN` one counts at zero. The submittal's total penalty is the sum of those already-rounded per-deviation points, rounded half up to two decimal places again; `compliance_score` is 100 minus that total, rounded half up to two decimal places, and floored at 0 so it never goes negative. Separately, `open_fundamental_count` is the number of child deviations whose `severity` is `FUNDAMENTAL` and whose `disposition` is either `PENDING` or `REJECTED` — an accepted or withdrawn fundamental deviation is not "open". The score and the count are re-derived on every deviation disclosure, on every disposition, and on every engineer decision.

**Dispositioning a deviation.** The `EngineerOfRecord` — the owner's design engineer, not the manufacturer — dispositions a deviation that is still `PENDING`, supplying `disposition` and an optional `disposition_note`. A second disposition on the same deviation is refused with `ERR_DEVIATION_ALREADY_DISPOSITIONED`, which is the only state gate on this operation carrying an error code. All three outcomes store the acting operator's identifier as `dispositioned_by_user_id`, rewrite the deviation's `weighted_penalty_points`, re-derive the parent submittal's `compliance_score` and `open_fundamental_count`, and advance both the deviation's `version` and the submittal's `version` by 1. The parent submittal's own status is deliberately *not* read by this operation — `PENDING` is the only precondition, so a deviation may be dispositioned while its submittal is still `DRAFT` just as readily as during the `SUBMITTED` negotiation window that is its natural home. The three outcomes differ only in what they leave behind: an `ACCEPTED` disposition moves the deviation `PENDING → ACCEPTED` and leaves a quarter of its severity weight standing as penalty; a `REJECTED` disposition moves the deviation `PENDING → REJECTED`, leaves the full severity weight standing, and keeps a fundamental deviation counted as open; a `WITHDRAWN` disposition moves the deviation `PENDING → WITHDRAWN`, writes its `weighted_penalty_points` to 0.00 so it contributes nothing, and requires only that the deviation still be `PENDING` — it is the one disposition the submittal-rejection cascade below issues on the engineer's behalf.

**Transmitting the submittal.** The authoring `ApplicationEngineer` submits the package with an optional `transmittal_note`, moving it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and stamping `submitted_at` with the logical clock. Four preconditions guard the transmission. The submittal must be in `DRAFT`. It must carry at least one disclosed deviation — a submittal claiming perfect equality with no deviation at all is refused with `ERR_COMPLIANCE_SCORE_TOO_LOW`, the same code used when the score itself is short. Its `compliance_score` must be **at least** the pinned floor of 70.00, so a score of exactly 70.00 passes and 69.99 does not; falling short is `ERR_COMPLIANCE_SCORE_TOO_LOW`. And the logical clock must be **strictly before** the project's `bid_due_at`, otherwise `ERR_BID_WINDOW_CLOSED` — a submittal cannot be transmitted at or after the moment bidding closes. Those four are the whole guard: the owning project's own lifecycle state is not read, so a submittal may be transmitted whatever state the project sits in, and only the project's `bid_due_at` matters here. The submittal's `version` advances by 1, and the project row is not touched.

**The engineer's decision.** An `EngineerOfRecord` decides a `SUBMITTED` submittal — any other submittal state is `ERR_SUBMITTAL_INVALID_STATE` — by supplying `decision` and an optional `decision_note`. The three decisions are `PRE_APPROVED`, `REQUEST_REVISION` and `REJECTED`, and each is a full branch with its own gates and its own cascade.

`PRE_APPROVED` is the only decision the quality gates guard. It requires `open_fundamental_count` to be exactly 0 — a single still-open fundamental deviation refuses the pre-approval with `ERR_FUNDAMENTAL_DEVIATION_OPEN` — and it requires `compliance_score` to be at least 70.00, refused otherwise with `ERR_COMPLIANCE_SCORE_TOO_LOW`. On success the submittal moves `SUBMITTED → PRE_APPROVED`, the acting operator's identifier is stored as `decided_by_user_id`, every child deviation still `PENDING` is dispositioned `ACCEPTED` and the compliance score is re-derived over the new dispositions, and the owning project is unblocked for bidding: a project in `SCOPED` moves `SCOPED → BID_READY`, and a project sitting in `BID_BLOCKED` moves `BID_BLOCKED → BID_READY`. That second edge is the recovery path — a project may legitimately cycle `SCOPED → BID_BLOCKED → BID_READY` across successive submittal rounds, since a later submittal's pre-approval rescues a project blocked by an earlier rejection. Both the submittal's and the project's `version` advance by 1.

`REQUEST_REVISION` is never blocked by the compliance floor or by open fundamentals — returning a weak submittal for rework is exactly what it is for. It moves the submittal `SUBMITTED → DRAFT`, increments `revision_round` by 1, stores `decided_by_user_id`, leaves `submitted_at` holding the instant of the last transmission rather than clearing it, and applies one pointed side effect: every child deviation that is still `PENDING` **and** of severity `FUNDAMENTAL` is dispositioned `REJECTED`, after which `compliance_score` and `open_fundamental_count` are re-derived — so a returned submittal comes back with its fundamental objections on the record at full weight rather than unresolved. The submittal's `version` advances by 1. This is the negotiation cycle: `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → DRAFT` may repeat.

`REJECTED` is likewise never blocked by the compliance floor, and it is the deepest cascade in the system — three links, each caused by the one before it. The submittal moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` and `decided_by_user_id` is stored. **Link one:** every child deviation still `PENDING` transitions `PENDING → WITHDRAWN`. **Link two:** because those deviations are now `WITHDRAWN`, their `weighted_penalty_points` collapse to 0.00, so the compliance score is re-derived — and it *rises*, while `open_fundamental_count` *drops*; a rejected submittal therefore ends up with a better score than it had when it was rejected, which is correct and intended, because the penalty of an argument nobody is making any more is zero. **Link three:** because this submittal is rejected, the project loses its only path to pre-approval, so the owning project transitions to `BID_BLOCKED`. That third link fires only when the project is in `SCOPED` or `BID_READY` and no *other* or-equal submittal of the project already holds `PRE_APPROVED` status — a project with a surviving pre-approval is not blocked by an unrelated rejection. The submittal's `version` and, where the project moved, the project's `version` each advance by 1.

### 3.6. Bidding, the Evaluated-Price Waterfall and the Award

Only a `ContractsAdministrator` posts a bid, and the body carries exactly one number: `offered_price_cents`, the price the agency will actually see, a whole-cent integer of at least 0. Everything else on the bid is derived, because the point of the exercise is to establish what the work costs the manufacturer *before* comparing it with what the manufacturer is willing to charge.

Five gates guard the posting, and their relative order matters. A project that has not yet reached bidding — one still in `DRAFT`, `SCOPED` or `BID_BLOCKED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`. A project that is at `BID_SUBMITTED` or anywhere beyond it (`AWARDED`, `IN_FABRICATION`, `COMMISSIONING`, `GUARANTEED`, `REMEDIATION`, `SERVICE_ACTIVE` or `CLOSED`) falls past that first gate and is instead refused with `ERR_BID_ALREADY_SUBMITTED`, because its bid is already on record; only a project in `BID_READY` proceeds. Next, the engineering must be complete and priced: every treatment stage of the project that is still `SELECTED` or `ENGINEERED` must carry exactly one equipment package whose status is `APPROVED`, `RELEASED_FOR_FAB`, `FAT_PASSED` or `SHIPPED` and whose `firm_cost_cents` is strictly greater than 0, and at least one of the project's or-equal submittals must hold `PRE_APPROVED`; either shortfall is `ERR_OR_EQUAL_NOT_PREAPPROVED`. Then the bid window: the logical clock must be strictly before the project's `bid_due_at`, and a bid posted at or after that instant is refused with `ERR_BID_WINDOW_CLOSED`. And the acting operator must not be the author of the pre-approved submittal the bid rests on, per section 2.7 and `ERR_SOD_AUTHOR_IS_BIDDER`; where the project carries more than one `PRE_APPROVED` submittal the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from *every* one of their `authored_by_user_id` values, since the bid rests on all of them. Per the ladder of section 2.9 this segregation check is evaluated *before* the engineering-complete, bid-window and margin gates and after the two lifecycle gates, so a self-authored bid on a fully engineered project reports the segregation failure; when the project holds no `PRE_APPROVED` submittal at all there is nothing to compare against and `ERR_OR_EQUAL_NOT_PREAPPROVED` is what the caller sees.

**The waterfall.** The evaluated price is built in five pinned steps, and the order is part of the contract because step 2 would otherwise be circular.

*Step 1 — the base, rounded per line and only then summed.* One line item is taken per non-superseded stage of the project, from that stage's equipment package in `APPROVED` status or later; `DRAFT` and `IN_REVIEW` packages contribute nothing. Working through the lines in ascending `stage_index`, each line's amount is that package's `firm_cost_cents` multiplied by one plus the pinned overhead of 18.5 percent, rounded half up to whole cents. Those already-rounded line amounts are then added as integers to give `base_bid_amount_cents`. Summing the unrounded products and rounding once at the end is the wrong order and produces the wrong number.

*Step 2 — the bond rate tier, keyed on the base.* The payment and performance bond rate is selected from `base_bid_amount_cents` alone — never from the evaluated total that the premium is later folded into, which is exactly what makes the definition well founded. The first matching tier wins: a base of at most 100,000,000 cents (one million dollars) takes a rate of 1.25 percent; a base above that but at most 500,000,000 cents (five million dollars) takes 1.00 percent; a base above five million dollars takes 0.85 percent.

*Step 3 — the bond premium.* Multiply `base_bid_amount_cents` by the selected rate and round half up to whole cents, giving `bond_premium_cents`.

*Step 4 — the contingency, keyed on the base and the posture.* Multiply `base_bid_amount_cents` by the percentage that corresponds to the project's current `delivery_posture` — 2 percent when the posture is `ON_TRACK`, 5 percent when it is `AT_RISK`, and 9 percent when it is `HELD` — and round half up to whole cents, giving `contingency_cents`. A troubled project therefore prices itself more expensively, which is the intended feedback loop from section 2.10.

*Step 5 — the evaluated floor.* `evaluated_bid_price_cents` is the integer sum of `base_bid_amount_cents`, `bond_premium_cents` and `contingency_cents`.

**The margin floor.** The offered price must clear the evaluated floor by at least eight percent, and the test is deliberately performed as an integer cross-multiplication rather than a floating-point division: `offered_price_cents` multiplied by 10,000 must be at least `evaluated_bid_price_cents` multiplied by 10,800. A margin of exactly eight percent therefore passes; anything below is refused with `ERR_BID_MARGIN_BELOW_FLOOR`.

When every gate clears, the four derived figures are stored on the project, `offered_price_cents` is captured from the request body, the project moves `BID_READY → BID_SUBMITTED`, and its `version` advances by 1.

**The award.** Later, the same role records the owner's answer by supplying `award_decision`, either `AWARDED` or `NOT_AWARDED`. Either way the project must be in `BID_SUBMITTED`, and any other state is `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`. An `AWARDED` decision moves the project `BID_SUBMITTED → AWARDED`, copies `offered_price_cents` into `contract_amount_cents` — the number that liquidated damages will later be computed against — and stamps `awarded_at` with the logical clock. A `NOT_AWARDED` decision moves the project `BID_SUBMITTED → CLOSED`, which is terminal, and cascades one cleanup: every or-equal submittal of the project still sitting in `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` is written to `REJECTED`, since there is no longer a bid for them to support. Either decision advances the project's `version` by 1.

### 3.7. The Shop Floor: Release, Skid Build Dimensions, Bay Booking and Factory Acceptance Testing

**Release to fabrication.** A `FabricationManager` releases an `APPROVED` package with an optional `release_note`. The package must be `APPROVED` and its parent stage must be `ENGINEERED` — the latter is guaranteed for an `APPROVED` package by the review step of section 3.4, and a package whose stage has since been superseded is refused, like a package in the wrong status, with `ERR_PACKAGE_INVALID_STATE` — and the owning project must be in `AWARDED` or `IN_FABRICATION` (`ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE` otherwise) — nothing is cut before the contract exists. This single operation evaluates its package and stage checks *before* that project check, the one place where `ERR_PACKAGE_INVALID_STATE` outranks `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`: a `DRAFT` package on a project that has not been awarded reports the package failure, not the project one. Release moves the package `APPROVED → RELEASED_FOR_FAB`, stamps `released_at` with the logical clock, advances the package's `version` by 1, and on the project's *first* release moves the project `AWARDED → IN_FABRICATION` with the project's `delivery_posture` recomputed and its `version` advanced by 1.

Release also mints exactly one **skid** — the physical thing the shop builds. The skid records its `equipment_package_id`, receives a shop `serial_no` composed as the literal prefix `SKID-` followed by the parent stage's `stage_index`, a hyphen, and the package's `revision_round` at release (so the second-stage package released on its first revision round carries `SKID-2-1`). Its `footprint_sf` is snapshotted at creation as the stage technology's `footprint_sf_per_mgd` multiplied by the stage's `design_flow_mgd`, rounded **up** to a whole square foot. Its `requires_exclusive_bay` is true exactly when the package's `code_stamp_required` is `ASME_U`, because hydrostatic proof testing of a stamped vessel occupies the whole bay. The skid starts with `mechanical_status` `FRAME_SET`, `controls_status` `PANEL_BUILD`, `fat_readiness` `NOT_READY` and `rework_count` at 0.

**Two independent build dimensions.** A skid is built along two axes that advance separately and never interfere. The mechanical axis walks `FRAME_SET`, `PIPING_COMPLETE`, `HYDROTEST_PASSED` and `MECH_COMPLETE`, with `REWORK` as an off-nominal state it can be knocked back into. The controls axis walks `PANEL_BUILD`, `IO_CHECKED`, `LOOP_TESTED` and `CONTROLS_COMPLETE`. A `FabricationManager` advances one axis at a time by supplying `dimension` — either `MECHANICAL` or `CONTROLS` — a `to_state`, and an optional `milestone_note`. The requested state must be the *immediate* successor of the skid's current state on the addressed axis; skipping a step, repeating one, or naming a state that belongs to the other axis is refused with `ERR_SKID_MILESTONE_OUT_OF_ORDER`. On the mechanical axis, both `FRAME_SET → PIPING_COMPLETE` and the recovery edge `REWORK → PIPING_COMPLETE` are legal, and `PIPING_COMPLETE` is the *only* legal target from `REWORK`; from there the axis walks `PIPING_COMPLETE → HYDROTEST_PASSED` and `HYDROTEST_PASSED → MECH_COMPLETE` as usual. On the controls axis the legal edges are `PANEL_BUILD → IO_CHECKED`, `IO_CHECKED → LOOP_TESTED` and `LOOP_TESTED → CONTROLS_COMPLETE`. Every accepted milestone leaves the other axis's status untouched, recomputes `fat_readiness`, and advances the skid's `version` by 1.

**Derived FAT readiness.** `fat_readiness` is a three-way fold of both axes plus the parent package's status, and it is never supplied by a client. If `mechanical_status` is `REWORK`, readiness is `BLOCKED`. Otherwise, if `mechanical_status` is `MECH_COMPLETE` **and** `controls_status` is `CONTROLS_COMPLETE` **and** the parent package's status is `RELEASED_FOR_FAB`, readiness is `READY`. In every other case readiness is `NOT_READY` — which is the practical consequence of dimension independence: driving the mechanical axis all the way to `MECH_COMPLETE` while the controls axis still sits at `LOOP_TESTED` leaves the skid `NOT_READY`, not ready-ish.

One milestone carries a cascade. When the mechanical axis reaches `MECH_COMPLETE` while the parent package is sitting in `REWORK_HOLD`, the package transitions `REWORK_HOLD → RELEASED_FOR_FAB`, the project's `delivery_posture` is recomputed, and the package's `version` advances by 1. That is the rework cycle closing: `RELEASED_FOR_FAB → REWORK_HOLD → RELEASED_FOR_FAB` may repeat as many times as the shop needs.

**Booking a test bay.** A `FabricationManager` books a factory acceptance test run by supplying `skid_id`, `test_bay_id`, `scheduled_start_at`, `scheduled_end_at` — which must be strictly after the start — and the `expected_skid_version` of section 2.8. The skid must be genuinely testable: its parent package must be in `RELEASED_FOR_FAB` and its `fat_readiness` must be `READY`, otherwise `ERR_SKID_NOT_FAT_READY`. The target bay's `status` must be `AVAILABLE`; an `OUT_OF_SERVICE` bay is `ERR_TEST_BAY_UNAVAILABLE`.

Occupancy is then evaluated over a **buffered half-open interval**. The booked window is treated as inclusive of its start and exclusive of its end, and it is widened by the bay's `changeover_buffer_minutes` at *both* ends for the purpose of finding conflicts. Formally, an existing run on the same bay whose status is still `SCHEDULED` is in the overlap set when the incoming run's start is earlier than that run's end extended forward by the buffer, and that run's start is earlier than the incoming run's end extended forward by the buffer. Runs in `PASSED`, `FAILED`, `CONDITIONAL`, `CANCELLED` or `SUPERSEDED` are excluded from the overlap set entirely, and so is any still-`SCHEDULED` run *of the same skid*: because the incoming booking supersedes that run outright (see below), the run never conflicts with, exclusively blocks, or contributes footprint against its own replacement, and a retest may therefore be re-booked into the identical window in the identical bay. The practical consequence is that adjacency is a conflict: two windows that merely touch, with a gap of zero, collide, and so do two windows separated by strictly less than the changeover buffer, while a gap of exactly the changeover buffer or more is free.

Two capacity rules then apply to that overlap set. If any run in the set has `requires_exclusive_bay` true, or if the incoming run requires an exclusive bay while the overlap set is non-empty, the booking is refused with `ERR_TEST_BAY_EXCLUSIVE_CONFLICT`. Otherwise several runs may share one bay, provided the summed `footprint_sf` of the overlap set plus the incoming run's own footprint stays at or below the bay's `max_skid_footprint_sf`; the comparison is strictly greater-than, so a sum exactly equal to the bay's capacity is accepted, and only a sum above it is refused with `ERR_TEST_BAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

An accepted booking creates a run in `SCHEDULED`, copying `footprint_sf` from the skid as a snapshot and deriving `duration_minutes` as the whole minutes between `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at`, discarding any partial minute. Before the new row is scored, any run of the *same skid* still sitting in `SCHEDULED` is moved `SCHEDULED → SUPERSEDED` and its footprint-minutes are released from its bay's ledger — a retest legitimately displaces an outstanding booking, which is precisely why this operation is guarded by optimistic concurrency on the skid. The new run's `round_no` is then one greater than the number of runs on that skid whose status is `PASSED`, `FAILED`, `CONDITIONAL`, `SUPERSEDED` or `CANCELLED`, and is at least 1. Finally the new run's `footprint_sf` multiplied by its `duration_minutes` is added to the bay's `reserved_footprint_minutes`, advancing that bay's `version` by 1, and the skid's `version` advances by 1 as well — the skid row is bumped by the booking even though none of its own fields change, which is exactly why the next booking must quote the new number. A new run also records its own `punchlist_item_count` at 0 with `witnessed_by_user_id` and `result_note` null.

**Witnessing the result.** A `QualityEngineer` records the outcome of a `SCHEDULED` run — any other run status is `ERR_FAT_RUN_NOT_SCHEDULED` — by supplying `result` — one of `PASS`, `FAIL` or `CONDITIONAL` — a `punchlist_item_count` that is a required integer of at least 0, and an optional `result_note`. The skid must still be `READY` (`ERR_SKID_NOT_FAT_READY` otherwise), and the witness must not be the operator who designed the package on test, per section 2.7 and `ERR_SOD_DESIGNER_IS_REVIEWER`. Every outcome stores `witnessed_by_user_id`, `punchlist_item_count` and `result_note`, releases the run's footprint-minutes from the bay ledger by subtracting exactly `footprint_sf` multiplied by `duration_minutes` (and the bay's own `version` advances by 1 for that release), and advances the run's `version` by 1. The skid row, by contrast, is rewritten only on the failure branch below: after a recorded `PASS` or `CONDITIONAL` the skid keeps the `fat_readiness` it held at booking — `READY` — even though its package has moved on, and its `version` does not advance.

The requested result is not always the recorded result. A witness who asks for `PASS` while recording **more than** three open punchlist items is recorded as `CONDITIONAL`, never `PASSED`; the comparison is strictly greater-than, so a `PASS` carrying exactly three punchlist items stays `PASSED`. Any other requested result is recorded as asked.

- A recorded `PASS` moves the run `SCHEDULED → PASSED` and the parent package `RELEASED_FOR_FAB → FAT_PASSED`. If, after that transition, every equipment package on a non-superseded stage of the project is in `FAT_PASSED` or `SHIPPED`, the project moves `IN_FABRICATION → COMMISSIONING`, its `delivery_posture` is recomputed, and its `version` advances by 1.
- A recorded `CONDITIONAL` moves the run `SCHEDULED → CONDITIONAL` and changes no package and no skid state at all — the package stays in `RELEASED_FOR_FAB` — but the project's `delivery_posture` is recomputed to `AT_RISK`, because a conditional acceptance is a known open risk.
- A recorded `FAIL` moves the run `SCHEDULED → FAILED` and starts the three-link rework cascade. **Link one:** the skid's mechanical axis reverts `MECH_COMPLETE → REWORK`, `rework_count` increments by 1, `fat_readiness` recomputes to `BLOCKED`, and the skid's `version` advances by 1 — while `controls_status` is deliberately preserved, since a mechanical failure does not undo controls integration. **Link two:** the parent package transitions `RELEASED_FOR_FAB → REWORK_HOLD` and its `version` advances by 1. **Link three:** the project's `delivery_posture` becomes `HELD`.

**Cancelling a booking.** A `FabricationManager` cancels a `SCHEDULED` run with an optional `cancel_reason`, moving it `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` and subtracting exactly the footprint-minutes the booking added, restoring the bay's `reserved_footprint_minutes` to its exact pre-booking value and advancing that bay's `version` by 1; the run's `version` advances by 1, while the skid and the package are left untouched. `PASSED`, `FAILED`, `CONDITIONAL`, `CANCELLED` and `SUPERSEDED` are all terminal for a run, and a run in any of them can be neither witnessed nor cancelled.

### 3.8. Commissioning and the Performance Guarantee Demonstration

Factory acceptance proves the equipment works on the shop floor. The performance guarantee proves it produces the promised water in the field, and it is the last and most consequential test in the domain: failing it twice puts the project into remediation and starts liquidated damages running.

**Recording a demonstration.** A `CommissioningEngineer` records a demonstration by supplying `project_id`, `skid_id`, `window_start_at`, `window_end_at` — strictly after the start — and `readings`, a non-empty array of `{ parameter, measured_value }` objects — each entry must itself be a JSON object — whose `parameter` values are drawn from the eight declared objective parameters of section 3.3 and whose `measured_value` is never negative. Membership in *this project's* own objective set is not checked at recording time: a reading naming a parameter the project does not target is accepted and then simply never consulted by the certification evaluation. Each entry is stored reduced to exactly those two keys, any other key in an entry being discarded, and the stored `readings` array is carried on the demonstration row and echoed back on every read of it. Several rows for the same parameter are expected and normal: a month of daily samples for one objective is many readings, not one.

Two gates apply in a pinned order. The retest cap comes first: if the project already has two demonstrations whose `result` is `PASSED`, `FAILED` or `CONDITIONAL_PASS`, the request is refused with `ERR_MAX_DEMO_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED`, and this check deliberately outranks the commissioning prerequisite below, because a project that has burned both rounds has by construction already left `COMMISSIONING` for `GUARANTEED` or `REMEDIATION` and the retest cap is the more specific diagnosis. Only then is the prerequisite checked: the project must be in `COMMISSIONING`, the referenced skid's equipment package must be in `FAT_PASSED` or `SHIPPED`, and the skid must belong to the same project — any shortfall is `ERR_GUARANTEE_PREREQ_NOT_MET`.

**Window occupancy on the skid, contrasted with the bay rule.** A demonstration window is a **closed** interval on the skid: both `window_start_at` and `window_end_at` are inclusive. There is no changeover buffer and no aggregate capacity, because a single skid under demonstration cannot serve two demonstrations at once. A conflict exists when any demonstration on the same skid whose `result` is still `PENDING` satisfies both: the incoming start is at or before that demonstration's end, and that demonstration's start is at or before the incoming end. Any conflict is `ERR_DEMO_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Because both endpoints are inclusive, a new window whose start is exactly the incumbent's end *is* rejected — which is the opposite of nothing, and precisely the opposite behaviour from the half-open bay interval of section 3.7, where the end instant is exclusive but a buffer is added to both ends. Demonstrations in `PASSED`, `FAILED` or `CONDITIONAL_PASS` never conflict with anything.

An accepted demonstration is created with `result` `PENDING`, `evaluation` an empty array, `min_margin_pct` null, `certified_by_user_id` null, `recorded_by_user_id` from the acting operator, and `round_no` one greater than the count of demonstrations on that project whose `result` is `PASSED`, `FAILED` or `CONDITIONAL_PASS`. Two cascades fire alongside: the skid's equipment package moves `FAT_PASSED → SHIPPED` (equipment being demonstrated in the field has by definition left the shop), and the project's `guarantee_status` moves `NOT_STARTED → DEMONSTRATING`. The project's `version` advances by 1.

**Certification: the threshold evaluation.** A `QualityEngineer` certifies a demonstration by supplying `certification`, either `PASS` or `FAIL`, and an optional `certification_note`. The demonstration's `result` must still be `PENDING` — an already-certified demonstration is `ERR_DEMO_ALREADY_CERTIFIED` — and the certifier must not be the operator who recorded the readings, per section 2.7 and `ERR_SOD_RECORDER_IS_CERTIFIER`.

The evaluation walks the project's treatment objectives whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, in ascending `parameter` and then ascending `created_at` and then insertion order; objectives that have been `RETIRED` are never evaluated and never appear in the output. If every objective of the project has been retired since the readings were recorded, so that the active set is empty, the certification is refused outright with `ERR_NO_TREATMENT_OBJECTIVES` and nothing is stored. For each active objective:

1. The samples are every reading on the demonstration whose `parameter` matches the objective's `parameter`. If there are none, the whole certification is refused with `ERR_DEMO_READING_MISSING` — nothing partial is stored.
2. The `compared_value` depends on the objective's declared averaging `basis`. On a `MAX_DAY` basis it is the **maximum** of the samples; on a `MONTHLY_AVERAGE` basis it is the **arithmetic mean** of the samples. Either way it is rounded half up to three decimal places. Because the two bases aggregate the same numbers differently, one reading set can pass on one basis and fail on the other — an objective with a single high outlier fails `MAX_DAY` while its average stays comfortably compliant.
3. The objective passes when `compared_value` is less than **or equal to** its `limit_value`; a compared value exactly on the limit passes.
4. The margin is the shortfall from the limit expressed as a percentage of the limit: the limit minus the compared value, divided by the limit, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places. It is negative when the objective fails.

Those six per-objective values — `parameter`, `basis`, `limit_value`, `compared_value`, `margin_pct` and `passed`, and nothing else — are stored in the `evaluation` array in the iteration order above, and `min_margin_pct` is the smallest `margin_pct` in that array, taken across every evaluated objective whether it passed or failed.

The overall `result` is then decided by a three-way band. If the certifier supplied `FAIL`, or if any objective did not pass, the result is `FAILED` — a `FAIL` certification is authoritative even when every number is comfortably compliant. Otherwise, if `min_margin_pct` is at least the guarantee margin band of 5.0 percent, the result is `PASSED`; the comparison is at-least, so a smallest margin of exactly 5.0 passes. Otherwise every objective was met but the thinnest one was met by less than the band, and the result is `CONDITIONAL_PASS`. Whatever the outcome, `evaluation`, `min_margin_pct` and `certified_by_user_id` are written, the project's `delivery_posture` is recomputed, and the demonstration's `version` and the project's `version` each advance by 1.

**The success chain.** A `PASSED` certification moves the demonstration `PENDING → PASSED` and starts a four-link cascade. Link one: the project's `guarantee_status` becomes `MET`. Link two: the project moves `COMMISSIONING → GUARANTEED`. Link three: every service agreement of that project sitting in `PENDING_ACTIVATION` moves to `ACTIVE`, each stamping `activated_at` with the logical clock, deriving `expires_at`, seeding `reserve_available_cents`, and advancing its own `version` by 1 — the details are in section 3.9. Link four: because at least one service agreement of the project is now `ACTIVE`, the project immediately moves `GUARANTEED → SERVICE_ACTIVE`. A project with no service agreement yet stops at link two and rests in `GUARANTEED` until one is opened.

**The failure chain and liquidated damages.** A `CONDITIONAL_PASS` moves the demonstration `PENDING → CONDITIONAL_PASS`, recomputes the project's `delivery_posture` to `AT_RISK` and, if the demonstration's `round_no` is below the two-round cap, changes nothing else: `guarantee_status` stays `DEMONSTRATING` and a retest round is permitted. A `FAILED` result moves the demonstration `PENDING → FAILED` and behaves the same way at a non-final round.

But when the demonstration that ends in `FAILED` or `CONDITIONAL_PASS` is the **second** round, the project's last chance is gone. It moves `COMMISSIONING → REMEDIATION`, its `guarantee_status` becomes `DEFAULTED`, its `delivery_posture` is recomputed (to `AT_RISK` or `HELD`), and liquidated damages are computed and written to `liquidated_damages_cents`. Note carefully that a *conditional* second round defaults the guarantee exactly as a failed one does: meeting every limit by a thin margin twice is not a pass.

The damages formula reads the project's own `contract_amount_cents` and `guarantee_deadline_at` against the logical clock. The elapsed delay is the whole days between `guarantee_deadline_at` and now, discarding any partial day and floored at zero. The daily rate is `contract_amount_cents` multiplied by 0.05 percent, rounded half up to whole cents *once*. The cap is `contract_amount_cents` multiplied by 5 percent, rounded half up to whole cents. The stored figure is the smaller of the daily rate multiplied by the whole elapsed days, and the cap. Because the daily amount is rounded once and only then multiplied by whole days, the cap binds at exactly 100 days of delay. And when the certification happens at or before `guarantee_deadline_at`, the elapsed days are 0 and `liquidated_damages_cents` stays 0 — the guarantee has still defaulted and the project is still in `REMEDIATION`, but no money is owed for lateness that never happened.

### 3.9. Closeout Deliverables, Long-Term Service and the Consumable Reserve

**Three closeout deliverables.** An `OwnerRepresentative` accepts or rejects each of the three non-equipment deliverables by supplying a `deliverable_type` — `OM_MANUAL`, `OPERATOR_TRAINING` or `SPARE_PARTS_LIST`, addressing `om_manual_status`, `operator_training_status` and `spare_parts_list_status` respectively — together with a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`. An `ACCEPT` writes the addressed field to `ACCEPTED`; a `REJECT` writes it to `REJECTED`, from which it can be accepted later. A deliverable that is already `ACCEPTED` is closed to further decisions in either direction and the request is refused with `ERR_DELIVERABLE_ALREADY_ACCEPTED`: acceptance is not revocable. Each accepted decision advances the project's `version` by 1. This operation reads no project lifecycle state, so a deliverable can be dispositioned at any point in the project's life.

**Opening a service agreement.** A `ContractsAdministrator` opens the long-term service, membrane replacement and consumable supply agreement by supplying `term_months` (at least 12), `annual_fee_cents` (strictly positive), `reserve_funded_cents` (strictly positive) and `membrane_installed_at`, the installation instant of the membrane elements that will serve as the warranty-credit baseline. The project must be in `GUARANTEED` or `SERVICE_ACTIVE`; any other lifecycle state is `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`. And all three closeout deliverable statuses must be `ACCEPTED` — a single `PENDING` or `REJECTED` deliverable is `ERR_DELIVERABLES_INCOMPLETE`. Owner acceptance of the paperwork is a hard prerequisite for the service relationship, not a formality.

A new agreement starts with `reserve_committed_accrual_cents`, `reserve_committed_settled_cents`, `reserve_available_cents` and `settlement_variance_cents` all at 0. Its status depends on where the project already stands. Opened against a project in `SERVICE_ACTIVE`, it is created in `PENDING_ACTIVATION` with `activated_at` and `expires_at` null. Opened against a project sitting in `GUARANTEED`, it activates immediately: it is created `ACTIVE`, `activated_at` is stamped with the logical clock, `reserve_available_cents` is seeded to `reserve_funded_cents`, and the project moves `GUARANTEED → SERVICE_ACTIVE` — the same link-four cascade as section 3.8, reached from the other direction. Either way the project's `version` advances by 1.

**Activation.** Whether it happens at creation or later as the cascade from a passing certification, activation always does the same four things: `activated_at` is stamped with the logical clock; `expires_at` is derived as `activated_at` plus `term_months` multiplied by thirty days (a pinned thirty-day month, not a calendar month); `reserve_available_cents` is written as `reserve_funded_cents` minus `reserve_committed_accrual_cents`; and the agreement's `version` advances by 1 — with the one exception that an agreement activated *at creation* is minted directly in `ACTIVE`, so it is born at `version` 1 with `created_at` and `updated_at` equal and no bump applies. The owning project must be in `GUARANTEED` for a `PENDING_ACTIVATION → ACTIVE` transition to occur.

**Posting a draw.** A `ContractsAdministrator` posts a consumable or membrane-replacement draw against the reserve by supplying `draw_type` (`CONSUMABLE` or `MEMBRANE_REPLACEMENT` — `REVERSAL` is a server-minted type and is rejected as an undeclared enum member here), `item_code`, `quantity`, `catalog_unit_cost_cents` and `invoiced_unit_cost_cents` — both unit costs being integers strictly greater than 0, enforced as ordinary shape constraints with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and the `expected_service_agreement_version` of section 2.8. The `quantity` must be an integer of at least 1, enforced as a business rule with `ERR_INVALID_DRAW_QUANTITY` rather than as a schema constraint, so a well-formed request carrying zero or a negative count reaches that rule and is refused by it.

Before any of that, the **lazy expiry sweep** runs. When the logical clock is at or after the agreement's `expires_at`, the agreement is written to `EXPIRED` — `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` or `SUSPENDED → EXPIRED`, whichever applies — its `version` advances by 1, the status change is persisted, and the draw is then refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_EXPIRED`. No draw row is created and the reserve totals are untouched. The sweep runs before every other draw check except authentication, authorization and existence, so an expired agreement never reports a reserve problem or a quantity problem — it reports expiry. `EXPIRED` is terminal. Only after the sweep is the agreement's status checked: a `SUSPENDED` or `PENDING_ACTIVATION` agreement accepts no draw and is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

**Two money lines per draw.** Every draw carries two independently computed amounts, and only one of them gates.

*The accrual line — the one that governs.* `accrual_amount_cents` is `quantity` multiplied by `catalog_unit_cost_cents`, marked up by 22 percent, rounded half up to whole cents. This is the figure the reserve is checked against and the figure that drives suspension.

*The settled line — reported, never gating.* `settled_amount_cents` starts from `quantity` multiplied by `invoiced_unit_cost_cents`, uplifted by 6.5 percent freight, rounded half up to whole cents, and then has the warranty credit subtracted. For a `MEMBRANE_REPLACEMENT` draw the credit is prorated over the unexpired part of a sixty-month membrane warranty: the months in service are the whole thirty-day months between the agreement's `membrane_installed_at` and now, discarding any partial month; the months remaining are sixty minus that, floored at zero; and `warranty_credit_cents` is `quantity` multiplied by `invoiced_unit_cost_cents` multiplied by one percent per remaining month, rounded half up to whole cents. For a `CONSUMABLE` draw `warranty_credit_cents` is 0. Because the two lines use different unit costs and different rates, identical draw data yields two different numbers, and the gap between them is reported — never hidden — as `settlement_variance_cents`.

*The fiscal gate.* The draw is refused with `ERR_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT` when its accrual line is **strictly greater** than the agreement's current `reserve_available_cents`. A draw whose accrual line exactly equals the available reserve is therefore accepted, spends the reserve down to exactly zero, and drives the agreement `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` in the same operation. The settled line is never consulted by this gate, however large it is.

An accepted draw creates an append-only row recording `service_agreement_id`, `draw_type`, `item_code`, `quantity`, both unit costs, both computed line amounts, `warranty_credit_cents`, and `posted_by_user_id` from the acting operator, with `reverses_draw_id` null. The agreement then adds the accrual line to `reserve_committed_accrual_cents`, adds the settled line to `reserve_committed_settled_cents`, decreases `reserve_available_cents` by the accrual line, recomputes `settlement_variance_cents` as the settled total minus the accrual total, and advances its `version` by 1. Draw rows are rounded individually as they are written and the agreement totals are integer sums of those already-rounded lines; re-deriving the totals from unrounded products and rounding once at the end is the wrong order.

**Reversal instead of mutation.** A posted draw is never edited and never deleted. A `ContractsAdministrator` compensates it by minting a reversal with an optional `reversal_reason`: a new row with `draw_type` `REVERSAL`, `reverses_draw_id` pointing at the target, the target's `item_code`, `quantity`, `catalog_unit_cost_cents` and `invoiced_unit_cost_cents` copied across verbatim, `accrual_amount_cents` and `settled_amount_cents` equal to the exact negations of the target's amounts, its own `warranty_credit_cents` at 0 — the credit is not re-derived and not negated, it lives on the row being compensated — and `posted_by_user_id` from the operator posting the reversal rather than from the original. A target that has already been reversed — meaning some row already carries its id in `reverses_draw_id` — cannot be reversed again, and neither can a row whose own `draw_type` is `REVERSAL`; either attempt is `ERR_DRAW_ALREADY_REVERSED`. The lazy expiry sweep applies here too, and a reversal against an expired agreement is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_EXPIRED` — the sweep running before the already-reversed check. Expiry is the *only* agreement-level bar on a reversal: no active-state check applies, so a `SUSPENDED` agreement may always be compensated (which is what makes the recovery below reachable) and so may one still in `PENDING_ACTIVATION`. A reversal takes no expected-version field.

A reversal subtracts the reversed accrual from `reserve_committed_accrual_cents`, subtracts the reversed settled amount from `reserve_committed_settled_cents`, adds the reversed accrual back to `reserve_available_cents`, recomputes `settlement_variance_cents`, and advances the agreement's `version` by 1. If the agreement was `SUSPENDED` and the reversal makes `reserve_available_cents` strictly positive again, it returns `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`; the suspension cycle `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` may repeat as often as the money requires. Because every reversal is the exact integer negation of the row it compensates, the conserved-ledger promises of section 2.11 hold to the cent: the accrual commitment and the available reserve always sum to `reserve_funded_cents`, and posting a draw and then reversing it returns both figures to exactly the values they held beforehand.
