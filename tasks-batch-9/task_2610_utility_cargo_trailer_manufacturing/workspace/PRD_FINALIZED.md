# Utility & Cargo Trailer Manufacturing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A utility and cargo trailer plant lives or dies by two things that rarely sit in the same system: a federally auditable paper trail and a sales channel that never runs out of stock. On the plant side, every trailer that leaves the building carries a certification label stating a Gross Vehicle Weight Rating that engineering must be able to defend from axle, tire, frame and coupler ratings, plus a Vehicle Identification Number that will follow the trailer through titling for the rest of its life. On the sales side, franchised dealers order against a monthly allocation quota, finance their stock through a floor-plan credit line, and file warranty claims that must be reimbursed at their own negotiated labor rate. Today those two worlds are reconciled by spreadsheet, and the reconciliation fails in exactly the places that matter — a label issued against a superseded model revision, a floor-plan draw that was never released after a cancellation, a claim paid at full coverage when the failure fell outside the warranty window.

This document specifies the API that replaces that reconciliation. The system tracks a trailer from a model configuration through jig-based frame welding with dimensional control, regulatory lighting and braking verification, custom option installation, road testing, VIN and certification-label issuance, dealer allocation and delivery, and finally warranty administration and part shipment. Two ideas dominate the design. First, a physical trailer carries **two independent status dimensions** at once — how far the build has progressed and whether regulatory verification has been achieved — and they advance on different operations, re-coupling only at the moment brakes and axles are signed off. Second, every derived quantity in the system — a dealer's outstanding floor-plan balance, a jig's booked minutes, a unit's payload capacity, a model's active build count — is a **conserved rollup** of the rows beneath it, recomputed on every mutation and every cascade, so that a create-then-reverse pair leaves the plant's books byte-identical to where they started.

Because regulatory work is auditable work, the specification is deliberately strict about who may do what and about what a record may still be trusted to say. The certification officer who signs a model's GVWR may never also issue the label for a trailer built to that model. A unit's rating is a snapshot pinned when it entered the weld jig, not a live read of the model that has since been revised. And the two shared plant assets whose calendars and balances are consumed by concurrent writers — the dealer account and the weld jig — are guarded by explicit optimistic-concurrency fields so that no client can price or book against a view of the world it never observed.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven recognized roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs before anything else, including before the system looks at the request body or resolves any identifier in the path.

The seven roles are `PlantEngineer`, `CertificationOfficer`, `WeldSupervisor`, `AssemblyTech`, `QualityInspector`, `SalesAdmin`, and `Dealer`. A `PlantEngineer` is the plant engineering administrator: it owns model configurations and the weld-jig and test-lane asset registry, but it may never certify a model it submitted, because the certification decision belongs exclusively to `CertificationOfficer`. A `CertificationOfficer` is the regulatory certification officer: it decides GVWR certification and mints VINs, labels and Manufacturer's Certificates of Origin. A `WeldSupervisor` opens build records, books jig windows and records dimensional inspections. An `AssemblyTech` installs lighting, brake and axle systems and custom options, but may neither book nor result a road test — quality sign-off is the exclusive lane of `QualityInspector`, which books road tests and records their brake and lighting results. A `SalesAdmin` is the dealer sales and warranty administrator: it enrolls dealers, allocates stock, invoices, administers the floor plan and adjudicates claims. A `Dealer` is a franchised dealership. When the role named in `X-User-Role` does not appear in the authorized-role list of the endpoint being called (see §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

Six of the seven roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `PlantEngineer`, `CertificationOfficer`, `WeldSupervisor`, `AssemblyTech`, `QualityInspector`, or `SalesAdmin`, the value of `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — a certification decision writes it to the model's `certified_by_user_id`, a frame inspection and a road-test result each write it to their `inspector_user_id`, a label issuance writes it to `issued_by_user_id`, and a claim review writes it to `reviewed_by_user_id` — and the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.11 compares two such recorded values to each other, but the server never compares an opaque `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`.

The `Dealer` role is different: its identity is **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `Dealer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Dealer` entity, and every ownership check compares the dealer-referencing field of the touched resource to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Dealer` caller must supply its own `id` as the body `dealer_id` when placing a dealer order or filing a warranty claim; when cancelling a dealer order, the order's own `dealer_id` must equal `X-User-ID`; when answering an information request on a claim, the claim's `dealer_id` must equal `X-User-ID`; when reading a single dealer record, the dealer identifier in the path must equal `X-User-ID`; and when listing dealer orders or warranty claims, the `dealer_id` filter must be supplied and must equal `X-User-ID`. Any of these mismatches is rejected with `ERR_DEALER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). A `SalesAdmin` is deliberately unscoped across dealers and is never subject to this check. A `Dealer` never writes a production record of any kind — no unit, reservation, inspection, systems stage, option install, road test or certification label.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints window their results with two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps how many items come back; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. The `offset` parameter says how many items to skip and defaults to 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Unless a specific endpoint's description in §4 states otherwise, every list endpoint returns its resources sorted by creation timestamp — `created_at` — in ascending order. Two list channels override this: a jig's reservations and a lane's road tests are both ordered by `window_start_at` ascending, and a unit's frame inspections come back in `inspection_seq` order. Where two rows tie on the ordering key — the same `created_at`, or the same `window_start_at`, which the logical clock of §2.4 makes commonplace — the tie is broken by the order in which the rows were written, oldest write first.

List responses carry four pagination fields inside the `meta` object of the envelope: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`. `total_count` reports the number of items matching the request's filters before the window is applied; `has_more` is true exactly when items remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. The Logical Plant Clock

Every window comparison, calendar-month rollup, calendar-year rollup and warranty-day computation in this system reads a **logical clock** rather than the host's wall clock. Until the clock is moved, the logical now is `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Any of the seven roles may move it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, and the response reports the new setting as `current_utc`. Moving the clock takes effect immediately for every subsequent request; it does not retroactively rewrite timestamps already stored on records.

The clock matters in more places than it first appears. A dealer's monthly and year-to-date allocation counters are bounded by the logical clock's current UTC calendar month and calendar year, not by any rolling window. The VIN's model-year character is selected from the logical clock's calendar year. Warranty expiry is computed from the delivery instant the logical clock reported at the time of delivery. Any test-driving of time-dependent behaviour therefore proceeds by setting the clock and then acting, in that order.

### 2.5. Record Identity, Audit Fields and Immutable Inspection Records

Every resource the API creates carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID, with no type prefix and no plant-specific encoding. Clients never supply an `id`. Alongside it, resources carry `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly one on every mutation of that row. `TrailerModel`, `Dealer`, `WeldJig`, `TestLane`, `TrailerUnit`, `JigReservation`, `OptionInstall`, `RoadTest`, `CertificationLabel`, `DealerOrder` and `WarrantyClaim` all carry the full set. Recomputing a derived field counts as a mutation: when a dealer's outstanding floor-plan balance is recomputed, or a unit's payload capacity is re-derived, that row's `updated_at` moves and its `version` increments even though no client field was touched. Refreshing a model's `active_unit_count` is the deliberate exception. The recomputed count is written to the model row, but that refresh on its own moves neither the model's `updated_at` nor its `version`; the model's `version` advances only on the two operations §3.1 names as incrementing it, a certification submission and a certification decision. Opening a build record against a model, scrapping a trailer built to it and delivering one therefore each leave the model's `active_unit_count` refreshed while the model's `updated_at` and `version` stand exactly where they were, so opening a build record never shifts the revision number that a later jig reservation will pin.

`FrameInspection` is the deliberate exception. A dimensional inspection is an immutable regulatory record: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version`, and there is no endpoint that updates or deletes one. A unit's inspection history therefore grows monotonically, and a corrected measurement is expressed as a new inspection rather than an edit of the old one.

### 2.6. Resource Addressing and Path Resolution Order

Every entity in this system is addressed by its own top-level collection. A model, a dealer, a jig, a lane, a unit, an option install, a road test, a dealer order and a warranty claim are each reached directly by their own identifier; parent context travels in foreign-key body fields, never in a nested URL chain. Sub-resource list channels exist — a unit's frame inspections, a unit's option installs, a unit's certification labels, a unit's jig reservations, a unit's road tests, a jig's reservations, a lane's road tests — but they are reverse-lookup read channels only, never the authoritative address of a resource.

When a path contains an identifier, it is resolved before the body is examined, and when more than one identifier appears the resolution is strictly left to right. The first identifier that names no existing row terminates the request with a 404 carrying the error for that resource type: `ERR_MODEL_NOT_FOUND` for a trailer model, `ERR_DEALER_NOT_FOUND` for a dealer, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` for a trailer unit, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` for a dealer order, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` for a warranty claim, and `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` for a weld jig, a test lane, a road test or an option install. Once the path resolves, every foreign key in the request body is resolved next, under the same 404 vocabulary — a body `model_id`, `unit_id`, `dealer_id`, `lane_id` or `order_id` that names no row produces `ERR_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEALER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` respectively. Body foreign-key resolution applies to unit creation, jig reservation, road-test booking, order placement, order allocation, claim filing and floor-plan adjustment.

### 2.7. Validation Precedence on the Plant Floor

Plant operators routinely submit a request that violates more than one rule at once — a stale jig version *and* a window conflict, a credit hold *and* an exhausted quota. The order in which the system reports those violations is not incidental; it is a total ordering, and the response always carries the error belonging to the earliest rule in that ordering. The rule that fires masks every later rule completely, so a request that trips step 3 never reveals whether it would also have tripped step 20. The ordering, from earliest to latest, is:

1. **Authentication** — both headers present and `X-User-Role` recognized, on every endpoint. Failure yields `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role permission** — the role appears in the called endpoint's authorized-role list, on every endpoint. Failure yields `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. **Dealer scoping** — the `Dealer` ownership comparisons of §2.1, applied to seven operations: placing a dealer order, cancelling a dealer order, filing a warranty claim, resubmitting a warranty claim, reading a single dealer, listing dealer orders, and listing warranty claims. Failure yields `ERR_DEALER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
4. **Path existence** — every identifier in the path resolves, left to right, on every endpoint, under the 404 vocabulary of §2.6.
5. **Body foreign-key existence** — every foreign key in the body resolves, on unit creation, jig reservation, road-test booking, order placement, order allocation, claim filing and floor-plan adjustment, under the same 404 vocabulary.
6. **Schema conformance** — on every endpoint: required fields present, enum members recognized, the numeric constraints of the entity definitions respected (including that `design_braked_axle_count` is not greater than `axle_count`, and that an option `quantity` is at least 1), `window_end_at` strictly later than `window_start_at`, every entry of `part_lines` carrying a positive integer `unit_cost_cents` and `quantity`, and every timestamp parseable as ISO 8601 UTC. Failure yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
7. **Dealer staleness** — `expected_dealer_version` matches the dealer's current `version`, on order allocation. Failure yields `ERR_STALE_DEALER_VERSION`.
8. **Jig staleness** — `expected_weld_jig_version` matches the jig's current `version`, on jig reservation. Failure yields `ERR_STALE_JIG_VERSION`.
9. **Unit build-state admission** — the unit's `build_status` is in the set the requested step allows, on jig reservation, frame inspection, systems installation, road-test booking, label issuance and option installation. Failure yields `ERR_UNIT_BUILD_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).
10. **Compliance stage ordering** — the systems stages run in order on the compliance dimension, on systems installation. Failure yields `ERR_COMPLIANCE_STAGE_OUT_OF_ORDER` (HTTP 409).
11. **Model state admission** — the model's `certification_status` permits the requested transition, on certification submission and certification decision. Failure yields `ERR_MODEL_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).
12. **Order state admission** — the order's `status` permits the requested transition, on allocation, delivery and cancellation. Failure yields `ERR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).
13. **Claim state admission** — the claim's `status` permits the requested transition, on review, resubmission and part shipment. Failure yields `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).
14. **Label singularity** — the unit carries no certification label whose `status` is `ISSUED`, on label issuance. Failure yields `ERR_LABEL_ALREADY_ISSUED` (HTTP 409).
15. **Road-test result singularity** — the road test's `status` is `SCHEDULED`, on recording a road-test result. Failure yields `ERR_ROAD_TEST_ALREADY_RESULTED` (HTTP 409).
16. **Option-line mutability** — the option install's `status` is `PENDING`, on option sign-off and on option removal. Failure yields `ERR_OPTION_NOT_REMOVABLE` (HTTP 409).
17. **Model buildability** — the referenced model's `certification_status` is `DRAFT`, `IN_ENGINEERING_REVIEW` or `CERTIFIED`, on unit creation. Failure yields `ERR_MODEL_NOT_BUILDABLE` (HTTP 409).
18. **Model certification** — the unit's model has `certification_status` exactly `CERTIFIED`, on jig reservation. Failure yields `ERR_MODEL_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 409).
19. **Segregation of duties** — `X-User-ID` differs from the model's `certified_by_user_id`, on label issuance. Failure yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).
20. **Lighting compliance** — the lamp and reflector requirements hold, on the `LIGHTING` stage of systems installation. Failure yields `ERR_LIGHTING_NOT_COMPLIANT` (HTTP 422).
21. **Brake compliance** — the brake and breakaway requirements hold, on the `BRAKE_AND_AXLE` stage of systems installation. Failure yields `ERR_BRAKE_REQUIREMENT_UNMET` (HTTP 422).
22. **Unit compliance gate** — the unit's `compliance_status` is `FULLY_COMPLIANT`, on road-test booking and on label issuance. Failure yields `ERR_UNIT_NOT_COMPLIANT` (HTTP 422).
23. **Code uniqueness** — `model_code`, `dealer_code`, `jig_code`, `lane_code`, `serial_number` and `claim_ref` are each unique across their collection, on model creation, dealer enrollment, jig commissioning, lane commissioning, unit creation and claim filing. Failure yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (HTTP 409).
24. **Jig tooling match** — the jig's `frame_design_code` equals the unit's model's `frame_design_code`, on jig reservation. Failure yields `ERR_JIG_DESIGN_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).
25. **Explicit-unit model match** — when the allocation request names a `unit_id`, that unit's `model_id` equals the order's `model_id`, on order allocation. Failure yields `ERR_UNIT_MODEL_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).
26. **Payload budget** — after charging the install, the unit's `payload_capacity_lbs` is not negative, on option installation. Failure yields `ERR_PAYLOAD_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).
27. **Labor-hour ceiling** — the claimed `labor_hours` does not exceed the published flat-rate ceiling for the `complaint_code`, on claim filing and on claim resubmission. Failure yields `ERR_LABOR_HOURS_EXCEED_FLAT_RATE` (HTTP 422).
28. **Explicit-unit eligibility** — when the allocation request names a `unit_id`, that unit satisfies the stock-eligibility predicate, on order allocation. Failure yields `ERR_UNIT_NOT_ALLOCATABLE` (HTTP 409).
29. **Stock availability** — when the allocation request omits `unit_id`, the selector's candidate set is not empty, on order allocation. Failure yields `ERR_NO_ALLOCATABLE_UNIT` (HTTP 409).
30. **Delivered-unit requirement** — the claimed unit's `build_status` is `SHIPPED` and its `warranty_start_date` is not null, on claim filing. Failure yields `ERR_UNIT_NOT_DELIVERED` (HTTP 409).
31. **Dealer credit standing** — the dealer's `status` is `ACTIVE`, on order allocation. Failure yields `ERR_DEALER_CREDIT_HOLD` (HTTP 409).
32. **Jig window conflict** — the requested welding window does not collide with the jig's occupancy calendar, on jig reservation. Failure yields `ERR_JIG_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).
33. **Lane capacity** — the requested test window does not breach the lane's concurrent-test capacity, on road-test booking. Failure yields `ERR_LANE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).
34. **Warranty window** — the `failure_date` is at most 90 days past the unit's warranty expiry, on claim filing and on claim resubmission. Failure yields `ERR_WARRANTY_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422).
35. **Allocation quota** — one more allocation this month would still fit inside the dealer's monthly quota, on order allocation. Failure yields `ERR_ALLOCATION_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).
36. **Floor-plan headroom** — the computed draw still fits inside the dealer's floor-plan limit, on order allocation. Failure yields `ERR_FLOOR_PLAN_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402).

Two consequences of this ladder are worth stating outright, because operators find them counter-intuitive. Order allocation is guarded by nine of these rules, and the ordering means a stale dealer version is reported before a credit hold, a credit hold before an empty candidate set, an empty candidate set before an exhausted quota, and an exhausted quota before a floor-plan breach. Jig reservation is guarded by five, and a stale jig version is reported before the build-state check, which is itself reported before the tooling mismatch, which is reported before the window conflict.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Plant Assets

Two rows in this system are shared aggregates whose whole content is consumed by the writer that touches them, and both are therefore protected by an explicit optimistic-concurrency field carried in the request body. Neither field is optional; omitting it is a schema violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A `POST /dealer-orders/{id}/allocation` request must carry `expected_dealer_version`, holding the `version` of the `Dealer` row that the client last read. Allocating an order consumes two dealer-level quantities — the floor-plan balance and the monthly allocation quota — and, under the volume tier of §3.8, the price of *this* order depends on the dealer's year-to-date allocation count at the moment of the draw. A caller whose `expected_dealer_version` is lower than the dealer's current `version` has therefore priced and credit-checked the order against a superseded view of that dealer: an earlier allocation, a cancellation that released a draw, a limit adjustment, or a warranty receivable credit has landed since the read. Such a request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEALER_VERSION` (HTTP 412) so that the client can re-read and re-decide; the same allocation retried with the fresh `version` succeeds.

A `POST /weld-jigs/{id}/jig-reservations` request must carry `expected_weld_jig_version`, holding the `version` of the `WeldJig` row that the client last read. A reservation is accepted or rejected against that jig's entire occupancy calendar and against its aggregate booked-minutes rollup. A caller whose `expected_weld_jig_version` is lower than the jig's current `version` booked its window against a superseded calendar — another reservation has been added, or a model revision released one and gave the minutes back — so the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_JIG_VERSION` (HTTP 412) rather than being silently squeezed into a calendar the caller never saw.

No other endpoint in this system takes a version field. Certification decisions, systems stages, frame inspections, option installs, road tests, label issuance, delivery, cancellation, claim review, resubmission and part shipment are all written without an optimistic-concurrency check.

### 2.9. Units, Precision and Rounding Discipline

The plant mixes four measurement systems on purpose, and each field carries its unit in its name. Weights and ratings are whole pounds. Money is whole cents; there are no fractional cents anywhere in the system, and no monetary value is ever stored or returned as a decimal string. Frame geometry is millimetres for nominal and measured dimensions but inches — to one decimal place — for overall trailer width, because the wide-body lamp rule is written in inches. Brake stopping distance is feet to one decimal place. Durations are whole minutes. Labor is hours to one decimal place.

Wherever a computation has to reduce an exact value to a whole number, the method is stated with the computation and there are exactly two methods in play. **Round half up** means: take the exact decimal value, round it to the nearest integer, and send an exact one-half away from zero. This is the method used for the volume discount amount, the floor-plan draw, the per-part-line marked-up amount, the labor reimbursement, and the final coverage-adjusted reimbursement total. **Floor to a step** means: divide by the step, discard any fraction, and multiply back. This is used in exactly one place — the certified GVWR is floored to a whole multiple of 10 pounds — and it is applied *after* any derate, never before. No intermediate value is rounded at any step where a rounding method is not explicitly named; in particular, an invoice subtotal, a parts subtotal accumulator and a gross reimbursement are all carried exactly.

Threshold comparisons in this system are inclusive unless the text says otherwise, and the boundary cases are load-bearing. A frame deviation exactly equal to its tolerance passes. An overall width of exactly 80.0 inches does pull in the wide-body lamp set. A unit rated at exactly 3000 pounds does require brakes. A floor-plan draw that lands exactly on the limit is accepted. A monthly allocation that lands exactly on the quota is accepted. A claim whose labor hours land exactly on the flat-rate ceiling is accepted. The two temporal geometries of §3.2 and §3.6 are the deliberate exceptions and each states its own comparison direction.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Conserved Rollups

Several operations in this system mutate more than one row, and a few mutate rows three levels away from the one named in the request. Those chains are specified in full in §3, but three global rules govern all of them. First, a cascade is atomic: either every hop lands or the request is rejected and nothing changes. Second, every hop that touches a row refreshes that row's `updated_at` and increments its `version`, including rows the client never named — but a row is counted once per request, not once per hop: when several hops of the same request touch the same row, or a hop is followed by a rollup recomputation of a row that has already moved, that row's `updated_at` and `version` still advance exactly once, so no single request raises any row's `version` by more than one, and a row created by the request stays at `version` 1 however many later hops of that same request revise it. Third, a cascade never partially updates a rollup — after the last hop, every conserved quantity in the system agrees with the rows beneath it.

Five conserved rollups exist, and each is defined as a sum or count over a filtered set rather than as an accumulator that drifts. A dealer's `floor_plan_outstanding_cents` equals the sum of `floor_plan_draw_cents` over that dealer's orders whose `floor_plan_status` is `DRAWN` — released draws and repaid draws are excluded, and `DEMO` orders never contribute because their draw is zero. A dealer's `warranty_receivable_cents` equals the sum of `reimbursement_total_cents` over that dealer's claims whose `status` is `CLOSED`. A jig's `reserved_minutes_total` equals the sum of `duration_minutes` over that jig's reservations whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `CONSUMED`. A model's `active_unit_count` equals the count of units built to that model whose `build_status` is neither `SHIPPED` nor `SCRAPPED`, and it is recomputed on every change to any of its units' `build_status`. A unit's `curb_weight_lbs` and `payload_capacity_lbs` are rederived from its non-removed option installs on every option mutation.

The consequence the plant cares about is **exact compensation**. An allocation followed by a cancellation must leave `floor_plan_outstanding_cents` identical to its pre-allocation value; an option install followed by its removal must leave `curb_weight_lbs` and `payload_capacity_lbs` identical to their pre-install values. No drift, no residue, no rounding remainder.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties in Certification

Certification is the one place where the system separates duty by *identity* rather than by role. Both halves of certification belong to `CertificationOfficer`: signing a model's GVWR, and minting the VIN and certification label for a trailer built to that model. But the same `X-User-ID` may never do both. When a model's GVWR is certified, the acting officer's `X-User-ID` is recorded on the model as `certified_by_user_id`. When a label is later issued for any unit built to that model, the issuing officer's `X-User-ID` is compared to that recorded value, and if they are equal the issuance is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Completing a build therefore always requires at least two distinct certification-officer identities. Every other separation in this specification is by role, not by identity.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Model Configuration and the GVWR Certification Loop

Everything the plant builds descends from a **trailer model** — a configuration record that a `PlantEngineer` registers before any steel is cut. The engineer supplies a `model_code`, which is the plant's designation for the configuration and must be 4 to 16 characters of uppercase A–Z, digits and the hyphen only, and which must be unique across all models with case taken into account; a free-text `description` carrying the marketing copy for the configuration; and then the capacity and geometry that certification will be argued from. `axle_count` is how many axles sit under the frame and must be at least 1 and at most 3. `axle_rating_lbs` is the manufacturer's rating of **one** axle assembly in pounds and must be greater than zero. `design_braked_axle_count` is how many of those axles the design specifies as braked; it must be at least zero and may not exceed `axle_count`. `tire_load_rating_lbs` is the load rating of **one** tire in pounds, greater than zero, remembering that two tires are mounted on every axle. `frame_design_code` is the frame family, exactly two uppercase letters, and `frame_design_rated_lbs` is that frame design's structural rating in pounds. `coupler_rating_lbs` rates the coupler and tongue assembly. `overall_width_in` is the overall trailer width in inches, greater than zero and carried to one decimal place. `nominal_length_mm` and `nominal_width_mm` are the engineering nominal frame dimensions in millimetres against which the weld floor's measurements will later be compared, both greater than zero. `base_curb_weight_lbs` is the curb weight of the base configuration with no options installed, and `base_dealer_price_cents` is the dealer net price of that base configuration in cents; both must be greater than zero.

A freshly registered model opens in `certification_status` `DRAFT`, with `revision_round` at 0 and `active_unit_count` at 0. Neither rating is populated yet: `provisional_gvwr_lbs` and `certified_gvwr_lbs` both stay null until the model enters review, and `certified_by_user_id` and `certified_at` stay null until an officer actually signs it.

Submitting a model for GVWR certification review is a `PlantEngineer` action that carries an `engineering_note` and drives `certification_status` from `DRAFT` to `IN_ENGINEERING_REVIEW`. The same submission is also how an engineering change is opened on an already-certified configuration, driving `CERTIFIED` back to `IN_ENGINEERING_REVIEW`; that back-edge is what makes the model lifecycle cyclic, so a model can legitimately travel `CERTIFIED` → `IN_ENGINEERING_REVIEW` → `DRAFT` → `IN_ENGINEERING_REVIEW` → `CERTIFIED` any number of times. A submission attempted from any other status is refused with `ERR_MODEL_STATE_INVALID`.

Each submission writes `provisional_gvwr_lbs`, the **engineering estimate**. It is computed from the model's own input fields and nothing else — no unit, option or dealer data participates, and the computation runs on the model row alone no matter how many trailers already reference it. Four capacities are formed. The axle capacity is `axle_count` multiplied by `axle_rating_lbs`. The tire capacity is `axle_count` multiplied by two — two tires per axle — multiplied by `tire_load_rating_lbs`. The frame capacity is simply `frame_design_rated_lbs`, and the coupler capacity is simply `coupler_rating_lbs`. At submission time the tire circuit has not yet been sourced, so **the tire capacity does not participate** and no brake derate is applied: `provisional_gvwr_lbs` is the smallest of the axle, frame and coupler capacities, taken as a whole number of pounds with no rounding step. Because it omits the tire limit and skips the derate, the provisional estimate is always greater than or equal to the certified rating that the same inputs will later produce. When the submission comes from `CERTIFIED`, the previously signed values are deliberately preserved: `certified_gvwr_lbs`, `certified_by_user_id` and `certified_at` are all retained unchanged, because trailers already pinned to the old revision must keep their snapshot. Every submission increments the model's `version`.

The certification decision belongs exclusively to a `CertificationOfficer`, who supplies a `decision` of `CERTIFIED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `WITHDRAWN` together with a `decision_note`. A decision is only accepted while `certification_status` is `IN_ENGINEERING_REVIEW`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_MODEL_STATE_INVALID`.

A `CERTIFIED` decision writes `certified_gvwr_lbs`, the **final rating**, and its evaluation order is pinned in three steps that may not be reordered. Step one takes the smallest of **all four** capacities — axle, tire, frame and coupler. Step two applies the unbraked-axle derate: if `design_braked_axle_count` is strictly less than `axle_count`, that smallest value is multiplied by ninety hundredths, a ten-percent reduction; if every axle is braked, the value passes through unchanged. Step three floors the result down to the nearest whole multiple of 10 pounds. Rounding happens after the derate and never before. Alongside the rating, the decision records `certified_by_user_id` as the acting officer's `X-User-ID` and `certified_at` as the logical clock's current instant, moves `certification_status` to `CERTIFIED`, and increments the model's `version`. Certification also unblocks work that was frozen: every trailer built to this model that is sitting in `BUILD_HOLD` moves `BUILD_HOLD` → `PLANNED`, and because those trailers left the hold state the model's `active_unit_count` is recomputed.

A `REVISION_REQUESTED` decision sends the model back to `DRAFT`, adds one to `revision_round`, and triggers a three-hop freeze. First, every trailer built to this model whose `build_status` is `PLANNED`, `JIG_RESERVED` or `FRAME_REWORK` moves to `BUILD_HOLD`. Second, because those trailers entered the hold state, every jig reservation belonging to one of them whose `status` is `RESERVED` moves `RESERVED` → `RELEASED`. Third, because those reservations were released, each affected jig has its `reserved_minutes_total` reduced by the released reservation's `duration_minutes` and its own `version` incremented. The model's `active_unit_count` is then recomputed — held trailers still count as active, so the number does not change on account of the hold itself — and the model's `version` increments.

A `WITHDRAWN` decision is terminal for the model and stops future builds without destroying work in process. Its cascade also runs three hops. Every trailer built to this model whose `build_status` is `PLANNED` or `BUILD_HOLD` moves to `SCRAPPED`; every `RESERVED` jig reservation belonging to one of those trailers moves to `RELEASED`; and each affected jig has `reserved_minutes_total` decremented by the released `duration_minutes` with its `version` incremented. `active_unit_count` is recomputed — scrapped trailers drop out of it — and the model's `version` increments. Trailers already past `PLANNED` and `BUILD_HOLD`, meaning welded or later, are deliberately left exactly as they are. A `WITHDRAWN` model accepts no further decisions and no new trailers: opening a build record against it is refused with `ERR_MODEL_NOT_BUILDABLE`, while `DRAFT`, `IN_ENGINEERING_REVIEW` and `CERTIFIED` models all accept new build records.

Reading a model back returns both ratings side by side, which is the intended audit view: `provisional_gvwr_lbs` shows what engineering estimated, `certified_gvwr_lbs` shows what the officer signed, and the gap between them is the tire limit plus the derate.

### 3.2. Build Records, Weld Jigs and the Occupancy Calendar

A `WeldSupervisor` opens a build record for one physical trailer by supplying a `serial_number` — the plant serial stamped on the frame rail, 6 to 20 characters of uppercase A–Z, digits and the hyphen, unique across all trailers — the `model_id` of the configuration it is being built to, and an advisory `planned_start_date` naming the intended weld-floor start. The build record opens with its two status dimensions independent and both at their starting values: `build_status` is `PLANNED` and `compliance_status` is `NOT_VERIFIED`. Nothing is pinned yet — `model_version_pinned`, `gvwr_lbs`, `curb_weight_lbs` and `payload_capacity_lbs` are all null until the jig reservation pins them — and the remaining system-managed fields open at their defaults: `dealer_order_id` null, `installed_braked_axle_count` null, `breakaway_kit_installed` false, `clearance_lamp_count` null, `frame_fail_count` 0, `rework_cycle_count` 0, `released_to_stock_at` null, `warranty_start_date` null and `vin` null. Opening a build record recomputes the model's `active_unit_count`.

Weld jigs and test lanes are plant assets that a `PlantEngineer` commissions. A jig is registered with a `jig_code` (4 to 16 characters of uppercase A–Z, digits and the hyphen, unique across all jigs), the `frame_design_code` it is tooled for as exactly two uppercase letters, and a `changeover_buffer_min` giving the minutes of tear-down and set-up that follow every reservation on it; when the buffer is omitted it defaults to 45 minutes, and it may not be negative. A newly commissioned jig opens with `reserved_minutes_total` at 0.

Reserving a welding window is the operation that converts a paper build record into a committed piece of work, and it is the single most heavily guarded write in the plant. The `WeldSupervisor` names the `unit_id` of the trailer, the `window_start_at` and `window_end_at` of the intended weld window, and the `expected_weld_jig_version` of §2.8. The window's end must be strictly later than its start. The trailer must be in `build_status` `PLANNED`; any other build state is refused with `ERR_UNIT_BUILD_STATE_INVALID`. The trailer's model must be in `certification_status` exactly `CERTIFIED` — a cross-entity gate, because no trailer may enter a jig on an uncertified rating — and a model in any other certification state produces `ERR_MODEL_NOT_CERTIFIED`. The jig's `frame_design_code` must equal the model's `frame_design_code`; a jig tooled for a different frame family produces `ERR_JIG_DESIGN_MISMATCH`.

The occupancy geometry of a jig is **half-open with a trailing buffer**. Each reservation occupies the interval that begins at its `window_start_at`, inclusive, and ends at its `window_end_at` plus the jig's `changeover_buffer_min` minutes, exclusive; that computed end is stored on the reservation as `occupancy_end_at`. The buffer is read live from the jig at request time. A new request conflicts with an existing reservation when the requested start is strictly earlier than the existing reservation's buffered end **and** the existing reservation's start is strictly earlier than the requested buffered end. Only reservations whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `CONSUMED` are considered; `RELEASED` reservations are ignored entirely, which is exactly what lets a window freed by a model revision be rebooked. Because both comparisons are strict, a gap of exactly the buffer length between one window's end and the next window's start is accepted, while a gap one minute shorter than the buffer is rejected with `ERR_JIG_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. A jig is single-occupancy: one conflicting reservation is enough to refuse.

An accepted reservation is created against that jig with `jig_id` set, `status` `RESERVED`, and two computed quantities: `duration_minutes`, the whole minutes between `window_start_at` and `window_end_at` with the changeover buffer **excluded**, and `occupancy_end_at` as described above. The jig's `reserved_minutes_total` grows by that `duration_minutes` and the jig's `version` increments. The trailer moves `PLANNED` → `JIG_RESERVED` and, on the same call, **pins** the model revision it will be built to for the rest of its life: `model_version_pinned` takes the model's current `version`, and `gvwr_lbs` takes a snapshot of the model's `certified_gvwr_lbs`. That snapshot — never the model's live value — is the trailer's own rating for every downstream weight and brake rule, so a later engineering change to the model cannot silently alter a trailer already on the floor. The same call seeds the weight budget: `curb_weight_lbs` takes the model's `base_curb_weight_lbs` and `payload_capacity_lbs` becomes the pinned `gvwr_lbs` less that curb weight.

Two read channels expose the jig floor. A trailer's own reservation history lists every reservation ever booked for it, `RELEASED` ones included, so that a revision-driven freeze remains visible in the audit trail. A jig's reservation list is ordered by `window_start_at` ascending and accepts an optional `status` filter for narrowing to reserved, consumed or released windows.

### 3.3. Dimensional Control, Weld Verdicts and the Scrap Rule

With the trailer in the jig, the `WeldSupervisor` records dimensional-control readings and weld defect counts. Each inspection carries a `measured_length_mm` and `measured_width_mm` for the welded frame, the two corner-to-corner readings `diagonal_a_mm` and `diagonal_b_mm`, and two defect tallies: `critical_weld_defect_count` for defects found on safety-critical welds — the coupler, the axle hangers, the A-frame — and `non_critical_weld_defect_count` for everything else. Neither tally may be negative. An inspection is admissible only while the trailer's `build_status` is `JIG_RESERVED` or `FRAME_REWORK`; anything else is refused with `ERR_UNIT_BUILD_STATE_INVALID`.

Every inspection receives an `inspection_seq`, the 1-based ordinal of that inspection within its trailer ordered by `created_at` ascending, so a trailer's first inspection is 1, its second is 2, and so on. Four deviations are then derived. `length_deviation_mm` is the absolute difference between `measured_length_mm` and the model's `nominal_length_mm`; `width_deviation_mm` is the absolute difference between `measured_width_mm` and the model's `nominal_width_mm`; `max_deviation_mm` is the larger of those two; and `squareness_delta_mm` is the absolute difference between `diagonal_a_mm` and `diagonal_b_mm`. Deviations are carried to one decimal place. The nominal dimensions are read live from the model at inspection time; earlier inspections of the same trailer do not influence this verdict at all — they only feed the running failure tally.

The verdict itself is assembled as a list of criterion codes in `failure_reasons`, and four criteria are tested. If `critical_weld_defect_count` is greater than zero, the code `CRITICAL_WELD_DEFECT` joins the list — a single critical weld defect is disqualifying. If `max_deviation_mm` is greater than 3.0 millimetres, `MAX_DEVIATION` joins the list; a deviation of exactly 3.0 millimetres is within tolerance and does not. If `non_critical_weld_defect_count` is greater than 2, `NON_CRITICAL_WELD_DEFECT` joins the list; exactly two non-critical defects are tolerated. If `squareness_delta_mm` is greater than 6.0 millimetres, `SQUARENESS` joins the list; exactly 6.0 millimetres passes. The assembled list is then sorted into ascending alphabetical order so that the stored output order is stable regardless of the order in which the criteria were evaluated. The `result` is `PASS` when and only when `failure_reasons` is empty, and `FAIL` otherwise; a passing inspection stores `failure_reasons` as an empty array. The acting supervisor's `X-User-ID` is stored as `inspector_user_id`.

A `PASS` moves the trailer to `build_status` `FRAME_WELDED` — from `JIG_RESERVED` or from `FRAME_REWORK`, both are valid origins — and moves the trailer's `RESERVED` jig reservation to `CONSUMED`. Consumption deliberately does **not** change the jig's `reserved_minutes_total`, because a consumed window still occupies the jig's calendar and must keep blocking overlapping bookings.

A `FAIL` recomputes the trailer's `frame_fail_count`, which is the count of that trailer's inspections whose `result` is `FAIL`, and then branches on that count. While the recomputed tally is below 3, the trailer moves to `FRAME_REWORK` and goes back on the jig; `FRAME_REWORK` and `JIG_RESERVED` together with `FRAME_WELDED` form the weld-floor rework loop, and a trailer may cycle through it. When the recomputed tally reaches exactly 3, the trailer is scrapped, and the scrap runs as a three-hop cascade. First, `build_status` becomes `SCRAPPED`, which is terminal. Second, because the trailer was scrapped, its jig reservation — whether it was `RESERVED` or already `CONSUMED` — moves to `RELEASED`. Third, because that reservation was released, the jig's `reserved_minutes_total` is reduced by the reservation's `duration_minutes` and the jig's `version` increments, giving the floor its minutes back. The model's `active_unit_count` is then recomputed, since a scrapped trailer no longer counts as active.

A trailer's inspection history is readable as a list in `inspection_seq` order, complete with each inspection's deviations, `squareness_delta_mm`, `result` and `failure_reasons` — the plant's dimensional-control evidence file.

### 3.4. Regulatory Systems Installation on the Compliance Dimension

A welded frame is not yet a road-legal trailer. Regulatory verification happens on the trailer's **second** status dimension, `compliance_status`, which an `AssemblyTech` advances by recording systems stages. Two stages exist, named by the `stage` field: `LIGHTING`, which installs the lamp and reflector set, and `BRAKE_AND_AXLE`, which installs the axles, the brakes and the breakaway kit. Either stage requires the trailer's `build_status` to be `FRAME_WELDED` or `SYSTEMS_REWORK`; any other build state is refused with `ERR_UNIT_BUILD_STATE_INVALID`.

The stages run in a fixed order on the compliance dimension. `LIGHTING` requires `compliance_status` to be `NOT_VERIFIED` or `NON_COMPLIANT`; `BRAKE_AND_AXLE` requires `compliance_status` to be exactly `LIGHTING_VERIFIED`. A stage submitted against the wrong compliance state — brakes before lamps, or lamps again on an already-verified trailer — is refused with `ERR_COMPLIANCE_STAGE_OUT_OF_ORDER`. Each stage carries its own fields and no others matter: the `LIGHTING` stage requires all five of `stop_tail_turn_lamp_count`, `clearance_lamp_count`, `side_marker_lamp_count`, `license_plate_lamp_count` and `reflex_reflector_count`, while the `BRAKE_AND_AXLE` stage requires both `installed_braked_axle_count` and `breakaway_kit_installed`. Fields belonging to the other stage are ignored when supplied, and a stage that omits any of its own required fields is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

The lighting requirement is a conjunction of four conditions, all of which must hold. There must be at least 2 stop/tail/turn lamps. There must be at least 1 license-plate lamp. There must be at least 4 reflex reflectors. And the wide-load provision must be satisfied: either the model's `overall_width_in` is strictly less than 80.0 inches, in which case nothing further is required, or the trailer is 80.0 inches or wider and must then carry at least 5 clearance lamps **and** at least 4 side-marker lamps. A trailer measuring exactly 80.0 inches is a wide-body trailer and does pull in that extra lamp set. Any of these conditions failing is reported as `ERR_LIGHTING_NOT_COMPLIANT` (HTTP 422).

The brake requirement reads the trailer's **pinned** `gvwr_lbs` — the snapshot taken at jig reservation — and never the model's live `certified_gvwr_lbs`, which is what makes a revised model unable to retroactively change a trailer's braking obligation. Below 3000 pounds there is no braking requirement at all. From 3000 pounds upward, inclusive, the trailer must have at least 1 braked axle installed and `breakaway_kit_installed` must be true. From 10000 pounds upward, inclusive, that first requirement still applies **and** the installed braked axle count must equal the model's `axle_count` exactly — every axle on a heavy trailer must be braked. A shortfall is reported as `ERR_BRAKE_REQUIREMENT_UNMET` (HTTP 422).

A satisfying `LIGHTING` stage records `clearance_lamp_count` on the trailer and moves `compliance_status` from `NOT_VERIFIED` to `LIGHTING_VERIFIED`. The re-verification path is identical in every respect: a trailer that fell to `NON_COMPLIANT` after a failed road test moves `NON_COMPLIANT` → `LIGHTING_VERIFIED` on the same stage, re-recording `clearance_lamp_count`, with the same four lamp conditions enforced. In both cases the trailer's `build_status` is deliberately left untouched — this is precisely where the two dimensions move independently, and a supervisor watching only the build dimension will see nothing happen.

A satisfying `BRAKE_AND_AXLE` stage is where the dimensions re-couple. It records `installed_braked_axle_count` and `breakaway_kit_installed` on the trailer, moves `compliance_status` from `LIGHTING_VERIFIED` to `FULLY_COMPLIANT`, and on the very same call moves `build_status` to `SYSTEMS_INSTALLED`. Both origins behave identically: a trailer arriving from `FRAME_WELDED` and a trailer arriving from `SYSTEMS_REWORK` both land in `SYSTEMS_INSTALLED` with `FULLY_COMPLIANT` compliance, both re-record the two brake fields, and both are gated by the identical brake requirement. A stage that fails its regulatory check returns its 422 and leaves **both** status dimensions untouched; the trailer's `version` increments only when a stage succeeds.

### 3.5. Custom Options and the Unit Weight Budget

Utility trailers sell on options, and options cost payload. An `AssemblyTech` charges an option to a trailer by naming an `option_code` — one of `REAR_RAMP_GATE`, `SIDE_CABINET_PKG`, `SPECIALTY_FLOORING`, `SPARE_TIRE_CARRIER` or `E_TRACK_KIT` — together with a `quantity` of at least 1, the `unit_weight_lbs` of a single unit of that option in pounds, the `unit_price_cents` of a single unit in cents, and the `labor_hours` the installation takes, carried to one decimal place. The weight and price may each be zero but not negative, and the labor hours may not be negative. An option may be charged while the trailer's `build_status` is any of `FRAME_WELDED`, `SYSTEMS_INSTALLED`, `SYSTEMS_REWORK`, `ROAD_TEST_PENDING`, `ROAD_TEST_PASSED` or `LABELED`; any other build state is refused with `ERR_UNIT_BUILD_STATE_INVALID`. Each install line opens with `status` `PENDING` and two extended amounts: `total_weight_lbs` is the `quantity` multiplied by `unit_weight_lbs`, and `total_price_cents` is the `quantity` multiplied by `unit_price_cents`.

Charging an option immediately rederives the trailer's weight budget. The option weight of a trailer is the sum of `total_weight_lbs` over that trailer's install lines whose `status` is `PENDING` or `COMPLETED`; lines whose `status` is `REMOVED` are excluded from that sum entirely. The trailer's `curb_weight_lbs` is the model's `base_curb_weight_lbs`, read live, plus that option weight. The trailer's `payload_capacity_lbs` is the trailer's pinned `gvwr_lbs` less its `curb_weight_lbs`. The invariant that `curb_weight_lbs` plus `payload_capacity_lbs` equals the pinned `gvwr_lbs` holds after every mutation of the trailer, cascades included, and every rederivation increments the trailer's `version`. The admission rule follows directly: an install is refused when charging it would drive `payload_capacity_lbs` below zero, and a `payload_capacity_lbs` of exactly zero is admissible — a trailer may be loaded to precisely its rating and no further. A refusal is reported as `ERR_PAYLOAD_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

Signing off an install as physically complete is a separate action carrying a `signoff_note`; it moves the line's `status` from `PENDING` to `COMPLETED`. Sign-off deliberately does not move the weight or price aggregates, because `PENDING` and `COMPLETED` lines are both inside the aggregate's input set. Removing a still-pending install carries a `removal_reason` and moves the line's `status` from `PENDING` to `REMOVED`, which drops it out of both the weight aggregate and the option-price aggregate of §3.8. Removal rederives `curb_weight_lbs` and `payload_capacity_lbs`, and a charge-then-remove pair must return both fields to their exact prior values with no drift whatsoever. Only a `PENDING` line may be signed off or removed; a `COMPLETED` or already-`REMOVED` line is refused with `ERR_OPTION_NOT_REMOVABLE`.

Option lines also gate one automatic build transition. A trailer sitting in `build_status` `LABELED` moves itself to `RELEASED_TO_STOCK` as soon as the count of its install lines whose `status` is `PENDING` reaches exactly zero, stamping `released_to_stock_at` with the logical clock's current instant. That evaluation runs immediately after a label is issued and again after every option sign-off and every option removal, so the last outstanding option line clearing — by completion or by removal — is what releases the trailer into stock and makes it visible to the allocation selector. A trailer's option lines are readable as a list which excludes `REMOVED` lines unless the request sets `include_removed` to true, and a single line is readable on its own with its extended weight and price.

### 3.6. Road Testing, Derived Verdicts and the Failure Cascade

Road tests run on lanes, and lanes are assets a `PlantEngineer` commissions with a `lane_code` (4 to 16 characters of uppercase A–Z, digits and the hyphen, unique across all lanes), a `surface_type` of `ASPHALT`, `GRAVEL` or `WASHBOARD`, and a `max_concurrent_tests` capacity of at least 1 that defaults to 2 when omitted.

A `QualityInspector` books a test by naming the `lane_id`, a `window_start_at` and `window_end_at` — end strictly later than start — and a `protocol_code` of `STANDARD_20MI`, `LOADED_50MI` or `BRAKE_FADE`. The trailer's `compliance_status` must be `FULLY_COMPLIANT`, and anything else is refused with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_COMPLIANT`. Booking is admissible from six build states — `SYSTEMS_INSTALLED`, `SYSTEMS_REWORK`, `ROAD_TEST_PASSED`, `LABELED`, `RELEASED_TO_STOCK` and `ALLOCATED` — which means a re-test may legitimately be booked on a trailer that has already been released into stock or even allocated to an order, pulling it back out of stock; any other build state is refused with `ERR_UNIT_BUILD_STATE_INVALID`.

Lane occupancy uses arithmetic deliberately different from the jig's. A lane window is **closed** — both endpoints included — and carries **no buffer**, and the constraint is aggregate rather than exclusive. The system counts the road tests on the same lane whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` and whose window overlaps the requested one, where overlap means the existing window's start is at or before the requested window's end **and** the requested window's start is at or before the existing window's end. Because both comparisons are inclusive, two windows that merely touch — one ending at the exact instant the next begins — do count as overlapping. Tests whose `status` is `PASSED` or `FAILED` have vacated the lane and never count, however recently they were resulted. If the overlapping count is already at or above the lane's `max_concurrent_tests`, the booking is refused with `ERR_LANE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; with the default capacity of 2, two overlapping scheduled tests are fine and a third is not. An accepted booking creates the test with `status` `SCHEDULED` and moves the trailer's `build_status` to `ROAD_TEST_PENDING`.

Recording a result is where the verdict is **derived rather than requested**. The inspector supplies a measured `brake_stop_distance_ft`, carried to one decimal place, a boolean `lighting_check_passed` from the lamp walk-around, and an optional `result_notes`. The test's `status` becomes `PASSED` when the stopping distance is at most 40.0 feet **and** the lighting check passed; it becomes `FAILED` when the stopping distance exceeds 40.0 feet **or** the lighting check did not pass. A stopping distance of exactly 40.0 feet with a passing lamp check is a pass. The measurements are written to `brake_stop_distance_ft` and `lighting_check_passed`, the note to `result_notes`, and the acting inspector's `X-User-ID` to `inspector_user_id`. A result may only be recorded against a test whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` and whose trailer is in `build_status` `ROAD_TEST_PENDING`; a second attempt on an already-resulted test is refused with `ERR_ROAD_TEST_ALREADY_RESULTED`. The two branches have deliberately disjoint side-effect sets.

A `PASSED` result is the simple branch: the test moves `SCHEDULED` → `PASSED` and the trailer moves `ROAD_TEST_PENDING` → `ROAD_TEST_PASSED`, ready for labelling.

A `FAILED` result moves the test `SCHEDULED` → `FAILED` and then unwinds the trailer through a four-hop cascade, which is the largest single cascade in the system. The trailer's `build_status` returns to `SYSTEMS_REWORK` and its `rework_cycle_count` — the count of that trailer's road tests whose `status` is `FAILED` — is recomputed. On the parallel dimension, `compliance_status` moves `FULLY_COMPLIANT` → `NON_COMPLIANT`, which is what forces the trailer back through the `LIGHTING` stage before it can be re-tested. Hop one: because the trailer left the tested state, its certification label whose `status` is `ISSUED`, if it has one, moves `ISSUED` → `VOIDED` with `voided_reason` recorded as `ROAD_TEST_FAILED`, and the trailer's `vin` becomes null. Hop two: because the label was voided, if the trailer was sitting on a dealer order whose `status` is `ALLOCATED`, that order moves `ALLOCATED` → `PENDING`, the order's `allocated_unit_id` and the trailer's `dealer_order_id` are both cleared to null, and the order's `floor_plan_status` moves `DRAWN` → `RELEASED`. Hop three: because the draw was released, the dealer's `floor_plan_outstanding_cents` is reduced by that order's `floor_plan_draw_cents`, `floor_plan_available_cents` is recomputed, and the dealer's `version` increments. The trailer's own `version` increments as well. A single failed brake measurement can therefore reach from the test lane into a dealer's credit line, and this chain is the intended one.

A trailer's road tests are readable in window order, a lane's road tests are readable ordered by `window_start_at` ascending with an optional `status` filter, and a single test is readable with its recorded `brake_stop_distance_ft` and `lighting_check_passed` verdict.

### 3.7. VIN Minting, Certification Labelling and Title Documentation

Issuing the federal certification label is the act that turns a road-tested trailer into a titleable vehicle, and it belongs exclusively to a `CertificationOfficer`. The officer supplies only one field, the `tire_size_code` to be printed on the label; everything else on the label is computed or snapshotted. Four preconditions guard the issuance. The trailer's `build_status` must be `ROAD_TEST_PASSED`, and any other build state is refused with `ERR_UNIT_BUILD_STATE_INVALID`. Its `compliance_status` must be `FULLY_COMPLIANT`, and anything else is refused with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_COMPLIANT`. The trailer must carry no certification label whose `status` is `ISSUED`, and a second attempt is refused with `ERR_LABEL_ALREADY_ISSUED`. And the acting officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the `certified_by_user_id` recorded on the trailer's model, per the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.11, with a match refused as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Issuance draws a `vin_sequence` from a **plant-wide monotonic counter**. The counter starts at 1 and advances by one for every label issued. A number is never reused — voiding a label does not return its number to the pool — so the sequence is a permanent, gap-free record of how many labels the plant has ever minted, and gaps in the set of *live* VINs are expected wherever a void has occurred.

The `vin` is exactly 17 characters, assembled left to right from nine pieces. It opens with the plant's three-character world manufacturer identifier, `5XY`. Then come the model's `frame_design_code`, two characters. Then the model's `axle_count` rendered as a single digit. Then a single brake character: `E` when the trailer's `installed_braked_axle_count` is at least 1, and `N` when it is not. Then the single body-style filler character `A`. Then the single check placeholder character `0`. Then the model-year character selected from the logical clock's calendar year, where 2026 maps to `T`, 2027 to `V`, 2028 to `W`, 2029 to `X` and 2030 to `Y`. Then the single plant character `P`. And finally the `vin_sequence` written as six digits with leading zeros. The dealer's title document travels alongside it: `mco_number`, the Manufacturer's Certificate of Origin number, is the literal text `MCO-` followed by that same `vin_sequence` zero-padded to six digits.

Three values are stamped onto the label as snapshots so that the label remains a faithful record of what was certified even after the model moves on. `gvwr_lbs` on the label copies the **trailer's** pinned `gvwr_lbs`, not the model's live certified rating. `gawr_per_axle_lbs`, the per-axle Gross Axle Weight Rating, copies the model's `axle_rating_lbs` read live at issuance. `model_version_pinned` on the label copies the trailer's own `model_version_pinned`, naming the exact model revision the label certifies. The label also records `issued_by_user_id` as the acting officer's `X-User-ID`, `issued_at` as the logical clock's current instant, opens with `status` `ISSUED` and `voided_reason` null, and carries the supplied `tire_size_code`.

On the trailer side, issuance sets `vin` to the newly minted VIN — the trailer's `vin` is always the VIN of its label whose `status` is `ISSUED`, and null when it has none — and moves `build_status` from `ROAD_TEST_PASSED` to `LABELED`. The automatic release of §3.5 is then evaluated immediately: if the trailer has zero option lines whose `status` is `PENDING`, it moves straight on to `RELEASED_TO_STOCK` with `released_to_stock_at` stamped from the logical clock, so a trailer with no outstanding options is labelled and released in a single request. A trailer's labels are readable as a list which excludes `VOIDED` labels unless the request sets `include_voided` to true — the voided ones remain permanently readable for audit, each still carrying its `vin`, its `vin_sequence` and its `voided_reason`.

### 3.8. Dealer Accounts, Floor Plan Credit and Stock Allocation

A `SalesAdmin` enrolls a franchised dealer with a `dealer_code` (4 to 16 characters of uppercase A–Z, digits and the hyphen, unique across all dealers), the legal `name` of the dealership, a `labor_rate_cents_per_hour` giving the warranty labor reimbursement rate for that dealer in cents per hour and required to be greater than zero, a `floor_plan_limit_cents` credit-line ceiling in cents that defaults to 0 and may not be negative, and an `allocation_quota_per_month` capping how many order allocations the dealer may receive inside one logical-clock UTC calendar month, which may be zero but not negative. A newly enrolled dealer opens with `status` `ACTIVE`, `floor_plan_outstanding_cents` at 0, `floor_plan_available_cents` equal to its `floor_plan_limit_cents`, and `allocations_current_month_count`, `allocations_ytd_count` and `warranty_receivable_cents` all at 0.

Floor-plan administration is a single `SalesAdmin` capability driven by an `adjustment_type` together with an `amount_cents`, an optional `order_id` and an optional `note`. A `LIMIT_INCREASE` raises `floor_plan_limit_cents` by `amount_cents` and a `LIMIT_DECREASE` lowers it by `amount_cents`; either way `floor_plan_available_cents` is recomputed as `floor_plan_limit_cents` less `floor_plan_outstanding_cents`, and that available figure is permitted to go negative after a decrease. A decrease that leaves `floor_plan_outstanding_cents` above the new `floor_plan_limit_cents` additionally sets the dealer's `status` to `CREDIT_HOLD` as a derived consequence. A `PAYOFF` names the `order_id` of a drawn order and moves that order's `floor_plan_status` from `DRAWN` to `REPAID`, which removes its `floor_plan_draw_cents` from the conserved sum so that the dealer's `floor_plan_outstanding_cents` drops by exactly that amount — delivery never repays a floor plan, only a payoff does. A `CREDIT_HOLD` sets the dealer's `status` to `CREDIT_HOLD` directly and a `CREDIT_RELEASE` sets it back to `ACTIVE`. Every adjustment increments the dealer's `version`, which is what makes a concurrent allocation's `expected_dealer_version` stale.

Placing an order is available to both a `SalesAdmin` and a `Dealer`. The order names the `dealer_id` it belongs to, the `model_id` of the configuration wanted, a `po_ref` dealer purchase-order reference of 4 to 24 characters, and a `stocking_program` of `STOCK`, `SOLD_ORDER` or `DEMO`. `DEMO` orders never draw floor plan. An order opens with `status` `PENDING`, `floor_plan_status` `NONE` and `floor_plan_draw_cents` 0, with `allocated_unit_id`, `invoice_subtotal_cents`, `volume_discount_pct`, `volume_discount_cents`, `freight_cents`, `invoice_total_cents`, `allocated_at`, `delivered_at` and `cancelled_at` all null until something happens to it.

Allocation is the plant's busiest operation and belongs to `SalesAdmin` alone. Beyond the mandatory `expected_dealer_version` of §2.8, the request may optionally name a `unit_id`. Omitting it runs the **ranked selector**. The candidate set is every trailer whose `model_id` equals the order's `model_id`, whose `build_status` is `RELEASED_TO_STOCK`, whose `dealer_order_id` is null, and for which a certification label with `status` `ISSUED` exists. Trailers whose label was voided, trailers on hold and trailers already allocated are all excluded. Candidates are then ranked on three keys in strict order: `released_to_stock_at` ascending first, so the oldest stock trailer is allocated first; `created_at` ascending as the tie-break when two trailers were released at the same instant; and `serial_number` in ascending lexicographic order as the final tie-break. The `id` of a trailer is never consulted at any ranking level. Timestamps are compared at millisecond resolution exactly as stored. The first candidate after that sort is the allocated trailer, and an empty candidate set is refused with `ERR_NO_ALLOCATABLE_UNIT`. Supplying a `unit_id` instead is the **explicit override**: no ranking runs, but the named trailer must itself satisfy the same eligibility predicate — `build_status` `RELEASED_TO_STOCK`, `dealer_order_id` null, and an `ISSUED` label — and a trailer that does not is refused with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_ALLOCATABLE`. A named trailer that belongs to a different model than the order's is refused earlier, with `ERR_UNIT_MODEL_MISMATCH`.

Once the trailer is resolved, the invoice is computed in a **pinned seven-step order**, and the order matters because step five depends on step three and step six depends on step five, which is how the apparent circularity between the total, the freight, the discount tier and the dealer's history is resolved in exactly one way. Step one sums `total_price_cents` over the selected trailer's option lines whose `status` is `PENDING` or `COMPLETED`, excluding `REMOVED` lines, to give the option total. Step two sets `invoice_subtotal_cents` to the model's `base_dealer_price_cents` plus that option total. Step three selects `volume_discount_pct` from the dealer's year-to-date allocation count **as observed before this allocation is counted** — counted live at request time, over exactly the set that defines `allocations_ytd_count` below and against the logical clock's calendar year as it stands for this request, rather than read from the figure last persisted on the dealer row: when that count is 0 the rate is zero, when it is 1 the rate is three hundredths, and when it is 2 or more the rate is five hundredths. In plain terms, a dealer's first allocation of the calendar year is undiscounted, its second earns 3 percent, and its third and every later one earns 5 percent. Step four sets `volume_discount_cents` to `invoice_subtotal_cents` multiplied by `volume_discount_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. Step five sets `freight_cents`, which is waived to 0 whenever any volume tier applies — that is, whenever `volume_discount_pct` is at least three hundredths — and is otherwise a flat 895 dollars, or 89500 cents; this is why freight cannot be evaluated before the tier is known. Step six sets `invoice_total_cents` to `invoice_subtotal_cents` less `volume_discount_cents` plus `freight_cents`. Step seven sets `floor_plan_draw_cents`: for a `DEMO` order it is 0, and for every other stocking program it is `invoice_total_cents` multiplied by ninety hundredths — 90 percent of the invoice — rounded half up to whole cents. Rounding half up happens at steps four and seven and nowhere else; no intermediate value is rounded.

Three admission checks then decide whether the allocation lands, and they are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.7. The dealer's `status` must be `ACTIVE`; a dealer on credit hold is refused with `ERR_DEALER_CREDIT_HOLD`. The dealer's month-to-date allocation count — likewise counted live at request time, over exactly the set that defines `allocations_current_month_count` below and against the logical clock's calendar month as it stands for this request, rather than read from the figure last persisted on the dealer row — plus one must be no greater than its `allocation_quota_per_month`, so an allocation that lands exactly on the quota is accepted and the next one is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`. And for any order whose `stocking_program` is not `DEMO`, the dealer's `floor_plan_outstanding_cents` plus this order's computed `floor_plan_draw_cents` must be no greater than its `floor_plan_limit_cents`; a draw landing exactly on the limit is accepted, and a breach is refused with `ERR_FLOOR_PLAN_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402). `DEMO` orders draw zero and skip the floor-plan check entirely.

A successful allocation moves the order `PENDING` → `ALLOCATED`, sets `allocated_unit_id` to the chosen trailer and `allocated_at` to the logical clock's current instant, and writes all six invoice fields. For a non-`DEMO` order it moves `floor_plan_status` from `NONE` to `DRAWN` and increases the dealer's `floor_plan_outstanding_cents` by `floor_plan_draw_cents`. The chosen trailer moves `build_status` `RELEASED_TO_STOCK` → `ALLOCATED` with its `dealer_order_id` set to the order. The dealer's `allocations_current_month_count`, `allocations_ytd_count` and `floor_plan_available_cents` are all recomputed and the dealer's `version` increments — which is precisely what makes a second allocation request holding the old `expected_dealer_version` stale. The two allocation counters count only orders whose `status` is `ALLOCATED` or `DELIVERED` and whose `allocated_at` falls inside the logical clock's current UTC calendar month and calendar year respectively; cancelled orders are excluded from both even though `allocated_at` remains on the row. Both figures are stored on the dealer row and rewritten from that definition whenever the dealer's rollups are recomputed — on an allocation, on the cancellation of an allocated order, on a floor-plan adjustment, when a failed road test unwinds an allocation, and on a claim closing — and are otherwise left exactly as last written, so moving the logical clock across a month or year boundary does not by itself rewrite the stored figures even though it does change what the live counts of the admission check and the discount tier return on the next allocation.

Cancelling an order is available to a `SalesAdmin` and to the owning `Dealer`, carries a `cancellation_reason`, and behaves in two very different ways depending on where the order stands. From `PENDING` it simply moves the order to `CANCELLED` and stamps `cancelled_at`; nothing was ever drawn and no trailer was ever attached, so there are no floor-plan or trailer effects at all. From `ALLOCATED` it is a full compensating action running three hops. Hop one moves the order to `CANCELLED`, stamps `cancelled_at`, and clears `allocated_unit_id` to null. Hop two, because the order released the trailer, moves the trailer's `build_status` `ALLOCATED` → `RELEASED_TO_STOCK` and clears its `dealer_order_id` to null; the trailer's `released_to_stock_at` is deliberately **not** rewritten, so it keeps its original stock age and its original place in the allocation ranking. Hop three moves `floor_plan_status` `DRAWN` → `RELEASED`, reduces the dealer's `floor_plan_outstanding_cents` by that order's `floor_plan_draw_cents`, recomputes `floor_plan_available_cents`, and increments the dealer's `version`. Both allocation counters are then recomputed, and cancelled orders drop out of both. The order's invoice fields and its `floor_plan_draw_cents` are retained on the row for audit; changing `floor_plan_status` is what removes them from the conserved sum, and an allocate-then-cancel pair must therefore leave `floor_plan_outstanding_cents` identical to its pre-allocation value. Cancellation is refused with `ERR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID` from any status other than `PENDING` or `ALLOCATED`.

Delivery is a `SalesAdmin` action carrying a `delivery_note`. It is admissible only from `ALLOCATED`, with the attached trailer also in `build_status` `ALLOCATED`; anything else is refused with `ERR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`. Delivery moves the order `ALLOCATED` → `DELIVERED` and stamps `delivered_at` from the logical clock. The trailer moves `build_status` `ALLOCATED` → `SHIPPED`, which is terminal, and its `warranty_start_date` is stamped with the same logical instant — the trailer's warranty runs for 730 days from that moment, so its expiry is that instant plus 730 days. The model's `active_unit_count` is recomputed, since shipped trailers drop out of it. Critically, the order's `floor_plan_status` stays `DRAWN`: delivering a trailer does not repay the dealer's floor plan, and only a payoff adjustment does that.

Two list channels serve the sales floor. Trailers can be listed filtered by `model_id`, by owning `dealer_order_id` and by `build_status`, windowed by `limit` and `offset` with `total_count` and `has_more` riding in the envelope. Dealer orders can be listed filtered by `dealer_id`, by `model_id`, by `allocated_unit_id` and by `status`, and a single order reads back with its pinned invoice breakdown and its floor-plan draw.

### 3.9. Warranty Adjudication, Reimbursement and Part Shipment

Warranty is the last chapter of a trailer's record and the only one written after it has left the plant. A claim may be filed by the owning `Dealer` or by a `SalesAdmin`, and it names a `claim_ref` — the dealer's own claim reference, 4 to 24 characters, unique across all claims — the `unit_id` of the failed trailer, the `dealer_id` of the claiming dealership, which must be the dealer that took delivery of that trailer under its dealer order and which a `Dealer` caller's own scope check of §2.1 already pins to `X-User-ID`, a `complaint_code` naming the failed system as one of `AXLE`, `BRAKE`, `LIGHTING`, `FRAME`, `FLOORING` or `COUPLER`, the `labor_hours` claimed carried to one decimal place and at least 0.1, an array of `part_lines`, and a `failure_date` naming the instant the failure occurred. Each entry of `part_lines` carries a `part_number`, a `quantity` of at least 1, and a `unit_cost_cents` that is a non-negative integer; the array may be empty, which makes the claim labor-only. The trailer must already have been delivered — its `build_status` must be `SHIPPED` and its `warranty_start_date` must not be null — and a claim against an undelivered trailer is refused with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_DELIVERED`.

Two substantive checks run at filing. The claimed `labor_hours` may not exceed the published flat-rate allowance for the `complaint_code`, uplifted by one quarter. The published allowances are 3.5 hours for `AXLE`, 2.5 for `BRAKE`, 1.5 for `LIGHTING`, 6.0 for `FRAME`, 4.0 for `FLOORING` and 2.0 for `COUPLER`, and the ceiling is that allowance multiplied by one and a quarter — so a brake claim may be filed for up to 3.125 hours and a frame claim for up to 7.5 hours. The comparison is inclusive: hours landing exactly on the ceiling are accepted, and hours above it are refused with `ERR_LABOR_HOURS_EXCEED_FLAT_RATE`. Separately, the `failure_date` must fall no more than 90 days past the trailer's warranty expiry, where expiry is the trailer's `warranty_start_date` plus 730 days; a failure later than that closes the goodwill window and is refused with `ERR_WARRANTY_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.

A filed claim opens with `status` `SUBMITTED`, `part_line_count` set to the number of entries in `part_lines`, `shipped_line_count` at 0 and `info_request_round` at 0. **No money is computed at filing.** `coverage_variant`, `coverage_factor`, `labor_cents`, `parts_subtotal_cents`, `freight_cents` and `reimbursement_total_cents` all stay null until the claim is approved, and `tracking_number`, `reviewed_by_user_id` and `denial_reason` stay null too.

Adjudication belongs to `SalesAdmin` and carries a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `DENY` or `REQUEST_INFO` together with a `review_note`. A review is admissible only while `status` is `SUBMITTED`; anything else is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. Every review records the acting administrator's `X-User-ID` as `reviewed_by_user_id`.

A `REQUEST_INFO` decision opens the negotiation loop, and the loop is bounded. While `info_request_round` is below 2, the decision adds one to `info_request_round` and moves the claim `SUBMITTED` → `INFO_REQUESTED`. The owning `Dealer` answers by resubmitting, which carries a mandatory `response_note` and may optionally carry a replacement `labor_hours` and a replacement `part_lines`; resubmission moves the claim `INFO_REQUESTED` → `SUBMITTED`, retains `info_request_round` exactly as it stands — the loop counter never resets — and recomputes `part_line_count` from whatever `part_lines` now holds. Omitting both optional fields resubmits the claim unchanged; supplying either replaces it and re-arms both the flat-rate ceiling and the warranty-window check against the new values. Money remains deferred to approval. Resubmission is admissible only from `INFO_REQUESTED` and only for the dealer named on the claim. When a third information request is attempted — that is, when `info_request_round` already stands at 2 — the loop terminates instead of continuing: `info_request_round` is raised to 3, the claim moves `SUBMITTED` → `DENIED`, and `denial_reason` is recorded as `INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT_REACHED`. No reimbursement fields are computed on that path.

A `DENY` decision moves the claim `SUBMITTED` → `DENIED` with `denial_reason` recorded as `REVIEWER_DENIED`. No money is computed, and the dealer's `warranty_receivable_cents` is left untouched. `DENIED` is terminal.

An `APPROVE` decision is where reimbursement is computed, and its rounding order is pinned. The parts subtotal is built **one line at a time**: for each entry of `part_lines`, the line's `unit_cost_cents` is marked up by thirty hundredths — a 30 percent parts markup, so the exact value is the cost multiplied by one and three tenths — that single marked-up figure is rounded half up to whole cents, the rounded figure is **then** multiplied by the line's `quantity`, and only then is the result added into `parts_subtotal_cents`. Summing the raw marked-up values and rounding once at the end is explicitly the wrong answer and will differ by cents. The labor amount, `labor_cents`, is the claim's `labor_hours` multiplied by the dealer's `labor_rate_cents_per_hour` read live at approval time, rounded half up to whole cents. Part freight, `freight_cents`, is 0 when `parts_subtotal_cents` is 0, is also 0 when `parts_subtotal_cents` is 500 dollars or more — that is, 50000 cents or more, the freight waiver — and is otherwise a flat 45 dollars, or 4500 cents. The gross amount is `labor_cents` plus `parts_subtotal_cents` plus `freight_cents`, carried exactly with no rounding.

Coverage is then decided from how far past expiry the failure fell, measured in whole days on the logical calendar: the whole number of days from the trailer's expiry instant — its `warranty_start_date` plus 730 days — to the claim's `failure_date`, discarding any fractional day. When that figure is zero or negative, meaning the failure happened on or before expiry, `coverage_variant` is `IN_WARRANTY_FULL` and `coverage_factor` is 1.00, so the reimbursement equals the gross amount exactly. Past expiry, coverage steps down through three 30-day goodwill bands, all with `coverage_variant` `GOODWILL_PRORATED`: from 1 through 30 days past, `coverage_factor` is 0.75; from 31 through 60 days past, it is 0.50; and from 61 through 90 days past, it is 0.25. Beyond 90 days there is no coverage, which is the same boundary the filing check enforces. `reimbursement_total_cents` is the gross amount multiplied by `coverage_factor`, rounded half up to whole cents. Rounding therefore happens once per part line and once more on the coverage multiplication, and nowhere else. Because the goodwill factors bite on the whole gross figure, identical labor hours and identical part lines reimburse strictly less outside the warranty window than inside it.

What happens next depends on whether there is anything to ship. When `part_line_count` is greater than zero the claim moves `SUBMITTED` → `APPROVED` and waits for parts. When `part_line_count` is exactly zero — a labor-only claim with nothing to ship — the claim moves straight through `APPROVED` to `CLOSED` on the same request, and the dealer's `warranty_receivable_cents` grows by `reimbursement_total_cents` with the dealer's `version` incremented.

Shipping parts is a `SalesAdmin` action carrying a `tracking_number` and an optional `line_count` naming how many of the claim's part lines this shipment covers; when `line_count` is omitted the shipment covers one line. A shipment is admissible while `status` is `APPROVED` — with `part_line_count` greater than zero — or `PARTS_SHIPPED`, and the running `shipped_line_count` plus this shipment's `line_count` may not exceed `part_line_count`; a violation of either condition is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. Each shipment adds its `line_count` to `shipped_line_count` and overwrites `tracking_number` with the newest shipment's number, so the field always names the most recent shipment. While lines remain outstanding the claim sits in `PARTS_SHIPPED`, and a further partial shipment leaves it in `PARTS_SHIPPED` while advancing the counter. Once `shipped_line_count` equals `part_line_count`, the claim moves to `CLOSED` and the dealer's `warranty_receivable_cents` grows by `reimbursement_total_cents` — credited exactly once, no matter how many shipments it took — with the dealer's `version` incremented. Closing a claim touches no floor-plan field. `CLOSED` and `DENIED` are the two terminal states of a claim.

Claims are readable individually, complete with their `coverage_variant`, `coverage_factor` and full reimbursement breakdown, and listable filtered by `unit_id`, by `dealer_id` and by `status` — with the `dealer_id` filter mandatory and self-referential for a `Dealer` caller, per §2.1.
