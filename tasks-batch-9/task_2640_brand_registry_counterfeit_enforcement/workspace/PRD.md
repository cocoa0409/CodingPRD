# Brand Registry & Counterfeit Enforcement API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Brand protection teams live with a structural asymmetry: a counterfeiter can publish a listing in minutes, while the rights holder needs a trademark registration, a marketplace brand-registry enrolment, a designated agent of record, a screened match, a substantiated claim, a sealed evidence package and a marketplace notice before a single listing comes down. Today that chain is stitched together out of spreadsheets, shared inboxes and per-marketplace portals, so the same infringing seller is chased three times under three different reference numbers, evidence goes missing between the test-buy courier and the outside counsel, and nobody can answer the board's only real question — what fraction of the notices we file actually result in removal, and which sellers keep coming back.

This document specifies the **Brand Registry & Counterfeit Enforcement API**, a single system of record for that entire chain. It carries the trademark portfolio and its marketplace enrolments, the authorised-representative designations that give individual humans the legal power to file notices, the crawler-fed listing monitor and its automated image and text matching, the covert test-buy programme that substantiates infringement with laboratory authentication, the evidence packages that back each notice, the takedown notices themselves with their counter-notice loop and marketplace decision timelines, the seller-identification graph that collapses alias accounts into one enforcement target, cease-and-desist correspondence with escalation to litigation, supply-chain leak investigations that trace diverted lot codes back to a distributor, customs recordation and border-seizure coordination, and finally the enforcement metrics that close the loop.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. First, **enforcement severity accumulates against sellers, not against listings** — a decayed strike ledger is maintained per seller account and per alias cluster, and it is the cluster total that drives investigation, blocking and the right to send correspondence. Second, **no single actor can complete an enforcement action alone**: the analyst builds the case, a designated representative files it, the marketplace decides it, a *different* designated representative authorises litigation, and counsel takes it to court. Every API surface described below is mounted under the `/brand-protection` prefix, with a single test-driving clock endpoint outside it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` does not name one of the six roles below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined. If both headers are present and well-formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the requested endpoint (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `BrandAdministrator`, `EnforcementAnalyst`, `AuthorizedRepresentative`, `MarketplaceLiaison`, `CustomsCoordinator` and `LegalCounsel`.

- A `BrandAdministrator` is the brand owner's registry administrator. It registers brands and trademark registrations, enrols brands in marketplace registries, and designates or revokes authorised representatives.
- An `EnforcementAnalyst` is the brand-protection analyst. It monitors and screens listings, opens, substantiates and dismisses claims, runs test buys, assembles evidence packages, maintains the seller alias graph, and files diversion evidence.
- An `AuthorizedRepresentative` is the designated agent of record for a marketplace enrolment. It files and refiles takedown notices, sends cease-and-desist letters, and authorises litigation.
- A `MarketplaceLiaison` is the marketplace-side counterparty. It activates and suspends brand enrolments, records the platform's decisions on notices, relays sellers' counter notices and resolves them.
- A `CustomsCoordinator` is the border-enforcement coordinator. It records marks with customs authorities and opens and concludes border-seizure and supply-chain-leak investigations.
- A `LegalCounsel` is outside or in-house counsel. It opens enforcement cases, records seller responses to cease-and-desist letters, files suit, and closes cases.

Identity semantics differ by role, and the difference is load-bearing.

When `X-User-Role` is `AuthorizedRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of an existing authorised-representative designation row. The server resolves that row on every request the role makes; an `X-User-ID` that names no such row is rejected with `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). Once resolved, that row's own lifecycle governs the operation: its `designation_status` and its effective window gate the action through `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_NOT_EFFECTIVE` (HTTP 409), and its `authority_scope` gates the action through `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_SCOPE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 403). Ownership and attribution checks on any resource that references a representative compare that resource's representative reference against `X-User-ID` — most importantly, authorising litigation compares `X-User-ID` against the case's `cease_desist_sent_by_rep_id` and refuses a match, and it additionally requires the acting designation to sit under an `ACTIVE` enrolment of the case's brand.

When `X-User-Role` is `BrandAdministrator`, `EnforcementAnalyst`, `MarketplaceLiaison`, `CustomsCoordinator` or `LegalCounsel`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example, it is the value stamped into a representative-attribution field only in the `AuthorizedRepresentative` case — but for these five roles it is never compared against any entity's `id`, and any syntactically non-empty value is accepted.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 when omitted and its minimum accepted value is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` that is not an integer in the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or an `offset` that is not an integer of at least 0, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

List responses carry `response_type` `"array"`, and their `meta` object carries the four pagination fields `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows matching the filters remain beyond the current window.

Rows are always returned ordered by `created_at` ascending. Because the system's clock only advances on demand (§2.5), two rows can legitimately share a `created_at` value; when they do, the tie is broken by persistence sequence ascending — the order in which the server actually stored the rows. A row's `id` must never participate in ordering, ranking, tie-breaking or any other observable decision.

Every filter documented for a list endpoint in §3 and §4 is optional, and filters combine conjunctively: a request supplying two filters returns only rows matching both. A filter value that is not a member of the corresponding enumeration is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

### 2.4. Resource Addressing, Identity and Record Metadata

All fourteen resource families in this domain are top-level peers. A resource is addressed at `/brand-protection/<collection>/{id}`, never nested inside its parent's URL, and reverse navigation is always the filtered-list form — to enumerate a brand's enrolments, list enrolments filtered by brand. Parent linkage is expressed purely by reference fields carried in request bodies.

Every resource carries four server-owned properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with exactly three fractional-second digits, both read from the logical clock described in §2.5; `created_at` is stamped once at creation and `updated_at` is refreshed on every mutating request that touches the row, including a mutation that only recomputes a derived field. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 for each mutating request that touches the row — even when that same request both recomputes derived fields and satisfies an optimistic-concurrency contract, the increment is exactly 1, not 2. Cascaded side effects that reach into other rows increment each of those rows' `version` by 1 as well, and refresh their `updated_at`. A request that changes nothing on a row leaves that row's `version` and `updated_at` untouched. All fourteen resource families carry all four properties; none is exempt.

Path identifiers are named for the resource they address: `brandId`, `registrationId`, `enrollmentId`, `representativeId`, `sellerId`, `listingId`, `claimId`, `testBuyId`, `packageId`, `takedownId`, `counterNoticeId`, `caseId`, `recordationId` and `investigationId`. When a URL carries more than one identifier they are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that resolves to nothing determines the 404 that is returned. References supplied in a request *body* are resolved after the path, in the field order declared for that endpoint, and each missing target yields the not-found error for its own entity family — `ERR_BRAND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SELLER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LISTING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEST_BUY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVIDENCE_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TAKEDOWN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COUNTER_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTOMS_RECORDATION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP status 404.

Creating a new resource returns HTTP 201; every other successful operation, including every action that advances a lifecycle and every read, returns HTTP 200. Unless a subsection below says otherwise, an operation that mutates a resource returns that resource's full representation in `data`.

### 2.5. The Logical Enforcement Clock and Business-Day Deadlines

Nothing in this domain reads wall-clock time. Every system-stamped instant — `created_at`, `updated_at`, `activated_at`, `revoked_at`, `ordered_at`, `received_at`, `submitted_at`, `decided_at`, `sealed_at`, `filed_at`, `resolved_at`, `cd_sent_at`, `detention_notice_at`, `concluded_at` and every deadline derived from them — is read from a single server-side logical UTC clock. At server start-up that clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it moves only when an operator sets it explicitly by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting capability; that capability responds with the resulting `current_utc` value and mutates no rows. Implementations must never fall back to real time, not even before the first clock call.

Every deadline in the domain is computed by the same business-day walk. Starting from a base instant, the walk advances one calendar day at a time and decrements its remaining count only on days that fall Monday through Friday; Saturday and Sunday are not business days and there is no holiday calendar. The walk never counts day zero, so the result is always strictly after the base instant, and it preserves the base instant's time of day exactly. A base instant that itself falls on a weekend is not first snapped forward to Monday — the walk simply steps over the weekend days it passes. For example, a notice submitted on Monday `2026-08-10T09:00:00Z` with a five-business-day allowance is due at `2026-08-17T09:00:00Z`, and a base instant of Saturday `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` with a five-business-day allowance yields Friday `2026-08-07T00:00:00Z`.

Five deadlines use this walk, each with its own allowance. A takedown notice's marketplace decision deadline is measured from the moment of submission, and the allowance depends on which marketplace the filing enrolment covers: five business days on `AMZ`, eight business days on `EBY` and ten business days on `WLM`. A seller's window to file a counter notice is ten business days from the marketplace's removal decision. The brand's window to rebut a counter notice is ten business days from the counter notice's filing. A seller's window to answer a cease-and-desist letter is fourteen business days from the moment the letter was sent. A brand's window to file evidence against a border detention is seven business days from the detention notice.

Every one of those windows is tested inclusively against the logical clock: acting at exactly the deadline instant is still in time, and one second past it is not.

### 2.6. Exact Decimal Arithmetic and Rounding Discipline

All money in this domain is carried as an integer number of cents. All match, deviation and completeness figures are carried to four decimal places. All strike-point and substantiation figures are carried to two decimal places. Every arithmetic step is performed in exact decimal arithmetic and never in binary floating point, and every rounding is half-up — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero. This is observable: multiplying 12.55 by 0.50 gives 6.275, which must round to 6.28; an implementation built on binary floating point produces 6.27 and is wrong.

Where a rounding step falls matters as much as the rounding mode, and the rules differ per calculation. The composite listing match score accumulates its weighted terms at full precision and is rounded exactly once, at the end, to four places. The seller strike ledger does the opposite: each individual strike's decayed contribution is rounded to two places *before* the contributions are summed, and the sum is then rounded to two places again. The test-buy service fee is rounded to a whole number of cents. The substantiation score is rounded to two places, and the seller-history term inside it is itself rounded to four places before it is weighted. Each of these is spelled out with its formula in §3, and none of them may be substituted for another.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Parents

Three write operations depend on a premise the client read from a parent row, and each of them therefore requires the client to quote the parent's `version` back to the server as a mandatory, non-optional request field. If the quoted value does not equal that parent's current `version`, the write is refused, nothing is persisted, and the refusal carries the error the rule nominates. Endpoints not named here take no version field at all.

Enrolling a brand in a marketplace registry pins that brand's portfolio and its `authorized_msrp_cents` into the enrolment decision, so the create requires `expected_brand_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target brand. If another writer has advanced the brand's `version` since the client read it, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BRAND_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Quoting the brand's current `version` instead returns 201 and increments the brand's `version` by 1. The refusal is caused only by the stale premise: a brand may hold one active enrolment per marketplace, so a second enrolment create that quotes the current brand `version` and names a different `marketplace_code` succeeds normally.

Opening an evidence package is assembled against a specific reading of the claim — the claim's `claimed_violation_type` determines which item types are required, and its substantiation posture determines whether a laboratory report is expected. The create therefore requires `expected_claim_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent claim, and a mismatch with the claim's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Quoting the current value returns 201 and increments the claim's `version` by 1. Again only the stale premise is refused: a claim legitimately holds several packages, so a second package create quoting the current claim `version` succeeds.

Linking an alias account to a canonical seller merges the alias's strike history into the canonical cluster, so the decision depends on the canonical row's current cluster membership and totals. The link therefore requires `expected_canonical_seller_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the canonical seller, and a mismatch with that seller's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SELLER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Quoting the current value returns 200, recomputes both strike variants across the enlarged cluster, and increments the canonical seller's `version` by 1. A canonical seller may absorb many aliases, so linking a different alias while quoting the current canonical `version` succeeds.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline and Derived-State Propagation

Enforcement state in this domain is layered, and a single request routinely rewrites rows several relationships away from the one it addresses. Three propagation habits are global.

First, **derived state is never client-supplied**. A brand's registry posture, a seller's escalation status, a listing's screening verdict and enforcement posture, a claim's lifecycle status, a case's rolled-up status, and every score, factor, rate and deadline in the domain are computed by the server from the rules in §3. A client-supplied value for any of them is ignored outright, not rejected.

Second, **cascades run to completion inside the request that triggered them, before the response is written**. Consistency is immediate; there is no deferred queue. When a marketplace enrolment leaves the active state, for example, the request that caused it also withdraws that enrolment's live notices, releases the listings those notices were holding, demotes the claims behind those listings, and re-derives the owning brand's posture — all before the 200 is returned.

Third, **every row a cascade actually changes has its `version` incremented by exactly 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed**, and rows the cascade merely inspects are left alone. A recompute that produces the value already stored is not a change and must not bump anything. This is what makes the time-driven sweep idempotent: replaying it at an unchanged clock value alters no row and reports all-zero counts.

Two derived values additionally *re-derive themselves* whenever their inputs move, from wherever the move originated. A brand's posture is re-derived after every create, activation, suspension or supersession of any of its marketplace enrolments and after any registration status change that cascades into them. Both seller strike-point variants, and the escalation status they drive, are re-derived whenever a strike is attributed or drops out of the ledger, whenever an alias is linked, and on the sweep's decay pass.

### 2.9. Order of Rejection

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned: the violation with the **lowest priority number** in the ladder below. Evaluation stops there and nothing is persisted — not even a value the server had already computed. The ladder is normative and complete; a later gate is never reached when an earlier one has already fired.

1. **Priority 1** — `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are both present and the role is one of the six declared names, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Priority 2** — the acting role appears in the target endpoint's authorised-role list, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Priority 3** — request shape: required fields present, enumerated values in range, integer amounts not negative, dates and timestamps ISO 8601, matcher sub-scores inside 0 through 1 inclusive, `effective_from` strictly earlier than `effective_to` when both are supplied, and `recorded_on` no later than `expires_on`; else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every write endpoint and to every list endpoint carrying filters.
4. **Priority 4** — every identifier embedded in the path exists, validated strictly left to right, else the matching not-found error (404). Applies to every endpoint with path parameters.
5. **Priority 5** — every reference supplied in the body exists, validated in the field order declared by that endpoint's input list, else the matching not-found error (404). Applies to every write endpoint carrying reference fields.
6. **Priority 6** — `limit` is an integer from 1 through 100 and `offset` is an integer of at least 0, else `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400). Applies to every list endpoint.
7. **Priority 15** — the supplied parent-version field equals the parent row's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_BRAND_VERSION` for the brand check on enrolment creation, `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` for the claim check on evidence-package creation, or `ERR_STALE_SELLER_VERSION` for the canonical-seller check on alias linking (412 in all three cases). Applies to exactly three operations: enrolment creation, evidence-package creation and alias linking.
8. **Priority 20** — the target row is in a state from which the requested transition exists, including the cross-entity state gates spelled out in §3 (the claim must be substantiated before a notice is filed, the package must be sealed before it is attached, a test buy must be received before it is authenticated, an investigation must have evidence submitted before it may be confirmed as diversion, and the litigation track must be untouched for a no-action closure); else `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to every action endpoint.
9. **Priority 22** — the referenced marketplace enrolment is `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to three operations: designating a representative, filing a notice, and refiling a notice.
10. **Priority 25** — the target evidence package is still `ASSEMBLING`, else `ERR_EVIDENCE_PACKAGE_SEALED` (409). Applies to appending an evidence item.
11. **Priority 28** — the target listing's `monitor_status` is `FLAGGED`, else `ERR_LISTING_NOT_FLAGGED` (409). Applies to opening a claim.
12. **Priority 30** — the acting representative designation is `DESIGNATED` and the logical clock now falls inside its half-open effective window, else `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_NOT_EFFECTIVE` (409). Applies to four operations: filing a notice, refiling a notice, sending a cease-and-desist letter, and authorising litigation.
13. **Priority 35** — the acting representative's `authority_scope` covers what the operation demands, else `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_SCOPE_INSUFFICIENT` (403). Applies to the same four operations: filing a notice, refiling a notice, sending a cease-and-desist letter, and authorising litigation.
14. **Priority 40** — segregation of duties: the representative authorising litigation is not the one that sent the cease-and-desist letter, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409). Applies to authorising litigation.
15. **Priority 42** — the case's seller is `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` or `BLOCKED`, else `ERR_SELLER_NOT_UNDER_INVESTIGATION` (422). Applies to sending a cease-and-desist letter.
16. **Priority 44** — the correspondence track has reached `CD_RESPONDED` or `CD_IGNORED`, else `ERR_CEASE_DESIST_NOT_ANSWERED` (409). Applies to authorising litigation.
17. **Priority 45** — the asserted trademark registration is enforceable, meaning its status is `REGISTERED` rather than `PENDING` or `LAPSED`, and, for enrolment activation, its `nice_class` matches the requested class; else `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422). Applies to four operations: activating an enrolment, drafting a takedown notice, filing a notice, and confirming a customs recordation.
18. **Priority 46** — the registration behind a customs recordation sits on the principal register, else `ERR_RECORDATION_NOT_PRINCIPAL_REGISTER` (422). Applies to confirming a customs recordation.
19. **Priority 48** — the test buy carries a non-null `chain_of_custody_ref`, else `ERR_CHAIN_OF_CUSTODY_INCOMPLETE` (422). Applies to recording an authentication verdict.
20. **Priority 50** — the evidence package satisfies both seal conjuncts, full required-type coverage and a `completeness_score` of at least 0.8000; else `ERR_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE` (422). Applies to sealing a package.
21. **Priority 52** — the full-variant substantiation score reaches 85.00, else `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBSTANTIATED` (422) with no field persisted, not even the freshly computed score. Applies to substantiating a claim.
22. **Priority 55** — a counter notice is filed at or before the takedown's `counter_notice_deadline_at`, else `ERR_COUNTER_NOTICE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (422). Applies to filing a counter notice.
23. **Priority 56** — border-seizure evidence is submitted at or before the investigation's `response_due_at`, else `ERR_SEIZURE_RESPONSE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (422). Applies to submitting diversion evidence.
24. **Priority 57** — no notice of the claim sits in a live status of `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED_REMOVED` or `COUNTER_NOTICED`, else `ERR_DECISION_WINDOW_OPEN` (409). Applies to dismissing a claim.
25. **Priority 58** — the source test buy of a test-buy-sourced investigation is `AUTHENTICATED_COUNTERFEIT` and its `lot_code` matches the investigation's, else `ERR_LOT_CODE_NOT_TRACEABLE` (422). Applies to two operations: opening a diversion investigation and submitting its evidence.
26. **Priority 60** — the evidence package belongs to the same claim as the notice, and on a refiling differs from the package already attached; else `ERR_EVIDENCE_PACKAGE_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to two operations: drafting a takedown notice and refiling one.
27. **Priority 62** — an alias link is at most one level deep and is not a self-link, else `ERR_ALIAS_CHAIN_NOT_ALLOWED` (422). Applies to alias linking.
28. **Priority 65** — at most one counter notice exists per takedown, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_COUNTER_NOTICE` (409). Applies to filing a counter notice.
29. **Priority 66** — the brand holds at most one `ACTIVE` enrolment per marketplace, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT` (409). Applies to enrolment creation.
30. **Priority 68** — `round_number` is below 3 before a refiling is accepted, else `ERR_MAX_TAKEDOWN_ROUNDS` (409). Applies to refiling a notice.
31. **Priority 70** — the designation's effective window does not overlap a `DESIGNATED` peer of the same enrolment with an intersecting authority scope, else `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to designating a representative.
32. **Priority 72** — the new test buy is more than seven days away from every non-cancelled test buy against the same seller, else `ERR_TEST_BUY_COOLING_PERIOD` (409). Applies to ordering a test buy.
33. **Priority 74** — the recordation's date interval does not intersect another recorded interval for the same brand, authority and class, else `ERR_RECORDATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to confirming a customs recordation.
34. **Priority 80** — the brand's enforcement budget can absorb the order, else `ERR_TEST_BUY_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (402). Applies to ordering a test buy. Fiscal checks run last, after every state and safety gate.

Three consequences of that ordering are worth stating outright, because they are easy to get backwards. Ordering a test buy against a dismissed claim for a brand whose spend already exhausts its budget returns `ERR_INVALID_STATE` with HTTP 409, not the budget error — state precedes every business rule and the fiscal gate is never reached. Drafting a takedown notice that both asserts a lapsed registration and attaches another claim's package returns `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_ELIGIBLE` with HTTP 422, not the mismatch error — asserting a dead trademark is the graver defect and its gate sits earlier. Filing a second counter notice against a takedown whose filing window has already closed returns `ERR_COUNTER_NOTICE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` with HTTP 422, not the duplicate error — a late duplicate reports lateness, not duplication.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Brand Onboarding, the Trademark Portfolio and Registry Posture

Everything begins with a brand. A `BrandAdministrator` registers the brand owner by supplying the `name` under which the brand appears on marketplaces (1 to 120 characters, not blank), the `owner_legal_entity` that owns the trademark portfolio (1 to 160 characters, not blank), the `home_jurisdiction` as an ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code written as exactly two uppercase letters, and `authorized_msrp_cents`, the brand-level authorised reference price in cents, which must be at least 1 because it serves as the denominator of the price-deviation term in listing screening. The administrator may also supply `enforcement_budget_cents`, the ceiling for cumulative test-buy spend on the brand; when omitted it defaults to 500000 cents, and when supplied it must be at least 0. A freshly registered brand starts with `status` `DRAFT` and `enforcement_spend_cents` at 0.

`enforcement_spend_cents` is a ledger mirror, not an input. It always equals the sum of `billable_total_cents` over every test buy whose claim resolves to this brand and whose status is `ORDERED`, `RECEIVED`, `AUTHENTICATED_AUTHENTIC` or `AUTHENTICATED_COUNTERFEIT`. It is never negative and never exceeds `enforcement_budget_cents`. Cancelled purchases leave the ledger, which is what makes the compensating reversal in §3.6 exact.

Brands can be read one at a time, or listed with pagination and optional filtering on `status` and on `home_jurisdiction`.

**Registry posture.** A brand's `status` is never supplied by a client; it is a projection over that brand's marketplace enrolments, re-derived after every enrolment create, activation, suspension or supersession, and after any registration status change that cascades into an enrolment. The precedence is ordered and the *strongest* posture the brand holds anywhere wins, regardless of how many weaker enrolments exist alongside it. If any enrolment of the brand is `ACTIVE`, the brand is `ENROLLED`. Otherwise, if any enrolment of the brand is `SUBMITTED`, the brand is `ENROLLMENT_PENDING`. Otherwise, if the brand has at least one enrolment at all — meaning only suspended or terminated rows remain — the brand is `ENROLLMENT_LAPSED`. If the brand has never had an enrolment, it stays `DRAFT`. There is no marketplace filtering and no age filtering in that projection: one `ACTIVE` enrolment on Amazon alongside one suspended enrolment on eBay yields `ENROLLED`; one `SUBMITTED` alongside one terminated yields `ENROLLMENT_PENDING`; two suspended enrolments yield `ENROLLMENT_LAPSED`.

Consequently a brand walks `DRAFT → ENROLLMENT_PENDING` on its first enrolment, `ENROLLMENT_PENDING → ENROLLED` when an enrolment is activated, `ENROLLED → ENROLLMENT_PENDING` when the last active enrolment leaves the active state while a submitted one remains, `ENROLLED → ENROLLMENT_LAPSED` when the last active enrolment is suspended, superseded or auto-suspended by the sweep and no submitted enrolment remains, `ENROLLMENT_PENDING → ENROLLMENT_LAPSED` when the last submitted enrolment is superseded, and `ENROLLMENT_LAPSED → ENROLLMENT_PENDING` on a re-enrolment. Each of those re-derivations increments the brand's `version` by 1, and the last one is reached in the very same request that suspended the enrolment — a brand can go from `ENROLLED` to `ENROLLMENT_LAPSED` inside a single registration-lapse cascade.

**The trademark portfolio.** A `BrandAdministrator` records each registration or pending application against the brand by supplying `brand_id`, the registry-issued `registration_number` (1 to 40 characters), the issuing registry's `jurisdiction` as exactly two uppercase letters, the `nice_class` covered by the registration as an integer from 1 through 45, the `registration_date` and `expiry_date` as ISO 8601 calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form with the expiry strictly after the registration date, the boolean `principal_register` flag that is true when the mark sits on the principal rather than the supplemental register, and the initial `status`. The pair of `jurisdiction` and `registration_number` is unique across the registry; a create that repeats an existing pair is refused as an invalid request with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Only `PENDING` and `REGISTERED` may be supplied as the initial `status`. The full domain of the field is `PENDING`, `REGISTERED` and `LAPSED`, but there is no operation in this version that promotes a pending application to registered, and `LAPSED` is reachable only through the sweep's auto-lapse pass, described in §3.11. A brand that intends to enrol therefore creates its row already `REGISTERED`. Registrations are readable individually and listable with optional filters on `brand_id`, `status`, `jurisdiction` and `nice_class`. Once recorded, a registration becomes assertable by enrolments, by claims and by customs recordations, and `principal_register` becomes the gate that separates ordinary marketplace enforcement from customs recordation.

### 3.2. Marketplace Enrolment, Supersession and Agents of Record

Enrolling a brand in a marketplace's own brand registry is the act that unlocks enforcement on that marketplace. A `BrandAdministrator` submits the enrolment with `brand_id`, the `asserted_registration_id` naming the registration offered as the enrolment's legal basis, the `marketplace_code` the enrolment covers — one of `AMZ`, `EBY` or `WLM` — the `requested_nice_class` the brand asks the marketplace registry to cover as an integer from 1 through 45, and the `expected_brand_version` described in §2.7. The new row lands in `enrollment_status` `SUBMITTED` with `activated_at`, `suspension_reason_code` and `superseded_by_enrollment_id` all null, and the owning brand's posture is immediately re-derived, which increments the brand's `version` by 1.

A brand may hold only one `ACTIVE` enrolment per marketplace; attempting to create a second while one is already active on the same `marketplace_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT` (HTTP 409). A `SUBMITTED` or `SUSPENDED` row on the same marketplace, by contrast, does **not** block the create — it is *superseded*. Supersession is the only path by which an enrolment ever reaches `TERMINATED`: the older row moves `SUBMITTED → TERMINATED` or `SUSPENDED → TERMINATED`, its `superseded_by_enrollment_id` is set to the new enrolment's `id`, and every representative designation still attached to it and still `DESIGNATED` moves to `REVOKED` with `revoked_at` stamped from the logical clock. The brand's posture is re-derived, and the superseded row, each revoked designation and the brand each have their `version` incremented by 1.

Enrolments are readable individually and listable with optional filters on `brand_id`, `asserted_registration_id`, `superseded_by_enrollment_id`, `marketplace_code` and `enrollment_status`.

**Activation.** The marketplace side of the conversation accepts the enrolment, taking it `SUBMITTED → ACTIVE`. A `MarketplaceLiaison` performs the acceptance, and three cross-entity gates must hold on the asserted registration. Its status must be `REGISTERED`: a `PENDING` or `LAPSED` registration is refused with `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). Its `expiry_date` must be no earlier than the logical clock's calendar date. And its `nice_class` must equal the enrolment's `requested_nice_class`, since a marketplace registry will not cover a class the mark does not claim; a mismatch carries the same `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. On success `activated_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the brand's posture is re-derived from `ENROLLMENT_PENDING` to `ENROLLED`, and the enrolment and the brand each have their `version` incremented by 1.

**Suspension and its three-level cascade.** A `MarketplaceLiaison` may suspend an `ACTIVE` enrolment, taking it `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, supplying a `reason_code` which is persisted as `suspension_reason_code` and must be one of `MANUAL`, `REGISTRATION_LAPSED` or `MARKETPLACE_ACTION`. Because the enrolment has left the active state, three levels of consequence follow inside the same request. At the first level, every takedown notice filed through this enrolment whose status is `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`. At the second level, because those notices were withdrawn, each of them reaches through its claim to that claim's listing, and any such listing whose `enforcement_status` is `TAKEDOWN_PENDING` is released back to `CLAIM_OPEN`. At the third level, because those listings left `TAKEDOWN_PENDING`, every claim on such a listing whose status is `ENFORCED` is demoted back to `SUBSTANTIATED`. The brand's posture is then re-derived, and every row touched at any of the three levels — plus the brand — has its `version` incremented by 1.

The identical cascade runs when the sweep auto-suspends an `ACTIVE` enrolment because the registration it asserts has just lapsed; in that case `suspension_reason_code` is set to `REGISTRATION_LAPSED` by the system rather than supplied by a caller.

**Agents of record.** A marketplace notice is a legal instrument, so it must be attributable to a named human with delegated authority. A `BrandAdministrator` designates such an agent against an enrolment by supplying `enrollment_id`, the `authority_scope` being delegated, the inclusive `effective_from` instant of the designation window, the `power_of_attorney_ref` document reference substantiating the designation (1 to 80 characters, not blank), and optionally the exclusive `effective_to` end of the window. The referenced enrolment must be `ACTIVE` at the moment of designation, else `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). The new row starts `DESIGNATED` with `revoked_at` null, and its own `id` becomes a valid `X-User-ID` for the `AuthorizedRepresentative` role.

`authority_scope` is one of `SUBMIT_TAKEDOWN`, `AUTHORIZE_LITIGATION` or `BOTH`, and it behaves as a *set* of powers: `SUBMIT_TAKEDOWN` grants only the power to file notices and send correspondence, `AUTHORIZE_LITIGATION` grants only the power to authorise litigation, and `BOTH` grants both powers.

The designation window is **half-open**: it includes `effective_from` and excludes `effective_to`, and an omitted or null `effective_to` means the window never closes. Two designations of the same enrolment conflict only when all three of the following hold: both are still `DESIGNATED`, their power sets intersect, and their windows overlap in the half-open sense — that is, the first window's start lies strictly before the second's end and the second's start lies strictly before the first's end. Because the end is exclusive, **adjacency is allowed**: a window ending at exactly the instant the next begins is not a conflict. A conflict is refused with `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Note carefully that `SUBMIT_TAKEDOWN` and `AUTHORIZE_LITIGATION` do not intersect each other, so two single-power designations may hold fully overlapping windows on the same enrolment, whereas two `BOTH` designations may not, and a `BOTH` designation conflicts with either single-power designation.

A `BrandAdministrator` may revoke a designation at any time while it is `DESIGNATED`, optionally supplying a `reason_code` for the audit trail; the row moves `DESIGNATED → REVOKED`, `revoked_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and its `version` is incremented by 1. Revocation releases the window-and-scope occupancy the row held, so a fresh designation may then cover an overlapping window. Rows already stamped with a revoked designation's `id` keep that attribution permanently — revocation is forward-looking only. Designations are readable individually and listable with optional filters on `enrollment_id`, `designation_status` and `authority_scope`.

### 3.3. Seller Identity, Alias Clusters and the Strike Ledger

Counterfeit sellers hide behind multiple storefronts, so the enforcement target in this system is a *seller cluster*, not a storefront. An `EnforcementAnalyst` registers each storefront under observation by supplying the `marketplace_code` on which the identity trades (`AMZ`, `EBY` or `WLM`), the marketplace-assigned `seller_ref` (1 to 40 characters), the `legal_name` obtained through seller identification (1 to 160 characters, not blank), and optionally the `jurisdiction` the seller declares, as exactly two uppercase letters. The pair of `marketplace_code` and `seller_ref` is unique across the system, and a create repeating an existing pair is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). A new seller row starts with `status` `ACTIVE`, `canonical_seller_id` null, and both `standalone_strike_points` and `cluster_strike_points` at 0.00. Sellers are readable individually and listable with optional filters on `canonical_seller_id` — which is how an entire alias cluster is enumerated — on `marketplace_code` and on `status`.

**Alias clustering.** When identification work establishes that one storefront is an alias of another, the analyst links the alias to its canonical seller, supplying `canonical_seller_id` naming the cluster root and the `expected_canonical_seller_version` described in §2.7. The path seller becomes the alias; its `canonical_seller_id` is persisted. Clusters are exactly one level deep, which imposes three refusals, all carrying `ERR_ALIAS_CHAIN_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 422): the row being linked must not already name a canonical seller, the target must not itself be an alias of somebody else, and a seller may not be linked to itself. On success both strike variants are recomputed for every member of the enlarged cluster, the escalation ladder is re-evaluated for each of them, and the alias row and the canonical row each have their `version` incremented by 1.

**Where strikes come from.** Exactly three events put a strike into the ledger, each with its own base weight, its own effective instant, and its own attribution path.

- A **takedown removal** strike carries a base of 12.55 points. It exists for as long as a takedown notice sits in status `APPROVED_REMOVED`; its effective instant is that notice's `decided_at`, and it is attributed to the seller that owns the listing behind the notice's claim.
- A **seizure confirmed** strike carries a base of 25.15 points — the heaviest single strike in the domain, enough to escalate a previously clean seller on its own. It exists for as long as a diversion investigation sits in status `DIVERSION_CONFIRMED`; its effective instant is that investigation's `concluded_at`, and it is attributed to the seller named on the investigation.
- A **cease-and-desist ignored** strike carries a base of 5.05 points. It exists for as long as an enforcement case's `correspondence_status` is `CD_IGNORED`; its effective instant is that case's `cd_response_due_at`, and it is attributed to the seller the case is aimed at.

Reversal is *exclusion from the input set*, never a negative contribution. A notice that leaves `APPROVED_REMOVED` — because it was reinstated or withdrawn — simply stops contributing, and the ledger is recomputed from scratch over whatever strikes remain. This is the mechanism by which an accepted counter notice undoes a removal strike, and it is also why traversing the notice cycle cannot double-count: returning to `APPROVED_REMOVED` after a rebuttal re-uses the same single strike rather than adding a second one.

**How the points are computed.** For a given set of seller rows, the ledger gathers every strike attributed to any seller in that set and enumerates them in a fully determined order: by effective instant ascending, and for two strikes sharing an effective instant, by the `created_at` of the source row ascending. A row's `id` is never consulted. For each strike in turn, its age in whole days is the number of complete 24-hour periods between its effective instant and the logical clock now — that is, the elapsed seconds divided by 86400 and truncated downward. A strike whose age is 180 days or fewer is **fresh** and carries its full base weight; a strike whose age is 181 days or more is **decayed** and carries half its base weight. The age boundary is therefore inclusive on the fresh side: at exactly 180 days a strike is still fresh, and at 181 days it is decayed.

Each strike's decayed contribution is rounded half-up to two decimal places **before** any summation. The contributions are then added together, the sum is rounded half-up to two decimal places, and the result is capped at 100.00. Rounding once at the end instead of per strike produces a different, wrong answer, and the difference is observable: one takedown-removal strike and one seizure-confirmed strike both aged 181 days contribute 6.28 (12.55 halved is 6.275, which rounds up) and 12.58 (25.15 halved is 12.575, which rounds up), totalling 18.86 — whereas summing the raw halves first gives 18.85. Likewise three takedown-removal strikes all aged 181 days total 18.84 per-strike-first, not 18.83.

The same procedure is run twice with two different input sets, producing the two variants that live side by side on every seller row. `standalone_strike_points` counts only strikes attributed to that exact seller row, ignoring every alias and the canonical root. `cluster_strike_points` counts strikes across the whole alias cluster: the cluster is the root — which is the row's own `canonical_seller_id` when it has one, and otherwise the row's own `id` — unioned with every row that names that root as its `canonical_seller_id`. A seller with zero standalone points can therefore still be blocked on the strength of a sibling's history.

**The escalation ladder.** `status` is a pure function of `cluster_strike_points` and of nothing else. At a cluster total of 60.00 or more the seller is `BLOCKED`; at a cluster total of at least 25.00 but below 60.00 the seller is `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`; below 25.00 the seller is `ACTIVE`. Every recompute rewrites `status` for **every member of the cluster** from the cluster total, so all members always share the same status, and every member whose status or point totals actually changed has its `version` incremented by 1. All six movements of that ladder are reachable: `ACTIVE → UNDER_INVESTIGATION` and `ACTIVE → BLOCKED` on accumulation, `UNDER_INVESTIGATION → BLOCKED` on further accumulation, and `UNDER_INVESTIGATION → ACTIVE`, `BLOCKED → UNDER_INVESTIGATION` and `BLOCKED → ACTIVE` on strike reversal or age decay. A blocked seller that decays below 60.00 but stays at or above 25.00 lands on `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` rather than jumping straight to `ACTIVE`.

Recomputes are triggered by an alias link, by a removal decision, by a counter-notice resolution or expiry, by a confirmed diversion, by the sweep's cease-and-desist-ignored pass and by the sweep's decay pass. To make the thresholds concrete: a single fresh takedown-removal strike is 12.55 points and leaves the seller `ACTIVE`; two fresh takedown-removal strikes total 25.10 and escalate the seller to `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`; one fresh seizure-confirmed strike is 25.15 and escalates on its own; one fresh cease-and-desist-ignored strike is 5.05.

### 3.4. Listing Monitoring and Automated Screening

Crawler output enters the system as a monitored listing. An `EnforcementAnalyst` places a marketplace listing under monitoring for a brand by supplying `brand_id`, the `seller_id` of the storefront that owns it, the `marketplace_code` hosting it, the marketplace's own `listing_ref` (1 to 40 characters, unique together with `marketplace_code`; a repeat is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, HTTP 400), the `title_text` captured by the crawler (1 to 300 characters, not blank) which is the input to text matching, the `image_hash` perceptual hash of the hero image (1 to 80 characters, not blank) which is the input to image matching, the `price_cents` at which the listing is offered (at least 0), and the `nice_class` the listed product falls in as an integer from 1 through 45. A new listing starts with `monitor_status` `NEW`, `enforcement_status` `NONE`, `violation_type` `NONE`, and `image_match_score`, `text_match_score`, `price_deviation_factor` and `match_score` all null until the first screening. Listings are readable individually and listable with optional filters on `brand_id`, `seller_id`, `marketplace_code`, `monitor_status`, `enforcement_status` and `violation_type`.

**Two independent dimensions.** A monitored listing carries two lifecycle dimensions on the same row, and they evolve independently. `monitor_status` is the *screening* posture and takes the values `NEW`, `CLEARED` and `FLAGGED`. `enforcement_status` is the *enforcement* posture and takes the values `NONE`, `CLAIM_OPEN`, `TAKEDOWN_PENDING`, `REMOVED` and `REINSTATED`. A re-screen may flip `FLAGGED → CLEARED` while the enforcement dimension stays at `REMOVED`, and a marketplace decision may move the enforcement dimension while the screening dimension stays `FLAGGED`. There are exactly two couplings between them: re-screening is blocked while a notice is pending, and a claim may only be opened against a listing whose `monitor_status` is `FLAGGED`.

**Screening.** An `EnforcementAnalyst` runs a screen by submitting the automated matcher's two sub-scores, `image_match_score` and `text_match_score`, each a decimal from 0 through 1 inclusive carried to four decimal places. Both are persisted on the listing as the last matcher output. A screen is permitted whenever the listing's `monitor_status` is `NEW`, `CLEARED` or `FLAGGED` — re-screening in either direction is expected and normal — but it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) whenever `enforcement_status` is `TAKEDOWN_PENDING`, because a marketplace decision is in flight and the evidence behind it must not shift underneath the marketplace.

The composite is derived in five steps.

First, the **price deviation factor** is derived and never supplied. Take the brand's `authorized_msrp_cents` as the reference and the listing's `price_cents` as the observed price, and compute the discount depth as the reference minus the observed price, divided by the reference. That raw ratio is then clamped into the range 0 through 1 — a listing priced above the authorised reference produces a negative ratio, which clamps to zero, so the factor is never negative — and rounded half-up to four decimal places to yield `price_deviation_factor`. A reference of 10000 cents against a price of 3000 cents gives 0.7000; against a price of 9500 cents it gives 0.0500; against a price of 12000 cents it gives 0.0000.

Second, three weighted terms are accumulated at full precision, with weights that sum to exactly 1.00: the image sub-score contributes forty-five hundredths of its value, the text sub-score contributes thirty-five hundredths of its value, and the price deviation factor contributes twenty hundredths of its value. No intermediate rounding takes place here.

Third, a **seller-risk boost** is added on a mutually exclusive ladder read from the listing's seller. If the seller's `status` is `BLOCKED`, the boost is 0.05. Otherwise, if the seller's `standalone_strike_points` is at least 10.00, the boost is 0.03. Otherwise the boost is 0.00. Note that this ladder consults the *standalone* variant, not the cluster variant, and that only the first matching rung applies.

Fourth, the weighted sum plus the boost is capped at 1.0000 and then rounded half-up, exactly once, to four decimal places. The result is `match_score`.

Fifth, the verdict: if `match_score` is at least 0.7000 the listing's `monitor_status` becomes `FLAGGED`, and otherwise it becomes `CLEARED`. The threshold comparison is inclusive — a score of exactly 0.7000 flags.

Sixth, the **violation classification** is assigned by an ordered ladder in which the first satisfied branch wins and the remaining branches are not consulted. If the verdict was `CLEARED`, `violation_type` is `NONE`. Otherwise, if `image_match_score` is at least 0.85, `violation_type` is `COUNTERFEIT`. Otherwise, if `text_match_score` is at least 0.80, `violation_type` is `TRADEMARK`. Otherwise, if `image_match_score` is at least 0.60 **and** `price_deviation_factor` is at least 0.50, `violation_type` is `COPYRIGHT`. Otherwise `violation_type` is `TRADEMARK`. The top-down evaluation matters: a listing with an image sub-score of 0.92 and a text sub-score of 0.88 is `COUNTERFEIT` and never `TRADEMARK`, even though both sub-conditions hold. `violation_type` is always `NONE` whenever `monitor_status` is `NEW` or `CLEARED`.

Three worked screens make the arithmetic unambiguous. With a reference of 10000 cents and a price of 3000 cents, an image sub-score of 0.92 and a text sub-score of 0.88 with no boost accumulate 0.414 plus 0.308 plus 0.14, giving a `match_score` of 0.8620, a `FLAGGED` verdict and a `COUNTERFEIT` classification. The same price setup with both sub-scores at 0.70 accumulates 0.315 plus 0.245 plus 0.14 for exactly 0.7000, which still flags, and classifies as `COPYRIGHT` — the image sub-score is below 0.85 so the first branch fails, the text sub-score is below 0.80 so the second fails, and the third branch holds because the image sub-score is at least 0.60 and the deviation factor is at least 0.50. Dropping the text sub-score to 0.6990 accumulates 0.69965, which rounds to 0.6997, clears the listing and resets `violation_type` to `NONE`.

Every screen increments the listing's `version` by 1, whether it flags or clears.

The enforcement dimension, by contrast, is never touched by screening. It advances `NONE → CLAIM_OPEN` when a claim is opened, returns `CLAIM_OPEN → NONE` when the last open claim on the listing disappears, advances `CLAIM_OPEN → TAKEDOWN_PENDING` when a notice is filed and `REINSTATED → TAKEDOWN_PENDING` when one is refiled, resolves `TAKEDOWN_PENDING → REMOVED` on a removal decision or `TAKEDOWN_PENDING → CLAIM_OPEN` on a rejection or an enrolment-suspension cascade, and finally moves `REMOVED → REINSTATED` when a counter notice on the listing's notice is accepted or expires. Each of those movements increments the listing's `version` by 1 and is described in the workflow that causes it.

### 3.5. Claims: Opening, Scoring, Substantiation and Dismissal

A flagged listing is a suspicion; a claim is an assertion. An `EnforcementAnalyst` opens a claim by supplying `brand_id`, the `listing_id` of the flagged listing the claim is filed against, the `asserted_registration_id` offered as the claim's legal basis, and the `claimed_violation_type` the brand asserts — one of `COUNTERFEIT`, `COPYRIGHT` or `TRADEMARK` — which also selects the evidence-item set the eventual package must cover. Two gates guard the create. The listing's `monitor_status` must be `FLAGGED`, else `ERR_LISTING_NOT_FLAGGED` (HTTP 409). And no claim on that same listing may already sit in status `DRAFT`, `SUBSTANTIATED` or `ENFORCED`, since a listing carries at most one live claim at a time; a second attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).

On success the claim lands in status `DRAFT`, its `registration_strength` and `preliminary_score` are derived immediately, `substantiation_score` starts at 0.00, `dismissal_reason_code` starts null, and the listing's `enforcement_status` advances `NONE → CLAIM_OPEN` with the listing's `version` incremented by 1. Claims are readable individually and listable with optional filters on `brand_id`, `listing_id`, `asserted_registration_id`, `status` and `claimed_violation_type`.

**The substantiation score.** The score draws on exactly four rows: the claim itself, its listing, that listing's seller, and the asserted registration. It is built from three weighted terms.

The **registration strength** term is an ordered ladder in which the first matching rung wins. If the asserted registration's status is `REGISTERED` and its `principal_register` flag is true and its `nice_class` equals the listing's `nice_class`, `registration_strength` is 1.00. Otherwise, if the registration's status is `REGISTERED`, `registration_strength` is 0.60. Otherwise, if the registration's status is `PENDING`, `registration_strength` is 0.30. Otherwise — meaning the registration has lapsed — `registration_strength` is 0.00.

The **seller history** term takes the listing seller's `standalone_strike_points`, divides it by a normaliser of 37.65 points, caps the ratio at 1, and rounds the result half-up to four decimal places before it is weighted. A seller with 12.55 standalone points therefore yields a history factor of 0.3333.

Those three terms are combined into an undiscounted base: the listing's `match_score` weighted by 60, plus `registration_strength` weighted by 25, plus the seller history factor weighted by 15.

**The two variants diverge from that base.** The *preliminary* variant runs at claim creation, when no evidence has been gathered yet: it multiplies the base by eighty hundredths and rounds half-up to two decimal places to produce `preliminary_score`. The preliminary variant never adds a corroboration bonus, even when a counterfeit-confirmed test buy already exists, and it needs no cap because eighty hundredths of the maximum possible base is 80, which is below 100 by construction. `preliminary_score` is stamped once at creation and is never recomputed afterwards, no matter what happens to the listing, the seller or the registration later.

The *full* variant runs when a claim is substantiated and again whenever a test buy on the claim is authenticated. It uses the undiscounted base and adds a corroboration bonus of 20.00 points when — and only when — there exists a test buy on this same claim whose status is `AUTHENTICATED_COUNTERFEIT` **and** whose `chain_of_custody_ref` is non-null. A merely received buy, a cancelled buy, an authentic-verdict buy, and a counterfeit buy whose custody reference was never captured all contribute nothing. The base plus the bonus is capped at 100.00 and rounded half-up to two decimal places, and the result is `substantiation_score`.

Worked examples: a `match_score` of 0.8620 with a registration strength of 1.00 and zero standalone points yields a base of 51.72 plus 25 plus 0, or 76.72; the preliminary score is 61.376, which rounds to 61.38; the full score without corroboration is 76.72, which is below the threshold, and with corroboration is 96.72, which clears it. A `match_score` of 0.7000 with strength 1.00 yields a base of 67.00, a preliminary score of 53.60, and a corroborated full score of 87.00. A `match_score` of 0.8620 with strength 0.60 yields a base of 66.72, a preliminary score of 53.376 rounding to 53.38, and a corroborated full score of 86.72. And a `match_score` of 0.8620 with strength 1.00 and 12.55 standalone points yields a history factor of 0.3333, a base of 51.72 plus 25 plus 4.9995, or 81.7195, a preliminary score of 65.3756 rounding to 65.38, and a corroborated full score that would be 101.7195 and is therefore capped at 100.00.

**Substantiation.** An `EnforcementAnalyst` asks the system to substantiate a `DRAFT` claim. The full variant is recomputed at that moment, and if the result reaches 85.00 the claim advances `DRAFT → SUBSTANTIATED`, `substantiation_score` is persisted, and the claim's `version` is incremented by 1. If the result falls below 85.00 the request is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_SUBSTANTIATED` (HTTP 422) and **nothing at all is persisted** — not the freshly computed score, not `updated_at`, not `version`. In practice this means a claim cannot be substantiated on screening evidence alone: without a corroborating counterfeit test buy the maximum base is 100 only in the extreme, and the ordinary path to 85.00 runs through the test-buy programme.

**Dismissal.** An `EnforcementAnalyst` may withdraw a claim that is `DRAFT` or `SUBSTANTIATED`, supplying a `reason_code` for the audit trail; the claim moves to `DISMISSED` and its `dismissal_reason_code` is set to `ANALYST_WITHDRAWN`. The withdrawal is refused with `ERR_DECISION_WINDOW_OPEN` (HTTP 409) while any notice on the claim sits in status `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED_REMOVED` or `COUNTER_NOTICED` — a claim may not be abandoned while a marketplace notice is still in flight or still in force. On success two cascades follow: the listing's `enforcement_status` is re-derived, falling `CLAIM_OPEN → NONE` once no open claim remains on it, and every evidence package of the claim whose status is `ASSEMBLING` or `SEALED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`. Every row touched has its `version` incremented by 1.

A claim can also be dismissed by the system rather than by an analyst, on the authentic branch of test-buy authentication described in §3.6; in that case `dismissal_reason_code` is `TEST_BUY_AUTHENTIC` instead. The full domain of `dismissal_reason_code` is `ANALYST_WITHDRAWN`, `TEST_BUY_AUTHENTIC` and `REGISTRATION_INVALID`, and the field stays null for as long as the claim is not dismissed. `DISMISSED` is terminal: a dismissed claim never returns to any other status.

**Bubble-up from notices.** The remaining two movements of the claim lifecycle are driven by its children rather than by any direct action. When a notice on the claim reaches `APPROVED_REMOVED`, the claim advances `SUBSTANTIATED → ENFORCED`. When the enforcing notice later leaves `APPROVED_REMOVED` — reinstated after a counter notice, or withdrawn by an enrolment suspension — and no notice on the claim remains at `APPROVED_REMOVED`, the claim falls back `ENFORCED → SUBSTANTIATED`. Each of those transitions increments the claim's `version` by 1.

### 3.6. The Test Buy Programme

Marketplaces rarely act on a screening score alone; they act on a physical sample and a laboratory report. The test-buy programme is how a claim acquires that corroboration, and because covert purchases cost real money and expose the brand to detection, it is fenced by a budget ceiling and a per-seller cooling period.

**Ordering.** An `EnforcementAnalyst` orders a test buy against a claim, supplying the marketplace's `order_ref` for the covert purchase (1 to 40 characters, not blank), the `purchase_price_cents` paid for the sample, the `shipping_cents` paid to have it delivered, and the `lab_fee_cents` for the authentication booked with the order — each of the three amounts at least 0. The order lands in status `ORDERED`, its `ordered_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and its `seller_id` is *copied* at creation from the claim's listing rather than supplied, so that the cooling period can be evaluated per seller.

**Pricing.** The three supplied amounts are added together to give `subtotal_cents`. A `service_fee_rate` is then selected from `subtotal_cents` **alone**, on an inclusive-upper-bound ladder: a subtotal of 5000 cents or less takes a rate of 0.15, a subtotal above that but no more than 20000 cents takes 0.12, a subtotal above that but no more than 50000 cents takes 0.09, and anything above 50000 cents takes 0.06. `service_fee_cents` is that rate applied to `subtotal_cents` and rounded half-up to a whole number of cents. `billable_total_cents` is `subtotal_cents` plus `service_fee_cents`. All four values are computed once, at order time, and are never re-derived afterwards.

The tier is emphatically **not** re-selected from `billable_total_cents`: there is exactly one pass and no fixed-point iteration, even when adding the fee pushes the total across the next band. A purchase of 4200 plus 700 shipping plus no lab fee gives a subtotal of 4900, which sits in the first band at 0.15, a fee of 735 and a billable total of 5635 — and although 5635 falls inside the second band, the rate is not revised to 0.12. Adding a 2000-cent lab fee to the same purchase gives a subtotal of 6900, the second band at 0.12, a fee of 828 and a billable total of 7728. A subtotal of exactly 5000 takes the first band inclusively, giving a fee of 750 and a total of 5750, while a subtotal of 5001 takes the second band, giving a fee of 600 and a total of only 5601 — one extra cent of subtotal makes the order 149 cents cheaper overall. That non-monotonicity is the intended contract, not a defect. A subtotal of exactly 20000 takes the second band inclusively, giving a fee of 2400 and a total of 22400.

**The budget ceiling.** Before the order is accepted, the brand's ledger is checked: the brand's current `enforcement_spend_cents` plus this order's `billable_total_cents` must not exceed the brand's `enforcement_budget_cents`. If it would, the order is refused with `ERR_TEST_BUY_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402) and nothing is persisted. Otherwise `billable_total_cents` is added to the brand's `enforcement_spend_cents` and the brand's `version` is incremented by 1. This fiscal gate is the very last check in the rejection ladder, so an order that is also invalid for a state or safety reason reports that reason instead.

**The cooling period.** Ordering two covert purchases from the same seller in quick succession is how brand-protection teams get detected, so a new order must be more than seven days away from every other non-cancelled test buy against the same seller. The interval is closed and inflated on both sides: an order at some instant conflicts with an existing order whenever the gap between their order instants is seven days or less in either direction, so **exactly seven days apart is a conflict** — the boundary instant itself is refused, which is the opposite of the representative-window rule in §3.2. Cancelled purchases are excluded from the comparison set entirely. A conflict is refused with `ERR_TEST_BUY_COOLING_PERIOD` (HTTP 409).

Test buys are readable individually and listable with optional filters on `claim_id`, `seller_id` and `status`.

**Receipt.** When the sample arrives, the analyst logs receipt, taking the row `ORDERED → RECEIVED`. The `lot_code` found on the received sample (1 to 40 characters, not blank) is required at receipt, because it is the traceability key for later diversion investigations. The `chain_of_custody_ref` custody-log reference (1 to 80 characters, not blank) is optional: when the courier supplied no custody paperwork the receipt is still accepted and the field stays null. `received_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the row's `version` is incremented by 1. A null custody reference is not free, though — it blocks authentication outright and, even if authentication had happened, it would suppress the corroboration bonus.

**Authentication — a genuine fork.** The analyst records the laboratory verdict on a `RECEIVED` row by supplying `authentication_result`, which is either `AUTHENTIC` or `COUNTERFEIT`, and the `lab_ref` report reference (1 to 80 characters, not blank). The row must be `RECEIVED`, and its `chain_of_custody_ref` must be non-null, else the verdict is refused with `ERR_CHAIN_OF_CUSTODY_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422). Both fields are persisted on either branch, but the branches then diverge sharply and neither one's consequences apply to the other.

On the **counterfeit** branch the row moves `RECEIVED → AUTHENTICATED_COUNTERFEIT`. The parent claim's `substantiation_score` is recomputed with the full variant — the 20.00-point corroboration bonus now applies, since this row is exactly the corroborating buy the variant looks for — and the recomputed value is persisted on the claim, whose `version` is incremented by 1 alongside the test buy's. The claim's status is **not** auto-advanced: reaching `SUBSTANTIATED` still requires the analyst to ask for substantiation explicitly. The row's `lot_code` also becomes traceable from this moment, unlocking a test-buy-sourced diversion investigation.

On the **authentic** branch the row moves `RECEIVED → AUTHENTICATED_AUTHENTIC`, and the enforcement thesis collapses. The parent claim is auto-dismissed with `dismissal_reason_code` `TEST_BUY_AUTHENTIC`; because the claim was dismissed, the listing's `enforcement_status` falls to `NONE`, its `monitor_status` is set to `CLEARED` and its `violation_type` is reset to `NONE`; and every evidence package of that claim still in `ASSEMBLING` or `SEALED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`. The counterfeit branch never touches the claim's status at all — that asymmetry is deliberate. Every row touched on either branch has its `version` incremented by 1.

Neither authentication branch refunds anything: the purchase genuinely happened, so the brand's `enforcement_spend_cents` is untouched by the verdict.

**Cancellation is the only compensating action.** An `ORDERED` purchase that never shipped may be cancelled by the analyst, supplying a `reason_code`; the row moves `ORDERED → CANCELLED`. The brand's `enforcement_spend_cents` has exactly this row's `billable_total_cents` subtracted from it, restoring the ledger to its pre-order value to the cent, and both the test buy and the brand have their `version` incremented by 1. The cancelled row also leaves the cooling-period comparison set, so the seller becomes immediately re-orderable. `AUTHENTICATED_AUTHENTIC`, `AUTHENTICATED_COUNTERFEIT` and `CANCELLED` are all terminal.

### 3.7. Evidence Assembly and Sealing

A marketplace notice must arrive with its exhibits attached, so evidence is assembled in a package that is append-only while open and frozen once sealed. An `EnforcementAnalyst` opens a package against a claim by supplying `claim_id`, an internal `package_ref` (1 to 40 characters, not blank), and the `expected_claim_version` described in §2.7. The package lands in status `ASSEMBLING` with an empty `items` array, `item_count` 0, `required_type_coverage` 0, `completeness_score` 0.0000 and `sealed_at` null; the parent claim's `version` is incremented by 1. Packages are readable individually and listable with optional filters on `claim_id` and `status`.

**Appending items.** The analyst appends one exhibit at a time, supplying its `item_type` and its `item_ref`. The element is appended to `items` as a record of `item_type`, `item_ref` and an `added_at` instant stamped from the logical clock, `item_count` is increased by 1, and the completeness figures are re-derived. Appending is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_PACKAGE_SEALED` (HTTP 409) once the package is no longer `ASSEMBLING` — a sealed or withdrawn package accepts nothing further. `item_count` is the raw length of `items`, duplicates included.

Twelve exhibit types exist. Seven of them are *required* types, in the sense that each appears in at least one violation type's mandatory set: `TEST_BUY_INVOICE`, `LAB_AUTHENTICATION_REPORT`, `LISTING_SCREENSHOT`, `REGISTRATION_CERTIFICATE`, `MARK_COMPARISON`, `COPYRIGHT_DEPOSIT_COPY` and `WORK_COMPARISON`. Five are *supporting* types: `SELLER_PROFILE_SNAPSHOT`, `PRICE_HISTORY_EXPORT`, `CUSTOMER_COMPLAINT`, `CUSTOMS_SEIZURE_NOTICE` and `SHIPPING_LABEL_PHOTO`.

Which of those are mandatory depends on the parent claim's `claimed_violation_type`. A `COUNTERFEIT` claim requires four types: `TEST_BUY_INVOICE`, `LAB_AUTHENTICATION_REPORT`, `LISTING_SCREENSHOT` and `REGISTRATION_CERTIFICATE`. A `TRADEMARK` claim requires three: `LISTING_SCREENSHOT`, `REGISTRATION_CERTIFICATE` and `MARK_COMPARISON`. A `COPYRIGHT` claim requires three: `LISTING_SCREENSHOT`, `COPYRIGHT_DEPOSIT_COPY` and `WORK_COMPARISON`.

**Completeness.** `required_type_coverage` is the number of distinct mandatory types for this claim's violation type that are present among the package's items. The coverage term is that count divided by the number of mandatory types for this violation type. The optional count is the number of items whose `item_type` is *not* in this claim's mandatory set — which includes a type that happens to be mandatory for some other violation type — and the optional term is that count divided by a target of 4, capped at 1. `completeness_score` is seventy hundredths of the coverage term plus thirty hundredths of the optional term, rounded half-up to four decimal places. A second item of a type that is already covered is ignored by both terms: it adds no further coverage and it is not counted as an optional item, though it does still increase `item_count`.

Because the mandatory set is read from the claim at scoring time, re-classifying the claim changes the score of an `ASSEMBLING` package on its next append. A `SEALED` package's items and score are frozen and are never re-derived.

**Sealing is a conjunction.** The analyst seals an `ASSEMBLING` package to make it attachable to a notice, taking it `ASSEMBLING → SEALED`. Two conditions must both hold: `required_type_coverage` must equal the full count of mandatory types for the claim's violation type, **and** `completeness_score` must be at least 0.8000. If either fails, the seal is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422). On success `sealed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the `items` array becomes immutable, and the package's `version` is incremented by 1.

The two conjuncts are genuinely independent, and the score alone is not sufficient. A counterfeit claim's package holding all four mandatory types plus two supporting items scores seventy hundredths of 1 plus thirty hundredths of one half, or 0.8500, and seals. The same four mandatory types plus only one supporting item scores 0.7750 and is refused on the score. All four mandatory types with no supporting items scores 0.7000 and is refused. All four plus four supporting items reaches 1.0000, and a fifth supporting item leaves it at 1.0000 because the optional term is capped. A trademark claim's package with all three mandatory types plus two supporting items scores 0.8500 and seals. But a counterfeit claim's package holding only three of its four mandatory types plus four supporting items scores seventy hundredths of three quarters plus thirty hundredths of 1, or 0.8250 — above the score threshold, yet **still unsealable**, because the coverage conjunct fails; that request receives `ERR_EVIDENCE_INCOMPLETE`. An empty package scores 0.0000 with `item_count` 0.

**Withdrawal.** A package may be withdrawn from either `ASSEMBLING` or `SEALED`, by the analyst supplying a `reason_code`, or by cascade from a claim dismissal or an authentic test-buy verdict. In every case the withdrawal is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409) while any notice referencing the package sits in status `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED_REMOVED` or `COUNTER_NOTICED` — a package still under marketplace consideration cannot be pulled. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal, and each withdrawal increments the package's `version` by 1.

### 3.8. Takedown Notices, Marketplace Decisions and the Counter-Notice Cycle

The takedown notice is the cyclic core of this domain. It is drafted, filed, decided, possibly contested by the seller, possibly reinstated, and possibly refiled — and the loop is bounded, not infinite.

**Drafting.** Either an `EnforcementAnalyst` or an `AuthorizedRepresentative` may draft the notice, binding together the `enrollment_id` through which the notice will be filed, the `claim_id` being enforced, and the `evidence_package_id` attached to the current round. The draft lands in status `DRAFT` with `round_number` 0, `submitted_by_rep_id` null, and `submitted_at`, `decision_due_at`, `decided_at`, `counter_notice_deadline_at` and `marketplace_case_ref` all null. Two gates apply at draft time. The registration asserted by the claim must be enforceable — `REGISTERED` rather than `PENDING` or `LAPSED` — else `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). And the supplied evidence package must belong to the same claim as the notice, else `ERR_EVIDENCE_PACKAGE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). When a single draft request violates both, the registration gate is the one reported. Notices are readable individually and listable with optional filters on `claim_id`, `enrollment_id`, `evidence_package_id`, `submitted_by_rep_id` and `status`.

**Filing.** Only an `AuthorizedRepresentative` may file the notice with the marketplace, taking it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. Five conditions are checked. The acting designation must be `DESIGNATED` and the logical clock must fall inside its half-open effective window, else `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_NOT_EFFECTIVE` (HTTP 409). The designation's `authority_scope` must include the power to submit notices — `SUBMIT_TAKEDOWN` or `BOTH` — else `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_SCOPE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 403). The referenced enrolment must still be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). The referenced claim must be `SUBSTANTIATED` or `ENFORCED`, and the referenced evidence package must be `SEALED`; either failure is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The asserted registration must still be enforceable, else `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422).

On a successful filing, `submitted_by_rep_id` is stamped with the acting `X-User-ID`, `submitted_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `round_number` becomes 1, and `decision_due_at` is computed by advancing `submitted_at` by the marketplace's own service-level allowance — five business days for `AMZ`, eight for `EBY`, ten for `WLM` — read from the filing enrolment's `marketplace_code`. The claim's listing advances `CLAIM_OPEN → TAKEDOWN_PENDING`, and both the notice and the listing have their `version` incremented by 1.

**The marketplace's decision.** A `MarketplaceLiaison` records the platform's answer on a `SUBMITTED` notice, supplying a `decision` of either `REMOVED` or `REJECTED` together with the platform's own `marketplace_case_ref` (1 to 60 characters, not blank), which is persisted on either branch. `decided_at` is stamped from the logical clock on either branch. The two branches then diverge completely.

On the **removed** branch the notice advances `SUBMITTED → APPROVED_REMOVED`, and a five-level cascade runs inside the same request. `counter_notice_deadline_at` is computed by advancing `decided_at` by ten business days, opening the seller's contest window. The claim's listing advances `TAKEDOWN_PENDING → REMOVED`. A takedown-removal strike, worth 12.55 base points effective at this `decided_at`, enters the ledger against the seller that owns that listing, and both of that seller's strike variants are recomputed across its cluster. If the recompute takes the cluster total to at least 25.00 the seller escalates out of `ACTIVE`, and if that escalation happens and no enforcement case for the pairing of the claim's brand and that seller is currently `OPEN`, one is auto-created with its `originating_takedown_id` set to this notice. Finally the parent claim advances `SUBSTANTIATED → ENFORCED`. Every row touched anywhere in that cascade has its `version` incremented by 1.

On the **rejected** branch the notice advances `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, `counter_notice_deadline_at` stays null, the claim's listing is released `TAKEDOWN_PENDING → CLAIM_OPEN`, and **no strike is attributed** — a rejected notice carries no enforcement consequence for the seller. Only the notice and the listing have their `version` incremented by 1.

**Refiling and the round cap.** A notice sitting in `REJECTED` or `REINSTATED` can be refiled by an `AuthorizedRepresentative`, taking it back to `SUBMITTED` — this is what makes the machine cyclic, and `APPROVED_REMOVED` is not terminal either. Refiling requires a `round_number` strictly below 3, else `ERR_MAX_TAKEDOWN_ROUNDS` (HTTP 409); after three rounds the brand must pursue the seller through correspondence and litigation instead. The refiling must supply an `evidence_package_id` that **differs** from the one currently attached and that belongs to the same claim — a violation of either is `ERR_EVIDENCE_PACKAGE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) — and that package must be `SEALED`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). Each pass around the loop therefore has to present genuinely new evidence, so the cycle cannot be traversed by replaying an identical request. The enrolment must still be `ACTIVE` and the acting designation must still be effective and in scope, with the same three errors as the first filing.

On a successful refiling, `round_number` is increased by 1, `evidence_package_id` is replaced with the newly supplied one, `submitted_at` and `decision_due_at` are recomputed from the current clock and the marketplace's allowance, and `decided_at`, `marketplace_case_ref` and `counter_notice_deadline_at` are all reset to null. The listing advances to `TAKEDOWN_PENDING` — from `CLAIM_OPEN` after a rejection, or from `REINSTATED` after a reinstatement — and both rows have their `version` incremented by 1.

**Withdrawal.** A notice in `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` when its enrolment is suspended or terminated, as described in §3.2, or when the sweep finds a submitted notice whose enrolment is no longer active. Withdrawing a `SUBMITTED` notice also releases its listing `TAKEDOWN_PENDING → CLAIM_OPEN`. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal, and each affected row has its `version` incremented by 1.

**The seller's counter notice.** A `MarketplaceLiaison` relays the seller's sworn counter notice, supplying the `takedown_id` of the removal being contested, the `seller_id` that swore the statement, and the `sworn_statement_ref` (1 to 80 characters, not blank). The target notice must be in `APPROVED_REMOVED`. The filing must land at or before that notice's `counter_notice_deadline_at`, else `ERR_COUNTER_NOTICE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422). And there must be no counter notice on that notice already, since exactly one is permitted per notice, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_COUNTER_NOTICE` (HTTP 409). When a request violates both the window and the uniqueness rule, the window error is the one reported.

The counter notice lands in status `FILED` with `filed_at` stamped from the logical clock, `resolved_at` and `rebuttal_ref` null, and `response_due_at` computed by advancing `filed_at` by ten business days — the brand's window to rebut. The parent notice moves `APPROVED_REMOVED → COUNTER_NOTICED` and has its `version` incremented by 1, and the listing **stays** `REMOVED` for the duration of the rebuttal window. Counter notices are readable individually and listable with optional filters on `takedown_id`, `seller_id` and `status`.

**Resolving the counter notice.** A `MarketplaceLiaison` resolves a `FILED` counter notice by supplying a `resolution` of either `REBUTTED` or `ACCEPTED`, and, when the resolution is `REBUTTED`, a `rebuttal_ref` referencing the brand's rebuttal (1 to 80 characters, not blank); a `REBUTTED` resolution without it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). The resolution must arrive at or before `response_due_at`; once that instant has passed the row must be expired by the sweep first, and a late resolution attempt is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).

On the **rebutted** branch the counter notice moves `FILED → REBUTTED`, `resolved_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `rebuttal_ref` is persisted, and the parent notice returns `COUNTER_NOTICED → APPROVED_REMOVED`, closing the cycle. Crucially, the strike attribution is **not** re-run: the original takedown-removal strike was never removed from the ledger while the notice sat in `COUNTER_NOTICED`, so returning to `APPROVED_REMOVED` must not add a second one. `counter_notice_deadline_at` is not extended either. Both rows have their `version` incremented by 1.

On the **accepted** branch the counter notice moves `FILED → ACCEPTED`, `resolved_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and a four-level reversal cascade runs. The parent notice moves `COUNTER_NOTICED → REINSTATED`. The listing moves `REMOVED → REINSTATED`. The takedown-removal strike attributed by that notice drops out of the ledger — because the notice no longer sits in `APPROVED_REMOVED` — and both strike variants are recomputed for the seller's whole cluster, de-escalating the seller from `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` back to `ACTIVE` when the cluster total falls below 25.00. And the parent claim falls `ENFORCED → SUBSTANTIATED`. Every row touched has its `version` incremented by 1.

If the brand simply lets the rebuttal window lapse, the sweep expires the counter notice instead, moving it `FILED → EXPIRED` with `resolved_at` set to `response_due_at` rather than to the clock value — so replaying the sweep is a genuine no-op — and running exactly the same reversal cascade as the accepted branch. `REBUTTED`, `ACCEPTED` and `EXPIRED` are all terminal for the counter notice.

### 3.9. Correspondence, Litigation and Case Closure

Once a seller has accumulated enough strikes to warrant direct pursuit, enforcement leaves the marketplace and becomes a legal matter. An enforcement case carries three state dimensions on one row, two of which advance independently while the third is a roll-up of the other two.

`correspondence_status` tracks the cease-and-desist conversation through `NONE`, `CD_SENT`, `CD_RESPONDED` and `CD_IGNORED`. `litigation_status` tracks the suit through `NONE`, `AUTHORIZED`, `FILED`, `SETTLED` and `DISMISSED`. `case_status` is derived after every mutation of the row and is never supplied: it is `IN_LITIGATION` whenever `litigation_status` is `AUTHORIZED` or `FILED`, it is `CLOSED` once the case has been closed, and it is `OPEN` in every other situation.

**Opening a case.** `LegalCounsel` opens a case manually by supplying `brand_id` and the `seller_id` of the cluster-root seller being pursued; the row starts with `correspondence_status` `NONE`, `litigation_status` `NONE`, `case_status` `OPEN`, `originating_takedown_id` null, and `cease_desist_sent_by_rep_id`, `litigation_authorized_by_rep_id`, `letter_ref`, `cd_sent_at`, `cd_response_due_at`, `response_kind`, `docket_ref` and `closure_outcome` all null. Cases are also opened automatically by the removal cascade of §3.8 and the diversion-confirmation cascade of §3.10, and only there — in an auto-created case, `originating_takedown_id` names the notice whose removal triggered it when the trigger was a removal, and stays null when the trigger was a confirmed diversion. Cases are readable individually and listable with optional filters on `brand_id`, `seller_id`, `originating_takedown_id`, `cease_desist_sent_by_rep_id`, `litigation_authorized_by_rep_id`, `case_status`, `correspondence_status` and `litigation_status`.

**Sending the cease-and-desist letter.** An `AuthorizedRepresentative` sends the letter, supplying its `letter_ref` (1 to 80 characters, not blank), and the case's `correspondence_status` advances `NONE → CD_SENT`. Four conditions are checked. The case's `case_status` must be `OPEN` and its `correspondence_status` must be `NONE`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The acting designation must be `DESIGNATED` and effective at the clock now, else `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_NOT_EFFECTIVE` (HTTP 409). Its `authority_scope` must include the power to submit notices or the power to authorise litigation — that is, any of `SUBMIT_TAKEDOWN`, `AUTHORIZE_LITIGATION` or `BOTH` satisfies this operation — else `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_SCOPE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 403). And the case's seller must be `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` or `BLOCKED`, because correspondence may only be aimed at an escalated seller; a still-`ACTIVE` seller is refused with `ERR_SELLER_NOT_UNDER_INVESTIGATION` (HTTP 422).

Sending the letter stamps `cease_desist_sent_by_rep_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` — this is the pin that the segregation-of-duties rule reads later — sets `cd_sent_at` from the logical clock, persists `letter_ref`, computes `cd_response_due_at` by advancing `cd_sent_at` by fourteen business days, and increments the case's `version` by 1.

**Recording the seller's answer.** `LegalCounsel` records the answer while the correspondence window is still open, supplying a `response_ref` and a `response_kind` of `DENIAL`, `SETTLEMENT_OFFER` or `COMPLIANCE_UNDERTAKING`. The case must be at `CD_SENT` and the logical clock must be at or before `cd_response_due_at`; the track then advances `CD_SENT → CD_RESPONDED`, `response_kind` is persisted, and `version` is incremented by 1.

If the seller never answers, the sweep moves the track `CD_SENT → CD_IGNORED` once the clock passes `cd_response_due_at`, and attributes a cease-and-desist-ignored strike of 5.05 base points against the case's seller with an effective instant of `cd_response_due_at` — not of the sweep's clock value — recomputing both strike variants. Both the case and the seller have their `version` incremented by 1.

**Authorising litigation — the segregation-of-duties spine.** A `second` `AuthorizedRepresentative` escalates the case to litigation, supplying an `authorization_ref`, and `litigation_status` advances `NONE → AUTHORIZED`. Five conditions guard it. `litigation_status` must be `NONE` and `case_status` must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The correspondence track must have been answered or have lapsed — `CD_RESPONDED` or `CD_IGNORED` — else `ERR_CEASE_DESIST_NOT_ANSWERED` (HTTP 409); a case still at `CD_SENT`, or one where no letter was ever sent, cannot reach litigation. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the case's `cease_desist_sent_by_rep_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). The acting designation must be `DESIGNATED` and effective at the clock now, else `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_NOT_EFFECTIVE` (HTTP 409). And its `authority_scope` must include the power to authorise litigation — `AUTHORIZE_LITIGATION` or `BOTH` — with the designation sitting under an `ACTIVE` enrolment of this case's brand, else `ERR_REPRESENTATIVE_SCOPE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 403).

The practical consequence is that a brand which has designated only one representative simply cannot reach `AUTHORIZED`; it must designate a second one. And because `SUBMIT_TAKEDOWN` and `AUTHORIZE_LITIGATION` do not intersect as power sets, those two designations can hold fully overlapping effective windows on the same enrolment without tripping the overlap rule of §3.2.

On success `litigation_authorized_by_rep_id` is stamped with the acting `X-User-ID`, the roll-up moves `case_status` `OPEN → IN_LITIGATION`, and `version` is incremented by 1.

**Filing suit.** `LegalCounsel` files the complaint against an `AUTHORIZED` case, supplying the court's `docket_ref` (1 to 80 characters, not blank) and the `court_code` in which it was filed. `litigation_status` advances `AUTHORIZED → FILED`, `docket_ref` is persisted, `case_status` stays `IN_LITIGATION`, and `version` is incremented by 1.

**Closure.** `LegalCounsel` closes the case with an `outcome` and an optional `closure_note`, and the permitted outcomes depend on where the litigation track stands. `SETTLED` and `DISMISSED` both require `litigation_status` to be `FILED`, and each advances the litigation track to the matching value — `FILED → SETTLED` or `FILED → DISMISSED`. `NO_ACTION` is available only while `case_status` is `OPEN` and `litigation_status` is still `NONE`; a case whose litigation track has been engaged can only be closed through `SETTLED` or `DISMISSED`, and attempting a no-action closure on it is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). On every outcome, `closure_outcome` is persisted, the roll-up sets `case_status` to `CLOSED`, and `version` is incremented by 1. Closure never reverses strikes already attributed — the ledger records what happened, and a settlement does not unsay it. On the `NO_ACTION` path `litigation_status` remains `NONE`.

### 3.10. Border Enforcement: Customs Recordation and Diversion Investigations

Enforcement also runs at the border. A brand records its registered marks with a customs authority so that the authority can detain suspect shipments, and the detentions that follow become supply-chain leak investigations.

**Recording a mark with customs.** A `CustomsCoordinator` drafts a recordation by supplying `brand_id`, `registration_id`, the `customs_authority_code` (`US_CBP`, `EU_TAXUD` or `CN_GACC`), the `nice_class` the recordation covers (an integer from 1 through 45), and the coverage window as two calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form: `recorded_on`, the inclusive first day, and `expires_on`, the inclusive last day, which must be a valid calendar date that is not earlier than `recorded_on`. The row is created with `status` `DRAFT` and `recordation_number` null. Recordations are readable individually and listable with optional filters on `brand_id`, `registration_id`, `customs_authority_code`, `nice_class` and `status`.

**Confirming the recordation.** The same role confirms the recordation with the authority by supplying the customs-issued `recordation_number` (1 to 40 characters, not blank), moving `status` `DRAFT → RECORDED` and incrementing `version` by 1. Three conditions apply beyond the state check. The referenced registration must be `REGISTERED` — a `PENDING`, `OPPOSED`, `ABANDONED` or `LAPSED` mark cannot be presented to customs, and the attempt is `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422). That registration's `principal_register` flag must be true, because supplemental-register marks are not recordable, and a false flag is `ERR_RECORDATION_NOT_PRINCIPAL_REGISTER` (HTTP 422). And the coverage window must not collide with another live recordation of the same mark scope.

The collision test is scoped to rows that already sit at `status` `RECORDED` and that share this row's `brand_id`, `customs_authority_code` and `nice_class` all three; `DRAFT` and `EXPIRED` rows are invisible to it, and a different authority or a different Nice class is a different scope entirely. The window `[recorded_on, expires_on]` is closed on both ends at whole-date granularity, so two windows collide whenever the first begins on or before the second ends and the second begins on or before the first ends. Sharing a single boundary date is therefore a collision: a window ending `2026-12-31` collides with one starting `2026-12-31`. A window starting the very next day, `2027-01-01`, is clean. A collision is `ERR_RECORDATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Note that this is the opposite convention from the representative designation windows of §3.2, which are half-open and permit exact adjacency; the two rules must not be implemented with shared code.

Only a `RECORDED` recordation is an eligible parent for a border-seizure investigation. The sweep expires a recordation once the date part of the logical clock has passed its `expires_on`, moving it `RECORDED → EXPIRED` and incrementing `version` by 1; `EXPIRED` is terminal and there is no renewal transition. Expiry does not touch investigations: `OPEN` investigations that reference the recordation stay open, because an in-flight seizure survives the recordation lapsing underneath it.

**Opening an investigation.** A `CustomsCoordinator` opens a supply-chain leak investigation, always supplying `brand_id`, the `source_kind` (`BORDER_SEIZURE` or `TEST_BUY`) and the `lot_code` being traced (1 to 40 characters, not blank), plus a conditional set: `recordation_id`, `source_test_buy_id`, `seller_id`, `seizure_ref`, `port_code`, `seized_qty` and `declared_value_cents`. The row is created with `status` `OPEN`.

The two source kinds require different shapes. A `BORDER_SEIZURE` must carry `recordation_id` pointing at a `RECORDED` recordation, and must carry all four detention details: `seizure_ref` (the customs detention notice reference, 1 to 60 characters, not blank), `port_code` (1 to 12 characters, not blank), `seized_qty` (at least 1) and `declared_value_cents` (zero or more). Its `source_test_buy_id` stays null. A `TEST_BUY` must carry `source_test_buy_id` and leaves `recordation_id` and the four detention details null. `seller_id` is optional on both kinds and stays null when the shipper could not be identified — it can be supplied only at creation.

The clock behaves differently across the two kinds as well. For a `BORDER_SEIZURE`, `detention_notice_at` is stamped from the logical clock at creation and `response_due_at` is computed by advancing `detention_notice_at` by seven business days, giving the brand a bounded window in which to answer the detention notice. For a `TEST_BUY`, both `detention_notice_at` and `response_due_at` stay null — there is no customs clock on a test-buy trace — and instead the lot code is verified at creation against the named source test buy: that test buy must be `AUTHENTICATED_COUNTERFEIT` and its `lot_code` must equal the investigation's `lot_code`, else `ERR_LOT_CODE_NOT_TRACEABLE` (HTTP 422). Investigations are readable individually and listable with optional filters on `brand_id`, `recordation_id`, `source_test_buy_id`, `seller_id`, `source_kind` and `status`.

**Filing the brand's evidence.** An `EnforcementAnalyst` files the brand's side by supplying `evidence_ref` and `suspected_distributor_ref` (each 1 to 80 characters, not blank), moving `status` `OPEN → EVIDENCE_SUBMITTED`, persisting both references and incrementing `version` by 1. For a `BORDER_SEIZURE` the logical clock must be at or before `response_due_at`; the boundary instant itself is still inside the window, and a submission past it is `ERR_SEIZURE_RESPONSE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422). For a `TEST_BUY` the traceability check is applied a second time here, so a source test buy that has since ceased to qualify blocks the submission with `ERR_LOT_CODE_NOT_TRACEABLE` (HTTP 422). Nothing else expires an investigation — the sweep does not close investigations whose response window has lapsed; they simply become unable to accept evidence.

**Concluding the investigation.** A `CustomsCoordinator` concludes by supplying an `outcome` and a `findings_ref` (1 to 80 characters, not blank). Both outcomes stamp `concluded_at` from the logical clock, persist `findings_ref` and increment `version` by 1, and both are terminal.

`DIVERSION_CONFIRMED` may be reached only from `EVIDENCE_SUBMITTED`, never directly from `OPEN`; attempting to confirm a diversion on an `OPEN` investigation is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). It additionally requires the row's `seller_id` to be non-null, because a confirmed diversion must name the seller who receives the strike; a null `seller_id` is likewise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). On success, a seizure-confirmed strike of 25.15 base points — the heaviest single charge in the domain — is attributed to that seller with an effective instant equal to `concluded_at`, both strike variants are recomputed, the seller escalates to `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` when its cluster total reaches 25.00 and is auto-blocked when it reaches 60.00, and every row touched has its `version` incremented by 1. A single fresh seizure-confirmed strike is by itself enough to escalate a seller. When that escalation occurs and no live enforcement case already exists for the pair of this investigation's `brand_id` and the seller, one is auto-created with `originating_takedown_id` null.

`RELEASED` is reachable from either `EVIDENCE_SUBMITTED` or directly from `OPEN` — a detention may be released without the brand ever having answered it. No strike is attributed and no case is created on this branch.

### 3.11. The Enforcement Sweep and Brand Enforcement Metrics

**The sweep.** Every time-driven rule in the domain fires only when the enforcement sweep is run — nothing in this system expires on its own between requests. The sweep is available to `MarketplaceLiaison` and `BrandAdministrator`, takes no body, evaluates every rule against the current value of the logical clock, and returns the ten integer counts described below. It increments `version` by 1 on every row it actually changes and on no other row.

The six steps run in a pinned order, and the order is normative rather than incidental.

Step 1 — lapse registrations. Every `REGISTERED` trademark registration whose `expiry_date` has passed moves to `LAPSED`. Each `ACTIVE` enrolment that asserts such a registration is suspended with `suspension_reason` `REGISTRATION_LAPSED`, which in turn cascades to that enrolment's representatives and its live takedown notices as described in §3.2 and §3.8, and the postures of every affected brand are re-derived.

Step 2 — expire counter notices. Every `FILED` counter notice whose `response_due_at` the clock has passed moves to `EXPIRED` with `resolved_at` set to `response_due_at` — the deadline instant, not the sweep's clock value. Its takedown moves `COUNTER_NOTICED → REINSTATED`, the listing behind it moves to `REINSTATED`, an `ENFORCED` claim falls back to `SUBSTANTIATED`, and the takedown-removal strike leaves the strike input set.

Step 3 — ignore cease-and-desist letters. Every case at `CD_SENT` whose `cd_response_due_at` the clock has passed moves to `CD_IGNORED` and attributes a cease-and-desist-ignored strike whose effective instant is `cd_response_due_at`.

Step 4 — expire recordations. Every `RECORDED` customs recordation whose `expires_on` has passed moves to `EXPIRED`, leaving `OPEN` investigations untouched.

Step 5 — decay and auto-block. Both strike variants are recomputed for every seller at the current clock, applying the 180-day decay weighting of §3.3, and any seller whose cluster total has reached 60.00 is auto-blocked.

Step 6 — escalate and de-escalate. The remaining escalation ladder is applied, moving sellers between `ACTIVE` and `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` in both directions.

The consequence of this ordering is that strikes removed in step 2 and strikes added in step 3 are both already in place when step 5 recomputes, so the sweep never reports a stale escalation. It also means a single sweep run can, for one seller, remove a reinstatement strike and add a cease-and-desist strike and then settle the seller's status once against the net result.

Two further constraints hold. No enforcement case row is ever created by the sweep — automatic case creation happens only in the marketplace-decision cascade of §3.8 and the diversion-conclusion cascade of §3.10, where the owning brand is unambiguous. And the sweep is idempotent with respect to the clock: running it a second time without advancing the clock changes nothing and returns every count as zero.

The response reports `lapsed_registration_count`, `suspended_enrollment_count`, `withdrawn_takedown_count`, `expired_counter_notice_count`, `reinstated_takedown_count`, `cd_ignored_case_count`, `expired_recordation_count`, `escalated_seller_count`, `blocked_seller_count` and `deescalated_seller_count`, each an integer counting rows the sweep actually transitioned in this run.

**Brand enforcement metrics.** A computed read on a brand, available to `BrandAdministrator`, `EnforcementAnalyst` and `LegalCounsel`, derives the brand's enforcement scorecard on the fly and persists nothing. It returns `listing_removal_rate` (a decimal), `repeat_infringer_count`, `decided_takedown_count`, `removed_takedown_count`, `open_case_count` and `enforcement_spend_cents`, all four of the latter as integers.

The scope is all-time and brand-scoped: every takedown notice whose claim belongs to this brand, with no marketplace filter and no date filter whatsoever.

`decided_takedown_count` counts those takedowns whose `status` is `APPROVED_REMOVED`, `REJECTED` or `REINSTATED`. `removed_takedown_count` counts those whose `status` is `APPROVED_REMOVED`. Notices at `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `COUNTER_NOTICED` or `WITHDRAWN` appear in neither count: an in-flight or abandoned notice is not a marketplace decision. A notice currently at `COUNTER_NOTICED` is excluded even though it was once `APPROVED_REMOVED` — only the current status matters.

`listing_removal_rate` is `removed_takedown_count` divided by `decided_takedown_count`, rounded to four decimal places using ROUND_HALF_UP. When `decided_takedown_count` is zero the rate is exactly 0.0000 — not null, not an error, and not 1.0000. Worked results: one removal and nothing else gives 1.0000; one removal against two rejections gives 3 decided and 1 removed, which is 0.3333 because 0.33333… rounds down at the fourth place; one removal alongside one still-submitted notice gives 1.0000 because the submitted row is invisible to both terms; a lone reinstated notice gives 1 decided and 0 removed, which is 0.0000; two removals plus one reinstatement gives 3 decided and 2 removed, which is 0.6667.

`repeat_infringer_count` counts cluster roots — never alias rows — whose `cluster_strike_points` has reached 25.00 and that have at least one strike attributable to this brand somewhere in the cluster. Attributability runs through the strike's origin: a takedown-removal strike belongs to the brand of its takedown's claim, a seizure-confirmed strike belongs to the `brand_id` of its investigation, and a cease-and-desist-ignored strike belongs to the `brand_id` of its case. A root that crossed 25.00 purely on another brand's strikes is not this brand's repeat infringer.

`open_case_count` counts enforcement cases of this brand whose `case_status` is `OPEN` or `IN_LITIGATION`; `CLOSED` cases are excluded. `enforcement_spend_cents` is read straight off the brand row, exactly as accumulated by the test buy programme of §3.6.
