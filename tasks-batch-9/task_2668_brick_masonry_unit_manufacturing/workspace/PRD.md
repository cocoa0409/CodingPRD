# Brick & Masonry Unit Manufacturing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A structural brick is not a commodity that can be made to a drawing and shipped from stock. It starts as tonnage in a clay pit, gets blended with shale to hit a fired colour and a weathering grade, is extruded and cut into green units that break if they are handled badly, and then spends a day in a tunnel kiln that is measurably hotter along its centreline than against its walls. Two cars that entered the same firing on the same recipe come out with different colour, and the only way to ship a wall that reads as one colour is to blend fired lots taken from different kiln positions until their combined colour coordinate sits inside a window the customer will accept. On top of that, brick is heavy and cheap per unit, so freight dominates the quoted price and the plant that serves a jobsite is chosen by haul cost rather than by preference.

This document specifies the API for a multi-plant brick and masonry unit manufacturer that has to hold all of that together in one system of record. It covers the raw-material side (proven pit reserves, body formulation, the supersession chain that lets a reformulated body still satisfy an architect's approved sample panel), the production side (extrusion and setting on kiln cars, green handling losses, tunnel-kiln scheduling with mandatory cooldown, firing-curve grading and overfire/underfire scrap), the inventory side (fired lots keyed by colour, texture, durability grade and dye lot, with a strict unit ledger), and the commercial side (freight-sensitive plant assignment inside a market radius, long lead times on special orders, packaging and banding, jobsite delivery with unloading-equipment requirements, and a freight bill that is re-quoted on real packaged weight).

Two design commitments run through every section. The first is that quantities are conserved: clay tonnage and fired units are ledgers, not counters, and every reservation, consumption and release must leave the ledger identity intact. The second is that quality decisions are segregated: the technician who composes a shipped blend may not certify it, and the technician who submits an architectural sample panel may not approve it. Where a plant is taken out of service, the consequences propagate downward through pits, formulations, production runs and kiln runs in a fixed order, and the specification below states that order explicitly rather than leaving it to implementation convenience.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — read or write — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role the operator is acting as. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the eight role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401). If the role is recognised but is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles are `PlantAdmin`, `PlantOperator`, `BodyEngineer`, `BlendTechnician`, `QualityManager`, `ArchitectReviewer`, `SalesRep` and `LogisticsCoordinator`. All eight may call every read endpoint and all eight may set the logical clock; write authority is partitioned as follows.

- `PlantAdmin` is corporate plant engineering. It exclusively registers plants, idles a plant, opens clay and shale pits, and commissions tunnel kilns. It shares kiln-car registration with `PlantOperator`.
- `PlantOperator` is the plant itself acting as an operator. It exclusively creates production runs, closes out green handling, schedules kiln runs, loads cars into a kiln run, starts a kiln run and grades a kiln run. It shares kiln-car registration with `PlantAdmin`.
- `BodyEngineer` owns shale blending and body formulation: creating a formulation, appending clay-pit components to it, and qualifying it.
- `BlendTechnician` is the product blender. It opens a shipped blend on an order, hand-picks fired lots into that blend, composes the blend, lays up an architectural sample panel and submits a panel for review.
- `QualityManager` is plant quality control. It quarantines a suspect fired lot, and certifies or rejects a composed blend.
- `ArchitectReviewer` is the architect of record. It requests a revision round on a submitted panel and approves a submitted panel.
- `SalesRep` is inside sales. It takes orders, runs plant assignment and cancels an order before delivery.
- `LogisticsCoordinator` is outbound logistics. It packages a certified blend and closes out jobsite delivery.

**How `X-User-ID` is interpreted.** When `X-User-Role` is `PlantOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Plant`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Plant` compare the resource's owning-plant reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `PlantOperator` may register a kiln car only under its own plant, may create a production run only with a `plant_id` equal to its own `X-User-ID`, may close out green handling only on a run whose `plant_id` matches, and may schedule, load, start or grade only kiln runs whose `Kiln` belongs to its own plant. Any violation is rejected with `ERR_PLANT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). When the owning plant cannot be derived at all because a body reference such as `kiln_id` or `production_run_id` does not resolve to a live row, this scope check is skipped and the body-reference 404 is returned instead.

When `X-User-Role` is `PlantAdmin`, `BodyEngineer`, `BlendTechnician`, `QualityManager`, `ArchitectReviewer`, `SalesRep` or `LogisticsCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands attribution — as `graded_by_user_id` on a graded kiln run, as `assigned_by_user_id` and `packaged_by_user_id` on an order, as `composed_by_user_id` and `certified_by_user_id` on a shipped blend, and as `submitted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a sample panel — but it never compares those values to any entity's `id`.

**Segregation of duties.** Two controls are two-person controls. Certifying a composed blend requires that `X-User-ID` differ from that blend's `composed_by_user_id`. Approving a submitted panel requires that `X-User-ID` differ from that panel's `submitted_by_user_id`. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Note that the two controls are enforced on different roles: composition is a `BlendTechnician` action while certification is a `QualityManager` action, and submission is a `BlendTechnician` action while approval is an `ArchitectReviewer` action, so the identity comparison is what carries the control rather than the role check alone.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are returned sorted by `created_at` ascending. Where a list endpoint also accepts filter parameters, filtering is applied before pagination, so `total_count` reflects the number of rows matching the filters rather than the number of rows in the collection. The four pagination fields that appear in `meta` on a list response are `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`; `has_more` is true exactly when there is at least one further matching row beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Columns and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource in this system carries four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix — the same opaque shape for a plant, a fired lot and a sample panel alike; clients never supply it. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations caused by a cascade the client did not directly request. There are no exceptions: all twelve resource types — plants, clay pits, body formulations, kilns, kiln cars, production runs, kiln runs, fired lots, orders, shipped blends, blend components and sample panels — carry `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, never wall-clock time. Its value at server startup is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it stays there until an operator moves it. An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied instant carried in the request field `to_iso8601`; the response body for that capability is the inline object `{ current_utc: string }` and no persisted entity is mutated by it. Every timestamp the server stamps — `idled_at` on an idled plant, `qualified_at` on a qualified formulation, `assigned_at`, `delivered_at` and `invoiced_at` on an order, `certified_at` on a blend, `approved_at` and `expires_at` on a panel — reads the logical clock, and every date-arithmetic rule (`promised_ship_date`, panel expiry) is computed against it. The server never falls back to real time if the clock has not been set.

### 2.5. Rule Precedence: How a Rejection Is Chosen

A single malformed or ill-timed request frequently violates several rules at once. The response must carry exactly one `error_id`, and it is always the error of the **lowest-numbered** violated rule in the ladder below; when a request is rejected, no state whatsoever is mutated. The ladder is global — it spans authentication, existence, concurrency, state, safety and capacity concerns — and it is the single most important cross-cutting contract in this document, because it decides which of two true failures the client is told about.

1. Both `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are present and non-empty, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`.
2. `X-User-Role` names one of the eight roles, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`.
3. The acting role is authorised for the endpoint, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
4. Every id embedded in the URL path resolves to a live row, on every endpoint that has one, validated left to right so that an outer segment's 404 wins over an inner segment's — `ERR_PLANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAY_PIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FORMULATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_KILN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_KILN_CAR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCTION_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_KILN_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FIRED_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BLEND_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_SAMPLE_PANEL_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate (all HTTP 404).
5. Plant scoping for a `PlantOperator`, on exactly these seven operations: kiln-car registration, production-run creation, green close-out, kiln-run scheduling, kiln load, kiln-run start and kiln-run grading — otherwise `ERR_PLANT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
6. Segregation of duties, on exactly these two operations: blend certification and panel approval — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
7. Request payload well-formedness, on every write operation: required fields present, types correct, enum values drawn from the declared vocabulary, numeric bounds and decimal scales honoured, required arrays non-empty, and a scheduled window's `end_at` strictly later than its `start_at` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
8. Every reference id supplied in the request **body** resolves to a live row, on exactly these seven operations: formulation creation, formulation component append, production-run creation, kiln-run scheduling, kiln load, blend component allocation and panel lay-up — reported as `ERR_CLAY_PIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FORMULATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_KILN_CAR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCTION_RUN_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_FIRED_LOT_NOT_FOUND` according to which reference failed.
9. Optimistic concurrency, on exactly these three operations: formulation component append, kiln load and blend component allocation — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (see §2.8).
10. The plant that owns the target resource is `ACTIVE`, on exactly these thirteen operations: plant idling, pit opening, kiln commissioning, kiln-car registration, formulation creation, formulation component append, formulation qualification, production-run creation, green close-out, kiln-run scheduling, kiln load, kiln-run start and kiln-run grading — otherwise `ERR_PLANT_IDLED`. On plant idling the plant itself is the target resource, so a second idle attempt on an already `IDLED` plant is rejected here.
11. Every clay pit referenced by the formulation's components is `OPEN`, on formulation qualification — otherwise `ERR_CLAY_PIT_SUSPENDED`.
12. The target body formulation is `DRAFT`, on formulation component append and formulation qualification — otherwise `ERR_FORMULATION_NOT_DRAFT`.
13. The target production run is in the required status, on green close-out and kiln load — otherwise `ERR_PRODUCTION_RUN_STATE_INVALID`.
14. The kiln car named by a new production run is `IDLE` and carries no current production run, on production-run creation — otherwise `ERR_KILN_CAR_NOT_IDLE`.
15. The target kiln run is in the required status, on kiln load, kiln-run start and kiln-run grading — otherwise `ERR_KILN_RUN_STATE_INVALID`.
16. The fired lot named by a manual blend component is allocatable, on blend component allocation — otherwise `ERR_FIRED_LOT_UNAVAILABLE`.
17. No fired lot involved in the operation is `QUARANTINED`, on exactly these four operations: blend component allocation, blend composition, blend certification and panel approval — otherwise `ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED`.
18. The target shipped blend is `DRAFT`, on blend component allocation and blend composition — otherwise `ERR_BLEND_NOT_DRAFT`.
19. The target shipped blend is `COMPOSED`, on blend certification and blend rejection — otherwise `ERR_BLEND_NOT_COMPOSED`.
20. The target order's `fulfillment_status` admits the operation, on exactly these six operations: plant assignment, blend creation, panel lay-up, packaging, delivery and cancellation — otherwise `ERR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`.
21. The target sample panel is in the required status, on panel submission, revision request and panel approval — otherwise `ERR_PANEL_STATE_INVALID`.
22. An order that requires panel approval has an `APPROVED` panel, on blend certification — otherwise `ERR_PANEL_NOT_APPROVED`.
23. That approved panel has not expired, on blend certification — otherwise `ERR_PANEL_APPROVAL_EXPIRED`.
24. Every allocated component's lot was fired from a formulation inside the panel's equivalence class, on blend certification — otherwise `ERR_DYE_LOT_MISMATCH`.
25. The blend sits inside the panel's colour tolerance, on blend certification — otherwise `ERR_PANEL_TOLERANCE_EXCEEDED`.
26. The blend's colour spread is within the shipped-blend span ceiling, on blend composition and blend certification — otherwise `ERR_COLOR_RANGE_SPAN_EXCEEDED`.
27. The allocated lot's durability grade ranks at least as high as the order's, on blend component allocation — otherwise `ERR_DURABILITY_GRADE_NOT_MET`.
28. The body formulation named by the operation is `QUALIFIED`, on exactly these five operations: production-run creation, kiln-run scheduling, panel lay-up, panel submission and panel approval — otherwise `ERR_FORMULATION_NOT_QUALIFIED`.
29. The order's shipped blend is `CERTIFIED`, on packaging — otherwise `ERR_BLEND_NOT_CERTIFIED`.
30. The unloading equipment reported at delivery is one the assigned plant operates, on delivery — otherwise `ERR_UNLOADING_EQUIPMENT_UNAVAILABLE`.
31. The unloading equipment reported at delivery equals the equipment the order requires, on delivery — otherwise `ERR_UNLOADING_EQUIPMENT_MISMATCH`.
32. An appended clay pit belongs to the formulation's own plant, on formulation component append — otherwise `ERR_PIT_PLANT_MISMATCH`.
33. The allocated lot matches the order on colour family and texture and was fired at the order's assigned plant, on blend component allocation — otherwise `ERR_LOT_ATTRIBUTE_MISMATCH`.
34. Codes are unique in their declared scope, on exactly these ten operations: plant registration, pit opening, kiln commissioning, kiln-car registration, formulation creation, production-run creation, kiln-run scheduling, order intake, blend creation and panel lay-up — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.
35. Component share totals are legal, on formulation component append and formulation qualification — otherwise `ERR_COMPONENT_SHARE_TOTAL_INVALID`.
36. The requested kiln position is inside the kiln's position range, on kiln load — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_KILN_POSITION`.
37. That kiln position is not already taken on the target kiln run, on kiln load — otherwise `ERR_KILN_POSITION_OCCUPIED`.
38. The new firing window does not collide with an existing window on the same kiln, on kiln-run scheduling — otherwise `ERR_KILN_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.
39. The kiln car is not already committed to an overlapping firing window, on kiln load — otherwise `ERR_KILN_CAR_OCCUPIED`.
40. The green units set do not exceed the car's capacity, on production-run creation — otherwise `ERR_KILN_CAR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
41. The append does not over-allocate the blend, on blend component allocation — otherwise `ERR_BLEND_OVER_ALLOCATION`.
42. The panel has revision rounds left, on revision request — otherwise `ERR_PANEL_REVISION_LIMIT`.
43. Plant assignment found at least one eligible plant, on plant assignment — otherwise `ERR_NO_PLANT_IN_MARKET_RADIUS`.
44. Every contributing pit can fund its share of the run's clay commitment, on production-run creation — otherwise `ERR_CLAY_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT`.
45. The composed blend is exactly filled, on blend composition — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BLEND_INVENTORY`.
46. The pallet count covers the certified blend, on packaging — otherwise `ERR_PALLET_COUNT_INSUFFICIENT`.
47. The band count covers the pallets, on packaging — otherwise `ERR_BAND_COUNT_INSUFFICIENT`.

Two consequences are worth spelling out. First, because rule 4 precedes rule 7, a request that both names a non-existent path resource and carries a malformed body reports the 404, not the 400. Second, because rule 9 precedes every status check from rule 10 onward, a stale-version append against a formulation that has meanwhile been qualified reports `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, not `ERR_FORMULATION_NOT_DRAFT`.

### 2.6. Path and Body Reference Resolution

Nested paths are resolved strictly from left to right. When an operation addresses a sub-collection of a plant, the plant id is resolved first: a request naming a plant that does not exist reports `ERR_PLANT_NOT_FOUND` even if the rest of the request is also invalid. The same applies to order sub-collections, where the order id resolves before any body reference is examined, and to the fired-lot allocation listing, where the fired lot resolves before any filter is applied.

Body references are resolved after payload well-formedness. A formulation created with a `plant_id` that names no plant reports `ERR_PLANT_NOT_FOUND`; the same request with a `supersedes_formulation_id` that names no formulation reports `ERR_FORMULATION_NOT_FOUND`. A component append naming an unknown `pit_id` reports `ERR_CLAY_PIT_NOT_FOUND`. A production run naming an unknown `formulation_id` reports `ERR_FORMULATION_NOT_FOUND` and one naming an unknown `kiln_car_id` reports `ERR_KILN_CAR_NOT_FOUND`. A kiln run naming an unknown `kiln_id` reports `ERR_KILN_NOT_FOUND`. A kiln load naming an unknown `production_run_id` reports `ERR_PRODUCTION_RUN_NOT_FOUND`. A blend component naming an unknown `fired_lot_id`, or a panel lay-up naming an unknown `dye_lot_id`, reports `ERR_FIRED_LOT_NOT_FOUND`.

Read endpoints expose the reverse direction of every relationship in the model, so that a client can navigate from a parent to its children without guessing. A plant's pits, kilns and kiln cars are each listable under that plant. Formulations are listable filtered by `plant_id`, by `supersedes_formulation_id`, by `status` and by `target_color_family`. Production runs are listable filtered by `plant_id`, `formulation_id`, `kiln_car_id`, `kiln_run_id` and `status`. Kiln runs are listable filtered by `kiln_id`, `formulation_id` and `status`. Fired lots are listable filtered by `production_run_id`, `color_family`, `texture`, `grade`, `durability_grade` and `status`, and the allocations drawn against one lot are listable under that lot filtered by `status`. Blends are listable filtered by `order_id` and `status`, and one blend's components are listable in append order filtered by `status` and by `source`. Sample panels are listable filtered by `order_id`, `formulation_id`, `dye_lot_id` and `status`. Orders are listable filtered by `plant_id`, `fulfillment_status` and `freight_status`. Plants are listable filtered by `status`, and pits additionally by `material_type`.

### 2.7. Numeric Precision and Rounding Discipline

Unless a rule says otherwise, every derived decimal quantity in this system is rounded to **two decimal places using round-half-up** — that is, a value exactly halfway between two representable results rounds away from zero. Three families of quantities depart from the two-place default and are called out here because getting them wrong changes downstream decisions rather than merely the presentation.

The two body-chemistry percentages, `blend_iron_oxide_pct` and `blend_lime_pct`, are held to **three** decimal places, round half up, matching the three-decimal precision at which a pit's own `iron_oxide_pct` and `lime_pct` are recorded. Cent-denominated freight amounts — `estimated_base_freight_cents`, `estimated_fuel_surcharge_cents`, `estimated_freight_cents`, `final_base_freight_cents`, `final_fuel_surcharge_cents`, `final_freight_cents` and `freight_variance_cents` — are whole integers produced by round half up, never by truncation. Unit counts and day counts are whole integers throughout; where a rule needs to convert a fractional quantity into a count of units or days it says explicitly whether it rounds up, and `distance_miles` is an exact integer with no rounding involved at all.

Two rounding disciplines are non-negotiable because they are the difference between two implementations that agree and two that drift apart. The first is **round once at the end of an accumulation**: where a weighted sum is formed — the share-weighted body chemistry, the unit-weighted blend colour — the products are accumulated at full precision and the single rounding is applied to the final quotient. Rounding each contribution before summing lets error accumulate and can flip a durability grade or push a panel colour deviation across a tolerance boundary. The second is **round at both levels of a two-level split**: where a total is divided among several recipients, the total is rounded first and each recipient's share is then derived from that already-rounded total and rounded itself, with the residual handled explicitly. These two disciplines pull in opposite directions on purpose; §3 states which applies where.

Comparisons are always made against the **stored, rounded** value, not against an unrounded intermediate. A loss percentage that computes to 6.004 before rounding is stored as 6.00 and compared as 6.00.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Three write operations append a child row to a parent that a client has previously read, and in all three the append is unconditionally legal against the parent's status — so the parent's status cannot express the conflict, and a client working from a stale read would silently corrupt an aggregate. Those three operations therefore take a non-optional expected-version field carrying the `version` the client last observed on the parent, and the server rejects the write if the parent's `version` has moved on.

- A `POST /formulations/{id}/components` request must carry `expected_formulation_version` matching the target `BodyFormulation`'s current `version`. If another writer has advanced the formulation's version since the client last read it, the append is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) — the stale client sized its `share_pct` against a component set whose shares no longer sum to what it saw, and accepting the append could push the body recipe past 100.00 percent.
- A `POST /kiln-runs/{id}/load` request must carry `expected_kiln_run_version` matching the target `KilnRun`'s current `version`. If the kiln run's version has advanced since the client last read it, the load is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) — the stale client chose its `kiln_position` from an occupancy map that no longer holds, and the position it picked may have been taken by the write that advanced the version.
- A `POST /blends/{id}/components` request must carry `expected_blend_version` matching the target `ShippedBlend`'s current `version`. If the blend's version has advanced since the client last read it, the allocation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) — the stale client sized its `units` against an `allocated_units` total that no longer holds, and accepting it would over-allocate fired inventory.

No other endpoint takes a version field of any kind. Passing one where it is not specified is simply an unknown field. All three rejections carry HTTP 412 and the single `error_id` `ERR_STALE_VERSION`; the response does not distinguish which parent was stale beyond the message text.

### 2.9. Conserved Quantities: the Clay Reserve and Fired Unit Ledgers

Two quantities in this system are ledgers with an invariant that must hold after every single request, successful or rejected.

**The clay reserve ledger.** A pit's `proven_reserve_tons` is stated once when the pit is opened and is never mutated afterwards. Against it stand three moving columns: `available_tons`, `committed_tons` and `consumed_tons`. At every observable moment, `available_tons` plus `committed_tons` plus `consumed_tons` equals `proven_reserve_tons` exactly, and `available_tons` is never negative. Tonnage moves from available to committed when a production run is created, from committed to consumed when green handling is closed out, and back from committed to available when a run is scrapped by the plant-idle cascade. It never moves out of consumed.

**The fired unit ledger.** A lot's `fired_units` is stamped once when the lot is minted and never changes. Against it stand `available_units`, `reserved_units` and `consumed_units`, and at every observable moment `available_units` plus `reserved_units` plus `consumed_units` equals `fired_units`, with `available_units` never negative. Units move from available to reserved when a blend component is allocated, from reserved to consumed when the order carrying that blend is delivered, and back from reserved to available when a component is voided by a blend rejection or an order cancellation. A lot's `status` tracks the reserved column: a lot whose `reserved_units` rises above 0 reads `RESERVED`, and a lot whose `reserved_units` falls back to exactly 0 returns to `AVAILABLE`.

Both ledgers are checked before they are mutated, not after. Where a single operation must debit several rows — the per-pit clay split is the canonical case — the funding check runs across **all** affected rows before **any** row is written, so a partially applied commitment can never be observed.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Version Propagation

Several operations in this domain are the visible tip of a multi-hop cascade: idling a plant, grading a kiln run, rejecting a blend, delivering an order and cancelling an order all mutate rows the caller did not name. Three rules govern all of them.

First, cascades run in a **fixed, stated order**, and a later hop sees the state a previous hop wrote. §3 gives the hop ordering for each cascade explicitly; implementations must not reorder hops even where the outcome looks order-independent, because the ordering is observable through intermediate ledger states and through which rejection surfaces.

Second, **every row a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1**, exactly as if it had been mutated directly. A client holding a version of a child row that a cascade has moved will therefore find its next optimistic-concurrency-guarded write rejected, which is the intended serialisation point.

Third, cascades are **all-or-nothing with the request that triggered them**. If any hop would violate an invariant, the whole request is rejected and nothing — not the primary mutation, not any hop — is persisted.

### 2.11. Code Uniqueness Scopes and Enumerated Vocabularies

Human-readable codes are how plant staff refer to physical things, and each code has a declared uniqueness scope. `plant_code`, `formulation_code`, `order_code`, `blend_code` and `panel_code` are unique across the whole system. A production run's `run_code` is unique across all production runs, and a kiln run's `run_code` is unique across all kiln runs; the two namespaces are independent of each other, but each is global, so a code already used at one plant may not be reused at another. `pit_name`, `kiln_code` and `car_code` are unique **within their owning plant**, so two plants may each have a car called the same thing. A `plant_code` is between 1 and 32 characters long. Any violation of any of these scopes is reported as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (HTTP 409).

The enumerated vocabularies used throughout are fixed and case-sensitive. Raw material is `CLAY` or `SHALE`. A fired colour family is `RED`, `BUFF`, `GRAY` or `BROWN`. A face texture is `WIRE_CUT`, `SMOOTH`, `SAND_FACED` or `TUMBLED`. A durability grade is `SW` (severe weathering), `MW` (moderate weathering) or `NW` (negligible weathering), and these are **ranked**: `SW` counts as 3, `MW` as 2 and `NW` as 1, so a higher-ranked body or lot can always satisfy a requirement for a lower-ranked one but never the reverse. A firing grade is `ON_COLOR`, `OFF_COLOR_SALVAGE`, `OVERFIRE_SCRAP` or `UNDERFIRE_SCRAP`. Unloading equipment is `CUSTOMER_FORKLIFT`, `TRUCK_MOUNTED_FORKLIFT` or `CRANE`. Banding material is `STEEL` or `POLY`. A blend component's `source` is `MANUAL` or `AUTO_FILL`.

Lifecycle vocabularies are equally fixed. A plant is `ACTIVE` or `IDLED`. A clay pit is `OPEN`, `SUSPENDED` or `DEPLETED`. A body formulation is `DRAFT`, `QUALIFIED` or `RETIRED`. A kiln is `AVAILABLE` or `MAINTENANCE`. A kiln car is `IDLE`, `SET`, `IN_KILN` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE`. A production run is `SETTING`, `SET`, `HELD`, `LOADED`, `FIRED` or `SCRAPPED`. A kiln run is `SCHEDULED`, `FIRING`, `GRADED` or `ABORTED`. A fired lot is `AVAILABLE`, `RESERVED`, `CONSUMED`, `QUARANTINED` or `SCRAP`. A shipped blend is `DRAFT`, `COMPOSED`, `CERTIFIED`, `RELEASED` or `VOID`. A blend component is `ALLOCATED`, `VOIDED` or `CONSUMED`. A sample panel is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `EXPIRED` or `WITHDRAWN`. An order carries two independent lifecycle fields: `fulfillment_status` is `DRAFT`, `ASSIGNED`, `IN_BLENDING`, `PACKAGED`, `DELIVERED` or `CANCELLED`, and `freight_status` is `UNQUOTED`, `ESTIMATED`, `FINALIZED`, `INVOICED` or `WAIVED`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Commissioning a Plant and Its Pit, Kiln and Car Assets

Everything in this system hangs off a plant. Corporate plant engineering registers one by supplying a `plant_code` between 1 and 32 characters and a display `name`, together with the plant's position on the freight grid as the integer pair `grid_x` (east–west, in miles) and `grid_y` (north–south, in miles), each between -5000 and 5000 inclusive. Three commercial parameters come with it: `market_radius_miles`, the greatest rectilinear jobsite distance this plant will serve, which must be greater than zero and whose boundary is **inclusive** — a jobsite exactly at the radius is served; `freight_rate_cents_per_ton_mile`, the outbound rate in whole cents per billable ton per mile, greater than zero; and `base_capacity_green_units_per_day`, the plant's nominal green (unfired) throughput per production day, greater than zero, which is the only capacity figure the lead-time promise reads. Finally, `unloading_equipment_available` is a non-empty array drawn from `CUSTOMER_FORKLIFT`, `TRUCK_MOUNTED_FORKLIFT` and `CRANE`, listing the equipment classes this plant can dispatch to a jobsite. A newly registered plant is created `ACTIVE`, with `fired_scrap_units` at 0, `idled_at` null and `version` 1.

Clay and shale pits are opened under a plant one at a time. Each carries a `pit_name` unique within that plant, a `material_type` of either `CLAY` or `SHALE`, a `proven_reserve_tons` figure greater than zero recorded to two decimal places, and the two chemistry readings that drive fired colour and durability: `iron_oxide_pct`, the ferric oxide content in percent from 0.000 to 20.000 inclusive at three decimal places, and `lime_pct`, the calcium oxide content in percent from 0.000 to 10.000 inclusive at three decimal places. A pit is created `OPEN` with `available_tons` equal to its `proven_reserve_tons` and both `committed_tons` and `consumed_tons` at 0.00. `proven_reserve_tons` is never mutated after the pit is opened — the three moving columns absorb every subsequent movement. A pit whose `available_tons` reaches **exactly** 0.00 as the result of a clay commitment moves `OPEN → DEPLETED`; the boundary is exact, so a pit left with 0.01 tons available stays `OPEN`.

Tunnel kilns are commissioned under a plant with a `kiln_code` unique within that plant, a `position_count` between 1 and 15 giving the number of across-the-car positions (position 1 is the outermost left, and the position numbered `position_count` is the outermost right), a `setpoint_peak_c` between 800 and 1300 degrees Celsius that the firing curve aims for, and a `cooldown_buffer_hours` figure of zero or more, recorded to one decimal place, that specifies the mandatory post-run cooldown. A new kiln is `AVAILABLE`.

Kiln cars are registered under a plant by either corporate engineering or the plant operator itself, each with a `car_code` unique within that plant and a `capacity_green_units` greater than zero that caps how many green units may be set on it in a single run. A new car is `IDLE` with `current_production_run_id` null. That field always names the one production run currently occupying the car — the run whose status is `SETTING`, `SET`, `HELD` or `LOADED` — and is null whenever the car is `IDLE` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE`.

### 3.2. Formulating and Qualifying a Body

A body formulation is the recipe that a plant extrudes. The ceramic body engineer opens one in `DRAFT` by naming the owning `plant_id`, a globally unique `formulation_code`, the `target_color_family` the body is formulated to develop (`RED`, `BUFF`, `GRAY` or `BROWN`), a `target_cdi` between 5.00 and 60.00 at two decimal places giving the colour development index the firing curve aims at, a `target_soak_hours` between 0.5 and 24.0 at one decimal place giving the intended peak-temperature soak duration, and a `clay_tons_per_thousand_units` greater than zero at two decimal places stating how much raw material 1000 extruded green units consume. A new formulation starts with an empty `components` array, `component_share_total_pct` at 0, `dequalified_count` at 0 and `qualified_at` null.

The optional `supersedes_formulation_id` names a predecessor this formulation replaces. When it is supplied and the named predecessor is `QUALIFIED`, that predecessor moves `QUALIFIED → RETIRED` and its own `version` is incremented by 1; the successor becomes reachable from the predecessor through the supersession chain, and that chain is exactly what makes dye lots fired from the old body still interchangeable with dye lots fired from the new one. The predecessor must belong to the same plant as the successor. Retiring a body does **not** invalidate the lots already fired from it.

Components are appended one at a time, in call order, and the append order is retained — it is the order that later governs how clay tonnage is split. Each append supplies a `pit_id` naming a clay pit and a `share_pct` giving that pit's share of the body by percentage, plus the `expected_formulation_version` guard described in §2.8. The pit must belong to the same plant as the formulation; if it does not, the append is rejected with `ERR_PIT_PLANT_MISMATCH`. The append is only legal while the formulation is `DRAFT` — otherwise `ERR_FORMULATION_NOT_DRAFT`. Two arithmetic conditions guard the recipe: the running `component_share_total_pct` after the append must not exceed 100.00, and the same clay pit must not appear twice in one formulation; either violation is rejected with `ERR_COMPONENT_SHARE_TOTAL_INVALID`. Note that `components` is an ordered array of objects, not a foreign-key column — a component object holds only its `pit_id` and its `share_pct`.

Every successful append recomputes the body's derived chemistry, and so does qualification, so the two always agree. The share-weighted ferric oxide content, `blend_iron_oxide_pct`, is the sum over components of each contributing pit's `iron_oxide_pct` multiplied by that component's `share_pct`, divided by 100, rounded half up to three decimal places; the products are accumulated at full precision and the single rounding happens at the end. The share-weighted calcium oxide content, `blend_lime_pct`, is formed the same way from each pit's `lime_pct`. The `shale_share_pct` is the exact two-decimal sum of `share_pct` over only those components whose pit has `material_type` `SHALE`, with no rounding applied.

From those three figures the body's durability follows. Absorption starts from a base of 18.0 and falls as iron and shale rise while rising with free lime: subtract 1.2 multiplied by `blend_iron_oxide_pct`, subtract 0.08 multiplied by `shale_share_pct`, and add 1.5 multiplied by `blend_lime_pct`. The result is clamped to the closed interval from 2.0 to 22.0 — anything below 2.0 becomes 2.0 and anything above 22.0 becomes 22.0 — and then rounded half up to two decimal places to give `absorption_index`. The `computed_durability_grade` is then read off `absorption_index` in this pinned order with both bounds inclusive: an index of 8.00 or less grades `SW`; otherwise an index of 14.00 or less grades `MW`; otherwise the body grades `NW`.

Qualification is the gate that lets a body be extruded. The body engineer qualifies a `DRAFT` formulation, and the transition `DRAFT → QUALIFIED` requires that `component_share_total_pct` equal **exactly** 100.00 — a total of 99.99 or 100.01 is rejected with `ERR_COMPONENT_SHARE_TOTAL_INVALID` — that every contributing pit be `OPEN`, and that the owning plant be `ACTIVE`. A contributing pit that is `SUSPENDED` or `DEPLETED` blocks qualification with `ERR_CLAY_PIT_SUSPENDED`; the pit is never silently dropped from the weighted sums, which is why the weights always add up to the recorded total. Qualification re-runs the chemistry, writing `blend_iron_oxide_pct`, `blend_lime_pct`, `shale_share_pct`, `absorption_index` and `computed_durability_grade`, stamps `qualified_at` from the logical clock and increments the formulation's `version` by 1. Qualification of a formulation that is already `QUALIFIED` or has been `RETIRED` is rejected with `ERR_FORMULATION_NOT_DRAFT`.

A formulation may cycle between `DRAFT` and `QUALIFIED` any number of times. The de-qualification direction is never a direct operator action: it happens only through the plant-idle cascade of §3.9, which clears `qualified_at` and increments `dequalified_count` by 1 each time it fires.

### 3.3. Extrusion, Setting and the Green-Handling Fork

A production run is the physical act of extruding a body, cutting it into units and setting those units on a kiln car. The plant operator opens one by supplying the owning `plant_id`, the `formulation_id` of the body being extruded, the `kiln_car_id` of the car the cut units are set on, a globally unique `run_code`, the face `texture` applied at the cutter (`WIRE_CUT`, `SMOOTH`, `SAND_FACED` or `TUMBLED`), and `green_units_set`, the count of green units actually cut and set, which must be greater than zero. The named formulation must be `QUALIFIED` at this moment — a `DRAFT` or `RETIRED` body is rejected with `ERR_FORMULATION_NOT_QUALIFIED`. The named car must be `IDLE` and must carry no `current_production_run_id`; a car that is `SET`, `IN_KILN` or `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, or one that already names a run, is rejected with `ERR_KILN_CAR_NOT_IDLE`. Because a car is a plant sub-resource and the acting operator's `X-User-ID` is its own plant, naming a car belonging to a different plant is rejected as a plant-scope violation. And `green_units_set` may not exceed the car's `capacity_green_units`; a run that overloads the car is rejected with `ERR_KILN_CAR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A successful run is created in `SETTING`, its `kiln_run_id` and `kiln_position` null, `green_loss_units`, `net_green_units`, `green_loss_pct` and `green_loss_variance_units` all null, and `scrap_units` at 0; the car moves `IDLE → SET` and its `current_production_run_id` is pointed at the new run.

**Committing clay.** Creating the run reserves raw material against the pits named in the body's recipe, and the arithmetic is deliberately two-level. First the run-level total: divide `green_units_set` by 1000, multiply by the formulation's `clay_tons_per_thousand_units`, and round half up to two decimal places. That figure is stored as `clay_tons_committed`. Second the per-pit split: walk the formulation's component objects in ascending `created_at` order — that is, in the order the body engineer appended them — and for each one take the already-rounded run total, multiply by that component's `share_pct`, divide by 100, and round half up to two decimal places. Accumulate those per-pit amounts as you go. The residual is the run total minus the accumulated sum of per-pit amounts, rounded half up to two decimal places, and it lands **entirely on the last component in that append order**. It is never spread across pits, never dropped, and never assigned by `id`. To make the intent unambiguous: three pits holding 33.33, 33.33 and 33.34 percent of a 32.00-ton run receive 10.67, 10.67 and 10.66 tons respectively — not 10.66, 10.67, 10.67 and not 10.67, 10.67, 10.67.

Before a single pit is written, the commitment checks that **every** contributing pit's `available_tons` is at least its own per-pit share. If any one pit falls short, the whole run is rejected with `ERR_CLAY_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT` and no pit is mutated. When the check passes, each contributing pit has its per-pit amount subtracted from `available_tons` and added to `committed_tons`, leaving `consumed_tons` untouched and the reserve identity of §2.9 intact. A pit whose `available_tons` reaches exactly 0.00 in this step moves `OPEN → DEPLETED`, which will block any later attempt to qualify a formulation naming it.

**Projecting the green yield.** At creation, before any real handling loss is known, the run records a conservative projection in `projected_net_green_units`. The projected loss is 350 basis points — 3.5 percent — of `green_units_set`, rounded **up** to a whole unit, plus a flat allowance of 12 units for the setting operation itself; `projected_net_green_units` is `green_units_set` minus that projected loss. The percentage part always rounds up, never to the nearest and never down, so the projection is never optimistic. This projection reads nothing but the units set and the two constants: it never consults `green_loss_units`, which is still null, and it does not move the run out of `SETTING`.

**Closing out green handling.** When the setters are done, the plant operator closes out the run by reporting the real `green_loss_units`, a count of zero or more that may not exceed `green_units_set`. The run must be `SETTING`; closing out a run in any other status is rejected with `ERR_PRODUCTION_RUN_STATE_INVALID`, and the owning plant must be `ACTIVE` or the request is rejected with `ERR_PLANT_IDLED`. The close-out writes three figures. `net_green_units` is `green_units_set` minus the reported `green_loss_units`. `green_loss_pct` is the reported loss multiplied by 100, divided by `green_units_set`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `green_loss_variance_units` is the signed gap `net_green_units` minus `projected_net_green_units` — negative when handling went worse than projected, positive when it went better. For a run of 10000 units set that loses 400, the projection netted 9638 while the actual nets 9600, so the variance is -38 on identical run data.

The close-out then **forks**, and this is the one branching decision in the production lifecycle. When `green_loss_pct` is 6.00 or less, the run moves `SETTING → SET`: handling was within tolerance and the car is now loadable into a kiln run. When `green_loss_pct` is greater than 6.00, the run moves `SETTING → HELD`: the loss goes to loss review, `HELD` is terminal, and the run can never be loaded because loading requires status `SET`. The boundary is inclusive on the passing side and is evaluated on the **rounded** percentage, so a raw loss of 6.004 percent stores as 6.00 and lands in `SET`, while 6.01 lands in `HELD`.

Both branches are otherwise identical. Both write `net_green_units`, `green_loss_pct` and `green_loss_variance_units`. Both move the committed clay into consumption: each contributing pit has its exact per-pit amount — the same amount computed at run creation — subtracted from `committed_tons` and added to `consumed_tons`, which leaves `available_tons` unchanged and again preserves the reserve identity. Both increment the run's `version` by 1. Neither branch touches the kiln car, which stays `SET`.

### 3.4. Kiln Scheduling, Loading, Firing and Grading

A kiln run is a scheduled firing window on one tunnel kiln for one body. The plant operator schedules one by supplying the `kiln_id` of the kiln, the `formulation_id` whose `target_cdi` and `target_soak_hours` the realised curve will be graded against, a globally unique `run_code`, the window bounds `start_at` and `end_at` as ISO 8601 UTC instants with `end_at` strictly later than `start_at`, a `planned_peak_temp_c` between 800 and 1300 degrees Celsius, and a `planned_soak_hours` between 0.50 and 24.00 at two decimal places. The named formulation must be `QUALIFIED` or the request is rejected with `ERR_FORMULATION_NOT_QUALIFIED`. A new kiln run is created `SCHEDULED` with `loaded_units` at 0, `actual_peak_temp_c`, `actual_soak_hours`, `base_cdi`, `firing_curve_deviation` and `graded_by_user_id` all null.

**Kiln windows must not collide.** A tunnel kiln cannot start a second firing until the previous one has cooled, so the scheduling check inflates windows by the kiln's own `cooldown_buffer_hours` on the trailing side and treats the result as a **closed** interval running from `start_at` to `end_at` plus the buffer. The new run's inflated interval must not intersect the identically inflated interval of any existing kiln run on that same kiln whose status is anything other than `ABORTED`. Touching endpoints **collide**: if one run's inflated interval ends at exactly the instant another's begins, that is a conflict, and the request is rejected with `ERR_KILN_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Runs already `ABORTED` are excluded from the comparison entirely, so aborting a run frees its window for reuse.

**Loading a car.** The operator loads one production run onto a numbered position of a `SCHEDULED` kiln run by supplying the `production_run_id`, the `kiln_position` the car occupies, and the `expected_kiln_run_version` guard of §2.8. Six conditions govern the load. The production run must be `SET` — a run still `SETTING`, one that went to `HELD`, or one already `LOADED` is rejected with `ERR_PRODUCTION_RUN_STATE_INVALID`. The target kiln run must be `SCHEDULED`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_KILN_RUN_STATE_INVALID`. The production run's plant must be the plant that owns the target kiln run's kiln. The production run's `formulation_id` must equal the kiln run's `formulation_id`, since the whole firing is graded against one body's targets. The `kiln_position` must be an integer from 1 up to and including the kiln's `position_count`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_KILN_POSITION`; and no other production run may already hold that position on this kiln run, or the request is rejected with `ERR_KILN_POSITION_OCCUPIED`.

One further temporal check guards the car rather than the kiln. A kiln car cannot be in two firings at once, so the car's occupancy is compared as a **half-open** interval running from `start_at` up to but not including `end_at`, with **zero** buffer, against the same half-open interval of every other non-`ABORTED` kiln run the car is already loaded into. Exact adjacency is therefore **allowed**: a car whose previous firing ends at precisely the instant the next one begins may be loaded, which is the opposite convention from the kiln window check. An actual overlap is rejected with `ERR_KILN_CAR_OCCUPIED`.

A successful load moves the production run `SET → LOADED` and writes its `kiln_run_id` and `kiln_position`; moves the car `SET → IN_KILN`; recomputes the kiln run's `loaded_units` as the sum of `net_green_units` over every production run pointing at it; and increments the kiln run's `version` by 1.

**Lighting off.** The operator starts a kiln run, moving it `SCHEDULED → FIRING` and incrementing its `version` by 1. Two conditions apply beyond the status check: at least one production run must be loaded onto it, and the owning kiln must be `AVAILABLE` rather than in `MAINTENANCE`. Starting a run that is not `SCHEDULED`, or starting one with no car loaded, is rejected with `ERR_KILN_RUN_STATE_INVALID`.

**Grading the firing.** Grading is where colour is actually decided, and it is the single richest cascade in the production half of the domain. The operator reports the realised curve as `actual_peak_temp_c`, an integer from 800 to 1300, and `actual_soak_hours`, a figure from 0.00 to 24.00 at two decimal places. The kiln run must be `FIRING`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_KILN_RUN_STATE_INVALID`.

At run level, two figures are written onto the kiln run itself. The centreline colour development index `base_cdi` is 0.62 multiplied by the quantity (`actual_peak_temp_c` minus 900) divided by 10, plus 4.0 multiplied by `actual_soak_hours`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The `firing_curve_deviation` is the absolute difference between `actual_peak_temp_c` and the kiln's `setpoint_peak_c` divided by 10, plus the absolute difference between `actual_soak_hours` and the formulation's `target_soak_hours`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The acting operator's `X-User-ID` is stamped into `graded_by_user_id`.

At position level, one fired lot is minted for **each** production run that is `LOADED` on this kiln run, and only those runs — a run that was reverted to `SET` by a plant idle is not in the set and mints nothing. A tunnel kiln is hotter along its centreline than against its walls, so the car's `kiln_position` shifts its realised peak. The kiln's centreline sits at the position count plus one, divided by two — position 3.0 in a five-position kiln. The `position_temp_delta_c` is the negative of 6.0 multiplied by the absolute difference between the car's `kiln_position` and that centreline, rounded half up to two decimal places; at the exact centreline the delta is 0.00. The lot's `position_cdi` is the already-rounded `base_cdi` plus 0.62 multiplied by the already-rounded `position_temp_delta_c` divided by 10, rounded half up to two decimal places. Rounding order matters here: `base_cdi` is rounded before `position_cdi` is derived from it, and `position_temp_delta_c` is rounded before it is folded in, because recomputing from unrounded intermediates can shift `position_cdi` by 0.01 and flip a lot sitting on a grade boundary. At the centreline the delta is exactly 0.00, so `position_cdi` equals `base_cdi`.

The lot's `grade` follows from comparing `position_cdi` against the formulation's `target_cdi`, evaluated in **exactly** this order with the first match winning. If `position_cdi` is strictly greater than the target plus 6.00, the grade is `OVERFIRE_SCRAP`. Otherwise, if `position_cdi` is strictly less than the target minus 6.00, the grade is `UNDERFIRE_SCRAP`. Otherwise, if the absolute difference between `position_cdi` and the target is 3.00 or less, the grade is `ON_COLOR`. Otherwise the grade is `OFF_COLOR_SALVAGE`. The boundary semantics are exactly as written: a `position_cdi` equal to the target plus 6.00 is **not** overfire — it falls through and, being more than 3.00 away from target, becomes `OFF_COLOR_SALVAGE` — while the target plus 6.01 is overfire.

The lot's shipped-blend colour coordinate `color_index` is 50.0 plus 3.2 multiplied by the signed difference (`position_cdi` minus the formulation's `target_cdi`), clamped to the closed range 0.00 to 100.00, then rounded half up to two decimal places.

Each minted lot copies a pinned identity from the run and body it came from. Its `production_run_id` names the run; its `lot_code` is the run's `run_code` followed by a hyphen, the letter `P` and the `kiln_position` — a run coded `R-1` fired at position 4 yields the lot code `R-1-P4`. Its `kiln_position` is copied from the run, and `fired_units` is copied from the run's `net_green_units`, which fixes the lot's ledger total for life. `color_family` is copied from the body's `target_color_family`, `texture` from the production run's `texture`, and `durability_grade` from the body's `computed_durability_grade` — pinned at grading time, so later edits to the formulation never move it. `source_formulation_id` records the body the lot was fired from. `dye_lot_code` is the body's `formulation_code`, a hyphen, the kiln run's `run_code`, a hyphen, the letter `P` and the `kiln_position`; that string is the dye-lot identity an architectural panel is approved against. The lot opens with `reserved_units` and `consumed_units` at 0, `available_units` equal to `fired_units`, and `quarantine_reason` null.

A lot's initial `status` depends on its grade. A lot graded `ON_COLOR` or `OFF_COLOR_SALVAGE` is created `AVAILABLE`. A lot graded `OVERFIRE_SCRAP` or `UNDERFIRE_SCRAP` is created directly in `SCRAP`, and two further effects follow from it: the production run's `scrap_units` is set to that lot's `fired_units`, and the owning plant's `fired_scrap_units` running total is increased by the same amount. Every other grade leaves both figures alone.

The grading cascade completes in a fixed order. First, one fired lot is synthesised per loaded production run and each of those runs moves `LOADED → FIRED`. Second, caused by the first hop, a scrap-graded lot writes the run's `scrap_units` and increments the plant's `fired_scrap_units`. Third, also caused by the first hop, every affected kiln car moves `IN_KILN → IDLE` and has its `current_production_run_id` cleared. Finally the kiln run itself moves `FIRING → GRADED` and its `version` is incremented by 1. `GRADED` is terminal.

**Quality holds.** Fired inventory can be pulled from circulation at any time by the quality manager, who supplies a `quarantine_reason`. A lot that is `AVAILABLE` or `RESERVED` moves to `QUARANTINED`, records the reason and has its `version` incremented by 1; a lot already graded `OVERFIRE_SCRAP` or `UNDERFIRE_SCRAP` cannot be quarantined because it is already `SCRAP`. Quarantining a `RESERVED` lot leaves its existing `ALLOCATED` components untouched — the units stay reserved — but the blend holding them can no longer be certified. `QUARANTINED` is terminal: a quarantined lot is excluded from auto-fill eligibility and from manual component addition, and any attempt to draw on it, compose or certify a blend that holds it, or approve a panel built from it is rejected with `ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED`.

### 3.5. Order Intake, Freight-Sensitive Plant Assignment and the Lead-Time Promise

Inside sales takes an order by recording where it is going and what it must be made of. The payload carries a globally unique `order_code`; the jobsite's position on the freight grid as the integer pair `jobsite_grid_x` and `jobsite_grid_y`, each between -5000 and 5000 inclusive; `requested_units`, the count of masonry units ordered, greater than zero; the required fired `color_family`; the required face `texture`; and `durability_grade`, the **minimum acceptable** weathering grade — a higher-ranked grade always satisfies a lower-ranked requirement, so an `SW` body can serve an `MW` order but an `MW` body cannot serve an `SW` order. Two optional booleans shape the promise: `is_special_order` marks a non-stock colour and texture combination, and `requires_panel_approval` marks an architectural project that cannot ship without an approved sample panel; both default to false when omitted. Two logistics fields are captured at intake rather than at dispatch: `unloading_equipment_required`, the equipment class the jobsite needs, and `banding_type`, either `STEEL` or `POLY`, which determines the banding weight the final freight bill carries. A new order is created with `fulfillment_status` `DRAFT` and `freight_status` `UNQUOTED`, and with `plant_id`, `distance_miles`, every freight figure, `lead_time_days`, `promised_ship_date`, `pallet_count`, `band_count`, `unloading_equipment_provided`, `assigned_by_user_id`, `packaged_by_user_id`, `assigned_at`, `delivered_at`, `invoiced_at` and `cancellation_reason` all null.

An order carries **two independent lifecycle dimensions on the same record**. `fulfillment_status` tracks where the bricks are in the make-and-ship pipeline; `freight_status` tracks where the money is in the quote-and-invoice pipeline. They advance on different triggers, and a given request may move one, the other, or both. The independence is real: opening a shipped blend on an assigned order advances `fulfillment_status` from `ASSIGNED` to `IN_BLENDING` and leaves `freight_status` exactly where it stood.

**Assigning a plant.** Brick is freight-dominated, so the plant is chosen by haul cost rather than by preference. When the sales rep runs assignment on a `DRAFT` order, the selector first builds an eligible pool. A plant is eligible when all four of the following hold: its `status` is `ACTIVE`, so an `IDLED` plant is excluded even when it is the nearest one; the rectilinear haul distance from the plant to the jobsite is no greater than that plant's own `market_radius_miles`, the boundary being inclusive; the order's `unloading_equipment_required` appears in that plant's `unloading_equipment_available`; and the plant has at least one body formulation whose `status` is `QUALIFIED`, whose `target_color_family` equals the order's `color_family`, and whose `computed_durability_grade` ranks at least as high as the order's required `durability_grade`. A plant holding only `DRAFT` or `RETIRED` formulations in the requested colour family is not eligible.

Haul distance on the freight grid is **rectilinear** — the absolute difference of the x coordinates plus the absolute difference of the y coordinates — never a straight-line or Euclidean distance, and `distance_miles` is stored as an exact integer with no rounding.

The eligible plants are then ranked, and the cheapest haul wins. The primary key is the estimated total freight in whole cents, quoted against each candidate plant's own `freight_rate_cents_per_ton_mile` and its own grid position, ascending. Two plants quoting the identical whole-cent total fall through to haul distance ascending, and a further tie falls through to the plant's `created_at` ascending. A plant's `id` is never a ranking or tie-break input. The first plant in that ordering wins. If the eligible pool is empty, the assignment is rejected with `ERR_NO_PLANT_IN_MARKET_RADIUS` and nothing is written.

A successful assignment writes the winner into `plant_id`, writes `distance_miles` for the winner, stamps `assigned_at` from the logical clock and `assigned_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, advances `fulfillment_status` `DRAFT → ASSIGNED`, advances `freight_status` `UNQUOTED → ESTIMATED` in the same request, and increments the order's `version` by 1. Assignment on an order whose `fulfillment_status` is anything but `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`.

**Quoting freight — the estimated variant.** The freight quote is a linehaul plus a fuel surcharge, and it is computed twice over an order's life from the same schedule but on different weights. At assignment, nothing has been blended or packaged, so the estimate bills brick weight only. A masonry unit weighs 4.5 pounds and a ton is 2000 pounds, so `estimated_billable_tons` is `requested_units` multiplied by 4.5, divided by 2000, rounded half up to two decimal places. `estimated_base_freight_cents` is `distance_miles` multiplied by the assigned plant's `freight_rate_cents_per_ton_mile` multiplied by those billable tons, rounded half up to a whole cent. Note the ordering: billable tons are rounded to two decimal places **before** the linehaul is computed from them.

The fuel surcharge is a **whole-bill** rate selected from the rounded base — not a marginal rate applied bracket by bracket — and the three arms are evaluated in exactly this order. A base below 50000 cents carries 6.5 percent. Otherwise a base below 150000 cents carries 4.5 percent. Otherwise the base carries 3.0 percent. `estimated_fuel_surcharge_cents` is the base multiplied by that percentage, divided by 100, rounded half up to a whole cent, and `estimated_freight_cents` is the base plus the surcharge.

That combination makes the schedule non-monotonic at the tier edges, and the effect is intentional rather than a defect to be smoothed away. Hauling 50 miles at 40 cents per ton-mile, 11106 units bills 24.99 tons for a base of 49980 cents, which sits in the first tier at 6.5 percent and totals 53229 cents; 11111 units bills exactly 25.00 tons for a base of 50000 cents, which just reaches the second tier at 4.5 percent and totals only 52250 cents. Shipping five more bricks lowers the bill. Keying the tier on the total instead of the base, or applying marginal bracket rates, changes both figures.

**Promising a ship date.** Lead time is computed from the winning plant's capacity alone; no queue of other orders is considered. Start from a base of 14 days. Add 28 days when `is_special_order` is true. Add 7 days when `requires_panel_approval` is true. Then add the run-out term: `requested_units` divided by the winning plant's `base_capacity_green_units_per_day`, rounded **up** to a whole day — never to the nearest, never down — so an order exactly equal to one day of capacity contributes 1 day and one unit more contributes 2. The sum is stored as `lead_time_days`, and `promised_ship_date` is the logical clock at assignment advanced by that many whole UTC days. With the clock at its startup default, a 12000-unit standard order at a plant rated 40000 green units per day promises 15 days out, at `2026-08-16T00:00:00Z`; the same order flagged both `is_special_order` and `requires_panel_approval` promises 50 days out, at `2026-09-20T00:00:00Z`.

### 3.6. Architectural Sample Panels and Dye-Lot Equivalence

An architectural project does not accept brick on a specification sheet; the architect of record accepts a laid-up sample panel and then holds the shipped blend to it. A blend technician lays up a panel against an order whose `fulfillment_status` is `ASSIGNED` or `IN_BLENDING`, supplying a globally unique `panel_code`, the `formulation_id` of the body the panel was laid up from, the `dye_lot_id` naming the physical fired lot the panel was actually built from, a `panel_color_index` between 0.00 and 100.00 at two decimal places measured on the laid-up panel, and a `tolerance_delta_e` between 0.00 and 20.00 at two decimal places giving the greatest absolute colour-index deviation the architect will accept between the approved panel and the shipped blend. The named formulation must be `QUALIFIED` or the lay-up is rejected with `ERR_FORMULATION_NOT_QUALIFIED`. A new panel is created `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0 and `revision_note`, `submitted_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, `approved_at`, `expires_at`, `approved_color_index`, `pinned_formulation_version` and `pinned_dye_lot_code` all null.

The review cycle is a loop rather than a line. The blend technician submits a panel, moving it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` (or `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` on a later round), which requires that the panel's body still be `QUALIFIED`, stamps `submitted_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` — overwriting whatever a previous round wrote — and increments the panel's `version` by 1. The architect reviewer may send it back, moving it `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED`, recording a `revision_note`, incrementing `revision_round` by 1 and incrementing `version` by 1. That loop is capped at three rounds: the request that would take `revision_round` from 3 to 4 is rejected with `ERR_PANEL_REVISION_LIMIT`. Submitting a panel that is neither `DRAFT` nor `REVISION_REQUESTED`, or requesting a revision on a panel that is not `SUBMITTED`, is rejected with `ERR_PANEL_STATE_INVALID`.

Approval is the other side of the two-person control. The architect reviewer approves a `SUBMITTED` panel, moving it `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, and four conditions apply: the panel must be `SUBMITTED`, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_user_id`, the panel's body formulation must still be `QUALIFIED`, and the panel's fired lot must not be `QUARANTINED` — a quarantined dye lot is rejected with `ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED`. Approval pins the evidence: `pinned_formulation_version` captures the formulation's `version` as observed at this instant and never moves again even if the body is later mutated; `pinned_dye_lot_code` captures the lot's `dye_lot_code`; `approved_color_index` copies `panel_color_index`; `approved_by_user_id` records the approver; `approved_at` records the logical clock; and `expires_at` is set to `approved_at` advanced by 180 whole days. The panel's `version` is incremented by 1.

An approval is good for that 180-day window and no longer. When a certification looks at an `APPROVED` panel and finds the logical clock at or past `expires_at`, the panel moves `APPROVED → EXPIRED` and the certification that observed the expiry is rejected with `ERR_PANEL_APPROVAL_EXPIRED`. The boundary is inclusive: a clock reading exactly `expires_at` expires the panel, while a clock one second earlier does not.

**Dye-lot equivalence.** Bodies get reformulated, and an architect who approved a panel from the old body would otherwise have to re-approve every time. The supersession chain solves this. Starting from the panel's own `formulation_id`, walk **upward** through each formulation's `supersedes_formulation_id`, collecting every formulation id encountered; the walk stops when the pointer is null, when it revisits an id already collected — which is what keeps a circular supersession chain from looping forever — or after 16 hops. The set collected is the panel's equivalence class, and dye lots fired from any formulation in it are interchangeable as far as this panel is concerned. A `RETIRED` formulation stays in the class: retiring a body does not invalidate lots already fired from it. Membership is exact set membership on formulation ids; there is no partial matching on `formulation_code`.

When an order requires panel approval, certification runs three checks over the panel's class in this pinned order, and the first failure is what the client is told. First, every allocated component's fired lot must have a `source_formulation_id` that lies inside the equivalence class; a lot from outside is rejected with `ERR_DYE_LOT_MISMATCH`. Second, the blend as composed must sit inside the approved panel's tolerance: `panel_delta_e` — the absolute difference between the blend's `weighted_color_index` and the panel's `approved_color_index`, rounded half up to two decimal places — must be no greater than `tolerance_delta_e`, and a strict excess is rejected with `ERR_PANEL_TOLERANCE_EXCEEDED`; the ceiling is inclusive, so a deviation exactly equal to the tolerance passes. Third, and independently of the panel, the blend's `color_range_span` must be no greater than 12.00, and a strict excess is rejected with `ERR_COLOR_RANGE_SPAN_EXCEEDED`; that ceiling is inclusive too, so a span of exactly 12.00 passes. Because the order is pinned, a blend that both draws an out-of-class lot and busts the tolerance reports `ERR_DYE_LOT_MISMATCH`. When the order does not require panel approval, none of the three checks runs and `panel_delta_e` stays null.

### 3.7. Blending Fired Lots into a Shippable Colour Range

Product blending is what turns position-to-position colour variation into a wall that reads as one colour. A blend technician opens a shipped blend on an order whose `fulfillment_status` is `ASSIGNED`, supplying a globally unique `blend_code`, the colour window the jobsite will accept as `target_color_index_min` and `target_color_index_max` — both between 0.00 and 100.00 at two decimal places, with the minimum strictly less than the maximum, and both bounds **inclusive** when lots are tested against the window — and `requested_units`, the count this blend must deliver, greater than zero and no greater than the order's own `requested_units`. The order must already have a `plant_id`, since a blend draws only on inventory fired at the order's assigned plant. The server computes `target_color_index_midpoint` as the mean of the two window bounds, rounded half up to two decimal places; that midpoint is the ranking key auto-fill uses. The blend is created `DRAFT` with `allocated_units` at 0, `dye_lot_count` at 0, `rejection_count` at 0, and `weighted_color_index`, `color_range_span`, `panel_delta_e`, `composed_by_user_id`, `certified_by_user_id`, `certified_at` and `rejection_reason` all null. Opening the blend advances the order `ASSIGNED → IN_BLENDING` and increments the order's `version` by 1; the order's `freight_status` is deliberately untouched.

**Hand-picking a lot.** The technician draws on inventory one lot at a time, supplying the `fired_lot_id`, the `units` to draw — greater than zero — and the `expected_blend_version` guard of §2.8. The blend must be `DRAFT`, or the allocation is rejected with `ERR_BLEND_NOT_DRAFT`. The lot must be `AVAILABLE` or `RESERVED`, must be graded `ON_COLOR`, and must have at least as many `available_units` as the units requested; a lot that is `CONSUMED`, `SCRAP`, graded anything other than `ON_COLOR`, or short of units is rejected with `ERR_FIRED_LOT_UNAVAILABLE`. A `QUARANTINED` lot is rejected with `ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED`. The lot's `durability_grade` must rank at least as high as the order's required `durability_grade`, or the allocation is rejected with `ERR_DURABILITY_GRADE_NOT_MET`. The lot's `color_family` and `texture` must match the order's, and the lot must have been fired at the order's assigned plant, or the allocation is rejected with `ERR_LOT_ATTRIBUTE_MISMATCH`. Finally, the resulting `allocated_units` must not exceed the blend's `requested_units`, or the allocation is rejected with `ERR_BLEND_OVER_ALLOCATION`.

A successful draw creates a blend component in `ALLOCATED` with `source` `MANUAL`, whose `blend_id` is the owning shipped blend addressed by the request and whose `fired_lot_id` is the lot drawn upon. Two audit figures are pinned at that moment: `color_index_at_allocation` snapshots the lot's `color_index` as it stands now, and `contribution_index` is `color_index_at_allocation` multiplied by `units`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `contribution_index` is a per-component traceability field only — it never feeds a decision and it is **never** summed to derive the blend's colour. The drawn units move out of the lot's `available_units` into its `reserved_units`, moving the lot `AVAILABLE → RESERVED`; the blend's `allocated_units` is recomputed as the sum of `units` over its `ALLOCATED` components; and the blend's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Composing.** Composition closes the draft and writes the blend's colour profile. The technician may pass the optional `auto_fill` flag; when it is true and `allocated_units` is still below `requested_units`, the server fills the shortfall itself before any of the composition conditions are evaluated.

Auto-fill is a strict waterfall over the plant's fired inventory. A lot is eligible when its `status` is `AVAILABLE` or `RESERVED`, its `available_units` is greater than zero, its `grade` is `ON_COLOR`, its `color_family` equals the order's `color_family`, its `texture` equals the order's `texture`, its `durability_grade` ranks at least as high as the order's `durability_grade`, the production run behind it was made at the order's assigned plant, and its `color_index` falls within the blend's window from `target_color_index_min` to `target_color_index_max` with both bounds inclusive. `QUARANTINED` and `SCRAP` lots, lots of other plants, off-family and off-texture lots, under-graded lots and lots outside the window are all excluded. A lot the technician has already drawn on manually remains eligible for whatever `available_units` it has left, and the waterfall sees the post-manual figure.

Eligible lots are ordered by three keys. The primary key is the absolute difference between the lot's `color_index` and the blend's `target_color_index_midpoint`, rounded half up to two decimal places before comparison, ascending — closest to the midpoint first. Two lots equidistant at two decimal places fall through to `available_units` descending, biggest lot first, and a further tie falls through to the lot's `created_at` ascending, oldest first. A lot's `id` is never a tie-break input. Walking that order, each bucket is filled completely before the next is touched: from each lot the waterfall takes the lesser of the units still needed and that lot's `available_units`, appends a blend component with `source` `AUTO_FILL`, and reduces the shortfall accordingly, stopping as soon as the shortfall reaches zero. Each appended component moves its lot's units from `available_units` to `reserved_units` and moves the lot `AVAILABLE → RESERVED`. If the eligible inventory runs out before the shortfall does, the whole composition is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BLEND_INVENTORY` and nothing at all is persisted — not the auto-filled components, not the reservations.

Once any auto-fill is done, composition requires that `allocated_units` equal `requested_units` **exactly**; a blend still short, whether because auto-fill was not requested or because the technician under-drew, is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_BLEND_INVENTORY`. It further requires that every `ALLOCATED` component's lot be `RESERVED` and graded `ON_COLOR`, and that no such lot be `QUARANTINED`, which is reported as `ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED`. And the composed spread must be within the shipped-blend ceiling of 12.00, or the composition is rejected with `ERR_COLOR_RANGE_SPAN_EXCEEDED`.

The colour profile is computed over the blend's `ALLOCATED` components only; `VOIDED` components are excluded, so a reject-then-recompose cycle recomputes the profile from scratch rather than accumulating the discarded rounds, and `CONSUMED` components are relevant only for a blend already `RELEASED`, whose profile is frozen. `allocated_units` is the integer sum of component `units`. `weighted_color_index` is the units-weighted mean fired colour: sum each component's lot `color_index` multiplied by that component's `units`, accumulating at full precision, divide the total by `allocated_units`, and round half up to two decimal places once at the end. Rounding each component's product before summing, or averaging the per-component rounded contributions, is forbidden — both drift, and both can push `panel_delta_e` across a tolerance boundary. `color_range_span` is the greatest lot `color_index` among the allocated components minus the least, rounded half up to two decimal places. `dye_lot_count` is the number of **distinct** `dye_lot_code` values across those components. Composition moves the blend `DRAFT → COMPOSED`, stamps `composed_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and increments the blend's `version` by 1. Composing a blend that is not `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_BLEND_NOT_DRAFT`.

**Certifying.** The quality manager certifies a `COMPOSED` blend, and may never be the operator who composed it. Beyond the segregation check, certification requires that no lot behind an `ALLOCATED` component be `QUARANTINED` (`ERR_LOT_QUARANTINED`), and, when the order's `requires_panel_approval` is true, that the order have a panel that is `APPROVED` (`ERR_PANEL_NOT_APPROVED`) whose `expires_at` is strictly after the current logical clock (`ERR_PANEL_APPROVAL_EXPIRED`), and that the three dye-lot equivalence checks of §3.6 pass. Certification re-verifies the colour profile, writes `panel_delta_e` when a panel applies, stamps `certified_by_user_id` and `certified_at`, moves the blend `COMPOSED → CERTIFIED`, and increments the blend's `version` by 1. Certifying a blend that is not `COMPOSED` is rejected with `ERR_BLEND_NOT_COMPOSED`.

**Rejecting.** The quality manager may instead send a `COMPOSED` blend back, supplying a `rejection_reason`. Rejection is a four-hop cascade, in this order. First, the blend moves `COMPOSED → DRAFT`, records `rejection_reason`, increments `rejection_count` by 1, and clears `weighted_color_index`, `color_range_span`, `dye_lot_count` and `composed_by_user_id`. Second, caused by that, every `ALLOCATED` blend component of the blend moves `ALLOCATED → VOIDED` and `allocated_units` returns to 0. Third, caused by that, every fired lot behind those components has the voided units moved out of `reserved_units` and back into `available_units`; a lot whose `reserved_units` reaches exactly 0 moves `RESERVED → AVAILABLE` with `available_units` restored to its pre-reservation value, which is precisely the fired-unit conservation identity of §2.9. Fourth, the blend's `version` is incremented by 1. The technician may then recompose, and the `DRAFT → COMPOSED → DRAFT` cycle is legal any number of times, with `rejection_count` recording the rounds. Rejecting a blend that is not `COMPOSED` is rejected with `ERR_BLEND_NOT_COMPOSED`.

### 3.8. Packaging, Jobsite Delivery, and the Freight Ledger

**Packaging.** Outbound logistics palletises and bands a certified blend, reporting `pallet_count`, the pallets actually banded, and `band_count`, the bands actually applied, both greater than zero. The order's `fulfillment_status` must be `IN_BLENDING` (otherwise `ERR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`) and its shipped blend must be `CERTIFIED` (otherwise `ERR_BLEND_NOT_CERTIFIED`). A pallet holds 500 masonry units, so `pallet_count` multiplied by 500 must be at least the certified blend's `allocated_units`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_PALLET_COUNT_INSUFFICIENT`; and each pallet needs at least one band, so `band_count` must be at least `pallet_count`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_BAND_COUNT_INSUFFICIENT`.

Packaging is where freight is re-quoted on real weight, and this is the second and final evaluation of the freight schedule. The final variant never reads `requested_units` at all; it bills what is actually on the truck. Add 60 pounds of pallet tare for each pallet, plus the banding weight for each band — a steel band weighs 3.0 pounds and a poly band weighs 1.2 pounds, selected by the order's `banding_type` — plus 4.5 pounds for each of the blend's `allocated_units`. Divide that total by 2000 pounds per ton and round half up to two decimal places to get `final_billable_tons`. Then `final_base_freight_cents` is `distance_miles` multiplied by the assigned plant's `freight_rate_cents_per_ton_mile` multiplied by `final_billable_tons`, rounded half up to a whole cent; the surcharge tier is selected from that rounded base on exactly the same three-arm schedule as the estimate — below 50000 cents carries 6.5 percent, otherwise below 150000 cents carries 4.5 percent, otherwise 3.0 percent — `final_fuel_surcharge_cents` is the base multiplied by that percentage divided by 100 and rounded half up to a whole cent, and `final_freight_cents` is the base plus the surcharge. Finally `freight_variance_cents` is `final_freight_cents` minus `estimated_freight_cents`, a signed figure that is normally positive because the estimate systematically under-quotes: it could not see pallet tare or banding, since `pallet_count` and `band_count` were still null when it ran. For a 12000-unit order hauled 60 miles at 42 cents per ton-mile, the estimate billed 27.00 tons for 71102 cents while the final bill, on 24 pallets and 48 steel bands, bills 27.79 tons for 73182 cents — a variance of 2080 cents on identical order data. The re-quote can also cross a surcharge tier the estimate did not.

Packaging stamps `packaged_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, advances `fulfillment_status` `IN_BLENDING → PACKAGED`, advances `freight_status` `ESTIMATED → FINALIZED` in the same request, and increments the order's `version` by 1.

**Delivery.** Logistics closes out the jobsite by reporting `unloading_equipment_provided`, the equipment actually dispatched. The order's `fulfillment_status` must be `PACKAGED`. Two equipment checks apply in order: the reported equipment must be one the assigned plant actually operates, meaning it appears in that plant's `unloading_equipment_available`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_UNLOADING_EQUIPMENT_UNAVAILABLE`; and it must equal the order's `unloading_equipment_required`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_UNLOADING_EQUIPMENT_MISMATCH`. Sending a crane to a jobsite that asked for a truck-mounted forklift is a failed delivery even when the plant owns both.

Delivery is the point at which reserved inventory becomes consumed inventory. It stamps `delivered_at` and `invoiced_at` from the logical clock, advances `fulfillment_status` `PACKAGED → DELIVERED` and `freight_status` `FINALIZED → INVOICED` in the same request, and increments the order's `version` by 1. It moves the shipped blend `CERTIFIED → RELEASED`, which moves every `ALLOCATED` blend component to `CONSUMED`, which in turn shifts each referenced lot's units out of `reserved_units` and into `consumed_units` and moves a lot whose reservations are fully drawn `RESERVED → CONSUMED`. Both `DELIVERED` and `INVOICED` are terminal, as are `RELEASED`, `CONSUMED` on a component and `CONSUMED` on a lot.

**Cancellation.** Inside sales may cancel an order at any point before delivery — that is, while `fulfillment_status` is `DRAFT`, `ASSIGNED`, `IN_BLENDING` or `PACKAGED` — supplying a `cancellation_reason`. Cancelling a `DELIVERED` or already `CANCELLED` order is rejected with `ERR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`. The cancellation cascade runs in this order. First, `fulfillment_status` moves to `CANCELLED`, the reason is recorded, and `freight_status` moves to `WAIVED` from whichever of `UNQUOTED`, `ESTIMATED` or `FINALIZED` it held. Second, caused by that, the order's shipped blend moves to `VOID` from whichever of `DRAFT`, `COMPOSED` or `CERTIFIED` it held. Third, caused by that, every `ALLOCATED` component of that blend moves to `VOIDED` and the blend's `allocated_units` returns to 0. Fourth, caused by that, every fired lot behind those components has the voided units moved out of `reserved_units` and back into `available_units`, and a lot whose `reserved_units` reaches 0 moves `RESERVED → AVAILABLE` — the same release the reject cascade performs. A separate branch, also caused by the first hop, withdraws paperwork: every sample panel of this order whose status is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`. Panels already `APPROVED` or `EXPIRED` are left alone. The order's `version` is incremented by 1, and every row the cascade touched has its own `version` incremented and `updated_at` refreshed.

Waiving freight does **not** erase the money. `estimated_billable_tons`, `estimated_base_freight_cents`, `estimated_fuel_surcharge_cents`, `estimated_freight_cents`, `final_billable_tons`, `final_base_freight_cents`, `final_fuel_surcharge_cents`, `final_freight_cents` and `freight_variance_cents` are all left exactly as they stood, so a `WAIVED` order still shows what the haul would have cost. Both `CANCELLED` and `WAIVED` are terminal, as is `VOID` on a blend and `VOIDED` on a component.

### 3.9. Idling a Plant: the Deepest Cascade in the Domain

Taking a plant out of service is the single most consequential write in this system, because a plant's raw material, recipes, cars, kilns and in-flight production all become unusable at once. Corporate plant engineering idles a plant that is `ACTIVE`; the transition `ACTIVE → IDLED` is one-way and `IDLED` is terminal — no endpoint reactivates a plant — so a second idle attempt on an already `IDLED` plant is rejected with `ERR_PLANT_IDLED`. Once a plant is `IDLED`, every write that touches it or any of its resources is likewise rejected with `ERR_PLANT_IDLED`: opening a pit, commissioning a kiln, registering a car, creating or amending or qualifying a formulation, creating a production run, closing out green handling, scheduling a kiln run, loading, starting and grading are all blocked.

The immediate effects are that `idled_at` is stamped from the logical clock, every kiln belonging to the plant moves to `MAINTENANCE`, and every kiln car belonging to the plant moves to `OUT_OF_SERVICE` — from `IDLE`, from `SET` or from `IN_KILN` alike — with `current_production_run_id` cleared wherever it was set.

Beyond that, the cascade runs in a **fixed order of hops**, and each hop reads the state the previous hop wrote.

- **Hop 1 — pits.** Every clay pit of this plant whose `status` is `OPEN` moves `OPEN → SUSPENDED`. This is what makes the recipes containing them ineligible and drives hop 2.
- **Hop 2 — recipes, caused by hop 1.** Every body formulation whose `status` is `QUALIFIED` and whose `components` contain a `pit_id` that hop 1 has just suspended moves `QUALIFIED → DRAFT`, has `qualified_at` cleared, and has `dequalified_count` incremented by 1. A formulation whose pits were all already suspended before this request is untouched, because the trigger is specifically a pit suspended by hop 1 of **this** request.
- **Hop 3 — in-flight extrusion, caused by hop 2.** Every production run whose `status` is `SETTING` and whose `formulation_id` names one of the formulations de-qualified in hop 2 moves `SETTING → SCRAPPED`. Runs already `SET`, `HELD`, `LOADED` or `FIRED` are not in scope for this hop.
- **Hop 4 — tonnage release, caused by hop 3.** For every run scrapped in hop 3, the clay commitment runs in release mode: each contributing pit has the **exact per-pit amount committed at run creation** — recomputed by the same two-level split, in the same component append order, with the same residual landing on the same last component — subtracted from `committed_tons` and added back to `available_tons`, restoring the reserve identity. A pit that was `DEPLETED` and whose `available_tons` becomes greater than 0.00 as a result of this release moves `DEPLETED → SUSPENDED`. Each scrapped run's kiln car moves to `OUT_OF_SERVICE` with `current_production_run_id` cleared.
- **Hop 3b — firings, also caused by hop 1 through the kiln branch, and running after hop 4.** Every kiln run on a kiln of this plant whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `FIRING` moves to `ABORTED`. `ABORTED` is terminal, and an aborted run's window is excluded from both the kiln firing-window collision check and the kiln-car occupancy check for every later request, so aborting a run frees the kiln and the car for reuse at another plant-active moment.
- **Hop 4b — loaded cars, caused by hop 3b.** Every production run whose `kiln_run_id` names one of the runs aborted in hop 3b and whose `status` is `LOADED` reverts `LOADED → SET` and has both `kiln_run_id` and `kiln_position` cleared. Its kiln car moves `IN_KILN → OUT_OF_SERVICE`, because the plant is idling. A run reverted this way is no longer in the input set of any later grading, so it mints no fired lot.

The plant's own `version` is incremented by 1, and every entity any hop touched — pits, formulations, production runs, kiln runs, kiln cars, kilns — has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1. Fired lots, orders, shipped blends, blend components and sample panels are **not** touched by this cascade: inventory already fired at the plant stays exactly as it was, and orders already assigned to the plant keep their assignment and their quote.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

All paths below are absolute; the API is mounted at the root, so no additional prefix applies. Endpoints that bring a brand-new resource into existence return HTTP 201 with the created resource in `data`. Endpoints that modify an existing resource — every state transition, every append that mutates its parent, every cascade trigger — return HTTP 200 with the updated resource in `data`; where an operation mutates a child but the caller's target is the parent, the response carries the parent.
