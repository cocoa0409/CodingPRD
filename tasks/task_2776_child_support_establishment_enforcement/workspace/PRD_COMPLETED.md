# Child Support Enforcement API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A state IV-D child support agency carries a case from the moment a public-assistance referral lands on a caseworker's desk to the moment the last dollar of arrears is collected, compromised, or written off — and every step in between is governed by statute rather than by preference. Paternity cannot be assumed; it must be acknowledged before a notary or proven by a laboratory, and an acknowledgment can be undone inside a statutory rescission window. A support amount cannot be negotiated; it is produced by a guideline worksheet whose income tiers, self-support reserve and multi-family credit are fixed in law, and any departure from that number requires written findings signed by someone other than the person who drafted the order. Money withheld from a paycheck cannot exceed a federal consumer-credit ceiling, and when the obligor works in a state whose own law is stricter, that stricter law governs. This service is the system of record for all of it.

The operational problem the agency faces today is that these rules interact. A genetic exclusion does not merely close a paternity question — it vacates orders, voids charges, releases liens and restores a suspended driver's license, all in one motion. An accepted arrears compromise does not merely reduce a balance — it stays every enforcement remedy in flight and writes off interest that must be reinstated the moment an installment is missed. Two different statutory readings of "accrued interest" coexist: one that certifies arrears for enforcement, charge by charge, and one that reports the ledger to a finance officer, month-end by month-end. The same case data produces two different numbers, and using the wrong one is a defensible-looking mistake that costs the agency a contested hearing.

This document specifies the API that models that world. It is written for engineers building the service and describes the domain rules first and the HTTP surface second. Every collection is mounted under `/child-support`. The service is a closed, deterministic system: it holds a logical clock rather than reading wall time, it derives every money figure from persisted charges and payments rather than storing summary totals it cannot re-prove, and it refuses any request whose preconditions the statute does not permit — with a specific, machine-readable reason.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Caseworkers, Parties and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API — without exception, including reads — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. If either header is absent, or present but empty, or present but containing nothing except whitespace, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). Both header values are trimmed of surrounding whitespace before anything else looks at them, and the role name is then matched exactly and case-sensitively. If `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the eight roles enumerated below, the rejection is `ERR_INVALID_ROLE` (HTTP 401). If the role is well formed but that role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles are `CaseWorker`, `SupervisingAttorney`, `FinanceOfficer`, `EmployerAgent`, `CustodialParty`, `NoncustodialParty`, `InterstateLiaison` and `ProgramAdmin`. A `CaseWorker` is the front-line IV-D worker who performs intake, locate, paternity intake, order drafting, withholding administration and enforcement proposal. A `SupervisingAttorney` holds the legal authority: genetic test orders and results, deviation findings, order entry and vacatur, contest review, modification proposals and case closure. A `FinanceOfficer` owns the ledger: charge posting and voidance, non-withholding collections, accrual runs, remittance lapse declarations, compromise offers and compromise defaults. An `EmployerAgent` is a payroll contact at an employer and may only see and remit against that employer's own withholding orders. `CustodialParty` and `NoncustodialParty` are the two human parties to a case. An `InterstateLiaison` owns every UIFSA transmittal. A `ProgramAdmin` operates the employer registry and the logical clock. There is no hierarchical override anywhere in this model: a `ProgramAdmin` cannot draft an order, post a payment or decide findings, and a `SupervisingAttorney` cannot post a charge.

Three of the eight roles are backed by a persisted entity, and for those three `X-User-ID` is not an arbitrary operator string. When `X-User-Role` is `CustodialParty`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Participant`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Participant` compare the resource's case's `custodial_party_id` to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `NoncustodialParty`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Participant`, and every read and every write is gated on `X-User-ID` equalling the target case's `noncustodial_party_id`. A mismatch in either direction — a custodial caller reaching for a case it is not the custodial party of, or a noncustodial caller reaching for a case it is not the obligor on — is rejected with `ERR_NOT_CASE_PARTY` (HTTP 403). This check guards the rescission of an acknowledgment, the contest of an enforcement action, the acceptance of a compromise offer, and every party-facing read. On a direct read of a `Participant` there is no case in the path to compare against, so the rule is expressed on the participant instead: a party caller may read itself, and may read any participant that appears on a case where the caller itself holds its own party role; every other participant answers with `ERR_NOT_CASE_PARTY`.

When `X-User-Role` is `EmployerAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Employer`; posting a remittance against a withholding order whose `employer_id` differs from `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_NOT_IWO_EMPLOYER` (HTTP 403), and the same check scopes the withholding-order list so that an `EmployerAgent` caller sees only rows whose `employer_id` equals `X-User-ID`.

The remaining five roles — `CaseWorker`, `SupervisingAttorney`, `FinanceOfficer`, `InterstateLiaison` and `ProgramAdmin` — carry an opaque identity. For these, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit anchor — as `drafted_by_user_id` and `findings_approved_by_user_id` on a support order, and as `proposed_by_user_id` and `review_decided_by_user_id` on an enforcement action — but it never compares those values to any entity's `id`.

Two operations in this system are deliberately impossible for a single operator to complete alone. Deviation findings may not be decided by the same `X-User-ID` recorded in the order's `drafted_by_user_id`, and the administrative review of a contested enforcement action may not be decided by the same `X-User-ID` recorded in the action's `proposed_by_user_id`. Both violations answer with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Note that the role is not what enforces this — both the drafter and the decider may be `SupervisingAttorney` users; it is the identifier comparison that enforces it, so a second distinct operator is always required.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Three error identifiers are produced by the transport layer rather than by the domain, and so sit outside the catalogue of §5: a request whose path matches no route answers `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), a request whose method the matched path does not support answers `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 405), and an unhandled server fault answers `ERR_INTERNAL` (HTTP 500). A trailing slash is not significant in routing: `/child-support/cases` and `/child-support/cases/` reach the same handler.

### 2.3. Pagination, List Ordering and Filters

Every collection endpoint accepts two pagination parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a payload violation and answers with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422), and so does either parameter carrying a value that is not a base-ten integer; `offset` has no upper bound at all, and either parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as omitted and takes its default. Collections are returned in ascending `created_at` order — oldest row first — and that ordering is stable and unconditional; no list endpoint offers an alternative sort. Because the clock is logical rather than real, many rows routinely share one `created_at` instant, and those ties are broken by the server's monotonic creation sequence, so among rows stamped at the same instant the one created earlier in the request stream sorts earlier; the random identifier is never a tie-break. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` counts the rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window.

Because the resource graph is flat — every collection lives directly under `/child-support` and no resource nests inside another in its own URL — foreign keys are traversed by filtering rather than by walking a URL tree, and each list endpoint exposes filters that make its links reverse-reachable. Participants may be filtered by `role_type`, `residence_state_code`, `locate_status` and `enforcement_risk_tier`. Cases may be filtered by `custodial_party_id`, `noncustodial_party_id`, `case_status`, `enforcement_posture`, `interstate_role` and `tanf_assistance`. Paternity actions accept `case_id`, `status` and `method`. Support orders accept `case_id`, `status`, `order_type`, `origin_state_code` and `superseded_by_order_id`. Employers accept `state_code` and `fein`. Withholding orders accept `order_id`, `employer_id`, `service_status`, `remittance_status` and `jurisdiction_state_code`. Charges accept `case_id`, `order_id`, `status` and `charge_type`. Payments accept `case_id`, `iwo_id`, `compromise_offer_id` and `source`. Modification reviews accept `case_id`, `order_id`, `proposed_order_id`, `status` and `trigger_type`. Enforcement actions accept `case_id`, `action_type` and `status`. Interstate referrals accept `case_id`, `controlling_order_id`, `status`, `direction` and `other_state_code`. Compromise offers accept `case_id` and `status`. All filters are optional and combine conjunctively, each one an exact match against the stored or computed field of the same name; a filter supplied as an empty string is treated as omitted, and a query parameter the endpoint does not recognise is ignored rather than refused. The one filter over a boolean field, `tanf_assistance`, accepts `true`, `1` or `yes` for true and `false`, `0` or `no` for false, in any letter case, and answers `ERR_VALIDATION` for any other spelling. The ledger read is the one computed read that accepts a temporal parameter rather than filters: an optional `as_of_date` fixes the date at which interest is measured, and when it is omitted the current logical clock date is used.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Stamps and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` — a UUID with no type prefix of any kind — and a `created_at` timestamp rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional-second digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same format, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Two resources are exceptions and carry only `id` and `created_at`: an `ObligationCharge` and a `PaymentTransaction` are immutable financial events. Neither has an `updated_at` field and neither has a `version` field, and neither exposes an update endpoint. Voiding a charge is the one operation that changes a charge's `status` after creation, and because the charge itself carries no version, that operation bumps the owning case's `version` instead.

Clients never supply `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` or `version` in a request body; a body that attempts to set them has those keys ignored — silently stripped, never an error. A request body that is absent, empty, or not well-formed JSON is read as the empty object, so an endpoint that requires no field at all accepts it and an endpoint that requires one answers `ERR_VALIDATION` for the missing field; a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object — an array, a string, a number — is itself refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. A field the schema treats as optional and that arrives explicitly as `null` is indistinguishable from an omitted field and takes that field's default, and a required field that arrives as `null` is treated as missing. Path variables are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the error. `{participant_id}` must resolve to a `Participant` or the answer is `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`; `{case_id}` to a `SupportCase` or `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`; `{paternity_action_id}` to a `PaternityAction` or `ERR_PATERNITY_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`; `{order_id}` to a `SupportOrder` or `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`; `{employer_id}` to an `Employer` or `ERR_EMPLOYER_NOT_FOUND`; `{iwo_id}` to an `IncomeWithholdingOrder` or `ERR_IWO_NOT_FOUND`; `{charge_id}` to an `ObligationCharge` or `ERR_CHARGE_NOT_FOUND`; `{payment_id}` to a `PaymentTransaction` or `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND`; `{modification_review_id}` to a `ModificationReview` or `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`; `{enforcement_action_id}` to an `EnforcementAction` or `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`; `{interstate_referral_id}` to an `InterstateReferral` or `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`; and `{compromise_offer_id}` to a `CompromiseOffer` or `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`. All twelve of these are HTTP 404. Foreign keys supplied in a request body rather than in the path — `custodial_party_id`, `noncustodial_party_id`, `employer_id`, `iwo_id`, `compromise_offer_id`, `signed_by_participant_id` and `participant_id` — are resolved after payload schema validation, and a body foreign key that does not resolve answers with `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

Time in this service is a logical clock, never wall time. The clock starts at the fixed instant 2026-08-01T00:00:00Z and must not fall back to real wall-clock time before the first clock-setting call. A `ProgramAdmin` may advance or set it by supplying a `to_iso8601` value; the response body carries no data. Setting the clock is not a purely administrative act: every `PaternityAction` sitting in `AOP_SIGNED` whose `rescission_deadline_at` falls strictly before the new clock value transitions to `ESTABLISHED` with `established_method` recorded as `VOLUNTARY_ACKNOWLEDGMENT` and `established_at` stamped with the new clock value, and each such action's `SupportCase` moves `case_status` to `ORDER_ESTABLISHMENT` — whatever that case's `case_status` was before, unless it is `CLOSED`, in which case the action still establishes but the closed case is left exactly as it stands. A clock value exactly equal to a `rescission_deadline_at` does not establish that action — the comparison is strict. No other entity is mutated by a clock set; deadlines are evaluated, never back-dated.

### 2.5. Money, Precision and Rounding

All monetary values in this system are integers denominated in cents, and every field carrying money ends in `_cents`. No monetary field is ever a decimal or a string. Percentages and ratios are the only non-integer numbers: `statutory_cap_pct`, `federal_cap_pct`, `ccpa_cap_pct`, `waiver_tier_pct`, `guideline_ncp_share_ratio` and `genetic_probability_pct`.

Rounding is not uniform, and the choice is deliberate in each place. The default across the guideline worksheet, the interest engine, the waiver computation, the mandatory-deduction derivation and the arrears-recovery derivation is round half up to the nearest whole cent — a value ending in exactly half a cent rounds away from zero. Two families of computation depart from that default. Any figure that acts as a *ceiling* is floored, never rounded up, because a statutory cap must never be exceeded by a rounding artefact: the CCPA cap amount and the aggregate withholding cap are both floored. And the installment split of a compromise settlement floors the recurring installment and pushes the entire remainder onto the final installment, so that the recurring amount multiplied by one fewer than the installment count, plus the final installment, equals the settlement total exactly. The guideline share ratio is the one intermediate kept as a decimal: it is rounded half up to four decimal places before it is used. Component sub-scores inside the risk model are floored to whole integers before weighting, and the weighted average is rounded half up once at the end. The payment waterfall performs no rounding at all, because every bucket takes the smaller of the money remaining and the balance in front of it, so the sum of all applied buckets plus unapplied credit always equals the payment amount exactly.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Orders and Cases

Two write operations in this system are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because in both cases a legitimate second write exists — so no state precondition can protect the client — while the figures being computed are read from a parent row that other writers move.

Issuing an income withholding order, `POST /child-support/orders/{order_id}/withholding-orders`, requires the non-optional field `expected_order_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `SupportOrder`. If that value does not equal the order's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A second withholding order against the same support order is legitimate product behaviour — an obligor may be withheld at two employers simultaneously — so the write cannot be refused on state grounds. But the withholding amount is derived from the order's current `monthly_obligation_cents` and from the aggregate cap already consumed by that order's existing withholding orders, both of which move when the order is written; a client holding a stale order version would compute its withholding against a superseded obligation, so the stale version is refused.

Creating a compromise offer, `POST /child-support/cases/{case_id}/compromise-offers`, requires the non-optional field `expected_case_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `SupportCase`. If that value does not equal the case's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Creating a further offer is likewise legitimate — the newer offer supersedes any `PROPOSED` predecessor — so no state precondition covers the write. But the offer's `principal_due_snapshot_cents`, its `interest_due_snapshot_cents` and its waiver tier are all frozen from the case at creation time, so an offer built on a stale view of the case would waive interest that has already been collected or already written off.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field, and no other endpoint may reject a write on version grounds.

### 2.7. Two Statutory Measures of Accrued Interest

Statutory interest on unpaid child support runs at an annual rate of 6 percent — 0.06 — applied as a monthly rate of one half of one percent, the annual rate divided by twelve, which is exactly 0.005. There is no grace period: the number of grace days before interest begins is zero.

The input set for interest is the same for both measures. Only `ObligationCharge` rows belonging to the case whose `status` is `OPEN` and whose `due_date` falls on or before the as-of date participate. Charges in `VOID` and charges in `SATISFIED` are excluded entirely and retroactively — once a charge is satisfied it contributes nothing to either measure, including for month-end snapshots that predate the payment that satisfied it. A charge stops accruing interest from the received-at instant of the payment that satisfies it onward.

What differs is *what* is multiplied by elapsed time, and this produces two different numbers from identical data.

The **per-charge statutory measure** walks the input set ordered by `due_date` and then by `created_at`, and for each charge computes the number of whole calendar months between that charge's own `due_date` and the as-of date — that count is zero outright when the as-of date is not later than the `due_date`, and is otherwise the difference between the two dates measured in calendar months, reduced by one when the as-of day-of-month is earlier than the `due_date` day-of-month and floored at zero — multiplies the charge's `unpaid_balance_cents` by the monthly rate of 0.005 and by that month count, rounds the product half up to whole cents, and adds it to a running total. A charge that has been outstanding for less than one whole calendar month contributes exactly zero. Rounding happens once per charge.

The **aggregate month-end measure** instead walks every calendar month-end date falling strictly before the as-of date. At each such snapshot it sums the `unpaid_balance_cents` of every charge in the input set whose `due_date` falls on or before that snapshot date, multiplies that pooled balance by the monthly rate of 0.005, rounds the product half up to whole cents, and adds it to a running total. Rounding happens once per snapshot rather than once per charge, and a charge whose `due_date` falls anywhere before a month end is included in that month's snapshot even though it has not been outstanding for a whole month. The two totals therefore diverge on identical data, and the divergence grows with the number of charges.

Which measure applies is fixed by purpose, never by caller choice. Every *certified* figure uses the per-charge statutory measure: an enforcement action's `arrears_certified_snapshot_cents`, a participant's `total_arrears_certified_cents`, each charge's own `interest_accrued_cents`, and the enforcement eligibility screen. Every *ledger and settlement* figure uses the aggregate month-end measure: the case ledger read, the case's `interest_receivable_cents`, and a compromise offer's `interest_due_snapshot_cents`. A participant's `total_arrears_certified_cents` is the sum, over every `SupportCase` whose `noncustodial_party_id` is that participant and whose `case_status` is anything other than `CLOSED`, of that case's `arrears_principal_cents` plus that case's per-charge statutory interest total.

Three case-level counters track what has happened to interest over time, and they are not derived from the accrual engine. `interest_collected_cents` is the running sum of the interest bucket of every payment posted on the case. `interest_written_off_cents` is the interest currently waived by a compromise offer in `ACCEPTED`, `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETED`, and it is reversed when an offer is declared in default. `interest_receivable_cents` is a computed field: the aggregate month-end interest total, less `interest_written_off_cents`, less `interest_collected_cents`, floored at zero so it never goes negative.

### 2.8. Parallel Status Dimensions and Cascade Discipline

Two entities in this system carry more than one independent status dimension, and the dimensions advance separately. A `SupportCase` carries three: `case_status` tracks the establishment lifecycle through `OPENED`, `LOCATE`, `PATERNITY_PENDING`, `ORDER_ESTABLISHMENT`, `ENFORCEMENT` and the terminal `CLOSED`; `enforcement_posture` tracks escalation through `NORMAL`, `ESCALATED` and `STAYED` and has no terminal value; and `interstate_role` tracks the state's UIFSA posture through `NONE`, `INITIATING` and `RESPONDING`, likewise with no terminal value. An `IncomeWithholdingOrder` carries two: `service_status` moves through `ISSUED`, `SERVED`, `AMENDED` and the terminal `TERMINATED`, while `remittance_status` moves through `NOT_DUE`, `CURRENT`, `DELINQUENT` and the terminal `DEFAULTED`. A transition on one dimension never implies a transition on another unless this document says it does — amending a withholding order, for instance, leaves `remittance_status` completely untouched.

Cascades run to whatever depth the domain requires, and every level of a cascade is part of the operation, not a background job. The deepest cascade in the system is genetic exclusion: the paternity action becomes `EXCLUDED`, which closes the case, which vacates the case's orders, which voids those orders' open charges and releases the case's enforcement actions, which in turn clears the obligor's participant-level remedy flags and recomputes `open_case_count` for both parties. Because the whole chain is one request, the response of that request already reflects the settled end state.

Whenever a cascade mutates a row that carries a `version`, that row's `version` increases by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed — including rows the caller never named. A single request can therefore bump the versions of an order, its case, several withholding orders, an employer and a participant. This matters directly: a client that read a case, then triggered any operation that touched it, holds a stale `version` and will be refused on the next concurrency-guarded write.

### 2.9. Order of Precedence Among Validations

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the first failing check answers.

Authentication and identity come first, in this internal order: header presence (`ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`), role recognition (`ERR_INVALID_ROLE`), role authorisation for the endpoint (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`), then party ownership for `CustodialParty` and `NoncustodialParty` callers (`ERR_NOT_CASE_PARTY`), then employer ownership for `EmployerAgent` callers (`ERR_NOT_IWO_EMPLOYER`). Next comes path-variable resolution, in left-to-right path order, yielding the appropriate not-found error. Next comes payload schema validation (`ERR_VALIDATION`): required fields present, enum values inside their declared sets, integer money values non-negative, `child_count` between 1 and 6 inclusive, `installment_count` between 3 and 36 inclusive, `genetic_probability_pct` between 0 and 100 inclusive, and ISO dates well formed. Types are checked rather than coerced: a field declared as an integer accepts a JSON integer, and accepts a JSON floating-point number only when it carries no fractional part, but never accepts a boolean and never accepts a numeric string; a field declared as a string rejects every non-string, and a required string may not be empty. A calendar-date field accepts a `YYYY-MM-DD` value and also accepts a longer ISO 8601 spelling of which only the leading ten characters are kept, so an instant supplied where a date belongs is read as its date part. An instant field accepts a `Z`-suffixed or offset-bearing ISO 8601 timestamp and normalises it to UTC, and also accepts a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` date, read as midnight UTC on that day. Next comes body foreign-key resolution (`ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`). Next comes the optimistic-concurrency check on the two guarded writes (`ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION`) — note that this sits *above* every state check, so a request that is both stale and state-invalid answers with the stale-version error.

Only then are state preconditions evaluated: `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATUS`, `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATUS`, `ERR_INVALID_PATERNITY_STATUS`, `ERR_INVALID_IWO_STATUS`, `ERR_INVALID_REVIEW_STATUS`, `ERR_INVALID_ACTION_STATUS`, `ERR_INVALID_REFERRAL_STATUS` and `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATUS`, each at HTTP 409. Below the state band sit the safety and compliance checks in this order: good-cause confidentiality (`ERR_GOOD_CAUSE_BARS_DISCLOSURE`), paternity settlement before order work (`ERR_PATERNITY_NOT_ESTABLISHED`), and segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`). Below those come the referential checks in this order: party role agreement (`ERR_PARTY_ROLE_MISMATCH`), assistance-flag agreement (`ERR_TANF_FLAG_MISMATCH`), obligor whereabouts (`ERR_NCP_NOT_LOCATED`), and continuing exclusive jurisdiction (`ERR_NO_CONTROLLING_ORDER_JURISDICTION`).

Below the referential band come the temporal checks, in this order: `ERR_RESCISSION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_COLLECTION_APPOINTMENT_NOT_REACHED`, `ERR_CONTEST_WINDOW_OPEN`, `ERR_CONTEST_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_ORDER_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`, `ERR_CHARGE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_YET_ELIGIBLE`, `ERR_MODIFICATION_EFFECTIVE_DATE_NOT_LATER`, `ERR_REMITTANCE_NOT_YET_DUE` and `ERR_INSTALLMENT_NOT_YET_LATE`. Then the counting and uniqueness constraints, in this order: `ERR_LOCATE_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`, `ERR_RESCISSION_LIMIT`, `ERR_SPECIMEN_REJECTION_LIMIT`, `ERR_DEVIATION_REJECTION_LIMIT`, `ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_PATERNITY_ACTION`, `ERR_DEVIATION_BELOW_MIN_PCT`, `ERR_ACTIVE_OFFER_EXISTS`, `ERR_OFFER_SUPERSEDED`, `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_STAYED`, `ERR_ASSIGNED_ARREARS_NOT_COMPROMISABLE` and `ERR_COMPROMISE_RISK_TOO_HIGH`. Last of all come the fiscal checks, in this order: `ERR_AGGREGATE_WITHHOLDING_EXCEEDS_CAP`, `ERR_WITHHOLDING_CAP_EXHAUSTED`, `ERR_ARREARS_BELOW_ENFORCEMENT_THRESHOLD`, `ERR_ARREARS_BELOW_COMPROMISE_MINIMUM`, `ERR_ARREARS_OUTSTANDING`, `ERR_CHARGE_PARTIALLY_PAID`, `ERR_IWO_DEFAULTED`, `ERR_PAYMENT_BELOW_INSTALLMENT` and `ERR_OFFER_SNAPSHOT_STALE`.

Three refinements to that ordering are worth stating explicitly, because each one moves an error that the general rule would otherwise select. First, when a support order is drafted on a case, the case-status check rejects only a `CLOSED` case; the requirement that the case has actually reached order establishment is owned by the paternity-settlement check, so a case that has not yet reached `ORDER_ESTABLISHMENT` answers with `ERR_PATERNITY_NOT_ESTABLISHED` and never with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATUS`. Second, when a rescission is attempted against a paternity action already in `ESTABLISHED` whose `established_method` is `VOLUNTARY_ACKNOWLEDGMENT`, the answer is `ERR_RESCISSION_WINDOW_CLOSED` rather than `ERR_INVALID_PATERNITY_STATUS`, because the elapsed sixty-day deadline is the specific reason the request fails; every other disallowed origin state on that operation — `PENDING`, `GENETIC_ORDERED`, `GENETIC_COLLECTED`, `AOP_RESCINDED`, `EXCLUDED`, `NOT_REQUIRED`, or an `ESTABLISHED` action whose `established_method` is `GENETIC_TEST` — still answers with `ERR_INVALID_PATERNITY_STATUS`. Third, when an acceptance is attempted against a compromise offer already in `SUPERSEDED`, the answer is `ERR_OFFER_SUPERSEDED` rather than `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATUS`, because displacement by a newer offer is the specific reason; `COMPLETED`, `DEFAULTED`, `ACCEPTED` and `ACTIVE` origin states on acceptance, and every disallowed origin state on a default declaration, still answer with `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATUS`.

Finally, a `CLOSED` case rejects every write — every write directed at the case itself and every write directed at a child of the case — with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATUS`.

### 2.10. Jurisdictions, Assignment and Good-Cause Confidentiality

Seven state codes are recognised anywhere a state appears in this system: `MN`, `TX`, `NY`, `WI`, `FL`, `CA` and `PA`. `MN` is this agency's home state — the state whose guidelines the worksheet implements and whose continuing exclusive jurisdiction gates modification authority. A `Participant`'s `residence_state_code`, an `Employer`'s `state_code`, a `SupportOrder`'s `origin_state_code` and an `InterstateReferral`'s `child_home_state_code` all draw from that same set of seven. A referral's `other_state_code` — the sister state on the transmittal — draws from the same set minus the home state, so it is one of `TX`, `NY`, `WI`, `FL`, `CA` or `PA`.

A case enters the IV-D program one of two ways, and the way it enters determines who owns its arrears. A `case_type` of `PA_REFERRAL` means the custodial household receives public assistance, which assigns the arrears to the state; `tanf_assistance` must be true on such a case. A `case_type` of `DIRECT_APPLICATION` means the family applied directly and retains its own arrears; `tanf_assistance` must be false. Any other combination is refused with `ERR_TANF_FLAG_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Assignment is what splits the arrears figure into two computed components: `assigned_arrears_cents` equals `arrears_principal_cents` when `tanf_assistance` is true and equals zero otherwise, and `unassigned_arrears_cents` is `arrears_principal_cents` minus `assigned_arrears_cents`. Assignment also caps what reaches the family from a collection, and it makes the arrears non-compromisable.

A family-violence good-cause claim, recorded as `good_cause_claim` on the case and defaulting to false, bars the agency from disclosing party information to anyone outside it. Three operations disclose: referring the obligor to locate services, sending a pre-deprivation enforcement notice, and transmitting an interstate request. All three are refused on a case whose `good_cause_claim` is true, with `ERR_GOOD_CAUSE_BARS_DISCLOSURE` (HTTP 409). The claim does not stop the agency from working the case internally — as described later, it is precisely what allows paternity work to begin without a locate step first.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Intake: Registering the Parties, Opening the Case, and Finding the Obligor

Before a case can exist, both people in it must exist. A `CaseWorker` registers each person as a `Participant`, declaring a `role_type` of either `CUSTODIAL_PARTY` or `NONCUSTODIAL_PARTY`, a `full_name` between 1 and 120 characters, an `ssn_last4` of exactly four digits used for locate matching, and a `residence_state_code`. Four further attributes are optional at registration. A self-reported `employer_name` may be supplied and defaults to absent; its presence is what makes a wage-registry locate attempt succeed. A `monthly_gross_income_cents` figure may be supplied and defaults to absent, and when present it must not be negative; absent means the income is unknown. The provenance of that figure is `income_verification_source`, one of `VERIFIED_WAGE_RECORD`, `SELF_REPORTED` or `NONE`, defaulting to `NONE`. A boolean `supports_second_family` records whether the obligor supports a second family in the same household and defaults to false; it is an input to both the guideline credit and the federal withholding tier. A boolean `has_valid_drivers_license` records whether the person holds an unsuspended state licence and defaults to true.

Everything else on a participant is the server's to maintain. `locate_status` starts at `NOT_NEEDED` and may become `PENDING`, `LOCATED` or `UNLOCATABLE`. `locate_attempt_count` starts at zero. Three remedy flags — `license_suspension_active`, `lien_recorded` and `credit_bureau_reported` — all start false and are true only while an `ACTIVE` enforcement remedy of the matching kind names the participant. Four computed figures round out the row: `enforcement_risk_score` with its band `enforcement_risk_tier`, `compromise_risk_score` with its band `compromise_risk_tier`, the cross-case `total_arrears_certified_cents`, and `open_case_count`, the number of `SupportCase` rows referencing this participant in either party field whose `case_status` is not `CLOSED`. Reading a participant is a computing read: both risk variants, `total_arrears_certified_cents` and `open_case_count` are all recomputed from current case data before the row is returned. For a participant whose `role_type` is `CUSTODIAL_PARTY`, both risk scores are always zero and both tiers are always `LOW`.

Income arrives late and changes often, so recording a new income figure is its own operation. A `CaseWorker` supplies a fresh `monthly_gross_income_cents` together with the `income_verification_source` that justifies it, and optionally a revised `employer_name`; both money and provenance are required on this operation, and an `employer_name` that is omitted or sent as null leaves whatever was already stored exactly as it was rather than clearing it. The participant's `version` increases by 1 and both risk variants are recomputed. What this operation deliberately does *not* do is disturb history: no order already in `ENTERED` is recalculated, and no charge already posted is adjusted. Only guideline calculations run after the update see the new figure. Neither this operation nor the locate result described next is a write against a case, so neither one is refused merely because a case naming that participant has closed.

Locate services are recorded in two places, and it is worth being clear about which is which. The *referral* is an act on the case, described below. The *result* is an act on the participant: a `CaseWorker` records a `locate_outcome` of either `LOCATED` or `UNLOCATABLE`, optionally attaching the `employer_name` the locate turned up, which writes that outcome into `locate_status` and increases the participant's `version` by 1. An attached `employer_name` overwrites the one on the record; a request that omits it — or sends it as null — leaves whatever `employer_name` was already stored exactly as it was, including leaving it absent when nothing was stored. Recording a result has no precondition of any kind: it may be recorded against either party regardless of `role_type`, from any current `locate_status` including a participant already `UNLOCATABLE` — so a later `LOCATED` result simply overwrites that terminal-looking value — it does not require that any locate referral was ever made, and it never changes `locate_attempt_count`, which only the referral moves. An `UNLOCATABLE` outcome is what makes every case of that participant eligible for closure on jurisdictional grounds.

Opening a case is a `CaseWorker` act. The payload names a `case_type`, the `custodial_party_id` and `noncustodial_party_id` of the two participants, a `child_count` between 1 and 6, the `tanf_assistance` flag that must agree with the case type, and optionally `good_cause_claim`. The two named participants must carry the matching roles — the custodial id must point at a `CUSTODIAL_PARTY` participant and the noncustodial id at a `NONCUSTODIAL_PARTY` participant — and the two ids must differ; any violation is `ERR_PARTY_ROLE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). A newly opened case sits at `case_status` `OPENED`, `enforcement_posture` `NORMAL` and `interstate_role` `NONE`, with `locate_referral_count` at zero, every money field at zero, and `closed_reason` and `closed_at` both absent. Because `open_case_count` is derived on every read of a participant rather than stored, opening a case writes nothing at all to either party's row: neither participant's `updated_at` or `version` moves, and the new count is simply visible on the next read of each of them. Reading a case is likewise a computing read: `arrears_principal_cents`, `assigned_arrears_cents`, `unassigned_arrears_cents` and `interest_receivable_cents` are all recomputed before the row is returned, with `arrears_principal_cents` defined as the sum of `unpaid_balance_cents` across the case's charges whose `status` is `OPEN` and whose `due_date` falls on or before the current logical clock date.

Referring the obligor to locate services drives `case_status` from `OPENED` to `LOCATE`, and a repeat referral is a self-transition from `LOCATE` back to `LOCATE`. The `CaseWorker` names a `locate_source`, one of `FEDERAL_PLS`, `STATE_DIRECTORY_OF_NEW_HIRES`, `CREDIT_BUREAU` or `EMPLOYER_TIP`. The first referral requires that the obligor's `locate_status` is `NOT_NEEDED` or `PENDING`; a repeat referral requires that it is already `PENDING` and that `locate_attempt_count` is still below three. Each referral increases the case's `locate_referral_count` by 1 and the case's `version` by 1, sets the obligor's `locate_status` to `PENDING` and increases the obligor's `locate_attempt_count` by 1. The referral whose increment brings `locate_attempt_count` up to three additionally sets that participant's `locate_status` to `UNLOCATABLE` — the agency has exhausted its statutory attempts. A fourth referral is refused with `ERR_LOCATE_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409). `OPENED` and `LOCATE` are the only two origin states the operation accepts; from `PATERNITY_PENDING`, `ORDER_ESTABLISHMENT`, `ENFORCEMENT` or `CLOSED` it answers `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATUS` (HTTP 409). A referral on a case whose `good_cause_claim` is true is refused with `ERR_GOOD_CAUSE_BARS_DISCLOSURE`, and because the confidentiality check sits below the state band, a good-cause case whose `case_status` has already moved past `LOCATE` answers the status error instead.

The obligor's risk profile is recomputed from scratch on every read of a `NONCUSTODIAL_PARTY` participant, and it is worth setting out here because two later decisions turn on it. Six component sub-scores are computed, each normalised to a whole number between 0 and 100 by flooring. The input universe is every `SupportCase` whose `noncustodial_party_id` is this participant and whose `case_status` is not `CLOSED`, together with those cases' charges, payments, withholding orders and enforcement actions. Let the *reference obligation* be the `monthly_obligation_cents` of the most recently created `ENTERED` order of type `CURRENT_SUPPORT` across those cases, or zero when there is none. The **arrears component** is the smaller of 100 and the floor of one hundred times the summed `arrears_principal_cents` of those cases divided by the greater of 1 and twelve times the reference obligation. The **delinquency component** is taken from the worst `remittance_status` among the participant's withholding orders whose `service_status` is not `TERMINATED`: `NOT_DUE` and `CURRENT` both score 0, `DELINQUENT` scores 60, `DEFAULTED` scores 100, and a participant with no withholding order at all scores 0. The **payment-history component** is 100 minus the floor of one hundred times the summed `total_collected_principal_cents` divided by the greater of 1 and the summed `total_charged_principal_cents`, and it is 0 outright when nothing has been charged; the result is then clamped into the 0-to-100 band, so a case that has collected more than it charged scores 0 rather than a negative number. The **locate component** reads `locate_status`: `NOT_NEEDED` and `LOCATED` both score 0, `PENDING` scores 50, `UNLOCATABLE` scores 100. The **enforcement-history component** is the smaller of 100 and twenty-five times the count of enforcement actions on those cases whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `RELEASED`. The **interstate component** is 100 when any of those cases has an `interstate_role` other than `NONE`, and 0 otherwise.

Those six components are then averaged under two different weightings, producing two independent scores from one set of components. The score is the sum of each weight multiplied by its component, divided by one hundred, rounded half up to a whole number; in each weighting the six weights sum to exactly 100. The **enforcement screen**, written to `enforcement_risk_score` and `enforcement_risk_tier`, weights arrears at 40, delinquency at 25, payment history at 20, locate at 10 and interstate exposure at 5, and weights enforcement history at 0 — past remedies are not evidence for a new remedy. The **compromise screen**, written to `compromise_risk_score` and `compromise_risk_tier`, weights payment history at 40, arrears at 25, delinquency at 20 and enforcement history at 15, and weights locate status and interstate exposure at 0. Both scores band identically: a score of at least 67 is `HIGH`, a score of at least 34 but below 67 is `MEDIUM`, and anything below 34 is `LOW`. Because the weightings differ, the same underlying data routinely lands the two screens in different tiers, and each tier gates a different decision.

### 3.2. Settling the Question of Parentage

A case carries at most one paternity action, ever. A `CaseWorker` opens it by naming a `method` — `VOLUNTARY_ACKNOWLEDGMENT`, `GENETIC_TEST` or `PRESUMED_MARITAL` — against a case, and a second attempt on a case that already has one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PATERNITY_ACTION` (HTTP 409). This one uniqueness check is evaluated ahead of the case-status band rather than below it, so a case that already carries an action answers the duplicate error whatever its `case_status` has since become; the sole exception is a `CLOSED` case, whose blanket refusal of every write still answers `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATUS` first. The action's `case_id` is taken from the case it is opened on and is what makes the action reverse-reachable from its case afterwards. Which `method` is chosen determines both the action's starting state and the case's next `case_status`.

The marital presumption is a short circuit. A `method` of `PRESUMED_MARITAL` creates the row directly in the terminal state `NOT_REQUIRED` — it is never observable in `PENDING` — and moves the case straight from `OPENED` to `ORDER_ESTABLISHMENT`, skipping `PATERNITY_PENDING` entirely. The other two methods create the row in `PENDING` and move the case to `PATERNITY_PENDING`. Both paths increase the case's `version` by 1. Whichever method is named, the rest of the row is created uniform: `notary_verified` false, `rescission_count` and `specimen_rejection_count` at zero, `genetic_cost_cents` at zero, `genetic_cost_liable_party` at `NONE`, and `signed_by_participant_id`, `aop_signed_at`, `rescission_deadline_at`, `genetic_test_ordered_at`, `collection_appointment_at`, `specimen_collected_at`, `chain_of_custody_ok`, `genetic_probability_pct`, `established_at` and `established_method` all absent — including on the `PRESUMED_MARITAL` row, which reaches its terminal `NOT_REQUIRED` state with `established_at` and `established_method` still absent.

Reaching `PATERNITY_PENDING` normally requires that the agency knows where the obligor is: the transition from `LOCATE` to `PATERNITY_PENDING` requires the obligor's `locate_status` to be `LOCATED`. There are two deliberate bypasses. On a case whose `good_cause_claim` is true, locate is barred outright, so paternity work may begin directly from `OPENED` without the case ever passing through `LOCATE`. The marital presumption is the second: a `method` of `PRESUMED_MARITAL` is never gated on the obligor being `LOCATED` either, whatever the good-cause flag says, because the presumption arises from the marriage rather than from any locate work — for that method, and for any case carrying a good-cause claim, the only `locate_status` that refuses the request is `UNLOCATABLE`. Outside those two bypasses, an obligor who is not `LOCATED` blocks both the opening of a paternity action and the drafting of a support order with `ERR_NCP_NOT_LOCATED` (HTTP 409), and an obligor whose `locate_status` is `UNLOCATABLE` blocks every establishment step regardless of the good-cause flag. As with the drafting of a support order, two bands divide that work: on the acknowledgment and genetic tracks the case-status check accepts a case in either `OPENED` or `LOCATE`, while the requirement that the obligor actually be `LOCATED` is owned by the locate check — so an `OPENED` case that carries no good-cause claim and whose obligor is not yet `LOCATED` answers with `ERR_NCP_NOT_LOCATED` and never with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATUS`. The `PRESUMED_MARITAL` short circuit is the narrower one: it accepts only an `OPENED` case.

On the acknowledgment track, a `CaseWorker` records the executed acknowledgment of parentage by supplying `notary_verified`, which must be true, and `signed_by_participant_id`, which must equal the case's `noncustodial_party_id`. Both of those are payload-shape requirements rather than referential ones: a `notary_verified` of false, and a `signed_by_participant_id` that resolves to a real participant who is simply not this case's obligor, each answer `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422) and never `ERR_PARTY_ROLE_MISMATCH`, and because they sit in the payload band they are decided before any state or case-status check — while an identifier that resolves to no participant at all still answers `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`. This drives `PENDING → AOP_SIGNED`, stamps `aop_signed_at` with the current logical clock, computes `rescission_deadline_at` as that instant plus a sixty-day rescission window, records `notary_verified` as true, and increases the action's `version` by 1. The `PENDING` origin additionally requires that the action's `method` is `VOLUNTARY_ACKNOWLEDGMENT`.

The obligor may take the signature back, but only in person and only inside the window. A `NoncustodialParty` caller drives `AOP_SIGNED → AOP_RESCINDED`, which increases `rescission_count` by 1 and clears both `aop_signed_at` and `rescission_deadline_at` back to absent. Two guards apply. The request must arrive at or before `rescission_deadline_at` — the boundary is inclusive, so a rescission landing exactly on the deadline instant succeeds and one landing strictly after it is refused with `ERR_RESCISSION_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). And `rescission_count` must still be below one, because the statute permits exactly one rescission per acknowledgment; a second attempt is refused with `ERR_RESCISSION_LIMIT` (HTTP 409).

A rescinded acknowledgment is not a dead end. The same acknowledgment operation re-executes from `AOP_RESCINDED` back to `AOP_SIGNED` — still requiring `notary_verified` to be true — and a completely fresh sixty-day window opens from the new `aop_signed_at`. What cannot happen twice is the rescission itself: on the second pass through `AOP_SIGNED`, `rescission_count` already equals the statutory limit of one, so the cycle `AOP_SIGNED → AOP_RESCINDED → AOP_SIGNED` closes permanently.

An acknowledgment that is never rescinded ripens by itself. When the logical clock is advanced to a value strictly after an action's `rescission_deadline_at`, that action moves `AOP_SIGNED → ESTABLISHED`, `established_at` is stamped with the new clock value, `established_method` is recorded as `VOLUNTARY_ACKNOWLEDGMENT`, and the case moves `PATERNITY_PENDING → ORDER_ESTABLISHMENT`. This is the only establishment in the system driven by the passage of time rather than by an operator, and the comparison is strict: a clock value exactly equal to the deadline leaves the action in `AOP_SIGNED`.

The genetic track is opened by a `SupervisingAttorney`, who supplies a `collection_appointment_at` instant that must be strictly later than the current logical clock; an appointment scheduled at or before the clock is refused with `ERR_COLLECTION_APPOINTMENT_NOT_REACHED` (HTTP 409). The transition runs `PENDING → GENETIC_ORDERED` when the action's `method` is already `GENETIC_TEST`, and also runs `AOP_RESCINDED → GENETIC_ORDERED`, in which case the action's `method` is rewritten to `GENETIC_TEST` — a rescinded acknowledgment converts the case onto the genetic track. Either way `genetic_test_ordered_at` is stamped with the current clock and `genetic_cost_cents` is stamped at 48,000 cents, which is 480 dollars; the caller never supplies that amount. `genetic_cost_liable_party` remains `NONE` until the result is in.

Specimen collection is recorded by a `CaseWorker` and may only happen once the clock has reached `collection_appointment_at` — at or after that instant; earlier is again `ERR_COLLECTION_APPOINTMENT_NOT_REACHED`. The `chain_of_custody_ok` flag in the payload decides the outcome. A true value drives `GENETIC_ORDERED → GENETIC_COLLECTED`, stamps `specimen_collected_at` with the current clock, and records `chain_of_custody_ok` as true. A false value keeps the action in `GENETIC_ORDERED` as a self-transition, increases `specimen_rejection_count` by 1, records `chain_of_custody_ok` as false, and leaves `specimen_collected_at` absent. A rejected specimen may be re-drawn only while `specimen_rejection_count` is still below two; the third rejected specimen is refused with `ERR_SPECIMEN_REJECTION_LIMIT` (HTTP 409). The limit counts rejections only, so it never blocks a good draw: a `chain_of_custody_ok` of true is accepted at any `specimen_rejection_count`, including two, and the count is left where it stands. Either outcome increases the action's `version` by 1.

The laboratory result closes the question, and a `SupervisingAttorney` records it by supplying `genetic_probability_pct` — a JSON number between 0 and 100 inclusive, typically carried to two decimal places, which the server stores and echoes back exactly as supplied without rounding it to any fixed number of places, and which is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` when it arrives as a boolean or as a numeric string. The action must have `chain_of_custody_ok` true, and an action that does not answers `ERR_INVALID_PATERNITY_STATUS`. A probability of at least 99.0 drives `GENETIC_COLLECTED → ESTABLISHED`, stamps `established_at` with the current clock, records `established_method` as `GENETIC_TEST`, sets `genetic_cost_liable_party` to `NCP` so that the test cost becomes collectable from the obligor, and moves the case `PATERNITY_PENDING → ORDER_ESTABLISHMENT`.

A probability below 99.0 drives `GENETIC_COLLECTED → EXCLUDED` and sets `genetic_cost_liable_party` to `STATE`, meaning the test cost is never billed to the obligor. Exclusion then runs the deepest cascade in the system, and every level of it is part of the same request. At the first level the case moves to `CLOSED` with `closed_reason` `NO_JURISDICTION` and `closed_at` stamped with the current clock — this is also the transition `PATERNITY_PENDING → CLOSED`. At the second level every support order on the case that is not already in a terminal state becomes `VACATED`. At the third level every `OPEN` charge belonging to those orders becomes `VOID`, and every enforcement action on the case that is not already terminal becomes `RELEASED` with `release_reason` `ORDER_VACATED`. At the fourth level the obligor's `license_suspension_active`, `lien_recorded` and `credit_bureau_reported` flags are all cleared, `has_valid_drivers_license` is restored to true, and `open_case_count` is recomputed for both parties. Both the action's `version` and the case's `version` increase by 1.

`ESTABLISHED`, `EXCLUDED` and `NOT_REQUIRED` are terminal: no write of any kind is accepted against a paternity action in any of them, and an attempt answers with `ERR_INVALID_PATERNITY_STATUS` (HTTP 409) — with the single exception, described in the precedence rules above, of a rescission attempted against a voluntarily-established action, which answers with the window error instead.

### 3.3. The Guideline Worksheet, Deviation Findings, and Entry of the Order

Once the paternity question is settled — the case's paternity action must be in `ESTABLISHED` or `NOT_REQUIRED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PATERNITY_NOT_ESTABLISHED` (HTTP 409) — a `CaseWorker` drafts a support order on the case. The payload names an `order_type` of either `CURRENT_SUPPORT` or `MEDICAL_SUPPORT`, an `origin_state_code` which defaults to `MN` and which carries the other state's code when a foreign order is being registered here under UIFSA, and an `effective_date` as an ISO calendar date in year-month-day form, which is the date the obligation starts and which may fall before the current clock date because retroactive support is permitted. Two money inputs are optional: `medical_support_monthly_cents`, the monthly cash medical add-on, defaults to zero and must not be negative; and `requested_monthly_cents`, the amount the caseworker asks the order to carry, defaults to absent and must not be negative when present. `requested_monthly_cents` is recorded on the order for the file and is never an input to anything: the worksheet ignores it, entry always takes the guideline figure, and a caseworker who wants the order to carry a different number must go through the deviation path.

The order is created in `DRAFT` with every guideline field absent — `guideline_monthly_cents`, `guideline_cp_income_cents`, `guideline_ncp_income_cents`, `guideline_basic_obligation_cents` and `guideline_ncp_share_ratio` all stay absent until the worksheet runs — with `multi_family_credit_cents` at zero, `imputation_applied` false, `monthly_obligation_cents` absent, `arrears_recovery_monthly_cents` at zero, `deviation_applied` false, `deviation_rejection_count` at zero, and `terminated_date`, `superseded_by_order_id`, `next_accrual_due_date`, `drafted_by_user_id`, `findings_approved_by_user_id`, `deviation_requested_cents`, `deviation_reason` and `vacate_reason` all absent — drafting records no user identifier of any kind, because the segregation-of-duties anchor is stamped by the worksheet run and not by the draft. Its `review_eligible_date` is derivable from the moment of drafting as the `effective_date` plus thirty-six months.

The `order_type` is a scoping key, not merely a label. Interval-overlap checking, the controlling-order determination and the enforcement obligation lookup are all scoped per `order_type`, which means a case may legitimately carry one live `CURRENT_SUPPORT` order and one live `MEDICAL_SUPPORT` order at the same time without either colliding with the other.

Running the worksheet drives `DRAFT → CALCULATED`, and re-running it is a self-transition from `CALCULATED` back to `CALCULATED` that keeps a draft in step with income updates; the self-transition is permitted only while `deviation_applied` is false. Either way the case must be in `ORDER_ESTABLISHMENT` or `ENFORCEMENT`, and the paternity settlement is re-checked below the status band. The worksheet imposes no income precondition at all and never refuses a run for want of data: a missing custodial figure is read as zero, and a missing or unattributed obligor figure is what triggers imputation, so the run always produces a number. The run stamps `drafted_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID` — the segregation-of-duties anchor — writes every guideline field, and increases the order's `version` by 1.

The worksheet itself proceeds in five ordered steps, and the order of the last two is observable in the result. **Step one** fixes each party's guideline income. The custodial figure is the custodial participant's `monthly_gross_income_cents`, or zero when that value is absent. The obligor's figure is imputed rather than taken whenever the obligor's `monthly_gross_income_cents` is absent *or* the obligor's `income_verification_source` is `NONE`: in that case the obligor is credited with an imputed monthly income of 182,000 cents, which is 1,820 dollars a month, and `imputation_applied` is recorded as true. Otherwise the obligor's stored figure is used and `imputation_applied` is recorded as false. Whichever figures are actually used are written to `guideline_cp_income_cents` and `guideline_ncp_income_cents`, so a reader can always see what the run was based on.

**Step two** selects one rate from a tiered table using the combined income — the sum of the two guideline incomes. Three bands exist: the first band covers combined incomes up to and including 400,000 cents a month, the second covers combined incomes up to and including 800,000 cents, and the third is unbounded. The band chosen is the first band whose ceiling is greater than or equal to the combined income, so a combined income of exactly 400,000 cents still falls in the first band and a combined income of exactly 800,000 cents still falls in the second. Within the chosen band the rate depends on the number of children on the case, with three or more children all sharing one rate: in the first band the rate is 0.20 for one child, 0.28 for two children and 0.33 for three or more; in the second band 0.17, 0.25 and 0.30; in the third band 0.14, 0.21 and 0.25. The basic obligation, written to `guideline_basic_obligation_cents`, is the combined income multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents.

**Step three** apportions the basic obligation between the parties. `guideline_ncp_share_ratio` is the obligor's guideline income divided by the combined income, rounded half up to four decimal places. The obligor's share is the basic obligation multiplied by that four-decimal ratio, rounded half up to whole cents.

**Step four** applies the multi-family credit, and it applies *before* the reserve. When the obligor's `supports_second_family` is true, `multi_family_credit_cents` is the obligor's share multiplied by 0.10 — a ten percent credit — rounded half up to whole cents; when it is false, `multi_family_credit_cents` is zero. The credited share is the obligor's share minus that credit.

**Step five** applies the self-support reserve, and it applies *last*. The obligor's available income is the obligor's guideline income minus a self-support reserve of 113,200 cents, which is 1,132 dollars a month. If that available amount is zero or negative, the guideline result is the statutory minimum order of 5,000 cents — 50 dollars a month — regardless of anything computed earlier. Otherwise the guideline result is the smaller of the credited share and the available amount. The result is written to `guideline_monthly_cents`. Because the credit is applied before the reserve floor rather than after it, crediting first and then flooring produces a different number from flooring first, and the earlier order is the one this system implements.

A caseworker who believes the guideline number is wrong requests a deviation, driving `CALCULATED → PENDING_DEVIATION_FINDINGS`. The payload carries `deviation_requested_cents` and a `deviation_reason` drawn from the four statutory bases: `EXTRAORDINARY_MEDICAL`, `SHARED_PARENTING`, `HIGH_INCOME` or `HARDSHIP`. A deviation must be material: the absolute difference between the requested amount and `guideline_monthly_cents` must be at least ten percent of `guideline_monthly_cents`, evaluated by integer cross-multiplication so that no floating-point artefact can move the boundary — the absolute difference multiplied by one hundred must be greater than or equal to `guideline_monthly_cents` multiplied by ten. A request that falls short is refused with `ERR_DEVIATION_BELOW_MIN_PCT` (HTTP 422). The requested amount and reason are recorded on the order and its `version` increases by 1.

Findings on a requested deviation are a `SupervisingAttorney` decision, delivered as a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, and the caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from the order's `drafted_by_user_id`. An `APPROVE` drives `PENDING_DEVIATION_FINDINGS → ENTERED`: `deviation_applied` becomes true, `findings_approved_by_user_id` is stamped with the deciding attorney's `X-User-ID`, `monthly_obligation_cents` is set to `deviation_requested_cents` rather than to the guideline figure, `next_accrual_due_date` is set to the order's `effective_date`, and the case moves to `ENFORCEMENT`. A `REJECT` drives `PENDING_DEVIATION_FINDINGS → CALCULATED`, increases `deviation_rejection_count` by 1, and clears both `deviation_requested_cents` and `deviation_reason` back to absent, leaving the caseworker free to try again. A rejection may only be recorded while `deviation_rejection_count` is still below two; a third rejection on the same order is refused with `ERR_DEVIATION_REJECTION_LIMIT` (HTTP 409). Either decision increases the order's `version` by 1.

An order that needs no deviation is entered directly at the guideline amount by a `SupervisingAttorney`, driving `CALCULATED → ENTERED`. Three preconditions apply. `guideline_monthly_cents` must be present, so an order that has never been calculated cannot be entered. No other `ENTERED` order of the same `order_type` on the case may have an overlapping effective interval; intervals are closed on both ends, running from `effective_date` to `terminated_date`, and abutment is legal only when the earlier order's `terminated_date` is exactly one day before the new order's `effective_date`. An overlap is refused with `ERR_ORDER_INTERVAL_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). An absent `terminated_date` runs to infinity for this comparison, so a live open-ended order collides with anything at or after its `effective_date`; the one order excluded from the comparison altogether is the predecessor this entry is about to supersede — when the order being entered was produced by a modification review, the reviewed order is skipped by the overlap test, since its open-ended interval would otherwise always collide with its own successor, and every other order on the case of the same `order_type` is still compared. And continuing exclusive jurisdiction must rest here: this state may only enter an order while no registered or enforcing referral on the case names a foreign `controlling_order_state_code`, as §3.7 sets out — a test that does not consult the case's `interstate_role` — and otherwise the answer is `ERR_NO_CONTROLLING_ORDER_JURISDICTION` (HTTP 409). Entry sets `monthly_obligation_cents` to `guideline_monthly_cents`, sets `next_accrual_due_date` to the order's `effective_date`, and moves the case `ORDER_ESTABLISHMENT → ENFORCEMENT`, from which point the case is eligible for withholding, enforcement and modification. Both the guideline path and the deviation path converge on `ENTERED`, and they differ in the `monthly_obligation_cents` the order ends up carrying and in the gates they pass on the way: the interval-overlap test and the jurisdiction test belong to this operation alone, and an approved deviation enters the order without consulting either. Everything else at the join point — the obligation figure, `next_accrual_due_date`, the case's move to `ENFORCEMENT` and the supersession of a modification predecessor described next — is identical on the two paths. Once an order is entered, `arrears_recovery_monthly_cents` becomes computable as `monthly_obligation_cents` multiplied by 0.20 — twenty percent — rounded half up to whole cents; before entry it is zero.

Entry is also where a modification lands. When the order being entered was produced by a modification review, the predecessor order moves `ENTERED → SUPERSEDED`: the predecessor's `superseded_by_order_id` is set to the successor's id, its `terminated_date` is set to exactly one day before the successor's `effective_date` under those closed-interval semantics, and no new charge may ever be created against it again. Every withholding order of the predecessor that had reached `SERVED` is `TERMINATED` with `terminated_reason` `ORDER_SUPERSEDED`. The supersession requires that the successor's `effective_date` is later than the predecessor's and that the two share the same `order_type`.

Vacatur is the other way out of an order, and a `SupervisingAttorney` performs it by supplying a `vacate_reason` of `PATERNITY_EXCLUDED`, `JURISDICTION_DEFECT` or `CLERICAL_ERROR`. Exactly two origin states are accepted, `DRAFT` and `ENTERED`; an order in `CALCULATED` or `PENDING_DEVIATION_FINDINGS` cannot be vacated any more than an already-`SUPERSEDED` or already-`VACATED` one can, and all four answer `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATUS` (HTTP 409). From `DRAFT` it is unconditional and consequence-free: no charge and no withholding order exists yet, so nothing cascades. From `ENTERED` it is guarded — no compromise offer on the case may be in `ACCEPTED` or `ACTIVE`, because a live settlement plan is priced against the very charges vacatur would destroy — and it cascades four ways. `terminated_date` is set to the current logical clock date. Every `OPEN` charge of the order becomes `VOID`. Any money that had already been applied to those charges is returned to the case's `unapplied_credit_cents`, so the ledger stays balanced rather than losing the payment. Every withholding order of the vacated order that had reached `SERVED` or `AMENDED` becomes `TERMINATED` with `terminated_reason` `ORDER_VACATED`, and the corresponding employer's `active_iwo_count` is decremented for each one. Both the order's `version` and the case's `version` increase by 1.

`SUPERSEDED` and `VACATED` are terminal for an order. Calculating a superseded order, entering a draft, requesting findings on an order that has none pending, issuing withholding against an order that is not entered, posting a charge against a vacated order, or opening a modification review on an order that is not entered all answer with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATUS` (HTTP 409).

### 3.4. Income Withholding: the Employer Registry and the Multi-State Ceiling

Employers are registered once by a `ProgramAdmin` and then reused across cases. Registration names a `legal_name` of between 1 and 200 characters, a `fein` in the two-digit, hyphen, seven-digit form that must be unique across all employers — a malformed one and a duplicate one are both payload-shape violations answered by `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422), and the uniqueness comparison is made only after every other field on the body has passed, so a body that is both duplicated and otherwise malformed answers for the other field — a `state_code`, and a `pay_frequency` of `WEEKLY`, `BIWEEKLY`, `SEMIMONTHLY` or `MONTHLY` describing the payroll cycle. An optional `remittance_fee_cents` records the per-payment administrative fee the employer may retain from the employee; it defaults to zero and may not exceed 500 cents — five dollars. On registration the employer's `statutory_cap_pct` is derived from its `state_code` and `active_iwo_count` starts at zero. `active_iwo_count` thereafter is a counter the server maintains incrementally rather than a figure recomputed on read: it rises by 1 when a withholding order naming that employer is served, falls by 1 when a served or amended one is terminated, and never falls below zero. A withholding order still sitting at `ISSUED` is therefore not counted, because the increment happens at service and not at issuance. There is no operation that edits an employer once registered. An `EmployerAgent` may read one employer and one employer only — its own, the row whose `id` equals its `X-User-ID` — and a read of any other employer row answers `ERR_NOT_IWO_EMPLOYER` (HTTP 403), while the employer list is not open to that role at all and answers `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

Two of the seven recognised states place their own ceiling on wage withholding: an employer whose principal place of business is in `TX` or in `WI` carries a `statutory_cap_pct` of 0.50, a fifty percent ceiling. Employers in `MN`, `NY`, `FL`, `CA` and `PA` have no state ceiling of their own — their `statutory_cap_pct` is absent, and those employers defer entirely to the federal ceiling.

A `CaseWorker` issues a withholding order against an `ENTERED` support order, naming the `employer_id` of the payor of income, supplying `expected_order_version` for the concurrency guard, and optionally supplying `reported_disposable_income_cents` when the employer has already reported the obligor's disposable income. The new row starts with `service_status` `ISSUED` and `remittance_status` `NOT_DUE`, with `served_at`, `first_withholding_due_at`, `next_remittance_due_at` and `terminated_reason` all absent and `missed_remittance_count` at zero. Its `jurisdiction_state_code` is stamped as a copy of the employer's `state_code` — always the employer's state, never the case's and never the order's `origin_state_code`, because under UIFSA it is the law of the obligor's principal place of employment that governs withholding limits. Issuance also increases the parent support order's `version` by 1.

The amount to be withheld is then computed, and the computation has four movements. First, **disposable income**. When `reported_disposable_income_cents` is supplied it is taken as given. When it is not, disposable income is derived from the obligor's `monthly_gross_income_cents` by subtracting a mandatory-deduction allowance of twenty-two percent — 0.22 — of that gross, the allowance being rounded half up to whole cents before subtraction; an obligor with no income figure on file at all derives a disposable income of zero, which in turn leaves no room under the cap. Whichever of the two paths produced it, the figure actually used is written back onto the withholding order's own `reported_disposable_income_cents`, so that field is never absent on a stored row even when the caller supplied nothing, and it is the value an amendment compares its own input against.

Second, **what the order asks for**. `arrears_recovery_cents` is the parent order's `monthly_obligation_cents` multiplied by 0.20 — twenty percent — rounded half up to whole cents, and it is zero when the case has no outstanding arrears principal. `requested_amount_cents` is then the order's `monthly_obligation_cents` plus the order's `medical_support_monthly_cents` plus `arrears_recovery_cents`.

Third, **the ceiling**. `federal_cap_pct` starts at 0.60 — sixty percent of disposable income — except that an obligor whose `supports_second_family` is true starts at 0.50 instead, because the federal tier is more protective of a second household. To that base a further 0.05 — five percentage points — is added when the case's `arrears_principal_cents` is at least three times the order's `monthly_obligation_cents`, which is the statutory twelve-week delinquency bump. The employer's state ceiling is then brought in: `ccpa_cap_pct` is the smaller of `federal_cap_pct` and the employer's `statutory_cap_pct` when that state ceiling exists, and is simply `federal_cap_pct` when the employer's state has none. `ccpa_cap_amount_cents` is the disposable income multiplied by `ccpa_cap_pct`, floored to whole cents — a cap is never rounded upward.

Fourth, **the fee shares the ceiling**. The room available for support is `ccpa_cap_amount_cents` less the employer's `remittance_fee_cents`. `withholding_amount_cents` is the smaller of `requested_amount_cents` and that room, and never less than zero. `employer_fee_cents` is the employer's `remittance_fee_cents` when `withholding_amount_cents` is greater than zero, and zero otherwise; the fee is never counted against the cap a second time. The consequence is deliberate: an order whose requested amount fits the cap exactly still loses the fee's worth of withholding, because the ceiling applies to the requested amount and the fee together as a single limit. If that arithmetic leaves `withholding_amount_cents` at zero, issuance is refused outright with `ERR_WITHHOLDING_CAP_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409).

One further ceiling operates across withholding orders rather than within one. Because an obligor may be withheld at two employers, the collection as a whole is checked against the most restrictive percentage in play: over all of the obligor's withholding orders whose `service_status` is not `TERMINATED`, the one being written included, the total reported disposable income is summed, the smallest `ccpa_cap_pct` among them is taken as the binding percentage, and the aggregate ceiling is that summed disposable income multiplied by the binding percentage, floored to whole cents. The summed `withholding_amount_cents` across those orders must not exceed that aggregate ceiling, or the request is refused with `ERR_AGGREGATE_WITHHOLDING_EXCEEDS_CAP` (HTTP 409). Every individual order can fit inside its own employer's state ceiling while the collection collectively breaches the strictest one, and this check is what catches that. It applies both when a withholding order is issued and when one is amended.

Serving the withholding order on the employer drives `service_status` `ISSUED → SERVED`. Service requires that `withholding_amount_cents` is greater than zero and that the parent support order is still `ENTERED`. Those three checks run in a fixed order: the `service_status` check answers first with `ERR_INVALID_IWO_STATUS`, then a parent order that is not `ENTERED` answers `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATUS` — which is how service of an order whose parent has since been vacated is refused — and only then does a withholding amount of zero answer `ERR_WITHHOLDING_CAP_EXHAUSTED`. It stamps `served_at` with the current logical clock, computes `first_withholding_due_at` as `served_at` plus a fourteen-day employer implementation period, sets `next_remittance_due_at` equal to `first_withholding_due_at`, and increases the employer's `active_iwo_count` by 1. `remittance_status` is deliberately left at `NOT_DUE` — the remittance dimension only starts running at the first due date. Service increases both the withholding order's `version` and the employer's `version` by 1.

When the employer reports a different disposable income than the one on file, a `CaseWorker` amends the order, driving `service_status` `SERVED → AMENDED`, or `AMENDED → AMENDED` on a further amendment; there is no limit on the number of amendments while the order stands. `reported_disposable_income_cents` is required on this operation, and it must differ from the stored value; an identical figure answers `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422), and that one comparison is deliberately made only after the `service_status` check has passed, so an order still merely `ISSUED` that is sent an identical figure answers `ERR_INVALID_IWO_STATUS` rather than the payload error. Amendment recomputes `ccpa_cap_pct`, `ccpa_cap_amount_cents`, `withholding_amount_cents` and `employer_fee_cents` through the same four movements, re-checks the aggregate ceiling, and increases the withholding order's own `version` by 1 — neither the parent support order, nor the case, nor the employer row is written by an amendment. `remittance_status` is untouched, because the two dimensions are independent.

Termination ends the service dimension from any of `ISSUED`, `SERVED` or `AMENDED`, and the `terminated_reason` recorded is one of `EMPLOYMENT_ENDED`, `ORDER_VACATED`, `ORDER_SUPERSEDED`, `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_IWO` or `CASE_CLOSED`. From `ISSUED` the employer's `active_iwo_count` is *not* decremented, because it was never incremented — that only happened at service. From `SERVED` or from `AMENDED` the employer's `active_iwo_count` is decremented by 1, `next_remittance_due_at` is cleared to absent, and the remittance dimension is frozen where it stands. Termination also frees up the obligor's aggregate withholding capacity, so a new withholding order that the aggregate ceiling would previously have refused may now be issued. `TERMINATED` is terminal on the service dimension, and any further write answers with `ERR_INVALID_IWO_STATUS` (HTTP 409) — as does serving an order that is already served, or amending one that is still merely `ISSUED`.

The remittance dimension is driven by the employer and by the finance office in opposite directions. An `EmployerAgent` posting a remittance supplies `amount_cents` and must be the employer named on the order; the order's `service_status` must be `SERVED` or `AMENDED`. The amount is required and must be a non-negative integer, and zero is accepted on this path — unlike the finance office's collection endpoint, an employer remittance is not required to be strictly positive, so a zero remittance still cures the remittance dimension and still records a zero payment; only a negative amount answers `ERR_VALIDATION`. A remittance drives `remittance_status` `NOT_DUE → CURRENT` on the first payment, `CURRENT → CURRENT` on each subsequent period, and `DELINQUENT → CURRENT` as a cure. In every case `missed_remittance_count` is reset to zero and `next_remittance_due_at` is advanced by one cycle of one month — calendar-month arithmetic that preserves the time of day and clamps the day-of-month down to the last day of a shorter target month, which is how every one-month and thirty-six-month advance in this system is computed. The remittance creates a payment on the case with `source` `INCOME_WITHHOLDING` and its `iwo_id` set to this order, which is then allocated and split exactly as described in the ledger section below; both the withholding order's `version` and the case's `version` increase by 1. Because the payment is the resource this operation creates, it answers HTTP 201 carrying the new `PaymentTransaction` in `data` rather than the withholding order. A remittance against an order whose `remittance_status` is already `DEFAULTED` is refused with `ERR_IWO_DEFAULTED` (HTTP 409).

A `FinanceOfficer` records the other side of the ledger — a cycle that closed with no withholding received. Declaring a lapse requires that the current logical clock is strictly past `next_remittance_due_at`; at or before it, the answer is `ERR_REMITTANCE_NOT_YET_DUE` (HTTP 409), and an order that has never been served carries no `next_remittance_due_at` at all, so a lapse against it answers that same error. A lapse declared against an order already `DEFAULTED` on the remittance dimension, or already `TERMINATED` on the service dimension, answers `ERR_INVALID_IWO_STATUS` and never `ERR_IWO_DEFAULTED` — that error belongs to the remittance path alone. A lapse increases `missed_remittance_count` by 1 and drives `remittance_status` `NOT_DUE → DELINQUENT` or `CURRENT → DELINQUENT`. From `DELINQUENT`, a further lapse drives `DELINQUENT → DEFAULTED` once the incremented `missed_remittance_count` reaches two — two missed cycles is employer default. A `DELINQUENT` order is what gives the obligor's enforcement risk profile its delinquency component on the next recomputation, and a `DEFAULTED` order is what makes the case eligible for a contempt referral. `DEFAULTED` is terminal on the remittance dimension.

### 3.5. The Obligation Ledger: Charges, Accrual, and the Allocation Waterfall

Money owed enters the system one charge at a time. A `FinanceOfficer` posts a charge against an `ENTERED` order, naming a `charge_type` of `CURRENT_SUPPORT`, `MEDICAL_SUPPORT`, `ARREARS_JUDGMENT` or `GENETIC_TEST_COST`, the `period_start_date` that inclusively opens the obligation period, the `period_end_date` that **exclusively** closes it, and the `due_date` on which the charge became payable. `amount_cents` is optional: omitted, it defaults to the order's `monthly_obligation_cents` for a `CURRENT_SUPPORT` charge and to the order's `medical_support_monthly_cents` for a `MEDICAL_SUPPORT` charge. The other two charge types have no default at all, so an `ARREARS_JUDGMENT` or `GENETIC_TEST_COST` charge posted without `amount_cents` answers `ERR_VALIDATION`; and because the default is resolved inside the payload band, an omitted amount that would have to be read from an order field that is itself absent answers `ERR_VALIDATION` too, ahead of the order-status check rather than after it. The `due_date` must satisfy `period_start_date` less than or equal to `due_date`, and `due_date` strictly less than `period_end_date`; a `due_date` outside that half-open window is a payload-shape violation answered by `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422). The `due_date` is deliberately not forced to the period start, because it is the anchor from which per-charge statutory interest is measured, and a charge payable on the fifteenth earns less than one payable on the first.

A new charge is created with `status` `OPEN`, `unpaid_balance_cents` equal to `amount_cents`, `paid_cents` at zero and `interest_accrued_cents` at zero. It adds its `amount_cents` to the case's `total_charged_principal_cents` and advances the order's `next_accrual_due_date` by one month, and it increases both the case's and the order's `version` by 1. A charge is immutable once posted: it carries no `version` field and there is no endpoint that edits it. The only way to unwind a charge is to void it.

Because the period is half-open, adjacency is legal and overlap is not. A new charge's `[period_start_date, period_end_date)` interval may not overlap the interval of any existing non-`VOID` charge of the *same* `charge_type` on the *same* order; a new period whose start equals a previous period's end touches but does not overlap and is accepted. An overlap answers with `ERR_CHARGE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). The scoping matters twice over: a medical charge and a current-support charge may cover exactly the same month on the same order without conflict, and a voided charge leaves the interval free for a corrected re-post.

Voiding is how a charge posted in error is withdrawn, and there are two paths to `VOID` that differ in exactly one respect. On the manual path a `FinanceOfficer` voids the charge, and the charge must have `paid_cents` of zero — a partly paid charge cannot be withdrawn by hand, and the attempt answers with `ERR_CHARGE_PARTIALLY_PAID` (HTTP 409). Voidance subtracts the charge's `amount_cents` from the case's `total_charged_principal_cents`, sets the voided charge's own `unpaid_balance_cents` to zero while leaving its `amount_cents` and `paid_cents` exactly as they stood, recomputes the case's `arrears_principal_cents`, and increases the case's `version` by 1 — the charge itself has no version to increase. There is no charge-status error code anywhere in this API, so a manual void aimed at a charge that is not `OPEN` is refused on the two errors this operation does own: a charge whose `paid_cents` is zero — an already-`VOID` charge, for instance — answers `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATUS` (HTTP 409), and a charge whose `paid_cents` is anything other than zero answers `ERR_CHARGE_PARTIALLY_PAID` whatever its status, so a fully `SATISFIED` charge answers the partially-paid error. On the cascade path, where a charge is voided because its order was vacated or because paternity exclusion closed the case, the `paid_cents`-is-zero precondition is deliberately not enforced: the cascade voids paid charges too, and any `paid_cents` already applied to the charge is returned to the case's `unapplied_credit_cents` so that no collected money disappears. In both cases the charge leaves every interest input set from that moment forward.

Interest is the ledger's most delicate arithmetic, and it is governed by the two measures described in §2.7. A `FinanceOfficer` posts an accrual run to bring the ledger up to the current logical clock date. The run writes each `OPEN` charge's `interest_accrued_cents` from the per-charge statutory measure, recomputes the case's `interest_receivable_cents` as the aggregate month-end measure less `interest_written_off_cents` less `interest_collected_cents`, and increases the case's `version` by 1. The run is idempotent for a fixed clock: running it twice without advancing the clock produces identical figures, because both measures are pure functions of the charge set and the as-of date rather than accumulators. The ledger read reports both measures side by side — `statutory_interest_cents` and `aggregate_interest_cents` — together with `interest_written_off_cents`, `interest_collected_cents`, `interest_receivable_cents`, `arrears_principal_cents`, `assigned_arrears_cents`, `unassigned_arrears_cents` and `unapplied_credit_cents`, and it accepts an optional `as_of_date` to value the ledger at a past or future date without mutating anything — an `as_of_date` that is not a well-formed calendar date answers `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422). The read echoes `case_id` and the `as_of_date` it actually used as a `YYYY-MM-DD` string, adds the five waterfall buckets consumed to date — `applied_current_cents`, `applied_medical_cents`, `applied_arrears_cents`, `applied_interest_cents` and `applied_genetic_cost_cents`, each summed across every payment ever posted on the case and therefore not sensitive to `as_of_date` — and repeats the case's `total_charged_principal_cents` and `total_collected_principal_cents`. It is a computed document rather than a case row, so it carries no `id`, no `version` and no audit stamps. The accrual run, in contrast, answers with the case row itself in exactly the shape a case read returns.

Every collection — whether it arrived as an employer remittance or as a payment posted directly by the finance office — is allocated through one waterfall of six buckets, in a fixed order, against the `OPEN` charges of the case only. `VOID` and `SATISFIED` charges are skipped entirely, and a case with no `OPEN` charges at all does not reject the money: the whole payment falls through to unapplied credit.

The waterfall starts with the full `amount_cents` as the remaining balance. **Bucket one, current support for the current period**: the `OPEN` `CURRENT_SUPPORT` charges of the case whose period contains the payment's `received_at` date — that is, `period_start_date` less than or equal to that date and `period_end_date` strictly greater — are taken in order of `due_date` and then `created_at`, and each absorbs the smaller of the remaining balance and its own `unpaid_balance_cents`, accumulating into `applied_current_cents`. **Bucket two, current medical support**: the identical loop over `MEDICAL_SUPPORT` charges in the same period window, accumulating into `applied_medical_cents`. **Bucket three, arrears principal**: every remaining `OPEN` charge of the case that buckets one and two did not consume, taken oldest `due_date` first and then `created_at`, each absorbing the smaller of the remaining balance and its own `unpaid_balance_cents`, accumulating into `applied_arrears_cents`. **Bucket four, accrued interest**: the smaller of the remaining balance and the case's interest receivable, accumulating into `applied_interest_cents` and adding the same amount to the case's `interest_collected_cents`. **Bucket five, genetic test cost**: only when the paternity action's `genetic_cost_liable_party` is `NCP`, the smaller of the remaining balance and the unreimbursed portion of `genetic_cost_cents`, accumulating into `applied_genetic_cost_cents`. **Bucket six**: whatever is still unabsorbed becomes the payment's `unapplied_credit_cents` and is added to the case's `unapplied_credit_cents`. The case's running `total_collected_principal_cents` then rises by buckets one, two, three and five taken together — the genetic-cost reimbursement counts as collected principal — while the interest bucket is recorded only in `interest_collected_cents` and the unabsorbed remainder only in `unapplied_credit_cents`, so no bucket is ever counted in two case-level totals.

Three properties of this waterfall are load-bearing. First, the interest receivable consumed by bucket four is **snapshotted at the start of the request**, before any of this payment's principal reductions land, so the interest bucket is independent of how much principal buckets one through three happened to clear. Second, interest sits *below* arrears principal, which means only a payment large enough to clear all outstanding principal ever touches interest at all. Third, ordering is by `due_date` and then `created_at` — the random identifier is never a tie-break — and there is no rounding anywhere in the waterfall, because every bucket takes a minimum of two integers, so the applied buckets plus the unapplied credit sum to `amount_cents` exactly. A `REVERSAL` carrying a negative `amount_cents` runs the identical six buckets with no special casing, and the same "smaller of the two" rule then hands the whole negative amount to the very first bucket that has any candidate at all: in buckets one through three that reduces the chosen charge's `paid_cents` and restores its `unpaid_balance_cents` by the full amount, leaving the charge `OPEN`; where no `OPEN` charge is a candidate the negative lands in the interest or genetic-cost bucket instead; and only when no bucket has a candidate does it fall through to a negative `unapplied_credit_cents`. A charge already in `SATISFIED` is never a candidate, so a reversal cannot reopen one.

Any charge that the waterfall drives to a zero balance transitions `OPEN → SATISFIED`: its `paid_cents` now equals its `amount_cents`, its `unpaid_balance_cents` is set to zero, the case's `arrears_principal_cents` is recomputed, and the charge stops accruing interest from the satisfying payment's `received_at` onward — retroactively, as §2.7 requires, so it also drops out of month-end snapshots that predate the payment.

Immediately after allocation, on the same request, the distributable total — the sum of `applied_current_cents`, `applied_medical_cents`, `applied_arrears_cents`, `applied_interest_cents` and `applied_genetic_cost_cents` — is split between the family and the state, and the split turns entirely on whether the case carries `tanf_assistance`. On a non-assistance case the family receives current support, medical support and arrears: `disbursed_to_cp_cents` is `applied_current_cents` plus `applied_medical_cents` plus `applied_arrears_cents`, and `retained_by_state_cents` is `applied_interest_cents` plus `applied_genetic_cost_cents`. On an assistance case the arrears are assigned to the state and only a capped pass-through of *current* support reaches the family: the pass-through is the smaller of `applied_current_cents` and 10,000 cents — one hundred dollars — `disbursed_to_cp_cents` is exactly that pass-through, and `retained_by_state_cents` is the whole distributable total less the pass-through. Both branches are exact integer arithmetic with no rounding step, and both satisfy the same invariant: `disbursed_to_cp_cents` plus `retained_by_state_cents` plus the payment's `unapplied_credit_cents` equals `amount_cents`.

A `FinanceOfficer` posts a non-withholding collection on the case by naming the `source` — `DIRECT_PAY`, `TAX_REFUND_INTERCEPT`, `LIEN_LEVY`, `COMPROMISE_INSTALLMENT`, `REVERSAL`, or `INCOME_WITHHOLDING` for a payment recorded outside the employer channel — and an `amount_cents`, optionally naming a `compromise_offer_id` when the money is a settlement installment. `amount_cents` must be strictly positive for every source except `REVERSAL`, which is the only source permitted a negative amount; a `REVERSAL` is how an intercepted refund that the taxpayer successfully challenged is backed out. A `REVERSAL` is in fact accepted at *any* non-zero amount, positive as readily as negative — the one figure it refuses is exactly zero, which answers `ERR_VALIDATION`. A supplied `compromise_offer_id` must resolve to an offer belonging to this very case; an offer on some other case is as unresolvable as an identifier naming nothing and answers `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`. `received_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `iwo_id` is absent on this path, and the payment is allocated and split exactly as above, increasing the case's `version` by 1. A `COMPROMISE_INSTALLMENT` payment is the one source for which `compromise_offer_id` is not optional — omitting it answers `ERR_VALIDATION` — and the referenced offer must be in `ACCEPTED` or `ACTIVE`, or the answer is `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATUS` (HTTP 409). Such a payment must then cover at least the referenced offer's `monthly_installment_cents`, or the offer's `final_installment_cents` when it is the last installment due, and a short payment is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_BELOW_INSTALLMENT` (HTTP 422); a sufficient one advances the offer along its installment track. Any other source may carry a `compromise_offer_id` too, but it is then merely recorded on the payment row: no offer status is checked, no installment floor applies, and the plan does not advance. A `TAX_REFUND_INTERCEPT` payment that clears the arrears releases the matching active tax-intercept enforcement remedy on the case with a release reason of arrears satisfied. Payments are readable individually with their full allocation breakdown, and listable filtered by case, by withholding order and by compromise offer, so that all three foreign keys are reverse-reachable.

### 3.6. Enforcement Remedies: Certification, Notice, Contest and Release

Enforcement is where the agency reaches past the employer and takes something from the obligor, so every remedy passes through a certification gate, a notice period, and an opportunity to be heard before it takes effect. A `CaseWorker` proposes a remedy on a case by naming an `action_type` of `LICENSE_SUSPENSION`, `TAX_REFUND_INTERCEPT`, `PROPERTY_LIEN`, `CONTEMPT_REFERRAL` or `CREDIT_BUREAU_REPORT`. The new action is created with `status` `PROPOSED`, its `proposed_by_user_id` stamped from the calling user's identifier — this is the anchor for the segregation-of-duties rule later — and with `notice_sent_at`, `contest_deadline_at`, `contested_at`, `contest_grounds`, `review_decision`, `review_decided_by_user_id`, `activated_at`, `released_at` and `release_reason` all absent, and `remedy_applied` false. Proposal carries no `case_status` precondition of its own beyond the blanket refusal of a `CLOSED` case, and it does not consult `enforcement_posture` either — a remedy may be proposed on a case still in `OPENED` or `LOCATE`, and on a case whose posture is `STAYED` by an accepted compromise. The certification gates below are the whole of the substantive test at this step; the posture check arrives only at notice and at activation.

Proposal computes and records two figures. `arrears_certified_snapshot_cents` is the case's `arrears_principal_cents` plus the **statutory per-charge** interest measure valued at the current logical clock date — certification always uses the statutory measure, never the ledger's aggregate measure, which is precisely why a certified balance can differ from the balance the ledger read shows for the same case at the same instant. `eligibility_threshold_cents` is the threshold the remedy had to clear, and it is fixed per remedy: 300,000 cents for `LICENSE_SUSPENSION`, 500,000 cents for `PROPERTY_LIEN`, 1,000,000 cents for `CONTEMPT_REFERRAL`, and 100,000 cents for `CREDIT_BUREAU_REPORT`. `TAX_REFUND_INTERCEPT` is the one conditional row: its threshold is 15,000 cents on a case carrying `tanf_assistance`, because assigned arrears are owed to the state and the state intercepts aggressively, and 50,000 cents on a case that does not.

Eligibility requires two gates to pass together. The certified snapshot must be at least `eligibility_threshold_cents`, and it must also be at least two times the monthly obligation of the most recently created `ENTERED` `CURRENT_SUPPORT` order on the case — a two-month delinquency floor that stops a remedy being pursued over a single missed payment on a large order. A case carrying no `ENTERED` `CURRENT_SUPPORT` order at all reads that obligation as zero, which makes the floor vacuous and leaves the fixed per-remedy threshold as the only binding gate. Both gates compare with "at least", so a certified balance exactly equal to a threshold *is* eligible. `CONTEMPT_REFERRAL` carries one further gate: the obligor's `enforcement_risk_tier`, computed under the enforcement weighting described in §3.1, must be `HIGH`. Failing any of these gates answers with `ERR_ARREARS_BELOW_ENFORCEMENT_THRESHOLD` (HTTP 409).

Sending the pre-deprivation notice drives `PROPOSED → NOTICE_SENT`. Notice requires that the case's `enforcement_posture` is not `STAYED` — a case under an accepted compromise is protected, and the attempt answers with `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_STAYED` (HTTP 409) — and that the case's `good_cause_claim` is false, since serving notice on the obligor discloses the existence of the case and a good-cause claim bars exactly that, answering with `ERR_GOOD_CAUSE_BARS_DISCLOSURE` (HTTP 409). Notice stamps `notice_sent_at` from the logical clock, computes `contest_deadline_at` as thirty days later, and increases the action's `version` by 1.

From `NOTICE_SENT` the lifecycle forks, and which branch runs depends entirely on the clock relative to `contest_deadline_at`. On the first branch the obligor contests: the `NoncustodialParty` named on the case — and only that participant — submits `contest_grounds` of `MISTAKE_OF_FACT`, `IDENTITY`, `PAYMENT_MADE` or `INABILITY_TO_PAY`, driving `NOTICE_SENT → CONTESTED` and stamping `contested_at`. The window is **inclusive**: a contest filed at an instant equal to `contest_deadline_at` is timely, and only an instant strictly after it is late, answering with `ERR_CONTEST_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). On the second branch nobody contests and a `CaseWorker` activates the remedy directly, driving `NOTICE_SENT → ACTIVE`. That path requires the clock to be **strictly after** `contest_deadline_at`, because the obligor is entitled to the whole window; activating at an instant exactly equal to the deadline is premature and answers with `ERR_CONTEST_WINDOW_OPEN` (HTTP 409). The two conditions are exact complements, so at any given instant precisely one of the two branches is open. Activation also re-checks that the `enforcement_posture` is not `STAYED`.

An action sitting in `CONTESTED` is decided by a `SupervisingAttorney` supplying a `review_decision` of `UPHOLD` or `VACATE`, and the deciding user's identifier must differ from `proposed_by_user_id`; the caseworker who proposed a remedy may never be the one who validates it, and an attempt answers with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Either way the decision stamps `review_decided_by_user_id`. `UPHOLD` drives `CONTESTED → ACTIVE`; `VACATE` drives `CONTESTED → DENIED`, which is terminal, and on that branch no participant flag is ever set and `remedy_applied` stays false.

`ACTIVE` is a genuine join point: the uncontested branch and the upheld-contest branch land there with **identical** side effects. `activated_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `remedy_applied` becomes true, the case's `enforcement_posture` becomes `ESCALATED`, and the participant-level flag matching the remedy is set — `LICENSE_SUSPENSION` sets `license_suspension_active` and simultaneously clears `has_valid_drivers_license`, `PROPERTY_LIEN` sets `lien_recorded`, and `CREDIT_BUREAU_REPORT` sets `credit_bureau_reported`. `TAX_REFUND_INTERCEPT` and `CONTEMPT_REFERRAL` set no participant flag, because neither leaves a standing mark on the person. Activation increases the action's, the participant's and the case's `version` by 1. Activation on the uncontested branch has one further reach: a `REGISTERED` interstate referral of the enforcement transmittal type on the same case moves to `ENFORCING`, because active enforcement is exactly what the responding state was registered to do.

A `SupervisingAttorney` releases an active remedy with a `release_reason` of `ARREARS_SATISFIED`, `COMPROMISE_ACCEPTED`, `ORDER_VACATED`, `PLAN_COMPLIANT` or `INTERCEPT_REVERSED`, driving `ACTIVE → RELEASED`. Release stamps `released_at`, sets `remedy_applied` back to false, and reverses the participant flag — but only when no other `ACTIVE` action of the same `action_type` still names that participant. That condition is what keeps two concurrent liens honest: releasing one of them must not record the obligor as lien-free while the other still stands. Where the flag is reversed, `LICENSE_SUSPENSION` sets `license_suspension_active` false and restores `has_valid_drivers_license` to true, `PROPERTY_LIEN` sets `lien_recorded` false, and `CREDIT_BUREAU_REPORT` sets `credit_bureau_reported` false. The case's `enforcement_posture` returns to `NORMAL` once no `ACTIVE` action remains on it; while any remains, the posture stays `ESCALATED`. Release increases the action's, the participant's and the case's `version` by 1.

Two system-driven paths reach `RELEASED` without an attorney. From `ACTIVE`, a compromise acceptance, an order vacatur or the paternity-exclusion cascade releases the remedy, setting `release_reason` to `COMPROMISE_ACCEPTED` or `ORDER_VACATED` as appropriate rather than deriving it from a caller's input, and reversing the participant flag in full. A caller-requested case closure is deliberately *not* one of these paths and releases nothing, because closure from `ENFORCEMENT` already requires that no action on the case is `ACTIVE`. From `NOTICE_SENT`, a compromise acceptance also releases the action, with `release_reason` `COMPROMISE_ACCEPTED` — but on that path **no participant flag is touched at all**, because the remedy never activated and no flag was ever set, and only the action's own row is written, so no participant `version` moves. This is the conditional cascade the reader must get right: the same triggering event reaches into two different from-states and does materially different work in each.

`DENIED` and `RELEASED` are terminal. Sending notice on an action that already has notice, contesting one that has not reached notice or has already been decided, activating a contested action, deciding an action that is not contested, or releasing one that never activated all answer with `ERR_INVALID_ACTION_STATUS` (HTTP 409).

### 3.7. Interstate Cases: Transmittals, Registration and the Controlling Order

When an obligor, a child or an employer sits outside this state, the case is worked through UIFSA transmittals. An `InterstateLiaison` drafts a referral on a case, naming a `direction` of `INITIATING` when this state is asking a sister state to act or `RESPONDING` when this state is acting on a sister state's request, an `other_state_code` naming the sister state — which may be `TX`, `NY`, `WI`, `FL`, `CA` or `PA`, but never this state itself — a `child_home_state_code` which may be any of the seven recognised states including this one, and a `transmittal_type` of `ESTABLISHMENT`, `ENFORCEMENT` or `MODIFICATION`. The referral is created with `status` `DRAFT` and with `transmitted_at`, `ack_due_at`, `registered_at`, `controlling_order_id`, `controlling_order_state_code` and `returned_reason` all absent. Drafting deliberately does not touch the case's `interstate_role`: the case's posture changes only when the referral is actually transmitted or registered. Nothing at all is written to the case row on a draft, so the case's `updated_at` and `version` do not move either.

Transmitting an initiating referral drives `DRAFT → TRANSMITTED`. It requires that the referral's `direction` is `INITIATING`, that the case has at least one `ENTERED` support order to transmit, and — because a transmittal discloses party information to another jurisdiction — that the case's `good_cause_claim` is false. The first two of those preconditions belong to the referral-status band and fail with `ERR_INVALID_REFERRAL_STATUS`, which sits above the confidentiality check: a case with no `ENTERED` order to transmit is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REFERRAL_STATUS` even when its `good_cause_claim` is also true, and `ERR_GOOD_CAUSE_BARS_DISCLOSURE` is reached only once an entered order exists. Transmittal stamps `transmitted_at` from the logical clock, computes `ack_due_at` as ten days later, sets the case's `interstate_role` to `INITIATING`, and increases both the referral's and the case's `version` by 1.

Registration is reachable from two different from-states with two different preconditions. From `DRAFT` it is the responding-state path: the referral's `direction` must be `RESPONDING`, and the case must have at least one `ENTERED` support order whose `origin_state_code` equals the referral's `other_state_code` — the foreign order being registered here must actually be the sister state's order. From `TRANSMITTED` it is the acknowledged-initiating path: the only precondition is that the current logical clock is at or before `ack_due_at`, and the boundary is inclusive, so an acknowledgment arriving at the instant of the deadline still registers while one arriving strictly after it answers `ERR_INVALID_REFERRAL_STATUS` — the elapsed acknowledgment window has no temporal error of its own on this path. Either way registration stamps `registered_at`, determines the controlling order, sets the case's `interstate_role` to `RESPONDING` on a responding referral, and increases both the referral's and the case's `version` by 1.

Determining the controlling order is a pure ranking with no arithmetic, and its ranking direction inverts on a single condition, which makes it the least intuitive rule in the system. The candidate set is the `ENTERED` support orders of the case whose `order_type` is `CURRENT_SUPPORT`; medical-support orders never compete, and neither does any order in `DRAFT`, `CALCULATED`, `PENDING_DEVIATION_FINDINGS`, `SUPERSEDED` or `VACATED`. Among the candidates, those whose `origin_state_code` equals the referral's `child_home_state_code` are the home-state orders. **If any home-state order exists**, a home-state order always outranks a foreign one, and among the home-state orders the one with the **greatest** `effective_date` prevails — the most recent home-state order controls. **If no home-state order exists**, the ranking direction flips: the candidate with the **smallest** `effective_date` prevails — the first order issued anywhere controls. In both branches ties are broken by the **earliest** `created_at`, and never by the identifier. The winner's identifier becomes `controlling_order_id` and its `origin_state_code` becomes `controlling_order_state_code`. When the candidate set is empty — the case carries no `ENTERED` `CURRENT_SUPPORT` order at all — registration still succeeds and simply leaves both `controlling_order_id` and `controlling_order_state_code` absent.

That state code is what governs continuing exclusive jurisdiction. A `controlling_order_state_code` naming this state leaves the agency free to enter and to modify orders; a foreign `controlling_order_state_code` closes the jurisdiction gate, and order entry on that case is refused with `ERR_NO_CONTROLLING_ORDER_JURISDICTION` (HTTP 409) for as long as the foreign order controls. The gate reads every referral on the case whose `status` is `REGISTERED` or `ENFORCING` and whose `controlling_order_state_code` is present, and one foreign state code among them is enough to close it. At order entry the test is exactly that and nothing more — the case's own `interstate_role` does not enter into it, so a foreign controlling order blocks entry even on a case whose `interstate_role` is `INITIATING` or `NONE`; the modification-proposal gate of §3.8 is the narrower one, applying the same referral test only while `interstate_role` is `RESPONDING`. A case with no such referral at all has no controlling-order constraint and the gate is open, and so does a case whose only registered referral has since been returned, because the return clears those two fields.

A `REGISTERED` referral moves to `ENFORCING` as a system cascade when an enforcement action on the same case reaches `ACTIVE`, and only when the referral's `transmittal_type` is `ENFORCEMENT`; an establishment or modification transmittal stays `REGISTERED` no matter what enforcement does, because active enforcement is not what it asked for.

Returning a transmittal unworked is the terminal path, and it is reachable from three from-states with three different preconditions. From `TRANSMITTED` the `returned_reason` may be `NO_ACK_RECEIVED`, but only when the current logical clock is **strictly past** `ack_due_at` — the sister state gets the whole ten days. From `REGISTERED` the `returned_reason` must be `INSUFFICIENT_INFO` or `NO_JURISDICTION`, and the return additionally clears `controlling_order_id` and `controlling_order_state_code`, which reopens the jurisdiction gate on that case. From `ENFORCING` the return requires that no enforcement action on the case is still `ACTIVE`; a live remedy must be released before its transmittal can be sent back, and this is the one path that constrains no reason at all, so any of the three `returned_reason` values — `NO_ACK_RECEIVED` included — is accepted from `ENFORCING`. Every one of those path preconditions, the wrong reason and the unelapsed acknowledgment window alike, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REFERRAL_STATUS`; the return operation owns no other error. The clearing of `controlling_order_id` and `controlling_order_state_code` is likewise the `REGISTERED` path's alone — a return from `TRANSMITTED` has nothing to clear, and a return from `ENFORCING` leaves both fields exactly as they stand, though the gate of §3.7 reopens regardless because a `RETURNED` referral is no longer read by it. Every return path sets the case's `interstate_role` back to `NONE` when no other live referral remains on the case — a referral counts as live for that test whenever its `status` is anything other than `RETURNED`, so a single untransmitted `DRAFT` referral elsewhere on the case is enough to hold `interstate_role` where it stands — and increases both the referral's and the case's `version` by 1.

`RETURNED` is terminal. Transmitting a responding referral, transmitting one that has already gone out, registering a referral that is already registered or enforcing, or returning one that is already returned all answer with `ERR_INVALID_REFERRAL_STATUS` (HTTP 409). Referrals are listable filtered by case and by controlling order, so both foreign keys are reverse-reachable.

### 3.8. Modification Review and the Successor Order

An entered order is not permanent. A `CaseWorker` opens a modification review against an `ENTERED` order, naming a `trigger_type` of `PARTY_REQUEST` when a party asks, `PERIODIC_36_MONTH` on the statutory three-year cycle, or `PA_MANDATORY` when the public-assistance program requires it. The review is created with `status` `REQUESTED`, its `case_id` copied from the reviewed order, its `current_obligation_snapshot_cents` captured from that order's `monthly_obligation_cents` — this snapshot, not the live order, is the denominator of the variance test — and its `review_eligible_date` computed as the reviewed order's `effective_date` plus thirty-six months. `recalculated_guideline_cents`, `variance_numerator_cents`, `substantial_change_met`, `info_requested_reason`, `screened_at` and `proposed_order_id` all start absent, and `info_request_count` starts at zero. A `PERIODIC_36_MONTH` review may not be created before `review_eligible_date`, and an early attempt answers with `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_YET_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 409). `PARTY_REQUEST` and `PA_MANDATORY` reviews are exempt from that date entirely — a party may ask at any time, and the program may compel a review at any time.

Screening is where the review earns its status, and it has two possible outcomes on the way in. The variance test requires both parties' incomes to be verified, so if either party's `income_verification_source` is anything other than `VERIFIED_WAGE_RECORD`, screening drives `REQUESTED → INFO_PENDING` instead of computing anything: `info_request_count` increases by 1 and `info_requested_reason` is set. **The obligor is checked before the custodial party**, so a review in which *both* incomes are unverified reports `NCP_INCOME_UNVERIFIED`, not `CP_INCOME_UNVERIFIED`. No guideline recomputation happens on this path, and `recalculated_guideline_cents` stays as it was.

Supplying the missing information drives `INFO_PENDING → REQUESTED`, closing the cycle. The `CaseWorker` names a `participant_id`, which must be one of the case's two parties — an identifier that resolves to a real participant sitting on neither side of this case answers `ERR_PARTY_ROLE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422), evaluated after the review-status check and before the request-count limit below — together with a `monthly_gross_income_cents` and an `income_verification_source`, both of which are required; the named participant's income fields are updated and that participant's `version` increases by 1, the review's `info_requested_reason` is cleared, and the review's `version` increases by 1. The response is the review row itself, not the participant. The cycle may run twice: an information submission is permitted while `info_request_count` is at most two, and a third information request on the same review is refused with `ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT` (HTTP 409). A review can therefore bounce out and back, be screened again, and bounce out once more before the agency must give up and open a fresh review.

When both parties are verified, screening runs the substantial-change test and writes `recalculated_guideline_cents`, `variance_numerator_cents`, `substantial_change_met` and `screened_at`, landing the review in either `THRESHOLD_MET` or `THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`. The recalculated figure is the full guideline worksheet of §3.3 re-run against the two participants' *current* incomes and the case's `child_count`. `variance_numerator_cents` is the absolute difference between that recalculated figure and `current_obligation_snapshot_cents`.

The test itself has two gates and the `trigger_type` decides how they combine. The **relative gate** asks whether the variance is at least fifteen percent of the snapshot, and it is evaluated by integer cross-multiplication — the absolute difference multiplied by 10,000 compared against 1,500 multiplied by the snapshot — so no floating-point rounding can shift the boundary, and because the comparison is "at least", a variance of exactly 15.00% qualifies. The **absolute gate** asks whether the variance is at least 5,000 cents, fifty dollars. A `PERIODIC_36_MONTH` review clears the relative gate automatically — the three-year cycle waives the percentage requirement entirely — and needs only the absolute gate. A `PARTY_REQUEST` or `PA_MANDATORY` review must clear **both** gates together. The consequence worth stating plainly is that on those two triggers a change can clear the fifty-dollar gate comfortably and still fail the review because it does not reach fifteen percent of a large obligation, and conversely a small obligation can move fifteen percent without reaching fifty dollars. `THRESHOLD_NOT_MET` is terminal: a review that fails cannot be re-screened, and a new review must be opened to try again.

From `THRESHOLD_MET`, a `SupervisingAttorney` proposes the successor order, driving `THRESHOLD_MET → ORDER_PROPOSED`, supplying the `effective_date` the new obligation is to take effect. Two preconditions apply. The supplied `effective_date` must be **strictly later** than the reviewed order's `effective_date`; an equal or earlier date is refused with `ERR_MODIFICATION_EFFECTIVE_DATE_NOT_LATER` (HTTP 422), because a successor that takes effect on or before its predecessor would leave the obligation ambiguous for a period. And continuing exclusive jurisdiction must rest here: either the case's `interstate_role` is not `RESPONDING`, or the case's registered referral names this state as its `controlling_order_state_code`. A foreign controlling order is refused with `ERR_NO_CONTROLLING_ORDER_JURISDICTION` (HTTP 409).

Proposing the successor sets off a four-level cascade. At the first level a successor support order is created directly in `CALCULATED` — not `DRAFT` — with its `guideline_monthly_cents` set to the review's `recalculated_guideline_cents`; it is returned by no dedicated read of its own and is discoverable only through the order list filtered by case. The rest of that successor row is derived from the reviewed order rather than recomputed: it copies the reviewed order's `order_type`, `origin_state_code` and `medical_support_monthly_cents`, and it copies the reviewed order's `guideline_cp_income_cents`, `guideline_ncp_income_cents`, `guideline_basic_obligation_cents`, `guideline_ncp_share_ratio`, `multi_family_credit_cents` and `imputation_applied` verbatim — so those component fields still show the *original* worksheet's inputs even though `guideline_monthly_cents` carries the freshly recalculated figure. Its `effective_date` is the date supplied on this request; `requested_monthly_cents`, `monthly_obligation_cents`, `next_accrual_due_date`, `terminated_date`, `superseded_by_order_id`, `findings_approved_by_user_id`, `deviation_requested_cents`, `deviation_reason` and `vacate_reason` are all absent; `deviation_applied` is false and `deviation_rejection_count` is zero; and `drafted_by_user_id` is stamped with the *proposing attorney's* `X-User-ID`, which is therefore the identifier the segregation-of-duties rule will compare against should a deviation ever be requested and decided on the successor. At the second level the review's `proposed_order_id` is set to that successor's identifier, and the review's `version` increases by 1. Although the operation creates an order, it answers HTTP 200 carrying the updated review row in `data` — never 201, and never the successor order. The remaining two levels do not fire yet: they fire when the successor is *entered*. At the third level, on that entry, the reviewed order becomes `SUPERSEDED` with its `superseded_by_order_id` naming the successor and its `terminated_date` set to the day before the successor's `effective_date`, so that the two orders abut without overlapping. At the fourth level, every `SERVED` withholding order of the reviewed order is terminated with a `terminated_reason` of `ORDER_SUPERSEDED`, which decrements the affected employers' `active_iwo_count`, because an employer must not keep withholding under an order that no longer governs.

`THRESHOLD_NOT_MET` and `ORDER_PROPOSED` are terminal. Screening a review that is `INFO_PENDING` or already screened, supplying information to a review that did not ask for any, or proposing an order on a review that has not reached `THRESHOLD_MET` all answer with `ERR_INVALID_REVIEW_STATUS` (HTTP 409). Reviews are listable filtered by case, by reviewed order and by proposed order, so all three foreign keys are reverse-reachable.

### 3.9. Arrears Compromise and Case Closure

Where arrears have grown past any realistic prospect of collection, the agency may settle. A `FinanceOfficer` builds a compromise offer on a case, supplying an `installment_count` between three and thirty-six inclusive and an `expected_case_version` for the concurrency guard described in §2.6. Four gates protect the offer, and they are evaluated in the order §2.9 fixes rather than in the order they are narrated here — the live-offer check answers first, then the assistance check, then the risk tier, and the arrears minimum last of all because it is a fiscal check. The case's `tanf_assistance` must be false, because arrears assigned to the state in exchange for public assistance are not the agency's to forgive, and an assistance case answers with `ERR_ASSIGNED_ARREARS_NOT_COMPROMISABLE` (HTTP 409). The obligor's `compromise_risk_tier`, computed under the compromise weighting described in §3.1, must not be `HIGH`, or the answer is `ERR_COMPROMISE_RISK_TOO_HIGH` (HTTP 409) — a high-risk obligor is a collection problem, not a hardship case. The case's `arrears_principal_cents` must be at least 100,000 cents, one thousand dollars, or the answer is `ERR_ARREARS_BELOW_COMPROMISE_MINIMUM` (HTTP 409); small balances are collected, not settled. And no other offer on the case may already be `ACCEPTED` or `ACTIVE`, or the answer is `ERR_ACTIVE_OFFER_EXISTS` (HTTP 409) — one live plan at a time.

The offer's terms are then computed and frozen. `principal_due_snapshot_cents` captures the case's `arrears_principal_cents`. `interest_due_snapshot_cents` captures the **aggregate month-end** interest measure valued at the current logical clock date — the settlement figure is a ledger figure, so it uses the ledger's measure and not the certification measure. `pre_waiver_total_cents` is the sum of the two, and it is the **only** input to the waiver-tier lookup. `waiver_tier_pct` is the rate of the first band whose ceiling is at least `pre_waiver_total_cents`: 0.25 up to and including 500,000 cents, 0.50 up to and including 2,000,000 cents, and 0.75 above that with no ceiling. The tier is keyed on the pre-waiver total and never recomputed after the waiver has been applied; recomputing it against the reduced total is a self-referential loop that can only produce a smaller waiver than the statute allows. `interest_waiver_cents` is `interest_due_snapshot_cents` multiplied by `waiver_tier_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. `settlement_total_cents` is `pre_waiver_total_cents` less `interest_waiver_cents`; because only interest is ever waived, `settlement_total_cents` is always at least `principal_due_snapshot_cents` — principal is never forgiven. `monthly_installment_cents` is `settlement_total_cents` divided by `installment_count`, floored to whole cents, and `final_installment_cents` is `settlement_total_cents` less `monthly_installment_cents` multiplied by one fewer than `installment_count`, so the whole remainder lands entirely on the last installment and the schedule sums to `settlement_total_cents` exactly.

The new offer is created with `status` `PROPOSED`, `compliance_payment_count` at zero, and `accepted_at`, `next_installment_due_date`, `defaulted_at` and `completed_at` absent, and it increases the case's `version` by 1. Creating an offer also drives any offer already `PROPOSED` on the same case to `SUPERSEDED` — which is a write on that older row, so its own `version` increases by 1 too — and `SUPERSEDED` is terminal: the older offer can never be accepted afterwards, and an attempt answers with `ERR_OFFER_SUPERSEDED` (HTTP 409) rather than the generic status error. The supersession is part of the successful creation and nothing else, so a creation refused on any of the four gates, on the concurrency guard or on the payload leaves an existing `PROPOSED` offer untouched.

Acceptance is the obligor's own act: the `NoncustodialParty` named on the case accepts, driving `PROPOSED → ACCEPTED`. Acceptance re-checks that no other offer on the case is `ACCEPTED` or `ACTIVE`, and it re-checks that the case's `arrears_principal_cents` still equals the offer's `principal_due_snapshot_cents` — a snapshot that has moved since the offer was built would waive interest against a balance that no longer exists, and a moved snapshot answers with `ERR_OFFER_SNAPSHOT_STALE` (HTTP 409). Acceptance stamps `accepted_at` from the logical clock, sets `next_installment_due_date` to one month after the acceptance date, adds `interest_waiver_cents` to the case's `interest_written_off_cents` — which is what removes the waived interest from `interest_receivable_cents` — and sets the case's `enforcement_posture` to `STAYED`. The stay is not merely a flag: every enforcement action on the case in `ACTIVE` *or* in `NOTICE_SENT` is released with a `release_reason` of `COMPROMISE_ACCEPTED`, and for the `ACTIVE` ones the participant remedy flags are reversed exactly as a manual release reverses them, so `license_suspension_active` becomes false with `has_valid_drivers_license` restored to true, `lien_recorded` becomes false and `credit_bureau_reported` becomes false. Acceptance increases the offer's `version` by 1, and it writes the obligor's row and the case's row unconditionally — even on a case carrying no enforcement action at all — so in the plainest case the participant's and the case's `version` each rise by 1. Each `ACTIVE` action released along the way is a further write of both of those rows, however, so with one `ACTIVE` remedy released the participant and the case each rise by 2 and with two such remedies by 3; a `NOTICE_SENT` release, by contrast, writes only that action's own row and moves no participant or case version. Acceptance sets the stay after those releases have run, so the posture settles at `STAYED` rather than being returned to `NORMAL` by the last release.

The plan then runs on payments. A compromise-installment payment referencing the offer drives `ACCEPTED → ACTIVE` on the first installment, setting `compliance_payment_count` to 1 and advancing `next_installment_due_date` by one month. Each further installment is a self-loop `ACTIVE → ACTIVE` while `compliance_payment_count` is still below one fewer than `installment_count`, increasing the count by 1 and advancing the due date by a further month. The payment whose arrival makes `compliance_payment_count` plus one equal `installment_count` is the final one, and it must cover at least `final_installment_cents` rather than `monthly_installment_cents`; it drives `ACTIVE → COMPLETED`, stamps `completed_at`, clears `next_installment_due_date` back to absent, makes the interest waiver permanent, returns the case's `enforcement_posture` to `NORMAL` unconditionally rather than by settling it against any surviving action, and satisfies the remaining `OPEN` charges of the case — each one's `unpaid_balance_cents` set to zero and its `status` moved to `SATISFIED`, with `paid_cents` left exactly as it stood — so that `arrears_principal_cents` becomes zero. On every one of those installment transitions the offer's own `version` increases by 1 and the case's `version` increases by 1 for the payment; no participant row is written, and the response the caller sees is the payment created by the collection endpoint, not the offer. `COMPLETED` is terminal.

A `FinanceOfficer` declares the plan in default from either `ACCEPTED` or `ACTIVE`, and the only precondition is that the current logical clock date is **strictly past** `next_installment_due_date` — an installment due today is not yet late, and a premature declaration answers with `ERR_INSTALLMENT_NOT_YET_LATE` (HTTP 409). Default stamps `defaulted_at`, subtracts `interest_waiver_cents` back out of the case's `interest_written_off_cents` so that the reinstated interest reappears in `interest_receivable_cents`, and sets the case's `enforcement_posture` to `ESCALATED`, which reopens enforcement eligibility. Installments already paid are **not** clawed back — the money stays applied to the charges it satisfied, and only the waiver is reversed. Nor is the schedule wound back: `next_installment_due_date` is left standing at the date the plan defaulted against, since only completion clears it, and `compliance_payment_count` keeps whatever count the plan had reached. Default increases the offer's and the case's `version` by 1, and it writes no participant row and releases no enforcement action. `DEFAULTED` is terminal, and every disallowed origin state on acceptance or on a default declaration answers with `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATUS` (HTTP 409).

Closure is the end of the case, and a `SupervisingAttorney` performs it by supplying a `closed_reason` of `ARREARS_SATISFIED`, `NO_JURISDICTION`, `CP_REQUEST` or `CHILD_EMANCIPATED`. Three distinct closure paths exist and each carries its own preconditions. From `LOCATE`, the case may close only when the obligor's `locate_status` is `UNLOCATABLE` and the `closed_reason` is `NO_JURISDICTION` — the agency cannot proceed against a person it cannot find. From `ENFORCEMENT`, the case may close only when no enforcement action on it is `ACTIVE` and no compromise offer on it is `ACCEPTED` or `ACTIVE`; a live remedy or a live plan must be resolved first. From `ORDER_ESTABLISHMENT`, the `closed_reason` must be `CP_REQUEST` or `CHILD_EMANCIPATED`, and the case's `tanf_assistance` must be false, because a case whose arrears are assigned to the state may not be closed at the custodial party's request. Every one of those path preconditions is a case-status refusal: a case sitting in `OPENED` or `PATERNITY_PENDING`, a `LOCATE` case whose obligor is not `UNLOCATABLE` or whose `closed_reason` is anything but `NO_JURISDICTION`, an `ENFORCEMENT` case still carrying a live action or a live plan, and an `ORDER_ESTABLISHMENT` case with the wrong reason or with `tanf_assistance` true all answer `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATUS` (HTTP 409), never a more specific error. Across every path, a `closed_reason` of `ARREARS_SATISFIED` additionally requires that the case's `arrears_principal_cents` and `interest_receivable_cents` are both zero, and any outstanding balance answers with `ERR_ARREARS_OUTSTANDING` (HTTP 409) — a fiscal check, so it is reached only once the path precondition above has passed.

Closure sets `case_status` to `CLOSED`, stamps `closed_at` from the logical clock, and increases the case's `version` by 1. It cascades in two directions: every withholding order on the case that had reached `SERVED` or `AMENDED` is terminated with a `terminated_reason` of `CASE_CLOSED` and the corresponding employers' `active_iwo_count` is decremented, and both participants' rows are written so that their recomputed `open_case_count` is accompanied by a refreshed `updated_at` and a `version` increased by 1 on each of them — both party rows are written even when the count being recomputed has not actually changed, which is the opposite of case creation, where nothing is written to either party at all. `CLOSED` is terminal on the case-status dimension, and as §2.9 states, a closed case rejects every subsequent write — against the case itself and against any child of it — with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATUS`.

One further closure path is never requested by a caller at all. When a paternity action reaches `EXCLUDED`, the case is closed as a system cascade from `PATERNITY_PENDING` with `closed_reason` `NO_JURISDICTION` and `closed_at` stamped from the logical clock, and that cascade runs four levels deep: the case closes, every non-terminal support order on it is vacated, every `OPEN` charge on it is voided with any `paid_cents` returned to the case's `unapplied_credit_cents`, every non-terminal enforcement action on it is released with a `release_reason` of `ORDER_VACATED`, and the participant remedy flags those actions had set are reversed. That path enforces none of the closure preconditions above, because a man excluded by the laboratory owes nothing regardless of what the ledger says.
