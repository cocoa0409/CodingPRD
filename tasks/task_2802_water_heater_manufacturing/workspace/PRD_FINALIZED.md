# Water Heater Manufacturing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A residential water-heater plant is one of the few manufacturing environments where a single production decision has to satisfy four different authorities at once: the welder who fabricates a steel tank, the glass-lining chemist whose slurry either bonds or does not, the federal efficiency regulator who publishes a rating on the jacket label, and — years later — the warranty adjudicator who has to explain why a tank in a hard-water county leaked at month sixty-one. Today those four conversations happen in four disconnected systems. Tank lots are tracked on a whiteboard, glass-lining furnace bookings live in a shared spreadsheet, certification paperwork is emailed between the compliance group and the independent approver, and recall scoping is done by hand from a serial log that nobody trusts. The result is predictable: models get labelled with ratings whose supporting test data has since been voided, component lots from a supplier that failed its audit last week are still going into cabinets, and when a tank-failure pattern does emerge nobody can say which production month it belongs to.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces all four systems with one plant-of-record. It is mounted under `/mfg` and it owns the entire chain of custody: supplier qualification and incoming component control, glass-lining furnace scheduling, tank fabrication with adhesion and holiday inspection, insulation foaming and jacket assembly, efficiency test scheduling and rating certification with an enforced approver/submitter split, unit-level serialisation and shipment, warranty adjudication against a per-model reserve, and serial-range recall management. The service is deliberately opinionated about two things. First, **containment is transitive**: disqualifying a supplier does not merely mark a row, it blocks that supplier's lots, holds every unit those lots went into, and restricts every catalogue model whose units are now held. Second, **money and material are conserved**: every tank in a lot is accounted for as work-in-process, good or scrapped at all times, every component piece drawn from a lot can be returned by a compensating disassembly, and the warranty reserve balance is always exactly the accrual minus what has been paid out.

Because the plant is audited, the service also has to be reproducible. All temporal behaviour — months in service, supplier qualification expiry, furnace and test-cell window elapse — reads a **logical UTC clock** that an administrator sets explicitly, never the host wall clock. That single decision is what makes a five-year warranty curve testable in an afternoon.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. Both must be present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` must name one of exactly eight declared roles. A request with either header missing or empty, or naming a role outside the declared set, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). There are no unauthenticated endpoints: every endpoint in the service, including the logical-clock read, requires both headers. Once the caller is authenticated, the service checks that the caller's role appears on the endpoint being invoked; if it does not, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). The role check applies to every endpoint without exception — the clock read, for instance, is open to all seven opaque-identity roles but closed to `SupplierRep`.

The eight roles are:

- **`PlantAdmin`** — plant systems administrator. Owns asset master data (furnaces and test cells), the logical clock, and the reinstatement of a restricted catalogue model. Exclusively authorised to set the logical clock, register furnaces, register test cells, and lift a model's restriction. Shares every read endpoint except the supplier-scoped reads.
- **`PlantOperator`** — production operator. Exclusively authorised to book and fire glass-lining batches, to open tank lots and drive them through welding, lining application, reline authorisation, foaming, jacketing and release, and to stage, assemble, label, ship, release-from-hold and disassemble heater units. Shares the production, catalogue, supplier and unit read endpoints.
- **`QualityInspector`** — quality inspector. Exclusively authorised to file lining inspections, to reverse a rework decision, to scrap a lining-rejected lot, to release or reject an incoming component lot, and to record an efficiency test result. Also permitted to book incoming component lots, and shares the production, catalogue, supplier and test read endpoints.
- **`ComplianceEngineer`** — efficiency-standards engineer. Exclusively authorised to create catalogue models, release them to channel, withdraw them, schedule efficiency tests, open rating certifications and submit them for approval. Shares the warranty-analysis and recall-completion computed reads plus the catalogue, test, certification, production, unit, claim and recall read endpoints.
- **`CertificationApprover`** — independent rating-certification approver. The only role that may approve, reject or revoke a certification, or suspend a model's certification. Shares the catalogue, test and certification read endpoints.
- **`SupplierQualityManager`** — supplier quality manager. Exclusively authorised to register suppliers, score them, disqualify them, requalify them, and unblock a contained component lot. Also permitted to book incoming component lots, and shares the supplier and component-lot read endpoints.
- **`WarrantyAnalyst`** — warranty and recall analyst. Exclusively authorised to file, validate, deny, pay and reverse-pay warranty claims, and to open, scope, announce, close and cancel recall campaigns as well as to notify, remedy and mark-unreachable individual recall notices. Shares the warranty-analysis and recall-completion computed reads plus the catalogue, unit, claim, certification and recall read endpoints.
- **`SupplierRep`** — external supplier representative. Read-only, and authorised on exactly three endpoints: the single-supplier read, the single-component-lot read, and the component-lot list. This role appears on no write endpoint anywhere in the service.

Seven of the eight roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `PlantAdmin`, `PlantOperator`, `QualityInspector`, `ComplianceEngineer`, `CertificationApprover`, `SupplierQualityManager` or `WarrantyAnalyst`, the value of `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `weld_operator_user_id` on a tank lot, as `inspector_user_id` on a lining inspection, as `submitted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a rating certification — and it compares those recorded values against each other for the segregation-of-duties rules described in §2.10, but it never compares `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`.

`SupplierRep` is the sole **entity-backed** role. When `X-User-Role` is `SupplierRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Supplier` row; ownership checks compare the resource's supplier reference to `X-User-ID`. A `SupplierRep` may read its own supplier record and no other, and may read a component lot only when that lot's `supplier_id` equals `X-User-ID`; the component-lot list is likewise narrowed to its own lots. Any read that reaches outside that scope is rejected with `ERR_SUPPLIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This scope rule guards exactly three endpoints: `GET /mfg/suppliers/{id}`, `GET /mfg/component-lots/{id}` and `GET /mfg/component-lots`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest row first. This ordering is not cosmetic: several cascades in this service are specified to visit their targets in `created_at` ascending order, and the list endpoints are the channel through which that order is observed.

For list responses the `meta` object carries `limit` (the effective page size applied), `offset` (the effective starting position applied), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filter before pagination) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page). Single-resource responses omit all four.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Immutable Inspection Record

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix, generated by the service and never accepted from the client. Alongside it, every resource carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the logical-clock instant at which the row was created.

Thirteen of the fourteen resource types are mutable and additionally carry `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every accepted mutation of that row — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every accepted mutation. Crucially, `version` increments on **cascade-induced** mutations too, not only on mutations the client requested directly: when a supplier disqualification blocks a component lot, holds a heater unit and restricts a product model, all four rows have their `version` incremented and their `updated_at` refreshed in the same request.

The one exception is the lining-inspection record. A `LiningInspection` is an immutable measurement: once written it is never mutated, only superseded by a newer record of the same inspection type. It therefore carries only `id` and `created_at`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are **absent** from every lining-inspection response — the single-record read and the per-lot sub-resource listing alike. Supersession is expressed by the `record_status` field on the record itself rather than by mutating history.

### 2.5. The Logical Plant Clock

All time-dependent behaviour in this service reads a single server-held logical UTC clock rather than the host's wall clock. On startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and until an administrator moves it, that is the instant every rule sees; the service never falls back to host wall-clock time under any circumstance. A `PlantAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant, and any role other than `SupplierRep` may read the clock, which returns the same `current_utc` field.

Moving the clock has no stored side effects, but it silently changes a great deal of derived state: every heater unit's `months_in_service` is recomputed from the new instant on the next read, and suppliers whose `qualified_until` now lies in the past stop being usable for component release and unit assembly. Window rules read the same clock — a glass-lining batch cannot be marked fired before the clock reaches its `firing_end`, and an efficiency test result cannot be booked before the clock reaches its `window_end`.

### 2.6. Precision and Rounding Discipline

Wherever this document specifies a rounded value, rounding is **half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero — and it is applied at exactly the decimal position and at exactly the point in the evaluation order that the rule names. The service never re-rounds an already-rounded intermediate, and never defers a rounding step that a rule places earlier.

The precisions in use are: supplier qualification scores and both recall completion percentages at 2 decimal places; achieved insulation R-values at 1 decimal place; holiday density, efficiency-test effective minimums and efficiency-test margins at 4 decimal places; tank-failure rates and the weighted exposure figure at 1 and 2 decimal places respectively; monetary amounts in whole integer cents; and quantities of tanks and component pieces as exact integers with no rounding at all. Months in service is the only figure that uses a different rule: it is truncated toward zero (floored) to a whole number of months and is never rounded.

Two rounding orders are load-bearing and are stated again where they are used, because getting them backwards produces a different published number: certified ratings round **each contributing test first and then the mean**, and warranty payouts prorate and round **each of the three allowance lines first and then sum them**.

### 2.7. Version Pinning on Shared Parents

Two write endpoints in this service create a child row whose acceptance silently reshapes a parent that other clients may also be working on. Both therefore require the client to pin the parent version it last observed, and both reject a stale pin with `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

`POST /mfg/rating-certifications` requires a non-optional `expected_product_model_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ProductModel`. If that value does not equal the model's current `version` at the moment the request is processed, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard exists because a new certification legitimately supersedes any `DRAFT` or `REJECTED` certification already open on the same model, and no state precondition blocks a second creation — the version pin is the only thing that stops a compliance engineer from superseding a draft they never saw. On acceptance, the model's `version` is itself incremented, so a client that creates two certifications back to back must re-read the model between them.

`POST /mfg/lining-inspections` requires a non-optional `expected_tank_lot_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `TankLot`. If that value does not equal the lot's current `version`, the filing is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Re-inspecting the same inspection type is legitimate and supersedes the previous current record, so again no state precondition blocks it; the version pin is what stops an inspector from overwriting a verdict — or a scrap decision — they never saw. Accepting the inspection increments the lot's `version`, which means the second of two inspections filed against the same lot must carry the version the first one produced.

No other endpoint in this service takes a version field. In particular, none of the action endpoints that drive a state machine accept one — their protection is the state precondition itself.

### 2.8. Request Admission Order

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined: the rules are evaluated in a fixed order and the **first** violated rule wins. Implementers must respect this order, because a great many observable behaviours are precisely the difference between two competing rejections.

The global gate runs first, in this sequence:

1. Authentication — headers present, role declared (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401), on every endpoint except the logical-clock read.
2. Authorisation — the caller's role is listed on this endpoint (`ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, 403), on every endpoint.
3. Path resolution — every `id` in the URL path resolves, left to right, to an existing row of the expected type. A missing row yields that entity's own not-found code: `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPONENT_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FURNACE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GLASS_LINING_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TANK_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LINING_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEST_CELL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EFFICIENCY_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATING_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HEATER_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WARRANTY_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECALL_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_RECALL_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404. For a nested path such as the per-lot inspection listing, the tank lot is resolved before anything else about the request is considered.
4. Schema validation (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400) — required inputs present, JSON types correct, enum members inside their declared sets, numbers inside their declared ranges, strings non-empty and within their maximum lengths, timestamps ISO 8601 with a trailing `Z`, and pagination parameters inside their bounds.
5. Body reference resolution — every foreign-key id supplied in the request **body** resolves to an existing row, yielding that referenced entity's not-found code. This applies to every create endpoint and to every action endpoint that takes an id in its body.
6. Supplier read scope (`ERR_SUPPLIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, 403) — the `SupplierRep` narrowing described in §2.1.
7. Parent version pinning (`ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION`, 412) — the two rules of §2.7. Note that this precedes every state and business check, so a stale pin is reported even on a request that would also have failed a later business rule.
8. State admissibility — the target row is in a state from which the requested transition is declared. Each entity family has its own code: `ERR_INVALID_SUPPLIER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_COMPONENT_LOT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_TANK_LOT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_MODEL_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_TEST_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CERTIFICATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_STATE` and `ERR_INVALID_NOTICE_STATE`, all HTTP 409. A product model carries two independent state dimensions, and the check consults only the dimension the requested action drives — so lifting a restriction on a model that is still `DRAFT`, and suspending the certification of a model that is still `UNCERTIFIED`, both report `ERR_INVALID_MODEL_STATE`.

After the global gate, the business rules are evaluated in the following order. Each is developed fully in §3; this list fixes only their relative precedence.

9. Certification approver/submitter separation — `ERR_SOD_APPROVER_IS_SUBMITTER` (403), on certification approval and certification rejection.
10. Inspector/welder separation — `ERR_SOD_INSPECTOR_IS_WELDER` (403), on lining-inspection filing.
11. Component lot released and supplier qualified — `ERR_COMPONENT_SUPPLIER_NOT_QUALIFIED` (409), on unit assembly, unit hold release, component-lot release and component-lot unblocking.
12. Model certified — `ERR_MODEL_NOT_CERTIFIED` (409), on unit labelling and on releasing a unit hold.
13. Model released to channel — `ERR_MODEL_NOT_RELEASED` (409), on tank-lot welding, unit labelling and unit shipment, including the shipment channel-match check.
14. Component category match — `ERR_COMPONENT_CATEGORY_MISMATCH` (422), on unit staging.
15. Gas-control applicability — `ERR_GAS_CONTROL_REQUIRED` or `ERR_GAS_CONTROL_NOT_APPLICABLE` (both 422), on unit staging.
16. Private-label base consistency — `ERR_PRIVATE_LABEL_BASE_INVALID` (422), on model creation.
17. Lining batch fired — `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FIRED` (409), on lining application.
18. Rework ceiling — `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409), on reline authorisation.
19. Inspection sample adequacy — `ERR_SAMPLE_SIZE_INSUFFICIENT` (422), on lining-inspection filing.
20. Certification test sample adequacy — `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TEST_SAMPLE` (409), on certification submission.
21. Serial range well-formedness — `ERR_SERIAL_RANGE_INVALID` (422), on recall scoping. Because this runs before the population is resolved, an inverted range always reports the range error and never the empty-population error it would otherwise also produce.
22. Recall population non-empty — `ERR_RECALL_SCOPE_EMPTY` (422), on recall scoping.

24. Furnace window exclusivity — `ERR_FURNACE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409), on glass-lining-batch creation.
25. Test-cell capacity and cooldown — `ERR_TEST_CELL_CAPACITY_CONFLICT` or `ERR_TEST_CELL_COOLDOWN_VIOLATION` (both 409), on efficiency-test creation.
26. Window orientation — `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (422), on glass-lining-batch creation and efficiency-test creation.
27. Supplier qualification not expired — `ERR_QUALIFICATION_EXPIRED` (409), on component-lot release and unit assembly.
28. Supplier score floor — `ERR_SUPPLIER_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR` (422), on supplier scoring and supplier requalification.
29. Warranty coverage still live — `ERR_WARRANTY_COVERAGE_EXPIRED` (422), on claim validation.

35. Foam thickness floor — `ERR_FOAM_THICKNESS_BELOW_MINIMUM` (422), on lot foaming.

37. Lot unit capacity — `ERR_LOT_UNIT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409), on unit staging.
38. Firing temperature ceiling — `ERR_FIRING_TEMP_EXCEEDS_FURNACE_MAX` (422), on glass-lining-batch creation.

40. Window elapsed — `ERR_TEST_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED` or `ERR_FIRING_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED` (both 409), on efficiency-test result recording and glass-lining-batch firing respectively.
41. Base model released — `ERR_BASE_MODEL_NOT_RELEASED` (409), on model channel release.

### 2.9. Transitive Containment and Cascade Discipline

Three events in this service are not local: supplier disqualification, rating-certification revocation, and repeated lining failure on one glass-lining batch. Each propagates through the plant in a fixed, numbered sequence of hops, and each hop is caused by the hop before it. Hops are always applied in full inside the same request — this service has no deferred or eventually-consistent propagation — and every row a hop touches has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed. Where a hop visits a set of rows, it visits them in `created_at` ascending order.

Two properties of these cascades matter enough to state globally. First, a cascade **terminates**: the rejections that a batch quarantine induces on other tank lots are marked as cascade-induced and deliberately do not feed back into the counter that triggered the quarantine, so the chain cannot loop. Second, a cascade **does not self-heal**: clearing the condition that caused a hold or a restriction does not automatically undo it. A restricted model returns to `RELEASED` only when an administrator explicitly lifts the restriction, and only once no unit of that model remains held; a blocked component lot returns to `RELEASED` only when a supplier quality manager explicitly unblocks it.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Two separations are enforced on `X-User-ID` values recorded earlier in a workflow.

On the shop floor, the operator who welded a tank lot may not sign that lot's lining inspections. When a lining inspection is filed, the caller's `X-User-ID` is compared against the lot's recorded `weld_operator_user_id`; if they are equal the filing is rejected with `ERR_SOD_INSPECTOR_IS_WELDER` (HTTP 403). The accepted record stores the caller's `X-User-ID` as `inspector_user_id`.

In the compliance office, the engineer who prepared a rating certification may not adjudicate it. Both approval and rejection compare the caller's `X-User-ID` against the certification's stored `submitted_by_user_id`, and reject the call with `ERR_SOD_APPROVER_IS_SUBMITTER` (HTTP 403) when they are equal. This target moves: because a rejected certification can be resubmitted, and the resubmitting engineer overwrites `submitted_by_user_id`, an approver who was eligible in the first round may become ineligible in the second.

### 2.11. Conserved Ledgers and Compensating Actions

Four running totals in this service are invariants, not merely computed fields, and they must hold before and after every single transition.

A tank lot's `planned_quantity` always equals the sum of its `wip_quantity`, `good_quantity` and `scrapped_quantity`. A component lot's `quantity` always equals the sum of its `remaining_quantity` and `consumed_quantity`. A product model's `warranty_reserve_balance_cents` always equals its `warranty_reserve_accrued_cents` minus its `warranty_paid_cents`. And a recall campaign's `notice_count` is always the number of notice rows attached to it, regardless of what state those notices have reached.

Three operations in the service exist purely to reverse an earlier decision while leaving these ledgers exactly as they were before it: reversing a lining rework, disassembling a staged or assembled unit, and reversing a warranty payment. Each is specified as the exact inverse of the operation it undoes, and none of them may leave a ledger in a state the forward operation could not have produced. In particular, no ledger is ever driven negative — a payment larger than the available reserve is refused outright rather than overdrawn.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Supplier Qualification and Incoming Component Control

Nothing enters a water heater without a qualified source. A `SupplierQualityManager` registers a component supplier with its legal `name` (non-empty, at most 120 characters) and the single component family it is approved to ship, given as `component_category` — one of `GAS_VALVE`, `THERMOSTAT`, `TP_RELIEF_VALVE`, `ANODE`, `COMPRESSOR` or `ECO_SWITCH`. Registration also captures the three inputs the plant scores on: `audit_score`, the latest on-site quality-system audit result between 0 and 100 inclusive; `defect_ppm`, the measured defective parts per million on incoming inspection, which may not be negative; and `open_capa_count`, the number of corrective-action requests still open against the supplier, which likewise may not be negative. A newly registered supplier begins at `qualification_status` `PENDING` with no `qualification_score`, no `qualified_until` and no `disqualification_reason_code`.

Scoring is a fresh reading, not a running average. When the supplier quality manager scores a supplier, they resupply `audit_score`, `defect_ppm` and `open_capa_count` together with the `qualified_until` instant the qualification is to run to, and the service computes a weighted score from those three request values alone — no historical score is blended in. The audit component is the `audit_score` as supplied. The parts-per-million component is 100 reduced by the `defect_ppm` divided by 50, with the result floored at 0 so a catastrophically bad supplier contributes zero rather than a negative amount. The corrective-action component is 100 reduced by 12.5 for each open corrective action, again floored at 0 — which means eight or more open actions contribute nothing. The three components are then combined with weights of 0.40 for the audit component, 0.35 for the parts-per-million component and 0.25 for the corrective-action component, and the weighted sum is rounded half up to two decimal places to become `qualification_score`.

Band selection reads the **rounded** score, never the unrounded weighted sum. A score of at least 85.00 earns `QUALIFIED`; a score of at least 70.00 but below 85.00 earns `CONDITIONAL`; anything below 70.00 is refused outright with `ERR_SUPPLIER_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR`, and the same score-floor rule is declared on the requalification action as well as on scoring. The supplied `qualified_until` must be strictly after the current logical clock; a value at or before it is rejected as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Because `qualified_until` is an **inclusive** expiry, a supplier whose `qualified_until` falls exactly on the current logical clock instant still counts as qualified everywhere downstream.

Which transition a scoring call performs depends jointly on the band and on where the supplier already stands. From `PENDING`, a qualifying-band score moves the supplier `PENDING → QUALIFIED` and a conditional-band score moves it `PENDING → CONDITIONAL` — the identical request reaches two different states purely on the score. From `QUALIFIED`, only a conditional-band score is a declared move (`QUALIFIED → CONDITIONAL`); from `CONDITIONAL`, only a qualifying-band score is (`CONDITIONAL → QUALIFIED`). There is deliberately no self-transition: re-scoring an already-`QUALIFIED` supplier into the qualifying band, or an already-`CONDITIONAL` supplier into the conditional band, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SUPPLIER_STATE`, as is any attempt to score a `DISQUALIFIED` supplier. Whichever branch is taken, the accepted call writes `qualification_score` and `qualified_until`, clears `disqualification_reason_code`, and increments the supplier's `version` by 1. One further gate applies only on the way out of `PENDING`: the supplier's `blocked_lot_count` must be 0, and a `PENDING` supplier scored into either band while lots of its remain blocked is refused with `ERR_BLOCKED_LOTS_REMAIN`. That count is derived, not stored: it is the number of the supplier's component lots whose `status` is `BLOCKED`.

Disqualification is the containment event. A `SupplierQualityManager` disqualifies a supplier that is currently `QUALIFIED` or `CONDITIONAL`, supplying a `reason_code`; the supplier moves to `DISQUALIFIED`, that `reason_code` is stored as `disqualification_reason_code`, and `qualified_until` is cleared. Four hops then fire inside the same request. **Hop 1** takes every component lot of that supplier whose `status` is `QUARANTINED` or `RELEASED`, in `created_at` ascending order, to `BLOCKED`, stamping each one's `reject_reason_code` with `SUPPLIER_DISQUALIFIED`. **Hop 2**, caused by hop 1, takes every heater unit whose `build_status` is `COMPONENTS_STAGED`, `ASSEMBLED` or `LABELED` and whose `gas_control_lot_id` or `safety_valve_lot_id` names one of the lots just blocked, again in `created_at` ascending order, to `HELD` with `hold_reason_code` set to `COMPONENT_LOT_BLOCKED`; a unit that was `ASSEMBLED` or `LABELED` records that state in `held_from_status`, while a unit that was `COMPONENTS_STAGED` leaves `held_from_status` null because that is the default state a hold release restores. A unit that was `LABELED` additionally has its `label_status` set to `INVALID`. **Hop 3**, caused by hop 2, has every product model that owns at least one unit held in hop 2 recompute its `held_unit_count`, and any such model whose `release_status` is `RELEASED` moves `RELEASED → RESTRICTED`, in `created_at` ascending order. Every entity touched by hops 1 through 3, and the supplier itself, has its `version` incremented by 1.

Recovery from a disqualification runs in a fixed order, and requalification deliberately comes first. Requalifying a `DISQUALIFIED` supplier — a `SupplierQualityManager` action carrying a `reason_code` — moves it `DISQUALIFIED → PENDING`, clears both `disqualification_reason_code` and `qualification_score`, and increments `version`; it unblocks nothing by itself. The containment gate is not on requalification but on the subsequent scoring step, precisely because requalification is what makes the blocked lots dispositionable at all: unblocking a lot is refused while its supplier is still `DISQUALIFIED`. So the sequence is requalify, then disposition every blocked lot, then score. Requalifying a supplier that is not `DISQUALIFIED` reports `ERR_INVALID_SUPPLIER_STATE`.

Incoming material is booked as a component lot, an action open to both the `SupplierQualityManager` and the `QualityInspector`. The booking names `supplier_id`, the manufacturer `part_number` printed on the lot label (non-empty, at most 40 characters, and unique among that one supplier's lots — a collision reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`), the `category` drawn from the same six-member component-family enumeration, the `quantity` of pieces received which must be at least 1, and optionally `certificate_of_conformance`, a boolean that defaults to `false` when omitted. A lot's `category` records the component family shipped, which is the same family its supplier is approved for, since a supplier is approved for exactly one. Every new lot starts at `status` `QUARANTINED` pending incoming inspection, with no `reject_reason_code`.

A `QualityInspector` releases a quarantined lot to the line once incoming inspection is satisfied, moving it `QUARANTINED → RELEASED` and clearing any `reject_reason_code`. Three conditions guard the release. The lot must carry a signed certificate of conformance — a lot whose `certificate_of_conformance` is `false` cannot be released and the call is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COMPONENT_LOT_STATE`. The owning supplier's `qualification_status` must be `QUALIFIED` or `CONDITIONAL`; a `PENDING` or `DISQUALIFIED` supplier yields `ERR_COMPONENT_SUPPLIER_NOT_QUALIFIED`. And the supplier's `qualified_until` must be at or after the current logical clock — a `qualified_until` strictly before it yields `ERR_QUALIFICATION_EXPIRED`, while a `qualified_until` exactly equal to the current instant passes.

Rejection is the terminal disposition, available on a lot that is either `QUARANTINED` or `BLOCKED` and carrying a `reason_code` that is written to (or overwrites) `reject_reason_code`. Rejecting a `BLOCKED` lot is one of the two ways containment is cleared: a rejected lot is no longer contained, so the supplier's `blocked_lot_count` is recomputed and both the lot's and the supplier's `version` are incremented. The other disposition is unblocking, which a `SupplierQualityManager` performs on a lot whose `status` is `BLOCKED`, returning it `BLOCKED → RELEASED`, clearing `reject_reason_code` and triggering the same `blocked_lot_count` recomputation. Unblocking is the single place in the service where a `PENDING` supplier is acceptable: it requires only that the supplier's `qualification_status` is not `DISQUALIFIED` — that is, that it is `PENDING`, `QUALIFIED` or `CONDITIONAL`, with `ERR_COMPONENT_SUPPLIER_NOT_QUALIFIED` otherwise — and it checks `qualified_until` against the logical clock **only** when the supplier is `QUALIFIED` or `CONDITIONAL`, since a freshly requalified `PENDING` supplier has a null `qualified_until` and the expiry check is skipped entirely. That single exception is what makes the `RELEASED → BLOCKED → RELEASED` cycle reachable at all. Unblocking a lot that is not `BLOCKED`, or releasing one that is already `REJECTED`, reports `ERR_INVALID_COMPONENT_LOT_STATE`.

Every read of a component lot recomputes its piece ledger. `consumed_quantity` is the number of heater units that name this lot as either their `gas_control_lot_id` or their `safety_valve_lot_id` **and** whose `build_status` is one of `ASSEMBLED`, `LABELED`, `SHIPPED` or `HELD`; `remaining_quantity` is the lot's `quantity` less that count. Units still at `COMPONENTS_STAGED` have not drawn their pieces yet, and units that reached `SCRAPPED` have returned theirs, so neither contributes. When an assembly draws the last remaining piece and `remaining_quantity` reaches 0, the lot itself moves `RELEASED → CONSUMED`, a terminal state derived entirely from the unit population that references it. Suppliers can be listed filtered by `qualification_status` or `component_category`; component lots can be listed filtered by `supplier_id`, `category` or `status`.

### 3.2. The Catalogue: Models, Private-Label Variants and Two Independent Postures

A `ComplianceEngineer` owns the model catalogue. Creating a model supplies its `model_code` — the catalogue code, unique across all models, non-empty, at most 24 characters and restricted to uppercase letters, digits and hyphens, which later becomes the model's serial-number prefix — together with `fuel_type` (`GAS`, `ELECTRIC` or `HEAT_PUMP`), `nominal_capacity_gallons` between 20 and 120 inclusive, the distribution `channel` the model is catalogued for (`TRADE`, `RETAIL` or `BUILDER`), and `insulation_r_value`, the declared insulation R-value between 10.0 and 40.0 inclusive. A duplicate `model_code` reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`.

Private labelling is expressed on the model row itself. `is_private_label` is a boolean defaulting to `false`. When it is `true`, the payload must also carry `base_model_id` naming the model being re-branded and `brand_label`, the retailer brand printed on the jacket (at most 40 characters); the named base model must itself have `is_private_label` false and must match the variant's `fuel_type` and `nominal_capacity_gallons` exactly. When `is_private_label` is `false`, `base_model_id` must be absent and so must `brand_label`. Any violation of that shape — a private-label variant without a base, a base that is itself a private label, a base whose fuel type or capacity differs, or a base supplied on a non-private-label model — is rejected with `ERR_PRIVATE_LABEL_BASE_INVALID`. A `base_model_id` that names no existing model resolves as `ERR_PRODUCT_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`.

Every model then evolves along **two independent state dimensions on the same row**, and confusing them is the most common mistake in this domain. The first dimension is `certification_status`, the efficiency-rating posture, which starts at `UNCERTIFIED` and moves through `TEST_PENDING`, `CERTIFIED`, `SUSPENDED` and terminally `DECERTIFIED`. The second is `release_status`, the channel posture, which starts at `DRAFT` and moves through `RELEASED`, `RESTRICTED` and terminally `WITHDRAWN`. Neither dimension gates the other's transitions: a model may be `RELEASED` while still `UNCERTIFIED`, and `CERTIFIED` while still `DRAFT`. The two dimensions are read together in exactly one place in the whole service — the labelling of a heater unit, which demands `CERTIFIED` and `RELEASED` simultaneously.

Channel release is a `ComplianceEngineer` action that moves a model `DRAFT → RELEASED`; a model whose `release_status` is anything other than `DRAFT` reports `ERR_INVALID_MODEL_STATE`. For a private-label variant there is one further guard: the base model's own `release_status` must already be `RELEASED` or `RESTRICTED`. A variant whose base is still `DRAFT`, or already `WITHDRAWN`, is refused with `ERR_BASE_MODEL_NOT_RELEASED`. Withdrawal, also a `ComplianceEngineer` action, carries a `reason_code` and applies to a model whose `release_status` is `RELEASED` or `RESTRICTED`, taking it to the terminal `WITHDRAWN`; withdrawing a model that is still `DRAFT` reports `ERR_INVALID_MODEL_STATE`. Withdrawal fans out down the private-label tree: every private-label variant whose `base_model_id` is this model and whose own `release_status` is `RELEASED` or `RESTRICTED` is withdrawn as well, in `created_at` ascending order, and `version` is incremented on the withdrawn base and on each withdrawn variant.

`RESTRICTED` is never entered by a client request — it is only ever reached as the third hop of a containment cascade, when a model that was `RELEASED` finds that its recomputed `held_unit_count` has become greater than zero. `held_unit_count` is derived: it is the number of heater units of this model whose `build_status` is `HELD`. Returning from restriction is a deliberate administrative act rather than an automatic recovery: a `PlantAdmin` lifts the restriction, moving the model `RESTRICTED → RELEASED`, and the call requires `held_unit_count` to be exactly 0. A model with units still held reports `ERR_HELD_UNITS_REMAIN`; a model that is not `RESTRICTED` at all reports `ERR_INVALID_MODEL_STATE`. The certification dimension is not consulted when lifting a restriction.

On the certification dimension, the model's first recorded efficiency-test result moves it `UNCERTIFIED → TEST_PENDING` automatically. Approval of a rating certification takes it to `CERTIFIED` from `UNCERTIFIED`, `TEST_PENDING` or `SUSPENDED`, and publishes four values onto the model: `uef_rated`, the certified Uniform Energy Factor; `fhr_rated`, the certified first-hour rating in whole gallons; `recovery_rate_rated`, the certified recovery rate in gallons per hour; and `label_serial_prefix`, which is set equal to the model's own `model_code`. All four are null until a first approval lands. A `CertificationApprover` may suspend the certification of a model that is `CERTIFIED`, supplying a `reason_code`; the model moves `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED` and — importantly — the four published values are **retained**, because a suspension does not erase the rating that has already gone out on labels, it only blocks further labelling. Suspending a model whose `certification_status` is not `CERTIFIED` reports `ERR_INVALID_MODEL_STATE`. A suspension is undone by approving a newer certification for the same model, which moves it `SUSPENDED → CERTIFIED` and overwrites `uef_rated`, `fhr_rated` and `recovery_rate_rated` from the newly approved certificate — the `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED → CERTIFIED` cycle. Suspension leaves `release_status` untouched.

`DECERTIFIED` is terminal on the certification dimension and is reached only through revocation of an approved certification, which clears `uef_rated`, `fhr_rated`, `recovery_rate_rated` and `label_serial_prefix` and fires the de-certification cascade described in §3.6. A model may be decertified from either `CERTIFIED` or `SUSPENDED`, with identical effect.

Every model row also carries the running warranty reserve for its population: `warranty_reserve_accrued_cents`, the cumulative accrual booked as its units shipped; `warranty_paid_cents`, the cumulative amount paid out on validated claims; and `warranty_reserve_balance_cents`, which is always exactly the accrual less the payments. Models can be listed filtered by `base_model_id` — the way to enumerate the private-label variants derived from a base — and by `channel`, `fuel_type`, `certification_status` or `release_status`.

### 3.3. Glass-Lining Furnaces and Firing Windows

Furnaces are asset master data, registered by a `PlantAdmin` with a `furnace_code` — plant-unique, non-empty, at most 20 characters, colliding as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` — and a `max_firing_temp_c`, the maximum sustained chamber temperature in Celsius, between 700 and 1000 inclusive. A furnace read reports `occupied_window_count`, derived as the number of glass-lining batches booked on that furnace whose `status` is `MIXED` or `FIRED`.

A `PlantOperator` books a slurry batch onto a furnace by naming `furnace_id`, a plant-unique `batch_code` (non-empty, at most 20 characters), the `slurry_mix_code` recipe code of the glass frit slurry (non-empty, at most 20 characters), the setpoint `firing_temp_c`, and the occupation interval as `firing_start` and `firing_end`. The interval is **half-open**: `firing_start` is inclusive and `firing_end` is exclusive, so a window that begins exactly at another window's `firing_end` is not an overlap and is accepted. Booking a window that does overlap any other non-terminal batch window — that is, any batch on the same furnace still in `MIXED` or `FIRED` — is refused with `ERR_FURNACE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. A window whose `firing_start` is at or after its `firing_end` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`, and a `firing_temp_c` above the furnace's `max_firing_temp_c` is refused with `ERR_FIRING_TEMP_EXCEEDS_FURNACE_MAX`; independently of the furnace, `firing_temp_c` must be at least 700, and a lower value fails schema validation as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Note the precedence among these three: the furnace exclusivity check is evaluated before the window-orientation check, which is in turn evaluated before the temperature ceiling, so a booking that is simultaneously overlapping and inverted reports the overlap.

Every new batch starts at `status` `MIXED`, with `rejected_lot_count` at 0, no `quarantine_reason_code`, and an `assigned_lot_count` derived as the number of tank lots whose `glass_lining_batch_id` names this batch. Marking the firing cycle complete is a `PlantOperator` action that moves the batch `MIXED → FIRED`, and it is refused before the logical clock reaches the batch's own `firing_end` with `ERR_FIRING_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`; the clock exactly at `firing_end` is sufficient. Firing a batch that is not `MIXED` — an already-`FIRED` batch, for instance — reports `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`. Only a `FIRED` batch may be applied to a tank lot.

`QUARANTINED` is the batch's terminal state and is never requested by a client. It is reached when repeated lining failure implicates the slurry itself: `rejected_lot_count` counts the lining inspections that failed **directly** on lots carrying this batch, and once that count reaches 2 the batch moves `FIRED → QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_reason_code` set to `REPEAT_LINING_FAILURE`. Rejections that this quarantine then induces on other lots are explicitly excluded from `rejected_lot_count`, which is what makes the chain terminate rather than loop. Batches can be listed filtered by `furnace_id` or `status`.

### 3.4. Tank Fabrication, Lining Verification and Lot Release

A tank production lot is opened by a `PlantOperator` naming the `product_model_id` whose tanks it fabricates, a plant-unique `lot_code` (non-empty, at most 20 characters, colliding as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`) and a `planned_quantity` between 1 and 1000 inclusive. At creation the whole planned quantity is work in process: `wip_quantity` equals `planned_quantity`, while `good_quantity` and `scrapped_quantity` are both 0. The lot also captures `production_period`, the `YYYY-MM` string taken from the logical clock at the moment of creation, which becomes the cohort key for all later warranty analysis. A new lot begins at `status` `FAB_QUEUED` with `rework_count` 0, `cascade_rejected` false, and `glass_lining_batch_id`, `weld_operator_user_id`, `last_scrap_quantity`, `foam_density_kg_m3`, `foam_thickness_mm` and `achieved_r_value` all null. Its `unit_count` is derived at read time as the number of heater units drawn from this lot whose `build_status` is anything other than `SCRAPPED`.

Welding takes the lot `FAB_QUEUED → WELDED` and pins accountability: the caller's `X-User-ID` is stored as `weld_operator_user_id`, which the later inspection separation rule reads. Welding is refused on a lot whose product model has already been withdrawn from all channels, reported as `ERR_MODEL_NOT_RELEASED`; welding a lot that is not `FAB_QUEUED` — welding twice, for example — reports `ERR_INVALID_TANK_LOT_STATE`.

Applying the glass-lining slurry is a `PlantOperator` action carrying the `glass_lining_batch_id` of the batch to draw from. It is accepted on a lot that is `WELDED` or `RELINE_QUEUED`, and in both cases moves the lot to `LINED`, writes the named batch onto `glass_lining_batch_id`, recomputes that batch's `assigned_lot_count` and sets `cascade_rejected` back to `false`. The named batch must be in `status` `FIRED`; anything else is refused with `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FIRED`. On a re-application the batch may legitimately differ from the one used before, and the new batch is the one whose `assigned_lot_count` is recomputed — this `WELDED → LINED` / `RELINE_QUEUED → LINED` pair is what closes the rework cycle `LINED → LINING_REJECTED → RELINE_QUEUED → LINED`.

Lining quality is measured by two independent inspections, both filed by a `QualityInspector` against a lot that is `LINED`. Each filing names `tank_lot_id`, an `inspection_type` of either `ADHESION` or `HOLIDAY`, a `sample_size` of at least 1 tank pulled from the lot, and the `expected_tank_lot_version` pin described in §2.7. An `ADHESION` record must carry `adhesion_class`, the worst adhesion class observed in the sample on a scale where 1 is best and 5 is worst, and must omit `holiday_count`; a `HOLIDAY` record must carry `holiday_count`, the total number of coating discontinuities found across the whole sample, which may not be negative, and must omit `adhesion_class`. Supplying the wrong one of the pair, or omitting the required one, fails schema validation as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Filing an inspection against a lot that is not `LINED` reports `ERR_INVALID_TANK_LOT_STATE`, and a caller whose `X-User-ID` equals the lot's `weld_operator_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SOD_INSPECTOR_IS_WELDER`.

Sampling adequacy is checked before any verdict is computed, and an inadequate sample is never allowed to masquerade as a failure. The required sample is the larger of 3 tanks and five percent of the lot's `planned_quantity` rounded up to a whole tank; a `sample_size` below that figure is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_SIZE_INSUFFICIENT` rather than producing a `FAIL` result. For a `HOLIDAY` record the service then computes `holidays_per_tank` as the `holiday_count` divided by the `sample_size`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and sets `result` to `FAIL` when that density is **strictly greater** than 0.50 and to `PASS` otherwise — a density of exactly 0.5000 passes. For an `ADHESION` record `holidays_per_tank` is null and `result` is `FAIL` when `adhesion_class` is greater than 2, `PASS` otherwise. Only the single record under evaluation contributes; earlier superseded records never feed the verdict. When the computed `result` is `FAIL` the filing must additionally carry `scrap_quantity`, the number of tanks condemned by this inspection, which must be at least 1 and at most the lot's current `wip_quantity`; when the result is `PASS`, `scrap_quantity` must be omitted. A `scrap_quantity` outside that range, or present on a passing record, or absent from a failing one, is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Every accepted record stores the caller's `X-User-ID` as `inspector_user_id` and enters at `record_status` `CURRENT`; any earlier record of the **same** `inspection_type` on the same lot moves to `record_status` `SUPERSEDED`. Records are never mutated afterwards, which is why they carry no `updated_at` and no `version`.

The lot's own state is then derived from the aggregate verdict of its current records. When the lot holds exactly one `CURRENT` `ADHESION` record with `result` `PASS` **and** exactly one `CURRENT` `HOLIDAY` record with `result` `PASS`, it moves `LINED → LINING_VERIFIED`. When instead the newly filed record's `result` is `FAIL`, the lot moves `LINED → LINING_REJECTED` and a four-hop chain fires inside the same request. **Hop 1** charges the scrap to the lot: `scrap_quantity` is added to `scrapped_quantity` and subtracted from `wip_quantity`, `last_scrap_quantity` is set to that same `scrap_quantity`, `rework_count` is increased by 1, and `cascade_rejected` is set to `false`. **Hop 2**, caused by hop 1, increases the lot's glass-lining batch's `rejected_lot_count` by 1. **Hop 3**, caused by hop 2, quarantines that batch if its `rejected_lot_count` has now reached 2, moving it `FIRED → QUARANTINED` with `quarantine_reason_code` set to `REPEAT_LINING_FAILURE`. **Hop 4**, caused by hop 3, takes every **other** tank lot carrying that same batch whose `status` is `LINED`, `LINING_VERIFIED` or `FOAMED`, in `created_at` ascending order, to `LINING_REJECTED` — each with `cascade_rejected` set to `true` and `rework_count` increased by 1, but with `scrapped_quantity` and `wip_quantity` left exactly as they were and `last_scrap_quantity` left null, since no tanks were condemned by an inspection of those lots. These cascade-induced rejections do **not** increase `rejected_lot_count`, so no further quarantine can follow from them. `version` is incremented by 1 on every entity any of the four hops touched.

From `LINING_REJECTED` a lot has three ways out. A `PlantOperator` may authorise another lining cycle, moving it `LINING_REJECTED → RELINE_QUEUED` and clearing `last_scrap_quantity`; this is refused with `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` when `rework_count` has already reached 2, since a lot may go around the loop at most twice, and refused with `ERR_INVALID_TANK_LOT_STATE` when nothing remains in process (`wip_quantity` below 1). Alternatively a `QualityInspector` may reverse the rejection outright, a compensating action carrying a `reason_code` that returns the lot `LINING_REJECTED → LINED`. Reversal is available **only** when the rejection was this lot's own direct inspection failure — `cascade_rejected` must be `false` — and only while `last_scrap_quantity` is not null and the lot's glass-lining batch is still `FIRED`; a cascade-rejected lot, a lot with nothing outstanding to reverse, or a lot whose batch has been quarantined is refused with `ERR_REWORK_REVERSAL_NOT_ALLOWED`. The reversal is the exact inverse of hops 1 and 2: `last_scrap_quantity` is added back to `wip_quantity` and subtracted from `scrapped_quantity`, `last_scrap_quantity` is then set to null, `rework_count` is decreased by 1, and the batch's `rejected_lot_count` is decreased by 1. The failing inspection record itself keeps `record_status` `CURRENT` even though the lot has returned to `LINED`, and `version` is incremented on both the lot and the batch. Finally a `QualityInspector` may scrap the lot, a terminal move `LINING_REJECTED → SCRAPPED` carrying a `reason_code`, which adds the entire remaining `wip_quantity` to `scrapped_quantity`, sets `wip_quantity` to 0 and leaves `good_quantity` untouched.

A verified lot proceeds to insulation. Foaming is a `PlantOperator` action on a lot that is `LINING_VERIFIED`, supplying `foam_density_kg_m3`, the insulation foam density, and `foam_thickness_mm`, the foam wall thickness in whole millimetres. The thickness floor is checked first: a `foam_thickness_mm` below 40 is refused with `ERR_FOAM_THICKNESS_BELOW_MINIMUM`. The service then derives `achieved_r_value` as the `foam_thickness_mm` multiplied by 0.4 R-value units per millimetre, rounded half up to one decimal place, and compares it against the model's declared `insulation_r_value`; an achieved value below the declared value is refused with `ERR_INSULATION_R_VALUE_SHORTFALL`, while an achieved value exactly equal to it passes. Because the thickness floor is evaluated ahead of this comparison, a lot foamed at, say, 30 mm reports the thickness error even though it would also have failed the R-value comparison. `foam_density_kg_m3` is recorded verbatim and plays no part in either check. On acceptance the lot moves `LINING_VERIFIED → FOAMED` with `foam_density_kg_m3`, `foam_thickness_mm` and the computed `achieved_r_value` all stored.

Jacket assembly then moves the lot `FOAMED → JACKETED`, and lot release moves it `JACKETED → RELEASED`, which is where the quantity ledger settles: `good_quantity` takes the value of `wip_quantity` and `wip_quantity` becomes 0, leaving the planned total still exactly split across the three counters. Release requires at least one tank still in process, and both actions report `ERR_INVALID_TANK_LOT_STATE` when the lot is not in the state they expect — releasing an unjacketed lot, for instance. Tank lots can be listed filtered by `product_model_id`, `glass_lining_batch_id`, `production_period` or `status`. Inspection records are readable one at a time by `id`, and the per-lot sub-resource listing returns every record ever written against a lot, `CURRENT` and `SUPERSEDED` alike, in `created_at` ascending order, optionally narrowed by `inspection_type` or `record_status`; it is the only channel through which superseded records, and the records belonging to lots swept up by a batch-quarantine cascade, can be discovered.

### 3.5. The Efficiency Test Programme

Test cells are the second class of asset master data. A `PlantAdmin` registers one with a plant-unique `cell_code` (non-empty, at most 20 characters, colliding as `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`), a `parallel_slots` count between 1 and 8 inclusive stating how many test windows the cell may run overlapping at one instant, and a `cooldown_hours` value between 0 and 72 inclusive giving the mandatory thermal recovery buffer the cell needs between two tests of the **same** product model.

A `ComplianceEngineer` schedules a test by naming the `product_model_id` under test, the `test_cell_id` whose window it occupies, the `test_type` — `UEF`, `FIRST_HOUR_RATING` or `RECOVERY` — the `sample_size` of production units drawn for the run (between 1 and 12 inclusive), and the occupation interval as `window_start` and `window_end`. As with furnace windows the interval is half-open, with `window_start` inclusive and `window_end` exclusive. Two cell constraints are checked together, ahead of the window-orientation check: the requested window must leave a free slot, meaning the number of non-terminal tests on that cell whose windows overlap the requested one must stay within `parallel_slots`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_TEST_CELL_CAPACITY_CONFLICT`; and the requested `window_start` must be at least `cooldown_hours` after the end of that cell's previous window for the **same** product model, or the booking is refused with `ERR_TEST_CELL_COOLDOWN_VIOLATION`. A `window_start` at or after its `window_end` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`, evaluated after both cell checks. Every scheduled test starts at `status` `SCHEDULED` with `measured_value`, `effective_minimum`, `margin` and `recorded_at` all null.

Recording the outcome is a `QualityInspector` action carrying the single instrument reading as `measured_value` — dimensionless for a `UEF` run, gallons for a `FIRST_HOUR_RATING` run, gallons per hour for a `RECOVERY` run. The call is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TEST_STATE` unless the test is still `SCHEDULED`, and with `ERR_TEST_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED` unless the logical clock has reached the test's own `window_end`; the clock exactly at `window_end` is sufficient.

For a `UEF` run the applicable floor is built in five steps, in this order and no other. **First**, the capacity tier is selected from the model: a `nominal_capacity_gallons` of 55 or less puts the model in the small tier, anything above 55 in the large tier. **Second**, the base federal minimum is looked up on both `fuel_type` and tier: a `GAS` model requires 0.58 in the small tier and 0.55 in the large tier; an `ELECTRIC` model requires 0.92 small and 1.90 large; a `HEAT_PUMP` model requires 2.20 small and 2.90 large. **Third**, a sample derate is added, keyed on the `sample_size` clamped at 3 — a single-unit sample adds 0.02, a two-unit sample adds 0.01, and a sample of three or more units adds nothing at all. **Fourth**, an insulation credit is subtracted: take the model's `insulation_r_value`, subtract 16, floor the result at 0, multiply what remains by 0.005 per R-value point, and cap the credit at 0.03. **Fifth**, `effective_minimum` is the base minimum plus the derate minus the credit, rounded half up to four decimal places; the derate is never reapplied after the credit has been taken.

For a `FIRST_HOUR_RATING` or `RECOVERY` run the floor is a flat constant and neither the model's `fuel_type` nor its `nominal_capacity_gallons` is consulted: `effective_minimum` is 30.0 gallons for a first-hour-rating run and 10.0 gallons per hour for a recovery run.

In all three cases `margin` is then the `measured_value` less the `effective_minimum`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and the outcome branches on its sign: a `margin` of 0 or more moves the test `SCHEDULED → PASSED`, while a negative `margin` moves it `SCHEDULED → FAILED`. A margin of exactly 0.0000 passes. Both branches store `measured_value`, `effective_minimum`, `margin` and `recorded_at`, the latter being the logical-clock instant at which the result was accepted, and both have the identical secondary effect on the catalogue: if the model's `certification_status` is still `UNCERTIFIED`, it moves `UNCERTIFIED → TEST_PENDING`. Only the target test state and the model's later certification eligibility differ between the two branches. `VOIDED` is the third terminal state and is never client-requested; a `SCHEDULED` test is voided only by the de-certification cascade of §3.6. Tests can be listed filtered by `product_model_id`, `test_cell_id`, `test_type` or `status`.

### 3.6. Rating Certification, Publication and Revocation

A rating certification is the document that turns test data into a published label. A `ComplianceEngineer` opens one against a model by naming `product_model_id` together with the `expected_product_model_version` pin of §2.7; the new certificate enters at `status` `DRAFT` with `certified_uef`, `certified_fhr`, `certified_recovery`, `uef_test_count`, `submitted_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, `effective_date`, `label_serial_prefix` and `reason_code` all null. Opening a certificate advances the model's own `version`, and it supersedes the model's earlier paperwork: any certification of the same model still sitting at `DRAFT` or at `REJECTED` moves to the terminal `SUPERSEDED`, with its `version` incremented.

Submission is the compliance engineer's assertion that the test programme is complete, and it is accepted from either `DRAFT` or `REJECTED` — the `SUBMITTED → REJECTED → SUBMITTED` revision loop. It requires the model to hold at least 3 `PASSED` `UEF` tests, at least one `PASSED` `FIRST_HOUR_RATING` test and at least one `PASSED` `RECOVERY` test; a model short on any of the three is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TEST_SAMPLE`, and on a resubmission all three counts are re-evaluated afresh against the model's current passed tests rather than reusing the earlier figures.

The certified ratings are then computed, and the rounding order here is pinned because reversing it publishes a different number. Only `PASSED` tests contribute — `SCHEDULED`, `FAILED` and `VOIDED` tests are excluded from every mean. For the Uniform Energy Factor, take each passing `UEF` test's `measured_value`, round it half up to two decimal places, sum those rounded per-test values, divide by the number of passing `UEF` tests, and round that mean half up to two decimal places again; the result is `certified_uef`, and the count of contributing tests is stored as `uef_test_count`. For the first-hour rating, do the same over the passing `FIRST_HOUR_RATING` tests but round each test to a whole gallon before averaging and round the mean to a whole gallon, giving `certified_fhr`. For the recovery rate, round each passing `RECOVERY` test to one decimal place before averaging and round the mean to one decimal place, giving `certified_recovery`. Averaging the raw readings and rounding only once at the end is forbidden: three `UEF` readings of 0.585, 0.585 and 0.584 yield 0.59 under the pinned order and 0.58 under the forbidden one. Submission also stores the caller's `X-User-ID` as `submitted_by_user_id`; on a resubmission it **overwrites** that value with the resubmitting engineer's id and clears `reason_code`, which is why the approver eligible in one round may not be eligible in the next.

Adjudication belongs exclusively to a `CertificationApprover`, and both outcomes require the caller's `X-User-ID` to differ from `submitted_by_user_id`, failing with `ERR_SOD_APPROVER_IS_SUBMITTER`. Approval takes the certificate `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`. The approval payload carries an `effective_date`, but the value the service stores as the certificate's `effective_date` is the logical-clock instant at which the approval was accepted. Approval also stores `approved_by_user_id` and stamps `label_serial_prefix` on the certificate equal to the model's `model_code`, then publishes onto the model itself: the model's `certification_status` moves to `CERTIFIED` from whichever of `UNCERTIFIED`, `TEST_PENDING` or `SUSPENDED` it occupied, and `certified_uef`, `certified_fhr`, `certified_recovery` and `label_serial_prefix` are copied onto the model's `uef_rated`, `fhr_rated`, `recovery_rate_rated` and `label_serial_prefix` respectively. Any certification of the same model that was already `APPROVED` becomes `SUPERSEDED`. `version` is incremented on the certificate and on the model.

Rejection takes the certificate `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, stores the supplied `reason_code`, and clears the computed `certified_uef`, `certified_fhr` and `certified_recovery` so that a resubmission must recompute them. Its effect on the catalogue is asymmetric and deliberate: a model whose `certification_status` was `UNCERTIFIED` or `TEST_PENDING` is returned to `TEST_PENDING`, while a model that is already `CERTIFIED` or `SUSPENDED` is left entirely untouched — a rejected revision must not strip a rating that is already lawfully published. `version` is incremented on the certificate, and on the model only when the model actually changed. Approving a certificate that is not `SUBMITTED` — a `DRAFT`, for instance — and revoking one that is `SUPERSEDED` both report `ERR_INVALID_CERTIFICATION_STATE`.

Revocation is the compliance emergency, available to a `CertificationApprover` on an `APPROVED` certificate and carrying a `reason_code`. The certificate moves to the terminal `REVOKED` and four hops fire inside the same request. **Hop 1** takes the model's `certification_status` to the terminal `DECERTIFIED`, from either `CERTIFIED` or `SUSPENDED` with identical effect, and clears `uef_rated`, `fhr_rated`, `recovery_rate_rated` and `label_serial_prefix`. **Hop 2**, caused by hop 1, takes every heater unit of that model whose `build_status` is `ASSEMBLED` or `LABELED`, in `created_at` ascending order, to `HELD` with `hold_reason_code` set to `MODEL_DECERTIFIED` and `held_from_status` set to the state the unit was in; units that were `LABELED` additionally have `label_status` set to `INVALID`, since the label they carry now advertises a rating that no longer exists. **Hop 3**, caused by hop 2, has the model recompute `held_unit_count` and, when its `release_status` is `RELEASED`, move `RELEASED → RESTRICTED`. **Hop 4**, caused by hop 1, takes every `SCHEDULED` efficiency test of that model to `VOIDED`, since there is no longer a live certification for them to support. Every entity touched by the four hops has its `version` incremented by 1. Certifications can be listed filtered by `product_model_id` or `status`.

### 3.7. Unit Staging, Assembly, Serialisation and Shipment

A heater unit is the junction where a tank, a catalogue model and two component lots meet. A `PlantOperator` stages one by naming the `tank_lot_id` the tank came from, the `safety_valve_lot_id` supplying its temperature-and-pressure relief valve — required for every fuel type without exception — and, where the fuel type calls for it, the `gas_control_lot_id` supplying its gas control valve. The unit's `product_model_id` is **never** supplied by the client: it is inherited from the named tank lot at creation, as is `production_period`, which is copied verbatim from the lot's own cohort key. A newly staged unit sits at `build_status` `COMPONENTS_STAGED` with `label_status` `NONE`, and with `serial_number`, `held_from_status`, `install_water_hardness_gpg`, `water_hardness_band`, `hardness_factor`, `shipped_at`, `months_in_service` and `hold_reason_code` all null.

Three checks guard staging, in this order. First the component families must match the slots they are being fitted to: `gas_control_lot_id` must name a lot whose `category` is `GAS_VALVE` and `safety_valve_lot_id` a lot whose `category` is `TP_RELIEF_VALVE`, or the staging is refused with `ERR_COMPONENT_CATEGORY_MISMATCH`. Then fuel-type applicability is enforced: a unit staged for a model whose `fuel_type` is `GAS` must supply `gas_control_lot_id`, and omitting it is refused with `ERR_GAS_CONTROL_REQUIRED`; a unit staged for an `ELECTRIC` or `HEAT_PUMP` model must **not** supply one, and doing so is refused with `ERR_GAS_CONTROL_NOT_APPLICABLE`. Finally the tank lot's output is capped: the number of units already drawn from that tank lot must be strictly less than its `good_quantity`, and one unit too many is refused with `ERR_LOT_UNIT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. That last check is also why a lot must be through its whole fabrication sequence before any unit can be staged from it — an unreleased lot has a `good_quantity` of 0 and so admits no units at all.

Assembly draws the pieces. A `PlantOperator` assembles a unit that is `COMPONENTS_STAGED`, moving it `COMPONENTS_STAGED → ASSEMBLED`, and the call demands that the source tank lot be in `status` `RELEASED` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TANK_LOT_STATE`), that **every** referenced component lot be in `status` `RELEASED` with at least one piece of `remaining_quantity` left, and that every referenced lot's supplier be `QUALIFIED` or `CONDITIONAL`. A lot that is not `RELEASED` — including one that has already reached `CONSUMED` because its last piece was drawn — or a supplier that is `PENDING` or `DISQUALIFIED`, is refused with `ERR_COMPONENT_SUPPLIER_NOT_QUALIFIED`; a supplier whose `qualified_until` is strictly before the current logical clock is refused with `ERR_QUALIFICATION_EXPIRED`, while a `qualified_until` exactly at the current instant is accepted. On acceptance each referenced lot recomputes `consumed_quantity` and `remaining_quantity`, and any lot whose `remaining_quantity` thereby reaches 0 moves `RELEASED → CONSUMED`; `version` is incremented on the unit and on each referenced lot. Assembling a unit that is not `COMPONENTS_STAGED` reports `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_STATE`.

Labelling is the moment the two catalogue dimensions are read together, and it is the only such moment in the service. A `PlantOperator` labels a unit that is `ASSEMBLED`, and the unit's model must have `certification_status` `CERTIFIED` — anything else, including `SUSPENDED` and `DECERTIFIED`, is refused with `ERR_MODEL_NOT_CERTIFIED` — and `release_status` `RELEASED`, with anything else refused as `ERR_MODEL_NOT_RELEASED`. The unit then moves `ASSEMBLED → LABELED` with `label_status` set to `APPLIED` and a freshly minted `serial_number`.

The serial is built from three parts joined by hyphens. The first part is the model's `label_serial_prefix`, which is the model's own `model_code` as stamped at certification approval. The second is the unit's `production_period` with its hyphen removed, so a cohort key of `2026-08` contributes `202608`. The third is a sequence number, left-padded with zeros to a width of five digits, whose value is one more than the count of units that share this unit's `product_model_id` **and** its `production_period` **and** already hold a non-null `serial_number`. The sequence is therefore assigned in labelling order, it is scoped to one model and one production month, it never consults the entity `id`, and it never reuses a number even after an already-labelled unit has been scrapped. Labelling a unit that is not `ASSEMBLED` — a still-staged unit, for instance — reports `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_STATE`.

Shipment carries two inputs that only make sense at the loading dock: `install_water_hardness_gpg`, the field water hardness in grains per gallon at the installation site, and `distribution_channel`, the channel the unit is going out through. A `PlantOperator` ships a unit whose `build_status` is `LABELED` and whose `label_status` is `APPLIED`, moving it `LABELED → SHIPPED`; a unit whose label was invalidated by a de-certification cannot be shipped and reports `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_STATE`. The model must still have `release_status` `RELEASED`, and the supplied `distribution_channel` must equal the model's own `channel`; either failure is reported as `ERR_MODEL_NOT_RELEASED`. Shipment records `shipped_at` as the logical-clock instant, stores `install_water_hardness_gpg`, and derives the water-chemistry pair that all later reliability analysis leans on.

Water chemistry is banded with boundaries that must be read exactly as written. A hardness below 3.5 grains per gallon is `SOFT`; from 3.5 up to and including 7.0 is `MODERATE`; above 7.0 up to and including 10.5 is `HARD`; above 10.5 is `VERY_HARD`. So 3.5 belongs to `MODERATE`, 7.0 belongs to `MODERATE`, and 10.5 belongs to `HARD`. The band is stored as `water_hardness_band` and its exposure multiplier as `hardness_factor`: 0.80 for `SOFT`, 1.00 for `MODERATE`, 1.35 for `HARD` and 1.80 for `VERY_HARD`. A claim's own reported hardness never overwrites the value captured here.

Shipment is also the accrual event for the warranty reserve. On each shipment the unit's product model adds a fixed per-unit accrual to both `warranty_reserve_accrued_cents` and `warranty_reserve_balance_cents`, keyed on the model's `fuel_type`: 42,000 cents — that is, 420 dollars — for a `GAS` model, 31,000 cents for an `ELECTRIC` model, and 98,000 cents for a `HEAT_PUMP` model. `version` is incremented on the unit and on the model. A subsequent hold never claws the accrual back, and a unit that was scrapped before shipping never accrued at all.

From shipment onward a unit read reports `months_in_service`, recomputed on every read as the whole number of 30-day months that have elapsed between `shipped_at` and the current logical clock, truncated downward and never rounded up; it stays null while `shipped_at` is null. This figure is derived from the logical clock alone and never from the host wall clock.

Holds are the containment mechanism at unit level, and they are never client-initiated. A unit reaches `HELD` from `COMPONENTS_STAGED`, `ASSEMBLED` or `LABELED` as hop 2 of a supplier disqualification, from `ASSEMBLED` or `LABELED` as hop 2 of a de-certification, or from `LABELED` as part of a recall announcement. In every case `hold_reason_code` records the cause — `COMPONENT_LOT_BLOCKED`, `MODEL_DECERTIFIED` or `RECALL_SCOPE` — and `held_from_status` records the state to return to, taking the value `ASSEMBLED` or `LABELED` and staying null when the unit was `COMPONENTS_STAGED`, since that is the state a hold release restores by default. A supplier disqualification and a de-certification each additionally set `label_status` to `INVALID` on a unit that was `LABELED`; a recall-scope hold is the one cause that leaves `label_status` exactly as it found it.

Releasing a hold is a `PlantOperator` action, and it restores the exact `build_status` the unit was held from — three symmetrical branches with individually different guards. When `held_from_status` is `ASSEMBLED` the unit returns `HELD → ASSEMBLED`, and every referenced component lot must be back in `status` `RELEASED`, failing with `ERR_COMPONENT_SUPPLIER_NOT_QUALIFIED`, while the model's `certification_status` must not be `DECERTIFIED`, failing with `ERR_MODEL_NOT_CERTIFIED`. When `held_from_status` is `LABELED` the unit returns `HELD → LABELED`; here the component lots are not re-examined, but `label_status` must still be `APPLIED` — a unit whose label was invalidated can never be restored to `LABELED` and reports `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_STATE` — and the model's `certification_status` must again not be `DECERTIFIED`, failing with `ERR_MODEL_NOT_CERTIFIED`. When `held_from_status` is null the unit returns `HELD → COMPONENTS_STAGED`, and every referenced component lot must be `RELEASED`, failing with `ERR_COMPONENT_SUPPLIER_NOT_QUALIFIED`. All three branches clear `hold_reason_code`, clear `held_from_status`, have the owning product model recompute `held_unit_count`, and increment `version` on both the unit and the model. Releasing a hold on a unit that is not `HELD` reports `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_STATE`.

Disassembly is the compensating action on the unit side. A `PlantOperator` disassembles a unit whose `build_status` is `COMPONENTS_STAGED`, `ASSEMBLED` or `HELD`, supplying a `reason_code` which is stored as `hold_reason_code`, and the unit moves to the terminal `SCRAPPED`. Each referenced component lot recomputes `consumed_quantity` and `remaining_quantity`, recovering the piece the unit had drawn, so that the lot's piece ledger ends exactly where it started. One asymmetry is deliberate: a lot that had already reached the terminal `CONSUMED` is **not** resurrected by the return and stays `CONSUMED` even though its `remaining_quantity` is now at least 1. When the disassembled unit was `HELD`, the owning model recomputes `held_unit_count`, and even when that count falls to 0 the model remains `RESTRICTED` until an administrator explicitly lifts the restriction. `version` is incremented on the unit, on each referenced component lot, and on the model where the model was touched. Shipping a `HELD` unit, or disassembling one that has already shipped, both report `ERR_INVALID_UNIT_STATE`. Units can be listed filtered by `tank_lot_id`, `product_model_id`, `gas_control_lot_id`, `safety_valve_lot_id`, `build_status`, `production_period` or `serial_number`.

### 3.8. Warranty Adjudication, the Reserve Ledger and Field Failure Analysis

A warranty claim is opened by a `WarrantyAnalyst` against a unit in the field. The claim names its `heater_unit_id`, a `failure_mode` drawn from `TANK_LEAK`, `ANODE_DEPLETION`, `THERMOSTAT`, `GAS_VALVE`, `COMPRESSOR` and `OTHER`, and a `reported_water_hardness_gpg` between 0.0 and 60.0 inclusive, which is the hardness the field technician measured at the site and which is recorded for reference only — it never overrides the `hardness_factor` frozen at shipment. The unit must be in service, meaning its `build_status` is `SHIPPED` and its `shipped_at` is not null; a claim against a staged, assembled, labelled, held or scrapped unit is refused with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_IN_SERVICE`. At creation the claim freezes `months_in_service_at_report` from the unit's elapsed service months at that moment, and that frozen number — not the value the unit reports later — is what coverage is decided on. A new claim opens in `status` `OPEN` with `coverage_pct`, `approved_payout_cents`, `paid_at` and `reason_code` all null.

Validation is where the coverage question is answered, and a `WarrantyAnalyst` performs it on a claim in `status` `OPEN`, moving it `OPEN → VALIDATED`. Coverage depends jointly on the failure mode and on `months_in_service_at_report`. The two tank-related modes carry the long warranty: for `TANK_LEAK` and `ANODE_DEPLETION`, coverage is the full 1.00 when the frozen service months are 72 or fewer, drops to a half share of 0.50 when they are more than 72 but 96 or fewer, and falls to 0.00 beyond 96. The three component modes carry a short warranty: for `THERMOSTAT`, `GAS_VALVE` and `COMPRESSOR`, coverage is 1.00 when the frozen service months are 12 or fewer and 0.00 otherwise. `OTHER` is never covered and always yields 0.00. Whenever the resolved coverage is 0.00 the validation is refused with `ERR_WARRANTY_COVERAGE_EXPIRED` and the claim stays `OPEN`; note that a claim can therefore be denied for being out of warranty, but it can never be *validated* at zero coverage.

The part allowance is a fixed catalogue figure keyed on the failure mode: 41,500 cents for both `TANK_LEAK` and `ANODE_DEPLETION`, 4,200 cents for `THERMOSTAT`, 8,200 cents for `GAS_VALVE`, 52,000 cents for `COMPRESSOR`, and 0 for `OTHER`. Labour is a flat 12,525 cents and freight a flat 3,325 cents for every claim regardless of mode. The payout is then assembled line by line, and the order of rounding is pinned: multiply the part allowance by the coverage share and round that product to whole cents, half up; multiply the labour allowance by the coverage share and round that product to whole cents, half up; multiply the freight allowance by the coverage share and round that product to whole cents, half up; and only then add the three rounded line amounts together. Rounding the sum of unrounded products is not equivalent and is not accepted. The three rounded lines are working figures that are not persisted individually; only their total is stored, as `approved_payout_cents`, alongside the resolved share in `coverage_pct`. At the half share of 0.50 the labour line of 12,525 cents halves to 6,262.5 cents and rounds half up to 6,263, and the freight line of 3,325 cents halves to 1,662.5 and rounds half up to 1,663 — the two places where the half-up direction is directly observable.

Denial is the alternative disposition, also a `WarrantyAnalyst` action from `status` `OPEN`, moving the claim `OPEN → DENIED` with a `reason_code` recorded from the request. Denial is unconditional in the coverage sense — it does not require that coverage have expired — and it explicitly writes `coverage_pct` to 0.00 and `approved_payout_cents` to 0, so a denied claim carries no residual monetary intent. `DENIED` is terminal.

Payment draws on the model's reserve. A `WarrantyAnalyst` pays a claim in `status` `VALIDATED`, moving it `VALIDATED → PAID`, and the claim's `approved_payout_cents` must be less than or equal to the `warranty_reserve_balance_cents` of the product model of the claim's unit; a payout exactly equal to the balance is allowed and drains the reserve to zero, while one cent more is refused with `ERR_WARRANTY_RESERVE_INSUFFICIENT` and the claim stays `VALIDATED`. On payment the claim records `paid_at` as the logical-clock instant, and the model's `warranty_reserve_balance_cents` is reduced by the payout while `warranty_paid_cents` is increased by it; `version` is incremented on both claim and model. `warranty_reserve_accrued_cents` is not touched by payment: it is a lifetime accrual figure that only shipments can raise.

Reversing a payment is the compensating action, performed by a `WarrantyAnalyst` on a claim in `status` `PAID` with a `reason_code`, and it returns the claim `PAID → VALIDATED` so it can be corrected and paid again. The reversal clears `paid_at` back to null, records the `reason_code`, adds the payout back to the model's `warranty_reserve_balance_cents` and subtracts it from `warranty_paid_cents`, and again leaves `warranty_reserve_accrued_cents` untouched. Because the accrual figure is never rewound, `warranty_reserve_balance_cents` always equals `warranty_reserve_accrued_cents` minus `warranty_paid_cents` no matter how many payments and reversals a model has seen. The ledger is never driven negative. Attempting to validate a claim that is not `OPEN`, to pay one that is not `VALIDATED`, or to reverse one that is not `PAID`, all report `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`. Claims can be listed filtered by `heater_unit_id`, `failure_mode` or `status`.

Field failure analysis is a computed read on a product model, available to a `WarrantyAnalyst`, a `ComplianceEngineer` or a `PlantAdmin`, and it is the report the plant uses to decide whether a tank problem is real or an artefact of where the units were installed. It takes a mandatory `production_period` query parameter — omitting it is a malformed request reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and an optional `variant` selecting between the two denominators, which takes the value `COHORT_NAIVE` or `EXPOSURE_WEIGHTED` and defaults to the naive cohort view when it is omitted. The cohort is the set of units of that model whose `build_status` is `SHIPPED` and whose `production_period` matches, enumerated in `created_at` ascending order. Units that never shipped, and units that shipped and were later held, are outside the cohort.

The report echoes `production_period` and `variant` and returns `shipped_unit_count` as the size of the cohort. `tank_leak_claim_count` counts the claims against cohort units whose `failure_mode` is `TANK_LEAK` and whose `status` is `VALIDATED` or `PAID`; claims still in `OPEN` and claims in `DENIED` are excluded from both variants, so a reversal from `PAID` back to `VALIDATED` leaves the count unchanged while a denial removes a claim from it. `weighted_exposure_unit_months` is the sum, across the cohort, of each unit's `months_in_service` multiplied by its `hardness_factor`, rounded to two decimal places half up, and it is the exposure measure that makes a soft-water unit count for less than a very-hard-water unit of the same age.

`failure_rate_ppm` is then reported in parts per million to one decimal place, rounded half up, and this is the only figure the two variants disagree on. The `COHORT_NAIVE` variant — the field-reporting view, and the one used when `variant` is omitted — divides `tank_leak_claim_count` by `shipped_unit_count` and multiplies by 1,000,000, treating every shipped unit as one unit of equal risk regardless of age or water chemistry. The `EXPOSURE_WEIGHTED` variant — the reliability-engineering view — divides the same `tank_leak_claim_count` by `weighted_exposure_unit_months` and multiplies by 1,000,000, expressing the rate per million weighted unit-months instead. Whenever the selected variant's denominator is zero — an empty cohort in the naive case, or a cohort in which every unit has zero elapsed months in the weighted case — the rate is reported as exactly 0.0 rather than as an error or an infinity. The report closes with the cohort's water-chemistry profile: `soft_unit_count`, `moderate_unit_count`, `hard_unit_count` and `very_hard_unit_count`, which are the counts of cohort units in each `water_hardness_band` and which always sum to `shipped_unit_count`.

### 3.9. Recall Campaigns and Serial-Based Remedy Tracking

A recall campaign is opened by a `WarrantyAnalyst` with a `campaign_code` that must be unique across all campaigns — a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`, the same code-uniqueness identifier that §2.8 position 23 and the §5 catalogue name — together with a `scope_product_model_id` naming the model under recall, a `remedy_type` drawn from `TANK_REPLACEMENT`, `GAS_VALVE_KIT`, `TP_VALVE_KIT` and `FULL_UNIT_REPLACEMENT`, and a `budget_cents` of zero or more. The campaign opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `serial_range_start`, `serial_range_end`, `affected_unit_count`, `per_unit_remedy_cost_cents`, `estimated_remedy_cost_cents` and `reason_code` all unset, and with `notice_count`, `remedied_count`, `completion_pct_notice_based` and `completion_pct_population_based` at their empty-campaign values of zero. Those last four are derived figures the campaign row carries and keeps in step with its notices: `notice_count` is the number of notices attached to the campaign, `remedied_count` the number of them in `status` `REMEDIED`, and the two percentages the two completion variants described below.

Scoping resolves who is actually affected and what it will cost. A `WarrantyAnalyst` scopes a campaign in `status` `DRAFT`, supplying `serial_range_start` and `serial_range_end`, and the campaign moves `DRAFT → SCOPED`. The range is validated before anything else is attempted: `serial_range_end` must not be lexicographically less than `serial_range_start`, and an inverted range is refused with `ERR_SERIAL_RANGE_INVALID` before the population is resolved, so an inverted range is never reported as an empty scope. The affected population is then the set of units belonging to `scope_product_model_id` that hold a non-null `serial_number` lying within the inclusive range under plain ASCII string comparison — not numeric comparison of the trailing sequence — and whose `build_status` is `LABELED`, `SHIPPED` or `HELD`. Units still `COMPONENTS_STAGED` or `ASSEMBLED` have no serial and are outside the scope by construction, and `SCRAPPED` units are excluded even when they were serialised. The population is enumerated in `serial_number` ascending order, and a scope that resolves to no units at all is refused with `ERR_RECALL_SCOPE_EMPTY`. Its size is stored as `affected_unit_count`.

The per-unit remedy cost is built in a fixed sequence, and the middle step is where implementations most often go wrong. First the labour charge: multiply the standard labour rate of 9,800 cents per hour by the remedy's standard hours — 3.5 hours for `TANK_REPLACEMENT`, 1.0 hour for `GAS_VALVE_KIT`, 0.5 hours for `TP_VALVE_KIT` and 4.0 hours for `FULL_UNIT_REPLACEMENT` — and round the product to whole cents, half up. Second the parts charge, a fixed figure per remedy: 41,500 cents for `TANK_REPLACEMENT`, 8,200 cents for `GAS_VALVE_KIT`, 3,100 cents for `TP_VALVE_KIT` and 68,000 cents for `FULL_UNIT_REPLACEMENT`. Third, add parts to labour to obtain a provisional subtotal. Fourth, choose the administrative fee rate from that provisional subtotal by band: 0.15 when the subtotal is 20,000 cents or less, 0.10 when it is more than 20,000 but 50,000 or less, and 0.06 when it is more than 50,000. Fifth, multiply the provisional subtotal by the chosen rate, round to whole cents half up, and add the fee to the subtotal to obtain `per_unit_remedy_cost_cents`.

The band is chosen **once**, from the provisional subtotal, and it is never re-selected after the fee has been added. A `GAS_VALVE_KIT` shows this plainly: labour is 9,800 times 1.0, or 9,800 cents; parts are 8,200; the provisional subtotal is 18,000, which is at or below 20,000 and therefore selects the 0.15 rate; the fee is 2,700; and the per-unit cost is 20,700 cents. An implementation that re-banded the 20,700 total, or that added the fee before selecting the rate, would arrive at 19,800 cents and would be wrong. `estimated_remedy_cost_cents` is then simply `affected_unit_count` multiplied by `per_unit_remedy_cost_cents`, with no further rounding, and it is a snapshot of the scope at the moment of scoping rather than a live figure. Re-scoping a campaign that is already `SCOPED`, `ANNOUNCED`, `IN_REMEDY`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` reports `ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_STATE`.

Announcement is the fan-out, and it is the single most consequential write in the service. A `WarrantyAnalyst` announces a campaign in `status` `SCOPED`, and the campaign's `estimated_remedy_cost_cents` must be less than or equal to its `budget_cents`; an estimate exactly equal to the budget is allowed, and one cent over is refused with `ERR_RECALL_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, leaving the campaign `SCOPED` so it can be re-scoped to a narrower range or reopened with more budget. The cost was computed and stored back at scoping but the budget is enforced only here, so an over-budget campaign may legitimately sit in `SCOPED` indefinitely. On acceptance the campaign moves `SCOPED → ANNOUNCED`.

The fan-out then splits the affected population by where each unit currently sits, and the split is deliberately asymmetric. Every affected unit whose `build_status` is `SHIPPED` receives exactly one recall notice, created in `status` `PENDING` with the unit's `heater_unit_id`, the campaign's `recall_campaign_id`, and its own `remedy_cost_cents` frozen from the campaign's `per_unit_remedy_cost_cents` at that moment, so that a later re-scoping of a different campaign can never retroactively change what an issued notice says it will cost. Notices are created in the affected units' `created_at` ascending order. Every affected unit whose `build_status` is `ASSEMBLED` or `LABELED` is instead moved to `HELD` with `hold_reason_code` set to `RECALL_SCOPE` and `held_from_status` set to the status it was holding, and receives **no** notice at all — there is no owner to notify for a unit that never left the plant. Units already `HELD` are left as they are and likewise receive no notice. Every unit the announcement touches has its `version` incremented. The scoped model then recomputes `held_unit_count` from the units now held against it and, if its `release_status` is `RELEASED`, moves `RELEASED → RESTRICTED`; a model already `RESTRICTED` or `WITHDRAWN` keeps its posture. The model's `version` is incremented.

Notices then progress through their own short lifecycle, and every notice action requires that the owning campaign be in `status` `ANNOUNCED` or `IN_REMEDY`; acting on a notice of a `DRAFT`, `SCOPED`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` campaign reports `ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_STATE`. A `WarrantyAnalyst` notifies a notice in `status` `PENDING`, moving it `PENDING → NOTIFIED` and recording `notified_at` as the logical-clock instant. The same role records the remedy on a notice in `status` `NOTIFIED`, moving it `NOTIFIED → REMEDIED` and recording `remedied_at`; a remedy cannot skip notification, and attempting it on a `PENDING` notice reports `ERR_INVALID_NOTICE_STATE`. The first remedy recorded against a campaign bubbles up and moves that campaign `ANNOUNCED → IN_REMEDY`, incrementing the campaign's `version`; subsequent remedies leave the campaign in `IN_REMEDY` and do not move it again.

A notice can also be marked unreachable, by a `WarrantyAnalyst`, from either `PENDING` or `NOTIFIED` — the two branches behave identically apart from the state they leave — recording a `reason_code` and moving the notice to the terminal `UNREACHABLE`. An unreachable notice is a failure to reach an owner, not a removal of an obligation: it stays in the issued-notice denominator, so making owners unreachable can never be used to inflate a completion percentage. `REMEDIED` and `UNREACHABLE` are both terminal, and acting further on either reports `ERR_INVALID_NOTICE_STATE`. Notices can be listed filtered by `recall_campaign_id`, `heater_unit_id` or `status`, and a single notice can be read on its own.

Campaign completion is reported by a computed read available to a `WarrantyAnalyst`, a `PlantAdmin` or a `ComplianceEngineer`, which returns `affected_unit_count` as snapshotted at scoping, `notice_count` as the number of notices actually issued, `remedied_count` as the number in `status` `REMEDIED`, `unreachable_count` as the number in `status` `UNREACHABLE`, and two completion percentages that are deliberately allowed to disagree. `completion_pct_notice_based` divides `remedied_count` by `notice_count`, multiplies by 100 and rounds to two decimal places half up: it measures how well the notification programme performed against the owners it could write to. `completion_pct_population_based` divides the same `remedied_count` by `affected_unit_count`, multiplies by 100 and rounds to two decimal places half up: it measures the recall against every unit in scope, including the in-plant units that were held rather than notified. Because held units enlarge the population denominator without ever entering the notice denominator, the population-based figure is the lower of the two whenever any unit was held. Either percentage is reported as exactly 0.00 when its own denominator is zero.

Closing a campaign is gated on the stricter of the two measures. A `WarrantyAnalyst` closes a campaign in `status` `IN_REMEDY`, and two conditions must both hold. First, `completion_pct_population_based` must be at least 80.00 — exactly 80.00 passes, and anything below is refused with `ERR_COMPLETION_BELOW_THRESHOLD` — which means the notice-based figure being at or above the threshold is not sufficient on its own. Second, no unit in the affected population may still be in `build_status` `HELD`, and a remaining hold is refused with `ERR_HELD_UNITS_REMAIN`; the in-plant units must have been either released back into production or disassembled and scrapped before the recall can be declared finished. On acceptance the campaign moves `IN_REMEDY → CLOSED`. `CLOSED` is terminal.

Cancellation is the escape hatch for a campaign that should never have run, and it is available only before announcement: a `WarrantyAnalyst` cancels a campaign in `status` `DRAFT` or `SCOPED`, supplying a `reason_code`, and the campaign moves to the terminal `CANCELLED`. Both branches retain whatever scope snapshot the campaign had accumulated — `serial_range_start`, `serial_range_end`, `affected_unit_count`, `per_unit_remedy_cost_cents` and `estimated_remedy_cost_cents` are preserved for the audit record rather than cleared — and neither branch touches any unit, since no unit was ever held or notified. An announced campaign can never be cancelled and reports `ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_STATE`; the only way out of `ANNOUNCED` or `IN_REMEDY` is to reach the closing threshold. Campaigns can be listed filtered by `scope_product_model_id` or `status`, and read individually.
