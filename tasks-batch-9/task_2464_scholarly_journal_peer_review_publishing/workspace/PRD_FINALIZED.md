# Scholarly Journal Peer Review & Publishing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An academic society that publishes a family of subject-specialised journals runs, today, on a patchwork of spreadsheets, mailing lists and a shared inbox. The editorial office loses days reconciling who is handling which manuscript, which reviewer has already been chased twice, whether an article processing charge was waived or merely forgotten, and whether the version of a manuscript a chief editor was looking at when she assigned an editor is still the version that exists. Every one of those failures is cheap in isolation and expensive in aggregate: a reviewer invited three times in the same fortnight quietly stops answering, a plagiarism report that arrives after a manuscript has already gone out for review has to be unwound by hand, and an article that reaches the archive with an unsettled invoice becomes an accounting problem that outlives the volume it appeared in.

This document specifies the service that replaces that patchwork. It is a single editorial and production backbone covering the whole arc of scholarly publishing: submission intake with scope and formatting screening, research-integrity screening for text similarity and figure manipulation, handling-editor assignment with conflict declarations and workload balancing, reviewer invitation against a shared society reviewer database with occupancy limits and a reminder cadence, decision categories with capped revision rounds and appeal adjudication, publication-ethics case management running through to correction and retraction, open-access licensing with income-band fee waivers and green-route embargoes, and a production workflow that carries copyediting, typesetting, proof approval, fee clearance and archival deposit before an article may be indexed and published.

Two properties of the domain drive most of the design. First, editorial work is **adversarially concurrent**: several officers legitimately act on the same manuscript, so a write that was composed against an outdated reading of a manuscript or a production job must be refused rather than merged. Second, editorial work is **audited**: a reviewer's reliability, a journal's acceptance rate and a journal's fee receivables are all quantities that regulators, boards and authors will later ask the system to justify, so the arithmetic behind them is pinned to an exact evaluation order rather than left to implementation taste. The specification that follows treats both properties as contract, not as advice.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that principal acts. There are exactly six declared roles: `SystemAdmin`, `EditorInChief`, `HandlingEditor`, `Reviewer`, `Author` and `ProductionStaff`. If `X-User-Role` is absent or carries any string outside those six, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_HEADER_MISSING` (HTTP 400). If the role is well-formed but is not among the roles authorised for the addressed endpoint, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Four of the six roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` must resolve to a persisted row:

- When `X-User-Role` is `EditorInChief`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Editor`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Editor` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `HandlingEditor`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Editor`; an assignment's `editor_id`, a decision's `decided_by_editor_id` and an appeal's `adjudicated_by_editor_id` are all compared against `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Reviewer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Reviewer`; an invitation's `reviewer_id` is compared against `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Author`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Author`; a manuscript's `corresponding_author_id` is compared against `X-User-ID`.

For any of those four roles, a missing `X-User-ID`, or one that matches no `Editor`, `Reviewer` or `Author` row, is rejected with `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 403). When the identity resolves but does not own the resource being addressed, the rejection is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

The remaining two roles are opaque:

- When `X-User-Role` is `SystemAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque editorial-office operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may echo or log it but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ProductionStaff`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque production-desk identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and is never compared to any entity's `id`.

`EditorInChief` and `HandlingEditor` are both backed by the same `Editor` entity and are separated by that editor's `role_level`, which is either `CHIEF` or `HANDLING`. Acting as `EditorInChief` on an `Editor` row whose `role_level` is `HANDLING`, or acting as `HandlingEditor` on an `Editor` row whose `role_level` is `CHIEF`, is rejected with `ERR_EDITOR_LEVEL_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403); so is an editor acting on a manuscript whose owning journal differs from the `journal_id` on that editor's row.

Role reach, in summary. `SystemAdmin` alone registers journals and updates their configuration, registers editors and authors, sets the logical clock and runs the reminder sweep; it shares reviewer registration with `EditorInChief`, integrity-screening entry with `HandlingEditor`, ethics-case opening with `EditorInChief`, and invoice settlement with `ProductionStaff`. `EditorInChief` alone creates editor assignments, adjudicates appeals, advances ethics cases and resolves them. `HandlingEditor` alone issues screening decisions, declares conflicts on its own assignment, creates and withdraws review invitations, and issues editorial decisions; recusal is shared between `HandlingEditor` and `EditorInChief`. `Reviewer` alone responds to invitations and submits reviews. `Author` alone submits manuscripts, changes licensing, submits revisions, files appeals and approves proofs. `ProductionStaff` alone issues APC invoices, books typesetting slots, advances the production content dimension, records archival deposits and publishes. Read endpoints are broadly shared, with the exception that the per-journal metrics read is limited to `SystemAdmin` and `EditorInChief`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds, or of the wrong JSON type, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a subsection below states otherwise. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` as they were applied, `total_count` as the number of rows matching the filters before the window was applied, and `has_more`, which is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of returned rows is strictly less than `total_count`.

Filter query parameters, where a list endpoint declares them, are combined conjunctively: `journal_id`, `role_level`, `status`, `state`, `subject_area`, `round`, `manuscript_id`, `reviewer_id`, `editor_id`, `replaced_assignment_id`, `corresponding_author_id`, `supersedes_manuscript_id`, `decision_id`, `decided_by_editor_id` and `adjudicated_by_editor_id`.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and the Editorial Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`, a UUID with no type prefix and no domain-derived structure; clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, fixed at creation and never rewritten.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations produced as a side effect of an operation addressed at some other resource. Three record types are append-only evidence rather than mutable state, and they carry only `id` and `created_at`: an `IntegrityScreening`, a `Review` and a `Decision` are each written once and never amended, so none of the three has an `updated_at` field and none of the three has a `version` field. `Journal`, `Editor`, `Author`, `Reviewer`, `Manuscript`, `EditorAssignment`, `ReviewInvitation`, `Appeal`, `EthicsCase`, `ProductionJob` and `APCInvoice` all carry the full set.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, not wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to the host's real time. A `SystemAdmin` moves it by supplying a `current_utc` instant; the clock may only move forward, so a `current_utc` earlier than the stored value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND` (HTTP 409). Every clock-derived field reads the stored logical clock at the moment its request is served: an invitation's `invited_at` and `due_at`, a reviewer's `responded_at`, a review's `submitted_at`, an invoice's `settled_at`, an ethics case's `resolved_at`, a production job's `published_at`, a manuscript's `retracted_at`, an invitation's `last_reminder_at`, an appeal's `adjudicated_at`, and the lower boundary of the rolling reliability window.

### 2.5. Version Guards on Editorial Writes

Three write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because in each case a second, legitimate write against the same parent is indistinguishable from an accidental write composed against an outdated reading — only the parent's `version` separates the two.

Creating an editor assignment — `POST /editor-assignments` — requires the non-optional field `expected_manuscript_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Manuscript`. A second assignment on the same manuscript legitimately supersedes an earlier `PROPOSED` one, so nothing in the assignment state machine refuses it; if `expected_manuscript_version` does not equal the manuscript's current `version` — because an integrity screening changed `integrity_status`, or a recusal cascade rewired the handling editor, since the client last read it — the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MANUSCRIPT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Issuing an article processing charge invoice — `POST /manuscripts/{id}/apc-invoices` — likewise requires the non-optional field `expected_manuscript_version`. Re-issuing after a cancelled invoice is a legitimate operation, so the invoice state machine does not refuse a second issue; the version guard is what stops an invoice being priced from an outdated view of the manuscript's licence, page count or author waiver band. A mismatch with the manuscript's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MANUSCRIPT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Booking a typesetting slot — `POST /production-jobs/{id}/typeset-slot` — requires the non-optional field `expected_production_job_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ProductionJob`. Rebooking a slot is permitted while the job's content dimension is at `COPYEDITING` or `TYPESETTING`, so only the version guard separates an intentional rebooking from one written against an outdated job view. A mismatch with the job's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PRODUCTION_JOB_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint takes a version field. Requests to endpoints outside those three neither require nor honour `expected_manuscript_version` or `expected_production_job_version`.

### 2.6. Segregation of Editorial Duties

The editorial process is designed so that no single officer can both create and ratify the same judgement. Three separations are enforced. The editor whose `X-User-ID` adjudicates an appeal must differ from the `decided_by_editor_id` frozen on the appealed `Decision`. The editor whose `X-User-ID` resolves an `EthicsCase` must differ from the editor who opened it. Both violations are rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Separately, an editor who has recused himself from a manuscript is permanently barred from being reassigned to it, and the corresponding author who approves the proof of an article is never the principal who settles that article's invoice — proof approval is an `Author` capability while settlement belongs to `ProductionStaff` and `SystemAdmin`.

### 2.7. Fee Arithmetic, Percentages and Rounding

All monetary amounts in this system are **integer cents**. There are no fractional-cent balances anywhere: `apc_base_cents`, `page_charge_cents_per_page`, `color_figure_charge_cents`, `base_cents`, `waiver_cents`, `institutional_cents`, `net_apc_cents`, `raw_surcharge_cents`, `provisional_total_cents`, `surcharge_cents`, `total_due_cents`, `apc_receivable_cents`, `apc_waived_cents` and the `amount_cents` supplied on a payment are all whole numbers of cents.

Where a fee computation multiplies by a multiplier or a percentage, the intermediate result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent before the next step consumes it. This staged rounding is contractual: computing a chain of discounts against unrounded intermediates and rounding only at the end produces a different number of cents, and that number is wrong.

Scores and rates are decimal numbers rather than cents, and each has a stated precision. A reviewer's `reliability_score_rolling` and `reliability_score_lifetime` are held to 2 decimal places, rounded half up, and clamped to the closed range from 0.00 to 100.00. A review's frozen `reliability_weight` is held to 4 decimal places, rounded half up, and never falls below 0.2500. A decision's `consensus_index` is held to 2 decimal places, rounded half up. The percentages reported by the metrics read — `acceptance_rate_pct` and `reviewer_invitation_acceptance_rate_pct` — and the `median_days_to_first_decision` when it is an average of two middle values, are all held to 2 decimal places, rounded half up. Every percentage field accepted in a request body must fall within the closed range from 0 to 100; a value outside it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Wherever a threshold is described in this document as "at least" a value, the comparison is inclusive of that value. Wherever it is described as "below" or "more than", the comparison excludes it. These boundary readings are part of the contract and are never approximated.

### 2.8. Conserved Ledgers and Monotone Audit Counters

Two families of derived numbers coexist on this system, and they behave differently on purpose.

**Conserved quantities** must return to their prior value when the event that raised them is undone. A journal's `apc_receivable_cents` rises by an invoice's `total_due_cents` when that invoice is issued and falls by exactly the same amount when the invoice is paid, waived or cancelled, so the receivable ledger of a journal always equals the sum of `total_due_cents` over that journal's invoices still in status `ISSUED`. A journal's `apc_waived_cents` rises by `total_due_cents` only on a waiver, and is untouched by payment and by cancellation. A reviewer's `open_invitation_count` rises by 1 when an invitation is created and falls by 1 whenever that invitation closes — by decline, by sweep expiry, by review submission or by withdrawal — so it always equals the count of that reviewer's invitations in state `INVITED` or `ACCEPTED`. An editor's `active_assignment_count` rises by 1 when an assignment is created for that editor and falls by 1 when the assignment leaves the open set, so it always equals the count of that editor's assignments in state `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE`. A journal's `published_article_count` rises by 1 at publication and falls by 1 when a published article is retracted; its `retracted_article_count` rises by 1 on the same retraction.

**Monotone audit counters** never fall. A reviewer's `invited_count` counts every invitation ever created for that reviewer and is never decremented, not even when the invitation is withdrawn seconds later. `accepted_count`, `declined_count`, `expired_count`, `submitted_count` and `late_days_total` are likewise lifetime tallies. A journal's `manuscript_intake_count` counts every manuscript ever submitted to it and never decreases, which is what makes a manuscript's `journal_sequence_number` permanently unique within its journal even after desk rejections and retractions.

### 2.9. Cascades, Parallel Stage Dimensions and Scoped Reads

A single request in this system routinely mutates several rows. Whenever it does, every mutated row has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1, and the mutations are applied within the request that triggered them — there is no deferred or eventual settlement anywhere in this API. Where a cascade touches an ordered collection of rows, the order is stated explicitly in §3 and is always by `created_at` ascending; `created_at` is unique per row at millisecond precision and rows are never ordered by `id`.

A `ProductionJob` is unusual in carrying **two independent state dimensions**. Its `content_stage` tracks the manuscript through copyediting, typesetting and proofing; its `clearance_stage` tracks licensing, fee settlement and archival deposit. Neither dimension constrains the other's progress, so a job can be at `clearance_stage` `FEE_CLEARED` while still at `content_stage` `COPYEDITING`, or at `content_stage` `PROOF_APPROVED` while still at `clearance_stage` `LICENSE_PENDING`. The two dimensions join only at publication, which requires both to have reached their final pre-publication values.

Path parameters are validated **left to right**. On a nested read such as `GET /manuscripts/{manuscript_id}/apc-invoices/{invoice_id}`, the manuscript named by `manuscript_id` is resolved first; if no such manuscript exists the answer is `ERR_MANUSCRIPT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). The invoice named by `invoice_id` is resolved next; a missing invoice yields `ERR_APC_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). Only once both rows exist does the server check that the invoice's `manuscript_id` equals the manuscript in the path; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_PARENT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409) rather than silently returning the invoice. The same left-to-right discipline, and the same parent-mismatch rule, apply to every write addressed at a child through its parent's path.

### 2.10. Order of Rejection

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned: the one belonging to the **highest-priority** violated rule. Evaluation stops at the first violation. The ladder, from first evaluated to last, is:

1. `X-User-Role` present and one of the six declared roles — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_HEADER_MISSING` (400).
2. The acting role is listed for the target endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. For an entity-backed role, `X-User-ID` present and resolving to an existing `Editor`, `Reviewer` or `Author` row — otherwise `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (403). This applies to every endpoint whose authorised roles include `EditorInChief`, `HandlingEditor`, `Reviewer` or `Author`.
4. Every path parameter references an existing row of its entity — otherwise the matching not-found error for that entity (404), on every endpoint that has a path parameter.
5. Every foreign key in the request body references an existing row — otherwise the matching not-found error for that entity (404), on every write endpoint that carries a foreign key.
6. A referenced child belongs to the parent named in the path — a review's invitation, an invitation's manuscript, a job's manuscript, an invoice's manuscript — otherwise `ERR_PARENT_MISMATCH` (409). This guards review submission, decision issuance, publication, invoice issuance and the manuscript-scoped invoice read.
7. The body carries every required field, each of its declared JSON type, every enum field carrying a declared member, every monetary and count field a non-negative integer, and every percentage field within 0 to 100 inclusive — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), on every write endpoint.
8. An interval does not end before it starts: `slot_end` is at or after `slot_start`, and a computed `due_at` is strictly after `invited_at` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (400). This guards typesetting-slot booking and invitation creation.
9. An OCC-guarded write's `expected_manuscript_version` or `expected_production_job_version` equals the parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_MANUSCRIPT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_PRODUCTION_JOB_VERSION` (412), on assignment creation, invoice issuance and slot booking respectively.
10. Declared unique keys do not collide — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (409). The keys are: a journal's `name`; the pair of an editor's `journal_id` and `email`; a reviewer's `email`; an author's `email`; at most one `ProductionJob` per manuscript; at most one `IntegrityScreening` per combination of manuscript, `round` and `scan_type`; and at most one unresolved `EthicsCase` per combination of manuscript and `case_type`. This guards journal, editor, reviewer and author registration, integrity-screening entry and ethics-case opening.
11. The acting entity-backed identity owns the resource it addresses — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This guards revision submission, appeal filing, invitation response, review submission and proof approval.
12. Segregation of duties holds — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403), on appeal adjudication and ethics-case resolution.
13. The subject entity currently sits in a state from which the requested transition is declared — otherwise the matching invalid-state error for that entity (409), on every action endpoint. Two carve-outs apply. For publication, the stage gate at rule 15 (`ERR_PRODUCTION_STAGE_NOT_READY`) is evaluated in place of this rule. For review submission, the invitation-state gate at rule 27 (`ERR_INVITATION_NOT_ACCEPTED`) is evaluated in place of this rule, so a review attempted against an invitation in any state other than `ACCEPTED` — `INVITED`, `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED`, `SUBMITTED` or `WITHDRAWN` — returns `ERR_INVITATION_NOT_ACCEPTED` and never `ERR_INVALID_INVITATION_STATE`.
14. The subject is not in a terminal state — otherwise `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED` (409), on every action endpoint. The terminal states are a `DESK_REJECTED` or `RETRACTED` manuscript, a `RECUSED` or `RELEASED` assignment, a closed invitation, an adjudicated appeal, a resolved ethics case, a settled invoice and a `TERMINATED` production job.
15. Publication requires `content_stage` `PROOF_APPROVED` and `clearance_stage` `DEPOSIT_CONFIRMED` — otherwise `ERR_PRODUCTION_STAGE_NOT_READY` (409).
16. A current-round integrity rollup of `BLOCK` bars advancing to review — otherwise `ERR_INTEGRITY_BLOCK` (409), on the screening decision.
17. No unresolved `EthicsCase` of severity `HIGH` exists for the manuscript — otherwise `ERR_ETHICS_HOLD` (409). This freezes three operations: decision issuance, publication and invoice issuance.
18. A manuscript advancing to review has `formatting_status` `COMPLIANT` — otherwise `ERR_FORMATTING_NON_COMPLIANT` (409), on the screening decision.
19. An assignment reaching `ACTIVE` carries a completed conflict declaration with `declared_conflict` false, and an editor who has declared a conflict on a manuscript is never reassigned to it — otherwise `ERR_EDITOR_CONFLICT_DECLARED` (409), on assignment creation and on the conflict declaration itself.
20. An explicitly named editor's `active_assignment_count` remains below `max_concurrent_assignments` and that editor covers the manuscript's `subject_area` — otherwise `ERR_EDITOR_WORKLOAD_EXCEEDED` (409), on assignment creation.
21. A reviewer invitation carries no conflict of interest — otherwise `ERR_REVIEWER_CONFLICT` (409), on invitation creation.
22. The reviewer's overlapping open windows number fewer than the journal's `reviewer_concurrent_window_limit` — otherwise `ERR_REVIEWER_WINDOW_CAPACITY` (409), on invitation creation.
23. A typesetting slot does not conflict with another live job of the same journal once the changeover buffer is applied — otherwise `ERR_TYPESET_SLOT_CONFLICT` (409).
24. The current round carries at least `min_completed_reviews_for_decision` submitted reviews — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_REVIEWS` (409), on decision issuance.
25. A revision decision does not exceed `max_revision_rounds` — otherwise `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (409), on decision issuance.
26. An appeal targets the manuscript's most recent rejection decision, within 30 days of it, and at most once per decision — otherwise `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409).
27. A review is submitted only against an `ACCEPTED` invitation and carries a declared `recommendation` — otherwise `ERR_INVITATION_NOT_ACCEPTED` (409).
28. Publication requires that the manuscript carry no invoice still in status `ISSUED` — otherwise `ERR_UNPAID_APC_INVOICE` (409).
29. A payment settlement supplies `amount_cents` exactly equal to `total_due_cents`, and `total_due_cents` is greater than 0 — otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (409).
30. A waiver settlement supplies a non-empty `waiver_reason`, and a cancellation settlement finds the manuscript either without a production job or with one still at `clearance_stage` `LICENSE_PENDING` — otherwise `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_ALLOWED` (409).
31. An embargo is requested only for the `GREEN_SELF_ARCHIVE` licence — otherwise `ERR_EMBARGO_NOT_APPLICABLE` (409), on the licensing change.
32. A `CORRECTION` resolution finds the manuscript `PUBLISHED`, and a `RETRACTION` resolution finds it either `PUBLISHED` or `CORRECTED` — otherwise `ERR_RETRACTION_NOT_SUPPORTED` (409).
33. An archival deposit finds the manuscript `IN_PRODUCTION` and the job at `clearance_stage` `FEE_CLEARED` — otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_NOT_READY` (409).
34. When the caller omits the explicit identifier, the selector finds at least one eligible candidate — otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_EDITOR` or `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_REVIEWER` (409), on assignment creation and invitation creation respectively.
35. The logical clock moves only forward — otherwise `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND` (409).

The remaining error identifiers in §5 are single-condition guards attached to individual workflows and are described alongside those workflows in §3.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Establishing a Journal and Its Editorial Community

Before a single manuscript can arrive, the editorial office registers the journal itself. A journal is created with a `name` and a non-empty array of `subject_areas`, each element an uppercase subject slug; that array is the journal's declared scope, and a manuscript whose `subject_area` is not a member of it is out of scope for the journal. Everything else on a journal is configuration, and every configuration field has a default, so a registration that supplies only the name and the subject areas still yields a fully governed journal.

The defaults are worth stating individually, because the whole editorial policy of a journal lives in them. DOI minting uses a registrant prefix `doi_prefix`, defaulting to `10.5555`. Peer review is governed by `min_completed_reviews_for_decision`, the number of reviews that must be submitted in the current round before a decision may be issued, defaulting to 2 and never below 1; by `max_revision_rounds`, defaulting to 2 and never below 0; by `review_target_days`, the number of days from invitation to deadline, defaulting to 21 and never below 1; by `reminder_cadence_days`, the gap between chase-up reminders, defaulting to 7 and never below 1; by `max_reminders_per_invitation`, defaulting to 2 and never below 0; and by `reviewer_concurrent_window_limit`, defaulting to 3 and never below 1. The journal's `consensus_mode` selects which variant of the consensus computation its decisions use and is either `UNWEIGHTED`, the default, or `RELIABILITY_WEIGHTED`. Formatting policy is set by `max_page_count`, defaulting to 30 and never below 1, by `max_figure_count`, defaulting to 10 and never below 0, and by `requires_data_availability_statement`, defaulting to true. Fee policy is set by `apc_base_cents`, the list article processing charge before any licence multiplier, defaulting to 180000 cents — one thousand eight hundred currency units — by `page_charge_cents_per_page`, defaulting to 4000 cents per charged page, and by `color_figure_charge_cents`, defaulting to 25000 cents per colour figure, each of the three never below 0. Production policy is set by `embargo_months`, defaulting to 12 and never below 0, by `typeset_changeover_buffer_days`, defaulting to 2 and never below 0, and by `max_proof_rounds`, defaulting to 2 and never below 1.

A newly registered journal starts every derived quantity at zero: `manuscript_intake_count`, `published_article_count`, `retracted_article_count`, `apc_receivable_cents` and `apc_waived_cents`. Journal names are unique across the society, so a second registration under an existing `name` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`.

Configuration can be revised later, and only the fields supplied in the revision change; the others keep their stored values, and the journal's `version` advances by 1. Revisions are deliberately **not** retroactive over evidence already written: a `Decision` keeps the `consensus_mode` and `consensus_index` frozen into it at the moment it was issued, and an `APCInvoice` keeps every amount frozen at issue. Revisions **are** read live by every gate that consults them, which is a consequential asymmetry. Raising `min_completed_reviews_for_decision` after a manuscript has already reached `DECISION_PENDING` blocks that manuscript's decision until enough further reviews arrive, because the threshold is read at decision time rather than captured when the manuscript entered the state.

Three kinds of people populate a journal's world. An editor is registered against a `journal_id` with an `email`, a `full_name`, an `affiliation`, a non-empty array of `subject_areas` the editor handles, and a `role_level` of either `HANDLING` — subject-area editors who run peer review — or `CHIEF` — chief editors who assign, adjudicate appeals and run ethics cases. An editor's `status` defaults to `ACTIVE` and may be `ON_LEAVE`, which excludes them from assignment entirely, and their `max_concurrent_assignments` defaults to 3 and is never below 1. Every editor starts with an `active_assignment_count` of 0. Editor emails are unique within a journal, so two editors on the same journal may not share an `email`, though the same address may appear on different journals' boards.

An author is registered with a unique `email`, a `full_name`, an `affiliation`, a `country_income_band` defaulting to `HIGH` and drawn from `HIGH`, `UPPER_MIDDLE`, `LOWER_MIDDLE` and `LOW`, and an `institutional_agreement` boolean defaulting to false. Those last two fields are what turn an author record into a fee outcome, as §3.9 sets out.

Reviewers live in a single society-wide database shared by every journal, rather than per-journal panels. A reviewer is registered with a unique `email`, a `full_name`, an `affiliation`, a non-empty array of `subject_areas` they cover, and a `status` defaulting to `ACTIVE`; the other permitted values are `PROBATION`, which keeps the reviewer invitable only when an editor names them explicitly, and `RETIRED`, which makes them never invitable at all. A fresh reviewer starts with `invited_count`, `accepted_count`, `declined_count`, `expired_count`, `submitted_count`, `late_days_total` and `open_invitation_count` all at 0, and with both `reliability_score_rolling` and `reliability_score_lifetime` seeded at 50.00 — a deliberately neutral starting point, so that a new reviewer is neither favoured nor punished by the selector before they have any track record. Reviewer and author emails are each unique across the whole database; a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`.

### 3.2. Submission Intake: Scope, Formatting and Licensing

A corresponding author submits a manuscript to a journal. The submission carries the `journal_id`, the `corresponding_author_id` — which must equal the acting `X-User-ID`, since an author may only submit on their own behalf — a `title`, a `subject_area` slug, and an `oa_license` chosen from `CC_BY`, `CC_BY_NC`, `CC_BY_NC_ND` and `GREEN_SELF_ARCHIVE`. It also carries a `page_count` of at least 1 and a `figure_count` of at least 0. Three fields are optional: `coauthor_emails`, an array defaulting to empty, which is the raw material for every conflict-of-interest check downstream; `data_availability_statement`, defaulting to absent; and `supersedes_manuscript_id`, defaulting to absent, which marks the submission as a resubmission and must point at a manuscript whose most recent decision carried the category `REJECT_AND_RESUBMIT`.

Intake writes several derived values at once. The manuscript begins at `SUBMITTED` with `revision_round` 0 and `integrity_status` `PENDING`, since nothing has been screened yet. It receives a `journal_sequence_number` equal to the journal's `manuscript_intake_count` plus 1, and the journal's `manuscript_intake_count` then rises by 1 — this is the deterministic component of the DOI the article will eventually carry, and because the intake counter never decreases, no two manuscripts on one journal ever share a sequence number. Its `formatting_status` is computed immediately and is `COMPLIANT` only when all three of the following hold: the `page_count` is at most the journal's `max_page_count`, the `figure_count` is at most the journal's `max_figure_count`, and either the journal does not require a data availability statement or the manuscript's `data_availability_statement` is a non-empty string. If any of the three fails, `formatting_status` is `NON_COMPLIANT`. Intake itself does not refuse a non-compliant submission — the paper is accepted into the system and the non-compliance surfaces later, when the handling editor tries to advance it.

Several other derived quantities on a manuscript start at their neutral values and are maintained by the workflows that follow: `submitted_review_count` at 0, `review_shortfall_flag` false, `handling_gap_flag` false, `consensus_index_at_last_decision` absent, `decision_count` at 0, and `publication_date`, `embargo_end_date`, `doi` and `retracted_at` all absent.

Licensing is revisable. The corresponding author may change the manuscript's `oa_license` at any point before the article becomes immutable — that is, while the manuscript's `status` is not `PUBLISHED`, `CORRECTED`, `RETRACTED` or `DESK_REJECTED`. Attempting a licence change once the manuscript has reached any of those four states is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_LOCKED`. The same request may carry `embargo_requested`, and here the licence choice is decisive: an embargo is meaningful only on the green self-archiving route, so `embargo_requested` set to true against `CC_BY`, `CC_BY_NC` or `CC_BY_NC_ND` is refused with `ERR_EMBARGO_NOT_APPLICABLE`, those three licences publishing immediately. A successful licence change advances the manuscript's `version` by 1, which — as §2.5 explains — is enough to invalidate a fee invoice that another desk had begun preparing from a stale reading.

### 3.3. Research-Integrity Screening and Ethics Escalation

Screening is evidence, not opinion, so each screening is an append-only record. A screening is filed against the manuscript by the handling editor or by the editorial office and carries a `scan_type` of either `PLAGIARISM` or `IMAGE_FORENSICS`. The two scan types take different measurements and the payload must match: a plagiarism scan supplies a `similarity_pct` between 0.00 and 100.00 and must omit `manipulated_figure_count`, while an image-forensics scan supplies a `manipulated_figure_count` of at least 0 and must omit `similarity_pct`. A payload that supplies the wrong measurement, or omits the required one, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The server stamps the screening's `round` with the manuscript's current `revision_round`, and only one screening of each `scan_type` may exist per manuscript per round; a second one collides with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`.

Each screening's `verdict` is derived from its own measurement, with inclusive thresholds. A plagiarism scan whose `similarity_pct` is at least 40.0 yields `BLOCK`; one at least 20.0 but below 40.0 yields `FLAG`; anything below 20.0 yields `PASS`. An image-forensics scan whose `manipulated_figure_count` is at least 2 yields `BLOCK`; exactly 1 manipulated figure yields `FLAG`; zero yields `PASS`.

The manuscript's `integrity_status` is then rolled up across **only the screenings of its current `revision_round`** — earlier rounds' screenings are retained for audit and never re-enter the rollup. With no screening at all in the current round, `integrity_status` is `PENDING`. Otherwise the worst verdict wins, in the severity order `BLOCK` above `FLAG` above `PASS`: if any current-round screening is `BLOCK`, the rollup is `BLOCK`; failing that, if any is `FLAG`, the rollup is `FLAG`; otherwise it is `PASS`.

A `BLOCK` verdict does more than set a flag. It automatically opens a publication-ethics case against the manuscript with `opened_by` set to `SCREENING_CASCADE` and `severity` set to `HIGH`, its `case_type` being `PLAGIARISM` for a text-similarity block and `IMAGE_INTEGRITY` for an image-manipulation block. That auto-opening is suppressed when the manuscript already has an unresolved case of exactly that `case_type`, which keeps a repeat block from generating duplicate investigations. Because the auto-opened case is `HIGH` severity and unresolved, it immediately freezes the manuscript's decisions, invoice issuance and publication, as §3.9 describes.

Screening also functions as the gate that reopens review after a revision. When the manuscript sits at `REVISION_SUBMITTED` and a newly filed screening for the current round yields `PASS` or `FLAG` while the manuscript's `formatting_status` is `COMPLIANT`, the manuscript automatically advances `REVISION_SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` within the same request, and its `review_shortfall_flag` is cleared to false. If that new verdict is instead `BLOCK`, the manuscript stays at `REVISION_SUBMITTED` and the ethics case is opened in its place. Either way the manuscript's `version` advances by 1, since `integrity_status` has changed.

### 3.4. Handling-Editor Assignment, Conflict Declaration and Desk Screening

A chief editor puts a handling editor on a manuscript. The request names the `manuscript_id`, carries the `expected_manuscript_version` guard described in §2.5, and may name an `editor_id`. When `editor_id` is supplied the new assignment records `selected_by` as `EXPLICIT`; when it is omitted the server ranks the eligible pool itself and records `selected_by` as `SELECTOR`. Either way the assignment is born at `PROPOSED`, the named editor's `active_assignment_count` rises by 1, and the manuscript's `version` advances by 1.

An explicitly named editor is still checked. If that editor's `active_assignment_count` already equals their `max_concurrent_assignments`, or if the manuscript's `subject_area` is not among that editor's `subject_areas`, the request is refused with `ERR_EDITOR_WORKLOAD_EXCEEDED`. If that editor has already recused himself from this manuscript, the request is refused with `ERR_EDITOR_CONFLICT_DECLARED` — a declared conflict is permanent and no amount of reassignment undoes it.

When no `editor_id` is supplied, the server selects one. The eligible pool is every editor who satisfies all of the following: the editor sits on the manuscript's journal; the editor's `status` is `ACTIVE`; the editor's `role_level` is `HANDLING`; the manuscript's `subject_area` appears among the editor's `subject_areas`; the editor's `active_assignment_count` is strictly less than their `max_concurrent_assignments`; the editor's `email` differs from the corresponding author's `email`; the editor's `email` does not appear among the manuscript's `coauthor_emails`; the editor's `affiliation` differs from the corresponding author's `affiliation`; and no assignment already exists on this manuscript for that editor in state `RECUSED`. When the selector is invoked from the appeal-overturn cascade, one further exclusion applies: the editor who issued the appealed decision is removed from the pool.

The surviving candidates are ranked by relative workload, lightest first: the primary sort key is each editor's `active_assignment_count` divided by their `max_concurrent_assignments`, ascending, compared as an exact ratio with no rounding. Ties on that ratio are broken by `max_concurrent_assignments`, descending, so between two equally-loaded editors the one with more headroom wins. Ties on both are broken by the editor's `created_at`, ascending. The first row after sorting is the winner. If the pool is empty, a direct assignment request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_EDITOR`, while a selector invoked from inside a cascade creates no assignment at all and instead sets the manuscript's `handling_gap_flag` to true, recording that the manuscript has been left without a handling editor.

A proposed editor must answer the conflict question before doing any editorial work. The declaration carries `declared_conflict` and, when that is true, a `conflict_type` drawn from `CO_AUTHOR`, `SAME_INSTITUTION`, `FINANCIAL` and `PERSONAL`. Only the editor named on the assignment may answer it. A declaration of false moves the assignment `PROPOSED → ACTIVE`, stores `declared_conflict` as false, and clears the manuscript's `handling_gap_flag` to false. A declaration of true without a `conflict_type` is refused with `ERR_EDITOR_CONFLICT_DECLARED`; with one, the assignment moves `PROPOSED → RECUSED` carrying `released_reason` `RECUSAL`, that editor's `active_assignment_count` falls by 1, and the replacement cascade runs: the selector is re-run with every editor who already holds a `RECUSED` assignment on this manuscript excluded, and on success a fresh assignment is created at `PROPOSED` with `replaced_assignment_id` pointing at the assignment it supersedes and the replacement editor's `active_assignment_count` rising by 1. On an empty pool no replacement is created and the manuscript's `handling_gap_flag` becomes true. Either way the original assignment's `version` advances by 1.

Recusal after activation works the same way, but is reachable by two principals: the editor named on the assignment, or a chief editor of the manuscript's journal. A `reason` may accompany it. It is permitted only while the manuscript's `status` is neither `DESK_REJECTED` nor `RETRACTED`. The assignment moves `ACTIVE → RECUSED` with `released_reason` `RECUSAL`, that editor's `active_assignment_count` falls by 1, and the identical replacement-selection cascade runs — same exclusions, same ranking, same `replaced_assignment_id` link, same `handling_gap_flag` fallback on an empty pool. An assignment can also leave `ACTIVE` without anyone asking: whenever the manuscript reaches `DESK_REJECTED`, `REJECTED` or `IN_PRODUCTION`, the `ACTIVE` assignment moves `ACTIVE → RELEASED` with `released_reason` `DECISION_FINAL` and that editor's `active_assignment_count` falls by 1. `RECUSED` and `RELEASED` are both terminal: a further transition attempt on such an assignment is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED`, and a transition requested from a state that does not declare it — activating an already-`ACTIVE` assignment, for instance — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`.

With an `ACTIVE` assignment in hand, the handling editor makes the desk call. The request carries an `action` of either `ADVANCE_TO_REVIEW` or `DESK_REJECT` and an optional `note`. Both actions require the manuscript to be at `SUBMITTED`, and both require that exactly one assignment on the manuscript is in state `ACTIVE` and that its `editor_id` equals the acting `X-User-ID`; when no such assignment exists, the request is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

`ADVANCE_TO_REVIEW` is the gated branch. It additionally requires that the manuscript's `integrity_status` is either `PASS` or `FLAG` — a `BLOCK` rollup is refused with `ERR_INTEGRITY_BLOCK`, and the only ways forward from there are a desk rejection or an ethics case resolved in the manuscript's favour; that the manuscript's `formatting_status` is `COMPLIANT`, a `NON_COMPLIANT` manuscript being refused with `ERR_FORMATTING_NON_COMPLIANT`; and that the manuscript's `subject_area` is a member of the journal's `subject_areas`, an out-of-scope paper being refused with `ERR_SUBJECT_AREA_OUT_OF_SCOPE`. On success the manuscript moves `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, its `review_shortfall_flag` is set to false, and its `version` advances by 1.

`DESK_REJECT` is the ungated branch, and the asymmetry is intentional: an editor must always be able to close a paper that cannot proceed. A desk rejection is permitted even while `integrity_status` is `BLOCK` or `formatting_status` is `NON_COMPLIANT`, and it does not consult the journal's subject areas. It moves the manuscript `SUBMITTED → DESK_REJECTED`, which is terminal, releases the `ACTIVE` assignment to `RELEASED` with `released_reason` `DECISION_FINAL` and decrements that editor's `active_assignment_count` by 1, and advances the manuscript's `version` by 1. A manuscript addressed by any action endpoint from a status that declares no such transition is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MANUSCRIPT_STATE`, and once `DESK_REJECTED` or `RETRACTED` the answer becomes `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED`.

### 3.5. Reviewer Invitation, Occupancy Windows and the Reminder Sweep

The handling editor invites reviewers for the manuscript's current round. The invitation names the `manuscript_id`, may name a `reviewer_id`, and may override the deadline with `due_days`. Only the editor holding the manuscript's `ACTIVE` assignment may invite; otherwise the refusal is `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The created invitation is stamped with `round` equal to the manuscript's `revision_round`, `invited_at` equal to the current logical clock, and `due_at` equal to `invited_at` plus a number of days taken from `due_days` when supplied and from the journal's `review_target_days` otherwise. A `due_days` value that is not a positive integer, or any interval whose computed `due_at` would not be strictly after `invited_at`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. The invitation begins at `INVITED`, records `selected_by` as `EXPLICIT` when the request named a reviewer and `SELECTOR` when the server chose one, and carries `reminder_count` 0, `last_reminder_at` absent, `responded_at` absent, `decline_reason` absent and `closed_reason` absent. Creating the invitation raises that reviewer's `invited_count` by 1 and their `open_invitation_count` by 1, and re-runs both reliability variants for that reviewer.

Conflict of interest is checked before anything is written. A reviewer is refused with `ERR_REVIEWER_CONFLICT` when their `email` equals the corresponding author's `email`, when their `email` appears among the manuscript's `coauthor_emails`, when their `affiliation` equals the corresponding author's `affiliation`, when they are the editor holding the manuscript's `ACTIVE` assignment, or when they already hold an open invitation on this same manuscript.

Reviewer capacity is enforced as an **aggregate**, not as exclusivity. Each open invitation occupies a **half-open** interval on its reviewer, starting at `invited_at` inclusive and ending at `due_at` exclusive. The proposed new invitation occupies the interval from the current clock instant inclusive to that instant plus the effective due days, exclusive. Two intervals overlap when the first starts strictly before the second ends and the second starts strictly before the first ends — strict on both sides, so two windows that merely touch, where one's start equals the other's end, do **not** overlap. The server counts the invited reviewer's own invitations in state `INVITED` or `ACCEPTED`, across every journal and every manuscript, whose window overlaps the proposed one. If that count is at least the journal's `reviewer_concurrent_window_limit`, the invitation is refused with `ERR_REVIEWER_WINDOW_CAPACITY`. A closed invitation releases its window immediately, so a reviewer who declines a fortnight's work is instantly available again.

When the editor omits `reviewer_id`, the server picks from the society database. The eligible pool is every reviewer whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; whose `subject_areas` contain the manuscript's `subject_area`; whose `email` differs from the corresponding author's `email`; whose `email` does not appear among the manuscript's `coauthor_emails`; whose `affiliation` differs from the corresponding author's `affiliation`; whose `email` differs from the `email` of the editor holding the manuscript's `ACTIVE` assignment; who holds no existing invitation on this manuscript in state `INVITED`, `ACCEPTED` or `SUBMITTED`; and whose overlapping open windows number fewer than the journal's `reviewer_concurrent_window_limit`. Reviewers on `PROBATION` are never auto-selected, though an editor may still invite them by naming the `reviewer_id` explicitly; `RETIRED` reviewers are invitable by neither route. Candidates are ranked by `reliability_score_rolling` descending, compared at its stored two-decimal precision, then by `open_invitation_count` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending; the first row after sorting wins. An empty pool is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_REVIEWER`.

The invited reviewer answers with a `response` of either `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE`, and a `DECLINE` may carry a free-text `decline_reason`. Only the reviewer named on the invitation may answer, and only while the invitation is at `INVITED`; an answer against any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INVITATION_STATE`, and against a closed invitation with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED`. An acceptance is additionally time-bounded: it is permitted only while the current clock instant is strictly before the invitation's `due_at`, so an attempt to accept at or after the deadline is refused with `ERR_INVITATION_EXPIRED`.

`ACCEPT` moves the invitation `INVITED → ACCEPTED`, sets `responded_at` to the current clock instant, raises the reviewer's `accepted_count` by 1, and re-runs both reliability variants. Notably, `open_invitation_count` is **unchanged** by acceptance: an `ACCEPTED` invitation still occupies the reviewer's window, because the work is still outstanding. `DECLINE` moves the invitation `INVITED → DECLINED` with `closed_reason` `REVIEWER_DECLINED` and `responded_at` set to the current clock instant, raises `declined_count` by 1, lowers `open_invitation_count` by 1, re-runs both reliability variants, and — because a manuscript may now be short of reviews — recomputes the manuscript's `review_shortfall_flag`. If the recomputed `reliability_score_rolling` falls below 40.00 while the reviewer has at least 3 closed invitations inside the rolling window, the reviewer moves `ACTIVE → PROBATION`. Either response advances the invitation's `version` by 1.

The handling editor may also pull an invitation back. Withdrawal carries an optional `reason` and applies to an invitation at either `INVITED` or `ACCEPTED`, so the declared moves are `INVITED → WITHDRAWN` and `ACCEPTED → WITHDRAWN`, each carrying `closed_reason` `EDITOR_WITHDRAWN`. The reviewer's `open_invitation_count` falls by 1, restoring the conserved open-load quantity to its pre-invitation value, while `invited_count` is deliberately **not** decremented — the invitation happened, and the audit trail says so. Both reliability variants are re-run and the invitation's `version` advances by 1. The two withdrawal branches, from `INVITED` and from `ACCEPTED`, behave identically in every respect.

Chasing and expiry are handled by a sweep the editorial office runs for one journal at a time, naming the `journal_id`. The sweep considers only invitations in state `INVITED` whose manuscript belongs to the swept journal, and processes them in `created_at` ascending order. Each such invitation receives **exactly one** action, with expiry taking precedence over reminding. If the current clock instant is at or after the invitation's `due_at` — the deadline instant itself expires — the invitation moves `INVITED → EXPIRED` with `closed_reason` `SWEEP_EXPIRY`, the reviewer's `expired_count` rises by 1 and `open_invitation_count` falls by 1, both reliability variants are re-run, the reviewer moves `ACTIVE → PROBATION` when the new `reliability_score_rolling` is below 40.00 with at least 3 closed invitations in the rolling window, the manuscript's `review_shortfall_flag` is recomputed, and the invitation's `version` advances by 1. Otherwise the sweep considers a reminder: the anchor is the invitation's `last_reminder_at` when one exists and its `invited_at` otherwise, and a reminder is emitted only when the invitation's `reminder_count` is strictly below the journal's `max_reminders_per_invitation` and the current clock instant is at or after the anchor plus the journal's `reminder_cadence_days` days. Emitting a reminder raises `reminder_count` by 1 and sets `last_reminder_at` to the current clock instant. Invitations in state `ACCEPTED` are never expired by the sweep — a reviewer who accepted may still deliver late, accruing lateness instead of losing the assignment.

The sweep reports what it did: `reminded_invitation_count`, `expired_invitation_count`, `probation_reviewer_count` — the number of reviewers this sweep moved from `ACTIVE` to `PROBATION` — and `shortfall_manuscript_ids`, the identifiers of manuscripts whose `review_shortfall_flag` became true during the run. That flag is true exactly when the manuscript's current round holds fewer submitted reviews than the journal's `min_completed_reviews_for_decision` **and** no invitation on the manuscript remains in `INVITED` or `ACCEPTED` — in other words, when there is no longer any prospect of the round completing itself.

### 3.6. Review Submission, Lateness and Reviewer Reliability

A reviewer who has accepted submits the review against that invitation, and the resulting `Review` row records the `invitation_id` of the invitation it answers. The submission carries a `recommendation` drawn from `ACCEPT`, `MINOR_REVISION`, `MAJOR_REVISION` and `REJECT`, three integer ratings each from 1 to 5 — `novelty_score` for novelty, `rigor_score` for methodological rigour and `clarity_score` for presentation clarity — and optional `comments` to the editor. Only the reviewer named on the invitation may submit, and the invitation must be in state `ACCEPTED`: a submission against an invitation in any other state, including `INVITED`, is refused with `ERR_INVITATION_NOT_ACCEPTED` rather than with a generic state error. The manuscript must additionally be at `UNDER_REVIEW` or `DECISION_PENDING`.

The review's `submitted_at` is the current clock instant, and its `days_late` is derived from the invitation's deadline: it is the number of whole days by which the submission overran, computed as the elapsed milliseconds from `due_at` to `submitted_at` divided by 86,400,000 and rounded **up** to a whole number, with any negative result replaced by 0. A review delivered at or before `due_at` therefore has `days_late` 0, and one delivered five minutes past the deadline has `days_late` 1.

Submission then closes the invitation and updates the reviewer: the invitation moves `ACCEPTED → SUBMITTED` with `closed_reason` `REVIEW_SUBMITTED`, the reviewer's `submitted_count` rises by 1, their `late_days_total` rises by this review's `days_late`, and their `open_invitation_count` falls by 1. The ordering of the next two steps is contractual: **both reliability variants are recomputed first**, and only then is this review's `reliability_weight` frozen from the freshly-written `reliability_score_rolling`. The weight is the reviewer's new rolling score divided by 100, rounded half up to 4 decimal places, and then floored at 0.2500 — that is, the larger of 0.2500 and the rounded quotient — so even a reviewer with a rolling score of zero still carries a quarter of a vote. Because the recomputation happens first, the weight already reflects the very submission it is attached to.

Reviewer reliability is the system's judgement of a reviewer's conduct, and it is computed twice on every closing event, under two variants. The scoring set for a variant is a set of that reviewer's **closed** invitations, closed meaning in state `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED`, `SUBMITTED` or `WITHDRAWN`. Invitations still `INVITED` or `ACCEPTED` are in flight and contribute nothing at all, which is why a new reviewer holding three open invitations keeps the neutral seed rather than being punished for silence. Conversely, a `WITHDRAWN` invitation counts as a closed non-delivery, which is exactly why the decision cascade's withdrawals move a reviewer's score. The `ROLLING` variant's set is the reviewer's closed invitations whose `created_at` is at or after the current clock instant minus 180 days, the lower bound being inclusive; it is written to `reliability_score_rolling`. The `LIFETIME` variant's set is every closed invitation the reviewer has ever had, so conduct older than 180 days keeps its full weight and the two variants disagree whenever behaviour changed across that boundary; it is written to `reliability_score_lifetime`. Both are written in the same request.

Within the chosen set, five tallies are taken: the number of closed invitations; the number of them in state `SUBMITTED`; the number in state `EXPIRED`; the number in state `SUBMITTED` whose review has `days_late` of 0; and the sum of `days_late` across the reviews of the `SUBMITTED` ones. When the set holds no closed invitation at all, the score is exactly 50.00 — the same neutral seed used at registration. Otherwise three rates are formed as exact fractions with no intermediate rounding: the **delivery rate** is the submitted count divided by the closed count; the **on-time rate** is the on-time count divided by the submitted count, taken as 0 when nothing was submitted; and the **responsiveness rate** is 1 minus the expired count divided by the closed count, so declining promptly costs a reviewer nothing while letting an invitation lapse does. A **lateness penalty** is then formed as the smaller of 20.0 and 2.5 multiplied by the total late days, capping the damage any single stretch of tardiness can do at twenty points. The raw score is 100 multiplied by the sum of three weighted rates — the delivery rate weighted 0.45, the on-time rate weighted 0.35 and the responsiveness rate weighted 0.20 — with the lateness penalty subtracted. That raw value is clamped into the closed range from 0.0 to 100.0 and then rounded half up to 2 decimal places to give the stored score.

Once the `ROLLING` score has been written, the reviewer's `status` is reconsidered. A reviewer at `ACTIVE` moves to `PROBATION` when the new `reliability_score_rolling` is strictly below 40.00 **and** the rolling set holds at least 3 closed invitations — the minimum-evidence requirement stops a single unlucky expiry from demoting a reviewer. A reviewer already at `PROBATION` returns to `ACTIVE` when the new `reliability_score_rolling` reaches 60.00 or more; the gap between the demotion floor and the reinstatement ceiling is deliberate hysteresis. A `RETIRED` reviewer's status is never altered by this computation. Only the `ROLLING` variant is consulted by this guard, by the reviewer selector's ranking, and by the frozen `reliability_weight`; the `LIFETIME` variant is reported for audit and used for nothing else.

Submitting a review also moves the manuscript. Its `submitted_review_count` is recomputed as the number of reviews whose invitation belongs to this manuscript and whose invitation `round` equals the manuscript's current `revision_round`. If that count reaches the journal's `min_completed_reviews_for_decision`, the manuscript automatically advances `UNDER_REVIEW → DECISION_PENDING` inside the same request, and its `version` advances by 1. Further reviews may still be submitted while the manuscript sits at `DECISION_PENDING`; the auto-transition is a signal that a decision has become possible, not a closing of the round.

### 3.7. Editorial Decisions, Revision Rounds and Appeals

The handling editor issues the decision. The request carries an `editorial_note`, the letter text sent to the author, and may carry an explicit `category` drawn from `ACCEPT`, `MINOR_REVISION`, `MAJOR_REVISION`, `REJECT` and `REJECT_AND_RESUBMIT`. The manuscript must be at `DECISION_PENDING`, the current round must hold at least the journal's `min_completed_reviews_for_decision` submitted reviews — fewer is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_REVIEWS` — and the manuscript must carry no unresolved `HIGH`-severity ethics case, which is refused with `ERR_ETHICS_HOLD`.

Before the category is resolved, the server computes the round's **consensus index**. The scoring set is every review whose invitation belongs to this manuscript, whose invitation `round` equals the manuscript's current `revision_round`, and whose invitation is in state `SUBMITTED`; reviews from earlier rounds and invitations that were declined, expired or withdrawn are excluded entirely. Each review contributes a numeric value from its `recommendation`: `ACCEPT` counts 4, `MINOR_REVISION` counts 3, `MAJOR_REVISION` counts 2 and `REJECT` counts 1. Under the journal's `UNWEIGHTED` mode, the index is the sum of those values divided by the number of reviews in the set, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, so a barely-credible reviewer moves the index exactly as much as an exemplary one. Under `RELIABILITY_WEIGHTED` mode, the index is the sum over the set of each review's recommendation value multiplied by that review's frozen `reliability_weight`, divided by the sum of those same weights, rounded half up to 2 decimal places — so the identical set of reviews can land in a different band than it would have unweighted.

The index is then banded into an `auto_category` using inclusive lower bounds: an index of 3.50 or more gives `ACCEPT`; below that, 2.50 or more gives `MINOR_REVISION`; below that, 1.75 or more gives `MAJOR_REVISION`; anything below 1.75 gives `REJECT`. An index of exactly 2.50 therefore resolves to `MINOR_REVISION`. Note that `auto_category` never takes the value `REJECT_AND_RESUBMIT` — that category exists only as an explicit editorial choice.

The stored decision records both readings. Its `category` equals the request's `category` when one was supplied, with `category_source` set to `EXPLICIT`; when the request omitted `category`, the stored `category` equals the computed `auto_category` and `category_source` is `AUTO`. `auto_category` is recorded either way, so an editor who overrode the mathematics leaves a trace of what the mathematics said. The decision also freezes `consensus_mode` from the journal's setting at that instant, stores the computed `consensus_index`, records `reviews_counted` as the number of reviews that entered the computation, stamps `round` with the manuscript's `revision_round`, and pins `decided_by_editor_id` to the acting `X-User-ID` — that last field being what makes appeal segregation of duties checkable later. Regardless of category, the manuscript's `consensus_index_at_last_decision` is set to this decision's `consensus_index`, its `decision_count` rises by 1, and its `version` advances by 1.

Where the manuscript goes next depends on the resolved category, and each branch carries its own cascade.

A resolved category of `MINOR_REVISION` or `MAJOR_REVISION` moves the manuscript `DECISION_PENDING → REVISION_REQUESTED`, but only while its `revision_round` is strictly less than the journal's `max_revision_rounds`; a revision decision that would open the round after the last permitted one is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. Every invitation on the manuscript still in `INVITED` or `ACCEPTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `closed_reason` `DECISION_CASCADE`, processed in invitation `created_at` ascending order; each such withdrawal lowers that reviewer's `open_invitation_count` by 1 and re-runs both reliability variants for that reviewer, and a resulting rolling score below 40.00 with at least 3 closed invitations in the rolling window moves that reviewer `ACTIVE → PROBATION`. The handling editor's assignment stays `ACTIVE`, because the same editor will handle the next round.

A resolved category of `ACCEPT` moves the manuscript `DECISION_PENDING → IN_PRODUCTION`, but only when the journal either does not require a data availability statement or the manuscript's `data_availability_statement` is a non-empty string; otherwise the decision is refused with `ERR_DATA_AVAILABILITY_REQUIRED`. Four cascade effects follow. One `ProductionJob` is created for the manuscript, at `content_stage` `COPYEDITING` and `clearance_stage` `LICENSE_PENDING` — production jobs are **never** created by a direct client call, so this decision is the only way one comes into existence, and a manuscript may have at most one. Every invitation still `INVITED` or `ACCEPTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `closed_reason` `DECISION_CASCADE`, in `created_at` ascending order, each lowering that reviewer's `open_invitation_count` by 1 and re-running both reliability variants. The `ACTIVE` assignment moves to `RELEASED` with `released_reason` `DECISION_FINAL`, lowering that editor's `active_assignment_count` by 1. And the manuscript's `version` advances by 1.

A resolved category of `REJECT` or `REJECT_AND_RESUBMIT` moves the manuscript `DECISION_PENDING → REJECTED`, with the same four-link cascade spelled out link by link. First the manuscript's status changes and its `consensus_index_at_last_decision` and `decision_count` are updated. Second, every invitation on the manuscript still in `INVITED` or `ACCEPTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `closed_reason` `DECISION_CASCADE`. Third, caused by that second link and applied once per withdrawn invitation in invitation `created_at` ascending order, each affected reviewer has `open_invitation_count` lowered by 1 and both reliability variants recomputed. Fourth, caused by that third link, any reviewer whose recomputed `reliability_score_rolling` is below 40.00 while holding at least 3 closed invitations in the rolling window moves `ACTIVE → PROBATION`. Alongside those, the `ACTIVE` assignment moves to `RELEASED` with `released_reason` `DECISION_FINAL`, lowering that editor's `active_assignment_count` by 1, and the manuscript's `version` advances by 1.

When a revision is requested, the corresponding author submits the revised paper. The revision carries a fresh `page_count` and `figure_count`, a `response_letter` addressing the reviewers, and optionally a replacement `data_availability_statement`. Only the manuscript's corresponding author may submit it, and only while the manuscript is at `REVISION_REQUESTED`. The manuscript moves `REVISION_REQUESTED → REVISION_SUBMITTED`, its `revision_round` rises by 1, the supplied `page_count`, `figure_count` and `data_availability_statement` replace the stored ones so that `formatting_status` is recomputed against the journal's limits, its `integrity_status` returns to `PENDING` because the new round has no screening yet, its `submitted_review_count` is recomputed for the new round and is therefore 0, and its `version` advances by 1. From there the manuscript re-enters review only through the screening gate described in §3.3, which is what closes the loop `REVISION_REQUESTED → REVISION_SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW → DECISION_PENDING → REVISION_REQUESTED` — a cycle the journal's `max_revision_rounds` bounds.

A rejection is not necessarily the end. The corresponding author may appeal, naming the `decision_id` of the rejected decision and stating their `grounds`. An appeal is permitted only against the manuscript's **most recent** decision, only when that decision's `category` is `REJECT` or `REJECT_AND_RESUBMIT`, only within 30 days of that decision, and at most once per decision; a request that fails any of those four conditions is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Filing an appeal creates it at state `FILED`, copies the decision's manuscript into the appeal's `manuscript_id` for filtering, leaves `adjudicated_by_editor_id`, `rationale` and `adjudicated_at` absent, and moves the manuscript `REJECTED → UNDER_APPEAL`, advancing the manuscript's `version` by 1.

Adjudication belongs to a chief editor of the manuscript's journal, and specifically **not** to the editor who issued the appealed decision: an acting `X-User-ID` equal to the decision's `decided_by_editor_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The adjudication carries an `outcome` of either `UPHELD` or `OVERTURNED` and an optional `rationale`, and requires the appeal to be at `FILED`; an appeal in any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPEAL_STATE`, and an already-adjudicated appeal with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED`. Both outcomes set `adjudicated_by_editor_id` to the acting `X-User-ID` and `adjudicated_at` to the current clock instant, and advance the appeal's `version` by 1.

`UPHELD` moves the appeal `FILED → UPHELD` and returns the manuscript `UNDER_APPEAL → REJECTED`, advancing the manuscript's `version` by 1. `OVERTURNED` moves the appeal `FILED → OVERTURNED` and triggers a four-link cascade. The first link is the appeal's own state change. The second, caused by it, moves the manuscript `UNDER_APPEAL → UNDER_REVIEW` and recomputes its `review_shortfall_flag`. The third, caused by the second, re-runs the editor selector over the eligible pool with the decision's `decided_by_editor_id` excluded and creates a fresh assignment at `PROPOSED`, or, when that pool is empty, creates nothing and sets the manuscript's `handling_gap_flag` to true. The fourth, caused by the third and only when an assignment was actually created, raises that editor's `active_assignment_count` by 1. The manuscript's `version` advances by 1.

### 3.8. Publication-Ethics Cases, Correction and Retraction

A publication-ethics case may be opened against a manuscript by a chief editor or by the editorial office. It carries a `case_type` drawn from `AUTHORSHIP_DISPUTE`, `DATA_AVAILABILITY`, `IMAGE_INTEGRITY` and `PLAGIARISM`, a `severity` of `LOW`, `MEDIUM` or `HIGH`, and a `description` carrying the allegation text. A manually opened case begins at state `OPEN` with `opened_by` set to `MANUAL`, and with `resolution_statement` and `resolved_at` both absent. At most one unresolved case of a given `case_type` may exist per manuscript, a second one colliding with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`.

Severity is what gives a case teeth. An unresolved case of severity `HIGH` — unresolved meaning its state is `OPEN`, `INVESTIGATION`, `AUTHOR_RESPONSE_REQUESTED` or `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` — freezes exactly three operations on its manuscript: decision issuance, invoice issuance and publication. All three are refused with `ERR_ETHICS_HOLD`. Cases of `LOW` or `MEDIUM` severity are investigated without freezing anything.

A chief editor walks the case forward one state at a time by naming the `to_state`, optionally with a `note`. The only declared step out of the opening state is `OPEN → INVESTIGATION`. From `INVESTIGATION` the path **forks**, and both branches are equally available: `INVESTIGATION → AUTHOR_RESPONSE_REQUESTED` is the route an authorship dispute normally takes, because the parties must be heard, while `INVESTIGATION → COMMITTEE_REVIEW` goes straight to the committee, skipping the author-response step where no response is needed. The only declared step out of the author-response state is `AUTHOR_RESPONSE_REQUESTED → COMMITTEE_REVIEW`, so the two branches rejoin there. A requested `to_state` that is not declared from the case's current state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ETHICS_CASE_STATE`, and each successful advance raises the case's `version` by 1.

Resolution happens only from `COMMITTEE_REVIEW`, carries a `resolution` and a `resolution_statement` — the text published alongside the outcome — and is barred to the editor who opened the case, an acting `X-User-ID` matching the opener being refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Every resolution sets `resolved_at` to the current clock instant, stores the `resolution_statement`, and raises the case's `version` by 1. The three resolutions diverge sharply in what they do to the article.

`NO_ACTION` moves the case `COMMITTEE_REVIEW → RESOLVED_NO_ACTION` and leaves the manuscript's status untouched. Its practical effect is release: because the case is no longer unresolved, a `HIGH`-severity hold lifts and decisions, invoicing and publication become possible again.

`CORRECTION` moves the case `COMMITTEE_REVIEW → CORRECTION_ISSUED` and moves the manuscript `PUBLISHED → CORRECTED`. A correction requires the manuscript to be at `PUBLISHED`; requesting one against a manuscript in any other status is refused with `ERR_RETRACTION_NOT_SUPPORTED`. A correction is a bibliographically visible amendment, not a withdrawal: the journal's `published_article_count` is unchanged, because `CORRECTED` still counts as published, and the production job's `indexing_status` remains `INDEXED`. The manuscript's `version` advances by 1.

`RETRACTION` moves the case `COMMITTEE_REVIEW → RETRACTION_APPROVED` and requires the manuscript to be at `PUBLISHED` or `CORRECTED`; against any other status it is refused with `ERR_RETRACTION_NOT_SUPPORTED`. It then drives a four-link cascade, and every link matters. The first link is the case's own resolution: the case reaches `RETRACTION_APPROVED` and its `resolved_at` is set to the current clock instant. The second, caused by the first, moves the manuscript `PUBLISHED → RETRACTED` — or `CORRECTED → RETRACTED` when a correction had already been issued — and sets its `retracted_at` to the current clock instant; `RETRACTED` is terminal. The third, caused by the second, drives the manuscript's production job to `content_stage` `TERMINATED`, `clearance_stage` `WITHDRAWN` and `indexing_status` `WITHDRAWN`, which as a side consequence frees that job's typesetting-slot occupancy for other articles. The fourth, caused by the third, lowers the journal's `published_article_count` by 1 and raises its `retracted_article_count` by 1. Both the manuscript's `version` and the production job's `version` advance by 1. The cascade is identical whether the retraction is reached from `PUBLISHED` or from `CORRECTED`. Once `RETRACTED`, no further transition is possible and any attempt is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED`; the same applies to a case already in `RESOLVED_NO_ACTION`, `CORRECTION_ISSUED` or `RETRACTION_APPROVED`.

### 3.9. Article Processing Charges and Fee Settlement

The fees desk issues the article processing charge invoice for a manuscript. Issuance is permitted at any point while the manuscript's `status` is neither `DESK_REJECTED` nor `RETRACTED`, so a journal may bill before production begins or during it. The request carries the `expected_manuscript_version` guard and, optionally, `page_charge_count` and `color_figure_count`, each defaulting to 0 and never negative — the pages and colour figures actually billable on this invoice. An unresolved `HIGH`-severity ethics case blocks issuance with `ERR_ETHICS_HOLD`. The created invoice begins at status `ISSUED`, freezes `license_at_issue` from the manuscript's `oa_license` at that instant, and leaves `waiver_reason` and `settled_at` absent.

Pricing runs in a fixed waterfall, and each step rounds to a whole cent before the next step consumes it. **Step one** converts the list price into a licence-adjusted base: `base_cents` is the journal's `apc_base_cents` multiplied by the multiplier for the frozen licence, rounded half up to a whole cent. The multipliers are 1.00 for `CC_BY`, 0.90 for `CC_BY_NC`, 0.85 for `CC_BY_NC_ND` and 0.00 for `GREEN_SELF_ARCHIVE` — the green self-archiving route carries no article processing charge at all. **Step two** applies the income-band waiver: `waiver_pct` is taken from the corresponding author's `country_income_band`, being 0 for `HIGH`, 25 for `UPPER_MIDDLE`, 50 for `LOWER_MIDDLE` and 100 for `LOW`, and `waiver_cents` is `base_cents` multiplied by `waiver_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to a whole cent. **Step three** applies the institutional discount, and the base it works from is pinned: `institutional_pct` is 15 when the corresponding author's `institutional_agreement` is true and 0 otherwise, and `institutional_cents` is the **post-waiver remainder** — `base_cents` less `waiver_cents` — multiplied by `institutional_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to a whole cent on its own before any subtraction. Computing both discounts against `base_cents` and subtracting them together yields a different number of cents and is not what this system does. **Step four** forms `net_apc_cents` as `base_cents` less `waiver_cents` less `institutional_cents`. **Step five** writes off small balances with a strict test: `auto_waived` starts false, and when `net_apc_cents` is greater than 0 **and** strictly below 2000 cents — the minimum billable amount, twenty currency units — `net_apc_cents` is forced to 0 and `auto_waived` becomes true. A `net_apc_cents` of exactly 2000 is billed as it stands; one that was already 0 leaves `auto_waived` false, because nothing was written off. Nothing in this computation reads any earlier invoice.

Surcharges are then computed in a second, single-pass waterfall. **Step one** forms `raw_surcharge_cents` as `page_charge_count` multiplied by the journal's `page_charge_cents_per_page`, plus `color_figure_count` multiplied by the journal's `color_figure_charge_cents`. **Step two** forms `provisional_total_cents` as `net_apc_cents` plus `raw_surcharge_cents`, and keys the relief bracket on that provisional figure and on nothing else: `surcharge_relief_pct` is 20 when `provisional_total_cents` is at least 250000 cents, otherwise 10 when it is at least 150000 cents, otherwise 0. **Step three** applies the relief to the surcharge alone: `surcharge_cents` is `raw_surcharge_cents` less the result of `raw_surcharge_cents` multiplied by `surcharge_relief_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to a whole cent — the single rounding in this whole computation. **Step four** forms `total_due_cents` as `net_apc_cents` plus `surcharge_cents`. The evaluation order is pinned to exactly one pass: even when the relief pulls `total_due_cents` back below the bracket floor that triggered it, the bracket is **never** re-derived from `total_due_cents`.

Issuing the invoice raises the journal's `apc_receivable_cents` by the invoice's `total_due_cents` and advances the manuscript's `version` by 1. An invoice whose `total_due_cents` computes to 0 — a green self-archived paper with no surcharges, or a fully waived one — needs no settlement at all, and its issuance immediately advances the manuscript's production job on the clearance dimension, `LICENSE_PENDING → FEE_CLEARED`. That advance disregards the zero-total invoice's own `ISSUED` status, but it is still held back by any **other** invoice of the same manuscript still at status `ISSUED`, and it simply does nothing — issuance still succeeding — when the manuscript has no production job yet or its job's clearance dimension has already left `LICENSE_PENDING`. Clearing the fee dimension does not settle the zero-total invoice itself: it stays at status `ISSUED` with `settled_at` absent, so rule 28 of §2.10 keeps counting it against publication until it is waived or cancelled.

Settlement closes an invoice, and only an invoice still at `ISSUED` may be settled; any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INVOICE_STATE`, and a settled invoice with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED`. The request carries an `action` of `PAY`, `WAIVE` or `CANCEL`, plus `amount_cents` or `waiver_reason` as the action demands. Every settlement sets `settled_at` to the current clock instant and advances the invoice's `version` by 1.

`PAY` is available to the production desk and requires the payment to be exact: `amount_cents` must equal the invoice's `total_due_cents` precisely, and `total_due_cents` must be greater than 0. Partial payments, over-payments and attempts to pay a zero-value invoice are all refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. On success the invoice moves `ISSUED → PAID` and the journal's `apc_receivable_cents` falls by `total_due_cents`.

`WAIVE` is reserved to the editorial office and requires a non-empty `waiver_reason`; omitting it is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_ALLOWED`. On success the invoice moves `ISSUED → WAIVED`, the journal's `apc_receivable_cents` falls by `total_due_cents`, and its `apc_waived_cents` rises by the same amount — the only event in the system that moves the waived ledger.

`CANCEL` withdraws an invoice that should not have been issued, and is permitted only while the manuscript either has no production job at all or has one still at `clearance_stage` `LICENSE_PENDING`; a cancellation attempted after the clearance dimension has moved on is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_ALLOWED`. On success the invoice moves `ISSUED → CANCELLED` and the journal's `apc_receivable_cents` falls by `total_due_cents`, restoring the receivable ledger to its pre-issue value, while `apc_waived_cents` is untouched. Because cancellation leaves the clearance dimension at `LICENSE_PENDING`, a corrected invoice can be issued in its place.

Both `PAY` and `WAIVE` advance the manuscript's production job on the clearance dimension, `LICENSE_PENDING → FEE_CLEARED`, once no invoice of that manuscript remains at status `ISSUED`. Where several invoices were issued for one manuscript, that condition is what makes the last settlement the one that clears the fees.

### 3.10. Production, Archival Deposit, Publication and Journal Metrics

A production job comes into existence only as the cascade of an accepting decision, and the only way a client discovers it is by listing the manuscript's production jobs — of which there is at most one. The job carries the two independent dimensions described in §2.9, starting at `content_stage` `COPYEDITING` and `clearance_stage` `LICENSE_PENDING`, with `proof_round` 0, `indexing_status` `NOT_INDEXED`, and `slot_start`, `slot_end`, `archival_repository`, `deposit_reference`, `issue_label` and `published_at` all absent.

The journal runs a **single typesetting desk**, so booking is exclusive rather than merely counted. The production desk books a closed date interval by supplying `slot_start` and `slot_end`, both whole calendar dates in the form YYYY-MM-DD and both inclusive endpoints, alongside the `expected_production_job_version` guard. A `slot_end` earlier than `slot_start` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. Conflict is then tested against every **other** production job whose manuscript belongs to the same journal, whose `slot_start` has been set, and whose `content_stage` is not `TERMINATED` — which is precisely why a retracted article releases its slot. Two closed intervals conflict when the journal's `typeset_changeover_buffer_days` buffer, applied symmetrically to both, brings them into contact: the proposed slot's start falls on or before the existing slot's end plus the buffer, **and** the existing slot's start falls on or before the proposed slot's end plus the buffer. Any single conflicting job is fatal, and the booking is refused with `ERR_TYPESET_SLOT_CONFLICT`. Concretely, with a two-day buffer, the earliest legal start after an existing slot is the day after the existing end plus the buffer — that is, the existing end plus three days. A successful booking stores `slot_start` and `slot_end` and advances the job's `version` by 1; rebooking is permitted while the content dimension is at `COPYEDITING` or `TYPESETTING`, subject to the same version guard.

The content dimension is advanced by the production desk one step at a time, naming the `to_stage`, and the whole dimension requires the manuscript to still be `IN_PRODUCTION`. Advancing `COPYEDITING → TYPESETTING` requires a booked slot: an advance attempted while `slot_start` is absent is refused with `ERR_SLOT_NOT_BOOKED`. Advancing `TYPESETTING → PROOF_SENT` raises the job's `proof_round` by 1, so the proof rounds are counted as they are sent rather than as they are answered. A `to_stage` that is not declared from the job's current stage pair is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PRODUCTION_STAGE`. Each successful advance leaves the clearance dimension completely untouched and raises the job's `version` by 1.

Proofs are answered by the corresponding author, and by nobody else. The response carries `approved` and optional `proof_comments`, and requires the content dimension to be at `PROOF_SENT`. An `approved` of true moves the content dimension `PROOF_SENT → PROOF_APPROVED`. An `approved` of false loops it back, `PROOF_SENT → TYPESETTING`, but only while the job's `proof_round` is strictly less than the journal's `max_proof_rounds`; a rejection attempted when `proof_round` has already reached that ceiling is refused with `ERR_PROOF_ROUND_LIMIT_REACHED`. Either answer raises the job's `version` by 1 and leaves the clearance dimension alone.

The clearance dimension advances on its own schedule. Fee clearance, `LICENSE_PENDING → FEE_CLEARED`, is driven by the invoice events of §3.9 rather than by any production call, and requires that no invoice of the manuscript remains at status `ISSUED` — except that a zero-total invoice does not count against itself at the moment of its own issuance, as §3.9 sets out. Archival deposit, `FEE_CLEARED → DEPOSIT_CONFIRMED`, is recorded by the production desk with an `archival_repository` drawn from `CLOCKSS`, `PORTICO` and `LOCKSS` and a `deposit_reference` carrying the archive's receipt identifier; it requires the manuscript to be `IN_PRODUCTION` and the clearance dimension to have reached `FEE_CLEARED`, an earlier attempt being refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_NOT_READY`. A successful deposit stores both fields, leaves the content dimension untouched, and raises the job's `version` by 1.

Publication is the **join** of the two dimensions, and the only operation that reads both. It requires the manuscript to be `IN_PRODUCTION`, the content dimension to be at `PROOF_APPROVED` and the clearance dimension to be at `DEPOSIT_CONFIRMED`; a request that arrives before either has landed is refused with `ERR_PRODUCTION_STAGE_NOT_READY`, which is evaluated in place of the ordinary state gate. It further requires that the manuscript carry no invoice still at status `ISSUED`, an outstanding invoice being refused with `ERR_UNPAID_APC_INVOICE`, and that the manuscript carry no unresolved ethics case of any severity in state `OPEN`, `INVESTIGATION`, `AUTHOR_RESPONSE_REQUESTED` or `COMMITTEE_REVIEW`, an open case being refused with `ERR_ETHICS_HOLD`.

The publication request supplies the issue's `publication_date`, a whole calendar date in the form YYYY-MM-DD, and may supply the `issue_label` — a string such as "2027-01" — used in the minted identifier. On success the manuscript moves `IN_PRODUCTION → PUBLISHED`, its `publication_date` is stored, its `doi` is minted as the journal's `doi_prefix`, then a solidus, then the job's `issue_label`, then a full stop, then the manuscript's `journal_sequence_number` left-padded with zeros to four digits, and its `embargo_end_date` is projected. The job's `indexing_status` becomes `INDEXED` and its `published_at` is set to the current clock instant. The journal's `published_article_count` rises by 1. Both the manuscript's `version` and the job's `version` advance by 1.

The embargo projection depends entirely on the licence. For a `GREEN_SELF_ARCHIVE` manuscript, the target month is the `publication_date`'s month advanced by the journal's `embargo_months`, normalised into a calendar year and month, and the `embargo_end_date` is that year and month with the day taken as the smaller of the `publication_date`'s day-of-month and the last calendar day of the target month — so a publication on the 31st clamps down to the last day of a shorter target month rather than spilling into the next one. For any `CC_BY`, `CC_BY_NC` or `CC_BY_NC_ND` manuscript, access is immediate and `embargo_end_date` simply equals `publication_date`.

Finally, the society's boards read a journal's performance on demand, and the whole figure is computed at read time and never stored. The scoring universe is every manuscript of that journal, in any status. `submitted_count` is the count of those manuscripts. `desk_rejected_count` counts those at `DESK_REJECTED`. `published_count` counts those at `PUBLISHED` or `CORRECTED`. `retracted_count` counts those at `RETRACTED`. `acceptance_rate_pct` divides the accepted set — manuscripts at `IN_PRODUCTION`, `PUBLISHED`, `CORRECTED` or `RETRACTED` — by the decided set — manuscripts at `DESK_REJECTED`, `REJECTED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, `PUBLISHED`, `CORRECTED` or `RETRACTED` — multiplies by 100 and rounds half up to 2 decimal places, yielding 0.00 when the decided set is empty; a retracted article still counts as accepted in both the numerator and the denominator, because it was accepted. `median_days_to_first_decision` is built from every manuscript of the journal whose `decision_count` is greater than 0: for each, the elapsed milliseconds from the manuscript's `created_at` to the `created_at` of its earliest decision are divided by 86,400,000 and rounded **up** to a whole number of days; those day counts are sorted ascending; with an odd number of entries the median is the middle one, and with an even number it is the mean of the two middle ones rounded half up to 2 decimal places. When no manuscript of the journal has a decision at all, `median_days_to_first_decision` is null — the one aggregate here that is null rather than 0.00 when empty. `reviewer_invitation_acceptance_rate_pct` takes every invitation belonging to that journal's manuscripts, divides the number in state `ACCEPTED` or `SUBMITTED` by the total, multiplies by 100 and rounds half up to 2 decimal places, yielding 0.00 when there are no invitations. `reviewers_on_probation_count` counts reviewers whose `status` is `PROBATION` across the entire society database rather than per journal, since the database is shared. `apc_receivable_cents` and `apc_waived_cents` are read straight from the journal's stored ledgers.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

All endpoints are rooted at the service origin with no additional prefix, so each path below is the complete route. Every endpoint that creates a new resource returns HTTP 201 with the created resource in the `data` field. Every endpoint that modifies an existing resource — including the state-transition, settlement and production endpoints, which are modelled as POST because they append a business event rather than replace a representation — returns HTTP 200 with the affected resource. Read endpoints return HTTP 200, single-resource reads with `response_type` "object" and list reads with `response_type` "array" plus the pagination `meta` fields of §2.3. Failures return the HTTP status recorded in §5.

| Method | Path | Description | Authorized Role(s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| POST | `/journals` | Register a society journal with its editorial, fee and production configuration. | SystemAdmin |
