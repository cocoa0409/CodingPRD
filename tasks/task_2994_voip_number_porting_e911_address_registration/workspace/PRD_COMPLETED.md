# VoIP Porting & E911 API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Moving a telephone number away from an incumbent carrier is the single most failure-prone moment in a VoIP subscriber's life. The losing carrier will only release a number when the data the subscriber claims about their old account matches, closely enough, the customer service record the carrier actually holds. When it does not, the port bounces back with a reason code, the subscriber corrects a digit, and the whole comparison runs again. Meanwhile the subscriber must not lose the ability to dial 911, which means every device on the account has to carry a dispatchable street address that a regulator has accepted — and if the device is nomadic and the network reports it somewhere else, that address has to be re-captured and re-verified before the device is trusted again.

This document specifies the API that Ooma's porting, numbering, E911 and billing operations teams use to run that machinery end to end. It governs the validation of a port-in request against a transcribed carrier record, the reason codes a rejection may carry and how many times a request may be corrected and resubmitted, the booking of cutover windows on a carrier's calendar subject to that carrier's firm-order-commitment interval and quiet-time buffer, the provisioning of a temporary bridging number that keeps the subscriber reachable across the cutover, and the reclamation of numbers that fall out of use. It also governs the regulatory half of the same story: the capture and validation of dispatchable locations, the detection of nomadic device relocation, the monthly remittance of 911 fees to the taxing jurisdiction, and the toll-fraud and international-spend controls that stop a compromised endpoint from generating an unpayable bill overnight.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **separation of duties is structural, not advisory**: the specialist who files a port request may not transcribe the losing carrier's record for it or reject it, the administrator who captures a dispatchable location may not be the one who validates it, and the accountant who assesses a fee may not be the one who voids it. Second, **derived state is never a client's responsibility**: inventory censuses, fee ledgers, compliance postures and risk scores are recomputed by the server at the end of every operation that could disturb them, and the cascades that propagate a single change across the port request, the cutover slot, the bridge, the reservation, the number and the block are specified here in full so that no implementation has to guess where a cascade stops.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section applies across the whole surface of the API. Where §3 narrates a workflow, it does so on top of these rules and does not restate them.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which it is acting. Surrounding whitespace is stripped from both header values before either is examined, so a header that carries only whitespace counts as empty. If either header is absent, present but empty or whitespace-only, or `X-User-Role` names something that is not one of the eight roles below, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined. If both headers are well formed but the named role does not appear in the authorized-role list of the endpoint being addressed (§4), the request is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The eight recognized roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `NumberingAdmin`, `PortingSpecialist`, `CarrierLiaison`, `AccountAdmin`, `E911Validator`, `SecurityAnalyst` and `BillingAdmin`.

- A `PlatformAdmin` is the platform operator. It owns the logical clock and the global reference data — accounts, carriers and jurisdictions are created by nobody else — and it is a permitted reader on every resource in the domain. It may also register devices, post network reports, place call attempts and capture dispatchable locations alongside subscriber-side administrators.
- A `NumberingAdmin` runs inventory: it creates blocks, mints numbers into them, holds and releases reservations, releases an assigned number back to the parking pool, and triggers reclamation runs.
- A `PortingSpecialist` is the Ooma-side porting operator. It files port requests, corrects and resubmits them, cancels them, opens cutover windows on a carrier's calendar, books a window for a validated request, provisions and activates temporary bridges, and completes cutovers. It may never transcribe the losing carrier's customer service record and may never record a carrier rejection.
- A `CarrierLiaison` is the counterpart to that restriction: it and only it transcribes the losing carrier's record onto a port request and records a carrier rejection.
- An `AccountAdmin` is a subscriber-side administrator, scoped to exactly one account.
- An `E911Validator` is the regulatory validator that accepts or rejects a captured dispatchable location. It cannot capture a location itself.
- A `SecurityAnalyst` is the fraud analyst that applies and lifts calling restrictions on a device.
- A `BillingAdmin` is the 911-fee accountant: it assesses provisional fees, voids them, and closes periods with remittance runs.

**Identity semantics.** When `X-User-Role` is `AccountAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Account`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Account` compare the resource's account-bearing field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, the comparison is against the account's own `id` when the account itself is addressed, against `account_id` when a `Device`, `NumberReservation`, `PortRequest`, `E911Registration` or `FeeAssessment` is addressed, and against `assigned_account_id` when a `PhoneNumber` is addressed. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403). The comparison only ever runs on an endpoint whose authorized-role list in §4 includes `AccountAdmin`; because no endpoint addressing a `PhoneNumber`, a `NumberReservation`, a `NumberBlock`, a `Carrier` or a `Jurisdiction` admits `AccountAdmin`, that role is turned away from all of them by `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` at rule 2 and the ownership comparand for those types never fires. This comparison is made before the request body is parsed or validated, so an `AccountAdmin` reaching for another account's resource sees `ERR_ACCOUNT_FORBIDDEN` and not `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Where the resource named in the path cannot be resolved at all, the ownership comparison has nothing to compare against and the applicable not-found error is returned instead. On creation endpoints the owning account is named in the body rather than the path — `account_id` when a port request or device is created, and the owning account of the `device_id` when a dispatchable location is captured — and the same comparison against `X-User-ID` applies to that named owner.

When `X-User-Role` is any of `PlatformAdmin`, `NumberingAdmin`, `PortingSpecialist`, `CarrierLiaison`, `E911Validator`, `SecurityAnalyst` or `BillingAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server never compares it to an entity's `id`. It does, however, record it where the segregation-of-duties rules of §2.10 need it: the identifier of the principal that files a port request is retained as `created_by_user_id` on that request, the identifier of the `BillingAdmin` that produces a fee assessment is retained as `assessed_by_user_id`, and the identifier of the `BillingAdmin` that voids one is retained as `voided_by_user_id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from uppercase letters and digits, for a total length of twenty-four characters.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- A request addressed to a path this API does not define, or to a path it defines with an HTTP method it does not define there, is refused with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) in this same envelope. A request body that is not parseable JSON is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` bounds the number of records returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be lower than 1 and may not be higher than 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, an `offset` below zero, or a non-integer value for either is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400). Either parameter supplied as an empty string is treated exactly as if it had been omitted and falls back to its default; whitespace surrounding an otherwise integral value is ignored, while a value carrying a decimal point, an exponent, a leading `+` or any other non-digit character is a non-integer. Pagination sanity is settled before any filter value is examined, so a request that combines an out-of-range `limit` with a filter value outside its field's enumeration reports `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` rather than `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with ties broken by insertion order so that the record written first appears first. Two list endpoints reverse this: the CSR-snapshot history of a port request and the call-attempt history of a device are both returned newest first, meaning `created_at` descending with ties broken so that the record written last appears first. Because the logical clock of §2.4 only moves when it is told to, several records can legitimately share a `created_at` value, which is exactly why the insertion-order tie-break is specified rather than left open.

The `meta` object of every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true when records remain beyond the current window — concretely, when `offset` plus the number of records actually returned on this page is still strictly less than `total_count`, which makes `has_more` false for any window that begins at or past the end of the matching set.

All list filters are combined conjunctively: a request supplying two filters returns only the records matching both. An unrecognized query parameter is ignored. A filter whose value is syntactically invalid for its field — for example a `status` value outside the field's enumeration, or a `period_month` filter that is not in `YYYY-MM` form — yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An id-shaped filter, by contrast, is never checked for existence: a filter naming a resource that does not exist is a well-formed request that simply matches no records and returns an empty page with `total_count` 0, not a not-found error.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and Temporal Semantics

Nothing in this domain reads the host's wall clock. Every deadline, cutover window, reservation interval, parking expiry, proration anchor, fraud window and remittance eligibility test reads a single server-side **logical clock**. A `PlatformAdmin` moves that clock by supplying a required `current_utc` instant; the response is an object whose `data` carries exactly two fields, the newly established `current_utc` and the `previous_utc` it replaced, both rendered in the canonical instant form. The supplied instant may lie before, at or after the current reading — moving the clock backwards is permitted and is not an error. Before the clock has ever been set it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, so the very first move reports that epoch as its `previous_utc`, and it must never fall back to real time. Moving the clock mutates no entity and triggers no recomputation of any derived field; it only changes what subsequent requests observe as "now".

All timestamps crossing the API boundary are ISO 8601 instants in UTC. Every instant the server emits carries exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`, for example `2026-08-14T09:30:00.000Z`, and is rendered by truncating the underlying reading to millisecond precision. A client-supplied instant is accepted in that same canonical shape and, somewhat more leniently, with the fractional part omitted altogether or written to any number of digits — digits beyond microsecond precision are discarded — with `T` or `t` as the date/time separator, and with `Z`, `z`, `+00:00` or `-00:00` as the UTC designator. Anything else — a missing UTC designator, a non-zero UTC offset, a date or time component outside its calendar range, or a value that is not a string at all — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Two interval conventions coexist deliberately and must not be conflated. **Cutover windows are half-open**: a window runs from `window_start` inclusive to `window_end` exclusive, so an instant equal to `window_end` is outside the window, and two windows that touch end-to-start do not collide. **Reservation intervals are closed at both ends**: a hold runs from `reserved_from` through `reserved_until` inclusive, so two holds that merely touch at an endpoint *do* intersect. Every overlap test in §3 is written against the convention its own entity uses.

Month-shaped values — the `period_month` of a fee assessment and the `last_remitted_period` of a jurisdiction — are strings in `YYYY-MM` form with a month component between `01` and `12`. A calendar month begins at `00:00:00.000Z` on its first day and ends at `23:59:59.999Z` on its last day.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and Immutable Records

Every resource the API mints carries an `id`: a server-generated UUID, with no type prefix and no client control over its value. Every resource also carries a `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits taken from the logical clock at the moment of creation.

Most resources additionally carry two mutation-tracking fields. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC instant refreshed to the current logical-clock reading on every change to the resource, including changes to purely derived fields such as an inventory counter or a compliance posture. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource, and it is the value that optimistic concurrency control (§2.6) compares against. Those two fields move together as a pair, and a change to any field of a resource — including a purely derived one — moves both, with exactly two narrow exceptions that §3 pins where they arise. A refused `REVIEW_UNBLOCK` stores the freshly computed `toll_fraud_score` on the device while leaving its `updated_at` and its `version` exactly as they stood, so that one field write is visible on the next read without the concurrency token having moved (§3.8). And a `FINAL` fee assessment has its `status` and its `remitted_at` settled inside its own construction rather than by a later mutation, so it is born `REMITTED` and is read at `version` 1 with `updated_at` equal to its `created_at` (§3.9). Nothing else in this API writes a field without advancing the pair. `Account`, `Jurisdiction`, `NumberBlock`, `PhoneNumber`, `NumberReservation`, `PortRequest`, `CarrierAccountSnapshot`, `CutoverSlot`, `TemporaryBridge`, `Device`, `E911Registration` and `FeeAssessment` all carry `updated_at` and `version`.

Two record types are immutable once written and therefore carry neither field. A `Carrier` is created once and never edited — it has no update operation, and its response body contains `id` and `created_at` but no `updated_at` and no `version`. A `CallAttempt` is an append-only audit row recording what the fraud and spend gates decided — it likewise exposes `id` and `created_at` only, with no `updated_at` and no `version`.

Unless an endpoint's description states otherwise, a successful response returns the complete resource, with every computed and system-managed field populated to its post-operation value. Fields documented as null-until-set are present in the response with a JSON null rather than omitted, and array-typed fields such as `requested_numbers`, `mismatch_fields`, `last_mismatch_fields` and `unit_fee_breakdown` are present as arrays, empty where nothing has been recorded yet.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Aggregates

Two write operations in this domain read a parent aggregate, reason about its contents, and then write a child against it. Both are protected by optimistic concurrency control on the parent's `version`, and in both the version field is a **required** input — omitting it is a plain `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, not a bypass.

Transcribing a losing carrier's customer service record onto a port request requires `expected_port_request_version` carrying the `version` of the `PortRequest` the liaison last observed. If it does not equal that port request's current `version`, the write is refused with `ERR_STALE_PORT_REQUEST_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This check is the only guard that distinguishes a liaison acting on a fresh carrier pull from one acting on a view that a resubmission or an earlier snapshot has already invalidated: posting a second snapshot is a legitimate product operation, the port request is still `SUBMITTED` in both cases, and no state precondition would catch the stale writer.

Creating a number reservation requires `expected_number_block_version` carrying the `version` of the `NumberBlock` the numbering admin last read. If it does not equal that block's current `version`, the write is refused with `ERR_STALE_NUMBER_BLOCK_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Reservations draw down a shared census — the block's `available_count` and its `max_concurrent_reservations` ceiling — so a reservation computed from a stale census would otherwise succeed without tripping any per-number precondition. The version check pins the census the caller actually reasoned about.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. Where an operation mutates several resources in one cascade, each mutated resource's own `version` increases by 1; a single request can therefore raise the `version` of a port request, a cutover slot and a number block simultaneously.

### 2.7. Validation Precedence

When a request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. The checks below are evaluated in the order listed, and the first one that fails produces the response. Nothing is persisted when a check fails, with exactly two deliberate exceptions. Both are pinned in §3.8, and both sit on the two device endpoints that score fraud. The first is the call-attempt audit row: a refusal from any gate that owns a `decision` value writes its `CallAttempt` before raising. The second is the scoring device itself — a call attempt refused at rule 51 or rule 53 has already committed the recomputed `toll_fraud_score`, and where the fraud gate fired the raised `fraud_block_count` and the tightened `calling_status` with them, taking the ordinary `updated_at` and `version` movement for that write; and a review unblock refused at rule 28 has already stored the recomputed `toll_fraud_score` on the device as a bare field write that moves neither `updated_at` nor `version`. No other refusal anywhere in this API leaves anything behind: every other check fails before its operation has written a single field.

1. Header presence and role recognition — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401), on every endpoint.
2. The acting role appears in the addressed endpoint's authorized-role list — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403), on every endpoint.
3. `AccountAdmin` ownership of the addressed resource, before any body *field* is validated — `ERR_ACCOUNT_FORBIDDEN` (403), on every endpoint whose authorized-role list includes `AccountAdmin`. The comparison is the one §2.1 describes, and it can only fire once the comparand has been determined, which places it after two things rather than strictly before everything below it. On an endpoint that requires a body, the body must first parse as a JSON object: an absent, unparseable or non-object body is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` under rule 4 even when the caller is an `AccountAdmin` reaching at another account's data. And where the owning account is named in the body rather than the path, the raw field is *peeked* rather than validated — `account_id` on filing a port request or registering a device, and the owning account of the body's `device_id` on capturing a dispatchable location. A peek that yields no usable comparand defers to the rung that owns the defect instead of guessing: an `account_id` that is absent, null, not a string or the empty string is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` under rule 4, and a `device_id` that is a well-formed non-empty string naming no device is `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_FOUND` under rule 7. Where the owner is reached through the path the row is loaded before the comparison, so a path id that resolves to nothing yields its own not-found error from rules 6 through 9 — a port-request id that does not exist is `ERR_PORT_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` and never `ERR_ACCOUNT_FORBIDDEN`. Once the comparand *is* in hand, the ownership refusal outranks every remaining body-field, pagination, reference, state, concurrency and cross-field check below.
4. Request-body shape: every required field present, of its declared JSON type, and inside its declared constraint — enum membership, string length, character set, numeric range, ISO 8601 instant syntax, `YYYY-MM` period syntax, non-empty array — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), on every endpoint with a request body. An endpoint that declares inputs requires the body to be a JSON object; a body that is absent, unparseable or not a JSON object is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A field present with a JSON null counts as absent, so an optional field falls back to its documented default and a required field fails. A boolean supplied where an integer is declared is a type error even though JSON booleans are integer-like. Unrecognized body fields are ignored, and an endpoint documented as taking no request body accepts and ignores whatever body is sent with it.
5. Pagination sanity: `limit` an integer between 1 and 100 and `offset` an integer of zero or more — `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400), on every list endpoint.
6. Every referenced `Account` id resolves, evaluated left to right with path segments before body fields — `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND` (404), on reading an account and on creating a port request, a number reservation, a device or a fee assessment.
7. Every referenced `Device` id resolves — `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_FOUND` (404), on reading a device, posting a network report, placing or listing call attempts, applying a calling restriction, and capturing a dispatchable location.
8. Every referenced `PortRequest` id resolves — `ERR_PORT_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` (404), on reading a port request, on every write nested beneath a port request, and on provisioning a temporary bridge.
9. Every other referenced id resolves — a `Carrier`, `Jurisdiction`, `NumberBlock`, `PhoneNumber`, `NumberReservation`, `CutoverSlot`, `TemporaryBridge`, `E911Registration` or `FeeAssessment` — `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404), on every endpoint.
10. The owning account's `status` is `ACTIVE` — `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409), on creating a port request, a number reservation, a device or a dispatchable location, and on placing a call attempt.
11. The port request is in a status from which the requested trigger is declared — `ERR_PORT_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID` (409), on transcribing a snapshot, resubmitting, rejecting, cancelling and completing a cutover.
12. Scheduling requires the port request to be `VALIDATED`, checked before the cutover window is examined at all — `ERR_PORT_REQUEST_NOT_SCHEDULABLE` (409), on scheduling.
13. Resubmission out of `REJECTED` requires a resubmittable rejection reason — `ERR_REJECTION_NOT_RESUBMITTABLE` (409), on resubmitting.
14. The named cutover slot is `OPEN` — `ERR_SLOT_NOT_OPEN` (409), on scheduling.
15. Bridge provisioning requires a `SCHEDULED` port request with a `BOOKED` slot naming it — `ERR_BRIDGE_SLOT_NOT_BOOKED` (409), on provisioning a bridge.
16. The bridge is `PROVISIONED` — `ERR_BRIDGE_STATE_INVALID` (409), on activating a bridge.
17. The phone number is in a status the operation accepts — `ERR_PHONE_NUMBER_STATE_INVALID` (409), on releasing a number, creating a reservation and creating a device.
18. The reservation is `HELD` — `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_HELD` (409), on releasing a reservation, provisioning a bridge and creating a device.
19. The registration is `UNVERIFIED` or `STALE` — `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_VERIFIABLE` (409), on recording a verification outcome.
20. The fee assessment is `ASSESSED` — `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_VOIDABLE` (409), on voiding an assessment.
21. `expected_port_request_version` matches — `ERR_STALE_PORT_REQUEST_VERSION` (412), on transcribing a snapshot.
22. `expected_number_block_version` matches — `ERR_STALE_NUMBER_BLOCK_VERSION` (412), on creating a reservation.
23. The device's `calling_status` is not `FULL_BLOCKED` — `ERR_DEVICE_CALLING_BLOCKED` (409), on placing a call attempt.
24. The device's `registration_status` is not `SUSPENDED_911` — `ERR_DEVICE_911_SUSPENDED` (409), on placing a call attempt.
25. The owning account's `e911_compliance_posture` is not `BLOCKED` — `ERR_ACCOUNT_E911_BLOCKED` (409), on placing a call attempt, on creating a number reservation, and on booking a cutover slot for a port request.
26. A number-holding device has a current registration in `VERIFIED` or `STALE` status — `ERR_E911_REGISTRATION_REQUIRED` (409), on placing a call attempt.
27. A relocating network report is refused on a non-nomadic device — `ERR_NON_NOMADIC_DEVICE_RELOCATION` (409), on posting a network report.
28. A review unblock requires a daily-review risk score below the fraud threshold and a currently `VERIFIED` registration — `ERR_UNBLOCK_REVIEW_SCORE_TOO_HIGH` (409), on applying a calling restriction.
29. Segregation of duties by `X-User-ID` — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION` (403), on transcribing a snapshot, rejecting a port request and voiding a fee assessment.
30. The cutover slot's carrier is the port request's losing carrier — `ERR_SLOT_CARRIER_MISMATCH` (422), on scheduling.
31. The consumed reservation's account matches its consumer's account — `ERR_RESERVATION_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH` (422), on provisioning a bridge and creating a device.
32. The consumed reservation's purpose matches its consumer — `ERR_RESERVATION_PURPOSE_INVALID` (422), on provisioning a bridge and creating a device.
33. The registration's `state_code` equals the named jurisdiction's `state_code` — `ERR_JURISDICTION_STATE_MISMATCH` (422), on capturing a dispatchable location.
34. Natural-key uniqueness — `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` (409), on creating an account, a carrier, a jurisdiction, a number block, a phone number and a device.
35. No other non-terminal port request of the account claims one of the same numbers — `ERR_ACTIVE_PORT_REQUEST_EXISTS` (409), on filing a port request.
36. The resubmission budget is not exhausted — `ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT_REACHED` (409), on resubmitting.
37. No `ASSESSED` assessment already exists for the same account, jurisdiction and period — `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROVISIONAL_ASSESSMENT` (409), on assessing a fee.
38. The same account, jurisdiction and period has no `REMITTED` assessment — `ERR_ASSESSMENT_PERIOD_ALREADY_REMITTED` (409), on assessing a fee.
39. The cutover window's length is legal — `ERR_CUTOVER_WINDOW_LENGTH_INVALID` (422), on opening a cutover slot.
40. The buffer-expanded candidate window does not overlap a live slot of the same carrier — `ERR_CUTOVER_SLOT_OVERLAP` (409), on opening a cutover slot.
41. The chosen slot starts no earlier than the firm-order-commitment floor — `ERR_CUTOVER_BEFORE_FOC` (422), on scheduling.
42. A cutover slot in `BOOKED` status still names the bridge's port request, *and* the logical clock sits inside that slot's half-open window — `ERR_BRIDGE_ACTIVATION_OUTSIDE_WINDOW` (409), on activating a bridge. Both halves of that conjunction report the same code, as §3.6 pins: a bridge whose slot was never booked or has since left `BOOKED` fails this rule exactly as one activated too early or too late does, and never with `ERR_BRIDGE_SLOT_NOT_BOOKED`.
43. The reservation interval is well formed and within the maximum span — `ERR_RESERVATION_WINDOW_INVALID` (422), on creating a reservation.
44. The remittance period is closed and the filing day has arrived — `ERR_REMITTANCE_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED` (422), on running a remittance.
45. Intersecting held reservations of the block plus this one stay within the block's ceiling — `ERR_BLOCK_RESERVATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409), on creating a reservation.
46. The block still has capacity to mint into — `ERR_BLOCK_CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED` (409), on minting a phone number.
47. Automatic selection finds an available number — `ERR_NO_NUMBERS_AVAILABLE` (409), on creating a reservation.
48. The selected number has no intersecting held reservation — `ERR_NUMBER_RESERVATION_OVERLAP` (409), on creating a reservation.
49. International calling is enabled on the account — `ERR_INTL_CALLING_DISABLED` (403), on placing a call attempt.
50. The device is not internationally blocked — `ERR_INTL_BLOCKED_ON_DEVICE` (409), on placing a call attempt.
51. The real-time toll-fraud score stays below the fraud threshold — `ERR_TOLL_FRAUD_THRESHOLD_BREACHED` (409), on placing a call attempt.
52. The assessment selects at least one billable unit — `ERR_NO_BILLABLE_UNITS` (422), on assessing a fee.
53. The projected daily international spend stays within the account's cap — `ERR_INTL_SPEND_CAP_EXCEEDED` (402), on placing a call attempt.

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis because they are easy to get backwards. Scheduling checks that the port request is `VALIDATED` (rule 12) *before* it looks at the slot's status (rule 14), its carrier (rule 30) or the firm-order-commitment floor (rule 41) — a request in the wrong status never learns whether its chosen window would have been acceptable. And the fiscal ceiling on international spend (rule 53) is the very last check in the entire ladder, so every safety refusal — a fully blocked device, a suspended location, a blocked account posture, a missing registration, a disabled or blocked international path, and a breached fraud threshold — outranks it. A call attempt that would both breach the fraud score and blow the daily cap reports the fraud breach.

### 2.8. Nested Addressing and Left-to-Right Resolution

Three paths in this API nest a child collection beneath a parent resource: phone numbers and reclamation runs beneath a number block, and call attempts and network reports beneath a device. Identifiers in a path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the error. A request for call attempts under a device that does not exist therefore returns `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_FOUND` and never proceeds to examine the rest of the request. When a path resolves but a body field names something that does not exist, the path failure would already have won; among body fields, references are resolved in the order the fields are listed in this document. That document order governs only references that share a rung of the §2.7 ladder: an unresolvable `Account` reference always outranks an unresolvable `NumberBlock`, `PhoneNumber` or other reference because rule 6 precedes rule 9, whatever order the fields appear in. Ownership of the parent is settled before any child rule is applied.

Several other relationships in this domain are *not* expressed as nested paths even though they are genuine parent-child relationships. A `CarrierAccountSnapshot` belongs to a port request and is created beneath it. A `NumberReservation`, a `CutoverSlot`, a `TemporaryBridge`, an `E911Registration` and a `FeeAssessment` are all top-level collections whose parents are named in the request body instead. The web of references is genuinely cyclic in one place: a cutover slot records the port request that booked it in `booked_port_request_id`, and the port request is discovered from the slot by that same field, so neither side owns the other.

### 2.9. Money, Counts and Rounding

Every monetary quantity in this domain is an integer number of **whole cents** — `monthly_fee_cents`, `amount_cents`, `pending_fee_cents`, `remitted_fee_cents`, `fee_liability_cents`, `intl_daily_cap_cents`, `rate_cents_per_minute`, `estimated_base_cents`, `day_subtotal_cents` and `projected_day_total_cents` are all integers, never decimals and never strings. Where a computation produces a fraction of a cent, the result is rounded **half up** to the nearest whole cent: a value whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero.

Two rounding orders are pinned rather than left to the implementer. In fee assessment, each billable unit's prorated fee is rounded to whole cents **first** and the rounded per-unit amounts are then summed; summing exact fractions and rounding once at the end is a different number and is not what this API does. In the international surcharge, exactly one rounding occurs, applied to the surcharge component alone and never to the base.

Risk scores and match points are plain integers with no rounding at all except where a multiplier is applied, in which case a single half-up rounding closes the calculation. Inventory counters, park cycles, resubmission counts, fraud block counts and billable-unit counts are exact integers.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Three operations compare the acting `X-User-ID` against an identifier the system recorded earlier, and refuse the request with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403) when they are equal.

A `CarrierLiaison` transcribing a losing carrier's customer service record onto a port request must not be the principal recorded in that request's `created_by_user_id`. The same applies when a `CarrierLiaison` records a carrier rejection against the request. Because `created_by_user_id` is written once when the request is filed and is deliberately *not* overwritten by any resubmission, correcting the subscriber's claimed data does not launder a conflict of interest away.

A `BillingAdmin` voiding a fee assessment must not be the principal recorded in that assessment's `assessed_by_user_id`. A fee produced by a remittance run carries the literal `system:remittance-run` in `assessed_by_user_id`, and remittance-produced fees reach `REMITTED` inside the run itself, so no human `BillingAdmin` ever collides with that sentinel.

### 2.11. Cascades and Derived-State Recomputation

The server, not the client, is responsible for keeping derived state true. Five families of derived values are recomputed at the end of every operation that could disturb them: the five inventory counters on a number block, the two fee counters on a jurisdiction together with an account's fee liability, an account's E911 compliance posture, a device's toll-fraud score, and an account's ported-number count.

Recomputation is not cosmetic — it can move state machines. An account posture recomputation can drive every nomadic device of that account into an international block, or drive every device of the account into a full block, or lift an international block that the posture itself imposed. A number release removes its device from the posture population and can therefore return an account to compliance. Every entity a cascade actually writes to has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1, including entities the caller never named — but a recomputation that changes nothing does not always count as a write, and the two families differ deliberately. A number block's census is rewritten unconditionally: every recomputation stores all five counters afresh and increments the block's `version` by 1 even when all five land on the values they already held. Three of the other four families are compared before they are written, so an account's `e911_compliance_posture`, an account's `fee_liability_cents`, a jurisdiction's `pending_fee_cents` and `remitted_fee_cents`, and an account's `ported_number_count` leave their resource entirely untouched — same `updated_at`, same `version` — when the recomputed value already equals the stored one. The jurisdiction's two fee counters carry one qualification on that, and it is a difference between write *paths* rather than a wobble in the rule: they are compared before being written when an assessment or a void triggers the shared recomputation, but the remittance run of §3.9 does not route through that shared recomputation at all. It writes `pending_fee_cents`, `remitted_fee_cents` and `last_remitted_period` straight onto the jurisdiction and increments its `version` by exactly 1 on every single run, including a re-run of an already-closed period that voids nothing, creates nothing and moves not one cent. So the same two fields obey compare-before-write under §3.9's assessment and void paths and unconditional-write under its remittance path; §3.9 states that contrast at the point where the run is specified, and there is nowhere else in the system where one field is governed by both disciplines. A device's toll-fraud score belongs to neither camp cleanly, and §3.8 pins it in detail: on a call attempt the device is written — `updated_at` refreshed, `version` incremented — only when the freshly computed score, the `fraud_block_count` or the `calling_status` actually changes, and then exactly once however many of those three moved, so an attempt that recomputes a score identical to the stored one onto an otherwise unchanged device leaves the device entirely alone; on a calling-restriction action, by contrast, an accepted action writes the device unconditionally and a refused review unblock stores the recomputed score on the device while leaving its `updated_at` and `version` untouched. The precise chains are specified in §3 alongside the operations that trigger them; there are no cascades beyond those specified there.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Reference Data: Accounts, Losing Carriers and 911 Jurisdictions

Nothing else in the domain can be built until a `PlatformAdmin` has laid down three kinds of reference data.

An **Account** is the Ooma-side billing relationship. It is registered with an `account_number` of 8 to 12 characters drawn only from uppercase letters and digits and unique across all accounts, a `billing_name` of 1 to 80 characters that later serves as one of the five customer-service-record comparison points, and a `service_state_code` that is exactly two uppercase letters naming the USPS state of the primary service address. Three further inputs are optional. `status` may be given as `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED` and defaults to `ACTIVE`; it is fixed at creation and is never mutated afterwards, so a suspended or closed account is one that was registered that way. `intl_calling_enabled` is a boolean defaulting to false, and while it is false every non-domestic call attempt on the account is refused. `intl_daily_cap_cents` is a non-negative integer defaulting to 2000 — twenty dollars — and it is the ceiling the projected daily international spend of §3.8 is measured against. Three fields are computed and are never accepted as input: `e911_compliance_posture` starts at `COMPLIANT` and may become `AT_RISK` or `BLOCKED`, `fee_liability_cents` starts at 0, and `ported_number_count` starts at 0. A duplicate `account_number` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`, and a request for an account id that does not exist yields `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`.

A **Carrier** describes a losing carrier and the terms under which it releases numbers. It carries a display `name` of 1 to 80 characters and an `ocn` — the operating company number — of exactly four characters drawn from uppercase letters and digits, unique across carriers and refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` when it collides. Its `csr_match_policy` is either `STRICT` or `RELAXED` and selects the match-point threshold the carrier applies when its record is compared against the subscriber's claim. Its `porting_buffer_minutes` is an integer from 0 through 240 giving the quiet time the carrier demands on *both* sides of every cutover window it grants. Its `foc_business_days` is an integer from 1 through 10 giving how many business days must elapse between a validated record comparison and the earliest firm-order-commitment cutover. All five of these inputs — `name`, `ocn`, `csr_match_policy`, `porting_buffer_minutes` and `foc_business_days` — are required, and a carrier has no optional and no derived inputs. A carrier is immutable: there is no update operation, and the record carries no `version`.

A **Jurisdiction** is a 911 taxing authority. It is identified by a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters and a `county_fips` of exactly five digits; the pair must be unique across jurisdictions, and a collision is an `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`. Its `fee_type` is `PER_LINE` when the monthly 911 fee is levied for each billable line or `PER_LOCATION` when it is levied for each distinct dispatchable location, and `monthly_fee_cents` is the full-month fee per billable unit, an integer from 1 through 10000. Its `remit_day_of_month` is an integer from 1 through 28 naming the day of the following month on or after which a closed period's remittance may be filed. All five of these inputs — `state_code`, `county_fips`, `fee_type`, `monthly_fee_cents` and `remit_day_of_month` — are required. Three fields are derived from the fee ledger and start out empty: `pending_fee_cents` at 0, `remitted_fee_cents` at 0, and `last_remitted_period` at null until the first remittance run for that jurisdiction completes.

### 3.2. Number Inventory: Blocks, Minting, Release and Reclamation

A `NumberingAdmin` organizes telephone numbers into **NumberBlocks**. A block is created against a `jurisdiction_id` — every number in the block bills into that 911 jurisdiction — with an `npa_nxx` of exactly six digits unique across all blocks, a `total_capacity` from 1 through 10000 capping how many numbers may ever be minted into it, and a `max_concurrent_reservations` of at least 1, with no upper bound, capping how many simultaneous holds the block will tolerate. All four of these inputs are required. Five census counters are derived and all begin at 0: `minted_count`, `available_count`, `reserved_count`, `assigned_count` and `parked_count`.

Those five counters obey a conservation law that holds after every single operation in this document. `minted_count` is the number of phone numbers belonging to the block, and `available_count`, `reserved_count`, `assigned_count` and `parked_count` are the numbers of those in `AVAILABLE`, `RESERVED`, `ASSIGNED` and `PARKED` status respectively. The four status counters always sum to exactly `minted_count`, because those four statuses are the whole of a number's status enumeration and reclamation never deletes a row — `minted_count` only ever grows. A hold followed by its release restores all four counters to their pre-hold values; an orphaned bridge instead moves one number out of `reserved_count` and into `parked_count`; and a later reclamation moves it from `parked_count` back into `available_count`. The sum is invariant at every intermediate step. The whole census is recomputed, and the block's `version` incremented by 1, at the end of every operation that mints, reserves, assigns, releases, parks or reclaims one of its numbers.

Minting a **PhoneNumber** into a block takes a single input: a `line_number` of exactly four digits, unique within that block. The block is taken from the path, and the number records it as `block_id`. The server derives `e164` by concatenating the literal `+1`, the block's `npa_nxx`, and the `line_number` — a derivation that deliberately reaches across the block boundary, so the same `line_number` in two different blocks yields two different E.164 strings. A freshly minted number is `AVAILABLE`, with `assigned_account_id` null, `parked_until` null and `park_cycle_count` at 0. Minting is refused with `ERR_BLOCK_CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED` once the block's `minted_count` has reached its `total_capacity`, and a `line_number` that already exists in that block is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`.

A number's inventory status moves through a genuine cycle rather than a one-way lifecycle: `AVAILABLE → RESERVED → ASSIGNED → PARKED → AVAILABLE`. Reservation drives `AVAILABLE → RESERVED` (§3.3). Device registration drives either `RESERVED → ASSIGNED`, when a held `NEW_LINE` reservation earmarked for that same account is consumed, or `AVAILABLE → ASSIGNED` directly when no reservation is involved (§3.7); in both cases `assigned_account_id` is set to the owning account of the new device.

Releasing an `ASSIGNED` number is a `NumberingAdmin` operation that takes no body and drives `ASSIGNED → PARKED`. It sets `parked_until` to the logical clock plus the cooling-off period of 168 hours — seven days — increments `park_cycle_count` by 1, and clears `assigned_account_id` to null. It also clears `assigned_number_id` to null on the device that held the number, and because that device no longer holds a number it drops out of the account's E911 posture population entirely, so `e911_compliance_posture` is recomputed for the former holder — a release can therefore lift an account out of `AT_RISK` or `BLOCKED`. The block's counters move one unit from `assigned_count` to `parked_count` and the block's `version` increments by 1. Releasing a number that is not `ASSIGNED` is refused with `ERR_PHONE_NUMBER_STATE_INVALID`.

A number can also reach `PARKED` from `RESERVED`, but only through the system cascade of §3.6: when a temporary bridge is orphaned by a rejection or cancellation, the reservation behind it is released and its number goes `RESERVED → PARKED` with the same seven-day `parked_until` and the same `park_cycle_count` increment. A number goes `RESERVED → AVAILABLE` instead when the reservation holding it is released manually or by a completed cutover.

**Reclamation runs** close the cycle. A `NumberingAdmin` triggers a run against a block with no request body; the run returns the block itself. It does two things. First, every `PARKED` number of the block whose `parked_until` is at or before the logical clock transitions `PARKED → AVAILABLE`, processed in ascending `line_number` order with ties broken by ascending `created_at` and then by insertion order; the transition clears `parked_until` back to null while deliberately retaining `park_cycle_count` as the number's reuse history. A `PARKED` number whose `parked_until` still lies in the future is left completely untouched — the run is not an override. Second, every `HELD` reservation of the block whose `reserved_until` is strictly before the logical clock transitions `HELD → EXPIRED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order with insertion order breaking ties, and its `RESERVED` number returns to `AVAILABLE` provided no other `HELD` reservation of that same number remains, in which case the number stays `RESERVED`. An expiring reservation whose number is not in `RESERVED` status leaves that number untouched, and expiry sets no `released_reason` — the field stays as it was, which for a reservation that has only ever been `HELD` means null. The run then recomputes the census and increments the block's `version` by 1. A run that finds nothing to do is still a success and still bumps the block.

Blocks, numbers and reservations are all readable individually and as filtered lists. Blocks may be listed filtered by `jurisdiction_id`. Numbers may be listed filtered by `block_id`, by the holding `assigned_account_id`, or by `status`. Reservations may be listed filtered by `number_id`, by `block_id`, by `account_id` or by `status`.

### 3.3. Reserving a Number over a Closed Interval

A **NumberReservation** earmarks one number out of one block for one account across a closed date interval. A `NumberingAdmin` creates it with `block_id` naming the drawing block, `account_id` naming the account the hold is for, `purpose` being either `PORT_BRIDGE` or `NEW_LINE`, `reserved_from` and `reserved_until` bounding the closed interval, and `expected_number_block_version` carrying the block `version` the admin last read. A sixth input, `number_id`, is optional: supplying it names the number explicitly, and omitting it delegates the choice to the server. The reservation is created in `HELD` status with `released_reason` null; `released_reason` is populated only on a released reservation and is one of `MANUAL`, `BRIDGE_ORPHANED` or `CUTOVER_COMPLETED`.

The order in which a creation request is adjudicated is pinned, because the same request can plausibly fail three different ways. First the interval itself is sanity-checked. `reserved_until` must be strictly later than `reserved_from`, `reserved_from` must not lie before the logical clock, and the span from `reserved_from` to `reserved_until` must not exceed 45 days; any of those three failures is `ERR_RESERVATION_WINDOW_INVALID`. Second the block's aggregate ceiling is tested: the server counts the `HELD` reservations of the same block whose closed intervals intersect the candidate interval, and if that count plus this new one would exceed the block's `max_concurrent_reservations`, the request is refused with `ERR_BLOCK_RESERVATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Two closed intervals intersect when the first starts no later than the second ends and the second starts no later than the first ends — so intervals that merely touch at an endpoint *do* intersect, in deliberate contrast to the half-open buffered arithmetic that governs cutover windows. Only `HELD` reservations occupy an interval; `CONSUMED`, `RELEASED` and `EXPIRED` rows never do. Third the number is selected. Fourth, and only after selection, the chosen number is tested for its own conflicts: if any other `HELD` reservation of that same number has a closed interval intersecting the candidate, the request is refused with `ERR_NUMBER_RESERVATION_OVERLAP`.

Selection, when `number_id` is omitted, considers exactly the numbers of the named block whose status is `AVAILABLE`. Those candidates are ranked by `line_number` ascending, compared as four-character strings, with ascending `created_at` as the tie-break and insertion order behind that — tie-breaks that can only fire on a same-`line_number` data anomaly, since `line_number` is unique within a block. The first candidate under that ordering is selected. The number's `id` is never consulted as a ranking key. If the candidate set is empty the request is refused with `ERR_NO_NUMBERS_AVAILABLE`, and because selection runs third, that refusal is only reached after the interval and block-ceiling checks have passed. Numbers in `RESERVED`, `ASSIGNED` or `PARKED` status are excluded from automatic selection entirely, even a `PARKED` number whose `parked_until` has already elapsed — only a reclamation run returns such a number to the selectable pool.

When `number_id` *is* supplied, no selection runs and the named number must be either `AVAILABLE` or `RESERVED`. An already-`RESERVED` number may legitimately take one further hold covering a disjoint interval, and whether the intervals are actually disjoint is settled by the same-number overlap check rather than by the status check. A number that is `ASSIGNED` or `PARKED` is refused with `ERR_PHONE_NUMBER_STATE_INVALID`.

A successful creation drives the selected number `AVAILABLE → RESERVED` when it was available, leaves it `RESERVED` when it was already held for a disjoint window, recomputes the block census so that `available_count` falls and `reserved_count` rises by 1, and increments the block's `version` by 1. In the already-`RESERVED` case the number itself is not rewritten at all — its `updated_at` and `version` stand — and the five counters land on the same values they already held, but the census is still recomputed and the block's `version` still increments by exactly 1, so the version the next caller must present has moved. Because the account's compliance posture gates the operation, a reservation against an account whose `e911_compliance_posture` is `BLOCKED` is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_E911_BLOCKED`, and a reservation against an account whose `status` is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Releasing a `HELD` reservation is a `NumberingAdmin` operation with no body. It drives `HELD → RELEASED` and sets `released_reason` to `MANUAL`. Its number returns `RESERVED → AVAILABLE`, but only when no other `HELD` reservation of that same number remains — otherwise the number stays `RESERVED`. The block's `available_count` and `reserved_count` are restored to their pre-reservation values and the block's `version` increments by 1; where the number stays `RESERVED` because another hold survives, the counters land unchanged but the census is still recomputed and the block's `version` still increments by exactly 1. A reservation that is already `CONSUMED`, `RELEASED` or `EXPIRED` cannot be released again and yields `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_HELD`.

A `HELD` reservation leaves that status in four ways, and the four are worth keeping distinct. It becomes `CONSUMED` when a device takes its number, which requires `purpose` to be `NEW_LINE` and the reservation's `account_id` to equal the new device's account. It becomes `CONSUMED` when a temporary bridge is provisioned against it, which requires `purpose` to be `PORT_BRIDGE` and the reservation's `account_id` to equal the port request's account; a `PORT_BRIDGE` reservation consumed this way keeps governing its number for the whole life of the bridge, and the number stays `RESERVED` rather than becoming `ASSIGNED`. It becomes `RELEASED` when released manually. And it becomes `EXPIRED` when a reclamation run finds its `reserved_until` already past. From `CONSUMED` there is one further hop: when the bridge holding it is released or orphaned, the reservation goes `CONSUMED → RELEASED` with `released_reason` set to `CUTOVER_COMPLETED` if the bridge was cleanly released and `BRIDGE_ORPHANED` if it was orphaned.

### 3.4. Filing a Port-In, Comparing the Carrier's Record, and Correcting a Claim

A **PortRequest** is the unit of work for moving numbers away from a losing carrier. Either a `PortingSpecialist` or the subscriber's own `AccountAdmin` files one, supplying `account_id` for the Ooma account the numbers are porting into, `losing_carrier_id` for the carrier they are porting away from, and `requested_numbers`: an array of 1 to 5 E.164 strings, each of which must consist of a leading plus sign, the digit 1, and exactly ten further digits, with no duplicates inside the array. Those strings are recorded as strings only, and the array is stored and echoed back in exactly the order it was supplied rather than sorted — a ported-in number is never minted into the inventory of §3.2 and never becomes a `PhoneNumber` row. Five further inputs carry what the subscriber *claims* about their old account: `claimed_account_number` of 1 to 32 characters, `claimed_pin` of 1 to 16 characters, `claimed_billing_name` of 1 to 80 characters, `claimed_address_line1` of 1 to 80 characters, and `claimed_zip` of exactly five digits. All five are stored verbatim on the port request and echoed back in full on every read of it — `claimed_pin` included, which is never masked, truncated or omitted from the representation.

Filing requires the owning account to be `ACTIVE`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ACTIVE`. It also requires that no other port request of the same account is simultaneously laying claim to any of the same numbers: if another request of that account is in `SUBMITTED`, `CSR_MISMATCH`, `VALIDATED`, `SCHEDULED`, `BRIDGED` or `REJECTED` status and its `requested_numbers` shares even one E.164 string with the new array, the filing is refused with `ERR_ACTIVE_PORT_REQUEST_EXISTS`. Only `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` requests release their claim on a number, because those two are the terminal statuses of the port lifecycle.

A new request begins in `SUBMITTED` with `resubmission_count` at 0, `last_rejection_code` null, `last_match_points` null, `last_mismatch_fields` an empty array, `validated_at` null and `earliest_cutover_at` null. The acting `X-User-ID` is stored as `created_by_user_id`, and that value is written exactly once — no resubmission and no other operation ever overwrites it, because it is the comparand of the segregation-of-duties rule in §2.10. Port requests can be read individually and listed with filters on `account_id`, on `losing_carrier_id` and on `status`.

**Transcribing the carrier's record.** Only a `CarrierLiaison` may post a **CarrierAccountSnapshot** against a port request, only while that request is `SUBMITTED` — any other status is `ERR_PORT_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID` — and only when its own `X-User-ID` differs from the request's `created_by_user_id`. The snapshot carries the five values the losing carrier actually holds: `account_number_on_file` of 1 to 32 characters, `pin_on_file` of 1 to 16 characters, `billing_name_on_file` of 1 to 80 characters, `address_line1_on_file` of 1 to 80 characters and `zip_on_file` of exactly five digits, together with the mandatory `expected_port_request_version` described in §2.6. That version is consumed purely as a precondition and is not written onto the snapshot row, so it never appears in the snapshot's representation. The snapshot records `port_request_id` from the path segment it was created beneath.

Before the snapshot row is persisted, the server scores it. Each of the five claims is normalized and compared against its counterpart: normalization uppercases the string and then removes every character that is not an uppercase letter or a digit, so punctuation, spaces and case differences never cause a mismatch. The comparison is made strictly on the two normalized forms, so a pair whose members both reduce to the empty string — two values built entirely out of punctuation — counts as equal and scores its full weight. Five comparison points contribute weight when their normalized values are equal — the comparison point named `account_number` contributes 40 points when `claimed_account_number` matches `account_number_on_file`, `pin` contributes 30 points when `claimed_pin` matches `pin_on_file`, `billing_name` contributes 15 points when `claimed_billing_name` matches `billing_name_on_file`, `service_address_line1` contributes 10 points when `claimed_address_line1` matches `address_line1_on_file`, and `service_zip` contributes 5 points when `claimed_zip` matches `zip_on_file`. `match_points` is the sum of the weights of the matching comparison points, so a perfect record scores 100. `mismatch_fields` lists the comparison-point names of the pairs that did *not* match, using exactly those five literal names, sorted ascending as plain strings — which puts them in the order `account_number`, `billing_name`, `pin`, `service_address_line1`, `service_zip`. `threshold_applied` is copied from the losing carrier's policy: 100 when `csr_match_policy` is `STRICT` and 85 when it is `RELAXED`. `match_status` is `MATCHED` when `match_points` is greater than or equal to `threshold_applied` and `MISMATCHED` otherwise, so a `RELAXED` carrier accepts a score of exactly 85 while a `STRICT` carrier demands all five comparison points. Only the five claims currently on the port request are scored against the five values of the snapshot being created; earlier snapshots are never re-scored.

Posting a snapshot has effects on both records. The previously current snapshot of that port request, if there is one, has its `superseded_at` set to the logical clock, and because that is a write to the snapshot row it also refreshes that row's `updated_at` and increments its own `version` by 1 — `superseded_at` is null exactly while a snapshot is the current one. The port request copies the new snapshot's `match_points` into `last_match_points` and its `mismatch_fields` into `last_mismatch_fields`. If `match_status` is `MATCHED`, the request moves `SUBMITTED → VALIDATED`, `validated_at` is set to the logical clock, and `earliest_cutover_at` is computed as described below. If `match_status` is `MISMATCHED`, the request moves `SUBMITTED → CSR_MISMATCH` and both `validated_at` and `earliest_cutover_at` are left exactly as they were — null on a first-pass mismatch. Either way the port request's `version` increments by 1. The snapshot history of a request is readable in full, newest first, including every superseded row.

**The firm-order-commitment floor.** Whenever a request enters `VALIDATED`, `earliest_cutover_at` is recomputed from scratch. Take the UTC calendar date on which `validated_at` falls. Advance forward one calendar day at a time, counting a day only when its UTC weekday is Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday or Friday, and stop once as many such days have been counted as the losing carrier's `foc_business_days`. `earliest_cutover_at` is midnight UTC at the start of that final counted day, that is `00:00:00.000Z` of that date. The advance begins with the day *after* the validation date, so the validation date itself is never counted. Saturdays and Sundays are skipped and never counted, and public holidays are ignored by design.

**Rejection reason codes.** When a losing carrier turns a port down, the reason it gives is recorded on the request as `last_rejection_code`, which is null until the first rejection and afterwards holds one of `CSR_MISMATCH`, `PIN_INVALID`, `ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `PENDING_BALANCE`, `NUMBER_NOT_PORTABLE` or `DUPLICATE_REQUEST`. Four of those six describe a fixable data problem and are resubmittable: `CSR_MISMATCH`, `PIN_INVALID`, `ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND` and `PENDING_BALANCE`. The other two are terminal judgements about the number itself rather than the paperwork — `NUMBER_NOT_PORTABLE` and `DUPLICATE_REQUEST` — and a request rejected for either of those can never be resubmitted; the attempt is refused with `ERR_REJECTION_NOT_RESUBMITTABLE`.

**Resubmission.** A `PortingSpecialist` or the subscriber's `AccountAdmin` corrects a claim by supplying all five `claimed_account_number`, `claimed_pin`, `claimed_billing_name`, `claimed_address_line1` and `claimed_zip` values again. Resubmission is permitted from exactly two statuses, `CSR_MISMATCH` and `REJECTED`; from anywhere else it is `ERR_PORT_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`. From `REJECTED` it additionally requires a resubmittable `last_rejection_code`. In both cases the request must not have exhausted its budget of two resubmissions: once `resubmission_count` has reached 2, a third attempt is refused with `ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT_REACHED` and the request stays exactly where it was.

A successful resubmission overwrites the five claimed values from the request body, increments `resubmission_count` by 1, and returns the request to `SUBMITTED` — closing the cycle `CSR_MISMATCH → SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED → SUBMITTED` so that the liaison can transcribe a fresh carrier record. It sets `superseded_at` to the logical clock on the currently non-superseded snapshot, if one exists, incrementing that snapshot's own `version` by 1 in the process, so that no stale comparison remains current. It clears `last_match_points` back to null and `last_mismatch_fields` back to the empty array, clears `validated_at` and `earliest_cutover_at` back to null, and clears `last_rejection_code` back to null. The request's `version` increments by 1. Because `created_by_user_id` is untouched, the liaison that was barred from transcribing the first snapshot is still barred from transcribing the next one.

### 3.5. Cutover Calendars and Booking a Window

A **CutoverSlot** is one bookable window on one carrier's porting calendar. A `PortingSpecialist` opens a slot by naming a `carrier_id` and bounding the window with `window_start` and `window_end`. A newly opened slot is `OPEN`, with `booked_port_request_id` null and `booked_at` null. Cutover windows are half-open, running from `window_start` inclusive to `window_end` exclusive.

Two tests guard slot creation and their order is fixed. The **length test runs first**: `window_end` must be strictly later than `window_start`, and the length of the window measured in whole minutes must be at least 60 and at most 480 — no shorter than an hour, no longer than an eight-hour shift. That length is the elapsed span truncated downward to a whole number of minutes, so residual seconds are discarded rather than rounded up: a window of eight hours and thirty seconds measures 480 minutes and passes, while one of fifty-nine minutes and fifty-nine seconds measures 59 and fails. A violation of either half is `ERR_CUTOVER_WINDOW_LENGTH_INVALID`.

The **overlap scan runs second**, and it is where the carrier's quiet-time demand bites. The candidate window is compared against every slot of the *same* carrier whose status is `OPEN` or `BOOKED`, examined in ascending `window_start` order with ascending `created_at` as the tie-break; slots of other carriers are irrelevant, and slots that are `CONSUMED` or `RELEASED` never block anything. Each existing slot is expanded by the carrier's `porting_buffer_minutes` on both sides — its effective start moves that many minutes earlier and its effective end that many minutes later — and the expanded interval is likewise treated as half-open. The candidate collides with an expanded interval when the candidate's start is strictly before the expanded interval's end and the expanded interval's start is strictly before the candidate's end. The first collision refuses the request with `ERR_CUTOVER_SLOT_OVERLAP`. Because both intervals are half-open, adjacency is permitted: a candidate whose `window_start` falls exactly on an expanded interval's end does not collide. All arithmetic here is at minute granularity, in deliberate contrast to the millisecond, inclusive-on-both-ends comparisons that govern reservation intervals.

Slots are readable individually and listable with filters on `carrier_id`, on `booked_port_request_id` and on `status`.

**Booking.** Only a `PortingSpecialist` may book a slot for a port request, supplying a single input, `cutover_slot_id`. Five conditions are checked in a fixed order. The request must be `VALIDATED`; any other status is refused with `ERR_PORT_REQUEST_NOT_SCHEDULABLE` before the cutover window is examined at all, so a request in `CSR_MISMATCH` never discovers whether its chosen window would have been legal. The named slot must be `OPEN`; a slot that is already `BOOKED`, or that has been `CONSUMED` or `RELEASED`, yields `ERR_SLOT_NOT_OPEN`. The owning account's `e911_compliance_posture` must not be `BLOCKED`, failing which the booking is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_E911_BLOCKED` — a subscriber whose dispatchable locations are in regulatory trouble does not get a cutover date. The slot's `carrier_id` must equal the request's `losing_carrier_id`, since a window on one carrier's calendar is meaningless for another, and a mismatch is `ERR_SLOT_CARRIER_MISMATCH`. Finally the slot's `window_start` must be at or after the request's `earliest_cutover_at`; a window starting earlier violates the firm-order-commitment interval and is refused with `ERR_CUTOVER_BEFORE_FOC`.

A successful booking moves the request `VALIDATED → SCHEDULED` and increments its `version` by 1. On the slot side it moves `OPEN → BOOKED`, sets `booked_port_request_id` to the booking request and `booked_at` to the logical clock, and increments the slot's `version` by 1. The mutual reference is now established from both directions: the request is discoverable from the slot, and the slot from the request, through that one field.

### 3.6. Bridging the Cutover, Completing It, and the Unwind Cascade

Bridging keeps a subscriber reachable while their numbers are in flight, and it is optional — a port can go straight from `SCHEDULED` to `COMPLETED` without one.

A **TemporaryBridge** is provisioned by a `PortingSpecialist` naming a `port_request_id` and a `reservation_id`. The port request must be `SCHEDULED` and there must be a cutover slot in `BOOKED` status whose `booked_port_request_id` is that request; otherwise the attempt is refused with `ERR_BRIDGE_SLOT_NOT_BOOKED`. The reservation must still be `HELD`, or the attempt is `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_HELD`; its `account_id` must equal the port request's `account_id`, or the attempt is `ERR_RESERVATION_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`; and its `purpose` must be `PORT_BRIDGE` rather than `NEW_LINE`, or the attempt is `ERR_RESERVATION_PURPOSE_INVALID`. On success the bridge copies the reservation's `number_id` into its own `bridge_number_id`, the reservation transitions `HELD → CONSUMED`, and the bridge is created `PROVISIONED` with `activated_at` and `released_at` both null. Crucially the underlying phone number does *not* become `ASSIGNED` — it stays `RESERVED` for the whole life of the bridge, because the consumed `PORT_BRIDGE` reservation continues to govern it until the bridge is released or orphaned.

**Activation** takes no body and is permitted only inside the booked window. The bridge must be `PROVISIONED`, failing which the attempt is `ERR_BRIDGE_STATE_INVALID`, and the port request's booked slot must still be `BOOKED`. Those last two demands share a single error code: the absence of any `BOOKED` slot naming the port request is reported as `ERR_BRIDGE_ACTIVATION_OUTSIDE_WINDOW` exactly like a clock outside the window, and never as `ERR_BRIDGE_SLOT_NOT_BOOKED`, which belongs to provisioning alone. The logical clock must lie inside that slot's half-open window — at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end` — and a clock outside it is refused with `ERR_BRIDGE_ACTIVATION_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`. Activation moves the bridge `PROVISIONED → ACTIVE`, records `activated_at` as the logical clock, and drives the port request `SCHEDULED → BRIDGED`, incrementing the port request's `version` by 1. Nothing caps a request at one bridge, and activation writes `BRIDGED` onto the request unconditionally rather than only when it is `SCHEDULED`, so activating a second bridge of a request that is already `BRIDGED` rewrites the same status and still increments the request's `version` by 1. Bridges are readable individually and listable with filters on `port_request_id`, on `reservation_id`, on `bridge_number_id` and on `status`.

**Completing the cutover** is a `PortingSpecialist` operation with no body, permitted from `BRIDGED` and from `SCHEDULED`. The `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` path is available only when no bridge of that request is still in `PROVISIONED` or `ACTIVE` status; a request sitting in `SCHEDULED` with a bridge that was provisioned but never activated must resolve that bridge first, and attempting completion regardless is refused with `ERR_PORT_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`. That guard hangs on the `SCHEDULED` path alone: completion out of `BRIDGED` is never blocked by an outstanding bridge, so a second bridge of the same request still sitting in `PROVISIONED` neither refuses the completion nor is settled by it — it stays `PROVISIONED` with its reservation still `CONSUMED` and its number still `RESERVED`. Completion drives the request to `COMPLETED`, a terminal status, and increments its `version` by 1. The booked slot moves `BOOKED → CONSUMED` while *retaining* both `booked_port_request_id` and `booked_at` as the permanent completion record, and its `version` increments by 1. Any `ACTIVE` bridge of the request moves `ACTIVE → RELEASED` with `released_at` set to the logical clock; because that bridge was released cleanly, its reservation moves `CONSUMED → RELEASED` with `released_reason` set to `CUTOVER_COMPLETED`; and because that reservation was released for a completed cutover, the bridge's phone number moves `RESERVED → AVAILABLE`, the block census is recomputed, and the block's `version` increments by 1. That last pair of links is conditional on two things: the number must currently be `RESERVED`, and no *other* `HELD` reservation of that same number may survive. If either condition fails the chain stops at the reservation — the number is not rewritten, and, in deliberate contrast to the manual reservation release of §3.3, the block census is *not* recomputed either, so the block's five counters and its `version` are left entirely alone. Finally the account's `ported_number_count` is recomputed as the total count of E.164 strings across the `requested_numbers` of every `COMPLETED` port request of that account, which in practice raises it by the length of this request's array.

**Rejection.** Only a `CarrierLiaison` records a carrier rejection, and only when its `X-User-ID` differs from the request's `created_by_user_id`. The body carries a mandatory `reason_code`, one of `CSR_MISMATCH`, `PIN_INVALID`, `ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `PENDING_BALANCE`, `NUMBER_NOT_PORTABLE` or `DUPLICATE_REQUEST`, and an optional free-text `note` of unbounded length that is validated as a string and then discarded — no field of the port request or of any other record retains it. Rejection is accepted from `SUBMITTED`, `CSR_MISMATCH`, `VALIDATED`, `SCHEDULED` and `BRIDGED`; from `REJECTED`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` it is refused with `ERR_PORT_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`. The request moves to `REJECTED`, `last_rejection_code` is set to the supplied `reason_code`, and the request's `version` increments by 1.

Rejection then unwinds everything the request had reserved, in a chain where each link fires because the previous one did — and each link fires only if the row it needs actually exists, so rejecting a request that never got as far as booking a slot simply stops after the first step. The booked cutover slot moves `BOOKED → RELEASED`, with `booked_port_request_id` and `booked_at` both cleared to null and the slot's `version` incremented by 1; a `RELEASED` slot is terminal, can never be re-booked, and no longer blocks future candidate windows in the overlap scan of §3.5. Because that slot was released, every temporary bridge of this request in `PROVISIONED` or `ACTIVE` status becomes `ORPHANED` with `released_at` set to the logical clock — a bridge that never activated is orphaned on exactly the same terms as one that did. Because a bridge became orphaned, its reservation moves to `RELEASED` with `released_reason` set to `BRIDGE_ORPHANED`. Because that reservation was released for an orphaned bridge rather than a completed cutover, its phone number moves `RESERVED → PARKED` rather than back to `AVAILABLE`, taking a `parked_until` of the logical clock plus 168 hours and a `park_cycle_count` incremented by 1. And because a number entered `PARKED`, the block census is recomputed so that `reserved_count` falls by one and `parked_count` rises by one, and the block's `version` increments by 1. Those two links carry the same two conditions as the completion path: the number must currently be `RESERVED`, and no other `HELD` reservation of that same number may survive. If one does survive, the number stays `RESERVED`, is never parked, takes no `parked_until` and no `park_cycle_count` increment, and the block census is not recomputed at all, so the block's `version` does not move. Only a later reclamation run returns a parked number to `AVAILABLE`.

**Cancellation** closes a request permanently. A `PortingSpecialist` or the subscriber's `AccountAdmin` may cancel, optionally supplying a free-text `reason_note` — the request body itself may be omitted entirely, and the note, like the rejection `note`, is validated as a string and then discarded rather than persisted — and cancellation is reachable from every non-terminal status — `SUBMITTED`, `CSR_MISMATCH`, `VALIDATED`, `SCHEDULED`, `BRIDGED` and `REJECTED` — so a rejected request can be abandoned rather than corrected. The request moves to `CANCELLED`, a terminal status, and its `version` increments by 1. Cancellation runs the identical slot-to-bridge-to-reservation-to-number-to-block cascade described for rejection, with the single difference that `last_rejection_code` is not touched: a cancellation is not a carrier judgement. Attempting to cancel a request that is already `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` is `ERR_PORT_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.7. Devices, Dispatchable Locations and Nomadic Relocation

A **Device** is an endpoint on a subscriber's account. Either the subscriber's own `AccountAdmin` or a `PlatformAdmin` registers one, supplying `account_id` for the owning account, a `mac_address` of exactly twelve characters drawn from digits and the uppercase letters A through F and unique across all devices, and a `device_type` of `BASE_STATION`, `HANDSET` or `SOFTPHONE`. Two inputs are optional: `nomadic`, a boolean defaulting to false that marks a device permitted to move legitimately between dispatchable locations, and `assigned_number_id`, which takes a phone number for the device immediately. Registration requires the owning account to be `ACTIVE`, and a colliding `mac_address` is `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`.

A device carries two state dimensions that evolve independently of one another, and conflating them is the most common way to get this domain wrong. `registration_status` tracks the E911 side and starts at `PENDING_REGISTRATION`, moving among `ADDRESS_UNVERIFIED`, `REGISTERED`, `RELOCATION_DETECTED` and `SUSPENDED_911`. `calling_status` tracks the calling-restriction side and starts at `UNRESTRICTED`, moving among `INTL_BLOCKED` and `FULL_BLOCKED`. A fraud-driven block never touches `registration_status`, and an address re-verification never by itself touches `calling_status` except through the account posture cascade described below. Four further fields are derived and start empty: `toll_fraud_score` at 0, `fraud_block_count` at 0, and `last_network_report_at`, `reported_state_code` and `reported_postal_code` all at null. One derived field is a deadline: `registration_prompt_deadline` is set to the device's `created_at` plus 72 hours — three days — and is the instant at which a device that still has no accepted dispatchable location stops being in grace.

When `assigned_number_id` is supplied, the named number must be either `AVAILABLE`, in which case it moves `AVAILABLE → ASSIGNED` directly, or `RESERVED`, in which case a `HELD` reservation on that number must exist whose `account_id` equals the new device's `account_id` and whose `purpose` is `NEW_LINE`; that reservation transitions `HELD → CONSUMED` and the number moves `RESERVED → ASSIGNED`. A number that is `PARKED`, or `ASSIGNED` to somebody already, is refused with `ERR_PHONE_NUMBER_STATE_INVALID`; a `RESERVED` number with no `HELD` reservation standing behind it at all yields `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_HELD`; a reservation earmarked for a different account yields `ERR_RESERVATION_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`; and a `PORT_BRIDGE` reservation offered to a device yields `ERR_RESERVATION_PURPOSE_INVALID`. Because a single `RESERVED` number may carry more than one `HELD` reservation over disjoint intervals, which of them the operation speaks for is pinned: the server takes the `HELD` reservations of that number in ascending `created_at` order with insertion order breaking ties, and chooses the first whose `account_id` equals the new device's account *and* whose `purpose` is `NEW_LINE`. Only when no such row exists does it fall back to the earliest `HELD` row under that same ordering, and that fallback row is the one whose account or purpose supplies the 422 — the account comparison being made before the purpose comparison, so a foreign `PORT_BRIDGE` hold reports `ERR_RESERVATION_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH` rather than `ERR_RESERVATION_PURPOSE_INVALID`. In every successful case the number's `assigned_account_id` is set to the device's owning account, the block census is recomputed and the block's `version` increments by 1. Devices are readable individually and listable with filters on `account_id`, on `assigned_number_id`, on `registration_status` and on `calling_status`.

**Capturing a dispatchable location.** An **E911Registration** records where a device physically is, in a form a 911 dispatcher can act on. The subscriber's `AccountAdmin` or a `PlatformAdmin` captures one by naming a `device_id` and a `jurisdiction_id` and supplying `address_line1` of 1 to 80 characters, `city` of 1 to 40 characters, `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters, `postal_code` of exactly five digits, and optionally `unit` — the floor, suite or apartment sub-address — of at most 20 characters, defaulting to the empty string when absent. The registration's `state_code` must equal the named jurisdiction's `state_code`, and a discrepancy is refused with `ERR_JURISDICTION_STATE_MISMATCH`. The owning account must be `ACTIVE`, and because rule 10 of §2.7 precedes rule 33, a location captured for a device on a `SUSPENDED` account reports `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ACTIVE` and never discovers that its state code was also wrong. There is no `account_id` input at all, so the `AccountAdmin` ownership check of §2.7 rule 3 is made against the account that owns the device named in the body; a `device_id` that resolves to no device leaves that check with nothing to compare and defers the refusal to `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_FOUND` rather than reporting `ERR_ACCOUNT_FORBIDDEN`.

On creation the server copies `account_id` from the device, sets `effective_from` to the logical clock, and derives `normalized_location_key` by taking `address_line1` uppercased with every non-alphanumeric character removed, then a vertical-bar separator, then `unit` uppercased with every non-alphanumeric character removed, then another vertical bar, then `postal_code` verbatim. That key is the grouping key by which a `PER_LOCATION` jurisdiction counts billable units, so two devices at the same street address and unit in the same postal code collapse into one unit however differently their addresses were typed. `verification_status` is initialised to `UNVERIFIED`, with `verified_at`, `superseded_at` and `rejection_note` all null.

Capturing a location supersedes whatever the device had before. Exactly one registration per device is ever current, meaning exactly one has `superseded_at` null. Any prior current registration of that device — whether it stood at `VERIFIED`, `STALE`, `REJECTED` or `UNVERIFIED` — has `superseded_at` set to the logical clock and its `verification_status` set to `SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal: a superseded row never re-enters verification and never re-enters the fee population for any period after its `superseded_at`. The device lands in `ADDRESS_UNVERIFIED` whichever of the five registration statuses it was in, which is how the relocation cycle `REGISTERED → RELOCATION_DETECTED → ADDRESS_UNVERIFIED → REGISTERED` closes and how a device suspended for a rejected address gets a second chance through `SUSPENDED_911 → ADDRESS_UNVERIFIED`. That write to the device is unconditional: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` incremented by exactly 1 even when it already stood at `ADDRESS_UNVERIFIED` and nothing about it actually changed. The superseded registration takes its own `version` increment as well. The account posture is then recomputed, which can move an account `BLOCKED → AT_RISK` once the offending rejected registration is out of the way. Registrations are readable individually and listable with filters on `account_id`, on `device_id`, on `jurisdiction_id` and on `verification_status`.

**Validating it.** Only an `E911Validator` records an outcome, and only on a registration whose `verification_status` is `UNVERIFIED` or `STALE`; a row that is already `VERIFIED`, or that is `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED`, is refused with `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_VERIFIABLE`. The body carries a mandatory `outcome` of either `VERIFIED` or `REJECTED`, plus an optional `rejection_note`. An outcome of `VERIFIED` moves the registration `UNVERIFIED → VERIFIED` or, for a re-verification in place after a relocation, `STALE → VERIFIED`; it sets `verified_at` to the logical clock and drives the device to `REGISTERED`. That device write is unconditional and never consults the device's prior `registration_status`: the ordinary path is `ADDRESS_UNVERIFIED → REGISTERED`, but a device sitting in `RELOCATION_DETECTED`, in `SUSPENDED_911` or in `PENDING_REGISTRATION` at the moment one of its registrations is verified is driven straight to `REGISTERED` all the same. Any `rejection_note` supplied alongside a `VERIFIED` outcome is ignored and the field stays null. An outcome of `REJECTED` moves the registration to `REJECTED`, stores the supplied `rejection_note` — null if none was given — leaves whatever `verified_at` the row already carried from an earlier verification standing rather than clearing it, and drives the device to `SUSPENDED_911`, again whatever `registration_status` it held, after which the device may place no calls at all, domestic or international. Both the registration's `version` and the device's `version` increment by 1, and the account posture is recomputed: a `VERIFIED` outcome can carry an account `AT_RISK → COMPLIANT`, while a `REJECTED` outcome drives it to `BLOCKED`.

**Relocation detection.** A network report tells the server where the device is currently seen. The subscriber's `AccountAdmin` or a `PlatformAdmin` posts `reported_state_code` — a two-letter state code — and `reported_postal_code` — a five-digit postal code — and receives the device back. The server first classifies the drift against the device's single current registration, comparing state and postal codes as exact strings with no case folding or trimming of its own — a `reported_state_code` that is not already exactly two uppercase letters, or a `reported_postal_code` that is not already exactly five digits, is refused as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` by the body-shape rule long before the comparison, so there is never anything left to normalize. When the device has no current registration at all the drift is **no-registration**. Otherwise, when `reported_postal_code` equals the current registration's `postal_code` the drift is **same-location**; when it differs but `reported_state_code` equals the current registration's `state_code` the drift is **intra-jurisdiction**; and when the state code differs too the drift is **cross-jurisdiction**.

The four classes have sharply different consequences. A **no-registration** report leaves every registration untouched and merely puts the device into `ADDRESS_UNVERIFIED`. A **same-location** report changes nothing but `reported_state_code`, `reported_postal_code` and `last_network_report_at` — no status anywhere moves. An **intra-jurisdiction** report moves the device `REGISTERED → ADDRESS_UNVERIFIED` and drives its current registration `VERIFIED → STALE`. A **cross-jurisdiction** report moves the device `REGISTERED → RELOCATION_DETECTED` — the device has crossed a 911 jurisdiction line and its dispatchable location is now positively wrong — and likewise drives its current registration `VERIFIED → STALE`. Both of those arrows are strictly conditional on the value they start from, in deliberate contrast to the unconditional `ADDRESS_UNVERIFIED` of a no-registration report. The device's `registration_status` is rewritten only when it stands at exactly `REGISTERED`; a nomadic device already in `PENDING_REGISTRATION`, `ADDRESS_UNVERIFIED`, `RELOCATION_DETECTED` or `SUSPENDED_911` keeps that status through a relocating report. And the current registration is driven to `STALE` only when it stands at exactly `VERIFIED`; one that is `UNVERIFIED` or `REJECTED` is left entirely alone and takes no `version` increment. In every case `reported_state_code`, `reported_postal_code` and `last_network_report_at` are stored on the device, `last_network_report_at` taking the logical clock, and the device's `version` increments by 1.

Both relocating classes are gated on the device being nomadic. When `nomadic` is false and the drift is intra-jurisdiction or cross-jurisdiction, the report is refused with `ERR_NON_NOMADIC_DEVICE_RELOCATION` and **nothing at all is mutated** — not the reported fields, not `last_network_report_at`, not the registration, not the posture. A stationary base station that suddenly reports from another postal code is a data problem, not a relocation. A non-nomadic device may still post same-location and no-registration reports normally.

**The account compliance posture.** `e911_compliance_posture` is a compound state derived from an account's devices, recomputed at the end of five operations: registering a device, capturing a dispatchable location, recording a verification outcome, posting a network report, and releasing an assigned number. The population it considers is exactly those devices of the account whose `assigned_number_id` is not null — a device holding no number is outside the population entirely, which is why releasing a number can improve an account's posture. Each device in the population is classified from its single current registration. It is **compliant** when that registration exists and is `VERIFIED`; **stale** when the registration exists and is `STALE`; **hard-failed** when the registration exists and is `REJECTED`. When there is no current registration, or the current one is still `UNVERIFIED`, the device is **overdue** if the logical clock is at or after its `registration_prompt_deadline` and merely **in grace** if the clock is strictly before it — so the instant of exact equality with the deadline is already overdue.

From those classifications the posture follows: `BLOCKED` if any device in the population is hard-failed or overdue; otherwise `AT_RISK` if any device is stale or in grace; otherwise `COMPLIANT`, including the vacuous case where the population is empty. Each posture transition drives a sweep across the account's devices. Moving to `AT_RISK` sends every device of the account that is `nomadic` and currently `UNRESTRICTED` to `INTL_BLOCKED`; non-nomadic devices are left alone by this sweep. Because registering a device is itself one of the five triggers, that sweep can catch the very device being registered: a nomadic device registered together with a number of its own enters the population immediately as in-grace, which drives a `COMPLIANT` account to `AT_RISK` and blocks the new device internationally inside its own creation request, so the 201 response reports it at `calling_status` `INTL_BLOCKED` with `version` already 2. Moving to `COMPLIANT` compensates that block, returning every device that is `INTL_BLOCKED` and whose `fraud_block_count` is 0 to `UNRESTRICTED` — a device blocked because of fraud rather than posture is deliberately *not* lifted here. Moving to `BLOCKED` sends every device of the account that is not already `FULL_BLOCKED` to `FULL_BLOCKED` in one sweep, and while the account is `BLOCKED` it may neither place calls, nor take new reservations, nor book a cutover window. Moving from `BLOCKED` back to `AT_RISK` — which happens once a rejected registration is superseded by a fresh capture — does **not** automatically lift those full blocks; each device must be reviewed and unblocked by a `SecurityAnalyst` as described in §3.8.

### 3.8. Placing Calls: E911 Gating, Toll-Fraud Scoring and International Spend

A **CallAttempt** is the record of one dial. The subscriber's `AccountAdmin` or a `PlatformAdmin` places one against a device by supplying `destination_e164`, the dialled number in E.164 form matching the pattern `^\+[0-9]{7,15}$`; `destination_country_code`, one to three digits which must additionally be a prefix of `destination_e164` once the leading plus sign is discarded; and `requested_duration_seconds`, an integer from 1 to 36000 used to project spend. A country code of `1` marks the attempt domestic; anything else marks it non-domestic. A `destination_country_code` that is not a prefix of the dialled number is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) like any other malformed input. `device_id` is taken from the addressed device rather than the body.

A `CallAttempt` is an immutable record. It carries `id` and `created_at` and neither `updated_at` nor `version`, it has no update endpoint, and — this is the point of the entity — **a row is persisted with its `decision` even when the HTTP response is an error**. The `decision` is one of `ALLOWED`, `BLOCKED_DEVICE`, `BLOCKED_E911`, `BLOCKED_INTL`, `BLOCKED_FRAUD` or `BLOCKED_SPEND_CAP`, and `decision_error_id` holds the `error_id` handed back to the caller, or null when the decision is `ALLOWED`. A row is written exactly when the request reached the gating stage — that is, the headers authenticated, the role and account ownership passed, the body was structurally valid, the addressed device resolved, and the device's owning account was found to be `ACTIVE`. A refusal from any of those earlier layers, including `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCOUNT_FORBIDDEN` and `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, persists nothing. That last layer is the one easiest to get backwards. Placing a call requires the owning account's `status` to be `ACTIVE`; a `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED` account is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409) under rule 10 of §2.7, and that refusal alone among this endpoint's 409s writes **no** `CallAttempt` row at all, because no `decision` enum value describes it. It also outranks every gate of the ladder below, so a fully blocked device on a suspended account reports `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ACTIVE` and leaves no audit trace whatsoever — while every gate that does own a `decision` value persists its row before raising.

**The gate ladder.** The gates are evaluated strictly in the order below, and the first one that fails both determines the HTTP error and fixes the `decision` recorded on the row.

1. The device's `calling_status` must not be `FULL_BLOCKED` — `ERR_DEVICE_CALLING_BLOCKED` (409), recorded as `BLOCKED_DEVICE`. This gate is unconditional: a fully blocked device may not dial even domestically.
2. The device's `registration_status` must not be `SUSPENDED_911` — `ERR_DEVICE_911_SUSPENDED` (409), recorded as `BLOCKED_E911`. A device whose dispatchable location was rejected places no calls at all, domestic or international.
3. The owning account's `e911_compliance_posture` must not be `BLOCKED` — `ERR_ACCOUNT_E911_BLOCKED` (409), recorded as `BLOCKED_E911`.
4. If the device holds a phone number — `assigned_number_id` is not null — it must have a current registration whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` or `STALE` — `ERR_E911_REGISTRATION_REQUIRED` (409), recorded as `BLOCKED_E911`. A number-holding device that is still `PENDING_REGISTRATION`, or whose only registration is `UNVERIFIED`, may not dial. A `STALE` registration is deliberately still good enough to place a call: the address is out of date, not absent. A device holding no number skips this gate entirely.
5. For a non-domestic attempt only, the owning account's `intl_calling_enabled` must be true — `ERR_INTL_CALLING_DISABLED` (403), recorded as `BLOCKED_INTL`.
6. For a non-domestic attempt only, the device's `calling_status` must not be `INTL_BLOCKED` — `ERR_INTL_BLOCKED_ON_DEVICE` (409), recorded as `BLOCKED_INTL`. Because this gate precedes the fraud gate, an `INTL_BLOCKED` device accrues further fraud points only on its domestic dialling.
7. The REALTIME toll-fraud score, computed with this attempt counted, must be strictly below 60 — `ERR_TOLL_FRAUD_THRESHOLD_BREACHED` (409), recorded as `BLOCKED_FRAUD`. This gate applies to domestic and non-domestic attempts alike.
8. For a non-domestic attempt only, and last of everything, `projected_day_total_cents` must not strictly exceed the account's `intl_daily_cap_cents` — `ERR_INTL_SPEND_CAP_EXCEEDED` (402), recorded as `BLOCKED_SPEND_CAP`. The fiscal ceiling is the very last check in the entire system, so every safety refusal above outranks it: a device that is fully blocked, 911-suspended or fraud-scoring over threshold reports that fact rather than a spend problem, even when the projection would also have breached the cap.

**Which computed fields a row carries.** Two of the pricing figures depend on nothing but the request body and are therefore always computed and always stored, on an allowed row and on every refused row alike. `billable_minutes` is the greater of 1 and the request's duration in seconds divided by 60 rounded up — so 1 second and 60 seconds both bill one minute, 61 seconds bills two. `rate_cents_per_minute` is looked up by country code: 12 cents for `44`, 60 for `53`, 95 for `234`, 140 for `252`, and 75 cents for every other country code including the domestic `1`, which is not in the table. `estimated_base_cents` is `rate_cents_per_minute` multiplied by `billable_minutes`, with no surcharge in it.

The three day-level figures require the account-wide ledger scan of the international spend control, and that scan runs only for a non-domestic attempt that has cleared every gate through the fraud gate. On every domestic attempt — cleared or refused — and on every non-domestic attempt refused at gates 1 through 7, `day_subtotal_cents`, `surcharge_pct_applied` and `projected_day_total_cents` are all recorded as 0. Likewise `realtime_risk_score` is computed at gate 7, so a row refused at gates 1 through 6 records `realtime_risk_score` as 0 and the device's `toll_fraud_score` is left exactly as it was; whenever the score *is* computed, it is stored on the row and copied onto the device's `toll_fraud_score` whether the call was allowed or refused. The device itself is only *written* when something on it actually moves. Exactly three of its fields can be disturbed by a call attempt — `toll_fraud_score`, `fraud_block_count` and `calling_status` — and the device's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` incremented by exactly 1 when at least one of those three differs from its pre-attempt value: one single increment even when all three move together, and no increment at all when the recomputed score lands on the value already stored and nothing else changed. A row refused at gates 1 through 6 therefore never touches the device's `version`, and a row refused at gate 8 has already taken whatever increment gate 7 earned and takes no second one.

**The REALTIME fraud score.** The score looks only at the one device: rows belonging to sibling devices on the same account never enter the window, however suspicious the account looks in aggregate. The window is the last 60 minutes, half-open on the left and closed on the right — an attempt whose `created_at` is exactly 60 minutes before the clock is outside it, one exactly at the clock is inside it. The rows are that device's persisted attempts inside the window whose `decision` is anything other than `BLOCKED_E911`, plus the attempt currently under evaluation, counted once. Fraud-blocked rows deliberately stay in the window, so a compromised device digs itself deeper with every retry.

Four components are added. Velocity contributes 4 points per row in the window, capped at 40 — ten rows saturate it. Geography contributes the single highest country risk over the rows, where the risk of a country code is 0 for `1`, 5 for `44`, 25 for `53`, 30 for `234`, 35 for `252`, and 20 for any country code not in that table; it is the maximum, not a sum, so ten calls to the same high-risk country score the same geography points as one. Spread contributes 6 points for every distinct `destination_country_code` over the rows beyond the first, so one distinct code scores 0 and four distinct codes score 18. Unsettled nomadism contributes a flat 10 points when the device's `nomadic` flag is true and its `registration_status` is anything other than `REGISTERED`, and 0 otherwise. Those four are summed into a raw figure, which is then multiplied by 1.5 when the device is younger than 72 hours — that is, when the clock minus the device's `created_at` is strictly less than 72 hours — and by 1.0 otherwise. Exactly one rounding is applied, half up, to the multiplied product, and the result is capped at 100. The comparison against the threshold is at-or-above, so a score of exactly 60 blocks the call and 59 does not.

A `BLOCKED_FRAUD` decision has consequences beyond the refusal. The device's `fraud_block_count` increments by 1. The full-block test is then applied first and on an at-or-above comparison: an attempt that leaves `fraud_block_count` at 3 or higher drives `calling_status` to `FULL_BLOCKED` out of whatever it held, and only when the incremented count is still strictly below 3 does the weaker rule apply, moving a device that was `UNRESTRICTED` to `INTL_BLOCKED` and leaving a device already `INTL_BLOCKED` exactly where it is. In the ordinary progression that reads as `UNRESTRICTED → INTL_BLOCKED` on the first fraud block and `INTL_BLOCKED → FULL_BLOCKED` on the third, after which every subsequent attempt is refused at gate 1. `registration_status` is untouched by any of this — fraud and E911 are separate dimensions.

**The international spend control.** For a non-domestic attempt that reaches gate 8, the day subtotal is this attempt's own `estimated_base_cents` plus the sum of `estimated_base_cents` over every call attempt that belongs to *any* device of the same account, whose `created_at` falls inside the clock's UTC calendar day, whose `destination_country_code` is not `1`, and whose `decision` is `ALLOWED`. Refused attempts never contribute to any later subtotal, so a blocked call does not poison the rest of the account's day. The subtotal deliberately excludes every surcharge ever applied — this is the pinned break in the apparent circularity between the total and the surcharge it feeds.

`surcharge_pct_applied` is then the percentage of the highest tier whose minimum is at or below `day_subtotal_cents`: 0 percent from 0 cents, 5 percent from 2000 cents, 12 percent from 5000 cents. The tier is keyed on the surcharge-free subtotal and never on the projected total; keying it on the total would make the definition circular. `projected_day_total_cents` is `day_subtotal_cents` plus the surcharge, where the surcharge is the subtotal multiplied by the percentage, divided by 100, rounded half up. That is the single rounding in the whole calculation — the base is never rounded, because whole-cent integers went in and whole-cent integers came out. A projection landing exactly on `intl_daily_cap_cents` is allowed; only a projection strictly greater than the cap breaches, is refused with `ERR_INTL_SPEND_CAP_EXCEEDED`, and is recorded as `BLOCKED_SPEND_CAP`.

Call attempts are listable per device, newest first — `created_at` descending, with rows sharing a `created_at` returned in reverse insertion order so the most recently written comes first — and the list includes refused attempts with their `decision` and `decision_error_id`. It accepts a `decision` filter and is readable by the owning `AccountAdmin`, by a `SecurityAnalyst` and by a `PlatformAdmin`.

**Calling restrictions.** Only a `SecurityAnalyst` applies or lifts a restriction on a device. The body carries a mandatory `action` of `BLOCK_INTL`, `BLOCK_ALL` or `REVIEW_UNBLOCK`, and an optional free-text `note` which is accepted for the operator's benefit and is not persisted on any entity. The device is returned with `version` incremented by 1 in every accepted case.

`BLOCK_INTL` moves a device `UNRESTRICTED → INTL_BLOCKED`. Applied to a device that is already `INTL_BLOCKED` or already `FULL_BLOCKED` it leaves `calling_status` untouched, because a restriction request never weakens a stronger block already in force. `BLOCK_ALL` moves any device that is not already `FULL_BLOCKED` to `FULL_BLOCKED`, whether it was `UNRESTRICTED` or `INTL_BLOCKED`, and is a no-op on `calling_status` for a device already there. Neither of these two actions computes a fraud score, so `toll_fraud_score` is left as it stands, and neither touches `fraud_block_count` or `registration_status`.

`REVIEW_UNBLOCK` is the analyst's release, and it is gated twice. The device's current registration must have `verification_status` exactly `VERIFIED` — a `STALE` address is good enough to keep dialling but not good enough to earn a release — and the DAILY_REVIEW fraud score must be strictly below 60. Failing either condition is `ERR_UNBLOCK_REVIEW_SCORE_TOO_HIGH` (409), and a device with no current registration at all fails the first condition and reports that same error even though nothing about its score was at fault. When both hold, the device moves to `UNRESTRICTED` from either `INTL_BLOCKED` or `FULL_BLOCKED` — this is the only way out of `FULL_BLOCKED`, including out of a full block imposed by an account posture sweep — and `fraud_block_count` is reset to 0, which also makes the device eligible for the compensating lift the next time its account recomputes to `COMPLIANT`. A `REVIEW_UNBLOCK` on a device that is already `UNRESTRICTED` is still subject to both gates and still resets `fraud_block_count` to 0, stores the score and increments `version`; its `calling_status` simply does not change. In all `REVIEW_UNBLOCK` cases, accepted or refused, the computed DAILY_REVIEW score is stored in the device's `toll_fraud_score` — the score is computed and written before either gate is consulted, so on a refusal that one field write survives the error response. It survives as a bare field write and nothing more: a refused review unblock leaves the device's `updated_at` and `version` exactly as they stood, so the fresh score is visible on the next read of the device without any concurrency token having moved, and only an accepted action takes the `version` increment.

The DAILY_REVIEW variant scores the same device on a deliberately gentler basis, which is the point of having two variants: the same four attempts that score 69 in REALTIME score 49 here. Its window runs from `00:00:00.000Z` of the clock's UTC date through the clock inclusive, so it resets at midnight rather than rolling. Its rows are that device's attempts in the window whose `decision` is neither `BLOCKED_FRAUD` nor `BLOCKED_E911` — the fraud blocks that the analyst is reviewing are discarded rather than held against the device. Velocity contributes 2 points per row, still capped at 40. Geography is again the maximum country risk over the rows, using the same table, and is 0 when there are no rows at all. Spread contributes 4 points per distinct country code beyond the first. Unsettled nomadism contributes the same flat 10 points under the same condition. The new-device multiplier is never applied. The sum is rounded half up, capped at 100, and compared at-or-above against 60.

### 3.9. 911 Fee Assessment, Voiding and Monthly Remittance

Every dispatchable location a subscriber keeps in a 911 jurisdiction attracts a monthly fee that the platform collects and remits to that jurisdiction. A **FeeAssessment** is one such charge: it names an `account_id` and a `jurisdiction_id`, a `period_month` in `YYYY-MM` form matching `^[0-9]{4}-(0[1-9]|1[0-2])$`, and it records how the money was arrived at. `variant` is `PROVISIONAL` when a `BillingAdmin` assessed it mid-period and `FINAL` when a remittance run produced it. `unit_basis` is copied from the jurisdiction's `fee_type` at assessment time, so a later change to the jurisdiction never retroactively rewrites how an existing assessment was computed. `billable_units` is the number of units selected, `unit_fee_breakdown` is the per-unit rounded cents in the pinned unit order described below, and `amount_cents` is exactly the sum of that array. `status` starts at `ASSESSED` and ends at either `VOIDED` or `REMITTED`, both terminal. `assessed_by_user_id` holds the acting `X-User-ID` of the `BillingAdmin` that produced the row, or the sentinel `system:remittance-run` for a `FINAL` row. `voided_by_user_id` is null until a void, then holds the voiding administrator's `X-User-ID`, or the same `system:remittance-run` sentinel when a run voided it. `remitted_at` is null until the row is remitted, then holds the logical-clock instant.

**Selecting the billable units.** The period runs from `00:00:00.000Z` of day 1 of `period_month` through `23:59:59.999Z` of its last day, and the day count of that calendar month is the divisor used for proration. A registration is eligible when its `jurisdiction_id` is the assessing jurisdiction, its `account_id` is the assessed account, its `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` or `STALE` **as of the moment the run executes**, its `effective_from` is at or before the period end, its `superseded_at` is either null or strictly after the period end, and the device it belongs to holds a phone number — `assigned_number_id` is not null. Registrations that are `UNVERIFIED`, `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED` at run time are excluded, which means an address that was verified throughout the period but has since been rejected does not bill for that period; the calculation reads the world as it stands when it runs, not as it stood during the month.

How eligible registrations become units depends on the jurisdiction's fee type. A `PER_LINE` jurisdiction charges one unit per eligible registration. A `PER_LOCATION` jurisdiction charges one unit per distinct `normalized_location_key` over the eligible set, so four handsets registered to the same suite at the same postal code are one billable location, not four. Each `PER_LOCATION` unit is anchored at the earliest `effective_from` inside its key group; where two registrations in a group share that earliest `effective_from`, the anchor is the one with the earlier `created_at`, and if those tie too, the one with the lexicographically smaller `id`. For a `PER_LINE` jurisdiction each unit is anchored at its own registration's `effective_from`. Units are then ordered by anchor `effective_from` ascending and, for equal anchors, by the anchor registration's `created_at` ascending, with the anchor registration's insertion order behind that as the final tie-break — deliberately *not* the same tail as the intra-group anchor key above, which falls back to the lexicographically smaller `id` rather than to insertion order. That order is the order of `unit_fee_breakdown`, and it is observable, so it is pinned rather than incidental.

**The provisional assessment.** A `BillingAdmin` assesses mid-period by naming `account_id`, `jurisdiction_id` and `period_month`. Both the account and the jurisdiction must resolve, and the account must be `ACTIVE`. No other assessment may exist for the same account, jurisdiction and period with status `ASSESSED` — a second one is `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROVISIONAL_ASSESSMENT` (409) — and the same triple must have no `REMITTED` assessment already, because a remitted period is closed both to further assessment and to voiding; that is `ERR_ASSESSMENT_PERIOD_ALREADY_REMITTED` (409). A voided assessment blocks nothing, so the correct way to re-assess a period is to void the standing row and write a new one.

The provisional branch narrows the eligible set once more, to registrations whose `effective_from` is at or before the logical clock: a location scheduled to take effect later in the month is not billed by a mid-period run. Each unit is then prorated. The unit's anchor day is the day-of-month of the later of its anchor `effective_from` and the period start — so a unit anchored before the month began counts from day 1 — and its active days are the days in the month minus that anchor day plus one, so a unit anchored on the 17th of a 31-day month is active for 15 days. The unit's fee is the jurisdiction's `monthly_fee_cents` multiplied by its active days, divided by the days in the month, **rounded half up to whole cents per unit**, and `amount_cents` is the sum of those rounded per-unit figures.

The rounding order is pinned and it is not cosmetic: round every unit to whole cents first, then sum. The rejected alternative — summing the exact fractions and rounding once at the end — is arithmetically different. With a `monthly_fee_cents` of 175 over a 31-day month and three units whose active days are 31, 15 and 15, the pinned order yields 345 cents and the rejected order yields 344. If the selection produces no units at all, the assessment is refused with `ERR_NO_BILLABLE_UNITS` (422) and nothing is written. Otherwise the row is created with `variant` `PROVISIONAL`, `status` `ASSESSED` and `assessed_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and the fee ledger is recomputed, raising the jurisdiction's `pending_fee_cents` and the account's `fee_liability_cents` by `amount_cents`.

**Voiding.** A `BillingAdmin` voids a standing assessment, optionally supplying a `void_note`, which is validated as a string, accepted for the operator's convenience and then discarded — no field of the assessment or of any other record retains it, and the request body may equally be omitted altogether. The row must be `ASSESSED`; one that is already `VOIDED` or `REMITTED` is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_VOIDABLE` (409), and that state check is made before the segregation check. The voiding administrator's `X-User-ID` must differ from the row's `assessed_by_user_id`, or the void is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION` (403) — the same principal may not both charge a subscriber and erase the charge. A successful void moves the row `ASSESSED → VOIDED`, records `voided_by_user_id`, increments the assessment's `version` by 1, and recomputes the fee ledger so that the jurisdiction's `pending_fee_cents` and the account's `fee_liability_cents` return to the exact cent values they held before the assessment existed. The voided row itself is retained for audit and is never deleted.

**The fee ledger.** Three derived money figures are recomputed at the end of every assessment, every void and every remittance run. A jurisdiction's `pending_fee_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over its assessments whose `status` is `ASSESSED`; its `remitted_fee_cents` is the same sum over those whose `status` is `REMITTED`; and an account's `fee_liability_cents` is the sum over its own assessments whose `status` is either `ASSESSED` or `REMITTED`. All three range over every period, not just the current one, and `VOIDED` rows contribute zero. The invariant the implementation must preserve is that for every jurisdiction, `pending_fee_cents` plus `remitted_fee_cents` equals the sum of `amount_cents` over all of that jurisdiction's assessments that are not `VOIDED`. No rounding occurs at reconciliation time; whole cents are simply added.

**The remittance run.** A `BillingAdmin` closes a period for one jurisdiction by naming a `period_month`. The period must be closed in two senses at once: `period_month` must be strictly before the clock's own `YYYY-MM`, and the clock's day-of-month must be at or after the jurisdiction's `remit_day_of_month`. Failing either is `ERR_REMITTANCE_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED` (422) — a jurisdiction that files on the 10th cannot be remitted on the 9th, and the month in progress cannot be remitted at all.

The run then does three things in order. First, every assessment of that jurisdiction with that `period_month`, `variant` `PROVISIONAL` and `status` `ASSESSED` moves `ASSESSED → VOIDED` with `voided_by_user_id` set to the sentinel `system:remittance-run`, taken in insertion order and each taking its own `version` increment; the segregation-of-duties rule does not apply to these system voids, and no `version`-based concurrency token is involved. Second, the run walks every account of the system in insertion order — the order in which the accounts were registered, which is what the run actually iterates rather than a re-sort on `created_at`, and the two can differ only where the logical clock was moved backwards between two registrations — computes the `FINAL` branch for that account in that jurisdiction and period, and for each account that selects at least one billable unit creates a `FeeAssessment` with `variant` `FINAL`, `unit_basis` copied from the jurisdiction, the computed `billable_units`, `unit_fee_breakdown` and `amount_cents`, `assessed_by_user_id` set to `system:remittance-run`, and `status` `ASSESSED` — then immediately drives that same row `ASSESSED → REMITTED` with `remitted_at` set to the logical clock, moving its money out of `pending_fee_cents` and into `remitted_fee_cents`. Both of those writes happen inside the row's own construction, so a `FINAL` row is *born* remitted and is returned and read at `version` 1 with `updated_at` equal to its `created_at` — never at `version` 2 — even though its `status` and `remitted_at` were both settled after the row was first laid down. An account that selects zero units is skipped silently; the no-billable-units refusal belongs to the provisional endpoint alone and never aborts a run. An account that already holds a `REMITTED` assessment for that account, jurisdiction and period is likewise skipped, so re-running an already-closed period voids nothing, creates nothing, and moves no money — it only re-stamps `last_remitted_period` and bumps the jurisdiction. Third, the ledger is recomputed for the jurisdiction and for every account the run touched, `last_remitted_period` is set to `period_month`, and the updated jurisdiction is returned. "Every account the run touched" means exactly those whose provisional row this run voided plus those for which it wrote a `FINAL` row, each listed once; an account skipped for zero units or for an existing `REMITTED` row has its `fee_liability_cents` left alone, and those touched accounts are compared before they are written in the ordinary way of §2.11. The jurisdiction is the deliberate exception. The run writes its `pending_fee_cents`, its `remitted_fee_cents` and its `last_remitted_period` unconditionally and increments the jurisdiction's `version` by exactly 1 on every single run — so a re-run of an already-closed period that voids nothing, creates nothing and moves not one cent still leaves the jurisdiction one `version` higher with a fresh `updated_at`, in contrast to the compare-before-write that governs the same two counters when an assessment or a void recomputes them.

The `FINAL` branch charges the full `monthly_fee_cents` for every unit and ignores anchors entirely: each entry of `unit_fee_breakdown` is the whole monthly fee and `amount_cents` is `monthly_fee_cents` multiplied by the unit count, with no proration and no rounding anywhere. This is why the same three registrations that yielded 345 cents provisionally yield 525 cents finally, and it is why the run voids the provisional row before writing the final one: the two are alternative charges for the same month, never cumulative ones.

Assessments are readable individually by a `BillingAdmin`, a `PlatformAdmin` or the owning `AccountAdmin`, including the full per-unit breakdown, and listable by a `BillingAdmin` or `PlatformAdmin` with filters on `account_id`, `jurisdiction_id`, `period_month`, `status` and `variant`, in the default `created_at` ascending order.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below other than the logical-clock endpoint — which sits at `/_clock/set_current_utc`, outside that prefix — is rooted at the `/voip` prefix, and all of them speak the envelope of §2.2. A trailing slash on any of these paths is accepted as equivalent to the path without one. Endpoints that create a resource answer with HTTP 201 and the created resource; endpoints that modify, transition or read a resource answer with HTTP 200 and the affected resource. Sub-resource actions return the entity named in the description rather than a bare acknowledgement — a cutover booking returns the port request, a remittance run returns the jurisdiction, and a calling-restriction action returns the device. All list endpoints accept `limit` and `offset` as described in §2.3, and the filters named in each description are combined conjunctively.
