# Farm And Ranch Package Underwriting API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Farm and ranch package business is the hardest line our carrier writes, and the reason is not actuarial — it is bookkeeping. A single operation brings a dwelling, a machine shed, a fleet of equipment, several thousand bushels of stored grain whose value triples between harvest and spring, a cattle herd that may be written per head or under a blanket limit, receipts from custom baling done for the neighbours, a corn maze that draws twelve thousand visitors every October, a chemical applicator's licence, and a crew of H-2A workers. Each of those exposures is priced by a different rule, each is inspected by a different discipline, and all of them compete for the same finite slice of catastrophe capacity that the treaty gives us for hail, wind and wildfire in that aggregation zone.

Today that reconciliation happens in spreadsheets, and it fails in predictable ways: a quote is bound after a loss-control inspection has already opened a critical finding; a pollution buy-back stays on the books after the compliance file went overdue; two policies on the same account are quoted for overlapping terms and the zone's wildfire capacity is double-counted. This service replaces the spreadsheets with an auditable underwriting workbench. Every premium figure on a policy is derived, never typed. Every cent of premium that a policy carries is backed by an append-only ledger entry, so the total can always be replayed from its history. Every unit of catastrophe capacity a policy occupies is a first-class row that moves from reserved to committed to released as the policy is quoted, bound and cancelled — and never leaks.

The document below specifies the contract in five parts: the global request and response conventions that every route honours, the cross-cutting principles that hold across the whole system, the business workflows that underwriters, producers, inspectors and compliance officers actually execute, the endpoint surface, and the error catalogue. Readers implementing the service should treat §2 and §3 together as the behavioural specification; §4 and §5 are indexes into it, not substitutes for it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Producer, Underwriting and Loss-Control Identities

Every request other than the logical-clock-setting capability described in §4 must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller is acting. A request that omits `X-User-ID`, or supplies it blank, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A request whose `X-User-Role` is not among the roles authorised for the route it addresses is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403), and a request that omits `X-User-Role` altogether falls into that same 403 rather than the 401 — only `X-User-ID` is authentication.

Exactly six role names exist, spelled here as they must appear on the wire: `Agent`, `Underwriter`, `SeniorUnderwriter`, `LossControlSpecialist`, `ComplianceOfficer` and `PlatformAdmin`.

Two of the six are backed by persisted rows, and the binding between header and row is what makes ownership enforceable:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Agent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting producer agency record; ownership checks on any resource that references a producer compare the resource's producer foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a farm account stores the producer that created it, and every policy, scheduled item, livestock group, liability exposure, labor profile, endorsement, inspection, recommendation, reservation and ledger entry reachable from that account inherits the same ownership. A producer that touches a row reachable from someone else's account is rejected with `ERR_NOT_ACCOUNT_AGENT` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `LossControlSpecialist`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting inspector record; ownership checks on an inspection compare the inspection's assigned-inspector foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Completing an inspection assigned to a peer is rejected with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_INSPECTOR` (HTTP 403).

The remaining four are opaque operator identifiers not tied to any persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `Underwriter`, `SeniorUnderwriter`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is a free-form staff identifier; the server records it wherever a workflow stamps an actor — as `referred_by_user_id` and `bound_by_user_id` on a policy, as `decided_by_user_id` on an endorsement, as `reviewed_by_user_id` on a recommendation, as `verified_by_user_id` on a labor profile — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

The duties of the underwriting desk are deliberately split. A `SeniorUnderwriter` whose rating produced a referral may not decide that same referral; the attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Binding and cancelling are reserved to the line `Underwriter` and are closed even to a `SeniorUnderwriter`. Reviewing loss-control evidence and verifying workers-compensation coordination belong exclusively to the `ComplianceOfficer`, who in turn may not rate, bind, decline, cancel, decide referrals, decide or rescind endorsements, or complete inspections. A `LossControlSpecialist` completes inspections but neither schedules nor re-schedules them, and never reviews the recommendations it raised. A `PlatformAdmin` owns reference data — producers, inspectors and catastrophe capacity pools — and is barred from the entire policy lifecycle: it may not create accounts or policies, rate, decide referrals, bind, decline, cancel, request or decide endorsements, complete inspections, submit evidence, review recommendations or verify workers-compensation proof. Read routes are open to all six roles, with the producer's reads confined to its own accounts.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Filtering

Every collection read accepts `limit` and `offset` as query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside 1 through 100, a `limit` or `offset` that is not a plain base-ten integer, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — with insertion order as the tie-break whenever two rows share a `created_at`, which the logical clock of §2.4 makes commonplace — unless a subsection below states otherwise, and the four pagination fields `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` are echoed in `meta`, where `total_count` counts the rows matching the filters before the window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether rows remain beyond it.

Filters are additive and, where a filter names an enumerated field, an out-of-set value is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The capacity-pool list filters on `zone_code`, `peril` and `status`. The account list filters on `agent_id`, `cat_zone_code` and `operation_type`. The policy list filters on `account_id` and independently on either status axis via `uw_status` and `compliance_status`. A policy's scheduled-item list filters on `status` and `item_class`; its livestock list on `species`; its liability list on `exposure_type`; its endorsement list on `status` and `form_code`; its ledger on `txn_type` and `reason_code`. The reservation list filters on `policy_id`, `pool_id`, `peril` and `status`. The inspection list filters on `policy_id`, `inspector_id`, `status` and `inspection_type`. An inspection's recommendation list filters on `status` and `severity` and is returned in `sequence_no` ascending order. A policy's ledger is likewise returned in `sequence_no` ascending order rather than by timestamp. A filter that names an identifier no row carries — an unknown `agent_id`, `account_id`, `policy_id`, `pool_id` or `inspector_id` — is never a 404: it simply matches nothing and yields an empty page with `total_count` 0.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Versioning and the Logical Clock

Every persisted row carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` that is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits taken from the logical clock described immediately below rather than from wall time — the envelope's `api_request_timestamp` is the one timestamp in the system that reads wall time. Most rows additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation and formatted identically and likewise clock-derived, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on each mutation the specification below declares. The premium ledger is the one exception: a ledger entry is immutable, so it carries only `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from a premium-transaction representation.

All temporal reasoning in this service reads a logical clock rather than wall time. If the clock has never been set, it reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Setting it via the request field `current_utc` changes no business row whatsoever; it changes only how later calls evaluate seasonal valuation months, due dates, quote expiry, workers-compensation expiry and inspector day loads. The clock-setting capability echoes back `current_utc` exactly as it was supplied, unnormalised, and is the only route in the system that requires no headers at all; it runs no role check either, so a caller presenting an unrecognised `X-User-Role` still sets the clock.

### 2.5. Cents, Rounding and Per-Line Pricing Discipline

Every monetary amount in the system is an integer number of cents; the API never transports a fractional currency value. Wherever a rounding step is specified below, it is round-half-up to a whole cent: a computed value of exactly one half cent rounds away from zero, upward. The single non-monetary rounded quantity is the labor exposure measure, which carries exactly two decimal places and is likewise rounded half up; the utilization ratio recorded on a catastrophe reservation is stored to six decimal places. Both of those, along with a pool's `participation_pct` and a reservation's `scarcity_factor`, travel on the wire as plain JSON numbers rather than as strings, so the trailing zeros of the stored scale are not preserved in the payload.

Where a total is built from many priced lines, the order of rounding and summing is part of the contract, not an implementation detail. Property premium and livestock premium both round **each line first and then add the already-rounded integers**; summing the unrounded products and rounding once at the end produces a different — and incorrect — total, and the difference is observable. Conversely, sums that add values which were already rounded when their own row was written (liability premium, exposed total insured value, the base and grand totals) are exact integer additions with no further rounding.

### 2.6. The Premium Ledger Invariant

A policy's premium is never stored as a bare number that someone edits. It is the projection of an append-only stream of signed premium transactions, and the governing invariant is absolute: **at every observable point in a policy's life, the sum of `amount_cents` across all of that policy's ledger entries equals its `total_premium_cents`.** Each entry carries a `sequence_no`, assigned 1, 2, 3 and upward per policy, and that number is the only ordering key for the ledger — `created_at` is never used to order entries. A positive `amount_cents` adds premium; a negative one removes it.

Each entry names the component it moves through `txn_type`, one of `BASE_PROPERTY`, `BASE_LIVESTOCK`, `BASE_LIABILITY`, `CAT_LOAD`, `ENDORSEMENT_ADD`, `ENDORSEMENT_REMOVE` or `REVERSAL`, and the action that appended it through `reason_code`, one of `RATING`, `RATING_REVERT`, `ENDORSEMENT_APPROVAL`, `ENDORSEMENT_RESCISSION`, `COMPLIANCE_SUSPENSION`, `COMPLIANCE_REINSTATEMENT`, `POLICY_DECLINE` or `POLICY_CANCELLATION`.

Nothing is ever deleted or edited. When a premium has to come off a policy wholesale, the system emits a **reversal batch**: it walks the policy's prior entries that do not yet have a reversal pointing at them, in `sequence_no` ascending order, and appends one entry of `txn_type` `REVERSAL` per source entry, whose `amount_cents` is the arithmetic negation of the source entry's amount and whose `reverses_sequence_no` is the source entry's `sequence_no`. An entry that already has a reversal pointing at it is never reversed a second time. On entries that are not reversals, `reverses_sequence_no` is null. Because a component worth zero cents is never written at all, a policy with no livestock emits no `BASE_LIVESTOCK` entry, and a first rating of such a policy therefore occupies `sequence_no` 1 through 3 rather than 1 through 4.

### 2.7. The Two Status Axes of a Farm Policy

A farm policy carries two status fields that advance independently and must never be conflated. The underwriting axis is `uw_status`, which starts at `DRAFT` and takes one of `DRAFT`, `RATED`, `REFERRED`, `BOUND`, `DECLINED` or `CANCELLED`; `DECLINED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. The loss-control axis is `compliance_status`, which starts at `NOT_INSPECTED` and takes one of `NOT_INSPECTED`, `RECS_OPEN`, `RECS_OVERDUE`, `SUSPENDED` or `COMPLIANT`; it has no terminal state and cycles for the life of the file. A policy can be `BOUND` while `RECS_OVERDUE`, or `DRAFT` while `COMPLIANT`; the axes constrain each other only where §3 says so.

Once a policy reaches a terminal `uw_status`, it and every row hanging off it are frozen: any mutating call on the policy or on any of its scheduled items, livestock groups, liability exposures, labor profile, endorsements, inspections or recommendations is rejected with `ERR_POLICY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409). The one deliberate exception is workers-compensation verification on a labor profile, which carries no terminal check at all and still succeeds against a `DECLINED` or `CANCELLED` policy.

Cascades in this system are declared, ordered and multi-hop. When one operation moves several rows, every hop bumps the `version` and refreshes the `updated_at` of the row it touches, and the specification in §3 names each hop explicitly. The implementer should assume nothing cascades that §3 does not say cascades — in particular, changing a producer's appointment standing or authority limit never revisits policies already bound, and deactivating an inspector never disturbs inspections already on the calendar.

### 2.8. Catastrophe Capacity Bookkeeping

Catastrophe capacity is held in pools, one per pair of aggregation zone and peril. A pool's `zone_code` names the zone, its `peril` is one of `HAIL`, `WIND` or `WILDFIRE`, and the pair must be unique across all pools. A pool's `total_capacity_cents` is the gross capacity it may hold and must be greater than zero. Its `participation_pct` is the fraction of a rated policy's exposed total insured value that occupies this pool; it must be greater than 0 and at most 1, with at most four decimal places. Its `status` is `OPEN` or `FROZEN` and defaults to `OPEN`; a frozen pool accepts no new reservations.

Three figures on a pool are derived and never supplied by a caller. `reserved_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the reservations on this pool whose `status` is `RESERVED`. `committed_cents` is the same sum over reservations whose `status` is `COMMITTED`. `available_cents` is `total_capacity_cents` less `reserved_cents` less `committed_cents`. All three start at zero on a new pool — so `available_cents` starts equal to `total_capacity_cents` — and all three are recomputed, with the pool's `version` incremented by 1, every single time a reservation on that pool changes status. Shrinking a pool below what it already holds is permitted and drives `available_cents` negative; the figure is reported as computed and is not clamped at zero.

A reservation row records the policy and pool it bridges through `policy_id` and `pool_id`, copies the pool's `peril` at the moment it is written, and carries the derived `amount_cents`, `utilization_at_rating`, `scarcity_factor` and `load_cents` described in §3.7. Its `status` starts at `RESERVED` and moves to `COMMITTED` on binding or to `RELEASED` when the occupation ends; `RELEASED` is terminal. Reserved capacity becoming committed leaves `available_cents` unchanged — the amount simply moves from one bucket to the other — while any release returns the amount to `available_cents`.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency on Policies and Pools

Two write operations in this system are guarded by an explicit version check, because in both cases the operation would otherwise succeed against a superseded view of its parent.

Requesting an endorsement on a policy requires the body field `expected_policy_version`, carrying the `version` of the target policy as the client last observed it. This field is not optional. If it does not equal the policy's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A policy legitimately accepts any number of endorsement requests, so nothing about its state forbids the request; the rejection exists because the client's view of the policy predates a premium, reservation or compliance movement another writer already applied, and the endorsement would be priced against that superseded picture. The same non-optional `expected_policy_version` field, checked the same way against the same error, guards rating, binding and cancellation.

Adjusting a capacity pool requires the body field `expected_pool_version`, carrying the `version` of the target pool as the client last observed it. It, too, is not optional, and a mismatch against the pool's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POOL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Repeated capacity adjustments are perfectly legitimate, so the pool's state forbids nothing; the rejection exists because the adjustment was computed against a utilization picture that reservations have since moved.

No other route in the system accepts a version field. Creating an account, creating a policy, adding or amending exposures, upserting the labor profile, deciding a referral, declining, deciding or rescinding an endorsement, scheduling or completing or re-scheduling an inspection, submitting or reviewing evidence, verifying workers-compensation proof, running the compliance sweep and setting the clock all take no version field, and supplying one is an unknown body field.

### 2.10. Path Resolution and Producer Scoping

Path parameters are validated strictly left to right. Where a route nests a child collection under a parent — a policy's scheduled items, a policy's endorsements, an inspection's recommendations — the parent `id` is resolved first, and the leftmost `id` that fails to resolve determines the 404 that comes back. Only once every path segment resolves does the server consider the body.

Each resource has its own not-found identifier, and all of them carry HTTP 404: an unresolvable producer `id` yields `ERR_AGENT_NOT_FOUND`, an inspector `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_FOUND`, a capacity pool `ERR_CAT_POOL_NOT_FOUND`, a farm account `ERR_FARM_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, a policy `ERR_FARM_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, a scheduled item `ERR_SCHEDULED_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, a labor profile `ERR_LABOR_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, an endorsement `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, a catastrophe reservation `ERR_CAT_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, an inspection `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND` and a recommendation `ERR_RECOMMENDATION_NOT_FOUND`.

Producer scoping is applied after path resolution and before any state check. A caller acting as `Agent` may read or write only rows reachable from an account whose producer is that caller; anything else is `ERR_NOT_ACCOUNT_AGENT`. The single read exempted from this scoping is `GET /cat-reservations/{id}`, which hands any reservation to any authorised role; the reservation *list* by contrast is filtered down to the caller's own accounts, as are all the other list and single-row reads. Ids referenced from the request body are resolved as well: the account named by `account_id` when a policy is opened, and the policy and inspector named by `policy_id` and `inspector_id` when an inspection is scheduled, must all exist. Body ids are checked in the order the fields are declared for the route.

### 2.11. The Rejection Ladder

Checks run in a strict ascending ladder of fifteen priorities. **The first violated rule determines the response; no later check is evaluated, and no side effect of any kind is applied when a check fails.** Where several checks share a priority, the order given here is the tie-break.

1. Authentication — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401) on every route except the clock setter.
2. Role permission — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Evaluated before any body parsing, so a role violation outranks a malformed payload.
3. Schema — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) for a missing or null required field, a wrong JSON type, an enumerated value outside its declared set, a numeric value outside its declared range, a blank or over-long string, bad pagination, or any unknown body field; an *optional* field supplied as an explicit `null` is rejected the same way, the only nullable fields in the whole system being the seasonal trio on a scheduled-item amendment, where `null` is precisely how the trio is cleared. Within this priority, missing required fields are reported before type errors, type errors before enum errors, and enum errors before range, string-length and date-format errors, walking fields in the order the owning entity declares them; the unknown-field scan runs last of all, so a payload that both omits a required field and carries an unknown one reports the omission. After those field-level checks, and still at this priority, opening a policy validates its term dates and rejects a reversed, empty or too-short term with `ERR_INVALID_TERM_DATES` (400).
4. Path existence — the appropriate not-found error (404), leftmost unresolvable `id` first.
5. Body-referenced existence — `ERR_FARM_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FARM_POLICY_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_FOUND` (404), in declared field order.
6. Ownership — `ERR_NOT_ACCOUNT_AGENT` (403) first, then `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_INSPECTOR` (403).
7. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
8. Optimistic concurrency — `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_POOL_VERSION` (412).
9. State — the target row must be in a state from which the requested transition exists, otherwise `ERR_POLICY_NOT_IN_DRAFT`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_RATED`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_REFERRED`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_BOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_TERMINAL`, `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_NOT_REQUESTED`, `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_INSPECTION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_RECOMMENDATION_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_WC_STATE_INVALID` (all 409). The required states are: rating needs `DRAFT`; adding or amending an exposure needs `DRAFT` or `RATED`; a referral decision needs `REFERRED`; binding needs `RATED`; declining needs `DRAFT`, `RATED` or `REFERRED`; cancelling needs `BOUND`; requesting or deciding an endorsement needs the policy in `RATED` or `BOUND`; an endorsement decision needs the endorsement in `REQUESTED` and a rescission needs it in `APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED`; completing an inspection needs `SCHEDULED` and re-scheduling one needs `COMPLETED`; submitting evidence needs `OPEN` or `OVERDUE`; an `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` review needs `SUBMITTED` while a `WAIVE` accepts `OPEN`, `OVERDUE` or `SUBMITTED`; and workers-compensation verification needs `PENDING_PROOF`.
10. Conditional-field coupling — `ERR_BLANKET_LIMIT_REQUIRED`, `ERR_PEAK_SEASON_FIELDS_REQUIRED` and `ERR_VISITOR_COUNT_REQUIRED` (all 422). The three guard disjoint routes and never collide.
11. Business safety, in this order: `ERR_COMPLIANCE_SUSPENDED` (409), then `ERR_CRITICAL_REC_OVERDUE` (422), then `ERR_BUYBACK_INSPECTION_REQUIRED` (422), then `ERR_BUYBACK_HAZARD_TOO_HIGH` (422). The buy-back pair is ordered deliberately: an inspection must exist before its hazard score can be judged.
12. Business constraints, in this order: `ERR_DUPLICATE_POOL` (409), then `ERR_INSPECTOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409), then `ERR_INSPECTOR_DAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409), then `ERR_OVERLAPPING_POLICY_TERM` (409), then `ERR_INSPECTOR_INACTIVE` (422), then `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_EXPOSURE_MISSING` (422). The two inspector-calendar checks and the inspector-standing check share a route, so their relative order is observable; the term-overlap and companion-exposure checks sit on other routes and never collide with them.
13. Business referential, in this order: `ERR_AGENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (422), then `ERR_WC_COORDINATION_UNVERIFIED` (422), then `ERR_WC_PROOF_MISSING` (422).
14. Business temporal, in this order: `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED` (409), then `ERR_WC_PROOF_EXPIRED` (422).
15. Business fiscal, in this order: `ERR_CAT_POOL_NOT_CONFIGURED` (422), then `ERR_NO_RATEABLE_EXPOSURE` (422), then `ERR_POOL_FROZEN` (409), then `ERR_CAT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409), then `ERR_AGENT_AUTHORITY_EXCEEDED` (422).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up Reference Data: Producers, Inspectors and Capacity Pools

Before any farm can be quoted, a `PlatformAdmin` populates three reference registries. Registering a producer captures `agency_name`, a non-blank string of 1 to 120 characters, and `license_state`, exactly two uppercase letters naming the state the producer holds a licence in. Two further fields may be supplied or left to their defaults: `appointment_status`, which is either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` and defaults to `ACTIVE`, and `authority_limit_cents`, the largest `total_premium_cents` this producer may have bound on its accounts, which must not be negative and defaults to 5000000 cents. The `id` returned by producer registration is precisely the value an `Agent`-role caller then presents as `X-User-ID`. A producer record may later be suspended, reinstated or re-authorised by supplying either or both of `appointment_status` and `authority_limit_cents` — both are optional on that route, so a body carrying neither is accepted and changes nothing but the stamps; the row's `version` climbs by 1 and nothing else in the system is revisited, because appointment standing and authority are read only at bind time.

Registering an inspector captures `full_name`, a non-blank string of 1 to 120 characters, and `region_code`, a string of 1 to 12 characters naming the territory covered. `status`, either `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE`, defaults to `ACTIVE` and gates whether the inspector may be given new work. `daily_capacity_minutes` is the number of inspection minutes the inspector may hold on a single UTC calendar date; it must be at least 30 and defaults to 480 minutes, a standard eight-hour day. As with producers, the returned `id` is what a `LossControlSpecialist` presents as `X-User-ID`, and later edits to `status` or `daily_capacity_minutes` — both optional, so here too an empty body is accepted and merely lifts the `version` — leave already-scheduled visits untouched.

Opening a capacity pool captures `zone_code`, `peril`, `participation_pct` and `total_capacity_cents`, with `status` optional and defaulting to `OPEN`. A second attempt to open a pool for a zone and peril that already have one is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_POOL`. Resizing or freezing a pool afterwards takes `expected_pool_version` together with either or both of `total_capacity_cents` and `status` — the latter two optional, so a body carrying only `expected_pool_version` is accepted and still re-derives and re-stamps the row; the pool's `available_cents` is re-derived from the new capacity against whatever is currently reserved and committed, and its `version` climbs by 1.

### 3.2. Opening an Account and a Draft Package

A producer creates the farm or ranch account that everything else hangs from. The submission carries `legal_name` (a non-blank string of 1 to 160 characters), `state_code` (exactly two uppercase letters), `county_name` (1 to 60 characters), `cat_zone_code` (1 to 24 characters, the catastrophe aggregation zone that will select the three capacity pools at rating time), `acreage` (the total operated acres, an integer of at least 1, and the sole driver of the general-farm liability charge) and `operation_type`, the dominant classification, which is one of `CROP`, `LIVESTOCK`, `DAIRY`, `RANCH` or `MIXED`. The account's `agent_id` is stamped by the server from the caller's `X-User-ID`; a client that tries to supply `agent_id` in the body has supplied an unknown field and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Opening a package on that account is likewise the producer's job. The submission names the account through `account_id` and supplies `term_start_date` and `term_end_date`, both plain calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form and both inclusive endpoints of the annual term, plus `deductible_cents`, the flat property deductible, which must not be negative. The term is checked immediately: `term_end_date` must fall strictly after `term_start_date`, and the interval they span must be at least 90 days, or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TERM_DATES`. A newly opened policy has `uw_status` `DRAFT`, `compliance_status` `NOT_INSPECTED`, `version` 1, every premium field and `exposed_tiv_cents` at 0, and `rating_valuation_date`, `quote_expires_at`, `referred_at`, `referred_by_user_id`, `referral_reason_code`, `bound_at`, `bound_by_user_id` and `closure_reason` all null.

### 3.3. Scheduling Property: Replacement Cost, Actual Cash Value and Seasonal Windows

Each article on the schedule is one row. The producer supplies `item_class` — one of `DWELLING`, `FARM_STRUCTURE`, `EQUIPMENT`, `STORED_CROP`, `HAY` or `INPUTS` — a `description` of 1 to 160 non-blank characters, a `valuation_basis` of either `RC` for replacement cost or `ACV` for actual cash value, a `stated_value_cents` greater than zero representing the article's off-peak value, an `age_years` between 0 and 100 inclusive, and a `condition_grade` of `A`, `B`, `C` or `D`. A fourth-generation seasonal trio may also appear: `peak_season_value_cents`, `peak_season_start_month` and `peak_season_end_month`. **All three of those fields must be present when `item_class` is `STORED_CROP`, `HAY` or `INPUTS`, and all three must be absent for `DWELLING`, `FARM_STRUCTURE` and `EQUIPMENT`; furthermore `peak_season_value_cents` must be greater than or equal to `stated_value_cents`.** A partial trio, a trio on the wrong class, a missing trio on a seasonal class, or a peak value below the off-peak value all draw `ERR_PEAK_SEASON_FIELDS_REQUIRED`. When present, `peak_season_value_cents` must be greater than zero and each month field must be between 1 and 12. The row also carries a `status` of `ACTIVE` or `REMOVED`, defaulting to `ACTIVE`; only `ACTIVE` items participate in rating. A freshly scheduled item is never returned unvalued: `insured_value_cents` and `valued_at` are already derived at the current logical clock instant when the row is created, and its `version` starts at 1.

The schedule can be revised: a producer may re-value an item, flip its `RC` / `ACV` election, correct its `age_years` or `condition_grade`, replace or clear the seasonal trio, or set `status` to `REMOVED` to take it off the schedule without deleting the history — those six are the whole amendable surface, so `item_class` and `description` are immutable and supplying either on an amendment is an unknown body field. Every such amendment re-derives the item's insured value, restamps `valued_at`, and lifts the item's `version` by 1.

The insured value of an item is computed in three steps, and both the seasonal step and the class cap apply on either valuation branch.

**Step one determines the seasonal effective value.** If `peak_season_start_month` is null the item is never in peak. Otherwise, take the calendar month (UTC) of the valuation instant — that instant is the policy's `rating_valuation_date` when the value is being derived as part of rating, and the current logical clock instant otherwise. When `peak_season_start_month` is less than or equal to `peak_season_end_month`, the item is in peak exactly when the valuation month lies at or after the start month and at or before the end month. When `peak_season_start_month` is greater than `peak_season_end_month` the window wraps across the turn of the year, and the item is in peak when the valuation month is at or after the start month *or* at or before the end month — a window running from October through February therefore includes December, January and February. The effective value is `peak_season_value_cents` when in peak and `stated_value_cents` otherwise.

**Step two branches on `valuation_basis`, and the two branches carry equal weight.** On the `RC` branch, the insured value is the smaller of the effective value and the class cap, rounded half up; `age_years` and `condition_grade` are read on this branch but contribute nothing at all. On the `ACV` branch, three quantities are formed from the effective value. First the depreciation fraction: the per-year depreciation rate for the item's class multiplied by `age_years`, but never allowed to exceed 0.60 — the rate is 0.015 per year for `DWELLING`, 0.025 for `FARM_STRUCTURE`, 0.070 for `EQUIPMENT`, and zero for `STORED_CROP`, `HAY` and `INPUTS`. Second the depreciated value: the effective value multiplied by one minus that depreciation fraction, then multiplied by the condition factor for the item's `condition_grade`, which is 1.00 for `A`, 0.92 for `B`, 0.80 for `C` and 0.45 for `D`. Third the salvage floor: 20 percent of the effective value. The insured value is then the larger of the depreciated value and the salvage floor, capped at the class cap, rounded half up. **The floor is applied before the cap; reversing those two would let a floored value escape its class cap.**

**Step three is the class cap and the single rounding, applied last on both branches.** The caps are 120000000 cents for a `DWELLING`, 60000000 for a `FARM_STRUCTURE`, 40000000 for `EQUIPMENT`, 25000000 for a `STORED_CROP` line, 15000000 for `HAY` and 10000000 for `INPUTS`. Rounding happens exactly once, at the end of whichever branch ran; no intermediate product is rounded.

Because a schedule change invalidates any price already struck, adding or amending a scheduled item on a policy whose `uw_status` is `RATED` drives that policy back through `RATED → DRAFT` with the full unwind described in §3.7. Adding or amending items on a `DRAFT` policy simply stores them. Attempting either on a `DECLINED` or `CANCELLED` policy draws `ERR_POLICY_TERMINAL`, and attempting either on a `REFERRED` or `BOUND` policy — the two live states that are neither `DRAFT` nor `RATED` — draws `ERR_POLICY_NOT_IN_DRAFT`.

### 3.4. Livestock Limits and Farm Liability Exposures

A livestock group names its `species` — one of `CATTLE`, `SWINE`, `POULTRY`, `SHEEP` or `EQUINE` — a `head_count` of at least 1, a `per_head_value_cents` greater than zero, a `limit_basis` of `PER_HEAD` or `BLANKET`, and a `peril_scope` of `FULL_PERIL`, `NAMED_PERIL` or `CATASTROPHE_ONLY`. **`blanket_limit_cents` is required when `limit_basis` is `BLANKET` and forbidden when it is `PER_HEAD`**; either mismatch draws `ERR_BLANKET_LIMIT_REQUIRED`, and when supplied the value must be greater than zero.

The group's `covered_limit_cents` is fixed at creation and is never recomputed by later ratings. On the `PER_HEAD` election it is the smaller of two figures: `head_count` multiplied by `per_head_value_cents`, and the per-head aggregate cap of 30000000 cents. On the `BLANKET` election it is `blanket_limit_cents` multiplied by the blanket coinsurance percentage of 0.80, rounded half up; the aggregate cap does **not** apply on this branch, and `head_count` and `per_head_value_cents` are recorded on the row but do not enter the limit. The same herd therefore attracts a materially different limit under the two elections, which is exactly the trade-off the producer is being asked to make.

Farm liability is scheduled as separate exposures, each naming an `exposure_type` of `GENERAL_FARM`, `CUSTOM_FARMING`, `AGRITOURISM` or `ROADSIDE_STAND`, together with `annual_receipts_cents`, which must not be negative. **`annual_visitor_count` is required when `exposure_type` is `AGRITOURISM` and forbidden for every other type**; either mismatch draws `ERR_VISITOR_COUNT_REQUIRED`, and when supplied it must not be negative.

Each exposure's `exposure_premium_cents` is likewise frozen at creation. A `GENERAL_FARM` exposure is priced as 25000 cents plus the account's `acreage` multiplied by 90 cents per acre, that acreage term rounded half up before it is added, and the result capped at 500000 cents. A `CUSTOM_FARMING` exposure is 45000 cents plus `annual_receipts_cents` multiplied by 0.0075, the receipts term rounded half up before addition, capped at 900000 cents. An `AGRITOURISM` exposure is 60000 cents, plus a per-visitor charge of 120 cents applied only to visitors beyond the first 500 — that is, `annual_visitor_count` less 500, or zero when the count does not exceed 500 — plus `annual_receipts_cents` multiplied by 0.0040 rounded half up, the whole capped at 800000 cents. A `ROADSIDE_STAND` exposure is 30000 cents plus `annual_receipts_cents` multiplied by 0.0050 rounded half up, capped at 300000 cents. The deductible credit described in §3.7 is deliberately **not** applied to liability lines; it is a property and livestock concept only.

Adding a livestock group or a liability exposure to a policy that is already `RATED` drives it back through `RATED → DRAFT` with the same unwind as a schedule change, is refused with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_IN_DRAFT` on a `REFERRED` or `BOUND` policy, and is refused with `ERR_POLICY_TERMINAL` once the policy is closed.

### 3.5. Labor and Workers-Compensation Coordination

A policy has at most one labor profile, and the producer maintains it as a whole-record upsert: the first submission creates the row, answering HTTP 201 with `version` 1, and every later submission overwrites it in place, lifts its `version` by 1 and answers HTTP 200. The submission always carries `full_time_count`, `seasonal_count` and `migrant_h2a_count`, each a non-negative integer, and may carry the three proof fields `wc_carrier_name`, `wc_policy_number` and `wc_expiry_date`, the last an inclusive `YYYY-MM-DD` date.

From the three headcounts the system derives `labor_exposure_units`: `full_time_count` weighted at 1.00, plus `seasonal_count` weighted at 0.50, plus `migrant_h2a_count` weighted at 0.75, with the sum rounded half up to exactly two decimal places. `wc_required` is true exactly when `labor_exposure_units` is at least 6.00 — the threshold is inclusive, so a crew of four year-round and four seasonal workers lands precisely on 6.00 and does require coverage, while three year-round and four seasonal workers reach only 5.00 and do not.

`coordination_status` is then recomputed from scratch on every submission: `NOT_REQUIRED` when `wc_required` is false, `PENDING_PROOF` when it is true. This recomputation is unconditional and always invalidates any prior verification — the transitions `NOT_REQUIRED → PENDING_PROOF`, `PENDING_PROOF → NOT_REQUIRED`, `VERIFIED → PENDING_PROOF` and `LAPSED → PENDING_PROOF` are all reachable purely by re-submitting the profile, the latter two whenever the recomputed `labor_exposure_units` reaches 6.00. In every case `verified_at` and `verified_by_user_id` are cleared. A profile submission never changes `uw_status` and never touches any premium figure. It is refused with `ERR_POLICY_TERMINAL` on a closed policy.

Only a `ComplianceOfficer` can move a profile `PENDING_PROOF → VERIFIED`, optionally recording a `note` with the act. Three conditions guard it: the `coordination_status` must actually be `PENDING_PROOF`, or the call is rejected with `ERR_WC_STATE_INVALID`; `wc_carrier_name`, `wc_policy_number` and `wc_expiry_date` must all be populated, or it is rejected with `ERR_WC_PROOF_MISSING`; and `wc_expiry_date` must be on or after the current logical clock date, or it is rejected with `ERR_WC_PROOF_EXPIRED`. On success `verified_at` takes the current clock instant, `verified_by_user_id` takes the acting officer's `X-User-ID`, and the profile's `version` climbs by 1. A verified proof does not stay verified forever: the compliance checkpoint moves `VERIFIED → LAPSED` once `wc_expiry_date` falls before the current clock date, clearing `verified_at` and `verified_by_user_id` and closing the coordination cycle that a fresh submission then reopens at `PENDING_PROOF`.

### 3.6. Rating the Package: Premium Build-Up, Catastrophe Aggregation and Referral

Rating is the centre of gravity of this service. Only an `Underwriter` or `SeniorUnderwriter` may drive it, the policy must be in `DRAFT` (a `REFERRED`, `BOUND` or already-`RATED` policy draws `ERR_POLICY_NOT_IN_DRAFT`), and the call carries `expected_policy_version` as its sole input.

Before anything is computed, a battery of preconditions is checked in the ladder order of §2.11 — which puts the term-overlap test at priority 12 ahead of every fiscal check at priority 15, so the sentences below read in narrative rather than evaluation order. The account's `cat_zone_code` must have a configured pool for each of `HAIL`, `WIND` and `WILDFIRE`, or the call fails with `ERR_CAT_POOL_NOT_CONFIGURED`. The policy must offer something rateable — at least one scheduled item whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, or at least one livestock group — so that `exposed_tiv_cents` will come out above zero; otherwise `ERR_NO_RATEABLE_EXPOSURE`. All three pools must be `OPEN`, or `ERR_POOL_FROZEN` — a frozen pool is refused before its utilization is even computed. No sibling policy on the same `account_id` whose `uw_status` is `RATED`, `REFERRED` or `BOUND` may have a term overlapping this one; policy terms are **closed-closed date intervals with no buffer**, so two terms overlap when this policy's `term_start_date` is on or before the sibling's `term_end_date` and this policy's `term_end_date` is on or after the sibling's `term_start_date`. Terms that merely share a single boundary date therefore do overlap and draw `ERR_OVERLAPPING_POLICY_TERM`. Siblings in `DRAFT`, `DECLINED` or `CANCELLED` are ignored entirely.

The rating pass then executes in a pinned sequence. **First, the release step:** every reservation of this policy still in `RESERVED` is set to `RELEASED`, and each pool so affected re-derives `reserved_cents`, `committed_cents` and `available_cents` and lifts its `version` by 1. On a first rating there is nothing to release and the step is a no-op, but it is declared because the arithmetic that follows must never see this policy's own stale hold. **Second, valuation:** `rating_valuation_date` is set to the current logical clock instant, and every `ACTIVE` item of the policy has its `insured_value_cents` and `valued_at` re-derived at that instant, which is what makes the seasonal window of §3.3 bite on the rating month rather than on whenever the item happened to be entered. A rating-driven revaluation refreshes each touched item's `updated_at` but, unlike a producer's amendment, leaves the item's `version` where it was.

**Third, the property line.** A deductible credit is selected from `deductible_cents` in four bands: a deductible below 100000 cents earns a credit of 1.00, below 500000 earns 0.95, below 1000000 earns 0.90, and 1000000 or more earns 0.85. The bands are strictly ordered on the upper edge, so a deductible of exactly 500000 cents lands in the 0.90 band, not the 0.95 one. Each `ACTIVE` item then contributes one line: its `insured_value_cents` multiplied by the property rate for its `item_class`, multiplied by the credit, **rounded half up to a whole cent on that line**. The property rates are 0.0040 for `DWELLING`, 0.0065 for `FARM_STRUCTURE`, 0.0090 for `EQUIPMENT`, 0.0110 for `STORED_CROP`, 0.0135 for `HAY` and 0.0120 for `INPUTS`. `property_premium_cents` is the exact sum of those already-rounded lines. Items are visited in `created_at` ascending order; addition of rounded integers commutes so the order does not change the total, but it does fix which line appears where in a per-line breakdown. Items whose `status` is `REMOVED` are excluded outright.

**Fourth, the livestock line.** Each group contributes its `covered_limit_cents` multiplied by the species rate, multiplied by the peril-restriction factor, multiplied by the same deductible credit the property line used, rounded half up per group. Species rates are 0.0125 for `CATTLE`, 0.0180 for `SWINE`, 0.0240 for `POULTRY`, 0.0150 for `SHEEP` and 0.0300 for `EQUINE`. The peril-restriction factor is 1.00 for `FULL_PERIL`, 0.85 for `NAMED_PERIL` and 0.60 for `CATASTROPHE_ONLY` — narrower cover means a smaller payable limit and therefore a smaller charge. `livestock_premium_cents` is the exact sum of the rounded group lines.

**Fifth, the liability line.** `liability_premium_cents` is the exact sum of `exposure_premium_cents` across every liability exposure of the policy, each of which was already rounded when its row was created.

**Sixth, the exposure base.** `exposed_tiv_cents` is the sum of `insured_value_cents` over the `ACTIVE` scheduled items plus the sum of `covered_limit_cents` over all livestock groups. Liability exposures and endorsement sublimits are **not** part of the catastrophe-exposed total insured value.

**Seventh, catastrophe aggregation, peril by peril in the fixed order `HAIL`, then `WIND`, then `WILDFIRE`.** For each peril the pool for the account's zone and that peril is taken, and five figures are derived in order. (1) `amount_cents` is `exposed_tiv_cents` multiplied by that pool's `participation_pct`, rounded half up. (2) The prospective utilization is the pool's `reserved_cents` plus its `committed_cents` plus this new `amount_cents`, all divided by the pool's `total_capacity_cents`, **evaluated as an exact ratio and never rounded before it is used**. (3) `scarcity_factor` is selected from that ratio in four bands: at most 0.50 gives 1.00, above 0.50 and at most 0.75 gives 1.15, above 0.75 and at most 0.90 gives 1.35, above 0.90 and at most 1.00 gives 1.60; a ratio strictly greater than 1.00 aborts the entire rating call with `ERR_CAT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` and leaves no reservation behind for any peril. (4) `load_cents` is `amount_cents` multiplied by the catastrophe rate for the peril — 0.0032 for `HAIL`, 0.0021 for `WIND`, 0.0045 for `WILDFIRE` — multiplied by `scarcity_factor`, rounded half up. Note that the scarcity factor multiplies the rated amount, not the raw total insured value. (5) A reservation row is written in `RESERVED` carrying `amount_cents`, `utilization_at_rating` (the prospective utilization rounded to six decimal places), `scarcity_factor` and `load_cents`, after which the pool's `reserved_cents` grows by `amount_cents`, `available_cents` is re-derived, and the pool's `version` climbs by 1. Because pools are per-peril, the growth on one pool is visible only to its own peril and the three amounts are independent of one another. `cat_load_cents` is then the sum of the three `load_cents`. The deductible credit is **not** applied to the catastrophe load.

**Eighth, the rollup.** `base_premium_cents` is `property_premium_cents` plus `livestock_premium_cents` plus `liability_premium_cents` plus `endorsement_premium_cents`, where the last is the sum of `premium_cents` over this policy's endorsements whose `status` is `APPROVED` — endorsements that are `SUSPENDED`, `REJECTED` or `RESCINDED` contribute nothing. `total_premium_cents` is `base_premium_cents` plus `cat_load_cents`. **Ninth, the ledger:** one entry is appended per non-zero component in the fixed order `BASE_PROPERTY`, `BASE_LIVESTOCK`, `BASE_LIABILITY`, `CAT_LOAD`, each with `reason_code` `RATING`, each with a positive `amount_cents`; a component worth zero is skipped and consumes no `sequence_no`.

**Tenth and last, the referral test**, applied only after every premium and reservation figure exists. Four triggers are evaluated in a pinned order and **the first match wins and becomes `referral_reason_code`; later matches are not recorded.** (1) `exposed_tiv_cents` strictly greater than 250000000 cents yields `TIV_ABOVE_LIMIT`. (2) The largest `annual_visitor_count` among the policy's `AGRITOURISM` exposures strictly greater than 2000 yields `AGRITOURISM_VISITORS`. (3) Any peril's prospective utilization — the unrounded ratio, not the six-decimal stored figure — strictly greater than 0.90 yields `CAT_UTILIZATION_HIGH`. (4) The `hazard_score` of the latest `COMPLETED` inspection on the policy at 40 or more yields `HAZARD_SCORE_HIGH`; "latest" means the greatest `completed_at`, and when two completions share that instant the one with the greater `created_at` wins, insertion order breaking any further tie. **The first three comparisons are strict; the fourth is inclusive.** A total insured value of exactly 250000000 does not refer, 2000 visitors do not refer, and a hazard score of exactly 40 does refer while 39 does not.

If no trigger matches, the policy advances `DRAFT → RATED`, `quote_expires_at` is set to `rating_valuation_date` plus 30 days as exact instant arithmetic, and `referred_at`, `referred_by_user_id` and `referral_reason_code` are cleared. If at least one trigger matches, the policy advances `DRAFT → REFERRED` instead, and every premium, reservation, pool and ledger effect above fires identically — the divergence is narrow and precise: on the `REFERRED` branch `quote_expires_at` stays null, while `referred_at` takes the current clock instant, `referred_by_user_id` takes the rating actor's `X-User-ID` and `referral_reason_code` takes the first matching trigger. Either branch lifts the policy's `version` by 1.

**The revision cycle.** Any exposure mutation on a `RATED` policy — adding a scheduled item, amending one, adding a livestock group, adding a liability exposure — drives the policy `RATED → DRAFT` and unwinds the quote completely: every `RESERVED` reservation of the policy becomes `RELEASED` with each touched pool re-deriving `reserved_cents` and `available_cents` and lifting its `version` by 1; a full reversal batch is appended, one `REVERSAL` entry per prior non-reversed entry replayed in `sequence_no` ascending order with `reason_code` `RATING_REVERT`; `property_premium_cents`, `livestock_premium_cents`, `liability_premium_cents`, `endorsement_premium_cents`, `base_premium_cents`, `cat_load_cents`, `total_premium_cents` and `exposed_tiv_cents` are all zeroed; `quote_expires_at` and `rating_valuation_date` are set to null; every endorsement of the policy in `APPROVED` moves to `SUSPENDED`, its `premium_cents` retained on the row because the ledger replay has already removed that money; and the policy's `version` climbs by 1. The policy must be rated afresh before it can be bound.

### 3.7. Referral Decisions, Binding, Declining and Cancellation

A referred policy waits for a `SeniorUnderwriter` to supply a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `RETURN` or `DECLINE`, optionally with a `decision_note` that is validated when present but persisted nowhere — the policy carries no field for it on any of the three branches. The policy's `uw_status` must be `REFERRED` or the call draws `ERR_POLICY_NOT_REFERRED`, and the deciding officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from `referred_by_user_id` or the call draws `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

`APPROVE` moves the policy `REFERRED → RATED`. It re-derives `quote_expires_at` as 30 days after the **original** `rating_valuation_date`, not 30 days after the decision, and clears `referred_at`, `referred_by_user_id` and `referral_reason_code`. Premiums, reservations and ledger entries are left completely untouched — a referral decision does not re-rate. `RETURN` moves the policy `REFERRED → DRAFT` with exactly the release, reversal and zeroing effects of the `RATED → DRAFT` revision cycle in §3.6, and likewise clears the three referral fields. `DECLINE` moves the policy `REFERRED → DECLINED` with the same release, reversal, zeroing and inspection-cancellation effects as a direct decline, the reversal entries carrying `reason_code` `POLICY_DECLINE`; because this route accepts no `closure_reason`, the policy's `closure_reason` is left exactly as it stood — null on a file that was never declined before — and unlike the other two branches it retains rather than clears `referred_at`, `referred_by_user_id` and `referral_reason_code`. All three branches lift the policy's `version` by 1.

Declining is open to either an `Underwriter` or a `SeniorUnderwriter` and takes a `closure_reason`, a required non-blank string of free text stored on the policy. It is available from `DRAFT`, `RATED` or `REFERRED`; attempting it on an already-bound policy draws `ERR_POLICY_NOT_BOUND`, and on a closed policy `ERR_POLICY_TERMINAL`. Whichever of the three states it starts from, the effects are identical: every `RESERVED` reservation becomes `RELEASED` with each touched pool re-deriving `reserved_cents` and `available_cents` and lifting its `version` by 1; a reversal batch is appended, one entry per prior non-reversed entry in `sequence_no` ascending order, with `reason_code` `POLICY_DECLINE`; every premium field and `exposed_tiv_cents` are zeroed and `quote_expires_at` is set to null, while `rating_valuation_date` and any `referred_at`, `referred_by_user_id` and `referral_reason_code` already on the row are retained and any `APPROVED` endorsement keeps its status and `premium_cents`; every inspection of the policy still in `SCHEDULED` is moved to `CANCELLED` with its own `version` lifted by 1 and no recommendation rows created; `closure_reason` is stored; and the policy's `version` climbs by 1.

Binding is the exclusive act of the line `Underwriter` and carries `expected_policy_version`. The policy must be `RATED`, or `ERR_POLICY_NOT_RATED`. Its `compliance_status` must not be `SUSPENDED`, or `ERR_COMPLIANCE_SUSPENDED`. No recommendation of `severity` `CRITICAL` on any inspection of the policy may be in `OVERDUE`, or `ERR_CRITICAL_REC_OVERDUE`. The producer on the account must have `appointment_status` `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_AGENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. A labor profile must exist whose `coordination_status` is `NOT_REQUIRED` or `VERIFIED` — a `PENDING_PROOF` or `LAPSED` profile draws `ERR_WC_COORDINATION_UNVERIFIED`, and a policy that never had a profile submitted at all draws `ERR_WC_PROOF_MISSING`. The current clock instant must be on or before `quote_expires_at`, or `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`; the expiry instant itself is still bindable, so the first non-bindable moment is one second later. If a proof is on file, its `wc_expiry_date` must be on or after the current clock date, or `ERR_WC_PROOF_EXPIRED`. Every pool holding one of this policy's `RESERVED` rows must still be `OPEN`, or `ERR_POOL_FROZEN`, and must still satisfy `reserved_cents` plus `committed_cents` no greater than `total_capacity_cents`, or `ERR_CAT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Finally `total_premium_cents` must not exceed the producer's `authority_limit_cents`, or `ERR_AGENT_AUTHORITY_EXCEEDED`.

On success every `RESERVED` reservation of the policy moves `RESERVED → COMMITTED`, and each touched pool re-derives `reserved_cents`, `committed_cents` and `available_cents` and lifts its `version` by 1 — the amount migrates from reserved to committed and `available_cents` does not move. `bound_at` takes the current clock instant, `bound_by_user_id` takes the binding underwriter's `X-User-ID`, each reservation's `version` climbs by 1, and the policy advances `RATED → BOUND` with its `version` up by 1. **No premium figure changes on bind.**

Cancelling a bound policy is also the line `Underwriter`'s exclusive act, and carries `expected_policy_version` together with a `closure_reason`, likewise a required non-blank string. The policy must be `BOUND`, or `ERR_POLICY_NOT_BOUND`. The cancellation is flat and cascades in declared hops. The first hop sets `uw_status` to `CANCELLED` and stores `closure_reason`. The second hop follows from the policy having left `BOUND`: every reservation of the policy in `COMMITTED` moves `COMMITTED → RELEASED`. The third hop follows from those rows having changed status: each referenced pool re-derives `committed_cents` and `available_cents` and lifts its `version` by 1, returning the amount to availability. Alongside those hops the ledger is reversed flat — one `REVERSAL` entry per prior non-reversed entry in `sequence_no` ascending order with `reason_code` `POLICY_CANCELLATION` — and every premium field, together with `exposed_tiv_cents`, is zeroed, while `quote_expires_at`, `rating_valuation_date`, `bound_at` and `bound_by_user_id` are all left standing as the record of what was bound. Every endorsement of the policy in `APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED` is moved to `RESCINDED` with `premium_cents` set to 0, and every inspection still `SCHEDULED` is moved to `CANCELLED`. The policy's `version` climbs by 1.

### 3.8. Endorsements: Buy-Backs and Coverage Increases

An endorsement request may be raised by either the producer or an `Underwriter` and carries `expected_policy_version`, a `form_code`, a `sublimit_cents` greater than zero, and a `requested_effective_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. `form_code` is one of `POLLUTION_BUYBACK`, `CHEMICAL_APPLICATION_BUYBACK`, `AGRITOURISM_LIABILITY` or `PEAK_SEASON_INCREASE`. The policy's `uw_status` must be `RATED` or `BOUND`, or the request draws `ERR_POLICY_NOT_RATED`, and its `compliance_status` must not be `SUSPENDED`, or `ERR_COMPLIANCE_SUSPENDED`. The new row lands in `status` `REQUESTED` with `premium_cents` 0, `decision_note` and `decided_by_user_id` null, and `requires_inspection` derived as true exactly when `form_code` is `POLLUTION_BUYBACK` or `CHEMICAL_APPLICATION_BUYBACK` and false for the other two forms. Requesting an endorsement changes no policy premium figure and — importantly for the version check that guards it — does **not** lift the policy's `version`.

Deciding a request belongs to an `Underwriter` or `SeniorUnderwriter` and supplies a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, optionally with a `decision_note`. The endorsement must be in `REQUESTED`, or `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_NOT_REQUESTED`. `REJECT` moves it `REQUESTED → REJECTED`, stores `decision_note` and `decided_by_user_id`, leaves `premium_cents` at 0, appends no ledger entry, changes no policy field, and lifts only the endorsement's `version` by 1.

`APPROVE` is guarded further. The policy must still be `RATED` or `BOUND` and must not be compliance-`SUSPENDED`. When `requires_inspection` is true, the policy must have at least one inspection in `COMPLETED`, or the approval draws `ERR_BUYBACK_INSPECTION_REQUIRED`, and the `hazard_score` of the latest `COMPLETED` inspection — greatest `completed_at`, and on a tie the greater `created_at` — must be no greater than 35, or it draws `ERR_BUYBACK_HAZARD_TOO_HIGH`. A `CHEMICAL_APPLICATION_BUYBACK` may only be approved on a policy that carries a `CUSTOM_FARMING` liability exposure, and an `AGRITOURISM_LIABILITY` form only on a policy that carries an `AGRITOURISM` exposure; a missing companion exposure draws `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_EXPOSURE_MISSING`.

On approval the endorsement's `premium_cents` is priced from its `form_code` and `sublimit_cents`. A `POLLUTION_BUYBACK` is `sublimit_cents` multiplied by 0.0150 and rounded half up, then raised to a minimum of 75000 cents if the product falls below it. A `CHEMICAL_APPLICATION_BUYBACK` is `sublimit_cents` multiplied by 0.0220 and rounded half up, then raised to a minimum of 90000 cents. An `AGRITOURISM_LIABILITY` form is `sublimit_cents` multiplied by 0.0090 and rounded half up, plus a flat 25000 cents added on top with no minimum. A `PEAK_SEASON_INCREASE` is simply `sublimit_cents` multiplied by 0.0110 and rounded half up. In every case the rounding happens before the minimum or the flat adder is applied. `decided_by_user_id` is stamped, the endorsement advances `REQUESTED → APPROVED`, an `ENDORSEMENT_ADD` entry with the positive `premium_cents` and `reason_code` `ENDORSEMENT_APPROVAL` is appended, and `endorsement_premium_cents`, `base_premium_cents` and `total_premium_cents` all rise by that amount. **`cat_load_cents` is never recomputed by an endorsement** — the catastrophe load is frozen at the rating that produced the current reservations. The endorsement's `version` and the policy's `version` each climb by 1.

An `Underwriter` may rescind an endorsement, optionally recording a `decision_note`. The row must be in `APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED`, or the call draws `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_NOT_APPROVED`, and the policy's `uw_status` must be `RATED` or `BOUND`. The two starting states differ in exactly one respect: rescinding from `APPROVED` appends an `ENDORSEMENT_REMOVE` entry whose `amount_cents` is the negation of `premium_cents` with `reason_code` `ENDORSEMENT_RESCISSION`, whereas rescinding from `SUSPENDED` appends **no** ledger entry at all, because the suspension already removed that money. In both cases `endorsement_premium_cents`, `base_premium_cents` and `total_premium_cents` fall by the amount actually removed, `premium_cents` is set to 0, `status` becomes `RESCINDED`, and both the endorsement's and the policy's `version` climb by 1.

Endorsements also move by cascade rather than by request. When a policy's `compliance_status` enters `RECS_OPEN`, `RECS_OVERDUE` or `SUSPENDED`, every endorsement of that policy with `requires_inspection` true and `status` `APPROVED` is moved `APPROVED → SUSPENDED`, its `premium_cents` retained on the row for later reinstatement, its `version` up by 1. When a policy reverts `RATED → DRAFT`, the same suspension is applied to **every** `APPROVED` endorsement regardless of `requires_inspection`. When a policy's `compliance_status` returns to `COMPLIANT`, every `SUSPENDED` endorsement moves `SUSPENDED → APPROVED` again with its retained `premium_cents`, and its `version` climbs by 1. Both directions carry matching ledger movements, described in §3.9. `REJECTED` and `RESCINDED` are terminal for an endorsement.

### 3.9. Loss Control: Inspections, Recommendations and the Compliance Sweep

An inspection is booked by an `Underwriter` or a `ComplianceOfficer` against a specific inspector's calendar. The booking names `policy_id` and `inspector_id`, an `inspection_type` of `INITIAL`, `RE_INSPECTION` or `LOSS_TRIGGERED`, a `scheduled_for` instant in ISO 8601 UTC, and a `duration_minutes` between 30 and 480 inclusive. The window an inspection occupies is **half-open**: it begins at `scheduled_for` and ends before `scheduled_for` plus `duration_minutes`. Booking imposes no requirement whatsoever on the policy's `uw_status` beyond the terminal freeze of §2.7, so a `DRAFT`, `RATED`, `REFERRED` or `BOUND` policy may all be sent an inspector.

Two calendar checks then apply, in this order, followed by an inspector-standing check. First, **window conflict**: each of an inspector's `SCHEDULED` inspections occupies its half-open window extended by a 90-minute travel buffer appended after the end instant. The requested window collides with an existing one when the requested start falls strictly before the existing window's buffered end, and the requested buffered end falls strictly after the existing window's start. Touching exactly at a buffer edge is allowed. `CANCELLED` and `COMPLETED` inspections never collide. A collision is rejected with `ERR_INSPECTOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Second, **day load**: the sum of `duration_minutes` across the inspector's `SCHEDULED` inspections whose `scheduled_for` falls on the same UTC calendar date as the requested window, plus the requested `duration_minutes`, must not exceed the inspector's `daily_capacity_minutes`. Equality is permitted; exceeding it is `ERR_INSPECTOR_DAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` inspections consume no capacity at all, so an inspector whose day is nominally full of finished visits is free to take more work. Third, the named inspector's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_INSPECTOR_INACTIVE`. A new inspection lands in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `hazard_score` 0, `recommendation_count` 0, and `completed_at` and `findings_summary` null. `CANCELLED` is terminal for an inspection and is reached only by cascade, never by direct request.

Only the assigned inspector may complete the visit, supplying a `findings_summary` narrative and a `findings` array. Each element of `findings` describes one loss-control recommendation and carries a `severity` of `CRITICAL`, `MAJOR` or `MINOR`, a `description` of 1 to 240 non-blank characters, and a `due_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form marking the inclusive compliance follow-up deadline. Those three are the only keys an element may carry: a fourth key, or an element that is not a JSON object at all, is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and an empty `findings` array is perfectly legal. The inspection must be `SCHEDULED`, or `ERR_INSPECTION_STATE_INVALID`; the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the inspection's `inspector_id`, or `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_INSPECTOR`; and the policy must not be `DECLINED` or `CANCELLED`, or `ERR_POLICY_TERMINAL`.

Completion is a four-hop cascade. **Hop one** advances the inspection `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`, stores `completed_at` at the current clock instant along with `findings_summary`, sets `recommendation_count` to the number of elements in `findings`, and computes `hazard_score` as the base score for the `inspection_type` plus the severity weight of each supplied finding, the whole capped at 100. The base is 10 for an `INITIAL` visit, 5 for a `RE_INSPECTION` and 20 for a `LOSS_TRIGGERED` visit; the severity weights are 30 for `CRITICAL`, 12 for `MAJOR` and 4 for `MINOR`. **Only the findings supplied to this completion are scored** — recommendations carried over from an earlier visit on the same policy contribute nothing to this inspection's `hazard_score`. Still in hop one, one recommendation row is created per element in `OPEN` status, and `sequence_no` continues from the highest `sequence_no` already present on **this** inspection: a first completion assigns 1 through N in array order, and a later re-inspection completion continues at N+1 through N+M. Each new row points back at the visit that raised it through `inspection_id` and copies its element's `severity`, `description` and `due_date`, with `evidence_note`, `completed_date` and `reviewed_by_user_id` null. **Hop two** moves the policy's compliance axis: with at least one finding it advances to `RECS_OPEN` — reachable from `NOT_INSPECTED` or from `COMPLIANT` alike, and from those two states only, so a policy already sitting in `RECS_OPEN`, `RECS_OVERDUE` or `SUSPENDED` keeps the axis it has and skips hops three and four entirely — while a completion with an empty `findings` array advances it to `COMPLIANT` provided that afterwards no recommendation on any inspection of the policy remains in `OPEN`, `OVERDUE` or `SUBMITTED`, a move available from `NOT_INSPECTED`, `RECS_OPEN`, `RECS_OVERDUE` or `SUSPENDED`. **Hop three** follows from the compliance axis leaving the clean states: every endorsement of the policy with `requires_inspection` true and `status` `APPROVED` is suspended. **Hop four** follows from those suspensions: one `ENDORSEMENT_REMOVE` entry per suspended endorsement, with `amount_cents` the negation of that endorsement's `premium_cents` and `reason_code` `COMPLIANCE_SUSPENSION`, appended in the endorsements' `created_at` ascending order, and `endorsement_premium_cents`, `base_premium_cents` and `total_premium_cents` decremented accordingly. The inspection's `version` and the policy's `version` each climb by 1, the policy's unconditionally — even on a completion that moved neither its compliance axis nor any endorsement. Where a completion instead lands the policy on `COMPLIANT`, the mirror cascade runs: every `SUSPENDED` endorsement returns to `APPROVED` and one `ENDORSEMENT_ADD` entry per reinstated endorsement, with the positive retained `premium_cents` and `reason_code` `COMPLIANCE_REINSTATEMENT`, is appended in `created_at` ascending order, with the three premium figures incremented accordingly.

A completed inspection can be sent back out. An `Underwriter` or `ComplianceOfficer` supplies a fresh `scheduled_for` and `duration_minutes`, moving the row `COMPLETED → SCHEDULED`, setting its `inspection_type` to `RE_INSPECTION` and clearing `completed_at`. The row must be `COMPLETED` or the call draws `ERR_INSPECTION_STATE_INVALID`, the policy must not be closed, the holding inspector's `status` must still be `ACTIVE`, and the new window must pass the same conflict and day-load checks — with the row being re-scheduled excluded from its own day load, since it is being replaced rather than duplicated, so the previous window stops consuming capacity as the new one starts. `hazard_score`, `recommendation_count` and `findings_summary` are all retained until the re-inspection completes — `completed_at` is the only field cleared — existing recommendation rows are untouched, the policy's `compliance_status` does not move, and the inspection's `version` climbs by 1. This follow-up cycle may repeat any number of times.

Remediation is the producer's responsibility. Submitting evidence on a recommendation in `OPEN` or `OVERDUE` stores an `evidence_note` and a `completed_date` — the date the producer states the work was finished — and advances the row to `SUBMITTED`; from `OVERDUE` the policy's `compliance_status` deliberately does not move and remains `RECS_OVERDUE` until every recommendation resolves. The recommendation must be in `OPEN` or `OVERDUE`, or the call draws `ERR_RECOMMENDATION_STATE_INVALID`; the caller must be the producer on the owning account; and the policy must not be closed. The row's `version` climbs by 1 and the policy's `version` does not move at all.

A `ComplianceOfficer` then reviews, supplying a `decision` of `ACCEPT`, `REJECT` or `WAIVE` and optionally a `review_note` — validated when present but persisted nowhere, as the recommendation carries no field for it — and stamping `reviewed_by_user_id` in every case. `ACCEPT` requires `SUBMITTED` and advances the row to `VERIFIED`. `REJECT` also requires `SUBMITTED` and sends the row back to `OPEN`, retaining `evidence_note` and `completed_date` for the next attempt and leaving the policy's `compliance_status` where it was — this is the remediation loop, and evidence can be resubmitted as often as necessary. `WAIVE` is broader: it is accepted from `OPEN`, `OVERDUE` or `SUBMITTED` and moves the row to `WAIVED`. An `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` on a row that is not `SUBMITTED`, and any review of a row already `VERIFIED` or `WAIVED`, draws `ERR_RECOMMENDATION_STATE_INVALID`. `VERIFIED` and `WAIVED` are terminal for a recommendation. Whenever an `ACCEPT` or a `WAIVE` leaves no recommendation of the policy in `OPEN`, `OVERDUE` or `SUBMITTED`, the policy's compliance axis advances to `COMPLIANT` — a move available from `RECS_OPEN`, `RECS_OVERDUE` or `SUSPENDED` — every `SUSPENDED` endorsement returns to `APPROVED`, one `ENDORSEMENT_ADD` entry with `reason_code` `COMPLIANCE_REINSTATEMENT` is appended per reinstated endorsement in `created_at` ascending order, the three endorsement-driven premium figures rise accordingly, and the policy's `version` climbs by 1. The recommendation's own `version` always climbs by 1.

Finally, the compliance checkpoint. A `ComplianceOfficer` or `PlatformAdmin` runs it, optionally narrowing it to one policy with `policy_id`, and it does three things at the current clock value. Every recommendation in `OPEN` whose `due_date` falls strictly before the current clock date is moved `OPEN → OVERDUE` and each affected policy advances `RECS_OPEN → RECS_OVERDUE`. Then, in the **same pass that aged the recommendation**, a policy standing in `RECS_OVERDUE` — whether this run aged it there or an earlier one did, since the escalation test reads the axis rather than the ageing that produced it — whose `uw_status` is `RATED` or `BOUND` and which now holds at least one `CRITICAL` recommendation in `OVERDUE` is escalated `RECS_OVERDUE → SUSPENDED`, and its endorsements with `requires_inspection` true and `status` `APPROVED` are suspended with the same `ENDORSEMENT_REMOVE` entries and `COMPLIANCE_SUSPENSION` reason code as the completion cascade — a single run therefore performs both hops for the same policy. Separately, every labor profile hanging off one of the policies this run actually scanned — so a run narrowed by `policy_id` lapses only that policy's profile and leaves every other file alone — that is in `VERIFIED` and whose `wc_expiry_date` falls strictly before the current clock date is moved `VERIFIED → LAPSED` with `verified_at` and `verified_by_user_id` cleared. Rows are processed policy by policy in policy `created_at` ascending order — every policy in the book, `DECLINED` and `CANCELLED` files included, so `policies_scanned` counts them all and their open recommendations still age; only the `SUSPENDED` escalation, gated on `RATED` or `BOUND`, passes a closed file by — and within a policy by recommendation `sequence_no` ascending. Each touched row lifts its own `version` by 1 for each hop that touches it, so a policy that both ages into `RECS_OVERDUE` and is then escalated to `SUSPENDED` in one run comes out with its `version` up by 2. The checkpoint is idempotent: running it twice at the same clock value changes nothing on the second run. It reports five counters — `policies_scanned`, `recommendations_marked_overdue`, `policies_marked_recs_overdue`, `policies_suspended` and `labor_profiles_lapsed` — and a `policy_id` that resolves to nothing draws `ERR_FARM_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`.
