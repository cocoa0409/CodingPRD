# Preschool Child Find API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every school district carries an affirmative statutory obligation to find, screen, evaluate and — where the team so determines — serve preschool-aged children with disabilities living within its boundaries. In practice that obligation is discharged by a dozen loosely coupled offices: an outreach team that runs community screening days at partner preschools, an intake desk that receives pediatrician faxes and early-intervention transition packets, a multidisciplinary evaluation team of psychologists, speech-language pathologists, occupational and physical therapists, early-childhood special-education teachers and social workers, a placement office that decides the least restrictive environment and buys the service minutes, and a records office that must be able to prove, months later, that written parental consent preceded every assessment and that every notice reached the family in a language it could actually read. Today those offices coordinate through spreadsheets and email. Deadlines are missed not because anybody wanted to miss them but because nobody could see, on one screen, which consent-anchored sixty-day clock was about to run out and which of those clocks was additionally truncated by a child's third birthday.

This document specifies the **Preschool Child Find API**, the district's system of record for that whole arc. The service is mounted under `/child-find`. It has two hard cores that share one entity graph. The first is statutory compliance: consent-anchored evaluation deadlines capped, for early-intervention transition cases, by the child's third birthday; language-access gating on every parent notice; and a strict segregation of duties between the chair of an evaluation and the specialists who assess its developmental domains. The second is finite-capacity allocation: screening appointments compete for a limited window on a host site, community-preschool placements compete for a limited number of IEP seats, and each itinerant provider carries two entirely independent capacity dimensions — a weekly service-minute ceiling inflated by travel overhead, and a concurrent evaluation caseload ceiling.

Two design consequences run through everything below. First, the system is **deterministic and clock-driven**: no rule reads wall-clock time, and every deadline, age and window computation reads a server-held logical clock that an administrator can set. Second, the system is **honest about interpretive disagreement**: where a statutory rule admits two defensible readings — when the sixty-day evaluation clock starts, which assessment methods may support an eligibility finding, whether draft service plans consume provider capacity, and in what order service minutes are rounded — the API pins exactly one reading for persistence and enforcement, and exposes the competing reading side by side on a dedicated read-only view so that compliance officers can see the divergence rather than argue about it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint, without exception, carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. A request with a missing or empty `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401), and this check precedes everything else including path resolution, so a request with no identity header aimed at a nonexistent path still answers 401 rather than 404. A request whose `X-User-Role` is absent, is not one of the six recognised role names, or is a recognised name that the target endpoint does not authorise, is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403); an unrecognised role string and a recognised-but-unauthorised role are deliberately indistinguishable to the caller, and both outrank body validation, so a forbidden role submitting a malformed payload still receives 403.

The six roles are spelled exactly `ChildFindCoordinator`, `EvaluationChair`, `Evaluator`, `PlacementAdministrator`, `Parent`, and `DistrictAdmin`.

A `ChildFindCoordinator` runs community outreach, screening events, referral intake and the parent-notice and consent process. When `X-User-Role` is `ChildFindCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `PlacementAdministrator`, who owns eligibility determination, setting and site decisions, service minutes, itinerant staffing and kindergarten transition: its `X-User-ID` is opaque, and the one place the server writes it down is `determined_by_user_id` on the service plan created when a determination is recorded. `DistrictAdmin`, the district-level superuser for the staff registry, data correction and logical-clock test driving, likewise presents an opaque `X-User-ID`.

The remaining three roles are entity-backed, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `EvaluationChair`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider`; ownership checks compare the target evaluation case's `chair_provider_id` to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_CASE_CHAIR` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `Evaluator`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Provider`; ownership checks compare the target domain assessment's `evaluator_provider_id` to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_EVALUATOR` (HTTP 403). An `Evaluator` may administer and record exactly one developmental-domain assessment at a time and can never schedule assessments, draft reports or determine eligibility. When `X-User-Role` is `Parent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Child` — the guardian holding educational rights is addressed through the child's own identifier — and ownership checks on any row that references a child compare that row's child linkage to `X-User-ID`, walking through the referral or evaluation case where the row does not carry the child directly. A `Parent` reaching a row belonging to another family is refused with `ERR_NOT_CHILD_GUARDIAN` (HTTP 403). A `Parent` may never create a referral, schedule anything, or read provider capacity.

`DistrictAdmin` bypasses the three identity checks above — guardian, case chair and assigned evaluator — but bypasses nothing else: chair eligibility, segregation of duties, state preconditions, temporal conflicts, structural constraints and every capacity ceiling apply to `DistrictAdmin` exactly as they apply to anyone else.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are always ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest record first — and no endpoint offers an alternative sort. When a list endpoint responds, its `meta` object carries the four pagination fields `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the size of the filtered result set before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when records remain beyond the current window. Every list endpoint additionally accepts a `status` filter matching the corresponding entity's own status enumeration, alongside the entity-specific filters described in §3 and §4.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps, Versions and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` in UUID form, with no type prefix and no client control over its value. Every resource also carries `created_at` and `updated_at`, both ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits, and an integer `version` that begins at 1 when the record is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record. Every entity in this system — `Child`, `CommunityPreschoolSite`, `Provider`, `ScreeningEvent`, `ScreeningSlot`, `Referral`, `ConsentRecord`, `EvaluationCase`, `DomainAssessment`, `ServicePlan` and `ServiceLine` — carries all four of these properties; there is no entity here that omits `updated_at` or `version`. A cascade that mutates a related record refreshes that record's `updated_at` and increments its `version` too, so a single request can advance several version counters at once.

No rule in this system reads real wall-clock time. Instead the server holds a **logical clock**, and every deadline, age, window, attendance and compliance computation reads it. Before any administrator sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `DistrictAdmin` may move the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting capability; the response echoes back `current_utc`, and every evaluation case's deadline posture is re-derived from the new instant on its next read.

### 2.5. Nested Paths and the Order of Refusal

Several endpoints address a child resource through its parent — an event's slots, a referral's consent requests, a case's domain assessments, a plan's service lines. Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right: the outer identifier is resolved before the inner one, so a request naming a nonexistent event and a nonexistent slot reports the event's absence. Each entity has its own not-found code, all of them HTTP 404: `ERR_CHILD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMMUNITY_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCREENING_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCREENING_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSENT_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVALUATION_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DOMAIN_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_SERVICE_LINE_NOT_FOUND`.

Because most write endpoints here are guarded by several rules at once, the order in which those rules are consulted is part of the contract, not an implementation detail. Validation is a single ordered ladder; the **first** rule that a request violates determines the response, and no later rule is consulted. The ladder, from first consulted to last, is:

1. `X-User-ID` present and non-empty, on every request, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. `X-User-Role` present, recognised, and listed among the target endpoint's authorised roles, on every request, else `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).
3. Request-shape validation on every request: the body parses, every required input field is present, and every supplied field matches its declared type, enumeration and range. This step also covers pagination bounds, ISO 8601 parseability, `weekly_minutes` being a multiple of 5 within 5 through 600 inclusive, and `window_end_utc` falling strictly after `window_start_utc`. Violations are `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
4. Every identifier appearing as a path segment resolves to an existing row of the expected entity, on every endpoint that has a path parameter, using the entity-specific 404 code above.
5. Every foreign key supplied in a request body resolves to an existing row, checked after all path identifiers and in the order the endpoint declares its inputs, again with the entity-specific 404 code. This applies to screening-event creation, slot booking, referral intake, consent-request issuance, evaluation-case opening, domain-assessment scheduling, service-plan creation, service-line creation and service-line staffing.
6. Guardian ownership: when the caller's role is `Parent`, `X-User-ID` must equal the `id` of the `Child` that the target row belongs to, else `ERR_NOT_CHILD_GUARDIAN` (403). This guards the single-child read, the single-referral read, the referral compliance view, the single-service-plan read, and the consent-decision write. Because it runs after existence, a `Parent` probing another family learns 403 for rows that exist and 404 for rows that do not.
7. Assigned-evaluator ownership: when the caller's role is `Evaluator`, `X-User-ID` must equal the target assessment's `evaluator_provider_id`, else `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_EVALUATOR` (403). This guards assessment completion and assessment invalidation. The chair of the same case is separately authorised on both of those endpoints and does not pass through this rule.
8. Case-chair ownership: when the caller's role is `EvaluationChair`, `X-User-ID` must equal the target case's `chair_provider_id`, else `ERR_NOT_CASE_CHAIR` (403). This guards domain-assessment scheduling, report drafting, determination, assessment completion and assessment invalidation. Chair identity is checked before any state or capacity rule, so the wrong chair acting on an already-cancelled case sees 403 and not a state error.
9. Chair eligibility: the provider named as `chair_provider_id` when an evaluation case is opened must be chair-eligible, else `ERR_CHAIR_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422). This is a discipline-based duty restriction independent of who is calling.
10. Optimistic concurrency: where an endpoint declares an expected-version input, the submitted value must equal the parent row's current `version`, else the rule's own 412 code. See §2.6.
11. Own-state precondition: the target row is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, else the entity's own 409 state code — `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_SLOT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_REFERRAL_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CONSENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ASSESSMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_LINE_STATE`. This rule covers terminal-state lockdown and repeat-trigger failures, and it deliberately sits above every referential, temporal, structural and capacity rule. It has two carve-outs, both forced by the referral's two parallel state dimensions, described in §3.4.
12. Related-entity state precondition, also 409, and the error always names the **related** entity rather than the row being created: booking a slot into an event that is not accepting bookings is `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE`; creating a service plan against a case that is not ready is `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`; adding a service line to a plan that is not a draft is `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`. Evaluation-case creation is deliberately absent from this rung: its related-entity gate is the referral itself, which rungs 11 and 13 partition between them.
13. Prior written parental consent for evaluation has been granted on the referral before any evaluation activity, else `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_GRANTED` (409), guarding evaluation-case opening.
14. Language access on parent notices, else `ERR_INTERPRETER_NOT_PROVIDED` (422), guarding both the evaluation-consent request and the service-consent submission. This is the highest business-safety rule below consent itself, and it is checked before the interpreter's own availability ceiling.
15. Language access on screening bookings, also `ERR_INTERPRETER_NOT_PROVIDED` (422), guarding slot booking. Same code, different surface; it sits above event capacity, so an unservable language is reported even on a fully booked event.
16. Segregation of duties, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (422), guarding domain-assessment scheduling and consent-request issuance.
17. Age eligibility at referral receipt, else `ERR_AGE_INELIGIBLE` (422), guarding referral intake.
18. The provider named on a write is `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_ACTIVE` (422). This guards four endpoints: evaluation-case opening, domain-assessment scheduling, service-line staffing and consent-request issuance. Its placement strictly below rungs 11 and 12 is the pinned tie-break for the state-versus-referential conflict.
19. Discipline match, else `ERR_DISCIPLINE_MISMATCH` (422), guarding service-line staffing and domain-assessment scheduling.
20. Site eligibility for the intended use, else `ERR_SITE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422). One code covers all three facets, and the message names the facet. This guards screening-event creation, service-plan creation and service-line creation.
21. Screening-event window overlap at the same site, else `ERR_SITE_EVENT_OVERLAP` (409), guarding screening-event creation.
22. Evaluator window conflict, else `ERR_EVALUATOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409), guarding domain-assessment scheduling.
23. Slot containment inside its event window, else `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_EVENT_WINDOW` (422), guarding slot booking. Its placement above the capacity rung is the pinned tie-break for the temporal-versus-capacity conflict.
24. Kindergarten transition conference lead time, else `ERR_TRANSITION_CONFERENCE_TOO_LATE` (422), guarding the transition endpoint and applying only to the conference-scheduling action.
25. All five distinct developmental domains have a completed assessment, else `ERR_DOMAINS_INCOMPLETE` (409), guarding report drafting and determination.
26. No duplicate live assessment of the same domain on one case, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_DOMAIN_ASSESSMENT` (409), guarding domain-assessment scheduling.
27. The revision-round ceiling, else `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (409), guarding revision requests.
28. No second evaluation-consent request while one is still live, else `ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_REQUESTED` (409), guarding consent-request issuance.
29. Only a granted evaluation consent may be revoked, else `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_REVOCABLE` (409), guarding consent decisions.
30. Service-setting requirements, else `ERR_PLAN_SETTING_REQUIREMENTS_UNMET` (422), guarding service-plan creation, service-line creation and service-consent submission. This is the fork guard for the branching plan lifecycle.
31. Override justification when the determination contradicts the team recommendation, else `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_OVERRIDE_JUSTIFICATION_REQUIRED` (422), guarding service-plan creation.
32. Kindergarten transition action ordering, else `ERR_TRANSITION_SEQUENCE_VIOLATION` (409), guarding the transition endpoint.
33. No unresolved bookings when an event is completed, else `ERR_SCREENING_SLOTS_UNRESOLVED` (409), guarding the event status endpoint.
34. Screening capacity, else `ERR_SCREENING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422), guarding slot booking.
35. IEP seat capacity, else `ERR_IEP_SEAT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422), guarding service-consent submission.
36. Evaluation caseload, else `ERR_EVALUATION_CASELOAD_EXCEEDED` (422), guarding evaluation-case opening.
37. Provider weekly-minute capacity, else `ERR_PROVIDER_CASELOAD_EXCEEDED` (422), guarding service-line staffing.
38. Interpreter availability, else `ERR_INTERPRETER_UNAVAILABLE` (422), guarding consent-request issuance and service-consent submission. This is the lowest rung in the ladder, so an unavailable interpreter is reported only once every language, referential, state and structural rule has passed.

The business meaning of each rung — what counts as an overlap, what counts as a qualifying domain, how a capacity ceiling is computed — is developed in §3.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Parents

Three write operations in this system consume a scarce resource held on a **parent** record, and in all three the client's decision to write depends on a view of that parent it read earlier. For those three, and only those three, the request must carry the client's last observed `version` of the parent, and the server refuses the write if the parent has moved on since. Nothing about this mechanism depends on requests arriving close together in time; a version read an hour ago and submitted now is stale in exactly the same way as one read a moment ago.

Booking a screening slot requires `expected_screening_event_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `ScreeningEvent`. If it does not equal that event's current `version`, the booking is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SCREENING_EVENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This is the check that catches a coordinator whose view of `booked_slot_count` and `effective_slot_capacity` was taken before some other booking consumed capacity; because a further booking is perfectly legitimate whenever capacity remains, no state precondition would have caught it.

Recording an eligibility determination requires `expected_evaluation_case_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `EvaluationCase`. A mismatch with the case's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_EVALUATION_CASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A second determination on the same case is legitimate product behaviour — it supersedes the earlier draft plan — so, again, only the version check makes the writer's stale view of the case observable.

Staffing a service line requires `expected_provider_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Provider` being staffed. A mismatch with that provider's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROVIDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Staffing a further line to the same provider is legitimate whenever the minute ceiling still holds, so nothing in the line's own lifecycle would reject it; the version check is what makes a stale caseload view visible.

No other endpoint takes a version field, and supplying one where it is not declared has no effect. All three checks are consulted at rung 10 of the ladder — after every authorisation and existence rule, and before every state, temporal, structural and capacity rule — so a stale version submitted against a fully booked event answers 412 and not the capacity error.

### 2.7. Language Access as a Precondition, Not a Courtesy

A child's `primary_language_code` is a lowercase two-letter ISO 639-1 code and defaults to `en`. From it the system derives `interpreter_required`, which is true exactly when `primary_language_code` is anything other than `en`. That single derived flag drives a hard gate on every parent-facing notice: when `interpreter_required` is true, the notice must either be written in the child's own `primary_language_code` — that is, the supplied `notice_language_code` equals it — or it must name an `interpreter_provider_id` whose provider has `discipline` of `INTERPRETER` and whose `languages` array contains the child's `primary_language_code`. A notice that satisfies neither condition is refused with `ERR_INTERPRETER_NOT_PROVIDED` (HTTP 422), on both the evaluation-consent request and the service-consent submission. When a child reads English, neither condition is required and the notice may be issued in any language the district chooses.

The same code, `ERR_INTERPRETER_NOT_PROVIDED`, guards a second and structurally different surface: a screening appointment may not be booked in a language the event cannot serve. Whenever the booking's `requested_language_code` is anything other than `en`, that code must appear in the parent event's `interpreter_languages` array, or the booking is refused. On both surfaces, the language rule is consulted before the relevant capacity ceiling, so a family whose language cannot be served hears about the language and not about a full calendar.

### 2.8. Arithmetic, Precision and Calendar Conventions

All numeric work in this system is exact decimal arithmetic, never binary floating point. Three conventions recur and are used consistently everywhere below.

**Whole completed months.** The age of a child between two calendar dates is a whole number of completed months, computed by taking twelve times the difference in years plus the difference in months, and then subtracting one if the day-of-month of the later date is less than the day-of-month of the earlier date. The result is never rounded; it is a count of months actually completed. This convention produces `age_months_at_referral` on a referral and `chronological_age_months` on a domain assessment.

**Percentages to one decimal place.** Every delay percentage — `percent_delay`, `weighted_percent_delay`, and the case-level `max_weighted_percent_delay` derived from them — carries exactly one decimal place and is rounded half up, meaning a value exactly on the halfway mark rounds away from zero.

**Minutes rounded up to the nearest five.** Every minute quantity the system computes rather than accepts is rounded **up** to the next multiple of five: the value is divided by five, raised to the next whole number if not already whole, and multiplied by five again. This applies to the per-domain service-minute recommendation and to a provider's travel overhead. Where a computation both scales a value and rounds it, the order of those two steps is pinned explicitly in §3 and matters to the answer.

**Anniversary dates.** Adding a whole number of years to a date lands on the same month and day of the later year, except that a February 29 birth date maps to March 1. This governs both `third_birthday` and the fifth birthday behind `kindergarten_eligibility_date`. Adding a number of days to a date is plain calendar-day addition; weekends and holidays are included, and no business-day adjustment is ever applied.

### 2.9. Cascades and Compensating Recomputation

Many operations in this system mutate more than one record. Two disciplines govern that.

The first is **cascade completeness**. When a parent record reaches a state that invalidates its children, every affected child is transitioned in the same request, and every rollup derived from those children is recomputed in the same request. The deepest such chain runs five records deep: revoking an evaluation consent moves the consent record, then the referral's two state dimensions, then the referral's live evaluation case, then that case's scheduled and completed domain assessments, and finally the chairing provider's evaluation-caseload rollup and the child's own derived status. Each record touched has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented.

The second is **compensating exactness**. Two quantities in this system are conserved: a community site's `reserved_iep_seat_count` and a provider's assigned weekly minutes. Whenever the action that consumed the quantity is reversed — a service plan leaving `PENDING_PARENT_CONSENT` for `DECLINED`, a plan exiting to kindergarten, a plan being superseded, or a service line being released from its provider — the quantity is recomputed from the current set of contributing rows rather than adjusted by a remembered delta. The consequence, and the requirement, is that releasing a provider from a line restores that provider's minute figures and capacity posture to exactly the values they held before the line was staffed, with no residue.

### 2.10. Pinned Rules and Their Reported Alternatives

Four rules in this domain admit a second defensible reading, and for each of them the API pins one reading for persistence and enforcement while exposing the other as read-only reporting. The evaluation deadline is pinned to the consent-anchored reading; the composite eligibility recommendation is pinned to whichever of the two method-weighting variants the case's `assessment_protocol` selects; the provider minute ledger is pinned to the projected reading that counts draft plans; and the service-minute recommendation is pinned to rounding each domain's minutes before summing them. Three derived read-only views — one on a referral, one on an evaluation case, one on a provider — report both readings side by side. Only the pinned value is ever persisted on an entity, ever compared against a ceiling, or ever used to decide a state transition. The reported alternative exists so that a compliance officer can see the size of the disagreement; it never changes behaviour.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Building the Roster: Children, Community Sites and Providers

Nothing else in the system can happen until a child exists in the child find roster. A `ChildFindCoordinator` (or a `DistrictAdmin`) registers one by supplying `first_name` and `last_name`, each non-empty and at most 80 characters; a `date_of_birth` as a plain calendar date in year-month-day form, which must fall strictly before the logical clock's current date; the `guardian_full_name` of the parent or guardian holding educational rights, non-empty; and a `district_of_residence` code, non-empty and at most 20 characters, because the child find obligation follows residence rather than enrolment. Three further inputs are optional. `primary_language_code` is the lowercase two-letter language of the home and defaults to `en`. `part_c_enrolled` states whether the child is currently served under early intervention and defaults to false. `part_c_exit_date` is the planned early-intervention exit date, a plain calendar date, defaulting to absent — but it is **required** whenever `part_c_enrolled` is true, and a registration claiming early-intervention enrolment without it is a request-shape violation.

Registration derives four values immediately. `interpreter_required` is set true exactly when `primary_language_code` is not `en`. `third_birthday` is the child's date of birth advanced by three years under the anniversary convention of §2.8, and it is the anchor of the Part B deadline cap. `kindergarten_eligibility_date` is computed by first advancing the date of birth by five years to obtain the fifth birthday, then taking September 1 of the year in which that fifth birthday falls: if the fifth birthday is on or before that September 1, the eligibility date is that same September 1; otherwise it is September 1 of the following year. A fifth birthday landing exactly on September 1 therefore uses that same September 1, while September 2 pushes the child to the next school year. Finally, the child's derived `status` is initialised to `NO_ACTIVE_REFERRAL`. Children can be listed and filtered by `district_of_residence`, `primary_language_code`, `part_c_enrolled` and `status`.

A `PlacementAdministrator` registers each **community preschool site** the district may place children into and, separately, may use as a screening venue. A site carries a `name`, a `region_code` naming the travel region it belongs to (non-empty, at most 20 characters), a `licensed_capacity` of at least 1 describing total licensed enrolment, and an `iep_seat_capacity` of at least 1 that may not exceed the licensed capacity, describing how many of those places are reserved for children with an IEP. Optionally the registration supplies `languages_supported`, the list of two-letter codes the site can serve without external interpretation, defaulting to a single entry of `en`; `screening_host_approved`, defaulting to false, stating whether the site may host child find screening events; and `status`, which is either `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE` and defaults to `ACTIVE`. The live seat counter `reserved_iep_seat_count` is initialised to 0 and thereafter maintained by the system alone. Sites can be listed and filtered by `region_code`, `screening_host_approved` and `status`.

Only a `DistrictAdmin` may register staff. A `Provider` record carries a `full_name`; a `discipline` drawn from `SCHOOL_PSYCHOLOGIST`, `SLP`, `OT`, `PT`, `ECSE_TEACHER`, `SOCIAL_WORKER` and `INTERPRETER`; a `home_region_code` naming where the provider is based (non-empty, at most 20 characters); an optional `languages` list of two-letter codes the provider can deliver service or interpretation in, defaulting to a single entry of `en`; and an optional `status` of `ACTIVE`, `ON_LEAVE` or `INACTIVE`, defaulting to `ACTIVE`. Three optional ceilings may be set at registration: `max_weekly_service_minutes`, a hard ceiling on assigned weekly minutes that must be a non-negative multiple of 5 and defaults to 1500 — that is, twenty-five hours a week; `max_evaluation_caseload`, a non-negative ceiling on concurrently open evaluation cases, defaulting to 12; and `max_weekly_interpreter_assignments`, a non-negative ceiling on open interpreter assignments that defaults to 0 and is only meaningful for a provider whose discipline is `INTERPRETER` — which means that an interpreter registered without an explicit ceiling can be named on no notices at all.

Registration derives `is_evaluation_chair_eligible` as true exactly when the provider's `discipline` is `SCHOOL_PSYCHOLOGIST` or `ECSE_TEACHER`, and false for every other discipline. It also initialises both capacity dimensions to their empty state: `direct_weekly_service_minutes`, `travel_overhead_minutes` and `assigned_weekly_service_minutes` all at 0 with `service_capacity_posture` at `OPEN`, and `active_evaluation_count` at 0 with `evaluation_intake_posture` at `OPEN`, alongside `assigned_interpreter_assignment_count` at 0. A `DistrictAdmin` may later amend a provider's `status`, `languages`, `max_weekly_service_minutes`, `max_evaluation_caseload` or `max_weekly_interpreter_assignments` — this is how a provider is placed `ON_LEAVE` — and any such amendment re-derives both capacity postures against the new ceilings and increments the provider's `version`. Providers can be listed and filtered by `discipline`, `home_region_code`, `service_capacity_posture`, `evaluation_intake_posture` and `status`. A provider whose `status` is `ON_LEAVE` or `INACTIVE` may not be given a new chair assignment, a new domain assessment, a new service line, or a new interpreter assignment; each such attempt is refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_ACTIVE`.

### 3.2. Community Outreach: Screening Events and Appointment Slots

A `ChildFindCoordinator` schedules a **screening event** — a community child find day — by naming the host `site_id`, a public `event_label` used in outreach materials, and the event's occupation window on that site: `window_start_utc`, inclusive, and `window_end_utc`, exclusive, both ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits, with the end strictly after the start. The coordinator declares a `slot_capacity` of at least 1, the maximum number of children staff are willing to see, and may declare a `slot_length_minutes` giving the fixed duration of every appointment in the event; that length must be a multiple of 5 between 10 and 60 inclusive, and it defaults to 20. Optionally the coordinator lists `interpreter_languages`, the two-letter codes with on-site interpreter coverage during the event, defaulting to an empty list. The host site must be `ACTIVE` **and** have `screening_host_approved` true; a site failing either facet is refused with `ERR_SITE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.

The declared capacity is not necessarily the binding one. On creation the system computes `effective_slot_capacity` as the lesser of the declared `slot_capacity` and the number of whole appointments the window can physically hold — that is, the whole number of minutes between `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc` divided by `slot_length_minutes` and truncated downward, never rounded up. A coordinator who declares thirty places in a two-hour window with twenty-minute appointments therefore gets six, silently, and the binding limit becomes the window rather than the declaration. The live counter `booked_slot_count` is the number of this event's appointments whose status is `BOOKED`, `ATTENDED` or `NO_SHOW`; cancelled appointments are excluded and free their capacity again. Both figures are recomputed on every booking and every cancellation, and both are re-derived on read.

Sites cannot be double-booked. A new event's window must not overlap the window of any other non-cancelled event at the same site, where two windows conflict exactly when the new start falls before the existing end **and** the existing start falls before the new end. No buffer is applied, so exact adjacency — a new event starting at the precise instant an existing one ends — is permitted, and cancelled events are ignored entirely. A conflict is `ERR_SITE_EVENT_OVERLAP`. Events can be listed and filtered by `site_id` and `status`.

A new event opens at `SCHEDULED` and advances through the coordinator's status endpoint, which takes a single `target_status` input. `SCHEDULED → OPEN` requires the host site still to be `ACTIVE` with `screening_host_approved` true and requires the logical clock to be earlier than `window_end_utc`; only an `OPEN` event accepts bookings. `OPEN → CLOSED` has no precondition beyond the from-state, and once an event is `CLOSED` further booking attempts are refused with `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE`. `CLOSED → COMPLETED` is permitted only when every appointment of the event has reached `ATTENDED`, `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED`; if even one is still `BOOKED` the completion is refused with `ERR_SCREENING_SLOTS_UNRESOLVED`. Cancellation is reachable from `SCHEDULED`, from `OPEN` and from `CLOSED`, in each case with no precondition beyond the from-state. Cancelling from `SCHEDULED` simply sets the event to `CANCELLED`. Cancelling from `OPEN` or from `CLOSED` additionally performs a **conditional** cascade: every appointment of that event still in `BOOKED` moves to `CANCELLED`, while appointments already `ATTENDED`, `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED` are left untouched, and `booked_slot_count` is recomputed and so drops to the number of attended and no-show appointments. Every one of these status changes increments the event's `version`. `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

Booking an appointment names the `child_id` to be seen, the `slot_start_utc` at which the appointment begins, and the `expected_screening_event_version` described in §2.6; optionally it names the `requested_language_code` the family asked for, defaulting to `en`. The appointment's `slot_end_utc` is derived, never supplied: it is `slot_start_utc` advanced by the parent event's `slot_length_minutes`. The appointment must fall entirely inside the event window — its `slot_start_utc` at or after `window_start_utc`, and its start plus the event's slot length at or before `window_end_utc` — or the booking is refused with `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_EVENT_WINDOW`. Booking into an event that is not `OPEN` is `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE`. When `booked_slot_count` has already reached `effective_slot_capacity`, the next booking is refused with `ERR_SCREENING_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; equality is enough to refuse, so the counter never exceeds the effective capacity. A successful booking creates the appointment at `BOOKED`, recomputes the parent event's `booked_slot_count`, and increments the parent event's `version`. Appointments can be listed and filtered by `screening_event_id`, by `child_id`, by `screening_result` and by `status`.

Attendance is recorded through the appointment's outcome endpoint, which takes an `attendance` value and, for an attended appointment, a `screening_result`. The parent event must be `OPEN` or `CLOSED` for an attendance record to be accepted, and the appointment itself must still be `BOOKED` — recording an outcome twice is `ERR_INVALID_SLOT_STATE`. `BOOKED → ATTENDED` requires a `screening_result` of `PASS`, `REFER` or `RESCREEN`; omitting it or supplying anything else is a request-shape violation. `BOOKED → NO_SHOW` leaves `screening_result` unset. `BOOKED → CANCELLED` has no precondition beyond the from-state and recomputes the parent event's `booked_slot_count`, since a cancelled appointment leaves the count. Each of the three increments the appointment's `version`, and each re-derives the child's own status. `ATTENDED`, `NO_SHOW` and `CANCELLED` are terminal for an appointment.

One outcome has a consequence that reaches outside the screening world. When an appointment is recorded as `ATTENDED` with a `screening_result` of `REFER`, the system itself opens a referral for that appointment's child, with `source` set to `SCREENING`, `originating_screening_slot_id` set to the appointment, and `received_at` stamped from the logical clock. That referral then runs the whole intake derivation of §3.3, including the possibility of being immediately declined on age grounds. Nothing in the response body of the outcome call names the new referral; it is discoverable by filtering referrals on `originating_screening_slot_id`.

### 3.3. Referral Intake, Age Pathways, and Displacement by a Later Referral

Referrals arrive from five directions, and the `source` field records which: `PEDIATRICIAN` for a physician's referral, `PART_C_TRANSITION` for a child aging out of early intervention, `SCREENING` for one the district's own screening day generated, `PARENT` for a family's self-referral, and `SCHOOL` for one raised by a school. When the source is `PEDIATRICIAN` the referring physician's national provider identifier must be supplied as `referring_provider_npi`, exactly ten digits; for other sources it may be omitted, and whenever it is present it must still be exactly ten digits. Intake also names the `child_id` the referral concerns, and may carry an `originating_screening_slot_id` pointing at the appointment behind it. `received_at` is never supplied by the caller — the system stamps it from the logical clock at the instant of intake, and it is that instant that fixes the child's age at referral.

From `received_at` and the child's `date_of_birth` the system computes `age_months_at_referral` as whole completed months, and from that number it selects the statutory timeline that will govern the referral, recorded as `eligibility_pathway`. Part B preschool eligibility spans 33 through 71 completed months, both bounds inclusive: a child of exactly 33 months is in scope and one of 32 is not, and a child of exactly 71 months is in scope while one of 72 is not. Outside that span the pathway is `AGE_INELIGIBLE`. Within it, a child whose `part_c_enrolled` is true and who is no more than 35 completed months old takes the `PART_C_TRANSITION_DEADLINE` pathway — the one whose deadline is truncated by the third birthday — while everyone else, including an early-intervention child who has already reached 36 months, takes the `STANDARD_60_DAY` pathway. The 35-month bound is inclusive: exactly 35 months with early-intervention enrolment takes the Part C pathway; 36 months takes the standard pathway even when `part_c_enrolled` is true.

The age rule bites differently depending on who raised the referral. A coordinator's manual intake whose computed pathway is `AGE_INELIGIBLE` is **refused** with `ERR_AGE_INELIGIBLE` and no referral row is written at all; the district cannot open an evaluation for a child outside the preschool span. A referral the system generated itself behind a `REFER` screening outcome is instead written, and then immediately moved by the system from `RECEIVED` to `DECLINED` on its intake dimension, with its consent dimension left at `NOT_REQUESTED` and its `version` incremented — the screening encounter happened and must remain on the record even though no evaluation can follow it.

A referral carries **two orthogonal state dimensions on the same row**, and understanding that is the key to everything in §3.4. `intake_status` tracks the intake workflow and runs `RECEIVED`, `CONSENT_PENDING`, `EVALUATION_AUTHORIZED`, `DECLINED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `SUPERSEDED`, initialised to `RECEIVED`, with `DECLINED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `SUPERSEDED` terminal. `consent_status` tracks the parental consent position and runs `NOT_REQUESTED`, `REQUESTED`, `GRANTED`, `REFUSED` and `REVOKED`, initialised to `NOT_REQUESTED`, with `REFUSED` and `REVOKED` terminal. The two advance independently in the sense that they are separate enumerations with separate legal transitions; but every consent event drives both of them in the same request. A referral may legitimately sit at `intake_status` of `CONSENT_PENDING` with `consent_status` of `REQUESTED`. The consent dimension is only ever moved off `NOT_REQUESTED` by the issuance of a notice, and only ever moved from `REQUESTED` to `GRANTED` or `REFUSED`, or from `GRANTED` to `REVOKED`, by a decision on the referral's evaluation-consent record. Decisions on a **service**-consent record never touch this dimension; they drive the service plan instead.

Only one referral per child may be live at a time, and the newest wins. When a new referral is created for a child — whether by a coordinator or by the system behind a screening outcome — every earlier referral of that same child whose `intake_status` is `RECEIVED`, `CONSENT_PENDING` or `EVALUATION_AUTHORIZED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED`, with `superseded_by_referral_id` set to the new referral's identifier and its `version` incremented. That displacement is not uniform across the three from-states. Displacing a referral from `RECEIVED` does nothing further. Displacing one from `CONSENT_PENDING` additionally moves any consent record on that referral whose `decision` is still `PENDING` to `REFUSED` — the family's undecided notice is closed out rather than left dangling. Displacing one from `EVALUATION_AUTHORIZED` additionally cancels that referral's evaluation case, provided that case has not already reached `ELIGIBILITY_DETERMINED` or `CANCELLED`, which in turn triggers the assessment and provider-rollup chain described in §3.5. After intake the child's own derived `status` is re-evaluated and, in the ordinary case, becomes `REFERRED`.

Once the family grants consent, the referral acquires a **pinned statutory deadline**. `consent_granted_at` records the logical-clock instant of the grant, and `evaluation_due_date` is computed from it and then persisted; until consent is granted, `evaluation_due_date` is absent, and a referral without a granted consent is excluded from every compliance aggregate. The pinned rule — the consent-anchored reading — takes the calendar date of `consent_granted_at` and adds 60 calendar days, weekends and holidays included. For a referral on the `PART_C_TRANSITION_DEADLINE` pathway only, the result is then capped: `evaluation_due_date` becomes the **earlier** of that consent-anchored date and the child's `third_birthday`, so that the child's IEP is in place by the third birthday even when consent arrived late. For a referral on the `STANDARD_60_DAY` pathway the consent-anchored date stands uncapped. Alongside it the system can compute, but never persists on the referral, the receipt-anchored reading: the calendar date of `received_at` plus the same 60 calendar days, with **no** third-birthday cap. Whenever consent lagged receipt, that companion figure is earlier than the pinned one, and it can even fall before it.

Both readings are laid out side by side on the referral's compliance view, a read-only endpoint that returns `referral_id`, the referral's `eligibility_pathway`, its `received_date` and `consent_granted_date` as plain calendar dates, the `receipt_anchored_due_date` and `consent_anchored_due_date` as computed above, the `third_birthday_cap_date` that the Part C pathway would apply, the pinned `evaluation_due_date` actually in force, the `days_remaining` until it, and the `timeline_compliance` posture of §3.5. Referrals can be listed and filtered by `child_id`, by `originating_screening_slot_id`, by `superseded_by_referral_id`, by `source`, by `eligibility_pathway`, by `consent_status` and by `intake_status`.

### 3.4. Prior Written Notice and the Two Consent Dimensions

Before the district may assess a child it must issue prior written notice and obtain written parental consent. A `ChildFindCoordinator` issues that packet against a referral, supplying the `notice_language_code` the notice was written in, a `notice_delivery_method` of `HAND_DELIVERY`, `MAIL`, `EMAIL` or `INTERPRETED_ORAL`, and — where language access requires it — an `interpreter_provider_id` naming the interpreter of record together with an `interpreter_modality` of `IN_PERSON`, `TELEPHONIC` or `VIDEO`. The issuance creates a `ConsentRecord` whose `kind` is `EVALUATION_CONSENT`; `kind` is set by the issuing endpoint and never by the client. The new record's `decision` starts at `PENDING`, its `requested_at` is stamped from the logical clock, its `decided_at` is absent, and its `service_plan_id` is absent because that field is populated only on service-consent records.

Issuance is guarded by four rules beyond the ladder's generic rungs, and their relative order matters. The referral's pathway must not be `AGE_INELIGIBLE`. A referral that **already** holds an evaluation-consent record whose `decision` is `PENDING` or `GRANTED` is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_REQUESTED`, and this refusal pre-empts the generic own-state rule — this is carve-out A of §2.5 rung 11. The practical effect is that re-issuing a notice while the family is still deciding reports `ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_REQUESTED`, and so does asking again after consent has already been granted; whereas a referral whose only record is `REFUSED` or `REVOKED` holds no blocking record at all, falls through to the own-state rule, and reports `ERR_INVALID_REFERRAL_STATE` for its `DECLINED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `SUPERSEDED` intake state. Third, the language-access rule of §2.7 applies in full. Fourth — and only after language access has passed — the named interpreter's own availability ceiling is tested: an interpreter whose `assigned_interpreter_assignment_count` has already reached `max_weekly_interpreter_assignments` is refused with `ERR_INTERPRETER_UNAVAILABLE`. Separately, the case chair may not be named as interpreter of record on the same case's notices, which is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and an interpreter whose `status` is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_ACTIVE`.

A successful issuance moves both dimensions of the referral in the same request: `intake_status` advances `RECEIVED → CONSENT_PENDING` and `consent_status` advances `NOT_REQUESTED → REQUESTED`. The named interpreter's `assigned_interpreter_assignment_count` is recomputed, and the referral's `version` is incremented. That count is, by definition, the number of consent records naming that provider as interpreter whose `decision` is `PENDING` or `GRANTED`; records that have been `REFUSED` or `REVOKED` no longer occupy an assignment.

The family answers through the consent record's decision endpoint, which takes a single `decision` input. Either a `Parent` or a `ChildFindCoordinator` may record it, and when the caller is a `Parent` the guardian check of §2.5 rung 6 requires `X-User-ID` to equal the identifier of the child reached through the record's `referral_id`, else `ERR_NOT_CHILD_GUARDIAN`. A decision on a record whose current `decision` does not permit it — granting an already-granted record, or any decision on a record that is `REFUSED` or `REVOKED` — is `ERR_INVALID_CONSENT_STATE`. Every accepted decision stamps `decided_at` from the logical clock and increments the record's `version`.

For an evaluation-consent record the three decisions have three quite different consequences. `PENDING → GRANTED` moves the referral's `intake_status` `CONSENT_PENDING → EVALUATION_AUTHORIZED` and its `consent_status` `REQUESTED → GRANTED`, sets `consent_granted_at` to the logical clock, and computes and pins `evaluation_due_date` exactly as §3.3 describes. `PENDING → REFUSED` moves `intake_status` `CONSENT_PENDING → DECLINED` and `consent_status` `REQUESTED → REFUSED`, leaves `evaluation_due_date` absent, and recomputes the named interpreter's `assigned_interpreter_assignment_count`, since a refused record no longer occupies an assignment. `GRANTED → REVOKED` is permitted only on an evaluation-consent record — attempting it on a service-consent record is `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_REVOCABLE` — and moves `intake_status` `EVALUATION_AUTHORIZED → WITHDRAWN` and `consent_status` `GRANTED → REVOKED`, while the already-pinned `evaluation_due_date` is retained as the historical deadline rather than cleared. Revocation also recomputes the interpreter's assignment count, and it starts the deepest cascade in the system: the referral's evaluation case, if its status is anything other than `ELIGIBILITY_DETERMINED` or `CANCELLED`, is cancelled, which invalidates that case's live assessments and re-derives the chairing provider's evaluation rollups and the child's status, as §3.5 sets out. `GRANTED` is thus non-terminal for an evaluation consent and absorbing for a service consent; `REFUSED` and `REVOKED` are terminal for either kind.

Evaluation activity may not begin until the consent dimension actually reads `GRANTED`, and this is the one refusal the system reports for every unconsented referral. Opening an evaluation case against a referral whose `consent_status` is `NOT_REQUESTED`, `REQUESTED`, `REFUSED` or `REVOKED` is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_GRANTED` — not with a state error — regardless of what the intake dimension currently reads. This is carve-out B of §2.5 rung 11: on evaluation-case creation the intake dimension is inspected **only** when `consent_status` is `GRANTED`, and the single state pair the consent dimension cannot explain is a referral whose consent is `GRANTED` but whose `intake_status` has since become `SUPERSEDED` by a newer referral for the same child, which alone yields `ERR_INVALID_REFERRAL_STATE` on that endpoint. Consent records can be listed and filtered by `referral_id`, by `interpreter_provider_id`, by `service_plan_id`, by `kind` and by `decision`.

### 3.5. Opening and Running the Multidisciplinary Evaluation

With consent granted, a `ChildFindCoordinator` opens the evaluation by naming a `chair_provider_id` and, optionally, an `assessment_protocol` of `STANDARDIZED` or `PLAY_BASED`. The protocol is the single most consequential choice on the case, because it pins which method-weighting variant the composite recommendation of §3.6 will use. Three referential rules guard the opening. The named provider must be chair-eligible — its `discipline` must be `SCHOOL_PSYCHOLOGIST` or `ECSE_TEACHER`, which is exactly what `is_evaluation_chair_eligible` records — or the request is refused with `ERR_CHAIR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. The named provider's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_ACTIVE`. And the chair must have room: a chair whose `active_evaluation_count` has **already reached** its `max_evaluation_caseload` before the new case is written is refused with `ERR_EVALUATION_CASELOAD_EXCEEDED`, so a chair sitting at exactly the ceiling takes no further case.

A provider's `active_evaluation_count` is the number of evaluation cases that provider chairs whose `status` is one of the five non-terminal values `PLANNED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `DOMAINS_COMPLETE`, `REPORT_DRAFTED` and `REVISIONS_REQUESTED`. Cases at `ELIGIBILITY_DETERMINED` or `CANCELLED` are excluded, which is precisely why cancelling a case frees capacity. From that count `evaluation_intake_posture` follows: it reads `AT_CAPACITY` when the count has reached or passed `max_evaluation_caseload`, and `OPEN` otherwise. This posture and the minute posture of §3.8 are entirely independent; a provider can be `AT_CAPACITY` on evaluations and `OPEN` on service minutes at the same time.

A new case is written at `PLANNED` with `revision_round` at 0 and `required_domain_count` fixed by the system at 5 — the five developmental domains the team must cover. The case copies `child_id` from its referral, immutable thereafter, and denormalises `due_date` from the referral's `evaluation_due_date`; that copy is re-derived whenever the referral's consent position changes. Because a case can only exist behind a granted consent, a case's `due_date` is never absent. Opening the case recomputes the chair's evaluation rollups, computes `timeline_compliance` against the logical clock, and re-derives the child's own `status` to `IN_EVALUATION`.

`timeline_compliance` is the case's deadline posture and takes one of `ON_TIME`, `AT_RISK` and `OVERDUE`. For a case still in flight — that is, in any of the five non-terminal statuses — the system takes the whole number of calendar days from the logical clock's current date to `due_date`: a negative number means the deadline has passed and the posture is `OVERDUE`; a number of 10 or fewer means `AT_RISK`; anything larger means `ON_TIME`. Ten days remaining is therefore `AT_RISK` while eleven is `ON_TIME`, and a case due today, with zero days remaining, is `AT_RISK`, while one day past due is `OVERDUE`. For a case that has left the in-flight set, the posture freezes: a `CANCELLED` case is always `ON_TIME`, and an `ELIGIBILITY_DETERMINED` case is `ON_TIME` when the date of its determination was on or before `due_date`, and `OVERDUE` when it was later. Because the posture reads the logical clock, moving the clock re-derives it for every case on the next read. Cases can be listed and filtered by `referral_id`, `child_id`, `chair_provider_id`, `assessment_protocol`, `timeline_compliance` and `status`.

Scheduling a developmental-domain assessment is done by the chair or by a coordinator, naming the `evaluator_provider_id` who will administer it, the `domain` being assessed from `COGNITIVE`, `COMMUNICATION`, `PHYSICAL_MOTOR`, `SOCIAL_EMOTIONAL` and `ADAPTIVE`, the `method` from `STANDARDIZED`, `PLAY_BASED`, `OBSERVATION` and `PARENT_REPORT`, and the evaluator's occupation window as `window_start_utc` (inclusive) and `window_end_utc` (exclusive), both ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits and the end strictly after the start. An `assessment_site_id` may be supplied to say where the assessment takes place; leaving it absent means the district assessment centre. Scheduling against a case that is `CANCELLED` or `ELIGIBILITY_DETERMINED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`.

Four rules then apply, in the ladder order of §2.5. **Segregation of duties** comes first among them: the evaluator of a domain assessment may not be the chair of the same case, so a request whose `evaluator_provider_id` equals the case's `chair_provider_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The evaluator's `status` must be `ACTIVE`. The evaluator's discipline must be able to assess the requested domain, or the request is refused with `ERR_DISCIPLINE_MISMATCH`: `COMMUNICATION` may be assessed by an `SLP` or an `ECSE_TEACHER`; `PHYSICAL_MOTOR` by an `OT` or a `PT`; `COGNITIVE` by a `SCHOOL_PSYCHOLOGIST` or an `ECSE_TEACHER`; `SOCIAL_EMOTIONAL` by a `SOCIAL_WORKER` or a `SCHOOL_PSYCHOLOGIST`; and `ADAPTIVE` by a provider of any discipline other than `INTERPRETER`. Then the case may not already hold a `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` assessment of the same `domain`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DOMAIN_ASSESSMENT`; assessments that have been `INVALIDATED` do not block, which is exactly how a domain is re-assessed after a spoiled administration.

Evaluators cannot be in two places at once, and crossing town costs time. A new assessment window conflicts with an existing `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` assessment of the **same** evaluator when the new start falls before the existing end plus a buffer **and** the existing start falls before the new end plus the same buffer. That buffer is zero when both assessments are at the same location, so genuinely back-to-back appointments in one building are legal; it is 30 minutes whenever the two are at different locations, where an absent `assessment_site_id` normalises to the district assessment centre for the purposes of that comparison. A conflict is refused with `ERR_EVALUATOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, and `INVALIDATED` assessments are ignored entirely by the comparison.

A scheduled assessment is written at `SCHEDULED`, with `chronological_age_months` computed as the whole completed months between the child's `date_of_birth` and the calendar date of `window_start_utc`, and with `age_equivalent_months`, `standard_score`, `percent_delay`, `weighted_percent_delay`, `completed_at` and `invalidation_reason` all absent and `qualifies_delay` false. The first assessment scheduled on a case moves the case `PLANNED → IN_PROGRESS`. Assessments can be listed and filtered by `evaluation_case_id`, `evaluator_provider_id`, `assessment_site_id`, `domain`, `method` and `status`.

The assigned evaluator — or the case chair — records the result, supplying `age_equivalent_months`, the child's developmental age equivalent in months, and optionally a `standard_score`, the norm-referenced score that accompanies a standardized administration and which must not be negative. `age_equivalent_months` is required at completion, must not be negative, and must not exceed the assessment's own `chronological_age_months`; a larger value is a request-shape violation. The parent case must be `IN_PROGRESS` or `DOMAINS_COMPLETE` for a completion to be accepted, and completing an assessment that is already `COMPLETED` or `INVALIDATED` is `ERR_INVALID_ASSESSMENT_STATE`. On acceptance the assessment moves `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`, `completed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the delay figures of §3.6 are computed, the parent case's `completed_domain_count`, `qualifying_domain_count`, `max_weighted_percent_delay`, `composite_eligibility_recommendation` and `recommended_weekly_minutes` are all recomputed, the assessment's `version` is incremented, and — once five distinct domains stand `COMPLETED` on the case — the case itself moves `IN_PROGRESS → DOMAINS_COMPLETE` and increments its own `version`.

An assessment whose administration was compromised can be invalidated by the case chair or by its own assigned evaluator, who supplies an `invalidation_reason` that is recorded on the row. Both `SCHEDULED → INVALIDATED` and `COMPLETED → INVALIDATED` are available, and `INVALIDATED` is terminal. Invalidating a `COMPLETED` assessment is additionally refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE` when the parent case has already reached `ELIGIBILITY_DETERMINED`; a determined case's evidentiary record is closed. Invalidation from `SCHEDULED` sets the status and reason and recomputes the evaluator provider's `active_evaluation_count`, which in turn re-derives that provider's `evaluation_intake_posture`. Invalidation from `COMPLETED` does the same and, in addition, retains `percent_delay`, `weighted_percent_delay` and `qualifies_delay` on the row as historical values while removing the row from every case aggregate, then recomputes the parent case's aggregates.

That recomputation has one — and only one — reversion edge. When the invalidation drops the case's `completed_domain_count` below `required_domain_count` and the case was standing at `DOMAINS_COMPLETE`, the case reverts `DOMAINS_COMPLETE → IN_PROGRESS`, its `composite_eligibility_recommendation` returns to `INDETERMINATE`, and its `version` increments. When the case was standing at `REPORT_DRAFTED` or `REVISIONS_REQUESTED`, the status is deliberately left untouched — the drafted report is not retracted — even though `completed_domain_count` has now fallen below five. That asymmetry is the only reachable way for a later report draft or determination to be refused with `ERR_DOMAINS_INCOMPLETE`.

Cancellation of a case is never requested directly; it arrives as a cascade from the referral, either because evaluation consent was revoked or because a newer referral displaced the old one. A case in any of `PLANNED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `DOMAINS_COMPLETE`, `REPORT_DRAFTED` and `REVISIONS_REQUESTED` moves to `CANCELLED` and increments its `version`. From `PLANNED` there is nothing further to do. From each of the other four, every assessment of the case standing at `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` moves to `INVALIDATED`, with an `invalidation_reason` recorded as `CASE_CANCELLED`, and each affected evaluator provider's `active_evaluation_count` and `evaluation_intake_posture` are re-derived. `CANCELLED` is the case's only terminal status; `ELIGIBILITY_DETERMINED` is absorbing except for the single self-loop described in §3.7.

### 3.6. Delay Arithmetic, the Composite Profile, and the Minute Recommendation

Each completed assessment yields a raw measure of developmental delay. `percent_delay` is the shortfall between the child's chronological age and the measured developmental age, expressed as a percentage of chronological age: take `chronological_age_months` less `age_equivalent_months`, multiply by one hundred, divide by `chronological_age_months`, and round the quotient half up to one decimal place. A child whose developmental age equals their chronological age therefore has a `percent_delay` of exactly 0.0.

The raw figure is then discounted according to the **method** used and the variant the case has pinned, and that discounted figure — `weighted_percent_delay` — is what every eligibility rule actually reads. Under the strict standardized variant, only assessments whose `method` is `STANDARDIZED` count at all, and they count at full weight; `PLAY_BASED`, `OBSERVATION` and `PARENT_REPORT` results are excluded outright. Under the inclusive play-based variant, `STANDARDIZED` counts at full weight, `PLAY_BASED` and `OBSERVATION` each count at a nine-tenths confidence discount, and `PARENT_REPORT` is still excluded. A case pins the strict variant when its `assessment_protocol` is `STANDARDIZED` and the inclusive variant when its `assessment_protocol` is `PLAY_BASED`.

When the pinned variant excludes an assessment's method, that assessment's `weighted_percent_delay` is left absent and its `qualifies_delay` is false. Otherwise the order of operations is pinned and matters: the already-rounded one-decimal `percent_delay` is multiplied by the method weight, and the product is rounded half up to one decimal place again. The unrounded quotient is never weighted. `qualifies_delay` is then true exactly when `weighted_percent_delay` is present and has reached 25.0 percent; the threshold is inclusive, so 25.0 qualifies and 24.9 does not.

At the case level those per-assessment figures roll up into a **composite profile**, and the roll-up reads only assessments of the case whose `status` is `COMPLETED`. Assessments still `SCHEDULED`, and assessments that have been `INVALIDATED`, contribute nothing — not to the counts, not to the entering set, and not to the maximum. `completed_domain_count` is the number of **distinct** `domain` values among the completed assessments. The set entering the delay profile is then narrowed by the pinned variant: under the strict standardized variant it is the completed assessments whose `method` is `STANDARDIZED`; under the inclusive play-based variant it is the completed assessments whose `method` is anything other than `PARENT_REPORT`. `qualifying_domain_count` is the number of distinct domains among the entering assessments whose `weighted_percent_delay` has reached 25.0 percent, and `max_weighted_percent_delay` is the largest `weighted_percent_delay` among the entering assessments — absent, never 0.0, when nothing enters. No time window and no ordering is involved in the roll-up, so no tie-break is needed.

From those three numbers `composite_eligibility_recommendation` follows by a three-step ladder. If `completed_domain_count` is below five, the recommendation is `INDETERMINATE` regardless of anything else. Otherwise, if `max_weighted_percent_delay` is present and has reached 33.0 percent — a single domain severe enough to stand on its own — **or** `qualifying_domain_count` has reached 2 — two or more domains each at or above the 25.0 threshold — the recommendation is `ELIGIBLE_RECOMMENDED`. Failing both, it is `NOT_ELIGIBLE_RECOMMENDED`. The consequence of the variant choice is visible here: on one and the same set of assessment rows, the inclusive variant can reach `ELIGIBLE_RECOMMENDED` where the strict variant reaches `NOT_ELIGIBLE_RECOMMENDED`, and a case whose delays were captured entirely through play-based work can show a `qualifying_domain_count` of 0 under the strict variant.

The same qualifying set drives the district's service-minute recommendation. Consider the qualifying domains — those entering assessments whose `weighted_percent_delay` has reached 25.0 percent. The recommendation allocates exactly **one line per distinct domain**; where two assessments of the same domain both qualify, the representative is the one with the greater `weighted_percent_delay`, and if those are equal, the one created earlier. Each domain's representative percentage selects a severity multiplier: a percentage that has reached 50.0 takes a multiplier of 1.4; one that has not reached 50.0 but has reached 33.0 takes 1.15; anything below 33.0 takes 1.0. Both band boundaries are inclusive lower bounds, so exactly 33.0 takes 1.15 while 32.9 takes 1.0, and exactly 50.0 takes 1.4. A base allocation of 50 minutes per qualifying domain is multiplied by that domain's multiplier in exact decimal, and the product is rounded **up to the next multiple of five for that domain alone**. Only then are the rounded per-domain figures added together to give `recommended_weekly_minutes`. When no domain qualifies, the recommendation is 0.

That rounding order is pinned and is not interchangeable with the alternative of summing the raw per-domain figures and rounding once at the end; the alternative yields a smaller total whenever the accumulated per-domain slack reaches five minutes. Two qualifying domains at 33.3 percent and 36.1 percent, for instance, both take the 1.15 multiplier and so both produce a raw allocation of 57.5 minutes: rounding each up to 60 and then adding gives 120 minutes, whereas adding first to 115.0 and rounding once gives 115. The pinned answer is 120.

A read-only eligibility view on the case makes the whole calculation auditable and reports both variants side by side. It returns `evaluation_case_id`; the `pinned_variant` the case's protocol selected; `completed_domain_count`; then, for the strict standardized reading, `standardized_strict_qualifying_domain_count`, `standardized_strict_max_weighted_percent_delay` and `standardized_strict_recommendation`; then, for the inclusive play-based reading, `play_based_inclusive_qualifying_domain_count`, `play_based_inclusive_max_weighted_percent_delay` and `play_based_inclusive_recommendation`; then the `composite_eligibility_recommendation` and `recommended_weekly_minutes` actually in force under the pinned variant; and finally both minute-rounding orders as `per_line_rounded_minutes` — the pinned figure — and `sum_then_rounded_minutes` — the discarded alternative — so that the divergence between them is observable rather than theoretical.

### 3.7. Determination, Report Drafting and the Revision Loop

Before eligibility can be determined the chair must draft the multidisciplinary evaluation report, supplying a `report_narrative_summary`. Only the case's own chair may draft it. Drafting requires all five distinct domains to stand `COMPLETED` — a case whose `completed_domain_count` falls short is refused with `ERR_DOMAINS_INCOMPLETE` — and drafting from any status other than `DOMAINS_COMPLETE` or `REVISIONS_REQUESTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. A successful draft from `DOMAINS_COMPLETE` moves the case to `REPORT_DRAFTED`, stores the supplied narrative in `report_narrative_summary`, stamps `report_drafted_at` from the logical clock, and increments the case's `version`.

Either a `PlacementAdministrator` or a `ChildFindCoordinator` may send a drafted report back for revision, supplying a `revision_note`. This is available only from `REPORT_DRAFTED` — asking for a revision at any other status is `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE` — and it moves the case `REPORT_DRAFTED → REVISIONS_REQUESTED`, increments `revision_round` by one, stores the note in `last_revision_note`, and increments the case's `version`. A case may absorb at most two revision rounds; once `revision_round` has reached 2, a third request is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. The chair closes the loop by drafting again, which moves the case `REVISIONS_REQUESTED → REPORT_DRAFTED`, replaces `report_narrative_summary` with the new narrative, re-stamps `report_drafted_at`, and again requires the chair's own identity and all five domains complete.

Recording the determination is the placement office's act, shared with the case chair, and it both closes the case and creates the plan. It carries the `eligibility_decision`, either `ELIGIBLE` or `NOT_ELIGIBLE`; the `expected_evaluation_case_version` of §2.6; and, depending on the decision, a `disability_category` of `DEVELOPMENTAL_DELAY`, `SPEECH_OR_LANGUAGE_IMPAIRMENT` or `OTHER_HEALTH_IMPAIRMENT`, a `setting` of `COMMUNITY_PRESCHOOL`, `DISTRICT_ECSE_CLASSROOM`, `HOME_BASED` or `ITINERANT_ONLY`, a `community_site_id`, and an `override_justification`. Determination is available only from `REPORT_DRAFTED` — and, as the self-loop below describes, from `ELIGIBILITY_DETERMINED` — with any other status refused as `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`; and it independently requires all five domains still to stand `COMPLETED`, refusing with `ERR_DOMAINS_INCOMPLETE` otherwise, which is the refusal a mid-flight invalidation of a completed assessment produces.

When `eligibility_decision` is `ELIGIBLE`, both `disability_category` and `setting` are required, and `community_site_id` may be supplied only when the `setting` is `COMMUNITY_PRESCHOOL` — a plan naming a site under any other setting, or naming `COMMUNITY_PRESCHOOL` with no site, is refused with `ERR_PLAN_SETTING_REQUIREMENTS_UNMET`. Where a site is named it must be `ACTIVE` and its `languages_supported` must cover the child's `primary_language_code`, or the determination is refused with `ERR_SITE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. When `eligibility_decision` is `NOT_ELIGIBLE`, both `setting` and `disability_category` must be absent. And whenever the decision contradicts the team's own `composite_eligibility_recommendation` — a decision of `ELIGIBLE` against a recommendation of `NOT_ELIGIBLE_RECOMMENDED`, or a decision of `NOT_ELIGIBLE` against a recommendation of `ELIGIBLE_RECOMMENDED` — a non-empty `override_justification` is required, and its absence is refused with `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_OVERRIDE_JUSTIFICATION_REQUIRED`.

A successful determination moves the case to `ELIGIBILITY_DETERMINED`, increments the case's `version`, and creates a `ServicePlan` at `DRAFT` that copies `evaluation_case_id`, copies `child_id` from the case, copies the case's `recommended_weekly_minutes` onto its own `recommended_weekly_minutes` so that the recommendation can later be compared against what was actually bought, stamps `determined_by_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID`, and starts with `assigned_weekly_minutes` at 0, `start_date` and `exit_date` absent, `kindergarten_transition_status` at `NOT_STARTED`, and `transition_conference_date` and `receiving_school_code` both absent. When the decision was `NOT_ELIGIBLE` the new plan is written at `DRAFT` and immediately moved by the system to `CLOSED_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, incrementing its `version`, and the child's derived `status` becomes `NOT_ELIGIBLE`. When the decision was `ELIGIBLE` the plan stays at `DRAFT`, waiting for service lines.

Determination is repeatable exactly once per outstanding draft. A second determination on a case already at `ELIGIBILITY_DETERMINED` is a self-loop on that status: it requires a matching `expected_evaluation_case_version`, and it requires the plan already on the case to be at `DRAFT` — a plan that has moved on is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`. The existing draft plan then moves `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED`, every service line belonging to it moves to `DISCONTINUED` with the provider minute and posture recomputation of §3.8 following, a fresh plan is created at `DRAFT`, and the case's `version` increments again. Plans can be listed and filtered by `evaluation_case_id`, `child_id`, `community_site_id`, `setting`, `eligibility_decision`, `kindergarten_transition_status` and `status`.

### 3.8. Buying the Minutes: Service Lines, Itinerant Staffing and Provider Capacity

A draft plan is a shell until service lines are added to it. Each line is one commitment of minutes in one discipline, and a `PlacementAdministrator` or the case chair adds it by supplying the `discipline` the minutes are drawn from — one of `SLP`, `OT`, `PT`, `ECSE_TEACHER` and `SOCIAL_WORKER`, never `INTERPRETER` and never `SCHOOL_PSYCHOLOGIST` — the `weekly_minutes` of direct service, which must be a multiple of 5 between 5 and 600 inclusive, and the `delivery_model`, one of `DIRECT`, `CONSULT` and `PUSH_IN`. Lines may only be added to a plan that is still at `DRAFT`; adding one to any other plan is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`. A new line is written at `PROPOSED`, with `provider_id` absent because nobody is staffed yet and `delivery_site_id` likewise absent until staffing time. Adding a line recomputes the parent plan's `assigned_weekly_minutes`, which is the sum of `weekly_minutes` across the plan's lines whose `status` is `PROPOSED`, `STAFFED` or `UNSTAFFED`; lines that have been `DISCONTINUED` drop out of that total.

Two setting-specific rules bind at line creation. Where the plan's `setting` is `ITINERANT_ONLY`, every line on the plan must have a `delivery_model` of `DIRECT` or `CONSULT` — a `PUSH_IN` line is incompatible with a purely itinerant model — and no single line may exceed 300 `weekly_minutes`; a violation of either is refused with `ERR_PLAN_SETTING_REQUIREMENTS_UNMET`. And where the plan names a community site, that site must remain eligible for the placement, or the line creation is refused with `ERR_SITE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Lines can be listed and filtered by `service_plan_id`, `provider_id`, `delivery_site_id`, `discipline`, `delivery_model` and `status`.

Staffing a line is the placement office's exclusive act. It names the `provider_id` who will deliver the minutes, the `expected_provider_version` of §2.6, and optionally a `delivery_site_id` saying where the minutes are actually delivered — an itinerant provider may serve a child at a partner site rather than the site holding the IEP seat. When `delivery_site_id` is omitted it defaults to the parent plan's own `community_site_id`; an absent value means the district centre, and that is the only legal value for any setting other than `COMMUNITY_PRESCHOOL`. Staffing is available from `PROPOSED` and from `UNSTAFFED`; attempting it on a line that is `STAFFED` or `DISCONTINUED` is `ERR_INVALID_LINE_STATE`. The parent plan must be at `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`. The named provider's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_ACTIVE`. The named provider's `discipline` must equal the line's own `discipline`, and a provider whose discipline is `INTERPRETER` may never be staffed to a service line at all; either failure is `ERR_DISCIPLINE_MISMATCH`.

The last gate is the minute ceiling, and it is computed against the provider's whole post-write load rather than the line in isolation. A provider's `direct_weekly_service_minutes` is the sum of `weekly_minutes` over every line that names that provider, stands at `STAFFED`, and belongs to a plan whose status is `DRAFT`, `PENDING_PARENT_CONSENT` or `ACTIVE`. Lines at `PROPOSED`, `UNSTAFFED` or `DISCONTINUED` contribute nothing, and neither do lines under a plan that has reached `DECLINED`, `SUPERSEDED`, `EXITED_TO_KINDERGARTEN` or `CLOSED_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Only lines whose `delivery_model` is `DIRECT` enter that sum; `CONSULT` and `PUSH_IN` lines are excluded from it.

Travel is then loaded on top, and the **order of the two steps is pinned**. First, the travel percentage is chosen from the direct total **alone**, before any travel minutes exist: a direct total of 300 minutes or fewer draws 5 percent, a total above 300 but not above 600 draws 10 percent, and a total above 600 draws 15 percent. Second, the number of distinct delivery locations is counted across the same in-scope set of staffed lines, where an absent `delivery_site_id` normalises to a single sentinel district-centre location so that every district-delivered line collapses into one place; `CONSULT` and `PUSH_IN` lines **do** participate in this count even though they were excluded from the direct total, because they too require the provider to travel. One location is free; each location beyond the first adds 15 minutes. `travel_overhead_minutes` is then the direct total multiplied by the chosen percentage, plus 15 minutes for each location beyond the first, with that **whole** expression rounded up once to the next multiple of five — the percentage part and the per-location part are never rounded separately. Finally `assigned_weekly_service_minutes` is the direct total plus the travel overhead. The tier is never re-chosen after the travel minutes have been added: a provider with 290 direct minutes across two distinct locations draws the 5 percent tier from 290, giving 14.5 plus 15, which is 29.5, rounded up to 30, for an assigned total of 320 — and not the 335 that would follow from re-reading the tier against 320.

That assigned total is what the ceiling test reads. A staffing request whose resulting `assigned_weekly_service_minutes` would **exceed** `max_weekly_service_minutes` is refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_CASELOAD_EXCEEDED`. The comparison is strictly greater, so a request landing exactly on the ceiling is accepted. From the same figures `service_capacity_posture` is re-derived on every recomputation: it reads `AT_CAPACITY` when `assigned_weekly_service_minutes` has reached or passed `max_weekly_service_minutes`, `NEAR_CAPACITY` when the ratio of assigned minutes to the ceiling has reached eight tenths, and `OPEN` otherwise. The ratio is compared as an exact decimal and never pre-rounded, so a provider at 320 assigned minutes against a 400-minute ceiling sits exactly on eight tenths and is `NEAR_CAPACITY`. A request that lands exactly on the ceiling is accepted and leaves the provider `AT_CAPACITY`; the next minute is refused.

A successful staffing moves the line `PROPOSED → STAFFED` or `UNSTAFFED → STAFFED`, sets `provider_id` to the named provider and `delivery_site_id` to the supplied or defaulted location, recomputes that provider's `direct_weekly_service_minutes`, `travel_overhead_minutes` and `assigned_weekly_service_minutes`, re-derives the provider's `service_capacity_posture`, recomputes the parent plan's `assigned_weekly_minutes`, and increments both the provider's `version` and the line's `version`.

Releasing a provider is the sanctioned reverse move, and the pair `STAFFED → UNSTAFFED → STAFFED` is a legitimate re-assignment loop that a line may travel repeatedly. Unstaffing takes an optional `unstaff_reason`, requires the parent plan to be at `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`, and requires the line to be `STAFFED` — unstaffing anything else is `ERR_INVALID_LINE_STATE`. It moves the line to `UNSTAFFED`, clears both `provider_id` and `delivery_site_id`, recomputes the released provider's three minute figures and re-derives that provider's `service_capacity_posture`, and increments both the provider's `version` and the line's `version`. Because the minute figures are recomputed from the current set of contributing lines rather than adjusted by a remembered delta, the released provider's numbers return to exactly what they were before the line was staffed. The parent plan's `assigned_weekly_minutes` is deliberately **unchanged** by unstaffing, because an `UNSTAFFED` line is still a commitment the plan has made and still counts toward the plan total.

Lines reach `DISCONTINUED`, their terminal status, only as a cascade from their plan reaching a terminal state — `DECLINED`, `EXITED_TO_KINDERGARTEN` or `SUPERSEDED`. A line cascading from `PROPOSED` or from `UNSTAFFED` simply takes the new status and increments its `version`. A line cascading from `STAFFED` retains `provider_id` and `delivery_site_id` for audit rather than clearing them, and because a discontinued line leaves the in-scope set, its provider's three minute figures are recomputed and `service_capacity_posture` re-derived, with the provider's `version` incremented alongside the line's.

The provider caseload view exposes the whole ledger, including the alternative reading the system does not enforce. It returns `provider_id`, `direct_weekly_service_minutes`, the `distinct_site_count` behind the travel calculation, the `travel_tier_pct` actually selected, `travel_overhead_minutes`, the pinned `assigned_weekly_service_minutes`, and `committed_weekly_minutes` — the same computation performed with `DRAFT` plans **excluded**, so that only parent-facing commitments count, which yields a lower direct total, possibly a lower travel tier, and a lower assigned total than the pinned figure whenever a provider is staffed onto draft plans. It also returns `max_weekly_service_minutes`, the exact `utilization` ratio, `service_capacity_posture`, and then the second capacity dimension: `active_evaluation_count`, `max_evaluation_caseload`, `evaluation_intake_posture` and `assigned_interpreter_assignment_count`. Only the projected reading, which holds minutes from the moment a line is staffed and before parental consent has even been requested, is ever compared against `max_weekly_service_minutes`.

### 3.9. Parental Consent to Services, IEP Seats, Kindergarten Transition, and the Child's Derived Status

A drafted plan becomes an offer only when it is sent to the family. Either a `PlacementAdministrator` or a `ChildFindCoordinator` submits it, supplying the same notice fields as an evaluation notice: `notice_language_code`, `notice_delivery_method`, and where language access requires it an `interpreter_provider_id` and `interpreter_modality`. The submission creates a second `ConsentRecord`, this one with `kind` of `SERVICE_CONSENT`, `decision` at `PENDING`, `requested_at` from the logical clock, `service_plan_id` set to this plan, and `referral_id` set to the referral behind the plan's evaluation case. The plan moves `DRAFT → PENDING_PARENT_CONSENT`, the named interpreter's `assigned_interpreter_assignment_count` is recomputed, and the plan's `version` increments. Submitting a plan that is not at `DRAFT` is `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`, as is submitting a plan whose `eligibility_decision` is not `ELIGIBLE` or whose `setting` is absent. The language-access rule of §2.7 applies in full here, and the interpreter's own availability ceiling is tested after it, with an over-committed interpreter refused as `ERR_INTERPRETER_UNAVAILABLE`.

Submission is also where the plan's `setting` finally has to be honoured, and the four settings fork into four requirement sets. Common to all four: at least one of the plan's service lines must stand `STAFFED`, and a plan submitted with no staffed line at all is refused with `ERR_PLAN_SETTING_REQUIREMENTS_UNMET`. Beyond that, a `COMMUNITY_PRESCHOOL` plan must name a `community_site_id`, that site must be `ACTIVE` and its `languages_supported` must contain the child's `primary_language_code` — failures of either being `ERR_SITE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` — and the site must have a free IEP seat, tested below. A `DISTRICT_ECSE_CLASSROOM` plan must have no `community_site_id` and must have at least one `STAFFED` line whose `discipline` is `ECSE_TEACHER`; either failure is `ERR_PLAN_SETTING_REQUIREMENTS_UNMET`. A `HOME_BASED` plan must have no `community_site_id` and must have at least one `STAFFED` line whose `delivery_model` is `DIRECT`; either failure is the same code. An `ITINERANT_ONLY` plan must have no `community_site_id`, again refused with the same code. All four branches then join at `PENDING_PARENT_CONSENT`.

Where the branches diverge is in what they consume. Only the community-preschool branch reserves a seat: on a successful submission the named site's `reserved_iep_seat_count` increases by exactly one. The district-classroom, home-based and itinerant-only branches reserve no seat on any site at all. `reserved_iep_seat_count` is, by definition, the number of service plans naming that site whose `status` is `PENDING_PARENT_CONSENT` or `ACTIVE`; a plan still at `DRAFT` holds no seat, and a plan that has reached `DECLINED`, `SUPERSEDED`, `EXITED_TO_KINDERGARTEN` or `CLOSED_NOT_ELIGIBLE` has released its seat. A submission naming a site whose `reserved_iep_seat_count` has already reached its `iep_seat_capacity` is refused with `ERR_IEP_SEAT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because reservation begins at submission rather than at drafting, two draft plans can both name a site with a single free seat and coexist quite legitimately — and only the first of them to be submitted for consent succeeds. On a successful submission the child's derived `status` is recomputed and deliberately stays `IN_EVALUATION`: a plan the family has not yet consented to is not yet a service.

The family's answer on the service-consent record drives the plan, and never touches the referral's consent dimension. `GRANTED` on a service-consent record moves the plan `PENDING_PARENT_CONSENT → ACTIVE`, sets `start_date` to the logical clock's current date, retains the IEP seat reserved at submission where the setting is `COMMUNITY_PRESCHOOL`, increments the plan's `version`, and recomputes the child's derived `status` to `SERVED` — this is the first request in which the child counts as served. A granted service consent cannot afterwards be revoked; withdrawal of services is expressed through the plan's own lifecycle instead. `REFUSED` on a service-consent record moves the plan `PENDING_PARENT_CONSENT → DECLINED` and unwinds the commitment in four steps: every service line of the plan standing at `PROPOSED`, `STAFFED` or `UNSTAFFED` moves to `DISCONTINUED`; for each line that left `STAFFED`, its provider's `direct_weekly_service_minutes`, `travel_overhead_minutes` and `assigned_weekly_service_minutes` are recomputed; each affected provider's `service_capacity_posture` is then re-derived from the new assigned total; and, on the community-preschool branch only, the named site's `reserved_iep_seat_count` decreases by one. The child's derived `status` becomes `SERVICES_DECLINED`, and the plan's `version` increments.

Kindergarten transition planning runs on an `ACTIVE` plan and is driven by a single endpoint that takes an `action` of `SCHEDULE_CONFERENCE`, `SEND_PACKET` or `EXIT`, plus `transition_conference_date` or `receiving_school_code` as the action requires. Any of the three attempted on a plan that is not `ACTIVE` is `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`, and the three must be taken in that order: `kindergarten_transition_status` must read `NOT_STARTED` for `SCHEDULE_CONFERENCE`, `CONFERENCE_SCHEDULED` for `SEND_PACKET`, and `PACKET_SENT` for `EXIT`, with any out-of-order attempt refused as `ERR_TRANSITION_SEQUENCE_VIOLATION`.

Scheduling the conference stores the supplied `transition_conference_date` and advances `kindergarten_transition_status` from `NOT_STARTED` to `CONFERENCE_SCHEDULED` while the plan's own `status` stays `ACTIVE` — a self-loop that advances only the transition sub-state — and increments the plan's `version`. The date is constrained twice: the number of whole days from `transition_conference_date` to the child's `kindergarten_eligibility_date` must be at least 90, giving the receiving school a full quarter of notice, and the date must fall strictly after the logical clock's current date. Either failure is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_CONFERENCE_TOO_LATE`. Sending the packet stores the supplied `receiving_school_code` — which must be supplied — and advances `kindergarten_transition_status` from `CONFERENCE_SCHEDULED` to `PACKET_SENT`, again as a self-loop on an `ACTIVE` plan with a `version` increment.

Exiting is the real transition and it closes the plan. It moves the plan `ACTIVE → EXITED_TO_KINDERGARTEN`, sets `exit_date` to the logical clock's current date, advances `kindergarten_transition_status` to `COMPLETE`, and then runs the same four-step unwind as a declined plan: every service line at `PROPOSED`, `STAFFED` or `UNSTAFFED` moves to `DISCONTINUED`; every provider that lost a staffed line has its `direct_weekly_service_minutes`, `travel_overhead_minutes` and `assigned_weekly_service_minutes` recomputed; each such provider's `service_capacity_posture` is re-derived; and, on the community-preschool branch only, the named site's `reserved_iep_seat_count` decreases by one. The child's derived `status` becomes `EXITED_TO_KINDERGARTEN`, and the plan's `version` increments. `DECLINED`, `EXITED_TO_KINDERGARTEN`, `SUPERSEDED` and `CLOSED_NOT_ELIGIBLE` are all terminal for a plan.

Finally, a child's own `status` is never written by a client; it is derived on every referral, evaluation-case and service-plan write that touches the child, by a ladder of clauses evaluated top to bottom in which the **first** matching clause wins. If the child has any service plan at `EXITED_TO_KINDERGARTEN`, the status is `EXITED_TO_KINDERGARTEN`. Otherwise, if the child has any plan at `ACTIVE`, the status is `SERVED`. Otherwise, if the child has any plan at `DECLINED`, the status is `SERVICES_DECLINED`. Otherwise, if the child has any plan at `CLOSED_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, the status is `NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Otherwise, if the child has any evaluation case in one of the five non-terminal statuses `PLANNED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `DOMAINS_COMPLETE`, `REPORT_DRAFTED` or `REVISIONS_REQUESTED`, the status is `IN_EVALUATION`. Otherwise, if the child has any plan at `DRAFT` or `PENDING_PARENT_CONSENT`, the status is again `IN_EVALUATION` — a plan the family has not consented to is not a service, so the child stays in evaluation even though the case behind it has already left the five in-evaluation statuses for `ELIGIBILITY_DETERMINED`. Otherwise, if the child has any referral whose `intake_status` is `RECEIVED`, `CONSENT_PENDING` or `EVALUATION_AUTHORIZED`, the status is `REFERRED`. Failing all of those, the status is `NO_ACTIVE_REFERRAL`. Plans at `SUPERSEDED`, and referrals at `DECLINED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `SUPERSEDED`, match no clause at all. The ladder is ordered by finality of outcome rather than by recency, so a child holding both an `ACTIVE` plan and a freshly `RECEIVED` referral reads `SERVED`, not `REFERRED`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is mounted under `/child-find` and requires both the `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` headers. Operations that bring a new resource into existence — registering a child, a site or a provider; scheduling a screening event; booking an appointment; intaking a referral; issuing a consent notice; opening an evaluation case; scheduling a domain assessment; determining eligibility and thereby creating a service plan; adding a service line; and submitting a plan for parental consent, which creates a service-consent record — return `201 Created`. Every other write, including all state advances, all outcome and decision recordings, staffing and unstaffing, report drafting, revision requests, kindergarten transition actions, provider amendment and setting the logical clock, returns `200 OK`. All reads return `200 OK`. Request bodies, response shapes and side effects are not repeated here; they are specified in §2 and §3.
