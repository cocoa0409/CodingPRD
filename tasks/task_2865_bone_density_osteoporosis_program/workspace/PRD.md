# Bone Density & Osteoporosis Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A bone densitometry program lives or dies on comparability. A dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry number is clinically meaningless in isolation: the question a referring clinician actually asks is whether *this* patient's bone mineral density has moved since *last* time, and that question can only be answered when the two acquisitions came off the same scanner, in the same calibration state, with a known measurement precision behind them. Today that chain of custody is maintained by hand — a technologist remembers which device the baseline was on, a physicist keeps the phantom control chart in a spreadsheet, and the least significant change threshold lives in a laminated card taped to the console. When any link breaks, the program silently reports change that is really noise, or funds a scan the payer will never reimburse.

This document specifies the API for a system that makes the whole chain explicit and machine-enforced. It covers the equipment side — device registry, daily phantom quality control with out-of-control detection, precision assessment and least-significant-change certification, and phantom cross-calibration whenever hardware or software changes underneath the program — and the patient side: clinical risk factor intake, payer frequency and lifetime-cap arbitration at order entry, slot booking against device capacity, acquisition of skeletal sites, an independent positioning quality review that can send a study back for repeat, and a diagnostic report that classifies the patient, compares against the correct prior, and derives the next screening interval. It closes the loop with treatment initiation and periodic adherence follow-up driven by a medication possession ratio computed from a refill ledger.

Two design commitments run through every section. First, **the program is auditable**: every derived number is stored on the resource that owns it, every state change names its actor, and every rejection carries a stable machine-readable identifier so that a denial can be explained to a payer months later. Second, **the program is deterministic**: time advances only when an operator moves a logical clock, and every threshold, tie-break, rounding step and evaluation order in this document is fixed rather than left to implementation taste. The system is mounted under the `/bone-density` path prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Users, Roles and Access Control

Every request — without exception, including read-only ones and the clock-advance capability — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting as. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request fails immediately with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401), before any path parameter is resolved and before any body is parsed. If `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the six recognised role names, or names a role that the addressed operation does not admit, the request fails with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `Scheduler`, `Technologist`, `QualityReviewer`, `InterpretingPhysician` and `PatientPortalUser`, spelled exactly as shown.

A `ProgramAdmin` administers the program and its equipment: payer policy registration, device registry, precision certification, cross-calibration adjudication and device retirement, and shares phantom quality-control acquisition with technologists and patient registration with schedulers. A `Scheduler` runs the front office: patient registration, risk factor intake, order entry, and — exclusively — slot booking and slot cancellation. A `Technologist` acquires phantom runs and patient scans, records skeletal sites and the vertebral fracture assessment add-on, and submits or repeats acquisitions; a technologist may also sit as a positioning reviewer, but only ever for a study somebody else acquired. A `QualityReviewer` exists solely to perform that independent positioning review. An `InterpretingPhysician` runs fracture risk assessments, authors, patches, finalizes and amends diagnostic reports, initiates therapy and conducts adherence reviews, and shares order entry and risk factor intake with schedulers and refill recording with patients. A `PatientPortalUser` is the patient.

Identity semantics differ between the staff roles and the patient role, and the distinction is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `Scheduler`, `Technologist`, `QualityReviewer` or `InterpretingPhysician`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow calls for provenance — as `performed_by_user_id` on a phantom run, `precision_technologist_user_id` on a device, `ordered_by_user_id` on an order, `technologist_user_id` on a session, `reviewer_user_id` on a positioning review, `assessed_by_user_id` on a risk assessment, `finalized_by_user_id` on a report, or `recorded_by_user_id` on a refill — and it compares that recorded value against the caller's `X-User-ID` where segregation of duties demands it, but it never resolves an opaque identifier to a row's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `PatientPortalUser`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Patient`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Patient` compare the resource's patient foreign key to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_PATIENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403) rather than with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. For a self-reported refill the server writes the acting patient's `id` into `recorded_by_user_id`.

Role admission is a property of the operation, not of the resource, and it is checked before existence. A role that is simply not admitted to an operation receives `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` even when the addressed resource does not exist; conversely, a `PatientPortalUser` who is admitted to an operation but addresses another patient's data receives the scope violation, not the role error. A `PatientPortalUser` has no visibility at all into densitometers, phantom quality-control runs, calibration events, appointments, sessions, site measurements, positioning reviews or fracture risk assessments, and attempting any of those reads yields `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Ordering and List Filters

Every collection endpoint is paginated through two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, accepts a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100, and a value outside that inclusive range is a malformed request. `offset` controls the starting position, defaults to 0, and has a minimum of 0 with no upper bound. Both values are echoed back in `meta` alongside `total_count`, the number of rows the filter matched before pagination was applied, and `has_more`, which is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page.

Unless a collection declares otherwise below, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with the row `id` ascending as the tie-break. The collections that declare their own order are: the phantom quality-control chart, ordered by `performed_at_utc` descending and then `created_at` descending, because a control chart is read newest-first; a device's calibration history, ordered by `created_at` descending; a patient's risk factor history, ordered by `recorded_at_utc` descending and then `created_at` descending; the device schedule, ordered by `start_utc` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; a session's skeletal site results, ordered by `created_at` ascending; a session's positioning reviews, ordered by `reviewed_at_utc` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; the fracture risk assessment history, ordered by `assessed_at_utc` descending and then `created_at` descending; and a plan's refill ledger, ordered by `dispensed_date` ascending and then `created_at` ascending.

Collections also accept domain filters, all optional and all combinable with pagination. The device inventory filters on `status` and `site_code`. The quality-control chart filters on `densitometer_id`, `calibration_event_id`, `purpose` and `result`, which is also how a caller reverses the device and calibration-event relationships. A device's calibration history filters on `status`. The patient roster filters on `primary_payer_policy_id`, `baseline_report_id`, `last_finalized_report_id` and `mrn`. A patient's risk factor history filters on `status`. The policy catalogue filters on `payer_code`. The order worklist filters on `patient_id`, `required_densitometer_id`, `status` and `indication`. The device schedule filters on `scan_order_id`, `densitometer_id`, `status`, and on the half-open instant window `from_utc` and `to_utc`. The session worklist filters on `scan_appointment_id`, `prior_session_id`, `densitometer_id`, `patient_id`, `acquisition_status` and `review_status`. A session's site results filter on `site_code` and on the boolean `include_superseded`, which defaults to false — superseded rows are hidden from the list unless the caller explicitly asks for them. The assessment history filters on `patient_id`, `risk_factor_profile_id`, `scan_session_id` and `variant`. The report list filters on `scan_session_id`, `frax_assessment_id`, `amends_report_id` and `status`. The plan list filters on `patient_id`, `initiating_report_id` and `status`. A plan's refill ledger filters on `source`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, the Logical Clock and Record Versioning

Every resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, generated by the server and never accepted from a client. Identity is identity only: no business decision anywhere in this specification reads an `id` as an ordering key or a tie-break. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits, stamped from the logical clock at the moment of creation, and used as a stable secondary sort key wherever rows must be totally ordered.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with the same format that is refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 on creation and increments by exactly one on every mutation of that resource — including mutations caused indirectly by a cascade from another resource. Four resource types are exceptions, because they are append-only records rather than mutable state: `PayerPolicy`, `QcRun`, `SiteMeasurement` and `AdherenceRefill` carry only `id` and `created_at`. For those four, `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the response entirely — not null, not zero, absent. A payer policy is immutable once registered; a phantom run, a site measurement and a refill row are historical facts. (A site measurement can be marked `superseded`, which is the one bookkeeping flag the system flips on it; that flip does not introduce `updated_at` or `version` onto the resource.)

All time in this system is logical. A fresh server starts its clock at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and the clock never advances on its own; it moves only when a caller of any role invokes the clock-setting capability with a `to_iso8601` instant, which returns HTTP 204 with an empty data payload. Every `created_at`, every field whose name ends in `_at_utc`, every age computation, every date comparison and every interval arithmetic step reads that clock. Age is always **completed whole years** between a birth date and the clock's calendar date, never a fractional or rounded-up value. Where a date is advanced by a whole number of calendar months, the day of the month is preserved and clamped to the last day of the target month when the target month is shorter — advancing 31 January by one month yields 28 February in a common year.

### 2.5. Densitometric Precision and Rounding Discipline

Bone densitometry is an arithmetic discipline where the third decimal place decides a diagnosis, so this specification fixes not just how many decimals each quantity carries but *when* the rounding happens. Every rounding operation in this document is **round half up** on the magnitude — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero, so 0.12345 at four places becomes 0.1235 and −0.12345 becomes −0.1235.

Bone mineral density quantities and their derived deviations carry **four decimal places**: phantom deviation in absolute units, device least significant change, phantom means, the proposed and applied cross-calibration factors, the cross-calibration-adjusted prior density, and the absolute density change on a report. Phantom deviation expressed in control-limit units carries **three decimal places**, as does a plan's medication possession ratio. Percentages carry **two decimal places** — the cross-calibration percent difference and the report's percentage density change — as do body mass index, every fracture-risk points term and the raw risk score. T-scores and Z-scores carry **exactly one decimal place**, as do height loss in centimetres and the two ten-year fracture probabilities. Interval months, degrees of freedom, defect severity, day counts, vertebral counts, grades and every conserved counter are whole integers with no rounding at all.

Three ordering rules matter more than the digit counts themselves, and they are restated where they apply. First, **classification always reads the rounded value, never the unrounded quotient**: a raw T-score of −2.45 becomes −2.5 and therefore does classify as osteoporosis, while a raw −2.0166 becomes −2.0 and does not. Second, **intermediate quantities are rounded before they are combined** wherever this document says so — the two phantom means are rounded to four places before a percent difference is taken from them, the cross-calibration-adjusted prior density is rounded to four places before it is differenced, and every fracture-risk points term is rounded to two places before the terms are summed. Third, and in deliberate contrast, **a percentage change is derived in one step from its two four-decimal inputs**, not from an already-rounded difference.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Patients and Densitometers

Two write operations in the program supersede a record that other decisions already depend on, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control. In each case the guard is a mandatory body field — not an optional one, and not a header — carrying the `version` the client last observed on the *parent* resource. When the supplied value does not equal the parent's current `version`, the write is refused and nothing is persisted.

Recording a new clinical risk factor profile at `POST /bone-density/patients/{id}/risk-factor-profiles` requires `expected_patient_version` matching the addressed `Patient`'s current `version`. If any writer has advanced that patient's version since the client last read it, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PATIENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason is that a new profile legitimately supersedes the incumbent `ACTIVE` profile: a writer holding a stale view of the patient would silently discard risk factors another writer has already recorded, and would silently change the inputs of any fracture risk assessment keyed on the `ACTIVE` profile.

Accepting a precision assessment at `POST /bone-density/densitometers/{id}/precision-assessment` requires `expected_densitometer_version` matching the addressed `Densitometer`'s current `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DENSITOMETER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here the stake is the least significant change: a new assessment overwrites the device's `rms_sd_g_cm2` and `lsc_g_cm2`, and a writer holding a stale view would overwrite a least-significant-change value that has already moved, silently changing the threshold every subsequently created acquisition snapshots.

No other endpoint accepts a version field of any kind. Where this document does not name `expected_patient_version` or `expected_densitometer_version`, the request body has no version input and no version check is performed.

### 2.7. The Rejection Ladder — the Order in Which a Request Is Refused

When a single request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined: the system evaluates checks in the fixed order below and returns the first failure. Priority is a global property of the check, not of the endpoint, so a check that appears earlier in this ladder always masks a later one on the same request. The ladder is stated here once; the sections that follow describe what each check means in its business context rather than repeating the ordering.

**Authentication, role and shape (1–3).** (1) Missing or empty `X-User-ID` or `X-User-Role` on any endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401. (2) An unrecognised role, or a role not admitted to the addressed operation, on any endpoint — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, 403. (3) On any write endpoint: a required body field absent, a field of the wrong JSON type, an enum value outside its declared set, a numeric value outside its declared range, or a date or instant string that is not valid ISO-8601 — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 422.

**Existence of addressed resources (4–15), evaluated left to right along the path.** (4) No densitometer with that id, on every endpoint whose path contains a densitometer id — `ERR_DENSITOMETER_NOT_FOUND`, 404. (5) No phantom run with that id, on `GET /bone-density/qc-runs/{id}` — `ERR_QC_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, 404. (6) No calibration event with that id, on `GET /bone-density/calibration-events/{id}`, `POST /bone-density/calibration-events/{id}/close` and `POST /bone-density/calibration-events/{id}/adjudicate` — `ERR_CALIBRATION_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, 404. (7) No patient with that id, on every endpoint whose path contains a patient id — `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, 404. (8) No order with that id, on `GET /bone-density/scan-orders/{id}` — `ERR_SCAN_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, 404. (9) No appointment with that id, on every endpoint whose path contains a scan appointment id — `ERR_SCAN_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, 404. (10) No session with that id, on every endpoint whose path contains a scan session id — `ERR_SCAN_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, 404. (11) No assessment with that id, on `GET /bone-density/frax-assessments/{id}` — `ERR_FRAX_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, 404. (12) No report with that id, on every endpoint whose path contains a diagnostic report id — `ERR_DIAGNOSTIC_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, 404. (13) No plan with that id, on every endpoint whose path contains a treatment plan id — `ERR_TREATMENT_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, 404. (14) `primary_payer_policy_id` not referencing an existing policy, on `POST /bone-density/patients` — `ERR_PAYER_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, 404. (15) `risk_factor_profile_id` not referencing an existing profile, on `POST /bone-density/frax-assessments` — `ERR_RISK_FACTOR_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, 404.

**Existence of body foreign keys (16).** After every path parameter has resolved, body foreign keys are resolved in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, and a miss returns the same per-entity not-found code declared at priorities 4 through 15. This applies to `POST /bone-density/scan-orders`, `POST /bone-density/scan-appointments`, `POST /bone-density/frax-assessments`, `POST /bone-density/diagnostic-reports`, `PATCH /bone-density/diagnostic-reports/{id}`, `POST /bone-density/treatment-plans` and `POST /bone-density/densitometers/{id}/qc-runs`, and covers `patient_id`, `scan_order_id`, `densitometer_id`, `scan_session_id`, `frax_assessment_id`, `initiating_report_id` and `calibration_event_id`. A dangling `scan_session_id` in a body therefore yields `ERR_SCAN_SESSION_NOT_FOUND` (404), a dangling `initiating_report_id` yields `ERR_DIAGNOSTIC_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, and so on.

**Patient scope (17).** A `PatientPortalUser` whose `X-User-ID` is not the owning patient's `id` is rejected with `ERR_PATIENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403) on `GET /bone-density/patients/{id}`, `GET /bone-density/patients/{id}/risk-factor-profiles`, `GET /bone-density/scan-orders`, `GET /bone-density/diagnostic-reports`, `GET /bone-density/treatment-plans/{id}`, `GET /bone-density/treatment-plans/{id}/adherence-refills` and `POST /bone-density/treatment-plans/{id}/adherence-refills`.

**Optimistic concurrency (18–19).** (18) A mismatched `expected_patient_version` on `POST /bone-density/patients/{id}/risk-factor-profiles` — `ERR_STALE_PATIENT_VERSION`, 412. (19) A mismatched `expected_densitometer_version` on `POST /bone-density/densitometers/{id}/precision-assessment` — `ERR_STALE_DENSITOMETER_VERSION`, 412.

**Resource state (20–34).** (20) The addressed densitometer is `RETIRED`, on `POST /bone-density/densitometers/{id}/qc-runs`, `POST /bone-density/densitometers/{id}/calibration-events`, `POST /bone-density/densitometers/{id}/precision-assessment`, `POST /bone-density/densitometers/{id}/retire`, `POST /bone-density/scan-appointments` and `POST /bone-density/scan-appointments/{id}/scan-sessions` — `ERR_DENSITOMETER_RETIRED`, 409. (21) The appointment's densitometer is in `QC_HOLD` or `CROSS_CAL_PENDING`, on `POST /bone-density/scan-appointments/{id}/scan-sessions` — `ERR_DENSITOMETER_NOT_ACTIVE`, 409. (22) The calibration event is not `OPEN`, on `POST /bone-density/calibration-events/{id}/close` — `ERR_CALIBRATION_EVENT_NOT_OPEN`, 409. (23) The calibration event is not `PHANTOM_SET_COMPLETE`, on `POST /bone-density/calibration-events/{id}/adjudicate` — `ERR_CALIBRATION_EVENT_NOT_CLOSED`, 409. (24) The referenced order is not `REQUESTED`, on `POST /bone-density/scan-appointments` — `ERR_ORDER_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`, 409. (25) The appointment is not `BOOKED`, on `POST /bone-density/scan-appointments/{id}/cancel` and `POST /bone-density/scan-appointments/{id}/scan-sessions` — `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_BOOKED`, 409. (26) The session's `acquisition_status` is neither `IN_PROGRESS` nor `ARCHIVED`, on `POST /bone-density/scan-sessions/{id}/site-measurements`, `POST /bone-density/scan-sessions/{id}/vfa` and `POST /bone-density/scan-sessions/{id}/submit-acquisition` — `ERR_SESSION_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`, 409; the `ARCHIVED` case is deliberately allowed through here so that it lands on the archived lock at priority 30. (27) The session's `acquisition_status` is neither `SUBMITTED` nor `ARCHIVED`, on `POST /bone-density/scan-sessions/{id}/positioning-reviews` and `POST /bone-density/diagnostic-reports` — `ERR_SESSION_NOT_SUBMITTED`, 409, with the same deliberate fall-through for `ARCHIVED`. (28) The session's `review_status` is not `NOT_REVIEWED`, on `POST /bone-density/scan-sessions/{id}/positioning-reviews` — `ERR_SESSION_ALREADY_REVIEWED`, 409. (29) The session's `review_status` is neither `REPEAT_REQUIRED` nor `ACCEPTED_WITH_LIMITATION`, on `POST /bone-density/scan-sessions/{id}/repeat-acquisition` — `ERR_SESSION_REPEAT_NOT_REQUIRED`, 409; the `ACCEPTED_WITH_LIMITATION` case falls through to the repeat-limit check at priority 40. (30) The session's `acquisition_status` is `ARCHIVED`, on `POST /bone-density/scan-sessions/{id}/site-measurements`, `POST /bone-density/scan-sessions/{id}/vfa`, `POST /bone-density/scan-sessions/{id}/positioning-reviews`, `POST /bone-density/scan-sessions/{id}/repeat-acquisition` and `POST /bone-density/diagnostic-reports` — `ERR_SESSION_ARCHIVED`, 409. (31) The report is not `DRAFT`, on `PATCH /bone-density/diagnostic-reports/{id}` and `POST /bone-density/diagnostic-reports/{id}/finalize` — `ERR_REPORT_NOT_DRAFT`, 409. (32) The report is not `FINALIZED`, on `POST /bone-density/diagnostic-reports/{id}/amend` and `POST /bone-density/treatment-plans` — `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FINALIZED`, 409. (33) Another report on the same `scan_session_id` is already `DRAFT`, on `POST /bone-density/diagnostic-reports` and `POST /bone-density/diagnostic-reports/{id}/amend` — `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_EXISTS`, 409. (34) The plan is `DISCONTINUED`, on `POST /bone-density/treatment-plans/{id}/adherence-refills` and `POST /bone-density/treatment-plans/{id}/adherence-review` — `ERR_TREATMENT_PLAN_TERMINAL`, 409.

**Clinical and equipment safety (35–41).** (35) `X-User-ID` equals the session's `technologist_user_id`, on `POST /bone-density/scan-sessions/{id}/positioning-reviews` — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403. (36) `X-User-Role` is `Technologist` and `X-User-ID` is not the session's `technologist_user_id`, on `POST /bone-density/scan-sessions/{id}/site-measurements`, `POST /bone-density/scan-sessions/{id}/vfa`, `POST /bone-density/scan-sessions/{id}/submit-acquisition` and `POST /bone-density/scan-sessions/{id}/repeat-acquisition` — `ERR_NOT_SESSION_TECHNOLOGIST`, 403. (37) The appointment's densitometer has a null `lsc_g_cm2`, on `POST /bone-density/scan-appointments/{id}/scan-sessions` — `ERR_DEVICE_PRECISION_NOT_CERTIFIED`, 409. (38) The order carries a non-null `required_densitometer_id` differing from the requested `densitometer_id`, on `POST /bone-density/scan-appointments` — `ERR_SERIAL_DEVICE_MISMATCH`, 409. (39) The session's `review_status` is neither `ACCEPTED` nor `ACCEPTED_WITH_LIMITATION`, on `POST /bone-density/diagnostic-reports/{id}/finalize` — `ERR_POSITIONING_NOT_ACCEPTED`, 409. (40) The session's `repeat_count` has already reached the permitted maximum, on `POST /bone-density/scan-sessions/{id}/repeat-acquisition` — `ERR_REPEAT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, 409. (41) The precision study's degrees of freedom fall below the minimum, on `POST /bone-density/densitometers/{id}/precision-assessment` — `ERR_PRECISION_STUDY_UNDERPOWERED`, 422.

**Referential coherence between clinical records (42–45).** (42) The owning order has `vfa_requested` false, on `POST /bone-density/scan-sessions/{id}/vfa` — `ERR_VFA_NOT_ORDERED`, 409. (43) The referenced profile's `patient_id`, or the referenced session's `patient_id`, differs from the supplied `patient_id`, on `POST /bone-density/frax-assessments` — `ERR_FRAX_PATIENT_MISMATCH`, 409. (44) The referenced profile is `SUPERSEDED`, on `POST /bone-density/frax-assessments` — `ERR_RISK_PROFILE_NOT_ACTIVE`, 409. **Clinical content constraints (46–55).** (46) No vertebral-fracture-assessment eligibility branch matches, on `POST /bone-density/scan-sessions/{id}/vfa` — `ERR_VFA_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, 409. (52) `serial_number` already registered, on `POST /bone-density/densitometers` — `ERR_SERIAL_NUMBER_TAKEN`, 409. (53) `mrn` already registered, on `POST /bone-density/patients` — `ERR_MRN_TAKEN`, 409. (54) `payer_code` already registered, on `POST /bone-density/payer-policies` — `ERR_PAYER_CODE_TAKEN`, 409. (55) `required_elements_complete` is false, on `POST /bone-density/diagnostic-reports/{id}/finalize` — `ERR_REPORT_ELEMENTS_INCOMPLETE`, 422.

**Temporal constraints (56–62).** (56) `end_utc` not strictly after `start_utc`, on `POST /bone-density/scan-appointments` — `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`, 422. (57) `start_utc` at or before the logical clock, on `POST /bone-density/scan-appointments` — `ERR_APPOINTMENT_IN_PAST`, 422. (58) A buffered overlap with an existing slot on the same device, on `POST /bone-density/scan-appointments` — `ERR_DEVICE_SLOT_CONFLICT`, 409. (59) The patient already has a live slot on the same UTC calendar date, on `POST /bone-density/scan-appointments` — `ERR_PATIENT_DOUBLE_BOOKED`, 409. (60) The device's daily capacity for that date is full, on `POST /bone-density/scan-appointments` — `ERR_DEVICE_DAILY_CAPACITY_FULL`, 409. (61) `dispensed_date` outside the plan's reporting window, on `POST /bone-density/treatment-plans/{id}/adherence-refills` — `ERR_REFILL_DATE_OUT_OF_WINDOW`, 422. (62) The review is attempted too early, on `POST /bone-density/treatment-plans/{id}/adherence-review` — `ERR_FOLLOWUP_NOT_DUE`, 409.

**Fiscal checks, evaluated last (63–65).** (63) The frequency verdict is too-soon and appeals are not allowed, on `POST /bone-density/scan-orders` — `ERR_PAYER_FREQUENCY_LIMIT`, 402. (64) The frequency verdict is lifetime-capped and appeals are not allowed, on `POST /bone-density/scan-orders` — `ERR_LIFETIME_SCAN_CAP_EXCEEDED`, 402. (65) The patient's policy does not cover vertebral fracture assessment, on `POST /bone-density/scan-sessions/{id}/vfa` — `ERR_VFA_NOT_COVERED`, 402.

That the fiscal band sits at the bottom has a visible consequence worth stating outright: a patient who meets no clinical criterion for a vertebral fracture assessment *and* whose policy does not cover it receives `ERR_VFA_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, never `ERR_VFA_NOT_COVERED`, because the clinical check at priority 46 fires before the coverage check at priority 65.

### 2.8. Path Hierarchy and Nested Resource Resolution

Several resources are addressed through their parent. A phantom run and a calibration event are created beneath a densitometer; a risk factor profile beneath a patient; a session beneath an appointment; site measurements, the vertebral fracture assessment add-on, positioning reviews, submission and repeat beneath a session; refills and adherence reviews beneath a treatment plan. In every such case the path segments are validated **strictly left to right**, and the leftmost failure is what the caller sees. A request naming a nonexistent densitometer and a nonexistent calibration event reports the densitometer as missing; a request naming a real densitometer but a nonexistent event reports the event.

Parent-derived foreign keys are taken from the path and never from the body. A phantom run's `densitometer_id`, a calibration event's `densitometer_id`, a risk factor profile's `patient_id`, a session's `scan_appointment_id`, a site measurement's `scan_session_id`, a positioning review's `scan_session_id` and a refill's `treatment_plan_id` are all read from the URL; if a client also supplies one of them in the body it is ignored rather than honoured. Foreign keys that identify a *sibling* rather than a parent do travel in the body, and are resolved after every path segment has resolved, in the order the operation declares its inputs.

Nested list reads are scoped to their parent and never leak across it: a device's calibration history contains only that device's events, a patient's risk factor history only that patient's profiles, a session's site measurements only that session's rows.

### 2.9. Conserved Counters, Cascades and Compensating Actions

Two counters on the `Patient` record are **conserved quantities**, meaning the system must be able to prove them from the rows they summarise at any instant, not merely increment them optimistically. `open_scheduled_order_count` must always equal the number of that patient's orders whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`. `lifetime_screening_scan_count` must always equal the number of `FINALIZED` reports whose session belongs to that patient. Every operation that could disturb either identity carries an explicit compensating step, and those steps are spelled out where the workflows are described: booking a slot increments the open-order counter and cancelling that slot decrements it, so book-then-cancel returns the counter to its pre-booking value; finalizing a report increments the lifetime tally and amending that report decrements it, so finalize-then-amend leaves the tally exactly as it was before the finalize, and finalizing the successor report restores it.

Three cascades in this program touch more than one aggregate, and each is referred to by name throughout §3 so that its links can be traced. **Cascade A** begins when a report is finalized and propagates through the patient's screening counters into the re-evaluation and possible denial of that patient's open orders and the cancellation of their booked slots. **Cascade B** begins when a phantom quality-control run drives a device onto hold and propagates into the cancellation of imminent slots on that device and the reversion of the orders behind them. **Cascade C** begins when a cross-calibration event is approved and propagates into the device's calibration generation and correction factor, and from there into how every later acquisition scores its prior. Each cascade link is a genuine mutation of the affected resource: it refreshes that resource's `updated_at` and increments its `version` exactly as a direct write would, and cascade-driven mutations of resources that carry neither field simply write the field values.

Cascade ordering is deterministic wherever more than one row is affected. Orders re-evaluated by cascade A are processed in `created_at` ascending order. Slots cancelled by cascade B are processed in `start_utc` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. Wherever a device's day of appointments changes — a booking, a cancellation, a system cancellation — the `slot_index` of every remaining non-cancelled slot on that device and that UTC calendar date is recomputed, so inserting a slot earlier in the day shifts the later ones up and removing one shifts them down.

### 2.10. Snapshot Semantics and the Comparability Invariant

Serial comparison only means something when the two studies being compared were produced under the same measurement conditions, and the system enforces that by **snapshotting** device state onto each acquisition at the moment the acquisition begins, rather than reading device state at report time. A session freezes the device's `calibration_generation`, its least significant change into `applied_lsc_g_cm2`, and its correction factor into `applied_cross_cal_factor`. It also freezes its serial-comparison prior — both the chosen `prior_session_id` and the comparability verdict `prior_comparison_status` — at creation, and never revisits either. The consequence is that a precision assessment accepted tomorrow cannot retroactively change how a study acquired today is interpreted, and that a study acquired before an equipment change keeps being interpreted against the calibration it was actually taken under.

Comparability is never assumed across hardware. Manufacturers differ irreconcilably in their reference databases and acquisition geometry, so a prior acquired on a different device is never treated as directly comparable, and a prior acquired on the *same* device but at an earlier calibration generation is comparable only after the device's correction factor is applied to it. Where the system cannot establish comparability, it says so explicitly on the report rather than quietly reporting a change, and it requires the interpreting physician to write a narrative explaining the gap before that report can be finalized.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Program Foundations: Payer Policies, Patients and Clinical Risk Intake

Before any imaging can be scheduled, the program administrator registers the payers it bills. A payer policy is created with a short `payer_code` of 1 to 16 characters that must be unique across the whole catalogue — a collision is refused with `ERR_PAYER_CODE_TAKEN` — and a human-readable `name` of 1 to 120 characters. The policy then carries the three coverage floors that arbitrate how often the payer will fund a scan, each expressed in whole months between 1 and 120 inclusive: `baseline_screening_min_interval_months`, which defaults to two years and governs both baseline screening and routine serial monitoring; `high_risk_min_interval_months`, which defaults to one year and governs high-risk case finding; and `treatment_monitoring_min_interval_months`, which also defaults to one year and governs monitoring of a patient already on therapy. Policies are immutable once registered: there is no update operation, and a policy row exposes only its `id` and `created_at`, with no `updated_at` and no `version`.

Four further policy fields shape behaviour further downstream and are worth reading together. `vfa_covered` defaults to true; when it is false the payer never funds the vertebral fracture assessment add-on, whatever the clinical picture. `vfa_requires_height_loss_cm` defaults to 4.0 centimetres, must lie between 0.0 and 20.0 inclusive with at most one decimal place, and names the historical height loss that on its own establishes clinical eligibility for that add-on. `repeat_scan_grace_days` defaults to 30 days, accepts 0 through 180 inclusive, and defines how far *before* a patient's next eligible date a scan is still funded — inside that window coverage is not lost, it is merely downgraded. `max_lifetime_screening_scans` defaults to 6, accepts 1 through 40 inclusive, and is a hard cap on the number of finalized reports the payer will ever fund for one patient. Finally, `appeal_allowed` defaults to false and is a conditional feature flag: it decides whether an order the payer would refuse is rejected outright or is instead persisted in a denied, appealable state.

A `Scheduler` or `ProgramAdmin` registers each patient with an `mrn` of 1 to 24 characters, unique across the roster — a duplicate is refused with `ERR_MRN_TAKEN` — together with `primary_payer_policy_id` naming the policy that supplies every frequency, grace and lifetime limit applied to them, a `birth_date` in year-month-day form that must be a valid calendar date strictly earlier than the logical clock's date, a `sex` of either `FEMALE` or `MALE`, an optional `postmenopausal` flag defaulting to false, and `baseline_height_cm`, the patient's historical maximum recorded height, which must be greater than 80.0 and at most 230.0 centimetres with at most one decimal place. A freshly registered patient has `open_scheduled_order_count` and `lifetime_screening_scan_count` both at zero and `baseline_report_id`, `last_finalized_report_id`, `current_screening_interval_months` and `next_eligible_scan_date` all null — which is precisely why a patient's very first order is unconditionally covered.

Clinical risk factors are recorded as a **profile**, not as patient columns, because they change over time and because fracture risk arithmetic must be reproducible against the exact profile it consumed. Either a `Scheduler` or an `InterpretingPhysician` records one, supplying `expected_patient_version` as described in §2.6, the patient's current standing `height_cm` (greater than 80.0, at most 230.0, at most one decimal place) and `weight_kg` (greater than 20.0, at most 300.0, at most one decimal place), and then the risk factors themselves: `prior_fragility_fracture` for a previous low-trauma fracture after age forty, `parental_hip_fracture`, `current_smoker`, `glucocorticoid_use` for current or past systemic steroid exposure, `rheumatoid_arthritis`, `secondary_osteoporosis` for a disorder strongly associated with bone loss — all six booleans defaulting to false — and `alcohol_units_per_day`, a number defaulting to zero, accepted from 0.0 through 40.0 inclusive with at most one decimal place.

Three values are derived at intake. `bmi` is the weight in kilograms divided by the square of the height expressed in metres — that is, the height in centimetres divided by one hundred — rounded half up to two decimal places. `height_loss_cm` is the patient's `baseline_height_cm` minus the profile's `height_cm`, rounded half up to one decimal place; it is allowed to be negative, because a patient may measure taller than their recorded historical maximum. `clinical_risk_factor_count` is the number of the six booleans that are true, plus one more when `alcohol_units_per_day` is at least 3.0. The profile stamps `recorded_at_utc` from the logical clock and is created with `status` `ACTIVE`; any profile that was previously `ACTIVE` for that patient is moved to `SUPERSEDED` in the same operation, so exactly one profile per patient is ever `ACTIVE`. Recording a profile is a mutation of the patient as well, so the patient's `version` increments — a caller who holds the pre-call version must re-read the patient before its next optimistic write.

### 3.2. The Densitometer Registry and Precision Certification

A densitometer enters the registry with its `serial_number` as printed on the gantry, 1 to 32 characters and unique across every device — a repeat registration is refused with `ERR_SERIAL_NUMBER_TAKEN`. Its `manufacturer` is one of `HOLOGIC`, `GE_LUNAR` or `NORLAND`; its `model` is the vendor designation, 1 to 64 characters; its `site_code`, 1 to 16 characters, names the imaging site the device physically lives at. Two phantom constants establish the device's quality-control chart: `phantom_baseline_bmd_g_cm2`, the established phantom mean that serves as the centre line, greater than 0.0 and at most 2.0 with exactly four decimal places, and `phantom_baseline_sd_g_cm2`, the established phantom standard deviation that serves as the control-limit unit, greater than 0.0 and at most 0.2, also with exactly four decimal places. Two scheduling parameters complete the record: `appointment_buffer_minutes`, defaulting to fifteen minutes and accepted from 0 through 120, applied on **both** sides of every occupied interval when testing for slot collisions, and `daily_scan_capacity`, defaulting to twelve and accepted from 1 through 60, capping how many live appointments the device will take on a single UTC calendar day.

A newly registered device is `ACTIVE`, at `calibration_generation` 1, with `cross_cal_factor` 1.0 and `consecutive_qc_pass_count` 0. Everything precision-related is still empty: `rms_sd_g_cm2`, `precision_degrees_of_freedom`, `lsc_g_cm2`, `precision_technologist_user_id` and `precision_certified_at` are all null, as are `last_qc_result`, `last_qc_run_at`, `qc_hold_started_at` and `qc_hold_reason_code`. A device in that condition cannot acquire anything: a null `lsc_g_cm2` blocks every acquisition on the device, because without a least significant change there is no way to interpret a change against noise. Attempting to start an acquisition on such a device is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_PRECISION_NOT_CERTIFIED`.

Certification comes from a **precision assessment**: an in-vivo duplicate-scan study in which a single technologist rescans a cohort of patients and reports the root-mean-square standard deviation of the repeats. A `ProgramAdmin` submits it with `expected_densitometer_version`, the opaque `technologist_user_id` of the technologist who performed the study, `patient_count`, `scans_per_patient`, and the study's `rms_sd_g_cm2`. The study's statistical power is the number of degrees of freedom it carries: `precision_degrees_of_freedom` is the patient count multiplied by one less than the scans per patient. That product must be **at least thirty**; below thirty the study is refused with `ERR_PRECISION_STUDY_UNDERPOWERED` and nothing whatsoever is persisted — the device keeps whatever precision state it already had. Thirty patients scanned twice yields exactly thirty degrees of freedom and is accepted; twenty-nine patients scanned twice yields twenty-nine and is refused; fifteen patients scanned three times also yields thirty and is accepted, because it is the product and not the patient count that carries the power.

When the study is accepted, the device's least significant change in absolute units is the reported root-mean-square standard deviation multiplied by 2.77 — the two-sided ninety-five-percent coefficient for a paired measurement — rounded half up to four decimal places, and written to `lsc_g_cm2`. A study reporting 0.0100 therefore certifies a least significant change of 0.0277. The device also records the supplied `rms_sd_g_cm2`, the acting study's `precision_technologist_user_id`, and `precision_certified_at` from the logical clock, and its `version` increments. Because sessions snapshot the threshold at creation, a later assessment moves the threshold only for acquisitions started after it: studies already in flight keep the value they froze.

Retirement is terminal. A `ProgramAdmin` may retire a device from `ACTIVE`, from `QC_HOLD` or from `CROSS_CAL_PENDING`, but only when no acquisition on that device is still `IN_PROGRESS` — a scan mid-flight must be finished or abandoned first. Retiring the device drives its `status` to `RETIRED` and unwinds the schedule around it: every appointment on the device whose `status` is `BOOKED` or `CHECKED_IN` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason_code` `DEVICE_QC_HOLD` and `cancelled_at_utc` stamped, and a `CHECKED_IN` slot is cancelled only when it holds no `IN_PROGRESS` acquisition. Each order behind a cancelled slot reverts from `SCHEDULED` to `REQUESTED` with `reschedule_required` set true, and the patient's `open_scheduled_order_count` decrements once per such order. Any calibration event on the device still `OPEN` or `PHANTOM_SET_COMPLETE` is closed out to `REJECTED` with `outcome_code` `REJECTED_EXCESSIVE_DRIFT` and `adjudicated_at_utc` stamped. Once `RETIRED`, the device refuses phantom runs, calibration events, precision assessments, a second retirement, any booking that names it and any acquisition on it, all with `ERR_DENSITOMETER_RETIRED`.

### 3.3. Phantom Quality Control, Out-of-Control Holds, and Cross-Calibration on Equipment Change

Each scanning day opens with a phantom scan. A `Technologist` — or a `ProgramAdmin` standing in — records the run against the device with a `purpose` of `DAILY_QC`, `CROSS_CAL_BASELINE` or `CROSS_CAL_NEW`, the measured `phantom_bmd_g_cm2` (greater than 0.0, at most 2.0, exactly four decimal places), and the `scan_mode` the phantom was acquired in, one of `ARRAY`, `FAST_ARRAY` or `HIGH_DEFINITION`. The `purpose` is polymorphic: it decides which control chart the run feeds, and it governs one conditional input. `calibration_event_id` is **required** when the purpose is `CROSS_CAL_BASELINE` or `CROSS_CAL_NEW`, and **must be absent** when the purpose is `DAILY_QC`; violating either half of that rule is a malformed request. The run stamps `performed_by_user_id` from the caller and `performed_at_utc` from the logical clock, and `performed_at_utc` is the primary sort key for every quality-control ordering, with `created_at` as the tie-break. A phantom run is an immutable historical fact: it exposes `id` and `created_at` and carries neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

Scoring is the same for every purpose. `deviation_g_cm2` is the measured phantom density minus the device's established `phantom_baseline_bmd_g_cm2`, rounded half up to four decimal places. `deviation_sd_units` is that deviation divided by the device's `phantom_baseline_sd_g_cm2`, rounded half up to three decimal places, and it keeps its sign. The `result` then reads the **already-rounded** three-decimal figure, never the raw quotient: an absolute value of at most 1.000 is a `PASS`, an absolute value greater than 1.000 but at most 1.500 is a `WARN`, and an absolute value greater than 1.500 is a `FAIL`. Both boundaries are inclusive on the gentler side, so exactly 1.000 passes and exactly 1.500 warns rather than failing.

A single out-of-limit reading is one kind of trouble; a sustained one-sided drift is another, and the chart detects it separately. `shift_detected` is computed only for `DAILY_QC` runs, and is true when this run together with the **two immediately preceding** `DAILY_QC` runs on the same device — the two ranked highest by `performed_at_utc` descending and then `created_at` descending among the runs before it — all show an absolute `deviation_sd_units` of at least 1.000 *and* all three share the same sign. A deviation of exactly zero has no sign and therefore breaks the chain. When fewer than two preceding daily runs exist the flag is false, so a device with only two daily runs in its whole history can never trip it. Note the deliberate asymmetry with the `PASS` band: the shift test is "at least 1.000" while the pass test is "at most 1.000", so a run whose own `result` is `PASS` at exactly 1.000 still counts toward a shift and can be the run that takes the device offline.

The device is then updated in exactly this order, and the first matching rule wins. A `FAIL` drives `status` from `ACTIVE` to `QC_HOLD` with `qc_hold_reason_code` `OUT_OF_CONTROL_FAIL`, `qc_hold_started_at` set to the run's `performed_at_utc`, and `consecutive_qc_pass_count` reset to zero. Otherwise a true `shift_detected` drives `status` to `QC_HOLD` with `qc_hold_reason_code` `SUSTAINED_SHIFT`, the same `qc_hold_started_at`, and the same reset — and this fires even when the run's own `result` is `PASS`, because three consecutive same-sided runs at or beyond the warning limit are an out-of-control condition regardless of any single reading. Otherwise a `WARN` leaves `status` untouched but still resets `consecutive_qc_pass_count` to zero. Otherwise, on a `PASS`, `consecutive_qc_pass_count` increments by one; and if the device was in `QC_HOLD` and the new count has reached two, the device returns to `ACTIVE` with `qc_hold_started_at` and `qc_hold_reason_code` both cleared to null. Two consecutive clean runs are required for release, so the first passing run inside a hold only advances the counter and the device stays on hold. In every case the device's `last_qc_result` and `last_qc_run_at` are refreshed from the most recent daily run.

**Cascade B** is what makes a hold consequential rather than advisory. When a device enters `QC_HOLD`, every appointment on that device whose `status` is `BOOKED` and whose `start_utc` falls **within forty-eight hours** of `qc_hold_started_at` is cancelled — that is the imminent-work horizon — processed in `start_utc` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, each moving `BOOKED` to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason_code` `DEVICE_QC_HOLD`. Each cancellation in turn reverts its owning order from `SCHEDULED` to `REQUESTED` with `reschedule_required` set true, and decrements that patient's `open_scheduled_order_count` by one. Slots further out than the horizon are left alone; they will be blocked at acquisition time if the hold persists. The `slot_index` of every remaining non-cancelled slot on the device and each affected UTC calendar date is recomputed. Releasing the device later does **not** restore anything: cancelled appointments stay cancelled and must be rebooked.

Cross-calibration runs behave differently in exactly two respects: they are scored identically but never change the device's `status` or its `consecutive_qc_pass_count`, and they never participate in shift detection. What they do instead is accumulate on the calibration event they name, incrementing its `baseline_run_count` or its `new_run_count`.

An equipment change invalidates the device's calibration outright, and the program handles it as a three-step adjudicated workflow. A `ProgramAdmin` opens a **calibration event** on the device with a `change_type` of `DETECTOR_REPLACEMENT`, `SOFTWARE_UPGRADE`, `XRAY_TUBE_REPLACEMENT` or `DEVICE_REPLACEMENT`, plus an optional free-text `change_note` of up to 240 characters referencing the service record. Opening is permitted only when the device is `ACTIVE` or `QC_HOLD` and no event on that device is currently `OPEN` or `PHANTOM_SET_COMPLETE`; because opening moves the device to `CROSS_CAL_PENDING`, an attempt to open a second concurrent event is refused with `ERR_DENSITOMETER_NOT_ACTIVE` rather than with any event-state error. The event is created with `status` `OPEN` and a `required_phantom_runs` count keyed to the severity of the change: three runs for a software upgrade, five for a detector replacement, five for an X-ray tube replacement, and ten for a whole-device replacement — required on **each** side of the change, not in total. The device's `consecutive_qc_pass_count` resets to zero. When the event is opened from `QC_HOLD`, the device's `qc_hold_started_at` and `qc_hold_reason_code` are deliberately preserved, so the original hold reason survives the calibration detour. Existing booked appointments are deliberately *not* cancelled here; they are blocked at acquisition time by `ERR_DENSITOMETER_NOT_ACTIVE` instead.

The technologist then scans the phantom repeatedly on the old configuration, tagging each run `CROSS_CAL_BASELINE`, and again on the new configuration, tagging each `CROSS_CAL_NEW`. Closing the event demands that both sides be complete: if either `baseline_run_count` or `new_run_count` is below `required_phantom_runs`, the close is refused with `ERR_PHANTOM_SET_INCOMPLETE`, and closing an event that is not `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_CALIBRATION_EVENT_NOT_OPEN`. On a successful close the event moves `OPEN` to `PHANTOM_SET_COMPLETE`, stamps `closed_at_utc`, and computes its comparison. `baseline_mean_bmd_g_cm2` is the arithmetic mean of `phantom_bmd_g_cm2` across the baseline runs, rounded half up to four decimal places; `new_mean_bmd_g_cm2` is the same mean across the new-configuration runs, likewise rounded to four places. Both means are rounded **before** anything is derived from them. `percent_difference` is one hundred multiplied by the new mean minus the baseline mean, divided by the baseline mean, rounded half up to two decimal places. From that figure the event precomputes its verdict: an absolute percent difference **below** 1.00 is `WITHIN_TOLERANCE_NO_FACTOR` with `proposed_cross_cal_factor` set to exactly 1.0000; an absolute percent difference from 1.00 through 5.00 inclusive is `FACTOR_APPLIED` with `proposed_cross_cal_factor` equal to the baseline mean divided by the new mean, rounded half up to four decimal places; and an absolute percent difference above 5.00 is `REJECTED_EXCESSIVE_DRIFT` with a null `proposed_cross_cal_factor`. A baseline mean of 1.0000 against a new mean of 1.0200 therefore gives a percent difference of 2.00, a `FACTOR_APPLIED` outcome, and a factor of 0.9804. Exactly 1.00 is `FACTOR_APPLIED`, not within tolerance; exactly 5.00 is still `FACTOR_APPLIED`, while 5.01 is excessive drift.

Adjudication is the final step and takes no request body at all — the verdict is derived entirely from the stored `percent_difference`, so there is nothing for a caller to influence. Adjudicating an event that is not `PHANTOM_SET_COMPLETE` is refused with `ERR_CALIBRATION_EVENT_NOT_CLOSED`. **Cascade C** runs when the absolute percent difference is at most 5.00: the event moves `PHANTOM_SET_COMPLETE` to `APPROVED` and stamps `adjudicated_at_utc`; the device's `cross_cal_factor` is set to exactly 1.0 when the `outcome_code` is `WITHIN_TOLERANCE_NO_FACTOR` and to the event's `proposed_cross_cal_factor` otherwise; the device's `calibration_generation` increments by one; the device returns from `CROSS_CAL_PENDING` to `ACTIVE`; and `qc_hold_started_at`, `qc_hold_reason_code` and `consecutive_qc_pass_count` are cleared to null, null and zero respectively. The third link of the cascade is the one that reaches into clinical interpretation: because the generation counter has moved, every acquisition created from now on snapshots the higher generation, and any prior study sitting at a lower generation is scored as cross-calibration-adjusted and has its density multiplied by the new factor before it is compared. Note that a within-tolerance outcome forces the device factor to exactly 1.0 even though the raw ratio of the two means is not exactly one.

The rejecting branch is deliberately not symmetric, and the difference matters. When the absolute percent difference exceeds 5.00 the event moves `PHANTOM_SET_COMPLETE` to `REJECTED` with `outcome_code` `REJECTED_EXCESSIVE_DRIFT` and `adjudicated_at_utc` stamped — but there is no generation bump, no change to `cross_cal_factor`, and no return to `ACTIVE`. The device stays in `CROSS_CAL_PENDING` and therefore stays unusable for acquisition until a fresh calibration event is opened, populated and approved. Drift that large means the new configuration is not a corrected version of the old one; it is a different instrument, and no multiplicative factor can honestly bridge it.

### 3.4. Ordering a Study and Booking It onto a Device

A `Scheduler` or an `InterpretingPhysician` opens a study by creating a **scan order** naming the `patient_id`, the clinical `indication` — one of `BASELINE_SCREENING`, `SERIAL_MONITORING`, `TREATMENT_MONITORING` or `HIGH_RISK_CASE_FINDING` — the `requested_sites` to be measured, the `requested_scan_date`, and optionally `vfa_requested`. The `requested_sites` array must be non-empty with unique members drawn from `LUMBAR_SPINE`, `FEMORAL_NECK`, `TOTAL_HIP` and `FOREARM_33`, and every site listed there must eventually be measured before the acquisition can be submitted, so the order is a binding checklist rather than a suggestion. The `requested_scan_date` is a `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date on or after the logical clock date; a date in the past is refused. `vfa_requested` defaults to false, and a vertebral fracture assessment offered on a session whose order did not request one is refused. The order records `ordered_by_user_id` from the caller and `ordered_at_utc` from the clock, and starts in `status` `REQUESTED`.

Two derived fields are written at creation. `required_densitometer_id` pins serial work to one instrument: for an `indication` of `SERIAL_MONITORING` or `TREATMENT_MONITORING` it is the `densitometer_id` of the scan session behind the patient's `baseline_report_id`, and it is null for `BASELINE_SCREENING` and `HIGH_RISK_CASE_FINDING` and null whenever `baseline_report_id` is itself null. `reschedule_required` starts false and is set true only when a system cascade reverts the order from `SCHEDULED` back to `REQUESTED`; a scheduler who cancels a slot on the patient's own initiative leaves it false, which is how the queue distinguishes "we broke this booking" from "the patient changed their mind".

The payer frequency test is then evaluated, and its order of operations is fixed. The **lifetime cap is checked first**: if the patient's `lifetime_screening_scan_count` has reached the policy's `max_lifetime_screening_scans`, `frequency_eligibility` is `INELIGIBLE_LIFETIME_CAP` and `earliest_allowed_date` is copied from `next_eligible_scan_date`, even if the requested date is years past that point. Otherwise, if `next_eligible_scan_date` is null — which is the case for every patient with no finalized report — the verdict is `ELIGIBLE` with a null `earliest_allowed_date`, so a patient's first study is unconditionally eligible. Otherwise `earliest_allowed_date` is set from `next_eligible_scan_date` and the requested date is compared against it: on or after it the verdict is `ELIGIBLE`; earlier than it but no earlier than that date less the policy's `repeat_scan_grace_days` whole days the verdict is `ELIGIBLE_WITH_GRACE`; earlier still the verdict is `INELIGIBLE_TOO_SOON`. Both comparisons are inclusive, so a request landing exactly on `earliest_allowed_date` is plainly eligible and one landing exactly the full grace window before it still qualifies for grace, while one day earlier than that does not. `payer_floor_months` is recorded from the policy at the same moment, selected by indication: `baseline_screening_min_interval_months` for `BASELINE_SCREENING` and `SERIAL_MONITORING`, `high_risk_min_interval_months` for `HIGH_RISK_CASE_FINDING`, and `treatment_monitoring_min_interval_months` for `TREATMENT_MONITORING`.

What happens to an ineligible verdict at creation depends on the policy's `appeal_allowed` flag, and the two branches differ in whether anything is stored at all. When `appeal_allowed` is false the request is rejected outright with `ERR_PAYER_FREQUENCY_LIMIT` for a too-soon verdict or `ERR_LIFETIME_SCAN_CAP_EXCEEDED` for a cap verdict, both carrying HTTP 402 Payment Required, and no order is persisted. When `appeal_allowed` is true the order **is** created, immediately in `status` `DENIED`, with `denial_reason_code` set to `PAYER_FREQUENCY_LIMIT` or `LIFETIME_CAP_EXCEEDED` — a durable record the program can appeal against. `denial_reason_code` is populated exactly when `status` is `DENIED` and is null otherwise, and its third value, `INTERVAL_REVISED_BY_REPORT`, is reserved for orders denied later by the finalization cascade described in §3.8.

A `Scheduler` books the order onto a device by creating a **scan appointment** carrying `scan_order_id`, `densitometer_id`, `start_utc` and `end_utc`. The window must be well formed: `start_utc` on or after the logical clock instant, `end_utc` strictly after `start_utc` and no more than 240 minutes after it. The interval is treated as half-open — inclusive of `start_utc`, exclusive of `end_utc` — and `scheduled_duration_minutes` is the whole number of minutes between them. Booking is permitted only when the order is in `status` `REQUESTED` and its `frequency_eligibility` is `ELIGIBLE` or `ELIGIBLE_WITH_GRACE`; a `DENIED` order can never be scheduled. If the order carries a non-null `required_densitometer_id` that differs from the requested `densitometer_id`, the booking is refused with `ERR_SERIAL_DEVICE_MISMATCH`, because a serial comparison performed on a different instrument is not a comparison at all.

Three separate capacity rules then guard the device's day. The overlap test applies the device's own `appointment_buffer_minutes` symmetrically, on **both** sides of every occupied interval: an existing appointment on the same `densitometer_id` in `status` `BOOKED` or `CHECKED_IN` conflicts when the new start falls before the existing end extended by the buffer **and** the existing start falls before the new end extended by the buffer, which is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_SLOT_CONFLICT`. Separately, a patient may hold only one live slot per UTC calendar date: another appointment for the same patient in `status` `BOOKED` or `CHECKED_IN` whose `start_utc` falls on the same UTC date is refused with `ERR_PATIENT_DOUBLE_BOOKED`. And a device may not exceed its `daily_scan_capacity`: when the device already holds that many appointments in `status` `BOOKED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `COMPLETED` starting on the requested UTC date, the booking is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_DAILY_CAPACITY_FULL`. Note that the capacity count includes `COMPLETED` slots while the overlap test does not, so a fully worked day blocks further booking even though none of its intervals collide.

A successful booking creates the slot in `status` `BOOKED`, moves the order from `REQUESTED` to `SCHEDULED`, increments the patient's `open_scheduled_order_count` by one — the forward half of the open-order invariant — and clears `reschedule_required` to false. It also assigns `slot_index`: the 1-based position of this appointment among all appointments on the same `densitometer_id` whose `start_utc` falls on the same UTC calendar date and whose `status` is `BOOKED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `COMPLETED`, ordered by `start_utc` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. Because the index is positional rather than assigned, it is recomputed for every affected sibling whenever that day's set changes: inserting a slot earlier in the day shifts the later ones up, and cancelling one shifts them back down. Cancelled slots are excluded from the numbering entirely.

A `Scheduler` may cancel a booked slot. Cancellation takes no request body: it moves `status` from `BOOKED` to `CANCELLED`, sets `cancellation_reason_code` to `PATIENT_REQUEST`, stamps `cancelled_at_utc` from the clock, reverts the owning order from `SCHEDULED` to `REQUESTED`, and decrements the patient's `open_scheduled_order_count`. That decrement is the compensating half of the open-order invariant: after a book-then-cancel pair the counter reads exactly its pre-booking value, and it must never drift below zero. The remaining non-cancelled slots on that device and UTC date have their `slot_index` recomputed. The other two values of `cancellation_reason_code` — `DEVICE_QC_HOLD` and `ORDER_DENIED` — are written only by system cascades, never by a scheduler, so the reason code identifies who broke the appointment.

### 3.5. Acquisition at the Device

A `Technologist` starts work by creating a **scan session** on a booked appointment, supplying only the `scan_mode` — `ARRAY`, `FAST_ARRAY` or `HIGH_DEFINITION` — since everything else is derived. The appointment must be in `status` `BOOKED` and moves to `CHECKED_IN`; the device must be `ACTIVE`, so a device on quality-control hold or awaiting cross-calibration adjudication is refused with `ERR_DENSITOMETER_NOT_ACTIVE`; the device's `lsc_g_cm2` must not be null, so an uncertified instrument is refused with `ERR_DEVICE_PRECISION_NOT_CERTIFIED`; and no other session on the same appointment may be `IN_PROGRESS`. The session is created with `acquisition_status` `IN_PROGRESS`, `review_status` `NOT_REVIEWED` and `repeat_count` zero, with `scan_appointment_id` taken from the path rather than the body, and with `technologist_user_id` taken from the caller's `X-User-ID`. That user is the only one permitted to post measurements, record a vertebral fracture assessment, submit or repeat this session — and, by the segregation-of-duties rule, the one user forbidden from reviewing its positioning.

Creation snapshots four values from the device onto the session, and the snapshot is the whole point: `densitometer_id`, `calibration_generation`, `applied_lsc_g_cm2` and `applied_cross_cal_factor`. `patient_id` is likewise denormalized from the owning order. None of them is ever refreshed. A precision assessment run tomorrow cannot retroactively change how today's study was interpreted, and a cross-calibration approved next week cannot silently redefine what today's session considered comparable.

The comparison prior is also fixed at creation, by a three-stage waterfall, and it is never recomputed afterwards. The candidate pool is every other session of the same patient whose `acquisition_status` is `SUBMITTED` or `ARCHIVED`, whose `review_status` is `ACCEPTED` or `ACCEPTED_WITH_LIMITATION`, and which carries at least one report in `status` `FINALIZED` — an unreviewed, repeat-required or unreported study is not a clinical prior no matter how recent. **Stage one** looks only at candidates on the same `densitometer_id` and takes the one with the highest `submitted_at_utc`, breaking ties by the highest `created_at`; `prior_comparison_status` is then `PRIOR_CROSS_CAL_ADJUSTED` when that prior's `calibration_generation` is lower than the new session's, and `COMPARABLE_SAME_DEVICE` when the generations match. **Stage two** runs only when stage one found nothing: it ranks the whole pool the same way regardless of device and marks the result `NOT_COMPARABLE_DEVICE_MISMATCH`. **Stage three** applies when the pool is empty: `prior_session_id` stays null and `prior_comparison_status` is `BASELINE_NO_PRIOR`. A same-device prior always beats a more recent prior on another device, because the ranking never runs across the two stages at once.

For each site the technologist posts a **site measurement** carrying `site_code`, the acquired `bmd_g_cm2`, `area_cm2` and `bmc_g`, and the four reference figures from the vendor database — `reference_mean_bmd_g_cm2`, `reference_sd_g_cm2`, `age_matched_mean_bmd_g_cm2` and `age_matched_sd_g_cm2`. Density values are greater than 0.0 and at most 2.5 with exactly four decimal places; reference standard deviations are greater than 0.0 and at most 0.5 with exactly four decimal places; `area_cm2` is greater than 0.0 and at most 200.0 with at most two decimals, and `bmc_g` greater than 0.0 and at most 400.0 with at most two decimals. `included_vertebrae`, a unique subset of `L1`, `L2`, `L3` and `L4`, is **required** when `site_code` is `LUMBAR_SPINE` and **must be absent** for every other site. `valid_vertebral_count` is the length of that array for a lumbar row and zero for every other site. At most one non-superseded measurement may exist per session per site, and a second one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SITE_MEASUREMENT`; the site must also appear in the order's `requested_sites`, or the row is refused with `ERR_SITE_NOT_REQUESTED`. A lumbar row with fewer than two valid vertebrae is refused outright with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_VALID_VERTEBRAE`, which is why the stored `site_usable_for_diagnosis` flag — false for `LUMBAR_SPINE` below two valid vertebrae, true otherwise — reads true on every row the system actually accepted. Each accepted row computes `t_score` as the measured density minus `reference_mean_bmd_g_cm2` divided by `reference_sd_g_cm2`, rounded half up to **one** decimal place, and `z_score` the same way against `age_matched_mean_bmd_g_cm2` and `age_matched_sd_g_cm2`. That one-decimal value, not the unrounded quotient, is what diagnosis and fracture-risk scoring consume downstream. `superseded` starts false, and `measured_site_count` on the session counts the non-superseded rows.

When the order requested the vertebral add-on, the technologist records it by supplying `genant_grades`, an array of integers from 0 through 3, one per assessed vertebral level. Three refusals are checked in this exact order: the add-on was never ordered, giving `ERR_VFA_NOT_ORDERED`; the patient does not meet clinical eligibility, giving `ERR_VFA_NOT_ELIGIBLE`; and the payer policy does not cover it, giving `ERR_VFA_NOT_COVERED`. Clinical eligibility precedes coverage deliberately — the program refuses to tell a payer it declined a study the patient did not qualify for. `vfa_eligibility_code` is decided by the first matching test against the patient's active risk-factor profile: a `height_loss_cm` at or above the policy's `vfa_requires_height_loss_cm` gives `ELIGIBLE_HEIGHT_LOSS`; otherwise a true `glucocorticoid_use` gives `ELIGIBLE_GLUCOCORTICOID`; otherwise an age of at least seventy years gives `ELIGIBLE_AGE_THRESHOLD`; otherwise the code is `NOT_ELIGIBLE` and the request is refused. On success `vfa_max_genant_grade` is the largest grade submitted, `vfa_fracture_count` is the number of entries at grade 1 or above, and `vfa_performed` becomes true.

Submitting the acquisition is the technologist's last act on the session and is gated on completeness. The session must be `IN_PROGRESS`, the caller must be its own `technologist_user_id`, `measured_site_count` must be at least one, **every** entry in the order's `requested_sites` must have a non-superseded measurement — otherwise `ERR_REQUESTED_SITES_INCOMPLETE` — and when `vfa_requested` is true, `vfa_performed` must also be true, otherwise `ERR_VFA_NOT_PERFORMED`. On success `submitted_at_utc` is stamped from the clock, and its calendar date becomes the scan date all later interval arithmetic is measured from. The diagnostic selection then runs, `acquisition_status` moves `IN_PROGRESS` to `SUBMITTED`, the appointment moves `CHECKED_IN` to `COMPLETED`, the owning order moves `SCHEDULED` to `COMPLETED`, and the patient's `open_scheduled_order_count` decrements — the second compensating path for that counter.

The diagnostic selection that runs at submission decides which single number carries the diagnosis. The candidate set is the non-superseded measurements with `site_usable_for_diagnosis` true whose `site_code` is one of the three central sites `LUMBAR_SPINE`, `FEMORAL_NECK` and `TOTAL_HIP`. Only when that set is empty do non-superseded `FOREARM_33` rows become the candidate set — the forearm is a fallback skeletal site, never a competitor to a central one, so a session with a central measurement never diagnoses off the forearm even when the forearm reads lower. `lowest_t_score` is the minimum `t_score` over the candidate set, and `lowest_t_score_site` is the `site_code` that supplied it, with ties broken by the fixed precedence `LUMBAR_SPINE`, then `FEMORAL_NECK`, then `TOTAL_HIP`, then `FOREARM_33` — never by `created_at` and never by `id`, so the answer does not depend on the order the technologist happened to enter the sites. `lowest_z_score` is the minimum `z_score` over that same candidate set and may well come from a different site than the one that supplied the lowest T-score.

### 3.6. Independent Positioning Review and the Repeat Cycle

Densitometry results are only as good as the patient's positioning, so every submitted study is reviewed for acquisition quality by someone other than the person who acquired it. A `QualityReviewer` may review any submitted session, and a `Technologist` may review a **colleague's** session — but if the caller's `X-User-ID` equals the session's `technologist_user_id` the review is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, which is the entire reason the technologist role appears on this operation at all. The session must be `SUBMITTED`, and its `review_status` must still be `NOT_REVIEWED`; a second review of the same session is refused with `ERR_SESSION_ALREADY_REVIEWED`. The review records `reviewer_user_id` from the caller and `reviewed_at_utc` from the clock, and like a phantom run it is an immutable observation carrying only `id` and `created_at` alongside its own fields.

The reviewer submits `defect_codes`, a possibly empty array of unique members drawn from `ROTATION_FEMUR`, `ABDUCTION_FEMUR`, `SPINE_NOT_STRAIGHT`, `ARTIFACT_IN_FIELD`, `INCORRECT_ROI`, `MOTION_ARTIFACT` and `INADEQUATE_SCAN_LENGTH`, plus an optional `reviewer_note` of up to 240 characters. `severity_score` is the weighted sum over the distinct codes present, and the weights encode how badly each defect corrupts the measurement: `ROTATION_FEMUR`, `INCORRECT_ROI` and `INADEQUATE_SCAN_LENGTH` weigh three each; `ABDUCTION_FEMUR`, `SPINE_NOT_STRAIGHT` and `MOTION_ARTIFACT` weigh two each; and `ARTIFACT_IN_FIELD` weighs one. An empty array scores zero. Because the members are unique the same defect can never be double-counted.

`repeat_criterion_met` is true when `severity_score` reaches three, so any single heavy defect is enough on its own, while two light defects — an artefact in the field plus a motion artefact, scoring three together — reach the threshold jointly. The `outcome` then depends on how many repeats the session has already been through: when the criterion is not met the outcome is `ACCEPTED`; when it is met and `repeat_count` is below two the outcome is `REPEAT_REQUIRED`; and when it is met but `repeat_count` has already reached two the outcome is `ACCEPTED_WITH_LIMITATION`. That third branch is the program's concession to reality — after two repeats the patient has absorbed enough radiation and enough of the schedule, so the study is accepted with its limitations documented rather than repeated a third time. Whatever the outcome, it is copied straight onto the session's `review_status`.

A `REPEAT_REQUIRED` verdict sends the session backwards, and only its own technologist may drive it there: the caller must be the session's `technologist_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_SESSION_TECHNOLOGIST`. Repeating moves `acquisition_status` from `SUBMITTED` back to `IN_PROGRESS` and `review_status` from `REPEAT_REQUIRED` back to `NOT_REVIEWED`, increments `repeat_count` by one, resets `submitted_at_utc`, `lowest_t_score`, `lowest_t_score_site` and `lowest_z_score` to null, and marks **every** existing measurement on the session `superseded`, which returns `measured_site_count` to zero and requires the whole site set to be re-acquired from scratch. Superseded rows are excluded from every aggregate and from the duplicate-site check, so the same `site_code` may be posted again. The appointment stays `COMPLETED` and the order stays `COMPLETED` throughout — the patient is still on the table for the same visit, so nothing about the schedule is undone. This is what makes the session's two state dimensions genuinely independent: `review_status` evolves on its own except for this one coupling, where `REPEAT_REQUIRED` drags `acquisition_status` back with it.

The two refusals around repeating are deliberately distinct. A session whose `review_status` is neither `REPEAT_REQUIRED` nor `ACCEPTED_WITH_LIMITATION` is refused with `ERR_SESSION_REPEAT_NOT_REQUIRED`, because there is nothing to repeat. An `ACCEPTED_WITH_LIMITATION` session falls through that check and is refused instead with `ERR_REPEAT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, because it reached that status precisely by exhausting its two repeats — the more specific answer, and the one that tells the technologist the door is closed rather than merely unopened.

### 3.7. Fracture Risk Assessment and Drafting the Diagnostic Report

An `InterpretingPhysician` may compute a **FRAX assessment** — a ten-year fracture probability — naming the `patient_id`, the `risk_factor_profile_id` it reads its clinical factors from, and optionally a `scan_session_id`. That last field alone selects between the two variants: supplying it produces `variant` `WITH_BMD`, omitting it produces `WITHOUT_BMD`. The profile must belong to the named patient and must be in `status` `ACTIVE`, so a `SUPERSEDED` profile is refused with `ERR_RISK_PROFILE_NOT_ACTIVE` and a profile or session belonging to a different patient is refused with `ERR_FRAX_PATIENT_MISMATCH`. When a session is supplied it must carry a usable, non-superseded `FEMORAL_NECK` measurement, since that is the only site FRAX accepts; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_FEMORAL_NECK_REQUIRED`. The assessment records `assessed_by_user_id` and `assessed_at_utc`.

Scoring is an additive points model, and every term is rounded to **two** decimal places before the terms are summed. `age_years` is the completed whole years between the patient's `birth_date` and the logical clock date. `age_points` is the years above the reference age of fifty multiplied by one tenth of a point per year, clamped at zero from below, so a patient younger than fifty contributes 0.00 rather than a negative amount. `sex_points` is 0.60 for a `FEMALE` patient and 0.00 for a `MALE` one. `bmi_used` is copied from the referenced profile's `bmi`, and `bmi_points` reads it in three tiers: 0.80 points below 20.00, 0.00 points from 20.00 through 30.00 inclusive, and −0.40 points above 30.00 — so exactly 20.00 sits in the middle tier while 19.99 falls into the low-BMI tier. `clinical_risk_points` is the sum of the weights of the risk factors that are true on the profile: 1.90 for `prior_fragility_fracture`, 1.30 for `parental_hip_fracture`, 0.90 for `current_smoker`, 1.60 for `glucocorticoid_use`, 1.20 for `rheumatoid_arthritis`, 0.80 for `secondary_osteoporosis`, and 1.00 when `alcohol_units_per_day` is at or above 3.0 — exactly 3.0 counts, 2.9 does not. `bmd_points` is 0.00 for the `WITHOUT_BMD` variant, and for `WITH_BMD` it is the femoral-neck `t_score` multiplied by −1.20. Watch the sign: the T-score of a low-density patient is negative, and multiplying it by a negative coefficient **increases** the score, so omitting the BMD term for an osteopenic patient lowers the estimated probability rather than raising it.

`raw_score` is the sum of those five already-rounded terms, itself rounded to two decimals. The two probabilities are then linear functions of it, each clamped and only then rounded to one decimal place: `major_osteoporotic_10yr_pct` is 3.0 plus 6.0 times the raw score, floored at 0.5 and capped at 60.0; `hip_fracture_10yr_pct` is 0.4 plus 1.6 times the raw score, floored at 0.1 and capped at 30.0. The clamps are applied before the final rounding, never after. `treatment_threshold_met` is true when the major-osteoporotic probability reaches 20.0 **or** the hip probability reaches 3.0, and `threshold_basis` records which: `BOTH` when both thresholds are met, `MAJOR_OSTEOPOROTIC` when only the major one is, `HIP` when only the hip one is, and `NONE` when neither is. A sixty-seven-year-old female with a `bmi_used` of 20.31, no clinical risk factors and a femoral-neck `t_score` of −2.0 therefore scores `age_points` 1.70, `sex_points` 0.60, `bmi_points` 0.00, `clinical_risk_points` 0.00 and `bmd_points` 2.40, for a `raw_score` of 4.70, a major probability of 31.2, a hip probability of 7.9 and a `threshold_basis` of `BOTH`. The same patient assessed `WITHOUT_BMD` scores a `raw_score` of 2.30, a major probability of 16.8 and a hip probability of 4.1 — still treatment-eligible, but on the hip threshold alone.

The physician then drafts a **diagnostic report** on the session, naming `scan_session_id` and optionally `frax_assessment_id`, `non_comparability_note` and `vfa_narrative`. The session must be `SUBMITTED`, so reporting a study still `IN_PROGRESS` is refused with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_SUBMITTED`, and at most one report per session may be in `status` `DRAFT` or `FINALIZED` at a time, so a second draft is refused with `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_EXISTS`. Any attached assessment must belong to the same patient as the session. The report is created in `status` `DRAFT` with a null `amends_report_id`, and the entire interpretation is computed at that moment. The same three fields — `frax_assessment_id`, `non_comparability_note` and `vfa_narrative` — are the **only** writable ones on an update, and every computed field is fully re-derived on each update, so attaching a FRAX assessment to an existing draft can flip its `recommended_action` outright. Updating a report that is no longer `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_REPORT_NOT_DRAFT`.

The interpretation runs as seven ordered steps, each reading the outputs of the ones before it. **Step one** classifies the density. `patient_age_years` is the completed whole years from `birth_date` to the clock date at report creation. The WHO T-score path applies when the patient is a `FEMALE` with `postmenopausal` true, or a `MALE` aged at least fifty; `classification_basis` is then `T_SCORE_WHO` and `diagnostic_category` reads the session's `lowest_t_score`: `OSTEOPOROSIS` at −2.5 or below, `OSTEOPENIA` above −2.5 and at or below −1.0, and `NORMAL` above −1.0. Both boundaries are inclusive on the more severe side, so exactly −2.5 is osteoporosis and exactly −1.0 is osteopenia. Otherwise the age-matched path applies, `classification_basis` is `Z_SCORE_AGE_MATCHED`, and `diagnostic_category` reads `lowest_z_score`: `BELOW_EXPECTED_RANGE_FOR_AGE` at −2.0 or below and `WITHIN_EXPECTED_RANGE_FOR_AGE` otherwise. A premenopausal woman or a man under fifty is never given a WHO label, because the young-adult reference is not the right comparison for them.

**Step two** interprets the serial change against the least significant change. When the session's `prior_session_id` is null, `lsc_interpretation` is `NO_PRIOR` and the change fields stay null. When `prior_comparison_status` is `NOT_COMPARABLE_DEVICE_MISMATCH`, it is `NOT_COMPARABLE_DEVICE` and the change fields stay null — a comparison across instruments is refused outright rather than attempted with a correction factor. Otherwise `lsc_site_code` is chosen as the first entry in the fixed precedence `TOTAL_HIP`, `LUMBAR_SPINE`, `FEMORAL_NECK` that has a non-superseded, diagnosis-usable measurement on **both** the current and the prior session; if no site in that precedence qualifies, `lsc_interpretation` is `NO_COMMON_SITE` with null change fields. With a site chosen, `current_bmd_used_g_cm2` is the current session's raw `bmd_g_cm2` at that site and `prior_bmd_used_g_cm2` is the prior session's density at that site multiplied by the cross-calibration factor — 1.0000 unless `prior_comparison_status` is `PRIOR_CROSS_CAL_ADJUSTED`, in which case it is the session's snapshotted `applied_cross_cal_factor` — rounded to four decimal places **before** any differencing. `bmd_change_g_cm2` is the current value minus that adjusted prior, signed, rounded to four decimals. `bmd_change_pct` is one hundred times the same difference divided by the adjusted prior, rounded to two decimals and derived in one step from the two four-decimal densities, never from the already-rounded change. The verdict then compares the change against the session's `applied_lsc_g_cm2`: an absolute change below the LSC is `NO_SIGNIFICANT_CHANGE`, a change at or below the negated LSC is `SIGNIFICANT_LOSS`, and a change at or above the LSC is `SIGNIFICANT_GAIN`. A prior total-hip density of 0.7000 carried at a cross-calibration factor of 0.9804 gives an adjusted prior of 0.6863, so a current 0.6700 is a change of −0.0163 against an LSC of 0.0277 and reads `NO_SIGNIFICANT_CHANGE` — whereas differencing against the unadjusted 0.7000 would have wrongly reported −0.0300 and declared a significant loss. A change of exactly −0.0277 against an LSC of 0.0277 is a `SIGNIFICANT_LOSS`; −0.0276 is not.

**Step three** sets `clinical_interval_months` from the category and then caps it. `OSTEOPOROSIS` gives twelve months. `OSTEOPENIA` gives twelve months when `lowest_t_score` is at or below −2.0 and twenty-four otherwise, so exactly −2.0 takes the shorter branch. `NORMAL` gives sixty months, `BELOW_EXPECTED_RANGE_FOR_AGE` twenty-four and `WITHIN_EXPECTED_RANGE_FOR_AGE` sixty. Then, if `lsc_interpretation` is `SIGNIFICANT_LOSS`, the interval is capped at twelve months; then, if `lsc_interpretation` is `NOT_COMPARABLE_DEVICE` or `NO_COMMON_SITE`, it is capped at twenty-four. The caps are minimums taken in that order and never lengthen the interval. **Step four** sets `payer_floor_months` from the order's `indication` and the patient's policy, exactly as at ordering time. **Step five** sets `recommended_interval_months` to the larger of the clinical interval and the payer floor, so the payer can delay a rescan but never bring one forward.

**Step six** picks `recommended_action`, and the first matching branch wins. A `diagnostic_category` of `OSTEOPOROSIS`, or an attached assessment with `treatment_threshold_met` true, yields `CONTINUE_TREATMENT` when the patient already has a plan in `ON_THERAPY`, `ADHERENCE_HOLD` or `DRUG_HOLIDAY`, and `INITIATE_TREATMENT` otherwise. Failing that, a `SIGNIFICANT_LOSS` yields `REFER_SECONDARY_WORKUP`. Failing that, a `recommended_interval_months` greater than `clinical_interval_months` yields `PAYER_LIMITED_INTERVAL`, which is how the report says out loud that the payer, not the clinician, set the interval. Otherwise the action is `ROUTINE_INTERVAL`. Because the branches are ordered, an osteoporotic patient held to a twenty-four-month payer floor still reads `INITIATE_TREATMENT` rather than `PAYER_LIMITED_INTERVAL`, and an osteopenic patient whose FRAX crosses only the hip threshold still reads `INITIATE_TREATMENT`.

**Step seven** audits the report for mandatory narrative. `missing_element_codes` lists, in this fixed order, `LSC_NON_COMPARABILITY_NOTE` when `lsc_interpretation` is `NOT_COMPARABLE_DEVICE` or `NO_COMMON_SITE` and `non_comparability_note` is null, then `VFA_FINDINGS` when the session's `vfa_performed` is true and `vfa_narrative` is null. `required_elements_complete` is true exactly when that list is empty. Both are recomputed at creation, on every update and again at finalization.

### 3.8. Finalization, the Interval Cascade and Amendment

Finalizing a report is the single most consequential act in the program: it is what makes the interpretation official, what advances the patient's screening clock, and what can retroactively invalidate work already scheduled. An `InterpretingPhysician` finalizes a report that is in `status` `DRAFT` on a session whose `acquisition_status` is `SUBMITTED` and whose `review_status` is `ACCEPTED` or `ACCEPTED_WITH_LIMITATION`. A session still `NOT_REVIEWED` or sitting at `REPEAT_REQUIRED` is refused with `ERR_POSITIONING_NOT_ACCEPTED` — unreviewed positioning may never be reported, and a study accepted with documented limitations may. The report's `required_elements_complete` must be true, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_REPORT_ELEMENTS_INCOMPLETE`; the physician cannot sign a report that is still missing a non-comparability explanation or a vertebral narrative.

**Cascade A** then runs, in four links, and every link is caused by the one before it. **Link one**: the report moves `DRAFT` to `FINALIZED` with `finalized_at_utc` taken from the clock and `finalized_by_user_id` from the caller, and the session moves `SUBMITTED` to `ARCHIVED`. **Link two**, caused by the finalization: the patient's `lifetime_screening_scan_count` increments by one, `last_finalized_report_id` is set to this report, `baseline_report_id` is set to this report **only when it was null**, `current_screening_interval_months` is set to the report's `recommended_interval_months`, and `next_eligible_scan_date` is set to the calendar date of the session's `submitted_at_utc` advanced by that many calendar months. The interval is measured from the date the images were acquired, not from the date the physician got round to signing. **Link three**, caused by `next_eligible_scan_date` moving: every order of this patient in `status` `REQUESTED` or `SCHEDULED` is re-evaluated against the new date and the new lifetime count, processed in `created_at` ascending order, and any whose verdict is now `INELIGIBLE_TOO_SOON` or `INELIGIBLE_LIFETIME_CAP` moves to `DENIED` — with `denial_reason_code` `INTERVAL_REVISED_BY_REPORT` for a too-soon verdict and `LIFETIME_CAP_EXCEEDED` for a cap verdict. Here the policy's `appeal_allowed` flag is **irrelevant**: the order already exists, so there is nothing to reject and no request to fail; the existing order is simply denied. That is how an order created perfectly `ELIGIBLE` can end up `DENIED` without anyone touching it. **Link four**, caused by each denial: the `BOOKED` appointments of every denied order move to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason_code` `ORDER_DENIED`, and the patient's `open_scheduled_order_count` decrements by one for each denied order that had been `SCHEDULED`.

A finalized report is immutable, but medicine is not, so it can be **amended**. An `InterpretingPhysician` amends a `FINALIZED` report by supplying an `amendment_reason` of 1 to 240 characters, and only when no other report on the same `scan_session_id` is already in `status` `DRAFT`. The addressed report moves `FINALIZED` to `AMENDED` — a terminal status; an amended report is never revised again, only superseded — and a successor report is created in `status` `DRAFT` with `amends_report_id` pointing at its predecessor, the supplied `amendment_reason` recorded, and every computed field re-derived from the session's current state. The successor, not the predecessor, is the response body.

Amendment also **compensates** the patient-level tallies that finalization moved, which is what keeps the numbers honest. The patient's `lifetime_screening_scan_count` decrements by one; `last_finalized_report_id` and `baseline_report_id` are re-derived from whatever `FINALIZED` reports remain, and either may become null; and `current_screening_interval_months` and `next_eligible_scan_date` are re-derived from the new `last_finalized_report_id`, or reset to null when no finalized report remains. The session moves `ARCHIVED` back to `SUBMITTED` so the successor can itself be finalized. A finalize followed immediately by an amend therefore leaves `lifetime_screening_scan_count` at exactly its pre-finalization value, and finalizing the successor restores it — the counter counts finalized studies, never finalization events.

### 3.9. Treatment Initiation, Refill Capture and Adherence Follow-Up

When a report recommends starting therapy, an `InterpretingPhysician` opens a **treatment plan** naming the `patient_id`, the `initiating_report_id`, the `drug_class`, the `start_date` and optionally the `adherence_source_policy`. The initiating report must be in `status` `FINALIZED` — a draft is refused with `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FINALIZED` — must belong to a session of the same patient, and must carry `recommended_action` `INITIATE_TREATMENT`, so a plan can never be opened off a routine-interval report. The `start_date` is a calendar date on or after the calendar date of the report's `finalized_at_utc`; therapy cannot be backdated behind the report that justified it. `drug_class` is one of `BISPHOSPHONATE_ORAL`, `BISPHOSPHONATE_IV`, `DENOSUMAB`, `ANABOLIC_PTH` or `SERM`, and it selects `followup_interval_months`: three months for `BISPHOSPHONATE_ORAL` and `ANABOLIC_PTH`, six for `DENOSUMAB` and `SERM`, and twelve for `BISPHOSPHONATE_IV`. The plan is created in `status` `INITIATED` with `next_followup_due_date` set to `start_date` advanced by that many calendar months, `adherence_review_count` and `consecutive_nonadherent_reviews` at zero, `adherence_status` `NOT_ASSESSED`, and `last_review_date`, `adherence_window_days`, `covered_days_supplied`, `medication_possession_ratio`, `denosumab_gap_days` and `discontinuation_reason_code` all null. `adherence_source_policy` defaults to `CLAIMS_ONLY`.

Refills accumulate between reviews. A `PatientPortalUser` may record their own, and an `InterpretingPhysician` may record any; a portal user posting to another patient's plan is refused with `ERR_PATIENT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Each **adherence refill** carries `dispensed_date`, `days_supplied` from 1 through 400, and `source` of `PHARMACY_CLAIM` or `PATIENT_REPORT`. The `dispensed_date` must fall on or after the plan's `start_date` and strictly before the logical clock date, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_REFILL_DATE_OUT_OF_WINDOW`. The row computes `source_weight` — 1.0 for a pharmacy claim and 0.5 for a patient report — and `coverage_end_date`, the `dispensed_date` advanced by `days_supplied` days. `recorded_by_user_id` holds the caller's `X-User-ID`, or the patient's own `id` when the recorder is a portal user. Recording a refill deliberately does **not** recompute the plan's ratio: the refill is evidence, and evidence is only scored at review time. Like a phantom run, a refill is an immutable observation with only `id` and `created_at`. A refill posted to a `DISCONTINUED` plan is refused with `ERR_PLAN_DISCONTINUED`.

An `InterpretingPhysician` performs an **adherence review**, optionally supplying a `requested_action`, a free-text `note` and a `discontinuation_reason_code`. A review is accepted only when the logical clock date is at or after `next_followup_due_date` less seven days — a one-week early window — and is otherwise refused with `ERR_FOLLOWUP_NOT_DUE`. Reviewing a `DISCONTINUED` plan is refused with `ERR_PLAN_DISCONTINUED`, since that status is terminal.

The medication possession ratio is then recomputed from scratch. `adherence_window_days` is the whole number of days from the plan's `start_date` to the clock date at review time. Eligible refills are those dispensed on or after the start date and strictly before the review date, so a refill dispensed on the review date itself is excluded — the window is half-open at its upper end, and because the refill entity already forbids a `dispensed_date` at or after the clock date, that edge is never actually contested. `covered_days_supplied` is the sum over eligible refills of `days_supplied` multiplied by the applicable weight, and this is where the plan's `adherence_source_policy` splits into two variants over the very same stored rows. Under `CLAIMS_ONLY`, rows whose `source` is `PATIENT_REPORT` are **dropped entirely**. Under `ALL_SOURCES_WEIGHTED` they contribute half of their supplied days. Overlapping coverage is deliberately **not** de-duplicated: two refills covering the same calendar days both count in full, which is exactly why the ratio is capped. `medication_possession_ratio` is `covered_days_supplied` divided by `adherence_window_days`, clamped to a maximum of 1.5, and then rounded half up to three decimal places; because of the half-weight, `covered_days_supplied` keeps one decimal place. Two hundred supplied days across a ninety-one-day window therefore reads 1.500, not 2.198. A ninety-one-day window holding two thirty-day pharmacy claims and one thirty-day patient report reads 0.659 and `PARTIAL` under `CLAIMS_ONLY` but 0.824 and `ADHERENT` under `ALL_SOURCES_WEIGHTED` — identical rows, different verdict, decided solely by the policy chosen when the plan was opened.

`adherence_status` reads the rounded ratio: `ADHERENT` at 0.800 or above, `PARTIAL` from 0.500 up to but not including 0.800, and `NON_ADHERENT` below 0.500. Exactly 0.800 is adherent and 0.799 is partial; exactly 0.500 is partial and 0.499 is non-adherent. A ninety-day window with forty-two plus thirty claimed days lands on exactly 0.800.

Denosumab is handled specially because a lapsed injection carries a rebound-fracture risk that a possession ratio does not capture. For a `drug_class` of `DENOSUMAB` only, `denosumab_gap_days` is the whole number of days from the latest eligible refill's coverage end — its `dispensed_date` advanced by `days_supplied` — to the review date, floored at zero so live coverage reads 0 rather than a negative number; it is null for every other drug class and null when no eligible refill exists. When that gap exceeds 210 days the plan is forced to `ADHERENCE_HOLD` and `adherence_status` is **overridden** to `NON_ADHERENT` regardless of the computed ratio, so a denosumab plan with a possession ratio of 1.000 can still be put on hold when the last injection lapsed more than 210 days ago.

Two further figures are refreshed at each review. `cumulative_therapy_months` is the completed whole calendar months between `start_date` and `last_review_date`. `holiday_eligible` is true only when all three conditions hold: the `drug_class` is `BISPHOSPHONATE_ORAL` or `BISPHOSPHONATE_IV`, `cumulative_therapy_months` has reached sixty, and the session behind the patient's `last_finalized_report_id` has a `lowest_t_score` above −2.5. Only bisphosphonates have the residual skeletal effect that makes a planned interruption safe, and only a patient who is no longer osteoporotic on their most recent study should be interrupted at all.

The plan's `status` then advances. From `INITIATED` or `ADHERENCE_HOLD`, an `ADHERENT` or `PARTIAL` verdict moves the plan to `ON_THERAPY` and resets `consecutive_nonadherent_reviews` to zero. From `INITIATED` or `ON_THERAPY`, a `NON_ADHERENT` verdict — or a denosumab gap beyond its limit — moves the plan to `ADHERENCE_HOLD` and increments `consecutive_nonadherent_reviews`. From `ADHERENCE_HOLD`, a further `NON_ADHERENT` verdict that brings `consecutive_nonadherent_reviews` to two moves the plan to `DISCONTINUED` with `discontinuation_reason_code` `ADHERENCE_FAILURE`. That closes the first cycle in this entity — `ON_THERAPY` to `ADHERENCE_HOLD` and back to `ON_THERAPY` — with a bounded escape hatch after two consecutive failures.

Three explicit `requested_action` values override the automatic resolution, and `AUTO` is the default that lets the verdict decide. `START_DRUG_HOLIDAY` moves an `ON_THERAPY` plan to `DRUG_HOLIDAY`, but only when `holiday_eligible` is true **and** the current verdict is `ADHERENT` — a patient who is not taking their medication is not granted a holiday from it — and sets `next_followup_due_date` to the review date advanced by twenty-four calendar months, since a holiday is monitored on a much longer cycle. `RESUME_THERAPY` moves a `DRUG_HOLIDAY` plan back to `ON_THERAPY` and restores the drug's own `followup_interval_months` cadence, closing the entity's second cycle. `DISCONTINUE` moves a plan in any of `INITIATED`, `ON_THERAPY`, `ADHERENCE_HOLD` or `DRUG_HOLIDAY` to `DISCONTINUED` and requires an explicit `discontinuation_reason_code` of `ADVERSE_EVENT`, `GOAL_MET`, `PATIENT_REQUEST` or `ADHERENCE_FAILURE` in the body. Every review, whatever its outcome, increments `adherence_review_count`, sets `last_review_date` to the clock date, and advances `next_followup_due_date` from that review date. `discontinuation_reason_code` is populated exactly when `status` is `DISCONTINUED` and null otherwise, and `DISCONTINUED` is terminal — the plan accepts neither refills nor reviews afterwards.
