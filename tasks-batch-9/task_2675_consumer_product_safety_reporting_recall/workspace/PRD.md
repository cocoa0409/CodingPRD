# Consumer Product Safety Reporting & Recall API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A consumer products safety compliance office lives or dies by its ability to turn a stream of unstructured complaints into a defensible regulatory record. Every hotline call, web form, retailer escalation and medical-examiner referral is a candidate signal; only a fraction of them describe a genuine product defect, and the office is legally obliged to distinguish the two quickly, document how it did so, and file a statutory report within a hard deadline once it concludes a product presents an unreasonable risk. Today that work is done in spreadsheets and shared mailboxes, which means the office cannot answer three questions its own general counsel asks weekly: which incidents are still unescalated past their internal deadline, how many recalled units have actually been corrected, and what the office's exposure is on a late filing.

This document specifies the service that replaces those spreadsheets. The system is a single API, mounted under `/product-safety`, that carries a product-safety matter end to end: incident and complaint intake with injury classification, hazard-pattern analysis over a bounded incident window, reportability evaluation under one of four statutory standards with an internal escalation clock, statutory report preparation with a corrective-action proposal, recall plan development covering remedy options and consumer participation goals, retailer and distributor notification with stop-sale and shelf-removal verification, consumer notification across direct notice, press release, registration-card and web channels, monthly progress reporting on units corrected with an effectiveness assessment, and finally civil penalty negotiation ending in an executed consent agreement.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and should be read as constraints on every requirement below. First, **the record is the product**: nearly every consequential figure — a severity weight, a risk index, a lateness surcharge, a penalty assessment — is computed once at a named moment and frozen, so that a later change of circumstances never silently rewrites history. Second, **containment outranks paperwork, and paperwork outranks money**: when several rules could reject the same request, the system reports the one highest in that hierarchy, because an inspector needs to hear "product is still on a shelf" before hearing "the participation rate is short". Time is likewise a first-class, controllable input: all deadlines, embargoes, windows and lateness computations read a server-held logical UTC clock rather than the host wall clock, so that a compliance scenario can be rehearsed deterministically.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` does not name one of the seven roles defined below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). An unrecognised role string is deliberately treated as a failure of authentication rather than of authorisation, because the server has no principal to authorise. When the headers are well formed but the named role does not appear in the authorised-role list for the endpoint being called (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `IntakeSpecialist`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `SafetyDirector`, `FieldAuditor`, `PenaltyCounsel` and `RetailerRep`.

A `ProgramAdmin` is the registry custodian: it owns the reference data the whole docket hangs off — product models and retailers — and it owns the logical clock, but it takes no substantive safety decision. An `IntakeSpecialist` is a hotline or web-form analyst who records incident reports, classifies injuries and hazard codes, and disposes of duplicates and no-action reports; it has no authority over patterns, reportability or recalls. A `ComplianceOfficer` is the case officer who builds the docket: hazard-pattern analysis, reportability evaluation, report drafting, recall plan authoring, consumer notification and the remedy ledger. A `SafetyDirector` is the signing official holding every approval a `ComplianceOfficer`'s filing requires — report review, plan approval, entry into monitoring, closure, shelf-removal verification and progress-report acceptance — and is *also* permitted to perform many of the filing steps, which is precisely what makes the two identity-based segregation gates described in §2.10 meaningful. A `FieldAuditor` schedules and completes shelf-removal audit visits and may sign the resulting verification, but cannot touch the docket, the recall plan or the penalty case. A `PenaltyCounsel` is the enforcement attorney and sole owner of the civil penalty docket: assessment, offers from either side, withdrawal and consent-agreement acceptance. A `RetailerRep` is an external retailer or distributor representative.

Six of the seven roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `IntakeSpecialist`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `SafetyDirector`, `FieldAuditor` or `PenaltyCounsel`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — it stores it as `submitted_by_user_id` on a recall when the plan is submitted, and as `prepared_by_user_id` on a progress report when the report is opened — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

`RetailerRep` is the one **entity-backed** role. When `X-User-Role` is `RetailerRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Retailer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Retailer` compare that resource's retailer foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `RetailerRep` acknowledging a stop-sale notification must supply an `X-User-ID` equal to that notification's `retailer_id`; any other value is rejected with `ERR_RETAILER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). A retailer representative may only acknowledge its own stop-sale notice, and may only read the recall, retailer and notification records it is party to.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside those bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Collection results are always ordered by `created_at` ascending — never by `id` — so that pagination is stable and so that a caller walking pages sees rows in the order the office recorded them. Where a cascade processes a set of sibling rows, it processes them in that same `created_at` ascending order, which makes cascade outcomes reproducible.

For list responses the `meta` object carries `limit` and `offset` as applied, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filters before pagination, and `has_more` indicating whether rows remain beyond the current page. Single-resource responses omit all four.

### 2.4. Docket Records, Identifiers and the Logical Clock

Every persisted record in the system — product models, incidents, hazard patterns, reportability evaluations, section reports, recalls, retailers, retailer notifications, audit visits, consumer notices, remedy claims, progress reports and civil penalty cases alike — carries the same four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and is never supplied by the client. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped when the row is first written. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, initially equal to `created_at` and refreshed on every mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row, including mutations that reach the row as the second or third link of a cascade rather than as the direct target of the request.

Time in this system is a controllable input rather than an ambient fact. The service holds a **logical UTC clock**, and every deadline, escalation window, embargo, audit window, reporting period and lateness computation reads that clock — never the host wall clock. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it must not fall back to real wall-clock time. A `ProgramAdmin`-only administrative capability, mounted outside the business prefix at `/_clock/set_current_utc`, replaces the clock value with a supplied instant given as `to_iso8601` and answers with the new value as `current_utc`. The clock may be moved forward or backward freely, it never advances on its own between requests, and setting it recomputes nothing: no stored row is re-derived as a side effect of a clock change.

### 2.5. Reading the Docket

The read surface is deliberately narrow: exactly one single-resource fetch per entity, plus one filtered collection fetch for each entity that carries a foreign key. Path identifiers are validated left to right in path order, and the first segment that does not resolve to a live row decides the response: the not-found error belongs to the entity that owns the failing segment, drawn from `ERR_PRODUCT_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HAZARD_PATTERN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SECTION_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECALL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RETAILER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RETAILER_NOTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_VISIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSUMER_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REMEDY_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROGRESS_REPORT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_PENALTY_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, each answered with HTTP 404. The same catalogue serves foreign keys supplied in a request body, but body foreign keys are only resolved *after* the body has passed its shape check, so a malformed payload never leaks the existence of a referenced row.

Because ownership in this domain is expressed through foreign-key fields rather than through nested URLs, the filtered collections are the only way to observe rows that a cascade created or mutated: no creation response ever contains the sibling rows a cascade touched. Each filtered collection therefore collapses *all* of its entity's foreign keys into one endpoint. The incident collection filters on `product_model_id`, `hazard_pattern_id`, `duplicate_of_incident_id`, `status`, `injury_class` and `hazard_code` — which is how an operator discovers which incidents a pattern clustered, and which merged reports fold into a surviving primary. The hazard-pattern collection filters on `product_model_id`, `pattern_status` and `analysis_basis`. The reportability-evaluation collection filters on `hazard_pattern_id`, `product_model_id`, `status`, `standard_basis` and `escalation_breached`. The section-report collection filters on `reportability_evaluation_id`, `status` and `corrective_action_type`. The recall collection filters on `section_report_id`, `plan_status`, `notification_status` and `effectiveness_flag`. The retailer-notification collection filters on `recall_id`, `retailer_id`, `status` and `stop_sale_required`, and is the only channel through which the stop-sale issuance cascade and the delinquency sweep become visible. The audit-visit collection filters on `retailer_notification_id`, `status` and `verdict`. The consumer-notice collection filters on `recall_id`, `channel` and `status`. The remedy-claim collection filters on `recall_id`, `status` and `remedy_type`. The progress-report collection filters on `recall_id`, `status`, `effectiveness_basis` and `assessment`. The penalty-case collection filters on `section_report_id`, `status`, `cooperation_credit_tier` and `cooperation_bonus_applied`. All filters are optional, all are combined conjunctively, and each accepts `limit` and `offset` as described in §2.3.

### 2.6. Numbers, Money and Rounding

Unless a rule below says otherwise, every derived decimal figure is rounded **half up**. Severity weights, severity sums, effectiveness rates and risk indices are stored to 2 decimal places. Concentration ratios and exposure normalisers are stored to 4 decimal places. Percentage goals supplied by a client carry at most 2 decimal places.

All money in this system is expressed as an integer number of cents and every field whose name ends in `_cents` is an integer; there are no floating-point currency values anywhere in the API. Where a money figure is derived from a multiplication, the specification below always names the exact point at which rounding occurs, because rounding at a different point produces a different — and wrong — integer. Unit counts are whole integers with no rounding anywhere.

Deadline arithmetic adds whole numbers of hours to an ISO 8601 UTC instant and introduces no sub-second component. Boundary inclusivity is specified rule by rule and is never left to interpretation: some comparisons are non-strict (an event exactly at its deadline is on time) and some are strict (an event exactly at a boundary is outside the interval), and each is stated where it applies.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency: Version Pinning

Two write operations in this system read a parent record, make a claim about it, and then rewrite state the parent owns. Both therefore require the client to pin the parent's `version`, so that a write derived from an out-of-date read is refused rather than applied.

Opening a hazard-pattern analysis requires a non-optional `expected_product_model_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `ProductModel` named by `product_model_id`. If that value does not equal the model's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PRODUCT_MODEL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard matters because an analysis is a claim about a specific version of the model's distribution figures — `units_distributed` feeds the exposure normaliser — and because creating a pattern legitimately supersedes every prior analysis of that model and re-points its clustered incidents. A writer working from a stale view of the model would otherwise silently discard an analysis it never saw.

Tabling a settlement offer on a civil penalty case requires a non-optional `expected_case_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `CivilPenaltyCase`. If that value does not equal the case's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PENALTY_CASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here there are two independent ways the case's version can move ahead of a client's read: a new offer supersedes the standing one and re-cuts the reserve, and the remedy-fulfilment cascade described in §2.11 can advance the case by setting `cooperation_bonus_applied`. The version check turns both forms of stale view into a deterministic rejection instead of a lost update.

No other operation in the system takes a version field. In particular, none of the recall-plan transitions, notification transitions, claim transitions, report transitions or offer resolutions accept an expected-version input.

### 2.8. Rule Evaluation Order and the First-Failure Principle

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is reported: the violation that comes first in the fixed ordering below. Evaluation stops at the first failure and no later rule is consulted. The ordering encodes the office's own priorities — identity and existence first, then state legality, then uniqueness of the docket, then safety and hazard containment, then temporal scheduling, then referential completeness of the filing package, then administrative constraints, and finally fiscal and unit-accounting rules. A shelf full of hazardous product outranks a paperwork gap, and a paperwork gap outranks a money question.

1. **Authentication** — both headers present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` a declared role, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role permission** — the acting role appears in the called endpoint's authorised-role list, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. **Path existence** — every identifier in the request path resolves to a live row, checked left to right, else the owning entity's not-found error.
4. **Request shape** — the body conforms to the endpoint's input contract, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
5. **Body foreign-key existence** — every foreign-key identifier in the body resolves to a live row, else that entity's not-found error.
6. **Retailer scope** — a `RetailerRep` acts only on its own notification, else `ERR_RETAILER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
7. **Segregation of duties** — the acting operator is not the operator who filed the work being approved, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
8. **Optimistic concurrency** — a supplied expected-version matches the parent's stored `version`, else `ERR_STALE_PRODUCT_MODEL_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_PENALTY_CASE_VERSION`.
9. **Own-state precondition** — the target row's own state field permits the requested transition, else that entity's state error.
10. **Cross-entity state precondition** — the state of a *different* row that the write depends on permits the operation, else that other entity's state error.
11. **Docket uniqueness** — no conflicting row already occupies this slot in the docket, else `ERR_MODEL_CODE_TAKEN`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_DOCKET_ENTRY` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_RETAILER_NOTIFICATION`.
12. **Safety and hazard containment** — in this order: model posture freeze (`ERR_MODEL_POSTURE_FROZEN`), unverified shelf removal blocking closure (`ERR_NOTIFICATION_NOT_SATISFIED`), no active stop-sale to inspect (`ERR_NO_ACTIVE_STOP_SALE`), shelf removal not evidenced (`ERR_SHELF_REMOVAL_UNVERIFIED`), verdict contradicting the shelf count (`ERR_SHELF_UNITS_PRESENT`), pattern not confirmable (`ERR_PATTERN_NOT_CONFIRMABLE`), determination unsupported by the scores (`ERR_DETERMINATION_UNSUPPORTED`).
13. **Temporal scheduling** — in this order: audit-window collision (`ERR_AUDIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`), reporting-period overlap (`ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`), reporting-period gap (`ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_GAP`), notice not yet due (`ERR_NOTICE_NOT_DUE`), embargo still in force (`ERR_EMBARGO_ACTIVE`).
14. **Referential completeness** — in this order: self-referential duplicate pointer (`ERR_SELF_DUPLICATE_REFERENCE`), duplicate chain (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CHAIN_FORBIDDEN`), remedy not offered (`ERR_REMEDY_OPTION_NOT_OFFERED`), missing direct notice (`ERR_DIRECT_NOTICE_REQUIRED`), no released notice (`ERR_CONSUMER_NOTICE_NOT_RELEASED`), no accepted progress report (`ERR_PROGRESS_REPORT_REQUIRED`).
15. **Administrative constraints** — registry reach ceiling (`ERR_REACH_EXCEEDS_REGISTRY`), then negotiation round cap (`ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`).
16. **Fiscal and unit accounting** — offer above the assessment (`ERR_OFFER_EXCEEDS_ASSESSMENT`), then claim quantity above the outstanding units (`ERR_UNITS_OUTSTANDING_EXCEEDED`), then correction rate short of goal at closure (`ERR_EFFECTIVENESS_NOT_MET`).

Two consequences of this ordering deserve to be called out because they are easy to get backwards. Scheduling an audit visit against a notification that is still `PENDING` passes the cross-entity state check at step 10 — `PENDING` is not the terminal `VERIFIED` state — and is then stopped at step 12 by `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_STOP_SALE`, because the retailer is not yet under an active stop-sale. And registering a remedy claim against a recall in the wrong plan state reports the recall's state error from step 10 even when the claimed quantity is also impossible, because unit accounting is deliberately the lowest-priority family.

The request-shape check at step 4 has a defined scope. It requires every non-optional field to be present and non-null; every enum-valued field to carry a declared member; every integer field to be an integer inside its declared range; every money field to be a non-negative integer of cents; every decimal field to respect its declared scale; the `remedy_options` array to be non-empty with unique members; every timestamp to be a valid ISO 8601 UTC instant; and every interval to be well ordered, meaning a hazard pattern's `window_start` strictly before its `window_end`, an audit visit's `window_start` strictly before its `window_end`, and a progress report's `period_start` strictly before its `period_end`. Conversely, unknown body keys and any attempt to supply a server-computed field are **ignored silently** rather than rejected — a client that posts `stop_sale_required`, `severity_weight` or `assessed_penalty_cents` in a creation body simply has those keys dropped.

Every action operation in this system is a state transition and none of them are idempotent: re-firing the same trigger on a row that has already advanced returns that entity's state error at step 9 rather than succeeding silently. The per-entity state errors are `ERR_INCIDENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PATTERN_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_EVALUATION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_SECTION_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_RECALL_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_NOTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_VISIT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_NOTICE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PROGRESS_REPORT_STATE_INVALID` and `ERR_PENALTY_CASE_STATE_INVALID`, all answered with HTTP 409.

### 2.9. Hazard Posture: the Model-Level Containment Gate

Each product model carries a `hazard_posture` of `MONITORED`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`, together with an `open_escalation_count`. The posture is a containment gate, not a derived summary: it is assigned **only** by the six named transitions listed below and is never re-derived by a query.

- Registering the model sets `hazard_posture` to `MONITORED` and `open_escalation_count` to 0.
- A reportability determination of `REPORTABLE` sets `hazard_posture` to `FROZEN`.
- A reportability determination of `NOT_REPORTABLE` sets `hazard_posture` to `MONITORED`.
- Filing the statutory report sets `hazard_posture` to `RESTRICTED`.
- A report review decision of `ACCEPTED` leaves `hazard_posture` at `RESTRICTED`.
- A report review decision of `RETURNED_FOR_INFO` sets `hazard_posture` to `FROZEN`.

Adjudicating a hazard pattern does **not** move the posture in either direction: neither a `CONFIRMED` nor a `DISMISSED` adjudication touches `hazard_posture`. Closing a recall is the one further point at which the posture is relaxed: when the closing recall was the model's last open escalation source, closure sets `open_escalation_count` back to 0 and `hazard_posture` back to `MONITORED`.

While a model is `FROZEN` — meaning it has at least one escalated incident on an open docket — the evidence base underneath it may not be narrowed. Both re-running a pattern's analysis and dismissing a pattern are refused with `ERR_MODEL_POSTURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409) while the model behind the pattern is `FROZEN`. Those are the only two operations this gate blocks, and it blocks them ahead of every temporal, referential, administrative and fiscal rule.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

The office's internal control is that the person who prepares work may not be the person who signs it off. Two gates implement this, and both compare **stored operator identifiers**, not roles: a `SafetyDirector` who personally filed a plan still cannot approve that plan. This is precisely why the filing steps are shared between `ComplianceOfficer` and `SafetyDirector` — the same user can reach both sides of a gate and be refused by identity rather than by permission.

When a recall plan is submitted, the acting `X-User-ID` is stored as `submitted_by_user_id` on the recall. Approving that plan requires an acting `X-User-ID` different from the stored `submitted_by_user_id`; an equal value is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). When a monthly progress report is opened, the acting `X-User-ID` is stored as `prepared_by_user_id` on the report. Accepting that report requires an acting `X-User-ID` different from the stored `prepared_by_user_id`, and an equal value is likewise rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. These two operations are the entire scope of the rule; no other approval in the system compares operator identifiers.

### 2.11. Cascades, Conserved Quantities and Compensating Actions

Several operations in this system mutate more than one record. Where they do, the whole cascade is part of the operation's contract: each touched row has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1, and the effects are described in full in §3 rather than left as an implementation detail. Two cascades run three or more links deep. Approving a recall plan reaches from the recall to its retailer notifications and from each notification to its retailer. Fulfilling a remedy claim reaches from the claim to the recall's unit counters, from those counters to the recall's effectiveness flag, and from that flag to the linked civil penalty case.

Two quantities in the system are **conserved** and must be checked after every write that touches either half. On a recall, the sum of `units_corrected` and `units_outstanding` always equals `units_in_commerce`, which is immutable after the recall is created; the pair is a strict partition of a fixed total, and `units_corrected` is never permitted to exceed `units_in_commerce`. On a civil penalty case, the sum of `reserved_cents` and `available_cents` always equals `assessed_penalty_cents`, which is frozen at assessment time.

Where a cascade can be undone, the undo is an **exact compensation** rather than a recomputation from scratch: it subtracts the same stored quantity it previously added, leaving no drift. Voiding a fulfilled remedy claim reverses all four links of the fulfilment cascade using the claim's own stored `quantity`. Withdrawing a standing penalty offer restores the reserve to its pre-offer split. What compensation never restores is a monotonic counter: a withdrawn offer still consumed a negotiation round, and a delinquency once recorded against a retailer stays recorded.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Reference Registry and the Incident Intake Queue

Nothing in the docket can exist until the office has registered the product it concerns. A `ProgramAdmin` registers a product model by supplying a `model_code` of between 1 and 40 characters, a `brand` of between 1 and 80 characters printed on the product, a `category` drawn from `KITCHEN_APPLIANCE`, `JUVENILE_PRODUCT`, `POWER_TOOL`, `SPORTING_GOODS` and `HOME_ELECTRONICS` (the product family the office uses for docket routing), a `units_distributed` count of at least 1 recording how many units were placed into commerce, and a `units_registered` count of at least 0 recording how many consumers are reachable through registration-card data. The `model_code` is globally unique across all product models; a second registration reusing an existing code is refused with `ERR_MODEL_CODE_TAKEN`. A newly registered model always starts with `hazard_posture` at `MONITORED` and `open_escalation_count` at 0, and neither may be supplied by the client. `units_distributed` is the denominator of the hazard-exposure term described in §3.2, and `units_registered` is the denominator of the participation-basis effectiveness variant described in §3.8 as well as the ceiling on registration-card reach described in §3.7.

Retailers and distributors are registered by the same `ProgramAdmin` with a legal `name` of between 1 and 120 characters, a `channel_type` of `BIG_BOX`, `ONLINE_MARKETPLACE`, `SPECIALTY` or `DISTRIBUTOR`, and a `store_count` of at least 1 giving the number of physical or virtual storefronts subject to shelf removal. A new retailer starts `CLEAR` in its `compliance_posture` with all three of `open_stop_sale_count`, `verified_removal_count` and `delinquent_count` at 0; §3.6 describes how those move.

Incident intake is the front door. An `IntakeSpecialist` (or a `ComplianceOfficer`) records a consumer complaint or injury report by submitting the `product_model_id` of the model it concerns, a `report_channel` of `CONSUMER_HOTLINE`, `WEB_FORM`, `RETAILER` or `MEDICAL_EXAMINER` naming the intake channel, an `incident_date` giving the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the harm or malfunction occurred, and a free-text `narrative` of between 1 and 2000 characters describing the complaint. A new report lands in `INTAKE` status with no injury classification at all: `injury_class` and `hazard_code` are both null, `injury_verified` is false, `severity_weight` is 0.00, `classified_at` and `escalation_deadline_at` are null, and both `hazard_pattern_id` and `duplicate_of_incident_id` are null. An incident's `status` moves through the enumeration `INTAKE`, `CLASSIFIED`, `CLUSTERED`, `ESCALATED`, `MERGED_DUPLICATE` and `CLOSED_NO_ACTION`, of which the last two are terminal.

Classification is where an intake report acquires analytical weight. Classifying an incident records an `injury_class` from `NO_INJURY`, `FIRST_AID`, `MEDICAL_TREATMENT`, `HOSPITALIZATION` and `FATALITY`, a `hazard_code` from `LACERATION`, `BURN`, `CHOKING`, `ELECTRIC_SHOCK`, `FALL` and `ENTRAPMENT`, and an `injury_verified` boolean stating whether the injury claim was corroborated by medical or retailer records. The operation stamps `classified_at` with the logical clock and recomputes the incident's `severity_weight`. From `INTAKE` it advances the report to `CLASSIFIED`; from `CLASSIFIED` it is a **reclassification self-loop** that stays in `CLASSIFIED` while overwriting all three inputs, re-stamping `classified_at` and recomputing the weight. The self-loop is only legal while the incident's `hazard_pattern_id` is null: once a hazard pattern has claimed the report, it is owned by that analysis and may not be reclassified, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INCIDENT_STATE_INVALID`. The same error answers any classification attempt on a `CLUSTERED` or `ESCALATED` report, or on one already in a terminal state. A reclassified weight is picked up by the *next* pattern analysis; it never retroactively edits an existing pattern's frozen snapshot.

The severity weight itself combines three factors and is frozen at classification time — it is never recomputed because the parent product model or some hazard pattern changed. Each injury class carries an injury base weight: 1 for `NO_INJURY`, 3 for `FIRST_AID`, 8 for `MEDICAL_TREATMENT`, 20 for `HOSPITALIZATION` and 50 for `FATALITY`. Each intake channel carries a credibility multiplier reflecting how much the office trusts the source: a report arriving through `CONSUMER_HOTLINE` or `WEB_FORM` is multiplied by 1.00, one arriving through a `RETAILER` by 1.20, and one arriving from a `MEDICAL_EXAMINER` by 1.50. The injury base weight is multiplied by the credibility multiplier of the channel fixed at intake; then, if `injury_verified` is false, that product is halved — an unverified claim counts for exactly half. The result is rounded half up to 2 decimal places and stored as `severity_weight`. Only the incident's own `injury_class`, `report_channel` and `injury_verified` participate; there is no aggregation over sibling reports.

Disposition closes an incident without escalating it, and takes a `disposition` of either `MERGED_DUPLICATE` or `CLOSED_NO_ACTION` plus an optional free-text `note`. A disposition of `MERGED_DUPLICATE` folds this report into a surviving primary and requires a `duplicate_of_incident_id` naming that primary; a disposition of `CLOSED_NO_ACTION` requires `duplicate_of_incident_id` to be absent. Both dispositions are legal from `INTAKE` and from `CLASSIFIED`, and in the `CLASSIFIED` case only while `hazard_pattern_id` is null — a clustered or escalated report cannot be disposed of, and the attempt draws `ERR_INCIDENT_STATE_INVALID`. Two pointer rules guard the merge, in this order. First, an incident may not be marked a duplicate of itself: a `duplicate_of_incident_id` equal to the incident's own `id` is refused with `ERR_SELF_DUPLICATE_REFERENCE` (HTTP 422). Second, duplicate pointers must be exactly one hop deep — the target's own `duplicate_of_incident_id` must be null, the target's `status` must not be `MERGED_DUPLICATE`, and the target must belong to the same `product_model_id` — and any breach is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHAIN_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 422). A report with a non-null `duplicate_of_incident_id` is permanently excluded from every hazard-pattern input set, as are `INTAKE` reports that were never classified and `CLOSED_NO_ACTION` reports.

### 3.2. Hazard Pattern Analysis

A hazard pattern is the office's formal claim that a cluster of incidents on one model, inside one date window, constitutes a pattern. A `ComplianceOfficer` or `SafetyDirector` opens one by naming the `product_model_id` under analysis, the inclusive lower bound `window_start` and inclusive upper bound `window_end` on incident dates, an `analysis_basis` of either `PRELIMINARY` or `FINAL`, and the `expected_product_model_version` guard described in §2.7. The window must be well ordered, with `window_end` strictly greater than `window_start`. The new pattern is created in `pattern_status` `ANALYZED`; the enumeration is `ANALYZED`, `CONFIRMED`, `DISMISSED` and `SUPERSEDED`, of which the last two are terminal.

**The qualifying input set.** The analysis considers incidents whose `product_model_id` equals the pattern's, whose `incident_date` falls inside the closed window from `window_start` through `window_end` with both endpoints inclusive, whose `status` is one of `CLASSIFIED`, `CLUSTERED` or `ESCALATED`, and whose `duplicate_of_incident_id` is null. The `analysis_basis` then filters that set further, and this is the one place in the system where the same underlying data yields two legitimately different answers. Under `PRELIMINARY` — the fast triage pass an analyst runs while field verification is still outstanding — the set is narrowed to incidents whose `injury_verified` is true; unverified reports are dropped entirely and contribute nothing to the incident count, nothing to the severity sum, and, critically, nothing to the hazard tally that decides the dominant code and the concentration ratio. Under `FINAL` — the closing pass filed with the reportability evaluation — the set widens to every qualifying incident regardless of `injury_verified`; unverified rows enter at their already-halved `severity_weight`, which raises the severity sum but can dilute the concentration ratio whenever the additional rows carry a different hazard code. The two effects pull in opposite directions, so a `FINAL` figure is neither reliably higher nor reliably lower than the `PRELIMINARY` figure on the same data: it must be computed, never extrapolated.

**The snapshot.** From that set the analysis derives seven stored figures. `incident_count` is the number of incidents in the set. `severity_sum` is the total of their `severity_weight` values, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. `exposure_per_10k` normalises the model's distribution volume: it is the model's `units_distributed` divided by 10,000, but never allowed below a floor of 1.0, and the result is rounded half up to 4 decimal places — the floor exists so that a tiny production run does not divide by a fraction and explode the index. `dominant_hazard_code` is the modal `hazard_code` across the set; when two hazard codes tie on tally, the winner is the code carried by the earliest-created incident in the set, taking incidents in `created_at` ascending order and letting the first row win — never by `id`. `concentration_ratio` is the tally of the dominant code divided by `incident_count`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. When `incident_count` is 0, `dominant_hazard_code` is null and `concentration_ratio` is exactly 0.0000. `risk_index` is then built in two steps from the stored figures: a density term is the `severity_sum` divided by `exposure_per_10k`, and a raw index is that density multiplied by the sum of a concentration base of 0.5 and the `concentration_ratio`. Neither the density nor the raw index is rounded on the way through; the raw index is capped at a ceiling of 100, and only then is the result rounded half up to 2 decimal places and stored as `risk_index`, which therefore always lies between 0.00 and 100.00. Finally `pattern_confirmed` is true only when all three of the following hold at once: `incident_count` is at least 3, `concentration_ratio` is at least 0.6000, and `risk_index` is at least 10.00. Each is a non-strict boundary — a ratio of exactly 0.6000 and an index of exactly 10.00 both qualify. `analyzed_at` is stamped with the logical clock on every analysis run.

**What creating a pattern does to its neighbours.** A new analysis is not a passive read. Every qualifying incident that was `CLASSIFIED` is flipped to `CLUSTERED` with its `hazard_pattern_id` set to the new pattern, processed in `created_at` ascending order; a qualifying incident that was already `CLUSTERED` to an older pattern is re-pointed to the new one, staying in `CLUSTERED`. Every other hazard pattern on the same product model whose `pattern_status` is `ANALYZED` or `CONFIRMED` is flipped to `SUPERSEDED`. The product model's `version` is incremented by 1 — which is why the next analysis of the same model needs a fresh `expected_product_model_version`. The snapshot itself is then immutable with respect to later intake: incidents classified after the pattern was created do not change any of its seven figures.

**Recomputation.** An `ANALYZED` pattern may be re-run any number of times, which is how the two analysis variants are observed on a single row. The recompute operation optionally supplies a new `analysis_basis`, `window_start` or `window_end`; each supplied value overwrites the stored one, and any omitted value is left as it was. It then re-derives all seven snapshot figures, re-stamps `analyzed_at`, and re-clusters: newly qualifying incidents move from `CLASSIFIED` to `CLUSTERED` on this pattern, while incidents that have dropped out of the qualifying set return to `CLASSIFIED` with `hazard_pattern_id` set back to null. The pattern stays in `ANALYZED`. Recomputation requires the pattern to be in `ANALYZED` — any other status draws `ERR_PATTERN_STATE_INVALID` — and is additionally blocked with `ERR_MODEL_POSTURE_FROZEN` while the product model behind it is `FROZEN`, because narrowing the evidence base under an open escalation is not permitted.

**Adjudication.** Adjudicating a pattern takes a `decision` and an optional `note` and forks into two branches with deliberately divergent effects. Both branches require the pattern to be in `ANALYZED`, otherwise `ERR_PATTERN_STATE_INVALID`. A `decision` of `CONFIRMED` moves the pattern `ANALYZED → CONFIRMED` and makes it eligible for a reportability evaluation; it requires the pattern's stored `pattern_confirmed` to be true, and a pattern that failed any of the three confirmability thresholds is refused with `ERR_PATTERN_NOT_CONFIRMABLE` (HTTP 422). This branch touches no incident and leaves the model's `hazard_posture` exactly where it was. A `decision` of `DISMISSED` moves the pattern `ANALYZED → DISMISSED` and releases the whole cluster: every incident whose `hazard_pattern_id` equals this pattern is flipped from `CLUSTERED` back to `CLASSIFIED` with its `hazard_pattern_id` cleared to null, processed in `created_at` ascending order, so those reports become eligible for a later pattern. This branch is gated on containment: it is refused with `ERR_MODEL_POSTURE_FROZEN` while the model is `FROZEN`, and like the confirm branch it leaves `hazard_posture` unchanged. The value `SUPERSEDED` is reserved for the system — it is reached only by the supersession cascade of a newer analysis — and is not an accepted `decision`, so supplying it is a malformed request answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 3.3. Reportability Evaluation and the Internal Escalation Clock

Once a pattern is `CONFIRMED`, the office must decide whether the product is reportable. A `ComplianceOfficer` or `SafetyDirector` opens an evaluation by naming the `hazard_pattern_id` under evaluation and the `standard_basis` — the legal standard being applied, one of `DEFECT`, `UNREASONABLE_RISK`, `NONCOMPLIANCE` and `VOLUNTARY_STANDARD_FAILURE`. The referenced pattern must be in `pattern_status` `CONFIRMED`; a pattern in any other status draws `ERR_PATTERN_STATE_INVALID`. Only one live evaluation may exist per pattern: if another evaluation on the same `hazard_pattern_id` is currently `OPEN`, `IN_REVIEW` or `REPORTABLE`, the request is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DOCKET_ENTRY`. On creation the evaluation copies `product_model_id` verbatim from the pattern's own `product_model_id`, stamps `opened_at` with the logical clock, and sets `escalation_breached` to false. Its `status` starts at `OPEN` and moves through `OPEN`, `IN_REVIEW` and then one of the two terminal outcomes `REPORTABLE` or `NOT_REPORTABLE`. The fields `review_started_at`, `determined_at`, `report_due_at`, `defect_score`, `risk_score` and `determination_note` are all null until the transitions below populate them.

The **internal escalation deadline** is derived at creation and stored as `internal_escalation_due_at`. It is `opened_at` plus a window whose length depends on how dangerous the underlying pattern is: when the pattern's `risk_index` is at least 25.00 the office gives itself only 48 hours (two days), and otherwise it allows 120 hours (five days). The threshold is non-strict, so a `risk_index` of exactly 25.00 buys the shorter window.

Beginning the internal review advances the evaluation `OPEN → IN_REVIEW`, stamps `review_started_at` with the logical clock, and settles the breach question once and for all: `escalation_breached` is set to true when `review_started_at` is **strictly later** than `internal_escalation_due_at`, and otherwise left false. Starting review at exactly `internal_escalation_due_at` is therefore on time. An evaluation not in `OPEN` cannot begin review and draws `ERR_EVALUATION_STATE_INVALID`.

Determination closes the evaluation and takes four inputs: a `determination` of either `REPORTABLE` or `NOT_REPORTABLE`, a `defect_score` integer between 0 and 10 scoring the defect standard, a `risk_score` integer between 0 and 10 scoring the unreasonable-risk standard, and a `determination_note` carrying the analyst's rationale. The evaluation must be `IN_REVIEW`, otherwise `ERR_EVALUATION_STATE_INVALID`. Whichever branch is requested, all three of `defect_score`, `risk_score` and `determination_note` are recorded and `determined_at` is stamped with the logical clock.

Both branches are then guarded by a **score-support test** keyed on the evaluation's own `standard_basis`, and the two branches are exact mirror images of each other. For a `determination` of `REPORTABLE`: under `DEFECT` the `defect_score` must be at least 5; under `UNREASONABLE_RISK` the `risk_score` must be at least 5; under `NONCOMPLIANCE` or `VOLUNTARY_STANDARD_FAILURE` the sum of `defect_score` and `risk_score` must be at least 8. For a `determination` of `NOT_REPORTABLE` the same three tests must fail strictly: under `DEFECT` the `defect_score` must be below 5; under `UNREASONABLE_RISK` the `risk_score` must be below 5; under `NONCOMPLIANCE` or `VOLUNTARY_STANDARD_FAILURE` the sum of `defect_score` and `risk_score` must be below 8. An analyst therefore cannot file a no-action determination on evidence that meets the reporting standard, nor a reportable determination on evidence that does not. Either failure is refused with `ERR_DETERMINATION_UNSUPPORTED` (HTTP 422).

The two branches diverge sharply in what they do to the rest of the docket. A `REPORTABLE` determination starts the statutory report clock and escalates the cluster: `report_due_at` is set to `determined_at` plus 240 hours (ten days); every incident whose `hazard_pattern_id` equals this evaluation's pattern and whose `status` is `CLUSTERED` is flipped `CLUSTERED → ESCALATED` with its `escalation_deadline_at` set to `determined_at` plus 120 hours (five days), processed in `created_at` ascending order; the number of incidents flipped is added to the product model's `open_escalation_count`, so that the counter equals the model's escalated-incident total; and the model's `hazard_posture` is set to `FROZEN`. Each escalated incident and the product model both have their `version` incremented as part of that cascade. A `NOT_REPORTABLE` determination does none of this: `report_due_at` stays null, every incident keeps its existing `status` and `escalation_deadline_at`, `open_escalation_count` is untouched, and the model's `hazard_posture` is relaxed to `MONITORED`.

### 3.4. Statutory Report Preparation, Filing, Review and Revision

A statutory report is drafted against a `REPORTABLE` evaluation. A `ComplianceOfficer` or `SafetyDirector` supplies the `reportability_evaluation_id`, a `corrective_action_type` of `REPAIR`, `REPLACE`, `REFUND` or `RETROFIT_KIT` naming the corrective action proposed to the regulator, and a `corrective_action_summary` of between 1 and 2000 characters narrating that action. The referenced evaluation must be in `status` `REPORTABLE`; anything else draws `ERR_EVALUATION_STATE_INVALID`. One report per evaluation: a second report against an evaluation that already has one in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DOCKET_ENTRY`. The new report is created in `DRAFT` with `days_late` at 0, `late_surcharge_cents` at 0, and `submitted_at` and `reviewed_at` both null. Its `status` moves among `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED` and `RETURNED_FOR_INFO`, with `ACCEPTED` the only terminal value.

Filing the report advances it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, stamps `submitted_at` with the logical clock, scores its lateness, and sets the product model's `hazard_posture` to `RESTRICTED`. A report not in `DRAFT` cannot be filed and draws `ERR_SECTION_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`.

**Lateness scoring.** Lateness is measured against the immutable `report_due_at` of the report's evaluation, reached through `reportability_evaluation_id`. The number of late seconds is the elapsed time from `report_due_at` to `submitted_at`, floored at zero so an early filing scores nothing. `days_late` is that figure divided by 86,400 seconds and then rounded **up** to a whole number, so any part-day counts as a whole day: one second late is one day late, exactly 86,400 seconds late is still one day late, and 86,401 seconds late is two. Filing at exactly `report_due_at` yields zero late seconds, a `days_late` of 0 and no surcharge — the boundary is inclusive and on time. When `days_late` is 0 the `late_surcharge_cents` is 0; otherwise it is 250,000 cents per late day, capped at 5,000,000 cents in total, so the charge is the smaller of the per-day product and the cap. Both figures are integers.

Review is the `SafetyDirector`'s exclusive decision and takes a `decision` plus an optional `note`. The report must be `SUBMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_SECTION_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`. Both branches stamp `reviewed_at` with the logical clock. A `decision` of `ACCEPTED` advances the report `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, clearing it for recall planning, and leaves the product model's `hazard_posture` at `RESTRICTED`. A `decision` of `RETURNED_FOR_INFO` advances it `SUBMITTED → RETURNED_FOR_INFO` and sets the model's `hazard_posture` to `FROZEN`, because the agency has an unanswered question about a product with an escalated cluster behind it.

Revision closes the loop. A report in `RETURNED_FOR_INFO` may be returned to `DRAFT`, optionally amending `corrective_action_type` and `corrective_action_summary` — each supplied value overwrites the stored one, and omitting both leaves the proposal as it was. `submitted_at` is reset to null while `days_late` and `late_surcharge_cents` keep their previous values until the next filing recomputes them. A report in any other status cannot be revised and draws `ERR_SECTION_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`. The cycle `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → RETURNED_FOR_INFO → DRAFT` may repeat, and because each filing re-scores lateness against the *same* unchanged `report_due_at`, a second late filing can only raise the surcharge, never lower it.

### 3.5. The Recall Plan: Authoring, Approval and Two Independent Dimensions

A recall plan is opened against an `ACCEPTED` statutory report. The author — a `ComplianceOfficer` or `SafetyDirector` — supplies the `section_report_id`, a `remedy_options` array giving the remedy menu offered to consumers, a `units_in_commerce` count of at least 1 giving the units the recall must account for, a `participation_goal_pct` consumer-participation goal expressed in percent, and a `correction_deadline_at` naming the contractual date by which corrections must reach that goal. The `remedy_options` array must be non-empty and its members must be distinct values drawn from `REPAIR`, `REPLACE`, `REFUND` and `RETROFIT_KIT`. The `participation_goal_pct` must be greater than 0 and no greater than 100, carrying at most 2 decimal places. The referenced report must be in `status` `ACCEPTED`, otherwise `ERR_SECTION_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`; and one recall per report, so a second recall on the same `section_report_id` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DOCKET_ENTRY`.

A new recall is created with `plan_status` at `DRAFT`, `notification_status` at `NOT_STARTED`, `units_corrected` at 0, `units_outstanding` initialised to `units_in_commerce`, `effectiveness_flag` at `PENDING`, and `stop_sale_effective_at`, `submitted_by_user_id` and `last_accepted_period_end` all null.

A recall carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**, and they do not move in lockstep. The plan dimension, `plan_status`, runs `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → APPROVED → MONITORING → CLOSED`, with `CLOSED` terminal. The notification dimension, `notification_status`, runs `NOT_STARTED → IN_PROGRESS`, may oscillate `IN_PROGRESS ↔ DEFICIENT` across successive compliance sweeps and late acknowledgements, and reaches the terminal `SATISFIED` from `IN_PROGRESS`. Every transition on either dimension increments the recall's `version` by 1; where a plan transition also drives the notification dimension, that is a single mutation of the row and a single version increment.

Submitting the plan advances `plan_status` `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and stores the acting `X-User-ID` as `submitted_by_user_id`. It is refused unless the recall already has at least one consumer notice on the `DIRECT_NOTICE` channel, in either `SCHEDULED` or `RELEASED` status — a plan without a direct-notice component is incomplete, and the attempt draws `ERR_DIRECT_NOTICE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). A recall whose `plan_status` is not `DRAFT` cannot be submitted and draws `ERR_RECALL_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. Both `ComplianceOfficer` and `SafetyDirector` may submit, which is exactly what gives the segregation gate on approval its teeth.

**Approval and the stop-sale cascade.** Plan approval is a `SafetyDirector`-only signature and requires `plan_status` to be `SUBMITTED`; it is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the stored `submitted_by_user_id`. On success it fires a three-deep cascade. The first link advances `plan_status` `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and stamps `stop_sale_effective_at` with the logical clock. The second link reaches every retailer notification on this recall whose `status` is `PENDING` and flips it `PENDING → ISSUED`, setting `stop_sale_required` to true, `issued_at` to that same logical-clock instant, and `ack_due_at` to `issued_at` plus 72 hours (three days), processing the notifications in `created_at` ascending order. A follow-on effect of that link moves the recall's `notification_status` `NOT_STARTED → IN_PROGRESS`, but only when at least one notification actually flipped: when the recall had no `PENDING` notification at approval time the notification dimension stays at `NOT_STARTED` while `plan_status` still advances to `APPROVED`, which is the clearest demonstration that the two dimensions are independent. The third link reaches each flipped notification's retailer, incrementing that retailer's `open_stop_sale_count` by 1 and setting its `compliance_posture` to `STOP_SALE_ACTIVE`. Every notification and every retailer touched has its `version` incremented by 1.

Entering the monitoring phase advances `plan_status` `APPROVED → MONITORING` and is likewise a `SafetyDirector` signature. It requires that the public has actually been told: at least one consumer notice on this recall must already be in `RELEASED` status, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_CONSUMER_NOTICE_NOT_RELEASED` (HTTP 409). A recall not in `APPROVED` draws `ERR_RECALL_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`.

**Closure.** Closing a recall advances `plan_status` `MONITORING → CLOSED` and is gated by four independent conditions which are evaluated in a fixed order, so the operator always hears the most urgent unresolved fact first. The recall must be in `MONITORING`, else `ERR_RECALL_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. Its `notification_status` must be `SATISFIED`, else `ERR_NOTIFICATION_NOT_SATISFIED` (HTTP 409) — this is the shelf-removal gate, and it deliberately outranks both the paperwork gate and the correction-rate gate, because product still sitting on a shelf is the most urgent fact about a recall. At least one progress report on this recall must be in `ACCEPTED` status, else `ERR_PROGRESS_REPORT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422); merely filing an effectiveness assessment is not enough, the agency must have reviewed it. And the recall's `effectiveness_flag` must equal `MET`, else `ERR_EFFECTIVENESS_NOT_MET` (HTTP 409). On success, closure relaxes the containment gate on the product model reached from the recall through its `section_report_id` and that report's `reportability_evaluation_id`: when this recall was that model's last open escalation source, the model's `open_escalation_count` is set back to 0 and its `hazard_posture` back to `MONITORED`.

### 3.6. The Retailer Channel: Enrollment, Stop-Sale, Sweeps and Shelf Audits

Retailers are enrolled in a recall's stop-sale distribution list one at a time, by a `ComplianceOfficer` or `SafetyDirector` naming the `recall_id` and the `retailer_id`. Enrollment is only legal while the recall's `plan_status` is `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED`; after approval the window closes, because a notification created later would never be reached by the approval cascade and would sit in `PENDING` forever, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_RECALL_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. At most one notification may exist per pairing of `recall_id` and `retailer_id`; a second is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RETAILER_NOTIFICATION` (HTTP 409). The enrollment body also tolerates a `stop_sale_required` key, but that field is server-owned and any supplied value is silently ignored: a new notification is always created in `PENDING` with `stop_sale_required` false and with `issued_at`, `ack_due_at`, `acknowledged_at`, `verified_at` and `units_on_shelf_reported` all null. A notification's `status` runs through `PENDING`, `ISSUED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `VERIFIED` and `DELINQUENT`, of which `VERIFIED` is terminal.

**Compliance sweeps.** A sweep is a query-and-mark capability over one recall's notifications, available to a `SafetyDirector` or `ComplianceOfficer`, and it requires the recall's `plan_status` to be `APPROVED` or `MONITORING`, otherwise `ERR_RECALL_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. Every notification on the recall that is still `ISSUED` and whose `ack_due_at` the logical clock has **strictly** passed is flipped `ISSUED → DELINQUENT`, processed in `created_at` ascending order; a notification whose `ack_due_at` exactly equals the current clock reading is not yet overdue and is left alone. For each notification flipped, the owning retailer's `delinquent_count` is incremented by 1 and its `compliance_posture` is set to `DELINQUENT`. When at least one notification was flipped, the recall's `notification_status` moves `IN_PROGRESS → DEFICIENT`. A sweep that finds nothing newly overdue changes nothing and is still answered successfully — it is a scan, not a state transition on the recall itself, and it always answers with the recall record.

**Acknowledgement.** The retailer acknowledges the stop-sale order and reports the units it still holds, supplying a `units_on_shelf_reported` count of at least 0. Acknowledgement is legal from `ISSUED` (on time) and from `DELINQUENT` (a late acknowledgement); from any other status it draws `ERR_NOTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID`. It stamps `acknowledged_at` with the logical clock and records `units_on_shelf_reported`. A `RetailerRep` may only acknowledge a notification whose `retailer_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`, per §2.1; a `ComplianceOfficer` may acknowledge on a retailer's behalf. When the notification was `DELINQUENT`, acknowledgement also repairs the retailer's posture: `compliance_posture` returns to `STOP_SALE_ACTIVE` when that retailer's `open_stop_sale_count` is at least 1, and to `CLEAR` otherwise. Acknowledgement does *not* reduce `delinquent_count` — that counter is monotonic, recording how many times this retailer has ever been swept into delinquency, and it is never decremented. Finally, when the acknowledgement cleared the last `DELINQUENT` notification on the recall, the recall's `notification_status` moves `DEFICIENT → IN_PROGRESS`; the recall may oscillate between those two values across successive sweeps and late acknowledgements.

**Shelf-removal audits.** A `FieldAuditor` or `SafetyDirector` schedules an on-site inspection by naming the `retailer_notification_id` whose stop-sale instruction the visit verifies, together with a `window_start` and a `window_end`. Audit windows are **half-open**: they include `window_start` and exclude `window_end`, and `window_end` must be strictly greater than `window_start`. The notification under audit must not already be `VERIFIED` — any of `PENDING`, `ISSUED`, `DELINQUENT` and `ACKNOWLEDGED` is acceptable, and the terminal state draws `ERR_NOTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID`. The retailer identified by that notification's `retailer_id` must actually be under an active stop-sale, meaning its `compliance_posture` is `STOP_SALE_ACTIVE` or `DELINQUENT`; a `CLEAR` retailer has nothing to inspect and the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_STOP_SALE` (HTTP 409). A new visit is created with `status` `SCHEDULED`, `verdict` `PENDING`, and `shelf_units_found` and `completed_at` both null; `status` runs `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` and `verdict` is one of `PENDING`, `PASS` and `FAIL`.

Because one inspector team covers a store rather than a docket, audit windows are deconflicted **per retailer across all recalls**. The comparison set is every other `SCHEDULED` audit visit whose `retailer_notification_id` belongs to the same `retailer_id`; `COMPLETED` visits are ignored. Each window is widened by a travel buffer of 24 hours on each side, and the new visit conflicts with an existing one when the new `window_start` falls before the existing `window_end` plus 24 hours **and** the existing `window_start` falls before the new `window_end` plus 24 hours. Because the interval is half-open, a new window starting at exactly the existing `window_end` plus 24 hours does not conflict. A conflict is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).

Completing a visit is a `FieldAuditor`-only act. It requires the visit to be `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_VISIT_STATE_INVALID`, and takes a `verdict` of either `PASS` or `FAIL` together with the `shelf_units_found` count of recalled units still on shelves. The verdict may never contradict the count: a `PASS` requires `shelf_units_found` to be exactly 0, and a `FAIL` requires `shelf_units_found` to be greater than 0. One remaining unit is a `FAIL`; a `FAIL` claimed with a count of 0 is equally invalid. Either contradiction is refused with `ERR_SHELF_UNITS_PRESENT` (HTTP 422). On success the visit advances `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`, its `verdict` moves from `PENDING` to the recorded value, and `completed_at` is stamped with the logical clock.

**Verification.** Verifying shelf removal — the act of a `SafetyDirector` or `FieldAuditor` — advances the notification `ACKNOWLEDGED → VERIFIED` and stamps `verified_at` with the logical clock. It requires the notification to be `ACKNOWLEDGED`, otherwise `ERR_NOTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID`, and it requires evidence: at least one audit visit on this `retailer_notification_id` must be `COMPLETED` with `verdict` `PASS`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_SHELF_REMOVAL_UNVERIFIED` (HTTP 409). Verification then cascades twice. On the retailer, `open_stop_sale_count` is decremented by 1 and `verified_removal_count` is incremented by 1, and `compliance_posture` is set to `CLEAR` when `open_stop_sale_count` reaches 0. On the recall, when this was the last notification of that recall not yet `VERIFIED` — that is, when every notification on the recall is now `VERIFIED` — the `notification_status` moves `IN_PROGRESS → SATISFIED`, which is the precondition closure will later demand.

### 3.7. Telling the Public: Consumer Notification Channels

Consumer notification is planned channel by channel. A `ComplianceOfficer` or `SafetyDirector` schedules a notice by naming the `recall_id` it belongs to, a `channel` of `DIRECT_NOTICE`, `PRESS_RELEASE`, `REGISTRATION_CARD` or `WEB_POSTING`, a `scheduled_for` instant giving the earliest moment at which the notice may be released, a `reach_count` of at least 0 giving the number of consumers the notice is expected to reach, and optionally an `embargo_until` instant. `embargo_until` defaults to null when omitted and is only meaningful for a press release; when it is present, release additionally requires the clock to have reached it. Scheduling is legal while the recall's `plan_status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `MONITORING`; outside that window the request draws `ERR_RECALL_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. Each notice is created in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `released_at` null, and its `status` runs `SCHEDULED → RELEASED` and then, when overtaken, `RELEASED → SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal.

One channel carries an extra ceiling. A `REGISTRATION_CARD` notice can only reach owners the firm actually has on file, so its `reach_count` may not exceed the `units_registered` of the product model behind the recall — a model reached by following the recall's `section_report_id` to the report, that report's `reportability_evaluation_id` to the evaluation, and the evaluation's `product_model_id` to the model. Exceeding that figure is refused with `ERR_REACH_EXCEEDS_REGISTRY` (HTTP 422). The `DIRECT_NOTICE`, `PRESS_RELEASE` and `WEB_POSTING` channels have no registry ceiling at all.

Releasing a notice advances it `SCHEDULED → RELEASED` and stamps `released_at` with the logical clock. Four conditions gate the release, and they are checked in this order. The notice must be `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_NOTICE_STATE_INVALID`. The recall's `plan_status` must be `APPROVED` or `MONITORING`, otherwise `ERR_RECALL_PLAN_STATE_INVALID` — nothing goes out to the public before the plan is signed. The logical clock must have reached `scheduled_for`, with a non-strict comparison so that releasing at exactly `scheduled_for` is allowed; releasing earlier is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_DUE` (HTTP 409). And when `embargo_until` is non-null, the logical clock must also have reached it, again non-strictly, so releasing at exactly `embargo_until` is allowed and releasing earlier is refused with `ERR_EMBARGO_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). Because the schedule check precedes the embargo check, a notice that is both un-due and embargoed reports the schedule problem first.

Release also supersedes the office's own earlier messaging on that channel. Every *other* notice on the same `recall_id` and the same `channel` whose `status` is `RELEASED` is flipped to `SUPERSEDED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, each with its `version` incremented. Notices on other channels of the same recall are untouched, so a fresh press release never supersedes a standing web posting. Only the filtered notice collection reveals these supersessions; the release response describes only the notice released.

### 3.8. The Remedy Ledger, Effectiveness and Monthly Progress Reporting

Consumer remedy claims are registered by a `ComplianceOfficer` against an `APPROVED` or `MONITORING` recall — any other `plan_status` draws `ERR_RECALL_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. The claim carries the `recall_id`, a `consumer_ref` of between 1 and 64 characters holding the opaque consumer reference supplied by intake, a `remedy_type` naming the remedy the consumer elected, a `quantity` of at least 1 giving the units submitted for correction, a `unit_price_cents` giving the documented retail price per unit, and a `shipping_cents_per_unit` giving the return-shipping cost reimbursed per unit. Both money inputs are non-negative integers of cents. The elected `remedy_type` must be a member of the recall's own `remedy_options` array — a consumer cannot elect a remedy the firm never offered — and a non-member is refused with `ERR_REMEDY_OPTION_NOT_OFFERED` (HTTP 422). The `quantity` may not exceed the recall's current `units_outstanding`, and a claim promising more units than remain uncorrected is refused with `ERR_UNITS_OUTSTANDING_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). A new claim is created in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `fulfilled_at` and `voided_at` null; its `status` runs through `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, `FULFILLED` and the terminal `VOID`. Registration deliberately moves no units: neither `units_corrected` nor `units_outstanding` changes until fulfilment.

**Reimbursement.** The claim's `reimbursement_total_cents` is computed once at registration and frozen; voiding the claim later does not recompute it, because the stored total is exactly what gets reversed. The per-unit base value depends on the remedy elected. A `REPAIR` claim uses a fixed repair-kit cost of 2,500 cents and **ignores** `unit_price_cents` entirely. A `RETROFIT_KIT` claim uses a fixed kit cost of 4,200 cents and likewise ignores `unit_price_cents`. A `REFUND` or `REPLACE` claim uses the supplied `unit_price_cents` as its base value. To that base value the `shipping_cents_per_unit` is added, giving a per-unit subtotal. A handling fee of 7.5 percent of that per-unit subtotal is then computed and **rounded half up to whole cents at the per-unit level, before any multiplication by quantity**; the rounded fee is added to the per-unit subtotal to give a per-unit total, and the per-unit total is multiplied by `quantity` to give `reimbursement_total_cents`. The evaluation order is mandatory: summing across units and rounding once at the end yields a different and incorrect number whenever the fee lands on a fractional cent. A consequence worth remembering as a self-check is that the stored total is always exactly divisible by `quantity`.

Approving a claim advances it `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and moves no units. It re-checks the quantity against the recall's `units_outstanding` as it stands at approval time, because other claims may have been fulfilled in the meantime, and a claim that no longer fits is refused with `ERR_UNITS_OUTSTANDING_EXCEEDED`. A claim in any other status draws `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`.

**Fulfilment and the four-link credit cascade.** Fulfilment is a `ComplianceOfficer`-only act, legal only from `APPROVED` and only while the recall's `plan_status` is `APPROVED` or `MONITORING`. Its first link advances the claim `APPROVED → FULFILLED` and stamps `fulfilled_at` with the logical clock. Its second link moves the recall's unit ledger: `units_corrected` increases by the claim's `quantity` and `units_outstanding` decreases by the same `quantity`, so that the conserved sum of the two continues to equal `units_in_commerce`. Its third link, triggered because `units_corrected` changed, re-evaluates the correction rate on the units basis and flips the recall's `effectiveness_flag` from `PENDING` to `MET` when that rate has reached the recall's `participation_goal_pct`. Its fourth link, triggered only when the third produced `MET`, reaches the civil penalty case whose `section_report_id` equals the recall's own `section_report_id` and whose `status` is `ASSESSMENT_OPEN`, `OFFER_MADE` or `COUNTERED`: that case has `cooperation_bonus_applied` set to true, its non-binding settlement preview recomputed, and its `version` incremented by 1. The recall's own `version` is incremented once for the whole cascade.

**The correction rate.** The rate always uses the recall's **cumulative** `units_corrected` as its numerator — never a period delta — multiplied by 100 and divided by a denominator chosen by the reporting basis, with the quotient rounded half up to 2 decimal places. On the `UNITS_BASIS` variant the denominator is the recall's `units_in_commerce`, that is every unit believed to be in consumer hands; this is the regulator-facing correction rate, and it is the variant that always drives `effectiveness_flag`. On the `PARTICIPATION_BASIS` variant the denominator is the product model's `units_registered`, floored at 1 so that a product with no registrations never divides by zero; this is the variant a firm with registration-card coverage uses to report against the population it can actually reach. Because `units_registered` is normally far smaller than `units_in_commerce`, the same `units_corrected` yields a much higher rate on the participation basis and can lift the assessment a whole band. The `effectiveness_flag` itself is one of `PENDING`, `MET` and `SHORTFALL`.

The narrative assessment attached to a filed progress report is derived from the rounded rate against the recall's goal, using non-strict comparisons so that a rate rounding up to exactly the goal counts as met. When the rate is at least `participation_goal_pct`, the `assessment` is `ON_TRACK`. Otherwise, when the rate is at least half of `participation_goal_pct` — that halved figure itself rounded half up to 2 decimal places before the comparison — the `assessment` is `BELOW_TARGET`. Below that, the `assessment` is `CRITICAL`. The enumeration also includes `PENDING`, which is the value a report carries before it is filed.

**Voiding.** A claim may be voided from `APPROVED` or from `FULFILLED`, with an optional `reason`; from any other status the attempt draws `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. Either way the claim moves to the terminal `VOID` and `voided_at` is stamped with the logical clock. Voiding from `APPROVED` reverses nothing, because an approved claim was never counted: the unit ledger, the `effectiveness_flag` and the penalty case are all left exactly as they were. Voiding from `FULFILLED` is the exact compensation of the fulfilment cascade and is additionally refused with `ERR_RECALL_PLAN_STATE_INVALID` when the recall's `plan_status` is already `CLOSED`. Its second link subtracts the claim's stored `quantity` from the recall's `units_corrected` and adds the same `quantity` back to `units_outstanding`, restoring the conserved total exactly. Its third link re-evaluates the rate on the units basis and, when the rate has fallen back below `participation_goal_pct`, reverts `effectiveness_flag` out of `MET` — to `PENDING` normally, or to `SHORTFALL` when at least one progress report on the recall has already been accepted. Its fourth link, triggered only when the flag actually reverted out of `MET`, reaches the same linked civil penalty case in `ASSESSMENT_OPEN`, `OFFER_MADE` or `COUNTERED` and sets `cooperation_bonus_applied` back to false, recomputes its settlement preview, and increments its `version` by 1. No drift is left behind in the unit ledger and no residual cooperation credit survives.

**Monthly progress reports.** A `ComplianceOfficer` or `SafetyDirector` opens a monthly report against a recall whose `plan_status` is `APPROVED` or `MONITORING`, supplying the `recall_id`, a `period_start`, a `period_end` and an `effectiveness_basis` of either `UNITS_BASIS` or `PARTICIPATION_BASIS`. Reporting periods are **closed** intervals: both `period_start` and `period_end` are inclusive, and `period_end` must be strictly greater than `period_start`. The acting `X-User-ID` is stored as `prepared_by_user_id`. The report is created in `status` `DRAFT` with `assessment` at `PENDING`, `effectiveness_rate` at 0, `units_corrected_in_period` at 0, `cumulative_units_corrected` at 0, and `submitted_at` and `accepted_at` null; its `status` runs `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, with `ACCEPTED` terminal.

Two temporal rules keep the monthly series honest, and they are checked in this order against the comparison set of progress reports on the *same* recall whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` — `DRAFT` reports are excluded from both, so an abandoned draft never blocks a real filing. First, periods may not overlap: because both endpoints are inclusive, the new period conflicts with an existing one when the new `period_start` is at or before the existing `period_end` and the existing `period_start` is at or before the new `period_end`, and a conflict is refused with `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Note the deliberate contrast with audit windows: a new reporting period beginning at exactly the previous `period_end` *does* overlap, whereas an audit window beginning at the previous `window_end` does not. Second, periods may not skip time: taking the latest `period_end` across that same comparison set, the new `period_start` must equal that latest `period_end` plus exactly 24 hours; and when no such prior report exists, the new `period_start` must equal the recall's `stop_sale_effective_at` exactly, so that the very first monthly period begins the instant the stop-sale took effect. Any other start is a reporting gap and is refused with `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_GAP` (HTTP 422).

Filing a progress report advances it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, stamps `submitted_at` with the logical clock, and fills in the three computed figures. `units_corrected_in_period` is the total `quantity` across this recall's claims that are in `status` `FULFILLED` and whose `fulfilled_at` falls inside the closed interval from `period_start` through `period_end`, both endpoints inclusive; claims that have been voided contribute nothing. `cumulative_units_corrected` is a snapshot of the recall's `units_corrected` at the moment of filing. `effectiveness_rate` is the correction rate computed under the report's declared `effectiveness_basis`, and `assessment` is derived from it. The period and cumulative unit figures are reported for the record and do **not** feed the rate. A report not in `DRAFT` draws `ERR_PROGRESS_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`.

Acceptance is the `SafetyDirector`'s act and requires the report to be `SUBMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_PROGRESS_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`; it is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the stored `prepared_by_user_id`. On success the report advances `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED` and `accepted_at` is stamped with the logical clock. Acceptance also writes back to the recall: `last_accepted_period_end` is set to this report's `period_end`, and when the accepted report's `assessment` is `CRITICAL` the recall's `effectiveness_flag` is set to `SHORTFALL`. The recall's `version` is incremented for that write.

### 3.9. The Civil Penalty Docket: Assessment, Negotiation and the Consent Agreement

A late or deficient filing exposes the firm to a civil penalty, and that exposure is docketed separately from the recall itself. `PenaltyCounsel` opens a case by naming the `section_report_id` of the filing at issue and a `violation_count` of at least 1 giving the number of chargeable violations the office alleges. The report must actually have been filed — its `status` must be `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_INFO`, so a report still sitting in `DRAFT` cannot carry a penalty and draws `ERR_SECTION_REPORT_STATE_INVALID`. At most one case may exist per `section_report_id`; a second attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DOCKET_ENTRY`. The case's `status` runs the negotiation cycle `ASSESSMENT_OPEN → OFFER_MADE → COUNTERED → OFFER_MADE`, with `OFFER_MADE → ASSESSMENT_OPEN` on withdrawal and `OFFER_MADE → AGREED` on acceptance; `AGREED` is terminal.

**The frozen assessment.** Opening the case computes `assessed_penalty_cents` once and never recomputes it — every later offer, credit and consent agreement is measured against that frozen number. The computation starts from a base of 1,000,000 cents per alleged violation, that is 1,000,000 multiplied by `violation_count`. It is then scaled by a severity multiplier drawn from the worst injury behind the docket. The relevant incidents are those reached by following the report's `reportability_evaluation_id` to its evaluation and that evaluation's `hazard_pattern_id` to the pattern, then taking the incidents carrying that same `hazard_pattern_id` whose `status` is `ESCALATED` — the incidents the determination actually escalated. Across that set the worst `injury_class` is selected under the ordering in which `NO_INJURY` is least severe, then `FIRST_AID`, then `MEDICAL_TREATMENT`, then `HOSPITALIZATION`, and `FATALITY` is most severe. When the worst injury is `FATALITY` the severity multiplier is 1.50; when it is `HOSPITALIZATION` the multiplier is 1.25; for every other worst injury the multiplier is 1.00. When the set is empty, the multiplier is likewise 1.00. The base is then scaled a second time by a lateness multiplier of 1.00 plus the smaller of 0.50 and the product of 0.05 and the report's stored `days_late`, so each full day of delay adds five percent up to a hard ceiling of fifty percent. The base, the severity multiplier and the lateness multiplier are multiplied together; the statutory ceiling of 17,150,000 cents is then applied by taking the smaller of the product and that ceiling; and only then is the result rounded half up to whole cents, exactly once. The ceiling is a hard cap, not a scaling factor: a product that lands above it is replaced by it outright rather than proportionally reduced.

A new case is created in `status` `ASSESSMENT_OPEN`, with `reserved_cents` at 0 and `available_cents` equal to `assessed_penalty_cents`, so the conserved reserve identity holds from the first instant. `current_offer_round` starts at 0, `current_offer_cents` and `current_offer_by` are null, `cooperation_bonus_applied` is false, `cooperation_credit_tier`, `cooperation_credit_cents` and `consent_agreement_total_cents` are null, `compliance_program_required` is false, and `reporting_obligation_months` is 0. Creation also computes the non-binding `consent_agreement_preview_cents`.

**Offers.** An offer carries an `amount_cents`, an `offered_by` of either `AGENCY` or `RESPONDENT`, and an `expected_case_version` pinning the case version the client last read. Four gates apply, in this order. The `expected_case_version` must equal the case's stored `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_PENALTY_CASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412); this guard is required rather than optional because the claim-fulfilment cascade can advance the case's `version` behind the client's back by granting the cooperation bonus. The case must then be in the right state for the party tabling the offer: an `AGENCY` offer is legal from `ASSESSMENT_OPEN` and from `COUNTERED`, while a `RESPONDENT` offer is legal only from `OFFER_MADE`, and a mismatch draws `ERR_PENALTY_CASE_STATE_INVALID`. Because the version check precedes the state check, an offer that is both stale and fired from the wrong state reports the stale version. Next, `current_offer_round` must be strictly less than 6, otherwise `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409) — the parties get six rounds and no more. Finally, `amount_cents` may not exceed `assessed_penalty_cents`, with equality allowed so that a full-value agency offer is legal, and a larger figure is refused with `ERR_OFFER_EXCEEDS_ASSESSMENT` (HTTP 422).

A legal offer increments `current_offer_round` by 1, overwrites `current_offer_cents` with `amount_cents` and `current_offer_by` with `offered_by`, moves the case's `status` accordingly — `ASSESSMENT_OPEN → OFFER_MADE` or `COUNTERED → OFFER_MADE` for an agency offer, `OFFER_MADE → COUNTERED` for a respondent offer — recomputes `consent_agreement_preview_cents` against the new standing offer, and increments the case's `version` by 1. The reserve moves too, and the two directions are deliberately asymmetric. An `AGENCY` offer commits the money the office is prepared to settle for: `reserved_cents` becomes `amount_cents` and `available_cents` becomes `assessed_penalty_cents` minus `amount_cents`. A `RESPONDENT` counter is not the office's own commitment, so it releases the agency reserve entirely: `reserved_cents` returns to 0 and `available_cents` returns to the full `assessed_penalty_cents`. Either way the two figures still sum to `assessed_penalty_cents`. The round counter is monotonic: it counts every offer entered into the record regardless of which side made it, is never decremented, and is capped at 6.

**Resolving the standing offer.** Disposing of an offer takes a `resolution` of either `WITHDRAWN` or `ACCEPTED` and an optional `note`. Both require the case to be in `OFFER_MADE` and additionally require `current_offer_by` to be `AGENCY`; a respondent counter is not the office's to withdraw or accept, and either failure draws `ERR_PENALTY_CASE_STATE_INVALID`. `WITHDRAWN` returns the case `OFFER_MADE → ASSESSMENT_OPEN`, clears `current_offer_cents` and `current_offer_by` back to null, and compensates the reserve exactly by setting `reserved_cents` to 0 and `available_cents` to `assessed_penalty_cents`. It pointedly does **not** give the round back: a withdrawn offer still consumed a round, so `current_offer_round` is left where it stands and six withdrawn offers exhaust the negotiation just as surely as six live ones. `ACCEPTED` advances the case `OFFER_MADE → AGREED`, which is terminal, and binds the consent agreement.

**The consent agreement.** The settlement arithmetic runs in two variants over the same formula. The settlement base is the pinned key for everything that follows. In the **accepted** variant the base is `current_offer_cents`, the agency offer on the table. In the **preview** variant the base is `current_offer_cents` when a standing offer exists and `assessed_penalty_cents` when none does. The cooperation credit tier is keyed on that **pre-credit** settlement base and never on the discounted total: when the base is below 5,000,000 cents the tier is `TIER_1` at ten percent; when the base is at least 5,000,000 and at most 12,000,000 cents the tier is `TIER_2` at fifteen percent; above 12,000,000 cents the tier is `TIER_3` at twenty percent. The edges are exact — 4,999,999 is `TIER_1`, 5,000,000 is `TIER_2`, 12,000,000 is `TIER_2`, and 12,000,001 is `TIER_3`. Keying the tier on the post-credit total would make the formula self-referential, because the credit would change the tier which would change the credit, and it must not be done. When `cooperation_bonus_applied` is true, five further percentage points are added to the tier's percentage, subject to an overall ceiling of thirty percent, so the effective rate is the smaller of the boosted rate and thirty percent; the recorded `cooperation_credit_tier` remains the tier the base selected. The credit is the settlement base multiplied by the effective percentage, rounded half up to whole cents exactly once, and the total is the exact integer difference of the base minus the credit, so credit plus total always reconstructs the base.

In the **accepted** variant all five settlement fields are bound for good: `cooperation_credit_tier`, `cooperation_credit_cents` and `consent_agreement_total_cents` are written, `compliance_program_required` is set true when `consent_agreement_total_cents` has reached 5,000,000 cents and false otherwise, and `reporting_obligation_months` is set to 24 when the compliance programme is required and to 12 when it is not. Acceptance also moves the reserve one last time, setting `reserved_cents` to `consent_agreement_total_cents` and `available_cents` to `assessed_penalty_cents` minus `consent_agreement_total_cents`, preserving the reserve identity in its final resting state. In the **preview** variant only `consent_agreement_preview_cents` is written and `cooperation_credit_tier`, `cooperation_credit_cents`, `consent_agreement_total_cents`, `compliance_program_required` and `reporting_obligation_months` are all left untouched at their null and zero values. The preview is recomputed in three places: when the case is opened, whenever an offer changes the standing number, and in the fourth link of the claim-fulfilment cascade when `cooperation_bonus_applied` flips. It is therefore a moving figure right up to the moment an offer is accepted — the same effectiveness milestone that earns the cooperation bonus visibly reduces the preview, which is exactly the incentive the docket is meant to create.
