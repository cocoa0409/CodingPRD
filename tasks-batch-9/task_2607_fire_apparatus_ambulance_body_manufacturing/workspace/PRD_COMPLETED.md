# Fire Apparatus Body Manufacturing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Building fire apparatus and ambulance bodies is not a catalogue business. A metropolitan fire department or county EMS agency arrives with a purchasing department, a multi-year award, a written bid specification running to hundreds of clauses, and an apparatus committee that will fly to the plant three times before the vehicle ever turns a wheel. Between the day the specification is written and the day the operator training is signed off, the same vehicle passes through chassis selection, weight-distribution engineering, pump or aerial or patient-compartment integration, third-party standards testing, two or three committee inspection trips that each generate change orders, a long-lead procurement slot that was reserved on a supplier's build line eighteen months earlier, final inspection, delivery and warranty startup. Every one of those steps can move the price, the axle loads, or both — and the contract has a not-to-exceed ceiling that the plant is not allowed to cross.

This API is the system of record for that whole arc. It exists because the three quantities that matter most in this business — committed contract money, axle load against chassis rating, and supplier slot capacity — are all *conserved* quantities that several parties amend concurrently, and none of them can be allowed to drift. An approved change order that adds a hundred kilograms to the rear axle must invalidate the certification the unit already passed; rescinding that change order must return the money and the kilograms to exactly where they were, even though the torn-up certificate is gone for good. A specification revision released two years into the program must mark every in-flight unit still pinned to the old revision as drifted, and must tear up their certifications too. A supplier slot released back to the line must free the slot count, orphan the unit that was counting on it, and cancel the pre-construction trip the committee had already booked into a plant bay.

The requirements below are written from that perspective: the physical and fiscal invariants come first, and the HTTP surface is simply how those invariants are reached. Section 2 fixes the contract-level mechanics — identity, envelopes, paging, the logical clock, precision, concurrency and the order in which rules are evaluated. Section 3 walks the business narrative from program opening through warranty expiry. Sections 4 and 5 summarise the endpoint surface and the error catalogue.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section applies across the whole API surface. Where Section 3 describes a workflow, the mechanics defined here are assumed rather than repeated.

### 2.1. Operating Roles and Request Identity

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting party, and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity that party is acting in. If either header is absent — or present but empty, which is treated exactly as absent — or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five roles named below matched exactly, the request fails with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` and HTTP 401. If the headers are well formed but the declared role is not authorised for the operation being attempted, the request fails with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` and HTTP 403.

The five roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `PurchasingOfficer`, `BodyEngineer`, `ThirdPartyTester` and `CustomerCommitteeRep`. They own deliberately disjoint slices of the lifecycle:

- A `ProgramAdmin` is the plant's program administration function. It opens, activates, suspends, reactivates and closes multi-year programs, registers supplier build lines, inspection bays and long-lead reservations, schedules committee trips, drives production start, final inspection, delivery and warranty training completion, and runs the deterministic clock sweep for a program.
- A `PurchasingOfficer` is the purchasing department. It authors bid specifications and their clauses, moves them through review, reworks and releases them, disposes compliance-matrix responses, awards unit orders against released specifications, and negotiates, approves, rejects or rescinds change orders.
- A `BodyEngineer` is body engineering. Its single write capability is answering a bid specification clause by clause in the compliance matrix.
- A `ThirdPartyTester` is the independent standards-testing house. It submits units for certification testing and records the signed result.
- A `CustomerCommitteeRep` is the purchasing agency's apparatus committee. It holds and cancels its own inspection trips and raises, submits and revises the change orders those trips produce.

Four of the five roles carry **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example as `authored_by_user_id` or `released_by_user_id` on a specification, `responded_by_user_id` on a compliance entry, `submitted_by_user_id` on a certification test, or `approved_by_user_id` on a change order, wherever a transition writes it) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `PurchasingOfficer`, for `BodyEngineer`, and for `ThirdPartyTester`: each of those three roles supplies an opaque operator identifier that is recorded where a transition writes it and is never resolved against a stored resource. A `BodyEngineer`'s opaque identifier is additionally the value a purchasing officer supplies as `weight_calc_engineer_user_id` when awarding a unit, and a `ThirdPartyTester` supplies an inspector identifier as `signed_by_user_id` when recording a result; both are compared to each other by the separation-of-duties rule, not to any `id`.

`CustomerCommitteeRep` is different, and this is the one place where the header is a foreign key. When `X-User-Role` is `CustomerCommitteeRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ApparatusProgram` — the program whose committee this representative speaks for. Ownership checks on any resource that references an `ApparatusProgram` compare that resource's program to `X-User-ID`. Concretely: for an inspection trip the server walks the trip's unit order and compares that unit's `program_id`; for a change order it walks the change order's unit order and compares that unit's `program_id`; for a unit order it compares `program_id` directly. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_COMMITTEE_SCOPE_VIOLATION` and HTTP 403. This scope rule guards exactly five operations: holding an inspection trip, cancelling an inspection trip, raising a change order, submitting a change order, and revising a change order.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the server's real UTC wall clock rather than from the logical plant clock of §2.4.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` is the maximum number of rows to return; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list is sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, so the oldest row is returned first and the newest row last. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` counts every row matching the filters before paging is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

Collection endpoints also accept domain filters, which combine conjunctively with each other and with paging. Each filter is an exact string comparison against the named field of the row; a filter supplied with an empty value is treated as absent, and no filter value is checked against an enumeration or against an existing row, so an unrecognised or unknown value simply matches nothing and returns an empty page rather than `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` or a 404. Programs may be filtered by `status`. Specifications may be filtered by `program_id`, by `supersedes_spec_id` (which narrows the list to the successors of one revision), and by `status`. Clauses may be filtered by `specification_id` and by `category`. Compliance entries may be filtered by `specification_id`, `clause_id` and `disposition`. Unit orders may be filtered by `program_id`, `specification_id`, `chassis_reservation_id` and `build_status`. Supplier build lines may be filtered by `component_type`. Reservations may be filtered by `program_id`, `slot_line_id`, `unit_order_id` and `status`. Inspection trips may be filtered by `unit_order_id`, `plant_bay_id`, `trip_type` and `status`. Change orders may be filtered by `unit_order_id`, `inspection_trip_id` and `status`. Certification tests may be filtered by `unit_order_id` and `status`. Warranty startups may be filtered by `unit_order_id` and `status`; that `unit_order_id` filter is the discovery channel for the warranty record that delivery creates, since delivery itself returns the unit order rather than the warranty record.

### 2.4. The Logical Plant Clock

Deadlines in this domain are long — a chassis slot reserved for a window eighteen months out, a warranty that runs ten years — so the server never reads wall-clock time for business decisions. Instead it keeps a **logical clock**, and every deadline, expiry, window comparison and warranty date is evaluated against it. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; this startup default is mandatory and the server must not fall back to real wall-clock time for any rule.

An operator in any of the five roles may move the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant, and the operation echoes back the `current_utc` now in force. The clock is held as the exact text it was last set with: the echo returns that text verbatim, and every business field that records a clock instant — a specification's `released_at`, a reservation's `confirmed_at`, a trip's `held_at`, a change order's `applied_at`, a unit's `delivered_at` — stores that same text unchanged rather than re-rendering it to three fractional digits, so while the clock is still at its startup default each of those fields reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Comparisons against the clock, by contrast, are made on the instant that text denotes. Moving the clock is deliberately inert: it never itself transitions anything. A reservation whose expiry instant has passed stays `RESERVED`, and a warranty whose end date has passed stays `ACTIVE`, until the program's deterministic sweep is run. That separation is what makes the sweep observable and repeatable.

### 2.5. Record Identity, Audit Fields and Revision Counters

Every resource the API mints carries an `id`, which is a server-generated UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at`, which is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written. Unlike the business fields, the audit stamps do read real time: `created_at`, and `updated_at` wherever a resource carries one, are written from the server's actual UTC wall clock at the moment of the write and are never taken from the logical plant clock of §2.4, which governs business decisions only.

Most resources — apparatus programs, bid specifications, compliance matrix entries, unit orders, supplier slot lines, chassis reservations, inspection trips, change orders, certification tests and warranty startups — additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp rendered like `created_at` that is identical to `created_at` on a freshly created row and is refreshed on every mutation to the wall-clock instant of that mutation, so it never precedes `created_at`, and `version`, an integer that is 1 on a freshly created row and increments by one on every mutation of that row — counted once per request, so a row that several rules or several cascade hops of one request all mutate still advances its `version` by exactly one and takes a single `updated_at` refresh. A recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation: when an approved change order rebuilds a unit's axle loads, or a clause addition rebuilds a specification's scores, the affected row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments even though no client wrote a field on it directly.

Two resources are immutable once created and therefore carry **only** `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from them entirely. Those two are the specification clause and the plant bay. A clause is appended to a draft specification and never edited; a bay is registered with its capacity and never edited. Nothing in the API mutates either row, so neither needs an audit timestamp or a concurrency counter.

Do not confuse `version` with the domain-level counters that share its flavour. A specification's `revision_number` is a business revision index, not a concurrency token: it is 1 on a first-revision specification created with no predecessor, it is one greater than the predecessor's `revision_number` when the draft names a predecessor it intends to replace, and it increments by one each time the specification is sent back from review to draft for rework. A change order's `revision_count` counts how many times that change order has been repriced out of negotiation, starting at 0.

### 2.6. Money, Weight, Score and Date Precision

**Money is integer cents everywhere.** `base_unit_price_cents`, `contract_ceiling_cents`, `committed_cents`, `price_cents`, `change_order_total_cents`, `price_delta_cents`, `reserved_price_cents`, `final_price_cents` and `price_variance_cents` are all integers, and none of them is ever expressed as a decimal currency amount. Wherever a percentage is applied to a cents figure the result is quantised back to a whole number of cents using round-half-up, and the specification of each calculation in Section 3 states exactly which steps round and which do not. Rounding at a step that the calculation does not nominate, or omitting a rounding the calculation does nominate, produces the wrong figure by a cent or more and is a defect.

**Weight is whole kilograms.** Axle loads, gross weight, curb weights, ratings and change-order weight deltas are integers. The one fractional intermediate in the weight model — the front share of the payload — is quantised with round-half-up, and the one intermediate that is truncated — the percentage-of-rating leg of payload capacity — is floored. No other rounding occurs anywhere in the weight model.

**Scores and ratios carry two decimal places** and are quantised with round-half-up: `compliance_score_strict`, `compliance_score_provisional` and `restrictive_clause_ratio_pct` are all two-decimal numbers; each is emitted as a JSON number rather than as a string, so a figure that quantises onto a whole number carries no trailing zeros in the response (a ratio of exactly forty renders as `40.0`). `escalation_index_pct_per_year` is a percentage expressed to one decimal place, and `training_hours` and `additional_training_hours` are decimal hour figures; the server does not quantise or otherwise restrict the fractional precision of these three client-supplied figures — each is stored and echoed exactly as supplied, and the cumulative training comparison against the sixteen-hour threshold is made on the exact accumulated value rather than on a value rounded to one digit.

**Date arithmetic is whole days and whole calendar months.** Reservation window buffers are added and subtracted as whole days. The warranty deferral is a whole number of days. The warranty term is a whole number of calendar months added to a start date, landing on the same day of month, with the day clamped down to the last day of the target month when that day does not exist in it. Every instant a client supplies — a slot window bound, an `expiry_at`, a trip window bound, a `tested_at`, a `delivery_date`, a `current_utc` — is accepted in any ISO 8601 form the language's own parser admits: a trailing `Z`, an explicit numeric offset (honoured and normalised to UTC), no offset at all (read as UTC), or a date-only text such as `2027-03-01` (denoting midnight UTC). The supplied text is stored and echoed back exactly as it was written, never re-rendered, and it is normalised to UTC only for comparison and for date arithmetic. Interval comparisons on inspection windows are therefore evaluated in UTC on the instants those texts denote, at whatever precision the texts themselves carry.

Rounding half-up, everywhere it appears, means "quantise to the stated unit, rounding a value exactly halfway between two representable results away from zero".

### 2.7. Version Echo on Long-Lead and Change-Order Writes

Two write operations in this API are decisions taken against a *picture* the client read a moment earlier, and both of them require the client to prove which picture it read. This is optimistic concurrency control on the parent record's `version`, and it applies to exactly these two operations and to no others. Every other endpoint in the API takes no version field at all; supplying one is neither required nor meaningful.

**Raising a change order requires `expected_unit_order_version`.** The field is a non-optional part of the request body and carries the `version` of the target `UnitOrder` as the client last read it; exactly like its slot-line counterpart below, it is an integer of at least 0, so an absent, negative or non-integer value is a rung-3 `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than a stale-version rejection. If that value does not equal the unit order's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_UNIT_ORDER_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and nothing is persisted. The business reason is concrete: a committee representative drafts a change order from a weight and price baseline it observed on the unit. If some other writer has advanced that unit's `version` since — most obviously by having an earlier change order approved, which legitimately rebuilds the unit's change-order total and axle loads — then the new draft would be priced and weighed against a unit that no longer exists in that form. The write is refused on the stale version rather than allowed to overwrite a baseline the client never saw.

**Reserving a supplier slot requires `expected_slot_line_version`.** The field is a non-optional part of the request body and carries the `version` of the target `SupplierSlotLine` as the client last read it; it is an integer of at least 0, so an absent, negative or non-integer value is a rung-3 `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than a stale-version rejection. If that value does not equal the slot line's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SLOT_LINE_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and nothing is persisted. Long-lead holds are booked against an observed free-slot picture; once another reservation, release or expiry has changed that picture, the booking decision rests on capacity the client never observed, and the hold is refused.

Both checks sit *below* the 404s of rungs 4 and 5 and below the committee-scope rule — every path and body identifier the request names is resolved first, so a raise naming an unknown unit order or trip, or a booking naming an unknown program, unit or slot line, reports that `not found` rather than a stale version — and *above* every own-state, cross-entity, referential-integrity, temporal, capacity and fiscal rule on the same request, so a request that is both stale and otherwise invalid in one of those later ways reports the stale version.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline: One Request, Many Rows

This system has no eventual consistency and no background reconciliation other than the explicit per-program sweep. Every consequence of a write lands inside the same request that caused it, and by the time the response is emitted every affected row is already at its final value. Three named cascades carry most of that weight, and each one is a chain in which every hop is *caused by* the hop before it rather than being an independent side effect:

- **Cascade A** starts at change-order approval. The unit's change-order total and axle loads are rebuilt; if the rebuilt weight verdict leaves `WITHIN_LIMITS` the unit's certification is reset and its passed certification tests are torn up; if that certification reset happens to a unit that had already passed final inspection, the build dimension is dragged backwards; and a unit dragged backwards has its still-scheduled final inspection trips cancelled. The program's money ledger is resynchronised last.
- **Cascade B** starts at the release of a specification revision that names a predecessor. The predecessor is superseded; its still-pending compliance responses are voided; every in-flight unit order still pinned to the predecessor is flagged as drifted; and every unit so flagged has its certification reset and its passed certification tests torn up.
- **Cascade C** starts at the release or expiry of a chassis reservation. The supplier line's slot counts are rebuilt; the freed slot orphans the unit that was counting on it, which loses its reservation link and goes at risk; and a unit gone at risk has its still-scheduled pre-construction inspection trips cancelled.

Every row a cascade touches is a mutation of that row: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments by one. Where a cascade hop can affect several rows at once — several compliance entries, several unit orders, several certification tests, several inspection trips — those rows are processed in ascending `created_at` order, so the outcome of a cascade is fully determined by the data and never by iteration accident. Cascades are also not permitted to leave a partial trail: if any rule fails, the entire request is rejected and no row is written at all.

Two of these chains are *compensating*. Rescinding an approved change order returns the unit's change-order total, axle loads and weight verdict, and the program's committed money, to exactly the values they held before the approval, because the rescinded change order simply leaves the input set of those aggregations. Releasing or expiring a reservation returns the supplier line's occupied slot count to exactly its pre-reservation value for the same reason. What is *not* compensated is certification: a certificate torn up by Cascade A stays torn up when the change order that tore it up is rescinded, and the build dimension is not pushed forward again either. Certification has to be re-earned.

### 2.9. Rule Evaluation Order

Rules are evaluated in a single fixed ladder, from the top down. The first rule that fails determines the response, nothing is persisted, and no lower rung is consulted. This matters whenever an operation carries several guards at once — a stale unit version *and* an invalid build state, or an axle overload *and* an outstanding change order — because the reader must be able to predict which of the two errors surfaces. The ladder runs identity, then request shape, then existence, then scope, then optimistic concurrency, then own-state, then cross-entity state, then referential integrity, then safety and separation of duties, then temporal, then capacity and constraint, and finally fiscal.

In full, and in order:

1. `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and the role is one of the five named roles — otherwise `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`, 401. Applies to every endpoint.
2. The acting role is authorised for the endpoint — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, 403. Applies to every endpoint.
3. Request shape: the body is parsed as JSON whatever `Content-Type` it arrives under, an absent or empty body reads as an empty JSON object (so a transition endpoint that requires no field accepts a bodyless request), and a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object is rejected — this body parsing runs only where the operation declares at least one body field, because a transition endpoint that declares none never inspects the body at all and therefore ignores whatever it carries; a key the operation does not declare is ignored too, at the top level of the body as much as inside a change-order line item, so an unrecognised field never provokes a rejection and is never stored or echoed back; a field supplied explicitly as `null` counts as omitted, so an optional field falls back to its default and a required field is reported as missing; every required input field is present, every field has the correct type — nothing is coerced from a string, a boolean is not accepted where a number or integer is expected, an integer field rejects any JSON number carrying a decimal point at all (`3.0` as much as `3.5`) while a decimal field accepts a bare integer, and a string's declared length is measured on the text exactly as supplied, which is never trimmed — every enumerated field carries a declared member, every numeric field falls inside its declared range, a change order's line-item array holds between 1 and 12 elements with each item's quantity at least 1 and each item's unit cost at least 0 cents, and pagination has `limit` between 1 and 100 with `offset` at least 0, where a `limit` or `offset` supplied as an empty string counts as omitted and takes its default while one whose text is not a base-ten integer (surrounding whitespace aside) is rejected — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400. Applies to every write endpoint and to every list endpoint that accepts pagination parameters.
4. Every identifier in the URL path resolves to an existing row, validated left to right — otherwise a 404 naming the first unresolved identifier: `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SPECIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPLIANCE_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SLOT_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_TRIP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHANGE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_TEST_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_WARRANTY_STARTUP_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint with path parameters.
5. Every reference supplied in the request body resolves to an existing row — `program_id`, `specification_id`, `supersedes_spec_id`, `clause_id`, `unit_order_id`, `slot_line_id`, `plant_bay_id` and `inspection_trip_id` — otherwise a 404 of `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SPECIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAUSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SLOT_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLANT_BAY_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_INSPECTION_TRIP_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to specification drafting, compliance-entry filing, unit-order award, reservation creation, inspection-trip scheduling, change-order raising and certification-test submission.
6. Committee scope: a `CustomerCommitteeRep`'s `X-User-ID` equals the program reached from the target resource — otherwise `ERR_COMMITTEE_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, 403. Applies to trip hold, trip cancel, change-order raise, change-order submit and change-order revise.
7. `expected_unit_order_version` equals the unit order's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_UNIT_ORDER_VERSION`, 412. Applies to change-order raising.
8. `expected_slot_line_version` equals the supplier slot line's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_SLOT_LINE_VERSION`, 412. Applies to reservation creation.
9. The program is in the state the requested operation allows — `DRAFT` to activate, `ACTIVE` to suspend, `SUSPENDED` to reactivate, `ACTIVE` to close, and either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` to run the sweep — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_STATE`, 409.
10. The specification is in the state the operation allows — `DRAFT` to append a clause, `DRAFT` to submit for review, `UNDER_REVIEW` to rework, `UNDER_REVIEW` to release, and `DRAFT` for the specification a new compliance entry targets — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SPEC_STATE`, 409. Applies to clause append, review submission, rework, release and compliance-entry filing.
11. The compliance entry's `disposition` is `PENDING` and its parent specification is `DRAFT` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENTRY_STATE`, 409. Applies to disposing an entry.
12. The unit's `build_status` permits the operation — `PRE_CON_HELD` to start production, `MID_CON_HELD` for final inspection, `FINAL_INSPECTED` to deliver, and either `SPEC_LOCKED` or `PRE_CON_HELD` to cancel — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_BUILD_STATE`, 409. The milestone check on *holding* an inspection trip reports this same `ERR_INVALID_BUILD_STATE` but is not evaluated at this rung: it sits below rungs 18 and 41, as §3.7 describes.
13. The unit is still amendable, meaning its `build_status` is none of `DELIVERED`, `WARRANTY_ACTIVE` or `CANCELLED` — otherwise `ERR_UNIT_NOT_AMENDABLE`, 409. Applies to all six change-order operations: raise, submit, negotiate, revise, approve and rescind.
14. The unit's `certification_status` is either `NOT_SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED_TESTING`, the two states from which a fresh test may be submitted — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CERTIFICATION_STATE`, 409. Applies to certification-test submission.
15. The certification test's `status` is `SUBMITTED` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TEST_STATE`, 409. Applies to recording a result.
16. The reservation's `status` permits the operation — `RESERVED` to confirm, and either `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED` to release — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_RESERVATION_STATE`, 409.
17. The reservation being confirmed has a non-null `unit_order_id` — otherwise `ERR_RESERVATION_UNASSIGNED`, 409. Applies to reservation confirmation.
18. The inspection trip's `status` is `SCHEDULED` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TRIP_STATE`, 409. Applies to trip hold and trip cancel.
19. The change order is in the state its transition allows — `DRAFT` to submit, `SUBMITTED` to negotiate, `IN_NEGOTIATION` to revise, `SUBMITTED` to approve, `SUBMITTED` to reject and `APPROVED` to rescind — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CHANGE_ORDER_STATE`, 409.
20. The warranty record's `status` is `PENDING_TRAINING` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WARRANTY_STATE`, 409. Applies to completing training.
21. Cross-entity, unit against program: the owning program's `status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE`, 409. Applies to unit-order award and to production start.
22. Cross-entity, unit against reservation: the unit's `chassis_reservation_id` is not null and that reservation's `status` is `CONFIRMED` — otherwise `ERR_CHASSIS_NOT_CONFIRMED`, 409. Applies to production start.
23. Cross-entity, certification against inspection: an inspection trip exists for the unit whose `trip_type` is `MID_CONSTRUCTION` and whose `status` is `HELD` — otherwise `ERR_MID_CON_INSPECTION_NOT_HELD`, 409. Applies to certification-test submission.
24. `test_standard` matches `unit_type` — otherwise `ERR_TEST_STANDARD_MISMATCH`, 422. Applies to certification-test submission.
25. Cross-entity, change order against inspection: the cited inspection trip's `status` is `HELD` — otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_TRIP_NOT_HELD`, 409. Applies to change-order submission.
26. The cited inspection trip's `unit_order_id` equals the `unit_order_id` on the request — otherwise `ERR_TRIP_UNIT_MISMATCH`, 422. Applies to change-order raising.
27. The pinned specification's `status` is `RELEASED` — otherwise `ERR_SPEC_NOT_RELEASED`, 409. Applies to unit-order award.
28. Program agreement between a specification and its counterpart: on unit-order award, the referenced specification's `program_id` equals the `program_id` on the request; on release, the predecessor's `program_id` equals the successor's — otherwise `ERR_SPEC_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`, 422. Applies to unit-order award and to specification release.
29. When `supersedes_spec_id` is not null, that predecessor's `status` is `RELEASED` — otherwise `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_RELEASED`, 409. Applies to specification release.
30. When a reservation names a `unit_order_id`, that unit's `program_id` equals the reservation's `program_id` — otherwise `ERR_RESERVATION_UNIT_MISMATCH`, 422. Applies to reservation creation.
31. Safety: the unit's `weight_status` is `WITHIN_LIMITS` — otherwise `ERR_AXLE_LOAD_EXCEEDED`, 422. Applies to final inspection and to delivery.
32. Safety: the unit's `certification_status` is `CERTIFIED` — otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_PASSED`, 409. Applies to final inspection.
33. Separation of duties — on specification release, `X-User-ID` differs from the specification's `authored_by_user_id`; on recording a certification result, the supplied `signed_by_user_id` differs from the unit's `weight_calc_engineer_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403. Applies to specification release and to recording a certification result.
34. The unit's `open_change_order_count` is 0, meaning no draft, submitted or in-negotiation change order is outstanding against it — otherwise `ERR_OPEN_CHANGE_ORDERS`, 409. Applies to final inspection and to unit cancellation.
35. `slot_end_date` is strictly later than `slot_start_date`, and `expiry_at` is strictly later than `slot_start_date` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SLOT_WINDOW`, 422. Applies to reservation creation.
36. `scheduled_end` is strictly later than `scheduled_start` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TRIP_WINDOW`, 422. Applies to inspection-trip scheduling.
37. `delivery_date` is not earlier than the `scheduled_end` of this unit's `HELD` `MID_CONSTRUCTION` inspection trip — otherwise `ERR_DELIVERY_BEFORE_MID_CON`, 422. Applies to delivery.
38. No occupying reservation on the same supplier line collides with the requested window once the changeover buffer is applied — otherwise `ERR_SLOT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, 409. Applies to reservation creation.
39. The supplier line still has at least one available slot before the new reservation is written — otherwise `ERR_SLOT_LINE_EXHAUSTED`, 409. Applies to reservation creation.
40. The bay admits the trip, meaning the visitors already booked over any intersecting window plus this trip's `committee_size` do not exceed the bay's `max_concurrent_visitors` — otherwise `ERR_BAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, 409. Applies to inspection-trip scheduling.
41. No other inspection trip for the same `unit_order_id` with the same `trip_type` is already `HELD` — otherwise `ERR_TRIP_TYPE_ALREADY_HELD`, 409. Applies to holding a trip.
42. `clause_code` is unique within the specification — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAUSE_CODE`, 409. Applies to clause append.
43. At most one compliance entry exists for a given specification-and-clause pair, and the clause belongs to that specification — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_COMPLIANCE_ENTRY`, 409. Applies to compliance-entry filing.
44. The specification's `clause_count` is at least 1 — otherwise `ERR_SPEC_HAS_NO_CLAUSES`, 422. Applies to review submission and to release.
45. The specification's `restrictive_clause_ratio_pct` is at most 40.00 — otherwise `ERR_RESTRICTIVE_CLAUSE_RATIO_EXCEEDED`, 422. Applies to release.
46. The specification's `compliance_score_strict` is at least 75.00 — otherwise `ERR_SPEC_COMPLIANCE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`, 422. Applies to release.
47. Every change-order line item's `markup_pct` is at most 22.0 — otherwise `ERR_MARKUP_CAP_EXCEEDED`, 422. Applies to change-order raising and to change-order revision.
48. When `option_unit` is true, the program's `options_exercised_count` is strictly less than its `max_option_units` — otherwise `ERR_OPTION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, 409. Applies to unit-order award.
49. The warranty record's `training_hours` plus the submitted `additional_training_hours` is at least 16.0 — otherwise `ERR_TRAINING_HOURS_NOT_MET`, 422. Applies to completing training.
50. Every unit order of the program has `build_status` of either `WARRANTY_ACTIVE` or `CANCELLED` — otherwise `ERR_UNITS_NOT_TERMINAL`, 409. Applies to program closure.
51. The program's `committed_cents` is at most its soft ceiling before it may leave `SUSPENDED` — otherwise `ERR_SOFT_CEILING_STILL_BREACHED`, 409. Applies to reactivation.
52. Fiscal, and last but one: the program's resulting `committed_cents` is at most its `contract_ceiling_cents` — otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_CEILING_EXCEEDED`, 409. Applies to unit-order award and to change-order approval.
53. Fiscal: `award_year` is not earlier than the program's `base_year` and not later than that base year plus five — otherwise `ERR_ESCALATION_YEAR_OUT_OF_RANGE`, 422. Applies to unit-order award.

Two consequences of this ladder are worth calling out because they are counter-intuitive. First, on final inspection the axle-overload check sits *above* the certification check, which sits above the open-change-order check: a unit that is simultaneously overloaded, uncertified and carrying an open change order reports `ERR_AXLE_LOAD_EXCEEDED`, not the other two. Second, on unit-order award the contract-ceiling check sits *above* the award-year range check, so a request that would both blow the ceiling and quote an out-of-range award year reports `ERR_CONTRACT_CEILING_EXCEEDED`.

### 2.10. The Program Ledger and its Two Ceilings

Each apparatus program carries a single conserved money figure, `committed_cents`, which is the plant's live view of what the program has committed against its award. It is not a free-form field: it is recomputed from the program's unit orders whenever anything that could change it happens, and its recomputed value is what gets persisted. Contributing to the sum are the award price and the accumulated approved change-order value of every unit order of the program whose `build_status` is any of `SPEC_LOCKED`, `PRE_CON_HELD`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, `MID_CON_HELD`, `FINAL_INSPECTED`, `DELIVERED` or `WARRANTY_ACTIVE`. `CANCELLED` is the only build state excluded, which is exactly why cancelling a unit decommits its money. A program with no qualifying units has a `committed_cents` of 0. The escalation reconciliation delta recorded on a delivered unit's warranty record is deliberately *not* part of this sum.

Against that figure sit two thresholds, and they behave differently. The **hard ceiling** is `contract_ceiling_cents`, the contractual not-to-exceed value supplied when the program is opened; it may never be less than the program's `base_unit_price_cents`, and a program-opening request that supplies a smaller ceiling is a request-shape failure rejected at rung 3 with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400. A write whose resulting `committed_cents` would exceed the hard ceiling is rejected outright with `ERR_CONTRACT_CEILING_EXCEEDED` and nothing is persisted — the program's `status` is left exactly as it was. The **soft ceiling** is ninety percent of the hard ceiling, computed by taking the hard ceiling, multiplying by 90, dividing by 100 and quantising the result to whole cents with round-half-up. A write whose resulting `committed_cents` strictly exceeds the soft ceiling while remaining at or below the hard ceiling *succeeds*, and the program is automatically moved from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` inside that very same request with `suspension_reason` set to `SOFT_CEILING_BREACH`. Committed money landing exactly on the soft ceiling is not a breach; the comparison is strict.

Recovery from a soft-ceiling suspension is never automatic. Cancelling a unit or rescinding a change order may pull `committed_cents` back under the soft ceiling, but the program stays `SUSPENDED` until a `ProgramAdmin` explicitly reactivates it — and reactivation is itself refused with `ERR_SOFT_CEILING_STILL_BREACHED` while committed money still exceeds the soft ceiling, whatever the original reason for the suspension was.

### 2.11. Separation of Duties on the Plant Floor

Two identity separations are hard-wired into this API, and both exist because the same person signing both sides of a control is how bad apparatus leaves a plant.

The first separates authorship from release. A bid specification records `authored_by_user_id`, the `X-User-ID` of the purchasing officer that drafted it, at creation time; when the specification is released, the releasing officer's `X-User-ID` is recorded as `released_by_user_id` and must differ from `authored_by_user_id`. The author may write, review and rework their own specification freely — but a second officer has to release it.

The second separates weight engineering from certification sign-off. Every unit order records `weight_calc_engineer_user_id`, the body engineer that owns that unit's weight distribution calculation, supplied when the unit is awarded. When a third-party result is recorded, the `signed_by_user_id` on that result must differ from the unit's `weight_calc_engineer_user_id`. The engineer who computed the load distribution may not be the signature on the certificate that says the load distribution is compliant.

Both violations surface as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403.

### 2.12. Deterministic Ordering and Interval Conventions

No rule, selector, ordering or tie-break anywhere in this system reads a row's `id`. Wherever a set of rows must be processed in a defined sequence — cascade fan-outs, sweep batches, aggregation over line items — the sequence is ascending `created_at`, except for a change order's line items, which are evaluated strictly in the order the client supplied them in the array. Because `created_at` is only millisecond-resolution, several rows written in quick succession can share one value; rows tied on `created_at` are ordered by the sequence in which the server wrote them, oldest write first, and that same rule breaks the tie for list ordering as well as for cascade and sweep fan-outs.

Interval arithmetic is not uniform across the domain, and the difference is deliberate. Inspection-bay windows are treated as **closed** intervals and their capacity constraint is an **aggregate** one: two windows that merely touch at an endpoint do overlap, and it is the *sum* of committee sizes over an overlapping window that is tested, never a pairwise collision. Supplier slot windows are treated as **half-open** intervals with a **changeover buffer**, and their constraint is **pairwise**: a single geometric collision with a single occupying reservation is fatal regardless of how much slot capacity remains. Section 3 states each of these two rules exactly where the workflow that uses it is described.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The narrative below follows a program from the day the agency signs the award to the day the last warranty expires. Where a paragraph describes an operation, the acting role and the state pair it drives are named; Section 4 supplies the HTTP contract for each of them.

### 3.1. Opening and Governing a Multi-Year Apparatus Program

Everything hangs off an **apparatus program** — one multi-year award from one purchasing agency. A `ProgramAdmin` opens a program by supplying a `name` for the program (a label of 1 to 120 characters, such as "Metro FD 2024-2029 Pumper Program"), the `agency_name` of the purchasing fire department or EMS agency (also 1 to 120 characters), a `base_year` between 2020 and 2030 that fixes the escalation basis, a `base_unit_price_cents` giving the base-year price of one non-option unit in integer cents (at least 1,000,000 cents), an `escalation_index_pct_per_year` between 0.0 and 15.0 carrying the annual contract escalation index in percent to one decimal place, a `max_option_units` between 0 and 20 capping how many additional option units the agency may exercise beyond the base award, and a `contract_ceiling_cents` giving the hard not-to-exceed contract value in integer cents. All escalation in this system is measured from 1 January of the base year.

A newly opened program lands in `DRAFT` with `options_exercised_count` at 0, `committed_cents` at 0 and `suspension_reason` at null. `options_exercised_count` is a system-maintained count of the program's unit orders that were awarded as options and have not been cancelled — that is, unit orders whose `option_unit` flag is true and whose `build_status` is anything other than `CANCELLED`.

The program's posture gates every child operation, and only a `ProgramAdmin` may move it. Activating a program takes `status` from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE`, clears `suspension_reason` to null and increments the program's `version`; only a `DRAFT` program may be activated, and only while a program is `ACTIVE` may unit orders be awarded against it or production started on its units. Suspending an `ACTIVE` program takes it to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `MANUAL`. Reactivating a `SUSPENDED` program takes it back to `ACTIVE` and clears `suspension_reason` to null, but only if the program's committed money is at most its soft ceiling — a reactivation attempted while committed money still exceeds ninety percent of the hard ceiling is refused with `ERR_SOFT_CEILING_STILL_BREACHED`, whether the suspension was manual or automatic. Closing an `ACTIVE` program takes it to the terminal `CLOSED` state, and it is a genuinely aggregate guard: the parent may close only when *every* unit order of the program has a `build_status` of either `WARRANTY_ACTIVE` or `CANCELLED`. One unit still in production blocks the close with `ERR_UNITS_NOT_TERMINAL`. Each of these four transitions increments the program's `version` by one.

There is a fifth way into `SUSPENDED`, and no operator asks for it. Whenever the program's committed money is recomputed — on unit award, on change-order approval, on unit cancellation, on change-order rescission and during the program sweep — the recomputed figure is compared against the soft ceiling. If the program is `ACTIVE` and the new figure strictly exceeds the soft ceiling while remaining at or below the hard ceiling, the program is moved to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `SOFT_CEILING_BREACH` inside that same request, its `version` increments, and *the triggering write still succeeds*. A figure that exceeds the hard ceiling is a different matter entirely: that write is rejected with `ERR_CONTRACT_CEILING_EXCEEDED`, nothing is persisted, and the program's `status` is untouched.

The **program sweep** is the only clock-driven batch in the system, it is available to a `ProgramAdmin`, and it may be run against a program that is either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` — any other program posture is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROGRAM_STATE`. It processes rows in ascending `created_at` order and does four things in this order. First, every chassis reservation of that program that is still `RESERVED` and whose `expiry_at` is at or before the current logical clock moves `RESERVED` to `EXPIRED`, applying exactly the same slot-count and long-lead consequences as an operator-driven release. Second, every warranty record of the program that is `ACTIVE` and whose `warranty_start_date` is at or before the current logical clock drives its unit order from `DELIVERED` to `WARRANTY_ACTIVE`; the record itself stays `ACTIVE`, and `warranty_activated_count` counts only the unit orders that were actually standing at `DELIVERED` and moved, so a record whose unit an earlier sweep already advanced contributes nothing to the count of a later one. Third, every warranty record that is `ACTIVE` and whose `warranty_end_date` is strictly earlier than the current logical clock moves `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED`. Fourth, the program's committed money is recomputed and the soft-ceiling auto-suspension is applied. The sweep reports back the `program_id` it ran for, an `expired_reservation_count`, a `warranty_activated_count`, a `warranty_expired_count`, the resulting `committed_cents` and the resulting `program_status`, and it increments the program's `version`.

### 3.2. Writing the Bid Specification and Its Clause Book

A **bid specification** is the purchasing department's written requirement document, and it is where the whole program starts in practice. A `PurchasingOfficer` drafts one by naming the owning `program_id` and a `title` of 1 to 160 characters, optionally naming a `supersedes_spec_id` — the released predecessor revision this draft is intended to replace, resolved after the `program_id`, so a draft naming both an unknown program and an unknown predecessor reports `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`. Drafting itself checks nothing else about the named predecessor: whatever its own `status` and `program_id`, those two conditions bind only at release. The draft lands in `DRAFT` with `authored_by_user_id` pinned to the acting officer's `X-User-ID`, `released_by_user_id` at null, `released_at` at null, `clause_count` and `mandatory_clause_count` at 0, `compliance_score_strict` and `compliance_score_provisional` at 0.00, and `restrictive_clause_ratio_pct` at 0.00. Its `revision_number` is 1 when no predecessor is named, and otherwise one greater than the named predecessor's `revision_number`.

Clauses are appended one at a time, and only while the specification is `DRAFT`; an attempt against a specification in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SPEC_STATE`. Each **clause** carries a `clause_code` of 1 to 24 characters that must be unique within its specification (a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAUSE_CODE`), a `category` naming the apparatus subsystem the clause governs — one of `CHASSIS`, `PUMP`, `AERIAL`, `PATIENT_COMPARTMENT`, `ELECTRICAL` or `PAINT` — a `requirement_text` of 1 to 500 characters stating the requirement the manufacturer must respond to, a `mandatory` flag that defaults to true, and an `is_restrictive` flag that defaults to false and is set when the clause names a proprietary or brand-specific requirement that narrows competition. A clause carries a `specification_id` identifying the specification it belongs to. Clauses are immutable: once appended, nothing edits or removes one.

Appending a clause recomputes five derived figures on the parent specification and increments its `version`. `clause_count` becomes the number of clauses belonging to that specification. `mandatory_clause_count` becomes the number of those clauses whose `mandatory` flag is true. Both compliance readiness scores are recomputed, and so is the restrictive-clause ratio.

The **restrictive-clause ratio** is the competitive-bidding gate, and it looks only at mandatory clauses. Take the specification's clauses whose `mandatory` flag is true. If there are none, `restrictive_clause_ratio_pct` is pinned to 0.00. Otherwise count how many of those mandatory clauses have `is_restrictive` true, multiply that count by one hundred, divide by the total number of mandatory clauses, and quantise the result to two decimal places with round-half-up. Optional clauses never enter the numerator or the denominator, even when they are themselves restrictive — an agency can name as many proprietary nice-to-haves as it likes so long as its *mandatory* requirements stay competitive.

A specification moves through a review cycle that may repeat as often as the purchasing department needs. Submitting for review takes `DRAFT` to `UNDER_REVIEW`, requires the specification to hold at least one clause (an empty specification is refused with `ERR_SPEC_HAS_NO_CLAUSES`), recomputes `compliance_score_strict`, `compliance_score_provisional` and `restrictive_clause_ratio_pct`, and increments `version`. Sending the document back for rework takes `UNDER_REVIEW` to `DRAFT`, increments `revision_number` by one and increments `version`. Both operations belong to a `PurchasingOfficer`. The cycle exists because clauses and compliance responses may be written *only* while the specification is `DRAFT`; a document under review is frozen, and getting it unfrozen means an explicit rework.

### 3.3. The Compliance Matrix: Responses, Dispositions and Two Readiness Scores

Against a `DRAFT` specification, a `BodyEngineer` answers the clause book one clause at a time. Each **compliance matrix entry** names the `specification_id` being responded to and the `clause_id` being answered (resolved in that order, so an entry naming both an unknown specification and an unknown clause reports `ERR_SPECIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`), carries a `response` of `COMPLY`, `EXCEPTION` or `ALTERNATE`, and may carry an `exception_text` narrative of at least one character with no upper length bound — which is what an `EXCEPTION` or `ALTERNATE` response is expected to supply. The entry lands with `disposition` at `PENDING`, `disposition_note` at null and `responded_by_user_id` pinned to the engineer's `X-User-ID`. At most one entry may exist for a given specification-and-clause pair, and the clause must actually belong to that specification; either violation is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_COMPLIANCE_ENTRY`. Filing an entry recomputes both readiness scores on the specification and increments the specification's `version`.

Purchasing then rules on each response. A `PurchasingOfficer` disposes a `PENDING` entry by supplying a `disposition` of `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED`, optionally with a `disposition_note` of at least one character with no upper length bound. The entry may only be disposed while its own `disposition` is `PENDING` *and* its parent specification is still `DRAFT`; otherwise the call is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ENTRY_STATE`. Disposing recomputes both readiness scores on the parent specification and increments both the entry's `version` and the specification's `version`. `PENDING`, `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED` and `VOID` are the only dispositions an entry can hold, and the latter three are terminal. Accepting an `EXCEPTION` or `ALTERNATE` response is the *only* way the strict score can count that clause as compliant; rejecting a response removes the clause from both scores' compliant sets.

The fourth disposition is never requested by an operator. When a specification is superseded, every one of its still-`PENDING` entries moves `PENDING` to `VOID` with `disposition_note` set to the text `voided by supersede` and its `version` incremented. Those entries are processed in ascending `created_at` order, and a `VOID` entry counts as non-compliant in both scores.

Both **readiness scores** are computed the same way and differ only in what counts as compliant. Every clause of the specification carries a weight: a mandatory clause weighs 3 and an optional clause weighs 1. The total weight is the sum of the weights of all the specification's clauses, with no status filter — clauses have no lifecycle, so every one of them counts. For each clause, the server finds the at-most-one compliance entry naming that specification and that clause; a clause with no entry at all is non-compliant in both scores. The compliant weight is the sum of the weights of the clauses whose entry counts as compliant. The score is one hundred multiplied by the compliant weight, divided by the total weight, quantised to two decimal places with round-half-up. When the total weight is zero the score is pinned to 0.00 and no division is attempted.

The difference between the two is one disposition. For `compliance_score_strict`, a clause's entry counts as compliant when its `response` is `COMPLY`, or when its `response` is `EXCEPTION` or `ALTERNATE` *and* its `disposition` is `ACCEPTED`; a still-pending exception counts as non-compliant. For `compliance_score_provisional`, a clause's entry counts as compliant when its `response` is `COMPLY`, or when its `response` is `EXCEPTION` or `ALTERNATE` *and* its `disposition` is either `PENDING` or `ACCEPTED`; a still-pending exception counts as compliant. The provisional figure is therefore never lower than the strict one, and the two diverge by exactly the weight of the clauses whose exceptions are still awaiting a ruling. Purchasing reads the provisional figure as the optimistic view of where the document will land, but it gates nothing whatsoever: the release gate reads `compliance_score_strict` and only `compliance_score_strict`.

### 3.4. Releasing a Revision, and What Supersession Does to Work in Flight

Release is the moment a specification becomes procurement-ready, and it is the most heavily gated operation in the document lifecycle. A `PurchasingOfficer` releases a specification, taking `status` from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `RELEASED`. Five conditions must hold. The specification must be `UNDER_REVIEW`. It must hold at least one clause, or the release is refused with `ERR_SPEC_HAS_NO_CLAUSES`. Its `restrictive_clause_ratio_pct` must be at most 40.00 — exactly 40.00 is admitted, and anything above it is refused with `ERR_RESTRICTIVE_CLAUSE_RATIO_EXCEEDED`. Its `compliance_score_strict` must be at least 75.00 — exactly 75.00 is admitted, and anything below it is refused with `ERR_SPEC_COMPLIANCE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`. And the releasing officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the specification's `authored_by_user_id`, or the release is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. When the draft names a `supersedes_spec_id`, two further conditions bind: that predecessor must itself be `RELEASED` (otherwise `ERR_PREDECESSOR_NOT_RELEASED`) and its `program_id` must equal the successor's `program_id` (otherwise `ERR_SPEC_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`).

A successful release sets `released_at` to the current logical clock, records `released_by_user_id` as the releasing officer's `X-User-ID`, and increments the specification's `version`.

If the released revision names a predecessor, the release then drives the supersession cascade, and its three hops are chained: each one happens *because* the hop before it happened.

The first hop moves the predecessor from `RELEASED` to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` state and increments its `version`. As part of that same hop, every compliance matrix entry belonging to the predecessor whose `disposition` is still `PENDING` moves to `VOID` — processed in ascending `created_at` order — with `disposition_note` set and each entry's `version` incremented.

The second hop reaches into work already in flight. Every unit order that pinned the predecessor as its specification and whose `build_status` is none of `DELIVERED`, `WARRANTY_ACTIVE` or `CANCELLED` has its `spec_drift_flag` set to true, processed in ascending `created_at` order, with each unit's `version` incremented. The flag is the plant's permanent record that a unit is being built to a revision the agency has since replaced.

The third hop is caused by the second: a unit whose `spec_drift_flag` has just become true and whose `certification_status` is `CERTIFIED` has that certification reset from `CERTIFIED` back to `NOT_SUBMITTED`, and every certification test of that unit whose `status` is `PASSED` moves `PASSED` to `INVALIDATED` — again in ascending `created_at` order — with `invalidated_reason` set to `SPEC_SUPERSEDED`. Each affected row's `version` increments. A certificate earned against a superseded document is worth nothing, so the unit has to be tested again. Note that this third hop resets the certification dimension only; it does not itself drag the build dimension backwards, because the build revert is a consequence of a *weight* reset rather than of a specification reset.

### 3.5. Awarding a Unit: Escalated Price and Axle Load Distribution

An award turns a released document into a physical vehicle to be built. A `PurchasingOfficer` awards one **unit order** at a time, and the award is the single richest payload in the API. It names the owning `program_id` and the `specification_id` of the released revision being pinned — pinned meaning it is never re-pointed for the life of the unit, which is precisely why supersession has to raise a drift flag rather than silently swap the document. Those two references are resolved in that order, `program_id` first and `specification_id` second, so a payload naming both an unknown program and an unknown specification reports `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`. It declares the `unit_type`, one of `PUMPER`, `AERIAL` or `AMBULANCE`, naming the body and apparatus family being built. It carries an `award_year`, the calendar year the unit is awarded, which drives the escalation exponent; the only bound rung 3 places on that integer is a wide sanity range of 0 through 9999, because the program-relative window that really governs it is rung 53 and has to be reportable as `ERR_ESCALATION_YEAR_OUT_OF_RANGE` rather than as a request-shape failure. It may carry `option_unit`, which defaults to false and is set true when this vehicle is an exercised contract option rather than a base-award unit. And it carries the chassis and body engineering data: a `gvwr_kg` between 4,000 and 40,000 giving the chassis gross vehicle weight rating in kilograms, a `front_axle_rating_kg` between 1,500 and 12,000, a `rear_axle_rating_kg` between 2,000 and 30,000, a `base_curb_front_kg` of at least 500 and a `base_curb_rear_kg` of at least 500 giving the dry body curb weight carried by each axle, a `crew_seat_count` between 0 and 8 giving the seated crew positions, and a `weight_calc_engineer_user_id` naming the body engineer who owns this unit's weight distribution calculation — a non-empty string with no upper length bound, stored verbatim and never resolved against any row.

Three referential conditions guard the award beyond the program's own posture. The pinned specification must be `RELEASED`, or the award is refused with `ERR_SPEC_NOT_RELEASED`. That specification's `program_id` must equal the `program_id` on the request, or the award is refused with `ERR_SPEC_PROGRAM_MISMATCH`. And when `option_unit` is true, the program's `options_exercised_count` must be strictly less than its `max_option_units`, or the award is refused with `ERR_OPTION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The `award_year` must also fall within the escalation window: not earlier than the program's `base_year` and not later than that base year plus five, or the award is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_YEAR_OUT_OF_RANGE`.

A successful award lands the unit with `build_status` at `SPEC_LOCKED`, `certification_status` at `NOT_SUBMITTED`, `long_lead_status` at `NOT_RESERVED`, `spec_drift_flag` at false, `chassis_reservation_id` at null, `open_change_order_count` at 0, `change_order_total_cents` at 0 and `delivered_at` at null. When `option_unit` is true the program's `options_exercised_count` increases by one. The program's committed money is then recomputed and the program's `version` increments; a soft-ceiling breach suspends the program while the award itself still succeeds, and a hard-ceiling breach rejects the award outright.

**The award price.** `price_cents` is computed at award time under the *award-year-locked* pricing rule and then frozen for the life of the unit — no later change order, cascade or rescission ever alters it. The computation runs in seven steps, in exactly this order, and the order matters because the performance-bond rate is keyed on the subtotal and never on the total, even though the bond fee is itself a component of the total.

1. The number of whole years elapsed is the unit's `award_year` minus the program's `base_year`, which is never negative.
2. The escalated base is the program's `base_unit_price_cents` multiplied by a compounding factor — one plus the program's `escalation_index_pct_per_year` divided by one hundred, raised to the power of the number of whole years elapsed — quantised to whole cents with round-half-up.
3. The option premium is three and a half percent of that escalated base, quantised to whole cents with round-half-up, when the unit's `option_unit` is true; it is 0 when `option_unit` is false.
4. The subtotal is the escalated base plus the option premium. This sum is *not* rounded, because both addends are already whole cents.
5. The performance-bond rate is selected from the subtotal against two tier ceilings: a subtotal of at most 50,000,000 cents (500,000 dollars) takes the first-tier rate of 1.25 percent; a subtotal above that but at most 90,000,000 cents (900,000 dollars) takes the second-tier rate of 1.00 percent; a subtotal above 90,000,000 cents takes the third-tier rate of 0.75 percent. Both tier boundaries are inclusive of the ceiling value.
6. The performance-bond fee is the subtotal multiplied by the selected rate and divided by one hundred, quantised to whole cents with round-half-up.
7. `price_cents` is the subtotal plus the performance-bond fee, with no further rounding.

Rounding happens at steps 2, 3 and 6 and nowhere else. As a worked illustration: a program with a base unit price of 62,500,000 cents, an escalation index of 4.0 percent per year and a base year of 2024, awarding a non-option unit in 2026, has two whole years elapsed, an escalated base of 67,600,000 cents, a subtotal of 67,600,000 cents that falls in the second bond tier, a bond fee of 676,000 cents and a `price_cents` of 68,276,000. The same program awarding that unit as an option adds a premium of 2,366,000 cents for a subtotal of 69,966,000 cents, still second tier, a fee of 699,660 cents and a `price_cents` of 70,665,660. The tier-keying order is what separates a correct implementation from a plausible one: a unit whose subtotal is exactly 50,000,000 cents takes the first-tier rate of 1.25 percent for a fee of 625,000 cents and a price of 50,625,000 cents, whereas keying the tier on the finished total would have selected the second tier and produced 50,500,000 — a divergence of 125,000 cents.

**The axle loads.** At the same moment, the unit's weight distribution is computed, writing `front_axle_load_kg`, `rear_axle_load_kg`, `gross_weight_kg` and `weight_status`. The model is a constraint propagation from the chassis rating down to a safety verdict, and it is re-run later whenever the accumulated approved change-order weight deltas change.

Payload capacity is the smaller of a fixed tank-or-module cap and a percentage of the chassis rating, where the percentage leg is truncated downwards to a whole kilogram. For a `PUMPER` the cap is 3,400 kilograms and the percentage is 28 percent of `gvwr_kg`. For an `AERIAL` the cap is 1,800 kilograms and the percentage is 12 percent of `gvwr_kg`. For an `AMBULANCE` the cap is 900 kilograms and the percentage is 20 percent of `gvwr_kg`. In each case the payload is whichever of the two figures is smaller, with the percentage figure floored before the comparison.

That payload is then split between the axles. The front share is one quarter of the payload, quantised to a whole kilogram with round-half-up, but capped at 450 kilograms — whichever of the two is smaller. The rear share is the whole payload minus the front share, so nothing is lost in the split.

Crew mass is 80 kilograms per seated position, and the seating layout puts the first two seats over the front axle. The front axle therefore carries whichever is smaller of `crew_seat_count` and 2, multiplied by 80 kilograms; the rear axle carries the number of seats beyond the first two — that is, `crew_seat_count` minus 2, or 0 when there are two seats or fewer — multiplied by 80 kilograms.

Change-order weight is summed from the unit's change orders whose `status` is `APPROVED`, and only those: the front contribution is the sum of their `weight_delta_front_kg` values and the rear contribution the sum of their `weight_delta_rear_kg` values. Change orders that are `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `IN_NEGOTIATION`, `REJECTED` or `RESCINDED` contribute nothing, which is exactly what makes rescission an exact reversal.

`front_axle_load_kg` is therefore `base_curb_front_kg` plus the front payload share plus the front crew mass plus the approved front delta. `rear_axle_load_kg` is `base_curb_rear_kg` plus the rear payload share plus the rear crew mass plus the approved rear delta. `gross_weight_kg` is the sum of the two axle loads. No rounding occurs anywhere in these sums; the only two quantisations in the whole weight model are the floor on the percentage-of-rating leg and the round-half-up on the front payload share.

The verdict, `weight_status`, is then decided by three strict comparisons evaluated in exactly this order. If `gross_weight_kg` is strictly greater than `gvwr_kg`, the verdict is `OVER_GVWR`. Otherwise, if `front_axle_load_kg` is strictly greater than `front_axle_rating_kg`, the verdict is `OVER_FRONT`. Otherwise, if `rear_axle_load_kg` is strictly greater than `rear_axle_rating_kg`, the verdict is `OVER_REAR`. Otherwise the verdict is `WITHIN_LIMITS`. Every comparison is strict, so a load exactly equal to its rating is `WITHIN_LIMITS` — a chassis rated to 10,110 kilograms on the rear axle carrying exactly 10,110 kilograms is a compliant vehicle, and it takes a single additional kilogram to make it `OVER_REAR`. The ordering also means a vehicle that is over its gross rating reports `OVER_GVWR` even when an individual axle is over its own rating too.

### 3.6. Long-Lead Procurement: Supplier Slots, Holds and Losing a Chassis

Chassis, aerial devices and patient modules are all long-lead items bought against a supplier's build calendar, and the plant models that calendar as a **supplier slot line**. A `ProgramAdmin` registers one by naming the `supplier_name` (1 to 120 characters — a chassis OEM, an aerial device builder or a module builder), the `component_type` whose build slots the line sells (one of `CHASSIS`, `AERIAL_DEVICE` or `PATIENT_MODULE`), a `total_slot_count` between 1 and 50 giving the conserved number of build slots the supplier has committed on that line, and optionally a `changeover_buffer_days` between 0 and 30 which defaults to 7 and is the mandatory line-changeover gap the supplier enforces between two occupied slot windows. A freshly registered line has `reserved_slot_count` at 0 and `available_slot_count` equal to its `total_slot_count`. Slot lines are read by every role, and the read is where a client picks up the `version` it must echo back when booking.

Occupancy is derived, never written directly. `reserved_slot_count` is the number of that line's reservations whose `status` is `RESERVED`, `CONFIRMED` or `CONSUMED`; `available_slot_count` is `total_slot_count` minus `reserved_slot_count`. A `RELEASED` or `EXPIRED` reservation leaves the occupied set entirely, which is what makes release and expiry exactly compensating against a booking. `available_slot_count` is a conserved quantity that must never go negative: a booking attempted on a line whose `available_slot_count` has already reached 0 is refused with `ERR_SLOT_LINE_EXHAUSTED`, and the zero boundary is strict.

A `ProgramAdmin` books a **chassis reservation** against a line for a program. The payload names the `program_id` the hold is taken for and the `slot_line_id` whose slot window is being occupied, an inclusive `slot_start_date` and an exclusive `slot_end_date` bounding the occupied window, a `reserved_price_cents` of at least 0 cents holding the quoted component price, an `expiry_at` instant after which a still-held reservation will be swept away, the `expected_slot_line_version` described in Section 2.7, and optionally a `unit_order_id` earmarking the slot for a specific unit. Those references are resolved in the order `program_id`, then `unit_order_id` when one is supplied, then `slot_line_id`, so a payload naming more than one unknown identifier reports the first miss in that order. A reservation with no `unit_order_id` is an unassigned speculative hold. When a `unit_order_id` *is* supplied, that unit's `program_id` must equal the reservation's `program_id`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_UNIT_MISMATCH`.

Two window rules guard the booking. First, the window must be coherent in itself: `slot_end_date` must be strictly later than `slot_start_date`, and `expiry_at` must be strictly later than `slot_start_date`; a violation of either is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SLOT_WINDOW`. Second, the window must not collide with any window already occupying that line, and this is where the changeover buffer bites. Each existing reservation on the same line whose `status` is `RESERVED`, `CONFIRMED` or `CONSUMED` is considered in turn, and its window is *inflated on both sides* by the line's `changeover_buffer_days`: its inflated start is its `slot_start_date` moved earlier by that many whole days, and its inflated end is its `slot_end_date` moved later by that many whole days. The comparison against the inflated window is half-open: a conflict exists when the new `slot_start_date` is strictly earlier than the inflated end *and* the inflated start is strictly earlier than the new `slot_end_date`. Any single collision with any single occupying reservation refuses the write with `ERR_SLOT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`; the aggregate capacity of the line is irrelevant to this test, because one supplier line builds one vehicle at a time in one window. With the default seven-day buffer, back-to-back windows do conflict — a new window starting exactly on an existing window's `slot_end_date` collides, and the earliest admissible start is seven days after that existing end. `RELEASED` and `EXPIRED` reservations impose no buffer at all.

A successful booking lands the reservation in `RESERVED` with `confirmed_at` at null, rebuilds the line's `reserved_slot_count` and `available_slot_count`, and increments the line's `version`. When the booking named a `unit_order_id`, that unit's `chassis_reservation_id` is set to point at the new reservation and its `long_lead_status` becomes `RESERVED` whatever it read before — `NOT_RESERVED` on a first hold, `AT_RISK` on a unit that lost an earlier slot — with the unit's `version` incremented. No build-state guard governs that earmark: the pointer and the `RESERVED` posture are written whatever the unit's `build_status`.

Confirming a hold takes the reservation from `RESERVED` to `CONFIRMED`, and only a reservation that actually names a unit may be confirmed — a speculative hold with a null `unit_order_id` is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_UNASSIGNED`. Confirmation stamps `confirmed_at` with the current logical clock, moves the linked unit's `long_lead_status` to `CONFIRMED`, and increments both the reservation's `version` and the unit's `version`. The line's slot counts do not move, because `RESERVED` and `CONFIRMED` both occupy a slot; confirmation locks the supplier slot and the quoted component price without changing how much of the line is taken.

Releasing a hold is available from either `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED` and takes the reservation to the terminal `RELEASED` state. It drives a three-hop cascade in which each hop is caused by the previous one. The first hop rebuilds the line's `reserved_slot_count` and `available_slot_count` and increments the line's `version` — the slot is genuinely back on the market. The second hop is caused by that freed slot: if the reservation named a `unit_order_id` and that unit's `build_status` is either `SPEC_LOCKED` or `PRE_CON_HELD`, the unit's `chassis_reservation_id` is cleared to null and its `long_lead_status` becomes `AT_RISK`. The third hop is caused by the unit going at risk: every inspection trip for that unit whose `trip_type` is `PRE_CONSTRUCTION` and whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` moves `SCHEDULED` to `CANCELLED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, releasing each trip's `committee_size` from its bay's aggregate occupancy. There is no point flying a committee out to inspect a pre-construction build that has lost its chassis slot. The reservation's own `version` increments alongside every row the cascade touched.

Expiry is the same story reached by the clock instead of by an operator. During a program's sweep, a reservation belonging to that program whose `status` is still `RESERVED` and whose `expiry_at` is at or before the current logical clock moves `RESERVED` to the terminal `EXPIRED` state. It rebuilds the line's slot counts and increments the line's `version`; and if it named a unit whose `build_status` is `SPEC_LOCKED` or `PRE_CON_HELD`, that unit's `chassis_reservation_id` is cleared and its `long_lead_status` becomes `AT_RISK`, with the unit's still-scheduled pre-construction trips cancelled as above. Expired reservations are processed in ascending `created_at` order within the sweep, and each increments its own `version`. Note that only a `RESERVED` hold expires — a `CONFIRMED` hold is a firm supplier commitment and the sweep leaves it alone regardless of `expiry_at`.

The fourth way out of a reservation is consumption, and it is not requested directly: when the unit the reservation is tied to starts production, the reservation moves from `CONFIRMED` to the terminal `CONSUMED` state and its `version` increments. A `CONSUMED` reservation still occupies its slot, so the line's `reserved_slot_count` and `available_slot_count` are recomputed on that transition and come out unchanged — but the recomputation is still a mutation of the line and still increments the line's `version`.

### 3.7. Committee Inspection Trips and Bay Occupancy

Contractual inspection trips are how the agency's committee sees its vehicle before accepting it, and they consume a physical resource: floor space. A `ProgramAdmin` registers each **plant bay** with a `name` of 1 to 80 characters labelling the inspection bay on the plant floor and a `max_concurrent_visitors` between 1 and 60 giving the bay's aggregate visitor capacity at any instant. Bays are immutable once registered.

A `ProgramAdmin` schedules an **inspection trip** by naming the `unit_order_id` being inspected, the `plant_bay_id` the committee will occupy (the two resolved in that order, so a payload naming both an unknown unit and an unknown bay reports `ERR_UNIT_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`), the `trip_type` marking which contractual milestone this is — `PRE_CONSTRUCTION`, `MID_CONSTRUCTION` or `FINAL` — an inclusive `scheduled_start` and inclusive `scheduled_end` bounding the bay occupation window, and a `committee_size` between 1 and 40 giving the number of customer committee visitors counted against the bay's capacity. `scheduled_end` must be strictly later than `scheduled_start`, or the trip is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRIP_WINDOW`. A newly scheduled trip lands in `SCHEDULED` with `held_at` at null.

Bay admission is an **aggregate** capacity test over **closed** windows, and both of those words carry weight. Two trips collide when their closed windows intersect, and touching endpoints do intersect: an existing trip whose `scheduled_end` falls exactly on the new trip's `scheduled_start` is a collision. Formally, two windows intersect when the first window's start is at or before the second window's end and the second window's start is at or before the first window's end. The server sums the `committee_size` of every trip in the same bay whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `HELD` and whose window intersects the requested window, adds the new `committee_size`, and admits the trip only if that sum is at most the bay's `max_concurrent_visitors`. A sum exactly equal to the capacity is admitted; anything above it is refused with `ERR_BAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A pairwise overlap on its own is not a conflict — two overlapping trips of six visitors each are perfectly fine in a bay rated for twelve. `CANCELLED` trips are excluded from the sum, which is precisely why cancelling a trip frees capacity for a later booking. Datetimes in this comparison are evaluated in UTC on the instants the supplied texts denote, at whatever precision those texts themselves carry, exactly as §2.6 describes.

Holding a trip is the operation that actually convenes the committee, and it is shared between a `ProgramAdmin` and the `CustomerCommitteeRep` of the owning program. It takes the trip from `SCHEDULED` to the terminal `HELD` state, stamps `held_at` with the current logical clock, and increments the trip's `version`. Three conditions bind beyond the trip's own state. At most one trip of a given `trip_type` may ever be held on a given unit; a second attempt is refused with `ERR_TRIP_TYPE_ALREADY_HELD`. The unit's build progress must match the milestone, and that check is evaluated last of all — below the committee-scope rule, below the trip's own state and below the one-of-a-type rule: a `MID_CONSTRUCTION` trip requires the unit's `build_status` to be `IN_PRODUCTION`, and a `FINAL` trip requires it to be `FINAL_INSPECTED`, either mismatch being refused with `ERR_INVALID_BUILD_STATE` and HTTP 409; a `PRE_CONSTRUCTION` trip carries no build-state requirement at all and may be held whatever the unit reads. And a `CustomerCommitteeRep` may only hold trips on units belonging to the program its `X-User-ID` identifies.

Holding a trip is also how the build dimension advances through its two inspection gates. A `PRE_CONSTRUCTION` trip held against a unit whose `build_status` is `SPEC_LOCKED` moves that unit `SPEC_LOCKED` to `PRE_CON_HELD` and increments the unit's `version`; the certification dimension is untouched and stays `NOT_SUBMITTED`. A `MID_CONSTRUCTION` trip held against a unit whose `build_status` is `IN_PRODUCTION` moves that unit `IN_PRODUCTION` to `MID_CON_HELD` and increments the unit's `version`. A `FINAL` trip advances nothing — it is the committee's acceptance walk-through on a vehicle that has already passed final inspection.

Cancelling a trip takes it from `SCHEDULED` to the terminal `CANCELLED` state and increments its `version`. It is available to a `ProgramAdmin` and to the owning program's `CustomerCommitteeRep`, and the trip's `committee_size` immediately stops counting against its bay's aggregate occupancy. Only a `SCHEDULED` trip may be held or cancelled; either operation against a trip that is already `HELD` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRIP_STATE`.

Trips are also cancelled by the system, never by request, in two cascade situations. A `FINAL` trip that is still `SCHEDULED` on a unit whose build dimension has just been dragged back from `FINAL_INSPECTED` to `IN_PRODUCTION` is cancelled. A `PRE_CONSTRUCTION` trip that is still `SCHEDULED` on a unit whose `long_lead_status` has just become `AT_RISK` is cancelled. In both cases the affected trips are processed in ascending `created_at` order, each has its `version` incremented, and each releases its `committee_size` from the bay's aggregate.

### 3.8. Change Orders: Negotiation, the Approval Cascade and Exact Reversal

Every committee trip produces a punch list, and a punch list becomes a **change order** — the negotiated amendment that moves both the unit's price and its weight. A `CustomerCommitteeRep` raises one against a unit of the program it represents, naming the `unit_order_id` whose price and weight are being amended and the `inspection_trip_id` of the committee trip the change was raised at (resolved in that order, so a payload naming both an unknown unit and an unknown trip reports `ERR_UNIT_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`). That cited trip must belong to the same unit: if the trip's own `unit_order_id` differs from the `unit_order_id` on the request, the write is refused with `ERR_TRIP_UNIT_MISMATCH`. The request also carries the `expected_unit_order_version` described in Section 2.7, without which the amendment could be priced against a baseline the committee never saw.

The substance of a change order is its `line_items`, an ordered array of between 1 and 12 entries. Each entry is a JSON object — any key beyond the five named here is ignored — carrying a non-empty `description` of what is being changed with no upper length bound, a `quantity` of at least 1, a `unit_cost_cents` of at least 0 cents, a `markup_pct` of at least 0.0, and an `is_taxable` boolean that is required on every line and has no default. Only the lower bound on `markup_pct` is a request-shape rule: a negative markup is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at rung 3, whereas a markup above the 22.0 cap passes rung 3 and is caught much further down the ladder as `ERR_MARKUP_CAP_EXCEEDED`. No line may carry a markup above 22.0 percent — the contractual markup cap — and a line that does refuses the whole write with `ERR_MARKUP_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Alongside the money, the change order declares its physical consequence: a signed `weight_delta_front_kg` and a signed `weight_delta_rear_kg`, each between negative 2,000 and positive 2,000 kilograms, stating how much weight this change adds to or removes from each axle. A change order may only be raised against a unit that is still amendable, meaning its `build_status` is none of `DELIVERED`, `WARRANTY_ACTIVE` or `CANCELLED`; the same amendability rule guards submission, negotiation, revision, approval and rescission, and a violation of it anywhere in that set is refused with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_AMENDABLE`.

A raised change order lands in `DRAFT` with `revision_count` at 0, `raised_by_user_id` set to the representative's `X-User-ID`, `approved_by_user_id` at null and `applied_at` at null. Raising it increases the unit's `open_change_order_count` by one and increments the unit's `version` — which, note, immediately invalidates any other client's cached unit version.

**Pricing a change order.** `price_delta_cents` is computed when the change order is raised and recomputed on every revision, and the rounding order is pinned: each line item is rounded on its own and the rounded line values are then summed. Summing unrounded line values and rounding once at the end produces a different figure and is wrong. Starting from zero, the server walks the `line_items` array in the exact order the client supplied it and, for each line: computes the line base as the `quantity` multiplied by the `unit_cost_cents`; computes the marked-up line value as that base multiplied by one plus the line's `markup_pct` divided by one hundred, quantised to whole cents with round-half-up; computes the line tax as the marked-up line value multiplied by the tax rate of six and a half percent — that is, multiplied by 6.5 and divided by one hundred — quantised to whole cents with round-half-up, or as 0 when the line's `is_taxable` is false; and adds the marked-up line value plus the line tax to the running total. Exactly two round-half-up quantisations occur per line — one on the markup, one on the tax — and none anywhere else. Only this change order's own line items participate; no other change order contributes a cent.

As a worked illustration of why the per-line ordering matters: a change order with three lines — three units at 133,333 cents with 12.5 percent markup and taxable; seven units at 45,555 cents with 7.5 percent markup and not taxable; one unit at 1,250,005 cents with 22.0 percent markup and taxable — yields line values of 479,249, 342,801 and 1,624,131 cents, for a `price_delta_cents` of 2,446,181. Summing before rounding would have produced 2,446,182, one cent high.

**The negotiation cycle.** A `CustomerCommitteeRep` submits a `DRAFT` change order, taking it to `SUBMITTED`; the cited trip must be `HELD` at that moment, or the submission is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_TRIP_NOT_HELD`. A `PurchasingOfficer` who is not satisfied with the pricing returns it to the committee, taking `SUBMITTED` to `IN_NEGOTIATION`. The committee then revises it, taking `IN_NEGOTIATION` back to `SUBMITTED` with a fresh `line_items` array and fresh `weight_delta_front_kg` and `weight_delta_rear_kg` values that wholly replace the previous ones; the markup cap is re-checked over the new lines, `price_delta_cents` is recomputed from scratch, and `revision_count` increases by one. The `SUBMITTED` to `IN_NEGOTIATION` to `SUBMITTED` loop may repeat without bound, and `revision_count` is the record of how many times it went round. Every one of these transitions increments the change order's `version`, and none of them touches the unit order at all: submission, negotiation and revision leave the unit's `open_change_order_count`, `updated_at` and `version` exactly as they were, because the change order never leaves the open set while the loop is running.

**Approval and Cascade A.** A `PurchasingOfficer` approves a `SUBMITTED` change order, and this is where the amendment becomes physical. Approval requires the unit to still be amendable and requires the program's resulting committed money to be at most its `contract_ceiling_cents`; a hard-ceiling breach refuses the approval with `ERR_CONTRACT_CEILING_EXCEEDED` and nothing at all is written. On success the change order moves `SUBMITTED` to `APPROVED`, records `approved_by_user_id` as the approving officer's `X-User-ID` and stamps `applied_at` with the current logical clock.

The cascade then runs, hop by hop, each hop caused by the one before:

- **Hop 1.** The unit's `change_order_total_cents` is rebuilt as the sum of the `price_delta_cents` of every change order of that unit whose `status` is `APPROVED` — an empty set giving 0, and no rounding involved because every addend is already whole cents. Its `open_change_order_count` is rebuilt as the count of that unit's change orders whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `IN_NEGOTIATION`, so approving the change order drops the count by one. And the unit's axle loads are re-run over the cumulative approved weight deltas, refreshing `front_axle_load_kg`, `rear_axle_load_kg`, `gross_weight_kg` and `weight_status`. The unit's `version` increments.
- **Hop 2**, caused by hop 1's verdict leaving `WITHIN_LIMITS`. If the rebuilt `weight_status` is anything other than `WITHIN_LIMITS` and the unit's `certification_status` is `CERTIFIED`, that certification is reset `CERTIFIED` to `NOT_SUBMITTED`, and every certification test of the unit whose `status` is `PASSED` moves `PASSED` to `INVALIDATED` in ascending `created_at` order with `invalidated_reason` set to `WEIGHT_STATUS_CHANGE`. Each touched row's `version` increments.
- **Hop 3**, caused by hop 2's certification reset. If the unit's `build_status` was `FINAL_INSPECTED` at the moment its certification was reset, it reverts `FINAL_INSPECTED` to `IN_PRODUCTION`. This is the one place in the system where the build dimension moves backwards, and it is triggered by the certification dimension rather than by any operator.
- **Hop 4**, caused by hop 3's build revert. Every inspection trip of that unit whose `trip_type` is `FINAL` and whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` moves `SCHEDULED` to `CANCELLED`, in ascending `created_at` order, freeing each trip's `committee_size` from its bay.
- **Ledger sync**, caused by hop 1's new change-order total. The program's `committed_cents` is recomputed and its `version` increments; if the recomputed figure strictly exceeds the soft ceiling and the program is `ACTIVE`, the program is suspended with `suspension_reason` set to `SOFT_CEILING_BREACH`.

The change order's own `version` increments alongside all of it.

**Rejection.** A `PurchasingOfficer` may instead reject a `SUBMITTED` change order, taking it to the terminal `REJECTED` state. This has no price or weight consequence whatsoever, because a rejected change order never entered the unit's change-order total in the first place; the only effect on the unit is that its `open_change_order_count` is rebuilt and therefore drops by one. Both the change order's `version` and the unit's `version` increment.

**Rescission — the compensating transition.** A `PurchasingOfficer` rescinds an `APPROVED` change order, taking it to the terminal `RESCINDED` state, provided the unit is still amendable. Rescission is designed to be an exact reversal of the fiscal and physical effects of the approval. The unit's `change_order_total_cents` is rebuilt over the *remaining* approved change orders and the axle loads are re-run over the *remaining* approved weight deltas, so both the money and the axle loads return precisely to their pre-approval values — the rescinded change order simply leaves the input set of both aggregations. The unit's frozen `price_cents` is untouched, because the award price is fixed for the life of the unit and was never part of what the change order moved. `approved_by_user_id` is cleared to null and `applied_at` is cleared to null. The program's `committed_cents` is recomputed back to exactly its pre-approval value. The change order's `version`, the unit's `version` and the program's `version` all increment.

What rescission deliberately does *not* undo is anything the approval's certification cascade destroyed. A certification test the approval moved to `INVALIDATED` stays `INVALIDATED`; a `certification_status` the approval reset to `NOT_SUBMITTED` stays `NOT_SUBMITTED`; a `build_status` the approval dragged back to `IN_PRODUCTION` stays `IN_PRODUCTION`; and a `FINAL` trip the approval cancelled stays `CANCELLED`. Certification has to be re-earned on the vehicle as it now stands. Nor does rescission ever *drive* that teardown: it re-runs the axle loads but never Cascade A's second hop, so a rescission whose recomputed verdict leaves `WITHIN_LIMITS` — which withdrawing a change order that had *removed* weight can do — writes the new `weight_status` and stops there, leaving the unit's `certification_status`, its `PASSED` certification tests and its `build_status` untouched. Similarly, a program that the approval suspended for a soft-ceiling breach stays `SUSPENDED` even when the rescission pulls committed money back under the soft ceiling — recovery is always an explicit `ProgramAdmin` reactivation.

### 3.9. Building the Vehicle: Production, Certification, Final Inspection, Delivery and Warranty

A unit order tracks two progress dimensions at once, and they are genuinely independent. `build_status` records physical progress through `SPEC_LOCKED`, `PRE_CON_HELD`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, `MID_CON_HELD`, `FINAL_INSPECTED`, `DELIVERED`, `WARRANTY_ACTIVE` and `CANCELLED`, with the last two terminal. `certification_status` records third-party testing progress through `NOT_SUBMITTED`, `IN_TESTING`, `CERTIFIED` and `REJECTED_TESTING`, none of which is terminal because certification can always be lost and re-earned. The two dimensions advance on entirely different triggers and touch each other at exactly two points: final inspection requires the certification dimension to read `CERTIFIED`, and a certification reset out of `CERTIFIED` drags the build dimension backwards out of `FINAL_INSPECTED`. Everywhere else they move without reference to one another — a unit can cycle through failed and repeated certification tests while its `build_status` sits unmoved at `MID_CON_HELD`.

**Starting production.** A `ProgramAdmin` starts production on a unit, taking `build_status` from `PRE_CON_HELD` to `IN_PRODUCTION`. Two cross-entity guards apply on two different partners: the owning program's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the call is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE`; and the unit's `chassis_reservation_id` must be non-null with that reservation's `status` reading `CONFIRMED`, or the call is refused with `ERR_CHASSIS_NOT_CONFIRMED`. A unit whose slot was released or expired out from under it — and which therefore sits at `long_lead_status` of `AT_RISK` with a null `chassis_reservation_id` — cannot start production until a fresh reservation has been booked and confirmed for it. Starting production consumes the reservation, moving it `CONFIRMED` to `CONSUMED`, recomputes the supplier line's `reserved_slot_count` and `available_slot_count` (which come out unchanged, since a consumed slot is still an occupied slot) and increments the line's `version`, the reservation's `version` and the unit's `version`.

**Submitting for certification.** Once the mid-construction committee trip has been held, a `ThirdPartyTester` submits the unit to standards testing. The submission names the `unit_order_id` under test and the `test_standard` the third party will test against — one of `NFPA_1901`, `NFPA_1911`, `NFPA_1917` or `AMD_STD_003` — and may carry a `tested_at` instant recording when the third party performed the test. Three conditions bind. The unit's `certification_status` must be `NOT_SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED_TESTING`, the two states from which a fresh test is meaningful; a submission while the unit is already `IN_TESTING` or already `CERTIFIED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CERTIFICATION_STATE`. An inspection trip of that unit with `trip_type` `MID_CONSTRUCTION` and `status` `HELD` must exist, or the submission is refused with `ERR_MID_CON_INSPECTION_NOT_HELD` — the standards house does not test a body the committee has not yet walked. And the standard must match the vehicle family: a `PUMPER` is tested against `NFPA_1901`, an `AERIAL` against `NFPA_1911`, and an `AMBULANCE` against either `NFPA_1917` or `AMD_STD_003`; any other pairing is refused with `ERR_TEST_STANDARD_MISMATCH`.

A successful submission creates a **certification test** row in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `result` at null, `invalidated_reason` at null, `signed_by_user_id` at null and `submitted_by_user_id` set to the acting operator's `X-User-ID`, and it moves the unit's `certification_status` from `NOT_SUBMITTED` to `IN_TESTING`, incrementing the unit's `version`. When the submission comes after a failure, the same operation moves the unit `REJECTED_TESTING` to `IN_TESTING` and creates another test row; this retest cycle may repeat, and `build_status` is untouched throughout.

**Recording the result.** A `ThirdPartyTester` records the outcome on a test whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` — any other test status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TEST_STATE`. The call supplies a `result` of `PASS` or `FAIL` and a `signed_by_user_id` identifying the third-party inspector signing the certificate — a non-empty string with no upper length bound — and that signer must differ from the unit's `weight_calc_engineer_user_id` or the call is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On a `PASS` the test moves `SUBMITTED` to `PASSED` with `result` set to `PASS`, and the unit's `certification_status` moves `IN_TESTING` to `CERTIFIED`. On a `FAIL` the test moves `SUBMITTED` to the terminal `FAILED` state with `result` set to `FAIL`, and the unit's `certification_status` moves `IN_TESTING` to `REJECTED_TESTING`. Either way the signer is stored on the test row alongside the `tested_at` instant the *submission* already recorded — recording a result declares no `tested_at` field of its own, so the test's `tested_at` remains whatever the submission supplied, null included — and both the test's `version` and the unit's `version` increment.

A `PASSED` test has one further, involuntary destination. When a cascade tears up a unit's certification, every `PASSED` test of that unit moves to the terminal `INVALIDATED` state with `invalidated_reason` recording why: `WEIGHT_STATUS_CHANGE` when the trigger was a change-order approval whose weight verdict left `WITHIN_LIMITS`, or `SPEC_SUPERSEDED` when the trigger was a specification revision that raised the unit's `spec_drift_flag`. Invalidation is never reversed.

**Final inspection.** A `ProgramAdmin` passes the unit through final inspection, taking `build_status` from `MID_CON_HELD` to `FINAL_INSPECTED`. This is the first of the two coupling points between the dimensions, and it carries three substantive gates evaluated in the ladder order of Section 2.9: the unit's `weight_status` must be `WITHIN_LIMITS` (otherwise `ERR_AXLE_LOAD_EXCEEDED`), its `certification_status` must be `CERTIFIED` (otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_PASSED`), and its `open_change_order_count` must be 0 (otherwise `ERR_OPEN_CHANGE_ORDERS`). No overloaded axle and no uncertified body passes final inspection, and no vehicle passes it with an amendment still under negotiation. The transition increments the unit's `version`.

**Delivery.** A `ProgramAdmin` delivers a `FINAL_INSPECTED` unit, taking `build_status` to `DELIVERED` and stamping `delivered_at` with the current logical clock. The handover payload carries a `delivery_date` recording the contractual delivery instant, a `training_hours` figure between 0.0 and 200.0 for the operator and maintainer training delivered at handover, and a `warranty_months` figure between 12 and 120 giving the warranty duration in whole months. Two gates apply: the unit's `weight_status` must still be `WITHIN_LIMITS`, or delivery is refused with `ERR_AXLE_LOAD_EXCEEDED`; and the `delivery_date` must not precede the `scheduled_end` of that unit's `HELD` `MID_CONSTRUCTION` inspection trip, or delivery is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_BEFORE_MID_CON`. Delivery increments the unit's `version` and returns the unit order itself, so the warranty record it creates is found by listing warranty startups filtered on `unit_order_id`.

Delivery creates exactly one **warranty startup** row for the unit, carrying that `unit_order_id`, the `delivery_date`, the `training_hours` and the `warranty_months` supplied at handover. The row is **always** created in `PENDING_TRAINING` with a deferred warranty start, whatever training was booked at handover: its `warranty_start_date` is the `delivery_date` moved thirty whole days later, and its `warranty_end_date` is that start date plus `warranty_months` whole calendar months, landing on the same day of month with the day clamped down to the last day of the target month when the target month has no such day. Both of those derived dates are rendered in the same textual style as the `delivery_date` text the client supplied: a fractional part of three digits only when that text carried a fractional part, a trailing `Z` when it ended in `Z`, and an explicit `+00:00` offset otherwise — so a `delivery_date` of `2027-03-01` yields dates like `2027-03-31T00:00:00+00:00`. A warranty start that is *not* deferred, as after training completes, is not re-rendered at all: it is the `delivery_date` text itself, byte for byte. Completing training is the only path to `ACTIVE`.

Delivery is also the moment the escalation is reconciled. `final_price_cents` is computed under the *delivery-year-actual* pricing rule, which differs from the award-year-locked rule at exactly one step: escalation is simple and pro-rata over whole months rather than compounded over whole years. The number of whole months elapsed is the delivery year minus the program's `base_year`, multiplied by twelve, plus the delivery month minus one — so a delivery in January of the base year has zero months elapsed. The escalated base is the program's `base_unit_price_cents` multiplied by one plus the product of the `escalation_index_pct_per_year` divided by one hundred and the months elapsed divided by twelve, quantised to whole cents with round-half-up. From there the computation is identical to the award-year-locked rule: the same option premium of three and a half percent when `option_unit` is true, the same unrounded subtotal, the same bond tier keyed on the subtotal (1.25 percent up to and including 50,000,000 cents, 1.00 percent above that up to and including 90,000,000 cents, 0.75 percent beyond), the same round-half-up on the bond fee, and the same unrounded final sum. `price_variance_cents` is then `final_price_cents` minus the unit's frozen `price_cents`, and it is deliberately excluded from the program's `committed_cents` — the variance is a reconciliation figure reported to the purchasing department, not a fresh commitment against the ceiling. Both figures are recorded on the warranty startup row the delivery creates, not on the unit order, which carries neither. Continuing the earlier illustration: the unit whose award-locked `price_cents` was 68,276,000 and which is delivered on 1 November 2026 against a 2024 base year has thirty-four whole months elapsed, a `final_price_cents` of 70,279,166 and therefore a `price_variance_cents` of 2,003,166.

**Warranty activation.** A `ProgramAdmin` completes training on a warranty record whose `status` is `PENDING_TRAINING` — any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WARRANTY_STATE`. The call supplies `additional_training_hours`, a decimal hour figure of at least 0.0 and with no upper bound at all — unlike the 0.0-to-200.0 `training_hours` taken at handover — which is added to the record's `training_hours` first, and the activation threshold is then evaluated against that accumulated figure: the record activates when cumulative `training_hours` reaches at least 16.0 hours, an inclusive boundary. On success the record moves `PENDING_TRAINING` to `ACTIVE`, its `warranty_start_date` is pulled back from the deferred date to the `delivery_date` itself, its `warranty_end_date` is recomputed as that pulled-back start plus `warranty_months` calendar months, and its `version` increments. If the accumulated figure would still fall short of 16.0 hours the call is refused with `ERR_TRAINING_HOURS_NOT_MET`, nothing is written, and the record keeps its `PENDING_TRAINING` status and its thirty-day deferred start. Exactly 16.0 cumulative hours activates the warranty and pulls the start back to the delivery date; 15.9 does not.

**Warranty run-out.** The unit's final forward step is not requested by anyone. During a program sweep, a unit whose `build_status` is `DELIVERED` and whose warranty record is `ACTIVE` with a `warranty_start_date` at or before the current logical clock moves `DELIVERED` to the terminal `WARRANTY_ACTIVE` state, incrementing the unit's `version`. Later, a warranty record that is `ACTIVE` and whose `warranty_end_date` is strictly earlier than the current logical clock moves `ACTIVE` to the terminal `EXPIRED` state, incrementing its `version`. A program may only be closed once every one of its units has reached `WARRANTY_ACTIVE` or `CANCELLED`.

**Cancelling a unit.** A `ProgramAdmin` may cancel a unit that has not yet entered production — `build_status` of `SPEC_LOCKED` or `PRE_CON_HELD`, and nothing later — taking it to the terminal `CANCELLED` state. Cancellation requires the unit's `open_change_order_count` to be 0, or it is refused with `ERR_OPEN_CHANGE_ORDERS`. The side-effect set is identical from either of the two permitted starting states. If the unit holds a `chassis_reservation_id` whose reservation is `RESERVED` or `CONFIRMED`, that reservation is moved to `RELEASED`, which rebuilds the supplier line's `reserved_slot_count` and `available_slot_count`; the unit's `chassis_reservation_id` ends up null in every case, whatever the status of whatever it pointed at. The unit is written `CANCELLED` *before* its hold is released, and that ordering decides how much of Cascade C runs: the at-risk hop recognises only a `SPEC_LOCKED` or `PRE_CON_HELD` unit, so a cancelled unit keeps the `long_lead_status` it was already reading rather than going `AT_RISK`, and none of its still-`SCHEDULED` inspection trips is cancelled — they stay `SCHEDULED` and keep counting their `committee_size` against their bay. If the unit's `option_unit` is true, the program's `options_exercised_count` decreases by one, freeing the option for another vehicle. The program's `committed_cents` is recomputed with the cancelled unit no longer in the input set, so its price and change-order total are decommitted from the ledger. Both the unit's `version` and the program's `version` increment. A program that was suspended for a soft-ceiling breach stays `SUSPENDED` after a cancellation, even when the decommitment brings it back under the soft ceiling.
