# Aging Life Care Coordination API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An Aging Life Care Association member practice sits at the centre of one of the most fragmented service landscapes in health care. A single older adult client may simultaneously be a hospital patient, a home-care customer, a legal client, a housing applicant, and the subject of an unresolved family disagreement about who is entitled to decide anything at all. The practice's care managers are expected to hold that whole picture: to assess the client comprehensively, author a care plan the family will actually approve, coordinate vendors across home care, medical, legal and housing verticals, convene and document family conferences, respond to crises and shepherd hospital-to-home transitions, and — because the practice is private-pay — bill every hour of it accurately against a retainer.

This document specifies the backend API that carries that practice. Its central concern is **defensibility**: every clinical judgement, every family decision, every dollar drawn from a client's retainer, and every referral relationship the practice holds with a vendor it recommends must be reconstructable after the fact from the record alone. That concern drives three structural commitments. First, authority is segregated — the care manager who authors an assessment may not accept it, the care manager who logs an hour may not approve it, and a conflict-of-interest disclosure on a referred vendor may only be acknowledged by a surrogate family decision maker, never by practice staff. Second, derived quantities are never trusted as stored facts; the client's risk posture, retainer balance, unbilled accrual and authorized coverage hours are recomputed from their inputs after every mutation that can move them, so that a compensating action leaves the ledger exactly where the forward action found it. Third, the system is explicit about capacity: whether an older adult is presumed capable, documented as diminished, or judicially adjudicated incapacitated changes who may decide what, and the transition into adjudication actively invalidates approvals that were pending under the old assumption.

The API is mounted under `/care`, with a single additional administrative surface under `/_clock` used to drive the server's logical clock. Every timestamp the system writes, every reporting-cadence window it evaluates, and every overdue calculation it performs reads that logical clock rather than wall-clock time. Implementers should treat the logical clock as the system's only notion of "now".

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API — including requests to the logical-clock surface — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the named role is not permitted to call the addressed operation, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `CareManager`, `PracticeSupervisor`, `FamilyDecisionMaker`, `BillingAdministrator`, and `PracticeAdmin`. Their spelling is exact and case-sensitive.

`CareManager` is the aging life care manager of record. When `X-User-Role` is `CareManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CareManager` record; ownership checks on any resource that references a `CareManager` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. A care manager owns assessment authorship, care-plan drafting, vendor authorization proposals, conference facilitation and documentation, crisis response, long-distance family reporting, and time capture. A care manager may never ratify or escalate a documented family decision, and may never touch the invoice or payment surfaces, the client / care-manager / vendor registration surfaces, or the logical clock.

`PracticeSupervisor` is a senior care manager, and is likewise entity-backed: when `X-User-Role` is `PracticeSupervisor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CareManager` record, which must exist and must carry `is_supervisor` set to true — an `X-User-ID` that resolves to no care manager, or to a care manager whose `is_supervisor` is false, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. That resolution is performed on the transitions whose authority the supervisor role itself supplies — the assessment review cycle, service-authorization state, time-entry state, the conference-decision lifecycle and the client-state operation — and is not performed where a supervisor is merely admitted alongside a care manager as an equal actor: a capacity determination, a care-plan state change and a crisis-episode transition admit the supervisor role without resolving `X-User-ID` against the care-manager register at all. The supervisor supplies the second pair of eyes: accepting or returning assessments for revision, ratifying and escalating documented decisions, approving or rejecting other care managers' time, and voiding time entries. A supervisor may not author the record it approves — it may not create time entries or conference decisions — and holds no billing authority.

`FamilyDecisionMaker` is entity-backed against the family circle: when `X-User-Role` is `FamilyDecisionMaker`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `FamilyContact`, and ownership checks compare the addressed resource's client linkage to that contact's own `client_id`; an `X-User-ID` that resolves to no family contact at all is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` on any operation that needs that scope, including every collection read. A family decision maker is attached to exactly one client. It may drive the family-approval decision on a care plan pending its approval, acknowledge a conflict-of-interest disclosure, and read that one client's record; every read it performs must resolve to its own client, and a read that resolves to a different client's record is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. It may never write clinical, authorization or billing state.

`BillingAdministrator` and `PracticeAdmin` are both opaque identities. When `X-User-Role` is `BillingAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`. The same holds when `X-User-Role` is `PracticeAdmin`. The billing administrator owns the retainer ledger, the invoice lifecycle and payment posting, and holds no clinical authority whatsoever; the practice administrator onboards clients, care managers, vendors and family contacts, maintains vendor standing, may discharge a client, and is the only role permitted to move the logical clock.

Several operations accept more than one role, and on those the acting role must additionally own the *specific* outcome it is requesting; a role that is listed on the operation but does not own the requested target state or action is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. On assessment state changes, `SUBMITTED`, the return to `DRAFT`, and withdrawal requested from `DRAFT` belong to `CareManager`, while `ACCEPTED` and `REVISION_REQUESTED` belong to `PracticeSupervisor` — as does withdrawal requested from `SUBMITTED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`. On care-plan state changes, `PENDING_FAMILY_APPROVAL` and the return to `DRAFT` belong to `CareManager`, while `ACTIVE` and `REJECTED` belong to `FamilyDecisionMaker`. On service-authorization state changes, `AUTHORIZED` belongs to `PracticeSupervisor` while `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` and `TERMINATED` belong to `CareManager`. On time-entry state changes, `SUBMITTED` and the return to `DRAFT` belong to `CareManager` while `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` and `VOIDED` belong to `PracticeSupervisor`. On conflict-of-interest events, the disclosure action belongs to `CareManager` and the acknowledgement action belongs to `FamilyDecisionMaker`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters `A`-`Z` and the digits `0`-`9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Three envelope-level rejections sit outside the workflow catalogue but use the same error envelope: a request addressed to a path this document does not define answers 404 with an `error_id` of `ERR_NOT_FOUND`, a request using a method the addressed path does not accept answers 405 with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and an unexpected server fault answers 500 with `ERR_INTERNAL`. A trailing slash on any path is accepted as equivalent to the same path without one.

### 2.3. Listing, Filtering and Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two pagination query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100; a `limit` outside that inclusive range is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `offset` controls the starting position, defaults to 0, and may not be negative; it carries no upper bound, and an `offset` at or beyond `total_count` simply yields an empty `data` array with `has_more` false. Either parameter supplied empty, or supplied as anything other than a base-10 integer, is likewise rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Both `limit` and `offset` are echoed in the `meta` object of the response together with `total_count` — the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied — and `has_more`, which is true exactly when records matching the filters remain beyond the current page.

Collections are returned in ascending `created_at` order, oldest record first. Because the logical clock only moves when it is explicitly set, records routinely share an identical `created_at`; such ties are broken by the order in which the server created the records, earliest creation first, and never by `id`. There is one deliberate exception to the direction: the client roster is returned newest first, in descending `created_at` order, because the practice reads it as an intake queue — and that reversal applies to the tie-break too, so among clients sharing a `created_at` the most recently created one leads.

Filters combine conjunctively — a record must satisfy every supplied filter to appear. Every filter is matched as an exact string comparison against the record's field rendered as text, a record whose filtered field is null matches no supplied value for that filter, and a query parameter that is neither `limit`, `offset` nor one of the filter names declared for that collection is ignored rather than rejected. Filter values are never validated against an enumeration: a value no record carries — including a value outside the declared vocabulary of that field — simply matches nothing and yields an empty page rather than `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The client roster filters on `status` and `risk_posture`. The vendor roster filters on `vendor_type` and `standing`. Family contacts filter on `client_id` and `report_status`. Assessments filter on `client_id`, `care_manager_id`, `reviewed_by_care_manager_id` and `status`. Care plans filter on `client_id`, `source_assessment_id`, `supersedes_care_plan_id`, `approved_by_contact_id` and `status`. Service authorizations filter on `client_id`, `care_plan_id`, `vendor_id`, `authorizing_decision_id`, `coi_acknowledged_by_contact_id`, `auth_status` and `coi_status`. Family conferences filter on `client_id` and `status`. Conference decisions filter on `family_conference_id`, `decided_by_contact_id`, `decision_type` and `status`. Crisis episodes filter on `client_id`, `assigned_care_manager_id` and `status`. Time entries filter on `client_id`, `care_manager_id`, `approved_by_care_manager_id`, `invoice_id`, `crisis_episode_id`, `status` and `service_date`. Invoices filter on `client_id` and `status`.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource in this system carries the same four housekeeping properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix — clients must never construct one. `created_at` is the logical-clock instant at which the record was created, and `updated_at` the logical-clock instant of its most recent mutation; both are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits. `version` is an integer that begins at 1 when the record is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record, including mutations the record suffers as a cascade from some other operation. No entity in this system omits `updated_at` or `version`.

Instants arriving in a request body — `to_iso8601`, `start_at`, `end_at`, `coverage_start`, `coverage_end`, `received_at`, `discharge_at`, `scheduled_start`, `scheduled_end`, `rescheduled_start` and `rescheduled_end` — are read as ISO 8601 and normalised to UTC: a trailing `Z` and a numeric UTC offset are both accepted, and a value carrying no zone designator at all is read as already being UTC. A string that is not a parseable ISO 8601 instant is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A single administrative capability sets the server's logical clock: it accepts a `to_iso8601` instant, answers 200 with a `data` object whose sole field is the resulting `current_utc` value, and accepts an instant earlier than the clock's present reading just as readily as a later one. The clock's startup default is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; until the clock is set, every logical-clock read returns that default, and the system never falls back to real wall-clock time. All subsequent logical-clock reads — `created_at`, `updated_at`, `accepted_at`, `activated_at`, `convened_at`, `ratified_at`, `escalated_at`, `opened_at`, `stabilized_at`, `closed_at`, `issued_at`, `last_report_sent_at`, and every reporting-cadence evaluation — use the instant most recently set.

### 2.5. Resource Addressing and Reference Resolution

Path variables are validated left to right, and the leftmost failure is the one reported. The addressable path variables are `clientId`, `careManagerId`, `vendorId`, `contactId`, `assessmentId`, `carePlanId`, `authorizationId`, `conferenceId`, `decisionId`, `episodeId`, `timeEntryId` and `invoiceId`. Each resolves to its own not-found error: a `clientId` or a body-carried `client_id` that matches no client yields `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_FOUND`; a `careManagerId` or a referenced `care_manager_id` yields `ERR_CARE_MANAGER_NOT_FOUND`; a `vendorId` or referenced `vendor_id` yields `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`; a `contactId` or a referenced `decided_by_contact_id`, `acknowledged_by_contact_id` or element of `invited_contact_ids` yields `ERR_CONTACT_NOT_FOUND`; an `assessmentId` or referenced `source_assessment_id` yields `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`; a `carePlanId` or a referenced `care_plan_id` or `supersedes_care_plan_id` yields `ERR_CARE_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`; an `authorizationId` yields `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_FOUND`; a `conferenceId` or referenced `family_conference_id` yields `ERR_CONFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`; a `decisionId` or referenced `authorizing_decision_id` yields `ERR_DECISION_NOT_FOUND`; an `episodeId` or referenced `crisis_episode_id` yields `ERR_CRISIS_EPISODE_NOT_FOUND`; a `timeEntryId` yields `ERR_TIME_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`; and an `invoiceId` yields `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`. All twelve are HTTP 404.

Existence is a weaker claim than parentage. Two records may both exist and still be an illegal pairing — a source assessment belonging to a different client, an authorizing decision reached at another family's conference, an attending contact who was never invited. Those failures are not 404s; they are reported as `ERR_ENTITY_RELATIONSHIP_INVALID` (HTTP 422) and are described alongside the workflows that create the pairings.

### 2.6. The Request Validation Ladder

Because many operations carry several independent guards, the order in which those guards run is part of the contract. Checks are evaluated in the ascending order given below and **the first failure wins** — a request that violates three rules reports only the earliest one.

1. Authentication: both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the five declared roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role admission: `X-User-Role` appears in the addressed operation's authorized-role list, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. Target-state ownership on multi-role operations, as enumerated in §2.1, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
4. Payload shape: the body parses, every required field is present, every type, enum value and numeric range is valid, `end_at` is strictly later than `start_at`, `coverage_end` is strictly later than `coverage_start`, and `limit` falls inside 1 through 100 — else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An absent or empty request body is read as an empty JSON object, so on an operation that requires fields the failure surfaces here as a missing required field; a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object is itself `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An optional field supplied as JSON `null` is treated exactly as if it had been omitted and selects the declared default, while a required field supplied as `null` is rejected here just as a wrongly typed value would be; an optional string supplied as the empty string is rejected as too short. A JSON boolean is never accepted where an integer is required, and no string is ever coerced to a number.
5. Existence of every addressed and referenced record, in the per-entity order given in §2.5, yielding the matching not-found error.
6. Ownership for entity-backed roles: the author-side ownership checks on assessments and time entries, the surrogate-status and self-identity checks on a family decision maker, and the supervisor resolution check — else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
7. Version pinning: the supplied expected parent version equals the stored one, else the stale-version error of §2.7.
8. State-machine reachability: the requested target state or action is reachable from the record's current state, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.
9. Client-status compatibility, care-plan and assessment status preconditions, and version-pinning of the source assessment — the `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_IN_SERVICE`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_ACCEPTED`, `ERR_PINNED_ASSESSMENT_STALE` and `ERR_CARE_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE` family, in that relative order.
10. Business-safety guards: vendor standing, conflict-of-interest acknowledgement, acknowledgement eligibility, capacity basis, surrogate authority, quorum, escalation preconditions, segregation of duties, and crisis context — in that relative order.
11. Referential-integrity guards across entities: `ERR_ENTITY_RELATIONSHIP_INVALID`.
12. Capacity and temporal guards: coverage capacity, time-entry overlap, travel buffer, and invoice-period overlap — in that relative order.
13. Fiscal guards: the daily billable cap, the empty-line-set check, payment overpayment, and retainer sufficiency — in that relative order.

A practical consequence worth stating plainly: on a service authorization being advanced to `AUTHORIZED`, the care-plan-not-active check precedes the vendor-standing check, which precedes the conflict-of-interest check, which precedes the coverage-capacity check, which precedes the retainer-sufficiency check. A request that is wrong in several of these ways reports the earliest one, so a barred vendor on an authorization whose plan is not `ACTIVE` reports `ERR_CARE_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`, not `ERR_VENDOR_BARRED`.

### 2.7. Version Pinning on Cross-Aggregate Writes

Two write operations reach across an aggregate boundary and therefore require the caller to prove that the parent record it reasoned about is still the parent record that exists. Both use the same mechanism: a mandatory request field carrying the parent's `version` as the client last observed it. Neither field is optional, and no other operation in this API accepts a version field of any kind.

Authoring a care plan — the care-plan creation operation, `POST /care/care-plans` — requires `expected_client_version` in the request body, carrying the client's `version` as the author last read it; it is a required integer of at least 1, so an absent, non-integer or zero-or-negative value fails at the payload-shape rung as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than reaching the version comparison at all. A care plan legitimately supersedes the client's prior `ACTIVE` plan when it activates, so the write is only safe while the author's view of the client — its `status`, its capacity determination, its `acuity_score` and its existing plan set — is still current. If `expected_client_version` does not equal the target client's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CLIENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412): a stale value means the author never saw the intervening capacity determination, assessment acceptance or plan activation that moved the client forward.

Proposing a service authorization — the authorization creation operation, `POST /care/service-authorizations` — requires `expected_care_plan_version` in the request body, carrying the care plan's `version` as the caller last read it; exactly like `expected_client_version` it is a required integer of at least 1, so an absent, non-integer or zero-or-negative value fails at the payload-shape rung as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than reaching the version comparison at all. A new authorization is reasoned about against a coverage picture — how much concurrent-caregiver capacity the client has left, whether the plan is still the live one — and every authorization transition on that plan advances the plan's `version`. If `expected_care_plan_version` does not equal the named care plan's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CARE_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412), because the caller's coverage view is no longer trustworthy: another authorization may already have consumed the capacity, or the plan may have been superseded.

### 2.8. Money, Minutes and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integer cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere in a request or a response, and no amount is ever stored or returned as a decimal string. Wherever a computation produces a fractional value, it is resolved by **rounding half up** — a value exactly at the half-cent rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude.

Where the rounding happens matters as much as how. Three disciplines are pinned and must not be interchanged. A time entry's billable amount is composed as a single product and rounded exactly once, on the fully composed product; rounding any intermediate factor — for instance, rounding the hourly-rate share before the after-hours factor is applied — produces a different and incorrect figure. An invoice's line amounts, by contrast, are already rounded per entry when the entry is created, and the invoice's line subtotal is the plain sum of those already-rounded line amounts: round per line, then sum. The invoice's administrative fee uses the opposite order: it is computed once on the summed subtotal and rounded once, and computing the fee per line and summing the roundings is explicitly wrong. A vendor authorization's coordination fee is likewise rounded once, on the fee itself.

Non-monetary quantities have their own precision rules. Elapsed minutes on a time entry are whole minutes with any partial minute discarded downward. Coverage hours on a service authorization are whole hours with any partial hour discarded downward. Overdue days on a family report are whole days with any partial day discarded downward. Acuity scores are integers between 0 and 100 inclusive, rounded half up on the final product only.

### 2.9. Derived Fields, Cascades and Reconciliation

A substantial part of this record is derived rather than declared, and derived fields are authoritative only as recomputations of their live input sets. The client's `risk_posture`, `acuity_score`, `retainer_deposits_total_cents`, `retainer_balance_cents`, `unbilled_accrual_cents`, `authorized_coverage_hours` and `protective_escalation_status` are all of this kind, as are a care plan's `coverage_gap_flag`, a family contact's `report_status` and `days_overdue`, a conference's `surrogate_present`, and every computed money field on a service authorization and an invoice. Each is recomputed after every mutation that can change its inputs, and a read of a record returns the recomputed value rather than a stale cache.

Cascades are ordered, and the order is observable. When an operation propagates — a discharge terminating authorizations and withdrawing plans, an issued invoice suspending a client and its live authorizations, a voided invoice releasing entries and reinstating the client, a guardianship adjudication reverting pending plans, a rescinded decision terminating the authorizations it justified — each hop happens because the previous hop happened, and each record the cascade touches has its own `version` increased by 1 and its own `updated_at` refreshed. A parent's `version` therefore advances not only when a caller edits it directly but whenever a child operation reaches back into it, which is precisely why the version-pinning rules of §2.7 exist.

Two quantities in this system are conserved, and reconciliation of both is a hard requirement rather than an aspiration. Money is conserved across the retainer ledger: the client's remaining retainer balance plus the total retainer drawn by invoices in `ISSUED` or `PAID` status always equals the total of every retainer deposit ever accepted, because deposits are append-only and are never deleted or reversed. Authorized caregiver-hours of coverage are conserved across authorization state changes: every suspend-then-reactivate lap and every terminate-then-re-authorize pair must leave the client's `authorized_coverage_hours` at exactly the value it held before the pair began. Likewise, a time entry's correction cycle — submitted, rejected, returned to draft, submitted again — must leave the client's `unbilled_accrual_cents` unchanged per lap.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties and Surrogate Authority

The practice's compliance posture rests on two people never being the same person. On the review of an assessment, and on the approval or rejection of a time entry, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the record's author — the assessment's `care_manager_id`, or the time entry's `care_manager_id`. When the acting user *is* the author, the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 422). This is distinct from, and evaluated later than, the ownership check that confirms an author-side transition is being driven by the author.

Surrogate authority is the family-side mirror of the same principle. A family contact carries `is_surrogate_decision_maker`, and only a contact for whom that flag is true may execute a decision on the client's behalf: approving or rejecting a care plan, being named as the decision maker on a documented conference decision, or acknowledging a vendor conflict-of-interest disclosure. Practice staff may never stand in for the family on any of those three. Where the client's own capacity is in question, the bar rises further, in the ways set out in the workflows that apply it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Client Intake, Capacity Determination and Risk Posture

A practice administrator opens the record by registering the older adult as a prospect. Registration requires the client's legal name in `full_name`, between 1 and 120 characters, and a `residence_setting` drawn from `PRIVATE_HOME`, `ASSISTED_LIVING`, `SKILLED_NURSING` and `HOSPITAL`. Four commercial parameters are optional and each has a practice default. `billing_increment_min` is the minute increment every logged hour is rounded up to; it defaults to 15 and must be either 6 or 15 — a tenth-of-an-hour practice and a quarter-hour practice being the only two conventions the firm supports. `travel_billing_pct` is the percentage of the care manager's hourly rate billed for travel, defaults to 50, and must be a whole number between 0 and 100 inclusive. `retainer_floor_cents` is the retainer balance below which the client is automatically suspended for nonpayment; it defaults to 50,000 cents — five hundred dollars — and may not be negative. `authorized_concurrent_caregivers` caps how many caregivers may be authorized across overlapping vendor coverage windows, defaults to 2, and must be at least 1, with no upper bound.

A freshly registered client is in `PROSPECT` status with `capacity_status` at `PRESUMED_CAPABLE`, `capacity_basis` null, `risk_posture` at `ROUTINE`, `acuity_score` at 0, `protective_escalation_status` at `NONE`, and every ledger quantity — `retainer_deposits_total_cents`, `retainer_balance_cents`, `unbilled_accrual_cents` and `authorized_coverage_hours` — at 0.

The client's own status is driven almost entirely by events happening elsewhere in the record. The first assessment opened against a `PROSPECT` advances the client to `ASSESSMENT_IN_PROGRESS`, bumping the client's `version` and refreshing `updated_at`. When a care plan for that client activates, an `ASSESSMENT_IN_PROGRESS` client advances to `ACTIVE_CARE`, its `version` increases and its `risk_posture` is recomputed. Issuing an invoice can push an `ACTIVE_CARE` client to `SUSPENDED_NONPAYMENT`, and restoring the retainer brings it back — a cycle that may repeat any number of times. Only discharge is requested directly, by a practice administrator or a practice supervisor supplying a `to_state` of `DISCHARGED` and an optional `reason`; the same request shape moves a `SUSPENDED_NONPAYMENT` client back to `ACTIVE_CARE` once the recomputed retainer balance has reached the floor again. `to_state` is mandatory and must name one of the five declared client statuses, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; `PROSPECT`, `ASSESSMENT_IN_PROGRESS` and `SUSPENDED_NONPAYMENT` are reachable only as consequences of events elsewhere and requesting any of them here is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. An `ACTIVE_CARE` request against a client that is not `SUSPENDED_NONPAYMENT`, or whose recomputed `retainer_balance_cents` is still short of its `retainer_floor_cents`, is likewise rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — the shortfall is a reachability failure here, not `ERR_RETAINER_INSUFFICIENT`. A successful restore also runs the same authorization-reinstatement cascade a retainer deposit runs, described in §3.9. Either accepted outcome increases the client's `version` by 1 and returns the updated client.

Discharge is available from `PROSPECT`, `ASSESSMENT_IN_PROGRESS`, `ACTIVE_CARE` and `SUSPENDED_NONPAYMENT`, and requires only that the client is not already `DISCHARGED` — a second discharge attempt on an already-discharged client is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, since `DISCHARGED` is terminal. From `ASSESSMENT_IN_PROGRESS`, `ACTIVE_CARE` and `SUSPENDED_NONPAYMENT` there is one further guard: no crisis episode of that client may be in `OPEN` or `TRANSITION_OVERSIGHT` status, because the practice does not close a file while it is still managing an active crisis. Discharge then cascades: every service authorization of that client whose `auth_status` is `PROPOSED`, `AUTHORIZED`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `TERMINATED` with `termination_reason` set to `CLIENT_DISCHARGED`; every care plan of that client whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `PENDING_FAMILY_APPROVAL` or `ACTIVE` moves to `WITHDRAWN`; the client's `authorized_coverage_hours` is recomputed to 0; and the client's `version` increases by 1.

Capacity is recorded deliberately and separately. A care manager or practice supervisor submits a capacity determination carrying the new `capacity_status` — one of `PRESUMED_CAPABLE`, `DIMINISHED_DOCUMENTED` or `ADJUDICATED_INCAPACITATED` — together with a free-text `basis` recording the evidence relied upon; `basis` is required on every determination, including one that records `PRESUMED_CAPABLE`, where the supplied text is discarded rather than stored. The basis is stored on the client as `capacity_basis`, the determination is stored as `capacity_status`, the client's `risk_posture` is recomputed, and the client's `version` increases by 1. A determination is accepted whatever the client's `status`, `DISCHARGED` included, and re-recording the status the client already carries is accepted too. The response is the updated client. These are the only two ways `capacity_status` is ever written: an explicit capacity determination, or an accepted guardianship escalation. While `capacity_status` remains `PRESUMED_CAPABLE`, `capacity_basis` is null.

The client's `risk_posture` is the practice's triage signal, and it is derived rather than set. Evaluation takes the **first matching branch**. If the client has any crisis episode whose status is `OPEN` or `TRANSITION_OVERSIGHT`, the posture is `CRISIS`. Otherwise the posture is `ELEVATED` if any one of four conditions holds: the client's `acuity_score` is at least 70 — a score of exactly 70 qualifies; or the client's `capacity_status` is `ADJUDICATED_INCAPACITATED`; or the client has at least one service authorization whose `coi_status` is `PENDING_DISCLOSURE` or `DISCLOSED`, meaning an undischarged conflict-of-interest obligation is outstanding; or the client has at least one care plan in `ACTIVE` status whose `coverage_gap_flag` is true, meaning an activated plan is standing with nobody authorized to deliver it. Failing all of those, the posture is `ROUTINE`. The posture is recomputed after every crisis-episode transition, every assessment acceptance, every capacity determination, every conflict-of-interest acknowledgement, and every authorization transition that can move a care plan's `coverage_gap_flag`.

### 3.2. Registering the People Around the Client: Staff, Providers and Family

Care managers are registered by the practice administrator with a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `credential` drawn from `CMC`, `CCM`, `CMAC` and `C_ASWCM`, and an `hourly_rate_cents` that must be greater than zero — this rate is the basis of every billable minute that care manager logs. Two flags default to sensible values: `is_supervisor` defaults to false and, when true, is what entitles that care manager's `id` to present itself with the supervisor role; `active` defaults to true, and registering a care manager with it false records that the care manager is retired from new assignments — it is a recorded attribute only, set once at registration, since no operation in this API updates a care manager after it is created, and no guard anywhere reads it.

Vendors are the coordinated providers across the four verticals the practice works in. Registration takes a `name` of 1 to 160 characters, a `vendor_type` of `HOME_CARE`, `MEDICAL`, `LEGAL` or `HOUSING`, and the disclosure-critical `has_referral_relationship` flag, which is true whenever the practice receives any consideration from — or shares ownership with — that provider. When `has_referral_relationship` is true, the accompanying `referral_fee_pct`, the percentage of the vendor's billings the practice receives, must be greater than zero; a referral relationship declared with a zero or absent fee is a contradictory payload and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Otherwise `referral_fee_pct` defaults to 0, and in all cases it must be a number between 0 and 100 inclusive — unlike the other percentages in this system it may be fractional, and it is echoed back exactly as it was supplied. A vendor's `standing` defaults to `APPROVED` and may also be `PROBATION` or `BARRED`.

Barring a vendor is a compliance action with teeth. The practice administrator may update a vendor's `standing`, its `referral_fee_pct`, or both; a payload carrying neither is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and the vendor's `version` increases by 1 on every accepted update even when the values written match the values already stored. There is no state machine over `standing`: it moves freely between `APPROVED`, `PROBATION` and `BARRED`, and lifting a bar reinstates nothing — authorizations the bar suspended or terminated stay exactly where the cascade left them. When the new standing is `BARRED`, every service authorization naming that vendor whose `auth_status` is `ACTIVE` moves to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `VENDOR_STANDING`, and every authorization of that vendor whose `auth_status` is `PROPOSED` or `AUTHORIZED` moves straight to `TERMINATED` with `termination_reason` set to `VENDOR_STANDING`. Each affected client's `authorized_coverage_hours` and `risk_posture` are then recomputed, and the vendor's own `version` increases by 1.

Family contacts are attached to a client by a practice administrator or a care manager. The payload names the `client_id` the contact belongs to, the person's `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, and a `contact_role` drawn from `POA_HEALTHCARE`, `POA_FINANCIAL`, `GUARDIAN`, `RESPONSIBLE_PARTY` and `INTERESTED_FAMILY`. Three optional attributes shape what the contact may do and what the practice owes them: `is_surrogate_decision_maker` defaults to false and, when true, marks the contact as able to execute decisions on the client's behalf; `is_long_distance` defaults to false and, when true, marks a contact living outside the client's metro area who is owed the cadence report; and `reporting_cadence_days`, the number of days between required long-distance reports, defaults to 30 and must fall between 1 and 180 inclusive. A new contact starts with `last_report_sent_at` null and `reports_sent_count` at 0.

Reporting cadence is evaluated on every read of a contact and after every accepted report, and it is where the practice's long-distance-family obligation becomes measurable. A contact who is not long-distance has `report_status` of `NOT_APPLICABLE` and `days_overdue` of 0, permanently. For a long-distance contact, the cadence anchor is `last_report_sent_at` when a report has already been sent, and the contact's own `created_at` before the first one. The report becomes due `reporting_cadence_days` days after the anchor, and the practice then has a grace period of one full week — 7 days — beyond that due instant. While the logical clock is strictly earlier than the due instant, `report_status` is `CURRENT` and `days_overdue` is 0. From the due instant up to but not including the end of grace, `report_status` is `DUE` and `days_overdue` is still 0 — the boundary is inclusive of `DUE`, so a clock reading exactly the due instant is `DUE`, not `CURRENT`. At the end of the grace period and thereafter, `report_status` is `OVERDUE` — again inclusive, so a clock reading exactly the end of grace is `OVERDUE` — and `days_overdue` is the number of whole days elapsed since the end of grace, with any partial day discarded downward.

Sending the report is a care manager's action. The payload carries `report_period_start`, `report_period_end` and a `summary` of what the family is being told; all three are required non-empty strings, are validated for nothing beyond that — no calendar-date shape is imposed and no ordering between the two period bounds is checked — and none of the three is persisted on the contact. A report may be sent to any family contact whatever its client's status, and a contact whose `is_long_distance` is false still has `last_report_sent_at` stamped and `reports_sent_count` incremented while its `report_status` stays `NOT_APPLICABLE` and its `days_overdue` stays 0. On acceptance, `last_report_sent_at` is set to the logical clock's current instant, `reports_sent_count` increases by 1, `report_status` and `days_overdue` are recomputed against the new anchor, and the contact's `version` increases by 1. The updated contact is returned.

### 3.3. The Comprehensive Geriatric Assessment and Its Supervisory Review

Assessment is where the practice forms its clinical opinion, and the opinion is scored so that it can be compared across a caseload. A care manager opens an assessment by naming the `client_id` under assessment and the `care_manager_id` authoring it — the authorship claim is taken exactly as supplied and need not equal the acting `X-User-ID`, even though every later author-side transition compares the two — and by supplying `domain_scores`: an object carrying exactly six integer keys — `adl`, `cognition`, `medical_complexity`, `social_support`, `safety` and `finances` — each valued between 0 and 5 inclusive. A missing key, an extra key, a non-integer value or a value outside that range is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An optional `narrative` records the prose. The new assessment is `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0, `screening_acuity` and `comprehensive_acuity` both null, `reviewed_by_care_manager_id` null and `accepted_at` null. If this is the client's first assessment and the client is a `PROSPECT`, the client advances to `ASSESSMENT_IN_PROGRESS` and the client's `version` increases by 1; on a second or later assessment, or against a client in any other status, the client record is not touched at all and its `version` does not move. An assessment may not be opened against a `DISCHARGED` client; that attempt is rejected with `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_IN_SERVICE`.

While the assessment is still a draft, its author may correct it. The edit payload must carry at least one of `domain_scores` and `narrative`, and one carrying neither is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; `domain_scores` supplied as JSON `null` reads as absent, whereas `narrative` supplied as `null` counts as a supplied correction and clears the stored narrative. A `domain_scores` edit is permitted only while `status` is `DRAFT`; attempting one on an assessment in any other status is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. A `narrative`-only correction is permitted while `status` is `DRAFT` or `ACCEPTED` — the practice must be able to fix a typo in an accepted report — and such a correction never re-derives `screening_acuity` or `comprehensive_acuity` and never changes `status`. Either edit increases the assessment's `version` by 1, and that increment is precisely what can render a care plan's pinned assessment version stale. Only the author may edit: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the assessment's `care_manager_id`.

The review cycle is driven by a `to_state` and an optional `review_note`. The author submits the draft, moving `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`; this requires that the acting user is the author and that the client is not `DISCHARGED`, and it stamps `screening_acuity` and increases the assessment's `version`. A practice supervisor then forks the assessment. Both fork branches require that the assessment is `SUBMITTED`, that the acting user resolves to a care manager whose `is_supervisor` is true, and that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the assessment's `care_manager_id` — a supervisor reviewing their own authored assessment is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The two branches then diverge sharply in what they write.

`SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED` is the branch that changes the client. It stamps `comprehensive_acuity`, sets `accepted_at` to the logical clock's current instant, records the reviewing supervisor in `reviewed_by_care_manager_id`, recomputes the client's `acuity_score` from that client's most recently accepted assessment, recomputes the client's `risk_posture` — which crosses into `ELEVATED` once `acuity_score` reaches 70 — and increases both the assessment's `version` and the client's `version` by 1. `ACCEPTED` is terminal. `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED` is the deliberately narrow branch: it records `reviewed_by_care_manager_id` and increases the assessment's `version` by 1, and it writes no acuity value at all, leaves `comprehensive_acuity` null, and touches neither the client's `acuity_score` nor its `risk_posture` nor its `version`.

From `REVISION_REQUESTED` the author takes the assessment back to `DRAFT`. That transition increases `revision_round` by 1, resets `screening_acuity` to null, and increases the assessment's `version` by 1. The loop from `DRAFT` through `SUBMITTED` to `REVISION_REQUESTED` and back to `DRAFT` is unbounded — round 0 is the first draft — and every lap bumps the assessment's `version`, which is the mechanism by which a revision cycle invalidates a care plan authored against an earlier reading of that assessment.

Withdrawal retires an assessment that will never be accepted. It is available while `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, and requires that no care plan naming this assessment as its `source_assessment_id` is in `PENDING_FAMILY_APPROVAL` or `ACTIVE` status, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — the practice may not pull the clinical basis out from under a plan the family is deciding on or living under. Because which role owns a withdrawal depends on the status the assessment is currently in, that ownership check is evaluated only once the assessment has been resolved rather than at rung 3 of §2.6, so a withdrawal aimed at an identifier matching no assessment reports `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND` whichever of the two roles asked. Withdrawal increases the assessment's `version` by 1, and `WITHDRAWN` is terminal.

Two acuity figures are computed from the same six domain scores by two deliberately different methods, and the difference between them is the point. The **screening** figure, stamped when the assessment is submitted, is the unweighted arithmetic mean of the six domain values — every domain counting equally — scaled to the 0-to-100 range: add `adl`, `cognition`, `medical_complexity`, `social_support`, `safety` and `finances`, divide the sum by 6, multiply by 20, and round the result half up to a whole number. The **comprehensive** figure, stamped when the assessment is accepted, is a priority-weighted sum instead: multiply `safety` by 0.30, `cognition` by 0.25, `adl` by 0.20, `medical_complexity` by 0.15, `social_support` by 0.05 and `finances` by 0.05, add those six products together, multiply the sum by 20, and round half up to a whole number. Safety and cognition together carry 55 percent of the weighted score, so a client whose risk is concentrated in those two domains scores materially higher on the comprehensive pass than the screening pass suggested on identical `domain_scores`. Both figures are integers between 0 and 100 inclusive, and in both cases the rounding is applied only to the final product.

The client's `acuity_score` is a roll-up with no ambiguity in its selector. The candidate set is that client's assessments whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`; candidates are ranked by `accepted_at` descending, ties are broken by `created_at` ascending — never by `id` — and the client's `acuity_score` is the `comprehensive_acuity` of the top-ranked candidate. When the candidate set is empty, the client's `acuity_score` is 0.

### 3.4. Authoring the Care Plan and Winning Family Approval

A care plan converts an accepted assessment into a commitment the family signs off on. The author names the `client_id`, the `source_assessment_id` the plan is written from, and `expected_client_version` as described in §2.7; supplies `goals` as an ordered array of between 1 and 12 non-empty strings; and may supply `review_interval_days`, the number of days between scheduled plan reviews, which defaults to 90 and must fall between 30 and 365 inclusive. When the plan is intended to replace a plan the client is already living under, the predecessor is named in `supersedes_care_plan_id`; a first plan leaves it null. The source assessment must be `ACCEPTED` — a plan authored from a draft, submitted, revision-requested or withdrawn assessment is rejected with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_ACCEPTED` — and both the source assessment and any named predecessor plan must belong to the same client as `client_id`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_ENTITY_RELATIONSHIP_INVALID`.

The new plan is created in `DRAFT` with `approved_by_contact_id` null, `activated_at` null, `rejection_rounds` at 0, and `coverage_gap_flag` true, and the client's `version` increases by 1. Crucially, the plan also captures `pinned_assessment_version`: the value of the source assessment's `version` at the instant the plan was created. That pin is the plan's claim about which clinical reading it encodes.

Sending a plan for family approval moves it from `DRAFT` to `PENDING_FAMILY_APPROVAL`, and is also the route out of a rejection — the transition is accepted while the plan's `status` is either `DRAFT` or `REJECTED`. Four further conditions must hold. The source assessment's `status` must still be `ACCEPTED`, else `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_ACCEPTED`. The plan's `pinned_assessment_version` must still equal the source assessment's current `version`; if the assessment has been edited or has gone around a revision lap since the plan was authored, the plan is out of date and the request is rejected with `ERR_PINNED_ASSESSMENT_STALE`. The client must not be `DISCHARGED`. And the client must have at least one family contact whose `is_surrogate_decision_maker` is true, because otherwise there is nobody with authority to answer, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_SURROGATE_AUTHORITY_MISSING`. The four are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.6 rather than the order just narrated: the client's discharge first, then the source assessment's status, then the pin, then the surrogate. The transition increases the plan's `version` by 1.

`PENDING_FAMILY_APPROVAL` forks, and only a surrogate family decision maker may drive either branch: the acting `X-User-ID` must be a family contact of the plan's client whose `is_surrogate_decision_maker` is true. Both branches accept an optional `decision_note`.

Approval — `PENDING_FAMILY_APPROVAL → ACTIVE` — carries two further preconditions. The client's `risk_posture` must not be `CRISIS`: a plan may not be activated while an episode is `OPEN` or under `TRANSITION_OVERSIGHT`, because the practice is not in a position to make forward commitments mid-crisis; a crisis posture is a reachability failure here and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. And when the client's `capacity_status` is anything other than `PRESUMED_CAPABLE`, the client's own `capacity_basis` must be non-null — the practice must have documented why it is relying on a surrogate at all — else `ERR_CAPACITY_BASIS_REQUIRED`. Nothing else about the client is guarded here: activation carries no client-status precondition of its own, so a client sitting in `SUSPENDED_NONPAYMENT` may still have a plan activated and keeps that status. Activation then does a great deal: it records the approving surrogate in `approved_by_contact_id`, sets `activated_at` to the logical clock's current instant, and recomputes `coverage_gap_flag`, which is true for a freshly activated plan because no authorization has yet been raised under it. If `supersedes_care_plan_id` is non-null, the named predecessor moves from `ACTIVE` to `SUPERSEDED`, and each of that predecessor's service authorizations whose `auth_status` is `PROPOSED`, `AUTHORIZED`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `TERMINATED` with `termination_reason` set to `PLAN_SUPERSEDED`; a named predecessor that is no longer `ACTIVE` is left exactly as it stands, cascading nothing, and the activation still succeeds. An `ASSESSMENT_IN_PROGRESS` client advances to `ACTIVE_CARE`. The client's `risk_posture` and `authorized_coverage_hours` are recomputed, and both the plan's `version` and the client's `version` increase by 1.

Rejection — `PENDING_FAMILY_APPROVAL → REJECTED` — is deliberately austere by comparison: it increases `rejection_rounds` by 1 and increases the plan's `version` by 1, and it does nothing else. No predecessor is superseded, `approved_by_contact_id` stays null, `activated_at` stays null, and the client's status, posture and coverage hours are untouched. A care manager then returns the rejected plan to `DRAFT` for re-authoring, which increases the plan's `version` by 1, and the plan may be sent for approval again — the rejection loop is unbounded and `rejection_rounds` records the laps.

There is one further, system-only route out of `PENDING_FAMILY_APPROVAL`, and it is neither approval nor rejection. When a guardianship petition on that client is accepted, the surrogate's pending approval authority has been superseded by a court, and each of that client's plans sitting in `PENDING_FAMILY_APPROVAL` falls back to `DRAFT` for re-authoring rather than to `REJECTED`. This edge is a cascade only: no actor may request it directly, `approved_by_contact_id` stays null, `rejection_rounds` is left unchanged because an adjudication is not a family rejection, and the plan's `version` increases by 1.

Two terminal outcomes complete the plan machine. An `ACTIVE` plan moves to `SUPERSEDED` when a newer plan naming it in `supersedes_care_plan_id` activates, which terminates the superseded plan's authorizations as described above, recomputes the client's `authorized_coverage_hours`, and increases the superseded plan's `version` by 1. And a plan in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_FAMILY_APPROVAL` or `ACTIVE` moves to `WITHDRAWN` when its client is discharged, increasing the plan's `version` by 1. Both `SUPERSEDED` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal, and neither may be requested on the state operation: a caller naming either as `to_state` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` once the plan itself has been resolved. `PracticeSupervisor` is admitted to the plan-state operation but owns none of its four requestable outcomes, so a supervisor naming `PENDING_FAMILY_APPROVAL`, `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `REJECTED` is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

A plan's `coverage_gap_flag` is derived, not declared: it is true whenever the plan has zero service authorizations whose `auth_status` is `AUTHORIZED` or `ACTIVE`, and the plan's own `status` does not enter that test at all — which is why a freshly created `DRAFT` reads true, and why a `REJECTED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `WITHDRAWN` plan with no live authorization beneath it also reads true. It is recomputed on every read of the plan and after every authorization transition beneath it. The plan's `status` enters only one rung higher, in the risk-posture trigger of §3.1, which counts a flagged plan only while that plan is `ACTIVE`; an active plan whose flag is true is one of the four `ELEVATED` triggers on the client's risk posture.

### 3.5. Facilitating Family Conferences and Documenting Decisions

A family conference is the practice's forum for decisions that no single person may make alone. A care manager schedules one against a `client_id`, supplying `scheduled_start` and a `scheduled_end` that must be strictly later than the start, and an `invited_contact_ids` array of between 1 and 12 unique family-contact identifiers, every one of which must be a family contact of that same client — an invitation list reaching into another family is rejected with `ERR_ENTITY_RELATIONSHIP_INVALID`. An optional `agenda` records what will be discussed. The conference is created in `SCHEDULED` status with `attended_contact_ids` empty, `quorum_met` false, `surrogate_present` false, `reschedule_count` at 0 and `convened_at` null. A conference may be scheduled whatever the client's `status`, `DISCHARGED` included, and scheduling one leaves the client record untouched.

Conference state is driven by a `to_state` together with whichever of `attended_contact_ids`, `rescheduled_start`, `rescheduled_end` and `reason` the requested outcome needs. Convening the conference — `SCHEDULED → CONVENED` — carries `attended_contact_ids`, supplied as between 1 and 12 unique identifiers — a duplicated element being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — which must form a non-empty subset of `invited_contact_ids`; an attendee who was never invited is rejected with `ERR_ENTITY_RELATIONSHIP_INVALID`. Quorum must then hold, else the request is rejected with `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`; notwithstanding the relative order of §2.6, on this transition the subset test runs first, so an attendance list that both reaches into another family and fails quorum reports `ERR_ENTITY_RELATIONSHIP_INVALID`. On success the attendance list is stored, `quorum_met` is set to true, `surrogate_present` is recomputed, `convened_at` is set to the logical clock's current instant, and the conference's `version` increases by 1. `quorum_met` is latched by that write rather than re-evaluated on later reads — a capacity determination recorded after the conference convened does not retroactively unseat it — while `surrogate_present` is recomputed from the stored attendance list on every read and therefore reads false for any conference that has never convened.

Quorum is evaluated as an exact integer comparison, deliberately avoiding any division or floating-point rounding. Count the distinct invited contacts and the distinct attending contacts supplied on the transition; the attendance half of the test passes when twice the attending count is greater than or equal to the invited count — so attendance of exactly half the invitation list passes. `surrogate_present` is true when at least one attending contact has `is_surrogate_decision_maker` set to true, and quorum always requires it. When the client's `capacity_status` is `DIMINISHED_DOCUMENTED` or `ADJUDICATED_INCAPACITATED`, one additional requirement applies: at least one attending contact must hold a `contact_role` of either `POA_HEALTHCARE` or `GUARDIAN`. Where the client is `PRESUMED_CAPABLE`, quorum is exactly the attendance-half test conjoined with surrogate presence.

Documenting the conference — `CONVENED → DOCUMENTED` — requires that at least one conference decision belonging to that conference exists whose `status` is not `RESCINDED`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; a conference with nothing surviving to record cannot be closed out. The transition increases the conference's `version` by 1, and `DOCUMENTED` is terminal. Rescheduling — `SCHEDULED → RESCHEDULED` — requires both `rescheduled_start` and `rescheduled_end`, with the end strictly later than the start; it overwrites `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end` with the supplied instants, increases `reschedule_count` by 1, and increases the conference's `version` by 1. A `RESCHEDULED` conference returns to `SCHEDULED` on request, increasing the conference's `version` by 1; the loop between the two is unbounded and `reschedule_count` records the laps. Cancellation, which moves the conference to `CANCELLED`, is available while the conference is `SCHEDULED` or `RESCHEDULED`, increases the conference's `version` by 1, and is terminal — a second cancellation attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Decisions are documented individually against a conference that has reached quorum. A care manager supplies the `family_conference_id`, the `decided_by_contact_id` of the family member who made the call, a `decision_type` drawn from `VENDOR_ENGAGEMENT`, `PLACEMENT_CHANGE`, `CARE_LEVEL_CHANGE`, `ESCALATE_GUARDIANSHIP_PETITION` and `ESCALATE_APS_REPORT`, and the verbatim `decision_text` of between 1 and 2,000 characters. The deciding contact must belong to the conference's client, else `ERR_ENTITY_RELATIONSHIP_INVALID`. The decision is created in `RECORDED` status with `ratified_at`, `escalated_at` and `rescinded_reason` all null, and the parent conference's `version` increases by 1. A decision may not be documented against a conference whose `quorum_met` is false; that attempt is rejected with `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`. The guard reads `quorum_met` alone and never the conference's `status`, so a conference that has already moved on to `DOCUMENTED` still accepts further decisions, and no client-status guard applies to decision documentation at all.

Two capacity-driven guards sit on decision documentation, and they fail differently. First, `capacity_basis` — the evidentiary basis for relying on a surrogate at all — is a request field of this operation, stored on the decision itself and echoed on every read of it, and the guard reads that supplied field rather than the client's own `capacity_basis`; documenting a decision never writes the client's `capacity_basis`. It is optional in general but mandatory whenever the client's `capacity_status` is anything other than `PRESUMED_CAPABLE`; a decision documented for a `DIMINISHED_DOCUMENTED` or `ADJUDICATED_INCAPACITATED` client without it is rejected with `ERR_CAPACITY_BASIS_REQUIRED`. Second, the person named in `decided_by_contact_id` must have `is_surrogate_decision_maker` set to true, and when the client's `capacity_status` is `ADJUDICATED_INCAPACITATED` that person's `contact_role` must additionally be `POA_HEALTHCARE` or `GUARDIAN`; a decision maker holding no adequate surrogate authority is rejected with `ERR_SURROGATE_AUTHORITY_MISSING`. The missing-basis check is evaluated before the surrogate-authority check.

Only a practice supervisor moves a decision through its lifecycle, supplying a `to_state` and an optional `note`; `RECORDED` is a creation status only, and naming it as `to_state` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Ratification — `RECORDED → RATIFIED` — requires that the parent conference's `status` is `CONVENED` or `DOCUMENTED`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and that its `quorum_met` is true, else `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`; it sets `ratified_at` to the logical clock's current instant and increases the decision's `version` by 1. Ratification matters beyond the conference record: only a `RATIFIED` decision may be named as the authorizing decision on a vendor engagement.

Escalation is the practice's route to guardianship or adult protective services, and it is gated tightly. `RATIFIED → ESCALATED` requires that the decision's `decision_type` is `ESCALATE_GUARDIANSHIP_PETITION` or `ESCALATE_APS_REPORT`, that the client's `capacity_status` is `DIMINISHED_DOCUMENTED` or `ADJUDICATED_INCAPACITATED`, and that the parent conference's `status` is `DOCUMENTED` — the practice does not petition a court on the strength of a conference it has not finished writing up. Any of those three unmet yields `ERR_ESCALATION_PRECONDITION_UNMET`. On success, `escalated_at` is set to the logical clock's current instant, the client's `protective_escalation_status` is recomputed to `PENDING`, the client's `risk_posture` is recomputed, and both the decision's `version` and the client's `version` increase by 1.

From `ESCALATED` the outcome forks. `ESCALATED → ESCALATION_DECLINED` recomputes the client's `protective_escalation_status` to `DECLINED` and increases both the decision's `version` and the client's `version` by 1; nothing else moves. `ESCALATED → ESCALATION_ACCEPTED` recomputes `protective_escalation_status` to `ACCEPTED` and then, when and only when the `decision_type` is `ESCALATE_GUARDIANSHIP_PETITION`, sets off a chain. The client's `capacity_status` becomes `ADJUDICATED_INCAPACITATED` and the client's `capacity_basis` is written as the literal text `Guardianship petition granted; decision ` followed by the decision's `id`. Because capacity has been adjudicated, every care plan of that client sitting in `PENDING_FAMILY_APPROVAL` reverts to `DRAFT`. Because those plans reverted, the client's `risk_posture` is recomputed — the adjudicated capacity landing it on `ELEVATED` unless a live crisis episode outranks that branch, in which case it reads `CRISIS`. Both the decision's `version` and the client's `version` increase by 1. All three of `ESCALATION_ACCEPTED`, `ESCALATION_DECLINED` and `RESCINDED` are terminal.

Rescission is the compensating action on a decision, available while the decision's `status` is `RECORDED` or `RATIFIED`. The supplied `note` is stored as `rescinded_reason`. Rescission then unwinds what the decision authorized: every service authorization whose `authorizing_decision_id` names this decision and whose `auth_status` is `PROPOSED`, `AUTHORIZED`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `TERMINATED` with `termination_reason` set to `DECISION_RESCINDED`. Because those authorizations left the authorized set, the client's `authorized_coverage_hours` is recomputed downward and each affected care plan's `coverage_gap_flag` is recomputed. Because a `coverage_gap_flag` may have flipped from false to true on a plan that is still `ACTIVE`, the client's `risk_posture` is recomputed — landing on `ELEVATED` when such a flip happened, and otherwise on whatever the first matching branch of §3.1 yields. Both the decision's `version` and the client's `version` increase by 1.

The client's `protective_escalation_status` is itself a projection over that client's escalation-typed decisions, and its selector is pinned. The candidate set is the decisions whose parent conference belongs to this client, whose `decision_type` is `ESCALATE_GUARDIANSHIP_PETITION` or `ESCALATE_APS_REPORT`, and whose `status` is `ESCALATED`, `ESCALATION_ACCEPTED` or `ESCALATION_DECLINED` — decisions still `RECORDED` or `RATIFIED`, and decisions that were `RESCINDED`, are excluded. Candidates are ranked by `escalated_at` descending with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, never by `id`. The top-ranked candidate's status determines the projection: `ESCALATED` projects to `PENDING`, `ESCALATION_ACCEPTED` projects to `ACCEPTED`, and `ESCALATION_DECLINED` projects to `DECLINED`. An empty candidate set projects to `NONE`.

### 3.6. Vendor Engagement: Two Independent Dimensions on One Authorization

A service authorization is how the practice commits a coordinated provider to a client's care, and it carries **two independent state dimensions** that advance on their own schedules. `auth_status` tracks the commercial and clinical life of the engagement through `PROPOSED`, `AUTHORIZED`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` and `TERMINATED`. `coi_status` tracks the conflict-of-interest obligation through `NOT_REQUIRED`, `PENDING_DISCLOSURE`, `DISCLOSED` and `ACKNOWLEDGED`. The two do not gate each other except at one point: a `PROPOSED` authorization can reach `ACKNOWLEDGED` on the disclosure dimension while sitting still on the commercial one, and an `AUTHORIZED` authorization can perfectly well remain at `NOT_REQUIRED`; only the step from `PROPOSED` to `AUTHORIZED` reads the disclosure dimension at all.

A care manager proposes an engagement by naming the `care_plan_id` it belongs under, the `vendor_id` being engaged, and `expected_care_plan_version` as described in §2.7, and by describing the coverage being bought: `coverage_start` and a `coverage_end` that must be strictly later than the start, `concurrent_caregivers` between 1 and 10 inclusive being the number of caregivers the vendor staffs simultaneously across that window, and the vendor's `hourly_cost_cents` per caregiver, which must be greater than zero. When a ratified family decision authorized this vendor, that decision is named in the optional `authorizing_decision_id`; it must be a conference decision whose `status` is `RATIFIED` and whose conference belongs to the care plan's client, else `ERR_ENTITY_RELATIONSHIP_INVALID`. Coverage windows are **closed intervals**: both `coverage_start` and `coverage_end` are inclusive, so two windows that merely touch at an endpoint are treated as overlapping.

The parent care plan must be `ACTIVE` at proposal time, else `ERR_CARE_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`, and the named vendor's `standing` must not be `BARRED`, else `ERR_VENDOR_BARRED`. No client-status precondition applies at proposal: the operation never reads the client's `status` at all, so an engagement may be proposed under an `ACTIVE` plan whose client is sitting in `SUSPENDED_NONPAYMENT`. On acceptance the authorization is created with `auth_status` of `PROPOSED`, `suspension_reason` of `NONE` and `termination_reason` of `NONE`, and its `client_id` is copied from the care plan's client and thereafter never edited. The disclosure dimension is initialised from the vendor: when the vendor's `has_referral_relationship` is true, `coi_status` is set to `PENDING_DISCLOSURE` and `disclosure_text` is minted at creation as the fixed statement `The practice has a referral relationship with {vendor name} and may receive consideration of {referral_fee_pct} percent of this vendor's billings. This disclosure requires acknowledgement by a surrogate family decision maker.`, with the vendor's `name` and its `referral_fee_pct` — rendered exactly as the vendor stores it — substituted for the two placeholders; when it is false, `coi_status` is set to `NOT_REQUIRED` and `disclosure_text` stays null. Because a pending disclosure is one of the `ELEVATED` triggers, the client's `risk_posture` is recomputed; the parent care plan's `version` and the client's `version` each increase by 1.

Costing is stamped once, at creation, and the evaluation order is pinned because the dependency would otherwise be circular. First, `coverage_hours` is the number of whole hours between `coverage_start` and `coverage_end`, with any partial hour discarded downward, multiplied by `concurrent_caregivers`. Second, `base_cost_cents` is `coverage_hours` multiplied by `hourly_cost_cents`. Third, the practice's coordination-fee tier is selected **from `base_cost_cents`, never from the projected total**: a base cost below 200,000 cents — two thousand dollars — takes an 8 percent fee; a base cost of 200,000 cents or more but below 500,000 cents — five thousand dollars — takes 6 percent; a base cost of 500,000 cents or more takes 4 percent. Fourth, `coordination_fee_cents` is `base_cost_cents` multiplied by the selected percentage and divided by 100, rounded half up once. Fifth, `total_projected_cost_cents` is `base_cost_cents` plus `coordination_fee_cents`. Selecting the tier from the total would be circular — the total depends on the fee, the fee depends on the tier — and is wrong: a base cost of 190,000 cents sits in the first tier and takes an 8 percent fee of 15,200 cents even though the resulting total of 205,200 cents would, read as a base, have fallen in the second tier.

Authorizing the engagement — `PROPOSED → AUTHORIZED` — is a practice supervisor's act and is the most heavily guarded transition in the system. It requires, in this order: the parent care plan is still `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_CARE_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`; the client's `status` is neither `SUSPENDED_NONPAYMENT` nor `DISCHARGED`, else `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_IN_SERVICE`; the vendor's `standing` is not `BARRED`, else `ERR_VENDOR_BARRED`; the authorization's `coi_status` is either `NOT_REQUIRED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`, else `ERR_COI_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`; aggregate coverage capacity holds, else `ERR_COVERAGE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; and the client's `retainer_balance_cents` is at least the client's `retainer_floor_cents`, else `ERR_RETAINER_INSUFFICIENT` — the one payment-required rejection in the whole API. On success the authorization's `coverage_hours` is added to the client's `authorized_coverage_hours`, the parent plan's `coverage_gap_flag` is recomputed and becomes false, the client's `risk_posture` is recomputed, and the authorization's `version`, the care plan's `version` and the client's `version` each increase by 1.

Aggregate coverage capacity is the practice's guarantee that it never authorizes more caregivers into a client's home at once than the family has agreed to. The test guards every transition whose target is `AUTHORIZED` and every transition whose target is `ACTIVE`. For the candidate authorization's coverage window, sum `concurrent_caregivers` across that client's *other* authorizations whose `auth_status` is `AUTHORIZED` or `ACTIVE` and whose closed coverage interval intersects the candidate's closed interval — touching endpoints count as intersecting — then add the candidate's own `concurrent_caregivers`. That total must be less than or equal to the client's `authorized_concurrent_caregivers`; a total exactly equal to the cap passes, and anything beyond it is rejected with `ERR_COVERAGE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

The client's `authorized_coverage_hours` is at all times the sum of `coverage_hours` over that client's service authorizations whose `auth_status` is `AUTHORIZED` or `ACTIVE`; rows that are `PROPOSED`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` are excluded from the sum entirely. It is recomputed on every authorization state change and on the discharge cascade, and because each row's `coverage_hours` is a whole number of hours multiplied by an integer caregiver count, the total never carries a fractional part.

Activation — `AUTHORIZED → ACTIVE` — is a care manager's act and requires two things: that the client's `status` is `ACTIVE_CARE`, else `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_IN_SERVICE`, and that aggregate coverage capacity still holds, else `ERR_COVERAGE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. It does not re-read the vendor's `standing` on this route, because a bar would already have terminated an `AUTHORIZED` row through the cascade of §3.2. Activation increases the authorization's `version` by 1, leaves the client record untouched so the client's `version` does not move, and moves no money and no coverage total, because the hours were already counted at authorization. Suspension of an `ACTIVE` authorization arrives by three routes and the route determines the recorded cause: a care manager suspending it directly sets `suspension_reason` to `FAMILY_REQUEST`, the client's slide into `SUSPENDED_NONPAYMENT` sets it to `NONPAYMENT`, and the barring of the vendor sets it to `VENDOR_STANDING`. In every case the authorization's `coverage_hours` is subtracted from the client's `authorized_coverage_hours`, the parent plan's `coverage_gap_flag` is recomputed, the client's `risk_posture` is recomputed, and the authorization's `version` increases by 1.

Returning a `SUSPENDED` authorization to `ACTIVE` — whether requested by a care manager or arriving as the cascade from the client's return to `ACTIVE_CARE` — requires that the vendor's `standing` is not `BARRED`, that the client's `status` is `ACTIVE_CARE`, and that aggregate coverage capacity holds. It resets `suspension_reason` to `NONE`, adds the authorization's `coverage_hours` back onto the client's `authorized_coverage_hours`, recomputes the parent plan's `coverage_gap_flag` and the client's `risk_posture`, and increases the authorization's `version` by 1. The loop between `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED` is unbounded, and each lap must leave the client's `authorized_coverage_hours` at exactly the value it held before the lap.

Termination is available from `PROPOSED`, `AUTHORIZED`, `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED`, and `TERMINATED` is terminal. A care manager may request it directly, in which case `termination_reason` is `CARE_MANAGER_REQUEST`; the other four causes arrive as cascades and set `DECISION_RESCINDED`, `PLAN_SUPERSEDED`, `VENDOR_STANDING` or `CLIENT_DISCHARGED` respectively. In all cases the client's `authorized_coverage_hours` is recomputed with this row now outside the authorized set, the parent plan's `coverage_gap_flag` and the client's `risk_posture` are recomputed, and the authorization's `version` increases by 1. All `auth_status` changes are requested with a `to_state` and an optional `reason`, and `to_state` must name one of the five declared `auth_status` values, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `PROPOSED` is a creation status only: no role owns it, so both admitted roles get past the ownership rung and are then rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` once the authorization itself has been resolved. On the three routes a caller may drive on this operation — activation, direct suspension and direct termination — neither the client nor the parent care plan is itself touched, so only the authorization's own `version` moves; `AUTHORIZED` is the single requestable transition that reaches back into both of them.

The disclosure dimension moves through its own two-step ritual, requested with an `action` naming either `DISCLOSE` or `ACKNOWLEDGE` — any other value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and, on the second step, an `acknowledged_by_contact_id` and an optional `note`. The disclosing step — `PENDING_DISCLOSURE → DISCLOSED`, requested with `DISCLOSE` — belongs to the care manager and requires that the authorization's `auth_status` is not `TERMINATED`; it increases the authorization's `version` by 1 and leaves the client record untouched, because `PENDING_DISCLOSURE` and `DISCLOSED` are both `ELEVATED` triggers and the posture therefore cannot move. The acknowledging step — `DISCLOSED → ACKNOWLEDGED`, requested with `ACKNOWLEDGE` — belongs to the family, and `acknowledged_by_contact_id` is required on it. The contact named there must be a family contact of the authorization's client whose `is_surrogate_decision_maker` is true and whose `contact_role` is one of `POA_HEALTHCARE`, `POA_FINANCIAL`, `GUARDIAN` or `RESPONSIBLE_PARTY` — an `INTERESTED_FAMILY` contact, or any non-surrogate, is rejected with `ERR_COI_ACK_BY_INELIGIBLE_PARTY`, and so is a contact who is a perfectly good surrogate of some *other* client, which is reported the same way rather than as a relationship failure — and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal `acknowledged_by_contact_id`, so nobody acknowledges on somebody else's behalf; that self-identity check is an ownership check and therefore runs before the state-reachability check on `coi_status`, which in turn runs before the eligibility test. Unlike the disclosing step, the acknowledging step reads no `auth_status` at all, so an authorization already `TERMINATED` whose `coi_status` still stands at `DISCLOSED` may be acknowledged. On success the acknowledging contact is recorded in `coi_acknowledged_by_contact_id`, the client's `risk_posture` is recomputed because the outstanding disclosure has cleared, and the authorization's `version` and the client's `version` each increase by 1. `NOT_REQUIRED` and `ACKNOWLEDGED` are both terminal on this dimension: a disclosure event submitted against an authorization whose `coi_status` is `NOT_REQUIRED`, or already `ACKNOWLEDGED`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

### 3.7. Crisis Response and Hospital-to-Home Transition Oversight

When something breaks, a care manager opens a crisis episode against the `client_id` affected, naming a `trigger_type` of `HOSPITAL_ADMISSION`, `FALL`, `CAREGIVER_NO_SHOW`, `ACUTE_BEHAVIORAL` or `ELDER_ABUSE_SUSPICION`. Where the trigger is a hospital admission, the facility is recorded in `hospital_name` — an optional string that is never required, not even on a `HOSPITAL_ADMISSION`, and that is accepted alongside any `trigger_type`. An episode may be opened whatever the client's `status`, `DISCHARGED` included. The episode is created in `OPEN` status with `opened_at` set to the logical clock's current instant, `readmission_count` at 0, and `stabilized_at`, `discharge_at` and `closed_at` all null; the opening care manager is recorded in `assigned_care_manager_id`, taken from the acting `X-User-ID` rather than from any request field, and this operation does not resolve that identifier against the care-manager register. Because an open episode is the first branch of the risk-posture evaluation, the client's `risk_posture` becomes `CRISIS` immediately and the client's `version` increases by 1.

Episode state moves on a `to_state` with an optional `note` and, at one step, a `discharge_at`. Either `CareManager` or `PracticeSupervisor` may drive any of the four transitions — no target state is owned by one of them to the exclusion of the other — and the acting user need not be the episode's `assigned_care_manager_id`. Stabilisation — `OPEN → STABILIZED` — sets `stabilized_at` to the logical clock's current instant, recomputes the client's `risk_posture` because a stabilised episode has left the crisis input set, and increases both the episode's `version` and the client's `version` by 1.

Handing the client to hospital-to-home transition oversight — `STABILIZED → TRANSITION_OVERSIGHT` — requires that `discharge_at`, the hospital discharge instant, is supplied on the request, and requires that the client has at least one service authorization whose `auth_status` is `AUTHORIZED` or `ACTIVE`: the practice does not accept oversight of a discharge home when there is no standing coverage to come home to, and a client with none is a reachability failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The supplied `discharge_at` is stored, the client's `risk_posture` is recomputed to `CRISIS` because transition oversight re-enters the crisis input set, and both the episode's `version` and the client's `version` increase by 1.

A readmission sends the episode back from `TRANSITION_OVERSIGHT` to `OPEN`. That transition increases `readmission_count` by 1, resets `stabilized_at` to null, recomputes the client's `risk_posture`, and increases the episode's `version` by 1 — and, alone among the four episode transitions, it leaves the client record untouched, so the client's `version` and `updated_at` do not move; the loop through `OPEN`, `STABILIZED`, `TRANSITION_OVERSIGHT` and back to `OPEN` is unbounded. Closing the episode — `TRANSITION_OVERSIGHT → CLOSED` — requires that every time entry naming this episode has reached `APPROVED`, `INVOICED`, `REJECTED` or `VOIDED` status; no crisis time may remain in `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED`, because closing an episode with unreviewed crisis hours against it would leave the practice unable to defend its crisis billing, and an episode still carrying such an entry is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Closing sets `closed_at` to the logical clock's current instant, recomputes the client's `risk_posture`, and increases both the episode's `version` and the client's `version` by 1. `CLOSED` is terminal.

### 3.8. Capturing and Rating Billable Time

Time capture is the practice's revenue engine and its most rule-dense write. A care manager logs an interval by naming the `client_id` being billed, the `care_manager_id` whose time it is — taken exactly as supplied and not required to equal the acting `X-User-ID`, even though the submit and return-to-draft transitions later compare the two — a `service_type` of `ASSESSMENT`, `CARE_COORDINATION`, `CRISIS_RESPONSE`, `TRAVEL` or `CONFERENCE`, the interval bounds `start_at` and `end_at` with the end strictly later than the start, and a `service_location_id` — an opaque location key of 1 to 40 characters such as `HOSPITAL_A` or `CLIENT_HOME`. An optional `note` records the activity, and `crisis_episode_id` links the entry to the episode it responded to. Time may not be logged against a `DISCHARGED` client; that attempt is rejected with `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_IN_SERVICE`. The entry is created in `DRAFT` with `approved_by_care_manager_id` and `invoice_id` both null, and the client's `unbilled_accrual_cents` is recomputed upward; the client's `version` increases by 1.

Crisis time must have a crisis to point at, and non-crisis time must not pretend to. When `service_type` is `CRISIS_RESPONSE`, a `crisis_episode_id` is mandatory, the named episode's `status` must be `OPEN`, `STABILIZED` or `TRANSITION_OVERSIGHT` — never `CLOSED` — and the episode must belong to the same client as the entry. When `service_type` is anything else, `crisis_episode_id` must be omitted. Any violation of those three conditions is rejected with `ERR_CRISIS_CONTEXT_REQUIRED`; an episode belonging to a different client is likewise a relationship failure and reports the same crisis-context rejection.

Three temporal and fiscal guards protect a care manager's day, and they are evaluated in the order given. First, the entry may not overlap another entry of the **same care manager** whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `INVOICED`; time-entry intervals are **half-open**, covering `start_at` up to but not including `end_at`, so an entry that begins at the exact instant another ends does not overlap. An overlap is rejected with `ERR_TIME_ENTRY_OVERLAP`. Second, exactly two neighbours are examined, both drawn from that same care manager's entries in the same four statuses and counted across every client: the nearest preceding entry, meaning the one with the greatest `end_at` that is at or before the candidate's `start_at`, and the nearest following entry, meaning the one with the least `start_at` that is at or after the candidate's `end_at`. When either of those two carries a different `service_location_id`, the gap between it and the candidate must be at least half an hour — 30 minutes — because the care manager has to physically travel; a gap of exactly 30 minutes passes, and anything shorter is rejected with `ERR_TRAVEL_BUFFER_VIOLATION`. A neighbour sharing the candidate's `service_location_id` imposes no buffer at all, and no entry beyond those two nearest neighbours is consulted, however differently located it may be. Third, the sum of `rounded_minutes` over that care manager's entries sharing the same `service_date`, across every client and drawn from the same four statuses, including the candidate, may not exceed ten hours — 600 minutes; a total of exactly 600 minutes passes and anything beyond it is rejected with `ERR_DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

Rating is stamped at creation and never recomputed afterwards, so a later crisis-episode transition does not retroactively re-rate an hour already logged. Six derived values are written. `service_date` is the UTC calendar date of `start_at` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and it is the aggregation key for the daily cap. `raw_minutes` is the number of whole minutes between `start_at` and `end_at`, with any partial minute discarded downward. `rounded_minutes` is `raw_minutes` rounded **up** to the next multiple of the client's `billing_increment_min`, so a 22-minute call on a quarter-hour client bills as 30 minutes. `rate_multiplier` is selected by evaluating the following branches in this exact order and taking the first match: if `service_type` is `TRAVEL`, the multiplier is the client's `travel_billing_pct` divided by 100; otherwise, if `service_type` is `CRISIS_RESPONSE` and the linked episode's `status` is `OPEN` or `TRANSITION_OVERSIGHT`, the multiplier is 1.5, the practice's crisis premium; otherwise, if `service_type` is `CRISIS_RESPONSE` — meaning the episode is `STABILIZED` or `CLOSED` — the multiplier is 1.0; otherwise, for `ASSESSMENT`, `CARE_COORDINATION` and `CONFERENCE`, the multiplier is 1.0. Note the branch order: travel is tested before crisis, so travel to a crisis is rated at the travel percentage, not the crisis premium. `after_hours_factor` is 1.20 when the UTC hour of `start_at` is earlier than 8 or is 18 or later, and 1.00 otherwise. Neither factor is a monetary amount, so neither is an integer; both are returned as plain JSON numbers rather than as strings or decimal-formatted text, carrying only the digits the value itself needs: `after_hours_factor` reads `1.2` or `1.0`, and `rate_multiplier` reads `1.5` for the crisis premium, `1.0` for ordinary time, and the client's `travel_billing_pct` divided by 100 for travel — so a 50-percent travel rate reads `0.5` and a zero travel rate reads `0.0`.

`billable_amount_cents` is then composed as a single product and rounded exactly once: multiply `rounded_minutes` by the care manager's `hourly_rate_cents`, by `rate_multiplier` and by `after_hours_factor`, divide by 60 to convert minutes to hours, and round the resulting figure half up to whole cents. No intermediate factor is rounded — rounding the hourly-rate share before applying `after_hours_factor`, for instance, is explicitly wrong and yields a different figure. The inputs to the rating are exactly one time entry together with the care manager named by `care_manager_id`, the client named by `client_id`, and — only when `service_type` is `CRISIS_RESPONSE` — the episode named by `crisis_episode_id`.

The entry's own lifecycle is driven by a `to_state` and an optional `reason`. Its author submits it, `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, which requires that the acting `X-User-ID` equals the entry's `care_manager_id` and increases the entry's `version` by 1. A practice supervisor whose `is_supervisor` is true, and who is not the entry's author, then forks it. Approval — `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` — records the approver in `approved_by_care_manager_id` and increases the entry's `version` by 1; because both `SUBMITTED` and `APPROVED` sit inside the accrual input set, the client's `unbilled_accrual_cents` does not move, and the client record is not touched at all, so its `version` does not move either. Rejection — `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` — recomputes the client's `unbilled_accrual_cents` downward, since a rejected entry leaves the accrual set, and increases both the entry's `version` and the client's `version` by 1. The author then returns a rejected entry to `DRAFT`, which recomputes the client's `unbilled_accrual_cents` upward as the entry re-enters the accrual set and increases both the entry's `version` and the client's `version` by 1. The correction loop through `SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED`, `DRAFT` and back is unbounded and must leave the client's `unbilled_accrual_cents` unchanged per lap. `to_state` must name one of the six declared entry statuses, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; `INVOICED` is reachable only as the invoice-side cascade described next, no role owns it, and naming it here is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` once the entry itself has been resolved. No transition on the entry's own lifecycle reads the client's `status`, so an entry may be submitted, approved, rejected, returned to draft or voided even after its client has been discharged — the discharge guard bites only on the creation of new time.

Two further edges are system cascades from the invoice side. When an invoice is issued, each approved entry appearing in that invoice's line set moves `APPROVED → INVOICED`, has its `invoice_id` set to the issuing invoice, and has its `version` increased by 1, while the client's `unbilled_accrual_cents` is recomputed downward. When an issued invoice is voided, each entry carrying that invoice in `invoice_id` moves `INVOICED → APPROVED`, has its `invoice_id` reset to null, and has its `version` increased by 1, while the client's `unbilled_accrual_cents` is recomputed upward.

Voiding an entry is a practice supervisor's act and is available while the entry's `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `REJECTED`, but only while the entry's `invoice_id` is null: an entry sitting on an issued invoice must be released by voiding that invoice first. Voiding recomputes the client's `unbilled_accrual_cents` downward, drops the care manager's daily rounded-minute total for that entry's `service_date` by the entry's `rounded_minutes` — freeing that room under the daily cap — and increases both the entry's `version` and the client's `version` by 1. `VOIDED` is terminal.

### 3.9. The Retainer Ledger, Invoicing and Payment

The practice is private-pay, and it holds the family's money before it earns it. A billing administrator posts a retainer deposit against a client by supplying `amount_cents`, which must be an integer greater than zero, and the `received_at` instant the funds arrived, which is required on the payload but participates in no computation — deposits are summed without regard to when they landed. A deposit is accepted whatever the client's `status`. Deposits are append-only and are never deleted or reversed, so the client's `retainer_deposits_total_cents` is simply the sum of `amount_cents` over every deposit ever accepted for that client. The client's `retainer_balance_cents` is that deposit total less the sum of `retainer_applied_cents` over that client's invoices whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `PAID` — invoices still in `DRAFT` and invoices that have been `VOIDED` contribute no draw at all. Alongside the retainer, the client's `unbilled_accrual_cents` is the sum of `billable_amount_cents` over that client's time entries whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`; entries that are `REJECTED`, `INVOICED` or `VOIDED` contribute nothing. Neither quantity involves any rounding, because every input is already an integer number of cents. Posting a deposit recomputes both and increases the client's `version` by 1 whether or not anything else moves, and if the client was `SUSPENDED_NONPAYMENT` and the recomputed `retainer_balance_cents` has reached at least the client's `retainer_floor_cents`, the client returns to `ACTIVE_CARE` and every authorization of that client whose `auth_status` is `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` of `NONPAYMENT` is then considered for reinstatement, in ascending `created_at` order with ties broken by creation order. Such a candidate returns to `ACTIVE` with `suspension_reason` reset to `NONE` unless one of two things stops it: its vendor's `standing` has since become `BARRED`, or reinstating it would breach the client's aggregate coverage capacity as §3.6 computes that test. A stopped candidate is skipped and left exactly as it stands — `SUSPENDED`, with `suspension_reason` still `NONPAYMENT` and its `version` unmoved — and the remaining candidates are still considered. Because each reinstated row counts toward the capacity test applied to the rows after it, where capacity is short the earliest-created candidates win. Authorizations suspended for `FAMILY_REQUEST` or `VENDOR_STANDING` are never reinstated by this cascade. The client's `authorized_coverage_hours` is recomputed upward as the reinstated authorizations re-enter the authorized set. The updated client is returned.

An invoice covers a billing period. The billing administrator names the `client_id` and the closed period window `period_start` and `period_end`, both `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar dates — a value of any other shape, or one of the right shape that names no real calendar date such as `2026-02-30`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — with the end no earlier than the start. Periods may not collide, and the collision test compares the two declared windows alone rather than the service dates of any entry inside them: if any other invoice of that client whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `PAID` has a window that intersects the candidate's — that is, the other invoice's `period_start` is no later than the candidate's `period_end` and the candidate's `period_start` is no later than the other's `period_end` — the request is rejected with `ERR_INVOICE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Both windows being closed, two that share even a single calendar date collide, while two that merely abut on consecutive days do not. An invoice may not be opened for a `DISCHARGED` client, which reports `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_IN_SERVICE`, and an invoice whose candidate line set under the provisional variant would be empty is rejected with `ERR_NO_BILLABLE_ACTIVITY`. The new invoice is created in `DRAFT` status with `variant` at `PROVISIONAL`, `amount_paid_cents` at 0, `issued_at` null and `voided_reason` null, and no money moves; the client record is not touched at all, so its `version` does not move.

Invoice arithmetic runs in four steps, and the `variant` field selects which branch of step one is authoritative. The candidate line set is the client's time entries whose `invoice_id` is null and whose `service_date` falls inside the invoice's closed period window — both `period_start` and `period_end` inclusive — filtered by status according to the variant. `line_entry_ids` is that candidate set ordered by `start_at` ascending, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending and any remaining tie broken by the order in which the server created the entries — never by `id`. Second, `line_subtotal_cents` is the plain sum of `billable_amount_cents` over those lines; each line amount was already rounded when its entry was created, so the discipline here is round per line, then sum. Third, `admin_fee_cents` is the practice's 7 percent administrative charge, computed as `line_subtotal_cents` multiplied by 7 and divided by 100, rounded half up **once on the summed subtotal** — computing the fee per line and summing the roundings is explicitly wrong and yields different cents — and `total_due_cents` is `line_subtotal_cents` plus `admin_fee_cents`. Fourth comes the retainer waterfall: `retainer_applied_cents` is the lesser of the client's `retainer_balance_cents` and `total_due_cents`, floored at 0 so that it is never negative — a balance that has somehow gone below zero draws nothing rather than inflating the bill — and `balance_due_cents` is `total_due_cents` less `retainer_applied_cents` — the retainer is drawn first and the family is billed only the remainder.

The two variants differ in exactly one thing, and the difference is consequential. Under `PROVISIONAL`, which governs while the invoice is `DRAFT`, the candidate status filter admits entries that are `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`: unapproved-but-submitted time is included so the family sees the bill the practice expects to send, and `retainer_applied_cents` is a projection against which no money moves. Under `FINAL`, which governs once the invoice is `ISSUED` or `PAID`, the filter admits `APPROVED` entries only: supervisor-unapproved time is dropped, so the same invoice over the same data yields a strictly smaller `line_subtotal_cents` whenever any candidate is still `SUBMITTED`, and `retainer_applied_cents` is actually debited from the client's `retainer_balance_cents`. A `DRAFT` invoice is re-derived under the provisional variant on every read, so it tracks time capture as it happens.

Issuing the invoice — `DRAFT → ISSUED`, requested with a `to_state` and an optional `reason` — is where money moves. It requires that recomputing under the final variant still yields a non-empty `line_entry_ids`, else `ERR_NO_BILLABLE_ACTIVITY`, and that the client is not `DISCHARGED`. On success `variant` becomes `FINAL` and every computed money field is recomputed under the approved-only input set; `issued_at` is set to the logical clock's current instant; every time entry in `line_entry_ids` moves from `APPROVED` to `INVOICED` with its `invoice_id` set; the client's `retainer_balance_cents` decreases by `retainer_applied_cents` and its `unbilled_accrual_cents` is recomputed; and both the invoice's `version` and the client's `version` increase by 1. If, after that final retainer draw, the client's recomputed `retainer_balance_cents` has fallen below its `retainer_floor_cents` and the invoice's `balance_due_cents` is greater than zero, an `ACTIVE_CARE` client slides to `SUSPENDED_NONPAYMENT`, which in turn moves every authorization of that client whose `auth_status` is `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `NONPAYMENT` and recomputes the client's `authorized_coverage_hours` downward as those rows leave the authorized set.

Payment is posted against an issued invoice with an `amount_cents` that must be an integer greater than zero, the `received_at` instant, and a non-empty `method` describing how the family paid; all three are required, and neither `received_at` nor `method` is persisted anywhere or read by any guard — they are recorded nowhere on the invoice. The running total `amount_paid_cents` is increased by `amount_cents` and the invoice's `version` increases by 1, while the client record is not touched, so the client's `version` does not move. When `amount_paid_cents` plus the new `amount_cents` exactly equals the invoice's `balance_due_cents`, the invoice moves `ISSUED → PAID`; when it is less, the invoice stays `ISSUED` and the family may pay again; when it is more, the payment is refused outright with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE` and nothing is recorded — so an invoice the retainer already reduced to a `balance_due_cents` of 0 admits no payment at all, since the smallest acceptable `amount_cents` already exceeds that balance, and it simply remains `ISSUED`. A payment posted against an invoice that is not `ISSUED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Voiding is the compensating action, and it behaves differently depending on what the invoice had already consumed. A `DRAFT` invoice may always be voided; the supplied `reason` is stored as `voided_reason`, the invoice's `version` increases by 1, and no money and no entry state changes at all, because a draft invoice never drew retainer nor moved any entry. The client record is not touched on this route, so its `version` does not move. The provisional money fields are, however, re-derived one final time and frozen onto the record as they stood at the instant of voiding, with `variant` left at `PROVISIONAL`; because re-derivation on read is confined to invoices still in `DRAFT`, every later read of that `VOIDED` invoice returns those frozen figures rather than recomputing them, even as the entries it once listed go on to be billed elsewhere. An `ISSUED` invoice may be voided only while its `amount_paid_cents` is 0 — a partly or fully paid invoice must be settled another way — and voiding it runs the full release chain. The invoice's `status` becomes `VOIDED` and `voided_reason` is stored, while its own `variant`, `line_entry_ids` and every money field are left exactly as they stood when it was issued and are never re-derived again — so a voided invoice goes on reporting the final figures it once charged, and goes on listing entries that have since been released. Because of that, every time entry whose `invoice_id` names this invoice moves from `INVOICED` back to `APPROVED` and has its `invoice_id` cleared. Because of that, the client's `unbilled_accrual_cents` is recomputed upward by exactly the released entries and the client's `retainer_balance_cents` is recomputed upward by exactly this invoice's `retainer_applied_cents`, since a voided invoice leaves the drawn set. Because of that, a client sitting in `SUSPENDED_NONPAYMENT` whose recomputed retainer balance has reached at least its `retainer_floor_cents` returns to `ACTIVE_CARE`. And because of that, every authorization of that client whose `auth_status` is `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` of `NONPAYMENT` returns to `ACTIVE` with `suspension_reason` reset to `NONE` — subject, exactly as on a retainer deposit, to the vendor-standing and coverage-capacity skips set out earlier in this section — and the client's `authorized_coverage_hours` is recomputed upward. On this route the invoice's `version` and the client's `version` each increase by 1, the client being touched whether or not the restore actually fired. Both `PAID` and `VOIDED` are terminal, and only `ISSUED` and `VOIDED` may be requested on the invoice state operation at all: naming `DRAFT` or `PAID` as `to_state` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` once the invoice has been resolved.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that bring a new, stand-alone record into existence — a client, a care manager, a vendor, a family contact, an assessment, a care plan, a service authorization, a family conference, a conference decision, a crisis episode, a time entry, an invoice — return `201 Created`. Every other operation returns `200 OK`, including the action-shaped POST operations that mutate an existing record rather than creating one: capacity determinations, retainer deposits, family reports, conflict-of-interest events, invoice payments, and setting the logical clock. All state transitions and all reads return `200 OK`.
