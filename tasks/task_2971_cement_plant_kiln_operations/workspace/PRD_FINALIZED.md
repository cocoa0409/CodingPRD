# Cement Plant & Kiln Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A portland cement plant is one of the few industrial settings where chemistry, thermodynamics, environmental law and commercial contracts all bind the same physical tonne of material at the same time. A tonne of limestone leaves the quarry as part of a chemically specified raw mix, is blended into a physical pile whose moisture profile silently shifts its chemistry away from the design, is burned in a rotary kiln whose fuel mix simultaneously determines clinker yield and stack emissions, is ground with gypsum and interground limestone into a cement type with a legally significant fineness and sulphate content, and is finally shipped against a mill certificate that a customer's own quality department will audit. Today the plant tracks these stages in disconnected spreadsheets, laboratory notebooks and a scheduling whiteboard. The consequences are routine and expensive: piles blended against a superseded recipe, kiln runs completed on a pile that could never have physically fed them, alternative fuels burned past the expiry of their trial-burn authorization, and cement loaded out against a certificate that quality had already revoked.

This document specifies the **Cement Plant & Kiln Operations API**, a single system of record for the pyroprocessing and distribution chain of a cement plant. The API is mounted under `/cement-ops`. Its purpose is not to optimise the plant — the process engineers already know how to do that — but to make every commitment auditable and every constraint enforced at the moment of commitment rather than at the month-end reconciliation. Three families of constraint dominate the design. **Permit constraints** cap the plant's NOx intensity, its annual NOx mass, its chlorine input per kiln run and its thermal substitution rate, and they gate the release of clinker rather than merely reporting after the fact. **Quality constraints** hold clinker and cement in an unsellable state until the laboratory says otherwise, and withdraw that permission again — retroactively, with cascading effect on downstream reservations — when a retest disagrees. **Commercial constraints** ensure that no tonne is promised twice: silo stock is decomposed into physical, reserved and unreleased components, and every reservation is traceable to a customer, a certificate and a loading appointment.

The system is deliberately conservative about two things. First, it refuses writes that were computed from a stale read: the three write operations that commit physical capacity out of a shared parent all require the caller to echo the parent's version. Second, it enforces two-person control on the three decisions that a single individual could otherwise use to certify their own work. The reader should treat every threshold in this document as exact and every rounding instruction as mandatory; in a domain where a customer's laboratory recomputes the mill certificate independently, a difference in the second decimal place is a commercial dispute.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. Both must be present and non-empty; a header transmitted as an empty string counts as absent, and the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Once authenticated, the role named in `X-User-Role` must be one of the roles the target endpoint declares in §4; otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). No endpoint infers a role from the user id, and no endpoint accepts a role it does not list.

Eight roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `PlantAdmin`, `ProcessEngineer`, `KilnOperator`, `LabTechnician`, `QualityManager`, `EnvironmentalOfficer`, `LogisticsPlanner`, and `Customer`. The first seven are the plant's internal roles and every one of them may perform every read (GET) operation in the API; write authority is partitioned among them as §4 records.

`PlantAdmin` owns plant master data and physical assets — it alone registers plants, kilns and silos, changes a silo's status, approves a kiln outage, and creates an outage whose window carries a peak-season override. It shares raw-material registration with `ProcessEngineer`, fuel registration with `EnvironmentalOfficer`, customer creation and customer amendment with `LogisticsPlanner`, plain outage planning with `ProcessEngineer`, and distribution planning with `LogisticsPlanner`. `ProcessEngineer` authors raw mix designs, amends and submits them, creates blended piles and appends additions to them, seals piles, creates cement batches, and starts and completes outages; it shares kiln-run scheduling with `KilnOperator`. `KilnOperator` fires kilns — lighting a run, charging fuel, completing and aborting a run — and drives the finish mill by starting and finishing grinding. `LabTechnician` records every measurement: laboratory tests, and the start and result of a trial burn; it shares burn-test creation with `EnvironmentalOfficer`. `QualityManager` requests revisions of and approves raw mix designs, releases clinker, and creates, issues and revokes mill certificates. `EnvironmentalOfficer` owns the air permit: it registers fuels and is the only role that may adjudicate a trial burn. `LogisticsPlanner` runs the terminal: loadout order creation, start of loading, completion and voiding, plus customer administration and distribution planning.

Identity semantics differ between the internal roles and the external one. When `X-User-Role` is `PlantAdmin`, `ProcessEngineer`, `KilnOperator`, `LabTechnician`, `QualityManager`, `EnvironmentalOfficer` or `LogisticsPlanner`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition says so — as `created_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a raw mix design, as `result_recorded_by_user_id` and `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a burn test, as `approved_by_user_id` on a kiln outage, as `recorded_by_user_id` on a laboratory test, and as `issued_by_user_id` on a mill certificate — and compares it against those recorded values for the two-person controls of §2.7, but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Customer` compare the resource's `customer_id` to `X-User-ID`.

The `Customer` role may write nothing at all. It may read exactly two collections, and only its own rows within them: mill certificates and loadout orders. On the two list reads, the result set is silently filtered to rows whose `customer_id` equals the caller's `X-User-ID` — an out-of-scope row is absent from the page rather than an error. On the two single-row reads, a request for a row belonging to another customer is refused with `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This scope check applies to those four read operations and to no others.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. When `limit` is omitted it defaults to 10; supplied values must be integers no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. When `offset` is omitted it defaults to 0; supplied values must be integers no smaller than 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page. Rows are ordered by `created_at` ascending; where two rows share an identical `created_at`, their `id` values break the tie in ascending order, and nothing else is ever used as a tie-break.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC notation with exactly three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and an integer `version` that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of that row, including mutations the row suffers as a downstream consequence of an operation on some other row. The one exception is the laboratory test resource: a `LabTest` row is immutable once written, so it carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither an `updated_at` nor a `version` field at all.

Path identifiers are named after the entity they address: `plant_id`, `raw_material_id`, `fuel_id`, `customer_id`, `design_id`, `pile_id`, `burn_test_id`, `kiln_id`, `kiln_run_id`, `outage_id`, `silo_id`, `cement_batch_id`, `lab_test_id`, `mill_certificate_id` and `loadout_order_id`. Every entity in this system is addressed as a peer collection directly beneath the API prefix; there are no nested resource URLs, and parentage is expressed exclusively through foreign-key fields carried in request bodies. Path identifiers are nevertheless validated left to right: an identifier that does not resolve to an existing row of its expected entity yields that entity's own not-found error with HTTP 404 before any body field is examined.

All temporal reasoning in this system reads a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. The clock is the only source of "now" for every comparison against a window boundary, every computed timestamp such as `sealed_at`, `lit_at`, `completed_at`, `started_at`, `approved_at`, `qualified_at`, `issued_at`, `revoked_at`, `grinding_started_at`, `ground_at`, `loading_started_at` and `valid_from`, and every rolling-window aggregate. On server startup the clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. It never advances on its own; it moves only when the clock-setting capability is invoked with a `to_iso8601` instant, and that capability responds with the new `current_utc` value. Moving the clock never retro-transitions any entity: windows are evaluated at the moment an endpoint is called, and no background sweep exists.

Row bookkeeping is the one exception to that rule. The `created_at` and `updated_at` stamps of §2.4 are not read from the logical clock at all: they are minted from the server's real wall-clock time, truncated to three fractional digits, and each newly minted stamp is forced to be strictly later than the one minted before it, so that `created_at` ascending is always the order in which rows were inserted — which is what the list ordering of §2.3 and every `created_at` ascending cascade walk in §3 depend on. Setting the logical clock therefore has no effect on either stamp, and neither stamp is expected to equal the logical clock's instant.

### 2.5. Measurement Precision and Rounding Discipline

All rounding in this system is **round half up**, never bankers' rounding. Tonnages, percentages, energies and monetary amounts are carried to two decimal places. Oxide contents — the calcium, silicon, aluminium and iron oxide percentages of a raw mix or a physical pile — are carried to four decimal places. Emission intensities, being small numbers whose third digit matters to a permit, are carried to three decimal places.

Where a calculation in this document names a rounding step explicitly, that rounding happens at that step, and the rounded value is what every later step consumes. No formula re-derives a value from unrounded intermediates once a rounded intermediate has been defined. This rule is not cosmetic: several results in this system are deliberately the sum of already-rounded components and therefore differ in the last decimal place from the same quantity computed in one unrounded pass. Where that happens, the round-then-sum answer is the correct one.

### 2.6. Stale-Read Protection on Committing Writes

Three write operations commit physical or contractual capacity out of a parent row that other operations are constantly moving. Each of them requires the caller to echo the parent's `version` as it stood when the caller last read it, and each refuses the write when that echo no longer matches. In all three cases the version field is a required input, not an optional one, and the rejection carries HTTP 412.

Creating a blended pile requires `expected_raw_mix_design_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the referenced `RawMixDesign`. If the design's current `version` differs, the create is refused with `ERR_STALE_DESIGN_VERSION`. A pile is a physical commitment to one revision of the chemical recipe, so this check guarantees that no pile is ever blended against a module target the caller has not observed. Note that a successful pile creation itself recomputes the design's `active_pile_count` and increments the design's `version`, so a second pile created from the same read version is refused.

Creating a cement batch requires `expected_clinker_silo_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the clinker `Silo` the batch draws from. If the silo's current `version` differs, the create is refused with `ERR_STALE_SILO_VERSION`. A silo's `version` increments on every reservation, every physical draw, every credit, every withdrawal and every status change, so any intervening silo movement invalidates the caller's read and prevents a reservation from being computed against a balance that has already moved.

Creating a loadout order requires `expected_mill_certificate_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the referenced `MillCertificate`. If the certificate's current `version` differs, the create is refused with `ERR_STALE_CERTIFICATE_VERSION`. A certificate's `version` increments on issue, on revoke, and on every order that consumes its `allocated_tonnes`, so an order can never be reserved against an allocation figure that has since been consumed or withdrawn.

Endpoints other than these three take no expected-version field, and supplying one to them has no effect.

### 2.7. Two-Person Controls

Three decisions in this system are two-person controls: the user who performed the upstream act may not perform the downstream adjudication, even when that user holds the correct role. The comparison is on `X-User-ID`, never on `X-User-Role`, and a violation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

The three pairs are: approving a raw mix design, where the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the design's `created_by_user_id`; adjudicating a trial burn, where the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the burn test's `result_recorded_by_user_id`; and issuing a mill certificate, where the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `recorded_by_user_id` of the newest 28-day compressive strength laboratory test on the certificate's batch.

### 2.8. The Validation Ladder

For any request, the implementation walks the validation rules whose scope matches that request in ascending priority order and returns the **first** violated rule's error. Two rules at the same priority can never both be violated by the same request, so the ladder is total and the outcome is deterministic. The ordering below is normative; the substantive condition behind each rung is developed in §2 and §3, and the error catalogue in §5 deliberately carries no trigger conditions of its own.

The ladder, in ascending priority:

1. **Priority 10 — authentication.** On every endpoint: both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present and non-empty, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Priority 20 — role permission.** On every endpoint: `X-User-Role` is one of the endpoint's declared roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Priority 30 — path existence.** On every endpoint carrying a path identifier: the identifier resolves to an existing row of the expected entity, else that entity's own not-found error (404). The fifteen not-found errors are `ERR_PLANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RAW_MATERIAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FUEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DESIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BURN_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_KILN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_KILN_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OUTAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SILO_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LAB_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`.
4. **Priority 35 — body foreign-key existence.** On every endpoint whose body carries a foreign key: every such key resolves to an existing row of its target entity, else that referenced entity's own not-found error (404). When several foreign keys in one body are unresolvable, the reported error is chosen by the order in which those foreign-key fields are declared on the entity in this document, reading top to bottom — not by the order the keys happen to appear in the request body.
5. **Priority 40 — request shape.** On every write endpoint: required fields present; numeric fields numeric, and non-negative wherever the field is a tonnage, a percentage or an energy; enum fields drawn from their declared value sets; datetime fields parseable as ISO 8601 UTC; array fields actually arrays; `gypsum_pct` between 2.00 and 7.00 inclusive; `blaine_target_m2_per_kg` inside the cement type's Blaine band; `as_of_month` between 1 and 12 inclusive. Any violation yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
6. **Priority 42 — pagination shape.** On every list endpoint: `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` at least 0, both integers when supplied, else `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).
7. **Priority 45 — customer scope.** On the certificate list read, the single-certificate read, the loadout-order list read and the single-order read: a `Customer` caller sees only rows whose `customer_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`; list reads are filtered, single reads of a foreign row are refused with `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403).
8. **Priority 50 — expected parent version.** On pile creation, cement batch creation and loadout order creation: the supplied expected-version field equals the parent's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_DESIGN_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_SILO_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_CERTIFICATE_VERSION` respectively (412).
9. **Priority 55 — segregation of duties.** On design approval, burn-test adjudication and certificate issue: the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the paired upstream actor, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
10. **Priority 60 — subject state.** On every transition endpoint: the subject's current state is a legal originating state for the requested transition, else that entity's own invalid-state error (409) — `ERR_INVALID_DESIGN_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PILE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_BURN_TEST_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_OUTAGE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CERTIFICATE_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`.
11. **Priority 62 — silo not blocked.** Every silo the operation touches is `OPEN`, else `ERR_SILO_BLOCKED` (409). This rung guards exactly seven operations: kiln-run completion, clinker release, cement batch creation, start of grinding, finish of grinding, loadout order creation, and start of loading.
12. **Priority 64 — design approved for pile work.** The referenced raw mix design is `APPROVED`, else `ERR_DESIGN_NOT_APPROVED` (409). This rung guards three operations: pile creation, appending an addition to a pile, and sealing a pile.
13. **Priority 65 — pile not empty at seal.** Sealing requires at least one addition, else `ERR_PILE_EMPTY` (409).
14. **Priority 66 — kiln available.** Lighting requires the kiln's `availability_state` to be `AVAILABLE` and no other run on that kiln to be `BURNING`, else `ERR_KILN_UNAVAILABLE` (409).
15. **Priority 67 — pile sealed for light.** Lighting requires the run's pile to be `SEALED`, else `ERR_INVALID_PILE_STATE` (409).
16. **Priority 68 — feed ready for grinding.** Starting grinding requires `feed_state` to be `FEED_READY`, else `ERR_BATCH_FEED_SHORT` (409).
17. **Priority 70 — chlorine within permit.** On a fuel charge: the run's chlorine input after the charge is within the plant's per-run chlorine ceiling, else `ERR_CHLORINE_INPUT_EXCEEDED` (422). This safety stop deliberately outranks the burn-test window checks below it, so a chlorine excursion is reported even when the fuel's authorization has also lapsed.
18. **Priority 72 — substitution rate within ceiling.** On a fuel charge: the run's thermal substitution rate after the charge is within the effective ceiling of §3.5, else `ERR_TSR_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422).
19. **Priority 74 — free lime in spec for release.** Clinker release requires at least one free-lime test on the run whose newest value is within the plant ceiling, else `ERR_FREE_LIME_OUT_OF_SPEC` (422).
20. **Priority 76 — rolling NOx intensity within permit.** Clinker release requires the plant's rolling NOx intensity to be within the permit cap, else `ERR_PERMIT_NOX_INTENSITY_EXCEEDED` (422).
21. **Priority 80 — burn-test authorization current.** On a fuel charge of an alternative fuel: an authorized trial burn must exist for that plant and fuel, else `ERR_BURN_TEST_NOT_AUTHORIZED` (422); and its window must cover the clock, else `ERR_BURN_TEST_EXPIRED` (422).
22. **Priority 81 — window ordering.** On kiln-run creation, outage creation and loadout order creation: `window_start` is strictly earlier than `window_end`, and `window_start` is at or after the logical clock, else `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (422).
23. **Priority 82 — run window active for light.** Lighting requires the clock to be at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end`, else `ERR_RUN_WINDOW_NOT_ACTIVE` (422).
24. **Priority 83 — run window elapsed for complete.** Completing requires the clock to be at or after `window_end`, else `ERR_RUN_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED` (422).
25. **Priority 84 — loadout window active.** Starting loading requires the clock to be at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end`, else `ERR_LOADOUT_WINDOW_NOT_ACTIVE` (422).
26. **Priority 85 — kiln window free of other runs.** On kiln-run creation, else `ERR_KILN_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
27. **Priority 86 — kiln window clear of outages.** On kiln-run creation, else `ERR_KILN_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
28. **Priority 87 — outage window free.** On outage creation and on outage approval, else `ERR_OUTAGE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
29. **Priority 88 — silo kind matches.** On kiln-run creation, cement batch creation and loadout order creation, else `ERR_SILO_KIND_MISMATCH` (422).
30. **Priority 89 — laboratory subject valid.** On recording a laboratory test, else `ERR_TEST_SUBJECT_INVALID` (422).
31. **Priority 90 — laboratory subject state valid.** On recording a laboratory test, else `ERR_LAB_SUBJECT_STATE_INVALID` (409).
32. **Priority 91 — design components sum.** On design creation and design amendment, else `ERR_COMPONENTS_SUM_INVALID` (422).
33. **Priority 92 — design module within tolerance.** On design submission, else `ERR_MODULE_TARGET_DEVIATION` (422).
34. **Priority 93 — revision budget remaining.** On requesting a revision, else `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409).
35. **Priority 94 — alternative raw substitution within cap.** On appending a pile addition, else `ERR_ALT_RAW_SUBSTITUTION_EXCEEDED` (422).
36. **Priority 95 — pile lime saturation on target at seal.** On sealing a pile, else `ERR_PILE_LSF_OFF_TARGET` (422).
37. **Priority 96 — interground limestone within cement-type cap.** On cement batch creation, else `ERR_LIMESTONE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). This rung deliberately outranks the capacity rungs below it.
38. **Priority 97 — sulphate within cement-type cap.** On cement batch creation, else `ERR_SO3_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422).
39. **Priority 98 — batch qualified for certificate.** On certificate issue, else `ERR_BATCH_NOT_QUALIFIED` (409).
40. **Priority 99 — customer specification met.** On certificate issue, else `ERR_CUSTOMER_SPEC_NOT_MET` (422).
41. **Priority 100 — NOx budget available.** On a fuel charge, else `ERR_NOX_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (422).
42. **Priority 101 — kiln feed sufficient.** On kiln-run completion, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_KILN_FEED` (422).
43. **Priority 102 — silo capacity available.** On kiln-run completion, on finish of grinding, and on voiding a completed loadout order, else `ERR_SILO_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422).
44. **Priority 103 — clinker availability.** On cement batch creation, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CLINKER` (422).
45. **Priority 104 — cement availability.** On loadout order creation, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CEMENT` (422).
46. **Priority 105 — certificate allocation available.** On loadout order creation: the certificate is `ISSUED`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_ISSUED` (422); its `customer_id` equals the order's `customer_id`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATE_CUSTOMER_MISMATCH` (422); and the allocation has room, else `ERR_CERTIFICATE_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDED` (422).
47. **Priority 106 — certified tonnes within batch.** On certificate issue, else `ERR_CERTIFIED_TONNES_EXCEED_BATCH` (422).
48. **Priority 107 — spout and draw-rate capacity.** On loadout order creation: spout contention yields `ERR_SILO_SPOUT_CAPACITY` (422) and an over-rate tonnage yields `ERR_DRAW_RATE_EXCEEDED` (422).
49. **Priority 108 — peak-season outage override.** On outage creation, else `ERR_PEAK_SEASON_OUTAGE` (422).
50. **Priority 109 — distribution plan has candidates.** On the distribution-plan read, else `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CUSTOMERS` (422).

Three collisions in this ladder are worth stating explicitly, because a naive implementation orders them the other way. A cement batch created against a clinker silo that is both `BLOCKED` and short of stock reports `ERR_SILO_BLOCKED`, not `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CLINKER`, because the state rung at priority 62 precedes the fiscal rung at priority 103. A chlorine-rich charge of a fuel whose trial-burn authorization has also lapsed reports `ERR_CHLORINE_INPUT_EXCEEDED`, not `ERR_BURN_TEST_EXPIRED`, because the safety rung at priority 70 precedes the temporal rung at priority 80. And a `TYPE_I` batch asking for 8.00 percent interground limestone against an under-stocked silo reports `ERR_LIMESTONE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, not `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CLINKER`, because the constraint rung at priority 96 precedes the fiscal rung at priority 103.

### 2.9. Silo Inventory Accounting

A silo is the plant's unit of physical storage, and the single most common source of double-promised tonnage in the paper-based world this API replaces. Each silo is registered against a plant with a `code` that is unique within that plant, a `material_kind` of either `CLINKER` or `CEMENT`, a physical `capacity_tonnes`, a `draw_rate_tonnes_per_hour` describing the fastest rate at which material can be extracted, and a `loadout_spouts` count describing how many loading appointments may overlap in time on that silo. `loadout_spouts` defaults to 1 when the caller omits it.

Stock in a silo is decomposed into four figures, all carried to two decimals and all recomputed by the system rather than supplied by callers. `tonnes_on_hand` is the physical stock actually present. `reserved_tonnes` is stock already promised — to `PLANNED` cement batches in the case of a clinker silo, or to `RESERVED` and `LOADING` loadout orders in the case of a cement silo. `unreleased_tonnes` is stock physically present but not yet usable or sellable: clinker belonging to kiln runs whose quality dimension is still `PENDING_LAB` or has fallen to `QUARANTINED` in a clinker silo, and cement belonging to batches that have not yet reached `QUALIFIED` in a cement silo. `available_tonnes` is what remains once both encumbrances are subtracted: it is the physical stock minus the reserved stock minus the unreleased stock, rounded to two decimals. All four figures begin at 0.00 when the silo is created.

`available_tonnes` may dip below zero for a single intermediate step inside a multi-hop cascade — the moment a withdrawal has been applied but the reservations that withdrawal invalidates have not yet been released. It is always recomputed to a final non-negative value by the time the cascade that caused the dip finishes, and no response ever exposes a negative availability as a settled state.

A silo's `status` is either `OPEN` or `BLOCKED`, starting at `OPEN`. A `BLOCKED` silo accepts no new draws and no new loadout orders, and blocking one has cascading consequences on whatever was already promised out of it (§3.6 and §3.8). When a silo is blocked, the reason is retained in `block_reason`; when it returns to `OPEN`, that reason is cleared. Every operation that names a silo also checks that the silo plays the right role: a `clinker_silo_id` must point at a silo whose `material_kind` is `CLINKER`, a `cement_silo_id` at one whose `material_kind` is `CEMENT`, and every silo named in a request must belong to the same plant as the operation itself. A mismatch on any of these is refused with `ERR_SILO_KIND_MISMATCH`.

Cement mass is conserved at plant level. The plant's `cement_tonne_balance_tonnes` is the sum of `tonnes_on_hand` across all of the plant's silos whose `material_kind` is `CEMENT`, plus the sum of `loaded_tonnes` across all loadout orders on those silos whose status is `COMPLETED`, rounded to two decimals. Cement is therefore either standing in a silo or shipped on a completed order — never both, never neither. Completing a loadout order leaves this balance unchanged because it moves tonnes from silo stock into shipped stock, and voiding a completed order leaves it unchanged because it moves them back; the balance changes only when cement is newly created by finish-grinding, or when the plant's set of silos changes. An implementation that drifts by 0.01 on the void path has got the compensation wrong.

### 2.10. The Air Permit Envelope

Each plant operates inside an air permit expressed as five numbers recorded on the plant when it is registered. `permit_nox_kg_per_tonne_clinker_cap` is the ceiling on the plant's **rolling** NOx intensity in kilograms of NOx per tonne of clinker, and it is this figure — not any single run's intensity — that gates clinker release. `permit_annual_nox_budget_kg` is the annual mass-based NOx allowance in kilograms. `permit_max_tsr_pct` is the ceiling on thermal substitution rate, the percentage of a kiln run's thermal energy that may come from alternative fuels. `permit_max_chlorine_kg_per_run` is the ceiling on total chlorine input in kilograms charged into a single kiln run. `clinker_free_lime_max_pct` is the inclusive maximum acceptable free lime percentage in clinker.

Two derived permit figures live on the plant. `permit_nox_budget_remaining_kg` is the annual allowance minus the sum of `nox_emitted_kg` over all kiln runs on kilns of that plant whose `process_state` is `SCHEDULED`, `BURNING` or `COMPLETED`, rounded to two decimals. `rolling_nox_intensity_kg_per_tonne` is the plant's rolling-window intensity, computed to three decimals as described in §3.6. A new plant starts with `permit_nox_budget_remaining_kg` equal to `permit_annual_nox_budget_kg`, `rolling_nox_intensity_kg_per_tonne` at 0.000, `cement_tonne_balance_tonnes` at 0.00 and `production_posture` at `NORMAL`.

The annual budget is a conserved quantity, not a high-water mark. Charged fuel consumes budget the instant it is charged, and aborting the run gives every kilogram back: an abort zeroes the run's `nox_emitted_kg` and simultaneously moves the run out of the set the budget sums over, so the remaining budget returns to exactly the figure it held before that run's fuel was charged.

Alongside the permit numbers, each plant records a `name`, a market `region_code` such as `US-MW`, and `peak_season_months` — an array of integers from 1 to 12 marking the months of peak cement demand. Those months matter twice: they force an override on outages (§3.4) and they scale contracted volumes in distribution planning (§3.9).

### 2.11. Cement Type Specifications

Four cement types exist: `TYPE_I`, `TYPE_IL`, `TYPE_II` and `TYPE_III`. Each carries a fixed specification that constrains proportioning at batch creation and acceptance at laboratory time. These four rows are constants of the system, not configurable data:

| Cement type | Max interground limestone (%) | Max cement SO3 (%) | Blaine band (m²/kg) | Min 28-day strength (MPa) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `TYPE_I` | 5.00 | 3.00 | 340 to 420 | 28.0 |
| `TYPE_IL` | 15.00 | 3.00 | 380 to 460 | 28.0 |
| `TYPE_II` | 5.00 | 3.00 | 320 to 400 | 28.0 |
| `TYPE_III` | 5.00 | 4.50 | 500 to 600 | 24.0 |

Every bound in this table is inclusive. A Blaine reading of 380 m²/kg is in specification for a `TYPE_IL` batch and out of specification for a `TYPE_III` batch, whose band begins at 500; the same number therefore passes one batch and fails another, which is why acceptance bounds are always evaluated against the subject's own cement type.

Two further proportioning constants apply to every type: gypsum must occupy between 2.00 and 7.00 percent of the batch mass inclusive, and the sulphate contributions of the three ingredients are fixed at 0.60 percent SO3 for clinker, 46.50 percent SO3 for gypsum and 0.05 percent SO3 for interground limestone.

### 2.12. Cascade Propagation, Compensation and Plant Posture

Several operations in this system mutate more than one row. Whenever a downstream row is mutated as a consequence of an upstream operation, that downstream row's `version` increments and its `updated_at` is refreshed exactly as if it had been written directly — the caller of the upstream operation sees only its own resource in `data`, but a subsequent read of the downstream row shows the advanced version. This matters commercially, because those version bumps are what invalidate other clients' stale reads under §2.6.

Cascades are described hop by hop in §3, and each hop is caused by the hop before it rather than by the original request. The two deepest chains run from a clinker release or re-quarantine outward across a silo, its planned batches and the plant's posture, and from a certificate revocation outward across a batch, a silo, its loadout orders and their customers. Implementations must apply the hops in the stated order, because intermediate figures such as a silo's `available_tonnes` are read by later hops.

The plant's `production_posture` is a computed summary of the feed situation, taking the value `NORMAL` or `CONSTRAINED`. It reads `CONSTRAINED` while at least one cement batch of that plant has `feed_state` equal to `FEED_SHORT`, and `NORMAL` otherwise. It is never set directly by a caller; it is recomputed at the end of any cascade that moves a batch into or out of `FEED_SHORT`.

Finally, two operations in this system are explicitly **compensating**: aborting a kiln run and voiding a completed loadout order. Each is designed to return a conserved quantity — the NOx budget in the first case, the plant cement balance in the second — to precisely the value it held before the act being compensated, with no residual drift.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Designing the Raw Mix

Before anything can be burned, the quarry's materials must be catalogued. A `ProcessEngineer` or a `PlantAdmin` registers each material with a globally unique `code`, a `category` drawn from `LIMESTONE`, `CLAY`, `IRON_ORE`, `SAND` and `ALT_RAW`, and its dry-basis chemistry: `cao_pct`, `sio2_pct`, `al2o3_pct`, `fe2o3_pct` and `so3_pct`. Two commercial attributes accompany the chemistry — `moisture_pct`, the free moisture as delivered expressed as a percentage of as-received mass, and `cost_per_tonne`, the delivered cost per as-received tonne. Materials that are industrial by-products rather than quarried rock are flagged with `is_alternative` set to true, which defaults to false, and carry a `max_substitution_pct` giving the largest share of a pile's as-received tonnage that material may occupy; that cap defaults to 0 and is only meaningful on alternative materials. Fly ash, granulated slag and spent foundry sand are the typical cases. The catalogue can be browsed with optional filters on `category` and `is_alternative`.

A raw mix design is the plant's chemical recipe. A `ProcessEngineer` authors one against a `plant_id` with a `code` unique within that plant, three module targets — `target_lsf` for lime saturation factor expressed as a percentage, `target_sm` for silica modulus and `target_am` for alumina modulus — and an ordered `components` array whose entries each name a `raw_material_id` and a `mass_fraction_pct`. Those mass fractions are dry-basis design fractions. Two tolerances may be supplied at authoring time and default otherwise: `module_tolerance_lsf`, the deviation between the achieved and the targeted lime saturation factor that submission will accept, defaults to 1.50; and `pile_lsf_tolerance`, the deviation the physical pile will be allowed at seal time, defaults to 2.00. The design is born in `DRAFT` with `revision_count` at 0, its author's `X-User-ID` stored in `created_by_user_id`, and `active_pile_count` at 0. Designs can be listed with optional filters on `plant_id`, on `status`, and on `superseded_by_design_id`.

The four **as-designed oxide contents** are recomputed on every authoring or amendment. For each of the four oxides in turn, multiply each component's `mass_fraction_pct` by that component's material content for the oxide, sum those products across the components, divide the sum by 100, and round half up to four decimal places. The results are stored as `as_designed_cao_pct`, `as_designed_sio2_pct`, `as_designed_al2o3_pct` and `as_designed_fe2o3_pct`.

The three **chemical modules** are then computed from those four-decimal oxide figures, never from unrounded intermediates. `as_designed_lsf` is the calcium oxide content divided by the quantity formed as 2.8 times the silica content plus 1.18 times the alumina content plus 0.65 times the iron oxide content, that quotient multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimals. `as_designed_sm` is the silica content divided by the sum of the alumina and iron oxide contents, rounded half up to two decimals. `as_designed_am` is the alumina content divided by the iron oxide content, rounded half up to two decimals.

Submission moves the design `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and is available only while the design is in `DRAFT`; attempting it from any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DESIGN_STATE`. Submission re-derives the four oxides and the three modules before it checks anything, then applies two substantive gates. The recipe must contain at least two components and every `raw_material_id` in it must resolve to a registered material, with the mass fractions summing to exactly 100.00 — an under-populated or unbalanced recipe is refused with `ERR_COMPONENTS_SUM_INVALID`, and an unresolvable material identifier is refused with `ERR_RAW_MATERIAL_NOT_FOUND`. Then the achieved lime saturation must sit near the target: the absolute difference between `as_designed_lsf` and `target_lsf`, rounded half up to two decimals, must be no greater than `module_tolerance_lsf`, with equality accepted. A wider deviation is refused with `ERR_MODULE_TARGET_DEVIATION`. The same component-sum gate also applies at authoring time and at amendment time.

Review by a `QualityManager` has two outcomes, and the recipe deliberately supports a loop between them. Requesting a revision moves the design `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED`, stores the manager's `revision_reason`, and increments `revision_count` by one. A design is allowed three completed revision rounds: the request is permitted only while `revision_count` is strictly less than 3, so the fourth request on the same design is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The engineer then amends the design — supplying any of `components`, `target_lsf`, `target_sm`, `target_am`, `module_tolerance_lsf` or `pile_lsf_tolerance` — which applies the edits, recomputes the oxides and modules, and moves the design `REVISION_REQUESTED → DRAFT`, closing the cycle `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED → DRAFT`. Amendment is also legal on a design that is already in `DRAFT`, in which case the edits and recomputation happen but the status does not move; amendment of a design in any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DESIGN_STATE`.

Approval by a `QualityManager` moves the design `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, records the approver in `approved_by_user_id` and the logical clock instant in `approved_at`. The approver's `X-User-ID` must differ from the design's `created_by_user_id`; an engineer who approves their own recipe is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Approval carries one cascade hop: the plant's previously `APPROVED` design, if one exists, moves `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED`, has its `superseded_by_design_id` set to the newly approved design's identifier, and has its `version` incremented. A superseded design is terminal — nothing further happens to it. Piles that were already `OPEN` or `SEALED` against it stay valid and can still be sealed and burned, because the physical material is already on the ground; only *new* piles against a design that is not `APPROVED` are refused, with `ERR_DESIGN_NOT_APPROVED`.

### 3.2. Blending the Pile

A blended pile is the physical realisation of a recipe on the blending bed, and the place where design chemistry and delivered chemistry part company. A `ProcessEngineer` opens one against a `plant_id`, a `raw_mix_design_id` and a `pile_code` unique within the plant, echoing `expected_raw_mix_design_version` as §2.6 requires. The pile is born in `OPEN` with an empty `additions` array and its four stock figures at zero. Each successful creation increments the design's `active_pile_count` — which counts the piles referencing that design whose status is `OPEN` or `SEALED` — and increments the design's `version`. Piles can be listed with optional filters on `plant_id`, on `raw_mix_design_id` and on `status`.

Material reaches the bed one truck at a time. Appending an addition supplies a `raw_material_id`, the `tonnes_as_received` delivered, and the `moisture_pct` measured **on that delivery**. The `additions` array is append-only: each entry retains its `raw_material_id`, `tonnes_as_received`, `moisture_pct` and the `added_at` instant taken from the logical clock, and nothing ever removes or edits an entry. Additions are accepted only while the pile is `OPEN` and only while its design is still `APPROVED`; an addition to a `SEALED` or `CONSUMED` pile is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PILE_STATE`, and an addition against a design that has left `APPROVED` is refused with `ERR_DESIGN_NOT_APPROVED`.

Every addition recomputes the pile's derived figures. `as_received_tonnes` is the sum of `tonnes_as_received` over the additions, rounded half up to two decimals. Each addition's dry contribution is its as-received tonnage multiplied by one minus its own `moisture_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to two decimals; `dry_tonnes` is the sum of those per-addition rounded dry tonnages, itself rounded half up to two decimals. `alt_raw_share_pct` is the as-received tonnage of additions whose material carries `is_alternative` true, divided by `as_received_tonnes`, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimals.

The **as-blended oxides and modules** use the identical three module formulas of §3.1 but a different mass basis, and that difference is the whole point. For each oxide, multiply each addition's rounded dry tonnage by that addition's material content for the oxide, sum across the additions, divide by the pile's total dry tonnage, and round half up to four decimals, giving `as_blended_cao_pct`, `as_blended_sio2_pct`, `as_blended_al2o3_pct` and `as_blended_fe2o3_pct`. From those four-decimal figures, `as_blended_lsf` is the calcium oxide content divided by the quantity 2.8 times silica plus 1.18 times alumina plus 0.65 times iron oxide, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimals; `as_blended_sm` is silica divided by the sum of alumina and iron oxide, rounded half up to two decimals; and `as_blended_am` is alumina divided by iron oxide, rounded half up to two decimals. Because a 2 percent-moisture limestone gives up almost all its as-received mass to the dry basis while a 12 percent-moisture clay gives up noticeably less, the same recipe fed as identical as-received proportions produces a *physically lime-richer* pile than the design fractions predict. Hitting the design on the ground therefore requires moisture-compensating the as-received tonnages, and the system reports the gap rather than hiding it: `lsf_deviation` is the absolute difference between `as_blended_lsf` and the design's `target_lsf`, rounded half up to two decimals.

Alternative raw materials are capped on an **as-received** basis, deliberately unlike the dry basis the modules use. After each addition, the pile's `alt_raw_share_pct` must be no greater than the smallest `max_substitution_pct` among the alternative materials present in the pile, with equality accepted; an addition that would push the share past that floor is refused with `ERR_ALT_RAW_SUBSTITUTION_EXCEEDED`.

Sealing moves the pile `OPEN → SEALED`, stamps `sealed_at` from the logical clock, freezes `dry_tonnes` against further change, and increments the pile's `version`. Four conditions guard it: the pile must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_INVALID_PILE_STATE`; the pile must have at least one addition, since an empty bed cannot be fed to a kiln, else `ERR_PILE_EMPTY`; the design must still be `APPROVED`, else `ERR_DESIGN_NOT_APPROVED`; and `lsf_deviation` must be no greater than the design's `pile_lsf_tolerance`, with equality accepted, else `ERR_PILE_LSF_OFF_TARGET`. The pile's terminal state is `CONSUMED`, which it reaches automatically when the kiln run referencing it is lit (§3.5); at that moment the pile leaves the `OPEN`/`SEALED` set, so the design's `active_pile_count` is recomputed and the design's `version` increments again. A pile's `consumed_dry_tonnes` records the dry feed the consuming run actually drew, and is set when that run completes.

### 3.3. Qualifying Fuels and Alternative Fuels

Fuels are registered by an `EnvironmentalOfficer` or a `PlantAdmin` with a globally unique `code`, a `category` drawn from `COAL`, `PETCOKE`, `NATURAL_GAS`, `RDF`, `TDF` and `BIOMASS`, and four physical attributes: `lhv_gj_per_tonne`, the lower heating value in gigajoules per as-fired tonne; `nox_kg_per_gj`, the NOx emission factor in kilograms per gigajoule of fuel energy; `co2_kg_per_gj`, the carbon dioxide emission factor on the same basis, recorded for reporting purposes; and `chlorine_pct`, the chlorine content as a percentage of as-fired mass. Fuels that displace conventional fossil fuel carry `is_alternative` set to true, which defaults to false. The register can be listed with optional filters on `category` and `is_alternative`.

Registering an alternative fuel does not make it burnable. A trial burn must first be requested, run, measured and adjudicated, and only an adjudication in the plant's favour opens a time-boxed window in which the fuel may be charged. A trial burn is requested by an `EnvironmentalOfficer` or a `LabTechnician` against a `plant_id` and the `fuel_id` under test, stating the `requested_max_tsr_pct` the plant is asking to be authorised for and the `valid_days` lifetime the authorisation should carry once granted. The trial burn is born `REQUESTED` with `authorized_max_tsr_pct` at 0. Trial burns can be listed with optional filters on `plant_id`, on `fuel_id` and on `status`.

A `LabTechnician` starts the trial burn, moving it `REQUESTED → IN_PROGRESS`, and afterwards records the result, moving it `IN_PROGRESS → RESULT_RECORDED`. Recording captures three measurements — `measured_chlorine_pct`, the chlorine found in the fuel during the burn; `measured_nox_kg_per_gj`, the stack NOx measured during the burn; and `stack_compliant`, whether the stack test met the permit limits — and stamps the recording technician's `X-User-ID` into `result_recorded_by_user_id`. Starting a trial burn that is not `REQUESTED`, or recording a result on one that is not `IN_PROGRESS`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BURN_TEST_STATE`.

Adjudication is the `EnvironmentalOfficer`'s exclusive act and is available only from `RESULT_RECORDED`. It carries a `decision` of either `AUTHORIZED` or `REJECTED`, optionally an `authorized_max_tsr_pct`, and optionally a `decision_reason`. In both branches the adjudicating `X-User-ID` must differ from `result_recorded_by_user_id`, so a technician may not sign off on their own stack test; a violation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

The two branches have deliberately divergent effects. A decision of `AUTHORIZED` moves the trial burn `RESULT_RECORDED → AUTHORIZED` and mints an authorisation: the supplied `authorized_max_tsr_pct` is stored, `valid_from` is stamped with the logical clock, `valid_until` is set to `valid_from` advanced by `valid_days` days, and `adjudicated_by_user_id` records the officer. From that point the referenced fuel is chargeable on that plant's kiln runs for as long as the clock remains strictly earlier than `valid_until`. Two conditions bound this branch: `stack_compliant` must be true, and the `authorized_max_tsr_pct` granted must be no greater than the `requested_max_tsr_pct` asked for and no greater than the plant's `permit_max_tsr_pct` — an officer cannot grant more than was requested, nor more than the permit itself allows. An `AUTHORIZED` decision that violates either of these is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A decision of `REJECTED` moves the trial burn `RESULT_RECORDED → REJECTED` and mints nothing at all: the `decision_reason` is stored, `authorized_max_tsr_pct` stays at 0, and both `valid_from` and `valid_until` stay null. The fuel remains unchargeable. Both `AUTHORIZED` and `REJECTED` are terminal — no further adjudication or restart is possible, and any attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BURN_TEST_STATE`.

Because a plant may re-test the same fuel repeatedly, several authorisation rows can exist for the same plant-and-fuel pair. When the system needs the applicable authorisation, it considers only rows whose `status` is `AUTHORIZED` and whose window covers the clock — that is, `valid_from` at or before the clock and `valid_until` strictly after it — and among those it takes the row with the latest `valid_from`, breaking a tie on the latest `created_at`.

### 3.4. Kiln Assets, Firing Windows and Outage Planning

A kiln is registered by a `PlantAdmin` against a `plant_id` with a `code` unique within that plant and three operating parameters. `nominal_shc_gj_per_tonne` is the nameplate specific heat consumption, and it is used for exactly one purpose — the provisional clinker estimate of §3.5 — and never as the figure that determines reported output. `cooldown_buffer_hours` is the clearance in hours that must separate any firing window on that kiln from any outage window on it. `reline_due_hours` is the number of refractory service hours after which the kiln must be relined. A new kiln has `refractory_hours_since_reline` at 0 and `availability_state` at `AVAILABLE`.

`availability_state` takes one of three values and is derived, never set by a caller. It reads `IN_OUTAGE` while an outage on that kiln is `IN_PROGRESS`. It reads `RELINE_DUE` once `refractory_hours_since_reline` has reached or passed `reline_due_hours`. Otherwise it reads `AVAILABLE`. Kilns can be listed with optional filters on `plant_id` and on `availability_state`.

Scheduling a firing window is shared between `KilnOperator` and `ProcessEngineer`. The scheduling request names the `kiln_id`, the `blended_pile_id` whose sealed material will be fed, the `clinker_silo_id` the resulting clinker will be credited to, and the `window_start` and `window_end` of the intended burn. `scheduled_hours` is computed as the difference between `window_end` and `window_start` expressed in hours, rounded half up to two decimals. The run is born with `process_state` at `SCHEDULED`, `quality_state` at `PENDING_LAB` and an empty `fuel_charges` array. Runs can be listed with optional filters on `kiln_id`, `blended_pile_id`, `clinker_silo_id`, `process_state` and `quality_state`.

Three window rules guard scheduling. First, `window_start` must be strictly earlier than `window_end`, and `window_start` must be at or after the logical clock — nobody schedules a burn into the past — with a violation refused as `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. Second, the new window must not overlap the window of any other run on the same kiln whose `process_state` is `SCHEDULED`, `BURNING` or `COMPLETED`. For this comparison, and for this comparison only, run windows are treated as **closed on both ends**: two runs that merely touch at a single instant still conflict, because a rotary kiln cannot be emptied and recharged instantaneously. Third, the new window must keep at least `cooldown_buffer_hours` of clearance from the window of every outage on that kiln whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `IN_PROGRESS` — either the run's `window_end` advanced by the cooldown buffer is at or before the outage's `window_start`, or the outage's `window_end` advanced by the cooldown buffer is at or before the run's `window_start`. Both the run-overlap and the outage-clearance failures are reported as `ERR_KILN_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. The `clinker_silo_id` must additionally name a `CLINKER` silo belonging to the same plant as the kiln, else `ERR_SILO_KIND_MISMATCH`.

Outages are the counterweight to firing. A `PlantAdmin` or `ProcessEngineer` plans one against a `kiln_id` with a `window_start`, a `window_end` and a `scope` of `REFRACTORY`, `MECHANICAL` or `COMBINED`. The outage is born `PLANNED` with `refractory_replaced` false. Its `demand_season_class` is computed from the calendar months the window intersects and takes the worst — that is, the highest-demand — class among them: `PEAK` if any intersected month is listed in the plant's `peak_season_months`, otherwise `OFF` only if every intersected month falls in December, January or February, and `SHOULDER` in every other case. Because an outage in peak season costs the plant sales it cannot recover, a window intersecting any peak month is permitted only when `peak_season_override` is true **and** the caller is a `PlantAdmin`; that flag defaults to false, and a peak-season window without a `PlantAdmin` override is refused with `ERR_PEAK_SEASON_OUTAGE`. Outage window ordering follows the same rule as firing windows and is likewise refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. Outages can be listed with optional filters on `kiln_id`, on `status` and on `demand_season_class`.

Outage windows must also be free. Both at planning time and again at approval time, the outage window must keep `cooldown_buffer_hours` of clearance from the window of every run on that kiln whose `process_state` is `SCHEDULED` or `BURNING`, and must not overlap — on a half-open comparison, so windows that merely touch are fine — the window of any other outage on that kiln whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `APPROVED` or `IN_PROGRESS`. Either failure is reported as `ERR_OUTAGE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Re-checking at approval time is deliberate: runs may have been scheduled in between.

Approval by a `PlantAdmin` moves the outage `PLANNED → APPROVED` and records `approved_by_user_id`. From that moment the window begins blocking new firing windows within the cooldown buffer, which is why the third scheduling rule above looks at `APPROVED` outages. A `ProcessEngineer` then starts the outage, moving it `APPROVED → IN_PROGRESS`, which requires the logical clock to have reached `window_start` and requires that no run on that kiln is currently `BURNING`; starting stamps `started_at`, drives the kiln's `availability_state` to `IN_OUTAGE` and increments the kiln's `version`.

Completion, also a `ProcessEngineer` act, moves the outage `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`, requires the clock to be at or after `window_start`, stamps `completed_at`, and stores the supplied `refractory_replaced` flag. That flag decides the kiln's fate, and the two branches are equally consequential. When `refractory_replaced` is true, the kiln's `refractory_hours_since_reline` is reset to 0 and its `availability_state` becomes `AVAILABLE`. When `refractory_replaced` is false, the accumulated hours are left completely untouched, so a kiln that had already reached or passed `reline_due_hours` returns to `RELINE_DUE` rather than `AVAILABLE` and still cannot be lit. Either way the kiln's `version` increments. `COMPLETED` is the outage's terminal state; approving, starting or completing an outage from any state other than the one named above is refused with `ERR_INVALID_OUTAGE_STATE`.

### 3.5. Pyroprocessing: Firing, Fuelling and the Two Ways a Run Ends

A kiln run carries two entirely independent state dimensions. `process_state` tracks the pyroprocessing itself and runs `SCHEDULED`, `BURNING`, `COMPLETED` and `ABORTED`. `quality_state` tracks the acceptability of the clinker the run produced and runs `PENDING_LAB`, `QUARANTINED` and `RELEASED`. The two evolve on their own schedules: a run can be `COMPLETED` and still `QUARANTINED`, and the laboratory can move the quality dimension back and forth while the process dimension stays exactly where it is. Only one transition in the system reads both dimensions at once, and §3.6 describes it.

Lighting the kiln is the `KilnOperator`'s act and advances the run `SCHEDULED → BURNING`, stamping `lit_at` from the logical clock. Four gates precede it, in the ladder order of §2.8. The run must be `SCHEDULED`, else `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`. The kiln's `availability_state` must be `AVAILABLE` and no other run on that kiln may be `BURNING`, else `ERR_KILN_UNAVAILABLE` — this is where a `RELINE_DUE` or `IN_OUTAGE` kiln refuses work. The run's pile must be `SEALED`, else `ERR_INVALID_PILE_STATE`. And the logical clock must be inside the half-open firing window, at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end`, else `ERR_RUN_WINDOW_NOT_ACTIVE`. Lighting also consumes the feed: the pile moves `SEALED → CONSUMED`, which removes it from the design's open-and-sealed set, so the design's `active_pile_count` is recomputed and the design's `version` increments.

Fuel is charged into a burning kiln one delivery at a time. A charge names a `fuel_id` and the `tonnes` fired, and appends an entry to the run's append-only `fuel_charges` array recording that `fuel_id`, the `tonnes`, the `energy_gj` the charge contributed, and the `charged_at` instant from the logical clock. A charge's `energy_gj` is its tonnage multiplied by the fuel's `lhv_gj_per_tonne`, rounded half up to two decimals, and it is that already-rounded per-charge figure that all downstream aggregation consumes.

Each charge recomputes the whole thermal picture of the run. **`gross_energy_gj`** is the sum of the per-charge `energy_gj` values, rounded half up to two decimals. **`provisional_clinker_tonnes`** is the gross energy divided by the kiln's `nominal_shc_gj_per_tonne`, rounded half up to two decimals; it exists solely to select a tier and is never the reported output. **`effective_shc_gj_per_tonne`** is then selected from the provisional tonnage on a three-tier ladder, because small burns are thermally wasteful and large burns approach best practice: a provisional tonnage below 600.00 tonnes selects a specific heat consumption of 4.00 gigajoules per tonne; a provisional tonnage from 600.00 tonnes up to but not including 900.00 tonnes selects 3.70; and a provisional tonnage of 900.00 tonnes or more selects 3.45. Both tier boundaries are inclusive upward, so a provisional figure of exactly 600.00 selects 3.70 and one of exactly 900.00 selects 3.45. **`clinker_tonnes`** is finally the gross energy divided by the selected `effective_shc_gj_per_tonne`, rounded half up to two decimals.

This tier selection is a strict single pass. The tier is chosen from the provisional tonnage and is never re-chosen from the final result, even when the final result lands in a different tier from the one that produced it — which happens routinely, since dividing by a smaller heat consumption yields a larger tonnage. An implementation that re-tiers after computing `clinker_tonnes` will report a materially different output and will be wrong.

Three further figures move with every charge. **`tsr_pct`**, the thermal substitution rate, is the summed `energy_gj` of those charges whose fuel carries `is_alternative` true, divided by `gross_energy_gj`, multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to two decimals; it is 0.00 when the run has no energy at all. **`nox_emitted_kg`** is the sum, over the charges, of each charge's tonnage multiplied by its fuel's `lhv_gj_per_tonne` multiplied by its fuel's `nox_kg_per_gj`, each product rounded half up to two decimals before summing, with the sum itself rounded to two decimals. **`chlorine_input_kg`** is the sum, over the charges, of each charge's tonnage multiplied by its fuel's `chlorine_pct` divided by 100 and then multiplied by 1000 to convert tonnes of chlorine into kilograms, each product rounded half up to two decimals before summing, with the sum rounded to two decimals.

The run's own emission intensity, **`nox_intensity_kg_per_tonne`**, is `nox_emitted_kg` divided by `clinker_tonnes`, rounded half up to three decimals, and **`nox_intensity_breach`** is true when that intensity is strictly greater than the plant's `permit_nox_kg_per_tonne_clinker_cap`. It is essential to understand that the per-run figure is purely diagnostic: `nox_intensity_breach` is an informational flag and gates nothing whatsoever. A run may be completed, and its clinker may be released, while its own intensity is above the cap, because the permit is written against the plant's rolling figure (§3.6), not against any single burn.

Four checks guard a charge, and their relative order is deliberate. First, chlorine: the run's `chlorine_input_kg` after the charge must be no greater than the plant's `permit_max_chlorine_kg_per_run`, with equality allowed, else `ERR_CHLORINE_INPUT_EXCEEDED`. This is a safety stop and it deliberately outranks the authorisation-window checks below, so a chlorine excursion is reported even when the fuel's trial-burn authorisation has also lapsed. Second, substitution rate: the `tsr_pct` the run *would* carry after the charge must be no greater than the effective ceiling described next, with equality allowed, else `ERR_TSR_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Third, authorisation currency for alternative fuels. Fourth, budget: the plant's `permit_nox_budget_remaining_kg` after the charge must be at least 0.00, with exactly 0.00 allowed, else `ERR_NOX_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. A `fuel_id` that resolves to nothing is refused with `ERR_FUEL_NOT_FOUND`, and a charge into a run that is not `BURNING` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`.

The **effective substitution ceiling** is not a single configured number; it is the tightest of several. It is the smallest of the plant's `permit_max_tsr_pct` and, for each *distinct* alternative fuel charged on the run, that fuel's `authorized_max_tsr_pct` taken from the applicable trial-burn authorisation selected as §3.3 describes. A second alternative fuel authorised to a lower ceiling therefore collapses the whole run's headroom to that lower figure, retroactively invalidating a mix that was legal a moment earlier. Before the ceiling is computed at all, every alternative fuel in the charge must actually be authorised: if no `AUTHORIZED` trial burn exists for that plant and fuel, the charge is refused with `ERR_BURN_TEST_NOT_AUTHORIZED`; if such a row exists but the clock has reached or passed its `valid_until`, the charge is refused with `ERR_BURN_TEST_EXPIRED`. The ceiling is thus only ever computed over fuels that are genuinely authorised. Conventional fuels — those with `is_alternative` false — require no authorisation and contribute nothing to the alternative energy total.

Completing a run advances it `process_state` `BURNING → COMPLETED`, stamps `completed_at` and freezes `clinker_tonnes` against further change. Completion requires the run to be `BURNING`, else `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`; requires the logical clock to have reached or passed `window_end`, else `ERR_RUN_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`; and requires at least one fuel charge to have been recorded, so that `gross_energy_gj` is greater than zero — a run with nothing fired is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`. Two physical checks then apply. The pile must have been able to feed the burn: each tonne of clinker consumes 1.55 dry tonnes of raw meal, so the clinker tonnage multiplied by 1.55 and rounded half up to two decimals must be no greater than the pile's frozen `dry_tonnes`, with equality allowed, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_KILN_FEED`. And the receiving clinker silo must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_SILO_BLOCKED`, with room for the delivery — its `tonnes_on_hand` plus the clinker tonnage must be no greater than its `capacity_tonnes`, with equality allowed, else `ERR_SILO_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

Completion then writes four consequences beyond the run itself. The clinker silo's `tonnes_on_hand` grows by the clinker tonnage **and** its `unreleased_tonnes` grows by the same amount, because the clinker is now physically present but not yet sellable or usable; its `available_tonnes` is recomputed and its `version` increments. The pile's `consumed_dry_tonnes` is set to the clinker tonnage multiplied by 1.55, rounded half up to two decimals. The kiln's `refractory_hours_since_reline` grows by the run's `scheduled_hours` rounded to a whole number of hours, its `availability_state` becomes `RELINE_DUE` if that accumulation has reached or passed `reline_due_hours`, and its `version` increments. Finally the plant's `rolling_nox_intensity_kg_per_tonne` is recomputed.

Aborting is the compensating alternative to completing, available only from `BURNING` and requiring an `abort_reason`. It advances the run's `process_state` `BURNING → ABORTED`, which is terminal. The compensation is exact: the run's `nox_emitted_kg` is set to 0 and its `clinker_tonnes` is set to 0, and because the run's `process_state` leaves the set of `SCHEDULED`, `BURNING` and `COMPLETED` states that the budget sums over, the plant's `permit_nox_budget_remaining_kg` returns to precisely the value it held before this run's fuel was ever charged. No clinker is credited to any silo. The consumed pile stays `CONSUMED` — the raw meal is physically gone and is not returned — and the plant's `rolling_nox_intensity_kg_per_tonne` is recomputed with the aborted run excluded. Aborting a run that is already `COMPLETED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`.

### 3.6. The Laboratory, Clinker Release and the Availability Cascade

Every measurement in this plant is recorded by a `LabTechnician` as an immutable laboratory test row. A test names its `test_type` — one of `FREE_LIME`, `BLAINE` or `COMPRESSIVE_STRENGTH_28D` — and the measured `value`, and identifies its subject by supplying exactly one of `kiln_run_id` or `cement_batch_id`. The subject is polymorphic and must match the test type: `FREE_LIME` is a clinker measurement and requires a `kiln_run_id` with `cement_batch_id` left null, while `BLAINE` and `COMPRESSIVE_STRENGTH_28D` are cement measurements and require a `cement_batch_id` with `kiln_run_id` left null. Supplying neither subject, supplying both, or supplying the wrong kind for the test type is refused with `ERR_TEST_SUBJECT_INVALID`. The subject must also be in a testable condition: a kiln run under a `FREE_LIME` test must have `process_state` of `BURNING` or `COMPLETED`, and a cement batch under a `BLAINE` or `COMPRESSIVE_STRENGTH_28D` test must have `status` exactly `GROUND`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_LAB_SUBJECT_STATE_INVALID`. The recording technician's `X-User-ID` is stored as `recorded_by_user_id`, and it is that stored value the certificate-issue two-person control later compares against. Tests can be listed with optional filters on `kiln_run_id`, on `cement_batch_id` and on `test_type`, and an unresolvable identifier on a single-test read is refused with `ERR_LAB_TEST_NOT_FOUND`.

A test derives its own acceptance bounds from its type and its subject, which is why the same number can pass one subject and fail another. For `FREE_LIME`, the `unit` is `PCT`, `spec_min` is null because the specification is one-sided, `spec_max` is the plant's `clinker_free_lime_max_pct`, and `in_spec` is true when the `value` is no greater than that inclusive ceiling. For `BLAINE`, the `unit` is `M2_PER_KG`, `spec_min` and `spec_max` are the lower and upper ends of the batch's cement-type Blaine band from §2.11, and `in_spec` is true when the `value` lies within that band inclusive at both ends. For `COMPRESSIVE_STRENGTH_28D`, the `unit` is `MPA`, `spec_min` is the batch's cement-type minimum 28-day strength, `spec_max` is null, and `in_spec` is true when the `value` is at or above that inclusive floor. Free-lime and strength values are carried to two decimals; Blaine values are whole numbers of square metres per kilogram.

A free-lime result drives the run's quality dimension directly, without any operator action. The run's `free_lime_pct` always reflects the newest `FREE_LIME` test on that run, taken by `created_at` descending. When a new free-lime reading exceeds the plant's `clinker_free_lime_max_pct` and the run is currently `PENDING_LAB`, the quality dimension moves `PENDING_LAB → QUARANTINED`, the reading is reflected in `free_lime_pct`, and the run's `version` increments; this transition requires the run's `process_state` to be `BURNING` or `COMPLETED`. When a later reading comes back at or below the ceiling on a run that is currently `QUARANTINED`, the quality dimension moves `QUARANTINED → PENDING_LAB` and the run's `version` increments again. The pair forms a retest loop: quality can oscillate `PENDING_LAB → QUARANTINED → PENDING_LAB` as many times as the laboratory reports, while the process dimension stays exactly where it was.

The plant's **rolling NOx intensity** is what the air permit actually gates on. It is computed over the runs on that plant's kilns whose `process_state` is `COMPLETED` and whose `window_end` falls within the rolling thirty-day lookback — at or after the instant thirty days before the logical clock, and at or before the clock itself. Runs that are `ABORTED`, `SCHEDULED` or `BURNING` are excluded entirely. `rolling_nox_intensity_kg_per_tonne` is the sum of `nox_emitted_kg` over that set divided by the sum of `clinker_tonnes` over the same set, rounded half up to three decimals. When the set is empty the value is 0.000 and the release gate below passes trivially. Because the figure aggregates the whole month, a dirty coal-fired run can be released on the strength of a clean gas-fired run that preceded it, and the same dirty run cannot be released once the clean run is aborted out of the window — the aggregate, not the individual burn, is the legal quantity.

Releasing clinker is the `QualityManager`'s act and it is the one transition that reads both of a run's dimensions at once: the run's `process_state` must be `COMPLETED` **and** its `quality_state` must be `PENDING_LAB`. A release attempted on a run that is still `BURNING`, or one whose quality dimension sits at `QUARANTINED` or already at `RELEASED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`. Three further gates then apply in ladder order: the receiving clinker silo must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_SILO_BLOCKED`; the run must have at least one `FREE_LIME` test recorded and its newest reading must be no greater than the plant's `clinker_free_lime_max_pct`, else `ERR_FREE_LIME_OUT_OF_SPEC` — a run with no free-lime test at all fails this gate exactly as an out-of-specification one does; and the plant's `rolling_nox_intensity_kg_per_tonne` must be no greater than its `permit_nox_kg_per_tonne_clinker_cap`, with equality allowed, else `ERR_PERMIT_NOX_INTENSITY_EXCEEDED`.

A successful release advances the quality dimension `PENDING_LAB → RELEASED`, increments the run's `version`, and then runs a three-hop cascade whose hops must be applied in order because each reads the state the previous one left behind. **Hop one**, caused by the release itself: the clinker silo's `unreleased_tonnes` is reduced by the run's `clinker_tonnes`, its `available_tonnes` is recomputed upward accordingly, and its `version` increments. **Hop two**, caused by hop one now that the silo has genuine availability: every cement batch drawing on that silo whose `feed_state` is `FEED_SHORT` is walked in `created_at` ascending order, and each one whose `clinker_tonnes_drawn` is no greater than the silo's *current* `available_tonnes` returns to `feed_state` `FEED_READY` and re-reserves its draw — the silo's `reserved_tonnes` grows by that batch's `clinker_tonnes_drawn` and `available_tonnes` is recomputed after each batch, so an early large batch can consume the headroom a later small one would otherwise have used. **Hop three**, caused by hop two once no cement batch of the plant remains `FEED_SHORT`: the plant's `production_posture` returns to `NORMAL`.

The reverse is equally consequential and is triggered by the laboratory rather than by an operator. When a free-lime reading above the ceiling arrives on a run whose quality dimension is already `RELEASED`, the run moves `RELEASED → QUARANTINED`, `free_lime_pct` reflects the new reading, and the run's `version` increments — and a three-hop withdrawal cascade follows. **Hop one**, caused by the re-quarantine: the clinker silo's `unreleased_tonnes` grows by the run's `clinker_tonnes` again, its `available_tonnes` is recomputed downward, and its `version` increments. **Hop two**, caused by hop one now that availability no longer covers what was promised: every cement batch on that silo whose `status` is `PLANNED` and whose `clinker_tonnes_drawn` exceeds the silo's recomputed `available_tonnes` moves to `feed_state` `FEED_SHORT` and has its reservation released, so the silo's `reserved_tonnes` shrinks by that batch's `clinker_tonnes_drawn`; the batches are walked in `created_at` ascending order. **Hop three**, caused by hop two once at least one batch of the plant is `FEED_SHORT`: the plant's `production_posture` becomes `CONSTRAINED`. It is during this cascade that a silo's `available_tonnes` may transiently read negative, as §2.9 warns; by the time the cascade finishes and the invalidated reservations have been released, it is non-negative again.

### 3.7. Finish Milling and Cement Qualification

Released clinker becomes a product only after the finish mill. A `ProcessEngineer` plans a cement batch against a `plant_id`, the `clinker_silo_id` it will draw from, the `cement_silo_id` the ground product will be deposited into, a `cement_type` of `TYPE_I`, `TYPE_IL`, `TYPE_II` or `TYPE_III`, the `clinker_tonnes_drawn` it intends to consume, the `gypsum_pct` and `limestone_pct` shares of total batch mass, and the `blaine_target_m2_per_kg` fineness the mill will aim for — echoing `expected_clinker_silo_version` as §2.6 requires. Batches can be listed with optional filters on `plant_id`, `clinker_silo_id`, `cement_silo_id`, `status` and `feed_state`.

Proportioning is computed at creation time in four ordered steps, and the rounding discipline of §2.5 is load-bearing throughout. First, `total_batch_tonnes` is the drawn clinker tonnage divided by the quantity one minus the sum of `gypsum_pct` and `limestone_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to two decimals; it is an intermediate figure and is never the reported product tonnage. Second, `gypsum_tonnes` is that total multiplied by `gypsum_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to two decimals, and `limestone_tonnes` is the same total multiplied by `limestone_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to two decimals. Third, `cement_tonnes` — the reported product tonnage — is the drawn clinker tonnage plus those two *already-rounded* component tonnages, that sum rounded half up to two decimals. Rounding before summing is mandatory here, and it is precisely why `cement_tonnes` can differ from `total_batch_tonnes` in the last decimal place; the round-then-sum figure is the correct one. Fourth, the blended sulphate content `so3_pct` is computed from the step-two tonnages and the step-three total: the drawn clinker tonnage multiplied by 0.60, plus `gypsum_tonnes` multiplied by 46.50, plus `limestone_tonnes` multiplied by 0.05, that sum divided by `cement_tonnes`, rounded half up to two decimals.

Four proportioning gates guard creation, and their ladder positions matter. `gypsum_pct` must lie between 2.00 and 7.00 inclusive and `blaine_target_m2_per_kg` must sit inside the cement type's Blaine band from §2.11 — either violation is a shape error and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `limestone_pct` must be no greater than the cement type's interground limestone cap, with equality allowed, else `ERR_LIMESTONE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`; a `TYPE_IL` batch may carry up to 15.00 percent while the other three types stop at 5.00 percent. The computed `so3_pct` must be no greater than the cement type's sulphate ceiling, with equality allowed, else `ERR_SO3_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`; that ceiling is 3.00 percent for `TYPE_I`, `TYPE_IL` and `TYPE_II`, and 4.50 percent for the rapid-hardening `TYPE_III`. Because a high gypsum share drives sulphate hard — gypsum is 46.50 percent SO3 against clinker's 0.60 — a batch can satisfy the gypsum shape rule and still fail the sulphate ceiling.

Creation also touches the physical inventory. Both named silos must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_SILO_BLOCKED`, and each must be of the right kind and belong to the same plant, else `ERR_SILO_KIND_MISMATCH`. The clinker silo's `available_tonnes` must cover the draw — `clinker_tonnes_drawn` no greater than that availability, with equality allowed — else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CLINKER`. A successful creation leaves the batch in `status` `PLANNED` with `feed_state` `FEED_READY`, `strength_branch_state` and `fineness_branch_state` both `PENDING`, and `certified_tonnes_allocated` at 0; it reserves the draw by growing the clinker silo's `reserved_tonnes` by `clinker_tonnes_drawn`, recomputing that silo's `available_tonnes`, and incrementing its `version`.

`feed_state` is a dimension of its own, independent of `status` and of the two laboratory branches, and it takes the values `FEED_READY` and `FEED_SHORT`. Only a batch whose `status` is `PLANNED` ever moves between them: it falls `FEED_READY → FEED_SHORT` when the clinker silo loses availability — a released run re-quarantined, or the silo blocked — and its `clinker_tonnes_drawn` exceeds the silo's availability after that withdrawal, releasing its reservation as it goes; and it recovers `FEED_SHORT → FEED_READY` when the silo regains availability and, at the moment this particular batch is reconciled in `created_at` ascending order, its `clinker_tonnes_drawn` is covered by the silo's then-current `available_tonnes`, re-taking its reservation. Each move increments the batch's `version`.

Grinding is the `KilnOperator`'s work. Starting grinding advances the batch `PLANNED → GRINDING` and stamps `grinding_started_at`. It requires `feed_state` to be `FEED_READY`, else `ERR_BATCH_FEED_SHORT` — this is the check that stops a batch whose clinker was quietly pulled out from under it — and requires both the clinker and the cement silo to be `OPEN`, else `ERR_SILO_BLOCKED`. Starting converts the reservation into a physical draw: the clinker silo's `tonnes_on_hand` shrinks by `clinker_tonnes_drawn` **and** its `reserved_tonnes` shrinks by the same amount, its `available_tonnes` is recomputed, and its `version` increments.

Finishing grinding advances the batch `GRINDING → GROUND` and stamps `ground_at`. The cement silo must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_SILO_BLOCKED`, and must have room — its `tonnes_on_hand` plus the batch's `cement_tonnes` no greater than its `capacity_tonnes`, with equality allowed — else `ERR_SILO_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The finished cement is credited to the cement silo by growing `tonnes_on_hand` by `cement_tonnes` **and** growing `unreleased_tonnes` by the same amount, because ground cement is not sellable before the batch qualifies; the silo's `available_tonnes` is recomputed and its `version` increments. Finishing also opens the two laboratory branches, leaving `strength_branch_state` and `fineness_branch_state` both at `PENDING`. Starting grinding on a batch that is not `PLANNED`, or finishing grinding on one that is not `GRINDING`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`.

Qualification is a fork and join over two independent branches, each driven purely by laboratory results on a `GROUND` batch. The strength branch moves `PENDING → PASSED` when a 28-day compressive strength result comes in at or above the cement type's minimum strength, and `PENDING → FAILED` when it comes in below; the fineness branch moves `PENDING → PASSED` when a Blaine result lands inside the cement type's band inclusive at both ends, and `PENDING → FAILED` when it lands outside that band. Every one of these four moves increments the batch's `version`, and in each case the measured number itself stays on the immutable laboratory row, which carries `in_spec` true on the passing moves and `in_spec` false on the failing ones.

The join fires only when the *second* of the two branches reaches `PASSED`: the batch then moves `GROUND → QUALIFIED`, stamps `qualified_at`, increments its `version`, and its cement becomes sellable — the cement silo's `unreleased_tonnes` shrinks by `cement_tonnes`, its `available_tonnes` is recomputed and its `version` increments. One passing branch on its own leaves the batch sitting in `GROUND` with no inventory effect whatsoever.

A failure on either branch short-circuits the join. The batch moves `GROUND → REJECTED`, its `rejection_reason` is set to `STRENGTH_OUT_OF_SPEC` when the strength branch failed or `FINENESS_OUT_OF_SPEC` when the fineness branch failed, and its `version` increments. `REJECTED` is terminal, and its inventory consequence is permanent: the batch's cement stays counted in the cement silo's `unreleased_tonnes` for good, so it is never sellable and never certifiable. `rejection_reason` is null on every batch that is not `REJECTED`.

Two further status moves belong to the commercial chain of §3.9 rather than to the mill, but they complete the batch's life. A batch moves `QUALIFIED → CERTIFIED` when a mill certificate covering it is issued, and `CERTIFIED → DECERTIFIED` — terminally — when that certificate is revoked. Throughout, `certified_tonnes_allocated` is the sum of `certified_tonnes` across the certificates for that batch whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `ISSUED`, recomputed from those certificates on every read rather than carried as a stored counter — so merely drafting a certificate raises it at once, before that certificate is ever issued — with one exception: on a `DECERTIFIED` batch it reads 0.00 whatever certificates the batch has.

### 3.8. Silo Blocking and Feed Reconciliation

A `PlantAdmin` may take a silo out of service, and quality may do so indirectly through the revocation cascade of §3.9. Either way the silo moves `OPEN → BLOCKED`, stores the supplied `block_reason`, and increments its `version`. A blocked silo accepts no new draws and no new loadout orders; that refusal surfaces as `ERR_SILO_BLOCKED` at every one of the seven operations listed at priority 62 in §2.8.

Blocking does not merely stop new promises — it withdraws the ones already made, and the withdrawal differs by the silo's `material_kind`. When a `CEMENT` silo is blocked, every loadout order on it whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `LOADING` moves to `CANCELLED_BY_QUALITY` with its `void_reason` set to the silo's `block_reason`, the silo's `reserved_tonnes` is reduced by each cancelled order's `tonnes`, and each affected customer's `open_reserved_tonnes` is recomputed downward. When a `CLINKER` silo is blocked, every cement batch whose `status` is `PLANNED` and which draws on that silo moves to `feed_state` `FEED_SHORT`, its reservation is released so the silo's `reserved_tonnes` shrinks by that batch's `clinker_tonnes_drawn`, and the plant's `production_posture` becomes `CONSTRAINED`.

Returning a silo to service moves it `BLOCKED → OPEN`, clears `block_reason` and increments its `version`. Unblocking then runs exactly the feed reconciliation of hops two and three of the release cascade in §3.6: the `FEED_SHORT` batches on that silo are walked in `created_at` ascending order and each one whose `clinker_tonnes_drawn` is covered by the silo's then-current `available_tonnes` is restored to `FEED_READY` with its reservation re-taken, and once no batch of the plant remains `FEED_SHORT` the plant's `production_posture` returns to `NORMAL`. Note the asymmetry that follows from the two branches above: unblocking a cement silo does **not** resurrect the loadout orders that blocking it cancelled, because `CANCELLED_BY_QUALITY` is terminal. Blocking a silo that is already `BLOCKED`, or unblocking one that is already `OPEN`, is not a legal transition. Silos can be listed with optional filters on `plant_id`, on `material_kind` and on `status`.

### 3.9. Customers, Certification, Loadout and Distribution Planning

Customers are maintained jointly by `PlantAdmin` and `LogisticsPlanner`. A customer record carries a legal `name`, a delivery `region_code`, a `contract_state` of `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` that defaults to `ACTIVE`, a `contract_priority` where a **higher** value is served earlier in the allocation waterfall, a `contract_monthly_tonnes` base volume before any seasonal scaling, the `required_cement_type` that is the only type this customer accepts, a `max_so3_pct` specification ceiling on cement sulphate, and a `credit_hold` flag defaulting to false. Two figures are derived: `open_reserved_tonnes` is the sum of `tonnes` over that customer's loadout orders whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `LOADING`, to two decimals; and `lifetime_loaded_tonnes` is the sum of `loaded_tonnes` over that customer's `COMPLETED` orders, to two decimals, starting at 0 and decremented again whenever a completed order is voided. Amendments may change `contract_state`, `credit_hold`, `contract_priority`, `contract_monthly_tonnes` or `max_so3_pct`, and each amendment increments the customer's `version`. A customer moved to `SUSPENDED`, or placed on `credit_hold`, drops out of distribution planning immediately but keeps its existing loadout orders untouched. Customers can be listed with optional filters on `region_code` and on `contract_state`, and an unresolvable customer identifier anywhere is refused with `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`.

A mill certificate is the document a customer's own laboratory audits, and it is the `QualityManager`'s instrument. Drafting one names the `cement_batch_id` being certified, the `customer_id` it is issued to, and the `certified_tonnes` of that batch the document covers. The draft immediately snapshots the batch's laboratory record: `certified_blaine_m2_per_kg` takes the `value` of the batch's newest in-specification `BLAINE` test by `created_at` descending, and `certified_strength_mpa` takes the `value` of the batch's newest in-specification `COMPRESSIVE_STRENGTH_28D` test on the same ordering. The certificate is born `DRAFT` with `allocated_tonnes` at 0. Certificates can be listed with optional filters on `cement_batch_id`, on `customer_id` and on `status`; an unresolvable certificate identifier is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND`, and an unresolvable batch identifier with `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`.

Issuing moves the certificate `DRAFT → ISSUED`, records `issued_by_user_id` and the logical-clock `issued_at`, and sets `certified_so3_pct` to the batch's `so3_pct` as it stands at that moment. Issuing from any state other than `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CERTIFICATE_STATE`. Four substantive gates then apply, in ladder order. The issuing `X-User-ID` must differ from the `recorded_by_user_id` of the batch's newest 28-day strength test, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the person who measured the strength may not certify against it. The batch's `status` must be `QUALIFIED` or already `CERTIFIED`, else `ERR_BATCH_NOT_QUALIFIED`; a `GROUND`, `REJECTED` or `DECERTIFIED` batch cannot be certified. The customer's specification must be met: the batch's `cement_type` must equal the customer's `required_cement_type` **and** the batch's `so3_pct` must be no greater than the customer's `max_so3_pct`, else `ERR_CUSTOMER_SPEC_NOT_MET`. And the batch must have the tonnage to give: the sum of `certified_tonnes` across the batch's **other** certificates whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `ISSUED`, plus this certificate's own `certified_tonnes`, that sum rounded half up to two decimals, must be no greater than the batch's `cement_tonnes`, with equality allowed, else `ERR_CERTIFIED_TONNES_EXCEED_BATCH`. The certificate being issued is deliberately excluded from the sum, because it is itself a `DRAFT` and is therefore already counted inside the batch's `certified_tonnes_allocated`; counting it twice would make a certificate covering the batch's whole `cement_tonnes` impossible to issue. Issuing also advances the batch: a `QUALIFIED` batch moves `QUALIFIED → CERTIFIED` and has its `version` incremented, while a batch that is already `CERTIFIED` from an earlier certificate is left entirely untouched by the issue. Issuing does not itself move `certified_tonnes_allocated` either, since the certificate was already counted in that sum while it was `DRAFT`; the figure simply goes on reading the definition given in §3.7.

Revocation is the deepest chain in the system. It requires the certificate to be `ISSUED`, else `ERR_INVALID_CERTIFICATE_STATE`, takes a `revoke_reason`, and moves the certificate `ISSUED → REVOKED` while stamping `revoked_at` from the logical clock; `revoked_at` is null while a certificate is `DRAFT` or `ISSUED`. Four hops follow, each caused by the one before it, and each must be applied in order. **Hop one**, caused by the revocation: the referenced batch moves `CERTIFIED → DECERTIFIED` and its `certified_tonnes_allocated` drops to 0.00. **Hop two**, caused by hop one now that the batch's cement is no longer certified: that batch's cement silo moves `OPEN → BLOCKED` with `block_reason` set to `DECERTIFIED_CEMENT`. **Hop three**, caused by hop two now that the silo is blocked: every loadout order on that silo whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `LOADING` moves to `CANCELLED_BY_QUALITY` with `void_reason` set to `DECERTIFIED_CEMENT`, and the silo's `reserved_tonnes` is reduced by each cancelled order's `tonnes`. **Hop four**, caused by hop three now that those orders are no longer open: each affected customer's `open_reserved_tonnes` is recomputed downward. The chain therefore runs from certificate to batch to silo to order to customer, and every row it touches has its `version` incremented.

Loadout is the `LogisticsPlanner`'s domain. Reserving an appointment names the `customer_id` collecting the cement, the `cement_silo_id` the truck will load from, the `mill_certificate_id` covering the material, the `window_start` and `window_end` of the appointment, and the `tonnes` reserved, echoing `expected_mill_certificate_version` as §2.6 requires. `scheduled_hours` is the difference between `window_end` and `window_start` in hours, rounded half up to two decimals. The order is born `RESERVED` with `loaded_tonnes` at 0.00, and creation grows the silo's `reserved_tonnes` by `tonnes`, grows the customer's `open_reserved_tonnes`, grows the certificate's `allocated_tonnes` — which is the sum of `tonnes` over the orders against that certificate whose `status` is `RESERVED`, `LOADING` or `COMPLETED` — and increments the certificate's `version`. Orders can be listed with optional filters on `customer_id`, `cement_silo_id`, `mill_certificate_id` and `status`; an unresolvable order identifier is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`.

Reservation is the most heavily guarded write in the system, and its checks run in the ladder order of §2.8. `window_start` must be strictly earlier than `window_end` and at or after the logical clock, else `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. The silo must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_SILO_BLOCKED`, and `cement_silo_id` must name a `CEMENT` silo of the operation's plant, else `ERR_SILO_KIND_MISMATCH`. The silo's `available_tonnes` must cover the reservation, with equality allowed, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CEMENT`. The certificate must be `ISSUED`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_ISSUED`; its `customer_id` must equal the order's `customer_id`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATE_CUSTOMER_MISMATCH`; and its `allocated_tonnes` plus the new `tonnes` must be no greater than its `certified_tonnes`, with equality allowed, else `ERR_CERTIFICATE_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDED`. Finally two physical terminal limits apply together. Counting only the orders on the same silo whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `LOADING` and whose **half-open** window overlaps the new one — windows that merely touch at an instant do not overlap, unlike the closed-interval rule for kiln runs — the overlap count must stay strictly below the silo's `loadout_spouts`, else `ERR_SILO_SPOUT_CAPACITY`. And the order's own `tonnes` must be no greater than the silo's `draw_rate_tonnes_per_hour` multiplied by the order's `scheduled_hours`, rounded half up to two decimals, else `ERR_DRAW_RATE_EXCEEDED`: a four-hour appointment on a 60-tonne-per-hour spout cannot promise more than 240.00 tonnes however much cement is standing in the silo.

Starting to load advances the order `RESERVED → LOADING` and stamps `loading_started_at`. It requires the order to be `RESERVED`, else `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`; the certificate still to be `ISSUED`, else `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_ISSUED`; the silo still to be `OPEN`, else `ERR_SILO_BLOCKED`; the logical clock to be inside the half-open appointment window, at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end`, else `ERR_LOADOUT_WINDOW_NOT_ACTIVE`; and the number of orders on the same silo already in `LOADING` to be strictly below the silo's `loadout_spouts`, else `ERR_SILO_SPOUT_CAPACITY`. Completing advances the order `LOADING → COMPLETED`, requires a `loaded_tonnes` that is strictly greater than zero and no greater than the reserved `tonnes` — anything else is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and stamps `completed_at`. Completion moves mass rather than creating or destroying it: the silo's `tonnes_on_hand` shrinks by `loaded_tonnes` while its `reserved_tonnes` shrinks by the full reserved `tonnes`, its `available_tonnes` is recomputed and its `version` increments; the customer's `open_reserved_tonnes` shrinks by `tonnes` and its `lifetime_loaded_tonnes` grows by `loaded_tonnes`. The plant's `cement_tonne_balance_tonnes` is unchanged, because the tonnes have simply moved from silo stock into shipped stock.

Voiding is available from three states and requires a `void_reason` in all of them, and the three source states give three distinct transitions: `RESERVED → VOIDED`, `LOADING → VOIDED` and `COMPLETED → VOIDED`. From `RESERVED`, voiding releases the promise: the silo's `reserved_tonnes` shrinks by `tonnes`, its `available_tonnes` is recomputed, and the customer's `open_reserved_tonnes` shrinks by `tonnes`. From `LOADING`, voiding does exactly the same three things and additionally frees the spout that order was occupying. From `COMPLETED`, voiding is a compensation for a shipment that should not have happened: the silo must be able to take the material back — its `tonnes_on_hand` plus `loaded_tonnes` no greater than its `capacity_tonnes`, with equality allowed, else `ERR_SILO_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` — and then `tonnes_on_hand` grows by `loaded_tonnes`, `available_tonnes` is recomputed, the silo's `version` increments, the customer's `lifetime_loaded_tonnes` shrinks by `loaded_tonnes`, and only afterwards is the order's own `loaded_tonnes` set to 0.00. The plant's `cement_tonne_balance_tonnes` returns to exactly its pre-completion value, and the silo returns to exactly its pre-completion figure with no residual drift in the second decimal. `VOIDED` is terminal, as is `CANCELLED_BY_QUALITY`, the status an order reaches by `RESERVED → CANCELLED_BY_QUALITY` or `LOADING → CANCELLED_BY_QUALITY` when the cement silo beneath it is blocked; in that case `void_reason` carries the silo's `block_reason` and `loaded_tonnes` stays 0.00 because nothing was ever shipped. Voiding an order that is already `VOIDED` or `CANCELLED_BY_QUALITY`, completing one that is still `RESERVED`, or starting to load one that is already `COMPLETED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`.

Distribution planning closes the chain. A `PlantAdmin` or `LogisticsPlanner` asks the plant for a plan for a given `as_of_month`, an integer from 1 to 12 inclusive — anything else is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The read is pure: it computes a plan and persists nothing. Eligibility is strict: only customers whose `contract_state` is `ACTIVE`, whose `credit_hold` is false, and whose `region_code` equals the plant's `region_code` are considered. If no customer satisfies all three conditions, the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CUSTOMERS` rather than returning an empty plan.

The plan is built in five steps. The **pool**, reported as `available_pool_tonnes`, is the sum of `available_tonnes` across the plant's silos whose `material_kind` is `CEMENT` and whose `status` is `OPEN`, rounded half up to two decimals. The **seasonal factor**, reported as `seasonal_factor`, is chosen by testing peak season *first*: the factor is 1.25 when `as_of_month` appears in the plant's `peak_season_months`; failing that, it is 0.70 when `as_of_month` is December, January or February; and it is 1.00 otherwise. Because peak is tested first, a plant that lists a winter month as a peak month gets 1.25 for it, not 0.70. Each eligible customer's `demand_tonnes` is its `contract_monthly_tonnes` multiplied by that factor, rounded half up to two decimals. The eligible customers are then **ranked** by `contract_priority` descending, then by `contract_monthly_tonnes` descending, then by `created_at` ascending; a row's `id` is never used as a tie-break. Finally the ranking is **walked** in order, carrying a running remainder that starts at the pool: each customer's `allocated_tonnes` is the smaller of its `demand_tonnes` and the remainder, rounded half up to two decimals; its `shortfall_tonnes` is its `demand_tonnes` minus that allocation, rounded half up to two decimals; and the remainder is then reduced by the allocation before the next customer is considered. Every eligible customer appears in the `allocations` array — each entry carrying its `customer_id`, its `contract_priority`, its `demand_tonnes`, its `allocated_tonnes` and its `shortfall_tonnes` — including those that receive 0.00 because the pool ran dry above them. The response also echoes the `plant_id` and the `as_of_month`, and reports `total_allocated_tonnes` as the sum of all allocations and `total_shortfall_tonnes` as the sum of all shortfalls, each rounded half up to two decimals.

## 4. API Endpoint Definitions

Creation endpoints — those that bring a new resource into existence — return HTTP 201 on success.
