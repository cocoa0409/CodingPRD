# Corporate Fitness Center Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

We operate corporate fitness centers under contract. A large employer hands us a room — or a floor, or a building — and we staff it, program it, keep the equipment safe, enrol the employer's people, and bill for the privilege. Today that work lives in eleven spreadsheets per site and a shared mailbox, and it fails in predictable ways: an instructor's CPR certification lapses without anyone noticing until an auditor asks; a group-exercise class runs in a studio that failed its cleanliness inspection two days earlier; a cost-plus client is invoiced above the annual not-to-exceed ceiling they negotiated; a dependent keeps badging in three months after the sponsoring employee left the company. Each of these is a small operational miss with an outsized liability tail.

This document specifies the API for a single system of record that closes those gaps. It spans the commercial relationship (contracts with either a flat management fee or a cost-plus structure, staffing models, and program-evolution amendments), the workforce (hiring, certification verification with strict segregation of duties, emergency-response drill recency, and a readiness score that gates programming), the physical plant (rooms with inspection postures, equipment with preventive-maintenance intervals, and automated external defibrillators whose consumables and inspection recency determine whether the site is allowed to operate at all), the membership (employees, their dependents, and retirees, each with an eligibility dimension and an independent badge-access dimension), the programming itself (group exercise, personal training, and wellness challenges measured against participation targets), and the money (monthly invoicing with tiered markups, service credits, coverage penalties, disputes, and rebills against a conserved year-to-date ledger).

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. The first is **that every consequential state change is enforced by the server, not by convention**: an offering cannot be activated when its lead trainer's certification has lapsed, a session cannot be started when the site has no ready defibrillator, and a badge cannot be granted without a current signed liability waiver on file. The second is **privacy by construction around health data**: members may consent to storing health-metric notes on their participation records, and those notes are visible to exactly two audiences — the member who owns them and the independent compliance auditor — while the aggregate utilization report that the client sponsor reads suppresses any member-class cohort too small to protect from re-identification.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operating Roles and Access Headers

Every request to this API must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal, and `X-User-Role` names the role that principal is acting in. If either header is missing or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names below spelled exactly, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) — note that an unrecognised role string is an authentication failure, not an authorization failure. When both headers are well-formed but the named role is not permitted to invoke the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). This role gate is evaluated before the system looks up any identifier in the URL, so a role with no access probing an identifier that does not exist still receives 403 rather than 404.

The six roles are `OperatorAdmin`, `SiteManager`, `FitnessStaff`, `ClientSponsor`, `Member`, and `ComplianceAuditor`.

- `OperatorAdmin` is the fitness-center operator's head-office administrator, owning commercial terms, hiring, billing, and the nightly readiness sweep. When `X-User-Role` is `OperatorAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it for audit purposes (for example as `verified_by_user_id` on a certification record or `reviewed_by_user_id` on an incident report) but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- `ComplianceAuditor` is the independent risk and compliance reviewer who adjudicates incidents, files liability claims, audits inspections, and holds unrestricted health-data read access. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; it may be recorded as `reviewed_by_user_id` or `verified_by_user_id` but is never matched against an entity's `id`.
- `SiteManager` is the on-site manager responsible for programming, staffing, facility standards, and participation operations. When `X-User-Role` is `SiteManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting staff member record; ownership and segregation-of-duties checks on any resource that references a staff member compare that resource's staff foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`.
- `FitnessStaff` covers the instructors, personal trainers, and wellness coordinators who actually work the sessions. When `X-User-Role` is `FitnessStaff`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting staff member record; a staffing assignment may be confirmed by a `FitnessStaff` principal only when the assignment's `staff_member_id` equals `X-User-ID`.
- `ClientSponsor` is the corporate client's benefits sponsor, who approves contract terms, owns member eligibility, and disputes or pays invoices. When `X-User-Role` is `ClientSponsor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting client contract; ownership checks compare the target contract's `id`, or the `contract_id` of the resource being acted on, to `X-User-ID`.
- `Member` is the employee, dependent, or retiree using the facility. When `X-User-Role` is `Member`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting member record; ownership checks compare the target member's `id`, or the `member_id` on the resource being acted on, to `X-User-ID`.

Holding the right role is not sufficient when the role is backed by an entity. A scope check runs immediately after the role gate on the client contract decision, invoice dispute, invoice payment, member enrolment, member eligibility decision, member badge-access decision, waiver filing, participation check-in, staffing-assignment confirmation, and contract utilization-report operations: for these ten operations, an entity-backed principal acting on a resource it does not own is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_SCOPE` (HTTP 403). The same check guards single-record reads whose subject belongs to a contract or a member — the contract, room, offering, session, member, waiver, participation-record, and invoice detail fetches, the per-member waiver listing, the readiness summary, and the offering attainment report — so a sponsor fetching another client's invoice receives 403 rather than the invoice. List endpoints behave differently and never reject on ownership: they silently narrow the result set instead, so a `ClientSponsor` listing contracts, rooms, offerings, sessions, members, or invoices sees only its own contract's rows and a `Member` listing participation records sees only their own, with `total_count` computed after that narrowing. The staff, credential, asset, assignment, and incident reads are not scoped at all — neither their listings, which are not narrowed, nor their detail fetches, which apply no ownership test — because their permitted roles are all operator-side; a site manager may consequently read another site's staff member, credential, asset, assignment, or incident report. Concretely, a `SiteManager` or `FitnessStaff` principal must resolve to a staff member of the contract that owns the target; a `ClientSponsor` principal must resolve to the target contract itself; and a `Member` principal must resolve to the target member. The staff-backed case is checked only when the principal resolves: a `SiteManager` or `FitnessStaff` `X-User-ID` that matches no staff member row at all is not treated as a scope violation and passes this gate, whereas a `ClientSponsor` or `Member` identifier is compared literally against the target's identifier and therefore always fails when it does not match.

Segregation of duties is enforced in two further places, each with its own error identity. A staff member may never accept or reject their own certification: when the acting principal's `X-User-ID` equals the credential's `staff_member_id`, the verification decision is refused with `ERR_SELF_VERIFICATION_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403), regardless of whether the acting role would otherwise be permitted to verify credentials. Likewise, the staff member who filed an incident report may not be the person who takes it into review: when `X-User-ID` equals the report's `reported_by_staff_id`, the review is refused with `ERR_SELF_REVIEW_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the only value in the system read from the host's wall clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.5, so it does not move when the clock is set.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position within the result set: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a structural violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), and so is a value of either parameter that is not a base-ten integer; an omitted parameter and an explicitly empty one both fall back to the default. `offset` has no upper bound, and an `offset` past the end of the result set simply yields an empty page with the true `total_count`.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with insertion order — the sequence in which the rows were written — as the tie-break whenever two rows share a `created_at`, which the logical clock makes common. The one documented exception is the per-member waiver listing, which is ordered newest-first so that the currently effective waiver appears at the top of the first page. Every equality filter named in §4 is compared as an exact string against the stored field, so a filter value matching no row yields an empty page rather than an error and an unrecognised enum value is not rejected; a filter parameter supplied empty is ignored exactly as if it had been omitted. The three stages run in a fixed order on every list endpoint: the named filters first, then the ownership narrowing of §2.1, then the `limit`/`offset` window.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Audit Trail and Version Counters

Every persisted record carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID. Identifiers have no type prefix and no embedded semantics; clients must never construct one, and any client-supplied `id` on a create is ignored. Every record also carries a `created_at` timestamp, expressed as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits, set once when the row is written and never modified thereafter.

Most records additionally carry `updated_at` — same format as `created_at`, refreshed on every mutation of the record — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the record is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record, including mutations caused by a cascade originating elsewhere. A write that resolves to no actual change — every field it would set already holds the value it would be set to, as when a cascade closes a room that is already closed or recomputes an attainment figure that is unchanged — is not a mutation: it leaves both `updated_at` and `version` exactly as they were. The single exception is the participation record. A participation check-in is an append-only event: it carries only `id` and `created_at`, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from its representation entirely. The one mutation a participation record ever undergoes — the correction that voids it — does not introduce either field.

More generally, fields whose values the server computes or assigns are never accepted from the client. When a request body contains such a field, the value is silently ignored rather than rejected; the request does not fail on that account. Unrecognised fields are likewise ignored.

Almost every string field this API reads is length-bounded, and a string that is empty or longer than its cap is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — this applies to optional fields too, so supplying `""` is never the same as omitting the field. The caps are 40 characters for `site_code`; 60 for `drill_type` and `waiver_version_code`; 80 for `asset_tag`, `employee_number`, and `badge_number`; 120 for `client_name`, `room_name`, `credential_number`, `claim_reference`, and `payment_reference`; 200 for every `full_name`, for `issuer`, and for `title`; 500 for `reason` and `void_reason`; 1000 for `dispute_reason`; 2000 for `notes`, `note`, `review_note`, `return_note`, `closure_note`, and `health_metrics_note`; and 4000 for `narrative`. Three free-text fields are exceptions the other way, being purely decorative: the `comment` on a client decision and the `reason` on a service-status action are read by no rule and are simply discarded, and `rejection_reason` is bounded only by the non-emptiness rule of §3.2 rather than by a length cap.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

All date, expiry, grace-window, and billing-period reasoning in this system reads a **logical clock** rather than the host's wall clock. The clock's startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. An `OperatorAdmin` may advance or rewind it through an administrative test-driving capability that accepts a mandatory `current_utc` instant and returns an object whose single field is the accepted `current_utc`, normalised to the three-fractional-digit UTC form of §2.4; a `current_utc` that is absent, not a string, or not a parseable ISO 8601 instant is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and the clock may be moved backwards as freely as forwards. Setting the clock mutates no entity, but every subsequent request — and in particular every nightly readiness sweep — evaluates its date comparisons against the new instant.

Where this document says "today", it means the calendar date of the logical clock in UTC. Where it says "now", it means the logical clock instant itself.

### 2.6. Flat Collections, Path Resolution and Site Containment

Every aggregate in this system is its own top-level collection: client contracts, staff members, staff credentials, facility rooms, facility assets, program offerings, program sessions, staffing assignments, members, liability waivers, participation records, incident reports, and monthly invoices. Parentage is never expressed through URL nesting; it is expressed through explicit foreign-key fields carried in request bodies — a staff member is bound to its site by `contract_id`, a session to its program by `offering_id` and to its space by `room_id`, an assignment to its session by `session_id` and to its person by `staff_member_id`, a participation record to its participant by `member_id`. Two of these references point back at the same entity type: an amendment contract records the `id` of the contract it replaces in `supersedes_contract_id`, and a dependent member records the sponsoring employee in `sponsor_member_id`. Two more are optional subjects rather than mandatory parents: an incident report may name a `member_id`, an `asset_id`, both, or neither.

Where a path does contain more than one segment of identity — a staff member's certifications, or a member's waivers — the segments are resolved strictly left to right. The parent identifier is looked up first, and if it does not resolve, the specific not-found error for that parent's type is returned before the child collection is considered at all.

Existence failures always name the entity type that failed to resolve, whether the identifier came from the URL or from the request body: `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAFF_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTICIPATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND` — all HTTP 404. Body-level foreign keys are checked after the request's shape has been validated, so a syntactically malformed identifier yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) while a well-formed identifier that matches no row yields the appropriate 404. This body-level referential check applies when creating a staff member, a staff credential, a facility room, a facility asset, a program offering, a program session, a staffing assignment, a member, a participation record, an incident report, and a monthly invoice.

We are a multi-site operator, and nothing may straddle two client contracts. Whenever a request references several entities at once, every one of them must belong to the same contract: the room and the offering when a session is scheduled, the staff member and the session when an assignment is created, the member and the session at check-in, the member and the asset named on an incident report, and the room when an asset is registered. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_CONTRACT_REFERENCE` (HTTP 422).

### 2.7. Stale-Read Protection on Waiver Filing and Invoice Drafting

Two write operations in this system can retire a record the caller may never have seen, and both are therefore guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check on the parent record's `version`.

Filing a liability waiver for a member requires the request to carry `expected_member_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` of that member as the client last read it. Because a newly filed waiver supersedes whatever signed waiver the member previously held, a client working from a stale view of the member would otherwise silently retire a waiver it never observed. If `expected_member_version` does not equal the member's current `version`, the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MEMBER_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Drafting a monthly invoice requires the request to carry `expected_contract_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` of the target client contract as the client last read it. A fresh draft supersedes any existing draft for the same period, and contract-level cascades — suspension, penalty accrual, eligibility-count movement — advance the contract's `version` between the client's read and its write. If `expected_contract_version` does not equal the contract's current `version`, the draft is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Both checks run before any state or business rule on the same request, so a client working from a stale view can never be shown a downstream error that would mislead it about why its write failed. No other operation in this API accepts a version field; where this document does not name `expected_member_version` or `expected_contract_version`, the request carries neither.

### 2.8. Money, Minutes and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary amount in this system is an integer number of cents. There are no fractional-cent representations anywhere in a stored record or a response body. Wherever a computation produces a fractional value, it is rounded **half up** to a whole cent — a value ending in exactly one half cent rounds away from zero.

The order in which rounding is applied matters and is specified per calculation, because rounding an aggregate is not the same as aggregating rounded values. As a rule of thumb for readers of §3: labor cost is rounded once per staffing assignment and only then summed; the administrative markup and the volume credit on a cost-plus invoice are each rounded independently, and the resulting net amount is never rounded again because it is already a whole-cent difference of whole-cent quantities; the per-session coverage penalty amount and the penalty ceiling are each rounded independently before they are compared.

Durations are whole minutes. Day counts derived from a pair of instants are whole days, rounded **up** to the next whole day. Percentage figures are whole integer percents rounded half up, except the two program attainment figures, which are stored as numbers with one decimal place of headroom but computed as integer percents rounded half up; either attainment figure may legitimately exceed 100. The readiness score for a staff member is an integer between 0 and 100 inclusive, with rounding applied exactly once to the final weighted sum.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Compensating Adjustments

Several operations in this system reach far beyond the record named in the URL. Suspending a contract pauses its programs, which cancels their open sessions, which releases those sessions' staffing assignments, which returns committed minutes to the affected staff members. A credential expiring suspends its holder from duty, which vacates that holder's live assignments, which puts their sessions at risk, which — for sessions close enough to their start time — cancels them and accrues a penalty. Reviewing a serious equipment incident pulls the implicated asset out of service, which restricts the room it sits in, which cancels that room's imminent sessions.

Three rules govern all such cascades. First, **every record the cascade touches is a mutation of that record**: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments, exactly as if it had been written directly. Second, **cascades are deterministic in order**. Where a cascade fans out over a set of sibling rows, program offerings are processed in ascending `created_at` order, and program sessions are processed in ascending `scheduled_start_utc` order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. Third, **cancellation is terminal and is never undone**. Resuming a suspended contract reactivates its paused offerings and reopens its rooms, but the sessions the suspension cancelled stay cancelled, and the assignments those cancellations released stay released.

Four counters in this system are conserved quantities, and every operation that moves one must also move it back when the underlying fact is retracted. A staff member's `committed_minutes_current_period` always equals the sum of `paid_minutes` across that person's assignments in `ASSIGNED`, `CONFIRMED`, or `COMPLETED` status; every subtraction from it is additionally floored at 0, so the counter is never negative. The same floor applies to a contract's `eligible_member_count`. A member's `incentive_points_balance` always equals the sum of `points_awarded` across that member's participation records still in `COUNTED` status. A contract's `ytd_billed_cents` always equals the sum of `net_amount_cents` across that contract's invoices in `ISSUED` or `PAID` status. A contract's `eligible_member_count` always equals the number of that contract's members whose `eligibility_status` is `ELIGIBLE`. §3 states, for each operation, exactly which of these counters moves and in which direction; the invariants above are the acceptance criteria.

### 2.10. Health-Data Confidentiality and Cohort Suppression

Health information collected alongside participation is treated as a separate confidentiality class from everything else in the system. A participation record may carry a free-text `health_metrics_note`, but only when the subject member's `health_data_consent` flag is true. Supplying the note for a member who has not consented is a hard rejection with `ERR_HEALTH_DATA_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403), not a silent drop of the field.

On the read side the guard is projection rather than refusal. `ComplianceAuditor` principals see `health_metrics_note` in full, and a `Member` principal sees the note on their own records. `SiteManager`, `FitnessStaff`, `OperatorAdmin`, and `ClientSponsor` principals always receive `health_metrics_note` as null when they list participation records or fetch one by identifier — the record is still readable, but the note is not, and the key is still present with a null value rather than omitted. No read ever fails on health-data grounds: `ERR_HEALTH_DATA_FORBIDDEN` arises only on the write side, when a note is supplied for a member who has not consented, so a role outside the permitted two simply receives the nulled projection with HTTP 200. This write-and-read boundary applies to exactly three operations: creating a participation record, listing participation records, and fetching a single participation record.

Aggregate reporting has a second, independent protection. The contract utilization report breaks participation down by member class, and any class cohort whose distinct-participant count falls below the privacy floor of 5 is reported as suppressed with null counts rather than with real numbers, so that a cohort of one or two people cannot be re-identified from the aggregate. `health_metrics_note` is never projected into the utilization report for any role, including `ComplianceAuditor`.

### 2.11. The Order in Which Rules Fail

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Rules are evaluated in one total order, and the **first** violated rule decides the response; later violations are never reported and never merged into the message. The ordering, from first evaluated to last, is:

1. Header presence and role recognition (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`).
2. Role permitted on the endpoint (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`).
3. Entity-backed ownership scope (`ERR_FORBIDDEN_SCOPE`).
4. Self-verification of one's own credential (`ERR_SELF_VERIFICATION_FORBIDDEN`).
5. Self-review of one's own incident report (`ERR_SELF_REVIEW_FORBIDDEN`).
6. Health-data write consent (`ERR_HEALTH_DATA_FORBIDDEN`) — the read-side projection of §2.10 raises nothing and so takes no place in this order.
7. Path-parameter existence (the entity-specific 404s).
8. Request-body shape, types, enum ranges, numeric bounds, date formats, and pagination bounds (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`).
9. Body-level foreign-key existence (the entity-specific 404s).
10. Waiver-filing stale member version (`ERR_STALE_MEMBER_VERSION`).
11. Invoice-drafting stale contract version (`ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION`).
12. State admissibility — the subject must be in a state from which the requested transition exists (`ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, or the specific state error for the operation: `ERR_SESSION_NOT_STAFFED`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_RENEWABLE`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_ISSUED`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_DISPUTED`, `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_ALLOWED`, `ERR_OPEN_SESSIONS`).
13. Terminal lockdown — the subject must not already be in a terminal state (`ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`).
14. Lead certification currency (`ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_VERIFIED`).
15. Site defibrillator readiness (`ERR_AED_NOT_READY`).
16. Room posture (`ERR_ROOM_NOT_OPEN`).
17. Signed and unexpired liability waiver (`ERR_WAIVER_REQUIRED`).
18. Staff duty status (`ERR_STAFF_NOT_ON_DUTY`).
19. Lead readiness threshold (`ERR_READINESS_BELOW_THRESHOLD`).
20. Same-contract containment (`ERR_CROSS_CONTRACT_REFERENCE`).
21. Dependent sponsorship (`ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`).
22. Room occupancy conflict in time (`ERR_ROOM_TIME_CONFLICT`).
23. Staff double-booking in time (`ERR_STAFF_TIME_CONFLICT`).
24. Session inside the contract term and not backdated (`ERR_SESSION_OUT_OF_TERM`).
25. Session roster capacity (`ERR_SESSION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`).
26. Member class within the program's scope (`ERR_MEMBER_CLASS_NOT_ELIGIBLE`).
27. Badge and eligibility active at check-in (`ERR_ACCESS_NOT_ACTIVE`).
28. Duplicate check-in (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CHECK_IN`).
29. Fee-term consistency (`ERR_FEE_TERMS_INVALID`).
30. Live invoice already exists for the period (`ERR_INVOICE_PERIOD_CONFLICT`).
31. Annual ceiling headroom at issue time (`ERR_NTE_EXHAUSTED`).
32. Clean billing position at termination (`ERR_OPEN_INVOICES`).
33. Site-manager staffing at submission (`ERR_SITE_MANAGER_REQUIRED`).
34. Claim subject and waiver on file (`ERR_CLAIM_SUBJECT_REQUIRED`).
35. Site-code uniqueness (`ERR_SITE_CODE_CONFLICT`).
36. Asset-tag uniqueness within the contract (`ERR_ASSET_TAG_CONFLICT`).
37. One live lead per session (`ERR_DUPLICATE_LEAD_ASSIGNMENT`).
38. Forecast attendance against physical room capacity (`ERR_ROOM_OCCUPANCY_EXCEEDED`).
39. Eligibility verified before a badge grant (`ERR_ELIGIBILITY_NOT_VERIFIED`).
40. Credential-decision integrity (`ERR_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED_ON_FILE`, `ERR_REJECTION_REASON_REQUIRED`, `ERR_RENEWAL_DATE_INVALID`).
41. Session start grace window (`ERR_SESSION_START_TOO_EARLY`).

Two refinements of this list matter in practice. First, ownership scope (rule 3) is checked against an identifier the request has already produced, so when the resource is named by a **path** parameter the path lookup of rule 7 runs first and a nonexistent identifier yields its 404 rather than `ERR_FORBIDDEN_SCOPE`; only when the identifier arrives in the **request body**, as the `member_id` of a check-in does, does the scope check genuinely precede existence and produce a 403 for an identifier that resolves to nothing. Second, whenever the subject of a lifecycle action is already in one of its terminal states, the terminal lockdown of rule 13 is evaluated **before** the state admissibility of rule 12, so a terminated contract, a completed or cancelled session, a released or completed assignment, a closed offering, a voided participation record, a closed incident, and a paid or superseded invoice all report `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE` rather than the operation's specific state error. Third, the health-data consent gate of rule 6 is evaluated against the note the request actually carries, and so on a check-in it sits *between* rules 8 and 9 rather than above them: an over-long `health_metrics_note` reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, a well-formed note for a member who has not consented reports `ERR_HEALTH_DATA_FORBIDDEN`, and a note offered against a `member_id` that resolves to nothing reports `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND` — the consent test is skipped entirely when the named member does not exist. Fourth, an identifier that arrives in the **query string** is treated like a body identifier for the purpose of rule 3, so the readiness summary scopes its `contract_id` before resolving it and a site manager of a different contract receives `ERR_FORBIDDEN_SCOPE` even for a `contract_id` that matches no row, whereas the utilization report — whose contract is a path segment — resolves first and returns `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`; a required query parameter that is missing or blank is a rule 8 violation and therefore outranks both.

Several further consequences of this ordering are deliberate and worth stating outright, because they are the cases where a naive implementation returns the wrong error. State admissibility sits above every safety rule, so attempting to start a session that is not `STAFFED` returns `ERR_SESSION_NOT_STAFFED` even when the site simultaneously has no ready defibrillator, and a check-in against a session that is not `IN_PROGRESS` returns `ERR_SESSION_NOT_IN_PROGRESS` even when the member also lacks a waiver. The waiver gate sits above roster capacity, so an unwaived member checking into a full session receives `ERR_WAIVER_REQUIRED` rather than `ERR_SESSION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The waiver gate also sits above the badge-grant eligibility check, so a member who is neither eligible nor waivered receives `ERR_WAIVER_REQUIRED` rather than `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_NOT_VERIFIED`. Fee-term consistency outranks the site-manager staffing requirement, so a draft management-fee contract that has neither a fee amount nor an active-duty site manager returns `ERR_FEE_TERMS_INVALID`. Staff double-booking outranks the one-live-lead rule, so a second lead who is also double-booked returns `ERR_STAFF_TIME_CONFLICT`. And the grace-window check on session start is the very last thing evaluated, below the state, defibrillator, and room checks alike.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Contract Origination and Governance

Everything in this system hangs off a **client contract**. An `OperatorAdmin` opens one by supplying the corporate `client_name` (between 1 and 120 characters), the operator's own `site_code` for the location, the commercial `fee_structure`, the `staffing_model`, the contracted staffing level as `budgeted_staff_fte`, the client's `participation_target_pct` goal, and the term boundaries `term_start_on` and `term_end_on` as calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. The `fee_structure` is either `MANAGEMENT_FEE` or `COST_PLUS` and is immutable once the contract exists — a commercial model change is expressed as an amendment producing a new contract, never as an edit. The `staffing_model` records who employs the on-site team and is one of `OPERATOR_EMPLOYED`, `CLIENT_EMPLOYED`, or `HYBRID`. `budgeted_staff_fte` must be greater than zero and carries at most two decimal places; `participation_target_pct` must be an integer from 1 through 100 inclusive; and `term_end_on` must be strictly later than `term_start_on`.

Fee terms must agree with the commercial model, and the agreement is exact in both directions. A `MANAGEMENT_FEE` contract must carry `management_fee_cents` — a flat monthly amount of at least zero — and must leave `not_to_exceed_cents` null. A `COST_PLUS` contract must carry `not_to_exceed_cents` — an annual ceiling strictly greater than zero — and must leave `management_fee_cents` null. Either mirror-image mistake, whether at contract creation, at amendment creation, or at submission time, is rejected with `ERR_FEE_TERMS_INVALID`. The operator's `site_code` must be unique across all contracts that have not been withdrawn; a collision is refused with `ERR_SITE_CODE_CONFLICT`. A withdrawn contract releases its `site_code` back into circulation.

A newly created contract lands in `DRAFT` with `revision_round`, `ytd_billed_cents`, `pending_penalty_cents`, `uncovered_session_count`, and `eligible_member_count` all initialised to 0, `supersedes_contract_id` null, and `approved_at` null. While the contract is a draft, the operator builds out the site around it — hires staff, registers rooms and equipment, sketches programs. Submission for client approval moves the contract `DRAFT → PENDING_CLIENT_APPROVAL`, and it is gated twice: the fee terms must already be complete for the declared `fee_structure`, and the contract must have at least one staff member whose `role_type` is `SITE_MANAGER` and whose `duty_status` is `ACTIVE_DUTY`. A submission with no such site manager on the roster is refused with `ERR_SITE_MANAGER_REQUIRED`. Submitting a contract that is not in `DRAFT` has no corresponding transition and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Submission mutates no child record.

The client sponsor then renders a `decision`, optionally attaching a free-text `comment`. A `decision` of `RETURN` sends the contract back `PENDING_CLIENT_APPROVAL → DRAFT` and increments `revision_round` by 1; the loop from draft to pending and back may repeat without bound, and `revision_round` is the count of those returns. A `decision` of `APPROVE` moves the contract `PENDING_CLIENT_APPROVAL → ACTIVE`, stamps `approved_at` with the logical clock instant, and — because approval is the moment the site becomes operable — reopens every room of the contract that currently sits in `CLOSED` with `open_deficiency_count` equal to 0, moving each such room `CLOSED → OPEN`. Rooms with outstanding deficiencies are left alone. A decision rendered against a contract that is not in `PENDING_CLIENT_APPROVAL` is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_PENDING`, and only the sponsor of that very contract may render it.

Service status is the operator's lever over a live site, and the requested `action` is one of `SUSPEND`, `RESUME`, or `TERMINATE`, optionally accompanied by a `reason`. **Suspension** takes the contract `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and triggers the system's deepest cascade, four links long. First, every program offering of the contract in `ACTIVE` status moves to `PAUSED`, processed in ascending order of when the offerings were created. Second, each offering's move to `PAUSED` cancels that offering's sessions in `SCHEDULED`, `STAFFED`, or `STAFFING_AT_RISK` status: each moves to `CANCELLED_CLIENT` with `cancel_reason` set to `CLIENT_REQUEST`, processed in ascending `scheduled_start_utc` order with creation order as the tie-break. Third, each session cancellation releases that session's staffing assignments in `ASSIGNED` or `CONFIRMED` status, moving them to `RELEASED`. Fourth, each release subtracts that assignment's `paid_minutes` from the staff member's `committed_minutes_current_period` and triggers a recomputation of the owning offering's `provisional_attainment_pct`. Independently of the four-link chain, every room of the contract moves to `CLOSED`, whether it was `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` at the time; rooms that were restricted keep their `open_deficiency_count` intact precisely so that a later resume can tell them apart.

**Resuming** takes the contract `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, returns every `PAUSED` offering of the contract to `ACTIVE`, and reopens the plant selectively: a room whose `open_deficiency_count` is 0 moves `CLOSED → OPEN`, while a room whose `open_deficiency_count` is greater than zero moves `CLOSED → RESTRICTED` and stays unbookable until a passing inspection clears it. The sessions that the suspension cancelled are not restored, and neither are the assignments those cancellations released. The suspend-and-resume cycle may repeat any number of times.

**Termination** is available from `ACTIVE` and from `SUSPENDED`, and it demands a clean billing position: if any invoice of the contract is in `DRAFT` or `DISPUTED` status, termination is refused with `ERR_OPEN_INVOICES`. Suspension carries no such requirement, and neither does the withdrawal of a draft described two paragraphs below — a `DRAFT` contract is withdrawn regardless of any invoice it might carry. A `SUSPEND` requested from anything but `ACTIVE`, a `RESUME` from anything but `SUSPENDED`, and a `TERMINATE` from `PENDING_CLIENT_APPROVAL` are each refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Terminating from `ACTIVE` runs the same four-link cascade as suspension — offerings paused, open sessions cancelled as `CANCELLED_CLIENT` with `cancel_reason` of `CLIENT_REQUEST`, assignments released, committed minutes returned and provisional attainment recomputed — and closes every room, and then goes further: every member of the contract moves `eligibility_status` to `TERMINATED` and `access_status` to `REVOKED`, every signed liability waiver of those members moves to `REVOKED` with the member's `current_waiver_id` cleared to null, and every staff member of the contract moves `duty_status` to `OFFBOARDED`. Terminating from `SUSPENDED` skips the offering-and-session cascade — it has already run — but still terminates and de-badges every member and offboards every staff member. Offboarding releases any assignment the staff member still holds in `ASSIGNED` or `CONFIRMED` status and resets that person's `committed_minutes_current_period` to 0, whether the person was on active duty or already suspended from it when the contract ended. These releases are deliberately **silent**: unlike an ordinary release, an offboarding release does not drop the session into `STAFFING_AT_RISK` and does not increment any offering's `coverage_gap_count`, because the contract that owned the programming is itself ending. Once the person is `OFFBOARDED` their `readiness_index` is recomputed by the ordinary formula against the now-zero committed minutes. `TERMINATED` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal: any further action on such a contract is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`.

One asymmetry deserves its own sentence. A `TERMINATE` action requested against a contract still in `DRAFT` does not terminate it — it withdraws it, moving the contract `DRAFT → WITHDRAWN`. Nothing cascades, because a draft contract has no live children worth cascading over, and the withdrawal releases the `site_code` for reuse.

Program evolution happens through **amendments**. An operator may create a successor draft against a contract that is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`; against any other status the request is refused with `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_ALLOWED`. The amendment payload carries a fresh `fee_structure`, `staffing_model`, `budgeted_staff_fte`, `participation_target_pct`, `term_start_on`, `term_end_on`, and whichever of `management_fee_cents` or `not_to_exceed_cents` the new commercial model requires. The result is a brand-new contract in `DRAFT` whose `supersedes_contract_id` names the predecessor and whose `client_name` and `site_code` are inherited from it; because the site code is inherited rather than chosen, amendments are exempt from the site-code uniqueness rule that governs originals. The predecessor is left entirely untouched — it keeps running, and keeps billing, until the operator terminates it. Contract listings can be filtered by `status`, by `fee_structure`, by `client_name`, and by `supersedes_contract_id`, which is what makes an amendment lineage discoverable.

Three counters on the contract are maintained by activity elsewhere and are described where that activity lives: `ytd_billed_cents` is the conserved billing ledger (§3.9), `pending_penalty_cents` and `uncovered_session_count` track coverage failures (§3.5 and §3.9), and `eligible_member_count` tracks the verified population (§3.6).

### 3.2. Staffing the Site: Hiring, Certification and Emergency-Response Readiness

Hiring binds a person to a site. The payload names the `contract_id` they will serve, their `full_name`, their `role_type`, their `employment_type` — either `OPERATOR_EMPLOYED` or `CLIENT_EMPLOYED`, which need not match the contract's own `staffing_model` for a hybrid site — and their fully-burdened `hourly_cost_cents`, which must be greater than zero and is the rate all labor costing reads. The `role_type` is one of `SITE_MANAGER`, `GROUP_EX_INSTRUCTOR`, `PERSONAL_TRAINER`, or `WELLNESS_COORDINATOR`, and it does one thing beyond labelling: it fixes the person's `required_credential_types` from a map the system owns. A `SITE_MANAGER` requires `CPR_AED`; a `GROUP_EX_INSTRUCTOR` requires `GROUP_EX_CERT`; a `PERSONAL_TRAINER` requires `PT_CERT`; a `WELLNESS_COORDINATOR` requires `FIRST_AID`. `required_credential_types` is derived at creation and any client-supplied value for it is ignored. A new hire starts with `duty_status` of `ACTIVE_DUTY`, `committed_minutes_current_period` at 0, `last_drill_on` null, and `readiness_index` computed immediately — which in practice means 0, since a fresh hire holds no verified credential yet.

Certifications are filed as separate records. A filing carries the `staff_member_id` of the holder, the `credential_type` — one of `CPR_AED`, `FIRST_AID`, `GROUP_EX_CERT`, or `PT_CERT` — the certifying body as `issuer`, the certificate number as `credential_number`, and the `expires_on` date printed on the card. Filing is not gated on what the person already holds: there is no uniqueness constraint on the pair of holder and credential type, so a second card of the same type may be filed alongside a live one and no filing is ever refused as a duplicate. Where a rule needs a single answer for a type, it reads the **newest** record of that type — the one most recently created — as the readiness formula below does; where a rule merely asks whether the person is covered, as reinstatement and assignment eligibility do, any record of that type in `VERIFIED` or `EXPIRING_SOON` status satisfies it. A new record lands in `PENDING_VERIFICATION` with `renewal_count` at 0, `verified_at` and `verified_by_user_id` null, and `rejection_reason` null, and the holder's `readiness_index` is recomputed on the spot. Certification records are reachable in both directions: by identifier, and as the certification list of a given staff member, filterable by `status` and `credential_type`.

Verification is a two-valued `decision`. An `ACCEPT` moves the record `PENDING_VERIFICATION → VERIFIED`, stamps `verified_at` with the logical clock instant and `verified_by_user_id` with the deciding principal's identifier, and recomputes the holder's `readiness_index`. Acceptance requires the record's `expires_on` to be strictly later than today; accepting a card that has already lapsed is refused with `ERR_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED_ON_FILE`. A `REJECT` moves the record `PENDING_VERIFICATION → REJECTED` and requires a non-empty `rejection_reason` in the payload; omitting it, supplying an empty or whitespace-only string, or supplying a value that is not a string at all is refused with `ERR_REJECTION_REASON_REQUIRED` rather than with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Either decision against a record that is not in `PENDING_VERIFICATION` is refused with `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_PENDING`, and — as §2.1 established — the holder may never be the decider. `verified_by_user_id` is an audit field only; no business rule ever reads it.

Acceptance can bring someone back onto duty, and rejection can push them off it. If the holder was `SUSPENDED_FROM_DUTY` and, after the acceptance, every type in their `required_credential_types` now has a record in `VERIFIED` or `EXPIRING_SOON` status, the holder moves `SUSPENDED_FROM_DUTY → ACTIVE_DUTY` automatically. Conversely, when a rejected type is one of the holder's required types, the holder's `readiness_index` recomputes to 0 (the credential component collapses) and the holder moves `ACTIVE_DUTY → SUSPENDED_FROM_DUTY`; the assignment and session consequences of that suspension are applied by the nightly readiness sweep described in §3.9.

Renewal closes the certification cycle. A record in `EXPIRED` or `REJECTED` status may be re-filed with a fresh `credential_number` and `expires_on`, moving it back to `PENDING_VERIFICATION` and incrementing `renewal_count` by 1 — the number of laps the card has taken around the verification loop. Renewal overwrites `expires_on` and `credential_number` with the supplied values, clears `verified_at` and `verified_by_user_id` back to null, and — when the record was coming out of `REJECTED` — clears `rejection_reason` back to null as well. The supplied `expires_on` must be strictly later than today; an already-lapsed renewal date is refused with `ERR_RENEWAL_DATE_INVALID`, and the status check runs first, so an unrenewable record reports that before the date is examined. Renewal attempted on a record that is neither `EXPIRED` nor `REJECTED` is refused with `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_RENEWABLE`, and that includes a record merely `EXPIRING_SOON` — the warning window is not a renewal window — as well as one still `PENDING_VERIFICATION` or already `VERIFIED`. Renewal is available on a credential whose holder has been offboarded.

Emergency preparedness is tracked separately from certification. Recording a completed emergency-response or defibrillator drill supplies the `drill_on` date and optionally a `drill_type` label; the effect is that `last_drill_on` is set to the supplied `drill_on` date and the person's `readiness_index` is recomputed. The `drill_on` date is not bounded — a future date is accepted and simply stored — but the person must not be `OFFBOARDED`, since that status is terminal for the duty dimension and a drill against it is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`. A drill on a `SUSPENDED_FROM_DUTY` person is accepted, though their index remains pinned at 0.

That readiness score is the system's single summary of whether a person is fit to lead programming, and it is a weighted blend of three components, each scored from 0 to 100. **Credential currency** contributes half the weight and takes the worst required type: if any required credential type has no record at all, or its newest record is `EXPIRED` or `REJECTED`, the credential component is 0; otherwise, if any required type's newest record is `EXPIRING_SOON`, the component is 60; only when every required type is `VERIFIED` does the component reach 100. **Drill recency** contributes three tenths of the weight and reads the age in days of `last_drill_on` measured against today: an age of at most 90 days scores 100, an age greater than 90 days but at most 180 days scores 60, and anything older scores 0. A person who has never drilled — `last_drill_on` still null — is treated as infinitely stale and scores 0 on this component; a `last_drill_on` dated later than today has a negative age and scores 100. **Workload headroom** contributes the remaining two tenths and reads `committed_minutes_current_period` against a soft cap of 100 hours: at or below 100 hours of committed time the component is 100; above it, take the excess minutes beyond the cap, divide by 60 and round up to a whole number of hours, multiply by 5, subtract that from 100, and floor the result at 0. The three components are combined as one half of the credential component plus three tenths of the drill component plus two tenths of the workload component, and that weighted sum is rounded half up, exactly once, to an integer — **except that a credential component of 0 collapses the entire index to 0**, short-circuiting the blend so that neither the drill nor the workload component can lift the score off the floor. That is why a fresh hire, who holds no verified credential but has a perfect workload component, scores 0 rather than 20, and why a rejection or an expiry of a required type recomputes the holder straight to 0. When the credential component is 60 or 100 the blend applies as written: a person with every required credential verified, no drill on file, and 90 hours of committed time therefore scores 70; the same person with 100 hours and 30 minutes committed scores 69, because one part-hour beyond the cap costs 5 points of the workload component and thus 1 point of the total. Independently of the formula, a person whose `duty_status` is `SUSPENDED_FROM_DUTY` reports a `readiness_index` of 0 for as long as the suspension lasts — the pin is a property of the duty status, not merely an effect of the moment of suspension — while an `OFFBOARDED` person's index is recomputed by the ordinary formula.

The threshold that matters is 70: an offering may only be activated when its lead's `readiness_index` is at least 70, so exactly 70 passes and 69 fails. The score is recomputed whenever a certification is verified, rejected, or expires, whenever a drill is recorded, whenever a suspended person is reinstated, and whenever `committed_minutes_current_period` changes for any reason — which includes assignment creation, assignment release, assignment vacancy, and contract offboarding.

Reinstatement to duty is an `OperatorAdmin` prerogative and requires that every type in the person's `required_credential_types` now has a record in `VERIFIED` or `EXPIRING_SOON` status; a type with no such record refuses the reinstatement with `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_VERIFIED`. It also requires the person to be in `SUSPENDED_FROM_DUTY`: an `ACTIVE_DUTY` person is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` and an `OFFBOARDED` one with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`, the terminal check being the earlier of the two. On success the person moves `SUSPENDED_FROM_DUTY → ACTIVE_DUTY` and their `readiness_index` is recomputed. Assignments vacated while they were suspended are **not** restored — someone else has presumably been found to cover, and re-attaching the original person would double-book the session. The duty cycle from `ACTIVE_DUTY` to `SUSPENDED_FROM_DUTY` and back may repeat indefinitely; `OFFBOARDED` is terminal and is only ever reached as a consequence of contract termination.

Staff listings can be filtered by `contract_id`, by `duty_status`, and by `role_type`; the single-staff read exposes `readiness_index` and `committed_minutes_current_period` directly.

### 3.3. Facility Standards: Rooms, Inspections, Equipment and Defibrillator Readiness

A site's bookable spaces are registered as rooms. Each carries the `contract_id` of the site that owns it, a `room_name`, a `room_type` drawn from `STUDIO`, `WEIGHT_FLOOR`, `CARDIO_FLOOR`, and `WELLNESS_SUITE`, and a `max_occupancy` greater than zero that encodes the fire-code ceiling. Unlike the site code of a contract and the asset tag of a piece of equipment, `room_name` carries **no** uniqueness constraint: two rooms of one site may share a name, and no registration is ever refused as a duplicate. Registration is not gated on the owning contract's status either, which is what lets the operator lay out the plant while the contract is still a draft. A newly registered room opens for business immediately: `room_state` is `OPEN`, `open_deficiency_count` is 0, and both `last_inspection_on` and `last_cleanliness_score` are null until the first inspection lands. Room listings can be filtered by `contract_id`, by `room_state`, and by `room_type`.

Cleanliness and safety inspections are recorded by the site manager or by the independent compliance auditor. An inspection supplies the `inspection_on` date, a `cleanliness_score` from 0 through 100 inclusive, a `deficiency_count`, and optionally free-text `notes`. An inspection **passes** when the `cleanliness_score` is at least 85 **and** the `deficiency_count` is exactly 0; anything else — a score below 85, or any deficiency at all, or both — **fails**. Every inspection writes `last_inspection_on`, `last_cleanliness_score`, and `open_deficiency_count` onto the room from the supplied values; `open_deficiency_count` therefore tracks the deficiencies recorded by the *most recent* inspection that have not since been cleared by a passing one.

A failing inspection on an `OPEN` room moves it `OPEN → RESTRICTED`, and the consequence reaches into the schedule. Every session booked in that room whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `STAFFED`, or `STAFFING_AT_RISK` and whose `scheduled_start_utc` falls at or before 24 hours from now moves to `CANCELLED_FACILITY` with `cancel_reason` set to `FACILITY`; each such cancellation releases that session's assignments in `ASSIGNED` or `CONFIRMED` status and subtracts each released assignment's `paid_minutes` from the corresponding staff member's `committed_minutes_current_period`. Sessions further out than the 24-hour lock window survive — there is still time to clean the room. A passing inspection on a `RESTRICTED` room moves it `RESTRICTED → OPEN`, resets `open_deficiency_count` to 0, and updates `last_cleanliness_score`; reopening a room additionally requires the owning contract to be `ACTIVE`, and an attempt while the contract sits elsewhere is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. A `CLOSED` room is a room whose contract is suspended or terminated; it is not reopened by inspection but by the contract returning to `ACTIVE`, as §3.1 describes. While a room is `RESTRICTED` or `CLOSED` it hosts nothing at all: both scheduling a new session in it and starting a session already booked in it are refused with `ERR_ROOM_NOT_OPEN`.

Equipment and defibrillators are registered as assets against a `contract_id` and a `room_id`, where the room must belong to the same contract. Each asset carries an `asset_type` of `AED`, `CARDIO`, or `STRENGTH`, an operator `asset_tag` that must be unique within the contract — a collision is refused with `ERR_ASSET_TAG_CONFLICT`, and because the scope is per-contract, two different sites may both carry a tag of AED-01 — and a `service_interval_days` preventive-maintenance interval that defaults to 90 and must fall between 1 and 365 inclusive. Defibrillators additionally require `pad_expiry_on` and `battery_expiry_on`, the electrode-pad and battery expiry dates; on any non-defibrillator asset both must be null. Either mistake — a defibrillator missing one of the two dates, or a `CARDIO` or `STRENGTH` asset supplying one — is a shape violation refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and neither date is bounded relative to today, so an already-lapsed pad date registers successfully and merely renders the unit `NOT_READY`. Registration also accepts optional `last_inspection_on` and `last_service_on` seed dates, so an asset transferred in with documented history can be recognised as ready from the moment it is registered rather than waiting for a fresh service event. A newly registered asset is `IN_SERVICE` with `out_of_service_since` null and `out_of_service_days_current_period` at 0, and its `readiness_status` is computed on the spot. Asset listings can be filtered by `contract_id`, by `room_id`, by `asset_type`, by `status`, and by `readiness_status`.

Defibrillator readiness is a four-valued judgement recomputed at registration, on every service event, and by the nightly sweep. On any asset whose `asset_type` is not `AED`, `readiness_status` is always `NOT_APPLICABLE`. For a defibrillator, the assessment runs in a fixed order. It is `NOT_READY` if any of the following holds: its `status` is anything other than `IN_SERVICE`; either `pad_expiry_on` or `battery_expiry_on` is null; `pad_expiry_on` falls at or before today; `battery_expiry_on` falls at or before today; `last_inspection_on` is null; or the number of days from `last_inspection_on` to today exceeds 30. Otherwise, if the earlier of `pad_expiry_on` and `battery_expiry_on` falls at or before 30 days from today, it is `PADS_EXPIRING`. Otherwise it is `READY`. The inspection-age boundary is inclusive on the ready side: a unit last inspected exactly 30 days ago is still eligible for `READY`, and one day older is `NOT_READY`. `PADS_EXPIRING` is a warning, not a pass — where this document requires a ready defibrillator, only `READY` satisfies the requirement.

That requirement bites in two places, and the error is the same in both: activating a program offering and changing a session's status both require the contract to have at least one asset whose `asset_type` is `AED` and whose `readiness_status` is `READY`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_AED_NOT_READY`.

Service events are the only way an asset's maintenance state moves under human control, and the `event_type` selects the behaviour. An `AED_INSPECTION` sets `last_inspection_on` to today, overwrites `pad_expiry_on` and `battery_expiry_on` with whichever of those two the payload supplies — a date it omits is left as it was, not cleared — and recomputes `readiness_status`; an optional `note` may be attached to any event. Alone among the four, `AED_INSPECTION` has no state precondition at all: it is admissible from every asset status, including `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, and it is not restricted to defibrillators — recorded against a `CARDIO` or `STRENGTH` asset it simply refreshes that asset's `last_inspection_on` and leaves its `readiness_status` at `NOT_APPLICABLE`. Each of the other three is a genuine transition, and requesting one from a status it does not leave is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. A `PREVENTIVE_SERVICE` on an asset in `MAINTENANCE_DUE` moves it `MAINTENANCE_DUE → IN_SERVICE` and sets `last_service_on` to today. A `TAKE_OUT_OF_SERVICE` on an asset in either `IN_SERVICE` or `MAINTENANCE_DUE` moves it to `OUT_OF_SERVICE` and stamps `out_of_service_since` with the current instant; when the asset is a defibrillator its `readiness_status` becomes `NOT_READY`, which is what silently blocks session starts across the whole site until it is repaired. A `REPAIR_COMPLETE` on an asset in `OUT_OF_SERVICE` returns it to `IN_SERVICE`, sets `last_service_on` to today, clears `out_of_service_since` back to null, recomputes `readiness_status` for defibrillators, and — importantly — accrues the outage: the number of days from the date the asset went out of service to today is **added** to `out_of_service_days_current_period`. That figure is a difference of **calendar dates** — today's date in UTC minus the UTC date on which `out_of_service_since` fell — floored at 0, so an outage opened and closed on the same logical day accrues nothing at all, and one opened yesterday and closed today accrues exactly one day regardless of the hours involved. Because each outage adds to the same accumulator, an asset may lap the in-service/out-of-service cycle repeatedly within one billing period and every lap counts. Preventive maintenance is also flagged automatically: the nightly sweep moves a non-defibrillator asset `IN_SERVICE → MAINTENANCE_DUE` when its `last_service_on` is null, or when the number of days from `last_service_on` to today is at least its `service_interval_days`. An asset flagged `MAINTENANCE_DUE` is still usable; nothing in the schedule changes.

Accrued downtime becomes money at invoice time, and the arithmetic is per asset rather than per site. For each asset of the contract, subtract a grace allowance of 3 days from that asset's `out_of_service_days_current_period` and floor the result at 0, then multiply the remaining chargeable days by a per-day rate that depends on the asset's type: 1200 cents per day for a defibrillator and 250 cents per day for any other asset. Sum those per-asset amounts across the contract's assets to obtain the invoice's `downtime_credit_cents`. Because the grace is applied per asset and not once globally, an asset out of service for exactly 3 days credits nothing, and a site with one defibrillator out for 6 days and one cardio unit out for 4 days credits three days of defibrillator downtime and one day of general downtime — 3600 plus 250, or 3850 cents.

### 3.4. Program Design and Attainment Measurement

A **program offering** is the unit of programming: a named, targeted, recurring activity. Creating one names the `contract_id` it belongs to, the `lead_staff_id` of the instructor or trainer who leads it — who must be a staff member of that same contract — the `program_type` (one of `GROUP_EXERCISE`, `PERSONAL_TRAINING`, or `WELLNESS_CHALLENGE`), a `title`, a `participation_target` greater than zero representing counted participations over the program's whole life, a `max_participants_per_session` roster cap greater than zero, and the `required_credential_type` that every staff member assigned to any of its sessions must hold. Optionally it names `eligible_member_classes`, the set of member populations allowed to attend, drawn from `EMPLOYEE`, `DEPENDENT`, and `RETIREE`; when omitted it defaults to `EMPLOYEE` alone. The nominated lead must be on active duty at creation time — a lead whose `duty_status` is `SUSPENDED_FROM_DUTY` or `OFFBOARDED` is refused with `ERR_STAFF_NOT_ON_DUTY`. A new offering begins life in `DRAFT` with `provisional_attainment_pct`, `final_attainment_pct`, `counted_participation_total`, and `coverage_gap_count` all at 0. Offering listings can be filtered by `contract_id`, by `lead_staff_id`, by `status`, and by `program_type`.

The offering's lifecycle is driven by an `action` of `ACTIVATE`, `PAUSE`, `RESUME`, or `CLOSE`, optionally with a `reason`. **Activation** — `DRAFT → ACTIVE` — is the single most heavily gated operation in the system, because it is the point at which we commit to delivering a program safely. Five conditions must all hold: the owning contract must be `ACTIVE` (otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`); the lead's `duty_status` must be `ACTIVE_DUTY`; the lead must hold a certification record of the offering's `required_credential_type` in either `VERIFIED` or `EXPIRING_SOON` status (otherwise `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_VERIFIED`); the lead's `readiness_index` must be at least 70 (otherwise `ERR_READINESS_BELOW_THRESHOLD`); and the site must have at least one `READY` defibrillator (otherwise `ERR_AED_NOT_READY`). Activation recomputes both attainment figures. Note that `EXPIRING_SOON` is deliberately acceptable for the certification gate — a card 30 days from expiry still certifies the holder — while it simultaneously drags the lead's `readiness_index` down to a level that may fail the threshold gate; the two rules are independent and the certification gate is evaluated first.

**Pausing** takes the offering `ACTIVE → PAUSED` and cancels its future: every session of the offering in `SCHEDULED`, `STAFFED`, or `STAFFING_AT_RISK` status moves to `CANCELLED_CLIENT` with `cancel_reason` of `CLIENT_REQUEST`, which releases those sessions' assignments in `ASSIGNED` or `CONFIRMED` status, which returns each released assignment's `paid_minutes` to its staff member's `committed_minutes_current_period`. The offering's `provisional_attainment_pct` is then recomputed. **Resuming** takes it `PAUSED → ACTIVE`, requires the owning contract to be `ACTIVE`, and permits new sessions to be scheduled again — it does not restore the sessions the pause cancelled. The active-and-paused cycle may repeat indefinitely. An `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED` offering may be **closed**, moving it to `CLOSED`, but only when it has no session left in `SCHEDULED`, `STAFFED`, `STAFFING_AT_RISK`, or `IN_PROGRESS` status; a close attempted with live sessions outstanding is refused with `ERR_OPEN_SESSIONS`. Closing recomputes `final_attainment_pct` one last time and freezes `provisional_attainment_pct` at whatever value it last held. `CLOSED` is terminal, so any subsequent action is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`. A pause requested on an offering that is not `ACTIVE`, or a resume requested on one that is not `PAUSED`, is refused with `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_ACTIVE` — the same error a caller receives when trying to schedule a session against an offering that is not in a status permitting new bookings. The two remaining actions use the generic error instead: an `ACTIVATE` against an offering that is not in `DRAFT`, and a `CLOSE` against one still in `DRAFT`, are each refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Attainment is where the client's participation goal meets reality, and the system deliberately keeps **two** figures because they answer different questions and legitimately disagree. Both share the same denominator, the offering's `participation_target`, and both are integer percentages rounded half up which may exceed 100.

The **provisional** figure is forward-looking and answers "are we on track?". Its numerator is the count of participation records still in `COUNTED` status that belong to sessions of this offering whose status is `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED`, **plus** the sum of `forecast_attendance` across every session of this offering still open — that is, still in `SCHEDULED`, `STAFFED`, or `STAFFING_AT_RISK`. Multiply that numerator by 100, divide by `participation_target`, round half up, and store the result in `provisional_attainment_pct`. It is recomputed on every participation event and every session status change while the offering is in `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, or `PAUSED`.

The **final** figure is retrospective and answers "what did we actually deliver?". Its numerator is the count of participation records still in `COUNTED` status belonging to sessions of this offering in `COMPLETED` status only; forecasts and open sessions are ignored entirely. Multiply by 100, divide by `participation_target`, round half up, and store the result in `final_attainment_pct`. It is recomputed when a session completes and frozen when the offering closes.

Cancelled sessions — whether `CANCELLED_NO_COVER`, `CANCELLED_CLIENT`, or `CANCELLED_FACILITY` — contribute to **neither** numerator. Their forecast leaves the provisional numerator the instant they are cancelled, which is exactly why suspending a contract visibly drops the provisional attainment of every program at that site. To make the divergence auditable, a dedicated computed read reports the two figures side by side along with the components each used: `offering_id`, `participation_target`, the `counted_done` count of participations on in-progress and completed sessions, the `forecast_open` sum across still-open sessions, the `counted_final` count on completed sessions only, both `provisional_attainment_pct` and `final_attainment_pct`, and `coverage_gap_count`. As an illustration, an offering targeting 40 participations with one completed session holding 14 counted check-ins and two open sessions forecasting 12 and 9 attendees reports a provisional figure of 88 — twenty-one forecast plus fourteen counted, against a target of forty — and a final figure of 35.

Two further counters live on the offering. `counted_participation_total` is the number of participation records in `COUNTED` status across all of the offering's sessions, incremented on every check-in and decremented on every void. `coverage_gap_count` is a cumulative tally of how many times a session of this offering has been pushed into `STAFFING_AT_RISK` by a loss of covered staff; it only ever grows. Recovering a session back to `STAFFED` does **not** decrement it, because the point of the counter is to record that a coverage failure happened at all.

### 3.5. Session Scheduling, Coverage and Completion

A **program session** is one occurrence of an offering in one room at one time. Scheduling it names the `offering_id` of the parent program and the `room_id` of the booked space — the room must belong to the offering's own contract — together with `scheduled_start_utc` (inclusive) and `scheduled_end_utc` (exclusive, and strictly later than the start), a `forecast_attendance` head count of at least zero that may not exceed the offering's `max_participants_per_session`, and optionally `direct_supply_cost_cents`, the program-supply spend that a cost-plus client is billed through, which defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A new session lands in `SCHEDULED` with `actual_attendance` at 0, `cancel_reason`, `started_at`, and `completed_at` all null, and a computed `scheduled_minutes` equal to the whole number of minutes between its start and end instants, any part-minute remainder being discarded. Because the new session's `forecast_attendance` immediately enters the provisional numerator, scheduling recomputes the parent offering's `provisional_attainment_pct` and leaves `final_attainment_pct` alone.

Scheduling is guarded by five checks beyond the shape of the payload, and §2.11 fixes which one wins. The owning contract must be `ACTIVE` and the offering must be in a status that accepts new bookings, failing which the caller sees `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` or `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_ACTIVE`. The target room must be `OPEN`, failing which `ERR_ROOM_NOT_OPEN`. Everything referenced must belong to one contract, failing which `ERR_CROSS_CONTRACT_REFERENCE`. Then the two temporal checks, and finally the occupancy check.

**Room occupancy in time** is where the domain's cleaning reality shows up in the arithmetic. Rooms need turnover between bookings, so a session occupies its room as a **closed** interval from `scheduled_start_utc` to `scheduled_end_utc`, **expanded by 15 minutes on the end side only**. The new session conflicts with an existing session of the same room whenever the new start falls earlier than the existing end plus 15 minutes **and** the existing start falls earlier than the new end plus 15 minutes; the comparison runs against every session of that room whose status is not one of the three cancelled statuses. Two consequences follow directly and must both hold in an implementation: two sessions that merely touch on their raw endpoints — the new one starting at exactly the instant the existing one ends — **do** conflict, because the room is still being cleaned; and a gap of exactly 15 minutes between them — the new one starting at exactly the existing end plus 15 minutes — does **not** conflict. A conflict is refused with `ERR_ROOM_TIME_CONFLICT`.

**Term containment** forbids backdating and overreach: `scheduled_start_utc` must be at or after the current logical clock instant, and the session's dates must fall inside the contract's `term_start_on` through `term_end_on` window inclusive. A violation of either half is refused with `ERR_SESSION_OUT_OF_TERM`. Finally, **physical capacity**: `forecast_attendance` may not exceed the target room's `max_occupancy`, and an over-full forecast is refused with `ERR_ROOM_OCCUPANCY_EXCEEDED`. Note that this is a distinct ceiling from the offering's `max_participants_per_session` roster cap, which is enforced against `forecast_attendance` as a shape bound on the same request and therefore outranks it — though because that bound has to read the offering row to know the cap, it is evaluated after the `offering_id` and `room_id` have resolved, so an unknown offering or room still reports its own 404 in preference to the roster-cap `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Sessions can be listed filtered by `offering_id`, by `room_id`, by `status`, and by a scheduling window expressed as `starts_after` and `starts_before`.

Staffing a session means creating a **staffing assignment** naming the `session_id`, the `staff_member_id` of the person, and their `role_on_session`, which is either `LEAD` or `SUPPORT` and defaults to `LEAD`. The distinction is not cosmetic: only a `LEAD` assignment satisfies a session's staffing requirement, and only one live lead may exist per session at a time. Creating a second lead for a session that already has one in `ASSIGNED`, `CONFIRMED`, **or `VACANT_NEEDS_COVER`** status is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LEAD_ASSIGNMENT`: only an outright **release** frees the slot for a replacement, because a vacancy is still the record of an expected lead and the site manager is expected to release it before naming a substitute. The session must also be bookable: an assignment may only be created against a session in `SCHEDULED`, `STAFFED`, or `STAFFING_AT_RISK`, so one already `IN_PROGRESS` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` and one completed or cancelled with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`. Nothing here consults the owning contract's status. The person being assigned must hold the offering's `required_credential_type` in `VERIFIED` or `EXPIRING_SOON` status (else `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_VERIFIED`), must be on `ACTIVE_DUTY` (else `ERR_STAFF_NOT_ON_DUTY`), and must belong to the session's contract (else `ERR_CROSS_CONTRACT_REFERENCE`).

**Staff availability in time uses deliberately different interval semantics from the room rule.** An assignment occupies the person over the **half-open** interval from the session's `scheduled_start_utc` up to but excluding its `scheduled_end_utc`, with **no buffer at all**. A new assignment conflicts with one of that person's existing assignments — those in `ASSIGNED`, `CONFIRMED`, or `VACANT_NEEDS_COVER` status — whenever the new start falls earlier than the existing end and the existing start falls earlier than the new end. Back-to-back sessions, where one ends at exactly the instant the next begins, are therefore perfectly legal for a person even though they would be illegal for a room; and the lead's setup allowance, though it is paid, is not part of the occupied interval. A conflict is refused with `ERR_STAFF_TIME_CONFLICT`.

Creating the assignment books the labor immediately. `paid_minutes` is computed as the session's `scheduled_minutes` plus a setup allowance of 15 minutes when the `role_on_session` is `LEAD`, and simply the session's `scheduled_minutes` when it is `SUPPORT`. That figure is added to the staff member's `committed_minutes_current_period`, which in turn triggers a recomputation of their `readiness_index`. The assignment starts in `ASSIGNED` with `confirmed_at` null and `labor_cost_cents` at 0 — labor cost stays 0 until the assignment actually reaches `COMPLETED`. Assignments can be listed filtered by `session_id`, by `staff_member_id`, by `status`, and by `role_on_session`.

Confirmation is the staff member's acknowledgement that they will work the session, and it may be performed by the assigned person themselves or by their site manager. The scope check here is asymmetric: a `FitnessStaff` principal may confirm only an assignment whose `staff_member_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`, and any other assignment is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_SCOPE`, whereas a `SiteManager` principal is not scope-checked on this operation at all and may confirm any assignment in the system. Confirmation requires the assignment to be in `ASSIGNED` — one in `VACANT_NEEDS_COVER` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` and one already released or completed with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE` — the session to be in `SCHEDULED`, `STAFFED`, or `STAFFING_AT_RISK`, failing which `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` again, and the person to be on `ACTIVE_DUTY`, failing which `ERR_STAFF_NOT_ON_DUTY`. It moves the assignment `ASSIGNED → CONFIRMED` and stamps `confirmed_at` with the current instant. When the confirmed assignment is the `LEAD`, the session itself advances: `SCHEDULED → STAFFED`, or `STAFFING_AT_RISK → STAFFED` if it was a replacement covering an earlier gap. The move out of `SCHEDULED` recomputes the offering's `provisional_attainment_pct`; the recovery from `STAFFING_AT_RISK` explicitly does **not** decrement `coverage_gap_count`.

Releasing an assignment, optionally with a `reason`, is available from `ASSIGNED`, `CONFIRMED`, or `VACANT_NEEDS_COVER` and moves it to `RELEASED`. Release **compensates** the committed-minutes ledger: the assignment's `paid_minutes` is subtracted from the staff member's `committed_minutes_current_period` — except when the assignment was already in `VACANT_NEEDS_COVER`, because vacating it already performed that subtraction and doing it twice would corrupt the invariant. When the released assignment was the `LEAD` and the session is in `SCHEDULED` or `STAFFED`, the session moves to `STAFFING_AT_RISK` and the offering's `coverage_gap_count` increments by 1. More generally, a session drops from `SCHEDULED` or `STAFFED` into `STAFFING_AT_RISK` precisely when no assignment with `role_on_session` of `LEAD` remains in `ASSIGNED` or `CONFIRMED` status, and every such drop increments `coverage_gap_count`. `RELEASED` and `COMPLETED` are both terminal for an assignment; a further action against either is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`, and an assignment identifier that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`.

Running a session is driven by an `action` of `START`, `COMPLETE`, or `CANCEL`, with `cancel_reason` supplied on the cancel branch. **Starting** moves the session `STAFFED → IN_PROGRESS` and stamps `started_at`, and it is gated four ways: the session must be in `STAFFED` (else `ERR_SESSION_NOT_STAFFED`), the contract must be `ACTIVE` (else `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`), the room must be `OPEN` (else `ERR_ROOM_NOT_OPEN`), the site must have at least one `READY` defibrillator (else `ERR_AED_NOT_READY`), and the clock must have reached at most 15 minutes before `scheduled_start_utc` — a start attempted earlier than that grace window is refused with `ERR_SESSION_START_TOO_EARLY`. Because the state check outranks all three business gates, a session that is not `STAFFED` reports `ERR_SESSION_NOT_STAFFED` even when the site is also without a ready defibrillator, and the grace-window check is evaluated dead last of the five.

**Completing** moves the session `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` (from any other status the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`), stamps `completed_at`, and sets `actual_attendance` to the number of that session's participation records still in `COUNTED` status. It then settles the labor: every assignment of the session in `CONFIRMED` status moves to `COMPLETED` and receives its `labor_cost_cents`. An assignment still merely `ASSIGNED` when the session completes is deliberately left exactly as it is — it stays `ASSIGNED` on a completed session with `labor_cost_cents` at 0, is never released, and keeps its minutes booked — because only a confirmed shift is treated as worked. Committed minutes stay booked on completion — `COMPLETED` is inside the set of statuses the committed-minutes invariant sums over — so no compensation happens here. Finally, both `final_attainment_pct` and `provisional_attainment_pct` are recomputed for the owning offering.

Labor costing has one subtlety that an implementation must get exactly right: **each assignment is rounded on its own, and only the already-rounded amounts are summed.** For each participating assignment — those in `CONFIRMED` status at completion time, and thereafter in `COMPLETED` — multiply the staff member's `hourly_cost_cents` by the assignment's `paid_minutes`, divide by 60, and round half up to a whole number of cents; store that as the assignment's `labor_cost_cents`. The session's labor total is the plain sum of those per-assignment values. Rounding the aggregate minute count once instead produces a different answer and is wrong. Concretely, a 55-minute session staffed by a lead and a support worker both costing 4700 cents an hour books 70 paid minutes for the lead and 55 for the support worker; the lead's cost is 5483 cents (from 5483.33 rounded down by half-up on a value below the half) and the support worker's is 4308 cents (from 4308.33), for a session total of 9791 cents. Computing 4700 times 125 minutes over 60 and rounding once yields 9792 — off by one, and not what this specification requires.

**Cancelling** branches on `cancel_reason`, and the three branches differ in exactly one consequential way: whether the cancellation counts as a coverage failure that the operator pays for.

A `cancel_reason` of `CLIENT_REQUEST` moves the session from `SCHEDULED`, `STAFFED`, or `STAFFING_AT_RISK` to `CANCELLED_CLIENT`. Its side effects are: assignments in `ASSIGNED` or `CONFIRMED` status move to `RELEASED`, each release subtracting that assignment's `paid_minutes` from the staff member's `committed_minutes_current_period`; and the offering's `provisional_attainment_pct` is recomputed. The contract's `uncovered_session_count` is **not** incremented and no penalty accrues. This is also the branch that the system itself takes when an offering is paused or a contract is suspended.

A `cancel_reason` of `NO_COVER` moves the session from `SCHEDULED`, `STAFFED`, or `STAFFING_AT_RISK` to `CANCELLED_NO_COVER`, and additionally requires that the session genuinely has no cover: no assignment of it may have `role_on_session` of `LEAD` in `ASSIGNED` or `CONFIRMED` status, and a session that still holds such a lead is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Its side effects are: assignments in `ASSIGNED`, `CONFIRMED`, **or** `VACANT_NEEDS_COVER` status move to `RELEASED` with the same committed-minutes compensation (and, as always, no double subtraction for one already vacated); the contract's `uncovered_session_count` increments by 1; the contract's `pending_penalty_cents` is recomputed; and the offering's `provisional_attainment_pct` is recomputed. This is the branch the nightly sweep takes for at-risk sessions inside the lock window.

A `cancel_reason` of `FACILITY` moves the session from `SCHEDULED`, `STAFFED`, or `STAFFING_AT_RISK` to `CANCELLED_FACILITY` and sets `cancel_reason` accordingly. Its side effects are: assignments released with the same committed-minutes compensation, and `provisional_attainment_pct` recomputed. Like the client branch, it does **not** increment `uncovered_session_count` and accrues no penalty — a failed inspection is a facility problem, not a staffing shortfall. This branch is the one the system takes automatically when a room is restricted by a failing inspection or by a serious equipment incident. All four session end-states — `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED_NO_COVER`, `CANCELLED_CLIENT`, and `CANCELLED_FACILITY` — are terminal, and any further action against them is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`.

The **coverage shortfall penalty** is what makes uncovered cancellations expensive, and it applies to management-fee contracts only. On a `COST_PLUS` contract the penalty is always 0, because the client is already bearing actual cost. On a `MANAGEMENT_FEE` contract, first take the contract's `uncovered_session_count`, subtract a one-session allowance, and floor the result at 0 to obtain the chargeable count. Then compute the per-session amount as 1.5 percent of `management_fee_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents, and the ceiling as 5 percent of `management_fee_cents`, likewise rounded half up to whole cents — the two roundings are independent of one another. The penalty is the chargeable count multiplied by the per-session amount, or the ceiling, whichever is smaller. On a monthly fee of 40000 cents that yields a per-session amount of 600 and a ceiling of 2000, so four uncovered sessions cost three chargeable sessions at 600 each, or 1800; six uncovered sessions would compute to 3000 and are therefore clipped to the ceiling of 2000. Client-requested and facility cancellations never touch `uncovered_session_count` and therefore never move this figure.

### 3.6. Membership: Eligibility, Waivers and Badge Access

Members are enrolled against a `contract_id` by the client sponsor or by the operator. Enrolment supplies the `member_class` — `EMPLOYEE`, `DEPENDENT`, or `RETIREE` — the person's `full_name`, and two conditionally-required identifiers. An `EMPLOYEE` must carry an `employee_number`, the client's HR identifier, and members of the other two classes must leave it null; either mistake about that field is a shape violation refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and it is evaluated before the `contract_id` is looked up. A `DEPENDENT` must carry a `sponsor_member_id` naming the sponsoring employee, and an `EMPLOYEE` or `RETIREE` must leave it null; mistakes about *that* field are sponsorship failures rather than shape failures, as the next sentence sets out. Enrolment is not gated on the contract's status — a member may be enrolled against a draft contract — though verifying their eligibility later will be. The dependent's sponsor must itself be a member whose `member_class` is `EMPLOYEE` and whose `eligibility_status` is `ELIGIBLE`; a missing sponsor, a sponsor who is not an eligible employee, or a non-dependent that supplies a sponsor at all is refused with `ERR_SPONSOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. This same sponsorship rule is re-evaluated on eligibility decisions, not only at enrolment. Enrolment also accepts `health_data_consent`, which defaults to false when omitted, and is the switch that governs whether health-metric notes may ever be attached to this person's participation.

A member carries **two independent state dimensions**, and keeping them independent is a design requirement rather than an implementation detail. `eligibility_status` answers "is this person entitled to use the facility?" and moves through `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, `ELIGIBLE`, `LAPSED`, and `TERMINATED`. `access_status` answers "does this person's badge open the turnstile?" and moves through `NO_BADGE`, `ACTIVE`, and `REVOKED`. A new enrolment starts at `PENDING_VERIFICATION` and `NO_BADGE`, with `incentive_points_balance` at 0, `current_waiver_id` null, and `last_check_in_utc` null. Member listings can be filtered by `contract_id`, by `sponsor_member_id`, by `member_class`, and by either dimension via `eligibility_status` and `access_status`.

Eligibility moves under an `action` of `VERIFY`, `LAPSE`, `REINSTATE`, or `TERMINATE`, optionally with a `reason`. **Verification** takes the member `PENDING_VERIFICATION → ELIGIBLE`, requires the contract to be `ACTIVE`, and for a dependent requires the sponsoring employee to be `ELIGIBLE` at that moment. It increments the contract's `eligible_member_count` by 1 and leaves `access_status` completely untouched — verifying eligibility does not hand out a badge. **Lapsing** takes the member `ELIGIBLE → LAPSED`, decrements `eligible_member_count` by 1, and has two cross-cutting consequences: if the member's `access_status` was `ACTIVE`, it moves to `REVOKED`, and if the member's `member_class` is `EMPLOYEE`, every dependent whose `sponsor_member_id` names this member and who is currently `ELIGIBLE` also moves to `LAPSED`, each with the same badge consequence and the same decrement. **Reinstatement** takes the member `LAPSED → ELIGIBLE`, again requiring the contract to be `ACTIVE` and, for a dependent, an `ELIGIBLE` sponsor, and increments `eligible_member_count` by 1. Each of these three actions is admissible from exactly the one status named — `VERIFY` only from `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, `LAPSE` only from `ELIGIBLE`, `REINSTATE` only from `LAPSED` — and any other starting status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, evaluated before the contract-status and sponsorship gates. Crucially, reinstatement leaves `access_status` at `REVOKED`: the dimensions do not re-couple automatically, and a badge must be granted afresh. The lapse-and-reinstate cycle may repeat indefinitely.

**Termination** is reachable from `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, `ELIGIBLE`, and `LAPSED`, and it is the one decision with a full fan-out. The member's `eligibility_status` becomes `TERMINATED`; `eligible_member_count` is decremented by 1 **only** when the prior status was `ELIGIBLE`, so terminating a pending or already-lapsed member leaves the counter alone; `access_status` moves to `REVOKED`; the member's liability waiver in `SIGNED` status, if any, moves to `REVOKED` and `current_waiver_id` is cleared to null; and every dependent sponsored by this member is terminated too, with all of the same consequences applied to each. `TERMINATED` is terminal for the eligibility dimension, and any further eligibility action is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`. Termination also arrives as a cascade from contract termination, as §3.1 describes.

The badge dimension moves under its own `action` of `GRANT` or `REVOKE`, with an optional `badge_number` accepted alongside a grant — it must be a non-empty string when supplied, but it is not persisted: the member representation carries no badge-number field, so no response ever echoes the value back. A **grant** takes the member from `NO_BADGE` or from `REVOKED` to `ACTIVE`, and is gated twice: the member's `eligibility_status` must be `ELIGIBLE`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_NOT_VERIFIED`; and the member must hold a liability waiver in `SIGNED` status whose `expires_on` falls at or after today, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_REQUIRED`. When both are missing, the waiver error is the one returned, because the waiver gate is evaluated earlier. A grant leaves `eligibility_status` unchanged. A **revoke** takes the member `ACTIVE → REVOKED` and likewise leaves `eligibility_status` unchanged; the badge cycle from `ACTIVE` to `REVOKED` and back may repeat indefinitely. Both directions are strict about their starting point and neither has a terminal state of its own: a `GRANT` against a member whose badge is already `ACTIVE`, and a `REVOKE` against one who is `NO_BADGE` or already `REVOKED`, are each refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, checked ahead of the waiver and eligibility gates. Revocation also arrives as a cascade — from waiver expiry, from an eligibility lapse, and from an eligibility termination.

**Liability waivers** are recorded per member and are what make participation lawful. Filing one supplies the `waiver_version_code` of the waiver text the person signed (for example a code of the form WVR-2026A), the `expires_on` date after which it no longer protects anyone, which must be strictly later than the signing date — that is, strictly later than today, an `expires_on` of today or earlier being a structural violation refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and `acknowledged_health_disclosure`, which must be present and must be true, a false or absent value being refused the same way. The filing also carries the mandatory `expected_member_version` described in §2.7, which must be an integer of at least 1; a missing, non-integer, or smaller value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and only a well-formed value that disagrees with the member's current `version` reaches `ERR_STALE_MEMBER_VERSION`. Filing is admissible in every eligibility and badge state, including for a member who has been terminated. On success the new waiver is written in `SIGNED` status with `signed_at` stamped from the logical clock, the member's previously `SIGNED` waiver — if there was one — moves to `SUPERSEDED`, and the member's `current_waiver_id` is repointed at the new waiver while the member's own `version` advances. Superseding a waiver does **not** touch `access_status`: a member who already had an active badge keeps it across a re-signing.

Waivers reach `EXPIRED` only through the nightly sweep, which moves a `SIGNED` waiver to `EXPIRED` once its `expires_on` falls strictly before today, clears the member's `current_waiver_id` to null, and — when the member's `access_status` was `ACTIVE` — revokes the badge. `EXPIRED`, `SUPERSEDED`, and `REVOKED` are all terminal waiver states. `current_waiver_id` is a convenience pointer rather than a declared relationship; the authoritative history of a member's waivers is the member's own waiver listing, which returns newest first and can be filtered by `status`, and each waiver is also readable individually by its identifier. A waiver identifier that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_FOUND`.

### 3.7. Participation Capture and the Wellness Incentive Programme

A **participation record** is the check-in: proof that one member attended one session. It is created by naming the `member_id` and the `session_id`, optionally with a `health_metrics_note`. Fitness staff and site managers check members in at the desk; a member may also check themselves in, but only for themselves — a `Member` principal naming somebody else's `member_id` is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_SCOPE`. Which of those happened is recorded on the record itself: `recorded_by_role` is derived from the caller's role header and stores `MEMBER` for a self check-in and `FITNESS_STAFF` for anybody else. The record is written in `COUNTED` status with `check_in_utc` stamped from the logical clock, `void_reason` null, and the member's `last_check_in_utc` updated to the same instant. Of the two body foreign keys the `member_id` is resolved first, so a request naming neither a real member nor a real session reports `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`. Nothing about a check-in consults the owning contract's status. Because a participation record is an append-only event, it carries only `id` and `created_at` and no `updated_at` or `version`, as §2.4 explains.

Six gates stand in front of a check-in, and their relative order — fixed by §2.11 — is the part most likely to be implemented wrongly. Everything referenced must belong to one contract, or `ERR_CROSS_CONTRACT_REFERENCE`. The session must be in `IN_PROGRESS`; a check-in against a session in any other status, including one that has already been completed, is refused with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`. The member must hold a liability waiver in `SIGNED` status whose `expires_on` falls at or after today, or `ERR_WAIVER_REQUIRED`. The session must have room: the number of that session's records still in `COUNTED` status must be strictly below the offering's `max_participants_per_session`, and a full session is refused with `ERR_SESSION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; because voided records are not counted, voiding one genuinely frees a slot. The member's `member_class` must appear in the offering's `eligible_member_classes` list — some programmes are employee-only while others admit dependents and retirees — and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_CLASS_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. The member must have `access_status` of `ACTIVE` **and** `eligibility_status` of `ELIGIBLE` at the moment of check-in, and a failure of either is refused with `ERR_ACCESS_NOT_ACTIVE` — a revoked badge blocks participation even for someone whose eligibility is intact. And the member must not already hold a `COUNTED` record for that same session, a repeat being refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHECK_IN`; a previously voided record does not block a replacement check-in.

Two of those collisions are deliberate and their outcomes are specified rather than incidental. An unwaived member checking into a session that is also full receives `ERR_WAIVER_REQUIRED`, not `ERR_SESSION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, because the waiver gate sits above the capacity gate. And a full session that is also not in progress receives `ERR_SESSION_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`, because the state check sits above everything in this group. A `health_metrics_note` supplied for a member whose `health_data_consent` is false is refused with `ERR_HEALTH_DATA_FORBIDDEN`, as §2.10 sets out.

A successful check-in awards **wellness incentive points**, and the award is a threshold computation with a streak bonus and a weekly ceiling. Begin by establishing the week: the week start is Monday at 00:00:00Z of the ISO week containing the current logical instant. Sum `points_awarded` over every one of that member's participation records still in `COUNTED` status whose `check_in_utc` falls at or after the week start; call that the points already earned this week. Separately, count the number of **distinct calendar dates** on which the member has a `COUNTED` record whose `check_in_utc` falls within the trailing seven days, **excluding today's date** — repeat visits on one day count once, and today's own visits do not feed the streak. The window's lower edge is inclusive: a check-in dated exactly seven days before today counts, so the eligible dates are the seven calendar days running from today minus seven up to yesterday. There is no upper edge beyond the exclusion of today, so a record dated later than today would also count. The uncapped candidate award is 10 points, plus a further 5 points when that distinct-prior-day count is 3 or more. The remaining headroom is 60 points minus the points already earned this week, floored at 0. Then `points_awarded` is the smaller of the candidate and the headroom; `points_capped` is true exactly when the candidate exceeded the headroom; and `streak_bonus_applied` is true exactly when the distinct-prior-day count was 3 or more — note that the streak flag records whether the bonus was *earned*, so it stays true even on a record whose award was clipped to zero by the cap. Finally the member's `incentive_points_balance` is increased by `points_awarded`. Worked through: a member who already holds 50 counted points this ISO week and who checked in on 3 distinct prior days inside the window has a candidate of 15 and a headroom of 10, and so receives 10 points with `points_capped` true and `streak_bonus_applied` true. Points are whole numbers throughout and nothing here rounds.

The check-in also updates the aggregate figures: the session's `actual_attendance` increases by 1, the offering's `counted_participation_total` increases by 1, and both of the offering's attainment percentages are recomputed.

Mis-scans happen — the wrong member is picked from the list, or someone is checked into the wrong class — so a site manager may **void** a record, supplying a mandatory `void_reason`. Voiding requires the record to be in `COUNTED` status — an already-voided record is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE` — and the session to be in `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED_NO_COVER`, `CANCELLED_CLIENT`, or `CANCELLED_FACILITY`, a session still merely scheduled, staffed, or at risk being refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; the record moves to `VOIDED`, which is terminal. Its effects are **compensating**: the member's `incentive_points_balance` is reduced by exactly the `points_awarded` stored on that record, restoring the invariant that a member's balance always equals the sum of `points_awarded` across their surviving `COUNTED` records; the session's `actual_attendance` and the offering's `counted_participation_total` are each reduced by 1; and both attainment percentages are recomputed. All three of those decrements — the points balance, the attendance figure, and the participation total — are floored at 0, so none of them can be driven negative. The `points_awarded`, `points_capped`, and `streak_bonus_applied` values already written on the voided record are left exactly as they are — voiding does **not** retroactively re-open the weekly cap for records that were written earlier in the week. What it does do is change the inputs for the future: a subsequent check-in recomputes its own headroom from the surviving `COUNTED` rows, so the voided points become available again from that point onward. Records can be listed filtered by `member_id`, by `session_id`, by `status`, and by a check-in window expressed as `checked_in_after` and `checked_in_before`; a record identifier that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_PARTICIPATION_NOT_FOUND`, and a member identifier that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`. Each window edge is independently optional and **inclusive** — a record whose `check_in_utc` falls exactly on either bound is kept — and a bound that is not a parseable ISO 8601 instant is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the same inclusive-edge and parse rules govern the `starts_after` and `starts_before` window on the session listing of §3.5.

### 3.8. Incident Response, Review and Liability Claims

Incidents — a member who feels faint on a treadmill, a slip on a wet floor, a machine that fails mid-set — are the part of facility operations with the sharpest liability tail, and this system treats an incident report as a governed record with a review step that a second person must perform.

Filing a report names the `contract_id` of the site, the `reported_by_staff_id` of the staff member who witnessed or responded to it, the `occurred_at_utc` instant, which may not be later than the current logical clock instant — a later instant is a shape violation refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, evaluated before any identifier on the request is resolved — a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MODERATE`, or `SERIOUS`, a `cause_category` of `EQUIPMENT`, `MEDICAL`, `SLIP_FALL`, or `OTHER`, and a free-text `narrative`. Three optional fields make the report specific: `member_id` names the person involved when there was one, `asset_id` names the equipment involved when there was any, and `aed_deployed` records whether a defibrillator was actually used, defaulting to false. Both subject fields are genuinely optional and independent — an incident may name a member without equipment, equipment without a member, both, or neither — but everything named must belong to the same contract, or the filing is refused with `ERR_CROSS_CONTRACT_REFERENCE`. The four references resolve strictly in the order `contract_id`, `reported_by_staff_id`, `member_id`, `asset_id`, so a filing naming two identifiers that do not exist reports the 404 of the earlier one in that order; all four are resolved before any containment test runs, and the containment tests are then applied in the same order — reporting staff, then member, then asset. A new report lands in `REPORTED` with `return_count` at 0 and `reviewed_by_user_id`, `claim_reference`, and `closure_note` all null. Reports can be listed filtered by `contract_id`, by `member_id`, by `reported_by_staff_id`, by `asset_id`, by `status`, by `severity`, and by `cause_category`; an identifier that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`.

**Review** moves the report `REPORTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and records the reviewer in `reviewed_by_user_id`, optionally alongside a `review_note` — which is length-validated like any other string field but is recorded nowhere on the report, the reviewer's identity being the only thing the review persists. It enforces **segregation of duties**: the reviewer's `X-User-ID` may not equal the report's `reported_by_staff_id`, and a self-review is refused with `ERR_SELF_REVIEW_FORBIDDEN` at 403. Reviewing a report that is not in `REPORTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Review is also the trigger for this system's longest cascade, and it fires only under a precise conjunction: the report's `severity` must be `SERIOUS`, its `cause_category` must be `EQUIPMENT`, and its `asset_id` must be non-null. When all three hold, four things happen in sequence. First, the named asset moves to `OUT_OF_SERVICE` with `out_of_service_since` set to the current instant — which starts its downtime-credit clock, as §3.3 describes. Second, because that asset is now out of service, the room housing it moves from `OPEN` to `RESTRICTED` and its `open_deficiency_count` increases by 1 — and only from `OPEN`: a room already `RESTRICTED` or `CLOSED` keeps its state and its `open_deficiency_count` unchanged. Third, and regardless of which of those two the room was in, every session of the room in `SCHEDULED`, `STAFFED`, or `STAFFING_AT_RISK` status whose `scheduled_start_utc` falls at or before 24 hours from now moves to `CANCELLED_FACILITY`, taken in ascending `scheduled_start_utc` order with creation order as the tie-break. Fourth, each of those cancellations releases its assignments and subtracts each released assignment's `paid_minutes` from the corresponding staff member's `committed_minutes_current_period`. Because the facility branch is the one taken, none of those cancellations increments `uncovered_session_count` and no coverage penalty accrues — a machine failure is not a staffing shortfall. Sessions of the same room that start more than 24 hours out are left alone, on the expectation that the equipment will be repaired or the class relocated in time. If any of the three conditions fails — a `MODERATE` equipment incident, a `SERIOUS` medical incident, a `SERIOUS` equipment incident with no `asset_id` — the review is still recorded and **none** of the four links fires.

A reviewer who needs more detail may **return** the report, supplying a mandatory `return_note` — mandatory but, like the review note, validated and then discarded rather than persisted on the report. Return moves it `UNDER_REVIEW → REPORTED`, increases `return_count` by 1, and clears `reviewed_by_user_id` back to null so that the next review is a fresh decision. The equipment cascade is explicitly **not** reversed: an asset taken out of service and a room restricted stay that way, and cancelled sessions stay cancelled, because those were safety actions and not clerical ones. Restoring them is the job of the service-event and inspection workflows in §3.3. The return loop may repeat as often as needed, so `return_count` accumulates across the whole life of the report.

From `UNDER_REVIEW` the report takes one of two exits, and only a compliance auditor may take either. **Filing a claim** moves it `UNDER_REVIEW → CLAIM_FILED` and stores the insurer's `claim_reference`, which must be present and non-empty. It also enforces that there is actually somebody to claim for: the report's `member_id` must be non-null, and that subject member must have at least one liability waiver row on file in **any** status — `SIGNED`, `EXPIRED`, `SUPERSEDED`, or `REVOKED` all satisfy it, because what matters for claims administration is that waiver paperwork exists at all, not that it is currently valid. A report with no `member_id`, or one whose subject member has never signed anything, is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_SUBJECT_REQUIRED`. **Closing without a claim** moves it `UNDER_REVIEW → CLOSED_NO_CLAIM` and stores the mandatory `closure_note`, leaving `claim_reference` null forever. A filed claim is later wound up by the same closing action, moving it `CLAIM_FILED → CLAIM_CLOSED` and likewise storing the `closure_note`; the `claim_reference` it already carries is retained. Both `CLOSED_NO_CLAIM` and `CLAIM_CLOSED` are terminal, and any further action against either is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`. Attempting to return, to file a claim, or to close-without-claim on a report that is not in `UNDER_REVIEW` is refused with `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`; note that both the state check and the terminal check outrank the claim-subject check, so a closed report on which somebody tries to file a claim reports its state problem rather than its missing subject.

### 3.9. Period Close: Invoicing, the Nightly Readiness Sweep and Utilization Reporting

Every month the operator bills each client, and this is where the two fee structures introduced in §3.1 diverge most sharply. Drafting an invoice names the `contract_id`, the `period_month` being billed as a four-digit year and two-digit month joined by a hyphen, and the mandatory `expected_contract_version` described in §2.7. That version comparison is the first thing evaluated once the contract resolves, ahead even of the terminal-state check: drafting against a contract that is `TERMINATED` or `WITHDRAWN` is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`, but any other status may be drafted — including `DRAFT`, `PENDING_CLIENT_APPROVAL`, and `SUSPENDED` — because the period's costs are billable regardless of the site's present posture, and only *issuing* insists on `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`. The draft **pins** the contract's current `fee_structure` into `fee_structure_snapshot`, so that a contract amended mid-dispute is still rebilled under the terms that were in force when the period was first invoiced; every subsequent recomputation of that invoice reads the snapshot rather than the live contract. Any existing invoice for the same contract and period that is still in `DRAFT` moves to `SUPERSEDED` — which changes no money on the contract, because a draft was never billed — and the contract's own `version` is bumped, which is precisely why the next drafter needs a fresh read. That bump is unconditional and larger than one: every successful draft writes `version + 1` onto the contract as an explicit field, and that write is itself a mutation under §2.4, so the contract's `version` finishes exactly **2** higher than the value the caller had to supply and its `updated_at` is refreshed — and this happens on the first draft of a period too, where there was no prior draft to supersede. A new invoice lands in `DRAFT` with `rebill_count` at 0 and `issued_at`, `disputed_at`, `paid_at`, and `dispute_reason` all null. A period may accumulate any number of `DRAFT` and `SUPERSEDED` invoices, but only one live billed one: if another invoice for the same contract and period is already `ISSUED`, `DISPUTED`, or `PAID`, drafting is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_PERIOD_CONFLICT`. Invoices can be listed filtered by `contract_id`, by `status`, and by `period_month`; an identifier that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`.

Three components are computed the same way for both fee structures. `labor_subtotal_cents` is the sum of `labor_cost_cents` over every staffing assignment in `COMPLETED` status whose session's `completed_at` falls inside the billed `period_month` — assignments that were released, vacated, or are merely confirmed contribute nothing, and the session's completion date rather than its scheduled date decides which month it lands in. `program_direct_cost_cents` is the sum of `direct_supply_cost_cents` over every session of the contract in `COMPLETED` status inside the period. `downtime_credit_cents` is the equipment-downtime credit computed exactly as §3.3 specifies, from the out-of-service days of the contract's assets over the period.

On a **management-fee** contract the billing is a flat retainer adjusted downward for service failures. `admin_markup_cents` is 0 and `volume_credit_cents` is 0 — neither concept applies. `coverage_penalty_cents` is the coverage shortfall penalty from §3.5, computed from the contract's `uncovered_session_count` against its `management_fee_cents`. `gross_amount_cents` is simply the contract's `management_fee_cents`. `net_amount_cents` is that gross less `coverage_penalty_cents` less `downtime_credit_cents`; that difference is **not** floored at zero, so a period whose penalty and downtime credit together exceed the retainer stores a negative `net_amount_cents`. And `nte_capped` is always false, because a management-fee contract carries no `not_to_exceed_cents`. Worked through: a contract with a monthly fee of 40000 cents that suffered four uncovered sessions and accrued a downtime credit of 3850 cents bills a gross of 40000, a penalty of 1800, and a credit of 3850, for a net of 34350 cents. Note that `labor_subtotal_cents` and `program_direct_cost_cents` are still computed and stored for transparency; they simply do not enter the arithmetic.

On a **cost-plus** contract the billing is actual cost plus an administrative margin, less a volume discount, capped by the annual ceiling. `coverage_penalty_cents` is 0 — the client is already paying only for work actually delivered, so a missed session costs them nothing to penalise. Start from a base equal to `labor_subtotal_cents` plus `program_direct_cost_cents`. The markup rate steps down as that base grows: 12 percent when the base is 15000 cents or less, otherwise 9 percent when it is 30000 cents or less, otherwise 7 percent. `admin_markup_cents` is the base multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents. `gross_amount_cents` is the base plus `admin_markup_cents`. The volume credit rate then steps **up** with the gross: 3 percent when the gross is 70000 cents or more, otherwise 1.5 percent when it is 35000 cents or more, otherwise nothing at all. `volume_credit_cents` is the gross multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents and independently of the markup rounding. A provisional net is the gross less `volume_credit_cents` less `downtime_credit_cents`, and it too is not floored at zero. Finally the annual ceiling bites: compute the remaining headroom as the contract's `not_to_exceed_cents` less its `ytd_billed_cents`, floored at 0. If the provisional net **strictly exceeds** that headroom, `net_amount_cents` is set to the headroom and `nte_capped` is set to true; otherwise `net_amount_cents` is the provisional net and `nte_capped` is false — so a provisional net exactly equal to the headroom is billed in full with `nte_capped` false, and a negative provisional net, being below any headroom, is stored as-is with `nte_capped` false. Both threshold ladders are evaluated at their **boundaries inclusively on the side stated**: a base of exactly 15000 takes the 12 percent rate, a base of exactly 30000 takes 9 percent, a gross of exactly 35000 earns the 1.5 percent credit, and a gross of exactly 70000 earns 3 percent.

Worked through with the session from §3.5: a period whose completed assignments total 9791 cents of labor and whose completed sessions carried 23522 cents of direct supply cost gives a base of 33313, which falls above 30000 and therefore takes the 7 percent tier, producing an `admin_markup_cents` of 2332 and a `gross_amount_cents` of 35645. That gross is at or above 35000, so the 1.5 percent credit applies, giving a `volume_credit_cents` of 535. With a downtime credit of 3850 the net is 31260 cents, and against an annual ceiling of 5000000 with nothing billed year to date there is ample headroom, so `nte_capped` is false.

**Issuing** moves the invoice `DRAFT → ISSUED`, stamps `issued_at`, and adds `net_amount_cents` to the contract's `ytd_billed_cents`. It requires the contract to be in `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` status — a suspended site is still billed for work already done — and it re-checks the period conflict, refusing with `ERR_INVOICE_PERIOD_CONFLICT` when another invoice for the same contract and period has meanwhile become `ISSUED`, `DISPUTED`, or `PAID`. For a cost-plus contract it also refuses outright when the annual ceiling is already exhausted: if `not_to_exceed_cents` less `ytd_billed_cents` leaves no positive headroom, the net would clip to zero and the request is refused with `ERR_NTE_EXHAUSTED` rather than issuing a zero-value invoice. Issuing anything that is not in `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_DRAFT`.

**Disputing** is the client's move and requires a non-empty `dispute_reason`; only the sponsor of that very contract may do it, as §2.1 sets out. It moves the invoice `ISSUED → DISPUTED`, stamps `disputed_at`, and **compensates** the ledger by subtracting `net_amount_cents` back out of the contract's `ytd_billed_cents`. Disputing anything not in `ISSUED` is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_ISSUED`. **Rebilling** is the operator's answer: it moves the invoice `DISPUTED → DRAFT`, increases `rebill_count` by 1, clears `issued_at` back to null, and recomputes every money component from current period data under the **pinned** `fee_structure_snapshot`. It clears only `issued_at`: `disputed_at` and `dispute_reason` are left exactly as the dispute wrote them, so a rebilled draft still carries the timestamp and the reason of the dispute that produced it, and a later re-issue overwrites `issued_at` without touching either. Rebilling anything not in `DISPUTED` is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_DISPUTED`. The `DRAFT → ISSUED → DISPUTED → DRAFT` cycle may repeat indefinitely, and the fiscal invariant of §2.9 — that `ytd_billed_cents` equals the sum of `net_amount_cents` over the contract's invoices in `ISSUED` and `PAID` status — holds after every lap, which is exactly what the paired increment on issue and decrement on dispute guarantee. **Paying** is again the client's move, optionally supplying a `payment_reference` for their own reconciliation — a field that is length-validated and then discarded, never stored on the invoice and never returned by any read; it moves the invoice `ISSUED → PAID`, stamps `paid_at`, and leaves `ytd_billed_cents` untouched, because issuing already counted it. `PAID` and `SUPERSEDED` are both terminal, and any further action against either is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`.

The **nightly readiness sweep** is the scheduled ops checkpoint. It is driven explicitly rather than by a background timer — an operator names a `contract_id` in the request body, which must resolve or the run is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, and the run evaluates that contract against the logical clock — and it executes seven steps in a **fixed order** that an implementation may not rearrange, because later steps consume the state that earlier steps produce. The contract's own status is not a precondition: the sweep is admissible against a contract in any status at all, `TERMINATED` and `WITHDRAWN` included, since its purpose is to record the passage of time rather than to transact business.

Step one handles **credential expiry**. Every credential of the contract's staff in `VERIFIED` status whose `expires_on` falls at or before today plus 30 days but strictly after today moves to `EXPIRING_SOON`; every credential in `VERIFIED` or `EXPIRING_SOON` status whose `expires_on` falls at or before today moves to `EXPIRED`. Those credential transitions are all applied first, sweeping the contract's staff in creation order and each holder's credentials in creation order within that; only once every credential has its new status does the four-link staffing cascade run, **once per affected holder**, in the order those holders were first encountered. So a staff member who loses two required credentials on the same night is suspended and vacated exactly once, not twice. First, when the expired credential's `credential_type` is one of those required for the holder's `role_type`, the holder moves from `ACTIVE_DUTY` to `SUSPENDED_FROM_DUTY` and their `readiness_index` is forced to 0. That first link is also the cascade's gate: it fires only for a holder who was on `ACTIVE_DUTY` at the moment the expiry is processed, so an expiry on someone already `SUSPENDED_FROM_DUTY` or `OFFBOARDED` — or an expiry of a type that is not required for the holder's `role_type` — records the credential's new status and stops there, vacating nothing and putting no session at risk. Second, every assignment of that suspended person in `ASSIGNED` or `CONFIRMED` status on a session in `SCHEDULED`, `STAFFED`, or `STAFFING_AT_RISK` status moves to `VACANT_NEEDS_COVER`, taken in ascending `created_at` order, each vacancy subtracting that assignment's `paid_minutes` from the person's `committed_minutes_current_period` and recomputing the person's `readiness_index`. Third, every session whose `LEAD` was just vacated — processed in ascending `scheduled_start_utc` order — moves to `STAFFING_AT_RISK` and the owning offering's `coverage_gap_count` increases by 1, and only for a session that was in `SCHEDULED` or `STAFFED` and now retains no `LEAD` in `ASSIGNED` or `CONFIRMED` status; a session already at risk is neither re-transitioned nor re-counted. Fourth, every session now at risk whose `scheduled_start_utc` falls at or before the current instant plus 24 hours is cancelled as an uncovered session: it moves to `CANCELLED_NO_COVER` with `cancel_reason` of `NO_COVER`, the contract's `uncovered_session_count` increases by 1, the contract's `pending_penalty_cents` is recomputed, and the offering's `provisional_attainment_pct` is recomputed. A session further out than the lock window is left at risk in the hope that a replacement lead confirms in time. Note that a credential moving merely to `EXPIRING_SOON` triggers none of this — an expiring credential still authorises work, which is exactly why the warning window exists.

Step two recomputes `readiness_index` for every one of the contract's staff, using the three-component formula of §3.2 — except that anyone currently `SUSPENDED_FROM_DUTY`, whether suspended tonight or long before, has their index pinned at 0 instead. An `OFFBOARDED` staff member is not exempt: their index is recomputed by the ordinary formula like anybody else's. Step three handles **waiver expiry**: every waiver in `SIGNED` status whose `expires_on` falls strictly before today moves to `EXPIRED`, the owning member's `current_waiver_id` is cleared to null when — and only when — it was pointing at the waiver that has just expired, and a member whose `access_status` was `ACTIVE` has it revoked. Step four sweeps up **badge revocation** for members whose `eligibility_status` is no longer `ELIGIBLE` but whose `access_status` is still `ACTIVE`, moving each to `REVOKED`. Step five recomputes the **equipment** picture: every non-defibrillator asset still `IN_SERVICE` whose preventive-maintenance interval has elapsed — `last_service_on` null, or at least `service_interval_days` whole days from `last_service_on` to today, exactly the rule §3.3 states — is flagged `MAINTENANCE_DUE`, and every defibrillator's `readiness_status` is recomputed by the four-valued algorithm of §3.3. The two halves are disjoint: defibrillators are outside the preventive-maintenance flag entirely and the sweep never moves an `AED` to `MAINTENANCE_DUE` whatever its service history, while a non-defibrillator's `readiness_status` stays `NOT_APPLICABLE`. An asset that is already `MAINTENANCE_DUE`, or that is `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, is passed over rather than re-flagged. Step six recomputes both **attainment** percentages for the contract's offerings. Step seven is the final **coverage sweep**: any session still in `STAFFING_AT_RISK` whose `scheduled_start_utc` falls inside the 24-hour lock window is moved to `CANCELLED_NO_COVER` with the same uncovered-session accounting and penalty accrual as step one's fourth link. Steps one and seven overlap deliberately, and the overlap must not double-count: a session already cancelled by step one is terminal and is simply not eligible for step seven.

The run returns a tally of what it did, so that an operator can see the night's damage at a glance: the `contract_id` it ran against, the `run_at_utc` instant it ran at, and then `credentials_expiring_soon`, `credentials_expired`, `staff_suspended`, `assignments_vacated`, `sessions_at_risk`, `sessions_cancelled_no_cover`, `waivers_expired`, `badges_revoked`, `assets_maintenance_due`, `aed_not_ready`, and `penalty_accrued_cents`. Nine of those figures are transition counts — the number of records the run itself moved — so a second run over an unchanged contract returns zeros for all nine. Two are not. `aed_not_ready` is a **standing** figure: the number of the contract's defibrillators whose freshly recomputed `readiness_status` is anything other than `READY`, so it keeps reporting the same non-zero number night after night until the units are serviced, and it is 0 for a site with no defibrillators at all. And `penalty_accrued_cents` is a **difference**: the contract's `pending_penalty_cents` after the run less its value before the run, floored at 0, so it reports only what this run added and never a negative number even if the penalty recomputation happened to lower it. Of the transition counts, `staff_suspended`, `assignments_vacated`, and `sessions_at_risk` are contributed only by step one's staffing cascade, while `sessions_cancelled_no_cover` accumulates across both step one's fourth link and step seven.

A companion read gives the same picture without changing anything, for a named `contract_id`. It reports the `contract_id`, then `aed_ready_count` and `aed_not_ready_count` across the site's defibrillators, `credentials_expiring_soon` and `credentials_expired`, `staff_suspended_count`, `sessions_at_risk`, `rooms_restricted`, and `assets_out_of_service`. It is available to the operator, to the site manager, and to the compliance auditor, and it never mutates a record — in particular it recomputes nothing, reporting the statuses as they currently stand rather than refreshing them the way the sweep does. Its `contract_id` is a required query parameter, and a missing or blank one is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every figure it reports is a standing count over the contract's rows, not a transition count: `credentials_expiring_soon` and `credentials_expired` span every credential of every staff member of the contract whatever its `credential_type` and whether or not that type is required for the holder's `role_type`; `staff_suspended_count` counts staff in `SUSPENDED_FROM_DUTY` only, not those `OFFBOARDED`; `sessions_at_risk` counts every session of the contract in `STAFFING_AT_RISK` regardless of how far off its start is, with no lock-window restriction; `rooms_restricted` counts rooms in `RESTRICTED` only, not those `CLOSED`; and `assets_out_of_service` spans all three asset types, so a defibrillator out of service is counted both there and in `aed_not_ready_count`.

Finally, the **utilization report** is what the client's HR director actually reads, and it is the report most tightly constrained by privacy. It is computed for a named `period_month` on one contract and is available to the operator, to the compliance auditor, and to that contract's own sponsor. The `period_month` is a required query parameter and must be a four-digit year and two-digit month joined by a hyphen; missing, blank, or otherwise shaped, it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and no bound is placed on how far the month may lie from the contract's term or from today — an unbilled or future month simply reports zeros. It reports the `period_month` requested, the contract's `eligible_member_count`, and `distinct_participants` — the number of distinct `member_id` values across participation records still in `COUNTED` status whose `check_in_utc` falls inside the period, counting only records whose member belongs to this contract. The report names no `contract_id` of its own: the contract is identified by the path the caller used. From those it derives `participation_rate_pct`, which is 0 when `eligible_member_count` is 0 and otherwise the distinct participant count multiplied by 100, divided by the eligible member count, and rounded half up to a whole percent. It restates the contract's `participation_target_pct` and reports `target_variance_pct` as the achieved rate minus the target, which may legitimately be negative when the programme is underperforming.

The report also breaks participation down by member class, and this is where the privacy floor applies. `class_breakdown` contains one entry for each of `EMPLOYEE`, `DEPENDENT`, and `RETIREE`, in that order, and every entry always names its `member_class`. When the number of distinct participants in that class is fewer than 5, the entry reports `suppressed` as true with `participant_count` and `check_in_count` both null, because with so few people in a cohort the aggregate would be re-identifiable. Otherwise the entry reports `suppressed` as false with the real `participant_count` and `check_in_count` values. A cohort of exactly 5 is reported with real counts — the floor excludes only cohorts strictly below it. `suppressed_cohort_count` reports how many of the three entries were suppressed, so the reader knows the breakdown is incomplete without being able to reconstruct what was hidden. And `health_metrics_note` is never projected into this report for any role whatsoever: the aggregate view exists to measure programme reach, never to expose an individual's health data.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Endpoints that bring a new record into existence return HTTP 201; endpoints that read, that move an existing record through its lifecycle, or that compute a report return HTTP 200. The thirteen aggregates of §2.6 are the only records this system persists, so recording a room inspection, an asset service event, or a staff drill creates nothing of its own: each of those three endpoints writes its effect onto the room, asset, or staff member named in its path and returns that updated record with HTTP 200.
