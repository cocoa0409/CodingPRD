# Union Grievance & Arbitration Tracking API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A labor relations office lives and dies by dates. A grievance that is filed one day past the contractual filing window is barred no matter how meritorious it is; an employer answer that lands one day past the step response window converts a procedural dispute into a default; an arbitration demand filed after the demand window closes forfeits the case entirely. Today these deadlines are tracked on spreadsheets maintained separately by the union side and the employer side, which means the two parties routinely disagree about what the deadline even was — and the disagreement usually surfaces only after the deadline has passed. This document specifies the API for a single shared system of record that computes every contractual deadline from the governing collective agreement, records every procedural act against that timeline, and exposes the resulting compliance posture identically to both parties.

The system covers the full arc of a case. A union steward files a grievance citing a specific contract clause and naming a respondent supervisor. The labor relations office schedules step meetings, records that they were held, and answers them; the union may appeal up a three-step ladder or, once the ladder is exhausted, demand arbitration. Along the way either party may put a settlement offer on the table, which the employer side must authorize through an authority ladder calibrated to the money at stake and reviewed against prior awards. Once a case reaches arbitration, the system issues a strike panel of arbitrators, runs the alternate-strike selection, coordinates the case file and the witnesses, books the hearing, and records the award and the remedy that follows from it. Every dollar the case consumes — arbitrator fees, transcripts, witness release time, travel, counsel and filing charges — is posted to a per-case ledger and split between the parties according to a published cost-sharing schedule.

Two design commitments run through everything below. First, the office needs analytics it can defend in bargaining: the system continuously derives which supervisors and which contract clauses generate grievances, and how often those grievances end adversely for the employer. Second, because contractual deadlines must be reproducible and testable rather than dependent on the machine's wall clock, the entire system reads a **logical clock** that an operator moves explicitly. Everything the API considers "now" — filing windows, response deadlines, appeal windows, offer expiry, interest accrual, hearing conclusion and remedy implementation deadlines — is measured against that clock and nothing else.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every route described in this document is mounted under the `/labor-relations` prefix, with the sole exception of the logical-clock control described in §2.4.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that operator is acting in. If either header is missing or empty, or if `X-User-Role` names anything other than one of the six roles below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorized for the requested operation, the rejection is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `UnionSteward`, `Grievant`, `LaborRelationsAnalyst`, `LaborRelationsDirector`, `ChiefNegotiator` and `ArbitrationAdministrator`.

- A `UnionSteward` is the union representative who files and prosecutes grievances. When `X-User-Role` is `UnionSteward`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Employee`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Employee` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. In particular, the steward who files a grievance is stamped onto it, and only that steward may take union-side action on it thereafter — an attempt by a different steward is rejected with `ERR_NOT_FILING_STEWARD` (HTTP 403). Stewards file grievances, appeal them, withdraw or consolidate them and demand arbitration; they share the drafting of settlement offers with the `LaborRelationsAnalyst`, share responses to authorized offers with the `LaborRelationsDirector`, share panel strikes with the `LaborRelationsDirector`, and share witness assignment with the `LaborRelationsAnalyst`. Stewards may call every listing and read operation except the two reads scoped to the `Grievant`.
- A `Grievant` is the aggrieved bargaining-unit member. When `X-User-Role` is `Grievant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Employee`; ownership checks compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. A `Grievant` has no write powers at all and may read only their own case: reading a single grievance, and listing that grievance's step records, are permitted only when the grievance's `grievant_id` equals the caller's `X-User-ID`. Any other grievance returns `ERR_NOT_CASE_PARTICIPANT` (HTTP 403).
- A `LaborRelationsAnalyst` is the case handler inside the labor relations office. When `X-User-Role` is `LaborRelationsAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow calls for it — as `responded_by_user_id` on a `GrievanceStep` when the analyst answers a step, and as `prepared_by_user_id` on a `SettlementOffer` the analyst drafts — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The analyst schedules step meetings, records that meetings were held, records employer answers, files case-file components, records award payments and posts costs; the analyst holds no settlement authority whatsoever.
- A `LaborRelationsDirector` heads the labor relations office. When `X-User-Role` is `LaborRelationsDirector`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example as `authorized_by_user_id` on a `SettlementOffer`, as `confirmed_by_user_id` on a `WitnessAssignment`, as `voided_by_user_id` on a `CostEntry`, or as `struck_by_user_id` on a `PanelStrike`) but does not compare it to any entity's `id`. The director registers collective agreements, clauses, people and precedents, approves witness releases, voids cost entries, drives the logical clock, holds the first tier of settlement authority, responds to union-side offers and takes employer-side panel strikes.
- A `ChiefNegotiator` is the senior management negotiator holding the upper tier of settlement authority. When `X-User-Role` is `ChiefNegotiator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it as `authorized_by_user_id` on the offer that is authorized but does not compare it to any entity's `id`. Authorizing settlement offers is this role's only write power, which is deliberate: it keeps approval strictly separated from case handling.
- An `ArbitrationAdministrator` is the neutral administrator of the arbitration track. When `X-User-Role` is `ArbitrationAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it where an arbitration act is logged but does not compare it to any entity's `id`. The administrator maintains the arbitrator roster, issues strike panels, books hearings and records awards. The administrator never takes a strike and never authorizes a settlement.

Beyond role membership, several rules turn on which **party** a role speaks for. The `UnionSteward` is the UNION party. The `LaborRelationsAnalyst`, the `LaborRelationsDirector` and the `ChiefNegotiator` are all the EMPLOYER party. The `ArbitrationAdministrator` and the `Grievant` belong to neither party and can never satisfy a party-alignment test. Where a rule requires the acting role's party to match — or to be the opposite of — a party recorded on a resource, a failure is reported as `ERR_PARTY_ROLE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list operation is paginated through two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and is never negative. Both are echoed back in the `meta` object of the response envelope alongside `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page).

Unless a list states otherwise, rows are returned in ascending `created_at` order. The following lists carry a different normative ordering, and every ordering in this system is total — no ordering, ranking, tie-break or selection rule anywhere in this specification reads an `id` value:

- Contract clauses of an agreement: ascending `clause_number`.
- People: ascending `employee_number`.
- Testimony slots reserved for one person, and the witnesses coordinated for one arbitration case: ascending `slot_start_at`, then ascending `created_at`.
- Grievances: ascending `created_at`, then ascending `case_number`.
- Step records of one grievance: ascending `step_number`, then ascending `created_at`.
- Precedent cases: descending `decided_on`, then ascending `citation`.
- Settlement offers: ascending `round_number`, then ascending `created_at`.
- Strikes taken against one arbitrator across all cases: ascending `created_at`, then ascending `strike_order`.
- Arbitration demands: ascending `demanded_at`, then ascending `created_at`.
- Strikes taken on one arbitration demand: ascending `strike_order`.
- Cost entries of one case: ascending `incurred_at`, then ascending `created_at`.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. At server startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it stays there until an operator moves it; it never advances on its own and never falls back to wall-clock time. A `LaborRelationsDirector` moves it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, and the response reports the clock's new reading as `current_utc`. Every subsequent evaluation of a deadline, a window, an expiry, an overlap, a compliance posture or an interest accrual reads the new instant.

Two arithmetic conventions apply wherever the clock is consulted. **Whole calendar days between two instants** means the number of complete 86,400-second periods separating them, with any remainder discarded — twenty-nine days and twenty-three hours is twenty-nine days, not thirty. **Adding days to an instant** means adding that many 86,400-second periods, so a deadline lands at the same time of day as its anchor. Deadline comparisons are stated individually in the rules below, and boundary inclusivity matters: an act performed at the exact instant of its deadline is inside the window in every rule in this document.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Every persisted resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional-second digits. Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of that row, and a `version` integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row.

Two resource types are exceptions and carry only `id` and `created_at`: a `PrecedentCase`, which is immutable reference data once recorded, and a `PanelStrike`, which is an immutable event record. Neither has an `updated_at` field and neither has a `version` field; those keys are absent from their payloads entirely.

Several fields on several resources are **derived at read time** rather than stored: they are recomputed from current data every time the resource is returned, and they never carry a stale value. The `status` and `open_grievance_count` of a `BargainingUnit`; the `filed_count`, `sustained_count`, `denied_count` and `sustain_rate_bps` of a `ContractClause`; the scorecard fields of an `Employee`; the `cited_count` of a `PrecedentCase`; the `status` of an `Arbitrator`; the timeliness and cost fields of a `Grievance`; and the `readiness_score` and `actual_cost_cents` of an `ArbitrationDemand` are all of this kind. Derivation at read time does not bump `updated_at` or `version` on the read resource.

### 2.6. Money, Hours and Rounding

All money in this system is carried as whole integer cents; there are no fractional currency units and no floating-point money fields. Wherever a computation produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded **half up** — to the nearest integer, with a value of exactly one half going away from zero — before it is stored or before it feeds the next named step of a calculation. No intermediate value is ever carried forward as a real number past its own rounding step; the calculations in §3 name each rounding point explicitly, and the order of those roundings is normative.

Rates and proportions are expressed in **basis points**, where 10,000 basis points is 100 percent. Both derived rate fields in the system — `sustain_rate_bps` on a clause and `adverse_rate_bps` on a supervisor — are exactly 0 when their denominator is 0. Zero, not null, and not an error.

Hours are the one quantity that is not an integer. Witness release time is measured in hours and is rounded **up to the next quarter of an hour**, never down and never to the nearest quarter; the money derived from those hours is then rounded half up to whole cents.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on the Grievance Record

Two operations may not proceed on a stale view of a grievance, and both are guarded by an optimistic concurrency check against `Grievance.version`.

Authorizing a settlement offer requires the request to carry `expected_grievance_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` the client last observed on the offer's parent grievance. If that value does not equal the grievance's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_GRIEVANCE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The check exists because an authorization legitimately supersedes any other authorized offer on the same grievance — the superseded one is retired — so a writer holding a stale view of the grievance would silently retire an authorization it never saw. The stale-version rejection forces that writer to re-read the grievance first.

Filing an arbitration demand likewise requires `expected_grievance_version` as a non-optional field, and likewise rejects a mismatch against the grievance's current `version` with `ERR_STALE_GRIEVANCE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A demand supersedes the grievance's step posture and freezes the settlement track, so a writer whose view predates an intervening authorization, step answer or appeal must re-read the grievance before committing the case to arbitration.

No other operation in this system takes a version field. Any request body field of that shape on any other operation is not part of this specification.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline and Version Semantics

Many acts in this system touch more than one record. When they do, every affected record is updated within the same request, and each mutated record has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented by 1. The cascades are enumerated in the workflow narratives of §3, and they are not optional or best-effort: a reader who fetches the affected records immediately after the request must observe every stated consequence.

Four acts additionally increment the parent grievance's `version` explicitly, because that counter is the anchor of the optimistic-concurrency checks in §2.7: authorizing a settlement offer, filing an arbitration demand, recording an award, and the closure of a grievance when its award becomes fully implemented. Consolidating one grievance into another increments the **surviving** grievance's `version` as well, because that grievance's remedy figures are mutated by the absorption.

### 2.9. Nested Resource Retrieval

Many routes address a child through its parent. Path parameters are validated **left to right**: the leftmost identifier is resolved first, and if it names nothing, the request fails immediately with that resource's own not-found error before any identifier further right is examined. The not-found errors are resource-specific — `ERR_BARGAINING_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GRIEVANCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STEP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRECEDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEMAND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ARBITRATOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AWARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COST_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WITNESS_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_EMPLOYEE_NOT_FOUND` — and all of them carry HTTP 404.

The same catalogue is used for identifiers supplied in a request body rather than in the path. A body naming a `bargaining_unit_id`, `grievant_id`, `respondent_supervisor_id`, `supervisor_id`, `clause_id`, `precedent_case_id`, `employee_id`, `arbitrator_id` or `consolidated_into_grievance_id` that resolves to nothing is rejected with the corresponding not-found error, where a missing clause is reported as `ERR_CLAUSE_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.10. Order of Evaluation and Error Precedence

When a single request would fail several checks, the outcome is not a matter of taste: the checks are evaluated strictly in the order below, and the **first** check that fails determines the error the caller receives. A later check never masks an earlier one, and an earlier check never yields to a later one merely because the later failure seems more interesting. The list is the complete precedence ladder for the whole system; the business meaning of each rule is developed in §3.

1. Both headers are present and `X-User-Role` names one of the six declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. The acting role is authorized for the requested operation — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. Every resource named by a path parameter exists, resolved left to right — otherwise the matching not-found error from §2.9.
4. A `Grievant` reading a grievance, or listing that grievance's step records, is the grievance's own `grievant_id` — otherwise `ERR_NOT_CASE_PARTICIPANT`.
5. A `UnionSteward` taking a union-side action is the steward who filed the grievance — otherwise `ERR_NOT_FILING_STEWARD`. This guards five acts: appealing a grievance, withdrawing or consolidating a grievance, demanding arbitration, taking a panel strike as the `UnionSteward`, and responding to a settlement offer as the `UnionSteward`.
6. The request body conforms to the operation's input schema — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Conformance means: every required field is present; every enum-typed field carries a declared member; integers constrained to be non-negative or strictly positive respect that constraint; datetimes parse as ISO 8601; `panel_memberships` is a non-empty array; `sustained_fraction_bps` is consistent with the award `outcome`; `interest_accrual_start` is present whenever `interest_rate_bps` is greater than 0; `counter_monetary_cents` is present whenever `decision` is `COUNTER`; `granted_amount_cents` is supplied only alongside a `disposition` of `GRANTED` or `PARTIAL`; and `reinstatement_effective_date` is supplied only for an award that ordered reinstatement.
7. Every resource named by a body field exists — `bargaining_unit_id`, `grievant_id`, `respondent_supervisor_id`, `supervisor_id`, `clause_id`, `precedent_case_id`, `employee_id`, `arbitrator_id` and `consolidated_into_grievance_id` — otherwise the matching not-found error from §2.9.
8. `expected_grievance_version` equals the grievance's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_GRIEVANCE_VERSION`. This guards settlement authorization and arbitration demand, the two operations named in §2.7.
9. The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — otherwise the state error for that resource type, all at HTTP 409: `ERR_INVALID_GRIEVANCE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_STEP_STATE`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, `ERR_DEMAND_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_AWARD_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_COST_ENTRY_ALREADY_VOIDED`.
10. The governing collective agreement derives to `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_EXPIRED`. This guards four acts: filing a grievance, scheduling a step meeting, appealing, and demanding arbitration.
11. The demand's selected arbitrator does not derive to `INACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_ARBITRATOR_INACTIVE`, on hearing scheduling.
12. The scheduled hearing has concluded before an award is recorded — otherwise `ERR_HEARING_NOT_CONCLUDED`.
13. The operator authorizing a settlement offer did not record an employer step answer on the same grievance — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
14. The employee called as a witness is not the steward who filed the grievance — otherwise `ERR_ADVOCATE_WITNESS_CONFLICT`.
15. The employee called as a witness is not `TERMINATED` — otherwise `ERR_WITNESS_NOT_EMPLOYED`.
16. The grievance's cited clause is arbitrable — otherwise `ERR_CLAUSE_NOT_ARBITRABLE`, on arbitration demand.
17. The grievant and the respondent supervisor both belong to the named bargaining unit — otherwise `ERR_UNIT_MEMBERSHIP_MISMATCH`, at intake.
18. The cited clause belongs to the named bargaining unit — otherwise `ERR_CLAUSE_UNIT_MISMATCH`, at intake.
19. A cited precedent construes the same clause the grievance cites — otherwise `ERR_PRECEDENT_CLAUSE_MISMATCH`, at offer drafting.
20. Party alignment holds — otherwise `ERR_PARTY_ROLE_MISMATCH`. On offer drafting the acting role's party must equal `offering_party`; on offer response it must be the opposite of `offering_party`.
21. The acting role's party equals the demand's `strike_turn` — otherwise `ERR_NOT_STRIKE_TURN`.
22. The struck arbitrator is on the panel and has not already been struck on this demand — otherwise `ERR_ARBITRATOR_NOT_ON_PANEL`.
23. Identifier uniqueness holds — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`. This guards four creations: a bargaining unit (`local_number` unique across the whole system), a contract clause (`clause_number` unique within its unit), a person (`employee_number` unique within their unit) and a precedent case (`citation` unique across the whole system).
24. `panel_size` is 3, 5 or 7 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PANEL_SIZE`.
25. An appeal is not taken from step 3 — otherwise `ERR_STEP_LADDER_EXHAUSTED`.
26. Case-file preparation has reached at least the briefs stage before an award is recorded — otherwise `ERR_PREP_INCOMPLETE`.
27. A step scheduled with extension days is permitted by the agreement — otherwise `ERR_TOLLING_NOT_PERMITTED`.
28. The eligible arbitrator pool holds at least `panel_size` candidates — otherwise `ERR_PANEL_POOL_INSUFFICIENT`, on panel request.
29. `incident_date` is at or before `discovery_date`, which is at or before the logical clock's current instant — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DISCOVERY_DATE`.
30. The filing window has not been exceeded — otherwise `ERR_GRIEVANCE_UNTIMELY`.
31. `meeting_scheduled_at` is strictly after the logical clock's current instant — otherwise `ERR_MEETING_DATE_IN_PAST`.
32. An appeal from `ANSWERED` is taken no later than the grievance's `appeal_deadline_at` — otherwise `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.
33. An arbitration demand is filed no later than the grievance's `arbitration_deadline_at` — otherwise `ERR_ARBITRATION_DEMAND_UNTIMELY`.
34. `hearing_end_at` is strictly after `hearing_start_at` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_HEARING_WINDOW`.
35. The arbitrator has no buffered calendar conflict — otherwise `ERR_ARBITRATOR_UNAVAILABLE`.
36. The testimony slot does not overlap another slot of the same person — otherwise `ERR_WITNESS_SLOT_CONFLICT`.
37. The person's daily release capacity is not exceeded — otherwise `ERR_WITNESS_RELEASE_CAP_EXCEEDED`.
38. The offer has not expired — otherwise `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`, on offer response.
39. The offer's authority tier is within the acting role's ceiling — otherwise `ERR_AUTHORITY_EXCEEDED`.
40. An offer whose base tier is `DIRECTOR` or above cites a precedent — otherwise `ERR_PRECEDENT_REVIEW_REQUIRED`.
41. The payment does not exceed the award's remaining net remedy — otherwise `ERR_OVERPAYMENT`.

Two consequences of this ladder are worth calling out because they are easy to get backwards. Because the stale-version check sits at position 8 and every state check sits at position 9 or later, a request that is *both* stale and aimed at a wrongly-stated resource reports `ERR_STALE_GRIEVANCE_VERSION`, not the state error. And because segregation of duties sits at position 13 while the authority ceiling sits at position 39, an operator who both answered the step and lacks the authority tier is told `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties

The office's compliance posture rests on one principle: the person who commits the employer to a position is never the person who reviewed it. Three separate mechanisms implement it. First, the operator who recorded an employer answer at any step of a grievance — captured as `responded_by_user_id` on that `GrievanceStep` — may never authorize a settlement offer on that same grievance, whatever role they hold; the attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Second, settlement authority is capped by role rather than by seniority, so no single operator can raise their own ceiling. Third, the union's advocate is separated from the union's evidence: the steward who filed a grievance may not also be called as a witness in its arbitration, which is rejected with `ERR_ADVOCATE_WITNESS_CONFLICT` (HTTP 422).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up an Agreement, a Roster and a Precedent Library

Everything in this system hangs off a **collective agreement**, modelled as a bargaining unit. A `LaborRelationsDirector` registers one by supplying a display `name`, a `local_number` identifying the union local, and the two instants that bound the agreement: `contract_effective_date`, the first instant the agreement is in force, and `contract_expiry_date`, which must be strictly after it. The `local_number` is between 1 and 32 characters and is unique across the entire system; a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`. Registration also fixes the procedural arithmetic that will govern every case filed under the agreement. `step1_response_days`, `step2_response_days` and `step3_response_days` are the calendar days the employer has to answer a meeting held at each rung of the ladder, defaulting to 10, 15 and 20; `appeal_window_days` is the union's window to appeal after a step resolves, defaulting to 7; `filing_window_days` is the window from discovery within which a grievance must be filed at all, defaulting to 30; and `arbitration_demand_days` is the window after step-3 resolution within which arbitration must be demanded, defaulting to 21. Each of those five day-counts must be at least 1. The boolean `tolling_enabled` defaults to false and, while false, forbids any step from being scheduled with agreed extension days.

The same registration fixes the money ceilings for settlement authority. `director_authority_cents` is the highest settlement exposure a `LaborRelationsDirector` may authorize and defaults to 250,000 cents — two and a half thousand dollars — while `negotiator_authority_cents` is the corresponding ceiling for a `ChiefNegotiator`, defaults to 1,500,000 cents, and must be strictly greater than the director's ceiling. Both are inclusive ceilings and neither may be negative. `precedent_deviation_tolerance_bps` defaults to 2,000 basis points — twenty percent — and is the amount by which a settlement may deviate from the precedent it cites before the authority required to approve it escalates. A registered agreement reports two derived fields: its `status`, which reads `ACTIVE` while the logical clock is before `contract_expiry_date` and `EXPIRED` from that instant onward, evaluated afresh at every read and at every write that names the unit; and `open_grievance_count`, the number of grievances filed under it whose status is not one of `SETTLED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `CONSOLIDATED` or `CLOSED`. An agreement that has derived to `EXPIRED` cannot be the subject of new procedural activity, and the four acts guarded by that rule are rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_EXPIRED`.

Clauses are added to an agreement one at a time by the same director. A clause carries a `clause_number` — the article designator, unique within its own agreement, colliding with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` — a human-readable `title`, and a `category` drawn from `DISCIPLINE`, `PAY`, `SENIORITY`, `SAFETY`, `LEAVE` and `SUBCONTRACTING`, which is the axis the office's trend analysis reports along and the axis the clause listing may be filtered on. Two clause flags reshape the arithmetic of every grievance citing them. `is_arbitrable` defaults to true; when it is false no arbitration may ever be demanded on a grievance citing that clause, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_CLAUSE_NOT_ARBITRABLE`. `expedited` defaults to false; when it is true the filing window and every step response window are halved, with the halved figure rounded **up** to a whole day. A third field, `monetary_cap_cents`, defaults to null meaning uncapped, must otherwise be zero or more, and puts a hard ceiling on the settlement exposure the office will recognize for grievances citing the clause. Each clause reports four derived counters described in §3.9 — `filed_count`, `sustained_count`, `denied_count` and `sustain_rate_bps` — all of which read 0 for a newly added clause.

People are registered by the director as well, and one registry covers everyone — grievants, supervisors and stewards alike. A person carries a `bargaining_unit_id`, a `full_name`, an `employee_number` unique within their own unit (again `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` on collision), a `hire_date`, a `seniority_date` that must be at or after the hire date, an `hourly_rate_cents` that must be strictly positive, and a `classification` of `PRODUCTION`, `MAINTENANCE`, `CLERICAL` or `SUPERVISOR`. The reporting line is self-referential: `supervisor_id` names another person in the registry and is null at the top of the chain, and the people listing may be filtered by it as well as by `bargaining_unit_id`, `classification` and `employment_status`. That last field is the person's employment standing — `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `TERMINATED` or `REINSTATED` — and defaults to `ACTIVE`. It may be seeded at registration so that reinstatement remedies are exercisable; after registration the only thing that ever changes it is the implementation of an award that ordered reinstatement, which moves it to `REINSTATED`. The hourly rate is the pivot of three separate money calculations: back pay in settlement exposure, back pay in an award remedy, and the cost of releasing the person from work to testify. Each person also reports the supervisor scorecard of §3.9 — `open_grievance_count`, `closed_grievance_count`, `sustained_against_count` and `adverse_rate_bps` — plus `witness_release_hours_ytd`, the release hours accumulated across every case where their assignment stands at `CONFIRMED` or `TESTIFIED`. All of those counters read 0 for a freshly registered person.

The **precedent library** is the office's institutional memory. A precedent records the `clause_id` it construes, a reporter `citation` unique across the whole library, the `decided_on` date the award issued, the `outcome` of that case as `SUSTAINED`, `DENIED` or `SPLIT`, the `monetary_value_cents` the award recognized, the `arbitrator_name` who wrote it, and its `binding_scope` — `UNIT_WIDE`, `DEPARTMENT` or `INDIVIDUAL`. Scope is not decorative: it discounts the precedent's money value when a settlement is measured against it, at 1.00 for `UNIT_WIDE`, 0.75 for `DEPARTMENT` and 0.50 for `INDIVIDUAL`. Precedents are immutable reference data — they carry no `updated_at` and no `version` — and report one derived counter, `cited_count`, the number of settlement offers naming them whose status is anything other than `VOIDED`, which reads 0 for a freshly recorded precedent. The library may be listed narrowed by `clause_id`, by `binding_scope` or by `outcome`.

Finally, the `ArbitrationAdministrator` maintains the **arbitrator roster**. An arbitrator carries a `full_name`, a non-empty `panel_memberships` array drawn from `AAA`, `FMCS` and `PERMANENT_UMPIRE`, a strictly positive `per_diem_cents` daily fee, a `cancellation_fee_cents` of zero or more charged when a scheduled hearing is abandoned, an `earliest_available_at` instant before which the arbitrator will accept no work, a `travel_buffer_hours` padding that defaults to 24 and is applied on **both** sides of every hearing they already have booked when calendar conflicts are tested, a `max_concurrent_cases` engagement ceiling defaulting to 3 and at least 1, and an `is_active` roster flag defaulting to true. The system maintains `active_case_count` itself, starting at 0 and counting the arbitration demands currently engaging that arbitrator, and derives `status` from the other three: `INACTIVE` whenever `is_active` is false; otherwise `FULL` once `active_case_count` has reached `max_concurrent_cases`; otherwise `AVAILABLE`. Roster maintenance by the administrator can take an arbitrator off the roster or put them back, move `earliest_available_at`, adjust `max_concurrent_cases` or revise `per_diem_cents`, and re-derives `status` immediately — but it never disturbs a hearing that is already booked.

### 3.2. Grievance Intake and the Filing Window

Only a `UnionSteward` files a grievance. The intake payload names the agreement it is filed under through `bargaining_unit_id`, the aggrieved member through `grievant_id`, the supervisor whose action is grieved through `respondent_supervisor_id`, and the contract clause alleged to have been violated through `clause_id`. It carries a narrative `statement`, an `incident_date` recording when the grieved act occurred and a `discovery_date` recording when the union became aware of it. Two optional money figures state what the union wants: `remedy_requested_cents`, defaulting to 0 and never negative, and `back_pay_hours_claimed`, also defaulting to 0 and never negative.

Intake enforces three referential rules before it looks at the calendar. The grievant and the respondent supervisor must both belong to the bargaining unit named in the body, or the filing is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_MEMBERSHIP_MISMATCH`; the cited clause must belong to that same unit, or the rejection is `ERR_CLAUSE_UNIT_MISMATCH`; and the agreement itself must derive to `ACTIVE`, or the rejection is `ERR_AGREEMENT_EXPIRED`. Note that the supervisor named as respondent is not required to be the grievant's own supervisor, and the two may sit anywhere in the reporting chain.

The calendar rules come next. The dates must be ordered: `incident_date` at or before `discovery_date`, and `discovery_date` at or before the logical clock's current instant. A discovery that precedes the incident, or one that lies in the future, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DISCOVERY_DATE`. Then the **filing window** is tested. The window begins as the agreement's `filing_window_days`; if the cited clause is `expedited`, that figure is halved and rounded up to a whole day, so a thirty-day window becomes fifteen days and a twenty-one-day window becomes eleven. The elapsed time is the number of whole calendar days from `discovery_date` to the clock's current instant. The filing is rejected with `ERR_GRIEVANCE_UNTIMELY` only when the elapsed days are **strictly greater** than the window — filing on exactly the last day of the window is timely. Note carefully what the window does *not* read: `incident_date` plays no part in it whatsoever and is only range-checked against `discovery_date`, and the only clause attribute that matters is the `expedited` flag.

A grievance that survives all of that is written with several server-controlled fields. Its `case_number` is minted as the literal prefix `GR-`, then the agreement's `local_number`, then another hyphen, then a four-digit zero-padded ordinal counting this grievance's position among the grievances of that same unit in ascending `created_at` order, starting at `0001`. Its `filed_at` records the clock's current instant, and `filed_by_steward_employee_id` records the `X-User-ID` of the filing steward — the value that every later union-side act on this case is checked against. Its `current_step` starts at 1 and only ever reads 1, 2 or 3; its `status` starts at `FILED`; its `timeliness_state` starts at `TIMELY`; its `settled_amount_cents` starts at 0; and `consolidated_into_id` starts null. Filing also moves two sets of analytics: the cited clause's `filed_count` and `sustain_rate_bps` change on the next read, and the respondent supervisor's `open_grievance_count` rises by exactly 1 on the next read.

Grievances may be listed with filters that cover every foreign key on the record — `bargaining_unit_id`, `grievant_id`, `respondent_supervisor_id`, `clause_id` and the self-referential `consolidated_into_id` — plus `status` and `timeliness_state`. Reading a single grievance returns its timeliness posture, both deadline instants and the four cost fields, all derived at the moment of the read.

### 3.3. The Step Ladder and the Timeliness Posture

The grievance procedure is a three-rung ladder, and the ladder is a cycle: an answered grievance may be appealed, an appeal schedules another meeting, that meeting is held and answered, and the cycle repeats until step 3 is exhausted. Concretely, the procedural status of a grievance runs `ANSWERED` → `APPEALED` → `STEP_MEETING_SCHEDULED` → `AWAITING_RESPONSE` → `ANSWERED`, with the loop bounded by `current_step` never exceeding 3.

A `LaborRelationsAnalyst` opens each rung by scheduling the step meeting. This is permitted only when the grievance sits at `FILED` — the first rung — or at `APPEALED` — every later rung — and it moves the grievance to `STEP_MEETING_SCHEDULED`. The payload carries `meeting_scheduled_at`, which must be strictly after the logical clock's current instant or the request is rejected with `ERR_MEETING_DATE_IN_PAST`, and an optional `extension_days` defaulting to 0 and never negative. Extension days are the parties' mutually agreed padding on the employer's response window, and they are a privilege of the agreement: any positive value requires the agreement's `tolling_enabled` to be true, and otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_TOLLING_NOT_PERMITTED`. The new step record starts at `SCHEDULED` with its `step_number` copied from the grievance's `current_step` — 1 on the first rung, then 2, then 3 — and `meeting_held_at`, `response_due_at`, `responded_at`, `disposition`, `response_summary` and `responded_by_user_id` all null, `granted_amount_cents` at 0 and `late_by_days` at 0. A step scheduled with positive extension days immediately re-derives the grievance's posture to `TOLLED`. Steps of one grievance may be listed and filtered by `status`.

Recording that the meeting was **held** is the instant the employer's response clock starts, and again the analyst does it. The step must be `SCHEDULED` and its grievance must be at `STEP_MEETING_SCHEDULED`; the step moves to `HELD`, the grievance moves to `AWAITING_RESPONSE`, and `meeting_held_at` is stamped with the clock's current instant. The response deadline is computed at that moment and written to `response_due_at`. It starts from the per-step window in the agreement — `step1_response_days` for a step numbered 1, `step2_response_days` for a step numbered 2, `step3_response_days` for a step numbered 3. If the cited clause is `expedited`, that window is halved and rounded **up** to a whole day, so ten days become five, fifteen days become eight and twenty days become ten. The step's own `extension_days` are then added. The order is normative: the halving and its upward rounding happen first, and the extension is added to the already-rounded figure, never before it. The result is added as whole days to `meeting_held_at` — the scheduled meeting instant is irrelevant to the deadline, only the instant the meeting was recorded as held anchors it. Holding a meeting also lifts a `TOLLED` posture back to `TIMELY`.

The employer's answer is recorded against a `HELD` step by the analyst, who supplies a `disposition` of `GRANTED`, `PARTIAL` or `DENIED`, a narrative `response_summary`, and — only when the disposition is `GRANTED` or `PARTIAL` — a `granted_amount_cents` figure of zero or more. Supplying money alongside a `DENIED` disposition is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The step moves to `ANSWERED`, `responded_at` takes the clock's current instant, and `responded_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID` — the value that the segregation-of-duties bar of §2.11 will consult if that same operator later tries to authorize a settlement on this grievance. `late_by_days` is then computed as the whole calendar days from `response_due_at` to `responded_at`, floored at 0, so an answer delivered early or exactly on time reads 0. Where the answer goes next depends on the disposition: a `DENIED` or `PARTIAL` answer moves the grievance from `AWAITING_RESPONSE` to `ANSWERED`, with a `PARTIAL` answer **adding** its granted amount to the grievance's `settled_amount_cents`; a `GRANTED` answer settles the case outright, moving the grievance from `AWAITING_RESPONSE` straight to `SETTLED`, **setting** `settled_amount_cents` to the granted amount, transitioning every settlement offer on that grievance still in `DRAFT` or `AUTHORIZED` to `VOIDED`, and freezing the timeliness posture at whatever value it held at that instant. A late answer — one where `late_by_days` is greater than 0 — re-derives the posture to `EMPLOYER_DEFAULT`.

**Appealing** is the union's move and belongs to the filing steward alone. It carries an `appeal_statement`, requires the agreement to still be `ACTIVE`, and requires `current_step` to be below 3 — an appeal attempted from step 3 is rejected with `ERR_STEP_LADDER_EXHAUSTED`. There are two ways in. The ordinary route runs from `ANSWERED`: the employer has answered, and the union appeals no later than the grievance's `appeal_deadline_at`, an appeal after that instant being rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. The second route runs from `AWAITING_RESPONSE` and exists for the case where the employer simply never answered: when the latest step is still `HELD` and the clock has passed its `response_due_at`, the union may appeal anyway. In that case the overdue step is first transitioned from `HELD` to `DEEMED_DENIED`, the grievance's `appeal_deadline_at` is recomputed from that step's `response_due_at` rather than from a non-existent `responded_at`, and the grievance's `timeliness_state` is set to `EMPLOYER_DEFAULT`. Either route increments `current_step` by 1 and leaves the grievance at `APPEALED`, ready for the next meeting to be scheduled. A step that is deemed denied stops the response clock from ever being satisfied, and both the appeal window and the arbitration window then anchor on `response_due_at`.

Steps have one further exit. When a grievance reaches `SETTLED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `CONSOLIDATED`, every step of that grievance still standing at `SCHEDULED` or `HELD` is transitioned to `CANCELLED`, and a cancelled step stops contributing to the timeliness posture entirely.

The **timeliness posture** itself is derived, not stored by hand, and it is re-evaluated at the end of every request that mutates a grievance or one of its steps and again on every read of the grievance. It reads five inputs: the grievance's own `status` and `current_step`, that grievance's non-`CANCELLED` step records, whether any arbitration demand exists for the grievance, and the agreement's `appeal_window_days` and `arbitration_demand_days`. The **latest step** means the non-cancelled step with the greatest `step_number`, and where two share that number, the one with the greater `created_at`. A step's **resolution instant** is its `responded_at` when it stands at `ANSWERED`, its `response_due_at` when it stands at `DEEMED_DENIED`, and undefined otherwise. From those, `appeal_deadline_at` is the latest step's resolution instant plus the agreement's `appeal_window_days`, and is null while no step is resolved; `arbitration_deadline_at` is the step-3 record's resolution instant plus the agreement's `arbitration_demand_days`, and is null unless `current_step` is 3 and that step is resolved.

The posture is then selected by the following branches, **in this order**, the first matching branch winning:

1. If the grievance's status is `SETTLED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `CONSOLIDATED` or `CLOSED`, the posture is frozen: it keeps the value it held when the grievance became terminal and is never recomputed again.
2. Otherwise, if there is no latest step at all, the posture is `TIMELY`.
3. Otherwise, if `current_step` is 3, the latest step has a resolution instant, no arbitration demand exists for the grievance, and the clock is strictly past `arbitration_deadline_at`, the posture is `BARRED`.
4. Otherwise, if the latest step has a resolution instant, the grievance stands at `ANSWERED`, and the clock is strictly past `appeal_deadline_at`, the posture is `UNION_DEFAULT`.
5. Otherwise, if the latest step stands at `DEEMED_DENIED`, or it stands at `ANSWERED` with `late_by_days` greater than 0, the posture is `EMPLOYER_DEFAULT`.
6. Otherwise, if the latest step stands at `SCHEDULED` with `extension_days` greater than 0, the posture is `TOLLED`.
7. Otherwise the posture is `TIMELY`.

That order is normative and it matters at the edges. A case sitting at step 3 past both the appeal window and the arbitration window satisfies branches 3 and 4 simultaneously, and `BARRED` must win. Every deadline comparison in the ladder is strictly greater-than, so an act performed at the exact instant of a deadline is still inside its window.

### 3.4. Settlement Offers, Precedent Review and the Authority Ladder

Either side may put terms on the table at any point while the grievance is alive. A `LaborRelationsAnalyst` drafts the employer's positions and a `UnionSteward` drafts the union's; the drafting payload declares which side it speaks for through `offering_party`, either `UNION` or `EMPLOYER`, and the acting role's party must equal that declaration or the draft is rejected with `ERR_PARTY_ROLE_MISMATCH`. The terms themselves are a `monetary_cents` lump sum of zero or more, an optional `back_pay_hours` figure defaulting to 0 and never negative, and an optional `remedy_terms` bundle defaulting to `NONE` and drawn from `NONE`, `RECORD_EXPUNGEMENT`, `SENIORITY_RESTORATION`, `POLICY_CHANGE` and `REINSTATEMENT`. An optional `precedent_setting` boolean, defaulting to false, marks an offer the office considers precedent-setting. An optional `precedent_case_id` cites a precedent from the library; when supplied, that precedent must construe the very clause the grievance cites, or the draft is rejected with `ERR_PRECEDENT_CLAUSE_MISMATCH`. Drafting is refused outright on a grievance that has already reached a terminal status. The new offer is written at `DRAFT` with `round_number` 1 and `prepared_by_user_id` set to the drafting operator's `X-User-ID`, and with `authorized_by_user_id`, `authorized_at` and `expires_at` all null.

**Exposure and the authority tier** are computed at drafting time and re-verified when authorization is attempted, and they run in four stages whose order is normative.

The first stage prices the terms. The back-pay component is the offer's `back_pay_hours` multiplied by the grievant's `hourly_rate_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents. The non-monetary bundle carries its own loading: `NONE` adds nothing, `RECORD_EXPUNGEMENT` adds 25,000 cents, `SENIORITY_RESTORATION` adds 75,000 cents, `POLICY_CHANGE` adds 150,000 cents, and `REINSTATEMENT` adds whichever is larger of a floor of 200,000 cents and the grievant's hourly rate multiplied by forty hours — one full working week at the grievant's rate. The raw exposure is the `monetary_cents` lump sum plus the priced back pay plus the term loading.

The second stage applies the precedent-setting multiplier and then the clause cap, in that order. When `precedent_setting` is true the raw exposure is multiplied by 1.25 and rounded half up; when it is false the multiplier is 1.00. Only then, if the cited clause carries a non-null `monetary_cap_cents`, the result is reduced to that cap if it exceeds it. The figure that survives both operations is stored as `exposure_cents`. Applying the cap before the multiplier is the wrong reading and produces a different number.

The third stage picks the base tier **from the capped exposure**. An exposure of 50,000 cents or less sits in the `ANALYST` tier; above that and up to the agreement's `director_authority_cents` it is `DIRECTOR`; above that and up to `negotiator_authority_cents` it is `NEGOTIATOR`; anything above that is `BOARD`. Every one of those comparisons is inclusive, so an exposure landing exactly on a ceiling stays in the lower tier.

The fourth stage is **precedent review**, and it can escalate the tier by exactly one level and never more. If no precedent is cited, `precedent_deviation_bps` is null and the `authority_tier` is simply the base tier. If a precedent is cited, its `monetary_value_cents` is first scope-adjusted — multiplied by 1.00 for `UNIT_WIDE`, 0.75 for `DEPARTMENT` or 0.50 for `INDIVIDUAL`, rounded half up — and `precedent_deviation_bps` is then the absolute difference between `exposure_cents` and that scope-adjusted value, multiplied by 10,000, divided by the scope-adjusted value or by 1 if that value is 0, and rounded half up. When the deviation is strictly greater than the agreement's `precedent_deviation_tolerance_bps`, the `authority_tier` becomes the next rung up the ladder `ANALYST` → `DIRECTOR` → `NEGOTIATOR` → `BOARD`, with `BOARD` escalating to itself; when the deviation is within tolerance, the `authority_tier` equals the base tier.

**Authorization** is where the employer's approval hierarchy bites, and only a `LaborRelationsDirector` or a `ChiefNegotiator` may attempt it. The offer must still be at `DRAFT`, or the attempt is rejected with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_DRAFT`. The request carries the mandatory `expected_grievance_version` described in §2.7 and an optional `authorization_note`. Three substantive bars then apply in the order given in §2.10. The operator must not be the `responded_by_user_id` of any step on that grievance, or the attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The offer's `authority_tier` must sit within the acting role's ceiling: a `LaborRelationsDirector` may authorize the `ANALYST` and `DIRECTOR` tiers, a `ChiefNegotiator` may authorize the `ANALYST`, `DIRECTOR` and `NEGOTIATOR` tiers, and **no role in this system may authorize a `BOARD`-tier offer at all** — every failure here is `ERR_AUTHORITY_EXCEEDED`. Finally, precedent review is compulsory above the lowest tier: an offer whose base tier is `DIRECTOR` or above must cite a precedent, so an offer with no `precedent_case_id` may only be authorized when its tier is `ANALYST`, and otherwise the attempt is rejected with `ERR_PRECEDENT_REVIEW_REQUIRED`.

An authorization that clears all three bars moves the offer from `DRAFT` to `AUTHORIZED`, stamps `authorized_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and `authorized_at` with the clock's current instant, and sets `expires_at` to ten days after that instant. It also has two consequences beyond the offer itself: every **other** offer on the same grievance standing at `AUTHORIZED` is transitioned to `LAPSED` and can no longer be responded to, and the parent grievance's `version` increments by 1. That version bump is exactly what the stale-version check of §2.7 protects — it is why a second authorizer working from an older read is turned away rather than silently retiring an authorization they never observed.

**Responding** to an authorized offer belongs to the other side of the table: a `UnionSteward` responds to employer offers and a `LaborRelationsDirector` responds to union offers, and in both cases the acting role's party must be the opposite of the offer's `offering_party`, failing with `ERR_PARTY_ROLE_MISMATCH`. The offer must be at `AUTHORIZED`, or the response is rejected with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, and the clock must be at or before `expires_at`, or it is rejected with `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`. The response carries a `decision` of `ACCEPT`, `REJECT` or `COUNTER`, an optional `response_note`, and — mandatory on a counter — `counter_monetary_cents`, alongside the optional `counter_back_pay_hours`, `counter_remedy_terms` and `counter_precedent_setting` that describe the counter-proposal's remaining terms.

Each decision behaves differently. `ACCEPT` moves the offer to `ACCEPTED` and settles the case: the grievance moves to `SETTLED` from whichever of `FILED`, `STEP_MEETING_SCHEDULED`, `AWAITING_RESPONSE`, `ANSWERED`, `APPEALED` or `ARBITRATION_DEMANDED` it occupied, its `settled_amount_cents` is set to the accepted offer's `exposure_cents`, and the terminal cascade of §3.5 fires. `REJECT` moves the offer to `REJECTED` and changes nothing else — the grievance is untouched and the negotiation may continue with a fresh draft. `COUNTER` moves the responded offer to `COUNTERED` and creates a **new** offer on the same grievance in `DRAFT`, with `offering_party` flipped to the responding side, `round_number` one greater than the countered offer's, and `prepared_by_user_id` set to the responding operator's `X-User-ID`. That new draft is deliberately **not** returned by the response; the only way to discover it is to list the settlement offers of the grievance, filtered by `grievance_id`. This is what makes the negotiation cyclic: draft, authorize, counter, and a fresh draft repeats the loop at a higher round number. Offers may also be listed filtered by `precedent_case_id`, by `status` or by `offering_party`.

### 3.5. Withdrawal, Consolidation and the Terminal Cascade

A grievance may be pulled off the table at any time before it becomes terminal, and only its filing steward may do it. The request carries a `reason` and, optionally, a `consolidated_into_grievance_id`. Without that identifier, the grievance moves to `WITHDRAWN`. With it, the grievance moves to `CONSOLIDATED` instead and is folded into the named survivor: the target must exist, must not itself be terminal, must share this grievance's `bargaining_unit_id` and `clause_id`, and must not be the grievance being withdrawn. On consolidation the withdrawing grievance's `consolidated_into_id` is set to the survivor's identifier, the survivor **absorbs** the remedy figures — its `remedy_requested_cents` grows by the consolidated grievance's `remedy_requested_cents` and its `back_pay_hours_claimed` grows by the consolidated grievance's `back_pay_hours_claimed` — and the survivor's own `version` increments by 1.

Whichever exit is taken, the same cascade runs. Every step of the grievance standing at `SCHEDULED` or `HELD` is transitioned to `CANCELLED`. Every settlement offer standing at `DRAFT` or `AUTHORIZED` is transitioned to `VOIDED`, which drops `cited_count` on any precedent those offers named. Any arbitration demand on the grievance standing at `DEMANDED`, `PANEL_ISSUED`, `STRIKING`, `ARBITRATOR_SELECTED` or `HEARING_SCHEDULED` is transitioned to `WITHDRAWN`, and where that demand had already fixed a `selected_arbitrator_id`, the arbitrator is released: `active_case_count` drops by 1 and the arbitrator's `status` is re-derived, which is how a `FULL` arbitrator becomes `AVAILABLE` again.

Acceptance of a settlement runs the same shape of cascade with two differences. The grievance goes to `SETTLED` rather than `WITHDRAWN` or `CONSOLIDATED`; every **other** offer standing at `DRAFT` or `AUTHORIZED` is voided while the accepted one stands at `ACCEPTED`; and any live arbitration demand goes to `SETTLED_PRE_HEARING` rather than to `WITHDRAWN`, again releasing the selected arbitrator and re-deriving that arbitrator's status.

Consolidation has one analytics consequence worth stating on its own, because it is easy to miss. Consolidated grievances are excluded from every clause counter and from both supervisor counters — the case is accounted for on the surviving grievance only — so the cited clause's `filed_count` observably drops by 1 on the next read after a consolidation.

Once a grievance is `SETTLED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `CONSOLIDATED` or `CLOSED`, it is terminal. Its timeliness posture is frozen at the value it held at that moment, and any attempt to act on it — schedule a step, appeal it, demand arbitration, withdraw it again, or post an operator cost entry against it — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_GRIEVANCE_STATE`.

### 3.6. Demanding Arbitration, Issuing the Panel and the Alternate Strike

Arbitration is the union's move after the step ladder is exhausted, and again only the filing steward may make it. The demand payload names the `panel_source` the candidates will be drawn from — `AAA`, `FMCS` or `PERMANENT_UMPIRE` — the `panel_size` of 3, 5 or 7 (any other value is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PANEL_SIZE`), and the mandatory `expected_grievance_version` of §2.7. The grievance's `current_step` must be 3, the cited clause must have `is_arbitrable` true, the agreement must still be `ACTIVE`, and the clock must be at or before the grievance's `arbitration_deadline_at` — a later demand is rejected with `ERR_ARBITRATION_DEMAND_UNTIMELY`.

Like appealing, demanding has two routes in. From `ANSWERED`, the step-3 record has been answered and the demand simply follows. From `AWAITING_RESPONSE`, the step-3 record is still `HELD` and the clock has passed its `response_due_at`; the demand may then be filed anyway, and in that case the overdue step is first transitioned from `HELD` to `DEEMED_DENIED` and the grievance's `timeliness_state` is set to `EMPLOYER_DEFAULT`. Either route creates the demand at `DEMANDED` with `prep_status` at `PREP_NOT_STARTED`, stamps `demanded_at` with the clock's current instant, copies the grievance's `arbitration_deadline_at` into the demand's `demand_deadline_at` as the record of the instant after which the demand would have been untimely, moves the grievance to `ARBITRATION_DEMANDED`, and increments the grievance's `version` by 1. The new demand starts with an empty `panel_arbitrator_ids`, a null `strike_turn`, `strikes_taken` at 0, `exhibit_count` at 0, `estimated_cost_cents` at 0 and null `selected_arbitrator_id`, `hearing_start_at`, `hearing_end_at` and `brief_due_at`.

**Issuing the panel** is the `ArbitrationAdministrator`'s act, and the server picks the candidates itself — the administrator supplies nothing. An arbitrator is eligible when all four of the following hold: `is_active` is true; `active_case_count` is strictly less than `max_concurrent_cases`; the demand's `panel_source` appears in the arbitrator's `panel_memberships`; and `earliest_available_at` is at or before ninety days after the demand's `demanded_at`. A fifth exclusion is carried across re-demands on the same case: an arbitrator who has ever been struck on **any** arbitration demand belonging to the same grievance is never eligible again on that grievance. In practice this means an arbitrator whose derived `status` is `FULL` or `INACTIVE` is never a candidate. The eligible pool is ranked by ascending `active_case_count`, then by ascending `per_diem_cents`, then by ascending `created_at` as the final tie-break, and the first `panel_size` candidates in rank order are stored in `panel_arbitrator_ids` in that order. If the eligible pool holds fewer arbitrators than `panel_size`, the request is rejected with `ERR_PANEL_POOL_INSUFFICIENT` and nothing at all is written. A successful issue moves the demand to `PANEL_ISSUED`, sets `strike_turn` to `UNION` — the union always strikes first — and leaves `strikes_taken` at 0. A second attempt to issue a panel on the same demand is rejected with `ERR_DEMAND_STATE_INVALID`.

**Alternate striking** then removes candidates one at a time until a single arbitrator survives. A strike is taken by a `UnionSteward` for the union or a `LaborRelationsDirector` for the employer, and the acting role's party must equal the demand's current `strike_turn`, or the strike is rejected with `ERR_NOT_STRIKE_TURN`. The payload names the `arbitrator_id` being removed, which must appear in `panel_arbitrator_ids` and must not already have been struck on this demand, failing otherwise with `ERR_ARBITRATOR_NOT_ON_PANEL`. Each accepted strike writes an immutable strike record carrying its `arbitration_demand_id`, its `arbitrator_id`, a `struck_by` party derived from the acting role rather than supplied, a 1-based `strike_order` within its demand, and the acting operator's `struck_by_user_id`; it increments the demand's `strikes_taken` by 1 and flips `strike_turn` to the other party. Strike records carry no `updated_at` and no `version`. They may be listed per demand or per arbitrator across all demands, filtered by `struck_by`.

What the strike *does* to the demand depends on how many candidates would remain. Let the remaining count be `panel_size` less `strikes_taken` less the one being struck now. While that remainder is greater than 1, the demand sits at `STRIKING` — the first such strike moves it from `PANEL_ISSUED` to `STRIKING`, and each intermediate strike on a five- or seven-member panel leaves it at `STRIKING`. When the remainder is exactly 1, the strike is the last one: whether the demand was at `PANEL_ISSUED` (a three-member panel's second strike) or at `STRIKING`, it moves to `ARBITRATOR_SELECTED`, `selected_arbitrator_id` becomes the one unstruck candidate, and `strike_turn` becomes null.

That final strike starts a three-step engagement cascade that is easy to under-implement. Because the demand entered `ARBITRATOR_SELECTED`, the selected arbitrator's `active_case_count` rises by 1 and their `status` is re-derived, which flips them from `AVAILABLE` to `FULL` when the ceiling is reached. Because an arbitrator became engaged, a system-origin cost entry is posted against the grievance with `category` `ARBITRATOR_FEE`, `bearing_party` `SPLIT` and `amount_cents` equal to the arbitrator's `per_diem_cents` multiplied by a two-day retainer. And because a cost entry was posted, the grievance's `total_cost_cents`, `union_cost_share_cents` and `employer_cost_share_cents` are re-derived by the allocation of §3.9 and its `cost_posture` may step from `COST_OK` to `COST_WATCH` or on to `COST_BREACH`.

### 3.7. Building the Case File and Coordinating Witnesses

An arbitration demand carries **two independent state dimensions on the same record**. Its procedural `status` walks the track described in §3.6 and §3.8; its `prep_status` walks a separate track measuring how ready the case file is. Neither dimension drives the other, but they gate each other: preparation may not begin before an arbitrator is selected, and an award may not be recorded before preparation reaches the briefs stage. Demands may be listed filtered by `grievance_id`, by `selected_arbitrator_id`, or by either dimension independently.

Preparation begins at `PREP_NOT_STARTED`. It may fall to `PREP_DEFICIENT` without anybody asking: when a hearing is booked, a demand whose preparation has still not started and whose hearing begins less than twenty-one days from the clock's current instant is marked deficient on the spot. `PREP_DEFICIENT` is not a dead end — it is a flag, and the same filings that advance an untouched case file advance a deficient one — but it leaves `readiness_score` at its pre-filing value and award recording still blocked.

A `LaborRelationsAnalyst` advances the case file by filing its components, one `filing_type` at a time. An `EXHIBIT_LIST` filing carries an `exhibit_count` that must be at least 1, records that count on the demand, and moves the preparation dimension from either `PREP_NOT_STARTED` or `PREP_DEFICIENT` to `EXHIBITS_FILED`; it is permitted only while the demand's procedural status is `ARBITRATOR_SELECTED`, `HEARING_SCHEDULED` or `HEARD`. A `BRIEF` filing moves `EXHIBITS_FILED` to `BRIEFS_FILED`, and this is the rung that unblocks award recording. A `STIPULATION` filing moves `BRIEFS_FILED` to `PREP_READY`, and it carries one extra condition: at least one witness on the demand must already stand at `CONFIRMED`. A filing attempted out of order is rejected with `ERR_DEMAND_STATE_INVALID`.

The demand's `readiness_score` is derived from those two dimensions together, as a sum of four independent contributions: 40 points once the preparation dimension is `EXHIBITS_FILED`, `BRIEFS_FILED` or `PREP_READY`; a further 30 points once it is `BRIEFS_FILED` or `PREP_READY`; a further 20 points when at least one witness assignment on the demand stands at `CONFIRMED`; and a further 10 points when the procedural status is `HEARING_SCHEDULED`, `HEARD` or `AWARDED`. A fully prepared, fully scheduled case therefore reads 100.

**Witnesses** are called by either a `UnionSteward` or a `LaborRelationsAnalyst`, and only while the demand stands at `ARBITRATOR_SELECTED` or `HEARING_SCHEDULED`. The payload names the `employee_id` being called, the `witness_type` — `FACT`, `EXPERT` or `CHARACTER` — the `called_by` party of `UNION` or `EMPLOYER`, and the reserved testimony slot as `slot_start_at` and its exclusive end `slot_end_at`, which must be strictly after the start. Two people-level bars apply: the called employee must not have `employment_status` `TERMINATED`, or the call is rejected with `ERR_WITNESS_NOT_EMPLOYED`, and the called employee must not be the steward who filed the grievance, or it is rejected with `ERR_ADVOCATE_WITNESS_CONFLICT` — the union's advocate does not also become the union's evidence.

Two calendar bars follow. Testimony slots are **half-open** intervals: a slot runs from its start inclusive up to its end exclusive. A new slot is rejected with `ERR_WITNESS_SLOT_CONFLICT` when it intersects any existing slot of the **same person on any demand anywhere in the system** whose assignment stands at `INVITED`, `CONFIRMED` or `TESTIFIED`. Because the intervals are half-open, slots that merely touch — one ending at the exact instant the next begins — do not conflict. Separately, an aggregate daily release cap applies: the person's total release hours for the UTC calendar date of the new slot's `slot_start_at`, counting assignments at `INVITED`, `CONFIRMED` or `TESTIFIED` and including the new one, may not be strictly greater than ten hours; a breach is rejected with `ERR_WITNESS_RELEASE_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

The **release time** an assignment consumes is computed per assignment, and the order of the arithmetic is normative. First take the exact hours between `slot_start_at` and `slot_end_at`. Then add a prep allowance of one and a half hours — an hour and a half of preparation is granted to every witness regardless of how long they are on the stand. Then round that sum **up to the next quarter of an hour** and store it as `release_hours`. Then multiply `release_hours` by the called employee's `hourly_rate_cents` and round half up to whole cents, storing the result as `release_cost_cents`. Every one of those steps happens per assignment before any assignment is added to any other: summing raw hours across several assignments and rounding once at the end is the wrong reading and yields a smaller figure than the correct one. A new assignment is written at `INVITED` with a null `confirmed_by_user_id` and a null `release_cost_entry_id`. Witnesses may be listed per demand or per person, filtered by `status` and, on the per-demand listing, by `called_by`.

**Confirming** a witness is the `LaborRelationsDirector`'s approval of the release from work, and it is permitted on an `INVITED` assignment while the demand stands at `ARBITRATOR_SELECTED` or `HEARING_SCHEDULED`. Confirmation moves the assignment to `CONFIRMED`, stamps `confirmed_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and cascades twice. Because the release was approved, a system-origin cost entry is posted against the grievance with `category` `RELEASE_TIME`, `bearing_party` `UNION` and `amount_cents` equal to the assignment's `release_cost_cents`, and that entry's identifier is stored on the assignment as `release_cost_entry_id`. Because a cost entry was posted, the grievance's cost allocation and `cost_posture` are re-derived. The confirmed hours also flow into the called employee's `witness_release_hours_ytd` on the next read of that person.

### 3.8. The Hearing, the Award and Remedy Implementation

Booking the hearing is the `ArbitrationAdministrator`'s act on a demand standing at `ARBITRATOR_SELECTED`. The payload carries `hearing_start_at` and `hearing_end_at`, the latter strictly after the former or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_HEARING_WINDOW`. The selected arbitrator must not derive to `INACTIVE`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_ARBITRATOR_INACTIVE`. Then the **buffered calendar-conflict test** runs, and it is deliberately conservative in the arbitrator's favour: every hearing that arbitrator already has booked is expanded by their `travel_buffer_hours` on **both** sides, and the request is rejected with `ERR_ARBITRATOR_UNAVAILABLE` when that expanded interval intersects the requested window at any point, endpoints included. These are closed intervals, so a requested hearing that begins at the exact instant an expanded existing hearing ends is a conflict. The existing hearings that count are those on other demands sharing the same `selected_arbitrator_id` whose status is `HEARING_SCHEDULED`, `HEARD` or `AWARDED`.

A successful booking moves the demand to `HEARING_SCHEDULED`, stores both instants, sets `brief_due_at` to thirty days after `hearing_end_at`, marks a not-yet-started case file `PREP_DEFICIENT` if the runway is under twenty-one days as described in §3.7, and writes `estimated_cost_cents` from the **forward-looking** cost variant. That variant works as follows: the hearing spans a number of days computed as the hours between `hearing_start_at` and `hearing_end_at` divided by 24 and rounded **up**, so any partial day counts as a whole day; the arbitrator fee estimate is `per_diem_cents` multiplied by the hearing days plus one study day; the release estimate is the sum of `release_cost_cents` over every witness assignment on the demand standing at `INVITED` or `CONFIRMED`; the transcript estimate is 120,000 cents per hearing day; and `estimated_cost_cents` is the sum of those three. It is written once, at booking, and it reads 0 before then.

`actual_cost_cents` is the demand's other cost figure and it is a genuinely different calculation, derived fresh on every read of the demand rather than stored. It reads only the posted cost ledger of the demand's grievance: entries whose `status` is `POSTED`, whose `category` is one of `ARBITRATOR_FEE`, `TRANSCRIPT`, `RELEASE_TIME` or `TRAVEL`, and whose `incurred_at` is at or before the clock's current instant. Those entries are processed chronologically — ascending `incurred_at`, then ascending `created_at` — accumulating `amount_cents` into a running total, and the total after the last one is `actual_cost_cents`. The two variants read different inputs and are expected to disagree for the same case: the estimate prices a study day, every merely-invited witness and a transcript for every scheduled day whether or not one has been charged, while the actual counts only money genuinely posted and not voided, ignoring study days, unconfirmed witnesses and not-yet-incurred charges, and excluding the `COUNSEL` and `FILING` categories entirely.

**Recording the award** is again the administrator's act, and it consumes several gates in one request. The demand must stand at `HEARING_SCHEDULED` and the clock must have reached or passed `hearing_end_at`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_HEARING_NOT_CONCLUDED`; when both hold, the system advances the demand from `HEARING_SCHEDULED` to `HEARD` inside the same request and then on to `AWARDED`. The case file must stand at `BRIEFS_FILED` or `PREP_READY`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_PREP_INCOMPLETE`. No award may already exist on the demand; a second attempt is rejected with `ERR_AWARD_STATE_INVALID`.

The award body records the arbitrator's decision. `outcome` is `SUSTAINED`, `DENIED` or `SPLIT`. `sustained_fraction_bps` defaults to 10,000 and must be exactly 10,000 for a `SUSTAINED` outcome, exactly 0 for a `DENIED` outcome, and strictly between 0 and 10,000 for a `SPLIT` outcome. `back_pay_hours_ordered` defaults to 0 and is never negative. `mitigation_offset_cents`, the interim earnings the grievant is credited with having made elsewhere, defaults to 0 and is never negative. `interest_rate_bps` is a simple annual rate defaulting to 0 and never negative; when it is greater than 0, `interest_accrual_start` is mandatory and must be at or before the award's `issued_at`. `reinstatement_ordered` defaults to false. `remedy_deadline_days` defaults to 30, must be at least 1, and sets how long the employer has to implement. The award is stamped with `issued_at` at the clock's current instant and with `implementation_deadline_at` that many days later, and `implemented_cents` starts at 0.

The remedy is then computed in two variants. Start with the ordered back pay: `back_pay_hours_ordered` multiplied by the grievant's `hourly_rate_cents`, rounded half up. Apply the split: multiply that figure by `sustained_fraction_bps`, divide by 10,000 and round half up — this is the sustained portion. Compute the interest days as the whole calendar days from `interest_accrual_start` to `issued_at`, taking 0 days whenever `interest_rate_bps` is 0. Interest is then the sustained portion multiplied by `interest_rate_bps`, multiplied by the interest days, divided by 10,000 and again by 365 days in a year, rounded half up. `gross_remedy_cents` is the sustained portion plus that interest. `net_remedy_cents` is the gross less `mitigation_offset_cents` less the grievance's `settled_amount_cents` as it stands at the instant the award is recorded, and it is floored at 0 — it never goes negative. The order here is normative and there are two tempting wrong readings: interest accrues on the sustained portion, that is **after** the split fraction is applied and **before** any mitigation or settlement offset is subtracted. Applying the split after interest, or accruing interest on the net figure, both produce different numbers. The gross figure is the make-whole number used for compliance reporting and ignores both mitigation and anything the employer already conceded through a step grant or accepted settlement; the net figure is the implementation target, which is why an award can occasionally be fully implemented with a zero payment. Cost entries never enter the remedy at all.

Recording the award fires the full cascade. The grievance moves from `ARBITRATION_DEMANDED` to `AWARDED` and its `version` increments by 1. Because the demand left the engaged set, the arbitrator's `active_case_count` drops by 1 and their `status` is re-derived, flipping a `FULL` arbitrator back to `AVAILABLE`. Every witness assignment on the demand standing at `CONFIRMED` whose slot lies inside the hearing window — that is, with `slot_start_at` at or after `hearing_start_at` and `slot_end_at` at or before `hearing_end_at` — moves to `TESTIFIED`, and its release-time cost entry stays posted. Every assignment standing at `CONFIRMED` whose slot lies wholly or partly outside that window moves to `NO_SHOW`, and because of that its release-time cost entry — the one identified by `release_cost_entry_id` — is transitioned from `POSTED` to `VOIDED` with a null `voided_by_user_id`, which re-derives the grievance's cost allocation and drops the witness's `witness_release_hours_ytd` back. Every assignment still standing at `INVITED` moves to `EXCUSED` and stops contributing to the forward-looking cost estimate.

The award's own status is decided in the same request. When `net_remedy_cents` is greater than 0 or `reinstatement_ordered` is true, the award moves from `ISSUED` to `IMPLEMENTATION_PENDING`. When `net_remedy_cents` is exactly 0 **and** `reinstatement_ordered` is false, there is nothing to implement: the award goes straight from `ISSUED` to `IMPLEMENTED` and the grievance is moved to `CLOSED` within that same request.

**Implementation** is recorded by a `LaborRelationsAnalyst`, one payment at a time. The payload carries `paid_cents` — which may legitimately be 0 when the money is already settled and only the reinstatement is outstanding — and, only for an award that ordered reinstatement, a `reinstatement_effective_date`. A payment larger than the award's remaining net remedy, meaning `net_remedy_cents` less `implemented_cents`, is rejected with `ERR_OVERPAYMENT`. Whenever a `reinstatement_effective_date` is supplied, the grievant's `employment_status` is set to `REINSTATED`. Where the payment lands depends on the clock and the balance. While the clock is at or before `implementation_deadline_at` and the payment leaves the balance short — either because `implemented_cents` plus `paid_cents` is still less than `net_remedy_cents`, or because reinstatement was ordered and the grievant is not yet `REINSTATED` — the award moves to `PARTIALLY_IMPLEMENTED`, from `IMPLEMENTATION_PENDING`, from `PARTIALLY_IMPLEMENTED` itself, or from `COMPLIANCE_DISPUTED`, and `implemented_cents` grows by the payment. When the clock is past `implementation_deadline_at` and the payment still leaves `implemented_cents` plus `paid_cents` below `net_remedy_cents`, the award moves from `IMPLEMENTATION_PENDING` or `PARTIALLY_IMPLEMENTED` to `COMPLIANCE_DISPUTED`: `implemented_cents` grows by the payment, and because the award went past its deadline unsatisfied, a system-origin cost entry is posted against the grievance with `category` `COUNSEL`, `bearing_party` `EMPLOYER` and `amount_cents` of 150,000 — the office's standing enforcement-counsel charge — which in turn re-derives the grievance's cost allocation and `cost_posture`.

The award completes when `implemented_cents` plus `paid_cents` equals `net_remedy_cents` **and** either no reinstatement was ordered or the grievant stands at `REINSTATED` after this call. The award then moves to `IMPLEMENTED` from `IMPLEMENTATION_PENDING`, `PARTIALLY_IMPLEMENTED` or `COMPLIANCE_DISPUTED`, `implemented_cents` is set to `net_remedy_cents`, and because the award became implemented the grievance moves from `AWARDED` to `CLOSED` with its `version` incremented by 1, freezing its timeliness posture. Because the grievance closed, the cited clause's `sustained_count` and `sustain_rate_bps` and the respondent supervisor's `sustained_against_count` and `adverse_rate_bps` all observably change on the next read. An attempt to record a payment against an award that is already `IMPLEMENTED` is rejected with `ERR_AWARD_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.9. The Case Cost Ledger, Allocation and Trend Analysis

Every case carries its own ledger. A `LaborRelationsAnalyst` posts an entry against a non-terminal grievance by supplying a `category` — `ARBITRATOR_FEE`, `TRANSCRIPT`, `COUNSEL`, `RELEASE_TIME`, `TRAVEL` or `FILING` — a strictly positive `amount_cents`, and a `bearing_party` of `UNION`, `EMPLOYER` or `SPLIT`. The entry is stamped with `incurred_at` at the clock's current instant, an `origin` of `MANUAL`, and a `status` of `POSTED`, with `void_reason` and `voided_by_user_id` both null. The three system-generated entries described elsewhere — the arbitrator retainer at selection, the release-time charge at witness confirmation and the enforcement-counsel charge at a missed implementation deadline — are identical in every respect except that their `origin` reads `SYSTEM`. The ledger of a case may be listed in full, system-generated and voided entries included, filtered by `category`, by `status` or by `bearing_party`.

A `LaborRelationsDirector` may **void** a posted entry, supplying a `void_reason`; the entry moves to `VOIDED` and `voided_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`. Voids performed by a cascade rather than by an operator — the one that follows a confirmed witness becoming a no-show — leave `voided_by_user_id` null. Voiding an entry that is already `VOIDED` is rejected with `ERR_COST_ENTRY_ALREADY_VOIDED`. A void must restore the case's cost figures to their **exact** pre-posting values, which is only true if voided entries are excluded from the allocation input set entirely rather than being offset by a compensating charge.

Cost allocation is re-derived whenever an entry is posted or voided, and again on every read of the grievance, and it proceeds in five steps. First, the base total is the sum of `amount_cents` over the grievance's `POSTED` entries only, irrespective of category or origin; voided entries contribute nothing to any output. Second, the cost-sharing schedule is selected **from that base total**: a base total of 500,000 cents or less puts the union's split at 5,000 basis points — an even division — a base total above that and up to 2,000,000 cents puts it at 6,000 basis points, and anything above that puts it at 7,000 basis points. Third, the union's share is summed over the same posted set: an entry borne by `UNION` contributes its full amount, an entry borne by `SPLIT` contributes its amount multiplied by the union split in basis points and divided by 10,000, rounded half up, and an entry borne by `EMPLOYER` contributes nothing. Fourth, the employer's base side is the base total less the union's share. Fifth, the administrative fee is 800 basis points — eight percent — of the **employer's base side**, rounded half up, and it is added to the employer's side alone: `employer_cost_share_cents` is the employer base plus that fee, `union_cost_share_cents` is the figure from step three, and `total_cost_cents` is the two of them added together.

Two things about that sequence are load-bearing. The sharing tier is selected from the base total of step one and is never re-selected from the grand total of step five after the administrative fee has been added — doing so yields different shares and is the wrong reading. And the identity that `total_cost_cents` always equals `union_cost_share_cents` plus `employer_cost_share_cents` holds after every posting and every void. Finally, `cost_posture` is derived from the grand total: `COST_OK` at 1,000,000 cents or less, `COST_WATCH` above that and up to 5,000,000 cents, and `COST_BREACH` above that.

The office's **trend analysis** rests on a single predicate: whether a grievance ended adversely for the employer. A grievance meets that predicate, counted once however many of the following are true, when an award exists on its arbitration demand with an `outcome` of `SUSTAINED` or `SPLIT`; or when its status is `SETTLED` or `CLOSED` and its `settled_amount_cents` is greater than 0; or when any of its steps carries a `disposition` of `GRANTED` or `PARTIAL`.

The **supervisor scorecard** is derived over the grievances naming that person as `respondent_supervisor_id`. `open_grievance_count` counts those whose status is not `SETTLED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `CONSOLIDATED` or `CLOSED`. `closed_grievance_count` counts those whose status is `SETTLED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `CLOSED` — note that `CONSOLIDATED` grievances are excluded from both the numerator and the denominator, so a consolidated case never scores against anybody. `sustained_against_count` counts the closed set that also meets the adverse-outcome predicate. `adverse_rate_bps` is `sustained_against_count` multiplied by 10,000, divided by `closed_grievance_count`, rounded half up — and exactly 0 when `closed_grievance_count` is 0.

The **clause scorecard** is derived over the grievances citing that clause. `filed_count` counts every one whose status is not `CONSOLIDATED`, in flight or not. `sustained_count` counts those same non-consolidated grievances that meet the adverse-outcome predicate. `denied_count` counts those whose status is `SETTLED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `CLOSED` and that do **not** meet the predicate. `sustain_rate_bps` is `sustained_count` multiplied by 10,000, divided by `filed_count`, rounded half up — and exactly 0 when `filed_count` is 0. Both scorecards are computed at read time, so a case filed a moment ago is reflected in the very next read, and grievances still in flight count only toward `open_grievance_count` on the supervisor side and `filed_count` on the clause side.
