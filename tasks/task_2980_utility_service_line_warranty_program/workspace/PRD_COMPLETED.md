# Utility Service Line Warranty Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A utility service line warranty program sells a homeowner peace of mind about the pipes and wires that nobody else will pay for: the water service line running from the curb stop to the house, the sewer lateral out to the main, the interior plumbing, the electric mast. The product is sold under a utility's own brand, marketed inside the utility's bill envelope, and paid for either on the utility bill or by annual prepayment. That distribution model is what makes the business hard. Every mail piece carries someone else's logo and therefore needs that partner's explicit approval; every address on the mailing file may be suppressed for half a dozen independent reasons, some of which the homeowner asked for and some of which a state regulator imposed; every benefit dollar paid out is measured against a cap that was filed with a public utility commission; and every price increase at renewal must be preceded by a written notice whose required lead time grows with the size of the increase.

This document specifies the API for that program. It covers the brand partners and the products filed against them, the premises marketing file and the suppression rules that govern it, campaign approval and mail-drop execution, enrollment and the waiting period before coverage attaches, claim intake and the coverage eligibility assessment, contractor network dispatch with its response service-level agreement and its permit and restoration scope, the benefit cap waterfall that decides what the program pays and what the homeowner pays, the denial and appeal cycle, regulator complaint intake and remedy, and finally cancellation with its refund or credit proration and the annual renewal run. The system is a compliance and audit spine wrapped around two independently conserved quantities: a benefit ledger that may never be overdrawn past a filed cap, and a contractor capacity counter that may never be oversubscribed.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **the system is deterministic and replayable**: there is no wall clock, no random tie-breaking, and no background thread. Time advances only when an operator advances the logical clock, batch work happens only when an operator triggers the overnight run, and every collection is processed in a pinned order so that two identical replays produce byte-identical results. Second, **nothing is ever silently overwritten**: a caller that mutates a record it has not freshly observed is refused rather than allowed to clobber an intervening decision, and money movements are recorded as immutable ledger rows rather than as mutable balances. All collections are mounted flat at the API root — there is no nested resource tree — and a child record names its parent through a foreign key in the request body rather than through the URL.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section applies to every endpoint unless a later section explicitly narrows it. Where §2 and §3 discuss the same rule, they are reinforcing each other, not competing: §2 fixes the contract, §3 explains the business reason.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the calling principal and `X-User-Role` names the role it is acting in. A request whose `X-User-ID` header is absent or empty is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request is inspected — including before the role is examined and before the URL is resolved. A request that carries a valid `X-User-ID` but whose `X-User-Role` is not among the roles the target operation admits is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Both headers are read with surrounding whitespace trimmed — so a header padded with spaces identifies the same principal and the same role — and an entirely absent `X-User-Role` is treated as an empty role name. A role name that is not one of the eleven exact spellings below is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` as well, and that check, like authentication itself, is applied before the URL is resolved, so an unrecognised role receives the same 403 even on a path the API does not define.

Eleven roles exist, and their spellings are exact: `PlatformAdmin`, `MarketingOps`, `PartnerBrandReviewer`, `CustomerServiceRep`, `BillingOps`, `Policyholder`, `ClaimsAdjuster`, `AppealsOfficer`, `NetworkContractor`, `RegulatoryAffairs`, and `OperationsAdmin`. The programme deliberately splits duties among them: the operator who submits a campaign for brand approval is never the one who approves it, and the adjuster who denies a claim is never the one who decides its appeal.

Eight of these roles are **opaque operator identities**. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `MarketingOps`, `CustomerServiceRep`, `BillingOps`, `ClaimsAdjuster`, `AppealsOfficer`, `RegulatoryAffairs`, or `OperationsAdmin`, the accompanying `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `submitted_by_user_id` or `decision_by_user_id` on a campaign, or as `triage_decided_by_user_id` or `appeal_decided_by_user_id` on a claim, wherever a transition captures the acting operator — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`.

The remaining three roles are **entity-backed**, and for them `X-User-ID` is a resource identifier that ownership checks compare against. When `X-User-Role` is `PartnerBrandReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `UtilityPartner`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `UtilityPartner` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, so a brand reviewer may only decide a campaign whose `partner_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Policyholder`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Premises`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Premises` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, so a policyholder may only appeal a claim whose enrollment's `premises_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `NetworkContractor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Contractor`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Contractor` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, so a contractor crew may only post field events on a dispatch whose `contractor_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`. Any of these three acting outside its own scope is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Ownership is a gate on writes only, and only on the three write operations §2.8 names: the read endpoints that admit an entity-backed role apply no ownership filter and no ownership check at all, so a policyholder may fetch any premises, enrollment or claim by identifier and a contractor may fetch or list any dispatch, and no other write operation compares an entity-backed `X-User-ID` to anything.

Segregation of duties is enforced separately from ownership and produces its own code. Two operations refuse a caller who already acted on the other side of the same record: a campaign decision made by the same `X-User-ID` that most recently submitted the campaign, and an appeal decision made by the same `X-User-ID` that recorded the claim's triage decision. Both are refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), and in both cases the caller's role is perfectly legitimate — it is only the identity collision that is refused.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It reports the **logical clock** of §2.5 rather than the host machine's wall clock, so every response carries the same envelope timestamp until the clock is moved, and that timestamp equals the `created_at` of any record the request just created.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection-returning endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. Both must be integers. A `limit` below 1, a `limit` above 100, an `offset` below 0, or a non-integer value for either is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400). `offset` has no upper bound: one past the end of the collection is perfectly legal and returns an empty page with the real `total_count` and `has_more` false. What counts as an integer is decided on the text as sent, with surrounding whitespace trimmed and an explicit leading `+` or `-` sign tolerated: `007` and `+7` are the integer 7, while an empty value, a value carrying a decimal point even when it is `10.0`, and any value with a non-digit in it are all refused. A parameter that is absent altogether takes its default and is never a refusal.

Every list endpoint returns its rows sorted by `created_at` ascending, so the oldest record appears first and a freshly created record appears last. Because `created_at` is stamped from the logical clock rather than from a wall clock, many records legitimately share the same `created_at` value; such ties are broken by the order in which the records were created, earliest first, so the ordering is total and stable rather than arbitrary. This ordering is not merely cosmetic: the same `created_at` ascending order is the pinned processing order for every batch and cascade described in §2.9, which means a list response is a faithful preview of the order in which the system will act on those same rows.

The `meta` object of a list response carries all four pagination fields: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values used for the page, `total_count` reports the number of rows matching the filter before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window. Single-resource responses omit all four.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Record Versions

Identifiers are server-minted. Every persisted record receives an `id` that is a UUID string carrying no type prefix of any kind — a claim's `id` and a dispatch's `id` are indistinguishable by shape, and the client never supplies or influences an `id`. Any identifier a client sends in a path segment or a request body must be a well-formed UUID; a malformed one is a schema failure rather than a lookup miss. Well-formedness admits either letter case in the hexadecimal digits, but matching an identifier against a stored `id` is an exact string comparison, so a well-formed UUID that differs from a real `id` only in letter case is a lookup miss and not a hit.

Almost every record also carries three bookkeeping properties. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant, rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits, at which the record was first written. `updated_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant, again with exactly three fractional-second digits, at which the record was last mutated; on a freshly created record it equals `created_at`. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the record is created and increases by exactly 1 on every accepted mutation of that record. These three are read-only outputs: no request body ever sets them. Because no write endpoint declares `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` or `version` among its inputs, sending any of them in a write request's body is an unknown field and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) rather than being silently ignored — a client cannot smuggle a server-minted value past the schema tier.

There is one deliberate exception, and it is the reason the programme can be audited. `LedgerEntry` rows are **immutable**. A ledger row carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has no `updated_at` and no `version`, because it is never modified after it is appended. There is no endpoint that edits or deletes a ledger row. A movement that needs to be undone is undone by appending a compensating row of the opposite sign — a benefit reversal against a benefit charge, a refund clawback against a proration refund — never by rewriting history. Any prose in this document that speaks of "correcting" or "reversing" a money movement means appending a compensating row.

Because `version` is the unit of observation as well as the unit of mutation, cascades matter to it. When one request mutates several records — and §2.9 catalogues the cases where it does — every record actually touched has its own `version` incremented by 1 and its own `updated_at` refreshed, not just the record named in the URL. The increment is **once per request per record**, not once per hop: when several hops of the same request reach the same record — a claim driven through two consecutive states, an enrollment written by both a settlement and a cap flip — that record's `version` still advances by exactly 1 for the whole request, while its `updated_at` is refreshed to the same logical-clock reading either way. A caller that holds a stale `version` of any of those collaterally touched records will be refused the next time it tries to write against them.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Date Arithmetic

The programme is full of deadlines — waiting periods, response service levels, appeal windows, notice lead times, cap years — and none of them read the host machine's wall clock. Instead, a single **logical clock** supplies "now" to every temporal rule in the system. At process start the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it moves only when an administrative capability sets it: that request supplies the target instant as `to_iso8601` and the response's `data` is a purpose-built object whose single field, `current_utc`, reports the resulting clock as a three-digit instant. No ordering constraint applies to that instant — the clock may be set forward, set to the reading it already holds, or set backward, and each is accepted alike. Setting the clock mutates no domain entity — no enrollment activates, no complaint goes overdue, no claim closes — it merely changes what every subsequent temporal comparison sees. Batch consequences of a clock move happen only when the overnight run described in §3.11 is invoked.

Temporal values come in two shapes and the distinction is load-bearing. **Instants** are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits — for example `2026-08-02T00:00:00.000Z`. `known_defect_reported_at`, `enrollment_date`, `coverage_effective_at`, `reported_at`, `denied_at`, `appeal_opened_at`, `triage_decided_at`, `decided_at`, `mail_dropped_at`, `assigned_at`, `responded_at`, `window_start`, `window_end`, `sla_deadline_at`, `received_at`, `response_due_at`, `responded_at`, `renewal_notice_sent_at` and the two envelope timestamps are all instants. That three-digit rendering is how the server always writes an instant back out; it is not, however, the only form the server will read. On input — `to_iso8601`, `known_defect_reported_at`, `window_start`, and every other instant-typed field — an instant is accepted with anywhere from zero to nine fractional digits, with the date and time separated by an upper- or lower-case `T`, and with UTC designated by `Z`, `z`, `+00:00` or `+0000`; anything else, including a non-zero UTC offset, is refused as a schema failure. Whatever form arrives is normalised to the three-digit rendering the moment it is stored, so more than three fractional digits are truncated rather than rejected and are never echoed back. **Calendar dates** are plain `YYYY-MM-DD` strings with no time component at all: `drop_date`, `term_start`, `term_end`, `cap_year_start`, `past_due_since`, `renewal_effective_date`, `cancellation_effective_date`, `license_expires_on`, `effective_date`, `requested_effective_date` and `license_expires_on` are dates, and comparing one against the logical clock means comparing it against the logical clock's calendar date.

Day counting is always in whole days, and month counting is always in whole calendar months. "Twelve calendar months after 2026-08-05" is 2027-08-05, not 365 days later. "Thirty whole days after 2026-08-05" is 2026-09-04. Month arithmetic keeps the day of the month and **clamps it down to the last day of the target month** when the target month is shorter: one calendar month after 2026-01-31 is 2026-02-28, and twelve calendar months after 2028-02-29 is 2029-02-28. A year is added as twelve calendar months by the same rule, and the clamp is not remembered — the anchor date is not rewritten, so each later addition is computed from the date it is applied to. Sub-day remainders are never carried: a rule expressed in days ignores the time of day on both sides unless the rule explicitly compares two instants.

### 2.6. Stale-Snapshot Rejection on Version-Guarded Writes

Five write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control. Each of them decides something on the basis of a record the caller previously read, and each therefore requires the caller to declare which `version` of that record its decision was based on. The declared field is **not optional** on these five operations: omitting it is a schema failure, and supplying a value that does not equal the parent record's current `version` is a stale-snapshot rejection carrying HTTP 412. No other endpoint in the system accepts a version field; sending one where it is not specified is an unknown field.

- **Dispatching an approved claim** requires `expected_claim_version` carrying the caller's last observed `version` of the target `Claim`. Any earlier accepted write on that claim — a triage decision, a previous dispatch, a supersede — has already advanced `Claim.version`, so a caller holding the pre-write snapshot is refused with `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` (HTTP 412) even when the claim is still perfectly dispatchable and the identical body with the current version would have been accepted.
- **Creating an enrollment** requires `expected_premises_version` carrying the caller's last observed `version` of the target `Premises`. Admission is decided from that premises snapshot — its `owned_segments`, its suppression state, its `last_mail_decision` — and a mail drop, an appeal-driven suppression recompute, or an earlier enrollment on the same address all advance `Premises.version` and invalidate it. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_PREMISES_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- **Issuing a renewal price change notice** requires `expected_enrollment_version` carrying the caller's last observed `version` of the target `Enrollment`. Renewal pricing is computed from the in-force price on that snapshot, and any billing event, cancellation, cap movement or earlier notice advances `Enrollment.version`. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_ENROLLMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- **Changing a partner's lifecycle status** requires `expected_partner_version` carrying the caller's last observed `version` of the target `UtilityPartner`. The change cascades campaign withdrawals, so it must be applied against the snapshot the administrator actually evaluated; an earlier status change advances `UtilityPartner.version`, and a status change is in fact the only operation that advances it — filing a product against the partner, adding a premises to its file or enrolling one of its addresses leaves the partner record itself untouched. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_PARTNER_VERSION` (HTTP 412) even when the requested transition is itself legal.
- **Filing the substantive response to a regulator complaint** requires `expected_complaint_version` carrying the caller's last observed `version` of the target `RegulatorComplaint`. A response moves money, and the overnight overdue-and-escalation sweep or a prior escalation advances `RegulatorComplaint.version`, so a response drafted against the pre-sweep snapshot is refused with `ERR_STALE_COMPLAINT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

None of these five rules is about requests overlapping in time. They are about **staleness**: the guard fires whenever the parent record has moved on since the caller read it, however long ago that was, and it does not fire when the declared version is current.

### 2.7. Money in Whole Cents, Percentages and Rounding

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere in a request or a response, and no amount is ever stored as a decimal. Because every input to the benefit settlement is already an integer number of cents, the settlement performs no rounding at all — its sums and its minima are exact.

Rounding therefore appears in exactly three places, and in all three the method is **round half up** — a value exactly halfway between two candidates rounds away from zero, to the larger magnitude.

1. **Proration of earned premium.** Each overlapped calendar month's share is rounded half up to whole cents individually, and only then are the monthly shares summed. The order is pinned and inverting it is wrong: summing the exact fractional monthly parts and rounding once at the end yields a different cent total and is explicitly not the specified behaviour.
2. **The bill-insert cancellation credit**, which is a single rounded-half-up quantity computed from the final billed month.
3. **Two derived decimals** — the contractor `selection_score` and the renewal price-change percentage — each rounded half up to exactly two fractional digits. Of the two, only `selection_score` is ever carried in a response, and it is carried as a JSON number rather than a string: two-decimal rounding fixes the *value*, so a score of 89.90 is transmitted as `89.9` and a whole-number score of 78 as `78.0`, and a client must compare numerically rather than by rendered text. The renewal percentage is an internal quantity that decides the notice tier and the filing gate; it appears in no response field.

Where a quantity is described as floored, it truncates toward zero rather than rounding: the contractor capacity-headroom percentage, the contractor rate score, `response_minutes`, and `days_overdue` are all floored integers, and the last of those is additionally clamped so that it is never negative.

### 2.8. The Order in Which a Request Is Refused

Many requests in this system violate more than one rule at once. Which error the caller sees is not a matter of implementation convenience: validations are evaluated in a strict ascending precedence, the **first** violated rule produces the response, and no later rule is evaluated. A caller can therefore rely on the error code to tell it which stage of admission it failed at, and a test of any deeper rule must satisfy every shallower one first. The precedence tiers, from earliest to latest:

1. **Authentication.** A missing or empty `X-User-ID` yields `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). This applies to every endpoint without exception.
2. **Role permission.** A role absent from the endpoint's admitted list yields `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). This too applies to every endpoint.
3. **Request-body schema.** On every operation that carries a body: a missing required field, a field the operation does not declare at all, a field of the wrong JSON type, an unknown enum member, or a violation of a declared length or range constraint yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Every write operation accepts exactly the fields its own description names and nothing else — including the operations whose body is otherwise empty — so any surplus key in the body is refused at this tier. Four further shape rules hold on every operation that carries a body. A body that is absent altogether, or present but empty, is read as the empty JSON object, so an operation all of whose declared fields are optional accepts a request with no body at all; a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object — an array, a bare number, a string, unparseable text — is refused. No declared field is nullable: sending an explicit JSON `null` is a schema failure for optional fields exactly as it is for required ones, and is not equivalent to omitting the key. A text field's minimum length and its non-blank requirement are measured after trimming surrounding whitespace, while its maximum length is measured on the value exactly as sent, and the value is stored and echoed back exactly as sent rather than trimmed. Integers are strict: a JSON boolean is not an acceptable integer, and neither is a number carrying a fractional part. Negative money amounts, a requested quantity below 1, an empty `owned_segments` array and a non-UUID identifier all belong to this tier. So do the **conditional** requirements whose shape depends on a sibling field: `amount_cents` must be present when a billing event's `event_type` is `PREMIUM_PAYMENT`, `INSERT_REMITTANCE` or `WRITE_OFF`, `remedy_amount_cents` must be present when a complaint response's `remedy_type` is `BENEFIT_REVERSAL` or `PREMIUM_REFUND`, and `denial_reason` must be present when a triage decision's `decision` is `DENY`. The very same payload shape is therefore valid for one `event_type` and invalid for another, and every one of these conditional failures also surfaces as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Each of them is a genuine member of this tier rather than a business rule wearing a schema code, so each is reported ahead of the path lookup of tier 5: a `DENY` carrying no `denial_reason` against a claim identifier that matches nothing reports the schema failure, not `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`. That precedence promise is confined to the failures named here, every one of which is decidable from the body alone. It does **not** extend to a cross-field mismatch that can only be decided by reading a record the body points at — a claim's filed `line_type` against its enrolled product's, which answers with the same `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) but is necessarily evaluated only after the identifiers it compares have resolved; §3.5 pins that check's exact position in the ladder.
4. **Pagination bounds.** On every list endpoint, an out-of-range or non-integer `limit` or `offset` yields `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).
5. **Path resource existence.** On every endpoint with a path parameter, a well-formed UUID that matches no row of the addressed collection yields that collection's own not-found code (404): `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PREMISES_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`.
6. **Body foreign-key existence.** Only after every path identifier resolves are the identifiers inside the body checked. Each of `partner_id`, `product_id`, `premises_id`, `source_campaign_id`, `contractor_id`, `supersedes_dispatch_id`, `recurrence_of_claim_id`, `claim_id` and `enrollment_id` must match an existing row, and a miss yields the referenced collection's not-found code (404). Because this tier sits below tier 5, a request with both a bad path identifier and a bad body identifier reports the path failure.
7. **Resource ownership.** An entity-backed caller acting outside its own scope yields `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Three operations are guarded: a campaign decision by a `PartnerBrandReviewer` whose `X-User-ID` is not the campaign's `partner_id`, a claim appeal by a `Policyholder` whose `X-User-ID` is not the premises reached through the claim's enrollment, and a dispatch field event by a `NetworkContractor` whose `X-User-ID` is not the dispatch's `contractor_id`.
8. **Segregation of duties.** Two operations yield `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403): an appeal decision whose `X-User-ID` equals the claim's `triage_decided_by_user_id`, and a campaign decision whose `X-User-ID` equals the campaign's `submitted_by_user_id`.
9. **Stale snapshots.** The five version guards of §2.6 fire next, each with its own 412 code on the operation that requires it: `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` on dispatch creation, `ERR_STALE_ENROLLMENT_VERSION` on a renewal notice, `ERR_STALE_PREMISES_VERSION` on enrollment creation, `ERR_STALE_PARTNER_VERSION` on a partner status change, and `ERR_STALE_COMPLAINT_VERSION` on a complaint response.
10. **State preconditions on the addressed record** (HTTP 409). A campaign that is not in a state the requested transition leaves from yields `ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_STATE` — submission requires `DRAFT` or `REJECTED`, a decision requires `PARTNER_REVIEW`, a mail drop requires `APPROVED`. A claim in the wrong state yields `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE` — a triage decision requires `TRIAGE`; dispatching requires `APPROVED`, or `DISPATCHED` when the request names an open dispatch of the same claim to supersede; opening an appeal requires `DENIED`; an appeal decision requires `APPEAL_OPEN`. A dispatch in the wrong state yields `ERR_INVALID_DISPATCH_STATE` — accepting requires `ASSIGNED`, arriving requires `ACCEPTED`, completing requires `ACCEPTED` or `ON_SITE` — and the same code is used when the dispatch nominated for supersession is already `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` or `SUPERSEDED`, or belongs to a different claim than the one in the path. A complaint in the wrong state yields `ERR_INVALID_COMPLAINT_STATE` — a response requires `OPEN`, `OVERDUE` or `ESCALATED`, an escalation requires `CLOSED_UNSUBSTANTIATED`. A partner status change whose target is unreachable from the partner's current status yields `ERR_INVALID_PARTNER_STATE`, and nothing at all leaves `TERMINATED`. An enrollment whose coverage has ended yields `ERR_ENROLLMENT_TERMINAL` on any operation that needs a live policy — cancellation, a renewal notice, a billing event, or filing a claim. Two counters also fire in this tier: a resubmission beyond the campaign revision limit yields `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`, and an appeal beyond the claim appeal limit yields `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`.
11. **Cross-entity state gates** (HTTP 409). `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_ACTIVE` fires on five operations, with one of them holding the partner to a wider set of admissible statuses than the other four: on creating a campaign, submitting a campaign, dropping mail and creating an enrollment it fires whenever the owning partner is not `ACTIVE`, while on filing a product it fires only when the sponsoring partner is `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED`, because a filing must also be possible while the partner is still `ONBOARDING` (see §3.1). `ERR_NO_FILED_PRODUCT` fires when a partner is activated **out of `ONBOARDING`** with no product in `AVAILABLE`; reinstating a `SUSPENDED` partner to `ACTIVE` is not gated on it at all, because the gate exists to stop a partner entering service with nothing filed, and a reinstated partner has already been in service. `ERR_PRODUCT_WITHDRAWN` fires when a campaign or an enrollment is created against a `WITHDRAWN` product. `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_MAILED` and `ERR_MAILING_SUPPRESSED` fire on enrollment creation when the named sourcing campaign has not been mailed, or was mailed but suppressed this address. `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` fires when a triage approval is attempted on an enrollment that is `SUSPENDED_NONPAY`, `CANCELLED` or `LAPSED`. `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_ACTIVE` fires when a manually named contractor is not `ACTIVE`.
12. **Safety gates** (HTTP 422). `ERR_CONTRACTOR_LICENSE_EXPIRED` fires when a manually named contractor's licence has expired, and `ERR_PERMIT_REQUIRED` fires both when a permit-covered product is dispatched to a contractor that may not pull permits and when a completion reports a permit fee without a permit number. Unlicensed work is a safety failure regardless of trade certification, which is why this tier deliberately outranks tier 13.
13. **Referential business rules** (HTTP 422): `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_CERTIFIED`, `ERR_INVALID_RECURRENCE_LINK`, `ERR_REMEDY_TARGET_MISSING`, `ERR_REMEDY_NOT_PERMITTED`, `ERR_BILL_INSERT_NOT_SUPPORTED`, `ERR_RESTORATION_SCOPE_NOT_COVERED` and `ERR_DENIAL_REASON_UNSUPPORTED`.
14. **Temporal business rules**: `ERR_DROP_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_CONTRACTOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_DROP_DATE_IN_FUTURE`, `ERR_WAITING_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`, `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED` and `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RENEWAL_NOTICE`, all HTTP 422, plus one member of this tier that is a request-shape failure rather than a business one: a cancellation whose `requested_effective_date` falls outside the coverage term is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
15. **Constraint rules**: `ERR_OWNERSHIP_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_ENROLLMENT`, `ERR_PRE_EXISTING_CONDITION`, `ERR_CANCELLATION_BLOCKED_APPEAL`, `ERR_CONTRACTOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CONTRACTOR` and `ERR_RATE_FILING_REQUIRED`, all HTTP 422, plus an approved quantity above the requested quantity, which is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CONTRACTOR` sits here because the automatic selector's filter is the capacity constraint and its four companions rolled into one test: it is answered only on an automatic dispatch, where no candidate survives the filter at all, so it never competes with the manual ladder's own refusals — but it does outrank the fiscal tier below, and an automatic dispatch on a claim whose occurrence allowance is also spent therefore reports the empty pool rather than `ERR_PER_OCCURRENCE_CAP_EXHAUSTED`.
16. **Fiscal rules**, evaluated last of all (HTTP 422): `ERR_ANNUAL_CAP_EXHAUSTED`, `ERR_PER_OCCURRENCE_CAP_EXHAUSTED`, `ERR_REFUND_NOT_AVAILABLE` and `ERR_REVERSAL_EXCEEDS_BENEFIT`.

Two consequences of this ladder are worth naming explicitly, because they are easy to get backwards. First, on a triage approval the coverage state gate outranks the cap check: an enrollment that is both `SUSPENDED_NONPAY` and cap-exhausted reports `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (tier 11), never `ERR_ANNUAL_CAP_EXHAUSTED` (tier 16). Second, on a manual dispatch the licence check outranks the certification check: a contractor that is both unlicensed and uncertified for the claim's service line reports `ERR_CONTRACTOR_LICENSE_EXPIRED` (tier 12), never `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_CERTIFIED` (tier 13).

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline: Pinned Ordering and Version Propagation

A single accepted request in this system frequently mutates records the caller never named. Those secondary mutations are not incidental — they are part of the contract, they are ordered, and each of them advances its own record's `version` by 1 and refreshes its own `updated_at`. Whenever a cascade touches a set of sibling records, that set is processed in `created_at` ascending order, so the outcome of a cascade over three campaigns or five dispatches is fully determined.

One distinction runs through the catalogue below and decides whether a `version` moves at all. A hop that **asserts** a value outright — opening an appeal asserting `retention_hold` true, a mail drop stamping its decision onto an address — writes and versions its record unconditionally, even when the value it writes is the value already stored there. A hop that **recomputes** a derived flag or dimension — `retention_hold` after an appeal decision, `regulator_hold` whenever a complaint intake, response or escalation reaches it, `cap_status` after a benefit charge or a benefit reversal — compares the recomputed value against the stored one and writes, versions and refreshes `updated_at` **only when the two differ**; when they agree the record is left completely untouched and its `version` does not move. That propagates: because an address's suppression view is re-derived only as a consequence of the owning enrollment's flag actually changing, a no-op recompute of `retention_hold` leaves the premises' `version` unmoved as well. The complete catalogue of cascades:

- **Partner suspension.** Suspending a partner sets its `suspension_reason` from the request and then, as one cascade hop, transitions every campaign of that partner whose status is `DRAFT`, `PARTNER_REVIEW` or `APPROVED` to `WITHDRAWN` in `created_at` ascending order, incrementing each campaign's `version`. In-force coverage is deliberately untouched: suspension stops marketing and new enrollments, and no enrollment or claim changes state.
- **Partner termination.** Terminating a partner performs the same campaign withdrawal cascade and, in addition, transitions every product of that partner to `WITHDRAWN` in `created_at` ascending order. `suspension_reason` records the reason given.
- **Partner reinstatement.** Returning a suspended partner to active clears `suspension_reason` back to null. Campaigns withdrawn by the earlier suspension are **not** revived — withdrawal is one-way.
- **Dispatch completion.** Completing a dispatch runs the benefit settlement, then in one request drives a fixed chain of hops in exactly this order, the same order §3.7 sets out: first the dispatch itself stores its invoice lines and settlement figures and moves to `COMPLETED`; then the assigned contractor's record is written, so its derived `open_dispatch_count` falls; then one benefit charge ledger row is appended for `benefit_paid_cents`, **and only when that figure is strictly positive** — a settlement that allocates nothing appends no ledger row at all; then the claim advances from dispatched through work-complete to paid, transiently and in a single write that moves its `version` by 1; then — only if the annual cap has been reached — the enrollment's cap dimension flips to `ANNUAL_CAP_EXHAUSTED`; and finally every other claim of that same enrollment still sitting in `TRIAGE` is denied with reason `CAP_EXHAUSTED` in `created_at` ascending order, each incrementing its own `version`. The ledger row is therefore appended **before** the claim leaves `DISPATCHED`, not as the claim moves.
- **Appeal opening.** Opening an appeal on a denied claim sets `retention_hold` to true on the owning enrollment and increments that enrollment's `version`, then recomputes `suppression_reason` and `suppressed` on that enrollment's premises and increments the premises' `version`.
- **Service-level breach and contractor probation.** A breached dispatch increments its contractor's `sla_breach_count`; if that count reaches the probation threshold while the contractor is still active, the contractor moves to `PROBATION`; every other dispatch of that contractor still in `ASSIGNED` is then cancelled in `created_at` ascending order, and each of those cancelled dispatches' claims reverts from dispatched back to approved — but that revert is **conditional**, exactly as §3.9 states it: a claim returns to `APPROVED` only once no dispatch of that claim is left in `ASSIGNED`, `ACCEPTED` or `ON_SITE`, so a claim another contractor is still working on stays `DISPATCHED` while its cancelled sibling dispatch leaves the open set regardless.
- **Enrollment cancellation.** Cancellation writes the proration outcome, appends its cash ledger rows, moves the billing dimension, and cancels every open dispatch belonging to this enrollment's claims, recomputing each affected contractor's `open_dispatch_count`.
- **Regulator complaint intake.** Logging a complaint that cites a denied claim reopens that claim's appeal and carries the appeal cascade with it; logging a complaint that cites an `enrollment_id` sets `regulator_hold` to true on that enrollment's premises and, being a guarded write in the sense named above, increments the premises' `version` **only when the flag was not already true**. A complaint that cites only a `claim_id` sets no hold whatever, even though that claim's own policy has an address of its own.
- **Complaint response and escalation.** Each remedy type has its own compensating ledger and state consequences, and both a response and an escalation recompute `regulator_hold` and re-derive `suppression_reason` on the affected premises.
- **Mail drop.** A drop touches every premises on the partner's file, mailed or suppressed alike, and each touched premises has its `version` incremented by 1.

### 2.10. Two Independently Conserved Quantities

Two running totals in this system must always be reconstructible from primary records rather than trusted as stored counters, and they are conserved independently of one another.

The **benefit ledger** is the first. `annual_benefit_used_cents` on an enrollment is the sum of the signed `amount_cents` of every ledger row for that enrollment whose `ledger_kind` is `BENEFIT`, whose `entry_type` is `BENEFIT_CHARGE` or `BENEFIT_REVERSAL`, and whose `cap_year_start` equals the enrollment's current `cap_year_start`. Reversal rows are negative and therefore reduce the total. `occurrence_used_cents` on a claim is the same sum restricted to the rows whose claim belongs to that claim's occurrence group — inheriting the cap-year filter, so it too counts only rows frozen under the policy's current `cap_year_start`. `benefit_paid_cents` on a claim is a third figure and the one deliberately **not** filtered that way: it is the signed sum of `amount_cents` over every `BENEFIT` row naming that claim, taken across **every** cap year rather than only the current one. It is what a regulator remedy measures a proposed reversal against, so a benefit charged in one cap year stays reversible in full after the cap year has rolled over even though it no longer shows up in `annual_benefit_used_cents`. `annual_benefit_reserved_cents` is a different animal again: it is the sum of `estimated_benefit_cents` over the dispatches of this enrollment's claims whose status is `ASSIGNED`, `ACCEPTED` or `ON_SITE` — a forward-looking view of the provisional exposure the policy is carrying before any invoice exists, which falls away the moment the dispatch leaves the open set. It is **reported only**, and this is the one place the word "reserved" could mislead: the benefit pool of §3.7 is the two caps less posted charges alone, so a reservation never narrows the pool that a later settlement, or a later provisional estimate, is filled against, and two dispatches open on one policy are each sized against the same undiminished remainder. On the cash side, `premium_collected_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over this enrollment's `CASH` rows whose `entry_type` is `PREMIUM_PAYMENT`, `INSERT_REMITTANCE`, `CANCELLATION_FEE` or `REFUND_CLAWBACK`, and `premium_refunded_cents` is the absolute value of the sum over its `CASH` rows whose `entry_type` is `PRORATION_REFUND`, `PRORATION_CREDIT` or `WRITE_OFF`. Every ledger row also carries an `effective_date`, the calendar date the movement is booked on, and an optional free-text `memo` for the audit trail; a row that settles work names that work through its `claim_id` alone, and no ledger row carries a dispatch identifier of any kind. Only a billing event ever supplies a `memo` — every row the system appends on its own initiative, whether a proration settlement, a cancellation fee, a benefit charge, a benefit reversal or a refund clawback, carries a null `memo`. `effective_date` likewise comes from the request only where a request offers one: a billing event books its row on the `effective_date` the caller named and a cancellation books all of its rows on the `cancellation_effective_date`, whereas every row a completion or a regulator remedy appends is booked on the logical clock's own calendar date. A row's `cap_year_start` is frozen from the enrollment at booking time and is null on every cash row, so a later cap-year advance can never retroactively move a historical row into a different year.

The **contractor capacity counter** is the second. `open_dispatch_count` on a contractor is the number of dispatches naming that contractor whose status is `ASSIGNED`, `ACCEPTED` or `ON_SITE`. It rises by one when a dispatch is created against that contractor and falls by one whenever such a dispatch completes, is cancelled or is superseded. It is never allowed to exceed `max_open_dispatches`, and because a supersede moves the open slot from the outgoing contractor to the incoming one in the same request, the sum of every contractor's `open_dispatch_count` always equals the number of dispatches actually in the open set. A contractor's `sla_breach_count`, by contrast, only ever rises.

### 2.11. Path Resolution, Referential Integrity and Collection Filters

Every collection is a peer at the API root: partners, products, premises, campaigns, enrollments, claims, contractors, dispatches, ledger entries, regulator complaints, and two operational capabilities. A sub-resource action is expressed as a verb segment beneath the single resource it acts on — a campaign's submission, a claim's appeals, a dispatch's events — and never as a two-level resource tree. Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right: the outermost identifier is looked up and, if it misses, reported before any deeper segment or any part of the body is inspected. A trailing slash is insignificant — `/partners` and `/partners/` address the same endpoint. Only after the path resolves completely are the body's foreign keys examined, and each of those reports the not-found code of the collection it points into.

The list endpoints share one filtering vocabulary and one important semantic. Partners may be filtered by `status`, `utility_type` and `state_code`. Products may be filtered by `partner_id`, `line_type` and `status`. Premises may be filtered by `partner_id`, by `last_mailed_campaign_id`, and by `suppressed` — the boolean derived view of standing suppression. Campaigns may be filtered by `partner_id`, `product_id` and `status`. Enrollments may be filtered by `premises_id`, `product_id`, `source_campaign_id` and `coverage_status`. Claims may be filtered by `enrollment_id`, by `recurrence_of_claim_id`, and by `status`. Contractors may be filtered by `state_code`, by `status`, and by `line_type` — and that last filter matches a contractor when the supplied value is a member of its `line_types_certified` array, which makes the contractor list the observable form of the dispatch candidate pool. Dispatches may be filtered by `claim_id`, `contractor_id`, `supersedes_dispatch_id` and `status`. Ledger entries may be filtered by `enrollment_id`, `claim_id`, `ledger_kind` and `entry_type`, and they are returned in append order, which is the same `created_at` ascending order as everything else. Regulator complaints may be filtered by `partner_id`, `claim_id`, `enrollment_id` and `status`. Every one of these filters is optional, an unrecognised enum value supplied to one of them is a schema failure, and supplying several narrows the result set conjunctively. The two kinds of filter are policed differently, and deliberately so. A filter drawn from a closed vocabulary — every `status`, `coverage_status`, `utility_type`, `line_type`, `piece_type`, `ledger_kind`, `entry_type` and the boolean `suppressed` — is validated against that vocabulary and refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) when the value is not a member, the boolean admitting `true` or `false` in any letter case and nothing else. Enum filter values are matched exactly, so a member name in the wrong letter case is a refusal rather than a match. A filter that names an identifier or a free-form code — every `*_id` filter and `state_code` — is not validated for shape or for existence at all: it is compared as a plain string against the stored field, so a syntactically impossible identifier, or a well-formed one naming nothing, simply matches no row and yields an empty page with `total_count` 0 rather than a 400 or a 404. Filter values are read with surrounding whitespace trimmed. A query parameter an endpoint does not declare — including a filter offered to a collection that does not support it — is ignored silently and never narrows or refuses the request.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Brand Partners, Filed Products and the Premises File

Everything in the programme hangs off a utility brand partner. A `PlatformAdmin` registers one by supplying its legal `name` — between 1 and 120 characters and not blank — the `utility_type` it operates, which is one of `WATER`, `WASTEWATER`, `ELECTRIC` or `COMBINATION`, and the `state_code` of the regulator with jurisdiction over it, which must be exactly two uppercase letters from A to Z. Four policy switches may be supplied in the same payload, and each has a default that applies when it is omitted. `brand_approval_required` defaults to true and records that no mail piece bearing this partner's logo may enter the mail stream until the partner's own reviewer has explicitly approved the campaign. `min_days_between_drops` defaults to 21 — three weeks of separation between consecutive mail drops — and must lie between 0 and 180 inclusive. `max_mailings_per_year` defaults to 2 and must lie between 1 and 12 inclusive; it is the contact-frequency cap, and an address that has already been mailed that many times in the current calendar year is suppressed from further drops. `rate_filing_required` defaults to false and marks a partner whose state requires a rate filing before a large renewal increase may even be noticed. `bill_insert_supported` defaults to true; when it is false, no enrollment on that partner's products may collect premium on the utility bill.

A newly registered partner is `ONBOARDING`, its `version` is 1, its `suspension_reason` is null and its `enrolled_premises_count` is 0. That last figure is a live cross-entity count rather than a stored tally: it is the number of distinct premises on this partner's file that hold at least one enrollment whose coverage is `PENDING_WAITING_PERIOD`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_NONPAY`. A cancelled or lapsed policy therefore stops contributing to it.

Partner lifecycle changes are recorded by a `PlatformAdmin` who names the `target_status`, gives a `reason`, and declares `expected_partner_version`. All three are required, `target_status` is any member of the four-state vocabulary — an unreachable one is a state refusal rather than a schema refusal — and `reason` is 1 to 500 non-blank characters. From `ONBOARDING` the only reachable state is `ACTIVE`, and even that transition is gated: a partner with no product currently in `AVAILABLE` may not be activated out of `ONBOARDING`, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_NO_FILED_PRODUCT`. Activation clears `suspension_reason` back to null, discarding the `reason` supplied with the activation itself; every other accepted target — `SUSPENDED` and `TERMINATED` alike — writes the supplied `reason` into `suspension_reason`. `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` records the supplied `reason` in `suspension_reason` and triggers the campaign withdrawal cascade; `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` reinstates the partner and clears `suspension_reason` again, which makes the pair a genuine cycle — a partner may be suspended and reinstated repeatedly. Both `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` and `SUSPENDED → TERMINATED` are permitted, record the `reason`, and withdraw the partner's products as well as its in-flight campaigns. `TERMINATED` is terminal in the strongest sense: no `target_status` whatsoever is reachable from it, and any attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PARTNER_STATE`. That same code answers any other undefined pairing, such as an attempt to suspend a partner that is still `ONBOARDING`.

Products are filed by a `PlatformAdmin` against a partner that has not been suspended or terminated, in a payload naming the sponsoring `partner_id`. Each product has a marketed `name` of 1 to 120 non-blank characters and a `line_type` drawn from `WATER_SERVICE_LINE`, `SEWER_LATERAL`, `INTERIOR_PLUMBING` and `EXTERIOR_ELECTRIC` — the single service line it repairs, which a claim's own service line must match exactly. Two ownership fields encode where the homeowner's responsibility begins. `ownership_boundary` is one of `CURB_STOP`, `METER`, `PROPERTY_LINE` or `INSIDE`, and it determines which physical segments of a failure the product will pay for. `required_owned_segment` is one of `WATER_CURB_STOP_TO_HOME`, `WATER_METER_TO_HOME`, `SEWER_PROPERTY_LINE_TO_MAIN`, `INTERIOR_PLUMBING_SYSTEM` or `EXTERIOR_ELECTRIC_MAST`, and an address must list that segment among the ones it owns before it may enrol. Pricing and cover limits follow: `monthly_price_cents` between 100 and 20000 inclusive, `waiting_period_days` between 0 and 90 inclusive, `per_occurrence_cap_cents` between 10000 and 2000000 inclusive, and `annual_cap_cents` between 10000 and 5000000 inclusive. Three optional fields complete the filing, each with a default that applies when it is omitted: `permit_covered`, which decides whether a municipal permit fee is a covered cost or falls to the homeowner and **defaults to false**; `restoration_covered_scope`, one of `NONE`, `BASIC_BACKFILL` or `FULL_HARDSCAPE`, which sets the restoration allowance tier and **defaults to `NONE`**, so a filing silent about restoration covers none of it; and `cap_reset_basis`, either `ENROLLMENT_ANNIVERSARY` or `CALENDAR_YEAR`, which decides whether the annual benefit window starts on the policy anniversary or on 1 January and **defaults to `ENROLLMENT_ANNIVERSARY`**. Every other field named above is required. A filed product opens in `AVAILABLE` with `version` 1, and it moves to `WITHDRAWN` only through the partner-termination cascade — no endpoint in this document withdraws a product directly or edits a filed one, so in practice a partner that wants different terms files a second product rather than amending the first. Nothing constrains how many products one partner may file, nor stops two of them sharing a `line_type`, a `name` or a `required_owned_segment`. The admissible statuses for the sponsoring partner are `ONBOARDING` and `ACTIVE`; filing against a partner that is `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` is refused with `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_ACTIVE`. `ONBOARDING` has to be admissible, because activation itself demands a product already in `AVAILABLE`, so a partner's very first filing necessarily happens before it is ever activated; were the filing gate to insist on `ACTIVE` the two rules would deadlock and no partner could ever be brought into service. Filing changes nothing on the partner record: the partner's own `version` does not advance (§2.6).
The marketing file is a list of service addresses, built by `MarketingOps` or a `PlatformAdmin`. Each entry names the `partner_id` whose service territory contains it, a single-line `street_address` of 1 to 200 non-blank characters that must be unique within that partner's file, and the `state_code` of the address as exactly two uppercase letters. Uniqueness is checked once the named partner resolves, by exact character-for-character comparison — so two addresses differing only in letter case or in punctuation are distinct entries — and a genuine collision is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400); the same street address may of course appear once on each of two different partners' files. Nothing requires the address's `state_code` to equal the partner's. The heart of the record is `owned_segments`: a non-empty array of distinct values drawn from the same five segment names a product may require, describing what the homeowner actually owns at this address. Three optional inputs colour the record further. `do_not_mail` defaults to false and records the homeowner's own opt-out. `address_status` defaults to `GOOD` and may be set to `BAD` to mark an undeliverable address. `known_defect_reported_at` defaults to null and, when supplied, is the instant at which a defect on this address was first reported to the utility — the fact that later decides whether a claim is a pre-existing condition.

Two further flags on a premises are maintained by the system rather than supplied. `regulator_hold` is set to true while at least one regulator complaint that **cites an `enrollment_id`** naming a policy at this address is still open, and cleared when the last such complaint closes. The citation has to be that explicit: a complaint that names only a `claim_id` — even one whose claim belongs to a policy at this very address — never sets the flag and never keeps it alive, because the hold is computed from the cited `enrollment_id` alone and never inferred through a cited claim (§3.8). `last_mail_decision` starts at `NOT_EVALUATED` and becomes `MAILED` or `SUPPRESSED` after the first drop that evaluates the address; alongside it, `last_mail_suppression_reason` records exactly which rule suppressed the address on that most recent drop, and is null whenever the last decision was to mail. `last_mailed_campaign_id` names the most recent campaign whose drop decided to mail this address, and remains null until that first mailing.

Three derived views make the file usable without replaying history. `mail_count_current_year` is the number of drops that decided to mail this address whose drop date falls in the logical clock's current calendar year. `suppression_reason` is the highest-precedence **standing** suppression reason for the address, drawn from `NONE`, `DNM_REQUEST`, `BAD_ADDRESS`, `REGULATOR_COMPLAINT_OPEN`, `OPEN_APPEAL` and `CONTACT_FREQUENCY_CAP`; it is evaluated in the same precedence order the mail drop uses, but with the two campaign-scoped reasons deliberately skipped, because whether an address is already enrolled or ownership-ineligible cannot be answered without knowing which product a campaign is marketing. When no standing reason matches, `suppression_reason` is `NONE`. `suppressed` is simply true exactly when `suppression_reason` is anything other than `NONE`, and false when it is `NONE`. A newly created premises therefore opens with `version` 1, `last_mail_decision` `NOT_EVALUATED`, `mail_count_current_year` 0 and a freshly derived `suppression_reason` — which is already `DNM_REQUEST` if the address arrived with a do-not-mail request, or `BAD_ADDRESS` if it arrived undeliverable.

### 3.2. Marketing Approval, the Revision Cycle and the Mail Drop

A campaign is a single mail piece for a single product. `MarketingOps` drafts it by naming the `partner_id` and the `product_id` — and the product must belong to that partner — together with an internal `name` of 1 to 120 non-blank characters, a `piece_type` that is one of `BILL_INSERT`, `DIRECT_MAIL_LETTER`, `POSTCARD` or `EMAIL`, the `drop_date` on which the piece is to enter the mail stream as a plain calendar date, and a `requested_quantity` between 0 and 1000000 inclusive. Every one of those six inputs is required. The campaign opens in `DRAFT` with `version` 1, `revision_count` 0, `mailed_count` 0 and `suppressed_count` 0, and its `approved_quantity`, `submitted_by_user_id`, `decision_by_user_id`, `decision_note`, `decided_at` and `mail_dropped_at` are all null. The requirement that the product belong to the named partner is a **request-shape** failure and not a state gate: once both identifiers resolve, a `product_id` whose owning `partner_id` differs from the one supplied is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), and that check is evaluated before either of the two gates that follow. Drafting against a partner that is not `ACTIVE` is then refused with `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_ACTIVE`, and drafting against a `WITHDRAWN` product with `ERR_PRODUCT_WITHDRAWN`.

Submitting the draft for brand review moves it `DRAFT → PARTNER_REVIEW` and stamps `submitted_by_user_id` with the submitting operator's `X-User-ID`. The submission may carry an optional `note` of up to 1000 characters, which is the operation's only declared field and is kept for the audit trail rather than published: no field of the campaign representation carries it back, so a submission with a note and one without are indistinguishable in the response. Two conditions outside the campaign itself must hold: the sponsoring partner must still be `ACTIVE`, and the campaign's drop date must not crowd another of that partner's drops. **Drop-window spacing** works like this. Every sibling campaign of the same partner whose status is `PARTNER_REVIEW`, `APPROVED` or `MAILED` is considered; siblings in `DRAFT`, `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN` are ignored entirely, which means withdrawing a campaign frees its window for another. For each considered sibling, the gap is the absolute difference in whole calendar days between the candidate's `drop_date` and the sibling's `drop_date`. A conflict exists when that gap is strictly fewer days than the partner's `min_days_between_drops`; a gap exactly equal to `min_days_between_drops` is permitted. With a spacing requirement of 21 days and a sibling already approved to drop on 2026-08-05, a candidate dropping on 2026-08-25 has a gap of 20 days and is refused with `ERR_DROP_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, while 2026-08-26 has a gap of exactly 21 days and is admitted — that is the first permitted date. Because `drop_date` carries no time component, no part of the comparison depends on a time of day.

The brand partner's own reviewer decides the campaign. The decision payload carries a `decision` that is exactly one of the two literals `APPROVE` and `REJECT` and a free-text `decision_note`, both required, the note running 1 to 1000 non-blank characters, and optionally an `approved_quantity`, which when supplied must itself lie between 0 and 1000000 inclusive. An approval moves the campaign `PARTNER_REVIEW → APPROVED` and sets `approved_quantity` to the supplied value, defaulting to the campaign's `requested_quantity` when the decision omits it; a supplied `approved_quantity` above `requested_quantity` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, a check made only on an approval — a rejection ignores any `approved_quantity` it carries, however large. A rejection moves the campaign `PARTNER_REVIEW → REJECTED` and leaves `approved_quantity` exactly as it was, which on a first-pass rejection means it stays null. Either decision records `decision_by_user_id`, `decision_note` and `decided_at`, the last being the logical clock at the moment of the decision. Deciding a campaign that is not in `PARTNER_REVIEW` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CAMPAIGN_STATE`.

Rejection is not the end. A rejected campaign may be resubmitted, which moves it `REJECTED → PARTNER_REVIEW` again, increments `revision_count` by 1, restamps `submitted_by_user_id`, and re-runs both the partner-active check and the drop-window check from scratch. The cycle is bounded: a campaign whose `revision_count` has already reached 2 may not be resubmitted again, and the third attempt is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. Marketing therefore gets the original submission plus two revisions before the campaign is dead. Note that submission and decision are guarded against the same operator playing both parts: a reviewer whose `X-User-ID` matches the campaign's `submitted_by_user_id` is refused, as is a reviewer whose `X-User-ID` is not the campaign's `partner_id` at all.

Finally, a campaign may leave the flow sideways. When the sponsoring partner is suspended or terminated, every campaign of that partner sitting in `DRAFT`, `PARTNER_REVIEW` or `APPROVED` is withdrawn — `DRAFT → WITHDRAWN`, `PARTNER_REVIEW → WITHDRAWN` and `APPROVED → WITHDRAWN` are all defined system transitions — and an `APPROVED` campaign that is withdrawn keeps its `approved_quantity` intact for audit rather than having it cleared. `WITHDRAWN` and `MAILED` are the two terminal campaign states.
Executing the drop is the moment the marketing file meets the compliance rules, and it is a single `MarketingOps` action that moves the campaign `APPROVED → MAILED`. Three preconditions guard it: the campaign must be `APPROVED` with a non-null `approved_quantity`, the sponsoring partner must still be `ACTIVE`, and the campaign's `drop_date` must be on or before the logical clock's calendar date. A drop attempted before its own drop date is refused with `ERR_DROP_DATE_IN_FUTURE`.

The drop then walks every premises on the partner's file in `created_at` ascending order and decides each one independently. **Exactly one** suppression reason is recorded per address — the first one that matches in a fixed precedence order, after which no further reason is evaluated for that address. The order, highest precedence first, is: `DNM_REQUEST` when the address has `do_not_mail` set to true; then `BAD_ADDRESS` when its `address_status` is `BAD`; then `REGULATOR_COMPLAINT_OPEN` when its `regulator_hold` is true; then `ACTIVE_ENROLLMENT` when the address already holds an enrollment on this campaign's own product whose coverage is `PENDING_WAITING_PERIOD`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_NONPAY`; then `OPEN_APPEAL` when any enrollment of the address carries a retention hold; then `CONTACT_FREQUENCY_CAP` when its `mail_count_current_year` has reached or exceeded the partner's `max_mailings_per_year`; and last `OWNERSHIP_INELIGIBLE` when the product's `required_owned_segment` is absent from the address's `owned_segments`. An address that is both marked do-not-mail and undeliverable reports `DNM_REQUEST`, not `BAD_ADDRESS`; an address that both carries a regulator hold and is already enrolled reports `REGULATOR_COMPLAINT_OPEN`.

Only once every address has been screened does capacity come into play. The survivors — the addresses that matched no suppression reason — are mailed in that same `created_at` ascending order until the campaign's `approved_quantity` has been filled, and every survivor beyond that count is suppressed with the reason `QUANTITY_CAP`. This reason sits deliberately outside the precedence list because it is a capacity outcome rather than a rule violation: with an approved quantity of 2 and three surviving addresses, the third — the one with the latest `created_at` — is the one that misses out. The degenerate case behaves the same way rather than erroring: an `approved_quantity` of 0 mails nobody, every survivor is suppressed with `QUANTITY_CAP`, and the campaign still reaches `MAILED` with `mailed_count` 0.

Each decision leaves a trace. For every mailed address the drop sets `last_mailed_campaign_id` to this campaign, sets `last_mail_decision` to `MAILED`, clears `last_mail_suppression_reason` to null, and recomputes `mail_count_current_year`. For every suppressed address it sets `last_mail_decision` to `SUPPRESSED` and `last_mail_suppression_reason` to the winning reason — which is why that field's vocabulary is wider than standing `suppression_reason`, admitting the two campaign-scoped reasons `ACTIVE_ENROLLMENT` and `OWNERSHIP_INELIGIBLE` as well as `QUANTITY_CAP`. Either way the address's `version` is incremented. On the campaign itself, `mailed_count` and `suppressed_count` are set to the two tallies and `mail_dropped_at` is stamped with the logical clock.

The drop's response is not the campaign record but a purpose-built summary containing `campaign_id`, the resulting `status`, the `mailed_count` and `suppressed_count` tallies, a `suppressed_by_reason` object mapping each reason that actually fired to its count — reasons with a count of zero are omitted from the map entirely, so a drop that suppressed nobody reports an empty object rather than a map of zeroes — and `mailed_premises_ids`, the identifiers of the addresses that were mailed, in the order they were mailed, which is again `created_at` ascending. Those six fields are the whole of the summary: it is not a campaign record and carries no `id`, `version` or `updated_at`, and because it settles a record that already exists rather than creating one, it returns HTTP 200. A drop over a partner whose file is empty is legal for the same reason the zero-quantity drop is: the campaign still reaches `MAILED`, both tallies are 0, `suppressed_by_reason` is empty and `mailed_premises_ids` is empty.

### 3.3. Enrollment: Admission Gates, Billing Mode and the Coverage Clock

A `CustomerServiceRep` enrols an address in a product. The payload names the `premises_id`, the `product_id`, a `billing_mode`, and `expected_premises_version` carrying the premises `version` the agent was looking at. Two optional inputs shape the rest: `source_campaign_id`, which attributes the sale to a specific mail drop, and `prepaid_amount_cents`, which may be stated explicitly but is otherwise derived.

`billing_mode` is one of two values and the choice reverberates through the whole policy. `BILL_INSERT` collects the premium on the utility bill month by month with no prepayment at all. `DIRECT_BILL` collects a twelve-month prepayment up front. A partner whose `bill_insert_supported` is false cannot host a bill-inserted policy, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_BILL_INSERT_NOT_SUPPORTED`.

Admission runs a battery of cross-entity checks. The sponsoring partner must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_ACTIVE`. The product must still be `AVAILABLE`, or `ERR_PRODUCT_WITHDRAWN`. Attribution, when claimed, must be genuine: a supplied `source_campaign_id` must name a campaign that has actually reached `MAILED`, and a campaign still in review or approved but undropped is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_MAILED`. Furthermore the address must actually have been mailed by that campaign — if the address's `last_mailed_campaign_id` is not that campaign, or its `last_mail_decision` is `SUPPRESSED`, the enrollment is refused with `ERR_MAILING_SUPPRESSED`, because selling to an address the compliance rules suppressed is exactly what the suppression rules exist to prevent. A direct inbound sale simply omits `source_campaign_id` and skips both of those gates entirely. Ownership must qualify: the product's `required_owned_segment` must appear in the address's `owned_segments`, or the request is refused with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. And an address may not hold two live policies on the same product — if an existing enrollment on that `product_id` is `PENDING_WAITING_PERIOD`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_NONPAY`, the second is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_ENROLLMENT`.

An accepted enrollment opens with `version` 1 and three status dimensions that thereafter advance independently: `coverage_status` at `PENDING_WAITING_PERIOD`, `billing_status` at `CURRENT`, and `cap_status` at `WITHIN_CAP`. Everything the later lifecycle fills in opens empty: `past_due_since` is null, `retention_hold` is false, `renewal_price_cents`, `renewal_effective_date` and `renewal_notice_sent_at` are null with `renewal_notice_status` at `NONE` and `renewal_count` at 0, and `cancellation_effective_date`, `cancellation_reason`, `refund_cents`, `credit_cents` and `cancellation_fee_cents` are all null. Money terms are **pinned at this moment and never repriced by later edits to the product**: `monthly_price_cents` is copied from the product's own `monthly_price_cents`, and a subsequent change to the product's list price leaves every existing policy alone. `prepaid_amount_cents`, when not supplied, is twelve times that pinned monthly price under `DIRECT_BILL` and zero under `BILL_INSERT`. When it **is** supplied it is stored exactly as sent under either billing mode, and it is reconciled against neither the mode nor twelve times the monthly price: a `BILL_INSERT` policy may therefore legitimately carry a non-zero prepayment and a `DIRECT_BILL` policy one that is nothing like a year's premium. The figure is inert until cancellation, where it is the base of the direct-bill refund and is ignored entirely under bill-insert billing. Dates are stamped from the logical clock: `enrollment_date` is the clock instant itself, `term_start` is the clock's calendar date and is the inclusive start of the coverage term, and `term_end` is exactly twelve calendar months after `term_start` and is the **exclusive** end of that term. `cap_year_start` is set to `term_start` when the product's `cap_reset_basis` is `ENROLLMENT_ANNIVERSARY`, and to 1 January of the year containing `term_start` when it is `CALENDAR_YEAR`.

The waiting period is where coverage differs from enrollment. `coverage_effective_at` is `term_start` advanced by the product's `waiting_period_days` whole days, taken at midnight UTC — that is, at `00:00:00.000Z` on the resulting date. A policy sold on 2026-08-05 under a product with a thirty-day waiting period has `coverage_effective_at` of `2026-09-04T00:00:00.000Z`. The comparison against it is inclusive on the coverage side: a claim reported at exactly that instant is inside coverage, and a claim reported one millisecond earlier, at `2026-09-03T23:59:59.999Z`, is still inside the waiting period. The coverage dimension advances from `PENDING_WAITING_PERIOD` to `ACTIVE` only when the overnight run observes that the logical clock has reached or passed `coverage_effective_at`; nothing about creating the enrollment activates it immediately.

Creating the policy also reaches sideways. The address's `suppression_reason` is re-derived — a live policy on a product means future drops for that same product will suppress the address as already enrolled — and the address's `version` is incremented. The sponsoring partner's `enrolled_premises_count` rises accordingly.

### 3.4. Premium, Arrears, Renewal Pricing and Cancellation Settlement

`BillingOps` posts money movements against a policy as billing events. Each event names an `event_type`, an `effective_date` on which the movement is booked, and optionally an `amount_cents` and a `memo` of at most 500 characters. Four event types exist and they drive the billing dimension along different edges — but on all four the ledger consequence and the status consequence are separate questions, and an event whose status precondition is unmet is **accepted and simply has no status effect** rather than being refused. There is no error code for a badly timed billing event; the only refusal the operation carries beyond its own schema is `ERR_ENROLLMENT_TERMINAL` on a `CANCELLED` or `LAPSED` policy.

A **missed payment** moves the billing dimension `CURRENT → PAST_DUE` and stamps `past_due_since` with the event's own `effective_date`; it writes no ledger row at all, because a missed payment moves no money. It takes that edge only from `CURRENT`, and only while coverage is still `PENDING_WAITING_PERIOD` or `ACTIVE`. A missed payment against a policy already `PAST_DUE` or `WRITTEN_OFF`, or against one whose coverage has already been suspended for non-payment, therefore changes nothing — which in particular means a second missed payment never re-anchors `past_due_since`, so the arrears clock of the overnight run keeps running from the first one however many further notices arrive.

A **premium payment** or a **bill-insert remittance** *always* appends one cash ledger row, carrying the positive `amount_cents` exactly as sent and the event's own `event_type` as its `entry_type`. Whether it also mends the billing dimension is decided separately: the edge `PAST_DUE → CURRENT`, with `past_due_since` cleared back to null, is taken only when the dimension was `PAST_DUE` **and** the amount is at least the policy's `monthly_price_cents`. A part payment below that threshold, or any payment on a policy that was not in arrears, banks the cash and leaves the dimension exactly where it stood.

A **write-off** likewise always appends its cash row — one row whose `amount_cents` is the negative of the non-negative amount supplied — and takes the edge to `WRITTEN_OFF` only from `PAST_DUE`. `WRITTEN_OFF` is a billing dead end as far as this operation is concerned: because the clearing edge starts at `PAST_DUE`, no later payment or remittance leaves it, and only a cancellation settlement or a regulator remedy moves the dimension again.

On every accepted billing event, whichever branch it took and whether or not it changed a single field, `premium_collected_cents` and `premium_refunded_cents` are re-derived, the enrollment's `version` is incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed, and the updated policy is returned. A payment or remittance that clears the arrears also has a coverage-side consequence, which is the one place the two dimensions couple in the customer's favour: a policy whose coverage had been suspended for non-payment returns `SUSPENDED_NONPAY → ACTIVE` in the same request, with `past_due_since` cleared. That makes the pair `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED_NONPAY → ACTIVE` a genuine reinstatement cycle.

Arrears are aged by the overnight run rather than by the clock alone. A policy whose coverage is `ACTIVE` while its billing dimension is `PAST_DUE` is suspended — `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED_NONPAY` — once thirty whole days have elapsed since `past_due_since`, meaning once `past_due_since` advanced by 30 days is on or before the logical clock's calendar date. A policy already suspended for non-payment lapses — `SUSPENDED_NONPAY → LAPSED` — once sixty whole days have elapsed since the same `past_due_since`. Lapsing also clears `retention_hold` to false. `LAPSED` is the terminal coverage state, and a lapsed or cancelled policy refuses cancellation, renewal notices, billing events and new claims alike with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_TERMINAL`.

**Renewal pricing** is where the programme's notice obligations bite. `BillingOps` issues a renewal price change notice naming the `new_price_cents`, the `renewal_effective_date` on which that price is to take effect, and `expected_enrollment_version`. `new_price_cents` need only be a non-negative integer: it is deliberately **not** confined to the 100-to-20000 band a filed product's own `monthly_price_cents` must lie in, so a policy may be noticed at any price at all, a decrease included. The size of the change decides how much advance warning the homeowner is owed. The percentage change is the new price minus the in-force `monthly_price_cents`, divided by that in-force price, multiplied by 100, and rounded half up to two fractional digits. Four notice tiers are then consulted **in order**, and the first one that accepts the percentage supplies the requirement: a change of at most 0 percent requires 15 days' notice; a change of at most 10 percent requires 30 days; a change of at most 25 percent requires 45 days; and anything larger requires 60 days. Because the tiers are consulted in that order, each boundary belongs to the lower tier — a change of exactly 10.00 percent falls in the 30-day tier, not the 45-day tier. The lead time actually offered is the number of whole days from the logical clock's calendar date to `renewal_effective_date`, and the notice is compliant when that lead time is greater than or equal to the required days; a lead time exactly equal to the requirement is compliant. Consider a policy at 1000 cents a month being repriced to 1100: the change is exactly 10.00 percent, so 30 days are required. With a `renewal_effective_date` of 2027-08-05, a notice issued while the clock reads 2027-07-06 offers exactly 30 days and is accepted; issued one day later, on 2027-07-07, it offers 29 days and is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RENEWAL_NOTICE`.

A second, independent gate follows the tier check, and the order between them is fixed: the tier is evaluated first, then the filing gate. When the partner's `rate_filing_required` is true and the percentage change exceeds 25 percent, the notice is refused with `ERR_RATE_FILING_REQUIRED` **even when the lead time would have satisfied the 60-day tier** — the state filing that such an increase demands is outside this system, so the increase simply cannot be noticed here. A compliant notice records `renewal_price_cents`, `renewal_effective_date` and `renewal_notice_sent_at`, and sets `renewal_notice_status` to `SENT`. Nothing stops a second compliant notice being issued while the first is still outstanding: it simply overwrites all four of those fields and leaves the status at `SENT`, and the superseded notice leaves no trace behind. A notice is admissible from `PENDING_WAITING_PERIOD`, `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED_NONPAY` alike — only the two terminal coverage states refuse it. That field's full vocabulary is `NONE` before any notice exists, `SENT` while a notice is outstanding, `VOIDED`, `APPLIED` once the renewal run has adopted the noticed price, and `NOT_APPLIED_NO_COMPLIANT_NOTICE` when the term rolled over without one. `VOIDED` is a member of that vocabulary which no operation in this document ever writes: there is no endpoint that withdraws an outstanding notice, so the value is reserved and unreachable. For the renewal run to adopt a noticed price, the notice's `renewal_effective_date` must equal the policy's `term_end`; a notice pointing at any other date is not applied.

**Cancellation** is handled by a `CustomerServiceRep` and settles the unearned premium. The request carries a `reason` — recorded as `cancellation_reason`, one of `CUSTOMER_REQUEST`, `MOVED`, `NONPAYMENT` or `REGULATOR_REMEDY` — and a `requested_effective_date`, which must fall on or after `term_start` and on or before `term_end`; outside that range the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Coverage may be cancelled from `PENDING_WAITING_PERIOD`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_NONPAY`, and in all three cases the settlement is identical. One thing blocks it outright: a policy whose `retention_hold` is true — meaning at least one of its claims has an appeal open — is refused with `ERR_CANCELLATION_BLOCKED_APPEAL`.

The settlement first fixes `cancellation_effective_date` to the requested date and then computes how much premium the programme actually earned. The earned span is the **half-open** interval from `term_start` up to but not including `cancellation_effective_date` — the effective date itself is not earned — split at calendar-month boundaries. For each overlapped calendar month, the month's share is the pinned `monthly_price_cents` multiplied by the number of days of that month inside the span, divided by the total number of days in that month, rounded half up to whole cents. **Each month is rounded before the sum, never after**, and the earned premium is the sum of those already-rounded monthly shares. Summing the exact fractional monthly parts and rounding once at the end is a different and incorrect answer. A cancellation fee is then decided by the free-look window: when strictly fewer than 30 whole days separate `term_start` from `cancellation_effective_date`, `cancellation_fee_cents` is 0; otherwise it is 2500 cents.

Two settlement variants follow, one per billing mode, and they are genuinely different calculations rather than two spellings of one. Under `DIRECT_BILL` the homeowner already paid the year up front, so unearned premium comes back as cash: `refund_cents` is `prepaid_amount_cents` less the earned premium less the cancellation fee, `credit_cents` is 0, and a proration-refund cash ledger row is appended for the negative of `refund_cents`. If that arithmetic comes out negative — earned premium plus fee exceeding what was prepaid — nothing is refunded and the request is refused with `ERR_REFUND_NOT_AVAILABLE`. Under `BILL_INSERT` the homeowner has been paying month by month, so `prepaid_amount_cents` is irrelevant and only the unused remainder of the final billed month is given back: the remaining days are the number of days in the month containing `cancellation_effective_date`, minus that date's day of the month, plus 1, and `credit_cents` is `monthly_price_cents` multiplied by those remaining days, divided by the number of days in that month, rounded half up. `refund_cents` is 0 and a proration-credit cash row is appended for the negative of `credit_cents`. In either mode, a non-zero `cancellation_fee_cents` is also recorded as a separate positive cancellation-fee cash row. The two kinds of row differ in exactly that respect, and deliberately: the proration row is appended **unconditionally**, so a cancellation that settles at zero — one effective on `term_start` itself, where the half-open earned span is empty and a direct-bill refund equals the whole prepayment — still leaves a proration row in the ledger, and one whose computed amount is zero leaves a zero-cent row rather than no row; the fee row, by contrast, is appended only when the fee is actually 2500.

A worked settlement makes the rounding order concrete. Take a policy at 2499 cents a month with `term_start` 2026-08-05 cancelled effective 2026-11-18. The monthly shares are 27 of August's 31 days giving 2177 cents, all 30 of September giving 2499, all 31 of October giving 2499, and 17 of November's 30 days giving 1416 — summing to 8591 cents earned. Summing the fractional parts first and rounding once would have produced 8627 cents, which is wrong. The 105 days elapsed exceed the free-look window, so the fee is 2500. Under `DIRECT_BILL` with 29988 cents prepaid, `refund_cents` is 29988 less 8591 less 2500, or 18897. Under `BILL_INSERT` over the identical dates, the remaining days are 30 minus 18 plus 1, or 13, and `credit_cents` is 2499 multiplied by 13 divided by 30, rounded half up, or 1083.

Cancellation finally moves the billing dimension and reaches into the field. The billing dimension is set to `REFUND_ISSUED` under `DIRECT_BILL` and to `CREDIT_ISSUED` under `BILL_INSERT`, and that assignment is unconditional rather than an edge out of `CURRENT` alone: a policy cancelled while `PAST_DUE` or while `WRITTEN_OFF` lands on the same settled value, and `past_due_since` is left standing exactly as it was rather than cleared. Every dispatch belonging to this policy's claims that is still `ASSIGNED`, `ACCEPTED` or `ON_SITE` is cancelled, each recording `CLAIM_DENIED` as its cancellation reason, and each affected contractor's `open_dispatch_count` is recomputed — but the claims those dispatches belonged to are **not** reverted and keep whatever status they were already in, which is the one respect in which this cascade is narrower than the probation cascade of §3.9. Nor is the address part of the cascade: cancellation re-derives nothing on the premises and leaves its `version` unmoved, even though the sponsoring partner's `enrolled_premises_count` falls as a consequence of the coverage ending.

### 3.5. Claims: The Eligibility Assessment, Recurrence Groups and Triage

A claim may be filed by a `CustomerServiceRep` taking the call or by the `Policyholder` directly. The filing names the `enrollment_id`, the `line_type` on which the failure occurred — which must equal the enrolled product's own `line_type`, a mismatch being refused as an invalid request — the `defect_location`, an `urgency`, and an `incident_description` of between 1 and 500 non-blank characters. `defect_location` is one of `CURB_STOP_TO_METER`, `METER_TO_HOME`, `INSIDE_HOME` or `PROPERTY_LINE_TO_MAIN`; `urgency` is one of `EMERGENCY`, `URGENT` or `ROUTINE` and is what later decides how fast the network must respond. A policy already `CANCELLED` or `LAPSED` cannot receive a claim at all and is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_TERMINAL`. The `line_type` mismatch is a cross-field shape failure rather than a state gate, and its position in the ladder follows from that: it is evaluated once the named `enrollment_id` and any referenced `recurrence_of_claim_id` have both resolved, but before the terminal-policy gate, so a mismatched `line_type` on a `LAPSED` policy reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` while a mismatch under an identifier that names nothing reports the not-found code. Those two refusals are the whole of the admission test. Nothing else about the surrounding record is examined: a claim may be filed against a policy whose product has since been `WITHDRAWN` or whose sponsoring partner has been suspended or terminated, and a policy that is `SUSPENDED_NONPAY`, still inside its waiting period, or cap-exhausted accepts the filing perfectly well. Every one of those facts surfaces in the coverage verdict below rather than as a refusal, which is why an uncovered claim is a recorded claim rather than a rejected request.

An accepted claim opens in `TRIAGE` with `version` 1, `appeal_count` 0, and `reported_at` stamped from the logical clock. It also opens with a computed `eligibility_assessment`, and this field is the heart of the claims process: the system renders its own coverage verdict at intake, before any human looks at the file, and that verdict then constrains what the human is allowed to conclude. The verdict is not a snapshot frozen at intake, however. `eligibility_assessment` is recomputed from the current state of the policy, the product and the address on **every** read of the claim, so a claim filed while coverage was live and read back after the policy was suspended, or after its annual cap was exhausted, reports the new verdict rather than the old one — and the triage gates below are applied against the verdict as it stands at the moment the adjuster decides, not against the one that was true when the call came in.

The verdict is the **first failing check in a pinned order**, and the order matters because a claim can fail several checks at once while only one reason is recorded. First, `COVERAGE_INACTIVE` when the policy's `coverage_status` is `SUSPENDED_NONPAY`, `CANCELLED` or `LAPSED`. Second, `OWNERSHIP_INELIGIBLE` when the reported `defect_location` is not among the locations the product's `ownership_boundary` covers, or when the product's `required_owned_segment` is absent from the address's `owned_segments`. The covered-location map is fixed: a `CURB_STOP` boundary covers `CURB_STOP_TO_METER` and `METER_TO_HOME`; a `METER` boundary covers only `METER_TO_HOME`; a `PROPERTY_LINE` boundary covers only `PROPERTY_LINE_TO_MAIN`; and an `INSIDE` boundary covers only `INSIDE_HOME`. Third, `PRE_EXISTING` when the address carries a non-null `known_defect_reported_at` that is strictly earlier than the policy's `enrollment_date` — a defect the utility already knew about before the policy was sold is not a covered surprise. Fourth, `WAITING_PERIOD` when the claim's `reported_at` is strictly earlier than the policy's `coverage_effective_at`. Fifth, `CAP_EXHAUSTED` when the policy's `cap_status` is `ANNUAL_CAP_EXHAUSTED`. A claim that survives all five is `ELIGIBLE`. Because a suspended policy is caught by the first check, a suspended policy whose annual cap is also exhausted is recorded as `COVERAGE_INACTIVE`, never as `CAP_EXHAUSTED`.

Recurring failures are pooled rather than treated as fresh events. When `recurrence_of_claim_id` is supplied, the new claim joins the earlier claim's occurrence group, and `occurrence_root_claim_id` is resolved by walking the chain of `recurrence_of_claim_id` links all the way to its **transitive root** — the referenced claim's own `occurrence_root_claim_id`, not merely the referenced claim's id. A claim filed with no link is the root of its own group and its `occurrence_root_claim_id` equals its own `id`. The link is never inferred: a second failure on the same line that arrives without an explicit link opens a brand-new occurrence group, with its own fresh per-occurrence pool. A supplied link is only valid when the referenced claim belongs to the same `enrollment_id`, carries the same `line_type`, and was reported no more than 30 days before this claim's `reported_at`; otherwise the filing is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RECURRENCE_LINK`. That age test is a whole-day count in the sense of §2.5 rather than an instant comparison: the elapsed days between the two `reported_at` instants are floored before being measured against 30, so a referenced claim reported 30 days and a few hours earlier still counts as inside the window and only a full 31 days of separation refuses the link. The consequence of pooling is the point of it: `occurrence_used_cents` sums the benefit paid across **every claim sharing the same `occurrence_root_claim_id`**, so a chain of recurrences draws down one shared per-occurrence allowance rather than one allowance each.

A `ClaimsAdjuster` then triages a claim that is in `TRIAGE`, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY`, a `coverage_note` of 1 to 1000 non-blank characters explaining the reasoning, and, when denying, a `denial_reason`. Those three are the whole of the body. `denial_reason` is required on a `DENY` and refused as a missing field when it is absent — the conditional shape rule of §2.8's tier 3 — while on an `APPROVE` it is simply optional and, if supplied, is accepted and then ignored rather than recorded. The `coverage_note` itself is kept for the audit trail rather than published: no field of the claim representation carries it back, so two triage decisions differing only in their note are indistinguishable in the response. Approval drives `TRIAGE → APPROVED`; denial drives `TRIAGE → DENIED`. Either way `triage_decided_by_user_id` records the deciding adjuster's `X-User-ID` and `triage_decided_at` records the instant, and on a denial `denied_at` is stamped as well — that instant, and not the claim's own `reported_at`, is what starts the appeal window.

Approval is where the intake verdict becomes binding. An adjuster cannot approve a claim the system found uncovered, and the specific refusal names the specific defect: a claim assessed `COVERAGE_INACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, one assessed `WAITING_PERIOD` with `ERR_WAITING_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`, one assessed `OWNERSHIP_INELIGIBLE` with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, one assessed `PRE_EXISTING` with `ERR_PRE_EXISTING_CONDITION`, and one assessed `CAP_EXHAUSTED` with `ERR_ANNUAL_CAP_EXHAUSTED`. Denial is constrained from the opposite direction: the recorded `denial_reason` must not contradict the assessment. The rule is an exact-match rule in both directions. On a claim the system found uncovered, the **only** admissible `denial_reason` is the name of the assessment itself, so denying a claim assessed as `WAITING_PERIOD` for the reason `PRE_EXISTING` is refused with `ERR_DENIAL_REASON_UNSUPPORTED` — and so, for the same reason, is denying it for `NOT_COVERED_SEGMENT`, which is admissible only where the assessment is `ELIGIBLE`. On a claim the system found `ELIGIBLE`, conversely, any denial reason other than `NOT_COVERED_SEGMENT` is refused. `NOT_COVERED_SEGMENT` is therefore the adjuster's sole discretionary denial — the one judgement call the system does not pre-empt, reserved for a covered policy whose particular failure nonetheless falls outside the filed scope.

### 3.6. Dispatching the Network: Selection Score, Visit Windows and the Response SLA

Contractors are onboarded by an `OperationsAdmin` or a `PlatformAdmin` with a `name` of 1 to 120 non-blank characters, a two-letter uppercase `state_code`, a `license_number` of 1 to 40 non-blank characters, a `license_expires_on` calendar date, a non-empty array of distinct `line_types_certified`, an `hourly_rate_cents` between 1000 and 40000, an `sla_compliance_pct` between 0 and 100 recording historical on-time performance, a `max_open_dispatches` capacity ceiling between 1 and 50, and an optional `permit_pull_authorized` flag, which **defaults to false** when the filing omits it, so a contractor is assumed unable to pull municipal permits until it says otherwise. A new contractor opens `ACTIVE` with `version` 1, `sla_breach_count` 0 and `open_dispatch_count` 0. The `status` vocabulary is `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION` and `SUSPENDED`, and only an `ACTIVE` contractor is dispatchable. No endpoint in this document changes a contractor's status, its licence, its rate card or its capacity ceiling: a contractor record is written once and thereafter mutated only by the system itself, and the single status transition the system performs anywhere is the overnight probation hop of §3.9. `PROBATION` is consequently permanent — nothing returns a contractor to `ACTIVE` — and `SUSPENDED` is a member of the vocabulary that no operation ever writes.

An `OperationsAdmin` dispatches an `APPROVED` claim by supplying a `window_start` — an ISO 8601 UTC instant, read in any of the input forms §2.5 admits and echoed back in the three-digit rendering — together with `expected_claim_version`. Two optional inputs change the shape of the request: `contractor_id`, which names the contractor explicitly, and `supersedes_dispatch_id`, which replaces an existing open dispatch on the same claim. A dispatch is admissible from `APPROVED`, and also from `DISPATCHED` when `supersedes_dispatch_id` names a supersedable dispatch of that same claim; anything else — including a bare dispatch request against a claim already `DISPATCHED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`. A dispatch is supersedable unless it has already finished or already left the board: a `supersedes_dispatch_id` pointing at a dispatch that is `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` or `SUPERSEDED`, or at a dispatch belonging to a different claim, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DISPATCH_STATE`. That leaves `ASSIGNED`, `ACCEPTED`, `ON_SITE` and — importantly — `SLA_BREACHED` as the supersedable states, so the ordinary remedy for a contractor who missed the response deadline is to redispatch over the breached record, driving it `ASSIGNED → SUPERSEDED`, `ACCEPTED → SUPERSEDED` or `SLA_BREACHED → SUPERSEDED` as the case may be. The claim's own status is the only thing about the surrounding policy that is examined: dispatch consults neither the coverage dimension nor the eligibility assessment, so an `APPROVED` claim stays dispatchable after its policy has been suspended for non-payment or cancelled outright, and a claim overturned on appeal is dispatchable on exactly the same terms as one approved at triage. What is checked, last of everything and only once a contractor has been settled on, is the occurrence pool: a claim whose occurrence group has already consumed its entire per-occurrence allowance cannot be worked and is refused with `ERR_PER_OCCURRENCE_CAP_EXHAUSTED` — the fiscal tier 16 of §2.8, so every contractor-eligibility and window refusal below outranks it and an exhausted occurrence group is reported only when the request would otherwise have succeeded.

**Who gets the job** depends on whether `contractor_id` was supplied. When it was, `selection_mode` is `MANUAL`, the scoring pass is skipped entirely, and the named contractor is used — but every eligibility condition is still enforced individually, so that a bad manual choice fails with a precise reason rather than a generic one: a contractor whose `status` is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_ACTIVE`, an expired licence with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_LICENSE_EXPIRED`, a missing certification with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_CERTIFIED`, a full book with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, and a contractor who cannot pull permits on a product whose `permit_covered` is true with `ERR_PERMIT_REQUIRED`. Those five are listed here in the order a reader meets them and not in the order they fire: they are evaluated strictly in the tier order of §2.8, which is `status` first, then the licence, then permit authority, then certification, then the visit-window conflict described below, and the full book last of the six. A manually named contractor that is both uncertified and out of capacity therefore reports `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_CERTIFIED`, and one that is both double-booked and out of capacity reports `ERR_CONTRACTOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. When `contractor_id` is omitted, `selection_mode` is `AUTOMATIC` and the system chooses.

Automatic selection first filters the network down to genuinely eligible candidates. A contractor qualifies only when **all** of the following hold: `status` is `ACTIVE`, so both `PROBATION` and `SUSPENDED` are excluded; `license_expires_on` is strictly later than the logical clock's calendar date, so a licence expiring today is already unusable; the claim's `line_type` appears in `line_types_certified`; the contractor's `state_code` equals the state code of the premises reached through the claim's enrollment; `open_dispatch_count` is strictly less than `max_open_dispatches`; and, when the product's `permit_covered` is true, `permit_pull_authorized` is true. Candidates are enumerated in `created_at` ascending order before scoring. If the filter empties the pool, the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CONTRACTOR` — the selector **never** relaxes a condition to find someone. The visit window is deliberately not one of the filter's conditions. The winner is settled on score alone and only then tested for a window conflict, and a conflict on that winner refuses the entire request with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT` rather than falling through to the runner-up: automatic selection finds the best contractor in the network, not the best one who happens to be free at that hour, and a caller whose preferred hour collides must move the window rather than expect the system to move the contractor.

Each surviving candidate is scored on three components. Capacity headroom is the floor of 100 multiplied by `max_open_dispatches` minus `open_dispatch_count`, divided by `max_open_dispatches` — an integer percentage of the book still free. The rate component is the larger of 0 and 100 minus the floor of `hourly_rate_cents` divided by 200, so cheaper labour scores higher and any rate at or above 20000 cents scores zero. The third component is `sla_compliance_pct` used directly. These three are combined by weights that **depend on the claim's urgency**, and the two weightings are genuinely different policies rather than two spellings of one. For an `URGENT` or `ROUTINE` claim, the weights are 0.45 on `sla_compliance_pct`, 0.35 on capacity headroom and 0.20 on the rate component: cost and availability dominate, so a cheap, idle contractor with a mediocre record wins. For an `EMERGENCY` claim the weights become 0.70, 0.25 and 0.05: reliability dominates, and the expensive contractor with the best record wins **the very same candidate pool**. The weighted sum is rounded half up to exactly two fractional digits and stored as `selection_score`; the two integer components are floored, never rounded. Both of those components are read from the contractor as it stands at the moment of the request and before the new dispatch exists, and capacity headroom in particular is computed from the contractor's live `open_dispatch_count` — which on a supersede still counts the dispatch being replaced, even though the capacity *gate* discounts it. The score a re-dispatch records can therefore be lower than the score the very same contractor would earn on a first dispatch.

The worked comparison shows the reversal directly. Contractor A, with `sla_compliance_pct` 92, `hourly_rate_cents` 18000 and 0 of 5 dispatches open, has capacity headroom 100 and a rate component of 10, scoring 78.4 under the standard weights and 89.9 under the emergency weights. Contractor B, with `sla_compliance_pct` 76, `hourly_rate_cents` 6000 and 0 of 4 open, has capacity headroom 100 and a rate component of 70, scoring 83.2 standard and 81.7 emergency. A `ROUTINE` claim therefore selects B, while an `EMERGENCY` claim over the identical pool selects A. Ties are broken deterministically: highest `selection_score` first, then the lower `hourly_rate_cents`, then the earlier `created_at`. `selection_score` is recorded on manual dispatches too, where it holds the score the named contractor **would** have received — the number is informational there, not a gate.

**The appointment window** is derived, not supplied. `window_end` is `window_start` advanced by a fixed visit duration that depends on the claim's `line_type`: 240 minutes for `WATER_SERVICE_LINE`, 300 for `SEWER_LATERAL`, 120 for `INTERIOR_PLUMBING` and 180 for `EXTERIOR_ELECTRIC`. The window is the half-open interval from `window_start` up to but not including `window_end`. Nothing constrains `window_start` relative to the logical clock: a window wholly in the past is admitted exactly as a future one is, so recording a visit that has already happened is a legitimate use of the operation. A contractor cannot be double-booked, and the overlap test is padded on both sides by a 45-minute travel buffer: the candidate window conflicts with an existing one when the candidate's `window_start` is earlier than the existing `window_end` plus the buffer **and** the existing `window_start` is earlier than the candidate's `window_end` plus the buffer. Only dispatches that are `ASSIGNED`, `ACCEPTED` or `ON_SITE` block a window; one that is `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED`, `SLA_BREACHED` or `SUPERSEDED` never does. Because of the buffer, two windows that merely touch at `window_end` still conflict, and the earliest non-conflicting start is exactly `window_end` plus 45 minutes. Concretely, an existing `WATER_SERVICE_LINE` dispatch starting at `2026-09-10T09:00:00.000Z` ends at `2026-09-10T13:00:00.000Z`; a second dispatch for the same contractor starting at `2026-09-10T13:44:00.000Z` is refused with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, while one starting at `2026-09-10T13:45:00.000Z` is admitted.

**The response deadline** is set once per claim and never renegotiated. On the claim's *first* dispatch, the claim's `sla_deadline_at` is computed as the claim's `reported_at` advanced by the hours the urgency demands — 4 hours for `EMERGENCY`, 24 for `URGENT`, 72 for `ROUTINE` — and the dispatch's own `sla_deadline_at` is stamped with that same instant. Every later dispatch on the claim, **including one created through `supersedes_dispatch_id`, inherits that instant unchanged**: re-dispatching a stalled job never buys the network more time. An `EMERGENCY` claim reported at `2026-09-10T08:00:00.000Z` carries a deadline of `2026-09-10T12:00:00.000Z`, and a supersede created at `2026-09-10T11:30:00.000Z` still carries `2026-09-10T12:00:00.000Z`, so an overnight run at exactly `2026-09-10T12:00:00.000Z` breaches it.

Creating a dispatch has consequences in three directions. The dispatch itself opens `ASSIGNED` with `version` 1 and `assigned_at` from the logical clock. The claim moves `APPROVED → DISPATCHED` and its `version` rises; a supersede that leaves the claim already `DISPATCHED` is the self-loop `DISPATCHED → DISPATCHED`, which is a legitimate transition rather than a no-op. The selected contractor's `open_dispatch_count` rises, and the policy's `annual_benefit_reserved_cents` rises by the provisional `estimated_benefit_cents` computed at assignment. That figure is a reported exposure and nothing more: as §2.10 insists, it narrows no pool, so it does **not** stop two jobs open on one policy from being sized against the same undiminished remaining cap, and a second dispatch is never refused merely because the first one's estimate is still outstanding. When `supersedes_dispatch_id` was supplied, the superseded dispatch moves to `SUPERSEDED` with `SUPERSEDED_BY_REDISPATCH` as its cancellation reason, **its** reservation falls away with it, and **its** contractor's `open_dispatch_count` falls — which is a different contractor from the new one whenever the redispatch was the point of the exercise. The outgoing dispatch is discounted **in advance** rather than after the fact: for the whole of the incoming request it is treated as though it had already left the open set, so its slot does not count against the incoming contractor's `max_open_dispatches` and its booked window does not conflict with the new one. A contractor whose book is exactly full can therefore be re-dispatched over one of its own bookings, and a replacement window that overlaps nothing but the window being replaced is admitted. Everything else on the outgoing record is left standing — its `window_start`, `window_end`, `sla_deadline_at`, `selection_score` and `estimated_benefit_cents` are all preserved exactly as they were — so a superseded dispatch remains a faithful account of the assignment that was withdrawn.

A contractor works the job by posting events against the dispatch, and only the `NetworkContractor` whose own identity matches the dispatch's `contractor_id` may do so; anyone else is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Acceptance moves `ASSIGNED → ACCEPTED`, stamping `responded_at` from the logical clock and computing `response_minutes` as the floored whole minutes between `assigned_at` and `responded_at`. Arrival moves `ACCEPTED → ON_SITE`. An event whose state precondition is unmet — accepting a dispatch that is not `ASSIGNED`, arriving at one that is not `ACCEPTED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DISPATCH_STATE`. All three event types share a single declared body: beyond the required `event_type`, every field the completion of §3.7 uses is optional on any event, and so is a free-text `note` of at most 1000 characters. A field an event type has no use for — an invoice line on an `ACCEPT`, say — is accepted and then ignored rather than refused, and `note` is ignored on all three: no field of the dispatch representation carries it back, so an accepted event with a note and one without are indistinguishable in the response. A key the body does not declare at all remains a schema failure.

### 3.7. Completion: Invoice Lines, the Benefit Waterfall and Cap Exhaustion

Completion is the request that spends money, and it is therefore the request with the most consequences. The contractor posts a completion event carrying the invoice lines: `labor_cents`, `materials_cents`, `permit_fee_cents`, `restoration_cents`, together with the `restoration_scope` actually performed and, when a municipal permit was pulled, the `permit_number`. Every one of those inputs is optional. An omitted money line is read as zero rather than as missing information, all four must be non-negative integers when they are sent, and `permit_number` is a string of 1 to 60 characters recorded exactly as sent even on an invoice that carries no permit fee at all. A completion that supplies nothing but its `event_type` is therefore a legal completion: it settles a zero invoice, pays a zero benefit and leaves the homeowner owing nothing. Completion is admissible from `ACCEPTED` or `ON_SITE` — a contractor may complete a job without having separately reported arrival — and from anywhere else it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DISPATCH_STATE`. Two invoice-level checks precede the arithmetic. A `permit_fee_cents` greater than zero with no `permit_number` supplied is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_REQUIRED`: the programme will not reimburse a permit fee that cannot be evidenced. And a `restoration_scope` richer than the product's `restoration_covered_scope`, in the ordering `NONE` below `BASIC_BACKFILL` below `FULL_HARDSCAPE`, is refused with `ERR_RESTORATION_SCOPE_NOT_COVERED`.

`invoice_total_cents` is simply the sum of the four lines, and it is the whole invoice regardless of what the warranty covers. What the warranty pays is decided by a **waterfall against a single pool**. Two of the four lines are first trimmed to what the product actually covers. The covered restoration is the lesser of `restoration_cents` and the allowance carried by the `restoration_scope` the completion **actually reports** — 0 cents for `NONE`, 50000 for `BASIC_BACKFILL`, 250000 for `FULL_HARDSCAPE`. The product's `restoration_covered_scope` is the ceiling on which scopes may be reported at all, not the allowance that gets spent, and the distinction is observable: a product filed for `FULL_HARDSCAPE` whose completion reports only `BASIC_BACKFILL` is trimmed to that scope's 50000, not to the 250000 the filing would have permitted. A completion that omits `restoration_scope` altogether is read as `NONE` and therefore covers no restoration whatever `restoration_cents` says. The covered permit is `permit_fee_cents` when the product's `permit_covered` is true, and 0 otherwise.

The pool is then the **smaller of two independent allowances**. The occurrence allowance is the product's `per_occurrence_cap_cents` less the benefit already posted against every claim sharing this claim's `occurrence_root_claim_id` — which is exactly why the recurrence chain matters — counting, as §2.10 defines that sum, only the rows frozen under the policy's current `cap_year_start`, so a cap-year rollover replenishes an old occurrence group's allowance exactly as it replenishes the annual one. The annual allowance is the product's `annual_cap_cents` less the policy's `annual_benefit_used_cents` for the current cap year. Neither allowance is reduced by `annual_benefit_reserved_cents`; outstanding estimates do not narrow the pool. Whichever is smaller is the pool — and a pool of zero, which is what an exhausted cap leaves, allocates nothing to any bucket and hands the homeowner the entire invoice. The four buckets are filled from it **strictly in the order labour, materials, permit, restoration**, each bucket receiving the lesser of what it asked for and what remains. `benefit_paid_cents` is the sum of the allocations, and `customer_responsibility_cents` is `invoice_total_cents` less `benefit_paid_cents`. The ordering is not cosmetic: an invoice that exhausts the pool during labour leaves the homeowner paying every later bucket in full, whereas the same total spread differently would leave a different balance. All of this is integer-cent arithmetic and no rounding occurs, because every input is already an integer.

A worked settlement: a product with `per_occurrence_cap_cents` 800000, `annual_cap_cents` 1000000, `permit_covered` true and `restoration_covered_scope` `BASIC_BACKFILL`, against an invoice of 260000 labour, 175000 materials, 42000 permit fee and 90000 restoration reported at scope `BASIC_BACKFILL`. The restoration is trimmed to the 50000 allowance that reported scope carries; the permit passes through in full; the pool is 800000; so the allocations are 260000, 175000, 42000 and 50000, giving `benefit_paid_cents` 527000 against an `invoice_total_cents` of 567000, leaving `customer_responsibility_cents` of 40000 — the 40000 of restoration above the allowance.

The **provisional variant** of the same settlement runs much earlier, at dispatch creation, when no invoice exists at all. It reserves against the pool using the assigned contractor's own rate card rather than actual lines: `estimated_benefit_cents` is the lesser of the pool and a fixed hours estimate for the claim's `line_type` multiplied by the contractor's `hourly_rate_cents`, plus a fixed materials estimate for that line type. The hour estimates are 6 for `WATER_SERVICE_LINE`, 8 for `SEWER_LATERAL`, 3 for `INTERIOR_PLUMBING` and 4 for `EXTERIOR_ELECTRIC`; the materials estimates are 120000, 200000, 45000 and 80000 cents respectively. The provisional variant has **no permit and no restoration component at all** — those costs are unknowable at assignment. The two variants routinely disagree, and they are supposed to: the same water-service job estimated at 6 hours times 18000 cents plus 120000, or 228000, settles finally at 527000, and under a cheaper contractor at 6000 cents an hour the estimate would have been 156000 against the identical final figure.

Because the estimate is only ever a reported exposure, the two figures never meet, and the reconciliation a reader might expect between them does not exist. The pool the waterfall is filled against is read while the dispatch is still open, and the reservation is not released by a step of its own: it simply ceases to exist the instant the dispatch's status becomes `COMPLETED`, because `annual_benefit_reserved_cents` is a live view over the policy's open dispatches rather than a stored balance that anyone debits. `annual_benefit_used_cents` moves only by the benefit row the settlement appends. The divergence between estimate and settlement therefore cannot leak into the used figure under any ordering whatever — the used figure is defined by posted charges alone — and a second dispatch still open on the same policy is sized against the same pool this one saw, reduced only by what this one actually posted.

Completion's full cascade, in order, is: the dispatch stores its invoice lines and settlement figures and moves to `COMPLETED`; the contractor's `open_dispatch_count` falls; the reservation falls away with the same status change; one benefit ledger row is appended for `benefit_paid_cents`, carrying the policy's `cap_year_start` frozen onto the row, **and only when that figure is strictly positive** — a settlement that allocates nothing, whether because the pool was exhausted or because the whole invoice fell outside what the product covers, appends no ledger row at all and leaves `annual_benefit_used_cents` exactly where it stood; the policy's `annual_benefit_used_cents` is recomputed; the claim's own `benefit_paid_cents` and `occurrence_used_cents` are recomputed; and the claim advances `DISPATCHED → WORK_COMPLETE → PAID`. `WORK_COMPLETE` is **transient within the single request** — it exists so the ledger posting has a well-defined state to happen in, and a completing dispatch always leaves its claim in `PAID`. No caller ever observes a claim resting in `WORK_COMPLETE`.

If the newly posted charge brings `annual_benefit_used_cents` to or past `annual_cap_cents`, the cap dimension moves `WITHIN_CAP → ANNUAL_CAP_EXHAUSTED`, and that in turn denies **every remaining claim of that policy still sitting in `TRIAGE`**, each recording `CAP_EXHAUSTED` as its `denial_reason` and the logical clock as its `denied_at`. Those automatic denials leave `triage_decided_by_user_id` null, because no human decided them — a null decider on a denied claim is the durable signal that the cap, not an adjuster, closed the file. The comparison is inclusive: usage landing exactly on `annual_cap_cents` exhausts the cap, and one cent less leaves the dimension at `WITHIN_CAP`. Continuing the example, a first dispatch posting 527000 against a 1000000 annual cap leaves usage at 527000 and the dimension `WITHIN_CAP`; a second dispatch invoicing 400000 labour and 73000 materials finds an annual allowance of exactly 473000, pays exactly that, and brings usage to precisely 1000000, which exhausts the cap.

### 3.8. Denial, Appeal, Regulator Complaint and Remedy

A denial is not the end of a claim. The `Policyholder` — and only the policyholder whose own address the claim was filed against, anyone else being refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — may appeal a `DENIED` claim by supplying an `appeal_reason` of 1 to 1000 non-blank characters, which is the operation's only declared field and which, like the adjuster's `coverage_note`, is kept for the audit trail rather than published: no field of the claim representation carries it back. Two limits apply. The claim must have been appealed fewer than 2 times, or the request is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`. And the appeal must arrive within 30 days of `denied_at`; once the logical clock passes `denied_at` plus 30 days the request is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, the boundary being inclusive on the appellant's side — an appeal at exactly the 30-day instant is still accepted. An accepted appeal moves the claim `DENIED → APPEAL_OPEN`, stamps `appeal_opened_at`, sets `appeal_source` to `POLICYHOLDER`, and increments `appeal_count`. It then cascades outward in a fixed order: the policy's `retention_hold` becomes true and its `version` rises, and the address's `suppression_reason` is re-derived to reflect the open appeal, with that address's `version` rising too. The practical effect is that a household in dispute is neither marketed to nor allowed to be cancelled out from under the dispute.

An `AppealsOfficer` decides the appeal with a `decision` and a `rationale` of 1 to 1000 non-blank characters — both required, and the rationale again recorded for the audit trail rather than carried in any response field — and one rule overrides everything else: **the officer may not be the adjuster who denied the claim**. A caller whose `X-User-ID` equals the claim's `triage_decided_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and this is checked ahead of any state or business condition. Three decisions are available. An overturn moves `APPEAL_OPEN → APPROVED` and **clears both `denial_reason` and `denied_at`**, so the claim becomes indistinguishable from one approved at triage and is dispatchable in the ordinary way. A remand moves `APPEAL_OPEN → DENIED` and **refreshes `denied_at` to the logical clock**, which restarts the 30-day appeal window from scratch — the cycle `DENIED → APPEAL_OPEN → DENIED` is therefore genuinely repeatable, bounded only by the two-appeal limit. An upheld denial moves `APPEAL_OPEN → APPEAL_UPHELD_DENIAL`. Every decision records `appeal_decided_by_user_id`, and every decision re-derives the policy's `retention_hold` — which returns to false only when no other claim on that policy is still `APPEAL_OPEN` — and the address's suppression along with it.

Claims that are simply left alone eventually close. The overnight run moves a `DENIED` claim to `CLOSED` once `denied_at` plus 30 days is strictly behind the logical clock. `CLOSED` is a lockdown: an appeal against a closed claim is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`, however sympathetic the circumstances.

**Regulator complaints** enter through `RegulatoryAffairs`, who records the `partner_id` whose regulator forwarded it, the `regulator` itself — one of `PUC`, `AG` or `BBB` — and the `allegation`, of 1 to 1000 non-blank characters. The complaint may optionally cite a `claim_id`, an `enrollment_id`, or both. It opens `OPEN` with `version` 1, `escalation_count` 0, and `received_at` from the logical clock.

The response deadline depends on the regulator and on how bad the underlying matter is. The base window is 10 days for `PUC`, 15 for `AG` and 21 for `BBB`. When the complaint cites a claim that is currently `DENIED` or `APPEAL_UPHELD_DENIAL`, the window becomes the **lesser** of the base days and 5 — the expedited path is a floor rather than a per-regulator subtraction, so a `BBB` complaint about a denied claim collapses from 21 days to 5 exactly as a `PUC` complaint collapses from 10 to 5. "Currently" means as the claim stood when the complaint arrived, before intake's own reopen touches it: the deadline is computed from the pre-intake status, so a complaint citing a `DENIED` claim earns the expedited window even though the very same request then drives that claim to `APPEAL_OPEN`. `response_due_at` is `received_at` advanced by the effective days. A `PUC` complaint received at `2026-09-20T00:00:00.000Z` citing nothing is due `2026-09-30T00:00:00.000Z`; the same complaint citing a `DENIED` claim is due `2026-09-25T00:00:00.000Z`. `days_overdue` is the floored whole days from `response_due_at` to the logical clock, floored at zero, and is zero once a response is filed.

Intake also reaches into the claim and the address. When the cited `claim_id` names a claim in `DENIED` **or in the otherwise terminal `APPEAL_UPHELD_DENIAL`**, that claim is driven to `APPEAL_OPEN` with `appeal_source` `REGULATOR`, `appeal_opened_at` stamped and `appeal_count` incremented, and the `retention_hold` and suppression cascade follows exactly as for a policyholder appeal. A regulator reopens a claim **even past the 30-day appeal window, even from `APPEAL_UPHELD_DENIAL`, and even when `appeal_count` has already reached the two-appeal limit** — the limits that bind a homeowner do not bind a regulator, and a regulator-driven reopen pushes `appeal_count` past 2 rather than being refused with `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`. A cited claim in any other status is left exactly as it is: a denial the overnight run has already moved to `CLOSED` is **not** reopened by a complaint, and neither is a claim in `TRIAGE`, `APPROVED`, `DISPATCHED` or `PAID`. Separately, when an `enrollment_id` is cited, that policy's address has `regulator_hold` set to true — and, being a guarded write in the sense of §2.9, its `version` is incremented only when the flag was not already true.

Filing the **response** requires `response_summary`, the `disposition` reached — either `SUBSTANTIATED` or `UNSUBSTANTIATED` — a `remedy_type`, and `expected_complaint_version`, plus `remedy_amount_cents` when the remedy moves money. A response is admissible from `OPEN`, `OVERDUE` or `ESCALATED`; from a closed state it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COMPLAINT_STATE`. The remedy must have something to act on: `BENEFIT_REVERSAL` with no cited claim, or `PREMIUM_REFUND` or `REINSTATEMENT` with no cited policy, is refused with `ERR_REMEDY_TARGET_MISSING`. And an `UNSUBSTANTIATED` finding may not move money at all — any `remedy_type` other than `NONE` or `APOLOGY_ONLY` alongside that disposition is refused with `ERR_REMEDY_NOT_PERMITTED`. Those two refusals share tier 13 of §2.8 and the target check is evaluated first, so an `UNSUBSTANTIATED` response asking for a `BENEFIT_REVERSAL` on a complaint that cites no claim reports `ERR_REMEDY_TARGET_MISSING`, not `ERR_REMEDY_NOT_PERMITTED`. `remedy_amount_cents` is recorded on the complaint exactly as supplied whatever the remedy type — a remedy that has no use for an amount still echoes one back when the caller sent it — and is null when the field was omitted.

Each remedy has its own compensating consequences. `NONE` and `APOLOGY_ONLY` change nothing but the record. `BENEFIT_REVERSAL` appends a negative benefit ledger row for `remedy_amount_cents`, carrying the policy's `cap_year_start`, which lowers `annual_benefit_used_cents` and may return the cap dimension `ANNUAL_CAP_EXHAUSTED → WITHIN_CAP` — a reversal genuinely gives cap room back. It may not reverse more than was paid: a `remedy_amount_cents` exceeding the cited claim's `benefit_paid_cents` is refused with `ERR_REVERSAL_EXCEEDS_BENEFIT`, and because that figure is the unfiltered across-all-cap-years sum of §2.10, a charge posted in an earlier cap year is still reversible in full. The row is booked on the logical clock's calendar date and frozen with the policy's **current** `cap_year_start`, which means a reversal filed after a rollover gives its cap room back to the new year rather than to the year the original charge sat in. What the reversal does not do is undo the denials the exhaustion caused: claims already denied with `CAP_EXHAUSTED` stay denied, and the room returned merely lets fresh claims be approved. `PREMIUM_REFUND` appends a negative cash proration-refund row — booked, again, on the clock's own date — and moves the billing dimension to `REFUND_ISSUED`. No ceiling of any kind is imposed on the refunded amount: it is not measured against `premium_collected_cents`, and it applies under either billing mode, so a bill-inserted policy that never prepaid a penny can still be given `REFUND_ISSUED`. `REINSTATEMENT` is the fullest reversal: the coverage dimension returns `CANCELLED → ACTIVE`, the billing dimension returns to `CURRENT`, a positive refund-clawback cash row is appended for the absolute value of whatever was paid out at cancellation — `refund_cents` or `credit_cents`, whichever applied — `cancellation_effective_date`, `cancellation_reason`, `refund_cents`, `credit_cents` and `cancellation_fee_cents` are all cleared to null, and `term_end` is restored to the value it held before cancellation, namely twelve calendar months after `term_start` plus a further twelve months for each completed renewal. The net cash position must land back exactly where it was before the cancellation; a reinstatement that leaves cash unaccounted for has not been performed correctly. Three details bound that reversal. The coverage edge is the single one named: only a `CANCELLED` policy is returned to `ACTIVE`, and a policy in any other coverage state — `LAPSED` above all, which no remedy rescues — keeps the coverage status it holds, while the billing dimension, the term and the five cancellation fields are reset either way. The clawback row is appended only when something actually went out: a cancellation that settled at zero leaves no `REFUND_CLAWBACK` row at all, and where a row is appended its amount is the sum of the absolute values of `refund_cents` and `credit_cents`, which is just the one that applied because cancellation leaves the other at zero. And the reset is confined to what is listed: `past_due_since` is left standing, and neither `retention_hold` nor `cap_status` is recomputed, so a reinstated policy can perfectly well be `ACTIVE` and `CURRENT` while still carrying an old arrears anchor.

Every response also re-derives `regulator_hold` and the standing `suppression_reason` on the affected addresses — the address of the cited policy and the address reached through the cited claim's own policy, both of them when the complaint cites both — and closes the complaint as `CLOSED_SUBSTANTIATED` or `CLOSED_UNSUBSTANTIATED`. That recompute counts only complaints that cite an `enrollment_id`: a complaint citing a claim and nothing else never holds a `regulator_hold` in the first place and never keeps one alive. As a §2.9 recompute it writes the premises only when the flag genuinely flips, so a response that leaves another open complaint standing on the same address moves no `version` there at all.

Closure is not always final. A complaint closed as `CLOSED_UNSUBSTANTIATED` may be escalated by the regulator, and `RegulatoryAffairs` records that with an `escalation_note`. Escalation moves `CLOSED_UNSUBSTANTIATED → ESCALATED`, increments `escalation_count`, and **clears `responded_at`, `response_summary`, `disposition`, `remedy_type` and `remedy_amount_cents` all back to null**, so the file genuinely reopens rather than carrying a stale finding. The `escalation_note` is required and non-blank but is kept for the audit trail rather than published: no field of the complaint representation carries it back, so an escalation is indistinguishable in the response from any other with a different note. It also restores `regulator_hold` on the address when the complaint cites a policy, incrementing that address's `version` only if the flag was not already true; it does **not** reopen the cited claim's appeal the way intake does. The complaint can then be responded to again, which is what makes `OPEN → CLOSED_UNSUBSTANTIATED → ESCALATED → CLOSED_*` a real cycle. `CLOSED_SUBSTANTIATED` is the one genuinely terminal outcome: an admission cannot be un-admitted. The overnight run participates in the same lifecycle from the other side, moving an `OPEN` complaint whose `response_due_at` has arrived to `OVERDUE`, and an `OVERDUE` complaint whose `days_overdue` has reached 5 to `ESCALATED` with `escalation_count` incremented.

### 3.9. The Nightly Operations Run

Everything in this system that happens because time passed happens in one place. There is no background scheduler and no wall clock: an `OperationsAdmin` or `PlatformAdmin` invokes the overnight run explicitly, and it performs every deadline-driven transition the logical clock has made due. The run takes no inputs. Because several phases feed each other, the phases execute in a **fixed order**, and within each phase the affected records are processed in `created_at` ascending order so that two runs over identical data produce identical results.

**Phase 1 — response breaches.** Every dispatch still `ASSIGNED` whose `sla_deadline_at` is on or before the logical clock moves `ASSIGNED → SLA_BREACHED`, and its contractor's `sla_breach_count` is incremented. The comparison is inclusive: a run at exactly `sla_deadline_at` breaches, and a run one second earlier does not. A dispatch that has already been accepted is past the response deadline in the sense that matters and is never breached by this phase.

**Phase 2 — probation and its fallout.** Every contractor whose `sla_breach_count` has reached 2 moves `ACTIVE → PROBATION`. This is why phase 1 runs first: breaches recorded moments ago count toward the threshold in the very same run. Probation then cancels that contractor's remaining `ASSIGNED` dispatches, each recording `CONTRACTOR_PROBATION` as its cancellation reason, and each such cancellation reverts its claim `DISPATCHED → APPROVED` — but only once the claim has no dispatch left in `ASSIGNED`, `ACCEPTED` or `ON_SITE`, so a claim that still has another contractor working it stays `DISPATCHED`. Releasing the cancelled dispatch's `estimated_benefit_cents` from `annual_benefit_reserved_cents` is **not** conditional on that revert: cancellation alone takes the dispatch out of the open set, so the policy's reported exposure falls whether or not the claim moved with it, and — because a reservation narrows no pool to begin with, as §2.10 sets out — what the release restores is the accuracy of a reported figure rather than any spendable cap room. A claim that did revert is dispatchable again from `APPROVED`.

**Phase 3 — regulator deadlines.** Every complaint in `OPEN` whose `response_due_at` has arrived moves `OPEN → OVERDUE` with `days_overdue` recomputed, and every complaint already `OVERDUE` whose `days_overdue` has reached 5 moves `OVERDUE → ESCALATED` with `escalation_count` incremented. Both hops can occur to the same complaint in one run when it is far enough past due.

**Phase 4 — the coverage clock and arrears.** Three transitions on the coverage dimension, evaluated in this order. Policies in `PENDING_WAITING_PERIOD` whose `coverage_effective_at` has been reached move `PENDING_WAITING_PERIOD → ACTIVE`. Policies that are `ACTIVE` while their billing dimension is `PAST_DUE`, and whose `past_due_since` is at least 30 days behind the clock's date, move `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED_NONPAY`. Policies already `SUSPENDED_NONPAY` whose `past_due_since` is at least 60 days behind move `SUSPENDED_NONPAY → LAPSED`, clearing `retention_hold`. Because the thresholds are 30 and 60 days from the same anchor, a policy that has been in arrears long enough may be suspended in one run and lapsed in a later one, but never both in the same run. Because each policy takes at most one of the three transitions per run, "later" can mean the very next invocation and no clock movement at all: a policy 70 days past its arrears anchor is suspended by one run and lapsed by the run immediately after it.

**Phase 5 — closing stale denials.** Every claim in `DENIED` whose `denied_at` plus 30 days is strictly behind the logical clock moves `DENIED → CLOSED`.

**Phase 6 — renewal and cap-year rollover.** The phase considers only policies whose coverage is still live — `PENDING_WAITING_PERIOD`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED_NONPAY` — and passes over a `CANCELLED` or `LAPSED` policy entirely, however far behind the clock its `term_end` may be; a policy that ended its coverage never renews and never rolls its cap year again. For every live policy whose `term_end` has arrived, the noticed price is applied or it is not. When `renewal_notice_status` is `SENT` and the notice was compliant — meaning its `renewal_effective_date` matches the `term_end` now reached, the lead time its tier demanded having been enforced when the notice was issued rather than re-tested here against the present clock — `monthly_price_cents` is replaced by `renewal_price_cents` and `renewal_notice_status` becomes `APPLIED`. When no such notice exists, `monthly_price_cents` is **kept unchanged** and `renewal_notice_status` becomes `NOT_APPLIED_NO_COMPLIANT_NOTICE`: a missing or defective notice never blocks the renewal itself, it only forbids the increase. Either way `term_end` and `cap_year_start` each advance by exactly one year, `renewal_count` is incremented, and the cap dimension returns to `WITHIN_CAP`. Advancing `cap_year_start` is what resets the benefit cap, and it does so without deleting anything: because every benefit ledger row froze the `cap_year_start` it was booked under, moving the policy's `cap_year_start` forward simply drops all of the prior year's rows out of the aggregation, and `annual_benefit_used_cents` falls to zero of its own accord. Membership of a cap year is decided by that frozen field alone and never by the row's `effective_date`: a row counts toward the policy's usage exactly when its own `cap_year_start` string equals the policy's current one, so a benefit row booked before the rollover stays in the old year however late its `effective_date` falls, and a row booked after it belongs to the new year even if it settles work done under the old.

The run's response is not an entity but a purpose-built audit record of what it did, and it names every affected record so that a caller can verify the run rather than trust it. It carries `ran_at`, the instant the run executed, and then eleven identifier lists, each in the order its phase processed them: `sla_breached_dispatch_ids`, `probated_contractor_ids`, `cancelled_dispatch_ids`, `overdue_complaint_ids`, `escalated_complaint_ids`, `activated_enrollment_ids`, `suspended_enrollment_ids`, `lapsed_enrollment_ids`, `closed_claim_ids`, `renewed_enrollment_ids` and `cap_year_reset_enrollment_ids`. A phase that had nothing to do contributes an empty list rather than being omitted. A run over a system with nothing due is legal, changes nothing, and returns eleven empty lists. Two of the eleven always agree, because phase 6 renews a term and rolls the cap year in one indivisible step: `renewed_enrollment_ids` and `cap_year_reset_enrollment_ids` are always the same list in the same order.

Every phase is guarded by a condition that its own action falsifies, so no record is processed twice by the same phase within a run, and once everything the clock has made due has actually been done, a further invocation changes nothing and returns eleven empty lists. That is a narrower promise than "the second of two runs is always empty", and the difference matters, because two phases advance a record one step at a time rather than all the way to the present. Phase 4 applies at most one coverage transition per policy per run, so a policy far enough into arrears is suspended by one run and lapsed by the next. Phase 6 advances `term_end` and `cap_year_start` by exactly one year per run, so a policy whose term ended two years ago renews once on each of two successive runs, and its `monthly_price_cents` is raised at most once because the first renewal consumes the notice. In both cases the clock has not moved and the second run is not empty; it is simply the next step of a backlog. Idempotency here means that a run repeated against a state it has already brought up to date is a no-op — not that any two consecutive runs must be.
