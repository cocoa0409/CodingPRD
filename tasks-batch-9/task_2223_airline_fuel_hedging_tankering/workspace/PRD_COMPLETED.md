# Airline Fuel Hedging & Tankering API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An airline's fuel bill is simultaneously its largest controllable cost and its least controllable market exposure. Two very different teams attack that exposure. Treasury attacks it financially, buying swaps, collars and call options against a forecast of how much jet fuel the fleet will actually burn, subject to board-approved limits on how much of that burn may be hedged, which dealers may face the airline, and how much earnings volatility the accounting treatment is permitted to leave on the income statement. Fuel operations attacks it physically, exploiting the fact that a gallon uplifted at a cheap station and carried to an expensive one can be cheaper than buying it on arrival — but only if the extra weight does not burn away the saving, and only if the origin station's tank farm can spare the gallons.

This API is the single system of record for both halves of that programme, and for the governance that binds them together. It carries the demand-side forecast, the hedge programme and its instrument book, counterparty credit and collateral, hedge-accounting designation and effectiveness testing, the monthly mark-to-market reporting cycle, station supply contracts and inventory, and the tankering directive lifecycle from proposal through reconciliation against flown data. Every one of those workflows is governed by a single fiscal-year hedging policy, and every breach of that policy either fails outright or is tolerated only under an explicitly approved, individually justified policy exception.

The design priorities are auditability and segregation of duties over convenience. The analyst who sizes a programme may not activate it; the accountant who computes a monthly revaluation may not publish it; the requester of an exception may not decide it. Derived figures — coverage percentages, credit utilisation, margin requirements, days of cover, tankering economics — are recomputed by the server from primary data rather than accepted from clients, and each recomputation is a versioned mutation with an audit stamp. The system is mounted under the `/fuel` prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. A request missing either header, or presenting an `X-User-Role` that is not one of the six roles defined below, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. A request whose role is well formed but is not among the roles authorised for the called endpoint is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.

The six roles are `RiskPolicyAdmin`, `FuelAnalyst`, `TreasuryRiskManager`, `HedgeAccountant`, `FuelOpsController` and `StationCoordinator`.

- A `RiskPolicyAdmin` owns reference data and the control surface for collateral and exceptions: hedging policies, counterparties, stations, supply contracts, manual collateral requirements, collateral posting and return, mark-to-market publication, and exception decisions.
- A `FuelAnalyst` builds the book from the demand side: consumption forecasts and their publication, programme creation, sizing and submission, instrument proposals, tankering proposals and revisions.
- A `TreasuryRiskManager` approves and transacts the book: returning, activating and closing programmes, executing and terminating trades, and recording counterparty credit reviews.
- A `HedgeAccountant` owns hedge accounting: designation, effectiveness testing, and opening and computing monthly mark-to-market runs. Notably, this role may not publish a run.
- A `FuelOpsController` owns tankering on the operational side: directive approval, rejection, revision requests, loading and reconciliation, plus flight-leg setup and network-wide inventory adjustments.
- A `StationCoordinator` is a single station's local fuel coordinator, with read-only visibility of stations, contracts, flight legs and directives, plus the ability to adjust inventory at its own station.

When `X-User-Role` is `RiskPolicyAdmin`, `FuelAnalyst`, `TreasuryRiskManager`, `HedgeAccountant` or `FuelOpsController`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `submitted_by_user_id` on a hedge programme, as `proposed_by_user_id` on an instrument or a tankering directive, as `approved_by_user_id` on a directive, as `tested_by_user_id` on an effectiveness test, and as `requested_by_user_id` or `decided_by_user_id` on a policy exception — but it is never compared to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `StationCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Station`; an inventory adjustment submitted by a `StationCoordinator` whose `X-User-ID` differs from the `Station` identified in the path is rejected with `ERR_STATION_NOT_OWNED`. A `FuelOpsController` performing the same adjustment is not subject to that ownership restriction and may adjust any station. The ownership test bites on the inventory adjustment alone: a `StationCoordinator`'s read access to stations, supply contracts, flight legs and tankering directives is not scoped to its own station, and its `X-User-ID` is never compared on those reads.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.4 rather than from wall-clock time.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field. An operation that creates a row answers HTTP 201; every other successful operation — every read, every list, every state transition and the clock capability — answers HTTP 200.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Filtering

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; it defaults to 10, may not be lower than 1, and may not be higher than 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip and defaults to 0. A `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer, a `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; either parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as absent and takes its default. Both are echoed back in the `meta` object of the envelope alongside `total_count` (the number of items matching the query before pagination) and `has_more` (true when items remain beyond the current window).

Unless a list endpoint states otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Three lists carry a domain-specific ordering instead: a station's supply contracts are ordered by `term_start_date` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; consumption forecasts are ordered by `period_start_month` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; flight legs are ordered by `departure_date` ascending and then `created_at` ascending.

Beyond pagination, list endpoints accept optional equality filters drawn from the fields of the listed resource. Collateral posting lists accept `status` and `origin`. The forecast list accepts `status`, `fleet_type`, `route_group` and `season`. The programme list accepts `policy_id`, `status` and `compliance_posture`. Instrument lists accept `trade_status`, `accounting_status`, `counterparty_id`, and — for the cross-programme list — `hedge_program_id` and `maturity_month`. The effectiveness-test list accepts `result`. Supply-contract lists accept `status`. Flight-leg lists accept `origin_station_id`, `destination_station_id`, `departure_date` and `fleet_type`. Directive lists accept `status`. The exception list accepts `hedge_program_id`, `hedge_instrument_id`, `tankering_directive_id`, `status`, `reason_code` and `origin`. A filter value that is not a member of the underlying enumeration is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, as is a `maturity_month` that is not a `YYYY-MM` month or a `departure_date` that is not an ISO 8601 date; the identifier filters (`policy_id`, `counterparty_id`, `hedge_program_id`, `hedge_instrument_id`, `tankering_directive_id`, `origin_station_id`, `destination_station_id`) are compared literally and are never checked for existence, so an unknown identifier simply matches nothing.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Stamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits. Almost every resource additionally carries an `updated_at` timestamp in the same form, refreshed on every mutation, and a `version` integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly one on every mutation the server applies to that row. Recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation: when a trade execution moves a counterparty's credit ledger, the counterparty's `version` advances and its `updated_at` is refreshed even though no client sent a field for it.

The single exception is the effectiveness test, which is an immutable audit record. An `EffectivenessTest` carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and the server never rewrites one once it has been created.

Two read operations are deliberately exempt from version accounting. Reading a single station recomputes `effective_inventory_gallons`, `days_cover` and `supply_posture` from live data, and reading a single flight leg recomputes `uplift_cap_gallons`, but neither read increments `version` or touches `updated_at`. Similarly, a supply contract's `status` is derived from the logical clock every time the contract is read or consulted by a validation, and that derivation is never itself a mutation.

All timestamps the server stamps — `published_at`, `executed_at`, `designated_at`, `posted_at`, `returned_at`, `computed_at`, `decided_at` — are read from a **logical clock** rather than from wall-clock time. That clock starts at the fixed instant `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and is moved only by an administrative test-driving capability that accepts a `current_utc` instant, available to all six roles; its `data` object carries the single field `current_utc`, echoing back verbatim the string the caller supplied, and it mutates no entity and no `version`. The server must never fall back to real wall-clock time, including before that capability has ever been invoked.

### 2.5. Validation Precedence

When a single request could fail several checks, the server evaluates them in a fixed order and reports the first failure it finds. Text order elsewhere in this document is not authoritative; this ladder is. The order is:

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` recognised, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorisation.** The caller's role is listed for the endpoint, otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Request shape.** On every write endpoint: every required input field present, every type correct, every enumeration member recognised, every numeric constraint honoured, the conditional strike fields consistent with the declared instrument type, and any required version-pinning field present. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). The free-text notes that accompany transitions — `return_note`, `termination_reason`, `reject_reason`, `revision_note`, `return_reason`, `decision_note` and an inventory adjustment's `reason` — are each a required non-empty string, and none of them is persisted on the mutated resource or echoed in its representation. A transition that declares no body field of its own — sizing, submission, activation, closure, forecast publication, trade execution, designation, run computation, run publication, and directive approval and loading — accepts an absent or empty JSON body; a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
4. **Path existence,** validated left to right. Every resource named by a path segment must exist; the first segment that does not resolve determines the error, which is one of `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COUNTERPARTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FORECAST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSTRUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COLLATERAL_POSTING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MTM_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FLIGHT_LEG_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DIRECTIVE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_EXCEPTION_NOT_FOUND` (404). A request for a supply contract under a station that does not exist therefore yields `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND` before the contract is ever looked up.
5. **Body foreign-key existence.** Every identifier supplied in a request body — `policy_id`, `counterparty_id`, `hedge_instrument_id`, `origin_station_id`, `destination_station_id`, `hedge_program_id` or `tankering_directive_id` — must resolve to an existing row, otherwise `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COUNTERPARTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSTRUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_DIRECTIVE_NOT_FOUND`, whichever matches the unresolved identifier (404). This applies to programme creation, manual collateral requirement creation under a counterparty, instrument proposal under a programme, flight-leg creation and policy-exception creation.
6. **Station ownership.** On a station inventory adjustment, a `StationCoordinator` caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the station's `id`, otherwise `ERR_STATION_NOT_OWNED` (403).
7. **State gates.** The target resource must be in a state from which the requested transition is declared, and any cross-entity state gate must hold — the owning programme must be `ACTIVE` for trade execution and for designation, and the owning trade must be `EXECUTED` for designation, for effectiveness testing and for collateral posting. Failures surface as `ERR_PROGRAM_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_INSTRUMENT_INVALID_TRADE_STATE`, `ERR_INSTRUMENT_INVALID_ACCOUNTING_STATE`, `ERR_DIRECTIVE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_POSTING_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_MTM_RUN_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_EXCEPTION_INVALID_STATE` or `ERR_FORECAST_INVALID_STATE` (409).
8. **Version pinning.** On the three parented creates described in §2.6, the supplied expectation must equal the parent's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
9. **Tankering weight and performance limits.** Uplift must not exceed the leg's `uplift_cap_gallons`, otherwise `ERR_TANKERING_WEIGHT_LIMIT` (422). This guards directive approval and directive reconciliation.
10. **Strictly positive uplift.** `requested_uplift_gallons` on directive creation and revision, and `actual_uplift_gallons` on reconciliation, must all be greater than zero, otherwise `ERR_ZERO_UPLIFT` (422). This guards directive creation, revision and reconciliation.
11. **Forecast period overlap** on forecast publication, otherwise `ERR_FORECAST_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409).
12. **Supply-contract term overlap** on contract creation, otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_TERM_OVERLAP` (409).
13. **Supply-contract changeover buffer** on contract creation, otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_BUFFER_VIOLATION` (409).
14. **Exactly one active supply contract** at each of the two stations a directive spans, otherwise `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_SUPPLY_CONTRACT` (409). This guards directive creation, directive revision and directive approval.
15. **Counterparty approval status** on trade execution, otherwise `ERR_COUNTERPARTY_NOT_APPROVED` (409).
16. **Permitted instrument type** on instrument proposal, otherwise `ERR_INSTRUMENT_TYPE_NOT_PERMITTED` (422).
17. **Uniqueness.** `policy_code`, `counterparty_code`, `iata_code`, `program_code`, a mark-to-market run's `period_month`, and the pair of `flight_number` with `departure_date` are each unique across the whole system; `contract_ref` is unique within a station. Violations yield `ERR_DUPLICATE_POLICY_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_COUNTERPARTY_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_STATION_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROGRAM_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_MTM_PERIOD`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_FLIGHT_LEG` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_CONTRACT_REF` (409) on policy, counterparty, station, programme, mark-to-market run, flight-leg and supply-contract creation respectively.
18. **Segregation of duties,** otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). This guards programme activation and both exception decisions, approval and denial.
19. **Coverage floor** on programme activation, otherwise `ERR_COVERAGE_FLOOR_NOT_MET` (422).
20. **Coverage ceiling** on trade execution, otherwise `ERR_COVERAGE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422).
21. **Approved exception required,** otherwise `ERR_EXCEPTION_REQUIRED` (422). This guards trade execution, where a single-counterparty concentration above the policy cap is tolerated only under an approved concentration exception, and designation, where re-designating a de-designated trade is tolerated only under an approved earnings-volatility exception.
22. **Counterparty credit limit** on trade execution, otherwise `ERR_COUNTERPARTY_CREDIT_EXCEEDED` (422).
23. **Collateral sufficiency** on collateral posting, otherwise `ERR_COLLATERAL_SHORTFALL` (422).
24. **Tankering economics** on directive approval, otherwise `ERR_TANKERING_NOT_ECONOMIC` (422).
25. **Origin station inventory,** otherwise `ERR_STATION_INVENTORY_SHORT` (422). This guards directive approval, where the origin station's `supply_posture` must not be `SHORT`, and directive loading, where the origin station's `inventory_gallons` must be at least the requested uplift.
26. **Forecast basis present** on programme sizing, otherwise `ERR_NO_FORECAST_BASIS` (422).
27. **No open instruments** on programme closure, otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_HAS_OPEN_INSTRUMENTS` (409).

The practical consequence of this ladder is that a weight-limit failure on directive approval is reported ahead of a supply-contract failure, which is reported ahead of an inventory failure, which is reported ahead of an economics failure — even though all four conditions are false in the same request.

### 2.6. Version Pinning on Parented Creates

Three create operations attach a child to a parent whose derived ledgers the client must have read in order to compute a sensible request. Each of them therefore requires a non-optional version expectation in the body, and each rejects a mismatch with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, HTTP 412. A version expectation that is absent altogether is a malformed body and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. No other endpoint accepts or requires a version field.

- Proposing a hedge instrument under a programme (`POST /fuel/hedge-programs/{id}/instruments`) requires `expected_hedge_program_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `HedgeProgram`. If the programme's current `version` differs, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). Adding a second instrument to a programme is perfectly legitimate, so no state precondition would catch this; the point of the pin is that the client's coverage-headroom arithmetic was computed against a `consumption_basis_gallons` or a `hedged_gallons` that has since moved.
- Raising a manual collateral requirement under a counterparty (`POST /fuel/counterparties/{id}/collateral-postings`) requires `expected_counterparty_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `Counterparty`. A mismatch with the counterparty's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). Several postings against one counterparty are legitimate, so again no state check would intervene; the `required_amount_usd` the client expects was derived from a `utilized_credit_usd` or `posted_collateral_usd` that has since changed.
- Proposing a tankering directive on a flight leg (`POST /fuel/flight-legs/{id}/tankering-directives`) requires `expected_flight_leg_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `FlightLeg`. A mismatch with the leg's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). Because a new directive legitimately supersedes any earlier undecided directive on the same leg, the create would otherwise succeed and silently discard a directive the client never saw.

### 2.7. Rounding Discipline

All arithmetic is decimal, and every rounding in this system is **round half up**. Where a formula rounds, it rounds exactly where stated and nowhere else; inserting an extra intermediate rounding is a defect, and so is deferring a stated one. Input scale is validated to match on the way in: a submitted per-gallon price may carry at most four decimal places, and a submitted percentage, US-dollar amount or `min_days_cover` at most two, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; gallon-, minute- and pound-valued inputs and an exception's `requested_limit_value` are accepted at any scale.

- **Whole gallons.** A programme's `consumption_basis_gallons` is rounded to zero decimal places, once, after the season-weighted products have been summed. The individual products are not rounded.
- **Prices, four places.** Every per-gallon price is carried to four decimal places: `into_plane_fee_usd_per_gal`, `index_price_usd_per_gal`, `differential_usd_per_gal`, `all_in_price_usd_per_gal`, `settlement_index_usd_per_gal`, `strike_usd_per_gal`, `cap_strike_usd_per_gal`, `floor_strike_usd_per_gal` and the two pinned directive prices.
- **Ratios, four places.** An effectiveness test's `period_ratio` and `cumulative_ratio` are rounded to four decimal places, and the payload ratio and penalty multiplier inside the tankering burn calculation are likewise rounded to four decimal places.
- **Gallon tenths.** A tankering burn penalty — `planned_burn_penalty_gallons` and `reconciled_burn_penalty_gallons` — is rounded to one decimal place.
- **Two places for everything else.** Percentages (`max_coverage_pct`, `min_coverage_pct`, `max_single_counterparty_pct`, `consortium_share_pct`, `target_coverage_pct`, `coverage_pct_notional`, `coverage_pct_delta`, `planned_coverage_pct_delta`, `utilization_pct`), delta-adjusted volumes, days of cover, uplift caps and every US-dollar amount are rounded to two decimal places.
- **Comparison bases differ from stored bases.** Two policy tests deliberately read an unrounded quantity rather than the stored, rounded field; those two cases are called out where they arise, in §3.4. Everywhere else, a threshold comparison reads the rounded value.

### 2.8. Ledger Integrity, Cascades and Compensating Reversals

No client ever writes a derived figure. Coverage percentages, credit utilisation, posted collateral, days of cover, margin requirements, mark-to-market values and tankering economics are all computed by the server from primary rows, and any operation that moves a primary row is responsible for propagating the consequence all the way through the graph in the same request. A cascade is not optional and it is not eventually consistent: by the time the response is returned, every affected parent, sibling and grandparent has been updated and each such row's `version` has been incremented by one.

Cascades in this system are strictly conditional and chained: a later hop fires only because an earlier hop actually changed something. If de-designating a trade leaves a programme's compliance posture unchanged, the hops that would have raised an exception and bumped the governing policy's open-exception counter simply do not run.

Several operations exist purely as **compensating actions** for an earlier operation, and their job is to restore a conserved quantity rather than to invent a new one. Terminating an executed trade restores `hedged_gallons`, `delta_adjusted_gallons`, `utilized_credit_usd` and `posted_collateral_usd` to what they were before execution. Returning collateral removes exactly what posting added. Reconciling a tankering directive trues the origin station's gallon ledger up so that the station's net debit for that directive equals the actual uplift and nothing else. Where a compensating action deliberately retains data for audit rather than zeroing it, that is stated explicitly at the point of use.

### 2.9. The Governing Policy

At most one `HedgePolicy` may be `ACTIVE` for any given `fiscal_year`, and the policy in force is the single row whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. Creating a new policy for a fiscal year that already has an `ACTIVE` policy sets the older row's `status` to `SUPERSEDED` and increments that row's `version` by one; the new row is created `ACTIVE` with `open_exception_count` at 0. A `SUPERSEDED` policy is never deleted and remains readable.

A hedge programme binds to a policy at creation via `policy_id` and is governed by that policy for its whole life, even after a newer policy has superseded it. Two limits, by contrast, are read from whichever policy is `ACTIVE` at the moment of evaluation — and, should several fiscal years each hold an `ACTIVE` policy, from the most recently created of them — rather than from a programme's bound policy: `max_tankering_uplift_gallons`, which caps a single leg's uplift, and `min_days_cover`, which classifies station supply posture. Where no `ACTIVE` policy exists at all, the uplift cap has no policy term to contribute and the station tier threshold is treated as zero, as described in §3.2 and §3.9.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Reference Data: Hedging Policy and Counterparty Credit Standing

A `RiskPolicyAdmin` opens the fiscal year by registering a hedging policy. The policy carries a `policy_code` of between 1 and 32 characters that must be unique across all policies whether they are still in force or already superseded, and a `fiscal_year` between 2020 and 2100 inclusive. Its risk appetite is expressed as a set of limits: `max_coverage_pct`, greater than zero and at most 100, is the ceiling on delta-adjusted hedge coverage for any programme it governs; `min_coverage_pct`, at least zero and strictly below `max_coverage_pct`, is the planned-coverage floor a programme must reach before it may be activated; `max_single_counterparty_pct`, greater than zero and at most 100, caps one dealer's executed notional as a percentage of a programme's consumption basis; `max_earnings_volatility_usd`, strictly greater than zero, is the absolute undesignated mark-to-market above which a programme is in breach; `max_tankering_uplift_gallons`, strictly greater than zero, is a hard ceiling on any single leg's uplift; and `min_days_cover`, strictly greater than zero, is the days-of-cover level below which a station is short. Two array limits complete the picture: `permitted_instrument_types` is a non-empty, duplicate-free subset of `SWAP`, `COLLAR` and `CALL_OPTION`, and `eligible_fleet_types` is a non-empty, duplicate-free subset of `NARROWBODY`, `WIDEBODY` and `REGIONAL` naming the fleets whose consumption forecasts are allowed into a sizing basis. The policy also exposes `open_exception_count`, which is the live number of policy exceptions still in `REQUESTED` status that reference a programme this policy governs. Reusing a `policy_code` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_POLICY_CODE`.

Counterparties are registered by the same role. A counterparty carries a `counterparty_code` of 1 to 16 characters, globally unique — a repeat yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_COUNTERPARTY_CODE` — a `legal_name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `credit_rating` drawn from `AA`, `A`, `BBB` and `BB`, and a `credit_limit_usd` strictly greater than zero. Registration normally produces a counterparty in `APPROVED` status, but a dealer registered at `BB` is created `SUSPENDED` immediately, on the theory that the airline will not face a name it has already downgraded. At creation `utilized_credit_usd`, `posted_collateral_usd`, `utilization_pct` and `open_margin_call_count` are all zero, `credit_posture` is `NORMAL`, and `last_review_note` is null until the first review is recorded.

Credit reviews may be recorded by either a `TreasuryRiskManager` or a `RiskPolicyAdmin`. A review supplies a new `credit_rating` and a `review_note`, and may optionally supply a replacement `credit_limit_usd`; the rating is always overwritten, the limit is overwritten only when supplied, and `last_review_note` takes the value of `review_note`. A review that lands on `BB` sets `status` to `SUSPENDED`; a review that lands on any rating above `BB` — that is, `BBB`, `A` or `AA` — sets `status` back to `APPROVED`, which is how a downgraded dealer is rehabilitated. Because the limit may have moved, the review recomputes `utilization_pct` and `credit_posture` and increments the counterparty's `version` by one.

Credit consumption is tracked continuously. A counterparty's `utilized_credit_usd` is the sum of `margin_requirement_usd` over its trades whose trade dimension reads `EXECUTED`; its `posted_collateral_usd` is the sum of `posted_amount_usd` over its collateral rows in `POSTED` status; and `open_margin_call_count` is the number of its collateral rows still sitting in `REQUIRED`. From those, `utilization_pct` is `utilized_credit_usd` divided by `credit_limit_usd`, multiplied by 100 and rounded to two decimal places. The rounded `utilization_pct` then selects a `credit_posture` from four tiers whose lower bounds are inclusive: below 70 percent the posture is `NORMAL`; from 70 percent up to but not including 90 percent it is `WATCH`; from 90 percent up to but not including 100 percent it is `CONSTRAINED`; and at 100 percent or above it is `BREACH`. Exactly 90.00 is therefore `CONSTRAINED`, and exactly 100.00 is `BREACH`. Utilisation and posture are refreshed on every event that moves `utilized_credit_usd` — trade execution, trade termination, the system settlement of a matured trade, and the counterparty refresh hop of a mark-to-market publication — as well as on every credit review.

### 3.2. Stations, Supply Contracts and the Inventory Ledger

A station is created by a `RiskPolicyAdmin` with an `iata_code` of exactly three uppercase letters, globally unique — a repeat yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_STATION_CODE` — a display `name` of 1 to 80 characters, an `into_plane_fee_usd_per_gal` of at least zero carried to four decimal places, and an `avg_daily_liftings_gallons` strictly greater than zero which serves as the denominator for days of cover. Consortium participation is optional: `consortium_member` defaults to false, `consortium_share_pct` (between 0 and 100 inclusive, two decimal places) defaults to 0, and `consortium_pool_gallons` (at least zero) defaults to 0. When `consortium_member` is false both of the consortium figures must be zero. A new station starts with `inventory_gallons` at 0, and its supply posture is computed immediately on creation.

Airline-owned gallons at a station move through exactly three doors: an explicit inventory adjustment, the loading of a tankering directive, and the reconciliation of a tankering directive. An adjustment is submitted by a `FuelOpsController` for any station or by a `StationCoordinator` for its own station, and supplies a `delta_gallons` amount together with a free-text `reason` recording whether this is a delivery, a meter correction or a pool restatement; a `consortium_pool_gallons` value of at least zero may optionally accompany it, in which case the station's pool figure is overwritten — and it is overwritten even at a station whose `consortium_member` is false, where the entitlement stays zero regardless. The delta may be negative, but the resulting `inventory_gallons` must remain at or above zero, and an adjustment that would drive it negative is a malformed request, reported after the station-ownership check of §2.5 has cleared. Each adjustment recomputes the station's derived supply fields and increments the station's `version` by one.

Supply posture is derived in three steps. First, the airline's consortium entitlement is zero when `consortium_member` is false, and otherwise is `consortium_share_pct` divided by 100, multiplied by `consortium_pool_gallons`, rounded to two decimal places. Second, `effective_inventory_gallons` is `inventory_gallons` plus that entitlement. Third, `days_cover` is `effective_inventory_gallons` divided by `avg_daily_liftings_gallons`, rounded to two decimal places. The rounded `days_cover` is then compared against the `min_days_cover` of the single `ACTIVE` hedging policy to set `supply_posture`: below that minimum the station is `SHORT`; from the minimum up to but not including twice the minimum it is `WATCH`; and at twice the minimum or above it is `NORMAL`. A station whose `days_cover` is exactly equal to `min_days_cover` is therefore `WATCH`, never `SHORT`. When no `ACTIVE` policy exists, the minimum is treated as zero and every station reads `NORMAL`.

Pricing at a station comes from its supply contracts, each added by a `RiskPolicyAdmin` under the station whose identifier the path supplies — a contract's `station_id` is always taken from the path and never from the body. A contract carries a `contract_ref` of 1 to 32 characters that must be unique within that station, an `index_basis` of `PLATTS_USGC`, `PLATTS_NWE` or `PLATTS_SGP`, an `index_price_usd_per_gal` strictly greater than zero at four decimal places, and a `differential_usd_per_gal` at four decimal places which may be negative provided the sum of index and differential remains strictly greater than zero. The term is a closed interval of ISO 8601 dates, `term_start_date` through `term_end_date`, both days inclusive, with the end no earlier than the start. Volume obligations are `min_monthly_gallons`, at least zero, and `max_monthly_gallons`, strictly greater than `min_monthly_gallons`. On creation the server computes `all_in_price_usd_per_gal` as the sum of `index_price_usd_per_gal`, `differential_usd_per_gal` and the station's `into_plane_fee_usd_per_gal`, rounded to four decimal places — this is the price every tankering calculation uses.

A contract's `status` is never stored by a client; it is derived from the logical clock each time the contract is read or consulted. Before `term_start_date` the contract is `SCHEDULED`; from `term_start_date` through `term_end_date` inclusive it is `ACTIVE`; after `term_end_date` it is `EXPIRED`.

Two temporal rules keep a station's contract history clean, and a duplicated `contract_ref` at the same station is separately rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CONTRACT_REF`. First, because contract terms are closed intervals, two contracts at the same station may not share even a single day; any intersection is rejected with `ERR_CONTRACT_TERM_OVERLAP`. Second, consecutive non-intersecting contracts at one station must be separated by a changeover buffer of at least a full week: the number of days from the earlier contract's `term_end_date` to the later contract's `term_start_date` must be at least seven, so a gap of exactly seven days is accepted while a gap of six days is rejected with `ERR_CONTRACT_BUFFER_VIOLATION`.

### 3.3. From Consumption Forecast to Activated Hedge Programme

Hedge sizing begins with demand. A `FuelAnalyst` records a consumption forecast naming a `fleet_type` of `NARROWBODY`, `WIDEBODY` or `REGIONAL`, a `route_group` of `DOMESTIC_SHORT`, `DOMESTIC_LONG`, `TRANSATLANTIC`, `TRANSPACIFIC` or `LATAM`, and a `season` of `WINTER`, `SPRING`, `SUMMER` or `FALL`. Its period is expressed as two `YYYY-MM` strings, and it is deliberately half-open: the forecast covers `period_start_month` inclusive up to but not including `period_end_month`, which must be strictly later than the start. The `forecast_gallons` figure is the unadjusted burn for the whole period and must be strictly greater than zero. A new forecast is created in `DRAFT` status with `published_at` null.

Publishing a forecast advances it `DRAFT → PUBLISHED`, stamps `published_at` with the logical clock instant and increments the forecast's `version` by one. Publication is the gate that lets a forecast enter a sizing basis, and it is guarded by an overlap rule that is enforced only against already-`PUBLISHED` siblings: no other `PUBLISHED` forecast with the same `fleet_type` *and* the same `route_group` may have a half-open month interval that intersects this one. Because the intervals are half-open, two forecasts that merely touch — where one's `period_end_month` equals the other's `period_start_month` — do not intersect and are both allowed. A genuine intersection is rejected with `ERR_FORECAST_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Drafts may overlap each other freely; the constraint bites only at publication. Publishing something already `PUBLISHED` is rejected with `ERR_FORECAST_INVALID_STATE`.

A hedge programme is created by a `FuelAnalyst` in `DRAFT` status, bound to a governing policy by `policy_id`, and identified by a globally unique `program_code` of 1 to 32 characters — a repeat yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROGRAM_CODE`. Unlike a forecast, a programme's month window is closed at both ends: `period_start_month` and `period_end_month` are both `YYYY-MM`, the end no earlier than the start, and both months are hedged. The programme names the `route_groups` in scope for sizing, a non-empty duplicate-free subset of the forecast route-group enumeration, and a `target_coverage_pct` greater than zero and at most 100 which is recorded for reporting and is never enforced against anything. At creation every computed ledger — `consumption_basis_gallons`, `hedged_gallons`, `delta_adjusted_gallons`, `coverage_pct_notional`, `coverage_pct_delta`, `planned_coverage_pct_delta`, `designated_mtm_usd`, `undesignated_mtm_usd`, `earnings_volatility_usd`, `open_instrument_count` and `return_count` — is zero, `compliance_posture` is `COMPLIANT`, and `submitted_by_user_id` is null.

**Sizing** is the analyst's act of freezing a demand basis onto the programme. It is valid as the transition `DRAFT → SIZING` and equally as a re-run `SIZING → SIZING`, and the first form additionally requires that the programme's bound policy still be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_INVALID_STATE`. A forecast enters the basis only if all four of the following hold: its `status` is `PUBLISHED`; its `fleet_type` is one of the governing policy's `eligible_fleet_types`; its `route_group` is one of the programme's `route_groups`; and its month window sits wholly inside the programme's. Because the programme window is inclusive at both ends while the forecast window excludes its own end month, containment is tested by requiring the forecast's `period_start_month` to be no earlier than the programme's `period_start_month` and the forecast's `period_end_month` to be no later than the month immediately after the programme's `period_end_month`. A forecast that only partially overlaps the programme window is excluded in its entirety; there is no pro-rating. If no forecast matches, sizing fails with `ERR_NO_FORECAST_BASIS`.

For the matched set, each forecast contributes its `forecast_gallons` multiplied by a seasonal weighting: `WINTER` counts at 1.00, `SPRING` at 0.95, `SUMMER` at 1.10 and `FALL` at 0.98. Those products are summed without any intermediate rounding, and the single sum is then rounded to whole gallons and written to `consumption_basis_gallons`, overwriting whatever a previous sizing run had left there. Sizing also recomputes the programme's three coverage percentages and increments the programme's `version` by one. Re-sizing is the only route by which a forecast published after an earlier sizing run enters an already-sized programme.

**Submission** advances a programme `SIZING → PENDING_APPROVAL` and requires that at least one instrument still be open on it — that is, `open_instrument_count` must be one or more, otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_INVALID_STATE`. Submission records the caller's `X-User-ID` in `submitted_by_user_id` and increments the programme's `version` by one. A `TreasuryRiskManager` reviewing the submission has two options. Returning the programme moves it `PENDING_APPROVAL → SIZING`, accompanies the move with a `return_note`, increments `return_count` by one, clears `submitted_by_user_id` back to null and increments the programme's `version`; this revision loop may repeat without bound. Activating it moves it `PENDING_APPROVAL → ACTIVE` and increments the programme's `version`, but only after two gates clear. The first is segregation of duties: the activating `X-User-ID` must differ from the `submitted_by_user_id` recorded at submission, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The second is the coverage floor: the programme's `planned_coverage_pct_delta` must be greater than or equal to the governing policy's `min_coverage_pct`, otherwise `ERR_COVERAGE_FLOOR_NOT_MET`. Because the floor reads the *planned* measure, instruments that are still merely proposed count toward clearing it.

**Closure** moves a programme `ACTIVE → CLOSED` and increments its `version`, and is permitted only when `open_instrument_count` has fallen to zero — every instrument on the book has either settled or been terminated. Closing a programme that still has proposed or executed trades is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_HAS_OPEN_INSTRUMENTS`. Any programme transition invoked from a state that does not declare it is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.4. The Instrument Book: Proposal, Execution and Termination

A hedge instrument is a child of a programme, and its `hedge_program_id` is always taken from the path rather than the body. It is proposed by a `FuelAnalyst` while the programme is `SIZING`, `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `ACTIVE`; a proposal against a `DRAFT` or `CLOSED` programme is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_INVALID_STATE`. The proposal names the facing dealer by `counterparty_id`, an `instrument_type` of `SWAP`, `COLLAR` or `CALL_OPTION`, a `notional_gallons` volume strictly greater than zero, a `maturity_month` in `YYYY-MM` form that must fall inside the programme's inclusive hedged window — a maturity outside that window is a request-shape failure, `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, reported once the path programme has resolved but before `counterparty_id` is looked up — and the programme version expectation described in §2.6. The strike fields are conditional on the type and are validated as part of request shape: a `SWAP` or a `CALL_OPTION` must supply `strike_usd_per_gal`, strictly greater than zero at four decimal places, and must omit both collar strikes; a `COLLAR` must omit `strike_usd_per_gal` — which is stored as null for a collar — and must supply both `floor_strike_usd_per_gal`, strictly greater than zero at four decimal places, and `cap_strike_usd_per_gal`, strictly greater than the floor, also at four decimal places. The `premium_usd` field, at least zero to two decimal places, defaults to 0 and must be 0 for a `SWAP` and for a `COLLAR`; only a `CALL_OPTION` carries a non-zero premium. An `instrument_type` that is not a member of the governing policy's `permitted_instrument_types` is rejected with `ERR_INSTRUMENT_TYPE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

Creation stamps `proposed_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID`, opens the instrument at `trade_status` `PROPOSED` and `accounting_status` `UNDESIGNATED`, writes `delta_factor` from the type — 1.00 for a `SWAP`, 0.65 for a `COLLAR`, 0.50 for a `CALL_OPTION` — and writes `delta_adjusted_gallons` as `notional_gallons` multiplied by `delta_factor`, rounded to two decimal places. It also increments the parent programme's `open_instrument_count` by one, recomputes the programme's `planned_coverage_pct_delta`, and increments the programme's `version` by one. The remaining computed figures — `mtm_value_usd`, `margin_requirement_usd`, `collateral_posted_usd`, `cumulative_hedge_change_usd` and `cumulative_item_change_usd` — start at zero, and `executed_at` and `designated_at` start null.

The two status dimensions of an instrument advance independently of each other. The trade dimension runs `PROPOSED`, then either `EXECUTED` or `TERMINATED`, with `SETTLED` and `TERMINATED` as its terminal values. The accounting dimension runs `UNDESIGNATED`, `DESIGNATED` and `DEDESIGNATED` and can cycle between the latter two without bound. A designation never touches `trade_status`, and an execution never touches `accounting_status`.

Coverage is reported on two bases from the programme's frozen basis. On the notional basis, `hedged_gallons` is the sum of `notional_gallons` over the programme's children whose `trade_status` is `EXECUTED`, and `coverage_pct_notional` is that sum divided by `consumption_basis_gallons`, multiplied by 100 and rounded to two decimal places; when the basis is zero the percentage is 0.00. On the delta-adjusted basis, the programme's `delta_adjusted_gallons` is the sum of the children's own `delta_adjusted_gallons` over the same executed set, and `coverage_pct_delta` is derived from it the same way — so a collar contributes only 65 percent of its notional and a call option only 50 percent, and an identical book therefore reads materially lower on the delta basis than on the notional basis. A third measure, `planned_coverage_pct_delta`, widens the input set to children whose `trade_status` is `PROPOSED` or `EXECUTED`, sums their `delta_adjusted_gallons`, and divides by the basis in the same way; this is the measure the activation floor reads. Settled and terminated trades are excluded from all three measures. All three are recomputed whenever the basis or either executed total moves: on sizing, on instrument proposal, on execution, on termination, and on the system settlement of a matured trade.

**Executing** a trade is a `TreasuryRiskManager` action that advances `trade_status` `PROPOSED → EXECUTED` and stamps `executed_at` from the logical clock. Executing a trade that is not `PROPOSED` yields `ERR_INSTRUMENT_INVALID_TRADE_STATE`, and that check on the trade's own dimension is made ahead of every other gate, so it answers even when the owning programme has also left `ACTIVE`. Four business gates then precede the transition, in the ladder order of §2.5. The owning programme must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE`. The facing counterparty must be `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_COUNTERPARTY_NOT_APPROVED`. The post-trade delta-adjusted coverage must not exceed the governing policy's `max_coverage_pct`, and this comparison is deliberately made on the *unrounded* quotient — the delta-adjusted gallons the programme would hold after this trade, divided by `consumption_basis_gallons` and multiplied by 100, with no rounding applied before the comparison. A book that would land at 60.00000909 percent against a 60.00 percent ceiling is therefore rejected with `ERR_COVERAGE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` even though `coverage_pct_delta` would have stored 60.00. Next comes concentration: the dealer's post-trade share is the sum of `notional_gallons` over this programme's *executed* trades facing the same counterparty, plus this trade's own `notional_gallons`, divided by `consumption_basis_gallons`, multiplied by 100 and rounded to two decimal places (0.00 when the basis is zero). Proposed, settled and terminated trades are excluded from that sum. If the rounded share exceeds the policy's `max_single_counterparty_pct` and no `APPROVED` policy exception with `reason_code` `COUNTERPARTY_CONCENTRATION` references this instrument, the execution is rejected with `ERR_EXCEPTION_REQUIRED`. Finally, the counterparty's `utilized_credit_usd` plus this trade's freshly computed `margin_requirement_usd` must be no greater than its `credit_limit_usd`, otherwise `ERR_COUNTERPARTY_CREDIT_EXCEEDED`.

The margin requirement itself is computed in a pinned six-step order, because the concentration add-on is keyed on counterparty exposure while exposure is itself a sum of margin requirements. Step one computes a base margin that does not depend on exposure at all: `notional_gallons` multiplied by a per-gallon margin rate that is 0.06 for a `SWAP`, 0.04 for a `COLLAR` and 0.02 for a `CALL_OPTION`, plus — only when the instrument's `mtm_value_usd` is negative — the absolute size of that negative mark-to-market multiplied by an adverse-mark factor of 1.10; a zero or positive `mtm_value_usd` contributes nothing. That base is rounded to two decimal places. Step two establishes a pre-add-on exposure as the counterparty's current `utilized_credit_usd` less whatever margin requirement *this* instrument was already carrying before the current pass; on an initial execution that subtrahend is zero, because the trade has never carried a requirement. Step three converts that pre-add-on exposure into a concentration percentage: divided by the counterparty's `credit_limit_usd`, multiplied by 100 and rounded to two decimal places. Step four selects an add-on rate from that rounded percentage, with lower bounds inclusive: 0.00 below 50 percent, 0.10 from 50 up to but not including 75 percent, 0.25 from 75 up to but not including 90 percent, and 0.50 at 90 percent or above. Step five multiplies the base margin by one plus the add-on rate and rounds to two decimal places, producing `margin_requirement_usd`. Step six advances the counterparty ledger — and only then, so that the *next* instrument's step three sees this requirement. The steps are never re-entered for the same instrument within one pass. When a mark-to-market publication revalues several trades facing the same counterparty, they are processed one at a time in `created_at` ascending order, so the add-on tier can escalate across the batch.

Execution then cascades. It writes the new `margin_requirement_usd`, adds that amount to the counterparty's `utilized_credit_usd` and recomputes the counterparty's `utilization_pct` and `credit_posture`; it adds the trade's `notional_gallons` to the programme's `hedged_gallons` and the trade's `delta_adjusted_gallons` to the programme's `delta_adjusted_gallons` and recomputes all three coverage percentages; and it increments the instrument's, the counterparty's and the programme's `version` by one each.

**Terminating** a trade is also a `TreasuryRiskManager` action, accompanied by a `termination_reason`, and behaves very differently depending on where the trade stands. Terminating a merely `PROPOSED` trade advances `trade_status` `PROPOSED → TERMINATED`, decrements the programme's `open_instrument_count` by one, recomputes `planned_coverage_pct_delta`, and increments the instrument's and the programme's `version` by one each; nothing financial has happened yet, so nothing financial is reversed. Terminating an `EXECUTED` trade is the full compensating action for execution and runs as a three-hop cascade. The first hop advances `trade_status` `EXECUTED → TERMINATED`, sets `margin_requirement_usd` to 0, moves `accounting_status` to `DEDESIGNATED` if and only if it was `DESIGNATED` — without reclassifying the programme's mark-to-market buckets or recomputing its `earnings_volatility_usd` and `compliance_posture`, which termination deliberately leaves alone — subtracts the trade's `notional_gallons` from the programme's `hedged_gallons` and its `delta_adjusted_gallons` from the programme's `delta_adjusted_gallons`, decrements `open_instrument_count` by one and recomputes all three coverage percentages. The second hop fires *because* the trade is now terminated: every collateral row against this trade that is in `POSTED` status advances `POSTED → RETURNED` with `returned_at` stamped from the logical clock, while rows still sitting in `REQUIRED` are deliberately left untouched. The third hop fires *because* those rows became returned: the counterparty's `posted_collateral_usd` is reduced by their `posted_amount_usd`, its `utilized_credit_usd` is reduced by the trade's pre-termination `margin_requirement_usd`, and its `utilization_pct` and `credit_posture` are recomputed. The instrument, the programme, the counterparty and every touched collateral row each have their `version` incremented by one. The net effect is that `hedged_gallons`, `delta_adjusted_gallons`, `utilized_credit_usd` and `posted_collateral_usd` all return to their pre-execution values. Terminating a trade that is already `SETTLED` or `TERMINATED` yields `ERR_INSTRUMENT_INVALID_TRADE_STATE`.

### 3.5. Margin Calls, Collateral Posting and Collateral Return

A collateral posting is the record of a margin demand and its satisfaction. Its `counterparty_id` is taken from the path when a `RiskPolicyAdmin` raises one manually, and from the covered trade when a mark-to-market publication synthesises one. A manual requirement names the trade it covers by `hedge_instrument_id` in the body, declares a `collateral_type` of `CASH` or `LETTER_OF_CREDIT`, and carries the counterparty version expectation of §2.6. The covered trade must be `EXECUTED`, otherwise `ERR_INSTRUMENT_INVALID_TRADE_STATE`. Creation opens the row in `REQUIRED` status with `origin` `MANUAL` and `posted_amount_usd` at 0, and freezes `required_amount_usd` at that moment as the trade's `margin_requirement_usd` less its `collateral_posted_usd`, floored at zero so a fully collateralised trade yields a requirement of 0.00. That frozen figure never moves again, even if the trade's margin requirement later changes. Creation also increments the counterparty's `open_margin_call_count` by one and its `version` by one, and leaves `posted_at` and `returned_at` null.

**Posting** collateral is a `RiskPolicyAdmin` action carrying an `amount_usd`, and advances the row `REQUIRED → POSTED`. The row's own status is tested first, so an attempt on a row that is not `REQUIRED` answers `ERR_POSTING_INVALID_STATE` even when the covered trade has also left `EXECUTED`; the covered trade must then still be `EXECUTED`, otherwise `ERR_INSTRUMENT_INVALID_TRADE_STATE`, and the `amount_usd` offered — at least zero, carried to two decimal places — must be greater than or equal to the row's frozen `required_amount_usd`, otherwise `ERR_COLLATERAL_SHORTFALL` — over-posting is allowed, under-posting is not. Posting writes `posted_amount_usd` from the offered amount, stamps `posted_at` from the logical clock, adds the posted amount to both the counterparty's `posted_collateral_usd` and the trade's `collateral_posted_usd`, decrements the counterparty's `open_margin_call_count` by one, and increments the posting's, the counterparty's and the trade's `version` by one each.

**Returning** collateral is the compensating action, again a `RiskPolicyAdmin` action, carrying a `return_reason`, and advances the row `POSTED → RETURNED`. It is permitted in exactly two circumstances: the covered trade's `trade_status` is `SETTLED` or `TERMINATED`, or the trade's `margin_requirement_usd` has fallen strictly below its `collateral_posted_usd`, meaning collateral is now surplus to requirement. In any other circumstance — including an attempt on a row that is not `POSTED` — the request is rejected with `ERR_POSTING_INVALID_STATE`. Return stamps `returned_at` from the logical clock and subtracts `posted_amount_usd` from both the counterparty's `posted_collateral_usd` and the trade's `collateral_posted_usd`, incrementing all three rows' `version` by one; crucially, `posted_amount_usd` is retained on the row for audit and is *not* zeroed. Attempting to post against a row that is not `REQUIRED` is likewise `ERR_POSTING_INVALID_STATE`.

The same `POSTED → RETURNED` transition also occurs as a system event, without any client request, when the covered trade is terminated from `EXECUTED`. In that path the transition stamps `returned_at`, subtracts `posted_amount_usd` from the counterparty's `posted_collateral_usd` and the trade's `collateral_posted_usd`, and increments the posting's `version` by one, exactly as the manual return does.

### 3.6. Hedge Accounting: Designation, Effectiveness Testing and De-designation

Designation is a `HedgeAccountant` action and moves the accounting dimension only, stamping `designated_at` from the logical clock and incrementing the instrument's `version` by one. Two entry points exist. The first, `UNDESIGNATED → DESIGNATED`, requires that the trade's `trade_status` be `EXECUTED` — otherwise `ERR_INSTRUMENT_INVALID_TRADE_STATE` — and that the owning programme be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE`. The second, `DEDESIGNATED → DESIGNATED`, is a re-designation and carries the same two gates plus a third: an `APPROVED` policy exception with `reason_code` `EARNINGS_VOLATILITY_BREACH` must already reference this instrument, otherwise `ERR_EXCEPTION_REQUIRED`. Re-designation additionally reclassifies the instrument's `mtm_value_usd` out of the programme's `undesignated_mtm_usd` and back into its `designated_mtm_usd`, then recomputes the programme's `earnings_volatility_usd` and `compliance_posture` and increments the programme's `version` by one. If that recomputation returns the programme to `COMPLIANT`, no policy exception row is closed or otherwise mutated as a result — the exception that authorised the re-designation stays exactly as it was. Designating an instrument whose `accounting_status` is already `DESIGNATED` is rejected with `ERR_INSTRUMENT_INVALID_ACCOUNTING_STATE`, and that check on the accounting dimension is made ahead of the trade-state and programme gates, so it answers first even when the trade is no longer `EXECUTED` or its programme is no longer `ACTIVE`. The `DESIGNATED → DEDESIGNATED → DESIGNATED` cycle may repeat without bound.

Effectiveness testing is the retrospective dollar-offset discipline that keeps designation honest, and it too belongs to the `HedgeAccountant`. Each test is filed against a trade whose `hedge_instrument_id` comes from the path and never the body, and whose `trade_status` must be `EXECUTED`, otherwise `ERR_INSTRUMENT_INVALID_TRADE_STATE`. The test names the `period_month` it covers in `YYYY-MM` form and supplies two signed amounts at two decimal places, neither of which may be zero: `hedge_change_usd`, the change in the hedging instrument's fair value over the period, and `hedged_item_change_usd`, the change in the hedged item's expected cash flow over the same period. In a working hedge these two are opposite in sign. The test records `tested_by_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID`.

Two ratios are then computed, and only one of them decides anything. The period ratio is the absolute value of `hedge_change_usd` divided by `hedged_item_change_usd`, rounded to four decimal places, and is stored in `period_ratio` purely for the monthly effectiveness memo — a single ugly month reads as a failure here without consequence. The authoritative measure is cumulative: the trade's running `cumulative_hedge_change_usd` is advanced by this test's `hedge_change_usd` and its running `cumulative_item_change_usd` by this test's `hedged_item_change_usd` — these two totals accumulate every prior test of that instrument in `created_at` ascending order and no test is ever excluded once written — and `cumulative_ratio` is the absolute value of the new cumulative hedge change divided by the new cumulative item change, rounded to four decimal places; when prior tests have driven the new cumulative item change to exactly zero, `cumulative_ratio` is recorded as 0.0000, which fails. The `result` field is `PASS` when the rounded `cumulative_ratio` falls between 0.8000 and 1.2500 with both bounds inclusive, and `FAIL` otherwise. Because the decision reads the cumulative figure, a `period_ratio` of 0.5000 can still produce a `PASS` when the cumulative ratio lands at 0.8000. Filing a test increments the parent instrument's `version` by one.

A `FAIL` on a trade whose `accounting_status` is `DESIGNATED` triggers a four-hop cascade. The first hop moves the accounting dimension `DESIGNATED → DEDESIGNATED`, leaving `trade_status` untouched. The second hop fires because the instrument left `DESIGNATED`: the programme's `designated_mtm_usd` and `undesignated_mtm_usd` are reclassified by moving the instrument's `mtm_value_usd` out of the designated bucket and into the undesignated one, after which the programme's `earnings_volatility_usd` is set to the absolute value of its `undesignated_mtm_usd` and its `compliance_posture` is recomputed — `BREACH` when `earnings_volatility_usd` is strictly greater than the governing policy's `max_earnings_volatility_usd`, and `COMPLIANT` otherwise. The third hop fires only because the posture became `BREACH`: a policy exception row is created with `origin` `SYSTEM_BREACH`, `reason_code` `EARNINGS_VOLATILITY_BREACH`, `status` `REQUESTED`, `hedge_program_id` set to this programme, `hedge_instrument_id` set to this instrument, `requested_by_user_id` null, and `justification` reading exactly `Auto-raised: undesignated mark-to-market exceeds policy earnings volatility limit.` The fourth hop fires only because that exception now exists in `REQUESTED`: the governing policy's `open_exception_count` is incremented by one. The instrument's, the programme's and the policy's `version` are each incremented by one. If the posture stays `COMPLIANT`, the third and fourth hops do not run at all, and a `FAIL` on a trade that was not `DESIGNATED` triggers no cascade whatsoever.

### 3.7. The Monthly Mark-to-Market Run

The reporting cycle is an explicit two-stage run, and the split between the two stages is the single most important control in this system. A `HedgeAccountant` opens a run for a `period_month` in `YYYY-MM` form, unique across all runs — a repeat yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_MTM_PERIOD` — together with the month-end `settlement_index_usd_per_gal`, strictly greater than zero at four decimal places, that will revalue the whole book. A new run is `OPEN`, with `instrument_count`, `total_mtm_usd`, `designated_mtm_usd`, `undesignated_mtm_usd`, `margin_calls_created` and `settled_instrument_count` all at zero and `computed_at` and `published_at` null.

**Computing** the run is also a `HedgeAccountant` action, valid as `OPEN → COMPUTED` and repeatable as `COMPUTED → COMPUTED`, in which case the report totals and every in-scope `mtm_value_usd` are simply recomputed and overwritten and `computed_at` is refreshed. Scope is every hedge instrument in the system whose `trade_status` is `EXECUTED` at the moment of the call, irrespective of `maturity_month` — maturity governs auto-settlement at publication, not revaluation scope — so proposed, settled and terminated trades are out of scope and a trade settled by an earlier run never re-enters. Instruments are revalued in `created_at` ascending order. A `SWAP` revalues at its `notional_gallons` multiplied by the settlement index less its `strike_usd_per_gal`. A `COLLAR` revalues at its `notional_gallons` multiplied by the difference between two floored quantities: the excess of the settlement index over `cap_strike_usd_per_gal` where that excess is positive and zero otherwise, less the shortfall of the settlement index below `floor_strike_usd_per_gal` where that shortfall is positive and zero otherwise. A `CALL_OPTION` revalues at its `notional_gallons` multiplied by the excess of the settlement index over `strike_usd_per_gal` where that excess is positive and zero otherwise, less its `premium_usd`. The rounding order is pinned: each instrument's exact value is rounded to two decimal places and written to its `mtm_value_usd` *first*, and only the already-rounded values are then summed. Summing exact values and rounding once at the end is wrong and can differ by whole cents on a three-trade portfolio. `instrument_count` is the size of the scope. `total_mtm_usd` is the sum of the rounded per-instrument values, `designated_mtm_usd` the sum over those whose `accounting_status` is `DESIGNATED`, and `undesignated_mtm_usd` the sum over those whose `accounting_status` is `UNDESIGNATED` or `DEDESIGNATED`; each total is rounded to two decimal places, which is a no-op over already-rounded inputs. The two accounting buckets partition the scope, so the designated and undesignated totals always add up to `total_mtm_usd`. Computing sets `computed_at` from the logical clock, increments the run's `version` by one and increments each touched instrument's `version` by one — and touches nothing else. It deliberately does not move a programme, a counterparty or a collateral row.

**Publishing** the run is reserved to a `RiskPolicyAdmin`, is valid only as `COMPUTED → PUBLISHED`, and is the ops-run checkpoint where the ledgers finally move. Computing an already-`PUBLISHED` run, or publishing a run that is still `OPEN` or already `PUBLISHED`, is rejected with `ERR_MTM_RUN_INVALID_STATE`. Publication sets `published_at` from the logical clock and then runs a four-part cascade followed by settlement, in that order. Its scope is re-derived at the publication instant on the same rule the computation used — every instrument whose `trade_status` is `EXECUTED` right then, in `created_at` ascending order — so a trade executed or terminated between computation and publication is respectively in or out of every hop below, while the run's own `instrument_count` keeps the figure computation left there and is not restated. The first hop re-runs the margin calculation of §3.4 for every in-scope instrument against the newly published `mtm_value_usd` and overwrites each `margin_requirement_usd`; because a negative mark-to-market feeds the base margin at the 1.10 adverse factor, a bad month mechanically raises margin. The second hop fires because those requirements moved: for every instrument whose `margin_requirement_usd` now exceeds its `collateral_posted_usd`, a collateral row is created with `origin` `MTM_MARGIN_CALL`, `status` `REQUIRED`, `collateral_type` `CASH` and `required_amount_usd` equal to the difference between the new `margin_requirement_usd` and the current `collateral_posted_usd`; the run's `margin_calls_created` counts them. The third hop fires because those rows now exist in `REQUIRED`: every counterparty facing an in-scope instrument — not merely those that drew a fresh margin call — has its `open_margin_call_count` recomputed, its `utilized_credit_usd` set to the sum of `margin_requirement_usd` over its executed instruments, its `utilization_pct` and `credit_posture` recomputed, and its `version` incremented by one. The fourth part reclassifies programmes: for every programme owning an in-scope instrument, `designated_mtm_usd` and `undesignated_mtm_usd` are set from that programme's buckets of this run, then `earnings_volatility_usd` and `compliance_posture` are recomputed; a programme whose posture becomes `BREACH` auto-raises a `SYSTEM_BREACH` policy exception with `reason_code` `EARNINGS_VOLATILITY_BREACH` in `REQUESTED` status and `requested_by_user_id` null, exactly as the de-designation cascade does except that `hedge_instrument_id` is left null — this breach is a programme-level reclassification rather than one trade's failure — and increments the governing policy's `open_exception_count` by one.

Only after those four parts have run does settlement occur. Every in-scope instrument whose `maturity_month` is no later than the run's `period_month` advances `trade_status` `EXECUTED → SETTLED`, so a matured trade still records one final margin recomputation before it leaves the book. Settlement subtracts the trade's `notional_gallons` from its programme's `hedged_gallons` and its `delta_adjusted_gallons` from the programme's `delta_adjusted_gallons`, decrements the programme's `open_instrument_count` by one and recomputes all three coverage percentages; it releases margin by setting `margin_requirement_usd` to 0 and reducing the counterparty's `utilized_credit_usd` by the pre-settlement requirement, then recomputes the counterparty's `utilization_pct` and `credit_posture`; and it increments the run's `settled_instrument_count` by one. Publication increments the run's `version` and the `version` of every touched instrument, programme, counterparty, policy and collateral row by one each.

### 3.8. Tankering: Flight Legs, Directives and Reconciliation

A flight leg is the operational unit a tankering decision attaches to, and may be created by either a `FuelOpsController` or a `FuelAnalyst`. It carries a `flight_number` of 1 to 8 characters and a `departure_date` as an ISO 8601 date, the pair of which must be unique across all legs — a repeat yields `ERR_DUPLICATE_FLIGHT_LEG`. The two stations it spans are named by `origin_station_id`, the uplift station whose price and inventory a tankering decision consumes, and `destination_station_id`, the station whose price the saving is measured against; the two must differ. Its `fleet_type` is `NARROWBODY`, `WIDEBODY` or `REGIONAL` and selects the tankering burn factor. Its performance envelope is `planned_block_minutes`, a positive integer; `planned_burn_gallons`, the planned trip burn excluding any tankered fuel, strictly greater than zero; `tank_capacity_gallons`, the usable capacity of the assigned airframe, which must be strictly greater than `planned_burn_gallons`; `payload_lbs`, the revenue payload, strictly greater than zero; `max_takeoff_payload_lbs`, the structural payload limit used as the payload-ratio denominator, strictly greater than `payload_lbs`; `landing_weight_lbs`, the forecast landing weight without tankered fuel, strictly greater than zero; and `max_landing_weight_lbs`, the structural maximum landing weight, which must be at least `landing_weight_lbs`. A new leg has `actual_block_minutes` and `actual_uplift_gallons` null until it is reconciled.

From that envelope the server derives `uplift_cap_gallons`, the maximum gallons this leg may ever tanker, as the smallest of three headrooms. Tank headroom is `tank_capacity_gallons` less `planned_burn_gallons` less a fixed 900-gallon reserve that is never available for tankering. Weight headroom is `max_landing_weight_lbs` less `landing_weight_lbs`, converted from pounds to gallons by dividing by 6.7 pounds per gallon. The third term is the `max_tankering_uplift_gallons` of the single `ACTIVE` hedging policy; when no `ACTIVE` policy exists, that term is treated as unbounded and does not constrain the minimum. The smallest of the three is rounded to two decimal places, and a negative result is clamped to 0.00. The cap is recomputed on every read of the leg and is re-evaluated at directive approval and at directive reconciliation; directive creation and directive revision do not test it, so an uplift above the cap may sit on a `PROPOSED` directive until approval refuses it.

A tankering directive is a child of a leg — its `flight_leg_id` always comes from the path, never the body — and is proposed by a `FuelAnalyst` supplying `requested_uplift_gallons`, strictly greater than zero, together with the leg version expectation of §2.6. An uplift that is not strictly positive is rejected with `ERR_ZERO_UPLIFT`. Creating a directive opens it in `PROPOSED` status with `revision_count` 0, `proposed_by_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID`, `approved_by_user_id` null, and every pinned and reconciled field — `pinned_origin_price_usd_per_gal`, `pinned_destination_price_usd_per_gal`, `pinned_origin_contract_ref`, `reconciled_burn_penalty_gallons`, `reconciled_net_savings_usd` and `savings_variance_usd` — null. As a system side effect, any earlier directive on the same leg whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `NEEDS_REVISION` is displaced — `PROPOSED → SUPERSEDED` and `NEEDS_REVISION → SUPERSEDED` respectively — and has its `version` incremented by one; the new directive is the only live one. The leg's own `version` is also incremented by one.

Pricing a directive requires that the origin station and the destination station each have **exactly one** supply contract whose clock-derived `status` is `ACTIVE` at that instant. Zero active contracts, or two, at either station is rejected with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_SUPPLY_CONTRACT` — the rule exists precisely so that no ranking or tie-break over competing contracts is ever needed. That check guards directive creation, directive revision and directive approval alike.

The burn penalty is the heart of the tankering economics, and four inputs interact in it: the uplift, the fleet, the block time and the payload ratio, which enters through a hinge. First, the payload ratio is `payload_lbs` plus the uplift converted to pounds at 6.7 pounds per gallon, all divided by `max_takeoff_payload_lbs`, rounded to four decimal places. Second, the penalty multiplier is one plus the excess of that payload ratio over the hinge point of 0.85 — where that excess is positive, and zero when the ratio is at or below the hinge — multiplied by a slope of 2.0, the whole rounded to four decimal places; a lightly loaded aircraft therefore carries a multiplier of exactly 1.0000. Third, the raw carriage burn is the uplift multiplied by a fleet factor of 0.030 for a `NARROWBODY`, 0.024 for a `WIDEBODY` or 0.038 for a `REGIONAL`, multiplied by the block time expressed in hours as the block minutes divided by 60 — that quotient is never rounded. Fourth and last, the raw carriage burn is multiplied by the penalty multiplier and the product is rounded to one tenth of a gallon.

The economics follow from two prices. Gross savings are the uplift multiplied by the destination price per gallon less the origin price per gallon, rounded to two decimal places. The penalty cost is the burn penalty in gallons multiplied by the *origin* price per gallon, rounded to two decimal places. Net savings are the gross savings less the penalty cost, rounded to two decimal places — the three dollar figures are each rounded independently, and gross and penalty are never summed before rounding. Each price is the `all_in_price_usd_per_gal` of the relevant station's single active contract, which already folds in that station's `into_plane_fee_usd_per_gal`.

On the **planned basis**, in force at creation and at every revision, the uplift is the directive's `requested_uplift_gallons`, the block time is the leg's `planned_block_minutes`, and both prices are read live from the active supply contracts at the moment of the call. The results are written to `planned_burn_penalty_gallons`, `planned_gross_savings_usd`, `planned_penalty_cost_usd` and `planned_net_savings_usd`.

A `FuelOpsController` then disposes of the directive. Rejecting it advances `PROPOSED → REJECTED` with a `reject_reason`, pins no prices, moves no inventory, and increments the directive's `version` by one. Requesting a revision advances `PROPOSED → NEEDS_REVISION` with a `revision_note` and increments the directive's `version`. A `FuelAnalyst` may then revise it, which advances `NEEDS_REVISION → PROPOSED`, overwrites `requested_uplift_gallons` with the new request value, re-runs the planned-basis burn penalty and savings against the *live* contracts so a contract change between passes is picked up, increments `revision_count` by one and increments the directive's `version` by one. The `PROPOSED → NEEDS_REVISION → PROPOSED` cycle may repeat without bound.

**Approving** a directive advances `PROPOSED → APPROVED`, records `approved_by_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID`, and increments the directive's `version` by one — but only after four gates clear in the ladder order of §2.5. The `requested_uplift_gallons` must be no greater than the leg's `uplift_cap_gallons`, otherwise `ERR_TANKERING_WEIGHT_LIMIT`. Each of the two stations must have exactly one `ACTIVE` supply contract, otherwise `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_SUPPLY_CONTRACT`. The origin station's `supply_posture` must not be `SHORT`, otherwise `ERR_STATION_INVENTORY_SHORT`. And `planned_net_savings_usd` must be strictly greater than zero, unless an `APPROVED` policy exception with `reason_code` `TANKERING_UNECONOMIC` references this directive; failing both, the approval is rejected with `ERR_TANKERING_NOT_ECONOMIC`. Approval then pins prices: `pinned_origin_price_usd_per_gal` and `pinned_destination_price_usd_per_gal` are frozen from the two active contracts' all-in prices as of the approval instant, and `pinned_origin_contract_ref` records the `contract_ref` of the origin contract selected. Reconciliation later prices against those pinned values and never against live contracts, so a post-approval contract renegotiation cannot move the reconciled outcome.

**Loading** the directive advances `APPROVED → EXECUTED` and requires that the origin station's `inventory_gallons` be at least the `requested_uplift_gallons`, otherwise `ERR_STATION_INVENTORY_SHORT`. Note that approval tests the station's posture while loading tests raw airline-owned gallons; the consortium entitlement that inflates `effective_inventory_gallons` is not liftable metal. Loading cascades in two hops: the first subtracts `requested_uplift_gallons` from the origin station's `inventory_gallons`, and the second fires because inventory moved, recomputing the origin station's `effective_inventory_gallons`, `days_cover` and `supply_posture`. The directive's and the station's `version` are each incremented by one.

**Reconciling** the directive advances `EXECUTED → RECONCILED` against flown data. The `FuelOpsController` supplies `actual_uplift_gallons` and `actual_block_minutes`, the latter a strictly positive integer on pain of `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the actual uplift must be strictly greater than zero, otherwise `ERR_ZERO_UPLIFT`, and no greater than the leg's `uplift_cap_gallons`, otherwise `ERR_TANKERING_WEIGHT_LIMIT`. Both values are written onto the leg, whose `version` is incremented by one. On the **reconciled basis**, the uplift is the leg's `actual_uplift_gallons`, the block time is the leg's `actual_block_minutes`, and the two prices are the pinned values frozen at approval; the payload hinge is re-evaluated from the actual uplift, so a smaller actual uplift can drop the penalty multiplier back to exactly 1.0000. The results are written to `reconciled_burn_penalty_gallons` and `reconciled_net_savings_usd`, and `savings_variance_usd` is the reconciled net savings less the planned net savings, rounded to two decimal places. Reconciliation also trues up the gallon ledger: the origin station's `inventory_gallons` is adjusted by the requested uplift less the actual uplift, so that the station's net debit across loading and reconciliation equals `actual_uplift_gallons` exactly; a smaller actual uplift credits gallons back, a larger one debits more. The station's `effective_inventory_gallons`, `days_cover` and `supply_posture` are then recomputed, and the directive's and the station's `version` are each incremented by one.

`REJECTED`, `RECONCILED` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal directive states. Any directive transition invoked from a state that does not declare it — including any action at all on a `SUPERSEDED`, `REJECTED` or `RECONCILED` directive — is rejected with `ERR_DIRECTIVE_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.9. Policy Exceptions: The Governed Override Channel

Every hard limit in this system either fails outright or fails unless a named, approved exception says otherwise, and the policy exception is the only such channel. An exception may be requested by a `FuelAnalyst`, a `TreasuryRiskManager`, a `HedgeAccountant` or a `FuelOpsController`. The request names a `reason_code` from `COVERAGE_LIMIT`, `COUNTERPARTY_CONCENTRATION`, `EARNINGS_VOLATILITY_BREACH`, `TANKERING_UNECONOMIC` and `INVENTORY_SHORT`, and a `justification` of between 10 and 500 characters. Its target is polymorphic and expressed through up to three optional references: `hedge_program_id` when the exception concerns a programme, `hedge_instrument_id` when it concerns a specific trade, and `tankering_directive_id` when it concerns a specific directive; each defaults to null. Whichever of the three are supplied must resolve, and they are resolved in the order `hedge_program_id`, then `hedge_instrument_id`, then `tankering_directive_id`, so the first unresolvable one of several determines the 404. An optional `requested_limit_value` records the limit the requester would like tolerated and is informational only — nothing in the system reads it as a threshold. A new exception opens in `REQUESTED` status with `origin` `MANUAL`, `requested_by_user_id` from the caller's `X-User-ID`, and `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at` null. When `hedge_program_id` is not null, creation also increments the `open_exception_count` of the policy governing that programme by one and increments that policy's `version` by one.

Exceptions also arise without a requester. When an earnings-volatility breach cascade fires — whether from a failed effectiveness test or from a mark-to-market publication — the server creates an exception with `origin` `SYSTEM_BREACH`, `reason_code` `EARNINGS_VOLATILITY_BREACH` and `requested_by_user_id` null.

Deciding an exception is reserved to a `RiskPolicyAdmin` and carries a `decision_note`. Approval advances `REQUESTED → APPROVED` and denial advances `REQUESTED → DENIED`; both stamp `decided_by_user_id` with the caller's `X-User-ID` and `decided_at` from the logical clock, both decrement by one the `open_exception_count` of the policy governing the exception's `hedge_program_id` when that reference is not null (incrementing that policy's `version` by one), and both increment the exception's `version` by one. Both decisions are subject to segregation of duties: the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from `requested_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Because a `SYSTEM_BREACH` row carries a null `requested_by_user_id`, that check can never block a decision on an auto-raised exception. Deciding an exception that is no longer `REQUESTED` is rejected with `ERR_EXCEPTION_INVALID_STATE`, and `APPROVED` and `DENIED` are both terminal.

Three enforcement points actually consult an approved exception, and they consult it narrowly. Trade execution tolerates a single-counterparty concentration above `max_single_counterparty_pct` only when an `APPROVED` exception with `reason_code` `COUNTERPARTY_CONCENTRATION` references that instrument. Re-designation of a `DEDESIGNATED` trade requires an `APPROVED` exception with `reason_code` `EARNINGS_VOLATILITY_BREACH` referencing that instrument. Directive approval tolerates non-positive `planned_net_savings_usd` only when an `APPROVED` exception with `reason_code` `TANKERING_UNECONOMIC` references that directive. The remaining reason codes, `COVERAGE_LIMIT` and `INVENTORY_SHORT`, are recordable for governance and reporting but do not by themselves unlock any gate: the coverage ceiling and the inventory checks reject regardless of any exception on file.
