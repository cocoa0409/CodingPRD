# Manufactured Home Transport & Set API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Champion Home Builders sells homes that are built in one state and lived in in another, and the distance between those two facts is where the money and the liability sit. A three-section home leaves the plant as three separate oversize loads, each needing its own state transport permit, its own escort and pilot-car coverage, and its own corridor schedule. It arrives at a pad that somebody else poured, on piers somebody else laid out, next to utility stub-outs somebody else claims are pressure-tested. A licensed installer crew then joins the floors along the marriage line, drives anchors and straps to a torque that HUD and the destination state both have opinions about, runs the utility crossovers under the floor, and argues with the dealer about who owns the skirting and the steps. Any damage discovered anywhere along that chain has three plausible authors — the plant, the hauler, and the installer — and today the network settles those arguments by email.

This document specifies the API for the installation network that replaces those emails. The system is a workflow orchestrator with a compliance-audit spine: it routes permits by width, height and state, holds dispatch until the site actually passes inspection, refuses to start a warranty until the anchoring package is certified by an inspector who did not sign the site-readiness verdict, and adjudicates damage claims into per-party chargeback balances that can suspend a hauler mid-route. Every collection is a peer resource mounted under the `/installations` namespace; relationships are carried by explicit reference fields rather than nested URLs, because a damage claim points at four independent parties at once and no single URL tree can express that.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **derived state is never client-supplied**: an order's posture, a crew's booked minutes, a party's chargeback balance and every fee are recomputed by the server from the rows that exist at the end of the request, never patched incrementally. Second, **rejections are ordered**: when a request breaks several rules at once, exactly one error comes back, and which one it is is fully determined by the priority ladder in §2.10. Implementers should treat that ladder as part of the contract, not as an implementation detail.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operators, Roles and Trading-Partner Identity

Every request to every endpoint — including reads — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before the body is parsed and before any path segment is resolved. If both headers are present but the named role is not authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403); this gate runs before schema validation, so a wrong-role request carrying a malformed body still returns `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` rather than a shape error.

Seven roles exist, spelled exactly: `NetworkAdmin`, `PlantDispatcher`, `TransportManager`, `PermitDesk`, `SetCrewLead`, `InstallationInspector`, and `ClaimsAdjuster`.

- A `NetworkAdmin` is the Champion installation-network administrator. It onboards plants, haulers, escort vendors and installer crews, books set jobs and authorises dispatch, and it is the only role that may drive the logical clock. It is not tied to any single trading partner.
- A `PlantDispatcher` is a manufacturing plant's shipping office. It declares the home's floors, records plant-QC measurements, and releases floors to the permit desk.
- A `TransportManager` is a motor carrier's transport manager. It remits permit fees, books escort and pilot-car crews, and reports departures and arrivals.
- A `PermitDesk` operator is a state transport-permit desk. It requests, issues and revokes oversize and superload permits. The role is deliberately separate from the carrier that pays the fee, so that no single principal can both pay for and issue the same permit.
- A `SetCrewLead` is a licensed installer crew lead. It records the marriage line, the skirting and steps scope split, the utility crossover, and the anchoring package.
- An `InstallationInspector` is a third-party installation inspector. It signs site readiness, certifies anchoring, and signs the final installation inspection that starts the warranty.
- A `ClaimsAdjuster` is the damage-in-transit claims adjuster. It reviews, adjudicates and reverses claims between plant, hauler and installer. It may not dispute a claim — only the named parties can.

Three of the seven roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` is not an opaque operator string but the primary key of a persisted trading partner:

- When `X-User-Role` is `PlantDispatcher`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Plant`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Plant` compare the resource's plant reference to `X-User-ID`. For a home section, the comparison target is the `plant_id` of the `InstallationOrder` the section belongs to.
- When `X-User-Role` is `TransportManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Hauler`; ownership checks compare the addressed resource's `hauler_id` to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `SetCrewLead`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `SetCrew`; ownership checks compare the addressed resource's `set_crew_id` to `X-User-ID`, resolving through the parent set job when the addressed row is a marriage joint or an anchoring certification.

The remaining four roles are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `NetworkAdmin`, `PermitDesk` or `ClaimsAdjuster`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `InstallationInspector`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, but the server does record it: a site-readiness verdict stores it in `last_inspector_user_id` on the site, an anchoring review stores it in `certifier_user_id` on the certification, and a final installation inspection stores it in `final_inspector_user_id` on the set job. Those recorded values are compared against each other — never against an entity `id` — to enforce segregation of duties.

An ownership violation by an entity-backed principal — a `PlantDispatcher` reaching for a floor built at a different plant, a `TransportManager` reaching for another carrier's leg, a `SetCrewLead` reaching for another company's set job — is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Ownership is evaluated only after the addressed row is known to exist, because it is an authorisation decision rather than an existence one.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Filters

Every collection endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. Collections are always returned sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, so a stable window can be walked by advancing `offset` alone. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the window is applied and `has_more` reports whether any matching row lies beyond the returned window. A single-resource read carries none of those four fields.

Because collections are peers rather than nested resources, reverse navigation from a parent to its children is provided uniformly by filtered lists. Each child collection accepts every one of its own reference fields as an optional filter, alongside a status filter of the appropriate flavour: plants accept `status` and `state_code`; haulers, escort crews and set crews accept `status`; installation orders accept `plant_id`, `posture` and `destination_state`; home sections accept `order_id`, `mates_to_section_id` and `transit_status` — the second of those being the self-referential marriage pairing, which is how a caller finds the floor mated to a given floor; sites accept `order_id` and `readiness_status`; transport permits accept `section_id`, `state_code` and `status`; route legs accept `permit_id`, `hauler_id` and `status`; escort assignments accept `route_leg_id`, `escort_crew_id` and `status`; set jobs accept `order_id`, `set_crew_id`, `progress_status` and `scope_status`; marriage joints accept `set_job_id`, which is the only discovery channel for joints because they are never embedded inside a set job's representation; and damage claims accept `order_id`, `section_id`, `hauler_id`, `set_crew_id` and `status`. Filters combine conjunctively, and an unrecognised filter value that violates its enumeration is a schema failure rather than an empty result.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every persisted row carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. Every row also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits, fixed at insertion. Almost every row additionally carries `updated_at`, formatted identically to `created_at` and refreshed on every mutation that changes the row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations that the row's owner never asked for, such as a recomputed balance or a cascaded status change.

One entity is deliberately different. A marriage joint is an append-only measurement event: it carries `id` and `created_at` only. It has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, because a recorded measurement is never edited and never revised — a corrected reading is a new joint row, and the pairing of set job and joint index is what prevents duplicates.

### 2.5. Stale-Parent Rejection on Anchored Writes

Two creation endpoints hang a new child off a parent whose state the client must have observed, and both therefore demand optimistic concurrency control against that parent's `version`. The field is a required, non-optional part of the request body in both cases; omitting it is a schema failure, not a default.

A request to create a set job must carry `expected_order_version` matching the current `version` of the `InstallationOrder` named in the body. If any other writer has advanced that order's `version` since the client last read it, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason this parent needs guarding is that a newly created set job legitimately supersedes the order's prior `SCHEDULED` set job, so a client working from a stale view of the order would silently retire a booking it never observed.

A request to create a transport permit must carry `expected_section_version` matching the current `version` of the `HomeSection` named in the body. If the section's `version` has advanced since the client last read it, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SECTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here the hazard is the fee: a new permit for the same section and issuing state supersedes any prior permit still in `REQUESTED` or `FEE_PAID` and freezes that permit's remittance without refund, so a client working from a stale section view would retire a permit whose paid fee it never saw.

No other endpoint takes a version field. In particular, none of the action endpoints — measuring, releasing, paying, issuing, revoking, departing, arriving, releasing an escort, dispatching, splitting scope, recording a crossover, closing the marriage line, inspecting, reviewing, resubmitting, disputing, adjudicating or reversing — accepts an expected-version input; those are guarded by state gates instead.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock

Every date-sensitive rule in this system — installer licence expiry, cargo-insurance expiry, permit validity, calendar-day capacity, and the warranty start date — reads a server-held **logical current UTC instant** rather than real wall-clock time. On startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must not fall back to real time under any circumstance. A `NetworkAdmin` may move it by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting capability; the response echoes `current_utc`, and every subsequent evaluation of every date rule reads the new instant. No business endpoint moves time as a side effect.

Where a rule compares dates rather than instants, the comparison is made against the logical clock's UTC calendar date. Where a rule stores a date, it stores it in `YYYY-MM-DD` form.

### 2.7. Money, Minutes and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in whole cents; the API never exchanges fractional currency. Wherever a monetary computation produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded half up to the nearest whole cent, and the specification pins exactly where in each formula that rounding happens — per-line rounding where the formula does not call for it is a defect, because it produces amounts that differ from the contract by one or two cents.

The permit fee assembles its whole subtotal first, applies the superload class multiplier once to that assembled subtotal, and rounds once at the very end. The installation invoice rounds in exactly two places: when the skirting line is priced from a linear-footage rate, and when the volume discount percentage is applied. The damage-claim approval haircut is rounded half up, but the three-way evidence split is not rounded at all — it floors each party's exact share and then distributes the leftover cents one at a time, so that the three charges sum to the approved amount exactly.

Escort reservation lengths are whole minutes, rounded half up from the reservation's duration at the moment the reservation is created and frozen thereafter; every subsequent aggregation over reservations is exact integer addition. Chargeback balances are exact integer sums with no rounding anywhere. Escort head-counts are integers with no rounding. The site-readiness defect count is an integer between 0 and 3 inclusive. Anchor, strap and pier counts are integers, while average anchor torque is compared as a decimal number.

The marriage-line closure score is the one non-monetary quantity carried to two decimal places, rounded half up — and it is rounded twice, deliberately: once per joint as the joint is recorded, and once again over the sum of those already-rounded per-joint figures. §3.7 states the arithmetic in full.

### 2.8. Order Posture: the Derived Gate over an Order's Descendants

`InstallationOrder.posture` is not a field a client writes; it is a derived aggregate over the order's descendants, recomputed by the server after every successful mutation to the order itself or to any of its home sections, sites, transport permits, route legs or set jobs. Its value is one of `OPEN`, `TRANSPORT_BLOCKED`, `SET_IN_PROGRESS`, `SITE_BLOCKED` or `CLOSED`, and it is derived by an **ordered, first-match-wins ladder** of five bands. The first band whose condition holds determines the posture; later bands are not consulted.

1. The posture is `CLOSED` when the order has at least one set job and every set job of the order whose progress dimension is not `SUPERSEDED` has reached `CLOSED`.
2. Otherwise the posture is `TRANSPORT_BLOCKED` when either the order has at least one home section whose transport status is `PERMIT_REVOKED_HOLD` or `DAMAGED_HOLD`, or the order has at least one route leg in `HAULER_HOLD` — a leg being attributed to the order by resolving the leg's permit to its section and the section to its order.
3. Otherwise the posture is `SET_IN_PROGRESS` when the order has at least one set job whose progress dimension is `DISPATCHED` or `SET_COMPLETE`.
4. Otherwise the posture is `SITE_BLOCKED` when the order has a site whose readiness is `READINESS_PENDING` or `READINESS_FAILED` **and** the order has at least one set job whose progress dimension is `SCHEDULED`. Both halves are required: a site that has not yet passed inspection does not block an order that nobody has booked a crew for.
5. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. A freshly created order with no descendants at all therefore reads `OPEN`.

Posture is a gate as well as a report. A dispatch attempt against an order whose posture is `TRANSPORT_BLOCKED` or `CLOSED` is rejected with `ERR_ORDER_POSTURE_BLOCKED`, as is a final-inspection attempt against an order whose posture is `TRANSPORT_BLOCKED`; booking a new set job against an order whose posture is `CLOSED` is rejected with the same code.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline, Version Propagation and Conserved Quantities

Several operations in this domain mutate more than the row they address, and the secondary effects are part of the contract rather than an optimisation. Whenever a cascade touches a row, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by exactly 1 — this includes parents whose derived posture was recomputed, escort crews whose booked minutes were recomputed, and trading partners whose chargeback balance moved. Creating a child that changes a parent's derived state bumps the parent too: recording a home section or a site raises the order's `version`, requesting a permit raises the section's `version`, adding a route leg raises the permit's `version`, reserving an escort raises the escort crew's `version`, and booking a set job raises the order's `version`.

Two quantities are conserved and must remain exact at every observable instant. The **network chargeback total** — the sum of every plant's, every hauler's and every set crew's `chargeback_balance_cents` — always equals the sum of `approved_amount_cents` over all adjudicated damage claims; reversing a claim therefore restores the total precisely, with no residue. The **escort reserved-minute ledger** is conserved per crew: a crew's `reserved_minutes` always equals the sum of the frozen `reserved_minutes` of its currently reserved assignments, so a reservation followed by its compensating release returns the crew to its exact prior figure with zero drift.

Derived quantities are always recomputed in full from the surviving rows rather than adjusted incrementally. That is why a reversed claim drops out of every chargeback sum, and why a released or completed escort assignment stops contributing minutes rather than being subtracted from a running counter.

### 2.10. The Validation Priority Ladder

Requests are validated in strictly ascending priority order and the **first** violated rule determines the response. Later violations are never reported, even when a single request breaks several rules at once. The ladder is:

1. **Priority 1** — `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are both present and non-empty, on every endpoint without exception. Violation: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). This runs before body parsing and before path resolution.
2. **Priority 2** — `X-User-Role` is one of the calling endpoint's authorised roles, on every endpoint. Violation: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Priority 3** — the request body parses and satisfies the endpoint's field schema: required fields present, types correct, enumerated values within range, numbers within their stated bounds, datetimes ISO 8601 UTC. Applies to every endpoint that has a request body. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). This is a pure shape check performed with no database reads.
4. **Priority 4** — every `{id}` path parameter resolves to an existing row of the addressed entity, on every endpoint that has a path parameter. Violation: the 404 not-found code for that entity — `ERR_PLANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HAULER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ESCORT_CREW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SET_CREW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROUTE_LEG_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ESCORT_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SET_JOB_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MARRIAGE_JOINT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ANCHOR_CERT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`. Path existence outranks body-reference existence, so a request with both a bad path id and a bad body reference returns the path error.
5. **Priority 5** — every reference field in the request body resolves to an existing row of the referenced entity, on every creation endpoint. Violation: the same family of 404 codes. Body references are resolved left to right in the order the fields are declared for that endpoint, so the leftmost broken reference is the one reported: a damage claim resolves `order_id`, then `section_id`, then `hauler_id`, then `set_crew_id`; a set-job booking resolves `order_id` then `set_crew_id`; a corridor leg resolves `permit_id` then `hauler_id`; an escort reservation resolves `route_leg_id` then `escort_crew_id`; a permit request resolves `section_id`; a floor resolves `order_id` then `mates_to_section_id`; a site resolves `order_id`; a marriage joint resolves `set_job_id`; an anchoring package resolves `set_job_id`; and an installation order resolves `plant_id`.
6. **Priority 6** — for an entity-backed role, the acting principal owns the addressed resource: a `PlantDispatcher`'s plant matches the resolved `Plant`, a `TransportManager`'s hauler matches the resolved `Hauler`, a `SetCrewLead`'s crew matches the resolved `SetCrew`. Violation: `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This rule as stated guards ten operations: `POST /installations/sections/{id}/measure`, `POST /installations/sections/{id}/release`, `POST /installations/route-legs/{id}/depart`, `POST /installations/route-legs/{id}/arrive`, `POST /installations/set-jobs/{id}/scope-split`, `POST /installations/set-jobs/{id}/utility-crossover`, `POST /installations/set-jobs/{id}/close-marriage-line`, `POST /installations/marriage-joints`, `POST /installations/anchor-certifications` and `POST /installations/anchor-certifications/{id}/resubmit`. Two further operations carry an ownership condition of their own, stated where their workflow lives rather than here — `POST /installations/escort-assignments/{id}/release`, where the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the leg's `hauler_id` (§3.5), and `POST /installations/damage-claims/{id}/dispute`, where the acting principal must be the party its role names (§3.9) — and each of those conditions is evaluated in this same position, that is ahead of the addressed row's state gate at priority 7, so a non-owner addressing a wrongly-stated row gets `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` rather than `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.
7. **Priority 7** — the addressed row's current state permits the requested transition. Applies to every action endpoint. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). All the specialised readiness rules below are evaluated only once the base transition is legal, so a row sitting in a terminal state always yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than a domain-specific complaint.
8. **Priority 8** — the order's site has readiness `READY`. Applies to `POST /installations/set-jobs/{id}/dispatch`. Violation: `ERR_SITE_NOT_READY` (409). This rule is deliberately ranked above the installer-licence check at priority 11, so a dispatch that is simultaneously site-blocked and crew-unlicensed reports `ERR_SITE_NOT_READY`.
9. **Priority 9** — the order's posture is not `TRANSPORT_BLOCKED`. Applies to `POST /installations/set-jobs/{id}/dispatch` and `POST /installations/set-jobs/{id}/final-inspection`. Violation: `ERR_ORDER_POSTURE_BLOCKED` (409).
10. **Priority 10** — the supplied expected parent version equals the parent row's current `version`. Applies to `POST /installations/set-jobs` and `POST /installations/permits`. Violation: `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_SECTION_VERSION` respectively (412). Concurrency is ranked below the state gates, so a wrong-state parent reports its state error even when the supplied version is also stale.
11. **Priority 11** — the assigned set crew's `status` is `ACTIVE` and its `license_expiry_date` falls on or after the logical current date. Applies to `POST /installations/set-jobs/{id}/dispatch` and `POST /installations/anchor-certifications`. Violation: `ERR_INSTALLER_LICENSE_INVALID` (422).
12. **Priority 12** — the escort crew carries an endorsement covering the permit class of the leg it is being reserved for. Applies to `POST /installations/escort-assignments`. Violation: `ERR_ESCORT_ENDORSEMENT_MISSING` (422). This safety qualification is deliberately ranked above the window-conflict test at priority 22, so an unendorsed crew with a clashing window reports the endorsement error.
13. **Priority 13** — the escort crew's `status` is `AVAILABLE`. Applies to `POST /installations/escort-assignments` and `POST /installations/route-legs/{id}/depart`. Violation: `ERR_ESCORT_CREW_OVERBOOKED` (422). The second scope is a cross-entity dependency: a leg may not roll while any reserved assignment on it belongs to an overbooked crew.
14. **Priority 14** — the route leg's hauler has `status` `ACTIVE`. Applies to `POST /installations/route-legs` and `POST /installations/route-legs/{id}/depart`. Violation: `ERR_HAULER_SUSPENDED` (422). A chargeback-suspended carrier can neither take a new leg nor put an existing one on the road.
15. **Priority 15** — the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the `last_inspector_user_id` recorded on the order's site. Applies to `POST /installations/set-jobs/{id}/final-inspection`. Violation: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (422).
16. **Priority 16** — referential coherence between the addressed rows. A route leg's `state_code` must equal the `state_code` of the transport permit it is filed under, because a permit authorises movement in exactly one state; and a home section's `mates_to_section_id` must point at a different section of the **same** order and must not point at a section that is already mated to a third section. Applies to `POST /installations/route-legs`, `POST /installations/sections` and `PATCH /installations/sections/{id}`. Violation: `ERR_LEG_PERMIT_MISMATCH` or `ERR_SECTION_MATE_INVALID` respectively (422).
17. **Priority 17** — every home section of the order has transport status `DELIVERED`, and the number of home sections equals the order's `section_count`. Applies to `POST /installations/set-jobs/{id}/dispatch`. Violation: `ERR_SECTIONS_NOT_DELIVERED` when a floor is not yet delivered, `ERR_SECTION_COUNT_MISMATCH` when the count is wrong (both 422).
18. **Priority 18** — an anchoring certification in status `CERTIFIED` exists for the set job. Applies to `POST /installations/set-jobs/{id}/final-inspection`. Violation: `ERR_ANCHOR_NOT_CERTIFIED` (422).
19. **Priority 19** — the set job's `closure_status` is `PASS` or `NOT_APPLICABLE`. Applies to `POST /installations/set-jobs/{id}/final-inspection`. Violation: `ERR_MARRIAGE_LINE_NOT_CLOSED` (422).
20. **Priority 20** — the set job's `scope_status` is `SCOPE_DELIVERED`, which is reachable only by passing through `SCOPE_SPLIT`, and its `crossover_recorded` flag is true. Applies to `POST /installations/set-jobs/{id}/final-inspection`. Violation: `ERR_SCOPE_SPLIT_INCOMPLETE` when the scope split has not been recorded, `ERR_UTILITY_CROSSOVER_INCOMPLETE` when the crossover has not been recorded (both 422).
21. **Priority 21** — interval well-formedness: `window_end` is strictly after `window_start`, and `scheduled_end_date` falls on or after `scheduled_start_date`. Applies to `POST /installations/escort-assignments` and `POST /installations/set-jobs`. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (422). Well-formedness is checked before any overlap or capacity arithmetic.
22. **Priority 22** — the requested escort window does not conflict with any reserved window of the same escort crew once both intervals are inflated by that crew's `buffer_minutes`. Applies to `POST /installations/escort-assignments`. Violation: `ERR_ESCORT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
23. **Priority 23** — for every calendar date in the candidate set job's inclusive date range, the set crew's committed job count stays within its `daily_capacity`. Applies to `POST /installations/set-jobs`. Violation: `ERR_SET_CREW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
24. **Priority 24** — scoped uniqueness holds: the pairing of order and `section_index` is unique, the pairing of permit and leg `sequence_no` is unique, the pairing of set job and `joint_index` is unique, and at most one anchoring certification per set job exists in a status other than `FAILED`. Applies to `POST /installations/sections`, `POST /installations/route-legs`, `POST /installations/marriage-joints` and `POST /installations/anchor-certifications`. Violation: `ERR_DUPLICATE_SECTION_INDEX`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_LEG_SEQUENCE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_JOINT_INDEX` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_ANCHOR_CERT` respectively (all 409). Uniqueness is the sanctioned conflict mechanism for repeated child creation; no wall-clock ordering is involved.
25. **Priority 25** — the number of marriage joints recorded on the set job equals the order's `section_count` less one, or zero when the order has a single section. Applies to `POST /installations/set-jobs/{id}/close-marriage-line`. Violation: `ERR_MARRIAGE_JOINT_COUNT_MISMATCH` (422).
26. **Priority 26** — escort sufficiency across the permit: the number of reserved escort-role assignments across the permit's non-cancelled legs is at least the permit's final required escort count, and when a pilot car is finally required, at least one reserved pilot-car assignment exists across those same legs. Applies to `POST /installations/permits/{id}/issue`. Violation: `ERR_PERMIT_ESCORT_SHORTFALL` (422). This rule is ranked above the fee check at priority 29, so a permit that is both under-escorted and under-paid reports the shortfall.
27. **Priority 27** — the anchoring certification's `revision_round` is below the revision ceiling. Applies to `POST /installations/anchor-certifications/{id}/resubmit`. Violation: `ERR_ANCHOR_REVISION_LIMIT` (422).
28. **Priority 28** — allocation-basis preconditions on adjudication: when the basis is evidence, the three evidence-point fields sum to at least 1; when the basis is stage, the claim's `discovered_stage` is one that has a defined owning party. Applies to `POST /installations/damage-claims/{id}/adjudicate`. Violation: `ERR_CLAIM_EVIDENCE_MISSING` or `ERR_CLAIM_STAGE_MISMATCH` respectively (422).
29. **Priority 29** — the permit's `paid_amount_cents` is at least its final assessed fee. Applies to `POST /installations/permits/{id}/issue`. Violation: `ERR_PERMIT_FEE_UNPAID` (402).
30. **Priority 30** — the claimed amount does not exceed the network's single-claim exposure ceiling. Applies to `POST /installations/damage-claims`. Violation: `ERR_CLAIM_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_CAP` (422).

### 2.11. Path Resolution and Reference Integrity

The `/installations` prefix is a namespace, not a resource. Every business collection lives directly beneath it and every single-resource read addresses one row by its `id`; there are no nested resource paths. Where a path does contain more than one segment after the collection — the order roll-up read being the only such case — segments are resolved strictly left to right, so an unknown order id yields `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` before the roll-up is attempted.

Relationships are therefore expressed by reference fields inside bodies and by the filtered lists of §2.3. A damage claim is the extreme case: it names an order, a floor of that order, a hauler and a set crew simultaneously, and the plant it charges is reached indirectly through the order's `plant_id`. The floor named by `section_id` must belong to the order named by `order_id`. Sections point at each other through `mates_to_section_id`, forming the marriage pairing, and that pointer is the one reference in the system whose target is a sibling of the same type.

That roll-up read deserves a note of its own, because it is a computed projection rather than a stored row. It returns `order_id`, the current `posture`, `section_count_declared` taken from the order's declared `section_count`, `section_count_actual` counting the home sections actually recorded, `sections_delivered_count` counting the floors that have reached delivery, `sections_on_hold_count` counting the floors sitting in either hold state, `site_readiness_status` reflecting the order's site, `issued_permit_count` counting the order's issued permits, `open_claim_count` counting the order's damage claims that have not yet reached a settled outcome, `network_chargeback_cents` reporting the conserved network chargeback total described in §2.9, and `invoice_total_cents` mirroring the order's invoice figure. Dispatchers use it to answer the single question the posture band cannot: *why* is this order blocked.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding the Installation Network

Before any home moves, four kinds of trading partner must exist in the network, and each is registered by a `NetworkAdmin`. A manufacturing plant is registered with its trade `name`, the two-uppercase-letter `state_code` it operates from, and its HUD-assigned `hud_plant_code`, which is expected to be unique across all plants — a data expectation on the network rather than a constraint the registration endpoint enforces, since §5 defines no error id for a collision and a repeated code is therefore accepted like any other string. A plant starts life with a `chargeback_balance_cents` of 0 and a `status` of `ACTIVE`; that balance is a computed running total of the plant's share of settled damage claims, and the plant flips to `PROBATION` once the balance reaches seven thousand five hundred dollars — that is, at or above 750,000 cents — falling back to `ACTIVE` if a reversal drops it below that line. `PROBATION` is a reporting state: it does not itself block any operation.

A motor carrier is registered with its trade `name`, its USDOT `dot_number` (expected unique across all haulers, and likewise not enforced at registration), its cargo-insurance expiry as `insurance_expiry_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and `endorsed_states`, a non-empty array of two-letter state codes in which the carrier is permitted to haul oversize loads. A hauler likewise begins with `chargeback_balance_cents` at 0 and `status` `ACTIVE`, but for carriers the threshold bites: once the carrier's chargeback balance reaches five thousand dollars — at or above 500,000 cents — the carrier's `status` becomes `SUSPENDED`, and a suspended carrier can neither be booked onto a new corridor leg nor put an existing one on the road, both refusals carrying `ERR_HAULER_SUSPENDED`. When a reversal drops the balance back below five thousand dollars the carrier returns to `ACTIVE`.

Escort vendors are registered with a `vendor_name`, `endorsed_states` listing the states in which the crew holds a certified-escort endorsement, `certified_roles` — a non-empty subset of `ESCORT` and `PILOT_CAR` naming the reservation roles the crew may take — and an `endorsement_class`, which is the highest permit class the crew is qualified to escort. A crew endorsed at `SUPERLOAD` may escort superload, oversize and standard loads; one endorsed at `OVERSIZE` may escort oversize and standard loads; one endorsed at `STANDARD` may escort standard loads only. Two optional scheduling controls round out the profile. `buffer_minutes` is the turnaround buffer that inflates both ends of every reserved window when overlaps are tested; it defaults to three quarters of an hour — 45 minutes — and must lie between 0 and 240 inclusive. `max_reserved_minutes` is the reserved-minute ceiling above which the crew counts as overbooked; it defaults to ten hours, i.e. 600 minutes, and must be at least 1. A new crew starts with `reserved_minutes` at 0 and `status` `AVAILABLE`, and its `status` becomes `OVERBOOKED` the moment its booked `reserved_minutes` exceeds `max_reserved_minutes` — a crew sitting exactly at the ceiling is still `AVAILABLE`.

Installer crews are registered with a `company_name`, a state-issued `hud_installer_license_no` that is expected to be unique across all set crews (again not enforced at registration), the issuing `license_state` as two uppercase letters, and a `license_expiry_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. `daily_capacity` is optional, defaults to 1, and must lie between 1 and 4 inclusive; it caps how many set jobs the crew may hold on any single calendar day. A set crew also carries a computed `chargeback_balance_cents` starting at 0 — its share of settled claims — and a computed `status` that reads `ACTIVE` while `license_expiry_date` falls on or after the logical clock's current UTC date and `LICENSE_EXPIRED` once that date has passed. An expired licence blocks two things: dispatching the crew, and submitting an anchoring package under its name. Both are refused with `ERR_INSTALLER_LICENSE_INVALID`.

### 3.2. Opening an Order and Declaring the Home's Floors

An installation order is the spine of the workflow, and it may be opened either by a `NetworkAdmin` or by the `PlantDispatcher` whose plant will build the home. Opening one takes the building plant's identifier as `plant_id`, the retail `dealer_name` that sold the home, the Champion `home_model` designation, the dealer-declared `section_count` (between 1 and 4 inclusive), the two-uppercase-letter `destination_state` of the delivery site, and the HUD `wind_zone` of that destination, which is one of `I`, `II` or `III`. The declared section count is load-bearing twice over: it fixes how many marriage joints must be measured before the line can be closed, and it multiplies the anchor count that HUD demands. A fresh order carries `posture` `OPEN`, a `warranty_start_date` of null and an `invoice_total_cents` of null; both of those latter fields stay null until a final inspection passes cleanly.

Each floor of the home is recorded by the plant's dispatcher as a home section, with the `order_id` it belongs to, a 1-based `section_index` between 1 and 4 inclusive, and four dealer-declared travel figures: `declared_width_in` between 120 and 240 inches, `declared_height_in` between 120 and 200 inches, `declared_length_in`, and `declared_weight_lb`. A second index on the same order is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SECTION_INDEX`. Optionally the floor may name its marriage-line partner through `mates_to_section_id`, a pointer at the sibling floor it will mate against; a single-wide leaves that pointer null. The pointer is checked for self-referential integrity: it may not name the section itself, it may not reach into a different order, and it may not name a floor that is already mated to a third floor — any of those three yields `ERR_SECTION_MATE_INVALID`. A floor's declared dimensions and its mate pointer may be amended by the same dispatcher while the floor is still `AT_PLANT`; the amendment accepts `declared_width_in`, `declared_height_in`, `declared_length_in` and `mates_to_section_id`, any subset of them, and once the floor has moved beyond `AT_PLANT` the amendment is refused by the generic state gate with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The mate pointer is re-checked on amendment exactly as it is on creation.

A floor's `transit_status` starts at `AT_PLANT` and walks a branching path with two hold branches: `AT_PLANT`, `MEASURED`, `PERMIT_PENDING`, `DISPATCH_READY`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `DELIVERED`, plus the holds `PERMIT_REVOKED_HOLD` and `DAMAGED_HOLD`. Plant QC advances the first step: recording `measured_width_in` and `measured_height_in` on a floor that is `AT_PLANT` moves it `AT_PLANT → MEASURED`, provided the acting dispatcher's plant is the plant on the floor's order. Those measured figures are the whole point of the step — every final permit evaluation reads them in preference to the declared ones, falling back to the declared value only where a measured value is still null. Releasing the floor to the permit desk then moves it `MEASURED → PERMIT_PENDING`, again only for the owning plant's dispatcher. Both steps raise the floor's `version` and trigger a recomputation of the order's posture; a floor whose owning plant does not match the caller is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, and a floor in the wrong transport status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

The remaining transitions of a floor are all system-driven and are described where their triggers live: `PERMIT_PENDING → DISPATCH_READY` and `PERMIT_REVOKED_HOLD → DISPATCH_READY` when a permit for that floor is issued (§3.4), `DISPATCH_READY → IN_TRANSIT` when a leg carrying it departs and `IN_TRANSIT → DELIVERED` when its last leg arrives (§3.5), `DISPATCH_READY → PERMIT_REVOKED_HOLD` and `IN_TRANSIT → PERMIT_REVOKED_HOLD` when the floor's last issued permit is revoked (§3.4), `IN_TRANSIT → DAMAGED_HOLD` and `DELIVERED → DAMAGED_HOLD` when a blocking damage claim names the floor, and `DAMAGED_HOLD → DELIVERED` when the last such claim is settled or reversed (§3.9). None of the floor's states is terminal — a held floor can always be re-permitted or released.

### 3.3. Preparing the Destination Site

The destination site is registered against the order by either a `NetworkAdmin` or an `InstallationInspector`, and it records what the pad crew actually built: the `order_id` it serves, a `pad_type` of `SLAB`, `RUNNER` or `CROSSING_BEAM`, the engineered `pier_plan_ref` document reference, the `pier_count` the pier plan lays out (which may be zero or more), and three booleans asserting the stub-outs — `utility_water` for a water stub-out present and pressure-tested at the pad, `utility_sewer` for a sewer stub-out present, and `utility_electric` for an energised electric service pedestal. A new site opens at `readiness_status` `READINESS_PENDING`, with `inspection_round` at 0, `blocking_defect_count` at 0 and `last_inspector_user_id` null. Registering a site raises the order's `version` and re-derives the order's posture.

An `InstallationInspector` then inspects the site, supplying three verdicts: `pad_pass`, `pier_plan_pass` and `utility_pass`. The evaluation combines each verdict with the stored facts before counting defects. The pier-plan dimension is satisfied only when the inspector passes the pier plan **and** the site's `pier_count` is at least six piers per declared section — so a two-section home needs at least twelve piers laid out, and a four-section home at least twenty-four, regardless of what the inspector says about the plan drawing. The utility dimension is satisfied only when the inspector passes the utilities **and** all three of `utility_water`, `utility_sewer` and `utility_electric` are true. The pad dimension is satisfied by `pad_pass` alone. `blocking_defect_count` is then the number of those three dimensions that are **not** satisfied, so it always lands between 0 and 3 inclusive, and the site reads `READY` when the count is 0 and `READINESS_FAILED` otherwise.

Four inspection outcomes are possible, and they are deliberately symmetric. From `READINESS_PENDING` a clean evaluation moves the site `READINESS_PENDING → READY`, writing `blocking_defect_count` as 0, incrementing `inspection_round` by 1, stamping the acting `X-User-ID` into `last_inspector_user_id`, and re-deriving the order's posture. From `READINESS_PENDING` a defective evaluation moves the site `READINESS_PENDING → READINESS_FAILED`, writing the computed defect count, incrementing `inspection_round` by 1, stamping `last_inspector_user_id`, and re-deriving the order's posture. From `READINESS_FAILED` a further defective evaluation is a self-loop, `READINESS_FAILED → READINESS_FAILED`: it writes the new defect count, increments `inspection_round` by 1 and stamps `last_inspector_user_id`, but it does **not** re-derive the order's posture, because the site's contribution to the posture ladder has not changed. The round counter is the only monotone witness that a re-inspection happened at all. From `READINESS_FAILED` a clean evaluation moves the site `READINESS_FAILED → READY`, writing `blocking_defect_count` as 0, incrementing `inspection_round` by 1, stamping `last_inspector_user_id`, and re-deriving the order's posture. Every one of the four outcomes raises the site's `version`.

`READY` is terminal for a site: once a site is ready it is not re-inspected, and a further inspection attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The stamped `last_inspector_user_id` has a long tail, though — it is the value that later forbids the same operator from signing the final installation inspection on that order, as §3.8 explains. An order whose set job is booked but whose site has not yet passed shows posture `SITE_BLOCKED`; if no set job is booked yet, the same unready site leaves the order at `OPEN`.

### 3.4. Permit Routing: Classing the Load, Sizing the Escort, Pricing the Move

A transport permit authorises the movement of exactly one floor through exactly one state, and only a `PermitDesk` operator may request one. The request names the floor as `section_id`, the issuing `state_code` as two uppercase letters, a `planned_leg_count` between 1 and 6 inclusive, and the mandatory `expected_section_version` described in §2.5; it may also set `night_move`, which defaults to false and declares that the move is authorised outside daylight hours. Requesting a permit for a section and issuing state that already has a permit sitting in `REQUESTED` or `FEE_PAID` supersedes that earlier permit: it moves `REQUESTED → SUPERSEDED` or `FEE_PAID → SUPERSEDED` as the case may be, with **no fee refund** — whatever had been remitted stays frozen on the superseded row's `paid_amount_cents`. Both `REVOKED` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal. A new permit opens at `status` `REQUESTED` with `paid_amount_cents` at 0, `final_fee_cents` null and `valid_until_date` null, raises the floor's `version`, and re-derives the order's posture.

**Classing the load and sizing the escort.** At request time the permit is evaluated against the floor's *declared* dimensions; at issuance the same evaluation is re-run against the *measured* ones. The evaluation takes five inputs — a width in inches, a height in inches, the permit's issuing `state_code`, its `night_move` flag, and a leg count — and produces three outputs. `permit_class` is `SUPERLOAD` when the width is at least eighteen feet (216 inches), otherwise `OVERSIZE` when the width is at least sixteen feet (192 inches), otherwise `STANDARD`; both comparisons are inclusive, so a box measuring exactly 192 inches is `OVERSIZE` and one measuring exactly 216 inches is `SUPERLOAD`. `escort_count_required` starts from the issuing state's base requirement — `AZ` contributes 0, `NM`, `TX` and `OK` each contribute 1, and `KS` and `CO` each contribute 2 — and then adds one escort for a width of at least 192 inches, one more for a width of at least 216 inches (so a superload-width box collects both increments), one for a travel height of at least thirteen and a half feet (162 inches, again inclusive), and one for a night move. The sum is finally capped at four escorts, which is the network's hard ceiling regardless of how the increments accumulate. `pilot_car_required` is true when any one of three conditions holds: the `permit_class` is anything other than `STANDARD`, or the leg count is at least 3, or the issuing `state_code` is `KS` and the travel height is at least 162 inches. Those three are alternatives, not a conjunction — one is enough.

**Pricing the move.** The fee is assembled as a single subtotal in cents before any multiplier or rounding is applied. The subtotal begins at a base of one hundred and twenty dollars (12,000 cents). To it is added two dollars fifty (250 cents) for every inch by which the width in use exceeds the fourteen-foot free allowance of 168 inches, where the excess is zero if the width is at or under the allowance. Then forty-five dollars (4,500 cents) is added for each leg in the leg count in use, and seventy-five dollars (7,500 cents) for each escort the requirement matrix demanded in the same evaluation. Finally, if the height in use is at least 162 inches, a flat over-height surcharge of ninety dollars (9,000 cents) is added; otherwise nothing is added on that account. The assembled subtotal is then multiplied by 1.25 when the `permit_class` from the same evaluation is `SUPERLOAD`, and left unchanged otherwise. The product is rounded half up to a whole number of cents, once, at the very end — the multiplier is applied once to the whole subtotal and no line is rounded on its own.

Two variants of that pricing exist and they must not be conflated. The **provisional** variant runs when the permit is requested, before the plant has released measured dimensions and before any corridor leg exists; it uses the floor's `declared_width_in` and `declared_height_in` and the permit's `planned_leg_count`, and its result is stored as `provisional_fee_cents`. The **final** variant runs at issuance; it uses the floor's `measured_width_in` and `measured_height_in`, falling back to the declared figures only where a measured figure is still null, and it uses the permit's *actual* leg count — the number of that permit's route legs whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_TRANSIT` or `ARRIVED`. Its result is stored as `final_fee_cents`. Both variants take their escort count and permit class from their own matrix evaluation, never from the stored `escort_count_required` and `permit_class` fields, which the final evaluation overwrites in the very same request. The practical consequence is that adding a detour leg or discovering a wider box at plant QC raises the fee, and can flip the class to `SUPERLOAD`, which both adds an escort and applies the 1.25 multiplier.

**Paying.** A `TransportManager` remits against the permit by supplying an `amount_cents` figure, which is simply added to the permit's `paid_amount_cents`. The permit moves `REQUESTED → FEE_PAID` only once the accumulated `paid_amount_cents` reaches or exceeds `provisional_fee_cents`; a remittance that leaves the total short of the provisional fee leaves the permit in `REQUESTED` and only raises the running total, so a carrier may pay in instalments. Each remittance raises the permit's `version`.

**Issuing.** A `PermitDesk` operator issues a permit that is in `FEE_PAID`, moving it `FEE_PAID → ISSUED`. Issuance re-runs both calculations in their final form and overwrites `permit_class`, `escort_count_required`, `pilot_car_required` and `final_fee_cents`, then enforces two gates in a fixed order. First, escort sufficiency: counting reserved escort assignments across the permit's legs whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_TRANSIT`, the number carrying `role` `ESCORT` must be at least the freshly computed `escort_count_required`, and when the freshly computed `pilot_car_required` is true, at least one reserved assignment carrying `role` `PILOT_CAR` must exist across those same legs. Sufficiency is aggregate across the permit rather than per leg. A shortfall on either arm is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_ESCORT_SHORTFALL`. Second, and only afterwards, fee coverage: `paid_amount_cents` must be at least the freshly computed `final_fee_cents`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_FEE_UNPAID` and HTTP 402. Because escort sufficiency outranks fee coverage, a permit that is both under-escorted and under-paid reports the shortfall. On success the permit's `valid_until_date` is set to the logical clock's current UTC date plus five days, the permit's floor moves `PERMIT_PENDING → DISPATCH_READY` — or `PERMIT_REVOKED_HOLD → DISPATCH_READY` when a held floor is being re-permitted, which is how a revocation hold is cleared — both rows' `version` values rise, and the order's posture is re-derived.

**Revoking.** A route condition, a bridge posting or a mis-measure lets a `PermitDesk` operator revoke an issued permit, supplying a `reason_code`. This is the widest cascade in the system and every link of it is part of the contract. The permit moves `ISSUED → REVOKED`. Every route leg of that permit whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`; legs already `IN_TRANSIT` or `ARRIVED` are left untouched. Because those legs cancelled, every reserved escort assignment on them moves `RESERVED → RELEASED`. Because those reservations were released, each affected escort crew's `reserved_minutes` is recomputed from its surviving reserved assignments and its `status` is refreshed, which may return an `OVERBOOKED` crew to `AVAILABLE`. Because the floor now has no permit in `ISSUED` status at all, the floor moves `DISPATCH_READY → PERMIT_REVOKED_HOLD` or `IN_TRANSIT → PERMIT_REVOKED_HOLD`. And because a floor entered a hold state, the order's posture is re-derived and lands on `TRANSPORT_BLOCKED`. Every row mutated anywhere in that chain has its `version` raised by one.

### 3.5. Corridor Legs, Escort Reservations and the Road

A corridor leg is the unit of actual movement, and either a `TransportManager` or a `PermitDesk` operator may add one to a permit. A leg names its `permit_id`, the `hauler_id` of the carrier that will run it, a 1-based `sequence_no` between 1 and 6 inclusive giving its order along the route, the named highway corridor as `corridor_id`, the `state_code` the leg runs through, and the movement window as `window_start` and `window_end`, both ISO 8601 UTC instants with three fractional digits, the latter strictly after the former. That last constraint is a field constraint on the leg rather than the interval rule of priority 21, whose `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` is scoped to escort reservations and set jobs: a leg whose `window_end` is not strictly after its `window_start` is a priority-3 shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), before any reference in the body is resolved. A repeated sequence number on the same permit is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LEG_SEQUENCE`. Because a permit authorises movement in exactly one state, a leg whose `state_code` differs from its permit's `state_code` is refused with `ERR_LEG_PERMIT_MISMATCH`, and a leg booked onto a carrier whose `status` is `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_HAULER_SUSPENDED`. Adding a leg raises the permit's `version`, and it is worth noting that it also raises the permit's final leg count — which can push the final fee above what has already been remitted and turn a previously fundable permit into an `ERR_PERMIT_FEE_UNPAID` at issuance. A new leg opens at `status` `SCHEDULED`; `ARRIVED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

Escort and pilot-car coverage is booked per leg by the carrier's `TransportManager`. A reservation names the `route_leg_id` it covers, the `escort_crew_id` being reserved, the `role` it is reserved in (`ESCORT` or `PILOT_CAR`), and its own `window_start` and `window_end` — the reservation window must open no earlier than the leg's `window_start`, close no later than the leg's `window_end`, and close strictly after it opens. Three checks then run in a fixed order. The crew's `endorsement_class` must cover the permit class of the leg's permit: a `SUPERLOAD` permit demands a crew endorsed `SUPERLOAD`; an `OVERSIZE` permit accepts `SUPERLOAD` or `OVERSIZE`; a `STANDARD` permit accepts any endorsement class. Failing that is `ERR_ESCORT_ENDORSEMENT_MISSING`. Next the crew's `status` must be `AVAILABLE`; an `OVERBOOKED` crew is refused with `ERR_ESCORT_CREW_OVERBOOKED`. Only then is the window tested for conflict, so an unendorsed crew whose window also clashes reports the endorsement error rather than the conflict.

**The conflict test.** Reservation windows are treated as half-open intervals — the closing instant is not itself occupied — and both the candidate window and each existing window are inflated symmetrically by the crew's `buffer_minutes` on each side before comparison. A conflict exists when some other reservation of the same crew, in `RESERVED` status, satisfies both of two strict comparisons: the candidate's opening instant falls before that reservation's closing instant advanced by the buffer, and that reservation's opening instant falls before the candidate's closing instant advanced by the buffer. Released and completed reservations never conflict. The consequence worth internalising is the adjacency case: when the buffer is greater than zero, a back-to-back booking that opens at the exact instant an existing reservation closes **does** conflict; when the buffer is zero, that same back-to-back booking is accepted, because the strict half-open comparison then fails. A conflict is refused with `ERR_ESCORT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` and HTTP 409.

**The minute ledger.** A reservation's own `reserved_minutes` is computed once, at creation, as the number of minutes between `window_start` and `window_end` — the elapsed seconds divided by sixty, rounded half up to a whole minute — and is frozen from then on. A crew's `reserved_minutes` is never incremented; it is recomputed in full as the sum of the frozen `reserved_minutes` of every one of that crew's reservations currently in `RESERVED` status. Released and completed reservations contribute nothing. After every recomputation the crew's `status` is refreshed: `OVERBOOKED` when the total exceeds `max_reserved_minutes`, `AVAILABLE` otherwise. That recomputation runs on four occasions: when a reservation is created, when one is released by the carrier, when a leg's arrival consumes its reservations, and inside the revocation cascade of §3.4. A carrier may release a reservation it owns while the reservation is `RESERVED` and the underlying leg is in `SCHEDULED`, `CANCELLED` or `HAULER_HOLD`, moving it `RESERVED → RELEASED`; the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the leg's `hauler_id`. Release is the exact compensating action for creation: the crew's booked minutes must return to their precise pre-reservation value.

**Rolling and arriving.** The carrier reports the load rolling, moving the leg `SCHEDULED → IN_TRANSIT`. Five conditions guard it: the leg is `SCHEDULED`, the acting `X-User-ID` equals the leg's `hauler_id`, the leg's permit is in `ISSUED` status, the carrier's `status` is `ACTIVE`, and every reserved escort assignment on the leg belongs to a crew whose `status` is `AVAILABLE`. Departure moves the permit's floor `DISPATCH_READY → IN_TRANSIT`, raises both rows' `version`, and re-derives the order's posture. Arrival, reported by the same carrier on a leg that is `IN_TRANSIT`, moves it `IN_TRANSIT → ARRIVED` and consumes its coverage: every reserved escort assignment on that leg moves `RESERVED → COMPLETED`, and each affected crew's `reserved_minutes` and `status` are recomputed. Whether the floor is now home depends on the leg's position: when the arriving leg carries the highest `sequence_no` among that permit's legs whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_TRANSIT` or `ARRIVED`, the floor moves `IN_TRANSIT → DELIVERED`; arriving on any earlier leg leaves the floor in `IN_TRANSIT`. Either way the versions of every mutated row rise and the order's posture is re-derived.

One further leg transition is not driven by the carrier at all. When a damage adjudication suspends a carrier, every leg of that carrier still in `SCHEDULED` moves `SCHEDULED → HAULER_HOLD`, and the order behind each such leg is driven to `TRANSPORT_BLOCKED`. When a later reversal drops that carrier's chargeback balance below the suspension threshold and returns it to `ACTIVE`, each of its held legs moves `HAULER_HOLD → SCHEDULED` and the affected orders' postures are re-derived. §3.9 tells that story from the claims side.

### 3.6. Booking the Installer Crew and Dispatching the Set

Booking a set job is a `NetworkAdmin` action. The booking names the `order_id` being set, the `set_crew_id` engaged, the committed calendar range as `scheduled_start_date` and `scheduled_end_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, the contracted `labor_base_cents` (which must be zero or greater and is the base amount before scope, crossover and volume discount), and the `expected_order_version` that guards the parent order. An end date earlier than the start date is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. A booking against an order whose posture has reached `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_ORDER_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. Booking a job on an order that already has a set job still sitting in `SCHEDULED` retires the old one: it moves `SCHEDULED → SUPERSEDED` and the order's posture is re-derived. `CLOSED` and `SUPERSEDED` are the terminal ends of the progress dimension.

**Crew day capacity.** A set job's day commitment is a **closed** calendar interval: both `scheduled_start_date` and `scheduled_end_date` are occupied days, as is every day between them. Before the row is created, the system walks every calendar date in the candidate's inclusive range and, for each one, counts how many of that crew's existing set jobs already occupy that date — counting only jobs whose progress dimension is `SCHEDULED`, `DISPATCHED` or `SET_COMPLETE`, since jobs that have reached `CLOSED` or `SUPERSEDED` release their days. If adding the candidate would take the count on any single date above the crew's `daily_capacity`, the booking is refused with `ERR_SET_CREW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 409. The consequence worth spelling out: two jobs that merely touch — one ending on a date and the next starting on that same date — both occupy it, so a crew with a `daily_capacity` of 1 cannot take the second. This is aggregate per-day counting on inclusive endpoints, and it is structurally different from the buffered, half-open, pairwise exclusion used for escort crews in §3.5; a crew with a `daily_capacity` of 2 will happily hold two overlapping jobs and refuse only the third.

**Dispatch.** Sending the crew to site is likewise a `NetworkAdmin` action, and it moves the job's progress dimension `SCHEDULED → DISPATCHED`. Six conditions must hold. The job's progress dimension is `SCHEDULED`. A site row exists for the job's order — an order with no site at all yields `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND` — and that site's `readiness_status` is `READY`, failing which the refusal is `ERR_SITE_NOT_READY`. The order's posture is neither `TRANSPORT_BLOCKED` nor `CLOSED`, failing which the refusal is `ERR_ORDER_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. The engaged crew's `status` is `ACTIVE`, failing which the refusal is `ERR_INSTALLER_LICENSE_INVALID`. The number of home sections recorded on the order equals the order's declared `section_count`, failing which the refusal is `ERR_SECTION_COUNT_MISMATCH`. And every one of those home sections has `transit_status` `DELIVERED`, failing which the refusal is `ERR_SECTIONS_NOT_DELIVERED` — no crew is sent to set a home whose floors are not all on the pad. Because the site check outranks the licence check, a dispatch that is both site-blocked and crew-unlicensed reports `ERR_SITE_NOT_READY`. On success the job's `version` rises, the order's posture is re-derived and lands on `SET_IN_PROGRESS`, and the job's scope dimension is left byte-identical.

### 3.7. Setting the Home: Two Independent Dimensions

A set job carries two status dimensions that advance on disjoint triggers and must never be collapsed into one. `progress_status` is the set-execution dimension and runs `SCHEDULED`, `DISPATCHED`, `SET_COMPLETE`, `CLOSED`, with `SUPERSEDED` as its retirement branch. `scope_status` is the scope-delivery dimension and runs `SCOPE_PENDING`, `SCOPE_SPLIT`, `SCOPE_DELIVERED`. Dispatching, closing the marriage line and the final inspection move `progress_status` only and leave `scope_status` untouched; recording the scope split and recording the utility crossover move `scope_status` only and leave `progress_status` untouched. Exactly one operation reads both dimensions, and that is the final inspection.

**Measuring the marriage line.** Each joint along the line is recorded by the job's own `SetCrewLead` as an append-only measurement event naming its `set_job_id`, a 1-based `joint_index` between 1 and 3 inclusive, and three readings in inches: `gap_in`, the measured horizontal gap, between 0 and 4; `vertical_offset_in`, the vertical offset between mating floors, between −4 and 4 and therefore possibly negative; and `ridge_beam_shim_in`, the total shim thickness driven under the ridge beam, between 0 and 2. A repeated joint index on the same job is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_JOINT_INDEX`. Each joint's `deviation_score` is computed at the moment it is recorded and is a penalty for exceeding tolerance rather than a measure of the raw reading: the gap contributes, at weight 1.0, however much the gap exceeds a quarter of an inch (0.25 inches), and nothing at all if the gap is 0.25 inches or less; the vertical offset contributes, at weight 0.6, however much the *absolute value* of the offset exceeds half an inch (0.5 inches), and nothing at all if that absolute value is 0.5 inches or less; and the shim contributes its full thickness at weight 0.4, with no tolerance allowance. Those three contributions are added and the sum is rounded half up to two decimal places, and that rounded figure is stored as the joint's `deviation_score`.

**Closing the line.** The job's `SetCrewLead` closes the marriage line, which moves `progress_status` `DISPATCHED → SET_COMPLETE`. The number of joints recorded on the job must equal the order's `section_count` less one, or zero when the order declares a single section; any other count is refused with `ERR_MARRIAGE_JOINT_COUNT_MISMATCH`, so a three-section home needs exactly two joints on file. The job's `closure_score` is then the sum of the **already-rounded** per-joint `deviation_score` values, taken in ascending `joint_index` order, with that sum itself rounded half up to two decimal places. The rounding order is pinned and it matters: rounding each joint first and then summing the rounded values is the specified reading, and summing the unrounded deviations and rounding once at the end is the wrong one — three joints whose raw deviations are each 0.125 give a `closure_score` of 0.39 under the pinned order and 0.38 under the mistaken one. The verdict lands in `closure_status`: `NOT_APPLICABLE` when the joint count is zero, otherwise `PASS` when the `closure_score` is at most 1.00, otherwise `FAIL`. A `FAIL` verdict does not close the line — the request is refused with `ERR_MARRIAGE_LINE_NOT_CLOSED`, and `closure_score` and `closure_status` are written only when the verdict is `PASS` or `NOT_APPLICABLE`. A single-section order therefore records no joints, closes with a `closure_score` of 0.00 and a `closure_status` of `NOT_APPLICABLE`, and passes the later gate vacuously. Before any closure attempt the job reads `closure_status` `NOT_ASSESSED` with `closure_score` null. Closing raises the job's `version`, re-derives the order's posture, and leaves `scope_status` unchanged.

**Splitting the skirting and steps scope.** Who owns the skirting and the steps is a commercial argument between the dealer and the installer, and the job's `SetCrewLead` records the outcome, moving `scope_status` `SCOPE_PENDING → SCOPE_SPLIT`. The split may be recorded while `progress_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `DISPATCHED` or `SET_COMPLETE` — it does not wait for dispatch. The submission names `skirting_owner` and `steps_owner`, each either `DEALER` or `INSTALLER`, together with `skirting_linear_ft`, the run length of skirting, and `steps_count`, the number of step units. Both owner fields are null until the split is recorded. Pricing follows directly: `skirting_fee_cents` is zero when `skirting_owner` is `DEALER`, and otherwise the skirting run length multiplied by eighteen dollars fifty per linear foot (1,850 cents per foot), rounded half up to whole cents; `steps_fee_cents` is zero when `steps_owner` is `DEALER`, and otherwise the step count multiplied by four hundred and twenty dollars per unit (42,000 cents), which needs no rounding. Together those two lines form the job's scope subtotal. Recording the split raises the job's `version` and leaves `progress_status` unchanged.

**Running the utility crossover.** Once the scope is split, the same `SetCrewLead` records the under-floor utility crossovers, moving `scope_status` `SCOPE_SPLIT → SCOPE_DELIVERED`. This step does wait for dispatch: `progress_status` must be `DISPATCHED` or `SET_COMPLETE`. The submission supplies `crossover_count`, the number of crossovers made between sections, and pricing is a flat six hundred dollars (60,000 cents) for mobilising the crossover work plus one hundred and eighty dollars (18,000 cents) for each crossover made, stored as `crossover_fee_cents`. The step also sets `crossover_recorded` to true, and that boolean rather than the count is what the final-inspection gate reads — a genuine single-wide with zero crossovers must be distinguishable from a job whose crossover step was simply never performed. Recording the crossover raises the job's `version` and leaves `progress_status` unchanged. Both scope steps are owner-scoped: a lead acting for a different crew is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

### 3.8. Anchoring Certification, Final Inspection, Invoice and Warranty Start

Anchoring is where HUD and the destination state both have a say, and the two verdicts are computed separately because they can disagree. The job's `SetCrewLead` submits the package naming the `set_job_id` it covers plus four figures, each of which must be zero or greater: `anchor_count`, the ground anchors installed; `avg_torque_ft_lb`, the mean anchor torque in foot-pounds measured across all anchors; `strap_count`, the over-the-top and frame straps installed; and `pier_count`, the piers set under the home. A submission for a job that already has a certification in any status other than `FAILED` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ANCHOR_CERT`, and a submission under a crew whose licence has lapsed is refused with `ERR_INSTALLER_LICENSE_INVALID`. A new package opens at `status` `SUBMITTED` with `revision_round` at 1 and `certifier_user_id` null.

The evaluation runs at submission and again on every resubmission. The anchor count HUD requires is the per-zone figure for the order's `wind_zone` multiplied by the order's `section_count`: eight anchors per section in zone `I`, twelve per section in zone `II`, and sixteen per section in zone `III` — so a three-section home in zone `II` needs thirty-six. `hud_pass` is true only when all three of the following hold: the `anchor_count` is at least that required figure, the `avg_torque_ft_lb` is at least one hundred and seventy-five foot-pounds, and the `strap_count` is at least one strap per installed anchor. `state_pass` is a separate and stricter count test on the anchors alone: the `anchor_count` must be at least the HUD-required figure, plus four additional anchors when the order's `destination_state` is `KS` or `CO`, and plus nothing in any other destination state. Both the torque comparison and every anchor-count comparison are inclusive — an average torque of exactly 175 foot-pounds passes.

An `InstallationInspector` then reviews the package, supplying a `decision` and free-text `findings`. Three decisions exist and each writes the acting `X-User-ID` into `certifier_user_id`. A decision of `CERTIFY` moves the package `SUBMITTED → CERTIFIED`, but only when both `hud_pass` and `state_pass` are true; if either is false the request is refused with `ERR_ANCHOR_COMPLIANCE_FAILED`. A decision of `REQUEST_CHANGES` moves it `SUBMITTED → CHANGES_REQUIRED`. A decision of `FAIL` moves it `SUBMITTED → FAILED`, which is terminal, as is `CERTIFIED`. When changes have been requested, the job's `SetCrewLead` resubmits corrected figures on the **same** row, moving it `CHANGES_REQUIRED → SUBMITTED`; the resubmission overwrites `anchor_count`, `avg_torque_ft_lb`, `strap_count` and `pier_count` from the payload, increments `revision_round` by 1, and re-runs the HUD and state evaluation. That closes the revision cycle `SUBMITTED → CHANGES_REQUIRED → SUBMITTED`, and the cycle is bounded: a resubmission is only accepted while `revision_round` is still below three, so the third round is the last and a fourth attempt is refused with `ERR_ANCHOR_REVISION_LIMIT`. Both review and resubmission raise the certification's `version`.

**The final installation inspection** is signed by an `InstallationInspector` and takes two inputs: `punch_item_count`, the number of outstanding punch items found, and free-text `notes`. It is the only operation that reads both of the job's status dimensions: `progress_status` must be `SET_COMPLETE` and `scope_status` must be `SCOPE_DELIVERED`. Beyond those, four gates apply in the ladder order of §2.10 — the order's posture must not be `TRANSPORT_BLOCKED` (`ERR_ORDER_POSTURE_BLOCKED`); the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `last_inspector_user_id` stamped on the order's site, because whoever signed the site-readiness verdict may not also sign the final inspection (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`); a certification in `CERTIFIED` status must exist for the job (`ERR_ANCHOR_NOT_CERTIFIED`); the job's `closure_status` must be `PASS` or `NOT_APPLICABLE` (`ERR_MARRIAGE_LINE_NOT_CLOSED`); and the scope work must be complete, meaning `scope_status` has reached `SCOPE_DELIVERED` (`ERR_SCOPE_SPLIT_INCOMPLETE` when it is still `SCOPE_PENDING`) and `crossover_recorded` is true (`ERR_UTILITY_CROSSOVER_INCOMPLETE` when it is not).

The inspection then forks on the punch count, and both branches are equally specified. When `punch_item_count` is 0, the job's `progress_status` moves `SET_COMPLETE → CLOSED`, `final_result` is set to `PASS`, the acting `X-User-ID` is stored in `final_inspector_user_id`, the invoice is assembled into `invoice_total_cents` and mirrored onto the order's own `invoice_total_cents`, the order's `warranty_start_date` is set to the logical clock's current UTC date, and the order's posture is re-derived and lands on `CLOSED`. When `punch_item_count` is 1 or greater, the job's `progress_status` stays at `SET_COMPLETE` — the row is re-inspectable — `final_result` is set to `PUNCH_LIST`, the acting `X-User-ID` is still stored in `final_inspector_user_id`, but `invoice_total_cents` remains null and the order's `warranty_start_date` remains null: no invoice is assembled and no warranty starts. Before any inspection, `final_result` reads `PENDING`. Both branches raise the job's and the order's `version`. An order reaches `CLOSED` when it has at least one set job and every set job of the order whose `progress_status` is not `SUPERSEDED` has reached `CLOSED`.

**Assembling the invoice.** The invoice is computed only on the passing branch, and its evaluation order is pinned because the crossover waiver and the volume discount look circular. First, the pre-discount total is the job's `labor_base_cents` plus the scope subtotal — that is, `skirting_fee_cents` plus `steps_fee_cents` — plus the full, un-waived `crossover_fee_cents`. Second, the discount tier is selected from that pre-discount total on inclusive lower bounds: ten percent when the pre-discount total is at least nine thousand dollars (900,000 cents), otherwise six percent when it is at least six thousand dollars (600,000 cents), otherwise three percent when it is at least three thousand dollars (300,000 cents), otherwise no discount at all. Third, the crossover line is waived: the billed crossover amount is zero whenever any discount applies, and equal to `crossover_fee_cents` when the discount is zero. Fourth, the billable base is `labor_base_cents` plus the scope subtotal plus that billed crossover amount. Fifth, the volume discount in cents is the discount percentage applied to the **pre-discount total from the first step** — never to the billable base — rounded half up to whole cents. Sixth, `invoice_total_cents` is the billable base less the volume discount. The tier chosen in the second step is never re-evaluated after the waiver or after the discount is subtracted, even though the waiver lowers the amount actually billed below the figure that qualified for the tier; that asymmetry is intentional and is what makes the crossover waiver worth something to the customer.

### 3.9. Damage-in-Transit Claims and the Chargeback Ledger

A damage claim names four parties at once, and any of `PlantDispatcher`, `TransportManager`, `SetCrewLead` or `ClaimsAdjuster` may open one. The submission carries the `order_id` the damaged home belongs to, the `section_id` of the damaged floor — which must be a floor of that same order — the `hauler_id` of the carrier named as potentially responsible, and the `set_crew_id` of the installer named as potentially responsible. The charged plant is never named directly: it is reached through the order's `plant_id`. The submission also carries `discovered_stage`, the stage at which the damage was found, one of `PLANT_QC`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `AT_SET`, `FINAL_INSPECTION` or `UNKNOWN`; and `claimed_amount_cents`, the repair amount claimed before any haircut, which must be at least 1 cent and must not exceed the network's single-claim ceiling of twenty-five thousand dollars (2,500,000 cents) — a claim above the ceiling is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_CAP`. Four optional fields complete it: `blocks_set`, which defaults to false and declares that the damage prevents the set from proceeding; and `plant_evidence_points`, `hauler_evidence_points` and `installer_evidence_points`, each an adjuster-scored integer between 0 and 10 inclusive. A new claim opens at `status` `SUBMITTED` with `dispute_round` at 0, `allocation_basis` and `approved_amount_cents` null, and `plant_charge_cents`, `hauler_charge_cents` and `installer_charge_cents` all at 0.

When `blocks_set` is true the named floor is held: it moves `IN_TRANSIT → DAMAGED_HOLD` or `DELIVERED → DAMAGED_HOLD`, both the floor's and the order's `version` rise, and the order's posture is re-derived and lands on `TRANSPORT_BLOCKED`, freezing downstream set work. The floor is released — moving `DAMAGED_HOLD → DELIVERED` — as soon as no blocking claim naming that floor remains in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `DISPUTED`, which in practice means when the last such claim is adjudicated or reversed.

**Review and dispute.** A `ClaimsAdjuster` takes the claim under review with an accompanying `notes` field, moving it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`. Any of the three named parties may then push back: a `PlantDispatcher` whose `X-User-ID` equals the order's `plant_id`, a `TransportManager` whose `X-User-ID` equals the claim's `hauler_id`, or a `SetCrewLead` whose `X-User-ID` equals the claim's `set_crew_id`. The pushback carries a `reason_code` and may simultaneously re-score `plant_evidence_points`, `hauler_evidence_points` and `installer_evidence_points`, which changes the eventual evidence-based split; it moves the claim `UNDER_REVIEW → DISPUTED` and increments `dispute_round` by 1. The adjuster's review action is also the re-entry point, moving the claim `DISPUTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and closing the negotiation cycle `UNDER_REVIEW → DISPUTED → UNDER_REVIEW` on the same row. An adjuster may not dispute a claim, and a party may not review one.

**Adjudication.** From `UNDER_REVIEW` a `ClaimsAdjuster` settles the claim by supplying an `allocation_basis` of either `STAGE` or `EVIDENCE`, moving it `UNDER_REVIEW → ADJUDICATED`. Whichever basis is chosen, the approved amount is computed the same way: the claimed amount less a ten percent approval haircut — that is, ninety percent of `claimed_amount_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents — and stored as `approved_amount_cents`. The two bases then differ entirely in how that amount is attributed, and the attribution always sums exactly: `plant_charge_cents` plus `hauler_charge_cents` plus `installer_charge_cents` equals `approved_amount_cents` to the cent, under both bases.

Under the `STAGE` basis, the entire approved amount is charged to the single party that owns the stage at which the damage was discovered, and the other two charges stay at 0; evidence points are ignored completely. Damage found at `PLANT_QC` belongs to the plant, damage found `IN_TRANSIT` belongs to the hauler, and damage found either `AT_SET` or at `FINAL_INSPECTION` belongs to the installer. A `discovered_stage` of `UNKNOWN` has deliberately **no** owning party, so a stage-based adjudication of such a claim is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_STAGE_MISMATCH`; a claim whose origin could not be established can only be settled on the evidence basis.

Under the `EVIDENCE` basis, the same approved amount is split across all three parties in proportion to their evidence points, so identical claim data yields three non-zero charges instead of one. The three point fields must sum to at least 1, since that sum is the divisor; a sum of zero is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_EVIDENCE_MISSING`. Each party's exact share is the approved amount multiplied by that party's points and divided by the total points, kept as an unrounded rational value. Each party is then provisionally charged the floor of its exact share, and the leftover — the approved amount less the sum of those three floors, which is always 0, 1 or 2 cents — is distributed one cent at a time to the parties ordered by the largest fractional part of their exact share first; ties on the fractional part are broken by the larger point score first; and ties on both are broken by the party's code in ascending alphabetical order, where the codes are the literal strings `HAULER`, `INSTALLER` and `PLANT` in that order. Concretely, an approved amount of 90,001 cents split on points of one, one and one gives the plant 30,000, the hauler 30,001 and the installer 30,000, rather than a single 90,001 charge. This floor-plus-largest-remainder scheme is why the split needs no rounding rule of its own.

**The chargeback cascade.** Adjudication fans out through five levels and the levels differ slightly between the two bases, because a stage adjudication charges one party while an evidence adjudication may charge three. First, the allocation is written onto the claim and its `status` moves to `ADJUDICATED`. Second, because the allocation was posted, the affected trading partners' `chargeback_balance_cents` are recomputed — under the `STAGE` basis this touches the single charged party, under the `EVIDENCE` basis all three — and `Plant.status`, `Hauler.status` and `SetCrew.status` are refreshed. Those balances are always recomputed in full, never adjusted: a plant's balance is the sum of `plant_charge_cents` over every claim in `ADJUDICATED` status whose order resolves to that plant; a hauler's balance is the sum of `hauler_charge_cents` over every claim in `ADJUDICATED` status naming that hauler; a set crew's balance is the sum of `installer_charge_cents` over every claim in `ADJUDICATED` status naming that crew. Claims in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `DISPUTED` or `REVERSED` status contribute nothing to any sum. Third, threshold refresh on inclusive bounds: a hauler whose balance has reached five thousand dollars moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, and a plant whose balance has reached seven thousand five hundred dollars moves to `PROBATION`. Fourth, because a hauler was suspended, every route leg of that hauler whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` moves `SCHEDULED → HAULER_HOLD`. Fifth, because a leg entered `HAULER_HOLD`, the posture of every order behind such a leg is re-derived and lands on `TRANSPORT_BLOCKED`. And separately, if the claim had `blocks_set` true, the held floor is released back to `DELIVERED`. Every row mutated anywhere in that fan-out has its `version` raised by one.

**Reversal.** New evidence or a wrongly named party lets the `ClaimsAdjuster` reverse an adjudicated claim, supplying a `reason_code`; the claim moves `ADJUDICATED → REVERSED`, which is terminal. Reversal must unwind adjudication with no residue. The three charge fields are zeroed, `allocation_basis` and `approved_amount_cents` are cleared back to null, and every party's `chargeback_balance_cents` is recomputed in full — dropping the reversed claim from every sum, which decreases each balance by exactly what the adjudication had added and restores the conserved network chargeback total of §2.9. `Plant.status`, `Hauler.status` and `SetCrew.status` are refreshed: a hauler whose balance has fallen below five thousand dollars returns to `ACTIVE`, a plant whose balance has fallen below seven thousand five hundred dollars returns to `ACTIVE`, and every leg of a restored hauler sitting in `HAULER_HOLD` moves `HAULER_HOLD → SCHEDULED`. Finally the posture of every affected order is re-derived, and a floor released by the reversal returns to `DELIVERED`. Every mutated row's `version` rises.
