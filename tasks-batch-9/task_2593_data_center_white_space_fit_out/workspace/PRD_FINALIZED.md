# Data Center White Space Fit-Out API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A data hall arrives as an empty concrete shell and leaves as revenue-bearing white space. Between those two moments, four trades work the same floor at once: an electrical crew pulls busway over the aisles and lands branch feeds on cabinets, a mechanical crew builds hot- and cold-aisle containment and proves it with leakage tests, a security crew erects tenant cages and commissions cameras and card readers, and a commissioning agent drives the whole assembly through integrated-systems levels with load banks. Every one of those trades competes for the same two finite pools — electrical kilowatts and cooling kilowatts — and every one of them can invalidate another trade's work by accident. This API is the system of record that makes those two pools impossible to overdraw and makes every act of overdrawing an explicit, named rejection instead of a discovered surprise at handover.

The product problem is not scheduling; it is **conservation under change**. Electrical capacity flows down a chain from a power distribution unit, through a busway run, through a branch feed, into a rack, and the ceiling on that chain is not a constant: it is computed from nameplate ratings at design time and re-computed from *measured* derate factors once the hall enters commissioning, which means a reservation that was perfectly legal yesterday can be illegal the moment the hall advances a phase. Cooling capacity behaves the same way from the other direction: a single hot aisle whose containment starts leaking silently reduces the airflow-derived capacity of every duty unit in the hall, which can push the hall out of thermal tolerance and freeze commissioning witnesses that were about to be signed. The API therefore treats both pools as derived quantities that are re-projected on every mutation, never as counters that are incremented and hoped over.

The second product problem is **working safely in occupied space**. Multi-tenant halls host concurrent occupancy: one tenant's cage may be in fit-out while the cage beside it is already released to operations and carrying production load. Once a hall is live, no contractor may touch a busway, a cooling unit or a containment zone without an approved, scheduled method of procedure (a MOP) that is actually in execution, and executing one deliberately degrades everything downstream of the target — feeds derate, racks fall to risk, cages degrade, accepted handovers suspend — with the guarantee that closing or backing out the MOP restores every one of those things to exactly the state it held before. Segregation of duties is enforced structurally rather than by policy: the agent who ran a test script may not witness it, the engineer who wrote a MOP may not approve it, and the agent who assembled a capacity-release packet may not accept it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Personas, Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API — including reads — must carry two headers. `X-User-Role` names the acting persona and `X-User-ID` identifies the acting individual. If `X-User-Role` is absent, or carries a string that is not one of the nine role names defined below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the role is recognised but is not among the roles authorised for the addressed operation in the endpoint table of §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Authentication is therefore always decided before authorisation, and both are decided before anything else about the request is examined.

The nine roles are `FacilityPlanner`, `ElectricalContractor`, `MechanicalContractor`, `CommissioningAgent`, `WitnessAuthority`, `ChangeManager`, `OperationsManager`, `SecurityAdministrator` and `TenantRepresentative`. Their remits divide the fit-out along trade lines:

- A `FacilityPlanner` owns the white space itself: creating halls and tenants, laying out rack positions, writing as-built asset tags, marking racks installed, and driving the hall phase ladder.
- An `ElectricalContractor` owns the electrical chain: adding power distribution units, recording their measured derate factors, adding busway runs, energising them, reserving and releasing rack branch feeds, and powering racks on.
- A `MechanicalContractor` owns the mechanical chain: adding cooling units, commissioning them, placing them into duty, creating containment zones, running leakage tests and putting failed zones into remediation.
- A `CommissioningAgent` owns phased commissioning: opening commissioning packages at levels `L1` through `L5`, approving and executing their scripts, scheduling, starting, completing and aborting load bank runs, and opening and submitting capacity-release packets.
- A `WitnessAuthority` exists only to witness executed commissioning packages. It has exactly one write capability and never executes a script — that separation is what makes a witness signature meaningful.
- A `ChangeManager` chairs change control. It votes on method-of-procedure decisions and is the only role permitted to schedule an approved MOP into a live-space window.
- An `OperationsManager` accepts or rejects capacity handover to operations and is the second peer approver on method-of-procedure decisions.
- A `SecurityAdministrator` builds security infrastructure: tenant cages, cameras and card readers, and the commissioning of those devices.
- A `TenantRepresentative` is a read-only tenant-side observer with no write capability at all.

The eight staff roles listed above `TenantRepresentative` share the staff read surface: every list and detail read in §4 that names them, plus the administrative clock capability. `TenantRepresentative` may read halls (list and detail), the capacity-summary view of a hall, a single tenant, racks (list and detail), cages (list and detail) and release packets (list and detail) — nothing else.

**Identity semantics.** For the eight staff roles — `FacilityPlanner`, `ElectricalContractor`, `MechanicalContractor`, `CommissioningAgent`, `WitnessAuthority`, `ChangeManager`, `OperationsManager` and `SecurityAdministrator` — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it on resources where a transition captures the acting individual (as `author_user_id` on a method of procedure, `executed_by_user_id` and `witnessed_by_user_id` on a commissioning package, `approver_user_id` on an approval row, and `accepted_by_user_id` on a release packet) and compares it against previously recorded identifiers for segregation-of-duties checks, but it never resolves it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `TenantRepresentative`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Tenant`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Tenant` compare the resource's tenant foreign key to `X-User-ID`, directly for a rack or a cage and through the owning cage for a release packet.

**Where `X-User-ID` is mandatory.** Every endpoint that records or compares an acting identity rejects a request whose `X-User-ID` header is missing with `ERR_MISSING_USER_ID` (HTTP 401). Those endpoints are exactly ten: commissioning-package script execution, the commissioning-package witness decision, method-of-procedure drafting, MOP submission, MOP approval decisions, MOP revision, MOP execution, MOP closure, MOP back-out, and release-packet acceptance.

**Tenant isolation.** When `X-User-Role` is `TenantRepresentative`, addressing a single cage, a single rack or a single release packet that belongs to a different tenant is rejected with `ERR_TENANT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403), and the same code is returned when such a caller requests the capacity-summary view of a hall in which its tenant owns nothing. On the tenant-scoped list surfaces — cages, racks and release packets — results are silently filtered to the caller's own tenant rather than rejected. The full set of reads governed by this rule is: cage detail, cage list, rack list, rack detail, release-packet list, release-packet detail, and the hall capacity-summary view.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated through query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of rows returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. The `offset` parameter states how many rows to skip and defaults to 0. Two aliases are also accepted on every list endpoint for the convenience of page-oriented clients: `page_size` is a synonym for `limit` and obeys the same default, minimum and maximum, and `page` is a one-based page index whose effect is an offset of the page index minus one, multiplied by the effective page size. When both spellings appear in one request, `limit` and `offset` take precedence.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` counts every row matching the request's filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the returned window.

A query parameter that the addressed endpoint does not define — a filter name that endpoint does not offer, for instance — is ignored rather than rejected: it narrows nothing and it raises no error, so the response is exactly the response the same request without that parameter would have produced. Each of the field filters an endpoint does offer selects by exact string equality between the field and the value as supplied.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties, Identity and the Logical Clock

Every resource is addressed by an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix. Clients never supply an `id`. Alongside it, every resource carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp rendered with three fractional digits.

**How instants are rendered back.** That three-fractional-digit rendering belongs to the server-minted bookkeeping stamps — `created_at`, `updated_at` and the envelope's `api_request_timestamp` — and those three always carry it. Every other instant a resource carries entered the system as text, and it is echoed back with exactly that text whenever the text is already a UTC rendering ending in `Z`: a client-supplied `contract_start_date`, `window_start` or `window_end` comes back character for character as it was sent, and the clock-stamped `released_at`, `executed_at` and `closed_at` come back as the text the logical clock currently holds — the startup default `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` until an operator moves it, and thereafter the `current_utc` text the clock was last set with. Only an instant that entered in some other well-formed form — an explicit `+00:00` offset, say — is re-rendered, in the canonical `Z` form with three fractional digits.

Thirteen of the fourteen resource types additionally carry `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly one on every mutation of that row, including mutations that only touch computed fields. The single exception is the method-of-procedure approval record: an approval row is immutable evidence and carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version` at all.

Recomputation counts as mutation. When a feed reservation re-derives a hall's power ledger, when a leakage test re-derives its cooling model, or when a cascade re-derives a cage's coverage percentages, the affected rows have their `updated_at` refreshed and their `version` incremented. This matters because two write operations described in §2.8 make a client prove it has observed the current `version` of a parent before it may proceed.

**The logical clock.** Every window, deadline and execution-window rule in this system is evaluated against a server-held logical clock, never against real wall-clock time. At startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it stays there until an operator moves it. An administrative test-driving capability available to all eight staff roles sets the clock to an absolute instant supplied as `current_utc` and echoes that instant back, returning a `current_utc` field whose value is the supplied text verbatim whatever form that text took. Everything time-derived follows this clock: the MOP window rules, the load-bank window rules, and the system-written instants `released_at` on a released feed, `executed_at` and `closed_at` on a method of procedure.

### 2.5. Path Resolution Order and Cross-Parent Consistency

Path variables are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that names no existing row decides the rejection. The variables in play across the surface are `hall_id`, `tenant_id`, `pdu_id`, `busway_id`, `zone_id`, `unit_id`, `cage_id`, `device_id`, `rack_id`, `feed_id`, `package_id`, `test_id`, `mop_id`, `packet_id`, and `id` on the approval-trail sub-resource of a method of procedure. Each maps to its own 404: `ERR_HALL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TENANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PDU_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUSWAY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTAINMENT_ZONE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COOLING_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SECURITY_DEVICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RACK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RACK_FEED_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOAD_BANK_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MOP_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_RELEASE_PACKET_NOT_FOUND`. A soft-deleted feed is still found: releasing a feed does not remove the row, so addressing a `RELEASED` feed by `feed_id` resolves normally rather than raising `ERR_RACK_FEED_NOT_FOUND`.

The same 404 vocabulary governs foreign keys supplied in a request body. On the create operations for power distribution units, busway runs, racks, rack feeds, cooling units, containment zones, cages, security devices, commissioning packages, load bank tests, methods of procedure and release packets, every supplied reference — `hall_id`, `tenant_id`, `pdu_id`, `busway_id`, `rack_id`, `containment_zone_id`, `cage_id`, `package_id`, and the `supersedes_packet_id` linkage where a prior packet is implicated — must resolve to an existing row, and the matching `ERR_<PARENT>_NOT_FOUND` is returned when it does not.

Beyond mere existence, the parents of a child row must agree about which white space they are in. A rack's containment zone and its cage must both belong to the rack's own hall; a busway's hall is the hall of its parent power distribution unit; a feed's busway must sit in the same hall as the feed's rack; a release packet's cage must belong to the packet's hall; a load bank test's package must belong to the hall the caller names; and a cage's tenant may not contradict the tenant of the racks the cage contains — so a rack that is placed inside a cage while naming a `tenant_id` of its own must name that cage's own tenant, whereas a rack that names no `tenant_id`, or that sits in no cage, contradicts nothing. Any of those disagreements is rejected with `ERR_HALL_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) on rack creation, busway creation, feed reservation, release-packet creation and cage creation.

### 2.6. Human-Readable Identifiers and Layout Uniqueness

Short codes and labels are how engineers on the floor refer to things, so the API keeps them unambiguous within their scope. `Hall.code` and `Tenant.code` are unique across the whole system; a busway `name` is unique within its parent power distribution unit; a containment zone `label` and a cage `label` are each unique within their hall; and a security device `label` is unique within its cage. A collision on any of these is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (HTTP 409) on hall creation, tenant creation, busway creation, containment-zone creation, cage creation and security-device creation. Codes and labels are between 1 and 16 characters long.

Floor geometry gets its own guard. A rack position is identified by its hall together with its `grid_row` (1 to 4 characters) and `grid_col` (an integer of at least 1), and that triple is unique: two racks may not occupy the same floor position, and an attempt to place one is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_GRID_POSITION` (HTTP 409). Branch feeds are constrained in the same spirit — a rack holds at most one live feed per redundant side, so a second non-released feed on the same `feed_side` of the same rack is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FEED_SIDE` (HTTP 409). Releasing a feed frees its side for re-reservation, because released feeds are excluded from that uniqueness set.

### 2.7. kW Arithmetic: Precision, Rounding and Electrical Constants

All rounding in this system is **round half up**. Kilowatt figures published on halls, tenants, racks, cooling units and release packets are carried at 2 decimal places. Three quantities in the electrical chain are carried at 3 decimal places because they are intermediate ceilings rather than published capacities: a busway's `raw_kw`, a power distribution unit's `usable_cap_kw`, and a feed's `allocated_kw` (together with the busway and rack sums that aggregate it, namely `committed_kw` on a busway and `connected_kw` on a rack). A cooling unit's `airflow_capacity_kw` is carried at 5 decimal places, because it is summed across duty units before the hall-level capacity is rounded to 2 decimal places. Percentages — leakage, camera coverage, asset-tag completion — are carried at 2 decimal places; a containment zone's `design_static_pressure_pa` and a cooling unit's `supply_delta_t_f` are carried at 1 decimal place, as is a cage's `perimeter_length_m`.

Where a formula pins the *order* of rounding, that order is part of the specification and departing from it produces wrong answers. Two cases matter especially: the busway ceiling takes its minimum over **unrounded** operands and rounds exactly once at the end, and the chargeable roll-up rounds **each rack** before summing rather than summing first.

Four physical constants recur across the electrical formulas and are fixed for the whole system: the three-phase constant is 1.732, the power factor is 0.95, the continuous-load factor is 0.80 (that is, a continuous load may occupy at most eighty percent of a nameplate rating), and the conversion between cooling BTU per hour and kilowatts is 3412 BTU per hour to the kilowatt with an airflow coefficient of 1.08.

### 2.8. Proving a Current View: Optimistic Concurrency

Two create operations legitimately *supersede* something that already exists, which means no state precondition can stop a second attempt. For those two, the API requires the writer to prove that its view of the parent is current before the supersede is allowed to happen.

Opening a release packet requires a non-optional `expected_cage_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Cage`. If that value does not equal the cage's current `version` — because a device commissioning, a rack cascade or an earlier packet acceptance has advanced it since the client read the cage — the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CAGE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The check exists because packet creation supersedes any prior live packet of the same cage, and the writer must be shown to have observed the cage's current coverage percentages, `operational_status` and rack membership before that supersede lands.

Opening a commissioning package requires a non-optional `expected_hall_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Hall`. A mismatch with the hall's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_HALL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Because every feed reservation, every feed release, every rack power-on, every posture recompute and every phase advance increments the hall's `version`, this forces the commissioning agent to re-read the hall's `committed_kw`, its reserve tier and its postures before superseding a package for the same hall and level.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Requests to any other operation neither require nor interpret one.

### 2.9. Derived Fields, Cascade Discipline and Conserved Quantities

Nothing in this system is a hand-maintained counter. Every computed field named in this document is re-derived from its inputs whenever any of those inputs change, within the same request, before the response is written. A single request routinely re-derives several layers: reserving one branch feed re-derives that busway's `committed_kw` and `feed_count`, the rack's `connected_kw`, and then the whole hall power ledger; executing a method of procedure against a busway re-derives feed statuses, rack statuses, rack custody counts, cage security states, release-packet statuses, the cooling model and the hall's release posture.

Three invariants must hold after every request, including after a request that only partially completed a cascade:

- The hall power ledger balances: `committed_kw` plus `commissioning_reserve_kw` plus `spare_kw` always equals the hall's `design_kw_capacity`.
- The rack custody partition is exhaustive and disjoint: `custody_ops_count` plus `custody_fitout_count` always equals `rack_total_count`, with the five rack statuses split between the two sides and no rack counted twice or missed.
- Compensation is exact: closing or backing out a method of procedure returns every entity the execution touched to the state it held immediately before execution, so the custody partition returns to its pre-execution values.

### 2.10. The Order of Rejection

When several rules could reject the same request, the API always returns the rejection that comes first in the ladder below. The ladder is a single global ordering, not a per-endpoint one, and it is what makes error responses predictable: a caller who fixes the reported problem and retries will see the next problem down the ladder, never a different one at the same level.

1. **Authentication (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401)** — `X-User-Role` present and naming one of the nine roles.
2. **Authorisation (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, 403)** — the acting role is listed for the addressed operation.
3. **Acting identity (`ERR_MISSING_USER_ID`, 401)** — `X-User-ID` present on the ten identity-recording operations named in §2.1.
4. **Request shape (`ERR_VALIDATION`, 422)** — every required field of the operation's payload is present; enum-typed fields carry in-domain values; numeric fields obey their declared constraints, including strict positivity where declared and 2 decimal places on kilowatt fields; datetimes are well-formed ISO 8601 UTC instants; every window-bearing payload has its `window_end` strictly after its `window_start`; and `step_pct_sequence` is a strictly ascending list of integers each between 1 and 100.
5. **Path existence (404)** — each templated path segment resolves, left to right, per §2.5.
6. **Body foreign-key existence (404)** — each foreign key in the body resolves, per §2.5.
7. **Polymorphic MOP target (`ERR_MOP_TARGET_NOT_FOUND`, 404)** — a method of procedure's `target_resource_id` resolves to a row of the entity named by its `target_resource_type` and that row sits in the MOP's own hall.
8. **Cross-parent hall consistency (`ERR_HALL_MISMATCH`, 422)** — per §2.5.
9. **Tenant isolation (`ERR_TENANT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, 403)** — per §2.1.
10. **Code and label uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE`, 409)** — per §2.6.
11. **Grid position uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_GRID_POSITION`, 409)** — per §2.6.
12. **Feed side uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_FEED_SIDE`, 409)** — per §2.6.
13. **Stale cage version (`ERR_STALE_CAGE_VERSION`, 412)** — per §2.8.
14. **Stale hall version (`ERR_STALE_HALL_VERSION`, 412)** — per §2.8.
15. **Declared from-state (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`, 409)** — the addressed row sits in a state from which the requested transition is declared.
16. **Hall phase gate (`ERR_HALL_PHASE_INVALID`, 409)** — the operation is permitted in the hall's current phase.
17. **PDU commissioning gate (`ERR_PDU_NOT_COMMISSIONED`, 409)** — a busway is energised only under a commissioned power distribution unit.
18. **Busway energisation gate (`ERR_BUSWAY_NOT_ENERGIZED`, 409)** — feeds are not reserved on isolated busway, and racks are not powered on over busway that is not energised.
19. **Script approval gate (`ERR_SCRIPT_NOT_APPROVED`, 409)** — load bank runs require an approved script on their package.
20. **Commissioning level prerequisite (`ERR_LEVEL_PREREQ_NOT_MET`, 409)** — lower levels of the hall are settled before a higher level's script is approved.
21. **MOP window exclusivity (`ERR_MOP_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, 409)** — the buffered window is free on the target resource.
22. **Load bank concurrency ceiling (`ERR_LOADBANK_CONCURRENCY_EXCEEDED`, 409)** — no more than two overlapping runs per hall.
23. **Execution window (`ERR_OUTSIDE_EXECUTION_WINDOW`, 409)** — the logical clock sits inside the relevant window.
24. **One live change per hall (`ERR_HALL_MOP_ALREADY_EXECUTING`, 409)** — no sibling MOP of the same hall is already in execution.
25. **Live-space change control (`ERR_MOP_REQUIRED`, 409)** — a covering MOP is in execution for live-space work.
26. **Risk classification floor (`ERR_RISK_CLASS_TOO_LOW`, 422)** — busway and cooling work in a live hall is classified `HIGH`.
27. **Witness independence (`ERR_WITNESS_SAME_AS_EXECUTOR`, 422)**.
28. **Approver independence (`ERR_APPROVER_IS_AUTHOR`, 422)**.
29. **Quorum integrity (`ERR_DUPLICATE_APPROVER`, 409)**.
30. **Approver role diversity (`ERR_APPROVAL_ROLE_NOT_DISTINCT`, 422)**.
31. **Execution custody (`ERR_MOP_EXECUTOR_NOT_AUTHOR`, 403)**.
32. **Containment integrity before energisation (`ERR_CONTAINMENT_NOT_PASSED`, 409)**.
33. **Thermal gate (`ERR_THERMAL_POSTURE_NOT_NOMINAL`, 409)**.
34. **Phase prerequisites (`ERR_PHASE_PREREQ_NOT_MET`, 409)**.
35. **Feed topology completeness (`ERR_FEED_TOPOLOGY_INCOMPLETE`, 422)**.
36. **Revision ceiling (`ERR_MOP_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`, 409)**.
37. **Concurrent-occupancy notification (`ERR_TENANT_NOTIFICATION_INCOMPLETE`, 422)**.
38. **Integrated-systems evidence (`ERR_LOADBANK_PREREQ_MISSING`, 409)**.
39. **Release scope non-empty (`ERR_RELEASE_SCOPE_EMPTY`, 422)**.
40. **As-built completeness (`ERR_ASSET_TAGS_INCOMPLETE`, 422)**.
41. **Security coverage floor (`ERR_CAGE_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`, 422)**.
42. **Release posture gate (`ERR_RELEASE_POSTURE_BLOCKED`, 409)**.
43. **Cooling capacity (`ERR_COOLING_MARGIN_EXCEEDED`, 422)**.
44. **Busway capacity (`ERR_BUSWAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, 422)**.
45. **White space capacity (`ERR_HALL_SPARE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, 422)**.
46. **Commissioning reserve (`ERR_COMMISSIONING_RESERVE_EXCEEDED`, 422)**.
47. **Tenant entitlement (`ERR_TENANT_CONTRACT_EXCEEDED`, 422)**.
48. **Branch breaker derate (`ERR_BREAKER_DERATE_EXCEEDED`, 422)**.
49. **Cooling acceptance floor (`ERR_AIRFLOW_BELOW_DESIGN`, 422)**.

Several consequences of this ordering are worth calling out explicitly. A rack power-on request that is simultaneously short a feed, sitting behind a failed containment zone and over the cooling margin reports the containment failure (level 32) before the feed topology problem (level 35) and before the cooling margin problem (level 43). A feed reservation that would breach both the busway ceiling and the hall's spare capacity reports the busway breach (level 44) first, and the branch breaker derate (level 48) is reported only after both pool checks have passed. A method-of-procedure approval by the author who also happens to have voted before reports author identity (level 28) rather than duplicate voting (level 29). And a request to a live hall that lacks a covering MOP reports `ERR_MOP_REQUIRED` (level 25) before any of the measurement-quality checks that follow it.

Where the business narrative of §3 mentions a rejection, it names the same error identifier that appears in this ladder and in the catalogue of §5. The narrative describes *when* a rule bites; this ladder decides *which* rule reports when several bite at once.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a White Space: Halls, Tenants and the Phase Ladder

A `FacilityPlanner` opens the engagement by registering the **white space** itself — a data hall. The registration carries a human label in `name`, a short `code` for floor use, the occupancy stance in `tenant_mode` (either `SINGLE_TENANT` or `MULTI_TENANT`), the design electrical capacity of the room in `design_kw_capacity` (strictly positive, two decimal places), the room's floor area in `gross_area_sqm` (strictly positive), the mechanical redundancy stance in `cooling_redundancy` (either `N` or `N_PLUS_1`), and the number of rack positions the layout plans for in `planned_rack_count` (at least 1). The hall is born in `PLANNING`.

Creation seeds every derived field on the hall. The power ledger opens with `committed_kw` at 0.00 and `commissioning_reserve_kw` at 25.00 — the first reserve tier — so `spare_kw` opens at the design capacity less 25.00. `chargeable_kw` and `thermal_load_kw` open at 0.00, as do `cooling_capacity_nameplate_kw`, `cooling_capacity_airflow_kw`, `cooling_capacity_kw` and `cooling_margin_kw`. `thermal_posture` opens at `NOMINAL` and `release_posture` at `OPEN`. All five counters — `rack_total_count`, `custody_ops_count`, `custody_fitout_count`, `at_risk_rack_count` and `open_mop_count` — open at 0. Whatever else changes later, one identity always holds on a hall: `committed_kw` plus `commissioning_reserve_kw` plus `spare_kw` equals `design_kw_capacity` exactly.

Tenants are registered separately by the same planner, with a legal `name`, a short `code`, the contracted electrical entitlement in `contracted_kw` (strictly positive, two decimal places) and the contract's ISO 8601 UTC start instant in `contract_start_date`. A tenant's `allocated_kw` opens at 0.00 and is thereafter always the sum of the designed density of every rack allocated to that tenant whose status is anything other than `PLANNED` — a planned rack position reserves floor space but books no entitlement.

**The phase ladder.** A hall walks `PLANNING → FIT_OUT → COMMISSIONING → LIVE`, and `LIVE` is terminal. The planner drives the first two steps by naming the desired phase in `target_status`. Only the immediate successor of the hall's current phase is accepted, and `LIVE` is never accepted from this capability at all; naming a non-successor, naming a phase the hall has already passed, or naming `LIVE` is rejected with `ERR_HALL_PHASE_INVALID`.

Advancing `PLANNING → FIT_OUT` demands that the electrical and mechanical skeletons exist: the hall must have at least one power distribution unit on `feed_side` `A` **and** at least one on `feed_side` `B` (redundant feed paths are not optional), at least one busway run, and at least one containment zone. Any of those missing is rejected with `ERR_PHASE_PREREQ_NOT_MET`. On success the hall's status becomes `FIT_OUT`, its `version` increments, and the power ledger is re-derived.

Advancing `FIT_OUT → COMMISSIONING` is the harder gate and demands four things: every containment zone of the hall must stand at `PASS`; at least one cooling unit must be `IN_SERVICE`; at least one rack must be `POWERED`; and — the subtle one — every busway of the hall must already have its committed load within its **commissioned** ceiling, that is, within the ceiling computed with the upstream unit's measured derate applied. The same `ERR_PHASE_PREREQ_NOT_MET` covers all four. This last precondition exists because the advance itself changes the arithmetic: on success, every busway of the hall flips its `usable_variant` from `DESIGN` to `COMMISSIONED` and recomputes `usable_kw` under that variant, which strictly lowers the ceiling on any busway whose upstream unit measured a derate below 1.00. Reservations that were legal at design time would otherwise become retroactively illegal, so the gate refuses to advance until they are not. The hall's status becomes `COMMISSIONING` and the power ledger is re-derived — though the variant flip changes neither `committed_kw` nor `commissioning_reserve_kw`, since those depend only on feed reservations.

The last step is never requested. `COMMISSIONING → LIVE` is evaluated by the system after **every** commissioning-package witness decision, and it fires when three conditions hold together: the hall has at least one commissioning package whose `package_state` is anything other than `SUPERSEDED`; every one of those non-superseded packages stands at `CERTIFIED`; and the set of commissioning levels among them includes both `L4` and `L5`. When it fires, the hall's status becomes `LIVE` and its release posture is re-derived. A hall with a single certified `L1` package does not go live; a hall whose `L4` package is certified but whose `L5` package is still `IN_EXECUTION` does not go live either.

**The reconciliation view.** Alongside the plain hall read, a computed capacity-summary read returns the whole reconciliation picture in one payload: `hall_id`, `status`, `design_kw_capacity`, `committed_kw`, `commissioning_reserve_kw`, `spare_kw`, `chargeable_kw`, `thermal_load_kw`, `cooling_capacity_nameplate_kw`, `cooling_capacity_airflow_kw`, `cooling_capacity_kw`, `cooling_margin_kw`, `thermal_posture`, `release_posture`, `rack_total_count`, `custody_ops_count`, `custody_fitout_count`, `at_risk_rack_count`, `open_mop_count`, and `worst_leakage_pct` — the highest measured leakage percentage currently selected across the hall's containment zones, or 0.00 when no zone currently contributes a measurement. Hall lists may be narrowed by `status` and by `tenant_mode`; tenant lists may be narrowed by `code`. Neither list offers any other filter — in particular there is no `code` filter on the hall list.

### 3.2. The Electrical Chain: Power Distribution Units and Busway Runs

Electrical capacity descends a four-link chain, and each link imposes its own ceiling. An `ElectricalContractor` starts at the top by adding a **power distribution unit** to a hall, naming the hall in the payload's `hall_id`, giving the unit a `name`, declaring which redundant `feed_side` it belongs to (`A` or `B`), and stating its nameplate rating in `rated_kw` (strictly positive, two decimal places). A new unit sits at `PLANNED`, its `measured_derate_factor` defaults to 1.00, and its `usable_cap_kw` is set immediately to the nameplate rating multiplied by the continuous-load factor of 0.80, rounded to three decimal places. That figure is the unit-level ceiling every busway beneath it will have to respect. Units may be listed filtered by `hall_id`, `feed_side` and `status`.

Acceptance testing eventually produces a real measurement, and recording it is what moves a unit `PLANNED → COMMISSIONED`. The write supplies `measured_derate_factor`, which must lie between 0.50 and 1.00 inclusive at two decimal places. On success the factor is stored, the unit's status becomes `COMMISSIONED`, and every busway beneath it recomputes its own `usable_kw` under whichever variant that busway currently sits in — which means a measured derate below 1.00 lowers the commissioned ceiling immediately while leaving the design ceiling untouched. In a hall that has already gone `LIVE`, this write is live-space work: it requires a covering method of procedure to be in execution, either targeting this unit's own busway or otherwise naming it, and without one it is refused with `ERR_MOP_REQUIRED`.

A **busway run** hangs beneath a unit. Its creation payload names the upstream unit in `pdu_id`, gives the run a `name`, and declares its trunking current rating in `rated_amps` (at least 1), its line-to-line `voltage_v` (exactly one of 400, 415 or 480), and its `redundancy_topology` (`N`, `N_PLUS_1` or `TWO_N`). Two fields are inherited rather than supplied: the run's `hall_id` is copied from the parent unit's hall at creation and never changes thereafter, and its `feed_side` is likewise copied from the parent unit. A new run sits at `INSTALLED` with `committed_kw` at 0.000 and `feed_count` at 0. Runs may be listed filtered by `pdu_id`, `hall_id` and `status`.

**How a busway's usable ceiling is computed.** The calculation runs on busway creation, on energisation, whenever the upstream unit's derate is recorded, and on the hall's advance into commissioning. It proceeds in five steps, and both the order and the rounding points are part of the specification:

1. The run's **raw** three-phase capacity, published as `raw_kw`, is the current rating multiplied by the line-to-line voltage, multiplied by the three-phase constant 1.732, multiplied by the power factor 0.95, then divided by 1000 to convert volt-amperes to kilowatts, and rounded to three decimal places.
2. A topology factor is selected from the run's `redundancy_topology`: 1.00 for `N`, 0.90 for `N_PLUS_1`, and 0.50 for `TWO_N`. A two-way redundant run may therefore be loaded to only half of its arithmetic capacity, because the other half is the standby path.
3. The upstream ceiling is the parent unit's nameplate rating multiplied by the continuous-load factor 0.80, rounded to three decimal places — the same figure the unit publishes as `usable_cap_kw`.
4. The trunk figure is the **unrounded** raw capacity multiplied by the continuous-load factor 0.80 and then by the topology factor, and the base figure is the smaller of that trunk figure and the upstream ceiling. The minimum is taken over unrounded operands; rounding before the comparison is wrong.
5. Under the `DESIGN` variant, `usable_kw` is the base figure rounded to two decimal places. Under the `COMMISSIONED` variant, `usable_kw` is the base figure multiplied by the upstream unit's `measured_derate_factor` and then rounded to two decimal places. The derate multiplies the unrounded base figure, and exactly one rounding to two decimal places closes the calculation.

Which variant applies is not a choice; it is read from the hall. A busway created while its hall is in `PLANNING` or `FIT_OUT` records `usable_variant` as `DESIGN`; one created while the hall is in `COMMISSIONING` or `LIVE` records it as `COMMISSIONED`. The `DESIGN` variant ignores the measured derate entirely, so design-time reservations are checked against a nameplate-derived ceiling; the `COMMISSIONED` variant applies it, so identical inputs yield a strictly smaller ceiling whenever the measured factor is below 1.00. Nothing about the run's feeds enters this calculation — only the run's own rating, voltage and topology, plus the parent unit's rating and measured derate.

Energising a run advances it `INSTALLED → ENERGIZED`. The hall must have left `PLANNING` (that is, it must be in `FIT_OUT`, `COMMISSIONING` or `LIVE`), or the request is refused with `ERR_HALL_PHASE_INVALID`; the parent unit must already be `COMMISSIONED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PDU_NOT_COMMISSIONED`; and the run must actually be sitting at `INSTALLED` — attempting it on a run that is already `ENERGIZED` or that has been driven `ISOLATED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On success the run's status becomes `ENERGIZED` and its `usable_kw` is recomputed under the variant the hall's current phase selects.

A run leaves `ENERGIZED` only by cascade. When a method of procedure that names this run as its target begins execution, the run moves `ENERGIZED → ISOLATED`, and every one of its feeds that was `ENERGIZED` moves to `DERATED` in the same request. When that same method of procedure is closed or backed out, the run moves `ISOLATED → ENERGIZED` provided the state captured at execution time was `ENERGIZED`, and every feed of the run that sits at `DERATED` moves back to `ENERGIZED`. Both directions are described in full in §3.8.

### 3.3. Branch Feeds and the White Space Power Ledger

A **rack branch feed** is the reservation instrument of this system: it is how a slice of busway capacity becomes a promise to a specific cabinet. An `ElectricalContractor` reserves one by naming the drawing rack in `rack_id`, the run it taps in `busway_id`, the redundant `feed_side` it lands on, the kilowatts wanted in `requested_kw`, and the tap-off breaker size in `breaker_amps` (at least 1). What is stored is `allocated_kw`, the requested figure rounded to three decimal places, and that stored figure — not the request — is what all subsequent arithmetic uses. The `feed_side` recorded on the feed is copied from the named busway at creation, and the busway in turn inherited it from its upstream unit, so a feed's side is always the side of the electrical path that actually carries it. A new feed sits at `RESERVED` with `released_at` null.

Reserving a feed is the single most heavily gated write in the system, because it draws on both finite pools at once. The hall must be in `FIT_OUT` or `COMMISSIONING` — reservations are a fit-out and commissioning activity, and attempting one in `PLANNING` or in a live hall is refused with `ERR_HALL_PHASE_INVALID`. The named run must not be `ISOLATED`; a feed cannot be reserved on a run that a change is currently holding out of service, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_BUSWAY_NOT_ENERGIZED`. (A run still at `INSTALLED` is acceptable — capacity may be reserved before the run is energised.) The rack must not already hold a live feed on the same side, per §2.6. Then the three capacity gates fire in order:

- **The run's ceiling.** The run's current `committed_kw` plus this feed's `allocated_kw` must not exceed the run's `usable_kw` as computed under the run's current `usable_variant`. A breach is `ERR_BUSWAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
- **The room's ceiling.** After the hall power ledger is re-derived with this reservation included, `spare_kw` must be at least 0.00. A breach is `ERR_HALL_SPARE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Note what this means in practice: the room can refuse a reservation that the run would happily have carried, because the room's reserve tier may have stepped up.
- **The branch breaker.** The reservation may not exceed the continuous-load rating of the breaker the contractor asked for. That rating is the breaker's ampacity multiplied by the run's line-to-line voltage, multiplied by 1.732, multiplied by the power factor 0.95, divided by 1000, then multiplied by the continuous-load factor 0.80, with the result rounded to two decimal places; `allocated_kw` must be no greater than it. A breach is `ERR_BREAKER_DERATE_EXCEEDED`.

On success the run's `committed_kw` grows by the allocation and its `feed_count` grows by one, the rack's `connected_kw` is re-derived, the hall power ledger is re-derived, and the hall's `version` increments.

**The hall power ledger.** Three fields on a hall move together and are always computed in exactly this order, never re-entered:

1. `committed_kw` is the sum of `allocated_kw` over every feed in the hall — reached through each feed's rack — whose status is `RESERVED`, `ENERGIZED` or `DERATED`, rounded to two decimal places. Feeds at `RELEASED` are excluded, which is precisely what makes reservation and release a conserving pair. A derated feed still counts: a change that temporarily takes a run out of service does not hand its kilowatts back to the room.
2. `commissioning_reserve_kw` is then looked up from a three-step tier table keyed **only** on the `committed_kw` just computed. Committed load at or below 100.00 kW holds a reserve of 25.00 kW; committed load above 100.00 kW but at or below 250.00 kW holds a reserve of 50.00 kW; committed load above 250.00 kW holds a reserve of 75.00 kW. Both tier boundaries are inclusive on their upper edge, so a hall sitting at exactly 100.00 kW committed remains in the first tier.
3. `spare_kw` is the hall's `design_kw_capacity` less `committed_kw` less `commissioning_reserve_kw`, rounded to two decimal places.

The dependency looks circular but is not: the tier is never recomputed from `spare_kw`, and after every request the three figures sum to the design capacity exactly. The practical consequence is a step function — a reservation that pushes committed load from 99.00 kW to 101.00 kW consumes 2.00 kW of load *and* a further 25.00 kW of reserve, so 27.00 kW of spare capacity disappears at once. This is why the room-level gate can bite when the run-level gate does not.

**Releasing a feed** is a soft delete. It is permitted only from `RESERVED`, and only while the owning rack is still at `PLANNED` or `INSTALLED`; a feed under a powered, at-risk or released rack may not be given back, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On success the feed moves `RESERVED → RELEASED`, `released_at` is stamped with the logical clock's current instant, the run's `committed_kw` and `feed_count` shrink, the rack's `connected_kw` is re-derived, the hall power ledger is re-derived so that committed load falls, the reserve tier steps back down and spare capacity is restored to its pre-reservation value, and the hall's `version` increments. The row itself remains queryable: feed lists hide released rows by default and reveal them when `include_released` is requested, and a released feed is still addressable by its identifier. Feed lists may otherwise be narrowed by `rack_id`, `busway_id` and `status`.

A feed's remaining transitions are all system-driven. `RESERVED → ENERGIZED` happens when the owning rack is powered on, and requires that the feed's run be `ENERGIZED` at that moment; the rack's `energized_feed_count` is re-derived. `ENERGIZED → DERATED` happens when the feed's run is isolated by a change in the same request, and `DERATED → ENERGIZED` happens when that run is restored — both are detailed in §3.8.

### 3.4. Layout, Installation and Energisation of Rack Positions

A `FacilityPlanner` lays the floor out one rack position at a time. The placement payload names the `hall_id`, the aisle containment zone the cabinet sits in via `containment_zone_id`, and the floor coordinates `grid_row` and `grid_col`, and declares the designed density in `design_kw` (strictly positive, two decimal places). Four fields are optional. `u_height` is the cabinet height in rack units and must be exactly 42, 45 or 48, defaulting to 42. `required_feed_count` states how many energised feeds the position needs before it may take load, must be either 1 or 2, and defaults to 2 — dual-corded by default. `cage_id` places the position inside a tenant cage and defaults to null for facility-owned positions. `tenant_id` allocates the position to a tenant and defaults to null while unallocated. An `asset_tag` may be supplied at placement time but defaults to null.

A new position sits at `PLANNED` with `pre_risk_status` null, `connected_kw` at 0.000 and `energized_feed_count` at 0, and its `chargeable_kw` is fixed at creation as the designed density multiplied by the rack overhead factor of 1.045 — a four-and-a-half percent uplift covering in-cabinet losses — rounded to two decimal places. Placement also re-derives the hall's `rack_total_count` and its custody partition. Rack lists may be narrowed by `hall_id`, `cage_id`, `containment_zone_id`, `tenant_id` and `status`.

As-built documentation is a first-class concern, not an afterthought, so the planner may write or correct a position's `asset_tag` at any status through a dedicated tagging write. Doing so re-derives the asset-tag completion figure of any release packet of that rack's cage that is still in `DRAFT`, since a packet under assembly must always reflect the current tagging reality.

**Installation** advances a position `PLANNED → INSTALLED` and is the moment the position starts costing a tenant something. The hall must be in `FIT_OUT`, `COMMISSIONING` or `LIVE`. An `asset_tag` may be supplied with the installation and is stored when it is. The tenant's `allocated_kw` then grows by the position's designed density, and if that would carry the tenant above its `contracted_kw` entitlement the installation is refused with `ERR_TENANT_CONTRACT_EXCEEDED`. On success the hall's chargeable roll-up and its custody partition are both re-derived.

**The chargeable roll-up** is where rounding order matters most. Each rack's own `chargeable_kw` is computed first, individually, as its designed density multiplied by 1.045 and rounded half up to two decimal places. Only then are those per-rack figures summed. The hall's `chargeable_kw` is the sum of the per-rack figures over every rack of the hall whose status is `INSTALLED`, `POWERED`, `AT_RISK` or `RELEASED`, rounded again to two decimal places (a no-op when every term is already at two places). `PLANNED` positions never contribute. Summing the unrounded products and rounding once at the end produces a different number and is wrong. The same per-rack-first discipline produces a release packet's `released_kw`, computed over the racks of that packet's cage whose status is `POWERED`, `AT_RISK` or `RELEASED`.

**Powering a rack on** is the electrical contractor's act and it advances the position `INSTALLED → POWERED`. Seven conditions must hold together:

- The hall is in `FIT_OUT`, `COMMISSIONING` or `LIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_HALL_PHASE_INVALID`.
- The hall's `release_posture` is not `LOCKED` — a change in execution freezes energisation.
- The position holds exactly as many `RESERVED` feeds as its `required_feed_count`, and when that count is 2 the two feeds carry different `feed_side` values. Neither a short count, nor a surplus, nor two feeds on the same side is acceptable; all three are refused with `ERR_FEED_TOPOLOGY_INCOMPLETE`.
- Every one of those feeds sits on a run whose status is `ENERGIZED`; otherwise `ERR_BUSWAY_NOT_ENERGIZED`.
- The position's containment zone stands at `PASS`; otherwise `ERR_CONTAINMENT_NOT_PASSED`.
- The hall's `cooling_margin_kw`, recomputed with this position's heat load included, is at least 0.00; otherwise `ERR_COOLING_MARGIN_EXCEEDED`.

On success every `RESERVED` feed of the position moves to `ENERGIZED` and `energized_feed_count` is re-derived, the position's status becomes `POWERED`, the hall's `custody_ops_count` grows by one while its `custody_fitout_count` falls by one, the hall cooling model is re-derived so that thermal load rises, the custody partition is re-derived, and the hall's `version` increments.

**The custody partition** is re-derived after every rack status change, including every cascaded one. `rack_total_count` counts every rack of the hall. `custody_ops_count` counts those at `POWERED` or `RELEASED` — the positions carrying operational load. `custody_fitout_count` counts those at `PLANNED`, `INSTALLED` or `AT_RISK` — the positions still in fit-out custody. `at_risk_rack_count` counts those at `AT_RISK` specifically. The two custody sets partition the five rack statuses with no overlap and no gap, so their counts always sum to the total; a change that drives racks from the operations side to the fit-out side must return exactly those racks when it closes.

A position leaves `POWERED` in two directions. It falls to `AT_RISK` when a cascade strips it of a feed, and it advances to `RELEASED` when operations accept the release packet covering its cage — both are described where those workflows live, in §3.8 and §3.9 respectively.

### 3.5. Containment, Airflow and the Thermal Gate

Cooling in a modern hall is not a matter of nameplates; it is a matter of whether the air you paid for actually reaches the inlets. Two entity families model that, and they interact.

**Containment zones.** A `MechanicalContractor` declares each aisle enclosure with the owning `hall_id`, a `label`, the aisle it encloses in `zone_type` (`HOT_AISLE` or `COLD_AISLE`), the construction in `containment_kind` (`RIGID_ROOF`, `CURTAIN` or `CHIMNEY`), the maximum leakage the zone is allowed to measure in `target_leakage_pct` (greater than 0 and at most 25.00, two decimal places), and the differential static pressure the design calls for in `design_static_pressure_pa` (strictly positive, one decimal place). A new zone stands at `UNTESTED` with `test_count` at 0 and both `measured_leakage_pct` and `measured_static_pressure_pa` null. Zone lists may be narrowed by `hall_id`, `leakage_status` and `zone_type`.

Proving a zone means running a leakage test, which reports `measured_leakage_pct` and `measured_static_pressure_pa`. The verdict is a conjunction of two conditions: measured leakage must be no greater than the zone's `target_leakage_pct`, **and** measured static pressure must be at least ninety percent of the design pressure — specifically, at least the design pressure multiplied by 0.90 and rounded to two decimal places. Both satisfied means `PASS`; either violated means `FAIL`. Whichever the verdict, both measured values are stored and `test_count` grows by one. A zone that already stands at `PASS` is never re-tested: the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A zone at `FAIL` cannot be re-tested either until it has been through remediation, and remediation may only be entered from `FAIL` — attempting it from `UNTESTED`, `PASS` or `REMEDIATION` is likewise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Remediation is the repair loop. Putting a failed zone into `REMEDIATION` optionally records a `remediation_note`, sets `leakage_status` to `REMEDIATION`, and clears both `measured_leakage_pct` and `measured_static_pressure_pa` back to null — which deliberately drops the zone out of the hall's worst-leakage selection while the crew is working on it. Testing again from `REMEDIATION` lands on `PASS` or `FAIL` under exactly the same two conditions and with exactly the same side effects as a first test, so a stubborn zone can cycle `FAIL → REMEDIATION → FAIL → REMEDIATION` as many times as the crew needs. In a hall that has gone `LIVE`, both the leakage test and the remediation write are live-space work and require a covering method of procedure to be in execution against that zone; without one they are refused with `ERR_MOP_REQUIRED`.

**Cooling units.** A cooling unit is declared with its `hall_id`, a `name`, its nameplate sensible capacity in `rated_sensible_kw` (strictly positive, two decimal places), its design supply airflow in `design_airflow_cfm` (at least 1 cubic foot per minute), its design supply-to-return temperature difference in `supply_delta_t_f` (strictly positive, one decimal place, in degrees Fahrenheit), and its `redundancy_role` — `PRIMARY` for a duty machine or `REDUNDANT` for the standby. A new unit stands at `INSTALLED` with `measured_airflow_cfm` null and `airflow_capacity_kw` at 0.00. Unit lists may be narrowed by `hall_id`, `status` and `redundancy_role`.

Commissioning a unit records `measured_airflow_cfm` and advances it `INSTALLED → COMMISSIONED`. It requires the hall to be in `FIT_OUT`, `COMMISSIONING` or `LIVE`, and it requires the measured airflow to reach at least ninety percent of `design_airflow_cfm`; a unit that cannot move the air it was specified for is refused with `ERR_AIRFLOW_BELOW_DESIGN`. On success the measurement is stored and the unit's `airflow_capacity_kw` is computed for the first time. In a live hall this too is change-controlled work and requires a covering method of procedure in execution, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_MOP_REQUIRED`. Placing a commissioned unit into duty advances it `COMMISSIONED → IN_SERVICE` and re-derives the hall cooling model, because only now does the unit contribute to either capacity variant. A unit still at `INSTALLED` cannot be placed in service, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

**The hall cooling model.** This calculation is re-derived on unit commissioning, on placement into service, on every leakage test, on every remediation, on every rack power-on, on every cascaded rack status change, and on every cascaded movement of a unit between duty and maintenance. Its evaluation order is pinned:

1. **Demand.** `thermal_load_kw` is the sum of `design_kw` over the hall's racks whose status is `POWERED`, `AT_RISK` or `RELEASED`, multiplied by the diversity factor 0.85 — no hall ever runs every cabinet at its designed density simultaneously — and rounded to two decimal places. Racks at `PLANNED` or `INSTALLED` contribute no heat.
2. **Worst leakage.** The zones that count are those whose `leakage_status` is `PASS` or `FAIL`; zones at `UNTESTED` or `REMEDIATION` are excluded because they carry no current measurement. If no zone counts, the leakage fraction is 0.00; otherwise it is the **highest** `measured_leakage_pct` among the counting zones, divided by 100. One bad aisle governs the whole room.
3. **Nameplate capacity.** The duty set is every cooling unit of the hall whose status is `IN_SERVICE`; units at `MAINTENANCE`, `COMMISSIONED` or `INSTALLED` are excluded. Gross nameplate capacity is the sum of `rated_sensible_kw` across the duty set. When the hall's `cooling_redundancy` is `N_PLUS_1` and the duty set is non-empty, the largest single `rated_sensible_kw` in the duty set is subtracted from that gross figure; when the stance is `N`, nothing is subtracted. The result, rounded to two decimal places, is published as `cooling_capacity_nameplate_kw`. This variant is blind to containment: a leaking hot aisle does not change it at all. Note that the hall-level redundancy stance, not each unit's own `redundancy_role`, is what decides whether a spare is held back; `redundancy_role` documents the designer's intent for the machine, while the capacity arithmetic treats every in-service unit as duty and withholds the single largest contribution.
4. **Airflow-limited capacity.** Each duty unit's `airflow_capacity_kw` is its `measured_airflow_cfm`, multiplied by one minus the hall's leakage fraction, multiplied by its `supply_delta_t_f`, multiplied by the airflow coefficient 1.08, divided by 3412 to convert BTU per hour into kilowatts, and rounded to five decimal places. A unit that has not been commissioned carries 0.00 here. Gross airflow capacity is the sum of those per-unit figures across the duty set, and — exactly as in the nameplate variant — when `cooling_redundancy` is `N_PLUS_1` and the duty set is non-empty the largest single per-unit figure is subtracted. The result, rounded to two decimal places, is published as `cooling_capacity_airflow_kw`.
5. **The gate.** `cooling_capacity_kw` is the smaller of the two variants, and `cooling_margin_kw` is that gating capacity less `thermal_load_kw`, rounded to two decimal places. Which variant binds flips as leakage changes, which is why both are published side by side.
6. **Posture.** A margin of 0.00 or better yields `thermal_posture` `NOMINAL`. A negative margin no worse than 25.00 kW short — that is, a margin at or above negative 25.00 — yields `CONSTRAINED`. A margin worse than 25.00 kW short yields `CRITICAL`.

When a rack power-on request is evaluated, the candidate rack's heat contribution is added into the demand figure exactly once for the purpose of the margin check, and the model is not re-entered afterwards.

**What the thermal posture gates.** A hall that is not `NOMINAL` cannot progress commissioning. Three operations require `thermal_posture` to be exactly `NOMINAL` and are refused with `ERR_THERMAL_POSTURE_NOT_NOMINAL` otherwise: executing a commissioning package's script, recording a witness decision on a package where that `decision` is `WITNESSED`, and starting a load bank run. A witness decision of `REJECTED` is exempt, since refusing to sign a test in a thermally degraded room must always be possible. Beyond that hard gate, a posture other than `NOMINAL` degrades the hall's release posture, as §3.8 describes.

**The containment-to-commissioning cascade.** A failing leakage test is not a local event; it propagates in three links. First, the zone's `leakage_status` becomes `FAIL` and its measured values are stored. Second, because the hall's worst measured leakage has risen, the cooling model is re-derived: `cooling_capacity_airflow_kw` falls, `cooling_margin_kw` falls with it, and `thermal_posture` may drop to `CONSTRAINED` or `CRITICAL`. Third, because the posture is no longer `NOMINAL`, every commissioning package of that hall at level `L4` or `L5` whose `witness_status` is `PENDING` is moved to `BLOCKED`, and each such package's `package_state` becomes `BLOCKED` with it. The reverse also holds automatically: whenever any recompute of the cooling model returns `thermal_posture` to `NOMINAL` — a passing re-test, a remediation that drops a bad zone out of the selection, a unit returning to service, or a rack ceasing to draw load — every package whose `witness_status` is `BLOCKED` returns to `PENDING` and its `package_state` is re-derived. Both a passing test and a remediation therefore re-derive the cooling model and the hall's release posture in addition to their own local effects.

### 3.6. Cages, Cameras and Card Readers

Multi-tenant white space needs physical segregation, and a **cage** is that instrument. A `SecurityAdministrator` builds one by naming the `hall_id` it stands in and the `tenant_id` occupying it, giving it a `label`, and stating its mesh `perimeter_length_m` (strictly positive, one decimal place). Two acceptance thresholds may be set at build time and both have defaults: `required_camera_coverage_pct`, the camera coverage the cage must reach before capacity inside it may be released (greater than 0 and at most 100.00, two decimal places, defaulting to 95.00), and `required_reader_zone_count`, the number of distinct access-control reader zones that must be live (at least 1, defaulting to 2). A new cage stands at `FIT_OUT` with `camera_coverage_pct` at 0.00 and `active_reader_zone_count` at 0. Cage lists may be narrowed by `hall_id`, `tenant_id` and `operational_status`.

Security hardware is then planned onto the cage one device at a time. A device declaration names its `cage_id`, its `device_type` — `CAMERA` or `CARD_READER` — and a `label`. Two fields are conditional on the type and each must be present for its own type and absent for the other: `covered_length_m` states how many metres of cage perimeter a camera can see (strictly positive, one decimal place) and is required for `CAMERA` while it must be omitted for `CARD_READER`; `reader_zone_label` names the access-control zone a reader arms and is required for `CARD_READER` while it must be omitted for `CAMERA`. A violation of either rule is a payload-shape failure and is reported as `ERR_VALIDATION`. Every device starts at `PLANNED` and, crucially, contributes nothing at all to coverage while it is there — an installed but uncommissioned camera leaves coverage at 0.00. Device lists may be narrowed by `cage_id`, `device_type` and `status`.

Commissioning a device advances it `PLANNED → ACTIVE`; attempting it on a device that is already `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On success the owning cage's two coverage figures are re-derived. `camera_coverage_pct` is 0.00 when the cage has no `ACTIVE` camera at all; otherwise it is the sum of `covered_length_m` across the cage's `ACTIVE` cameras, divided by the cage's `perimeter_length_m`, multiplied by 100, capped at 100.00 so that overlapping camera fields cannot report more than full coverage, and rounded to two decimal places. `active_reader_zone_count` is the number of **distinct** `reader_zone_label` values among the cage's `ACTIVE` card readers — two readers arming the same zone count once. Comparisons against the cage's requirements are inclusive, so a cage measuring exactly 95.00 against a 95.00 requirement satisfies it.

The cage's own state follows from those figures. When the re-derived coverage reaches or exceeds `required_camera_coverage_pct` and the re-derived distinct reader-zone count reaches or exceeds `required_reader_zone_count`, and the cage is still at `FIT_OUT`, the cage advances `FIT_OUT → SECURED` in that same request. Device commissioning also increments the cage's `version`, which matters because that version is what a release packet must prove it has observed.

Beyond securing, a cage has three further movements, all system-driven and all consequences of what happens to the racks inside it. A cage at `SECURED` or at `RELEASED` falls to `DEGRADED` the moment any rack inside it becomes `AT_RISK`. A cage at `DEGRADED` returns to `SECURED` once no rack inside it is `AT_RISK` any more and no release packet of that cage stands at `ACCEPTED`. A cage at `DEGRADED` instead returns to `RELEASED` once no rack inside it is `AT_RISK` and a release packet of that cage returns to `ACCEPTED`. And a cage at `SECURED` advances to `RELEASED` when operations accept the release packet naming it. §3.8 and §3.9 give the full chains.

### 3.7. Phased Commissioning: Packages, Load Banks and Independent Witnessing

Commissioning is where the building stops being a construction project and starts being a machine. A `CommissioningAgent` opens a **commissioning package** for a hall that is in `COMMISSIONING` — no earlier and no later; attempting it in any other phase is refused with `ERR_HALL_PHASE_INVALID`. The package names its `hall_id`, its integrated-systems `level` (one of `L1`, `L2`, `L3`, `L4` or `L5`), a `title`, the load its scripts must demonstrate in `target_load_kw` (strictly positive, two decimal places), and the hall version the agent has observed in `expected_hall_version` as §2.8 requires. A new package opens with `script_revision` at 0, `executed_by_user_id`, `witnessed_by_user_id`, `superseded_by_package_id` and `measured_load_kw` all null. Package lists may be narrowed by `hall_id`, `level` and `package_state`.

**Two dimensions that move independently.** A package carries two parallel state fields. `script_status` walks `NOT_STARTED → SCRIPT_APPROVED → EXECUTED` and tracks the test script. `witness_status` walks between `PENDING`, `WITNESSED`, `REJECTED` and `BLOCKED` and tracks the independent signature. Neither dimension constrains the other's storage: approving a script leaves `witness_status` untouched, and a witness decision does not by itself move `script_status` forward. What the reader sees as *the* state of a package is `package_state`, a third field that is **never written directly** — it is always re-derived from the other two, in this fixed branch order:

1. If `superseded_by_package_id` is set, the package state is `SUPERSEDED`.
2. Otherwise, if `witness_status` is `BLOCKED`, the package state is `BLOCKED`.
3. Otherwise, if `witness_status` is `REJECTED`, the package state is `FAILED`.
4. Otherwise, if `script_status` is `EXECUTED` and `witness_status` is `WITNESSED`, the package state is `CERTIFIED`.
5. Otherwise, if `script_status` is either `SCRIPT_APPROVED` or `EXECUTED`, the package state is `IN_EXECUTION`.
6. Otherwise the package state is `DRAFT`.

The branch order is the specification: being superseded outranks everything, and being blocked outranks having been rejected. The projection runs after every write to either dimension and on supersede.

**Superseding.** Opening a package for a hall and level that already has a package whose `package_state` is anything other than `SUPERSEDED` supersedes that prior package: its `superseded_by_package_id` is set to the new package's identifier, its `package_state` becomes `SUPERSEDED`, and it stops participating in the hall's evaluation for going live. Because that supersede is unconditional — no state precondition blocks a second create — the hall version check of §2.8 is what forces the agent to have observed the hall's current committed load, reserve tier and postures first. The create also increments the hall's `version`.

**Approving a script** advances `script_status` from `NOT_STARTED` to `SCRIPT_APPROVED` and stores the supplied `script_revision`. Three conditions apply beyond the phase gate: the package must not be `SUPERSEDED`, its `script_status` must actually be `NOT_STARTED` (attempting approval on a package already at `SCRIPT_APPROVED` or `EXECUTED` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`), and — the phased-commissioning rule — every **lower** level already created for that hall must be settled, meaning its `package_state` is either `CERTIFIED` or `SUPERSEDED`. An unsettled lower level is refused with `ERR_LEVEL_PREREQ_NOT_MET`; in particular an `L5` package cannot have its script approved until the hall's `L4` package is certified. On success `package_state` moves from `DRAFT` to `IN_EXECUTION`.

**Load bank runs** are the evidence that a script's target load is real. A run is scheduled against a package by naming the `package_id`, the load bank set point in `target_kw` (strictly positive, two decimal places), a `window_start` and `window_end` (the latter strictly after the former), and a `step_pct_sequence` — between one and six integers, strictly ascending, each between 1 and 100, whose last element must be exactly 100, expressing the load steps as percentages of the set point. The parent package's `script_status` must already be `SCRIPT_APPROVED`, or the run is refused with `ERR_SCRIPT_NOT_APPROVED`. A new run stands at `SCHEDULED` with `measured_kw`, `measured_return_delta_t_f`, `result` and `abort_reason` all null. Run lists may be narrowed by `package_id` and `status`.

Scheduling a run competes for the hall's commissioning reserve, and the competition is evaluated over **closed** windows: two runs whose windows merely touch at an endpoint *do* overlap, since a run occupies both endpoints of its window. The peer set is every load bank run of the same hall — reached through each run's package — whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING` and whose window overlaps the requested one, where overlap means the requested start is at or before the peer's end and the requested end is at or after the peer's start. Completed and aborted runs release their slice and are never peers. Two gates then apply, both inclusive:

- The number of peers plus this run must be no more than 2. A third overlapping run in the same white space is refused with `ERR_LOADBANK_CONCURRENCY_EXCEEDED`.
- The reserve demand — the sum of `target_kw` across the peers plus this run's own `target_kw`, rounded to two decimal places — must be no more than the hall's current `commissioning_reserve_kw`. A breach is refused with `ERR_COMMISSIONING_RESERVE_EXCEEDED`. Since that reserve is itself a tiered function of the hall's committed load, a hall carrying little load has only 25.00 kW of reserve to lend to load banking, and two 20.00 kW runs in the same window will already exceed it.

Starting a run advances it `SCHEDULED → RUNNING` and requires three things beyond its own from-state: the logical clock must sit at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end`, or the request is refused with `ERR_OUTSIDE_EXECUTION_WINDOW`; the parent package's `script_status` must still be `SCRIPT_APPROVED`, or `ERR_SCRIPT_NOT_APPROVED`; and the hall's thermal posture must be `NOMINAL`. Completing a run is permitted only from `RUNNING`, advances it `RUNNING → COMPLETED`, and records `measured_kw` and `measured_return_delta_t_f`. Grading is a conjunction: the measured load must be at least the run's `target_kw` multiplied by 0.95 and rounded to two decimal places (a five percent tolerance), **and** the measured return-air delta-T must be at least 8.0 degrees Fahrenheit. Both satisfied gives `result` `PASS`; either short gives `FAIL`. Completion then re-derives the parent package's `measured_load_kw` as the highest `measured_kw` among that package's `COMPLETED` runs, and the run stops occupying the hall's reserve window. Aborting is permitted from either `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING`, drives the run to `ABORTED`, records the supplied `abort_reason`, leaves `measured_kw` null, and frees both the run's reserve slice and its concurrency slot; `COMPLETED` and `ABORTED` are the two terminal states of a run. Starting, completing or aborting a run outside its declared from-state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

**Executing a script** advances `script_status` from `SCRIPT_APPROVED` to `EXECUTED` and stamps `executed_by_user_id` with the acting agent's `X-User-ID`. The hall's thermal posture must be `NOMINAL`. For packages at level `L4` or `L5` there is an additional evidence requirement: the package must already hold at least one load bank run that is `COMPLETED`, whose `result` is `PASS`, and whose `measured_kw` is at least the package's `target_load_kw`. A package that cannot show that run is refused with `ERR_LOADBANK_PREREQ_MISSING`. Levels `L1` through `L3` carry no load bank prerequisite. One further side effect closes the retry loop: if `witness_status` was `REJECTED`, executing the script resets it to `PENDING` so that the witness dimension re-opens for the fresh run. `package_state` is re-derived afterwards.

**Witnessing** is the `WitnessAuthority`'s single act, and the whole point of it is independence. The decision names `decision` as either `WITNESSED` or `REJECTED` and may carry a `witness_note`. The package's `script_status` must be `EXECUTED` — witnessing before execution, or witnessing a `SUPERSEDED` package, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and the witnessing `X-User-ID` must differ from the package's `executed_by_user_id`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_WITNESS_SAME_AS_EXECUTOR`. A `WITNESSED` decision additionally requires the hall's thermal posture to be `NOMINAL`; a `REJECTED` decision may be recorded regardless of posture, because refusing to sign a test in a thermally degraded room is exactly the right outcome.

The two decisions are symmetrical in structure and opposite in effect. `WITNESSED` sets `witness_status` to `WITNESSED`, stamps `witnessed_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, brings `package_state` to `CERTIFIED`, and then triggers the hall's evaluation for going live described in §3.1 — incrementing the hall's `version` when that transition actually fires. `REJECTED` sets `witness_status` to `REJECTED`, brings `package_state` to `FAILED`, and rolls the script dimension **backwards** from `EXECUTED` to `SCRIPT_APPROVED` so that the agent can re-run the script against the same approved revision. That backwards roll is what makes the script dimension cyclic, and it is why the hall's evaluation for going live is re-run after every witness decision, not only after successful ones.

### 3.8. Change Control in Live Space: Methods of Procedure

Once a hall carries production load, a **method of procedure** is the only lawful way to touch it. An `ElectricalContractor` or a `MechanicalContractor` drafts one against exactly one target resource, naming the `hall_id`, the `target_resource_type` (`BUSWAY`, `COOLING_UNIT`, `CONTAINMENT_ZONE` or `RACK`), the `target_resource_id`, a `title`, the `risk_class` (`LOW`, `MEDIUM` or `HIGH`), a written `backout_plan`, and the intended `window_start` and `window_end`. The target reference is polymorphic: it must resolve to a row of the entity type the `target_resource_type` names, and that row's hall must be the same hall the MOP names. A reference that resolves to nothing, or to a resource in another white space, is refused with `ERR_MOP_TARGET_NOT_FOUND`. A new MOP stands at `DRAFT` with `author_user_id` stamped from the drafting `X-User-ID`, `revision_count` at 0, `approval_count` at 0, `required_approval_count` at 0, `tenant_notified_ids` empty, and `pre_execution_target_state`, `executed_at` and `closed_at` all null. MOP lists may be narrowed by `hall_id`, `status`, `target_resource_id` and `risk_class`.

**Submission** advances the MOP `DRAFT → UNDER_REVIEW`. The `backout_plan` must be non-empty; a plan with no back-out is not reviewable. The `window_start` must be at or after the logical clock's current instant — a window that has already begun cannot be submitted, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_OUTSIDE_EXECUTION_WINDOW`. And there is a risk floor: when the hall is `LIVE` and the target type is `BUSWAY` or `COOLING_UNIT`, the MOP's `risk_class` must be `HIGH` before it may leave `DRAFT`, since isolating a busway or pulling a cooling unit out of duty in an occupied room is never routine. A lower classification is refused with `ERR_RISK_CLASS_TOO_LOW`. On success `required_approval_count` is derived: 2 for a `HIGH` risk MOP, 1 for `LOW` or `MEDIUM`.

**Approval decisions** are recorded by a `ChangeManager` or an `OperationsManager` as peer approvers, each decision naming a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `REQUEST_REVISION` or `REJECT` and optionally a `comment`. Every decision appends an immutable approval record carrying the `mop_id` it decides, the `approver_role` and `approver_user_id` captured from the request headers, the `decision`, the `comment`, and a `sequence` that is the one-based position of this record among that MOP's approvals ordered by creation time. Approval records are the audit trail of change control: they are never modified, they carry no `updated_at` and no `version`, and the only way to read them is the approval-trail listing of their MOP, returned in creation order.

Three independence rules guard the quorum. The approving `X-User-ID` may never equal the MOP's `author_user_id` — an author approving their own change is refused with `ERR_APPROVER_IS_AUTHOR`. No `approver_user_id` may appear twice among the `APPROVE` decisions of the current review round, so one person cannot manufacture a two-vote quorum; a second vote from the same individual is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_APPROVER`. And on a `HIGH` risk MOP the second `APPROVE` of the round must come from a **different** `approver_role` than the first, so that a high-risk change always carries one `ChangeManager` and one `OperationsManager`; two votes from the same role are refused with `ERR_APPROVAL_ROLE_NOT_DISTINCT`.

**What a review round is.** The quorum arithmetic counts only the current round. The round begins at the creation instant of the most recent approval record of that MOP whose `decision` was `REQUEST_REVISION`, or — when no such record exists — at the MOP's own creation instant. `approval_count` is then the number of approval records of that MOP whose `decision` is `APPROVE` and whose creation instant is strictly later than the round's start. Ordering is always by creation time ascending and never by identifier. `REJECT` and `REQUEST_REVISION` records never count toward a quorum. `required_approval_count` remains 2 for `HIGH` and 1 otherwise.

The four outcomes of a decision follow from that count. An `APPROVE` that brings the count exactly to the requirement — that is, where the existing `approval_count` plus one equals `required_approval_count` — advances the MOP `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED`. An `APPROVE` that leaves the count short of the requirement appends its record, raises `approval_count` by one, and leaves the MOP at `UNDER_REVIEW`. A `REQUEST_REVISION` appends its record and moves the MOP `UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED`, and because that record closes the round, `approval_count` falls back to 0. A `REJECT` appends its record and moves the MOP `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED`, which is terminal.

**Revision** is the author's answer to a `REVISION_REQUESTED` MOP, and only the author may perform it: a caller whose `X-User-ID` differs from `author_user_id` is refused with `ERR_MOP_EXECUTOR_NOT_AUTHOR`. A revision may supply any of `window_start`, `window_end`, `backout_plan` and `risk_class`, and each supplied value overwrites the stored one. `revision_count` grows by one, the MOP returns `REVISION_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, `required_approval_count` is re-derived from the possibly-new `risk_class`, and `approval_count` starts the fresh round at 0. The cycle `UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW` may run at most twice: a MOP whose `revision_count` has already reached 2 refuses a third revision with `ERR_MOP_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` and stays in `REVISION_REQUESTED`.

**Scheduling** is the `ChangeManager`'s exclusive act and advances an approved MOP `APPROVED → SCHEDULED`. Two rules gate it. The first is window exclusivity on the target resource, evaluated over **half-open** windows with a symmetric settle buffer of sixty minutes — one hour — applied to both sides. The candidate collides with another MOP when the candidate's `window_start` falls strictly before that other MOP's `window_end` extended by the buffer, **and** the candidate's `window_end` extended by the buffer falls strictly after that other MOP's `window_start`. Only MOPs on the same `target_resource_id` whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_EXECUTION` are candidates for collision; MOPs at `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `CLOSED`, `BACKED_OUT` or `REJECTED` occupy no window at all. Because the comparisons are strict, a gap of exactly sixty minutes between two windows does **not** collide. A collision is refused with `ERR_MOP_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Note the deliberate contrast with load banking: MOP windows are half-open and buffered and exclude pairwise, whereas load bank windows are closed, unbuffered and pooled against an aggregate ceiling.

The second scheduling rule is concurrent-occupancy notification. The request may carry `tenant_notified_ids`, the list of tenant identifiers recorded as notified of the change window. When the hall's `tenant_mode` is `MULTI_TENANT` and the MOP's `risk_class` is `HIGH`, that list must contain every tenant that owns at least one cage in the hall — a neighbour whose cage sits beside the work must be told before the work is scheduled. An incomplete list is refused with `ERR_TENANT_NOTIFICATION_INCOMPLETE`. On success the list is stored, the hall's `open_mop_count` is re-derived, and the hall's release posture is re-derived.

**The hall release posture** is a three-way projection re-derived after every MOP schedule, execution, closure and back-out, after every rack status change, and after every recompute of the cooling model. Its branch order is the specification: if the hall has at least one MOP whose status is `IN_EXECUTION`, `release_posture` is `LOCKED`; otherwise, if the hall's `at_risk_rack_count` is greater than 0 **or** its `thermal_posture` is anything other than `NOMINAL`, the posture is `RESTRICTED`; otherwise it is `OPEN`. `LOCKED` therefore outranks `RESTRICTED`. Alongside it, `open_mop_count` is the number of MOPs of that hall whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_EXECUTION`. A `LOCKED` posture blocks rack power-on; anything other than `OPEN` blocks release acceptance.

**Execution** is where change control meets physics, and only the author may drive it. The MOP must be `SCHEDULED`; the logical clock must sit at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end`, or the request is refused with `ERR_OUTSIDE_EXECUTION_WINDOW`; the acting `X-User-ID` must equal `author_user_id`, or `ERR_MOP_EXECUTOR_NOT_AUTHOR`; and no other MOP of the same hall may already be `IN_EXECUTION`, since one live change at a time per white space is the whole discipline — a second concurrent execution is refused with `ERR_HALL_MOP_ALREADY_EXECUTING`.

On success the MOP moves `SCHEDULED → IN_EXECUTION`, `executed_at` is stamped with the logical clock's current instant, and `pre_execution_target_state` captures the target resource's status verbatim so that it can be put back later. The hall's `release_posture` becomes `LOCKED`, because `open_mop_count` now includes an executing MOP. Then the cascade fires, and its shape depends on the target type:

- A `BUSWAY` target is driven `ENERGIZED → ISOLATED`. Because the run is isolated, every feed on it that was `ENERGIZED` moves to `DERATED` — and a derated feed still counts toward that run's `committed_kw` and toward the hall's `committed_kw`, so the ledger does not move. Because each affected rack has lost an energised feed, `energized_feed_count` is re-derived on those racks, and any rack whose energised feed count now falls below its `required_feed_count` records its current status in `pre_risk_status` and moves to `AT_RISK` — from `POWERED` or from `RELEASED`, whichever it held. The hall's `custody_ops_count` falls by one for each such rack, its `custody_fitout_count` rises by one, and its `at_risk_rack_count` rises by one. Because those racks are at risk, each one's cage (where the rack has a `cage_id`) moves from `SECURED` or `RELEASED` to `DEGRADED`, and any release packet of such a cage that stood at `ACCEPTED` moves to `SUSPENDED`.
- A `COOLING_UNIT` target is driven `IN_SERVICE → MAINTENANCE`, and because the unit has left the duty set, the hall cooling model is re-derived — which can lower `cooling_margin_kw` and drop `thermal_posture`, with all the consequences §3.5 describes.
- A `CONTAINMENT_ZONE` or `RACK` target has its status captured in `pre_execution_target_state` in the same way; what the covering MOP authorises is the live-space work on that resource described in §3.5 and §3.4.

Execution also re-derives the custody partition and the release posture, and increments the hall's `version`.

**Closing and backing out** are the two branches out of `IN_EXECUTION`, and they are deliberately identical in effect and different only in verdict. Closing records the work as completed and may carry a `closeout_note`; backing out records it as abandoned and may carry a `backout_note`. Both require the acting `X-User-ID` to equal `author_user_id`, and both stamp `closed_at` with the logical clock's current instant — closing moves the MOP to `CLOSED`, backing out moves it to `BACKED_OUT`, and both are terminal.

Both then compensate the entire cascade, exactly. The target resource is restored to `pre_execution_target_state`: an isolated run returns `ISOLATED → ENERGIZED` when the captured state was `ENERGIZED`, and a unit in maintenance returns `MAINTENANCE → IN_SERVICE` when the captured state was `IN_SERVICE`. Because the run is energised again, every feed on it that sits at `DERATED` returns to `ENERGIZED` and the affected racks' `energized_feed_count` is re-derived. Each rack at `AT_RISK` whose energised feed count is once more equal to its `required_feed_count` returns to whatever `pre_risk_status` recorded — to `POWERED` if that is what it held, or to `RELEASED` if that is what it held — and `pre_risk_status` is cleared back to null. The hall's custody counters and `at_risk_rack_count` are restored. Each cage at `DEGRADED` with no remaining at-risk rack returns to `SECURED` when no packet of that cage stands at `ACCEPTED`, or to `RELEASED` when a packet of that cage returns to `ACCEPTED`; each packet at `SUSPENDED` whose cage has no remaining at-risk rack returns to `ACCEPTED`. A unit returning to service re-derives the cooling model. Finally the custody partition and the release posture are re-derived, and the custody counts land exactly on their pre-execution values — that identity is the test of whether the compensation was complete.

### 3.9. Capacity Release to Operations

The last act of a fit-out is a handover: a `CommissioningAgent` hands a cage's racks, and the load they draw, to the operations team, and operations either takes custody or refuses it. The instrument is a **release packet**, opened against exactly one cage by naming the `hall_id`, the `cage_id`, the `as_built_drawing_ref` identifying the drawing set that accompanies the handover (non-empty, between 1 and 64 characters), and the cage version the agent has observed in `expected_cage_version` as §2.8 requires. A new packet stands at `DRAFT` with `accepted_by_user_id` and `rejected_reason` null, and with three figures computed on the spot. Packet lists may be narrowed by `hall_id`, `cage_id` and `status`, and — because a packet is reachable from a cage and a cage is owned by a tenant — a `TenantRepresentative` sees only the packets of their own cages.

**What is in scope.** The in-scope set is every rack whose `cage_id` is this cage and whose status is `POWERED`, `AT_RISK` or `RELEASED`. Racks at `PLANNED` or `INSTALLED` are not part of a handover, because nothing has been energised for them yet. From that set, `rack_in_scope_count` is simply the count. `released_kw` is the cage-restricted branch of the chargeable roll-up of §3.4: each in-scope rack's `design_kw` multiplied by 1.045 and rounded to two decimal places **first**, then summed, then rounded to two decimal places again. And `asset_tag_completion_pct` is the share of the in-scope set that carries an `asset_tag` — a rack counts as tagged only when its `asset_tag` is neither null nor the empty string — expressed as the tagged count divided by `rack_in_scope_count`, multiplied by 100, rounded to two decimal places. When `rack_in_scope_count` is 0 the percentage is 0.00, **not** 100.00: an empty scope is vacuously untagged, not vacuously complete. All three figures are recomputed on packet creation and again on submission.

**Superseding.** Opening a packet for a cage that already has a packet at `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED` or `SUSPENDED` supersedes that prior packet: the prior packet's status becomes `SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal, and the new packet's `supersedes_packet_id` records the prior packet's identifier. Packets already at `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED` are left alone, since they no longer represent a live claim on the cage. The create also increments the cage's `version`, which is why the version check matters: a second agent working from a stale cage view would silently discard a submission in flight, and `ERR_STALE_CAGE_VERSION` with status 412 is what prevents it.

**Submission** advances a packet `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and is the agent's assertion that the paperwork is complete. Three gates apply, checked after the figures are recomputed. The scope must be non-empty — `rack_in_scope_count` at least 1 — or the request is refused with `ERR_RELEASE_SCOPE_EMPTY`. Asset tagging must be exactly complete — `asset_tag_completion_pct` equal to 100.00, with no tolerance — or the request is refused with `ERR_ASSET_TAGS_INCOMPLETE`; a cage of forty racks with thirty-nine tagged sits at 97.50 and does not submit. And the cage's physical security must meet both of its floors: `camera_coverage_pct` at or above `required_camera_coverage_pct`, **and** `active_reader_zone_count` at or above `required_reader_zone_count`. Either floor short is refused with `ERR_CAGE_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` — the same conjunction that governs the cage's own securing in §3.6, applied a second time at handover because a device can fail between the two moments. On success `released_kw` and `asset_tag_completion_pct` are frozen for the review, so that operations reviews the numbers the agent submitted.

**Acceptance** is the `OperationsManager`'s act and advances a packet `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`. Three preconditions guard it. The hall must be `LIVE`, since operations cannot take custody of a room that has not been certified, and any other phase is refused with `ERR_HALL_PHASE_INVALID`. The hall's `release_posture` must be `OPEN`, which by the projection of §3.8 means no MOP of the hall is `IN_EXECUTION`, the hall has no at-risk racks, and its `thermal_posture` is `NOMINAL`. And every rack of the cage that currently sits at `POWERED` must have `energized_feed_count` equal to `required_feed_count`, so that no rack is handed over already running on fewer feeds than its topology demands. A posture that is `LOCKED` or `RESTRICTED`, or a `POWERED` rack short of a feed, is refused with `ERR_RELEASE_POSTURE_BLOCKED`.

Acceptance is the only place custody actually changes hands, and its side effects are the point of the whole packet. The packet's status becomes `ACCEPTED` and `accepted_by_user_id` is stamped with the accepting manager's `X-User-ID`. Every rack of the cage at `POWERED` moves `POWERED → RELEASED`. The cage itself moves `SECURED → RELEASED`. The hall's custody partition is then re-derived — and because both `POWERED` and `RELEASED` sit on the operations side of the partition, `custody_ops_count` and `custody_fitout_count` do not move at all when racks are released; what changes is only which rack statuses make up the operations side. The cage's `version` increments.

**Rejection** advances a packet `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` on the `OperationsManager`'s word, recording the supplied `rejected_reason`. Rejection is terminal: a rejected packet is never revived, re-submitted or amended, and the only way forward is for the agent to open a fresh packet for the cage — which, since a `REJECTED` packet is not in the supersede set, does not touch the rejected record at all. The rejected packet remains as the audit trail of why the first handover attempt failed.

**Suspension and re-entry** are the release packet's answer to live-space work, and they make `SUSPENDED` the only re-entrant state in this API. When cascade A drives a rack of an accepted cage to `AT_RISK` — because a MOP isolated the busway feeding it — the packet moves `ACCEPTED → SUSPENDED`, since operations cannot be said to hold custody of a rack that is running short of feeds. When the covering MOP is closed or backed out and the compensation of §3.8 restores every rack of the cage out of `AT_RISK`, the packet moves back `SUSPENDED → ACCEPTED`, and the cage returns from `DEGRADED` to `RELEASED` in the same step. A suspended packet may also be superseded outright, if the agent chooses to redo the handover rather than wait for the change window to close. The precondition on re-entry is exact — the cage must have zero racks at `AT_RISK` — so a cage caught by two overlapping MOPs on different busways stays suspended until the last of them is settled.
