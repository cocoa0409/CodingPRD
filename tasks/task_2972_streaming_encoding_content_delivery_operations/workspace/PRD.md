# Streaming Encoding & Delivery Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A modern streaming service does not "upload a video and press publish". Between a studio's delivery of a source master and a subscriber pressing play sit half a dozen specialist desks, each with its own vocabulary, its own failure modes, and its own veto: quality control inspects the master for visual artifacts, loudness compliance and caption presence; encoding authors a rung ladder across resolutions, bitrates and codecs and proves it against a device compatibility matrix; rights and packaging wrap the renditions in DRM and author the entitlement rules a license server will enforce; delivery negotiates multi-vendor CDN capacity, steers traffic on measured performance, and reserves burst capacity for live events; and the catalog desk holds the contract dates that decide, to the millisecond, when a title may legally be watched in a given region. Today those desks coordinate through spreadsheets and chat messages, which is why a codec deprecation can quietly leave a flagship title unplayable on a third of the installed base, and why a contract expiry can slip past unnoticed until a licensor complains.

This document specifies the operations backbone that replaces that coordination with a single enforced state model. The system's ambition is narrow but strict: every consequence of an operator's action must be computed by the server, not remembered by the operator. When an encoding engineer locks a new ladder, the previously shipping ladder of the same codec family must be superseded, the DRM packages bound to it must go stale, the title's packaging readiness must drop, and — if the title was live — it must come down and its licensing windows must close, all inside the one request. When a vendor's measured playback quality collapses, every routing policy pointing at that vendor must move off it without a human noticing first. When a reprocessing job is aborted, the encode budget it held must return to the title with zero drift.

Three design commitments follow from that ambition and run through every section below. First, **time is explicit**: the server keeps a logical UTC clock and every window, expiry and sweep decision reads that clock rather than wall time, so an operations team (and a test harness) can drive a contract year through the platform in seconds. Second, **derived values are never client-supplied**: readiness scores, health scores, compatibility scores, cost estimates, selected vendors and capacity ledgers are all computed from stored facts under pinned evaluation orders, and the API exposes them read-only. Third, **conserved quantities must balance**: reserved CDN bandwidth moves between vendors on failover without changing in total, and encode budget released by an abort returns to exactly its pre-run value. An implementation that gets the happy paths right but lets either quantity drift has failed the requirement.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operator Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the desk the caller acts for. If either header is absent, is an empty string, or contains nothing but whitespace, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The same 401 with the same `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` identifier is returned when `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not exactly one of the seven role names below — matching is case-sensitive and exact, and an unknown or differently-cased role is rejected before any endpoint's own permission list is consulted. Only once the role is recognised does the system ask whether that role is authorised for the requested operation; when it is not, the answer is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `ContentOps`, `QcAnalyst`, `EncodingEngineer`, `DrmOperator`, `DeliveryEngineer`, `PlatformAdmin` and `VendorOperator`. They are **peer** roles, not a hierarchy: each owns a different slice of the pipeline and no role is a superset of another.

- `ContentOps` is the catalog and licensing desk. It alone registers titles, edits title metadata and budget, issues legal takedowns, schedules and cancels licensing windows, registers source masters, and re-ingests corrected masters. It shares the availability sweep with `PlatformAdmin` and can read titles, windows, masters, QC reports, ladders, DRM packages, routing policies, live reservations and reprocessing jobs.
- `QcAnalyst` is the quality-control desk. It alone runs QC on an ingested master and requests remediation of a failure, and deliberately cannot register or edit the masters it judges. It reads titles, a title's masters, individual masters and their QC reports.
- `EncodingEngineer` is the encoding desk. It alone authors, validates and locks encoding ladders and creates, runs and aborts reprocessing jobs. It reads titles, a title's masters, ladders, device profiles and reprocessing jobs.
- `DrmOperator` is the rights and packaging desk. It alone creates DRM packages, packages and revokes them, and attaches entitlement rules. It shares playback entitlement evaluation with `DeliveryEngineer`, and reads titles, device profiles, ladders, packages and entitlement rules.
- `DeliveryEngineer` is the delivery desk. It alone creates routing policies and re-steers them, and alone creates, arms, fails over and cancels live-event reservations. It shares playback-metric ingestion with `PlatformAdmin` and entitlement evaluation with `DrmOperator`, and reads titles, device profiles, individual vendors, packages, entitlement rules, policies, metric windows, reservations and a title's licensing windows.
- `PlatformAdmin` is the platform operator. It alone sets the logical clock, registers device profiles and registers CDN vendors. It shares the availability sweep with `ContentOps`, metric ingestion with `DeliveryEngineer`, and vendor updates with `VendorOperator`. Every read endpoint listed for the other roles is also readable by `PlatformAdmin`, including the per-asset QC report list, which is readable by exactly `ContentOps`, `QcAnalyst` and `PlatformAdmin`. `PlatformAdmin` is emphatically **not** an override role: it cannot create titles, ladders, packages, policies or reservations.
- `VendorOperator` is a CDN vendor's own operator. It creates nothing. It reads and updates its own vendor record and reads only the delivery data scoped to itself.

Six of the seven roles carry an **opaque** identity: when `X-User-Role` is `ContentOps`, `QcAnalyst`, `EncodingEngineer`, `DrmOperator`, `DeliveryEngineer` or `PlatformAdmin`, the accompanying `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record or log it, but it never compares it to any entity's `id` and no ownership check anywhere in this specification consults it.

`VendorOperator` is the single exception and is **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `VendorOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CdnVendor` row, and two consequences follow. First, that identifier must resolve: if no stored `CdnVendor` has that `id`, the request fails with `ERR_VENDOR_IDENTITY_UNKNOWN` (HTTP 403), and this check applies to every endpoint whose authorised-role list contains `VendorOperator`. Second, the vendor's reach is scoped to itself: reading or updating a single vendor record requires the path identifier to equal `X-User-ID`; listing playback metrics requires a `vendor_id` filter equal to `X-User-ID`; listing live reservations requires one of `primary_vendor_id`, `failover_vendor_id` or `active_vendor_id` to be supplied and equal to `X-User-ID`; and listing routing policies requires `primary_vendor_id` or `backup_vendor_id` to be supplied and equal to `X-User-ID`. A `VendorOperator` addressing another vendor's record, or listing any of those three collections without the required self-scoping filter, is rejected with `ERR_VENDOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). The scope rule governs exactly five operations: reading one CDN vendor, updating one CDN vendor, listing playback metrics, listing live reservations, and listing routing policies.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

List endpoints in this API are paged by two query parameters: `page`, which is 1-based and defaults to 1, and `page_size`, which defaults to 20, accepts a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. A `page` below 1, or a `page_size` below 1 or above 100, is a payload violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being silently clamped. For list responses the `meta` block carries the four pagination fields `page`, `page_size`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before paging and `has_more` states whether any row remains beyond the returned page.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Five collections override that default because their business ordering is load-bearing, and each override is stated with the endpoint that owns it: a title's licensing windows are ordered by `window_start_at` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; an asset's QC reports are ordered by `attempt_number` ascending; playback metric windows are ordered by `window_start_at` descending and then `created_at` descending; live reservations are ordered by `window_start_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending; a package's entitlement rules are ordered by `priority` descending then `created_at` ascending; and reprocessing jobs are ordered by `priority_rank` descending then `created_at` ascending. No ordering, ranking, selection or tie-break rule anywhere in this system consults a resource's `id`; wherever a comparison would otherwise be undecided, `created_at` ascending is the final tie-break.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID string with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp recording when the row was stored. Timestamps everywhere in this API — `created_at`, `updated_at`, and every domain instant such as `window_start_at`, `window_end_at`, `activated_at`, `closed_at`, `packaged_at`, `failover_at`, `evaluated_at` and `last_sweep_at` — are ISO 8601 UTC with exactly three fractional digits.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every successful mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on each mutation of that row. Three resources are deliberately immutable and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at`, with **no** `updated_at` and **no** `version` field at all: the `QualityControlReport` produced by a QC run, the `EntitlementRule` attached to a DRM package, and the `PlaybackMetricWindow` ingested from the delivery pipeline. These three are written once and never edited or deleted; a superseding decision is expressed by writing a newer row, not by amending an older one. Every other resource — `Title`, `MezzanineAsset`, `EncodingLadder`, `DeviceProfile`, `DrmPackage`, `CdnVendor`, `RoutingPolicy`, `LiveEventReservation`, `AvailabilityWindow` and `ReprocessJob` — carries the full set of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, in the order they appear in the URL. When a request addresses a sub-resource of a parent, the parent is looked up first, and if it does not exist the parent's own not-found error is returned without the child ever being consulted. Each entity family has its own 404 identifier: `ERR_TITLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LADDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEVICE_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_JOB_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_METRIC_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`. The same catalogue governs foreign keys arriving in a request body or query string — a `title_id`, `ladder_id`, `source_ladder_id`, `derived_from_ladder_id`, `vendor_id`, `primary_vendor_id`, `failover_vendor_id`, `device_profile_id` or `package_id` that names nothing stored produces the matching 404 — with one difference: body and query foreign keys are resolved in the order the fields are declared in the endpoint's input list, so the first unresolvable reference in that order is the one reported. `ERR_METRIC_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND` arises only when reading a single playback metric window by its path identifier, and `ERR_DEVICE_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND` covers both an unresolvable `device_profile_id` and a playback entitlement check naming a `device_class` for which no `ACTIVE` device profile exists.

### 2.5. The Logical Broadcast Clock and the Four-Phase Availability Sweep

Nothing in this system reads the host machine's wall clock. The server maintains a **logical UTC clock**, and every temporal decision — whether a licensing window has opened, whether a live event has ended, whether a metric window is admissible, whether a title has expired — is taken against that clock's current value. At process startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; before any operator moves it, that instant is what the server sees, and it must never fall back to real time. A `PlatformAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant, and the response echoes the `current_utc` value now in force. Moving the clock is a pure assignment: on its own it transitions nothing, expires nothing and publishes nothing.

The consequences of time passing are applied by the **availability sweep**, an operations checkpoint runnable by `PlatformAdmin` or `ContentOps`. The sweep applies four phases against the clock's current value, in this fixed and load-bearing order:

1. **Phase 1 — licensing windows.** Each `AvailabilityWindow` in `SCHEDULED` whose `window_start_at` is at or before now, and whose `window_end_at` is strictly after now, becomes `ACTIVE` with `activated_at` set to the clock's current instant. Each window in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` whose `window_end_at` is at or before now becomes `CLOSED` with `closed_at` set to the current instant. A window whose entire interval already lies in the past when it is first swept therefore goes straight to `CLOSED` and never passes through `ACTIVE`.
2. **Phase 2 — live-event reservations.** Each `LiveEventReservation` in `ARMED` whose `window_start_at` is at or before now, while now is still strictly before `window_end_at`, becomes `LIVE`. A reservation still sitting in `RESERVED` is never started by the sweep — arming is a manual precondition. Each reservation in `LIVE` or `FAILED_OVER` whose `window_end_at` is at or before now becomes `ENDED`, and its held bandwidth is released from whichever vendor currently carries it.
3. **Phase 3 — reprocessing jobs.** Each `ReprocessJob` in `RUNNING` either completes or fails, and a completing job creates its result ladder in state `LOCKED`, which supersedes the source ladder and cascades staleness into DRM packaging.
4. **Phase 4 — title publication.** Each `Title` is re-evaluated for a publication change, and **at most one** `availability_state` transition is applied per title per sweep. The publication gate is `readiness_score_certified` of at least 80.00.

The ordering matters because later phases read what earlier phases wrote. Phase 4 evaluates window activity as it stands *after* Phase 1, and it evaluates `packaging_state` as it stands *after* Phase 3 — so a job that completes in Phase 3 and drives the title's packaging to `STALE` causes the very same sweep to take the title down in Phase 4 rather than publish it.

The sweep returns a per-phase count object with exactly nine integer fields: `windows_activated`, `windows_closed`, `reservations_started`, `reservations_ended`, `jobs_completed`, `jobs_failed`, `titles_published`, `titles_taken_down` and `titles_expired`. Each counts the transitions that this invocation actually applied. The sweep is idempotent for a fixed clock value: running it a second time without moving the clock transitions nothing and returns all nine counts as zero.

### 2.6. Request Validation Order

Requests are validated as an ascending ladder of twenty rules. The **first** rule violated determines the single error returned, and no later rule is evaluated once a rule has fired — even when a later rule's condition also holds. This is the difference between a diagnosable API and a guessing game, so the ladder is specified in full:

1. **Rung 1 — credentials present.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty; absent, empty or whitespace-only fails with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Rung 2 — role recognised.** `X-User-Role` is exactly one of the seven role names. Unknown or differently-cased values fail with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401) here, before any endpoint's role list is consulted. Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Rung 3 — role permitted.** The caller's role appears in the endpoint's authorised-role list; otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
4. **Rung 4 — vendor identity resolves.** When the role is `VendorOperator`, `X-User-ID` equals the `id` of an existing `CdnVendor`; otherwise `ERR_VENDOR_IDENTITY_UNKNOWN` (403). Applies to every endpoint whose role list includes `VendorOperator`.
5. **Rung 5 — vendor scope.** A `VendorOperator` addresses only its own vendor scope, as described in §2.1; otherwise `ERR_VENDOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). Applies to exactly five operations: reading one CDN vendor, updating one CDN vendor, listing playback metrics, listing live reservations, and listing routing policies.
6. **Rung 6 — payload schema.** Every required input is present; no unknown field is supplied; every value has the correct JSON type; every enum value is drawn from its declared set; every instant parses as ISO 8601 UTC; decimals sit within their declared scale; integers sit within their declared range; every `region_code` is two uppercase letters (or the single literal `*` where a rule explicitly allows a wildcard); `page` is at least 1 and `page_size` is between 1 and 100 inclusive. A `rungs` array that is empty, or whose elements do not each carry `label`, `height_px` and `bitrate_kbps`, also fails here. The error is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), and it applies to every endpoint.
7. **Rung 7 — path existence.** Every identifier in the URL path resolves to a stored row, checked left to right, returning that family's 404 identifier from §2.4. Applies to every endpoint with a path parameter.
8. **Rung 8 — reference existence.** Every foreign key in the request body or query string resolves, checked in the order the endpoint declares its inputs, returning `ERR_TITLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LADDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEVICE_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate. Applies to nine creates: source-master registration, ladder creation, DRM package creation, package packaging, routing-policy creation, playback-metric ingestion, live-reservation creation, reprocessing-job creation and playback entitlement evaluation.
9. **Rung 9 — natural-key uniqueness.** A `Title`'s `external_ref`, a `CdnVendor`'s `vendor_code` and a `DeviceProfile`'s `device_class` are each unique across all stored rows, compared case-sensitively, failing with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EXTERNAL_REF`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_VENDOR_CODE` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_DEVICE_CLASS` (all 409). Applies to title registration, vendor registration and device-profile registration.
10. **Rung 10 — own-entity state.** The entity named in the path is in a state that admits the requested transition, failing with `ERR_ASSET_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_LADDER_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PACKAGE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_RESERVATION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_JOB_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_WINDOW_STATE_INVALID` (all 409). Applies to every action endpoint on a source master, an encoding ladder, a DRM package, a live reservation, a reprocessing job or a licensing window. A `RoutingPolicy` is deliberately exempt: every one of its states admits a re-steer, so a re-steer never fails this rung.
11. **Rung 11 — cross-entity state.** Preconditions that live on a *different* entity than the one being mutated, failing with `ERR_NO_PASSED_ASSET`, `ERR_ASSET_REMEDIATION_PENDING`, `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_READY`, `ERR_LADDER_NOT_LOCKED`, `ERR_LADDER_NOT_BINDABLE` or `ERR_TITLE_LEGALLY_BLOCKED` (all 409). Applies to six operations: locking a ladder, arming a reservation, creating a DRM package, packaging a DRM package, creating a reprocessing job and scheduling a licensing window. One arm of this rung is **cascading**: the requirement that a reprocessing job's source ladder be `LOCKED` is evaluated only when the title already owns at least one source master whose `qc_state` is not `QC_REJECTED`. A title with no eligible source master therefore skips this rung entirely and falls through to rung 18's `ERR_REPROCESS_NO_SOURCE_ASSET` (422) instead of reporting `ERR_LADDER_NOT_LOCKED`.
12. **Rung 12 — optimistic concurrency.** Where a concurrency rule requires it, `expected_title_version` equals the current `version` of the target `Title`; otherwise `ERR_STALE_TITLE_VERSION` (412). Applies to ladder creation and reprocessing-job creation.
13. **Rung 13 — content-protection floor.** An entitlement rule's `hdcp_level` is at or above its package's `hdcp_floor` under the ordering where `NONE` is lowest, `TYPE_0` is next and `TYPE_1` is highest, failing with `ERR_HDCP_FLOOR_VIOLATION`; a rule may not set `offline_playback_allowed` to true when its package's `persistent_license_allowed` is false, failing with `ERR_OFFLINE_NOT_LICENSED`; and a package's `drm_systems` must carry at least one system and must include `WIDEVINE` whenever its `hdcp_floor` is `TYPE_1`, failing with `ERR_DRM_SYSTEM_UNSUPPORTED`. All three are 422 and apply to DRM package creation and entitlement-rule attachment.
14. **Rung 14 — cross-aggregate consistency.** Five referential rules, all 422: a package's `ladder_id` must name a ladder of the same `title_id`, and a job's `source_ladder_id` likewise, failing with `ERR_LADDER_TITLE_MISMATCH`; an ingested metric window's `region_code` must appear in the named vendor's `regions`, failing with `ERR_VENDOR_REGION_NOT_SERVED`; a reservation's title must have `content_type` of `LIVE_EVENT`, failing with `ERR_NOT_LIVE_EVENT_TITLE`, and its `failover_vendor_id` must differ from its `primary_vendor_id`, failing with `ERR_FAILOVER_SAME_AS_PRIMARY`; a job's `target_codec_family` must differ from the source ladder's `codec_family`, failing with `ERR_REPROCESS_CODEC_UNCHANGED`; and an entitlement rule's `region_code` must be the wildcard or a region covered by some `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` licensing window of the package's title, failing with `ERR_RULE_REGION_NOT_LICENSED`. Applies to DRM package creation, metric ingestion, reservation creation, job creation and entitlement-rule attachment.
15. **Rung 15 — interval well-formedness and placement.** All 422: `window_start_at` must be strictly before `window_end_at` on every interval-bearing create, failing with `ERR_WINDOW_BOUNDS_INVALID`; a new licensing window or live reservation must start at or after the current logical clock, failing with `ERR_WINDOW_IN_PAST`; an ingested metric window must end at or before the current logical clock, failing with `ERR_METRIC_WINDOW_IN_FUTURE`; and a live reservation's interval must fall entirely inside one single `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` licensing window of its title — in any region, because a reservation is not region-scoped — failing with `ERR_RESERVATION_OUTSIDE_LICENSE`. Applies to licensing-window scheduling, reservation creation and metric ingestion.
16. **Rung 16 — licensing-window overlap.** A new licensing window must not overlap any `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` window of the same title and the same `region_code` under half-open comparison; abutting endpoints do not overlap. The error is `ERR_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409) and it applies only to licensing-window scheduling.
17. **Rung 17 — shape and cardinality.** All 409: a ladder's `rungs` array must hold between 2 and 8 entries with strictly increasing `height_px` in stored array order and no duplicate `label`, failing with `ERR_RUNG_SHAPE_INVALID`; an entitlement rule's combination of `region_code`, `subscription_tier` and `device_class_scope` must be unique within its package, failing with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENTITLEMENT_RULE`; a reprocessing job may not be created while another job of the same title is `QUEUED` or `RUNNING`, failing with `ERR_REPROCESS_JOB_IN_FLIGHT`; and a title may hold at most one routing policy per `region_code`, failing with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROUTING_POLICY`. A re-steer of a policy whose `excluded_vendor_ids` already covers every eligible vendor is *not* an error under this rung — it is accepted and drives the policy to `UNROUTABLE`. Applies to ladder creation, entitlement-rule attachment, job creation and routing-policy creation.
18. **Rung 18 — selector pool non-emptiness at creation.** Routing-policy creation requires at least one eligible `CdnVendor`, failing with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VENDOR` (422) with nothing persisted; reprocessing-job creation requires at least one source master of the title whose `qc_state` is not `QC_REJECTED`, failing with `ERR_REPROCESS_NO_SOURCE_ASSET` (422).
19. **Rung 19 — bandwidth capacity.** On the vendor being charged, the already-held bandwidth plus the requested `reserved_gbps` must not exceed that vendor's `committed_gbps`; equality is accepted. The error is `ERR_VENDOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422) and it applies to reservation creation and reservation failover.
20. **Rung 20 — encode budget.** Starting a reprocessing job may only debit its `cost_estimate_usd` when that amount is at or below the title's `encode_budget_remaining_usd`; equality is accepted. The error is `ERR_ENCODE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (422) and it applies to the job-run operation.

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis. Authentication and authorisation always precede existence, so probing for a valid identifier with a bad role yields 403 and never 404. And the fiscal rungs are last, so a request that is both unauthorised and over budget reports the authorisation problem.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on the Title Aggregate

Two creates in this system author artefacts whose correctness depends on the shape of the title at the moment the client last observed it, and both therefore require an optimistic-concurrency token naming the parent title's `version`.

Creating an `EncodingLadder` requires the non-optional input field `expected_title_version`, carrying the `version` the client last read from the target `Title`. If that value does not equal the title's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TITLE_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and nothing is stored. The reason is the supersede chain: a newly locked ladder legitimately supersedes the prior locked ladder of the same `codec_family` and cascades staleness into DRM packaging, so a writer holding an outdated view of the title would otherwise author a ladder against a catalog shape that no longer exists.

Creating a `ReprocessJob` likewise requires `expected_title_version` as a non-optional input field carrying the client's last observed `Title` `version`, and likewise rejects a mismatch with `ERR_STALE_TITLE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) without persisting anything. Here the stake is the frozen estimate: a job pins its `cost_estimate_usd` from the title's current source-master durations and the source ladder's rung shape, and its run debits the title's encode budget, so an estimate pinned against a stale view would commit money against inputs that have since moved.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field of any kind. In particular, editing a title, locking a ladder, packaging or revoking a DRM package, re-steering a policy, arming or failing over a reservation, running or aborting a job, cancelling a window and ingesting metrics all carry no `expected_title_version` and no equivalent — supplying one is an unknown field and fails rung 6 with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. What makes a captured token go stale is any mutation that bumps `Title.version`: editing title metadata or budget, scheduling a licensing window, taking a title down manually, creating a ladder, locking a ladder, creating a reprocessing job, running one, and aborting a running one.

### 2.8. Numeric Precision and Rounding Discipline

Money in this system is US dollars carried to **two decimal places**, and every rounding operation anywhere in this specification rounds **half up** unless a rule explicitly states a different scale. Monetary quantities include `encode_budget_usd`, `encode_budget_remaining_usd`, `cost_estimate_usd`, every element of `per_rung_cost_usd`, `committed_usd` and `projected_egress_cost_usd`. Bandwidth quantities — `committed_gbps`, `reserved_gbps` and `released_gbps` — are likewise two decimal places, rounded half up. Score-shaped values are two decimal places rounded half up: `readiness_score_provisional`, `readiness_score_certified`, `device_compat_score`, `severity_index`, `artifact_penalty`, `loudness_deviation_db`, `caption_coverage_pct`, `latest_severity_index`, `quality_index`, `health_score_rolling`, `health_score_contract` and `priority_rank`. Three values carry a wider scale, always rounded half up: `rebuffer_ratio` and `error_rate` are four decimal places, and `cost_per_gb_usd` is four decimal places. The composite ranking score used by weighted traffic steering is computed to four decimal places. Instant comparisons are made at millisecond resolution on ISO 8601 UTC values.

Where a calculation rounds more than once, the **order of rounding is pinned and is part of the contract**, because the order changes the answer. Two places in this specification depend on it and both are called out where the calculation is described: the per-rung encode cost is rounded to cents *before* the rung costs are summed, and the egress surcharge tier is keyed on the rounded base cost *before* the surcharge is added. An implementation that defers rounding to the end of either computation will produce plausible-looking but incorrect figures.

### 2.9. Cascades, Compensations and Conserved Quantities

A single request in this system routinely mutates several resources. The rule is that **every consequence is applied inside the originating request**, transitively, and each affected row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` incremented, exactly as if it had been mutated directly. Two cascades are deep enough to deserve names, and both are specified where the workflow that triggers them is described: the **ladder-lock cascade** runs five hops from a ladder lock through a supersede, a package going stale, a title's packaging dimension going stale, a takedown, and the closure of licensing windows; and the **metric-ingest cascade** runs from a metric window's breach flag through vendor health and vendor state to automatic policy re-steering and a recomputed egress projection. Each hop is caused by the *previous* hop, not by the original call, which matters when a hop's own precondition is not met — the chain simply stops there.

Titles are special in carrying **two independent state dimensions**. `availability_state` is the publication dimension, taking the values `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED`, `PUBLISHED`, `TAKEN_DOWN` and `EXPIRED`, with `DRAFT` as its initial value and `EXPIRED` as its only terminal value. `packaging_state` is the encode-and-DRM readiness dimension, taking the values `UNPACKAGED`, `PACKAGING`, `PACKAGED` and `STALE`, with `UNPACKAGED` as its initial value and no terminal value. Neither dimension constrains the other's transitions directly; they interact only through the specific cascades and gates described in §3.

Two quantities in this system are **conserved**, and the invariants below must hold after every request without drift.

The first is **bandwidth**. A vendor's `reserved_gbps` is always the sum, rounded to two decimals, of `reserved_gbps` over the live reservations charged to that vendor through `active_vendor_id` whose `state` is `RESERVED`, `ARMED`, `LIVE` or `FAILED_OVER`; reservations in `ENDED` or `CANCELLED` hold nothing. Summing `reserved_gbps` across all vendors must therefore equal the sum of `reserved_gbps` across all reservations in those four states. A failover is the compensating action for a primary-side hold: it debits the outgoing vendor and credits the incoming vendor by the identical amount, so the global total is unchanged while the per-vendor split moves. A cancellation or a sweep-driven end instead reduces the global total by exactly the reservation's `released_gbps`.

The second is **money**. A title's `encode_budget_remaining_usd` is always its `encode_budget_usd` minus the sum, rounded to two decimals, of `cost_estimate_usd` over that title's reprocessing jobs whose `state` is `RUNNING` or `COMPLETED`; jobs in `QUEUED`, `ABORTED` or `FAILED` contribute nothing and carry `committed_usd` of 0.00. Equivalently, `encode_budget_remaining_usd` plus the sum of `committed_usd` over all of the title's jobs always equals `encode_budget_usd`. Aborting a running job and a sweep-driven job failure are the compensating actions for the run debit: each returns the remaining budget to exactly its pre-run value and zeroes that job's `committed_usd`.

### 2.10. Two Interval Geometries

This system compares time intervals in two deliberately different ways, and confusing them is the most expensive mistake an implementer can make here.

**Licensing intervals are half-open and buffer-free.** An `AvailabilityWindow` covers `window_start_at` inclusive up to `window_end_at` exclusive. Two windows of the same title and the same `region_code` conflict when the stored window's `window_start_at` is strictly before the requested `window_end_at` **and** the requested `window_start_at` is strictly before the stored window's `window_end_at`; only windows in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` are considered, because `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` windows free their interval for reuse. Under this geometry abutting windows are legal — a window ending at an instant and another beginning at that same instant do not conflict — while a single millisecond of true overlap is rejected outright with `ERR_WINDOW_OVERLAP` rather than aggregated. The same predicate defines a title's **covered regions**: the distinct `region_code` values over its windows in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE`.

**Capacity intervals are closed and buffer-inflated.** A `LiveEventReservation` occupies its `window_start_at` through `window_end_at` inclusive, and each side of a comparison is inflated by its *own* `buffer_minutes` — the request's interval is widened by subtracting its `buffer_minutes` from its start and adding its `buffer_minutes` to its end, and each stored reservation's interval is widened by its own `buffer_minutes` in the same way. `buffer_minutes` defaults to 15 and accepts 0 through 60. A stored reservation collides with the request when its inflated interval starts at or before the request's inflated end and ends at or after the request's inflated start — closed comparison, so merely *touching* inflated endpoints counts as colliding. Only reservations charged to the vendor under examination through `active_vendor_id`, in `state` `RESERVED`, `ARMED`, `LIVE` or `FAILED_OVER`, participate, and a reservation never collides with itself when its own failover is being evaluated. Colliding is not itself an error under this geometry: overlap is expected and is simply aggregated, and only the resulting total exceeding the vendor's `committed_gbps` is rejected.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening the Catalog: Titles, Budgets and Source Masters

Everything begins at the catalog desk. A `ContentOps` operator registers a **title** by supplying its `external_ref`, the studio-side catalog reference, which must be 1 to 40 characters of uppercase letters, digits and hyphens and must be unique across every title ever registered — a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EXTERNAL_REF`. Alongside it come the human-readable `title_name` of 1 to 200 characters, the `content_type`, which is one of `MOVIE`, `EPISODE` or `LIVE_EVENT` and which alone decides whether live capacity may ever be reserved against this title, and the owning `studio_code` of 2 to 10 uppercase characters, which is carried for reporting and influences nothing else. The operator may optionally set `encode_budget_usd`, the total reprocessing budget granted to the title, which must be zero or greater at two decimal places and which defaults to 5000.00 when omitted.

A freshly registered title is inert but fully described. Its `availability_state` reads `DRAFT` and its `packaging_state` reads `UNPACKAGED`; `takedown_reason` reads `NONE`, because it has never been taken down; both `readiness_score_provisional` and `readiness_score_certified` read 0.00; `locked_ladder_count` reads 0; `encode_budget_remaining_usd` equals `encode_budget_usd` exactly; and `last_sweep_at` is null, because no availability sweep has evaluated it yet. Reading a title back exposes all of this together with its `version`, which is the token later required for ladder and reprocessing-job creation. The catalog list supports filtering by `content_type`, by `availability_state` and by `packaging_state`, alongside the usual `page` and `page_size` controls.

`ContentOps` may later edit `title_name`, `studio_code` and `encode_budget_usd`; every successful edit increments the title's `version` and refreshes its `updated_at`, which is precisely what invalidates a previously captured `expected_title_version`. Raising the budget is always allowed. Lowering it is not always allowed: because `encode_budget_remaining_usd` is recomputed after the edit, a new `encode_budget_usd` below the sum of `committed_usd` across the title's `RUNNING` and `COMPLETED` reprocessing jobs would drive the remaining budget negative, and such an edit is refused with `ERR_ENCODE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`.

Source masters arrive next. A **mezzanine asset** is registered by `ContentOps` against a title, carrying the payload's `title_id`, the storage `source_uri` of 1 to 300 characters, the runtime `duration_seconds` in whole seconds of at least 1, the source `frame_rate`, which must be exactly one of 23.976, 24.0, 25.0, 29.97, 50.0 or 59.94, the measured program loudness `peak_audio_lufs` in LUFS somewhere between -60.0 and 0.0 inclusive, the `artifact_defect_count` reported by the ingest scanner, which is zero or greater, the `audio_language` as a two-character lowercase language code, and optionally `caption_languages`, an array of two-character lowercase language codes with no duplicates that defaults to empty. Caption coverage is always judged against `audio_language`, never against the array's length.

Every new master is stored with `qc_state` of `INGESTED`, `qc_attempt_count` of 0, `latest_severity_index` of 0.00 and `blocking_defect_code` of `NONE`. Registering it immediately recomputes **both** readiness variants on the parent title, and a title's masters can be listed in registration order with an optional `qc_state` filter.

**Readiness scoring** is the catalog desk's headline number, and it exists in two variants that are recomputed together after *any* mutation of the title's masters, ladders or DRM packages. Both variants blend three components with fixed weights: the source-master component contributes half the score, the ladder component contributes one fifth, and the DRM-package component contributes the remaining three tenths. Each component is first expressed as a percentage from 0.00 to 100.00, rounded half up to two decimals, and only then multiplied by its weight; the weighted sum is rounded half up to two decimals for storage.

The **provisional** variant is optimistic and is stored on `readiness_score_provisional`. Its master component is 100.00 times the count of the title's masters whose `qc_state` is `INGESTED` or `QC_PASSED`, divided by the count of *all* the title's masters regardless of state, and is 0.00 when the title owns no masters at all. Its ladder component is 100.00 when the title owns at least one ladder whose `state` is `DRAFT`, `VALIDATED` or `LOCKED`, and 0.00 otherwise. Its package component is 100.00 when the title owns at least one DRM package whose `state` is `PENDING` or `PACKAGED`, and 0.00 otherwise. It is the operator-facing pipeline view: it counts an ingested master as if it will pass, a draft ladder as if it will lock, and a pending package as if it will package.

The **certified** variant is evidence-only and is stored on `readiness_score_certified`. Its master component uses the same denominator — every master of the title — but its numerator counts only masters whose `qc_state` is exactly `QC_PASSED`, and it is 0.00 when the title owns no masters. Its ladder component is 100.00 only when at least one ladder of the title is `LOCKED`, and its package component is 100.00 only when at least one DRM package of the title is `PACKAGED`. Because its inclusion sets are subsets of the provisional variant's, the certified score is always at or below the provisional score computed from the same stored data. A master in the terminal `QC_REJECTED` state stays in the denominator of both variants and leaves both numerators, so an abandoned master permanently depresses readiness until other masters are added; a `REVOKED` DRM package counts for neither variant. The certified variant is the **only** variant any gate consults: automated publication requires `readiness_score_certified` of at least 80.00, and 80.00 exactly is sufficient.

### 3.2. Quality Control: Verdicts, Remediation and Terminal Rejection

A `QcAnalyst` runs quality control against a master whose `qc_state` is `INGESTED`; attempting it against any other state — including anything at all against the terminal `QC_REJECTED` — is refused with `ERR_ASSET_STATE_INVALID`. A run is permitted only while `qc_attempt_count` is below 3, and each run produces an immutable **quality control report** whose `asset_id` names the master it scores and whose `attempt_number` is the master's `qc_attempt_count` incremented by one, so the first run records attempt 1. The report also records the `evaluated_at` instant taken from the logical clock. Every run, including one that fails the master, is a recorded outcome rather than an error, and all three branches answer 201 Created with the report body.

The verdict is computed from the master's **current** stored values — a resubmission replaces them before the next run, so a run always judges the latest measurements, and no other master participates. Three penalty components are derived first. The `loudness_deviation_db` is the absolute difference between the master's `peak_audio_lufs` and the platform loudness target of -24.0 LUFS, rounded half up to two decimals. The `caption_coverage_pct` is 100.00 when the master's `audio_language` appears among its `caption_languages`, and 0.00 otherwise. The `artifact_penalty` is the `artifact_defect_count` multiplied by 4.0, rounded half up to two decimals. A loudness penalty is then taken as the greater of zero and the `loudness_deviation_db` reduced by the tolerance of 2.0 decibels, multiplied by 6.0, rounded half up to two decimals — so a master within 2.0 decibels of target is penalised nothing for loudness. A caption penalty is 0.00 when `caption_coverage_pct` is 100.00 and is otherwise a flat 30.0. The `severity_index` is the sum of the artifact penalty, the loudness penalty and the caption penalty, rounded half up to two decimals.

`blocking_defect_code` is then determined from a **set** of blocking conditions, evaluated independently so their order does not matter. An `artifact_defect_count` of 8 or more contributes `ARTIFACT_BURST`. A `loudness_deviation_db` strictly greater than 6.0 contributes `LOUDNESS_OUT_OF_SPEC`; a deviation of exactly 6.0 does not, while 6.01 does. A `caption_coverage_pct` of 0.00 contributes `CAPTION_MISSING`. If two or more conditions contributed, `blocking_defect_code` is `MULTI_DEFECT`. If exactly one contributed, it is that one. If none contributed but the `severity_index` reached 40.0 or more, the code is `MULTI_DEFECT` as an aggregate-severity breach — a `severity_index` of exactly 40.00 is blocking while 39.99 is not. Otherwise the code is `NONE`.

From there the `verdict` follows in one step. Any non-`NONE` `blocking_defect_code` yields `FAIL`. Otherwise, a `severity_index` strictly greater than 0.00, or an `artifact_defect_count` of 3 or more, yields `WARN`. Everything else yields `PASS`. Both `PASS` and `WARN` are shipping verdicts: they drive the master from `INGESTED` to `QC_PASSED`, increment `qc_attempt_count` by one, copy the report's `severity_index` onto the master's `latest_severity_index`, set the master's `blocking_defect_code` to `NONE`, and recompute both readiness variants on the title. Only `FAIL` branches away.

A `FAIL` on the first or second attempt — that is, while `qc_attempt_count` stood at 0 or 1 before the run — drives the master from `INGESTED` to `QC_FAILED`. The report is written with `verdict` of `FAIL` and a non-`NONE` `blocking_defect_code`, `qc_attempt_count` increments by one, and both the report's `severity_index` and its `blocking_defect_code` are copied onto the master's `latest_severity_index` and `blocking_defect_code`. The title's `readiness_score_certified` is recomputed and drops, since the failed master leaves the certified numerator and only the ladder and package components remain. A `FAIL` on the third attempt — that is, with `qc_attempt_count` standing at exactly 2 before the run — instead drives the master from `INGESTED` straight to the terminal `QC_REJECTED`: the report is written with `attempt_number` of 3, `qc_attempt_count` becomes 3, and `readiness_score_provisional` is recomputed because a rejected master leaves the optimistic numerator too.

Recovery from a failure is a two-step loop, deliberately split between two desks. A `QcAnalyst` requests remediation of a master whose `qc_state` is `QC_FAILED`, moving it to `REMEDIATION_REQUESTED` and incrementing the master's `version`; requesting remediation of a master in any other state is `ERR_ASSET_STATE_INVALID`. While *any* master of a title sits in `REMEDIATION_REQUESTED`, that title's ladders cannot be locked. Then a `ContentOps` operator resubmits the corrected master, which requires `qc_state` of `REMEDIATION_REQUESTED` and `qc_attempt_count` below 3, and which accepts corrected values for `source_uri`, `peak_audio_lufs`, `artifact_defect_count` and `caption_languages`; any of those four that is omitted keeps its current value. The resubmission returns the master to `INGESTED`, resets its `blocking_defect_code` to `NONE` and its `latest_severity_index` to 0.00, and recomputes both readiness variants because the master re-enters the optimistic numerator. Crucially, `qc_attempt_count` is **not** reset, so the loop `INGESTED` → `QC_FAILED` → `REMEDIATION_REQUESTED` → `INGESTED` can be walked at most twice: the third failing verdict is terminal no matter how many resubmissions preceded it.

The per-attempt evidence is only observable through a master's report list, which returns its reports ordered by `attempt_number` ascending. That list is the sole channel exposing each attempt's own `severity_index`, `artifact_penalty`, `loudness_deviation_db`, `caption_coverage_pct` and `verdict`; the master itself retains only the most recent `latest_severity_index` and `blocking_defect_code`.

### 3.3. The Device Matrix and the Encoding Ladder

The compatibility matrix is reference data owned by `PlatformAdmin`. A **device profile** describes one playback family through its `device_class`, which is one of `SMART_TV_4K`, `SMART_TV_HD`, `MOBILE_HIGH`, `MOBILE_LOW`, `SET_TOP_LEGACY` or `BROWSER_HTML5` and which must be unique across all profiles — a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_DEVICE_CLASS`, and that uniqueness includes profiles that have been retired. The profile declares the tallest rung it can decode as `max_height_px` between 240 and 2160, the codec families it can decode as `supported_codecs`, an array of 1 to 3 distinct members drawn from `H264`, `HEVC` and `AV1`, and the bitrate window it will accept as `min_bitrate_kbps` and `max_bitrate_kbps`, each between 200 and 40000 with the maximum at or above the minimum. It also declares `hdcp_required`, a boolean defaulting to false stating whether the device enforces a protected output path; `required_drm_system`, one of `WIDEVINE`, `PLAYREADY`, `FAIRPLAY` or `NONE` and defaulting to `NONE`, where `NONE` means unencrypted playback is acceptable to this device; `market_share_pct` between 0.00 and 100.00 at two decimals, which is the weight the profile carries in compatibility scoring; and `quality_normalization_factor` between 0.50 and 1.00 at two decimals, a penalty multiplier that discounts the measured playback quality of weaker device classes.

A profile's `state` is either `ACTIVE` or `RETIRED` and may be set at registration, defaulting to `ACTIVE`. A `RETIRED` profile never participates in compatibility scoring and never satisfies an entitlement rule's device scope, yet its `quality_normalization_factor` is still read when a historical playback measurement naming it is ingested, because that measurement describes what really happened on that hardware. Registering a new profile does **not** re-score ladders that have already been validated — a ladder's compatibility figures are a snapshot taken at validation time and are never refreshed behind the operator's back. The profile list can be filtered by `state` and by `device_class`, with `page` and `page_size`, so an operator can confirm exactly which profiles the matrix will consider before validating anything.

An `EncodingEngineer` authors an **encoding ladder** for a title by supplying the payload's `title_id`, the `codec_family` every rung of the ladder will use — one of `H264`, `HEVC` or `AV1` — and the `rungs` array itself, together with the mandatory `expected_title_version` described in §2.7. Each element of `rungs` is an object carrying a `label`, a `height_px` between 240 and 2160, and a `bitrate_kbps` between 200 and 40000. The array must hold between 2 and 8 elements, its `height_px` values must be strictly increasing in stored array order, and no `label` may repeat within the array; a violation of any of those three is `ERR_RUNG_SHAPE_INVALID`, while an empty array or an element missing one of the three keys is caught earlier as a payload violation. An engineer cloning an existing ladder may name the ancestor in `derived_from_ladder_id`, which is null for hand-authored ladders and which — together with `superseded_by_ladder_id`, null while the ladder is current — traverses the ladder lineage in both directions.

A newly authored ladder is stored in `state` `DRAFT` with `rung_count` set to the number of elements in `rungs`, `peak_bitrate_kbps` set to the largest `bitrate_kbps` across the rungs and `peak_height_px` set to the largest `height_px`. Its `device_compat_score` reads 0.00, its `compatible_device_count` reads 0 and its `validation_reason_code` reads `NONE`, because nothing has been evaluated yet. Authoring it increments the parent title's `version` by one, re-derives the title's `packaging_state`, and recomputes both readiness variants. Ladders may be listed filtered by `title_id`, by `codec_family`, by `state`, and by either lineage link, `derived_from_ladder_id` or `superseded_by_ladder_id`, with `page` and `page_size`.

**Validation** is the ladder's proving step, available to an `EncodingEngineer` while the ladder's `state` is `DRAFT`; any other state answers `ERR_LADDER_STATE_INVALID`. It scores the ladder against the matrix and writes `device_compat_score` and `compatible_device_count`, and it is a branching decision whose two arms both answer 200 OK with the ladder body — a rejection here is a recorded verdict, not an error response.

Scoring begins with the candidate set: every device profile whose `state` is `ACTIVE`. Retired profiles never participate. A candidate profile counts as compatible when all three of the following hold. First, the ladder's `codec_family` is among the profile's `supported_codecs`. Second, at least one single rung of the ladder simultaneously satisfies the profile's window — its `height_px` is at most the profile's `max_height_px`, its `bitrate_kbps` is at least the profile's `min_bitrate_kbps`, and its `bitrate_kbps` is at most the profile's `max_bitrate_kbps`. Third, either the profile's `required_drm_system` is `NONE`, or the ladder's title already owns at least one DRM package whose `state` is `PENDING` or `PACKAGED` and whose `drm_systems` array contains that required system; packages in `STALE` or `REVOKED` never satisfy a device's DRM requirement. The `compatible_device_count` is the number of candidates that pass all three tests.

The score itself layers a coverage discount and a resolution bonus on top of reach. A coverage multiplier is taken as the lesser of 1.00 and the ladder's `rung_count` divided by the target of 5 rungs, so a two-rung ladder keeps only two fifths of the reach it would otherwise claim while a five-rung or longer ladder keeps all of it. The base figure is the sum of `market_share_pct` across all compatible profiles, multiplied by that coverage multiplier, and only then rounded half up to two decimals. A bonus of 5.00 is added when the ladder's `peak_height_px` is 2160 or greater **and** its `compatible_device_count` is at least 1; otherwise the bonus is 0.00. The stored `device_compat_score` is the lesser of 100.00 and the sum of the base and the bonus, rounded half up to two decimals.

The **accepting arm** applies when the ladder's `rungs` are strictly increasing in both `height_px` and `bitrate_kbps` in stored array order, no two rungs share a `height_px`, and the computed `device_compat_score` is strictly greater than 0.00. The ladder moves from `DRAFT` to `VALIDATED`, its `validation_reason_code` is set to `OK`, `device_compat_score` and `compatible_device_count` are written, its `version` increments by one, and both readiness variants are recomputed on the title. The **rejecting arm** applies when at least one of the opposite conditions holds: the rungs are not strictly increasing, or two rungs share a `height_px`, or the computed score is exactly 0.00 — which is what an empty `ACTIVE` candidate pool always produces. The ladder moves from `DRAFT` to the terminal `REJECTED` and can never be locked. Its `validation_reason_code` diagnoses the arm in a fixed precedence: `BITRATE_NOT_MONOTONIC` is checked first, then `DUPLICATE_HEIGHT`, and `NO_COMPATIBLE_DEVICE` last, so a ladder that is both non-monotonic and unservable reports the monotonicity problem. Both figures are still written on this arm, and they read 0.00 and 0 on the `NO_COMPATIBLE_DEVICE` branch.

### 3.4. Locking a Ladder: the Five-Hop Staleness Cascade

Locking is the moment a ladder becomes the title's shippable encode, and it is the most consequential single call in this system. An `EncodingEngineer` may lock a ladder whose `state` is `VALIDATED`; a ladder that is `DRAFT`, already `LOCKED`, `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED` answers `ERR_LADDER_STATE_INVALID`, which also means a second lock of the same ladder is refused. Two cross-entity guards then apply, both drawn from the quality-control desk's work rather than the encoding desk's: the title must own at least one master whose `qc_state` is `QC_PASSED`, otherwise `ERR_NO_PASSED_ASSET`, and no master of the title may sit in `REMEDIATION_REQUESTED`, otherwise `ERR_ASSET_REMEDIATION_PENDING` — the second holds even when another master has already passed. Stated positively, the transition's own precondition is that the title owns at least one master and that every master of the title is in `QC_PASSED`; a title with no masters at all fails with `ERR_NO_PASSED_ASSET`, and a title with a master awaiting remediation fails with `ERR_ASSET_REMEDIATION_PENDING`.

When those gates open, the lock fires a transitive chain five hops deep. Each hop is caused by the hop before it, not by the lock call itself, so a chain whose next hop has no applicable subject simply stops.

- **Hop 1.** The ladder moves from `VALIDATED` to `LOCKED`, the title's `locked_ladder_count` is recomputed as the number of that title's ladders in `LOCKED`, and the title's `version` increments by one.
- **Hop 2.** Because this ladder locked, any *other* ladder of the same `title_id` **and** the same `codec_family` that was in `LOCKED` moves to the terminal `SUPERSEDED`, with its `superseded_by_ladder_id` pointing at the ladder that just locked. A locked ladder of a different codec family is untouched — the two coexist deliberately.
- **Hop 3.** Because that ladder became `SUPERSEDED`, every DRM package bound to it whose `state` was `PACKAGED` moves to `STALE` with `stale_reason` of `LADDER_SUPERSEDED`.
- **Hop 4.** Because a DRM package of the title is now `STALE`, the title's `packaging_state` moves from `PACKAGED` to `STALE` and `readiness_score_certified` is recomputed — it drops, because no `PACKAGED` package remains.
- **Hop 5.** Because the title's `packaging_state` became `STALE`, a title whose `availability_state` was `PUBLISHED` moves to `TAKEN_DOWN` with `takedown_reason` of `PACKAGING_STALE`, and every licensing window of that title whose `state` was `ACTIVE` moves to `CLOSED` with `closed_at` set to the logical clock's current instant.

A `LOCKED` ladder can also be superseded from the other direction — by a reprocessing job's result ladder replacing it, described in §3.9. Whichever cause applies, the `SUPERSEDED` transition always carries the same two consequences beyond the state change itself: the ladder's `superseded_by_ladder_id` is set to the replacing ladder, every `PACKAGED` DRM package bound to the superseded ladder goes `STALE` with `stale_reason` of `LADDER_SUPERSEDED`, and the title's `locked_ladder_count` is recomputed.

The title's `packaging_state` is not written by hand anywhere; it is **derived**, and it is re-derived after every mutation of that title's ladders or DRM packages, in one fixed precedence. If any DRM package of the title is `STALE`, the dimension reads `STALE`. Otherwise, if any package is `PACKAGED`, it reads `PACKAGED`. Otherwise, if `locked_ladder_count` is 1 or more, it reads `PACKAGING`. Otherwise it reads `UNPACKAGED`. Packages in `REVOKED` are ignored by every branch of that precedence, as though they had never existed. Three consequences of the derivation are worth stating as transitions in their own right, because each recomputes readiness as it fires: a title with at least one locked ladder and no package in `PACKAGED` or `STALE` moves from `UNPACKAGED` to `PACKAGING` and has both readiness variants recomputed; a title whose last `PACKAGED` or `STALE` package is revoked while a locked ladder remains moves from `PACKAGED` back to `PACKAGING` with `readiness_score_certified` recomputed; and a title whose only `STALE` package is repackaged or revoked leaves `STALE` for `PACKAGED` — or for `PACKAGING`, if nothing packaged remains — again recomputing `readiness_score_certified`.

### 3.5. Rights, Packaging and Playback Entitlement

A `DrmOperator` creates a **DRM package** by binding it to one of a title's encoding ladders. The payload names the `title_id` and the `ladder_id`, and that ladder must belong to the same title — a ladder from another title's catalog is `ERR_LADDER_TITLE_MISMATCH`. The bound ladder may be `DRAFT`, `VALIDATED` or `LOCKED`, and binding before validation is deliberate rather than sloppy: a device profile that demands a DRM system only counts as compatible once a `PENDING` or `PACKAGED` package already advertises that system, so packaging intent has to be expressible before the ladder is proven. A ladder in `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED` cannot be bound and answers `ERR_LADDER_NOT_BINDABLE`.

The package declares `drm_systems`, an array of 1 to 3 distinct members drawn from `WIDEVINE`, `PLAYREADY` and `FAIRPLAY`; `license_server_url`, the HTTPS endpoint that will issue content keys, which must begin with `https://` and run 8 to 300 characters; `key_rotation_hours`, the content-key rotation period in whole hours from 1 to 168, defaulting to 24; and `persistent_license_allowed`, a boolean defaulting to false that states whether offline licenses may ever be issued for this package. On creation the package's `state` is `PENDING`, its `stale_reason` is `NONE`, its `entitlement_rule_count` is 0 and its `packaged_at` is null. The `stale_reason` field takes exactly one of three values — `NONE`, `LADDER_SUPERSEDED` and `CODEC_STANDARD_CHANGE`; only the first two are ever written by the transitions in this specification, and `CODEC_STANDARD_CHANGE` is a reserved value that no operation sets.

The package's `hdcp_floor` is derived, not supplied: it reads `TYPE_1` when the bound ladder's `peak_height_px` is 2160 or greater, `TYPE_0` when that height is 1080 or greater but below 2160, and `NONE` otherwise. The floor is the minimum output-protection level every entitlement rule on the package must meet, and it carries one packaging obligation of its own: whenever `hdcp_floor` is `TYPE_1`, the package's `drm_systems` must include `WIDEVINE`, and a 4K-capable package that omits it is refused with `ERR_DRM_SYSTEM_UNSUPPORTED`. Creating a package re-derives the title's `packaging_state` and recomputes both readiness variants. Packages can be listed filtered by `title_id`, by `ladder_id` and by `state`, ordered oldest first — the same order the entitlement selector uses when a title owns several packaged packages.

**Entitlement rules** are the license server's policy, authored by a `DrmOperator` and attached to a package. Each rule names the `region_code` it applies to, either two uppercase letters or the single wildcard character `*` meaning any region; the `subscription_tier` it applies to, one of `BASIC`, `STANDARD`, `PREMIUM` or `ANY`, where `ANY` matches every tier; and optionally the `device_class_scope`, one of `ALL`, `TV_ONLY` or `MOBILE_ONLY` and defaulting to `ALL`, where `TV_ONLY` covers `SMART_TV_4K`, `SMART_TV_HD` and `SET_TOP_LEGACY` and `MOBILE_ONLY` covers `MOBILE_HIGH` and `MOBILE_LOW`. The rule then states the ceiling it licenses: `max_concurrent_streams`, the number of simultaneous sessions permitted, from 1 to 10; `max_playback_height_px`, the tallest rendition licensed, from 240 to 2160; `hdcp_level`, the output protection it mandates, one of `NONE`, `TYPE_0` or `TYPE_1`; `offline_playback_allowed`, a boolean defaulting to false; and `priority`, the selector rank from 1 to 100 where a higher number wins.

Three checks guard rule attachment. The rule's `hdcp_level` must sit at or above its package's `hdcp_floor` under the ordering where `NONE` is lowest, `TYPE_0` is next and `TYPE_1` is highest, otherwise `ERR_HDCP_FLOOR_VIOLATION`. A rule may not set `offline_playback_allowed` to true against a package whose `persistent_license_allowed` is false, otherwise `ERR_OFFLINE_NOT_LICENSED`. And a concrete `region_code` must be a region the title is actually licensed in — that is, a region covered by at least one of the title's licensing windows in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_RULE_REGION_NOT_LICENSED`; the wildcard is exempt from that check. Additionally, the combination of `region_code`, `subscription_tier` and `device_class_scope` must be unique within the package, and a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENTITLEMENT_RULE`. A successful attachment increments the package's `entitlement_rule_count` by one. Rules are immutable — they carry no `updated_at` and no `version`, are never edited and are never deleted, and a superseding policy is expressed by attaching a higher-`priority` rule. A package's rules can be listed with an optional `region_code` filter, returned by `priority` descending and then oldest first, which is exactly the ranking the selector applies after filtering.

**Packaging** is the operation that turns intent into protected content, and it serves two states. From `PENDING` it requires that the bound ladder's `state` is `LOCKED`, that the title owns at least one master and every master of the title is in `QC_PASSED`, and that the package's `entitlement_rule_count` is 1 or more — an unlicensed package may not be packaged. From `STALE` it is a **repackage**, and it additionally requires the request to supply a `ladder_id` naming a ladder that is `LOCKED` and belongs to the same title, because the ladder the package was bound to is precisely the one that went away; the same master-quality requirement applies. Attempting to package a package that is already `PACKAGED` or is `REVOKED` is `ERR_PACKAGE_STATE_INVALID`. When the bound — or newly rebound — ladder is not `LOCKED`, the answer is `ERR_LADDER_NOT_LOCKED`, and that includes the common mistake of repackaging a `STALE` package without supplying a `ladder_id` at all.

A successful packaging sets the package's `state` to `PACKAGED`, sets `packaged_at` to the logical clock's current instant, clears `stale_reason` back to `NONE`, and re-derives `hdcp_floor` from the possibly rebound ladder's `peak_height_px`. On the repackage path the package's `ladder_id` is rewritten to the supplied ladder. The title's `packaging_state` is then re-derived: from `PACKAGING` it becomes `PACKAGED` provided no package of the title remains `STALE`, and from `STALE` it becomes `PACKAGED` provided no *other* package of the title is still `STALE`. Both readiness variants are recomputed on the first path; `readiness_score_certified` is recomputed on the repackage path.

The reverse operation is **revocation**, available from `PENDING`, `PACKAGED` or `STALE` into the terminal `REVOKED`; revoking an already-`REVOKED` package is `ERR_PACKAGE_STATE_INVALID`. From every one of the three source states the title's `packaging_state` is re-derived with `REVOKED` packages ignored, which means revoking the last `STALE` package clears the staleness, and revoking a `PACKAGED` package drops the title to `PACKAGING` when a `LOCKED` ladder remains or to `UNPACKAGED` when none does. Revocation from `PACKAGED` and from `STALE` recomputes `readiness_score_certified`; a `REVOKED` package is invisible to the entitlement selector, to compatibility scoring and to both readiness variants alike.

A **playback entitlement check** is how the delivery and rights desks ask what a specific session would be allowed to do. Available to `DeliveryEngineer` and `DrmOperator`, it is a computed read carried in a request body and it persists nothing, answering 200 OK. The request supplies `title_id`, `region_code`, `subscription_tier`, `device_class`, `requested_streams` and optionally `offline_requested`.

Evaluation proceeds in five stages, and the order determines which rejection a caller sees. First, **package selection**: the candidate set is the title's DRM packages whose `state` is `PACKAGED`, and if that set is empty the check is refused with `ERR_NO_PACKAGED_DRM` (HTTP 409); otherwise the oldest such package by `created_at` is selected and no other package is consulted. Second, the requesting device is resolved as the device profile whose `device_class` equals the requested one and whose `state` is `ACTIVE`; when none exists the answer is `ERR_DEVICE_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`. Third, **rule eligibility**: a rule of the selected package qualifies when its `region_code` either equals the requested `region_code` or is the wildcard; its `subscription_tier` either equals the requested tier or is `ANY`; its `device_class_scope` is `ALL`, or is `TV_ONLY` and the requested `device_class` is one of `SMART_TV_4K`, `SMART_TV_HD` or `SET_TOP_LEGACY`, or is `MOBILE_ONLY` and the requested `device_class` is one of `MOBILE_HIGH` or `MOBILE_LOW`; and, when the resolved device's `hdcp_required` is true, its `hdcp_level` is either `TYPE_0` or `TYPE_1` — a device demanding protected output cannot be served by a rule mandating `NONE`. Rules belonging to `PENDING`, `STALE` or `REVOKED` packages never participate at all.

Fourth, **ranking**: the qualifying rules are ordered by `priority` descending, then by region specificity descending — a rule whose `region_code` exactly equals the requested region outranks a wildcard rule at the same priority — and finally by `created_at` ascending, and the first rule in that order wins. `priority` dominates specificity, so a wildcard rule at priority 90 beats an exact-region rule at priority 50; specificity only breaks ties between equal priorities. If no rule qualified, the check is refused with `ERR_ENTITLEMENT_NO_RULE` (HTTP 403). Fifth, two **post-match gates** are applied in this order: if `requested_streams` is strictly greater than the winning rule's `max_concurrent_streams` the answer is `ERR_CONCURRENCY_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 403), where asking for exactly the licensed number of streams is allowed; and if `offline_requested` is true while either the winning rule's `offline_playback_allowed` or the selected package's `persistent_license_allowed` is false, the answer is `ERR_OFFLINE_PLAYBACK_DENIED` (HTTP 403).

A check that clears all five stages answers with `decision` of `ALLOW` and `reason_code` of `MATCHED`, together with `matched_rule_id` naming the winning rule, `package_id` naming the selected package, `max_concurrent_streams` echoing the winning rule's ceiling, `offline_granted` — true when `offline_requested` was true and both offline permissions held, false otherwise, including when offline playback was never requested — and `effective_height_px`, the playback ceiling actually available, which is the smallest of three numbers: the winning rule's `max_playback_height_px`, the resolved device's `max_height_px`, and the `peak_height_px` of the ladder the selected package is bound to. Stream counts and heights are whole numbers and are never rounded.

### 3.6. Licensing Windows, Publication, Takedown and Expiry

Contract dates enter the system as **licensing windows**, scheduled by `ContentOps` against a title. Each window names the `region_code` it licenses as two uppercase letters, the half-open interval `window_start_at` inclusive to `window_end_at` exclusive, and the `license_tier`, which is the lowest subscriber tier licensed in that window and is one of `BASIC`, `STANDARD` or `PREMIUM`. The interval must be well-formed, with `window_start_at` strictly before `window_end_at`, otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_BOUNDS_INVALID`; it must not begin in the past, meaning `window_start_at` is at or after the current logical clock, otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_IN_PAST`; and it must not overlap any `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` window of the same title and the same region under the half-open comparison of §2.10, otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Scheduling is also refused outright on a title that is `TAKEN_DOWN` with `takedown_reason` of `MANUAL_LEGAL`, with `ERR_TITLE_LEGALLY_BLOCKED` — legal takedowns are not routed around by adding fresh contract dates.

A new window is stored in `state` `SCHEDULED` with `activated_at` and `closed_at` both null. Scheduling the **first** window of a title that is still in `DRAFT` also advances the title's `availability_state` from `DRAFT` to `SCHEDULED` and increments the title's `version` by one; windows added while the title is already `SCHEDULED`, `PUBLISHED` or `TAKEN_DOWN` leave `availability_state` exactly as it was. A title's windows can be read back filtered by `region_code` and by `state`, returned earliest-starting first.

`ContentOps` can cancel a window whose `state` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE`; a window already `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` answers `ERR_WINDOW_STATE_INVALID`. Cancellation moves the window to the terminal `CANCELLED`, records `closed_at` at the logical clock's current instant, and releases the interval so a later window may legally occupy it. Cancelling an `ACTIVE` window has one further consequence: when it was the title's last `ACTIVE` window and the title's `availability_state` was `PUBLISHED`, the title moves to `TAKEN_DOWN` with `takedown_reason` of `CONTRACT_END`.

Publication itself is never a manual act. During Phase 4 of the availability sweep each title is offered **at most one** `availability_state` transition, evaluated against the windows as Phase 1 left them and the packaging dimension as Phase 3 left it, and each transition that fires stamps `last_sweep_at` with the logical clock's current instant.

- A title in `SCHEDULED` moves to `PUBLISHED` when at least one of its windows is `ACTIVE` after Phase 1, its `packaging_state` is `PACKAGED` after Phase 3, and its `readiness_score_certified` is at least 80.00. Its `takedown_reason` is set to `NONE`.
- A title in `PUBLISHED` moves to `TAKEN_DOWN` with `takedown_reason` of `CONTRACT_END` when, after Phase 1, none of its windows is `ACTIVE` and its `packaging_state` is still `PACKAGED` — the contract simply ran out.
- A title in `TAKEN_DOWN` moves back to `PUBLISHED`, with `takedown_reason` reset to `NONE`, when its `takedown_reason` is anything other than `MANUAL_LEGAL`, at least one of its windows is `ACTIVE` after Phase 1, its `packaging_state` is `PACKAGED`, and its `readiness_score_certified` is at least 80.00. This closes the cycle `PUBLISHED` → `TAKEN_DOWN` → `PUBLISHED`: a title taken down for a contract gap or for stale packaging republishes itself once the gap closes and the packaging is repaired, while a `MANUAL_LEGAL` takedown is sticky and is never republished automatically.
- A title in `SCHEDULED` or in `TAKEN_DOWN` moves to the terminal `EXPIRED` when it owns at least one window, every one of its windows is `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`, and the current logical clock has reached or passed the latest `window_end_at` across those windows. Both source states use identical conditions and identical effects; there is no other route into `EXPIRED`, and there is no route out.

Separately from the sweep, `ContentOps` can take a `PUBLISHED` title down by hand. The request carries `takedown_reason`, and only the value `MANUAL_LEGAL` is accepted here — `CONTRACT_END` belongs to the sweep and `PACKAGING_STALE` belongs to the packaging cascade, and neither may be asserted by an operator. A manual takedown moves `availability_state` from `PUBLISHED` to `TAKEN_DOWN`, sets `takedown_reason` to `MANUAL_LEGAL`, increments the title's `version` by one, and closes every one of the title's `ACTIVE` windows with `closed_at` set to the logical clock's current instant. Because the reason is sticky, no later sweep will bring the title back.

### 3.7. CDN Delivery: Vendor Roster, Traffic Steering and Quality Feedback

A `PlatformAdmin` registers each delivery partner as a **CDN vendor**, supplying the unique `vendor_code` of 2 to 16 uppercase characters — a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_VENDOR_CODE` — plus `regions`, an array of 1 to 20 distinct two-uppercase-letter region codes the vendor serves; `committed_gbps`, the contracted egress ceiling in Gbps, strictly greater than zero at two decimals; and `cost_per_gb_usd`, the egress unit price, strictly greater than zero at four decimals. A new vendor's `state` is `ACTIVE`, its `reserved_gbps` is 0.00, its `breach_window_count` is 0, and **both** health figures — `health_score_rolling` and `health_score_contract` — are seeded at 100.00, which is the value the health calculation yields when no measurement rows exist yet.

Commercial terms are editable by `PlatformAdmin` or by the vendor's own `VendorOperator`, covering `committed_gbps`, `cost_per_gb_usd` and `regions`, and each successful update increments the vendor's `version`. Lowering `committed_gbps` below the vendor's current `reserved_gbps` is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, since capacity already sold cannot be un-sold. Changing `cost_per_gb_usd` or `regions` deliberately does **not** re-steer existing routing policies: a policy keeps its frozen selection until something re-runs the selector.

A **routing policy** steers one title in one region and is created by a `DeliveryEngineer` naming the `title_id`, the `region_code` and the `steering_mode`, which is one of `PERFORMANCE`, `COST` or `WEIGHTED`. A title may hold at most one policy per region, and a second attempt for the same pair is `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROUTING_POLICY`. The caller does not choose the vendor — the server does, and the policy exposes the outcome through `primary_vendor_id`, `backup_vendor_id`, `state`, `excluded_vendor_ids`, `resteer_count`, `selection_reason_code` and `projected_egress_cost_usd`. A newly created policy starts with an empty `excluded_vendor_ids` and a `resteer_count` of 0.

**Vendor selection** runs in three stages. Eligibility first: a vendor is a candidate when its `state` is exactly `ACTIVE`, the policy's `region_code` appears in the vendor's `regions`, its `health_score_contract` is at least 60.00, and its `id` is absent from the policy's `excluded_vendor_ids`. Vendors in `DEGRADED` or `DRAINED` are never candidates. Ranking second, by `steering_mode`: under `PERFORMANCE` candidates are ordered by `health_score_contract` descending, then `cost_per_gb_usd` ascending, then `created_at` ascending; under `COST` they are ordered by `cost_per_gb_usd` ascending, then `health_score_contract` descending, then `created_at` ascending; under `WEIGHTED` a composite figure is computed for each candidate as its `health_score_contract` reduced by its `cost_per_gb_usd` multiplied by 1000.0, rounded half up to four decimals, and candidates are ordered by that composite descending, then `created_at` ascending. In all three modes `created_at` ascending is the final and exhaustive tie-break. Assignment third: `primary_vendor_id` becomes the first-ranked candidate, `backup_vendor_id` becomes the second-ranked candidate when two or more candidates survive and is null otherwise, `state` becomes `ACTIVE` with two or more candidates and `STANDBY_ONLY` with exactly one, and `selection_reason_code` records which branch chose the primary — `PERFORMANCE_RANK`, `COST_RANK` or `WEIGHTED_RANK` according to the mode.

An **empty candidate pool** is handled differently depending on who asked. At creation time it is fatal: the policy is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VENDOR` (HTTP 422) and nothing at all is persisted. On a re-steer, whether operator-driven or cascaded, it is a legitimate outcome: the policy's `state` becomes `UNROUTABLE`, `primary_vendor_id` and `backup_vendor_id` both become null, `selection_reason_code` becomes `NO_CANDIDATE` and `projected_egress_cost_usd` becomes 0.00.

The **projected egress spend** is recomputed whenever a policy is created, re-steered by an operator, or re-steered automatically, and it follows a pinned five-step order. Step one, demand: the play attempts are summed as `play_attempts` across every ingested playback metric window whose `title_id` and `region_code` both match the policy's, with no state filter of any kind since metric windows are immutable events; the projected monthly volume is that attempt count multiplied by 2.5 gigabytes per play, rounded half up to two decimals, or a flat 1000.00 gigabytes when the attempt count is zero. Step two, base cost: if the policy currently has no `primary_vendor_id`, `projected_egress_cost_usd` is 0.00 and evaluation stops there; otherwise the base cost is the projected monthly volume multiplied by the **current primary vendor's** `cost_per_gb_usd`, rounded half up to two decimals. Step three, surcharge rate: the tier is keyed on that rounded base cost and **never** on the eventual total — a base cost below 500.00 takes a rate of 0.08, a base cost of 500.00 or more but below 2000.00 takes 0.05, and a base cost of 2000.00 or more takes 0.03; a policy whose `steering_mode` is `WEIGHTED` then has a further 0.02 added to whichever rate was selected. Step four: the steering surcharge is the base cost multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to two decimals. Step five: `projected_egress_cost_usd` is the base cost plus the surcharge, rounded half up to two decimals. The pinned order matters because the total and the tier look circular: a base cost of 480.00 under `PERFORMANCE` steering takes the 0.08 rate for a surcharge of 38.40 and a total of 518.40, whereas re-keying the tier on that 518.40 total would wrongly yield 504.00.

**Re-steering** is available to a `DeliveryEngineer` from every policy state, which is why a re-steer never fails a state precondition. It appends the outgoing `primary_vendor_id` to `excluded_vendor_ids` when one exists, re-runs the selector, rewrites `primary_vendor_id`, `backup_vendor_id`, `state` and `selection_reason_code` from the result, increments `resteer_count` by one and recomputes `projected_egress_cost_usd`. Exclusions are permanent and are never cleared automatically, so repeated re-steers monotonically shrink the pool and converge first on `STANDBY_ONLY` and then on `UNROUTABLE`. The full set of outcomes is enumerated below, and every one of them answers 200 OK with the policy body.

- From `ACTIVE`, when the selector still returns two or more candidates after excluding the outgoing primary, the policy stays `ACTIVE` with both vendor slots rewritten.
- From `ACTIVE`, when exactly one candidate remains after exclusions, the policy becomes `STANDBY_ONLY` with `primary_vendor_id` set to that single candidate and `backup_vendor_id` set to null.
- From `ACTIVE` or from `STANDBY_ONLY`, when the exclusions leave **no** candidate at all, the policy becomes `UNROUTABLE` with both vendor slots null, `selection_reason_code` `NO_CANDIDATE` and `projected_egress_cost_usd` 0.00. This is reached both by an operator re-steer that exhausts the pool — a re-steer whose `excluded_vendor_ids` already covers every otherwise-eligible vendor of the region is still accepted and simply drives the policy unroutable rather than failing — and by the automatic re-steer described below.
- From `STANDBY_ONLY`, when two or more candidates remain, the policy returns to `ACTIVE` with both slots rewritten.
- From `STANDBY_ONLY`, when exactly one candidate remains after excluding the outgoing primary, the policy stays `STANDBY_ONLY`: the outgoing primary joins `excluded_vendor_ids`, `primary_vendor_id` becomes the single candidate and `backup_vendor_id` stays null.
- From `UNROUTABLE`, when two or more candidates have become available again — for instance because a degraded vendor recovered and was never excluded — the policy returns to `ACTIVE` with both slots rewritten, completing the cycle `ACTIVE` → `UNROUTABLE` → `ACTIVE`.
- From `UNROUTABLE`, when exactly one candidate is available, the policy becomes `STANDBY_ONLY` with that candidate as primary, a null backup, and `selection_reason_code` updated to the mode's rank code.
- From `UNROUTABLE`, when no candidate is available, the policy stays `UNROUTABLE`: both vendor slots stay null, `selection_reason_code` stays `NO_CANDIDATE`, `excluded_vendor_ids` is unchanged because there is no outgoing primary to exclude, and `projected_egress_cost_usd` stays 0.00. Only `resteer_count` moves — the attempt is recorded rather than rejected.

In every one of those outcomes `resteer_count` increments by one and `projected_egress_cost_usd` is recomputed against whichever vendor now holds the primary slot. Policies can be listed filtered by `title_id`, by `primary_vendor_id`, by `backup_vendor_id`, by `region_code` and by `state`, with `page` and `page_size`.

**Playback telemetry** closes the loop. A `DeliveryEngineer` or `PlatformAdmin` ingests one immutable **metric window** describing a title, a device class, a vendor and a region over a closed measurement period. The payload carries `title_id`, `device_profile_id`, `vendor_id`, `region_code`, the `window_start_at` and `window_end_at` bounds with the end strictly after the start, the `play_attempts` count of at least 1, the `rebuffer_events` count of zero or more, the `startup_ms_p95` 95th-percentile startup latency between 0 and 60000 milliseconds, and the `error_count` of fatal playback errors, zero or more. The window must already be closed against the logical clock — a `window_end_at` strictly after the current clock is `ERR_METRIC_WINDOW_IN_FUTURE` — and the named vendor must actually serve the named region, meaning `region_code` appears in that vendor's `regions`, otherwise `ERR_VENDOR_REGION_NOT_SERVED`.

Ingestion computes the window's quality figures before anything else happens. The `rebuffer_ratio` is `rebuffer_events` divided by `play_attempts`, rounded half up to four decimals, and the `error_rate` is `error_count` divided by `play_attempts`, likewise to four decimals. A raw penalty is then assembled from three terms and rounded half up to four decimals: the `rebuffer_ratio` multiplied by 100 and then by a weight of 2.5; plus the greater of zero and the `startup_ms_p95` reduced by the target of 2000 milliseconds, divided by 100.0 and multiplied by a weight of 1.5; plus the `error_rate` multiplied by 100 and then by a weight of 4.0. Because of the clamp, a window whose startup latency is at or below 2000 milliseconds contributes exactly nothing from that term and is not rewarded for being faster. The raw penalty is then multiplied by the `quality_normalization_factor` of the device profile named by `device_profile_id` and rounded half up to four decimals — and that factor is read even when the profile's `state` is `RETIRED`, because the measurement is historical fact. Finally the `quality_index` is 100.00 reduced by that penalty, clamped into the range 0.00 to 100.00, and rounded half up to two decimals, and the `breach_flag` is true exactly when `quality_index` is strictly below 70.00; a `quality_index` of exactly 70.00 is not a breach while 69.99 is.

The vendor's health is then rewritten in **two variants** computed over every metric window ever ingested for that vendor, across all titles, regions and device classes. The `health_score_rolling` variant sorts those rows by `window_start_at` descending with `created_at` descending as its only tie-break, takes the newest 3 rows, and stores their unweighted arithmetic mean of `quality_index`, rounded half up to two decimals; when the vendor has fewer than 3 rows the mean is taken over however many exist, and when it has none at all the value is 100.00. The `health_score_contract` variant instead takes every row and computes a `play_attempts`-weighted mean: the sum over all rows of `quality_index` multiplied by `play_attempts`, divided by the sum of `play_attempts` across those rows, rounded half up to two decimals, and again 100.00 when no rows exist. The `breach_window_count` is simply the number of the vendor's rows whose `breach_flag` is true. The two variants answer different questions and are consulted by different machinery: the rolling variant is the **only** figure the vendor state machine reads, so one catastrophic recent window can degrade a vendor with an excellent long-run record, while the contract variant is the **only** figure traffic steering reads for eligibility and for `PERFORMANCE` and `WEIGHTED` ranking, so a low-traffic bad window barely moves it.

Vendor state follows from those figures, and the drain arm is evaluated **first**. A vendor whose `breach_window_count` has reached 5 or more after the ingest moves to the terminal `DRAINED`, from either `ACTIVE` or `DEGRADED`. Otherwise, while `breach_window_count` is below 5, a vendor in `ACTIVE` whose `health_score_rolling` fell below 50.00 moves to `DEGRADED`, and a vendor in `DEGRADED` whose `health_score_rolling` reached 75.00 or more moves back to `ACTIVE`, which is the recovery arm of the `ACTIVE` → `DEGRADED` → `ACTIVE` cycle. Recovery re-steers nothing: the vendor merely re-enters future candidate pools, and it stays out of any policy whose `excluded_vendor_ids` already lists it.

A vendor leaving `ACTIVE` is what triggers the automatic re-steer. If and only if the vendor moved to `DEGRADED` or to `DRAINED`, every routing policy whose `primary_vendor_id` is that vendor re-runs the selector with that vendor added to its `excluded_vendor_ids` and its `resteer_count` incremented by one, landing in `ACTIVE`, `STANDBY_ONLY` or `UNROUTABLE` exactly as an operator-driven re-steer would; policies in `ACTIVE` and `STANDBY_ONLY` are both eligible for this cascade. When such a re-steer changes the primary, that policy's `projected_egress_cost_usd` is recomputed against the new primary's `cost_per_gb_usd`. Live-event reservations already held on a drained vendor are deliberately **not** moved: their capacity stays counted against that vendor until a failover, a cancellation or a sweep-driven end releases it. Metric windows themselves can be read individually or listed filtered by `title_id`, `device_profile_id`, `vendor_id`, `region_code` and `breach_flag`, returned newest-window-first — the same ordering the rolling health variant uses to pick its three rows.

### 3.8. Live Events: Capacity Reservation, Arming, Failover and Release

A live broadcast is not merely published, it is **reserved**: a `DeliveryEngineer` books egress capacity on a named vendor for a named broadcast window. The request carries `title_id`, `primary_vendor_id`, the `window_start_at` inclusive start and the `window_end_at` inclusive end of the reserved broadcast window, and `reserved_gbps`, the egress capacity held on the active vendor, strictly greater than zero at two decimals. Two inputs are optional: `failover_vendor_id`, the vendor holding the standby stream, which defaults to null when no failover is contracted, and `buffer_minutes`, the setup and teardown buffer added to both ends of the window for capacity-overlap arithmetic, which defaults to 15 and must lie between 0 and 60 inclusive.

Four business checks guard the booking, beyond the interval and clock rules of §2.6. The title must be a live event: a `content_type` other than `LIVE_EVENT` is `ERR_NOT_LIVE_EVENT_TITLE`. A contracted standby must be a genuinely different partner: a `failover_vendor_id` equal to the `primary_vendor_id` is `ERR_FAILOVER_SAME_AS_PRIMARY`. The broadcast must sit inside licensed dates: the reservation's window must fall **entirely inside one single** `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` availability window of the same title, in any region — a reservation is not region-scoped, so it is enough that one licensing window of any region covers it end to end, and a broadcast straddling the boundary between two abutting licensing windows is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_OUTSIDE_LICENSE` even though the two windows jointly cover it. And the primary vendor must have the capacity, per the aggregate check below, or `ERR_VENDOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

**Aggregate capacity** is evaluated on the vendor being charged, using the closed buffer-inflated geometry of §2.10. The requested interval is inflated on both sides by the request's own `buffer_minutes`. Every reservation already charged to that vendor through its `active_vendor_id`, whose `state` is `RESERVED`, `ARMED`, `LIVE` or `FAILED_OVER`, and whose own interval inflated by **its own** `buffer_minutes` touches or overlaps the inflated request interval, is collected as occupied; reservations in `ENDED` or `CANCELLED` hold nothing and are excluded, and the reservation being moved by a failover is excluded from its own check so a failover never collides with itself. Their `reserved_gbps` values are summed and rounded half up to two decimals to give the held figure. The booking is accepted when that held figure plus the requested `reserved_gbps` is less than **or equal to** the vendor's `committed_gbps`, and refused with `ERR_VENDOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` only when it strictly exceeds it: exact equality is a legal, fully-booked vendor, and one hundredth of a Gbps beyond equality is a rejection. Note that colliding intervals are not themselves an error here — the buffers exist to make concurrent events share a ceiling, not to forbid them.

A successful booking stores the reservation in `state` `RESERVED` with `active_vendor_id` set to `primary_vendor_id`, `failover_at` null and `released_gbps` 0.00, and immediately recomputes the primary vendor's `reserved_gbps`. That figure is always the rounded sum of `reserved_gbps` over every reservation charged to the vendor through `active_vendor_id` in `RESERVED`, `ARMED`, `LIVE` or `FAILED_OVER` — it is a derived ledger, never an incrementally patched counter, and §2.9's bandwidth invariant holds over it at every observable moment.

The reservation then moves through four operator actions and two sweep actions.

- **Arming.** A `DeliveryEngineer` arms a `RESERVED` reservation, which moves it to `ARMED` and increments its `version`; no capacity moves, because the hold already sits on the active vendor. Three guards apply. The title must own at least one DRM package in `state` `PACKAGED`, otherwise `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_READY` — a live event may not be armed against unprotected content. The title must own at least one routing policy in `state` `ACTIVE` or `STANDBY_ONLY`, so there is somewhere for the traffic to go; a title whose only policy is `UNROUTABLE` cannot arm. And the broadcast must not already be over: the current logical clock must be strictly before `window_end_at`. Arming anything that is not `RESERVED`, or arming a reservation whose window has already elapsed, answers `ERR_RESERVATION_STATE_INVALID`.
- **Going live.** Phase 2 of the sweep moves an `ARMED` reservation to `LIVE` once the logical clock has reached `window_start_at` and is still strictly before `window_end_at`. A reservation still in `RESERVED` is **never** started by the sweep no matter how far the clock has advanced — it must be armed by an operator first, and an unarmed reservation whose window elapses simply sits in `RESERVED` holding its capacity until it is cancelled. No capacity moves on going live.
- **Failover.** A `DeliveryEngineer` fails a reservation over from either `ARMED` or `LIVE` — both source states lead to `FAILED_OVER` under identical guards and identical effects. The reservation must name a `failover_vendor_id` that is not null, that vendor's `state` must not be `DRAINED`, and the failover vendor must itself pass the aggregate capacity check for the same `reserved_gbps`, excluding this reservation from its own occupied set, or the move is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` and nothing changes. On success `active_vendor_id` becomes the `failover_vendor_id`, `failover_at` records the logical clock's current instant, and `state` becomes `FAILED_OVER`. This is the compensating action for the primary-side hold: the primary vendor's `reserved_gbps` falls by exactly `reserved_gbps` while the failover vendor's rises by exactly the same amount, so the per-vendor split moves and the global held total is byte-for-byte unchanged. A failover attempted from `RESERVED`, from `FAILED_OVER` again, or from either terminal state answers `ERR_RESERVATION_STATE_INVALID`; there is no route back to the primary vendor and no second failover.
- **Ending.** Phase 2 of the sweep moves a `LIVE` or a `FAILED_OVER` reservation to the terminal `ENDED` once the logical clock has reached or passed `window_end_at`. Both arms behave identically: `released_gbps` is set to `reserved_gbps` and the capacity is dropped from whichever vendor was active — the primary in the `LIVE` case, the failover in the `FAILED_OVER` case. The global held total falls by exactly `released_gbps`.
- **Cancellation.** A `DeliveryEngineer` cancels a reservation from `RESERVED` or from `ARMED`; both arms move it to the terminal `CANCELLED`, set `released_gbps` to `reserved_gbps` and release the whole hold from whichever vendor currently carries it, restoring that vendor's pre-reservation `reserved_gbps` exactly. A cancel attempted while the reservation is `LIVE`, `FAILED_OVER`, `ENDED` or `CANCELLED` answers `ERR_RESERVATION_STATE_INVALID` — a broadcast in flight is ended by the clock, not by an operator.

Reservations are readable individually, exposing `active_vendor_id`, `failover_at` and `released_gbps`, and listable filtered by `title_id`, `primary_vendor_id`, `failover_vendor_id`, `active_vendor_id` and `state`, ordered by `window_start_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending. A `VendorOperator` listing reservations must supply one of the three vendor filters — `primary_vendor_id`, `failover_vendor_id` or `active_vendor_id` — equal to its own `X-User-ID`, per §2.1.

### 3.9. Catalog Reprocessing and the Encode Budget

Codecs move on, and a locked ladder eventually needs re-encoding into a newer family. An `EncodingEngineer` queues a **reprocess job** naming the `title_id`, the `source_ladder_id` of the `LOCKED` ladder whose rung shape is to be re-encoded, the `trigger_reason` explaining why the catalog is being reprocessed — one of `CODEC_DEPRECATION`, `STANDARD_UPDATE`, `QUALITY_REGRESSION` or `DEVICE_MATRIX_CHANGE` — the `target_codec_family` the result ladder will use, one of `H264`, `HEVC` or `AV1`, and the mandatory `expected_title_version` of §2.7.

Four business rules guard creation. The target codec must actually be a change: a `target_codec_family` equal to the source ladder's `codec_family` is `ERR_REPROCESS_CODEC_UNCHANGED`. The source ladder must belong to the named title, or `ERR_LADDER_TITLE_MISMATCH`. Only one job per title may be in flight: a second creation while another job of the same title is `QUEUED` or `RUNNING` is `ERR_REPROCESS_JOB_IN_FLIGHT`, while jobs in `COMPLETED`, `FAILED` or `ABORTED` block nothing. And there must be something to re-encode: a title whose every master is `QC_REJECTED`, or which owns no master at all, is `ERR_REPROCESS_NO_SOURCE_ASSET`, which as §2.6 explains wins over `ERR_LADDER_NOT_LOCKED` even when the named source ladder is also not `LOCKED`. A job that clears all four is stored in `state` `QUEUED` with `result_ladder_id` null, `committed_usd` 0.00, `abort_reason` `NONE`, and the title's `version` incremented by one. No budget is debited yet.

The **cost estimate** is computed once at creation and frozen onto `cost_estimate_usd`, `per_rung_cost_usd` and `priority_rank`; it is never recomputed, not when the source ladder changes, not when the title's budget changes, and not when the job runs. It is derived in a pinned order. First the billable duration: over the title's masters whose `qc_state` is anything other than `QC_REJECTED`, the largest `duration_seconds` is taken, divided by 60, and rounded **up** to the next whole number to give the duration in integer minutes — a 6001-second master bills 101 minutes, not 100.02. Next the codec factor, read from the job's `target_codec_family`: 1.00 for `H264`, 1.35 for `HEVC` and 2.10 for `AV1`. Then, walking the source ladder's `rungs` in their stored array order and **not** in any re-sorted order, each rung contributes one entry to `per_rung_cost_usd`, computed as the duration in minutes multiplied by the rung's `bitrate_kbps` divided by 1000.0, multiplied by the codec factor, multiplied by the rate of 0.02 US dollars per minute per megabit per second, and rounded half up to two decimals. The `cost_estimate_usd` is then the sum of those **already-rounded** per-rung entries, re-rounded to two decimals. The order is load-bearing: 100 billable minutes of `HEVC` over source rungs of 1150, 2350, 4850 and 9650 kilobits per second give per-rung costs of 3.11, 6.35, 13.10 and 26.06 and a total of 48.62, whereas summing the unrounded products first and rounding once would give 48.60, which is the wrong answer.

The `priority_rank` follows from the same figures: it is the weight of the job's `trigger_reason` — 40.0 for `CODEC_DEPRECATION`, 30.0 for `STANDARD_UPDATE`, 20.0 for `QUALITY_REGRESSION` and 10.0 for `DEVICE_MATRIX_CHANGE` — plus the lesser of 20.0 and the `cost_estimate_usd` divided by 10.0, rounded half up to two decimals. Bigger jobs therefore outrank smaller jobs of the same reason, but the cost contribution saturates at 20.0, so no amount of cost lets a `DEVICE_MATRIX_CHANGE` overtake a `CODEC_DEPRECATION`. Jobs list newest-priority-first — `priority_rank` descending, then `created_at` ascending, never by `id` — filterable by `title_id`, by `source_ladder_id`, by `result_ladder_id` and by `state`, and each job reads back with its `per_rung_cost_usd`, `committed_usd`, `priority_rank`, `result_ladder_id` and `abort_reason`.

Running a job is where money is actually committed. An `EncodingEngineer` runs a `QUEUED` job — a run against any other state is `ERR_JOB_STATE_INVALID` — provided no other job of the same title is `RUNNING`, the source ladder is still `LOCKED`, and the frozen `cost_estimate_usd` fits inside the title's `encode_budget_remaining_usd`. The fit is generous at the boundary: a cost exactly equal to the remaining budget is accepted, and a cost one cent above it is refused with `ERR_ENCODE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. On success the job's `state` becomes `RUNNING`, its `committed_usd` becomes its `cost_estimate_usd`, the title's `encode_budget_remaining_usd` falls by that amount and the title's `version` increments by one.

The remaining budget is a derived ledger, exactly as vendor capacity is. At every observable moment `encode_budget_remaining_usd` equals the title's `encode_budget_usd` less the rounded sum of `cost_estimate_usd` over that title's jobs whose `state` is `RUNNING` or `COMPLETED`, to two decimals. Jobs in `QUEUED`, `ABORTED` and `FAILED` contribute nothing and carry `committed_usd` of 0.00, and §2.9's money invariant — remaining budget plus the sum of every job's `committed_usd` equals `encode_budget_usd` — holds with zero drift.

A running job leaves `RUNNING` in one of three ways.

- **Sweep completion.** Phase 3 of the sweep completes a `RUNNING` job whose source ladder is still `LOCKED`, and this drives the depth-4 cascade of §2.9. Hop one: a new encoding ladder is created for the same title with `codec_family` set to the job's `target_codec_family`, its `rungs` copied from the source ladder, `derived_from_ladder_id` set to the `source_ladder_id`, `state` `LOCKED` and `validation_reason_code` `OK` — the result ladder is born locked, without passing through authoring or validation. Hop two: because that result ladder locked, the source ladder moves from `LOCKED` to `SUPERSEDED` with its `superseded_by_ladder_id` pointing at the result ladder. Hop three: because the source ladder became `SUPERSEDED`, every DRM package bound to it in `state` `PACKAGED` moves to `STALE` with `stale_reason` `LADDER_SUPERSEDED`. Hop four: because a package went `STALE`, the title's `packaging_state` becomes `STALE` and `readiness_score_certified` is recomputed. The job itself becomes `COMPLETED` with `result_ladder_id` set to the new ladder, and its `committed_usd` is left untouched — the encode happened, so the budget stays spent. Because Phase 3 runs before Phase 4, the very same sweep sees the freshly stale packaging and takes the title down rather than publishing it.
- **Sweep failure.** Phase 3 fails a `RUNNING` job whose source ladder is no longer `LOCKED`, which happens when something superseded it while the job was in flight. The job's `state` becomes `FAILED`, its `abort_reason` becomes `ENCODE_FAILURE` and its `committed_usd` returns to 0.00, and the title's `encode_budget_remaining_usd` rises by exactly the `cost_estimate_usd`, restoring the money invariant. No result ladder is created and no packaging cascade fires.
- **Operator abort.** An `EncodingEngineer` aborts the job, supplying `abort_reason` in the request. Only `OPERATOR_ABORT` is accepted from an operator; `ENCODE_FAILURE` belongs to the sweep and `BUDGET_RECLAIM` is reserved for system-initiated reclamation, so neither may be asserted by a caller. Aborting from `RUNNING` is the compensating action for the run debit: `state` becomes the terminal `ABORTED`, `committed_usd` returns to 0.00, `encode_budget_remaining_usd` returns to exactly its pre-run value with zero drift, and the title's `version` increments by one. Aborting from `QUEUED` is also legal and is the cheaper case: nothing was ever debited, so `committed_usd` stays 0.00 and `encode_budget_remaining_usd` is unchanged. An abort attempted on a job already `COMPLETED`, `FAILED` or `ABORTED` answers `ERR_JOB_STATE_INVALID`.
