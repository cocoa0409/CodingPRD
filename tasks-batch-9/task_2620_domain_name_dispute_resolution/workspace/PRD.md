# Domain Name Dispute Resolution API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An accredited domain name dispute resolution provider sits between three parties who do not trust one another: a trademark owner who claims a registered domain was taken in bad faith, a domain registrant who may be a legitimate business or may be a cybersquatter hiding behind a privacy proxy, and a registrar of record whose commercial relationship is with the registrant rather than with the complainant. The provider's authority is entirely procedural. It cannot seize a domain, it cannot compel testimony, and it cannot survive an appeal to a national court. What it can do — and what this API exists to do reliably — is run a fixed administrative procedure in which every deadline is computed rather than negotiated, every neutral is screened for conflicts before being seated, every domain is frozen against transfer for the duration of the proceeding, and every decision is held back long enough for a losing party to seek relief in a court of mutual jurisdiction.

The operational pain the system addresses is that the procedure is riddled with deadlines that are not derivable from a single clock reading. A response deadline is projected at dispatch from an assumed transit allowance and then re-anchored on the observed receipt instant, and the second value can fall either side of the first. A correction period runs from the issuance of a deficiency notice. An implementation waiting period is counted in provider business days across weekends and a published holiday calendar, and it restarts from scratch every time a court challenge is lifted. Meanwhile the money moves in the opposite direction from the procedure: a complaint is priced provisionally at filing on the assumption of a single-member panel, and a respondent who elects a three-member panel silently re-prices the case upward for the complainant while creating a new obligation for itself — leaving a case that had paid in full instantly short.

This document specifies the complete contract for that system: the resources the provider persists, the fixed order in which conflicting rules refuse a request, the arithmetic behind every fee, honorarium, conflict score, deadline and precedent weight, and the cascades that fire when a case closes. The API is mounted under `/udrp`. All behaviour described here is evaluated against a server-held logical clock, never against wall-clock time, so that an entire multi-month proceeding can be driven deterministically end to end.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Authentication and the Segregation of Provider Duties

Every request to every endpoint — including the two test-driving endpoints — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that user acts. Six roles are recognised, spelled exactly as follows: `ProviderAdmin`, `CaseManager`, `Complainant`, `Respondent`, `Panelist`, and `RegistrarAgent`. A request that omits either header, or whose `X-User-Role` is not one of those six strings, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A request whose role is well-formed but is not among the roles authorised for the target operation is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). These two checks run ahead of everything else, in that order.

The six roles do not all resolve identity the same way, and the difference matters because ownership checks depend on it.

- When `X-User-Role` is `Complainant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Complainant` row. Ownership checks on any case-scoped resource compare the case's `complainant_id` to `X-User-ID`; a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This applies when a complainant asserts a ground, files a correction, withdraws a complaint, or files a court challenge as the complainant.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Panelist`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Panelist` row. A conflict screening is admissible only from the neutral who holds the seat, meaning `X-User-ID` must equal that appointment's `panelist_id`; a ballot is admissible only from a neutral holding an `ACCEPTED` seat on the decision's case. Either failure is `ERR_NOT_SEAT_HOLDER` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `RegistrarAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Registrar` row. The only registrar permitted to answer a verification request or to execute a decision against a domain is the one recorded as that domain's `registrar_id`; any other registrar is refused with `ERR_REGISTRAR_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `ProviderAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server never compares it to any row's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `CaseManager`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity — case managers are addressed by pinned operator ids such as `cm-001` and `cm-002`. The server does record it: the issuer of a deficiency notice is stored as that notice's `issued_by_user_id` and copied to the case's `last_deficiency_issuer_user_id`, and the manager who clears compliance is stored as `compliance_cleared_by_user_id`. Those stored values are compared to one another, never to an entity `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Respondent`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity either — there is no `Respondent` resource at all, because the true registrant is unknown to the provider until the registrar discloses it. A respondent instead proves identity per request by supplying `respondent_email` in the payload, which must equal the case's `verified_registrant_email`; a mismatch is `ERR_RESPONDENT_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

Provider-side work is deliberately split. Roster admission, registrar accreditation, decision publication, court dispositions, the logical clock and the deadline sweep belong to `ProviderAdmin`; the day-to-day conduct of a proceeding belongs to `CaseManager`. A second constraint operates inside the `CaseManager` role itself: the operator whose `X-User-ID` issued a case's most recent deficiency notice may not be the operator who clears that same case through administrative compliance review. That attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The three party-side roles are peers over disjoint resources.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, must be at least 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0. Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by the resource's creation timestamp ascending — oldest first.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope. `total_count` counts the rows that satisfy the request's filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the returned window.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock, the Holiday Calendar and the Deadline Sweep

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. All deadlines, decay factors, interval comparisons and timestamps are evaluated against a single server-held logical clock. Before any caller moves it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A `ProviderAdmin` moves it by supplying a `current_utc` instant; moving the clock touches no stored row and re-evaluates no deadline on its own.

Time-driven transitions are therefore never spontaneous — they are applied by an explicit administrative sweep, which advances everything that has become due as of the current clock reading, in a fixed three-stage order: first, every `OPEN` deficiency notice whose correction deadline has passed expires and closes its case with a full refund; second, every `REQUESTED` registrar verification past its due instant becomes `OVERDUE`; third, every `COMMENCED` case whose response window and late grace have both lapsed without a response records a default. The sweep reports what it did as four counters in its response body: `expired_notice_count`, `overdue_verification_count`, `defaulted_case_count` and `closed_case_count`. It is idempotent — running it twice at the same clock reading changes nothing on the second pass and returns all four counters as zero.

Two distinct calendars are in play. Most periods in this specification are counted in whole days or whole hours of elapsed time, preserving the time-of-day of whatever instant anchors them. The implementation waiting period alone is counted in provider business days, which skips Saturdays, skips Sundays, and skips every date on the provider holiday calendar. That calendar contains exactly one date: `2026-09-07`.

### 2.5. Docket Identity and Common Resource Properties

Every resource the provider persists carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` that is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, taken from the logical clock at the moment of creation. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increments by one on every mutation of that row. Recomputing a derived field counts as a mutation for both purposes: when a cascade rewrites a case's money fields or its response deadline, that case's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented.

Two record types are immutable event rows and are the exception: a panel ballot and a fee ledger line carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither has an `updated_at` and neither has a `version` — once written, they are never modified, and nothing in the system rewrites them.

Separately from its UUID, each proceeding receives a human-facing provider docket number in `case_number`, minted as the literal prefix `D-2026-` followed by a six-digit zero-padded sequence number drawn from a monotonic counter. That counter increments once per accepted case creation, in `created_at` ascending order, so the first accepted complaint is `D-2026-000001`, the second `D-2026-000002`, and so on.

### 2.6. Resolving Case-Rooted and Nested Paths

Identifiers in a path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that names nothing aborts the request with the 404 that belongs to that position. A path rooted at a case resolves the case first and reports `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND` when it names no case; a domain path reports `ERR_DOMAIN_NOT_FOUND`; a panelist path reports `ERR_PANELIST_NOT_FOUND`; an appointment path reports `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`; a decision path reports `ERR_DECISION_NOT_FOUND`. Only once the parent has resolved does the server look at a nested sub-resource identifier — a verification id or a notification id — and such an identifier must both exist and belong to the case named earlier in the same path; failing either, the answer is `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. A court challenge identifier is resolved the same way, with the same error.

The same discipline extends to identifiers carried in a request body rather than in the path. A `complainant_id`, `domain_id`, `panelist_id`, `notice_id`, `drafted_by_panelist_id`, `replacement_panelist_id` or any member of a `cited_decision_ids` array must name an existing row of the entity it is expected to name; if not, the request is refused with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. Body-referenced existence is checked after path existence and before the schema check.

### 2.7. Version Pinning on Panel-Composition Writes

Two operations read a case's panel plan and then write against it, and both require the caller to pin the version it read. Nominating a panelist to a seat — `POST /udrp/cases/{id}/appointments` — requires `expected_case_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` value the caller last observed on that case. Opening a decision draft — `POST /udrp/cases/{id}/decisions` — likewise requires `expected_case_version`. In both cases the field is mandatory, not optional. If the supplied value does not equal the case's current stored `version`, the write is refused with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing at all is persisted. On success the case's `version` advances by one, so a caller performing two nominations in sequence must re-read the case between them.

The reason the pin exists is that both reads are structural. A nomination is computed against the case's seat plan, its paid totals and its existing appointment rows; because a respondent's election of a three-member panel changes `required_seat_count` and re-prices the case, a nomination computed against a pre-election snapshot would try to place a seat that no longer exists in the current plan. A decision draft snapshots the constituted panel and the seat count that the eventual vote tally will be measured against; if a seat has been replaced since the caller's read, the stored version has moved and the draft is refused, so a draft can never be attached to a panel composition that no longer holds.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Operations not named above are processed without any version check, and supplying a version to them has no defined effect.

### 2.8. Money, Cents and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary quantity in this system — assessed fees, party shares, honoraria, ledger lines — is an integer number of cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere and no floating-point money fields. Where a percentage produces a fractional cent, the specification states which direction it goes, and the two directions are used deliberately: the expedited surcharge and the panelist honoraria pool are rounded half up, whereas the respondent's half of the three-member supplement and each individual seat's raw honoraria share are floored. Refund amounts are rounded half up, computed independently per payer.

Two non-monetary computed numbers carry two decimal places and are rounded half up: a panel appointment's `conflict_score` and a precedent entry's `precedent_weight`. Vote counts, seat counts, load counters and population counters are exact integers with no rounding at all.

### 2.9. Cascade Integrity and the Running Counters

State changes in this system are rarely confined to one row. Closing a case releases the transfer lock on its domain, discharges its seated neutrals, adjusts the complainant's population counters and may mint refund lines on its ledger; replacing a seated neutral un-constitutes the panel and voids any draft decision on the case. Each such cascade is applied immediately and in full — there is no deferred reconciliation and no eventual consistency window. Every row a cascade touches has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented, so a cascade on a case that also unlocks a domain advances two independent version numbers.

Throughout this document, the phrase *non-terminal case status* means exactly one of these eleven values: `FILED`, `DEFICIENT`, `VERIFICATION_PENDING`, `NOTIFICATION_PENDING`, `COMMENCED`, `RESPONSE_RECEIVED`, `DEFAULT`, `PANEL_APPOINTMENT`, `PANEL_CONSTITUTED`, `IMPLEMENTATION_WAITING`, `STAYED`. The four terminal statuses are `IMPLEMENTED`, `ADMIN_CLOSED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `TERMINATED_COURT`.

Four counters are maintained incrementally but must at every moment agree with their recount definition:

- A panelist's `active_case_load` gains one when a seat of theirs becomes `ACCEPTED` and loses one when an `ACCEPTED` seat of theirs becomes `REPLACED` or `DISCHARGED`. It must always equal the number of that panelist's appointment rows in status `ACCEPTED` whose case is in a non-terminal status. Seats in `NOMINATED` or `WAIVER_PENDING` deliberately carry no load — which is exactly what makes a screening that lands in `WAIVER_PENDING` observably different from one that lands in `ACCEPTED`.
- A complainant's `open_case_count` gains one when a complaint is accepted and loses one on every arrival at `IMPLEMENTED`, `ADMIN_CLOSED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `TERMINATED_COURT`. It must always equal the number of that complainant's cases in a non-terminal status.
- A complainant's `adverse_rdnh_findings` is a signed counter: it gains one when an issued decision on one of that complainant's cases carries `rdnh_finding` true, and loses one again if that decision is later voided by a sustained court challenge. It therefore always equals the number of non-voided decisions carrying `rdnh_finding` true across all of that complainant's cases.
- A registrar's `overdue_verification_count` gains one when one of its verification requests moves from `REQUESTED` to `OVERDUE`, and loses one when an `OVERDUE` request is either answered by the registrar or re-requested by the provider. It must always equal the number of that registrar's verification rows currently in status `OVERDUE`.

### 2.10. The Order of Refusal

Many requests in this system fail more than one rule at once, and which failure is reported is part of the contract rather than an implementation detail. Checks are evaluated in the fixed order below, and the first check that fails is the one reported; no later check is evaluated. The bands are listed from earliest to latest.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` naming a declared role, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role authorisation.** The acting role appears among the roles authorised for the operation, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Identity and ownership**, in this internal order: a complainant acting on a case-scoped resource must own it (`ERR_NOT_OWNER`); a registrar agent must be the case domain's registrar of record (`ERR_REGISTRAR_MISMATCH`); a respondent's supplied `respondent_email` must match the case's verified registrant address (`ERR_RESPONDENT_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`); a panelist must hold the seat being acted on or voted from (`ERR_NOT_SEAT_HOLDER`).
4. **Existence**, path identifiers first and left to right (`ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DOMAIN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PANELIST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DECISION_NOT_FOUND`), then nested sub-resource and court-challenge identifiers, then body-referenced identifiers (`ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` for both of the latter).
5. **Schema.** Required fields present, enum values drawn from their declared sets, integer fields non-negative where the field requires it, and any two-letter country code exactly two letters — else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
6. **Duplicate response.** A case carries at most one response; a second filing is `ERR_RESPONSE_ALREADY_FILED`. This check deliberately sits ahead of the state gates that follow, because filing a response moves the case out of `COMMENCED`: were it ordered later, a duplicate filing would always surface as a state error and this rule would be unreachable.
7. **State gates.** The target case must be in a status from which the operation is defined (`ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`); a response specifically requires a case in `COMMENCED` (`ERR_CASE_NOT_IN_RESPONSE_WINDOW`); the target appointment must be in an admitting status (`ERR_INVALID_APPOINTMENT_STATE`); the target decision must be in an admitting status (`ERR_INVALID_DECISION_STATE`); a decision may not be published twice (`ERR_ALREADY_PUBLISHED`); a case may only be withdrawn from a pre-appointment status (`ERR_WITHDRAWAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`). Two operations are admitted from a deliberately wider set of case statuses at this band so that their sharper rules stay reachable: opening a decision draft and issuing a decision both accept a case in `PANEL_APPOINTMENT` or in `PANEL_CONSTITUTED` here, and it is the panel-completeness rule far later in this ladder — not this gate — that refuses issuance while a seat is still unfilled.
8. **Version pinning.** `expected_case_version` equals the stored case `version`, else `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION`.
9. **Governance and safety**, in this internal order: the segregation-of-duties bar at compliance clearance (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`); a suspended registrar answering a verification (`ERR_REGISTRAR_SUSPENDED`); a verification response that does not attest the lock (`ERR_DOMAIN_LOCK_NOT_APPLIED`); a domain that is not locked at notification or at implementation (`ERR_DOMAIN_NOT_LOCKED`); a waiver attempted on a disqualifying conflict (`ERR_CONFLICT_NOT_WAIVABLE`).
10. **Referential uniqueness and occupation**, in this internal order: domain-name uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_DOMAIN_NAME`); a domain already occupied by a live proceeding (`ERR_DOMAIN_PROCEEDING_OVERLAP`); a domain still inside a closed case's cooling-off interval (`ERR_REFILING_BAR_ACTIVE`); a second ground of the same type on one case (`ERR_DUPLICATE_GROUND_TYPE`); a cited precedent that is not citable (`ERR_CITED_PRECEDENT_NOT_PUBLISHED`).
11. **Business constraints**, in this internal order: the deficiency-round ceiling (`ERR_DEFICIENCY_ROUND_LIMIT`); insufficient evidence on some ground at clearance (`ERR_GROUND_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT`); a seat the case's seat count does not admit (`ERR_SEAT_NOT_APPLICABLE`); a seat already held (`ERR_SEAT_ALREADY_FILLED`); a named panelist failing the non-temporal eligibility clauses (`ERR_PANELIST_INELIGIBLE`); an engagement-window collision (`ERR_PANELIST_WINDOW_CONFLICT`); the aggregate engagement ceiling (`ERR_PANELIST_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`); a seat voting twice (`ERR_VOTE_ALREADY_CAST`). The window check is ordered ahead of the capacity check on purpose: adjacent-but-legal windows are the only route by which the capacity error becomes observable, so ordering them this way makes each independently reachable.
12. **Temporal windows**, in this internal order: a lapsed correction period (`ERR_CORRECTION_PERIOD_EXPIRED`); a response past its deadline plus grace (`ERR_RESPONSE_DEADLINE_PASSED`); an implementation attempted while the waiting period is still running (`ERR_IMPLEMENTATION_PERIOD_ACTIVE`); a court challenge filed after the implementation deadline (`ERR_CHALLENGE_WINDOW_CLOSED`). The response-deadline check sits behind the state gates of band 7, so a case that has already defaulted reports its state rather than the lateness of the attempted filing.
13. **Fiscal**, in this internal order: the complainant's share unpaid at clearance (`ERR_FILING_FEE_SHORTFALL`); the assessed total unpaid at nomination (`ERR_PANEL_SUPPLEMENT_UNPAID`). Both sit behind the governance band, which is why a case that is simultaneously unpaid and tainted by a segregation-of-duties failure reports the governance failure.
14. **Completeness**, in this internal order: the three mandatory grounds missing at clearance (`ERR_MISSING_REQUIRED_GROUND`); a panel that is not fully constituted at issuance (`ERR_PANEL_NOT_CONSTITUTED`); an empty eligible set when the provider must select a neutral (`ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PANELIST`); fewer ballots than seats at issuance (`ERR_VOTES_INCOMPLETE`); a waiver missing a required consent (`ERR_WAIVER_INCOMPLETE`).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Building the Docket: Registrars, Domains, Complainants and the Roster

Before a single complaint can be filed, the provider must populate four reference registers. A `ProviderAdmin` accredits a registrar by supplying its display `name`, its IANA registrar identifier as `iana_id`, and the abuse and verification mailbox as `contact_email`. The `iana_id` must be unique across all registrars and between 1 and 16 characters long. Two service-level numbers ride along on the registrar record: `verification_response_hours`, the number of hours the registrar is given to answer a verification request before the provider sweeps it to overdue, which defaults to 48 when omitted and must be between 1 and 336; and `lock_sla_hours`, the number of hours the registrar commits to for applying a transfer lock, which defaults to 24, is likewise bounded between 1 and 336, and is recorded purely for audit — no gate anywhere in this specification enforces it. A newly accredited registrar begins with `status` `ACTIVE` and `overdue_verification_count` at 0. Its `status` may later be moved between `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED`, and a registrar sitting in `SUSPENDED` can no longer answer verification requests.

Disputed domains are registered separately from the complaints that attack them. Registering one requires the fully qualified `domain_name`, the sponsoring registrar as `registrar_id`, the registrant identity as it appeared in public WHOIS at filing time via `registrant_name_public` and `registrant_email_public`, and the registration's `registration_date` and `expiry_date` as ISO 8601 UTC instants. The `domain_name` is stored lowercased, must be between 4 and 253 characters, and is globally unique — a second attempt at a name already on the docket is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DOMAIN_NAME`. A newly registered domain starts at `lock_status` `UNLOCKED` with both `locked_at` and `executed_at` null. Domains can be listed with a filter on `registrar_id` or on `lock_status`.

The transfer lock also has an out-of-band operator surface, used by the registrar of record (and available to the provider's administrators) to apply or release the lock outside the verification flow. It accepts a `lock_status` of either `UNLOCKED` or `LOCKED`; any other value, and any attempt to touch a domain whose current `lock_status` is `TRANSFER_EXECUTED` or `CANCELLATION_EXECUTED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Applying the lock records `locked_at` at the current logical-clock instant; releasing it returns `locked_at` to null. Either way the domain's `version` advances. This surface is what makes it possible for a domain to be unlocked while a proceeding is mid-flight, which in turn is what makes `ERR_DOMAIN_NOT_LOCKED` reachable at notification and again at implementation.

Complainants are registered with a `legal_name`, an ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 `country_code` of exactly two uppercase letters, and a correspondence `email`. The `country_code` is not decoration — it feeds the panelist nationality exclusion described later. A new complainant starts with `open_case_count` and `adverse_rdnh_findings` both at 0, and both counters are readable on the complainant record.

Roster admission is a `ProviderAdmin` prerogative. A neutral is admitted with a `full_name`, an ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 `nationality_code` of exactly two uppercase letters, and a `roster_admitted_at` date recording admission to the roster. Three optional attributes may accompany the admission: `firm_name`, the law-firm affiliation disclosed during conflict screening, which is null for a sole practitioner; `specializations`, an array of subject tags recorded strictly for audit and never used as a selector filter or as a ranking key; and `max_overlapping_engagements`, the aggregate ceiling on engagement windows that may overlap a new candidate window on that neutral, which defaults to 5 and must be between 1 and 20. A newly admitted panelist begins at `status` `AVAILABLE` with `active_case_load` at 0.

A panelist's own availability can be revised later, either by the neutral or by a provider administrator, in a single call that carries any subset of four attributes: `status`, which may be `AVAILABLE`, `UNAVAILABLE` or `SUSPENDED`; `unavailable_from` and `unavailable_to`, the inclusive start and inclusive end of a declared blackout window; and `max_overlapping_engagements`. Any attribute omitted from the call is left exactly as it was, and supplying `unavailable_from` as null clears the declared blackout interval entirely. Because the blackout interval is closed at both ends, an engagement window that merely touches `unavailable_to` still collides with it — a deliberate asymmetry against engagement windows, which are treated as half-open. Only a panelist in `AVAILABLE` ever enters the provider's candidate set; `UNAVAILABLE` and `SUSPENDED` neutrals are filtered out before any ranking happens.

### 3.2. Filing a Complaint: Occupation, Grounds and the Provisional Assessment

A complaint is filed by naming the filing party as `complainant_id`, the single disputed domain as `domain_id`, the country the complainant submits to for court challenge as `mutual_jurisdiction` (exactly two uppercase letters), and the relief sought as `remedy_requested`, which is either `TRANSFER` or `CANCELLATION`. Two optional attributes shape the proceeding from the outset: `panel_election`, the panel size the complainant elects, which is `SINGLE` by default and may be `THREE`; and `expedited_requested`, a boolean defaulting to false that buys expedited administrative handling in exchange for a surcharge. When the caller acts as `Complainant`, the supplied `complainant_id` must equal `X-User-ID`.

Before the row is written, the provider checks that the domain is free. A domain is occupied by any case on it that has not reached a terminal status, and that occupation interval is open-ended on the right — it simply has no end until the case closes. Any existing case on the same `domain_id` sitting in a non-terminal status refuses the new filing with `ERR_DOMAIN_PROCEEDING_OVERLAP`, regardless of what the clock reads. A closed case leaves behind a cooling-off interval on that same domain instead, running from its `closed_at` to its `refiling_bar_until`, where `refiling_bar_until` is `closed_at` plus 30 days. Both endpoints of that interval are inclusive, so a filing attempted at the exact instant of `refiling_bar_until` is still barred with `ERR_REFILING_BAR_ACTIVE` and the domain frees up one instant later. Cooling-off intervals are never merged across cases: every closed case on the domain is tested on its own, which means the latest `refiling_bar_until` governs even when an earlier bar has already lapsed. The bar is domain-scoped, not party-scoped — the identity of the earlier complainant is irrelevant, and cases on other domains never participate.

An accepted filing lands in `status` `FILED` with `version` 1, mints its `case_number`, and sets `respondent_panel_election` to `NONE`. The `required_seat_count` a case carries is 3 whenever either the complainant's `panel_election` or the respondent's `respondent_panel_election` is `THREE`, and 1 otherwise; with the default `SINGLE` election it therefore stands at 1 at filing. The filing also increments the complainant's `open_case_count` by one and immediately prices the proceeding.

That pricing is the **provisional variant** of the filing fee assessment, and the case records which variant produced its current money fields in `fee_variant_applied`, which starts at `PROVISIONAL`. The provisional variant deliberately prices a single-member panel: the seat count used for pricing is forced to 1, so no three-member supplement enters the assessment and `respondent_share_cents` is 0. The arithmetic runs as follows. The base administrative fee is 150000 cents. The three-member supplement, which the provisional variant never applies, is 250000 cents. The expedited surcharge, applied only when `expedited_requested` is true, is 12.5 percent of the base administrative fee alone, rounded half up to a whole cent — it is never charged on the three-member supplement, which is why the surcharge is a constant 18750 cents whenever expedited handling was bought, at either seat count. The `assessed_fee_cents` is the base administrative fee plus the applicable supplement plus the applicable surcharge. The respondent's share of the assessment, `respondent_share_cents`, is one half of the raw three-member supplement rounded down to a whole cent when the priced seat count is 3, and 0 otherwise; it is never re-scaled by the surcharge. The complainant's share, `complainant_share_cents`, is whatever is left: `assessed_fee_cents` minus `respondent_share_cents`. Finally, `honoraria_pool_cents` is 70 percent of `assessed_fee_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent; the remaining 30 percent is the provider's administrative retention and is not stored on the case. A standard single-member filing therefore assesses 150000 cents entirely to the complainant with a pool of 105000 cents, and an expedited single-member filing assesses 168750 cents entirely to the complainant with a pool of 118125 cents.

The complaint's substance arrives as three separate assertions, one per mandatory element. Each is written with a `ground_type` of `TRADEMARK_RIGHTS`, `NO_LEGITIMATE_INTEREST` or `BAD_FAITH`, a narrative pleading in `assertion_text` between 10 and 4000 characters, and an `evidence_count` between 0 and 200 recording how many annexes were filed in support. A case may carry at most one assertion per `ground_type`; a second attempt at the same element is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_GROUND_TYPE`. Three further attributes are required when — and only when — the element being asserted is `TRADEMARK_RIGHTS`, and are ignored on the other two elements: `mark_registration_number`, the trademark registration number; `mark_jurisdiction`, the two-letter registry of the asserted mark; and `similarity_claim`, the confusion theory pleaded, which is either `IDENTICAL` or `CONFUSINGLY_SIMILAR`. Each assertion also carries `cited_decision_ids`, an array of earlier decisions relied on as precedent, defaulting to empty.

Every asserted element is graded the moment it is written. Its `sufficiency_status` is `SUFFICIENT` when `evidence_count` is at least 1, and `DEFICIENT` otherwise — the threshold is inclusive, so a single annex suffices and zero annexes does not. Zero is nevertheless accepted at filing time; it is only administrative compliance review that refuses to let such a case proceed. The grade is recomputed on every write that changes `evidence_count`.

Citations are validated at the moment the assertion is written, not later. Every identifier inside `cited_decision_ids` must name a decision that is currently citable — meaning its publication dimension is `PUBLISHED`, or it is `REDACTED` with a redaction level of `PARTY_NAMES`, and its precedent entry has not been superseded by the decision being voided. A citation of a decision that is still unpublished, or one that was published under a full redaction, is refused with `ERR_CITED_PRECEDENT_NOT_PUBLISHED`.

### 3.3. The Case Ledger, Withdrawal and Refunds

Money on a proceeding lives in an append-only ledger of case-scoped lines, each recorded with a `kind` of `FILING_FEE`, `PANEL_SUPPLEMENT`, `REFUND` or `WRITE_OFF`, a `payer` of `COMPLAINANT`, `RESPONDENT` or `PROVIDER`, a signed `amount_cents`, an optional `reference` retained for audit, and a `recorded_at` taken from the logical clock. Inflows are positive and refund lines are negative; the magnitude must lie between -10000000 and 10000000 and may never be exactly 0. The `PROVIDER` attribution exists only for write-off lines. Ledger lines are immutable once written — nothing in the system amends or deletes one, and a correction is expressed as a further line.

After every ledger write, and after every refund cascade, the case's three derived money fields are recomputed from the whole ledger rather than adjusted in place: `complainant_paid_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the case's lines attributed to `COMPLAINANT`, `respondent_paid_cents` is the same sum over lines attributed to `RESPONDENT`, and `paid_cents` is the sum over every line on the case regardless of payer. Because provider write-offs are counted in `paid_cents` but in neither party subtotal, `paid_cents` is always at least the sum of the two party subtotals. A filing fee of 150000 cents from the complainant followed by a panel supplement of 125000 cents from the respondent therefore leaves the two subtotals at 150000 and 125000 with `paid_cents` at 275000; a further provider write-off of 20000 cents leaves the subtotals untouched and takes `paid_cents` to 295000.

A complainant may abandon a proceeding before the panel is appointed. Withdrawal is admitted from `FILED`, `DEFICIENT`, `VERIFICATION_PENDING`, `NOTIFICATION_PENDING`, `COMMENCED`, `RESPONSE_RECEIVED` and `DEFAULT`; attempting it from `PANEL_APPOINTMENT` or any later status is refused with `ERR_WITHDRAWAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`, and a complainant attempting it on someone else's case is refused with `ERR_NOT_OWNER`. An optional `reason` may accompany the request. The case moves to `WITHDRAWN`, its `closed_at` is stamped at the current logical-clock instant, and its `refiling_bar_until` is set 30 days later.

How much money comes back depends purely on how far the case had travelled at the moment of withdrawal. A case in `FILED` or `DEFICIENT` refunds in full — 100 percent. A case in `VERIFICATION_PENDING` or `NOTIFICATION_PENDING` refunds half — 50 percent. A case in `COMMENCED` or anything beyond it refunds nothing at all — 0 percent — and no refund line is minted. The refund is then computed payer by payer in a fixed order, the complainant first and the respondent second, never in identifier order. For each of those two payers, the provider sums that payer's contributions on the ledger, multiplies by the applicable percentage, rounds half up to a whole cent, and — only when the result is strictly greater than zero — mints one refund line for that payer carrying the negative of that amount and the reference `WITHDRAWAL-REFUND`. Provider write-off lines never participate and are never refunded. The three derived money fields are then recomputed from the ledger. A withdrawn case in `VERIFICATION_PENDING` that had taken 293750 cents from the complainant and 125000 cents from the respondent therefore yields two refund lines of -146875 and -62500, leaving `paid_cents` at 209375. Because refunds are negative lines attributed back to the original payer, a payer whose contributions have already been fully returned sums to zero and produces no further line.

The withdrawal also tidies up everything the proceeding had opened. Any deficiency notice still in `OPEN` becomes `EXPIRED`. Any registrar verification in `REQUESTED` or in `OVERDUE` becomes `OVERDUE`. A domain sitting in `LOCKED` returns to `UNLOCKED`. The complainant's `open_case_count` drops by one. The case's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` advances.

### 3.4. Administrative Compliance Review, the Correction Loop and Registrar Verification

Every complaint passes an administrative compliance review before the respondent ever hears about it. A case manager who finds the filing defective issues a deficiency notice against it. The case must be in `FILED`; a case already sitting in `DEFICIENT` with a notice still open cannot receive a second one, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. The case's `deficiency_round_count` must be below the ceiling of 2 rounds; a third notice is refused with `ERR_DEFICIENCY_ROUND_LIMIT`.

The notice carries `deficiency_codes`, an array of between 1 and 7 distinct codes drawn from a closed set of seven: `MISSING_GROUND`, `MISSING_MARK_EVIDENCE`, `MISSING_SIGNATURE`, `ILLEGIBLE_EVIDENCE_ANNEX`, `FEE_SHORTFALL`, `MUTUAL_JURISDICTION_ABSENT` and `DOMAIN_MISMATCH`. It may also carry `correction_days`, the length of the cure window in days, which defaults to 5 when omitted and must be between 1 and 20. The notice is created in `status` `OPEN`, stamps `issued_at` from the logical clock, records the issuing case manager in `issued_by_user_id`, sets `round_number` to the case's `deficiency_round_count` plus one — never exceeding 2 — and sets `correction_deadline_at` to `issued_at` plus `correction_days` days. The case moves from `FILED` to `DEFICIENT`, its `deficiency_round_count` advances by one, and the issuer's identity is copied onto the case as `last_deficiency_issuer_user_id`. Notices issued on a case are readable through the case's own notice list, in the round order in which they were issued.

Curing a deficiency is the complainant's move, and only the owning complainant may make it. The case must be in `DEFICIENT`, and the request must name the notice being cured as `notice_id`. That notice must belong to this case and must still be in `OPEN`; naming a notice that has already been cured or has expired is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. The cure must arrive no later than the notice's `correction_deadline_at` — the deadline is inclusive, so a cure filed at that exact instant is accepted and one instant later is refused with `ERR_CORRECTION_PERIOD_EXPIRED`. The complainant supplies `correction_notes` describing what was fixed. The notice becomes `CURED` with `cured_at` stamped from the clock, and the case returns from `DEFICIENT` to `FILED`. Nothing about the asserted elements is re-graded by the cure itself; sufficiency is only re-tested at clearance, so a complainant who cures without actually adding annexes will be refused again at the compliance gate. Because the round ceiling is 2, the notice-and-cure loop can run at most twice before the case must either clear compliance or be closed.

A deficient case that is never cured does not linger. When the deadline sweep finds a notice in `OPEN` whose `correction_deadline_at` has passed, the notice becomes `EXPIRED` and the whole case is closed administratively: the case moves from `DEFICIENT` to `ADMIN_CLOSED`, `closed_at` is stamped from the clock, and `refiling_bar_until` is set 30 days later. The automatic close always refunds in full, at 100 percent, regardless of how far the case had travelled, and its refund lines carry the reference `AUTO-CLOSE-REFUND` instead of the withdrawal reference. Both party subtotals therefore land on exactly 0, while `paid_cents` lands on the sum of any provider write-off lines, which are never refunded — so `paid_cents` reaches 0 only on a case that carries no write-off. The complainant's `open_case_count` drops by one, and a domain sitting in `LOCKED` returns to `UNLOCKED`.

Clearance is the gate that lets the proceeding out of administrative review, and it is where the provider's segregation of duties bites. A case manager clears a case in `FILED`, optionally recording `reviewer_notes`. The clearing case manager may not be the person recorded in `last_deficiency_issuer_user_id`; if they are the same, the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. That refusal is checked ahead of the money check, so a case manager who issued the last notice is turned away for the conflict even when the complainant has also underpaid — the fee shortfall is never the reported reason in that situation. Beyond the duties check, clearance requires four things: the case's `mutual_jurisdiction` must be present as two uppercase letters; the complainant must have paid at least their share, meaning `complainant_paid_cents` is greater than or equal to `complainant_share_cents`, or the request is refused with `ERR_FILING_FEE_SHORTFALL`; the case must carry exactly one asserted element of each of the three mandatory `ground_type` values, or it is refused with `ERR_MISSING_REQUIRED_GROUND`; and every one of those elements must be graded `SUFFICIENT`, or the request is refused with `ERR_GROUND_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT`. Note that the completeness check outranks neither the money check nor the duties check — both of those are reported in preference to it. The evidence-sufficiency check runs the other way round: a case whose grounds are all present but one of them unsupported reports the insufficient evidence in preference to any fee shortfall.

A successful clearance records the clearing officer on the case as `compliance_cleared_by_user_id`, moves the case from `FILED` to `VERIFICATION_PENDING`, and cascades into the registrar: a verification request row is created for the case in `status` `REQUESTED`, with `requested_at` at the current logical-clock instant and `due_at` that many hours later, where the number of hours is the sponsoring registrar's own `verification_response_hours`. That row is created by the cascade and is never returned by the clearance response, so the case's verification list is the only channel through which it can be discovered.

A verification row records the provider's demand and the registrar's answer. Alongside `case_id`, `requested_at`, `due_at` and `status`, it carries `responded_at`, the instant the registrar answered; `verified_registrant_name` and `verified_registrant_email`, the true registrant identity as disclosed from the registrar's own records rather than from public WHOIS; `lock_applied`, the registrar's confirmation that the transfer lock is in place; `registrar_remarks`, free-text notes from the registrar; and `escalation_count`, the number of times this particular request has gone overdue, starting at 0.

When the deadline sweep finds a verification row still in `REQUESTED` whose `due_at` has passed, the row moves to `OVERDUE`, its own `escalation_count` advances by one, and the sponsoring registrar's `overdue_verification_count` advances by one as well. The case itself stays in `VERIFICATION_PENDING`; an unresponsive registrar stalls the proceeding rather than killing it.

A case manager may then re-request verification. The row must be in `OVERDUE` and the case in `VERIFICATION_PENDING`. The same row returns to `REQUESTED` with a fresh `requested_at` taken from the current clock and a fresh `due_at` that many hours later, again drawn from the registrar's `verification_response_hours`, and the registrar's `overdue_verification_count` drops back by one — the registrar is credited for the re-request even though the row's own `escalation_count` keeps accumulating across laps. This operation reports the reactivated row as a newly created resource. A row can go overdue and be re-requested repeatedly, each lap adding one to `escalation_count` while `overdue_verification_count` oscillates.

The registrar answers by disclosing the registrant and confirming the lock. Only a `RegistrarAgent` whose `X-User-ID` equals the disputed domain's `registrar_id` may answer; any other registrar agent is refused with `ERR_REGISTRAR_MISMATCH`. That registrar must be in `status` `ACTIVE`, or the answer is refused with `ERR_REGISTRAR_SUSPENDED` — a check that comes before the lock check, so a suspended registrar is turned away for suspension even when it also failed to apply the lock. The verification row must be in `REQUESTED` or in `OVERDUE`; answering a row already in `RESPONDED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. The answer supplies `lock_applied`, `verified_registrant_name` and `verified_registrant_email`, plus optional `registrar_remarks`. If `lock_applied` is not true, the answer is refused with `ERR_DOMAIN_LOCK_NOT_APPLIED` and nothing is written.

A valid answer moves the row to `RESPONDED` with `responded_at` stamped from the clock; if it was answered out of `OVERDUE`, the registrar's `overdue_verification_count` drops by one at that moment. A domain sitting in `UNLOCKED` moves to `LOCKED` with `locked_at` stamped from the clock and the domain's `version` advancing; a domain already locked is left as it stands. The disclosed identity is copied onto the case as its own `verified_registrant_name` and `verified_registrant_email` — this is the address against which the respondent's identity is later checked. Finally the case moves from `VERIFICATION_PENDING` to `NOTIFICATION_PENDING`. `RESPONDED` is the terminal state of a verification row: it never re-opens, and a further request on the case would be a new row.

### 3.5. Notification, Delivery Outcomes and the Response Window

Formal commencement is the dispatch of the complaint to the respondent, and the provider will not dispatch against an unlocked domain. The first dispatch requires the case to be in `NOTIFICATION_PENDING`, the domain to be in `lock_status` `LOCKED` — otherwise the attempt is refused with `ERR_DOMAIN_NOT_LOCKED`, which is exactly the situation the out-of-band lock release creates — and no existing notification attempt on the case to be sitting in `SENT`, since an attempt whose outcome has not yet been recorded blocks a further dispatch with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`.

Each attempt records the `channel` used, one of `EMAIL`, `POSTAL` or `COURIER`, and the `address` it was sent to. The attempt is created in `status` `SENT` with `dispatched_at` stamped from the clock, `attempt_number` 1 on the first dispatch, and `transmission_allowance_days` fixed by the channel: 0 days for `EMAIL`, 2 days for `COURIER`, and 5 days for `POSTAL`. The first dispatch also moves the case from `NOTIFICATION_PENDING` to `COMMENCED` and stamps `commenced_at` from the clock.

The response deadline is then projected in its **provisional** form: both `provisional_response_due_at` and `response_due_at` are set to the attempt's `dispatched_at` plus its `transmission_allowance_days` days plus the 20-day response period, and the case records `deadline_variant_applied` as `PROVISIONAL`. An email dispatch on 1 April therefore falls due on 21 April, a courier dispatch on the same day falls due on 23 April, and a postal dispatch on 26 April. Only the attempt bearing the highest `attempt_number` ever contributes to the projection; earlier attempts are historical record.

A case manager then records what became of the attempt. The attempt must be in `SENT`, and the recorded `outcome` is either `DELIVERED` or `UNDELIVERABLE`. On `DELIVERED`, the receipt instant may be supplied as `deemed_received_at` and defaults to the current logical clock when omitted; it may not be earlier than the attempt's own `dispatched_at`, and an earlier value is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Delivery re-anchors the deadline in its **final** form: `response_due_at` is replaced by `deemed_received_at` plus the 20-day response period — with no transmission allowance, since actual receipt is now known — and `deadline_variant_applied` becomes `FINAL`. The final deadline may fall earlier or later than the provisional one and is never clamped to it: a postal attempt dispatched on 1 April but actually received on 4 April moves the deadline from 26 April back to 24 April, while the same attempt received on 10 April pushes it out to 30 April. The provisional value stays visible in `provisional_response_due_at` for audit. On `UNDELIVERABLE`, the supplied `failure_reason` is stored and the deadline is left standing at the provisional value projected from that attempt, until and unless a further attempt is dispatched.

Re-dispatch is how the provider works through a bad address. It requires the case to be in `COMMENCED` and the highest-numbered attempt to be in `UNDELIVERABLE`; re-dispatching over an attempt still in `SENT` or already `DELIVERED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. The new attempt takes the previous highest `attempt_number` plus one, and since `attempt_number` may not exceed 5, a sixth dispatch is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Because the new attempt becomes the highest-numbered one, the provisional projection is recomputed from its own `dispatched_at` and `transmission_allowance_days`, which is how a switch from email to postal service moves the deadline outward mid-proceeding.

The respondent answers on the record of a `COMMENCED` case. At most one answer is ever accepted: a second attempt is refused with `ERR_RESPONSE_ALREADY_FILED`, and that refusal is deliberately checked ahead of the state gates, so the duplicate is reported as a duplicate even on a case that has already moved on to `RESPONSE_RECEIVED`. A case that is not in `COMMENCED` at all is refused with `ERR_CASE_NOT_IN_RESPONSE_WINDOW`. The answering party must prove they are the disclosed registrant: the supplied `respondent_email` must equal the case's `verified_registrant_email`, and any other address is refused with `ERR_RESPONDENT_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. Finally the answer must arrive within the 24-hour grace period that follows the deadline: when the clock is later than `response_due_at` plus 24 hours the answer is refused with `ERR_RESPONSE_DEADLINE_PASSED` and nothing at all is written.

The answer carries `content_summary`, the substantive reply between 10 and 4000 characters, and two optional attributes: `requests_three_member_panel`, a boolean defaulting to false, and `nominated_panelist_ids`, the respondent's advisory list of preferred neutrals. That list is recorded for audit and is deliberately excluded from the provider's selection machinery — it is neither a candidate set nor a ranking input, so a respondent's preferences never influence who is actually nominated. The answer is created with `submitted_at` stamped from the clock and `timeliness` set once and for all: `ON_TIME` when `submitted_at` is no later than `response_due_at`, and `LATE` otherwise. An answer arriving inside the grace period is therefore accepted and stamped `LATE`, not rejected. The stamp is never revised afterwards — if a later delivery record moves `response_due_at` outward, an answer already stamped `LATE` stays `LATE`. The case moves from `COMMENCED` to `RESPONSE_RECEIVED`.

When the answer elects a three-member panel, the case is re-shaped and **re-priced** on the spot. `respondent_panel_election` moves from `NONE` to `THREE`, `required_seat_count` moves from 1 to 3, and the filing fee assessment is re-run in its **final** variant with the seat count now genuinely 3, setting `fee_variant_applied` to `FINAL`. The 250000-cent three-member supplement now enters `assessed_fee_cents`, `respondent_share_cents` becomes half of that supplement rounded down, or 125000 cents, and `complainant_share_cents` is the remainder. The consequence is counter-intuitive and intended: because the surcharge is charged on the base fee alone and the respondent is billed only half the raw supplement, the **complainant's** share rises when the respondent elects a larger panel — from 150000 to 275000 cents on a standard case, and from 168750 to 293750 cents on an expedited one. `honoraria_pool_cents` is recomputed as 70 percent of the new assessment, rounded half up. The re-pricing writes no adjusting ledger line: all four money fields are overwritten in place while every existing ledger line stands untouched, so a case that was fully paid a moment earlier is instantly in arrears by the difference, and the parties must pay the gap before the panel can be nominated.

A respondent who never answers defaults. When the deadline sweep finds a `COMMENCED` case with no answer on file and the clock past `response_due_at` plus the 24-hour grace, the case moves from `COMMENCED` to `DEFAULT` and `default_recorded` is set to true. The proceeding continues: a panel is still appointed and still decides on the complainant's record alone. The one downstream consequence is on conflict waivers — a defaulted respondent cannot be asked to waive anything, so the provider proceeds on the complainant's waiver alone.

### 3.6. Constituting the Panel: Nomination, Selection and Conflict Screening

A panel is assembled one seat at a time. Which seats exist is fixed by the case's `required_seat_count`: a single-member panel has exactly one seat, `SOLE`, and a three-member panel has exactly three, `PRESIDING`, `CO_PANELIST_1` and `CO_PANELIST_2`. Nominating `SOLE` on a three-member case, or any of the three co-panelist seats on a single-member case, is refused with `ERR_SEAT_NOT_APPLICABLE`. A seat already held by a nomination in `NOMINATED`, `WAIVER_PENDING` or `ACCEPTED` cannot be nominated again and is refused with `ERR_SEAT_ALREADY_FILLED`; seats whose previous holder ended in `DECLINED`, `CONFLICTED`, `REPLACED` or `DISCHARGED` are free.

Nomination is a case manager's act on a case in `RESPONSE_RECEIVED` or `DEFAULT` for the first seat — which moves the case to `PANEL_APPOINTMENT` — or on a case already in `PANEL_APPOINTMENT` for the seats that follow. Because nomination competes with other writes on the same case, it is one of the two version-pinned operations: `expected_case_version` is mandatory and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION`. The parties' money must also be in before neutrals are engaged: `paid_cents` must be greater than or equal to `assessed_fee_cents`, or the nomination is refused with `ERR_PANEL_SUPPLEMENT_UNPAID`. This is the gate that catches a case re-priced by a three-member election whose supplement has not yet been funded, and it is checked after the seat and conflict gates but before the eligibility machinery.

The nomination records `seat` and `nominated_by`, the participant who put the name forward, which is `COMPLAINANT`, `RESPONDENT` or `PROVIDER` and is retained strictly for audit — it never influences who may be selected. The caller may also name a candidate with `panelist_id`. When `panelist_id` is omitted the provider selects one itself, and the resulting row records `selected_by_provider` as true; when the caller names one, that flag is false, but the named candidate is still put through the identical eligibility filter.

Eligibility has six conditions, all of which must hold. The candidate's `status` must be `AVAILABLE`. The candidate's `nationality_code` must differ from the complainant's `country_code`. The candidate's `nationality_code` must also differ from the case's `mutual_jurisdiction`. The candidate must have no engagement-window or blackout collision, as described below. The candidate's `active_case_load` must be strictly less than their own `max_overlapping_engagements`. And the candidate must not already hold a row on this same case in `NOMINATED`, `WAIVER_PENDING` or `ACCEPTED` — a neutral cannot occupy two seats on one panel, though a neutral whose earlier row on the case ended in `DECLINED`, `CONFLICTED` or `REPLACED` may be nominated afresh.

When the provider is selecting, the eligible set is built by applying all six conditions to the whole roster. If that set is empty the nomination is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PANELIST`, reported as an unprocessable request. Otherwise the set is ordered by three keys in strict sequence: ascending `active_case_load` first, so the least-burdened neutral is preferred; ascending `roster_admitted_at` second, so seniority on the roster breaks a load tie; and ascending `created_at` third as the final tiebreak. The record identifier is never a ranking key, so identifier order must not be relied on to break any tie. The first candidate in that order takes the seat.

When the caller names a candidate, the same conditions apply but the refusals are reported separately. The non-temporal conditions — roster status, either nationality exclusion, and the same-case duplication bar — are refused with `ERR_PANELIST_INELIGIBLE`. The temporal conditions are then evaluated against the prospective engagement window, which runs from the current logical clock to 14 days later and is half-open: it includes its start instant and excludes its end. Two kinds of collision are refused with `ERR_PANELIST_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. The first is an overlap with another engagement: every seat the candidate holds in `ACCEPTED` on some other non-terminal case occupies the interval from its `accepted_at` to its `decision_due_at`, also half-open, so a new window that begins exactly when an existing engagement ends does **not** collide — back-to-back engagements are allowed. The second is an overlap with the candidate's declared blackout, the interval from `unavailable_from` to `unavailable_to`, which is closed at both ends, so a window that merely touches either boundary **does** collide. After the window check, the load ceiling is tested and a candidate whose `active_case_load` has reached `max_overlapping_engagements` is refused with `ERR_PANELIST_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` — the window conflict is deliberately reported in preference to the capacity ceiling when both are true. Only seats in `ACCEPTED` on non-terminal cases are visible to any of these three checks: a merely nominated seat, an unscreened seat, and every seat on a withdrawn, closed, implemented or court-terminated case are all invisible.

An accepted nomination creates the seat in `status` `NOMINATED` with `created_at` from the clock, and advances the case's `version`. Appointments can be listed across the docket with filters on `case_id`, on `panelist_id`, on `status` and on `seat`, which is how the successor row minted by a replacement is located.

The nominated neutral then files conflict disclosures on their own seat; another panelist attempting it is refused with `ERR_NOT_SEAT_HOLDER`, and a seat not in `NOMINATED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPOINTMENT_STATE`. Five disclosures are made: `disclosed_prior_representation`, that the neutral previously acted for one of the parties; `disclosed_same_firm`, that a colleague at the neutral's `firm_name` acts for a party; `disclosed_financial_interest`, that the neutral holds a financial interest in a party; `prior_appointments_with_complainant`, the number of earlier appointments involving this same complainant, between 0 and 50; and optionally `months_since_last_contact`, the months elapsed since the most recent disclosed contact, between 0 and 600, which when omitted or null means the relationship is current so no decay applies.

The score is computed from those disclosures alone. A prior representation contributes 45 points, a same-firm relationship 35 points, and prior appointments 8 points each, capped at 24 points no matter how many are disclosed. Those three components form the decayable subtotal. The decay factor is 1.00 when `months_since_last_contact` is null or omitted; otherwise it is one minus the disclosed months divided by the 24-month horizon, with a floor of 0.25 — so 12 months yields 0.50, 24 months or more yields the floor 0.25, and the factor never falls below it. The financial interest is deliberately carved out before decay: its 60 points are added back at full value after the decayable subtotal has been decayed and rounded half up to two decimals, so a financial interest never ages away. The final `conflict_score` is that sum capped at 100.00. A neutral disclosing a prior representation and three prior appointments with the last contact 12 months ago scores 45 plus 24, decayed by 0.50, for 34.50; the same disclosures with a financial interest added score 34.50 plus 60, or 94.50; a financial interest alone always scores exactly 60.00 whatever the elapsed months.

`conflict_factors` records which disclosure keys contributed a non-zero term, drawn from `FINANCIAL_INTEREST`, `PRIOR_APPOINTMENTS`, `PRIOR_REPRESENTATION` and `SAME_FIRM`, sorted in ascending order, and is empty when the score is 0.00. `screened_at` is stamped from the clock.

The score alone routes the seat, on two inclusive lower bounds. A score at or above the disqualification threshold of 60 sends the seat straight to `CONFLICTED`, taking no load and requiring no further step — exactly 60.00 disqualifies. A score at or above the waiver threshold of 30 but below 60 sends the seat to `WAIVER_PENDING`, again taking no load, and the seat waits on the parties' consent — exactly 30.00 requires a waiver. Anything below 30 seats the neutral directly: the engagement-window and capacity checks are re-run at this moment against the same half-open window and the same closed blackout interval, and if they pass, the seat moves to `ACCEPTED` with `accepted_at` stamped from the clock, `decision_due_at` set 14 days later, and the neutral's `active_case_load` incremented by one. The temporal checks are therefore evaluated twice per seat — once when the name is put forward, and again at the moment the seat is actually taken — because the clock and the roster may both have moved in between.

A waivable conflict is cleared by a case manager, not by the neutral. The seat must be in `WAIVER_PENDING`, and its `conflict_score` must be below 60, or the request is refused with `ERR_CONFLICT_NOT_WAIVABLE`. Both parties must consent: `waiver_complainant` must be true, and `waiver_respondent` must be true as well — unless the case carries a recorded default, meaning `default_recorded` is true and no answer was ever filed, in which case the respondent's consent may be omitted. An incomplete waiver is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_INCOMPLETE`, the lowest-precedence refusal in the whole catalogue. Both consents are stored on the seat, the engagement-window and capacity checks are re-run exactly as they are on direct acceptance, and the seat moves from `WAIVER_PENDING` to `ACCEPTED` with `accepted_at`, `decision_due_at` and the load increment.

The panel becomes constituted at the moment the count of seats in `ACCEPTED` on the case reaches `required_seat_count` and those seats cover the required set exactly once — `SOLE` alone on a single-member case, or `PRESIDING`, `CO_PANELIST_1` and `CO_PANELIST_2` on a three-member case. The case then moves to `PANEL_CONSTITUTED` and the honoraria are allocated across the seats. Seats in `NOMINATED`, `WAIVER_PENDING`, `CONFLICTED`, `DECLINED`, `REPLACED` or `DISCHARGED` are never counted toward completeness.

Allocation divides `honoraria_pool_cents` by seat, and it is a waterfall, not a proportional split. A `SOLE` seat takes 100 percent of the pool exactly. On a three-member panel the `PRESIDING` seat is entitled to 40 percent and each co-panelist to 30 percent; each seat's raw entitlement is floored to a whole cent independently, and then the entire rounding remainder is handed to the `PRESIDING` seat so that the three `honoraria_cents` values sum to the pool exactly. From a pool of 297500 cents the co-panelists take 89250 each and the presiding neutral takes 119000, which is 500 cents more than a flat 40 percent share. Only seats in `ACCEPTED` participate; the allocation is re-run whenever the panel becomes constituted again after a seat was vacated.

Vacating a seat is a case manager's act and always mints a successor in the same call. The predecessor's fate depends on where it stood: a seat in `NOMINATED` or `WAIVER_PENDING` becomes `DECLINED`, while a seat in `CONFLICTED` or `ACCEPTED` becomes `REPLACED`. In every case a successor row for the same `seat` is created in `NOMINATED`, chosen by the provider's selector when `replacement_panelist_id` is omitted and by name when it is supplied, and the predecessor's `replaced_by_appointment_id` is set to point at that successor. An optional `reason` may be recorded. The response reports the **updated predecessor**, not the successor, so the successor's identifier must be read out of `replaced_by_appointment_id` rather than from the reported row's own identifier.

Vacating a seat that was in `ACCEPTED` unwinds everything that acceptance had built: the seat's `honoraria_cents` returns to 0, the neutral's `active_case_load` drops by one, the case falls back from `PANEL_CONSTITUTED` to `PANEL_APPOINTMENT`, and every decision on the case still in `DRAFT` is moved to `VOIDED` with `voided_at` stamped from the clock. The ballots already cast on a voided draft remain readable but are dead — they never count toward any successor decision. Vacating a seat in `CONFLICTED` takes no load adjustment, because a conflicted seat never held any load to begin with, and it leaves the case status alone. The other seats' `honoraria_cents` are left as they stand until the panel is constituted again, at which point allocation re-runs across the new set of accepted seats.

### 3.7. Deliberation, Ballots, Tally and Issuance

A decision is opened as a draft by a case manager while the case is in `PANEL_APPOINTMENT` or in `PANEL_CONSTITUTED`. The admission is deliberately wider than completeness requires: a draft may exist before every seat is filled, and it is issuance — not drafting — that demands a fully constituted panel. Drafting is the second version-pinned operation, so `expected_case_version` is mandatory and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION`. The draft carries `drafted_by_panelist_id`, which must name a panelist holding a seat in `ACCEPTED` on this case — the `SOLE` seat on a single-member panel or the `PRESIDING` seat on a three-member one — and `draft_summary`, the reasoned narrative of the panel's findings, between 10 and 8000 characters.

A new draft is created in `status` `DRAFT` with `publication_status` `UNPUBLISHED`, `outcome` and `outcome_basis` null, `rdnh_finding` and `dissent_present` false, `transfer_votes`, `cancellation_votes` and `deny_votes` at 0, and `issued_at`, `implementation_deadline_at`, `implemented_at` and `voided_at` all null. A case may accumulate several drafts over its life — voided ones remain listed on the case alongside the live one.

Seated neutrals then ballot on the draft. The caller supplies the `seat` they are casting for, which must match the seat held by their own appointment in `ACCEPTED` on the decision's case; a caller with no such appointment, or one claiming a seat they do not hold, is refused with `ERR_NOT_SEAT_HOLDER`. The ballot carries `vote`, which is `TRANSFER`, `CANCELLATION` or `DENY`; optionally `rdnh_vote`, the neutral's ballot on a reverse domain name hijacking finding, defaulting to false; and optionally `rationale`, per-seat reasoning retained verbatim for publication. The server derives `voter_panelist_id` itself as the `panelist_id` of the accepted appointment holding that seat, records the draft being balloted on as the ballot's `decision_id`, and stamps `cast_at` from the clock. Ballots are immutable once written and no seat may ballot twice on the same decision — a second attempt is refused with `ERR_VOTE_ALREADY_CAST`. Ballots on a decision that was later voided remain readable through the decision's ballot list.

Issuance is a case manager's act on a decision still in `DRAFT`; a decision already `ISSUED`, `STAYED`, `IMPLEMENTED` or `VOIDED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DECISION_STATE`. Two completeness gates then apply. The panel must be genuinely constituted: the case must be in `PANEL_CONSTITUTED`, and its seats in `ACCEPTED` must cover each seat required by `required_seat_count` exactly once, with seats in `REPLACED`, `DECLINED`, `CONFLICTED` or `DISCHARGED` disregarded; a failure here is refused with `ERR_PANEL_NOT_CONSTITUTED`. And every seat must have balloted: the count of ballots on the decision must equal `required_seat_count`, or issuance is refused with `ERR_VOTES_INCOMPLETE`. Because drafting admits a wider set of case statuses than issuance, a draft opened on a `PANEL_APPOINTMENT` case is refused at issuance by the panel-completeness gate rather than by a state gate.

The tally then converts ballots into an operative outcome. Each ballot's `vote` is relabelled onto the outcome vocabulary: `TRANSFER` stays `TRANSFER`, `CANCELLATION` stays `CANCELLATION`, and `DENY` becomes `COMPLAINT_DENIED`. The three raw counts are written to `transfer_votes`, `cancellation_votes` and `deny_votes`. The leading count is the largest of the three. When twice the leading count exceeds `required_seat_count`, that leading label becomes the `outcome` and `outcome_basis` is `STRICT_MAJORITY` — which covers every single-member decision, and every three-member decision where two or three seats agree. Otherwise no label commands a majority, and the outcome is taken from the ballot cast by the `PRESIDING` seat, with `outcome_basis` recorded as `PRESIDING_CASTING`. On a three-member panel that splits one vote each way, the presiding neutral's ballot therefore decides the case single-handedly.

Two further findings are computed at the same moment. `dissent_present` is true when the ballots were not unanimous — equivalently, when the leading count is below `required_seat_count`. `rdnh_finding` is true only when the `outcome` is `COMPLAINT_DENIED` and twice the number of ballots carrying `rdnh_vote` true exceeds `required_seat_count`; the hijacking ballots are counted across the whole panel, not merely among the seats that voted to deny, so a panel of three that denies the complaint two-to-one records a hijacking finding only if at least two seats — including possibly the dissenter — flagged it.

Issuance moves the decision from `DRAFT` to `ISSUED` with `issued_at` stamped from the clock, computes `implementation_deadline_at`, and moves the case from `PANEL_CONSTITUTED` to `IMPLEMENTATION_WAITING`. When `rdnh_finding` came out true, the complainant's `adverse_rdnh_findings` counter advances by one — the only place in the system that counter rises.

### 3.8. The Waiting Period, Court Challenges and Registrar Execution

The implementation waiting period is ten **business** days measured on the provider's own calendar. The anchor is the decision's `issued_at`, or, where a stay has just been lifted, the challenge's `resolved_at`. The anchor day itself is never counted. The provider then steps forward one calendar day at a time, counting only days that are neither Saturday nor Sunday nor a listed provider holiday, until ten such days have been counted; the tenth counted day, carrying the anchor's own time of day, is `implementation_deadline_at`. The provider's holiday calendar for this specification contains a single date, 2026-09-07. A decision issued on Friday 2026-08-28 therefore falls due on Friday 2026-09-11: the two intervening weekends and the holiday of Monday 2026-09-07 are all skipped, so ten business days consume fourteen calendar days.

The deadline is the boundary between two mutually exclusive rights, and it belongs to the challenge side. While the clock is at or before `implementation_deadline_at` a court challenge may be filed and the registrar may not act — an attempt to execute is refused with `ERR_IMPLEMENTATION_PERIOD_ACTIVE`. Once the clock is strictly past it, the registrar may act and the challenge window has shut — an attempt to file is refused with `ERR_CHALLENGE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. At the exact deadline instant, therefore, the challenge is admitted and the execution is not.

Either party may lodge a challenge against a decision in `ISSUED`; a decision in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DECISION_STATE`. The filing carries `jurisdiction`, the two-letter country of the seized court; `docket_number`, the court's own docket reference; and `filed_by`, which is `COMPLAINANT` or `RESPONDENT`. A caller acting as `Complainant` must own the case, and a caller acting as `Respondent` must additionally supply `respondent_email` matching the case's `verified_registrant_email`, or the filing is refused with `ERR_RESPONDENT_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. The challenge is created in `status` `FILED` with `filed_at` stamped from the clock and `resolved_at` and `resolution_note` null. The decision moves from `ISSUED` to `STAYED` and the case from `IMPLEMENTATION_WAITING` to `STAYED`. Challenges on a decision are readable through the decision's own challenge list.

A provider administrator records how the court disposed of a challenge still in `FILED`; a challenge already resolved is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_STATE`. The recorded `outcome` is `DISMISSED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `SUSTAINED`, with an optional `resolution_note`.

A `DISMISSED` or `WITHDRAWN` disposition lifts the stay. The challenge takes that status with `resolved_at` stamped from the clock, the decision returns from `STAYED` to `ISSUED`, and the waiting period is recomputed **in full** from `resolved_at` — a fresh ten business days, with no credit for the days that elapsed before the stay. The case returns to `IMPLEMENTATION_WAITING`. A challenge filed on day two and dismissed on day nine therefore pushes the registrar's earliest execution instant well beyond the original deadline.

A `SUSTAINED` disposition destroys the proceeding, in a cascade of five linked effects plus their counter adjustments. The challenge becomes `SUSTAINED` with `resolved_at`; the decision becomes `VOIDED` with `voided_at`; the case becomes `TERMINATED_COURT` with `closed_at` stamped from the clock and `refiling_bar_until` set 30 days later; every seat on the case in `ACCEPTED` becomes `DISCHARGED`; and each affected neutral's `active_case_load` drops by one, while their `honoraria_cents` are preserved — a discharged neutral keeps what the panel earned. In addition, the domain returns to `UNLOCKED`, the complainant's `open_case_count` drops by one, and where the voided decision had carried `rdnh_finding` true, that finding is **un-counted** from the complainant's `adverse_rdnh_findings`, which is the only place that counter ever falls. If the voided decision had already been published, its precedent entry's `superseded` becomes true while its `publication_status` is left exactly as it was — publication is never rolled back, only marked as superseded.

Execution belongs to the registrar of record. Only a `RegistrarAgent` whose `X-User-ID` equals the disputed domain's `registrar_id` may execute; any other is refused with `ERR_REGISTRAR_MISMATCH`. The decision must be in `ISSUED` and the case in `IMPLEMENTATION_WAITING`, the clock must be strictly past `implementation_deadline_at`, and the domain must still be in `lock_status` `LOCKED`, or execution is refused with `ERR_DOMAIN_NOT_LOCKED` — the same refusal the out-of-band lock release produces at notification. An optional `registrar_action_reference` records the registrar's own ticket for the action.

Execution moves the decision from `ISSUED` to `IMPLEMENTED` with `implemented_at` stamped from the clock, and translates the outcome onto the domain: an outcome of `TRANSFER` drives `lock_status` to `TRANSFER_EXECUTED`, an outcome of `CANCELLATION` drives it to `CANCELLATION_EXECUTED`, and an outcome of `COMPLAINT_DENIED` simply releases the lock back to `UNLOCKED`. On either executed outcome `executed_at` is stamped from the clock, and both are terminal for the domain: no lock operation is admitted on a domain in either of them. The domain's `version` advances. The case then moves to `IMPLEMENTED` with `closed_at` stamped from the clock and `refiling_bar_until` set 30 days later, every seat in `ACCEPTED` becomes `DISCHARGED` with each neutral's `active_case_load` dropping by one and each seat's `honoraria_cents` preserved, and the complainant's `open_case_count` drops by one.

### 3.9. Publication and the Precedent Database

Publication is a provider administrator's act and runs on a dimension entirely independent of enforcement. A decision may be published while it is still merely `ISSUED`, and a decision that is later `VOIDED` stays published. The only status requirement is that the decision must not still be in `DRAFT`, which is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DECISION_STATE`; and a decision whose `publication_status` has already left `UNPUBLISHED` cannot be published twice, which is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_PUBLISHED`.

Publication carries `redaction_level`, which is `NONE`, `PARTY_NAMES` or `FULL`, and `precedent_tags`, a non-empty array of between 1 and 10 lowercase topic tags used by the precedent search. An optional `digest_text` may be supplied, but the digest actually stored is assembled by the server at publication from the case's `case_number`, then the decision's `outcome`, then the literal text `rdnh=` followed by the decision's `rdnh_finding`, with those three parts joined by a space-pipe-space separator. A redaction level of `NONE` drives `publication_status` to `PUBLISHED`; either `PARTY_NAMES` or `FULL` drives it to `REDACTED`. Exactly one precedent entry exists per decision, created with `published_at` stamped from the clock, the supplied `redaction_level` and `precedent_tags`, `superseded` false, and `citable` true unless the redaction level was `FULL`.

Citability is what governs whether the entry may be named in a later complaint's `cited_decision_ids`: an entry is citable when its `redaction_level` is anything other than `FULL` **and** its `superseded` is false. A fully redacted decision is therefore never citable no matter how it was decided, and a decision voided by a sustained court challenge stops being citable the moment the challenge is recorded, even though its entry remains published.

The citation graph is measured on read, never cached. An entry's `citation_count` is the number of asserted ground rows anywhere in the system whose `cited_decision_ids` contains this decision — the count is over grounds, not over cases, so a single case that cites the same decision from two of its three mandatory elements contributes two, and citations from cases that were later withdrawn or administratively closed still count. `precedent_weight` then decays that count by age: the whole months elapsed from the decision's `issued_at` to the current logical clock are truncated toward zero, the age weight is one minus those months divided by the 60-month horizon with a floor of 0.20, and `precedent_weight` is the citation count multiplied by that age weight, rounded half up to two decimals. A decision cited four times and issued 30 months ago therefore weighs 2.00, the same decision at 60 months or more weighs 0.80 and never less, and an uncited decision weighs 0.00 at any age. Both figures are re-evaluated on every read of the entry and on every precedent search, against the clock as it reads at that moment — advancing the clock alone changes the reported weight with no write anywhere.

The precedent database is searched with three optional filters plus pagination: `outcome`, restricting to decisions with that operative outcome; `citable`, restricting to entries currently citable; and `include_superseded`, which defaults to false. Entries whose decision was voided are excluded from the search unless `include_superseded` is true, and entries published under a `FULL` redaction are never citable and never listed at all, regardless of any filter. A decision's own entry is also readable directly from the decision, as a list of zero or one row, with the same on-read re-evaluation.
