# Neighborhood Fiber Build & Preorder Campaign API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Building fiber to the home is only economical where enough neighbours want it at the same time. Our program therefore does not build street by street on a planner's hunch; it carves a city into **fiberhoods** — named build zones with a known household base — publishes a preregistration threshold for each one, and breaks ground only where residents put money down before a deadline. That single business decision produces an unusually wide surface of operational obligations: a campaign has to hold refundable deposits it may have to return in bulk, negotiate building access with multi-dwelling-unit owners who are not its customers, chase municipal permits and pole-owning utilities that answer to nobody, sequence construction across zones that all qualified at once, and then repair the lawns and irrigation lines its own crews cut open.

This document specifies the service that runs that program end to end. It covers campaign and zone setup, address serviceability determination, MDU owner-agreement negotiation, preregistration with deposit collection, deadline extension and threshold relief, the qualification run that decides which fiberhoods get built, permit and pole-attachment readiness, construction sequencing and suspension, preorder-to-order conversion with install settlement, crew scheduling with drop-bury and restoration follow-up, construction damage claims, and the unserved-address exception path that ends in an alternative-technology offer. Every endpoint is mounted at the service root, so the paths in §4 are the literal request paths.

Two properties of this system deserve early emphasis, because they shape almost every rule that follows. First, **all derived state is recomputed inside the request that changes its source rows** — preregistration rates, ledger-backed account balances, crew occupancy, zone readiness posture. There is no background worker and no eventual consistency window; a caller that mutates a preorder sees the zone's new rate on the very next read. Second, **time is a server-controlled input, not wall-clock reality.** Deadlines, permit validity, agreement expiry, restoration SLAs and claim filing windows are all evaluated against a logical clock that an operator sets explicitly, which makes every temporal rule in this document deterministically reachable.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this service — without exception, including every read — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent, or present but an empty string, or if `X-User-Role` carries a name outside the eight roles enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else about it is examined. When the headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorized for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles are `ProgramManager`, `PermitOffice`, `UtilityOperator`, `InstallCoordinator`, `ClaimsAssessor`, `ClaimsApprover`, `Subscriber` and `MduOwner`.

- A `ProgramManager` is the fiber program operator. It creates campaigns, build zones, service addresses and accounts, opens MDU access negotiations and sends fee offers, files permit applications and submits them, raises pole-attachment requests and accepts make-ready quotes, publishes alternative-technology options and extends fallback offers, records serviceability determinations and unserved-address exceptions, adjusts thresholds and grants deadline extensions, and drives the qualification run, the sequencing run, ground-break, suspension, resumption and service-ready declaration. It also drives the logical clock, and it may cancel or convert any subscriber's preorder.
- A `PermitOffice` principal is the municipal permitting authority and is the only actor that can approve or reject a submitted permit application.
- A `UtilityOperator` principal is the pole-owning utility. It files field surveys with make-ready quotes, denies attachment outright, and confirms physical attachment.
- An `InstallCoordinator` registers crews, books appointment windows and records how each visit ended.
- A `ClaimsAssessor` prices filed damage claims against the schedule caps and the filing-timeliness factor, and may not decide the claims it priced.
- A `ClaimsApprover` pays or denies an assessed claim.
- A `Subscriber` is a resident or business preregistering an address: it signs up, cancels, converts to an install order, answers alternative-technology offers, and files and appeals damage claims.
- An `MduOwner` is the owner of a multi-dwelling-unit property and is the only actor that can sign, counter or decline the access agreement for its building.

Six of these roles carry **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramManager`, `PermitOffice`, `UtilityOperator`, `InstallCoordinator`, `ClaimsAssessor` or `ClaimsApprover`, the accompanying `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any row's `id`. It may still be *recorded*: a permit decision stamps the acting operator's `X-User-ID` onto `reviewer_user_id`, a claim assessment stamps it onto `assessed_by_user_id`, and a claim decision stamps it onto `decided_by_user_id`. Those stored values are compared to each other — never to an entity identifier.

The remaining two roles are **account-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `Subscriber`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Account`, and ownership checks compare it to the owning account foreign key on the resource being touched: a preorder's `account_id`, a damage claim's `claimant_account_id`, an alternative-technology offer's `account_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `MduOwner`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Account`, and it is compared to the agreement's `owner_account_id`. A mismatch in any of these comparisons is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). When a `Subscriber` creates a preorder, the `account_id` supplied in the payload must equal the acting `X-User-ID`; when a `Subscriber` files a damage claim, the supplied `claimant_account_id` must likewise equal it.

Segregation of duties is enforced on the damage-claim chain and nowhere else: the `X-User-ID` recording a claim decision must differ from the `X-User-ID` already recorded in that claim's `assessed_by_user_id`. One human operator is free to hold both the `ClaimsAssessor` and the `ClaimsApprover` role, but may not use both on the same claim; the violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls how many rows are skipped before the page begins: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific endpoint's narrative in §3 pins a different order for its response body — the sequencing run, for instance, returns the zones it ranked in assigned build order. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` indicating whether any matching row remains beyond this page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource in this system is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID string. Identifiers carry no type prefix, no embedded sequence and no domain meaning; clients must treat them as opaque and must never construct one. All thirteen versioned entity types additionally carry `created_at` (the instant the row was inserted), `updated_at` (the instant of the row's most recent mutation) and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on insert and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Timestamps — both these system columns and every domain instant such as `prereg_deadline_at`, `signed_at` or `window_start_at` — are ISO 8601 UTC values with three fractional digits.

`LedgerEntry` is the single deliberate exception. Ledger rows are immutable and append-only, so they carry `id` and `created_at` and nothing else from that set: a ledger entry has **no** `updated_at` field and **no** `version` field, and no endpoint exists to update or delete one. A correction is expressed as a new entry with the opposite sign, never as a mutation of the original.

The instant that all temporal rules are evaluated against is a **logical clock** held by the server, not the host's wall clock. At startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `ProgramManager` may move it to any instant by supplying `current_utc`. Moving the clock is a pure clock operation: it writes no row and evaluates no expiry eagerly. Instead, every expiry in this system is **lazy** — a `SENT` or `COUNTERED` MDU agreement whose `expires_at` the clock has reached, an `APPROVED` permit application whose `expires_at` the clock has reached, and an `OFFERED` fallback offer whose `expires_at` the clock has reached are each materialized to `EXPIRED` by the next read or write that touches the row, together with whatever cascade that expiry implies. This is why the observable effect of advancing the clock only appears on the following request.

### 2.5. Parallel State Dimensions

Two entities in this domain do not have a single status column; they carry two independent state dimensions on the same row, and a transition in one dimension never implies a transition in the other.

A `BuildZone` carries `campaign_status`, one of `PREREG_OPEN`, `PREREG_EXTENDED`, `QUALIFIED` or `NOT_QUALIFIED`, tracking the preregistration campaign; and `construction_status`, one of `NOT_STARTED`, `SEQUENCED`, `IN_CONSTRUCTION`, `SUSPENDED` or `SERVICE_READY`, tracking physical build progress. Granting a deadline extension moves `campaign_status` and leaves `construction_status` exactly as it was; suspending construction moves `construction_status` and leaves `campaign_status` exactly as it was. `QUALIFIED` and `NOT_QUALIFIED` are terminal for the campaign dimension, and `SERVICE_READY` is terminal for the construction dimension.

A `Preorder` likewise carries `preorder_status`, one of `PENDING`, `QUALIFIED`, `CONVERTED`, `CANCELLED` or `LAPSED`, and `deposit_status`, one of `HELD`, `REFUNDED`, `APPLIED` or `FORFEITED`. The money dimension is driven only as a consequence of lifecycle movement — it is never addressed directly by any endpoint — but it is a distinct dimension nonetheless, with `REFUNDED`, `APPLIED` and `FORFEITED` all terminal, and `CONVERTED`, `CANCELLED` and `LAPSED` terminal on the lifecycle side.

A `ServiceAddress` similarly separates its engineering determination (`serviceability`) from its administrative escalation path (`exception_status`), and the two evolve independently: none of the three exception actions changes `serviceability`, and a serviceability determination does not by itself open an exception except in the one cascade described in §3.4.

### 2.6. Recomputation, Cascades and Version Semantics

Derived values in this system are never stored stale. Whenever a write changes a row that feeds a derived field, that field is recomputed inside the same request, and so is every field derived from *it*. Four recomputation families exist and are referenced throughout §3:

A `BuildZone`'s `provisional_qualifying_count`, `prereg_rate_pct`, `make_ready_cost_cents`, `readiness_posture` and `outstanding_restoration_count` are all zone-level aggregates over child rows. An `Account`'s `deposit_held_cents` and `credit_balance_cents` are full replays of that account's ledger stream. An `InstallCrew`'s `booked_minutes_total` and `scheduled_appointment_count` are aggregates over that crew's `SCHEDULED` appointments. A `ServiceAddress`'s and a `Preorder`'s `counts_toward_threshold` are per-row predicates recomputed whenever anything they read changes.

Cascades run to their natural depth. A pole-attachment denial, for example, suspends the zone, the suspension cancels the zone's scheduled appointments, and the cancellations release each affected crew's booked minutes — three consequential hops beyond the row the caller actually addressed. Every hop that mutates a row bumps that row's `version` by 1 and refreshes its `updated_at`; a hop that only recomputes a derived field on a row that was already being mutated does not bump the counter a second time. Where §3 states that an operation "increments the zone's `version`", it means exactly one increment for that operation, and callers holding an older number are affected as described in §2.9.

`readiness_posture` deserves its own sentence because it is purely derived and never written directly. It reads `BLOCKED` when `construction_status` is `NOT_STARTED` and none of the zone's required permit types has an `APPROVED` application; `PERMIT_PENDING` when at least one required permit type still lacks an `APPROVED` application; `POLE_PENDING` when every required permit type has an `APPROVED` application but no pole-attachment request of the zone has reached `ATTACHED`; `SUSPENDED` whenever `construction_status` is `SUSPENDED`; and `READY` in every remaining case.

### 2.7. Money, Rates and Rounding

All monetary amounts in this system are integer numbers of cents. There are no fractional-cent values anywhere in a request or a response, and no currency field: `deposit_amount_cents`, `access_fee_cents`, `make_ready_cost_cents`, `claimed_amount_cents`, `monthly_price_cents` and every other amount-bearing field is a plain integer.

Two families of value do involve decimals. Preregistration percentages — `default_threshold_pct`, `threshold_pct`, `prereg_rate_pct` and `final_rate_pct` — carry exactly two decimal places, and every percentage the server computes is rounded **half up** to two decimal places. Comparison against a threshold is performed on the *rounded* two-decimal value, so a true rate of 9.995 percent rounds to 10.00 and therefore satisfies a threshold of 10.00. The readiness score is likewise rounded half up to two decimal places, and that rounding is applied exactly once to the fully composed score rather than to each contributing factor, so factor-level drift cannot accumulate. The `late_filing_factor` carries exactly two decimal places as well.

Where a money amount is produced by multiplying an integer by a rate — the conversion discount and the damage-claim assessment — the multiplication result is rounded half up to a whole number of cents, once, at the step the relevant narrative in §3 nominates. Allocation of a pooled cost across recipients uses integer arithmetic only and therefore involves no rounding mode at all; see §3.6.

### 2.8. The Append-Only Deposit and Claim Ledger

Every movement of money in this system is recorded as a `LedgerEntry`, and account balances are never incremented in place — they are recomputed by replaying the account's entries. An entry names the `account_id` whose balances it moves, an `entry_type`, a signed `amount_cents`, a human-readable `memo` written by the side effect that appended it, and `occurred_at`, the logical-clock instant of the append. Exactly one of two optional references is set: `preorder_id` for every deposit movement, and `damage_claim_id` for a claim payout. Deposit entries leave `damage_claim_id` null; payout entries leave `preorder_id` null.

`entry_type` is one of `DEPOSIT_HELD`, `DEPOSIT_REFUNDED`, `DEPOSIT_APPLIED`, `DEPOSIT_FORFEITED` or `CLAIM_PAYOUT`. The sign convention is fixed: `DEPOSIT_HELD` and `CLAIM_PAYOUT` entries carry a positive `amount_cents`, while `DEPOSIT_REFUNDED`, `DEPOSIT_APPLIED` and `DEPOSIT_FORFEITED` entries carry a negative one. An account's `deposit_held_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over that account's entries whose `entry_type` is one of the four deposit types, and its `credit_balance_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over that account's `CLAIM_PAYOUT` entries. Entries are never filtered by date and never pruned, so each recomputation is a full replay of the account's stream and is independent of the order in which the entries are visited. Because of the sign convention, `deposit_held_cents` is always the total of the `deposit_amount_cents` of that account's preorders whose `deposit_status` is still `HELD`, and it returns to exactly its pre-preorder value once a deposit is refunded, applied or forfeited.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency on Zones and Crews

Two creation endpoints in this system are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a *parent* row's `version`, because in both cases the product rules genuinely permit two similar creations to succeed and no state precondition can distinguish a legitimate second create from one issued against terms the client can no longer see.

A `POST /preorders` request must carry `expected_build_zone_version` — a non-optional field — holding the `version` of the target `BuildZone` as the client last observed it. If that number does not equal the zone's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BUILD_ZONE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The rule exists because every preorder creation, every preorder cancellation, every serviceability determination and every threshold adjustment or deadline extension in that zone bumps the zone's `version`; a client holding an older number quoted the subscriber a `threshold_pct`, a `prereg_deadline_at` or a deposit amount the zone no longer carries, and must re-read the zone before signing anybody up.

A `POST /install-appointments` request must likewise carry `expected_crew_version` — also non-optional — holding the `version` of the target `InstallCrew` as the client last observed it. A value that does not equal the crew's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CREW_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Every appointment creation, every recorded outcome and every cascade cancellation on that crew bumps the crew's `version`, so a stale number means the client planned against `booked_minutes_total` and buffer geometry that have since moved.

No other endpoint takes a version field. In particular, threshold adjustment, deadline extension, ground-break, suspension, conversion, cancellation, permit and pole actions, owner responses, claim actions and offer responses all accept no expected-version input, and supplying one is simply ignored rather than validated.

### 2.10. The Order in Which a Request Is Rejected

Many operations in this system are guarded by several rules at once, and which rejection a caller sees is part of the contract, not an implementation detail. Checks are evaluated in the single strict ascending order below; the **first** rule that fails determines the response, and no later rule is evaluated. The ordering is deliberate — structural problems surface before business problems, state problems before capacity problems, and fiscal complaints last of all, so that a payment error can never mask a structural one.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` names one of the eight roles in §2.1. A missing header, an empty-string header and an unknown role name all fail here with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorization.** The acting `X-User-Role` appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list in §4. Otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path existence.** Every identifier embedded in the URL path resolves to an existing row. Path segments are resolved strictly left to right, and all of them are resolved before any part of the request body is inspected. The rejection is the not-found code of the entity that failed to resolve — `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUILD_ZONE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_ADDRESS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MDU_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERMIT_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PREORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSTALL_CREW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSTALL_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DAMAGE_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FALLBACK_OPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FALLBACK_OFFER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint that has a path parameter.
4. **Body and filter existence.** Every foreign-key identifier supplied in a request body or as a query filter resolves to an existing row. Body references are resolved only after all path references have resolved, and among themselves in the order the endpoint's input list declares them. The rejection is drawn from the same not-found catalogue, selected by the entity that was referenced. This applies to `POST /build-zones`, `POST /service-addresses`, `POST /mdu-agreements`, `POST /permit-applications`, `POST /pole-attachment-requests`, `POST /preorders`, `POST /install-crews`, `POST /install-appointments`, `POST /damage-claims`, `POST /fallback-options`, and to every list endpoint that accepts a foreign-key filter.
5. **Resource ownership.** For an account-backed role acting on a resource it must own, `X-User-ID` equals that resource's owning account foreign key, as detailed in §2.1; a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Because ownership is checked only after existence, a reference to a row that does not exist yields 404 rather than 403. This applies to `POST /preorders`, `POST /preorders/{id}/cancel`, `POST /preorders/{id}/conversion`, `POST /damage-claims`, `POST /damage-claims/{id}/appeal`, `POST /fallback-offers/{id}/response` and `POST /mdu-agreements/{id}/owner-response`.
6. **Segregation of duties.** The `X-User-ID` deciding a damage claim differs from the claim's `assessed_by_user_id`; otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. This applies to `POST /damage-claims/{id}/decision` alone.
7. **Request shape.** Every required field is present and non-null, every enumerated field carries one of its declared values, every timestamp parses as an ISO 8601 UTC instant, every integer sits inside its declared range, and pagination stays inside its bounds. The declared ranges enforced here are: `household_count` at least 1; `threshold_pct` (and `default_threshold_pct`) at least 0.01 and at most 100.00; `claimed_amount_cents` at least 1; `distance_to_node_m` at least 0; `travel_buffer_minutes` at least 0 and at most 240; `daily_capacity_minutes` at least 60 and at most 1440; `max_concurrent_bury_ops` at least 1 and at most 10; `limit` at least 1 and at most 100; `offset` at least 0. Any violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Applies to every endpoint.
8. **Cross-reference coherence.** Two references supplied in the same payload must agree with each other: on a preorder sign-up, the referenced address's `build_zone_id` equals the supplied `build_zone_id`; on an MDU agreement, the referenced address's `dwelling_type` is `MDU`; on a damage claim, the referenced appointment's preorder's `service_address_id` equals the supplied `service_address_id`; and on a restoration follow-up appointment, the referenced `parent_appointment_id` points at an appointment for the same `preorder_id`. Any of these is `ERR_ADDRESS_LINKAGE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). This applies to `POST /preorders`, `POST /mdu-agreements`, `POST /damage-claims` and `POST /install-appointments`.
9. **Crew certification.** The crew's `crew_type` matches the work booked, as spelled out in §3.8; a mismatch is `ERR_CREW_TYPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). This applies to `POST /install-appointments`.
10. **State admissibility.** The target row is in a state from which the requested transition exists. This is checked before any capacity, temporal or fiscal rule, so a terminal row always reports its state rather than a downstream business failure. The rejection is the state code of the entity being driven — `ERR_BUILD_ZONE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_SERVICE_ADDRESS_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_MDU_AGREEMENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PERMIT_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_POLE_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PREORDER_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_INSTALL_APPOINTMENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_DAMAGE_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_FALLBACK_OFFER_STATE_INVALID`, each HTTP 409. Applies to every action endpoint that drives a state machine.
11. **Optimistic concurrency.** The supplied `expected_build_zone_version` equals the zone's current `version`, and the supplied `expected_crew_version` equals the crew's current `version`, as described in §2.9. This is evaluated *after* the parent's own state precondition, so a stale client acting on a zone whose preregistration window has already closed learns about the window first. Applies to `POST /preorders` and `POST /install-appointments`.
12. **Permit readiness.** Every permit type in the zone's `required_permit_types` has a permit application in status `APPROVED` whose `expires_at` is strictly after the logical clock. Lazy expiry runs first, so an application that was `APPROVED` but is now past its `expires_at` is materialized to `EXPIRED` and then fails this check. The rejection is `ERR_PERMIT_READINESS_UNMET`, and it applies to `POST /build-zones/{id}/construction-start`.
13. **Pole readiness.** The zone has at least one pole-attachment request in status `ATTACHED`; a merely `ACCEPTED` or `SURVEYED` request is not enough to break ground. The rejection is `ERR_POLE_READINESS_UNMET`, and it applies to `POST /build-zones/{id}/construction-start`.
14. **Zone construction active.** The zone's `construction_status` is `IN_CONSTRUCTION` or `SERVICE_READY`. Booking an install at an address in a zone that has not broken ground, or whose construction is `SUSPENDED`, is `ERR_ZONE_CONSTRUCTION_NOT_ACTIVE`. This applies to `POST /install-appointments`.
15. **Restoration cleared.** The zone's `outstanding_restoration_count` is zero. The rejection is `ERR_RESTORATION_OUTSTANDING`, and it applies to `POST /build-zones/{id}/construction-complete`.
16. **Preregistration window open.** The zone's `campaign_status` is `PREREG_OPEN` or `PREREG_EXTENDED` and the logical clock is at or before `prereg_deadline_at` — the deadline instant itself is still inside the window. Otherwise `ERR_PREREG_WINDOW_CLOSED`. This applies to `POST /preorders`, `PATCH /build-zones/{id}/threshold` and `POST /build-zones/{id}/extend-deadline`.
17. **Deadline reached.** A qualification run requires that at least one still-open zone of the campaign has a `prereg_deadline_at` strictly earlier than the logical clock; zones still inside their window are silently skipped rather than rejected, but when every open zone is still inside its window the run itself is refused with `ERR_PREREG_DEADLINE_NOT_REACHED`. This applies to `POST /campaigns/{id}/qualification-run`.
18. **Window geometry.** `window_end_at` is strictly after `window_start_at`, both instants land on exact minute boundaries with zero seconds and zero fractional seconds, the duration is at least 30 minutes and at most 480 minutes inclusive at both ends, and `window_start_at` is at or after the logical clock. Any violation is `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (HTTP 422), and it applies to `POST /install-appointments`.
19. **Restoration window.** A restoration follow-up appointment's `window_start_at` is at or before the parent appointment's `restoration_due_by`, and the parent genuinely still owes restoration — its `restoration_required` is true and its `restoration_satisfied_at` is still null. Otherwise `ERR_RESTORATION_WINDOW_MISSED` (HTTP 422), on `POST /install-appointments`.
20. **Claim filing window.** The claim is filed no more than the campaign's `claim_filing_window_days` whole days after the appointment's `completed_at`, and the appointment is `COMPLETED` with `restoration_required` true so that a `restoration_due_by` exists at all. Filing later than that, or against an appointment that never disturbed the property, is `ERR_CLAIM_FILING_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. This applies to `POST /damage-claims`.
21. **Crew window conflict.** The new appointment's buffered interval does not overlap the buffered interval of any appointment already `SCHEDULED` on the same crew, with adjacency at exactly the buffer edge permitted; see §3.8 for the exact geometry. The rejection is `ERR_CREW_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, on `POST /install-appointments`.
22. **Simultaneous bury cap.** The number of `SCHEDULED` `DROP_BURY` appointments in the same build zone whose raw windows overlap the new one is strictly less than the zone's `max_concurrent_bury_ops`. The rejection is `ERR_ZONE_BURY_CONCURRENCY_EXCEEDED`, on `POST /install-appointments`.
23. **Crew daily capacity.** The crew's already-booked minutes for the UTC calendar date of `window_start_at`, plus the new appointment's `occupied_minutes`, is at most the crew's `daily_capacity_minutes`; an exact fit at capacity is accepted. Exceeding it is `ERR_CREW_DAILY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, on `POST /install-appointments`.
24. **Address not ruled out.** The address's `serviceability` is anything other than `NOT_SERVICEABLE`. An `UNDETERMINED` address may still be preordered — that is the entire point of a fiberhood campaign — so only a definite negative determination blocks the operation. The rejection is `ERR_ADDRESS_NOT_SERVICEABLE`, and it applies to **both** `POST /preorders` and `POST /install-appointments`.
25. **MDU signature at conversion.** For an address whose `dwelling_type` is `MDU`, conversion requires an MDU owner agreement for that address in status `SIGNED`. A `CONDITIONAL_MDU` address may collect preorders but cannot be built into without the owner's signature. The rejection is `ERR_MDU_AGREEMENT_REQUIRED`, on `POST /preorders/{id}/conversion`.
26. **Preorder uniqueness.** At most one preorder may exist for a given pairing of `service_address_id` and `account_id` whose `preorder_status` is `PENDING`, `QUALIFIED` or `CONVERTED`. A `CANCELLED` or `LAPSED` predecessor does not block a fresh sign-up. A duplicate is `ERR_DUPLICATE_PREORDER`, on `POST /preorders`.
27. **Extension cap.** The zone's `extension_count` is strictly less than the campaign's `max_deadline_extensions`; otherwise `ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_REACHED`, on `POST /build-zones/{id}/extend-deadline`.
28. **Counter cap.** The agreement's `counter_round` is strictly less than its `max_counter_rounds` before a counter is accepted; otherwise `ERR_COUNTER_LIMIT_REACHED`, on `POST /mdu-agreements/{id}/owner-response`.
29. **Resubmission cap.** A rejected permit application may be resubmitted only while its `submission_round` reduced by 1 is strictly less than `max_resubmissions`; otherwise `ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT_REACHED`, on `POST /permit-applications/{id}/submit`.
30. **Appeal cap.** A denied claim may be appealed only while `appeal_count` is strictly less than `max_appeals`; otherwise `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`, on `POST /damage-claims/{id}/appeal`.
31. **Threshold relief only.** A threshold adjustment may only lower the bar: the new `threshold_pct` is strictly less than the zone's current `threshold_pct` and is at least 0.01. Supplying a value greater than or equal to the current one is `ERR_THRESHOLD_INCREASE_FORBIDDEN`, on `PATCH /build-zones/{id}/threshold`.
32. **Fallback pool non-empty.** The alternative-technology candidate pool for the address is non-empty after eligibility filtering and after exclusion of previously declined options; an empty pool is `ERR_NO_FALLBACK_OPTION_AVAILABLE`, on `POST /service-addresses/{id}/fallback-offers`.
33. **Deposit exactness.** The deposit tendered on a preorder equals the campaign's `deposit_amount_cents` exactly — neither a partial payment nor an overpayment is accepted. This is checked **last**, after every state, temporal and constraint rule, so that a fiscal complaint never masks a structural one. The rejection is `ERR_DEPOSIT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 402), on `POST /preorders`.

### 2.11. Filters and Reverse Reachability

The entity graph in this domain is flat: rows reference each other by explicit foreign keys and every collection is addressed at the top level, so there are no nested ownership paths. That makes list filters the only way to traverse a relationship backwards, and every list endpoint therefore accepts the filters that make its parents reachable. All filters are optional, all are combined conjunctively, and all are applied before pagination so that `total_count` reflects the filtered population.

Campaigns are filterable by `city`. Build zones are filterable by `campaign_id`, `campaign_status` and `construction_status`. Service addresses are filterable by `build_zone_id`, `serviceability`, `exception_status` and `dwelling_type`. Accounts are filterable by `account_type`. MDU agreements are filterable by `service_address_id`, `owner_account_id` and `status`. Permit applications are filterable by `build_zone_id`, `permit_type` and `status`. Pole-attachment requests are filterable by `build_zone_id` and `status`. Preorders are filterable by `build_zone_id`, `service_address_id`, `account_id`, `preorder_status` and `deposit_status`. Install crews are filterable by `crew_type`. Install appointments are filterable by `preorder_id`, `crew_id`, `parent_appointment_id`, `status` and `appointment_type` — the `parent_appointment_id` filter is what makes the self-referential restoration relationship navigable in both directions. Damage claims are filterable by `install_appointment_id`, `service_address_id`, `claimant_account_id`, `damage_type` and `status`. Alternative-technology options are filterable by `campaign_id`, `option_status` and `technology`. Alternative-technology offers are filterable by `service_address_id`, `fallback_option_id`, `account_id` and `status`. Ledger entries are filterable by `account_id`, `preorder_id`, `damage_claim_id` and `entry_type`, and because most ledger rows are appended by cascade rather than by a direct call, this list is the only channel through which those rows can be discovered.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Launching a Campaign and Carving Fiberhoods

A campaign is the policy container for a whole city's build. When a `ProgramManager` opens one it supplies the marketing `name` (between 1 and 120 characters, and not blank), the `city` it covers (between 1 and 80 characters), the instant preregistration opens as `prereg_window_open_at`, and the campaign-wide default closing instant as `prereg_window_close_at`, which must fall strictly after the opening instant. It also fixes the commercial terms: `default_threshold_pct`, the preregistration rate percentage a fiberhood must reach to qualify, expressed with exactly two decimal places and lying between 0.01 and 100.00 inclusive; and `deposit_amount_cents`, the refundable deposit every preorder in the campaign must remit, which may be zero but never negative. Preregistration is not accepted before `prereg_window_open_at`; a sign-up attempted earlier is outside the window and draws `ERR_PREREG_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

Five further policy constants are optional at creation and fall back to defaults when omitted. `max_deadline_extensions` caps how many extensions any single fiberhood may be granted; it defaults to 2 and must lie between 0 and 5 inclusive. `extension_grant_days` is the number of whole days each granted extension adds to a zone's deadline; it defaults to 30 and must lie between 1 and 90 inclusive. `claim_filing_window_days` is how many whole days after an install visit a resident may still file a damage claim; it defaults to 45 and must lie between 1 and 365 inclusive. `restoration_sla_days` is how many whole days after an install visit a restoration follow-up must be booked; it defaults to 7 and must lie between 1 and 60 inclusive. `late_filing_factor` is the multiplier applied to a late claim's capped base amount; it defaults to 0.60, carries exactly two decimal places, and must lie between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive. A freshly created campaign reports `zone_count` as 0; thereafter `zone_count` is simply the number of build zones whose `campaign_id` names this campaign, with no filtering by either state dimension.

Accounts are the counterparties the program transacts with, and they are created by the `ProgramManager` rather than self-registered. An account carries `full_name` (between 1 and 120 characters), `email` (which must contain an at-sign and must be unique across all accounts), `phone` (between 1 and 32 characters) and `account_type`, one of `RESIDENT`, `PROPERTY_OWNER` or `BUSINESS`. Both ledger-derived balances, `deposit_held_cents` and `credit_balance_cents`, start at 0. The account's `id` is what a `Subscriber` or an `MduOwner` presents in `X-User-ID`, which is why account creation is an operator responsibility.

Carving a fiberhood out of the campaign's service area produces a `BuildZone`. The `ProgramManager` supplies the owning `campaign_id`, the resident-facing `name` (between 1 and 120 characters), a short operator `zone_code` of between 1 and 16 characters drawn from uppercase letters, digits and the underscore and unique among the zones of the same campaign, and `household_count` — at least 1 — which is the immutable denominator of every preregistration-rate calculation for this zone and counts single-family homes plus MDU units. Three fields are optional. `threshold_pct` defaults to the referenced campaign's `default_threshold_pct` and otherwise follows the same two-decimal, 0.01-to-100.00 rule. `required_permit_types` defaults to a list containing only `ROW` and must otherwise be a non-empty, duplicate-free subset of `ROW`, `TRAFFIC_CONTROL` and `ENVIRONMENTAL`; each named type must later hold an approved, unexpired permit before construction may begin. `max_concurrent_bury_ops` defaults to 2 and caps how many scheduled drop-bury windows in this zone may overlap at any single instant.

A newly carved zone starts with `campaign_status` `PREREG_OPEN` and `construction_status` `NOT_STARTED`, and its `prereg_deadline_at` is seeded from the campaign's `prereg_window_close_at`. Both `extension_count` and `cumulative_extension_days` start at 0; `suspension_reason` is null; `make_ready_cost_cents` and `outstanding_restoration_count` are 0; `final_qualifying_count`, `final_rate_pct`, `readiness_score` and `build_sequence_index` are all null, because no qualification run and no sequencing run has evaluated this zone yet. Its `readiness_posture` is computed on the spot from the rules in §2.6, which for a brand-new zone with no permits at all yields `BLOCKED`, and the owning campaign's `zone_count` is recomputed to include it.

### 3.2. The Address Registry, MDU Access and the Unserved-Address Path

Addresses are registered into a zone one at a time by the `ProgramManager`, who supplies the owning `build_zone_id`, the `street_address` (between 1 and 160 characters and unique within its zone), the `dwelling_type` — either `SINGLE_FAMILY` or `MDU` — the `unit_count`, and `distance_to_node_m`, the whole number of metres from the property to the nearest aggregation node, which may be zero but never negative. `unit_count` defaults to 1 and must be at least 1; beyond that it is constrained by the dwelling type, being exactly 1 for a `SINGLE_FAMILY` address and at least 2 for an `MDU` building. A new address begins with `serviceability` `UNDETERMINED`, `exception_status` `NONE`, `serviceability_determined_at`, `not_serviceable_reason`, `exception_reason` and `active_fallback_technology` all null, and `counts_toward_threshold` true — an address nobody has surveyed yet still counts while the campaign is running. Registering an address bumps the owning zone's `version`.

The flag `counts_toward_threshold` on an address is derived, never supplied. It reads true when `serviceability` is `UNDETERMINED` or `SERVICEABLE`, and also when `serviceability` is `CONDITIONAL_MDU` and at least one MDU owner agreement for that address stands in status `SIGNED`. It reads false when `serviceability` is `NOT_SERVICEABLE`, and false for a `CONDITIONAL_MDU` address with no signed agreement. On a preorder the same-named flag is true exactly when that preorder's `preorder_status` is `PENDING` or `QUALIFIED`, its `deposit_status` is still `HELD`, and its address's own `counts_toward_threshold` is true.

Recording an engineering determination is the `ProgramManager`'s act and it moves `serviceability` out of `UNDETERMINED`. The payload carries a `serviceability` value of `SERVICEABLE`, `CONDITIONAL_MDU` or `NOT_SERVICEABLE`, plus `not_serviceable_reason` where relevant. Reaching `SERVICEABLE` from `UNDETERMINED` requires that the address is `SINGLE_FAMILY`, or else is `MDU` and already has an agreement in status `SIGNED`. Reaching `CONDITIONAL_MDU` requires the address's `dwelling_type` to be `MDU` — a single-family home can never be conditional. Reaching `NOT_SERVICEABLE` requires that `not_serviceable_reason` be supplied, drawn from `NO_ROW_ACCESS`, `EXCESSIVE_DISTANCE`, `MDU_AGREEMENT_UNAVAILABLE` and `BUILD_NOT_QUALIFIED`; an operator may also move an address from `SERVICEABLE` to `NOT_SERVICEABLE` this way, again with a supplied reason. Whichever branch is taken, `serviceability_determined_at` is stamped with the logical clock, `not_serviceable_reason` is cleared to null for any determination other than `NOT_SERVICEABLE`, `counts_toward_threshold` is recomputed on the address and on every `PENDING` preorder standing at it, and the zone's `provisional_qualifying_count` and `prereg_rate_pct` are recomputed while the zone's `version` is incremented by 1. A `CONDITIONAL_MDU` determination therefore leaves `counts_toward_threshold` false until a signature arrives.

An MDU building cannot be built into without its owner's consent, and that consent is negotiated as its own resource. The `ProgramManager` opens an agreement by naming the `service_address_id` — which must be an `MDU` address — and the `owner_account_id` of the property owner who will respond, together with the `access_fee_cents` the program is willing to pay monthly (zero or more), the `expires_at` instant after which the negotiation dies, and optionally `max_counter_rounds`, which defaults to 2 and must lie between 0 and 5 inclusive. The agreement opens in status `DRAFT` with `offer_round` and `counter_round` both 0, `countered_fee_cents` null and `signed_at` null.

Sending the owner an offer is the operator's move and drives the agreement from `DRAFT` to `SENT`, or from `COUNTERED` back to `SENT`; in either case the logical clock must be strictly before `expires_at`, the supplied `access_fee_cents` replaces whatever fee stood before, and `offer_round` increases by 1. Responding is the `MduOwner`'s exclusive move, carried in a `response` field valued `SIGN`, `DECLINE` or `COUNTER`, with `countered_fee_cents` accompanying a counter. A `COUNTER` is admissible only from `SENT`, only while `counter_round` is strictly less than `max_counter_rounds` — otherwise `ERR_COUNTER_LIMIT_REACHED` — and only while the clock is strictly before `expires_at`; it moves the agreement `SENT → COUNTERED`, records `countered_fee_cents`, and increases `counter_round` by 1. Offer and counter therefore alternate, `SENT → COUNTERED → SENT`, for as many rounds as the cap allows, until the owner signs, declines, the clock passes `expires_at`, or `counter_round` reaches `max_counter_rounds`.

Signing is admissible from `SENT` and equally from `COUNTERED`, in both cases only while the clock is strictly before `expires_at`, and the two branches behave identically: `signed_at` is stamped with the logical clock and the agreement becomes `SIGNED`, which is terminal. What is signed is the standing `access_fee_cents`, not `countered_fee_cents` — a counter is a request, not an agreement, so an owner who signs while the row sits in `COUNTERED` accepts the operator's last offered fee. Signing then cascades three hops beyond the agreement: the referenced address moves `CONDITIONAL_MDU → SERVICEABLE` with `serviceability_determined_at` stamped and `counts_toward_threshold` recomputed to true; every `PENDING` preorder standing at that address has its own `counts_toward_threshold` recomputed to true as a consequence; and as a consequence of that, the zone's `provisional_qualifying_count` and `prereg_rate_pct` are recomputed and the zone's `version` is incremented by 1.

Declining is likewise admissible from `SENT` and from `COUNTERED`, again only while the clock is strictly before `expires_at`, and again the two branches behave identically. The agreement becomes `DECLINED`, which is terminal, and provided no *other* agreement for that address stands in status `SIGNED`, the address moves `CONDITIONAL_MDU → NOT_SERVICEABLE` with `not_serviceable_reason` set to `MDU_AGREEMENT_UNAVAILABLE` and `counts_toward_threshold` recomputed to false; the zone's `provisional_qualifying_count` and `prereg_rate_pct` are recomputed and its `version` is incremented by 1. Expiry produces exactly the same consequences with exactly the same conditions, and it applies to a `SENT` agreement and to a `COUNTERED` one alike: when the logical clock has reached or passed `expires_at`, the next read or write that touches the row materializes it as `EXPIRED` — terminal, with `signed_at` still null — and runs the same address and zone cascade. There is no background job, so the way to observe this transition is simply to read the agreement. An offer sent to an already-`SIGNED` agreement, or an owner response against a `DRAFT` or `EXPIRED` one, is `ERR_MDU_AGREEMENT_STATE_INVALID`.

When an address genuinely cannot be reached, the program owes the resident an administrative path rather than silence, and `exception_status` tracks it independently of `serviceability` — none of the three exception actions changes the engineering determination. The `ProgramManager` supplies an `action` of `REQUEST`, `GRANT` or `DENY`. `REQUEST` moves `NONE → REQUESTED` and is admissible only while `serviceability` is `NOT_SERVICEABLE`; it records the free-text `exception_reason`, which is either null or between 1 and 240 characters. `GRANT` moves `REQUESTED → GRANTED` and likewise requires `serviceability` to be `NOT_SERVICEABLE`; a granted exception is precisely what authorizes an alternative-technology offer for the address. `DENY` moves `REQUESTED → DENIED` and requires only that the exception currently stand in `REQUESTED`; once denied, no alternative-technology offer may ever be created for that address. Both `GRANTED` and `DENIED` are terminal. Granting an exception that was never requested, or attempting an offer for an address whose `exception_status` is not `GRANTED`, is `ERR_SERVICE_ADDRESS_STATE_INVALID`.

Alternative technologies are published campaign-wide before any address needs them. Each option names its `campaign_id` and its `technology` — `FIXED_WIRELESS`, `DSL_PARTNER` or `SATELLITE_PARTNER` — together with the guaranteed `min_downstream_mbps` (at least 1), the `monthly_price_cents` (zero or more), the `max_serviceable_distance_m` beyond which the technology cannot reach the premises, and `priority_rank`, an operator preference of at least 1 where a smaller number means a stronger preference. Two flags are optional: `supports_mdu` defaults to false and governs whether the option may be offered to a multi-dwelling building at all, and `option_status` defaults to `ENABLED` with `DISABLED` as its only alternative — a disabled option is invisible to selection even when it is the only one that would physically fit.

Extending an offer is the `ProgramManager`'s act against a granted exception, and the client does **not** choose the technology: the server selects exactly one option and creates the offer for it. The candidate pool is drawn from the options of the campaign that owns the address's zone, keeping only those whose `option_status` is `ENABLED`, whose `max_serviceable_distance_m` is greater than or equal to the address's `distance_to_node_m` — equality at the boundary is eligible — and whose `supports_mdu` is true whenever the address's `dwelling_type` is `MDU`, with no MDU requirement for a single-family address. Any option for which this address already holds an offer in status `DECLINED` is permanently removed from the pool for that address, though only for that address; an offer that merely lapsed to `EXPIRED` excludes nothing, so the same option can be offered again after expiry. If the pool is empty the request is refused with `ERR_NO_FALLBACK_OPTION_AVAILABLE`. Otherwise the winner is the candidate with the highest `min_downstream_mbps`; ties are broken by the lowest `monthly_price_cents`, then by the lowest `priority_rank`, then by the earliest `created_at`. The row `id` is never consulted as a tie-break.

The resulting offer records the `service_address_id` it was made for, the `account_id` supplied in the payload as the party entitled to answer, and the winning option as `fallback_option_id` — a server-chosen value that a client may never supply. It snapshots the winner's `technology`, `min_downstream_mbps` and `monthly_price_cents` onto itself, so that later edits to the published option leave the standing offer untouched. `offered_at` is stamped with the logical clock, `expires_at` is supplied by the operator in the payload and must fall strictly after the logical clock — there is no derived default window — the status opens at `OFFERED`, and `responded_at` is null. The `Subscriber` named by `account_id` answers with a `response` of `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE`, admissible only from `OFFERED` and only while the clock is strictly before `expires_at`. Accepting moves the offer to `ACCEPTED`, stamps `responded_at`, and writes the offer's snapshotted technology onto the address's `active_fallback_technology`. Declining moves it to `DECLINED`, stamps `responded_at`, and permanently removes that option from this address's future candidate pools. An `OFFERED` offer the clock has carried to `expires_at` is materialized as `EXPIRED` by the next read or write touching it, with `responded_at` left null and no option excluded. All three of `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal, and answering an offer that is no longer `OFFERED` — already answered, or lazily expired — is `ERR_FALLBACK_OFFER_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.3. Preregistration: Sign-Ups, Deposits and the Live Rate

Preregistration is the commercial heart of the program. A `Subscriber` signing up names the `build_zone_id` whose threshold the sign-up will count toward, the `service_address_id` to be served — whose own zone must be the same zone, or the request is `ERR_ADDRESS_LINKAGE_MISMATCH` — and the `account_id` remitting the deposit, which must equal the acting `X-User-ID`. It chooses a `plan_tier` of `BASIC_300`, `GIGABIT_1000` or `MULTIGIG_2000`, and for a unit inside an MDU building it supplies `unit_designator`, a label of between 1 and 24 characters that is null for a single-family address. It tenders the deposit as `tendered_deposit_cents`, which must equal the campaign's `deposit_amount_cents` exactly — a partial payment and an overpayment are refused alike with `ERR_DEPOSIT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. And it carries `expected_build_zone_version` as described in §2.9.

Sign-up is admissible only while the zone's preregistration window is open, only at an address whose `serviceability` is something other than `NOT_SERVICEABLE`, and only once per address-and-account pairing while an earlier preorder for that pairing is still `PENDING`, `QUALIFIED` or `CONVERTED`; a `CANCELLED` or `LAPSED` predecessor is no obstacle, and a second live sign-up is `ERR_DUPLICATE_PREORDER`. The new row opens with `preorder_status` `PENDING` and `deposit_status` `HELD`, and `deposit_amount_cents` is snapshotted from the campaign at this moment so that a later campaign edit cannot retroactively change what this subscriber owes. `make_ready_share_cents`, `drop_method`, `conversion_pre_discount_total_cents`, `conversion_discount_cents`, `conversion_total_due_cents`, `converted_at`, `cancelled_at` and `lapse_reason` are all null on a fresh preorder. The deposit is recorded, not merely asserted: one ledger entry of `entry_type` `DEPOSIT_HELD` is appended for the amount of `deposit_amount_cents` as a positive `amount_cents`, referencing this preorder, and the account's `deposit_held_cents` is recomputed from its stream. Finally the preorder's own `counts_toward_threshold` is computed from the address's serviceability posture, the zone's `provisional_qualifying_count` and `prereg_rate_pct` are recomputed, and the zone's `version` is incremented by 1.

The zone's live preregistration rate is what residents and operators watch, and it is recomputed on every write that can move it: a sign-up, a cancellation, a serviceability determination, an owner response to an MDU agreement, the lazy expiry of an MDU agreement, a threshold adjustment and a deadline extension. Two variants of the same calculation exist, differing only in which addresses they admit. The **provisional** variant is the one written to `provisional_qualifying_count` and `prereg_rate_pct` by all of those ordinary writes; it admits an address whose `serviceability` is `UNDETERMINED`, `SERVICEABLE` or `CONDITIONAL_MDU`, optimistically assuming an unresolved address will turn out buildable. The **final** variant, described in §3.4, is stricter. In both variants the numerator counts the preorders of the zone whose `preorder_status` is `PENDING` or `QUALIFIED` **and** whose `deposit_status` is still `HELD` **and** whose address the variant admits: `CANCELLED`, `LAPSED` and `CONVERTED` preorders are excluded, and so is any preorder whose deposit has left `HELD` for `REFUNDED`, `APPLIED` or `FORFEITED`, because the held deposit is the evidence of demand. The rate is that count multiplied by 100 and divided by the zone's `household_count`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The denominator is always the zone's immutable `household_count` — never the number of registered addresses — and because `household_count` is at least 1 there is no division by zero. A zone qualifies when that rounded two-decimal rate is greater than or equal to `threshold_pct`; sitting exactly at the bar is qualifying.

Cancellation is available to the owning `Subscriber` and to any `ProgramManager`, and its fiscal consequence depends entirely on which lifecycle state the preorder is in. From `PENDING` the transition is `PENDING → CANCELLED`: `cancelled_at` is stamped with the logical clock and the money dimension moves `HELD → REFUNDED`, appending one `DEPOSIT_REFUNDED` entry whose `amount_cents` is the negative of `deposit_amount_cents` and recomputing the account's `deposit_held_cents`. From `QUALIFIED` the transition is `QUALIFIED → CANCELLED` and the money moves `HELD → FORFEITED` instead, appending a `DEPOSIT_FORFEITED` entry for the same negative amount: once the zone has qualified and the program has committed capital to building it, a walk-away costs the deposit. Either way the zone's `provisional_qualifying_count` and `prereg_rate_pct` are recomputed downward and the zone's `version` is incremented by 1. Cancelling a `CONVERTED` preorder, or converting one that is not `QUALIFIED`, or converting a second time, is `ERR_PREORDER_STATE_INVALID`.

Two policy levers can be pulled while the window is still open, and both are `ProgramManager` acts on the zone. The first is **threshold relief**: supplying a new `threshold_pct` that is strictly lower than the zone's current value and at least 0.01 replaces the bar and recomputes the provisional rate's qualifying verdict against it, and the zone's `version` is incremented — which by design invalidates any client that was about to sign a subscriber up against the old terms. Raising the bar is forbidden outright, because subscribers signed up against the advertised number. The second lever is a **deadline extension**, and the client supplies no date at all: the endpoint always adds exactly the campaign's `extension_grant_days` whole days to the zone's current `prereg_deadline_at`, so the new deadline is fully determined by campaign policy. An extension is admissible only while the zone's `extension_count` is strictly less than the campaign's `max_deadline_extensions`; the eleventh extension of a two-extension campaign is `ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_REACHED`. Each grant advances `prereg_deadline_at`, increases `extension_count` by 1, adds `extension_grant_days` to `cumulative_extension_days`, and increments the zone's `version` by 1. The first grant moves `campaign_status` `PREREG_OPEN → PREREG_EXTENDED`; every subsequent grant is a self-loop `PREREG_EXTENDED → PREREG_EXTENDED` that keeps the state and only advances the counters. Neither lever touches `construction_status`, which is a wholly separate dimension.

### 3.4. Qualification and Its Cascades

The qualification run is the moment the program decides which fiberhoods it will actually build, and it is a campaign-wide `ProgramManager` operation that returns the zones it evaluated. A zone is eligible for evaluation when its `campaign_status` is still `PREREG_OPEN` or `PREREG_EXTENDED` and its `prereg_deadline_at` is at or before the logical clock. Zones still inside their window are silently skipped rather than rejected; but when the campaign has no still-open zone whose deadline the clock has reached, the run itself is refused with `ERR_PREREG_DEADLINE_NOT_REACHED`.

For each evaluated zone the run computes the **final** variant of the preregistration rate. The final variant applies the same numerator rule as the provisional one — `preorder_status` `PENDING` or `QUALIFIED`, `deposit_status` `HELD` — but demands a resolved, contractually usable address: it admits an address whose `serviceability` is `SERVICEABLE`, and admits a `CONDITIONAL_MDU` address only when some MDU owner agreement for that address stands in status `SIGNED`. Addresses still `UNDETERMINED` are dropped entirely. Because the final variant admits a subset of what the provisional one admits, `final_rate_pct` is never greater than `prereg_rate_pct`, and a zone that looked comfortably above its bar all campaign can still fail at the deadline. The resulting count and percentage are stamped onto `final_qualifying_count` and `final_rate_pct`, and the percentage — rounded half up to two decimal places, then compared — decides the outcome: greater than or equal to `threshold_pct` gives `QUALIFIED`, strictly below gives `NOT_QUALIFIED`. Both are terminal for the campaign dimension, both leave `construction_status` untouched, and either outcome increments the zone's `version` by 1.

When a zone reaches `PREREG_OPEN → QUALIFIED` or `PREREG_EXTENDED → QUALIFIED` — the two transitions carry identical consequences — the run walks that zone's preorders. Each one whose `preorder_status` is `PENDING` and whose `counts_toward_threshold` is true moves `PENDING → QUALIFIED`; its `deposit_status` stays `HELD` and **no** ledger entry is appended, because nothing moved. Each one whose `preorder_status` is `PENDING` but whose `counts_toward_threshold` is false moves `PENDING → LAPSED` with `lapse_reason` `ADDRESS_NOT_COUNTED`, and that lapse drives the money dimension `HELD → REFUNDED`, appending one `DEPOSIT_REFUNDED` entry for the negative of `deposit_amount_cents` and recomputing that account's `deposit_held_cents`. A subscriber whose building never got its owner's signature therefore gets their money back even though the fiberhood as a whole succeeded.

When a zone reaches `PREREG_OPEN → NOT_QUALIFIED` or `PREREG_EXTENDED → NOT_QUALIFIED` — again two transitions with identical consequences — the cascade runs four hops deep and is worth stating hop by hop. The first hop is the zone's own verdict, with `final_qualifying_count`, `final_rate_pct` and `campaign_status` written. The second hop lapses every preorder of the zone whose `preorder_status` is `PENDING`, each with `lapse_reason` `ZONE_NOT_QUALIFIED`. The third hop is caused by the second: each of those preorders moves `deposit_status` `HELD → REFUNDED`, and each such move appends exactly one ledger entry of `entry_type` `DEPOSIT_REFUNDED` whose `amount_cents` is the negative of that preorder's `deposit_amount_cents` — one entry per preorder, never one aggregate entry per account. The fourth hop is caused by the third: every account touched by those refunds has its `deposit_held_cents` recomputed from its ledger stream, which returns it to the value it held before these preorders existed.

The same run also closes the address registry for a failed zone. Every address of the zone whose `serviceability` is `UNDETERMINED` or `CONDITIONAL_MDU`, and every address at which a preorder just lapsed with `ZONE_NOT_QUALIFIED` even where that address had already been determined `SERVICEABLE`, moves to `NOT_SERVICEABLE` with `not_serviceable_reason` set to `BUILD_NOT_QUALIFIED`, and `counts_toward_threshold` is recomputed to false. Each address at which a preorder just lapsed additionally has its exception dimension opened on the resident's behalf, moving `exception_status` `NONE → REQUESTED` with `exception_reason` recorded as `zone build did not qualify` — this is the one path by which an exception is requested without an operator asking for it, and it is what feeds the alternative-technology workflow of §3.2. Finally the zone's `provisional_qualifying_count` and `prereg_rate_pct` are recomputed, both falling to their post-lapse values. A sign-up, threshold adjustment or extension attempted against a zone that has already reached `QUALIFIED` or `NOT_QUALIFIED` is outside the window and draws `ERR_PREREG_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

### 3.5. Permits, Pole Attachments and Build Sequencing

Qualifying a fiberhood earns it the right to be built, not the ability. Two outside parties still have to say yes, and the zone's `readiness_posture` is the single derived field that summarizes where it stands with them.

A permit application is opened by the `ProgramManager` against a zone's `build_zone_id` for one `permit_type` — `ROW`, `TRAFFIC_CONTROL` or `ENVIRONMENTAL`. Two policy fields are optional: `valid_days`, the number of whole days an approval remains valid, defaulting to 120 and constrained to between 1 and 730 inclusive; and `max_resubmissions`, the number of resubmissions permitted after rejections, defaulting to 2 and constrained to between 0 and 5 inclusive. The application opens in status `DRAFT` with `submission_round` 0 and `approved_at`, `expires_at`, `rejection_reason` and `reviewer_user_id` all null, and opening it recomputes the zone's `readiness_posture`. Submitting moves `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and increases `submission_round` by 1, so the first submission is round 1.

Deciding is the `PermitOffice`'s exclusive act, carried in a `decision` field valued `APPROVE` or `REJECT`. Approval moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, stamps `approved_at` with the logical clock, sets `expires_at` to `approved_at` advanced by `valid_days` whole days, clears `rejection_reason` back to null, records the acting operator's identifier in `reviewer_user_id`, and recomputes the zone's `readiness_posture` — which steps from `PERMIT_PENDING` to `POLE_PENDING` at the moment the last of the zone's required permit types has an approval. Rejection moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, requires that `rejection_reason` be supplied as a note of between 1 and 240 characters, records that note and the acting `reviewer_user_id`, and likewise recomputes the zone's posture. A rejected application can be sent back for review: resubmission moves `REJECTED → SUBMITTED`, is admissible only while `submission_round` reduced by 1 is strictly less than `max_resubmissions`, increases `submission_round` by 1 and clears `rejection_reason` to null. Reject and resubmit therefore cycle until `submission_round` reduced by 1 reaches `max_resubmissions`, at which point further attempts are `ERR_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT_REACHED`. Deciding a `DRAFT` application, or submitting one that is `APPROVED` or `EXPIRED`, is `ERR_PERMIT_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID`.

Approvals do not last forever, and their lapse is the system's most consequential lazy transition. Once the logical clock has reached or passed an approved application's `expires_at`, the next read or write that touches the application materializes it as `EXPIRED` — terminal — and the readiness check inside a ground-break attempt materializes it too, so an expired permit can never sneak past that gate. Expiry recomputes the zone's `readiness_posture` to `PERMIT_PENDING`, and if the zone is mid-build with `construction_status` `IN_CONSTRUCTION` it also suspends that zone with `suspension_reason` `PERMIT_EXPIRED`, which in turn cancels the zone's scheduled appointments and releases their crews' minutes exactly as described later in this subsection.

The pole owner's track runs in parallel. A pole-attachment request names the zone's `build_zone_id`, the `utility_owner` that owns the poles as a name of between 1 and 120 characters, and `pole_count`, the number of poles involved, which is at least 1. It opens in status `REQUESTED` with `make_ready_cost_cents` 0 and `survey_completed_at`, `denial_reason` and `attached_at` all null, and opening it recomputes the zone's posture. The `UtilityOperator` then files the field survey, which moves `REQUESTED → SURVEYED`, records the quoted `make_ready_cost_cents` (zero or more) and stamps `survey_completed_at` with the logical clock, again recomputing the zone's posture.

From `SURVEYED` the request forks, and the two branches carry deliberately disjoint consequences. The **accept** branch is the `ProgramManager`'s, expressed as a `decision` of `ACCEPT`: it moves `SURVEYED → ACCEPTED`, recomputes the zone's `make_ready_cost_cents` to include this request's quote, and increments the zone's `version` by 1. Nothing is cancelled and nothing is suspended — accepting a quote keeps the build alive and merely adds cost. The **deny** branch is the `UtilityOperator`'s, expressed as a `decision` of `DENY` accompanied by a `denial_reason` of `INSUFFICIENT_CLEARANCE`, `SAFETY_VIOLATION` or `CAPACITY_FULL`: it moves `SURVEYED → DENIED`, and the zone's `make_ready_cost_cents` now *excludes* this request. It then cascades. Where the zone's `construction_status` is `IN_CONSTRUCTION`, the second hop suspends it with `suspension_reason` `POLE_DENIED`; the third hop, caused by that suspension, cancels every appointment whose preorder belongs to that zone and whose status is `SCHEDULED`, each with `cancellation_reason` `ZONE_SUSPENDED`; and the fourth hop, caused by those cancellations, recomputes each affected crew's `booked_minutes_total` and `scheduled_appointment_count` downward by the released `occupied_minutes` and increments each such crew's `version` by 1. Either branch ends by recomputing the zone's `readiness_posture` and incrementing the zone's `version`. Finally, the `UtilityOperator` confirms physical attachment, which moves `ACCEPTED → ATTACHED`, stamps `attached_at` with the logical clock, recomputes the zone's posture — stepping `POLE_PENDING` to `READY` once the permits are in hand too — and increments the zone's `version` by 1. `DENIED` and `ATTACHED` are both terminal, and a decision before a survey, an attachment before an acceptance, or any action on a request that is already `DENIED` or `ATTACHED`, is `ERR_POLE_REQUEST_STATE_INVALID`.

A zone's `make_ready_cost_cents` is therefore the sum of `make_ready_cost_cents` over just those of its pole-attachment requests whose status is `ACCEPTED` or `ATTACHED`; a request still `REQUESTED` or `SURVEYED`, and a request `DENIED`, all contribute nothing.

Once several fiberhoods have qualified, the program must decide which one its crews attack first, and the sequencing run does that for a whole campaign at once. It considers only zones whose `campaign_status` is `QUALIFIED`; zones sitting in `PREREG_OPEN`, `PREREG_EXTENDED` or `NOT_QUALIFIED` are skipped entirely, keeping `readiness_score` and `build_sequence_index` null and receiving no `version` bump at all. For each qualified zone the run composes a readiness score from three factors and one penalty. The **demand factor** is the zone's `final_rate_pct` divided by its `threshold_pct`, capped at 1.0 so that beating the bar by a wide margin earns nothing extra. The **permit factor** is the number of distinct permit types named in the zone's `required_permit_types` that have at least one application in status `APPROVED`, divided by the number of types the zone requires; only applications whose `permit_type` appears in `required_permit_types` contribute. The **pole factor** is the best of the zone's pole-attachment requests rather than an average: each request contributes 1.00 when its status is `ATTACHED`, 0.50 when `ACCEPTED`, 0.25 when `SURVEYED`, 0.10 when `REQUESTED` and 0.00 when `DENIED`, and the pole factor is the largest of those contributions, or 0.00 when the zone has no requests at all. The **make-ready penalty** is the zone's `make_ready_cost_cents` divided by a saturation cap of 1,000,000 cents — ten thousand dollars of make-ready work is as bad as the model gets — capped at 1.0 and then multiplied by a weight of 15.

The raw score is the demand factor multiplied by 50, plus the permit factor multiplied by 25, plus the pole factor multiplied by 20, minus the make-ready penalty. That raw value is rounded half up to two decimal places exactly once, after composition, and the result is written to `readiness_score`; it is not clamped, so a heavily penalised zone may in principle score below zero. The run then ranks the campaign's qualified zones by `readiness_score` descending, breaking ties by the earlier `prereg_deadline_at`, then by the larger `household_count`, then by the earlier `created_at`, and writes `build_sequence_index` as the one-based position in that order. Each sequenced zone moves `construction_status` `NOT_STARTED → SEQUENCED`, has its `readiness_posture` recomputed and its `version` incremented by 1, and the run returns the zones in assigned build order.

### 3.6. Breaking Ground, Suspension and Declaring a Fiberhood Ready

Breaking ground is a `ProgramManager` act on one zone and it advances `construction_status` `SEQUENCED → IN_CONSTRUCTION`. It requires that the zone's `campaign_status` be `QUALIFIED` and that its construction dimension actually be `SEQUENCED` — attempting it on a zone that was never sequenced is `ERR_BUILD_ZONE_STATE_INVALID`. Two readiness gates then apply, in this order: first every permit type in `required_permit_types` must have an application in status `APPROVED` whose `expires_at` is strictly after the logical clock, failing which the attempt is `ERR_PERMIT_READINESS_UNMET`; then the zone must have at least one pole-attachment request in status `ATTACHED`, failing which it is `ERR_POLE_READINESS_UNMET`. A quote that has merely been accepted is not enough to break ground — the cables have to be on the poles. Success recomputes the zone's `readiness_posture` to `READY` and increments the zone's `version` by 1.

Breaking ground is also the moment the make-ready bill is divided among the residents who will benefit from it, and the order of operations here is pinned because it is the only order that conserves cents. The recipients are the zone's preorders whose `preorder_status` is `QUALIFIED` at this instant — `PENDING` preorders are excluded because they never qualified, and `LAPSED`, `CANCELLED` and `CONVERTED` ones are excluded too — sorted by `created_at` ascending, with ties broken by the ascending `street_address` of the preorder's address as a final deterministic tie-break. If there are no such recipients, no allocation is written and the ground-break still succeeds. Otherwise, let the total be the zone's `make_ready_cost_cents` and the recipient count be however many rows the sorted list holds. Each recipient first receives the integer quotient of the total divided by the count, discarding any fraction. The residue — the total minus that quotient multiplied by the count, a value that is at least zero and strictly less than the recipient count — is then distributed one extra cent at a time to the recipients at the front of the sorted order, so that the first *residue* recipients receive the quotient plus one cent and everyone after them receives the quotient exactly. Each recipient's share is written to its `make_ready_share_cents`. The arithmetic is integer throughout, there is no rounding mode, and the shares sum to the total with zero residue left over. Computing a rounded per-recipient share first and reconciling the drift onto the last row is **not** an acceptable substitute: for a total of 100 cents split eight ways it produces 104 cents. The recipient pool is frozen at this instant: a preorder that converts afterwards keeps the share written here, and a later cancellation does not trigger a reallocation.

Construction does not always run to completion undisturbed. A `ProgramManager` may pause and restart a zone by supplying an `action` of `SUSPEND` or `RESUME`, with an optional `suspension_reason`. Suspending moves `construction_status` `IN_CONSTRUCTION → SUSPENDED` and records the reason, defaulting to `MANUAL` when the caller supplies none; the reasons this field can carry are `MANUAL` for the operator action, `POLE_DENIED` for the pole cascade of §3.5 and `PERMIT_EXPIRED` for the permit lapse of §3.5, and the field is null whenever `construction_status` is anything other than `SUSPENDED`. Suspension carries the same cascade whichever of the three causes triggered it: every appointment of that zone whose status is `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` `ZONE_SUSPENDED`, and every crew that held one of those appointments has its `booked_minutes_total` and `scheduled_appointment_count` recomputed downward by the released `occupied_minutes` with its own `version` incremented by 1. The zone's `readiness_posture` becomes `SUSPENDED` and the zone's `version` is incremented by 1.

Resuming moves `construction_status` `SUSPENDED → IN_CONSTRUCTION` and is admissible only when no pole-attachment request in the zone stands in status `DENIED` unless some other request in the same zone stands in `ATTACHED` — the program may not simply resume its way past a utility's refusal. It clears `suspension_reason` back to null, recomputes `readiness_posture` and increments the zone's `version` by 1. Cancelled appointments are **not** restored; the coordinator must book them again. A zone may be suspended and resumed any number of times, and attempting to resume a zone that is not `SUSPENDED` is `ERR_BUILD_ZONE_STATE_INVALID`.

Declaring a fiberhood finished moves `construction_status` `IN_CONSTRUCTION → SERVICE_READY`, which is terminal. It is admissible only while the zone's `outstanding_restoration_count` is zero — that is, only when every completed visit that disturbed a property has had its restoration follow-up completed. A zone cannot be signed off while lawns and driveways are still torn up, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_RESTORATION_OUTSTANDING`. Success recomputes the zone's `readiness_posture` to `READY` and increments its `version` by 1.

### 3.7. Converting a Preorder into an Install Order

Conversion is the moment a preregistration becomes a real order, and it is available to the owning `Subscriber` and to any `ProgramManager`. The transition is `QUALIFIED → CONVERTED` on the lifecycle dimension, so a preorder that is still `PENDING`, or that has already lapsed, been cancelled or been converted, is refused with `ERR_PREORDER_STATE_INVALID`. Beyond its own state the preorder's zone must actually be under construction: the zone's `construction_status` must be `IN_CONSTRUCTION` or `SERVICE_READY`, and a zone that has not broken ground or that is currently suspended yields `ERR_BUILD_ZONE_STATE_INVALID`. And where the address's `dwelling_type` is `MDU`, some MDU owner agreement for that address must stand in status `SIGNED`; a `CONDITIONAL_MDU` address may collect preorders all campaign long but cannot be built into without the owner's signature, and the attempt is `ERR_MDU_AGREEMENT_REQUIRED`.

The only field the caller supplies is `drop_method`, either `BURY` or `AERIAL`, which is pinned onto the preorder at this moment and never changes afterwards. Everything else in the settlement is derived, in nine pinned steps. First, the **install base** is read from the `plan_tier` frozen onto the preorder at sign-up: 9,900 cents for `BASIC_300`, 14,900 cents for `GIGABIT_1000` and 24,900 cents for `MULTIGIG_2000`. Second, the **drop surcharge** is read from the `drop_method` just supplied: 15,000 cents for `BURY`, which means trenching, and 5,000 cents for `AERIAL`. Third, the preorder's **make-ready share** is taken from its frozen `make_ready_share_cents`, treated as 0 when that field is null because the zone's ground-break allocation never ran for this row. Fourth, those three amounts are added to give the **pre-discount total**, which is persisted to `conversion_pre_discount_total_cents`.

Fifth, the loyalty tier is chosen from the pre-discount total — and it is chosen from *that* figure, not from the install base alone and not from any post-deposit amount. A pre-discount total of at least 50,000 cents earns a discount rate of 0.10; otherwise a total of at least 30,000 cents earns 0.05; otherwise the rate is 0.00. Sixth, the discount amount is the pre-discount total multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to a whole number of cents, and persisted to `conversion_discount_cents`; this is the only rounding in the whole settlement. Seventh, the net total is the pre-discount total minus the discount. Eighth, the subscriber's held deposit is credited at face value — never discounted, never scaled — so the amount still owing is the net total minus the preorder's `deposit_amount_cents`. Ninth, that figure is persisted to `conversion_total_due_cents`, and it may legitimately be negative where the deposit exceeds the net total. No other preorder of the account or the zone participates in this calculation.

Conversion then settles the money dimension. `deposit_status` moves `HELD → APPLIED`, appending one ledger entry of `entry_type` `DEPOSIT_APPLIED` whose `amount_cents` is the negative of `deposit_amount_cents` and recomputing the account's `deposit_held_cents`; and `converted_at` is stamped with the logical clock. The deposit has now been consumed rather than returned, which is exactly why cancelling a `QUALIFIED` preorder forfeits it while cancelling a `PENDING` one refunds it.

### 3.8. Crews, Appointment Windows and Visit Outcomes

Field capacity is modelled as crews, and the `InstallCoordinator` registers them. A crew carries a dispatch `name` of between 1 and 80 characters and a `crew_type` of `BURY`, `AERIAL` or `RESTORATION`, which fixes the work it is certified for. Two capacity parameters are optional: `travel_buffer_minutes`, the trailing buffer appended to every one of this crew's windows for both conflict detection and capacity accounting, defaulting to 30 and constrained to between 0 and 240 inclusive; and `daily_capacity_minutes`, the maximum occupied minutes the crew may hold on any single UTC calendar date, defaulting to 480 — eight hours — and constrained to between 60 and 1440 inclusive. Both `booked_minutes_total` and `scheduled_appointment_count` start at 0. Thereafter `booked_minutes_total` is the sum of `occupied_minutes` over all of this crew's appointments whose status is `SCHEDULED` across every date, and `scheduled_appointment_count` is how many such appointments there are; note that this running total spans all dates while the capacity gate below is evaluated per date.

Booking a window names the `preorder_id` being served, the `crew_id` that will hold the window, the `appointment_type` — `DROP_BURY`, `AERIAL_DROP` or `RESTORATION_FOLLOWUP` — the inclusive `window_start_at` and the exclusive `window_end_at`, and `expected_crew_version` as described in §2.9. A `parent_appointment_id` is supplied only for a `RESTORATION_FOLLOWUP`, where it is required and must name the `COMPLETED` appointment whose restoration obligation this visit discharges; for every other appointment type it is null. The crew's certification must match the work: `DROP_BURY` requires a crew whose `crew_type` is `BURY`, `AERIAL_DROP` requires `AERIAL`, and `RESTORATION_FOLLOWUP` requires `RESTORATION`, with any mismatch refused as `ERR_CREW_TYPE_MISMATCH`. The zone reached through the preorder must have `construction_status` `IN_CONSTRUCTION` or `SERVICE_READY`, otherwise `ERR_ZONE_CONSTRUCTION_NOT_ACTIVE`; and the address must not be `NOT_SERVICEABLE`, otherwise `ERR_ADDRESS_NOT_SERVICEABLE` — the same address rule that guards sign-up guards scheduling.

Window geometry is checked before any capacity arithmetic. `window_end_at` must be strictly after `window_start_at`; both instants must land on exact minute boundaries with zero seconds and zero fractional seconds, which is why no rounding is ever needed downstream; the duration must be at least 30 minutes and at most 480 minutes, inclusive at both ends; and `window_start_at` must be at or after the logical clock, so nobody books into the past. From those instants the appointment's `occupied_minutes` is derived as the whole minutes between start and end **plus** the crew's `travel_buffer_minutes`, and that single figure is the unit of both the conflict geometry and the daily capacity accounting.

Three capacity gates then apply, and they are evaluated in the order below. The **pairwise crew gate** treats each appointment as occupying a half-open interval running from its `window_start_at` up to but not including its `window_end_at` advanced by the crew's `travel_buffer_minutes`; the trailing buffer belongs to the earlier appointment, and the leading edge gets no buffer at all, so booking back-to-back-plus-buffer is legal. A conflict exists against an appointment that is `SCHEDULED` on the same crew when the new window's start falls strictly before that appointment's end advanced by the buffer *and* that appointment's start falls strictly before the new window's end advanced by the buffer. A new start exactly equal to an existing end advanced by the buffer is therefore not a conflict, while anything earlier is `ERR_CREW_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. The **simultaneous bury gate** counts the appointments that are `SCHEDULED`, have `appointment_type` `DROP_BURY`, belong through their preorder to the same build zone as the new booking, and whose **raw** windows overlap the new raw window — that is, the existing start falls strictly before the new end and the new start falls strictly before the existing end. That count must be strictly less than the zone's `max_concurrent_bury_ops`, otherwise `ERR_ZONE_BURY_CONCURRENCY_EXCEEDED`. Note the deliberate asymmetry: the pairwise gate buffers its intervals, this one does not, and this one is measured across every crew in the zone while the pairwise gate is scoped to a single crew. The **daily capacity gate** sums `occupied_minutes` over the crew's `SCHEDULED` appointments whose `window_start_at` falls on the same UTC calendar date as the new booking's `window_start_at`, adds the new appointment's own `occupied_minutes`, and requires the result to be at most the crew's `daily_capacity_minutes`; an exact fit at capacity is accepted, and exceeding it is `ERR_CREW_DAILY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Only `SCHEDULED` appointments occupy anything at all — `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW` and `CANCELLED` rows occupy neither minutes nor interval — so ending an appointment frees both.

A successfully booked appointment opens in status `SCHEDULED` with `restoration_required` false and `restoration_due_by`, `restoration_satisfied_at`, `completed_at` and `cancellation_reason` all null, and the holding crew's `booked_minutes_total` and `scheduled_appointment_count` are recomputed upward while the crew's `version` is incremented by 1.

Recording how a visit ended is also the `InstallCoordinator`'s act, and it accepts an `outcome` of `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED`, optionally accompanied by `restoration_required` and `cancellation_reason`. All three outcomes are admissible only from `SCHEDULED` — an appointment that already has an outcome yields `ERR_INSTALL_APPOINTMENT_STATE_INVALID` — and all three are terminal. All three share one consequence: the crew's `booked_minutes_total` and `scheduled_appointment_count` are recomputed downward by this row's `occupied_minutes`, restoring both to their pre-booking values, and the crew's `version` is incremented by 1.

`COMPLETED` stamps `completed_at` with the logical clock and records `restoration_required` from the request. Where that flag is true, `restoration_due_by` is set to `completed_at` advanced by the campaign's `restoration_sla_days` whole days and the zone's `outstanding_restoration_count` rises by 1. Where the appointment being completed is itself a `RESTORATION_FOLLOWUP`, the parent appointment's `restoration_satisfied_at` is stamped with the logical clock, which lowers the zone's `outstanding_restoration_count` by 1 and is precisely what unblocks the service-ready declaration of §3.6. `NO_SHOW` leaves the preorder entirely untouched — its `deposit_status` stays `APPLIED` and no ledger entry is appended — and opens no restoration obligation. `CANCELLED` records `cancellation_reason`, which an operator supplies as `SUBSCRIBER_REQUEST` or `CREW_UNAVAILABLE`, and which the zone-suspension cascade of §3.6 writes as `ZONE_SUSPENDED`.

### 3.9. Restoration Obligations and Construction Damage Claims

Trenching a drop across a front lawn leaves a mark, and the program takes on two distinct obligations when it does: it must come back and restore the property, and it must pay for what it broke. The first obligation is tracked as the zone's `outstanding_restoration_count`, which is simply how many appointments reachable through that zone's preorders are `COMPLETED` with `restoration_required` true and `restoration_satisfied_at` still null. Booking the follow-up is subject to two extra rules beyond §3.8's geometry: its `window_start_at` must fall at or before the parent appointment's `restoration_due_by`, and the parent must genuinely still owe restoration — `restoration_required` true and `restoration_satisfied_at` null — with any violation refused as `ERR_RESTORATION_WINDOW_MISSED`.

The second obligation is the claims process. A `Subscriber` files a claim naming the `install_appointment_id` during which the damage occurred, the `service_address_id` of the damaged property — which must be the address that appointment's preorder serves — and the `claimant_account_id`, which must equal the acting `X-User-ID`. It states a `damage_type` of `LAWN`, `IRRIGATION`, `DRIVEWAY` or `UTILITY_LINE`, a `claimed_amount_cents` of at least 1, and a `description` narrative that is either null or between 1 and 500 characters. Optionally it sets `max_appeals`, the number of appeals permitted on this claim, which defaults to 1 and must lie between 0 and 3 inclusive. Filing is admissible only against an appointment that is `COMPLETED` — otherwise `ERR_INSTALL_APPOINTMENT_STATE_INVALID` — and only where that appointment had `restoration_required` true so that a `restoration_due_by` exists at all, and only within the campaign's `claim_filing_window_days` whole days of the appointment's `completed_at`; filing later than that, or against a visit that never disturbed the property, is `ERR_CLAIM_FILING_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. The claim opens in status `FILED` with `appeal_count` 0, `filed_at` stamped from the logical clock, and `assessed_amount_cents`, `assessment_variant`, `assessed_by_user_id`, `decided_by_user_id`, `denial_reason` and `paid_at` all null.

Pricing is the `ClaimsAssessor`'s exclusive act and moves the claim `FILED → UNDER_ASSESSMENT`; a claim that returned to `UNDER_ASSESSMENT` after an appeal may be priced again, while pricing a claim that is already `PAID` is `ERR_DAMAGE_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. The calculation has exactly two steps and their order is pinned. First the claim is capped by a published schedule keyed on `damage_type`: 120,000 cents for `LAWN`, 250,000 cents for `IRRIGATION`, 400,000 cents for `DRIVEWAY` and 750,000 cents for `UTILITY_LINE`. The base amount is the lesser of `claimed_amount_cents` and that cap. Second, a timeliness factor is applied to that base — and only to the already-capped base, never to the raw claim. The factor is 1.00 when the claim's `filed_at` is at or before the appointment's `restoration_due_by`, and in that case `assessment_variant` is recorded as `STANDARD_SCHEDULE`; the factor is the campaign's `late_filing_factor`, which defaults to 0.60, when `filed_at` falls after `restoration_due_by`, and in that case the variant is `LATE_FILING_SCHEDULE`. Note that the variant is selected purely by *when* the claim was filed, not by what was damaged. The base multiplied by the factor, rounded half up to a whole number of cents, is written to `assessed_amount_cents`; the rounding happens only on that final multiplication. To make the pinning concrete, a 150,000-cent lawn claim is capped to 120,000 and assesses at 120,000 when filed on time, and at 72,000 when filed late under the default factor. The acting operator's identifier is recorded in `assessed_by_user_id`.

Deciding is the `ClaimsApprover`'s exclusive act, is admissible only from `UNDER_ASSESSMENT`, and is subject to the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.1 — the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `assessed_by_user_id`, or the request is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Either branch records the acting identifier in `decided_by_user_id`. An `APPROVE` decision moves `UNDER_ASSESSMENT → PAID`, which is terminal, stamps `paid_at` with the logical clock, and appends one ledger entry of `entry_type` `CLAIM_PAYOUT` whose `amount_cents` is the positive `assessed_amount_cents`, referencing this claim through `damage_claim_id` and crediting the `account_id` named by the claim's `claimant_account_id`; that account's `credit_balance_cents` is then recomputed from its stream. A `DENY` decision moves `UNDER_ASSESSMENT → DENIED`, requires a `denial_reason` note of between 1 and 240 characters, and appends **no** ledger entry at all — a denial moves no money.

A denial is not the end of the road. The claimant may appeal, which moves the claim `DENIED → UNDER_ASSESSMENT`, increases `appeal_count` by 1 and clears `denial_reason` back to null; `assessed_amount_cents` and `assessment_variant` are retained from the previous round until the next assessment overwrites them. An appeal is admissible only while `appeal_count` is strictly less than `max_appeals`, and beyond that it is `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`. Denial and appeal therefore cycle, `DENIED → UNDER_ASSESSMENT → DENIED`, until the appeal allowance is exhausted. Deciding a claim that has not been assessed, or appealing one that is `PAID` or currently `UNDER_ASSESSMENT`, is `ERR_DAMAGE_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is served under the root path, so the paths in this table are the literal request paths. Endpoints that bring a new resource into existence answer with HTTP 201; every other successful call — reads, lists, and the transition and workflow endpoints that modify an existing resource — answers with HTTP 200. All of them require the identity headers of §2.1 and all of them return the envelope of §2.2; list endpoints additionally honour the pagination and filter parameters of §2.3 and §2.11. Where the Authorized Roles column reads "All eight roles", every role named in §2.1 may call the endpoint: `ProgramManager`, `InstallCoordinator`, `PermitOffice`, `UtilityOperator`, `ClaimsAssessor`, `ClaimsApprover`, `Subscriber` and `MduOwner`.
