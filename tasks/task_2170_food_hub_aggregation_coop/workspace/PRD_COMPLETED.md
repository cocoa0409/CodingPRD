# Food Hub Aggregation Cooperative API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A regional food hub sits in an awkward middle: it is neither a farm nor a distributor, but a member-owned cooperative that must behave like both. Dozens of small producers each grow a little of everything; a handful of institutional buyers — school districts, hospitals, universities and correctional facilities — each want a lot of one thing, delivered cold, on a fixed day, at a price they can defend to a procurement committee. The hub's job is to make many small offers look like one reliable supply line. Today that work is done in spreadsheets: a member coordinator chases lapsed food-safety certificates by email, an operations lead hand-splits a 400-case carrot order across five farms, and the bookkeeper discovers only at invoicing time that two of those farms delivered short. Nobody can say, on demand, what any given member is owed.

This document specifies the API that replaces those spreadsheets. It covers the entire path a case of produce travels: a prospective member's onboarding file and its food-safety documentation, the catalog item and the offer window that makes it purchasable, the buyer's demand line, the aggregation run that splits that demand across competing producers, the cross-dock appointment where the pallets physically arrive and their temperature is recorded, the refrigerated route that carries them to the buyer's receiving door, the invoice that follows delivery, and — when a buyer refuses part of a shipment — the shrink apportionment that decides whether the producer or the hub eats the loss. It closes with the cooperative dimension that distinguishes a food hub from a broker: member equity, voting shares, and the annual patronage distribution that returns surplus to the members who supplied it.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below and are worth stating up front. First, **money is computed twice over the same rows**: once provisionally at aggregation, from the quantities the hub reserved, and once finally at invoicing, from the quantities that actually arrived — and the second figure is not a proportional scaling of the first, because the margin tier is re-selected from the smaller subtotal. Second, **supply is a finite pool that two independent failures can release**: a producer suspended for a lapsed certificate and a producer who never shows up at the dock both unwind reservations that other parts of the system have already priced, scheduled and loaded onto a truck. The correctness of this system lives almost entirely in how faithfully those two commitments are implemented.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Cooperative Roles and Request Identity

Every request to the API carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity that principal is acting in. Both must be present and non-empty; a request missing either, or supplying an empty value for either, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The role value must be one of exactly five strings — `HubAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `Producer`, `Buyer`, `BoardDirector` — and any other value, however plausible, is likewise rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` rather than a permission error, because an unrecognised role is not an identity the hub can authenticate at all. Once the role is recognised, it must appear in the set of roles the target operation admits; if it does not, the request fails with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). Section 4 lists the admitted roles for every operation.

The five roles divide the cooperative's duties as follows. A `HubAdmin` is food hub operations staff: they register producers and buyers, run the aggregation waterfall, open cross-dock doors, close out appointments, build and dispatch routes, record deliveries, invoice, and execute a ratified patronage distribution. A `ComplianceOfficer` is the food-safety and claims adjudicator: they verify or reject submitted documents, approve or request changes on a membership application, and apportion shrink when a rejection claim is approved. A `Producer` is a member farm acting on its own membership file, its own catalog, its own offers and its own dock appointments. A `Buyer` is an institutional customer acting on its own orders, its own instalment payments and its own rejection claims. A `BoardDirector` is an elected board member whose sole write capability is the vote that ratifies or rejects a proposed patronage cycle.

Two of these roles are **entity-backed**, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `Producer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Producer record; ownership checks on any resource that references a producer compare that resource's producer foreign key to `X-User-ID`, so a `Producer` may submit or resubmit only its own application, attach documents only to its own onboarding file, create and re-offer only catalog items whose `producer_id` is its own, and book dock appointments only under its own `producer_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Buyer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Buyer record; a `Buyer` may open orders only under its own `buyer_id`, add lines and apply payments only to its own orders, file rejection claims only against allocations belonging to its own orders, and withdraw only claims on its own orders. A violation of any of these ownership checks is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

The remaining three roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `HubAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `BoardDirector`, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to any record's `id`. It is, however, recorded wherever the cooperative's audit trail needs it: as `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a document a compliance officer verified or rejected and on a rejection claim that was approved, denied or withdrawn; as `filed_by_user_id` on the claim at filing time; as `proposed_by_user_id` on a patronage cycle a hub administrator created; and as `ratified_by_user_id` on the cycle a board director voted on. Those recorded identifiers are not decoration — they are the raw material of the cooperative's segregation-of-duties rules, described in §2.9, which reject a self-dealing action with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Separately, several operations accept a `decision` value that only certain roles may issue; submitting a decision outside the acting role's authority is rejected with `ERR_DECISION_ROLE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403), which is distinct from being barred from the operation altogether.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical hub clock of §2.5 rather than from wall-clock time.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and its minimum is 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), and so is a `limit` or `offset` that is supplied but cannot be parsed as an integer.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific narrative below says otherwise, so paging through a collection yields a stable oldest-first walk. Because the logical clock only moves when an administrator moves it, records routinely share an identical `created_at`; ties are then broken by the order in which the records were created, which keeps the walk deterministic. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`: `total_count` is the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when the offset plus the number of returned records is less than `total_count`.

Beyond `limit` and `offset`, collection endpoints accept domain filters that are how the system exposes reverse navigation from a parent to its children. A producer's document list filters on `doc_type` and `status`. The catalog list filters on `producer_id`, `commodity_code`, `storage_class` and `status`. The order list filters on `buyer_id`, `route_id`, `fulfillment_status` and `settlement_status`. An order's line list filters on `status`. The allocation list filters on `order_line_id`, `product_id` and `status`. The dock appointment list filters on `dock_slot_id`, `producer_id` and `status`. The claim list under an allocation filters on `status`. All filters are exact-match and combine conjunctively, comparing the raw query-string value against the stored field without validating it against the enum it names; a filter naming a value that no record matches — including a value outside that enum altogether — yields an empty page with a `total_count` of 0, never an error.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource the API creates is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients never supply an `id`. Alongside it, every entity in this system records `created_at` and `updated_at` as ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional-second digits, and `version` as an integer that begins at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource. All twelve entity types — producers, producer documents, products, buyers, buyer orders, order lines, allocations, dock slots, dock appointments, delivery routes, rejection claims and patronage cycles — carry the full set; there is no entity in this specification that omits `updated_at` or `version`.

The `version` field is not merely informational. It is the currency of the concurrency control described in §2.7, and it is also the observable signal that a cascade touched a record the client was not looking at. Whenever a rule in §3 says a record's state, quantity or money field changes, that record's `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock instant and its `version` is incremented by 1, including when the change was caused indirectly — by a producer suspension unwinding a reservation, by a no-show releasing a dock drop, or by an order being detached from a route. A read that returns an unchanged resource never advances its `version`.

Nested paths are resolved strictly left to right. When a URL names a parent and then a child, the parent id is looked up first: if a producer id in the path does not resolve, the request fails with `ERR_PRODUCER_NOT_FOUND` before the request body is examined at all, and the same holds for every other path segment with its own matching not-found code — `ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DOCK_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`. Foreign keys supplied inside a request body are resolved the same way and with the same vocabulary: a catalog item created against an unknown `producer_id` fails with `ERR_PRODUCER_NOT_FOUND`; an order opened against an unknown `buyer_id` fails with `ERR_BUYER_NOT_FOUND`; an appointment naming an unknown `dock_slot_id` fails with `ERR_DOCK_SLOT_NOT_FOUND` and one naming an unknown `producer_id` with `ERR_PRODUCER_NOT_FOUND`; a route stop naming an unknown `order_id` fails with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`; a rejection claim naming an unknown `allocation_id`, and a delivery record naming an allocation that is unknown or that belongs to a different order than the one in the path, both fail with `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`. Because that resolution sits above the shape rung, a body foreign key that is present but is not a string cannot resolve either and is reported with its own not-found code rather than as a malformed request; and where one body carries two foreign keys, they are resolved in the order the narrative names them, so an appointment booked with both an unknown `dock_slot_id` and an unknown `producer_id` reports `ERR_DOCK_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.5. The Logical Hub Clock

Every deadline, expiry, window boundary and due-date comparison in this specification is evaluated against a **logical clock** the server maintains, never against real wall-clock time. At startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it stays at that instant until an administrator moves it: an administrative capability accepts a `to_iso8601` instant and sets the clock to it. Any parseable instant is accepted, including one earlier than the clock's current reading; the sweep that follows only ever moves things forward, so winding the clock back never returns an `EXPIRED` document to `VERIFIED` and never reinstates a producer an earlier sweep suspended. Timestamps the server writes — `created_at`, `updated_at`, `invoice_due_date` — are taken from this clock.

Moving the clock is not a passive assignment; it runs a four-part sweep, and every consequence of that sweep is specified in §3. Verified food-safety documents whose expiry instant has been reached become expired. Every producer's compliance is then re-evaluated, and a producer that was active and has just lost compliance is suspended, firing the full delist-and-unwind cascade. Dock slots whose bookable window has ended are closed. Finally, invoiced orders that have passed their due date plus the write-off grace period while money is still owed are written off.

### 2.6. Cents, Basis Points and Rounding

All money in this system is stored and transported as whole cents in integer fields; there are no decimal currency values anywhere in the API. All margin and fault rates are expressed in **basis points**, where 10000 basis points is 100 percent — so a rate of 1500 basis points is 15 percent and a rate of 9000 basis points is 90 percent.

Wherever a rate is applied to an amount, the product is divided by 10000 and the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent: a computed value whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero to the next whole cent. Two rounding disciplines in this specification are deliberate and must not be "simplified". First, when an order's margin is computed, the rounding happens **once per allocation and the rounded results are then summed**; computing the subtotal first and rounding a single time at the end produces a different number of whole cents and is wrong under this specification. Second, when pallets are counted for an order, the ceiling to a whole pallet is taken **per allocation and the ceilings are then summed**; a single ceiling over the summed units is wrong for the same reason. Quantities of goods are always whole units — there is no fractional allocation, no fractional delivery and no fractional rejection.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Contended Resources

Three resources in this system are contended: a catalog item whose availability pool many orders draw from, a dock slot whose pallet capacity many producers book against, and a delivery route whose vehicle capacity and stop windows many orders compete for. Writes against all three are guarded by optimistic concurrency control on the parent record's `version`, and the guard is a required input, never an optional one.

Publishing an offer — the write at `POST /products/{id}/offers` — requires `expected_product_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` of the product as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the product's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PRODUCT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). An offer replaces the product's window, quantity and price wholesale, so the server cannot merge it with a view the client never saw; and because allocation commits and delisting cascades also advance a product's `version`, a client's view can go stale without any competing offer ever being published.

Booking a cross-dock appointment — the write at `POST /dock-appointments` — requires `expected_dock_slot_version`, carrying the `version` of the dock slot as the client last observed it. A mismatch with the slot's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DOCK_SLOT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Both the capacity test and the sanitation-separation test are functions of the slot's current appointment set, so a booking computed against a stale slot view can look feasible to the client and be infeasible in fact.

Adding a stop to a route — the write at `POST /delivery-routes/{id}/stops` — requires `expected_route_version`, carrying the `version` of the route as the client last observed it. A mismatch with the route's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ROUTE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A no-show cascade can detach an order from a route and change its loaded pallet count without any competing stop write, which is precisely why the version, and not a recomputed capacity check, is the proof that the planner's view is current.

No other write in this system takes a version field. Creating a product, opening an order, adding a line, running aggregation, closing out an appointment, dispatching, recording delivery, invoicing, paying, filing or adjudicating a claim, and every governance step all proceed without an expected-version input, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.8. The Request Screening Ladder

Requests are screened in a fixed order, and the **first** check that fails determines the response. This ordering is a contract, not an implementation detail: when a request violates two rules at once, the earlier rule's error is the one the caller sees. The ladder runs as follows.

1. Header presence — `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` both present and non-empty, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role recognition — the supplied role is one of the five cooperative roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
3. Endpoint role admission — the acting role is listed for the target operation, else `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
4. Path existence — every id in the URL resolves, checked left to right, else the not-found code matching the failing segment.
5. Body foreign-key existence — every foreign key in the request body resolves, else the not-found code matching that key.
6. Request shape — required body fields present, enum values legal, numbers inside their declared ranges, datetimes parseable as ISO 8601 UTC, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
7. Resource ownership — for the two entity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` equals the owning entity's id, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. This applies to submitting an application, attaching a document, creating a product, publishing an offer, booking a dock appointment, opening an order, adding a line, applying a payment, filing a claim, and adjudicating a claim.
8. Decision authority — the submitted `decision` is one the acting role may issue, else `ERR_DECISION_ROLE_MISMATCH`. This applies to membership review and to claim adjudication.
9. Optimistic concurrency — the supplied expected parent version equals the parent's current `version`, else the matching stale-version code, as described in §2.7.
10. Transition legality — the target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, else the state code matching the resource: `ERR_INVALID_PRODUCER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_DOCUMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_APPOINTMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ROUTE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`.
11. Payment admissibility — an instalment is applied only to an order whose settlement dimension is `INVOICED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`, else `ERR_ORDER_NOT_INVOICED`.
12. Segregation of duties — the acting `X-User-ID` did not perform the conflicting prior step, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
13. Producer standing — the producer whose supply, catalog or dock drop is involved is `ACTIVE` with compliance satisfied, else `ERR_PRODUCER_NOT_COMPLIANT`.
14. Application completeness — the required food-safety document set is present, else `ERR_MISSING_REQUIRED_DOCUMENTS`.
15. Temperature-regime agreement on routing, else `ERR_STORAGE_CLASS_MISMATCH`.
16. Delivery-record allocation ownership — each receipted allocation belongs to a line of the order in the path, else `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`.
17. Review-cycle ceiling, else `ERR_REVIEW_CYCLE_LIMIT`.
18. Interval well-formedness — an interval's end is strictly after its start and a route stop lasts at least one minute, else `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`.
19. Appointment containment — the appointment interval lies inside its slot's bookable window, else `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`.
20. Aggregate dock capacity, else `ERR_DOCK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
21. Sanitation separation, else `ERR_SANITATION_BUFFER_VIOLATION`.
22. Exclusive route service window, else `ERR_STOP_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
23. Route pallet capacity, else `ERR_ROUTE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
24. Staging completeness before dispatch, else `ERR_ALLOCATIONS_NOT_STAGED`.
25. Supply availability at aggregation, else `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SUPPLY`.
26. Rejection quantity headroom, else `ERR_REJECTION_EXCEEDS_DELIVERED`.
27. Fiscal-year uniqueness, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_FISCAL_YEAR`.
28. Patronage basis positivity at ratification, else `ERR_NO_PATRONAGE_BASIS`.
29. Payment headroom against the invoiced balance, else `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`.
30. Buyer credit exposure at aggregation, else `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

Two consequences of this ordering are worth calling out because they are easy to get backwards. Because dock capacity (rung 20) is screened before sanitation separation (rung 21), an appointment that violates both returns `ERR_DOCK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. And because the concurrency check (rung 9) sits above every state and business rule, a request that carries a stale parent version is rejected with the stale-version code even when the operation would also have failed a later business rule.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

The cooperative's governance rests on three separations, each enforced by comparing the acting `X-User-ID` against an identifier the system recorded earlier. A compliance officer may not approve a membership application if that same user id appears as the `adjudicated_by_user_id` on any document of that producer that currently stands verified — the officer who checked the paperwork is not the officer who admits the member. A board director may not ratify or reject a patronage cycle whose `proposed_by_user_id` equals their own user id. And a claims adjudicator may not approve or deny a rejection claim whose `filed_by_user_id` equals their own user id. Each violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline

Several operations in this system mutate records the caller never named. When a producer is suspended, its catalog is delisted, reservations drawn against that catalog are unwound, the demand lines those reservations filled are re-graded, the orders those lines belong to are re-priced, and orders left with nothing allocated are detached from their delivery routes — five hops from a single decision, reaching four aggregates away from the producer record. A dock no-show fires an abbreviated form of the same unwinding. In every case the rule is the same: each record the cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1 — exactly once for the whole request, however many hops of the same cascade reach the same record — and every derived quantity and money field on every touched record is recomputed from the surviving rows rather than patched incrementally. A cascade never leaves a parent's rollup disagreeing with the children it was computed from.

### 2.11. Two Kinds of Interval

This system schedules two different physical resources, and their interval geometry deliberately differs. A **dock slot** is a shared door with a pallet capacity: many appointments may occupy it at once, and the constraint is that the pallets of all mutually overlapping appointments must fit within the door's capacity. A **delivery route** is a single vehicle at a single buyer's receiving door: its stop windows are exclusive, and no two stops on the same route may overlap at all.

Both use half-open intervals — the start instant is included, the end instant is excluded — so two intervals that merely touch at a shared endpoint do not overlap. Two appointments where one ends exactly when the other begins are therefore both bookable on the same door, and two route stops arranged back-to-back at a shared instant are both legal. The sanitation-separation rule described in §3.6 is the one place where touching is not enough: it demands a positive gap.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Bringing a Farm Into Membership

Membership begins when a hub administrator registers a prospective member. The registration payload is deliberately thin — the farm's `legal_name`, which must be a non-blank string of 1 to 120 characters; a `region_code`, which must be exactly three uppercase letters and is used for growing-region reporting; and a `contact_email`, which must contain an at sign. The new producer lands in `onboarding_status` `DRAFT` with nothing proven about it: compliance not yet satisfied, a `food_safety_tier` of `TIER_NONE`, a `review_cycles` counter and a `suspension_count` counter both at 0, and every cooperative money field — `paid_in_capital_cents`, `patronage_volume_cents`, `shrink_charge_cents`, `retained_patronage_cents` — at 0, along with `voting_shares` at 0.

A `DRAFT` producer is a file, not a supplier. Before it can be reviewed it must assemble its food-safety paperwork (§3.2), and only then may it submit its application, moving `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. Submission is gated on completeness rather than on adjudication: for each of the three required document categories, the producer must hold at least one document that is either awaiting adjudication or already verified. A producer missing any of the three is refused with `ERR_MISSING_REQUIRED_DOCUMENTS`, whether the gap is a category never uploaded or one whose only document was rejected outright.

Review is the compliance officer's decision point, and the producer's file cycles through it as often as the paperwork requires. Requesting changes moves the producer `UNDER_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED` and increments `review_cycles` by 1; the producer fixes what was flagged and submits again, which moves it `CHANGES_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` under the same completeness gate as the first submission, and the first adjudication of any of its documents then takes it back to `UNDER_REVIEW`. This revision loop is bounded: a producer may be sent back for changes at most three times, and a fourth `REQUEST_CHANGES` decision — one issued when `review_cycles` has already reached the ceiling of 3 — is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_CYCLE_LIMIT`. Approval, by contrast, moves the producer `UNDER_REVIEW → ACTIVE`, but only if its compliance evaluation currently passes; an approval attempted against a producer whose required documents are not all verified and unexpired is refused with `ERR_PRODUCER_NOT_COMPLIANT`. Approval also refreshes the producer's `food_safety_tier` from the document set as it stands at that moment.

Which decisions a role may issue is itself a rule. `APPROVE` and `REQUEST_CHANGES` are the compliance officer's; `SUSPEND`, `REINSTATE` and `TERMINATE` belong to hub operations. A compliance officer attempting to suspend a member, or a hub administrator attempting to approve one, is refused with `ERR_DECISION_ROLE_MISMATCH` — the caller is permitted at the review capability but not for that decision. Every review decision may carry an optional free-text `note`, which is validated as a string and then discarded — it is neither stored on the producer nor returned in the response.

Termination is the exit door, and it is guarded by a settlement condition rather than a state condition: a producer may be terminated along `ACTIVE → TERMINATED`, `SUSPENDED → TERMINATED` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED → TERMINATED`, but only when no allocation drawn on any of its catalog items is still `RESERVED` or `STAGED`. Goods the hub has committed to a buyer, or already accepted at its dock, cannot be orphaned by an exit; an attempt to terminate over such an allocation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PRODUCER_STATE`. When termination proceeds from `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, every one of the producer's catalog items becomes `DELISTED` with its offered quantity set to 0. `TERMINATED` is final — there is no path back.

Equity is the other half of membership, and it moves on its own track entirely independent of onboarding state. A hub administrator records a capital contribution with an `amount_cents` of at least 0 — a negative amount is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and an optional `memo`, which is validated as a string but neither persisted nor echoed back; the amount is added to the producer's `paid_in_capital_cents`, and this endpoint is the only thing in the entire system that changes that figure. Voting weight is then re-derived: `voting_shares` is the paid-in capital divided by the share unit of 25000 cents — that is, one share per 250 dollars of capital — taken down to the whole share below, and then capped at 10 shares no matter how much more capital a member contributes. The boundary is inclusive at the share unit: exactly 25000 cents earns 1 share, while 24999 cents earns none. Retained patronage never converts to voting weight; only cash capital does.

### 3.2. Food-Safety Paperwork, Tiers and the Loss of Standing

A producer's onboarding file holds documents, each attached with a `doc_type` drawn from `GAP_AUDIT`, `LIABILITY_INSURANCE`, `HACCP_PLAN`, `ORGANIC_CERT` and `WATER_TEST`, an `issued_on` instant and an `expires_on` instant that must fall strictly after `issued_on` — an `expires_on` at or before `issued_on` is a malformed body and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, not with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. A newly attached document enters `SUBMITTED` with no `adjudicated_by_user_id` and no `rejection_reason` recorded. Producers attach documents to their own file; a hub administrator may also attach on a member's behalf, and attaching is gated on ownership alone — a document may be filed whatever `onboarding_status` the producer currently sits in.

Adjudication is the compliance officer's act on a single document, carried by a `decision` that must be exactly `VERIFY` or `REJECT`. Verifying it moves the document `SUBMITTED → VERIFIED`; rejecting it moves the document `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` and captures the supplied `rejection_reason`, which is itself optional and stays null when the rejection omits it, just as it stays null on a verification. Either way, the acting user id is stamped into `adjudicated_by_user_id`, and a document that is not currently `SUBMITTED` cannot be adjudicated at all — a second decision on the same document is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DOCUMENT_STATE`. Verification carries one extra precondition of its own: a document whose `expires_on` has already been reached on the logical clock cannot be verified, and the attempt is refused with the same `ERR_INVALID_DOCUMENT_STATE`. Verification also retires the paperwork it replaces — any other document of the same producer and the same `doc_type` that currently stands `VERIFIED`, `REJECTED` or `EXPIRED` is retired along `VERIFIED → SUPERSEDED`, `REJECTED → SUPERSEDED` or `EXPIRED → SUPERSEDED` respectively, and `SUPERSEDED` is where a document's life ends. And the first adjudication of any document, verify or reject alike, advances a producer sitting in `SUBMITTED` along `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`; that advance happens as part of the adjudication request and lands **before** compliance is recomputed.

Compliance is a strict reading of the verified set only. A document counts toward compliance if and only if it is `VERIFIED` and its `expires_on` is strictly later than the current logical clock instant; documents that are `SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED`, `EXPIRED` or `SUPERSEDED` never count, and a document whose expiry instant exactly equals the clock is already expired. A producer's `compliance_ok` is true when, and only when, it holds such a document for each of the three required categories: `GAP_AUDIT`, `LIABILITY_INSURANCE` and `HACCP_PLAN`. On top of that, `food_safety_tier` is `TIER_AUDITED` when the producer is compliant **and** additionally holds a counting document for both `ORGANIC_CERT` and `WATER_TEST`; it is `TIER_BASIC` when the producer is compliant without both of those extras; and it is `TIER_NONE` otherwise. This evaluation re-runs on every document adjudication, on every clock movement, and immediately before a catalog item of that producer is considered for allocation.

Time is what makes this dangerous. Advancing the logical clock past a verified document's `expires_on` moves that document `VERIFIED → EXPIRED` — again strictly, so an expiry instant equal to the new clock instant is expired — and then re-evaluates the owning producer. If that producer was `ACTIVE` and its compliance has just flipped from satisfied to unsatisfied, it is suspended automatically, `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, with exactly the same consequences as a hand-issued suspension. The two triggers differ; the cascade body does not.

That cascade is the most consequential sequence in this specification, and it runs in five hops. **Hop 1**: the producer's `suspension_count` is incremented by 1. **Hop 2**, caused by hop 1: every catalog item of that producer currently `LISTED` becomes `DELISTED` and its `quantity_offered` is set to 0, which drives its available pool to 0. **Hop 3**, caused by hop 2: every allocation still `RESERVED` that draws on any catalog item of that producer — the items hop 2 has just delisted, and equally any that were already `DRAFT` or `DELISTED` before the suspension — and whose order sits in either `AGGREGATED` or `SCHEDULED`, moves `RESERVED → REVERSED`, and the item's `quantity_committed` is decremented by that allocation's quantity. **Hop 4**, caused by hop 3: every demand line touched by those reversals recomputes its allocated quantity — a line left with nothing allocated returns to `PENDING`, and a line left with some but not all of its requested quantity becomes `SHORT`. **Hop 5**, caused by hop 4: every order touched by those lines re-prices under the provisional valuation described in §3.5, and an order whose lines have all fallen back to `PENDING` returns to `DRAFT`, is detached from its route by clearing `route_id`, `route_sequence_no`, `planned_arrival_at` and `service_minutes`, and the route it left recomputes its `stop_count` and `pallets_loaded`.

Reinstatement is the return leg, and it is asymmetric on purpose. A hub administrator may move a producer `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, but only if compliance is satisfied again at that moment — otherwise `ERR_PRODUCER_NOT_COMPLIANT` — and reinstatement refreshes `food_safety_tier`. What reinstatement does **not** do is un-delist anything. Catalog items delisted by the suspension stay `DELISTED` with zero offered quantity until the producer publishes a fresh offer against each of them, and the reversed allocations are gone for good; the buyer's order must be aggregated again to get supply back.

### 3.3. The Catalog and the Offer Window

A catalog item describes one sellable thing from one farm. Creating it requires the owning `producer_id`, a `commodity_code` of 1 to 40 characters drawn only from uppercase letters and underscores (this is the hub-wide identifier buyers order against, such as `CARROT_LOOSE`), a `unit_of_measure` of `CASE`, `LB` or `EACH`, a `storage_class` of `AMBIENT`, `CHILL` or `FROZEN`, a `pack_weight_lb` greater than zero giving the net weight of one sales unit, a `units_per_pallet` of at least 1, and a `producer_unit_price_cents` of at least 1 cent — the amount the producer is paid per sales unit before any hub margin. Only a producer in good standing may hold a catalog: an attempt to create an item for a producer that is not `ACTIVE`, or that is `ACTIVE` but not currently compliant, is refused with `ERR_PRODUCER_NOT_COMPLIANT`. A producer creating a catalog item for a `producer_id` other than its own is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

A newly created item is `DRAFT`: it has `quantity_offered` at 0, `quantity_committed` at 0, `quantity_available` at 0, and both `offer_window_start` and `offer_window_end` unset. Nothing can be bought from it. It becomes purchasable only when the producer publishes an offer, which moves it `DRAFT → LISTED` and sets three things at once: the `quantity_offered` for the coming window, an integer of at least 0, the `offer_window_start` that opens it, and the `offer_window_end` that closes it. The window is half-open — a delivery date exactly at the start instant is inside the window, and one exactly at the end instant is outside it — and an offer whose end is not strictly after its start is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. An offer may optionally restate `producer_unit_price_cents`, which overwrites the item's price when supplied and leaves it untouched when omitted. Every offer publication requires `expected_product_version`, as §2.7 sets out, and every successful publication increments the item's `version` by 1.

Publishing is a producer's own act and a producer's own risk. If the owning producer is not `ACTIVE` at that moment the publication is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PRODUCER_STATE`, and if it is `ACTIVE` but not compliant it is refused with `ERR_PRODUCER_NOT_COMPLIANT`. Re-offering is an ordinary and expected operation: an item already `LISTED` may be re-offered, staying `LISTED → LISTED`, which replaces the window, quantity and (when given) price wholesale. The one thing a re-offer may not do is offer less than the hub has already committed — a `quantity_offered` below the item's current `quantity_committed` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, because the hub cannot un-promise cases it has already reserved for buyers. That test is the last one the publication runs, below the window's well-formedness check, so an offer that is both mis-ordered in time and short of the committed quantity is reported as `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. And an item that was delisted by a suspension may be re-offered once its producer is reinstated, closing the `LISTED → DELISTED → LISTED` cycle; that publication carries the same expected-version requirement as any other.

The three quantity fields form the availability arithmetic that the rest of the system depends on. `quantity_offered` is what the producer published. `quantity_committed` is what allocations currently hold — specifically, the summed quantity of every allocation of this item in `RESERVED`, `STAGED` or `DELIVERED`. `quantity_available` is the offered quantity minus the committed quantity, floored at zero, and it is the pool the allocation waterfall draws from. Re-deriving it is not optional bookkeeping: it is recomputed whenever an offer is published, whenever an allocation is created, and whenever an allocation is reversed or short-closed. Existing allocations, meanwhile, are immune to later price edits — each allocation snapshots the producer unit price at the moment it was created, and a re-offer at a new price re-prices only future allocations.

### 3.4. Institutional Buyers, Orders and Demand Lines

An institutional buyer is registered by hub operations with a `legal_name` of 1 to 120 non-blank characters, a `buyer_type` of `SCHOOL_DISTRICT`, `HOSPITAL`, `UNIVERSITY` or `CORRECTIONS`, a `dock_zone_code` of 1 to 12 characters naming the receiving dock the routing team plans against, a `payment_terms_days` that must be exactly 15, 30 or 45, and a `credit_limit_cents` of at least 0. The buyer's `outstanding_balance_cents` starts at 0 and thereafter tracks, across all of that buyer's orders, the invoiced total less the cash applied less the credit memos issued.

An order opens with just two facts: the `buyer_id` it belongs to and a `requested_delivery_date`, the instant the institution wants its food. That date is not merely informational — it is the instant tested against each candidate catalog item's offer window during aggregation, so an order asking for delivery outside every producer's published window will find no supply. A new order sits in two independent status dimensions at once: `fulfillment_status` `DRAFT`, tracking the physical journey of the goods, and `settlement_status` `UNBILLED`, tracking the money. These two dimensions advance on their own schedules and, with a single exception noted in §3.8, neither drives the other. Every money rollup on a new order — `producer_subtotal_cents`, `hub_margin_cents`, `order_total_cents`, `margin_tier_bp`, `invoiced_total_cents`, `paid_total_cents`, `credit_memo_cents`, `order_pallets` — starts at 0, and `invoice_due_date`, `route_id`, `route_sequence_no`, `planned_arrival_at` and `service_minutes` all start unset.

Demand is expressed as lines, added to an order while it is still `DRAFT`; adding a line to an order that has moved past `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. Each line names a `commodity_code` of 1 to 40 characters matched exactly against catalog items — the line's own code is checked for length only, not for the uppercase-and-underscore shape a catalog item's code must take — a required `storage_class`, a required `unit_of_measure`, a `quantity_requested` of at least 1, and a `max_unit_price_cents` of at least 1 — the institution's price ceiling per unit, inclusive of the hub's margin. A new line is `PENDING` with `quantity_allocated`, `quantity_delivered` and `quantity_rejected` at 0 and `line_producer_cents`, `line_margin_cents` and `line_total_cents` at 0. Buyers add lines only to their own orders.

### 3.5. Aggregation: Splitting Demand Across Competing Farms

Aggregation is the hub's defining operation and the moment a pile of small offers becomes one order. A hub administrator runs it against an order that is still `DRAFT` and that carries at least one demand line; an order in any later fulfillment state, or one with no lines at all, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. The run has two passes — first the physical split, then the money — and their order is fixed.

**The physical pass.** Lines are processed one at a time in ascending order of when they were created, and each line is filled as fully as the market allows before the next line is considered. For a given line, a catalog item is a candidate only if every one of the following holds: the item is `LISTED`; its `commodity_code` equals the line's `commodity_code`; its `storage_class` equals the line's `storage_class`; its `unit_of_measure` equals the line's `unit_of_measure`; its `quantity_available` is greater than zero; the order's `requested_delivery_date` falls at or after the item's `offer_window_start` and strictly before its `offer_window_end`; the owning producer is `ACTIVE` with compliance currently satisfied; and the item passes the buyer's price ceiling. That last clause is worth stating exactly: the item's `producer_unit_price_cents`, plus the margin obtained by multiplying that price by 1800 basis points, dividing by 10000 and rounding half up to a whole cent, must be less than or equal to the line's `max_unit_price_cents`. The ceiling deliberately uses the **worst** margin rate the hub ever charges — 1800 basis points, 18 percent — because the order's real margin tier is not known until the physical pass has finished. The screen is therefore conservative in the buyer's favour, and it is never re-evaluated afterwards even if the order lands on a cheaper tier.

Candidates are then ranked, and the ranking is applied in exactly this sequence: cheapest `producer_unit_price_cents` first; among items at the same price, the producer with the smaller `patronage_volume_cents` first, which is how the cooperative spreads business toward members who have sold less through the hub; and among those still tied, the item created earlier first. Walking that ranking, the system draws from each item in turn while the line still has unfilled units, taking the smaller of the units still needed and the item's `quantity_available` — where that availability is read net of whatever earlier lines of the same run have already drawn from the item, so no unit is offered twice inside one aggregation. Each draw creates an allocation recording its `order_line_id`, its `product_id`, the `quantity` drawn, and a snapshot of the item's `producer_unit_price_cents`, and the allocation enters `RESERVED`; the item's `quantity_committed` rises by the drawn quantity and its available pool is re-derived. Each new allocation starts with `delivered_quantity`, `rejected_quantity`, `producer_charge_cents` and `hub_absorbed_cents` at 0, no `staged_temp_c` recorded and `temp_excursion` false.

When a line's walk ends, its `quantity_allocated` is the units it actually obtained. A line that got everything it asked for becomes `ALLOCATED`; a line that got some but not all becomes `SHORT`; a line that found no candidate at all stays `PENDING` with nothing allocated — and, crucially, the run continues to the next line rather than failing. The empty-pool fallback is per line, not per order. Only when **every** line of the order ends at zero allocated units does the whole request fail, with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_SUPPLY`, and in that case no allocation is persisted and the order remains `DRAFT`.

**The money pass, and the tier that keys on itself.** Once the split is fixed, each allocation in the valuation set is valued in ascending order of creation: its `producer_amount_cents` is the valuation quantity multiplied by its snapshotted `producer_unit_price_cents`. At aggregation the valuation quantity is the reserved `quantity`, and the valuation set is the order's allocations in `RESERVED` or `STAGED`; allocations that have been `REVERSED` or `SHORT_CLOSED` contribute nothing, and every rollup additionally zeroes their own `producer_amount_cents`, `hub_margin_cents` and `buyer_amount_cents` while leaving the `margin_bp` they last carried untouched. Summing those amounts gives the order's `producer_subtotal_cents`, and that **pre-margin** figure alone selects the margin tier: a subtotal below 50000 cents takes `margin_tier_bp` 1800; a subtotal from 50000 cents up to but not including 200000 cents takes 1500; from 200000 cents up to but not including 500000 cents takes 1250; and 500000 cents or more takes 1000. Every tier's lower bound is inclusive and its upper bound exclusive. The tier is selected once, from the subtotal before margin, and is **never** re-selected from the order total after the margin has been added, even when adding the margin pushes the order across a higher boundary; there is no fixed-point iteration here.

With the tier chosen, each allocation records it in `margin_bp` and computes its own `hub_margin_cents` by multiplying its `producer_amount_cents` by the tier rate, dividing by 10000 and rounding half up — per allocation, as §2.6 insists, and only then summed into a raw margin total. The order's `hub_margin_cents` is that raw total, except that the hub charges a floor of 2500 cents — 25 dollars — on any order it aggregates: when the raw total is below the floor, the order's margin is the floor instead, and the difference is added to the `hub_margin_cents` of one single allocation, namely the one with the largest `producer_amount_cents`, with a tie broken in favour of the allocation created earliest. Each allocation's `buyer_amount_cents` is then its producer amount plus its (possibly topped-up) margin, and the order's `order_total_cents` is `producer_subtotal_cents` plus `hub_margin_cents`. Each demand line rolls up its own allocations in parallel: `line_producer_cents` is the sum of their producer amounts, `line_margin_cents` the sum of their margins, and `line_total_cents` the sum of those two. Finally, `order_pallets` is computed by dividing each allocation's `quantity` by its item's `units_per_pallet`, rounding **each** result up to a whole pallet, and summing those whole pallets — over exactly the valuation set, and always from the reserved `quantity`, which means the final rollup of §3.8 recounts the pallets of the `DELIVERED` allocations from their reserved quantities and never from what actually arrived.

One guard protects the margin floor from manufacturing money out of nothing. When an order has no valuation allocation at all — every allocation reversed or short-closed — then `producer_subtotal_cents`, `hub_margin_cents`, `margin_tier_bp` and `order_total_cents` are all 0 and the 2500-cent floor is **not** applied, because there is no allocation row to carry the top-up.

**The credit gate.** Before the run is allowed to stand, the buyer's exposure is tested: the buyer's current `outstanding_balance_cents` plus this order's provisional `order_total_cents` must not exceed the buyer's `credit_limit_cents`. An order that would push the institution past its limit is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, and, as with a failed supply search, nothing is persisted — no allocation, no committed quantity, no state change. When all gates pass, the order moves `DRAFT → AGGREGATED`, each demand line has moved `PENDING → ALLOCATED` or `PENDING → SHORT`, and every catalog item the run drew from has had its `version` incremented by 1. Every demand line of the order has its `version` incremented by 1 as well — the run rewrites each line's allocated quantity and money rollup, so even a line that found no supply and stays `PENDING` comes back one version higher.

### 3.6. Cross-Dock Doors, Cold-Chain Receipt and the No-Show

Physical consolidation happens at the hub's cross-dock. Operations opens a door window by declaring a `door_code` of 1 to 12 characters, the `storage_class` the door is conditioned for, a `window_start` and a `window_end` that must be strictly later than the start — a door window whose end is not strictly after its start is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` — a `pallet_capacity` of at least 1, a required `temp_setpoint_c` giving the cold-storage setpoint in Celsius for goods received there — any number, positive or negative, and the only door field with no default — and optionally a `sanitation_buffer_minutes` of 0 or more, which defaults to 45 minutes — three quarters of an hour of wash-down between incompatible loads. A new door opens in status `OPEN` with `pallets_booked` at 0. Thereafter the door's status is derived, never set by hand: it is `CLOSED` once the logical clock has reached or passed its `window_end`; otherwise it is `FULL` when its booked pallets have reached or exceeded its capacity; otherwise `OPEN`.

A producer books a drop-off by naming the `dock_slot_id` and its own `producer_id`, an `arrival_start` and an `arrival_end` strictly after it, the number of `pallets` it will drop (at least 1), and a `risk_class` of `RAW_ANIMAL`, `PRODUCE` or `READY_TO_EAT`. Only a producer that is `ACTIVE` with compliance satisfied may book at all, and one that is not is refused with `ERR_PRODUCER_NOT_COMPLIANT`. The requested interval must sit inside the door's own bookable window — at or after `window_start` and ending at or before `window_end` — and an interval spilling outside it, like one whose end is not strictly after its start, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. Booking is guarded by `expected_dock_slot_version` as §2.7 describes.

Two feasibility tests then run against the door, and they use different geometry on purpose. The **capacity** test is an aggregate over overlap: the requested `pallets`, added to the pallets of every appointment on the same door that is `BOOKED` or `RECEIVED` and whose interval overlaps the requested one, must not exceed the door's `pallet_capacity`. Overlap here is half-open, so an appointment that ends exactly when the new one starts does not overlap it and contributes nothing. Exceeding capacity is refused with `ERR_DOCK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The **sanitation** test is a separation requirement, and it is not restricted to overlapping neighbours: every appointment on the door that is `BOOKED` or `RECEIVED` and whose `risk_class` differs from the requested one must be separated from the requested interval by at least the door's `sanitation_buffer_minutes` on one side or the other — either the neighbour's end plus the buffer is at or before the new start, or the new end plus the buffer is at or before the neighbour's start. Anything closer is refused with `ERR_SANITATION_BUFFER_VIOLATION`. Appointments sharing the same `risk_class` need no separation whatever. The two tests are ordered: capacity is evaluated first, so a request that violates both is reported as a capacity failure. Appointments that are `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED` release their pallets and count in neither test.

A successful booking enters status `BOOKED` with `received_pallets` at 0 and no `recorded_temp_c`, the door's `pallets_booked` is recomputed as the summed pallets of its `BOOKED` and `RECEIVED` appointments, the door's derived status is refreshed, and the door's `version` is incremented by 1.

Closing out an appointment is a hub administrator's act and takes an `outcome` of `RECEIVED`, `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED`; an appointment not currently `BOOKED` cannot be closed out and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPOINTMENT_STATE`. Receiving additionally requires the dropping producer to still be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PRODUCER_STATE`.

A `BOOKED → RECEIVED` outcome stores the `received_pallets` actually accepted — an optional integer of 0 or more that defaults to 0 when the close-out omits it — and the `recorded_temp_c` measured at the door, itself optional and left null when no reading is supplied; and it is the event that stages goods. Every allocation still `RESERVED` whose catalog item belongs to this appointment's producer, and whose order has reached `SCHEDULED`, moves `RESERVED → STAGED`. Each staged allocation copies the appointment's `recorded_temp_c` into its own `staged_temp_c`, and its `temp_excursion` flag is set to true exactly when that staged temperature is **greater than** the door's `temp_setpoint_c` plus the cold-chain tolerance of 2.0 degrees Celsius — a reading exactly at setpoint plus tolerance is within tolerance and does not flag, and a receipt that recorded no temperature at all copies a null `staged_temp_c` and leaves the flag false. That flag is frozen at this moment and, as §3.9 shows, it decides who pays when the buyer later rejects the goods. Staging can also complete a truck: when a route has at least one stop and no order on it holds an allocation still `RESERVED` — that is, every allocation of every assigned order has reached `STAGED` — the route advances `PLANNED → LOADED` on its own. That promotion is swept across every `PLANNED` route, but only as part of a `RECEIVED` close-out; no other event re-evaluates it, so a route made whole by some other means stays `PLANNED` until the next receipt.

A `BOOKED → NO_SHOW` outcome is the compensating path — it leaves `received_pallets` at 0 and `recorded_temp_c` null however the close-out body was filled in, as does a cancellation — and it unwinds in four hops. **Hop 1**: the door's `pallets_booked` is recomputed without this appointment and its derived status refreshed, releasing the space. **Hop 2**, caused by hop 1 releasing that drop: every allocation still `RESERVED` whose catalog item belongs to this appointment's producer, and whose order sits in either `AGGREGATED` or `SCHEDULED`, moves `RESERVED → REVERSED`, with the item's `quantity_committed` decremented by the allocation's quantity and its available pool re-derived. **Hop 3**, caused by hop 2: each affected demand line recomputes its allocated quantity and moves `ALLOCATED → SHORT` when some units survive, or `ALLOCATED → PENDING` or `SHORT → PENDING` when none do. **Hop 4**, caused by hop 3: each affected order re-prices under the provisional valuation, and an order left with no allocated units at all falls back `AGGREGATED → DRAFT`, is detached from its route by clearing `route_id`, `route_sequence_no`, `planned_arrival_at` and `service_minutes`, and the route recomputes its `stop_count` and `pallets_loaded`.

A `BOOKED → CANCELLED` outcome is the gentle case: the door's `pallets_booked` is recomputed without the appointment and its status refreshed, and no allocation anywhere is touched. Closing out advances the door's own `version` only when that recomputation actually changes `pallets_booked`, so a `NO_SHOW` and a `CANCELLED` each advance it while a `RECEIVED` — which keeps the pallets counted against the door — leaves it exactly where it was. `RECEIVED`, `NO_SHOW` and `CANCELLED` are all end states — an appointment closed out once is closed out for good.

### 3.7. Loading the Truck: Routes, Stops, Dispatch and Delivery

A delivery route is one refrigerated vehicle's day. Operations creates it with a `route_date` naming the service date, a `vehicle_code` of 1 to 12 characters, the `storage_class` the vehicle runs at, a `pallet_capacity` of at least 1, and a `depot_departure_at` instant for the planned departure from the hub depot. It begins `PLANNED` with `stop_count` and `pallets_loaded` both at 0.

Assigning an order to a route creates a stop, and it is the act that schedules a delivery. The planner supplies the `order_id`, a `sequence_no` recording the stop's ordinal on the route — any integer, neither required to be unique across the route's stops nor required to agree with their chronological order — a `planned_arrival_at` instant and an integer `service_minutes` duration, together with `expected_route_version`. Four conditions guard the assignment. The order must currently be `AGGREGATED`, else `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. Every one of the order's demand lines must carry the same `storage_class` as the route, since one vehicle runs at one temperature — a mismatch anywhere on the order is refused with `ERR_STORAGE_CLASS_MISMATCH`. The service interval, which runs from `planned_arrival_at` inclusive to that instant plus `service_minutes` exclusive, must last at least one minute — a stop of fewer than one minute, zero-length and negative alike, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` rather than as a malformed number — and must not overlap the interval of any other stop already on this route, since the vehicle can only be at one dock at a time; an overlap is refused with `ERR_STOP_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, while two stops arranged back-to-back, where one begins exactly as the other ends, are perfectly legal. And the vehicle must have room: the route's current `pallets_loaded` plus the order's `order_pallets` must not exceed the route's `pallet_capacity`, else `ERR_ROUTE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

A successful assignment writes `route_id`, `route_sequence_no`, `planned_arrival_at` and `service_minutes` onto the order and moves it `AGGREGATED → SCHEDULED`. The route then recomputes both of its derived counts — `stop_count` as the number of orders currently pointing at it, `pallets_loaded` as the summed `order_pallets` of those orders — and its `version` is incremented by 1. Orders detached by a cascade free their service window immediately, and the route's counts fall accordingly.

The route's own state is largely a function of things two aggregates away. It reaches `LOADED` on its own, as §3.6 describes, once it has at least one stop and no order on it holds an allocation still `RESERVED`. Dispatch is then a hub administrator's act, and it requires the route to be `LOADED`: dispatching a route in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ROUTE_STATE`, and specifically, a route still sitting in `PLANNED` because at least one allocation on it remains `RESERVED` is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATIONS_NOT_STAGED`, while a `PLANNED` route carrying no such allocation — one with no stops on it at all, say — falls back to `ERR_INVALID_ROUTE_STATE`. Dispatch also re-checks the load, refusing with `ERR_ROUTE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` if `pallets_loaded` exceeds `pallet_capacity`. On success the route moves `LOADED → DISPATCHED` and every order assigned to it moves `SCHEDULED → IN_TRANSIT`.

Delivery is recorded per allocation, at the buyer's receiving door. The hub administrator submits `line_receipts`, a collection in which each entry names an `allocation_id` and the `delivered_quantity` actually handed over; a `line_receipts` that is absent, is not an array, or is an empty array is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The order must be `IN_TRANSIT`, else `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`; every referenced allocation must belong to a demand line of the order named in the request path, else `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`; and each `delivered_quantity` must lie between 0 and the referenced allocation's reserved `quantity` inclusive, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An allocation of the order that no receipt entry names is left exactly as it stands — still `STAGED`, and therefore outside the final valuation of §3.8 — because the record only touches what it was handed. Each referenced allocation records what arrived, its own current status being taken as read rather than re-screened, and moves `STAGED → DELIVERED` when the delivered quantity is greater than zero, or `STAGED → SHORT_CLOSED` when it is exactly zero — and a short-closed allocation releases its hold, decrementing its catalog item's `quantity_committed`, and contributes nothing at all to any money rollup thereafter. Each demand line then recomputes `quantity_delivered` from its allocations, and a line with any delivered units at all moves `ALLOCATED → DELIVERED` or `SHORT → DELIVERED` according to where it stood; a line that received nothing keeps whatever status it already held, `PENDING` included. The order itself moves `IN_TRANSIT → DELIVERED`. Finally, when every order assigned to the route has reached `DELIVERED` — or has already been carried past it to `CLOSED` — the route advances `DISPATCHED → COMPLETED` without anyone asking it to.

### 3.8. Settlement: Billing What Arrived, Crediting What Did Not

The money dimension only starts moving once the goods have landed. Invoicing requires an order whose `fulfillment_status` is `DELIVERED` and whose `settlement_status` is still `UNBILLED`; an order that has not been delivered, or that has already been invoiced, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`.

Invoicing re-runs the entire pricing rollup of §3.5 under its **final** valuation, and this is the single most consequential difference in the specification. In the final valuation the set of allocations that count is those in `DELIVERED`, and each one's valuation quantity is its `delivered_quantity` rather than the quantity that was reserved; allocations that ended `SHORT_CLOSED` or `REVERSED` are excluded, exactly as they are excluded from the provisional run. Because the subtotal computed from delivered quantities is smaller than the provisional one whenever anything arrived short, the margin tier is re-selected from that smaller `producer_subtotal_cents` and may land on a **higher** basis-point rate than the provisional run used. The final margin is therefore not a proportional scaling of the provisional margin, and an implementation that scales rather than recomputes will be wrong. Every downstream figure follows from the recomputed rollup: per-allocation `producer_amount_cents`, `margin_bp`, `hub_margin_cents` and `buyer_amount_cents`; the order's `producer_subtotal_cents`, `margin_tier_bp`, `hub_margin_cents` and `order_total_cents`, including the 2500-cent margin floor and its top-up to the largest allocation; and each line's `line_producer_cents`, `line_margin_cents` and `line_total_cents`. That recomputed order total is then frozen into `invoiced_total_cents`, which never moves again. The `invoice_due_date` is set to the logical clock instant at invoicing plus the buyer's `payment_terms_days` days, the buyer's `outstanding_balance_cents` rises by the invoiced total, and the order moves `UNBILLED → INVOICED`.

Institutions pay in instalments, and each instalment carries a single `amount_cents` of at least 1. A payment may only be applied to an order whose settlement dimension is `INVOICED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`; anything else is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_INVOICED`. The amount must fit inside the remaining balance — the invoiced total less what has already been paid less any credit memos issued against the order — and an over-payment is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`. A payment that leaves the account short of the invoiced total moves the order `INVOICED → PARTIALLY_PAID`, or holds it at `PARTIALLY_PAID → PARTIALLY_PAID` for every subsequent instalment, of which there may be any number; a payment that brings paid cash plus credit memos to exactly the invoiced total closes the order, either `INVOICED → PAID` in one shot or `PARTIALLY_PAID → PAID` at the end of a series. Either way, `paid_total_cents` rises by the amount and the buyer's `outstanding_balance_cents` falls by the same amount.

Reaching `PAID` is what triggers the cooperative's patronage credit and what closes the order physically. Every producer that owns a `DELIVERED` allocation on the paid order has its `patronage_volume_cents` increased by that allocation's `producer_amount_cents` — the gross figure, with no deduction for shrink charges. At the same moment the order moves `DELIVERED → CLOSED` on its fulfillment dimension and every one of its demand lines moves `DELIVERED → SETTLED`. Note the direction of this coupling carefully: the money axis closes the physical axis, and never the reverse.

Not every invoice is paid. When the logical clock is advanced strictly past an order's `invoice_due_date` plus the write-off grace period of 30 days — a clock instant landing exactly on that sum is not yet late — while a balance is still outstanding — that is, while the invoiced total less paid cash less credit memos remains greater than zero — the order is written off, moving `INVOICED → WRITTEN_OFF` or `PARTIALLY_PAID → WRITTEN_OFF`. The buyer's `outstanding_balance_cents` is reduced by the remaining balance, the order moves `DELIVERED → CLOSED` and its lines move to `SETTLED` exactly as a paid order's would, but **no patronage whatsoever is credited to any producer**: a member earns patronage on cash the hub collected, not on food it shipped.

Between invoicing and settlement sits the rejection claim, the mechanism by which a buyer refuses part of what arrived. A claim names an `allocation_id`, a `quantity_rejected` of at least 1, a `reason_code` of `TEMP_ABUSE`, `DAMAGE`, `SPEC_MISMATCH`, `SHORT_COUNT` or `LATE_DELIVERY`, and an optional `note`. Claims may only be filed against goods that have actually been billed: the allocation itself must be `DELIVERED`, its order must be `DELIVERED` on the fulfillment axis, and its settlement axis must be `INVOICED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`; anything else is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. The claimed quantity must also fit within what is left to reject — the allocation's `delivered_quantity` less its already `rejected_quantity` — and a claim beyond that headroom is refused with `ERR_REJECTION_EXCEEDS_DELIVERED`. A filed claim enters `FILED` with `filed_by_user_id` stamped from the acting user, no `adjudicated_by_user_id`, and its own `producer_fault_bp`, `buyer_credit_cents`, `producer_charge_cents` and `hub_absorbed_cents` all at 0 — the claim carries those four figures itself, and an approval is what fills them in.

Adjudication resolves the claim exactly once — a claim not currently `FILED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE` — and the acting user id is recorded in `adjudicated_by_user_id` on every outcome; an optional free-text `note` on the decision is validated and then discarded. A compliance officer may `APPROVE` or `DENY`; the buyer that owns the claim's order may `WITHDRAW` it, and only that buyer, since a withdrawal by any other user id is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. A decision outside the acting role's authority is refused with `ERR_DECISION_ROLE_MISMATCH`, and an officer who tries to adjudicate a claim they themselves filed is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A `DENY` decision moves the claim `FILED → DENIED` and a `WITHDRAW` decision moves it `FILED → WITHDRAWN`; neither moves a single cent, and both of those states, alongside `APPROVED`, are terminal.

Approval, which moves the claim `FILED → APPROVED`, is where the shrink is apportioned, and it runs in a fixed order. First the buyer's credit: `buyer_credit_cents` is the allocation's `buyer_amount_cents` multiplied by the claimed `quantity_rejected`, divided by the allocation's `delivered_quantity`, rounded half up to a whole cent. Then the fault rate. A base rate in basis points is read from the reason: `DAMAGE` is 5000, `SPEC_MISMATCH` is 9000, `SHORT_COUNT` is 10000, and `LATE_DELIVERY` is 0, because a truck arriving late is the hub's problem and never the farm's. `TEMP_ABUSE` inverts on the cold-chain evidence the hub itself recorded at staging: when the allocation's `temp_excursion` flag is true, the hub's own door logged the goods above tolerance and the hub therefore owns most of the loss, so the base rate charged to the producer is only 2000; when that flag is false, the hub's receipt was clean and the cold-chain failure is attributed upstream to the farm, so the base rate is 8000. Next, relief for the best-documented farms: a producer whose `food_safety_tier` is `TIER_AUDITED` has 1500 basis points subtracted from the base rate, floored at zero. The tier consulted is the producer's tier as it evaluates at the instant of adjudication, re-read from the document set then and there rather than frozen at delivery or at staging. Finally the cap: `producer_fault_bp` is the relieved rate or 9000 basis points, whichever is smaller. The relief is applied **before** the cap, not after, so an audited producer on a `SHORT_COUNT` claim carries 8500 basis points rather than the 9000 an unaudited producer would carry.

With the rate fixed, `producer_charge_cents` is the buyer credit multiplied by `producer_fault_bp`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up, and `hub_absorbed_cents` is whatever is left of the buyer credit after that charge — computed by subtraction, so that the producer's charge and the hub's absorption always add back to the buyer credit exactly, with the hub carrying the rounding residue. Approval then moves that money across four records: the allocation's `rejected_quantity`, `producer_charge_cents` and `hub_absorbed_cents` all rise by the newly computed amounts; the demand line's `quantity_rejected` rises by the claimed quantity; the order's `credit_memo_cents` rises by the buyer credit and the buyer's `outstanding_balance_cents` falls by the same buyer credit; and the producer's `shrink_charge_cents` rises by the producer charge. An approval never re-runs the pricing rollup: `invoiced_total_cents`, the order's `producer_subtotal_cents`, `margin_tier_bp`, `hub_margin_cents` and `order_total_cents`, and every allocation's `producer_amount_cents`, `hub_margin_cents` and `buyer_amount_cents` all stand exactly as invoicing left them, and the allocation's `quantity` and `delivered_quantity` are likewise untouched. Because credit memos count toward closing the balance, an approved claim can leave an order fully settled with less cash than the invoice originally demanded.

### 3.9. The Cooperative Dimension: Patronage Basis, Ratification and Distribution

What makes this a cooperative rather than a broker is that surplus goes back to the members who generated it, in proportion to what they sold rather than to what they invested. Two different measures of "what they sold" exist in this system, and confusing them produces the wrong distribution. The **gross** measure is the running `patronage_volume_cents` on each producer, credited allocation by allocation as orders reach `PAID` and carrying no deduction for shrink. The **net** measure is that same figure less the producer's cumulative `shrink_charge_cents`, floored at zero, and it exists only at ratification time. For identical underlying rows the two measures give different per-member bases and therefore different shares — a member with approved rejection claims against it looks larger on the gross measure than on the net one.

A distribution begins as a proposal from hub operations naming a `fiscal_year`, which must be an integer from 2000 through 2100 and must not already be claimed by another cycle in any status whatever, `CANCELLED` and `DISTRIBUTED` cycles counting just as much as live ones — a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FISCAL_YEAR` — and a `distributable_pool_cents` of at least 1 cent, the board-proposed surplus. The cycle enters `PROPOSED` with `proposed_by_user_id` stamped, no `ratified_by_user_id`, and `basis_snapshot_total_cents`, `eligible_producer_count`, `distributed_total_cents` and `remainder_cents` all at 0.

The board then votes, and only a board director may. A cycle not currently `PROPOSED` cannot be voted on and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`, and a director whose own user id proposed the cycle is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A `RATIFY` decision moves the cycle `PROPOSED → RATIFIED` and freezes the membership snapshot: a producer is eligible if it is `ACTIVE`, its compliance is currently satisfied, and its net basis is greater than zero; `basis_snapshot_total_cents` becomes the sum of the net bases of the eligible producers and `eligible_producer_count` becomes how many there were. If that frozen total is not greater than zero — no eligible member has any net basis at all — ratification is refused with `ERR_NO_PATRONAGE_BASIS` and the cycle stays `PROPOSED`. A `REJECT` decision moves the cycle `PROPOSED → CANCELLED` with no distribution and no money movement. Either decision records the acting user id in `ratified_by_user_id`.

Distribution is executed by hub operations against a cycle that is `RATIFIED`, and any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`. The waterfall is a capped largest-remainder allocation, and its steps are pinned. First, a per-member ceiling is computed: no member may receive more than 3000 basis points — 30 percent — of the pool, and that ceiling is the pool multiplied by 3000, divided by 10000, taken **down** to the whole cent below. Then, for each eligible member as frozen at ratification, an exact proportional share is formed by multiplying the pool by that member's net basis — the value frozen at ratification, which no later patronage credit or shrink charge disturbs — and dividing by the frozen `basis_snapshot_total_cents`, keeping the exact rational value without rounding; the member's base share is that value taken down to the whole cent below, and then reduced to the ceiling if it exceeds it. Summing those base shares and subtracting from the pool gives the leftover cents, and each member's remaining headroom is the ceiling less their base share.

Those leftover cents are then walked out one at a time. Members are ordered by the fractional part of their exact proportional share, largest first; ties are broken by the larger net basis; and any remaining ties are broken by the earlier producer creation instant. Walking that order, one cent is given to each member that still has headroom, decreasing both that member's headroom and the leftover by 1, and the walk stops the instant the leftover reaches zero; the list is walked repeatedly for as long as leftover cents remain and any member still has headroom. Each eligible member's `retained_patronage_cents` then rises by its final share, ceiling-limited and inclusive of any cents won in the walk; a member whose final share works out to 0 cents is not written to at all and its `version` does not move.

The cycle records the outcome: `distributed_total_cents` is the sum of what was actually credited and `remainder_cents` is the pool less that sum, and the two always add back to `distributable_pool_cents` exactly. The frozen membership itself is held privately behind those totals — a cycle's representation exposes only `basis_snapshot_total_cents` and `eligible_producer_count`, never a per-member breakdown — so the individual shares are observable only as the movement in each member's `retained_patronage_cents`. The cap makes this waterfall lossy on purpose — when the per-member ceiling binds for every eligible member, the pool simply cannot be fully distributed, and the undistributed balance stays with the hub as an unallocated reserve rather than being forced onto members. Ratification is also the point at which the membership is fixed: a producer suspended between ratification and distribution still receives its frozen share, because the cooperative distributes on the year that was, not on the standing of the day. Distribution moves the cycle `RATIFIED → DISTRIBUTED`, which is where it ends; `CANCELLED` is the other ending.
