# Automated Grocery Fulfillment Center Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A customer fulfillment centre for online groceries is not a warehouse with a shopping list bolted onto it. It is a tightly coupled machine in which a single physical event — one tote arriving at a pick station one unit short — has to propagate, inside the same request, into a customer-facing substitution offer, a re-computed order total, a re-derived posture on the delivery wave that the order belongs to, and ultimately a gate on whether the van may leave the dock at all. This document specifies the service that runs that machine: order slotting into delivery waves by route and time window, robotic grid retrieval sequencing and pick-station assignment, temperature-zone segregation across ambient, chilled and frozen totes, substitution logic with customer approval before dispatch, weighted item picking with tolerance and price adjustment, tote consolidation, van loading sequence and route optimisation, delivery-slot capacity release management with surge pricing, inventory accuracy audits, and grid maintenance windows.

Three properties drove the design. First, **immediate consistency**: every derived figure — the delivery-slot capacity ledger, the SKU unit ledger, order totals, wave posture, station tote counts, zone accuracy scores — is reconciled inside the same request that mutates its sources, so a client never has to poll for a settled view. Second, **conservation**: delivery capacity units and physical inventory units are conserved quantities, and every forward action has an exact compensating action that restores the ledger with zero drift. Third, **determinism**: everything that could otherwise depend on wall-clock time or on iteration order — surge lead times, wave cutoffs, maintenance windows, substitution expiry, retrieval sequencing, station selection, waitlist promotion, van loading order — is pinned to an explicitly settable logical clock and to total orderings whose tie-breaks are fully specified.

The operator population is deliberately small and segregated. A site administrator owns the standing plant topology and the capacity levers; the shopper owns their own basket and the approval decision on any substitution; a wave planner forms and dispatches waves; a pick operator drives totes and retrieval tasks; a grid engineer owns the two levers — maintenance windows and audit counts — that can take a grid zone out of service. Where money leaves the building, the service enforces that two different human beings are involved: the operator who consolidates a wave may not be the operator who dispatches it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint, without exception, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role the principal is acting in. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined. If `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the five role names defined below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401). If the role is recognised but is not among the roles authorised for the target endpoint in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are spelled exactly as follows: `FulfillmentAdmin`, `Customer`, `WavePlanner`, `PickOperator`, and `GridEngineer`.

- **`FulfillmentAdmin`** is the site administrator. It owns the standing plant topology — delivery routes, dock bays, grid zones, pick stations and the SKU catalogue — plus customer account registration and delivery-slot capacity release management.
- **`Customer`** is the grocery shopper. It owns its own baskets, its slot bookings, and the approval or refusal of any substitution offered before dispatch.
- **`WavePlanner`** is the wave planning and dispatch operator. It forms waves, slots orders into them, drives a wave from picking through consolidation, loading and dispatch, and retires substitution proposals the customer never answered.
- **`PickOperator`** is the pick-station and grid operator. It opens, seals and reopens totes, drives every retrieval task through to completion, and offers substitutes for short lines.
- **`GridEngineer`** is the grid maintenance and inventory-accuracy engineer. It owns maintenance windows and audit counts.

Identity semantics differ by role, and the difference matters because ownership checks depend on it. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CustomerAccount`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `CustomerAccount` compare the resource's customer foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Customer` whose `X-User-ID` does not resolve to any existing `CustomerAccount` is rejected with `ERR_CUSTOMER_IDENTITY_UNKNOWN` (HTTP 403), and a `Customer` acting on a `CustomerOrder` — directly, or indirectly through one of its `OrderLine` rows or `SlotBooking` rows — whose `customer_id` differs from `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_OWNED` (HTTP 403).

When `X-User-Role` is `FulfillmentAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `PickOperator` and for `GridEngineer`. When `X-User-Role` is `WavePlanner`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, but the server does record it: consolidating a wave writes the acting `WavePlanner`'s `X-User-ID` into that wave's `consolidated_by_user_id` field, and the dispatch step later compares the dispatching `X-User-ID` against that recorded string. It is never resolved against any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query-string parameters. `limit` sets the page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` sets how many records to skip before the page begins: it defaults to 0 when omitted and its minimum accepted value is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, or either parameter supplied as something other than an integer, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 422).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, every list endpoint returns its records sorted by `created_at` in ascending order — oldest first. The `meta` object of a list response carries four pagination fields: `limit` echoing the effective page size, `offset` echoing the effective skip count, `total_count` giving the number of records that match the request's filters before paging is applied, and `has_more` set to true exactly when records matching the filters remain beyond the current page.

Filtered list endpoints double as the reverse-reachability channel for every foreign key in the model. Two resource families are created only as a side effect of other operations and therefore have no creation response of their own: retrieval tasks, which come into existence when a wave starts picking or when a substitution is approved, and automatically generated substitution proposals. For both, the corresponding filtered list endpoint is the sole discovery channel.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every persisted resource in this service — a delivery route, a dock bay, a grid zone, a pick station, an inventory SKU, a maintenance window, a delivery slot, a customer account, a customer order, an order line, a slot booking, a delivery wave, a tote, a pick task and a substitution proposal — carries the same four common properties, and there are no exceptions: none of the fifteen resource types omits any of them.

- `id` is a server-minted UUID. It is generated by the server, never supplied by the client, and it carries no type prefix — it is a bare UUID string, not `order_` followed by a UUID.
- `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the resource was created, rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z`.
- `updated_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the most recent mutation of the resource, in the same format. At creation it equals `created_at`.
- `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and is incremented by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource. Any request that mutates a resource also refreshes that resource's `updated_at`.

The `version` counter is not decorative: it is the anchor of the stale-write protection described in §2.7, and several operations bump it as a documented cascade rather than as an incidental detail. Adding a line to a basket, booking a slot, confirming a booking, slotting an order into a wave, completing a retrieval task, approving a substitution, accepting a shortage, consolidating a wave, computing a loading sequence, dispatching a wave, cancelling an order, and being bumped out of a slot by a capacity withholding each increment the affected `CustomerOrder`'s `version` by 1 and refresh its `updated_at`. Creating a substitution proposal increments the parent `OrderLine`'s `version` by 1. When an operation touches several member orders of a wave at once — starting picking, consolidating, sequencing the load, dispatching — every member order's `version` advances by 1, not just the first.

Wherever a foreign-key identifier appears in a URL path, the identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that fails to resolve determines the response. A request naming an order that does not exist and, in the same path, a line that does not exist, reports the order failure. Each resource family has its own not-found code, listed in §5; the resolution is never a generic 404 body.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Every temporal rule in this specification — delivery-slot booking windows, surge lead times, wave cutoffs, maintenance window activation, substitution proposal expiry, and the audit timestamp — reads a single server-side **logical clock** rather than the host's wall clock. The clock starts, on server boot, at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, and it must not fall back to real wall-clock time before it has been explicitly set: a freshly booted server that has never been told otherwise reports that instant as the current time.

Any of the five roles may move the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant, which becomes the new current time and is echoed back. Moving the clock is not inert: because the surge multiplier of a delivery slot depends on how many hours remain before the slot's window opens, every delivery slot's `surge_multiplier` is recomputed as part of the same request that moves the clock.

Throughout this document, phrases such as "the current instant", "the clock", or "now" refer to this logical clock and to nothing else.

### 2.6. Money, Weight and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this service are integers denominated in cents. There are no fractional-cent fields, and no monetary field is ever stored or returned as a decimal string. Weights are integers denominated in grams; distances are decimal kilometres with exactly two decimal places; a temperature tolerance is a fraction with at most four decimal places.

Wherever a computation produces a non-integral money figure, the result is rounded **half up** to whole cents — a value exactly on the half-cent boundary rounds away from zero, upward. The two accuracy scores of a grid zone are rounded half up to **four** decimal places, and a delivery slot's surge multiplier is rounded half up to **two** decimal places. Rounding is never applied more than the specification says, and never earlier: each calculation in §3 states exactly how many rounding operations it performs and at which step, because the number and placement of those roundings is observable in the resulting cents. Two rules recur and are worth stating once, globally:

- **Round-per-item-then-sum.** Where a total aggregates per-item figures — the merchandise subtotal over order lines, the transport cost over delivery stops — each item's figure is rounded to whole cents first and the rounded integers are then added exactly. Summing unrounded item figures and rounding once at the end is explicitly *not* the rule, and yields a different total whenever two or more items land on a half cent.
- **Ratios are exact.** Where a rule compares a ratio against a threshold — the catchweight deviation ratio against a SKU's tolerance, a slot's utilisation against a surge tier boundary, a zone's accuracy against the audit hold threshold before that score is rounded for storage — the ratio is evaluated as an exact rational quantity and compared directly. It is never pre-rounded to a percentage and then compared.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Basket and Line Writes

Two creation operations in this service can silently overwrite a decision the client never observed, and both are therefore guarded by an explicit optimistic-concurrency check against the parent resource's `version`. In both cases the guard is a pure stale-version comparison; nothing about it depends on requests overlapping in time.

**Creating a slot booking.** A `POST /slot-bookings` request must carry `expected_order_version` as a required, non-optional field holding the `version` of the target `CustomerOrder` as the client last observed it. If `expected_order_version` does not equal that order's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no booking is created. This matters because re-slotting is a legitimate product operation — a second booking on a different slot supersedes the shopper's earlier reservation — so a writer holding a stale view of the order would otherwise replace a reservation it had never seen.

**Creating a substitution proposal.** A `POST /substitution-proposals` request must carry `expected_order_line_version` as a required, non-optional field holding the `version` of the target `OrderLine` as the client last observed it. If `expected_order_line_version` does not equal that line's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no proposal is created. A fresh proposal on a line legitimately supersedes that line's previously declined or expired proposal, so a writer whose view of the line predates the previous round would otherwise open a new round chain from a stale base.

No other endpoint in this service takes a version field. `POST /slot-bookings` and `POST /substitution-proposals` are the only two write operations with an optimistic-concurrency input; every other write is guarded by state preconditions alone.

### 2.8. Validation Precedence — One Ordered Gate for Every Request

Many of the operations in this service are guarded by several checks at once, and the *order* in which those checks are evaluated is part of the contract, not an implementation detail. Every request runs through the following ordered gate. The checks are evaluated from the first to the last; the **first** check whose condition is violated determines the response, and no later check is evaluated at all. A request that reaches the end of the gate proceeds to do its work.

1. Both the `X-User-Role` and the `X-User-ID` headers are present and non-empty. Violation: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. `X-User-Role` names one of the five roles `FulfillmentAdmin`, `Customer`, `WavePlanner`, `PickOperator`, `GridEngineer`. Violation: `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
3. The acting role appears in the target endpoint's authorised-role list in §4. Violation: `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
4. When the acting role is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing customer account. Violation: `ERR_CUSTOMER_IDENTITY_UNKNOWN` (403). Applies to every endpoint whose authorised roles include `Customer`.
5. Every identifier embedded in the URL path exists, validated left to right. Violation yields the not-found code of the resource family that failed to resolve, always with HTTP 404: `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WAVE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TOTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PICK_TASK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ZONE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PICK_STATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DOCK_BAY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SKU_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_MAINTENANCE_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint carrying a path parameter.
6. Every foreign-key identifier supplied in the request body exists, validated in the order in which the fields are listed for that endpoint. Violation yields the same resource-specific not-found code as check 5, with HTTP 404. Applies to every `POST` endpoint whose body contains an identifier field.
7. When the acting role is `Customer`, the customer order reached through the path or the body — directly, or through the owning order of an order line or a slot booking — has a `customer_id` equal to `X-User-ID`. Violation: `ERR_ORDER_NOT_OWNED` (403). Applies to exactly seven operations: `POST /customer-orders/{orderId}/lines`, `POST /customer-orders/{orderId}/cancel`, `POST /order-lines/{lineId}/accept-shortage`, `POST /slot-bookings`, `POST /slot-bookings/{bookingId}/confirm`, `POST /substitution-proposals/{proposalId}/approve` and `POST /substitution-proposals/{proposalId}/decline`.
8. The request body contains every required field and no unknown field; every value has the correct JSON type; every enum-typed value is drawn from that field's declared set; every quantity or money amount is a non-negative integer; every decimal respects its declared scale; every instant parses as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). Applies to every `POST` endpoint.
9. When supplied on a list endpoint, `limit` is an integer between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` is an integer of at least 0. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (422). Applies to every `GET` list endpoint.
10. The optimistic-concurrency guard of §2.7 holds: `expected_order_version` equals the target order's `version` on `POST /slot-bookings`, and `expected_order_line_version` equals the target line's `version` on `POST /substitution-proposals`. Violation: `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). Applies to those two endpoints only.
11. The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition exists, and every precondition of that transition that concerns the target resource itself holds — for instance a tote must be `OPEN` to be sealed, a retrieval task must be `RETRIEVING` to be completed, a wave must be `CONSOLIDATED` to be sequenced for loading, and a booking must be `HELD` to be confirmed. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to every action-style `POST` endpoint.
12. The delivery wave's derived posture is not `FROZEN`. A `FROZEN` posture — meaning some grid zone serving an active retrieval task of that wave is in `MAINTENANCE` or `AUDIT_HOLD` — blocks all wave-level and tote-level progression. Violation: `ERR_WAVE_FROZEN` (409). Applies to exactly seven operations: `POST /delivery-waves/{waveId}/consolidate`, `POST /delivery-waves/{waveId}/assign-dock`, `POST /delivery-waves/{waveId}/loading-sequence`, `POST /delivery-waves/{waveId}/dispatch`, `POST /totes`, `POST /totes/{toteId}/seal` and `POST /totes/{toteId}/unseal`.
13. The grid zone holding the SKU being retrieved has status `OPERATIONAL`; a zone in `MAINTENANCE` or in `AUDIT_HOLD` may not be entered by the robotic grid. Violation: `ERR_ZONE_NOT_OPERATIONAL` (409). Applies to three operations: `POST /pick-tasks/{taskId}/start-retrieval`, `POST /delivery-waves/{waveId}/start-picking` and `POST /substitution-proposals/{proposalId}/approve`.
14. Cold-chain segregation holds: the tote's `temperature_zone` equals the SKU's `temperature_zone`, so a chilled or frozen item is never placed in a tote of a different class. Violation: `ERR_TEMPERATURE_ZONE_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to `POST /pick-tasks/{taskId}/assign-tote`.
15. The pick station named in the request lists the new tote's `temperature_zone` among the entries of its `temperature_zone_capability`. Violation: `ERR_STATION_ZONE_UNSUPPORTED` (409). Applies to `POST /totes`.
16. For a catchweight SKU, the deviation ratio between the measured and the nominal total weight does not exceed the SKU's `weight_tolerance_pct`; a ratio exactly equal to the tolerance is accepted and only a strictly greater ratio is rejected. Violation: `ERR_WEIGHT_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE` (422). Applies to `POST /pick-tasks/{taskId}/complete`.
17. Segregation of duties holds: the `X-User-ID` dispatching a wave differs from the `consolidated_by_user_id` recorded on that wave when it was consolidated. Violation: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to `POST /delivery-waves/{waveId}/dispatch`.
18. The delivery slot's `route_id` equals the `home_route_id` of the customer account owning the order being booked. Violation: `ERR_ROUTE_MISMATCH` (409). Applies to `POST /slot-bookings`.
19. An order being slotted into a wave holds a slot booking in status `CONFIRMED` whose `delivery_slot_id` equals the wave's `delivery_slot_id`, and the wave's `route_id` equals that slot's `route_id`. Violation: `ERR_WAVE_SLOT_MISMATCH` (409). Applies to `POST /delivery-waves/{waveId}/slot-orders`.
20. The substitute SKU is a different SKU from the ordered one, in the same `temperature_zone` and carrying the same `substitution_group_code`. Violation: `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_NOT_COMPARABLE` (422). Applies to `POST /substitution-proposals`.
21. When `supersedes_proposal_id` is supplied, it names a proposal on the same `order_line_id` whose status is `DECLINED` or `EXPIRED`. Violation: `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_INVALID` (422). Applies to `POST /substitution-proposals`.
22. The proposed maintenance window does not overlap any existing window on the same grid zone whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE`, once each interval is widened by fifteen minutes on both sides. Violation: `ERR_MAINTENANCE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to `POST /maintenance-windows`.
23. Aggregate dock concurrency holds: at every instant of the requested half-open loading interval, the number of *other* waves already assigned to the same dock bay whose own loading intervals overlap is strictly less than that bay's `parallel_van_capacity`. Violation: `ERR_DOCK_BAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to `POST /delivery-waves/{waveId}/assign-dock`.
24. The delivery slot's booking window is still open, meaning the clock is strictly earlier than the slot's `window_start`. Violation: `ERR_SLOT_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409). Applies to `POST /slot-bookings` and `POST /slot-bookings/{bookingId}/confirm`.
25. Substitution timing holds: a proposal in `PROPOSED` status may be approved or declined only while the clock is strictly earlier than its `expires_at`, and a violation there is `ERR_PROPOSAL_EXPIRED` (409); conversely, retiring a proposal as expired requires the clock to be at or after `expires_at`, and a violation there is `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_EXPIRED` (409). Applies to `POST /substitution-proposals/{proposalId}/approve`, `POST /substitution-proposals/{proposalId}/decline` and `POST /substitution-proposals/{proposalId}/expire`.
26. Wave-shape prerequisites hold: a wave being consolidated has at least one tote in status `SEALED`, and a violation there is `ERR_NO_TOTES_TO_CONSOLIDATE` (409); a wave being sequenced for loading has a dock bay assigned, and a violation there is `ERR_DOCK_NOT_ASSIGNED` (409); a wave being started has at least one member order, and a violation there is `ERR_WAVE_HAS_NO_ORDERS` (409). Applies to `POST /delivery-waves/{waveId}/consolidate`, `POST /delivery-waves/{waveId}/loading-sequence` and `POST /delivery-waves/{waveId}/start-picking`.
27. The pick-station selection pool is non-empty — some station is in `IDLE` or `ACTIVE`, supports the SKU's temperature class, and currently holds fewer open totes than its own maximum. Violation: `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PICK_STATION` (409). Applies to `POST /delivery-waves/{waveId}/start-picking` and `POST /substitution-proposals/{proposalId}/approve`.
28. Opening a tote at a station would not push that station's `current_tote_count` above its `max_concurrent_totes`. Violation: `ERR_STATION_TOTE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to `POST /totes`; the same code also guards the reopening of a sealed tote, described in §3.5.
29. No member order of the wave is dispatch-blocked — equivalently, no order line of any member order is left awaiting a substitution decision. Violation: `ERR_ORDER_DISPATCH_BLOCKED` (409). Applies to `POST /delivery-waves/{waveId}/loading-sequence` and `POST /delivery-waves/{waveId}/dispatch`.
30. Single-occupancy and shape prerequisites hold: the order being booked has no existing slot booking in status `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED`, and a violation there is `ERR_DUPLICATE_SLOT_BOOKING` (409); the retrieval task being bound to a tote does not already have one, and the tote being sealed has at least one retrieval task assigned to it, and a violation of either is `ERR_TOTE_NOT_ASSIGNED` (409); the line accepting a shortage is awaiting a substitution decision, and a violation there is `ERR_LINE_NOT_SHORT` (409); the line receiving a new proposal has no proposal already in `DRAFT` or `PROPOSED`, and a violation there is `ERR_PROPOSAL_ALREADY_OPEN` (409). Applies to `POST /slot-bookings`, `POST /pick-tasks/{taskId}/assign-tote`, `POST /totes/{toteId}/seal`, `POST /order-lines/{lineId}/accept-shortage` and `POST /substitution-proposals`.
31. The SKU has enough uncommitted stock: its `on_hand_units` is at least the number of units the request would allocate, summed across all lines of the wave when a wave starts picking. Violation: `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_INVENTORY` (409). Applies to `POST /delivery-waves/{waveId}/start-picking` and `POST /substitution-proposals/{proposalId}/approve`.
32. Slot capacity arithmetic stays inside the ledger. On a capacity withholding, the requested number of `units` is at most the slot's `total_capacity_units` minus its current `withheld_units`, so withholding may never push the total withheld above the slot's physical capacity; a violation is `ERR_WITHHELD_UNITS_EXCEEDED` (422). Withholding *more* than the currently available units is entirely legal and deliberately drives the available figure negative, which bumps confirmed bookings — that is a feature, not an error. On a booking, the requested `units` is at most the slot's `total_capacity_units` minus its `withheld_units`, because a larger booking could never be satisfied even from a completely empty slot; a violation is `ERR_SLOT_UNITS_EXCEED_CAPACITY` (422). A booking whose `units` merely exceeds the slot's *current* available figure is not an error at all — it is admitted to the waitlist. Applies to `POST /delivery-slots/{slotId}/withhold-capacity` and `POST /slot-bookings`; the same `ERR_WITHHELD_UNITS_EXCEEDED` code also rejects a capacity release whose `units` exceeds the slot's current `withheld_units`.
33. The order meets the minimum basket value: its `merchandise_subtotal_cents` is at least ten dollars, that is 1,000 cents, at the moment a slot booking is created. Violation: `ERR_MINIMUM_ORDER_NOT_MET` (422). Applies to `POST /slot-bookings`.

Two consequences of this ordering are deliberate and are worth calling out, because a naive implementation that evaluates its checks in a different sequence will report the wrong code. First, the state check at position 11 outranks the cold-chain check at position 14: binding a retrieval task that is not `QUEUED` to a tote of the wrong temperature class reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, not `ERR_TEMPERATURE_ZONE_MISMATCH`, because the state violation is found first. Second, the route check at position 18 outranks the minimum-basket check at position 33: a shopper booking a slot on a route that is not their home route, with a basket below the minimum, receives `ERR_ROUTE_MISMATCH` and never sees `ERR_MINIMUM_ORDER_NOT_MET`.

### 2.9. Conserved Quantities: The Slot Capacity Ledger and the SKU Unit Ledger

Two quantities in this system are conserved, and the invariants below must hold after every forward action *and* after every compensating action. They are not eventually-consistent aggregates recomputed by a nightly job; they are recomputed inside the request that moved any of their inputs.

**Delivery capacity units.** For a given delivery slot, `held_units` is the sum of the `units` field over every slot booking whose `delivery_slot_id` names that slot and whose status is `HELD`. `confirmed_units` is the same sum taken over bookings in status `CONFIRMED`. `available_units` is the slot's `total_capacity_units` less its `withheld_units`, less `held_units`, less `confirmed_units`. The conserved identity is therefore that `total_capacity_units` always equals `withheld_units` plus `held_units` plus `confirmed_units` plus `available_units`. Bookings in status `WAITLISTED`, `RELEASED` or `BUMPED` contribute zero to every term — a waitlisted booking occupies no capacity whatsoever, which is precisely what makes the waitlist safe. This recomputation is exact integer arithmetic with no rounding of any kind, and it runs on every status change of any booking on the slot as well as on every capacity release and every capacity withholding.

**Physical inventory units.** For a given SKU, `allocated_units` is the sum of the `allocated_units` field over every retrieval task whose `sku_id` names that SKU and whose status is `QUEUED` or `RETRIEVING`; tasks in status `COMPLETED`, `SHORT` or `CANCELLED` contribute zero. `on_hand_units` is that SKU's `physical_units` less its `allocated_units`. The conserved identity is that `physical_units` always equals `on_hand_units` plus `allocated_units`, again with exact integer arithmetic and no rounding. Reserving stock when a wave starts picking moves units from the on-hand figure into the allocated figure while leaving `physical_units` untouched. Cancelling a retrieval task is the exact compensating action: the task leaves the allocated set, so both figures return to their pre-allocation values with zero drift. Completing a task at full quantity lowers `physical_units` by the units picked *and* removes the same task from the allocated set, so `on_hand_units` is unchanged across a full-quantity completion — a property worth remembering when reading the pick workflow. This ledger is recomputed when a wave starts picking, when a retrieval task completes, when a retrieval task is cancelled, when a substitution is approved, when an order is cancelled, and when an audit rewrites counted stock figures.

### 2.10. Cold-Chain Segregation Across Ambient, Chilled and Frozen

Everything that moves through the building carries exactly one temperature class, drawn from the three values `AMBIENT`, `CHILLED` and `FROZEN`. A grid zone declares the class of everything stored in it. A SKU declares its own class, and the class must equal that of the grid zone that physically stores it — a SKU cannot be registered into a zone of a different class. A tote is opened for one class and one class only. A pick station, by contrast, declares a *set* of classes it is equipped to handle through its `temperature_zone_capability` array, which must be a non-empty array whose members are distinct values drawn from `AMBIENT`, `CHILLED` and `FROZEN`.

From those declarations the service derives three hard segregation rules that appear in the ordered gate of §2.8 and again in the workflows of §3. A tote may only be opened at a station whose capability set contains that tote's class, and an attempt to open one elsewhere is rejected with `ERR_STATION_ZONE_UNSUPPORTED`. A retrieval task may only be bound to a tote whose class equals the class of the SKU actually being retrieved, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_TEMPERATURE_ZONE_MISMATCH`. A substitute offered for a short line must share the ordered item's class, and one that does not is rejected with `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_NOT_COMPARABLE`.

Cold chain also shapes *ordering* rather than merely legality. Wherever the service has to impose a deterministic order across temperature classes — sequencing retrievals within a wave so that frozen goods spend the least time out of the freezer, and sequencing totes into the van so that frozen totes go into the freezer compartment — the classes are ranked with `FROZEN` first, `CHILLED` second and `AMBIENT` third.

### 2.11. Wave Posture: The Derived Gate Over Zones and Orders

A delivery wave carries, alongside its own lifecycle `status`, a second and entirely derived field called `posture`, whose value is one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. Posture is never written directly by any client. It is recomputed from scratch whenever any retrieval task, tote, order line, substitution proposal, grid zone or member order belonging to that wave is mutated, and it is the gate through which wave-level and tote-level progression must pass.

The recomputation looks at two populations. The first is the set of grid zones reached by the wave's *active* retrieval tasks — that is, the distinct zones named by tasks belonging to the wave's member orders whose status is `QUEUED` or `RETRIEVING`. The service counts how many of those zones are in status `MAINTENANCE` or `AUDIT_HOLD`. The second is the set of the wave's member orders that have not been cancelled, and the service counts how many of them are dispatch-blocked. An order is dispatch-blocked exactly when at least one of its order lines has a `substitution_resolution` of `PENDING`; the order's `dispatch_blocked` flag is that predicate and nothing more.

The posture then follows a strict precedence. If the count of held zones is greater than zero, the posture is `FROZEN`. Otherwise, if the count of dispatch-blocked member orders is greater than zero, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. A zone problem therefore masks a substitution problem: a wave with both a zone in `AUDIT_HOLD` and an unresolved short line reports `FROZEN`, not `RESTRICTED`.

The two non-`OPEN` postures gate differently. A `FROZEN` posture blocks consolidation, dock assignment, loading sequencing, dispatch, opening a tote, sealing a tote and unsealing a tote, all with `ERR_WAVE_FROZEN`. A `RESTRICTED` posture does not block tote handling at all — pick operators keep working — but it does block the loading sequence and the dispatch, with `ERR_ORDER_DISPATCH_BLOCKED`. Only an `OPEN` posture lets the van's load be sequenced.

### 2.12. Independent Status Dimensions and Segregation of Duties

A customer order carries **two independent status dimensions** that advance without lockstep, and confusing them is the most common way to get this domain wrong. The `fulfillment_status` dimension tracks the physical journey of the groceries and takes the values `DRAFT`, `SLOTTED`, `WAVED`, `PICKING`, `CONSOLIDATED`, `LOADED`, `DISPATCHED` and `CANCELLED`, starting at `DRAFT`, with `DISPATCHED` and `CANCELLED` as its two terminal values. The `billing_status` dimension tracks the money and takes the values `PENDING_AUTH`, `AUTHORIZED`, `ADJUSTED`, `CAPTURED` and `REVERSED`, starting at `PENDING_AUTH`, with `CAPTURED` and `REVERSED` as its two terminal values. Some requests move both dimensions at once — confirming a booking moves the order from `DRAFT` to `SLOTTED` and simultaneously from `PENDING_AUTH` to `AUTHORIZED` — but from that point on each dimension advances on its own triggers. An order can sit at `PICKING` while its money oscillates between `AUTHORIZED` and `ADJUSTED` any number of times.

Where money finally leaves the building, the service enforces **segregation of duties**. Consolidating a wave records the acting operator's `X-User-ID` on the wave as `consolidated_by_user_id`. Dispatching that same wave requires the acting `X-User-ID` to be a *different* string; if the two match, the dispatch is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Completing a wave therefore always requires two distinct `WavePlanner` operator identifiers. The comparison is a plain string comparison against the recorded identifier and involves no lookup of any entity.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Standing Plant: Routes, Bays, Zones, Stations, the Catalogue and Shopper Accounts

Before a single basket can be opened, a `FulfillmentAdmin` has to describe the physical site. Registering a **delivery route** captures a `route_code` — a human-readable identifier of between 1 and 32 characters that must be unique across all routes, with a blank or duplicated value rejected as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — together with a `service_area_name` of between 1 and 80 characters naming the geography the van covers. Three commercial parameters ride on the same registration: `van_capacity_totes`, the largest number of totes one van on this route can carry, which must be at least 1 and at most 400; `base_delivery_fee_cents`, the pre-surge delivery fee in cents, which must be at least 0 and at most 5,000, that is at most fifty dollars; and `per_km_cost_cents`, the transport cost per kilometre in cents, which must be at least 1 and at most 2,000.

A **dock bay** is even simpler: a `bay_code` of 1 to 32 characters, unique across all bays, and a `parallel_van_capacity` between 1 and 6 inclusive stating how many vans may occupy that bay at any one instant. That single integer is the aggregate bound that the dock-scheduling rule of §3.4 tests against.

Registering a **grid zone** captures a `zone_code` of 1 to 32 characters, unique across all zones, the `temperature_zone` class of everything the zone stores, and a `bot_count` between 1 and 1,200 giving the number of retrieval bots serving it. A newly registered zone is `OPERATIONAL`; both of its accuracy figures, `accuracy_score_unit_weighted` and `accuracy_score_sku_weighted`, are null until the zone has been audited at least once, as is `last_audited_at`. The `zone_code` is not merely cosmetic — it is a deterministic sort key in the retrieval sequencing of §3.5, so two zones' codes decide which of their SKUs is retrieved first.

A **pick station** is registered with a `station_code` of 1 to 32 characters, unique across all stations, the `temperature_zone_capability` set described in §2.10, and a `max_concurrent_totes` between 1 and 24 stating how many open totes may sit at the station at once. A station's `current_tote_count` is a derived figure: it is the count of totes whose `pick_station_id` names this station and whose status is `OPEN`. It starts at 0 and may never exceed the station's own `max_concurrent_totes`. The station's `status` is derived from that count with no free parameters — it is `IDLE` exactly when `current_tote_count` is zero, and `ACTIVE` otherwise.

The **catalogue** is where most of the modelling subtlety sits. A SKU registration carries a `sku_code` of 1 to 32 characters, unique across the catalogue and a secondary sort key in retrieval sequencing; a customer-facing `name` of 1 to 120 characters; a `grid_zone_id` naming the zone that physically stores the item, which must exist and whose `temperature_zone` must equal the SKU's own `temperature_zone` — a registration whose two classes differ is rejected as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, not with any of the three cold-chain segregation codes of §2.10; a `substitution_group_code` of 1 to 32 characters declaring which items are interchangeable with this one; a boolean `is_catchweight` that defaults to false; a `unit_price_cents` between 1 and 500,000 giving the price of one unit; and a `physical_units` count between 0 and 1,000,000 giving the units currently present in the grid. Three further fields are conditional and the condition is symmetric in both directions: `price_per_kg_cents`, `nominal_weight_grams` and `weight_tolerance_pct` are **required** when `is_catchweight` is true and **must be omitted** when it is false, and violating either half of that rule is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. When present, `price_per_kg_cents` is at least 1 and at most 500,000; `nominal_weight_grams`, the declared per-unit weight, is at least 1 and at most 100,000; and `weight_tolerance_pct`, the fractional deviation the operation will accept between declared and measured weight, is strictly greater than 0, at most 0.50, and carries at most four decimal places. Each of the three is null on a non-catchweight SKU.

Registration also initialises the SKU's ledger and audit fields. `allocated_units` starts at 0 and `on_hand_units` starts equal to `physical_units`, exactly as the ledger identity of §2.9 requires. The three audit-trail fields — `last_count_system_units`, `last_count_counted_units` and `last_count_variance_units` — are all null until the SKU is counted for the first time. The catalogue can be browsed as a filtered collection, narrowing by `grid_zone_id`, by `temperature_zone` or by `substitution_group_code`, which is also how a client discovers which SKUs live in a given zone.

Finally, a **shopper account** is registered with an `email` that must contain an at-sign and must be unique across all accounts, a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, and a `loyalty_tier` drawn from `BASIC`, `PLUS` and `PREMIER` which defaults to `BASIC` and which later selects the discount schedule of the charge waterfall. The account also names a `home_route_id`, which must reference an existing route: it is the *only* route the shopper may ever book delivery slots on, and it is the route a wave must serve for that shopper's orders to be slotted into it. Accounts can be listed filtered by `home_route_id` or by `loyalty_tier`.

### 3.2. Baskets, Line Pricing and the Charge Waterfall

A shopper opens a basket by supplying a single figure: `stop_distance_km`, the road distance in kilometres from the fulfillment centre to their delivery address, which must be at least 0.01, at most 99.99, and carry exactly two decimal places. Everything else about the new order is server-determined. Its `customer_id` is taken from the `X-User-ID` header of the acting `Customer` and is never read from the request body, so a shopper cannot open a basket on someone else's behalf. The order starts with `fulfillment_status` `DRAFT` and `billing_status` `PENDING_AUTH`; its `wave_id` is null because it has not been waved; its `merchandise_subtotal_cents` is 0 while `delivery_fee_cents`, `loyalty_discount_cents` and `order_total_cents` are all null; `authorized_amount_cents` and `captured_amount_cents` are null; `dispatch_blocked` is false; and `open_substitution_count` is 0. That distance figure will matter twice much later — once as the per-stop input to the wave's transport cost and once as the ordering key that decides which stop the van serves first.

Adding a line to a `DRAFT` basket takes a `sku_id` naming the catalogue item and an `ordered_qty` of at least 1 and at most 200. The new line records the `order_id` it belongs to and the `sku_id` originally ordered — and that `sku_id` is never rewritten, not even when a substitution is later approved, because the record of what the shopper actually asked for has to survive. The line begins with `line_status` `PENDING` and `substitution_resolution` `NOT_REQUIRED`; its `final_line_price_cents`, `picked_units` and `picked_weight_grams` are all null. For a catchweight item the line also snapshots the SKU's `nominal_weight_grams` onto itself at creation time, so that a later change to the catalogue cannot retroactively move the tolerance band the line was priced against; for a non-catchweight item the line's own `nominal_weight_grams` stays null. Lines are discoverable as a filtered collection, narrowed by `order_id`, by `sku_id` or by `line_status`.

**Line pricing has two variants of the same rule, and they are computed at different moments from different inputs.** The *provisional* variant runs at line creation and writes `provisional_line_price_cents`. For an item that is not sold by weight, the provisional price is the SKU's `unit_price_cents` multiplied by the line's `ordered_qty`; because both are integers, the product is already whole cents. For a catchweight item, the service first forms the nominal total weight by multiplying the SKU's `nominal_weight_grams` by the line's `ordered_qty`, then multiplies the SKU's `price_per_kg_cents` by that nominal total weight, divides the product by 1,000 — the number of grams in a kilogram — and rounds the result half up to whole cents. That single division is the only place rounding occurs in the provisional variant. The provisional figure is what enters the subtotal until a physical pick actually happens.

The *final* variant runs when the line's retrieval completes, or when a substitution on the line is approved, and it writes `final_line_price_cents`. For an item not sold by weight it is the SKU's `unit_price_cents` multiplied by the number of units actually retrieved. For a catchweight item it is the SKU's `price_per_kg_cents` multiplied by the measured total weight in grams, divided by 1,000, rounded half up to whole cents — the same shape as the provisional variant but reading the *measured* weight instead of the declared one. When the line's final price arises instead from an approved substitution, it is the substitute SKU's `unit_price_cents` multiplied by the line's `ordered_qty`. Because identical order input can yield a different final price than provisional price, this is exactly the mechanism that drives an order's money dimension from `AUTHORIZED` to `ADJUSTED`. A line whose `line_status` is `CANCELLED` is never re-priced by either variant.

**The order charge waterfall** produces the four money figures on the order, and its evaluation order is pinned because the dependencies look circular: the delivery fee's waiver depends on the merchandise subtotal, and the loyalty discount's tier depends on a figure that already contains the delivery fee. The four steps are evaluated strictly in the order 1, 2, 3, 4 and are never re-entered within a single request.

*Step 1 — the merchandise subtotal.* The service selects the order's lines whose `line_status` is `PENDING` or `PICKED`; lines in `SHORT` or `CANCELLED` are excluded entirely. For each selected line it takes that line's `final_line_price_cents` when that field is not null, and its `provisional_line_price_cents` otherwise. Those per-line integers are added exactly to give `merchandise_subtotal_cents`. Because each line was already rounded once when it was priced, the subtotal is a pure integer sum: the rounding order is round-per-line-then-sum, never sum-then-round.

*Step 2 — the delivery fee.* The service locates the order's slot booking whose status is `HELD` or `CONFIRMED`. If the order has no such booking, then `delivery_fee_cents`, `loyalty_discount_cents` and `order_total_cents` are all set to null and the waterfall stops there — which is why a basket that has never been booked shows a subtotal but no total. When such a booking exists, the fee is the `base_delivery_fee_cents` of the route serving the booked slot, multiplied by the booking's frozen `surge_multiplier_at_booking`, rounded half up to whole cents. Then the waiver applies: if `merchandise_subtotal_cents` is at least one hundred dollars — 10,000 cents — the fee is overwritten with 0. A subtotal of exactly 10,000 cents earns the free delivery; 9,999 does not.

*Step 3 — the loyalty discount.* The service forms the pre-discount total as `merchandise_subtotal_cents` plus `delivery_fee_cents`, and this is the figure the discount tier is keyed on. A `BASIC` shopper receives a discount rate of zero per cent regardless of the amount. A `PLUS` shopper receives five per cent when the pre-discount total is at least fifty dollars — 5,000 cents — and zero per cent below that. A `PREMIER` shopper receives ten per cent when the pre-discount total is at least 5,000 cents and five per cent below that; a `PREMIER` shopper is never without a discount. The rate is multiplied by the pre-discount total and the product is rounded half up to whole cents to give `loyalty_discount_cents`.

*Step 4 — the total.* `order_total_cents` is the pre-discount total less `loyalty_discount_cents`. Across the whole waterfall, rounding half up is applied exactly twice: once on the fee multiplication in step 2 and once on the discount multiplication in step 3. No other step rounds anything.

The waterfall is re-run, in full and in that order, on every event that can move its inputs: adding a line to a basket, creating a slot booking, confirming a booking, completing a retrieval task, approving a substitution, accepting a shortage, consolidating a wave, and cancelling an order. Whenever a re-run produces an `order_total_cents` that differs from the value already stored, and the order's `billing_status` is `AUTHORIZED`, the money dimension advances `AUTHORIZED → ADJUSTED` and all four money figures are rewritten — but `authorized_amount_cents` is deliberately left untouched, so the delta between what was authorised and what the order now costs stays observable. An order already in `ADJUSTED` that is adjusted again stays in `ADJUSTED`: the money dimension self-loops `ADJUSTED → ADJUSTED` as often as the picking floor requires, with the totals rewritten each time. When a re-run produces the same total that is already stored, the `billing_status` does not move at all.

The two snapshot fields close the money story. `authorized_amount_cents` is a copy of `order_total_cents` taken at the moment `billing_status` first becomes `AUTHORIZED`, and is null before that. `captured_amount_cents` is a copy of `order_total_cents` taken at the moment `billing_status` becomes `CAPTURED`, and is null before that. Orders can be browsed as a filtered collection, narrowed by `customer_id`, by `wave_id` or by `fulfillment_status`.

### 3.3. Delivery Slots: Capacity, Surge Pricing, Booking, Waitlists and Bumping

Opening a **delivery slot** is a `FulfillmentAdmin` action. It names the `route_id` the slot serves, a customer-facing window given by `window_start` — the inclusive start of the window and, incidentally, the reference instant for surge lead time — and `window_end`, its exclusive end, which must be strictly after `window_start`. Both instants are ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. The slot's `total_capacity_units` is its physical capacity in delivery units and must be at least 1 and at most 5,000, while `withheld_units` states how many of those units are held back from sale; it defaults to 0, may never be negative, and may never exceed `total_capacity_units`. A slot may therefore open with capacity already withheld. On creation, `held_units` and `confirmed_units` are 0, `available_units` is computed from the ledger identity of §2.9, and the slot's `surge_multiplier` is computed immediately. Slots can be listed filtered by `route_id`.

**Surge pricing** is a derived multiplier on the slot, recomputed on the events enumerated here and on no others: when the slot is created, when a booking is created on it, when a booking on it is confirmed, when capacity is released on it, when capacity is withheld on it, when an order cancellation releases one of that slot's own bookings — a booking in `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED` — and, across every slot in the service, when the logical clock is moved. Two further triggers act on every slot of one route rather than on a single slot: a delivery wave on that route starting to pick (§3.5) and that wave being dispatched (§3.7). Nothing else re-prices a slot, even when it moves one of the three inputs below: in particular, opening a wave, slotting an order into a wave, assigning a dock bay to a wave, consolidating a wave and sequencing a wave's load all leave every slot's stored `surge_multiplier` exactly as it stood, so a wave's contribution to the congestion premium enters the stored figures of its route's slots when that wave starts picking and leaves them when that wave is dispatched. The computation adds three independent premiums to a base of 1.00.

The first premium reflects how full the slot already is. The service forms an effective capacity as the greater of 1 and the slot's `total_capacity_units` less its `withheld_units` — the floor of 1 exists purely so the division is always defined. Utilisation is then the sum of `held_units` and `confirmed_units` divided by that effective capacity, evaluated as an exact ratio rather than as a rounded percentage. When utilisation is at least 0.85 the premium is 0.35; otherwise, when it is at least 0.60 the premium is 0.15; otherwise it is 0.00. Both boundaries are inclusive: utilisation of exactly 0.85 earns 0.35, and exactly 0.60 earns 0.15.

The second premium reflects how little notice the shopper is giving. The service takes the amount of time from the current instant to the slot's `window_start`, expresses it in seconds, divides by 3,600 and rounds *down* to a whole number of hours. When that whole-hour figure is fewer than 12 the premium is 0.20; otherwise, when it is fewer than 36 the premium is 0.10; otherwise it is 0.00. Both boundaries are strict: exactly 12 hours of lead time earns 0.10, and exactly 36 hours earns nothing.

The third premium reflects how busy the route already is. The service counts the delivery waves on the same `route_id` as the slot whose `status` is `FORMING` or `PICKING`, caps that count at 3, and multiplies the capped count by 0.05 — so the congestion premium is at most 0.15.

The three premiums are added to 1.00, the sum is rounded half up to two decimal places, and the rounded figure is then clamped into the closed interval from 1.00 to 1.80: anything below the floor becomes 1.00 and anything above the ceiling becomes 1.80. Rounding happens once, before the clamp.

**Booking a slot** is a `Customer` action naming the `delivery_slot_id`, the `customer_order_id` whose capacity is being reserved, the number of `units` the booking occupies — at least 1 and at most 500 — and the `expected_order_version` guard of §2.7. A shopper may hold at most one live booking per order, so an order that already has a booking in `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SLOT_BOOKING`. The slot's window must still be open, meaning the current instant is strictly before `window_start`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SLOT_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The slot's route must be the shopper's home route, or the request is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_MISMATCH`. The basket must be worth at least ten dollars — 1,000 cents of `merchandise_subtotal_cents` — or the request is refused with `ERR_MINIMUM_ORDER_NOT_MET`. And the requested `units` may not exceed the slot's `total_capacity_units` less its `withheld_units`, because such a booking could never be satisfied even from a completely empty slot; that refusal is `ERR_SLOT_UNITS_EXCEED_CAPACITY`.

A booking that survives all of those checks is admitted, but it is admitted onto **one of two branches**, and the branch is chosen purely by comparing the slot's current `available_units` against the requested `units`. If `available_units` is greater than or equal to `units`, the booking is created in status `HELD` and it occupies that capacity: the slot's `held_units`, `confirmed_units` and `available_units` are recomputed by the ledger, the slot's `surge_multiplier` is recomputed, the charge waterfall runs on the order so that `delivery_fee_cents`, `loyalty_discount_cents` and `order_total_cents` become non-null, and the order's `version` is incremented by 1. If `available_units` is less than `units`, the booking is instead created in status `WAITLISTED`; it occupies no capacity at all, so `available_units` is unchanged, the order stays at `fulfillment_status` `DRAFT`, and the charge waterfall still runs — the waitlisted booking counts as the order's booking for the purposes of step 2, so the totals still become non-null. The order's `version` is likewise incremented by 1. Both branches capture the price context identically: `surge_multiplier_at_booking` is set to the slot's `surge_multiplier` as it stands at that instant and is frozen thereafter, and `quoted_fee_cents` is the route's `base_delivery_fee_cents` multiplied by that frozen multiplier, rounded half up to whole cents. A booking's `status` is one of `HELD`, `CONFIRMED`, `WAITLISTED`, `RELEASED` and `BUMPED`, of which `RELEASED` and `BUMPED` are terminal, and bookings can be listed filtered by `delivery_slot_id`, by `customer_order_id` or by `status`.

**Confirming a booking** is what actually slots the order. The shopper's own booking must be in status `HELD`, the current instant must still be strictly before the slot's `window_start`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `customer_id` of the booking's order. The booking then advances `HELD → CONFIRMED`, which moves its `units` out of `held_units` and into `confirmed_units` with `available_units` recomputed, and the slot's `surge_multiplier` is recomputed. In the same request the order advances on both dimensions at once: `fulfillment_status` goes `DRAFT → SLOTTED` and `billing_status` goes `PENDING_AUTH → AUTHORIZED`, with `authorized_amount_cents` snapshotted from the order's `order_total_cents`, and the money dimension only advances if the waterfall has produced a non-null total. Confirmation also requires the basket still to be worth at least 1,000 cents. The order's `version` increments by 1.

**Releasing capacity** puts held-back units back on sale. A `FulfillmentAdmin` supplies a number of `units`, which is subtracted from the slot's `withheld_units`; a request whose `units` exceeds the slot's current `withheld_units` is refused with `ERR_WITHHELD_UNITS_EXCEEDED`. The ledger is then recomputed, which raises `available_units`, and because capacity has just appeared, the waitlist is examined. The **waitlist promotion selector** considers the slot's bookings in status `WAITLISTED` whose `units` is less than or equal to the post-recompute `available_units`. It orders those candidates by `created_at` ascending — strict first-come order — and breaks ties first by `units` descending, then by `quoted_fee_cents` descending; the booking's `id` is never a sort key. The single winner is promoted to `CONFIRMED`, and then the selector stops: at most one promotion happens per request, the ledger is recomputed once more, and no second promotion is attempted even if further capacity remains. Promotion cascades into the promoted booking's own order, which moves `fulfillment_status` `DRAFT → SLOTTED` and `billing_status` `PENDING_AUTH → AUTHORIZED` with `authorized_amount_cents` set from its `order_total_cents`. If no waitlisted booking is small enough to fit, no promotion occurs and `available_units` simply stays raised. Either way the slot's `surge_multiplier` is recomputed at the end.

**Withholding capacity** is the reverse lever, and it is deliberately allowed to overcommit. A `FulfillmentAdmin` supplies a number of `units` which is added to the slot's `withheld_units`; the request is refused with `ERR_WITHHELD_UNITS_EXCEEDED` only when those `units` exceed `total_capacity_units` less the current `withheld_units`. Withholding more than the currently available units is legal and drives `available_units` below zero, and the service then repairs the ledger by **bumping** confirmed bookings. The bump selector considers the slot's bookings in status `CONFIRMED`, orders them by `created_at` descending — most recently made first — and breaks ties by `units` ascending, and it bumps them one at a time, recomputing after each, until `available_units` is back at zero or above. A booking whose order has already been waved is never bumped: a waved order is past the point of no return. Each bumped booking advances `CONFIRMED → BUMPED`, and its order falls back on both dimensions: `fulfillment_status` goes `SLOTTED → DRAFT`, `billing_status` goes `AUTHORIZED → PENDING_AUTH`, `authorized_amount_cents` reverts to null, and because the order no longer holds a booking in `HELD` or `CONFIRMED`, the waterfall's step 2 now stops early, so `delivery_fee_cents`, `loyalty_discount_cents` and `order_total_cents` all revert to null as well. Each bumped booking's order has its `version` incremented by 1. When every bump is done the ledger is recomputed and the slot's `surge_multiplier` is recomputed. Zero bookings may be bumped, if the withholding fits inside what was available.

### 3.4. Wave Formation, Order Slotting and Dock Scheduling

A **delivery wave** is the unit of work that one van performs for one delivery slot. A `WavePlanner` opens a wave by naming the `route_id` the van will drive and the `delivery_slot_id` whose orders the wave fulfils; the slot must exist and its own `route_id` must equal the wave's `route_id`, and at most one wave may ever exist for a given `delivery_slot_id`. The wave also carries a `wave_code` of 1 to 32 characters that is unique across all waves, and a `cutoff_at` instant after which no further order may be slotted into it — an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits that must be strictly earlier than the slot's `window_start`. A newly opened wave has `status` `FORMING` and `posture` `OPEN`, an `order_count` and a `tote_count` of 0, an empty `load_sequence` array, a null `transport_cost_cents`, a null `consolidated_by_user_id`, and a null `dock_bay_id` with null `load_start` and `load_end`. Its `status` progresses through `FORMING`, `PICKING`, `CONSOLIDATED`, `LOADING` and finally the terminal `DISPATCHED`. Waves are discoverable as a filtered collection, narrowed by `route_id`, by `delivery_slot_id`, by `dock_bay_id` or by `status`.

Two of the wave's counters are pure aggregates. `order_count` is the number of customer orders whose `wave_id` names this wave and whose `fulfillment_status` is anything other than `CANCELLED`. `tote_count` is the number of totes whose `wave_id` names this wave and whose status is `OPEN`, `SEALED`, `CONSOLIDATED` or `LOADED`; it may never exceed the `van_capacity_totes` of the wave's route, since the van physically cannot carry more.

**Order slotting** attaches one already-slotted order to a wave that is still `FORMING`, and the wave does not change status when it happens — this is a self-loop on `FORMING`. The `WavePlanner` supplies the `customer_order_id`. The current instant must be strictly earlier than the wave's `cutoff_at`. The order must be at `fulfillment_status` `SLOTTED`, and it must hold a slot booking in status `CONFIRMED` whose `delivery_slot_id` equals the wave's `delivery_slot_id`; if it does not, the request is refused with `ERR_WAVE_SLOT_MISMATCH`. The order's owning account must have a `home_route_id` equal to the wave's `route_id`; if it does not, the request is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_MISMATCH`. On success the order's `wave_id` is set to the wave, the order advances `SLOTTED → WAVED`, the wave's `order_count` is recomputed, the wave's `posture` is recomputed, and the order's `version` is incremented by 1. A wave with no member order at all cannot be started; that refusal is `ERR_WAVE_HAS_NO_ORDERS`.

**Dock scheduling** reserves a bay for the wave's van, and it too is a self-loop on `FORMING`. The `WavePlanner` supplies the `dock_bay_id` together with `load_start` and `load_end`, which define a half-open occupation interval — the start instant is inside the interval and the end instant is not. The scheduling rule is an aggregate-capacity test rather than a simple exclusivity test: at every instant of the requested interval, the number of *other* waves already assigned to the same bay whose own occupation intervals cover that instant must be strictly less than the bay's `parallel_van_capacity`. All waves assigned to that bay count towards the total regardless of their `status`, because a wave is never cancelled and so never gives its bay slot back. Two intervals that merely touch — where the existing wave's `load_end` equals the new wave's `load_start`, or vice versa — do **not** overlap and never contribute to the count. A request that would breach the bay's parallel capacity at any instant is refused with `ERR_DOCK_BAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. On success the wave's `dock_bay_id`, `load_start` and `load_end` are written and the wave's `status` remains `FORMING`. Dock assignment is one of the operations gated by posture: a wave whose posture is `FROZEN` cannot be given a bay.

### 3.5. Robotic Grid Retrieval: Sequencing, Station Assignment, Totes and Picking

Starting the picking phase is the single most consequential request in the system, because it converts a paper wave into physical work and reserves stock. A `WavePlanner` starts a wave that is `FORMING`, has at least one member order, and whose posture is not `FROZEN`. Before anything is created, three feasibility checks run over every order line of every `WAVED` member order whose `line_status` is `PENDING`: the line's SKU must have an `on_hand_units` of at least the line's `ordered_qty`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_INVENTORY`; the grid zone holding that SKU must be `OPERATIONAL`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ZONE_NOT_OPERATIONAL`; and the station-selection pool for that line must be non-empty, or the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PICK_STATION`. The inventory test is taken across all the lines of the wave together, so two lines competing for the same scarce SKU cannot both be admitted.

On success the wave advances `FORMING → PICKING`, every `WAVED` member order advances `WAVED → PICKING`, and one **retrieval task** is created in status `QUEUED` for each of those `PENDING` lines. Each new task records the `order_line_id` it serves, a `sku_id` copied from the line's own `sku_id`, a `grid_zone_id` copied from that SKU's `grid_zone_id`, a `pick_station_id` chosen by the station selector below, a `sequence_index` from the retrieval sequence below, and an `allocated_units` equal to the line's `ordered_qty`. Its `tote_id` is null because no tote has been bound yet, and its `picked_units` and `actual_weight_grams` are null because nothing has been retrieved. A task's `status` is one of `QUEUED`, `RETRIEVING`, `COMPLETED`, `SHORT` and `CANCELLED`, of which the last three are terminal. Every affected SKU's `allocated_units` and `on_hand_units` are recomputed by the unit ledger, every delivery slot on the wave's route has its `surge_multiplier` recomputed because the wave's `status` has just changed, and each member order's `version` is incremented by 1. Tasks are discoverable only as a filtered collection — they have no creation response of their own — narrowed by `order_line_id`, by `sku_id`, by `grid_zone_id`, by `pick_station_id`, by `tote_id` or by `status`.

**The retrieval sequence** imposes a deterministic total order on the lines being turned into tasks, and frozen goods go first so they spend the least time out of the freezer. The population is every order line with `line_status` `PENDING` belonging to a member order of the wave whose `fulfillment_status` is `WAVED`. Those lines are sorted by four keys in strict priority order: first by the temperature class of the line's SKU, ranking `FROZEN` ahead of `CHILLED` ahead of `AMBIENT`; then, among lines of the same class, by the `zone_code` of the grid zone holding the SKU, in ascending lexicographic order; then by the SKU's `sku_code`, in ascending lexicographic order; and finally, as the last tie-break, by the line's own `created_at` in ascending order. The line's `id` is never used as a sort key. Each task's `sequence_index` is its one-based position in that ordering, so the first task retrieved carries index 1.

**The station selector** then places each task at a pick station, and it is evaluated for the tasks in the retrieval-sequence order just described, which matters because each assignment changes what the next assignment sees. The eligible pool is every pick station whose `status` is `IDLE` or `ACTIVE`, whose `temperature_zone_capability` contains the temperature class of the SKU being retrieved, and whose `current_tote_count` is strictly less than its own `max_concurrent_totes` — evaluated against the station's live count at the moment this particular task is assigned, so a station filled by an earlier task in the same request is correctly excluded from a later one. Eligible stations are ranked by `current_tote_count` ascending, so the emptiest station wins; ties are broken by `max_concurrent_totes` descending, so the roomiest station wins; then by `station_code` ascending; and finally, when all three rank keys tie, by `created_at` ascending. The station's `id` is never a rank key. The highest-ranked station's identifier becomes the task's `pick_station_id`. If the pool is empty for any task being created, the whole request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PICK_STATION` and nothing is created. The same selector runs again, on its own, whenever an approved substitution creates a fresh retrieval task.

**Opening a tote** is a `PickOperator` action. It names the `wave_id` the tote is being filled for, the `pick_station_id` where it will be filled, a `tote_code` of 1 to 32 characters that is unique across all totes, and the single `temperature_zone` class the tote is segregated for. The named station must list that class in its `temperature_zone_capability`, or the request is refused with `ERR_STATION_ZONE_UNSUPPORTED`, and the station must have room — opening a tote that would push its `current_tote_count` above its `max_concurrent_totes` is refused with `ERR_STATION_TOTE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Opening a tote is also blocked while the wave's posture is `FROZEN`. The new tote starts with `status` `OPEN` and an `assigned_line_count` of 0; that counter is the number of retrieval tasks whose `tote_id` names this tote and whose status is `QUEUED`, `RETRIEVING`, `COMPLETED` or `SHORT`. Opening it recomputes the station's `current_tote_count` and flips the station's `status` from `IDLE` to `ACTIVE`, and it recomputes the wave's `tote_count`. A tote's `status` is one of `OPEN`, `SEALED`, `CONSOLIDATED` and `LOADED`, with `LOADED` terminal, and totes can be listed filtered by `wave_id`, by `pick_station_id` or by `status`.

**Binding a task to a tote** is a self-loop on `QUEUED`: the task stays `QUEUED`, which is exactly what makes re-binding a different tote before retrieval begins legitimate. The `PickOperator` supplies the `tote_id`. The task must be `QUEUED` and must not already hold a tote, since binding a task that already has one is refused with `ERR_TOTE_NOT_ASSIGNED`. The named tote must be `OPEN` and must belong to the same wave as the task. And cold-chain segregation applies: the tote's `temperature_zone` must equal the `temperature_zone` of the SKU actually being retrieved, or the request is refused with `ERR_TEMPERATURE_ZONE_MISMATCH`. On success the task's `tote_id` is set and the tote's `assigned_line_count` is recomputed.

**Sending the bots into the grid** advances the task `QUEUED → RETRIEVING`. It requires that the task already has a bound tote — a task whose `tote_id` is still null is refused with `ERR_TOTE_NOT_ASSIGNED` — that the grid zone named by the task's `grid_zone_id` is `OPERATIONAL`, which is a cross-entity dependency on that zone's own lifecycle and is refused with `ERR_ZONE_NOT_OPERATIONAL` otherwise, and that the task's wave is still `PICKING`. The task remains inside the SKU's allocated set throughout retrieval, so no ledger figure moves.

**Sealing a tote** advances it `OPEN → SEALED`. Every retrieval task bound to the tote must already be in a terminal status — `COMPLETED`, `SHORT` or `CANCELLED` — and the tote must have at least one task bound to it at all, since sealing an empty tote is refused with `ERR_TOTE_NOT_ASSIGNED`. The tote's wave must still be `PICKING` and its posture must not be `FROZEN`, which is refused with `ERR_WAVE_FROZEN`. Sealing releases the tote's slot at the station: the station's `current_tote_count` is decremented and its `status` is recomputed, becoming `IDLE` when the count reaches zero.

**Unsealing a tote** advances it `SEALED → OPEN` and closes the cycle. It exists for one purpose: to drop a late substitute item into a tote that had already been closed. The tote's wave must still be `PICKING` and its posture must not be `FROZEN`, again with `ERR_WAVE_FROZEN`; and the station must have room, since a station whose `current_tote_count` has reached its `max_concurrent_totes` refuses the reopening with `ERR_STATION_TOTE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Unsealing increments the station's `current_tote_count` and recomputes the station's `status`, moving it from `IDLE` back to `ACTIVE`.

**Completing a retrieval** is where weighed goods are reconciled. The `PickOperator` reports `picked_units`, the number of units actually retrieved, which must be at least 0 and at most the task's `allocated_units`; and for a catchweight SKU also `actual_weight_grams`, the measured total weight in grams, which is required for a catchweight SKU and must be omitted for any other. The task must be `RETRIEVING`. For a catchweight SKU the **tolerance gate runs first, before any price is written**: the service forms the nominal total weight by multiplying the line's snapshotted `nominal_weight_grams` by the line's `ordered_qty`, takes the absolute difference between the reported `actual_weight_grams` and that nominal total, and divides that difference by the nominal total to get a deviation ratio evaluated as an exact rational. If the ratio is strictly greater than the SKU's `weight_tolerance_pct`, the request is refused with `ERR_WEIGHT_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE`; a ratio exactly equal to the tolerance is accepted.

Once the gate is passed, the outcome branches on quantity. When `picked_units` equals the task's `allocated_units`, the task advances `RETRIEVING → COMPLETED` and the line advances `PENDING → PICKED`: the line's `picked_units` is copied from the task's `picked_units`, the line's `picked_weight_grams` is copied from the task's `actual_weight_grams`, and the line's `final_line_price_cents` is written by the final pricing variant of §3.2. The SKU's `physical_units` is reduced by the units picked and the task leaves the allocated set, which is precisely why the SKU's `on_hand_units` is unchanged across a full-quantity completion. The tote's `assigned_line_count` is recomputed, the charge waterfall is re-run on the parent order, the money dimension advances `AUTHORIZED → ADJUSTED` when the total changed, and the order's `version` is incremented by 1. When `picked_units` is strictly less than `allocated_units` — including a pick of zero — the task advances `RETRIEVING → SHORT` instead, and the four-hop cascade of §3.6 fires.

**Cancelling a retrieval** is the exact compensating action for the allocation made when picking started, and it is available from both `QUEUED` and from `RETRIEVING`, in each case only while the task's wave is still `PICKING`. From `QUEUED` the task advances `QUEUED → CANCELLED`: it leaves the SKU's allocated set, so the unit ledger restores both `allocated_units` and `on_hand_units` to exactly their pre-allocation values with zero drift, and the tote's `assigned_line_count` is recomputed. From `RETRIEVING` the task advances `RETRIEVING → CANCELLED` with the same ledger effect, and the SKU's `physical_units` is explicitly left unchanged, because nothing was ever removed from the grid.

### 3.6. Shortages, Substitution Rounds and Customer Approval

A short pick is the event that ties the whole system together, and it propagates four hops inside the request that reports it. Consider a retrieval that was allocated six units and comes back with four.

*Hop one.* Because the task ended `SHORT`, the order line advances `PENDING → SHORT` and its `substitution_resolution` moves `NOT_REQUIRED → PENDING`. The line's `final_line_price_cents` stays null — a short line is not priced. The SKU's `physical_units` is reduced by the units that *were* picked, and the task leaves the allocated set.

*Hop two.* Because the line's `substitution_resolution` has become `PENDING`, the substitute selector runs automatically, and when it finds a candidate a substitution proposal is created in status `DRAFT` with `round_no` 1. The **substitute selector** starts from the SKU originally ordered on the line and considers every other catalogue SKU that is not that SKU itself, shares its `temperature_zone`, carries the same `substitution_group_code`, is stored in a grid zone whose status is `OPERATIONAL`, and has an `on_hand_units` of at least the line's `ordered_qty`. Candidates are ranked by how close their price is to the ordered item: first by the absolute difference between the candidate's `unit_price_cents` and the ordered SKU's `unit_price_cents`, ascending; then, among equally distant candidates, by the candidate's own `unit_price_cents` ascending, so the cheaper of two equidistant items wins; then by `sku_code` ascending; and finally, when all three rank keys tie, by `created_at` ascending. The candidate's `id` is never a rank key. If no candidate qualifies at all, **no proposal is created**: the line keeps `substitution_resolution` `PENDING`, so the order stays dispatch-blocked, and the shopper's only way forward is to accept the shortage.

*Hop three.* Because a `DRAFT` proposal now exists on a line of this order, the order's `open_substitution_count` and `dispatch_blocked` are recomputed. The count is the number of substitution proposals whose `order_line_id` belongs to any line of this order and whose status is `DRAFT` or `PROPOSED`. The flag becomes true, because a line is now awaiting a substitution decision. In the same recomputation the charge waterfall re-runs and drops the `SHORT` line out of `merchandise_subtotal_cents` entirely, and the money dimension advances `AUTHORIZED → ADJUSTED` because the total has changed.

*Hop four.* Because a member order has become dispatch-blocked, the wave's posture is recomputed and moves `OPEN → RESTRICTED`, which is what will later stop the loading sequence.

A proposal records the `order_line_id` of the short line, the `substitute_sku_id` offered in its place, and an optional `supersedes_proposal_id` naming the earlier proposal on the same line that this one replaces — null for the first proposal on a line. Its `round_no` is 1 for the first proposal on a line and one more than the highest `round_no` already present on that line thereafter. Its `proposed_price_delta_cents` is the substitute SKU's `unit_price_cents` multiplied by the line's `ordered_qty`, less the line's `provisional_line_price_cents`; it is an exact integer difference and may well be negative when the substitute is cheaper. Its `status` runs through `DRAFT`, `PROPOSED`, and then one of the three terminal values `APPROVED`, `DECLINED` and `EXPIRED`; `DRAFT` and `PROPOSED` are precisely the statuses that keep the line's pending resolution visible to the wave posture. Its `expires_at` is null while the proposal is still `DRAFT`. Proposals are discoverable as a filtered collection — the automatically created `DRAFT` ones appear in no creation response, so this is their sole discovery channel — narrowed by `order_line_id`, by `substitute_sku_id`, by `supersedes_proposal_id` or by `status`.

**Offering a substitute by hand** is a `PickOperator` action, used after an earlier round was declined or expired. It names the `order_line_id`, whose line must currently have `substitution_resolution` `PENDING`; the `substitute_sku_id`, which must be a different SKU sharing the ordered item's `temperature_zone` and `substitution_group_code`, refused with `ERR_SUBSTITUTE_NOT_COMPARABLE` otherwise; the `expected_order_line_version` guard of §2.7; and optionally `supersedes_proposal_id`, which when supplied must name a proposal on the *same* line whose status is `DECLINED` or `EXPIRED`, refused with `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_INVALID` otherwise. A line that already has a proposal in `DRAFT` or `PROPOSED` refuses a new one with `ERR_PROPOSAL_ALREADY_OPEN`. The new proposal is created in `DRAFT` with its `round_no` and `proposed_price_delta_cents` computed as described above and its `expires_at` still null; the order's `open_substitution_count` is recomputed, and the parent line's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Sending a proposal to the shopper** advances it `DRAFT → PROPOSED` and starts its approval clock. The proposal must be `DRAFT`, its line's `substitution_resolution` must be `PENDING`, and the substitute SKU must have an `on_hand_units` of at least the line's `ordered_qty` or the request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_INVENTORY`. On success `expires_at` is set to the current instant plus two hours — that is, plus 120 minutes.

**Approval by the shopper** is the happy path and it advances the proposal `PROPOSED → APPROVED`. The current instant must be strictly before the proposal's `expires_at`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROPOSAL_EXPIRED`; the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `customer_id` of the line's order, or the request is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_OWNED`; the line's wave must still be `PICKING`; the substitute SKU's grid zone must be `OPERATIONAL`, refused with `ERR_ZONE_NOT_OPERATIONAL` otherwise; the substitute must still have enough uncommitted stock, refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_INVENTORY` otherwise; and the station selector must yield a non-empty pool, refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PICK_STATION` otherwise. On success the line advances `SHORT → PENDING`, closing the line's cycle back onto its initial status, with `substitution_resolution` moving `PENDING → SUBSTITUTED` and `picked_units` reset to null because the earlier short pick no longer describes the line's outcome. The line's `final_line_price_cents` is rewritten as the substitute SKU's `unit_price_cents` multiplied by the line's `ordered_qty`. A brand-new retrieval task is created in `QUEUED` with its `sku_id` set to the substitute SKU, its `grid_zone_id` copied from that substitute's zone, its `pick_station_id` from the station selector, its `sequence_index` from the retrieval sequence, and its `allocated_units` equal to the line's `ordered_qty`; the substitute SKU's `allocated_units` and `on_hand_units` are recomputed by the unit ledger. Finally the order's `open_substitution_count` and `dispatch_blocked` are recomputed — the flag clears to false when no other line of the order is awaiting a decision — the charge waterfall re-runs with the money dimension advancing `AUTHORIZED → ADJUSTED` when the total changed, the wave's posture is recomputed, and the order's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Refusal by the shopper** advances the proposal `PROPOSED → DECLINED`, and its consequences diverge sharply from approval, which is exactly why it is spelled out at the same length. The current instant must be strictly before `expires_at`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROPOSAL_EXPIRED`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `customer_id` of the line's order. On success the line's `substitution_resolution` **stays** `PENDING`. The line therefore remains short, the order's `dispatch_blocked` stays true, and the wave's posture stays `RESTRICTED`. Only the order's `open_substitution_count` moves: it is decremented, because a `DECLINED` proposal is no longer open. The line is now a candidate for a fresh proposal in a higher round, which is what `supersedes_proposal_id` is for.

**Retiring an unanswered proposal** is a `WavePlanner` action and advances it `PROPOSED → EXPIRED`. It requires the current instant to be at or after the proposal's `expires_at`; calling it too early is refused with `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_EXPIRED`. Like a refusal, it leaves the line's `substitution_resolution` at `PENDING` and merely decrements the order's `open_substitution_count`, so the order stays dispatch-blocked.

**Accepting the shortage** is the shopper's escape hatch, and it is the only path that closes a shortage without a substitute. The line must have `substitution_resolution` `PENDING`, or the request is refused with `ERR_LINE_NOT_SHORT`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `customer_id` of the line's order. On success the line advances `SHORT → CANCELLED` with `substitution_resolution` moving `PENDING → SHORTAGE_ACCEPTED`. Every substitution proposal on that line whose status is `DRAFT` or `PROPOSED` is moved to `EXPIRED` — both the `DRAFT → EXPIRED` and the `PROPOSED → EXPIRED` edges fire here, and each one decrements the order's `open_substitution_count`. The charge waterfall re-runs and the now-cancelled line drops out of `merchandise_subtotal_cents`, with the money dimension advancing `AUTHORIZED → ADJUSTED` when the total changed. The order's `dispatch_blocked` is recomputed and clears once no line of the order is awaiting a decision, the wave's posture is recomputed — typically `RESTRICTED → OPEN` — and the order's `version` is incremented by 1.

### 3.7. Consolidation, the Van Loading Sequence, Transport Cost and Dispatch

**Consolidation** closes the picking phase. A `WavePlanner` consolidates a wave whose `status` is `PICKING` and whose `posture` is not `FROZEN` — a frozen wave is refused with `ERR_WAVE_FROZEN`. Every retrieval task belonging to the wave must already be in a terminal status, meaning `COMPLETED`, `SHORT` or `CANCELLED`; and at least one tote of the wave must be in status `SEALED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_NO_TOTES_TO_CONSOLIDATE`. Note what consolidation does *not* require: a `RESTRICTED` posture is perfectly acceptable here, because an unresolved shortage does not stop the physical work of closing the picking floor.

On success every tote of the wave whose status is `SEALED` advances `SEALED → CONSOLIDATED` and the wave's `tote_count` is recomputed; the wave advances `PICKING → CONSOLIDATED`; the acting operator's `X-User-ID` is recorded on the wave as `consolidated_by_user_id`, which is the segregation-of-duties anchor for the dispatch step; every member order advances `PICKING → CONSOLIDATED`; and the charge waterfall is re-run for each member order, with any order whose `billing_status` is `AUTHORIZED` or `ADJUSTED` and whose recomputed `order_total_cents` differs from the stored value moving to `ADJUSTED`. Every member order's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Sequencing the load** is the last computational step before the van leaves, and it is where the three posture values become sharply distinguishable. The wave must be `CONSOLIDATED`; it must have a `dock_bay_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DOCK_NOT_ASSIGNED`; and its `posture` must be exactly `OPEN`. A `FROZEN` posture is refused with `ERR_WAVE_FROZEN`, while a `RESTRICTED` posture — meaning some member order still has a line awaiting a substitution decision — is refused with `ERR_ORDER_DISPATCH_BLOCKED`. The two refusals are different codes for different reasons and must not be conflated.

The **stop order** is computed first over the wave's member orders whose `fulfillment_status` is `CONSOLIDATED` — the moment matters, because this runs after consolidation moved them into that status and before the loading step moves them out of it. Those orders are sorted by `stop_distance_km` ascending, so the nearest stop is served first, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending; the order's `id` is never a sort key. Each order's stop position is its one-based index in that ordering.

The **tote load order** is then computed as a last-in-first-out arrangement against that stop order, with the cold chain respected inside each stop. The loadable population is the wave's totes whose status is `CONSOLIDATED`. Each tote is attributed a stop position: it is the stop position of the customer order that owns any retrieval task bound to that tote, and when a tote holds tasks belonging to several different orders it takes the **largest** of their stop positions, while a tote holding no task at all takes the position 0. Totes are then sorted by attributed stop position **descending**, so the tote for the last stop is loaded first and comes out last; then by temperature class ascending in the ranking `FROZEN` before `CHILLED` before `AMBIENT`, so frozen totes go into the freezer compartment; then by `tote_code` ascending; and finally by `created_at` ascending. The tote's `id` is never a sort key — it is the *payload*: the wave's `load_sequence` is the list of tote identifiers in exactly that computed order, each appearing exactly once.

The wave's **transport cost** is computed in the same request. The stops are again the member orders whose `fulfillment_status` is `CONSOLIDATED`; cancelled orders are excluded. For each stop, the route's `per_km_cost_cents` is multiplied by that order's `stop_distance_km` and the product is rounded half up to whole cents. Those per-stop integers are then added exactly to give `transport_cost_cents`. The rounding order here is mandatory and is the round-per-item-then-sum rule of §2.6: summing the exact kilometre products and rounding once at the end is explicitly not the rule, and produces a different total whenever two or more stops land on a half cent. Because integer addition is associative, the order in which the stops are visited does not affect the total — but the per-stop rounding does.

Alongside those two outputs, the wave advances `CONSOLIDATED → LOADING`, every tote of the wave in status `CONSOLIDATED` advances `CONSOLIDATED → LOADED`, every member order advances `CONSOLIDATED → LOADED`, and every member order's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Dispatch** releases the van and captures the money. The wave must be in `status` `LOADING`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the wave's recorded `consolidated_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. No member order may be dispatch-blocked, or the request is refused with `ERR_ORDER_DISPATCH_BLOCKED`, and the wave's posture must not be `FROZEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_WAVE_FROZEN`. On success the wave advances `LOADING → DISPATCHED`, which is terminal. Every member order whose `fulfillment_status` is `LOADED` advances `LOADED → DISPATCHED`, and on the money dimension every such order moves to `CAPTURED` — from `AUTHORIZED → CAPTURED` if it was never adjusted and from `ADJUSTED → CAPTURED` if it was — with `captured_amount_cents` snapshotted from that order's `order_total_cents`. Because the wave's `status` has changed, every delivery slot on the wave's route has its `surge_multiplier` recomputed. Every member order's `version` is incremented by 1.

### 3.8. Grid Maintenance Windows and Inventory-Accuracy Audits

The `GridEngineer` owns the two levers that can take a grid zone out of service: the maintenance window and the audit count. Neither lever touches an order, a booking or a wave directly; both act on a `GridZone`, and the effect reaches the commercial side only through the derived posture of §2.11.

**Scheduling a window** names an existing grid zone, an inclusive `window_start` and an exclusive `window_end`, each an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits, with `window_start` strictly before `window_end`. An unknown zone is refused with `ERR_ZONE_NOT_FOUND`. The interesting rule is the **buffered non-overlap** rule: engineers need turnaround time between jobs on the same zone, so a proposed window is refused with `ERR_MAINTENANCE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` and HTTP 409 when it collides with any existing window on the *same* zone whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE`, after both intervals have been widened by `MAINTENANCE_BUFFER_MINUTES` — 15 minutes — on each side. Concretely, the proposal is rejected exactly when the new window's start is earlier than the existing window's end plus fifteen minutes **and** the existing window's start is earlier than the new window's end plus fifteen minutes; both halves of that conjunction must hold for a rejection. Both comparisons are strict, so a new window starting exactly fifteen minutes after an existing window ends is accepted, and one starting fourteen minutes and fifty-nine seconds after is rejected. Windows already in status `COMPLETED` are ignored by this rule, as are windows on any other zone. A window is created in status `SCHEDULED`, and the zone remains `OPERATIONAL` — scheduling alone never takes the grid down.

Windows are readable one at a time, and the collection listing accepts an optional grid-zone filter and an optional status filter, which is the reverse-reachability channel from a zone to the windows scheduled against it.

**Activating a window** is what actually stops the bots. The window must be `SCHEDULED`; the logical clock must lie inside the declared interval, meaning `window_start` is at or before `clock_now` and `clock_now` is strictly before `window_end` — a window activated too early or after its end has passed is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; and the zone must currently be `OPERATIONAL`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ZONE_NOT_OPERATIONAL`. That last precondition means a zone already sitting in `AUDIT_HOLD` cannot be put into maintenance, and two windows on the same zone can never both be active. On success the window advances `SCHEDULED → ACTIVE` and the zone advances `OPERATIONAL → MAINTENANCE`. The posture of every delivery wave that has an active retrieval task in that zone is then recomputed, and any such wave becomes `FROZEN`, which is how a maintenance decision taken on the engineering side halts loading and dispatch on the commercial side. While the zone is in `MAINTENANCE` no retrieval may be started into it — the attempt is refused with `ERR_ZONE_NOT_OPERATIONAL`.

**Completing a window** requires only that the window be `ACTIVE`. The window advances `ACTIVE → COMPLETED`, which is terminal, and the zone advances `MAINTENANCE → OPERATIONAL`, closing the first of the zone's two cycles: a zone travels `OPERATIONAL → MAINTENANCE → OPERATIONAL` and is then free to be taken down again by a later window. Posture is recomputed for every affected wave, and a `FROZEN` wave clears — to `RESTRICTED` if some member order still has an unresolved shortage, otherwise to `OPEN`.

**Recording an audit** submits a `counts` array of `{ sku_id, counted_units }` objects against one zone. Every named SKU must belong to the audited zone; an unknown SKU identifier is refused with `ERR_SKU_NOT_FOUND`. Recording proceeds in a fixed order. For each SKU named in the payload, the SKU's `last_count_system_units` is set to that SKU's `physical_units` **as it stood immediately before this call**, `last_count_counted_units` is set to the submitted `counted_units`, and `last_count_variance_units` is set to the counted figure minus the system figure — so a physical surplus is positive and a shrink is negative. Only after those three fields are written is the SKU's `physical_units` overwritten with the counted figure, and its `allocated_units` and `on_hand_units` are recomputed by the SKU unit ledger of §2.9. Because an audit rewrites `physical_units` while leaving the allocated set untouched, the conserved identity still holds afterwards, with the surplus or shrink absorbed entirely by `on_hand_units`.

The **scored population** is then fixed, and it is deliberately wider than the payload. It is every SKU of the audited zone whose `last_count_system_units` is not null and is strictly greater than zero. A SKU counted in an *earlier* audit and absent from today's payload therefore still contributes, carrying its stale variance and stale system figure; a SKU that has never been counted contributes nothing, because its last-count fields are null; and a SKU whose pre-call `physical_units` was zero contributes nothing, which is what keeps the unit-weighted denominator away from zero. If that population is empty, no score can be formed and neither score changes.

Two scores are then computed over that same population, and they are computed differently on purpose.

The **unit-weighted score** pools first and divides once: the absolute values of every scored SKU's variance are added into a single total variance, every scored SKU's system figure is added into a single total system count, and the score is one minus the ratio of those two totals. Because the pooling happens before the division, a large and accurate SKU can mask a small and badly wrong one.

The **SKU-weighted score** divides first and averages after: for each scored SKU, one minus the ratio of that SKU's absolute variance to that SKU's own system figure gives a per-SKU accuracy ratio, those ratios are added, and the sum is divided by the number of scored SKUs. Here every SKU carries equal weight regardless of size, so a single small SKU that is badly miscounted drags the average down sharply.

Each score is rounded half up to four decimal places, once, at the very end; no intermediate quantity in either formula is pre-rounded. Both scores are written to the zone as `accuracy_score_unit_weighted` and `accuracy_score_sku_weighted`, and both are readable. The two are genuinely different measurements of the same counts and will frequently disagree, including disagreeing about whether the zone is above or below the hold threshold — that disagreement is expected and is not an error.

The **hold gate is pinned to the unit-weighted score alone**. When `accuracy_score_unit_weighted` is **strictly less** than `ACCURACY_HOLD_THRESHOLD` — 0.9800 — the zone's status is set to `AUDIT_HOLD`; when it is greater than or equal to the threshold the zone's status is set to `OPERATIONAL`. A score of exactly 0.9800 therefore passes. The SKU-weighted score is reported for operator dashboards and never moves the zone's status, no matter how low it falls. In either branch the zone's `last_audited_at` is set to the current logical clock instant.

That gate drives the zone's second cycle, and all three of its edges are reachable from this one endpoint. A passing zone that fails an audit goes `OPERATIONAL → AUDIT_HOLD`. A held zone whose next audit passes goes `AUDIT_HOLD → OPERATIONAL`, which closes the `OPERATIONAL → AUDIT_HOLD → OPERATIONAL` cycle — a second audit pass is the *only* way out of a hold, and completing a maintenance window cannot clear it. A held zone whose next audit also fails takes the self-loop `AUDIT_HOLD → AUDIT_HOLD`: both accuracy scores and every counted SKU's `physical_units` are rewritten, `last_audited_at` advances, and the hold persists. In all three cases the posture of every delivery wave with an active retrieval task in the zone is recomputed, since `AUDIT_HOLD` and `MAINTENANCE` are equally freezing for a wave.

### 3.9. Cancellation, Compensating Actions and the Waitlist Cascade

Cancellation is the system's compensating action, and it is held to the conservation discipline of §2.9: after a cancellation both ledger identities must still balance exactly, with no drift left behind on any slot or any SKU.

A shopper may cancel an order whose `fulfillment_status` is `DRAFT`, `SLOTTED` or `WAVED`, and from no other status — an order that has reached `PICKING`, `CONSOLIDATED`, `LOADED` or `DISPATCHED` is past the point of no return and the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, as is a second cancellation of an already-`CANCELLED` order. Cancelling from `WAVED` carries one extra precondition: the order's wave must still be `FORMING`. Once a planner has started picking, the order can no longer be pulled out of the wave, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The acting `X-User-ID` must equal the order's `customer_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_OWNED`; an administrator acting on a shopper's behalf is exempt from that ownership equality.

**Cancelling from `DRAFT`** is the cheapest case because no money has been authorized. Every line of the order whose `line_status` is `PENDING` or `SHORT` advances to `CANCELLED`, taking the `PENDING → CANCELLED` and `SHORT → CANCELLED` edges respectively; every slot booking of the order whose status is `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED` advances to `RELEASED`, which is the first hop of the cascade; the order advances `DRAFT → CANCELLED`; the `billing_status` stays exactly where it was, at `PENDING_AUTH`, because there is no authorization to reverse; and the order's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Cancelling from `SLOTTED`** advances the order `SLOTTED → CANCELLED` and runs the full four-hop cascade, and each hop exists because the previous one changed something. Its lines are cancelled exactly as on the draft path, by the `PENDING → CANCELLED` and `SHORT → CANCELLED` edges.

*Hop 1 — the bookings are released.* Every slot booking of the order whose status is `HELD`, `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED` advances to `RELEASED`, which is terminal — the `HELD → RELEASED`, `CONFIRMED → RELEASED` and `WAITLISTED → RELEASED` edges. All three edges are reachable from this one action, and they differ in what they do to the ledger: releasing a `HELD` booking returns its units from `held_units`, releasing a `CONFIRMED` booking returns them from `confirmed_units`, and releasing a `WAITLISTED` booking changes no ledger term at all — that booking never held capacity, so it simply leaves the waitlist.

*Hop 2 — the slot ledger and price are recomputed.* Because units left `held_units` or `confirmed_units`, the slot's `held_units`, `confirmed_units` and `available_units` are recomputed by the capacity ledger and its `surge_multiplier` is recomputed by the surge calculation. Utilization has fallen, so the surge premium may drop a tier.

*Hop 3 — the waitlist is drained by exactly one.* Because `available_units` rose, the promotion selector runs against the freed slot. The eligible candidates are that slot's bookings whose status is `WAITLISTED` and whose `units` are less than or equal to the slot's freshly recomputed `available_units`; a waitlisted booking larger than the freed capacity is passed over rather than partially filled. Candidates are ranked strictly first-come, by `created_at` ascending; ties are broken by `units` **descending**, so that when two shoppers joined the waitlist at the same instant the larger basket takes the freed capacity, and any remaining tie by `quoted_fee_cents` descending. The booking's `id` is never a ranking key. Exactly one booking is promoted per request — at most one, never two, even when the freed capacity would comfortably admit a second — and the promotion then stops. If no candidate is eligible, no promotion occurs and `available_units` simply stays raised.

*Hop 4 — the promoted shopper's order is slotted.* The winning booking advances `WAITLISTED → CONFIRMED`, and because that booking is now confirmed its own customer order advances `fulfillment_status` `DRAFT → SLOTTED` and `billing_status` `PENDING_AUTH → AUTHORIZED`, with `authorized_amount_cents` snapshotted from *that* order's `order_total_cents`. The slot ledger is recomputed once more to absorb the promotion, and no second promotion is attempted on the resulting figures.

Alongside the cascade, the cancelling order's own money dimension is reversed: a `billing_status` of `AUTHORIZED` moves `AUTHORIZED → REVERSED` and one of `ADJUSTED` moves `ADJUSTED → REVERSED`, in both cases leaving `authorized_amount_cents` unchanged as the historical record of what was authorized and leaving `captured_amount_cents` null, because nothing was ever captured. `REVERSED` is terminal and there is no path back. The order's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Cancelling from `WAVED`** advances the order `WAVED → CANCELLED`, runs that same four-hop booking, slot, waitlist and promoted-order cascade with the same line cancellations and the same reversal of the money dimension, and adds the wave detachment: the order's `wave_id` is set to null, the wave's `order_count` is recomputed, and the wave's `posture` is recomputed — a cancellation can be what clears a `RESTRICTED` posture, because the order carrying the unresolved shortage is no longer a member. The wave itself is never cancelled; it simply has one fewer stop.

Cancellation also cleans up the picking and substitution work in flight. Every retrieval task of the order whose status is `QUEUED` or `RETRIEVING` advances to `CANCELLED` — both the `QUEUED → CANCELLED` and the `RETRIEVING → CANCELLED` edges — and because a cancelled task leaves the SKU's allocated set, the SKU unit ledger restores `allocated_units` and `on_hand_units` to exactly their pre-allocation values with zero drift while `physical_units` is left untouched; the affected totes have their `assigned_line_count` recomputed. Every substitution proposal on the order's lines whose status is `DRAFT` or `PROPOSED` advances to `EXPIRED`, so no shopper is left holding a decision on an order that no longer exists.

The same promotion selector is reused verbatim by the capacity-release action of §3.3, which is the other way `available_units` can rise; both call sites promote at most one waitlisted booking and both cascade the promoted booking's order to `SLOTTED` and `AUTHORIZED`.
