# Senior Living Dining Therapeutic Diets API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Dining is the most visible service a senior living community delivers, and it is also the one place where clinical risk, hospitality operations, food-safety regulation and monthly billing collide inside a single sitting. A nurse writes a texture-modified diet order; a dietitian must verify it before a tray leaves the kitchen; a dining services manager has to seat that resident in a venue licensed for their level of care, at a table that is actually free; a satellite kitchen lead has to prove the hot-holding well never dropped below temperature; and at month end a billing specialist has to explain why the point-of-service ticket ledger and the reservation book disagree by a few thousand cents. Today these five people work from five disconnected systems, and the seams between them are where residents get served the wrong tray.

This document specifies a single back-end service that owns the whole chain: **diet order intake and dietitian review**, the **allergy / texture / preference substitution profile** that turns a published menu into a resident-specific tray card, **venue capacity and meal-time scheduling** across levels of care, **therapeutic menu extensions** and their review loop, **meal-count reconciliation to billing** including guest and takeout meals, **weight-loss and intake monitoring** that automatically opens a plan-of-care review, **temperature compliance** in satellite kitchens, and the **resident food committee** feedback loop that drives menu cycle refreshes.

Two design commitments run through every rule below. First, safety gates are hard gates: a resident with an unresolved allergen line or a pending therapeutic extension cannot be seated, and a venue that has drifted out of temperature range stops serving before anybody has to make a judgement call. Second, every number the system reports must be reproducible — the same inputs must always produce the same cents, the same percentage, and the same tier, which is why evaluation order and rounding order are pinned normatively rather than left to the implementation. The service is deployed at the root of its host; there is no additional URL prefix in front of the paths listed in section 4.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming that user's role. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` holds a value outside the seven roles enumerated below, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being addressed, the request is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `ProgramAdministrator`, `ClinicalStaff`, `RegisteredDietitian`, `DiningServicesManager`, `SatelliteKitchenLead`, `BillingSpecialist` and `ResidentRepresentative`, spelled exactly as shown.

- A `ProgramAdministrator` sets up communities, venues and residents, moves residents between dining participation states, and owns the logical clock.
- `ClinicalStaff` originate diet orders, maintain the substitution profile, and record intake and weight observations.
- A `RegisteredDietitian` reviews diet orders, authors and reviews therapeutic menu extensions, signs off menu cycles, and closes plan-of-care reviews — never as the author of the artifact under review.
- A `DiningServicesManager` builds menu cycles, runs the reservation book, seats residents, and executes committee-driven refreshes.
- A `SatelliteKitchenLead` owns hot-holding, cold-holding and reheat temperature compliance at their venue, and issues and serves tickets there.
- A `BillingSpecialist` reconciles meal counts to billing, finalizes periods, and voids tickets.
- A `ResidentRepresentative` is the resident or their authorized representative.

For the six operator roles — `ProgramAdministrator`, `ClinicalStaff`, `RegisteredDietitian`, `DiningServicesManager`, `SatelliteKitchenLead` and `BillingSpecialist` — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a rule says so (as `ordered_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` on a diet order, as `authored_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` on a therapeutic extension, as `signed_off_by_user_id` on a menu cycle, as `issued_by_user_id` on a meal ticket, as `logged_by_user_id` on a temperature log, as `recorded_by_user_id` on an intake observation, as `completed_by_user_id` on a plan-of-care review, and as `finalized_by_user_id` on a reconciliation), and it compares one recorded identifier against another for segregation of duties, but it never compares an operator's `X-User-ID` against any entity's `id`.

`ResidentRepresentative` is different: when `X-User-Role` is `ResidentRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Resident`, and it must resolve to an existing resident — if it does not, the request fails authentication with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Ownership checks then compare the addressed resource's resident reference to `X-User-ID`: a `ResidentRepresentative` may read or write only the record of the resident their `X-User-ID` resolves to, whether that resident is named by a path id, by a `resident_id` in the payload, or indirectly as the owning resident of an addressed diet order, seating reservation, plan-of-care review or intake observation. Addressing any other resident's record yields `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. Where the resident is named in the payload the ownership refusal is raised as soon as that one field has itself been read, so a payload that omits `resident_id` altogether is still a shape error; and where the owning resident can only be learned from the addressed row — a diet order, seating reservation, plan-of-care review or intake observation named by a path id — that row is resolved first, so a path id that resolves nowhere reports its own 404 rather than the ownership refusal. The resident-scoped collection reads `/diet-orders`, `/seating-reservations` and `/plan-of-care-reviews` are implicitly narrowed to the acting representative's own resident, and an explicit `resident_id` filter naming a different resident is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. Community-level, venue-level and menu-level reads are not resident-scoped and are open to every role listed on the endpoint, though a `resident_id` filter supplied to the cycle ballot list is still held to the representative's own resident in the same way.

Segregation of duties is enforced on top of role authorization and is a separate refusal, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403): the dietitian recording a decision on a diet order must not be the operator identified by that order's `ordered_by_user_id`; the dietitian reviewing a therapeutic extension must not be the operator identified by its `authored_by_user_id`; and the dietitian completing a plan-of-care review must not be the operator identified by the `recorded_by_user_id` of the intake observation that triggered it.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, must be at least 1, and must not exceed 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and must be at least 0. A value outside these bounds is a malformed request rather than a silently clamped one, and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific endpoint declares otherwise; the two endpoints that do declare otherwise are the venue temperature-log stream, which is also `created_at` ascending, and the resident intake-observation list, which is ordered by `observation_date` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before the window is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current window).

Collection reads also accept domain filters, all optional and all combinable, and all applied before pagination. The community venue list filters on `venue_kind`, `temp_compliance_posture` and `status`; the community resident list on `care_level` and `dining_status`; the community menu-cycle list and the community reconciliation list on `status`. The venue temperature-log stream filters on `probe_point` and `in_range`. The resident substitution-rule list filters on `trigger_type` and `status`; the resident intake-observation list on `from_date`, `to_date` and `meal_period`, where `from_date` and `to_date` bound `observation_date` inclusively; the resident ballot list on `status` and `sentiment`. The diet-order list filters on `resident_id`, `supersedes_order_id`, `clinical_status` and `review_status`. The seating-reservation list filters on `venue_id`, `resident_id`, `service_date`, `meal_period` and `status`. The menu-cycle item list filters on `day_index`, `meal_period` and `extension_coverage_status`; the cycle ballot list on `resident_id`, `sentiment` and `status`; the menu-item extension list on `diet_code` and `review_status`. The meal-ticket list filters on `venue_id`, `resident_id`, `reservation_id`, `billed_reconciliation_id`, `service_date`, `meal_period`, `ticket_type` and `status`. The plan-of-care review list filters on `resident_id`, `trigger_observation_id`, `status` and `outcome`. Two reads are computed rather than stored and take mandatory parameters instead of filters: seat availability requires `service_date` and `meal_period`, and the tray card requires `service_date` and `meal_period`; for both of them the missing-or-malformed-parameter refusal is raised only after the path id has resolved, so an unresolvable venue or resident id reports its 404 first. A filter value outside its declared enumeration, a non-integer `day_index`, a non-date `from_date`, `to_date` or `service_date` and a non-boolean `in_range` are all `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while a filter carrying a syntactically acceptable id that resolves to nothing is not an error at all and simply matches no rows.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` — a UUID with no type prefix and no domain-flavoured formatting — and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits, stamped from the logical clock at the moment of creation.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits refreshed on every mutation of that row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. This applies to `Community`, `DiningVenue`, `Resident`, `DietOrder`, `SubstitutionRule`, `SeatingReservation`, `MenuCycle`, `MenuItem`, `TherapeuticExtension`, `MealServiceTicket`, `BillingReconciliation` and `PlanOfCareReview`.

Three record types are append-only observations and carry **only** `id` and `created_at` — they have no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and neither field should appear in their responses: `IntakeObservation`, `TemperatureLog` and `CommitteeFeedback`.

Version increments follow the row that actually changed. When an operation cascades into a second row — a superseded diet order, an archived menu cycle, a released reservation, a resident whose denormalized metrics were recomputed — that second row's `updated_at` and `version` move as well, because it too was mutated. A recompute that reproduces the values already stored is not a mutation, however: a resident, venue, menu item or menu cycle whose recomputed fields come out byte-for-byte identical keeps both `updated_at` and `version` exactly as they were. Two operations bump a parent's `version` even though the parent's own business fields are untouched, and this is deliberate: creating a diet order increments the target `Resident`'s `version` by 1, and creating or releasing a seating reservation increments the target `DiningVenue`'s `version` by 1. Those two increments are what make the stale-version guards of section 2.6 bite.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The service never reads wall-clock time. It reads a logical clock that, before any call to the clock-setting endpoint, reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; there is no fallback to real time under any circumstance. A `ProgramAdministrator` moves the clock by supplying `current_utc`, and the response echoes the new `current_utc` back verbatim in exactly the representation the caller sent, not renormalized to three fractional digits. Every window, every threshold comparison and every timestamp stamped by the system — `created_at`, `updated_at`, `activated_at`, `seated_at`, `logged_at_utc` — reads the logical clock at the instant the request is processed.

Dates supplied by clients are ISO 8601 calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form; instants are RFC 3339 UTC datetimes. Where a rule speaks of "the clock date", it means the calendar date part of the logical clock in UTC. Two date-range invariants hold at request-shape level: `period_end_date` must not precede `period_start_date`, and `effective_end_date` must not precede `effective_start_date`. A seating reservation's `service_date` must equal the UTC calendar date of its `seat_start_utc`. All three violations are shape errors reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.6. Validation Precedence

Because most write endpoints are guarded by several checks at once, the order in which those checks run is part of the contract: the first failing check determines the error the caller sees, and later checks never mask an earlier one. Every request is evaluated in the following order.

1. **Authentication** — headers present and well-formed, role among the seven, and for a `ResidentRepresentative` an `X-User-ID` that resolves to an existing resident: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Authorization** — the role is listed on the endpoint, and a `ResidentRepresentative` is addressing its own resident: `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).
3. **Request shape** — required fields present, JSON types correct, enum values inside their declared sets, integers and numbers inside their declared ranges, dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, datetimes RFC 3339 UTC, `service_date` equal to the UTC date of `seat_start_utc`, `period_end_date` not before `period_start_date`, `effective_end_date` not before `effective_start_date`, and decimal fields at or inside their declared precision — a value carrying fewer decimal places than declared, an integer included, is accepted: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Query-string shape belongs to this tier too, but two of its checks necessarily run later and still report `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`: the mandatory `service_date` and `meal_period` of the two computed reads are validated only after tier 4 has resolved the path id, and a range bound that depends on a path row — a menu item's `day_index` ceiling of `week_count` multiplied by seven — is checked only after that row resolves.
4. **Path existence** — every id embedded in the URL resolves, checked strictly left to right in path order, so that a nested read of a cycle's items reports the missing cycle before it looks at anything else. Depending on which segment fails, the error is `ERR_COMMUNITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DIET_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MENU_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MENU_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXTENSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TICKET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECONCILIATION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404.
5. **Body reference existence** — every foreign key supplied in the payload resolves, checked in the order the fields are declared on the owning entity. A reservation create therefore checks `resident_id` before `venue_id`, and a ticket issue checks `venue_id`, then `resident_id`, then `reservation_id`. The applicable error is `ERR_COMMUNITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DIET_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` (for an unresolvable `supersedes_order_id`), `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MENU_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MENU_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TICKET_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_OBSERVATION_NOT_FOUND` (for an unresolvable `trigger_observation_id`), each with HTTP 404.
6. **Community boundary** — `ERR_CROSS_COMMUNITY_REFERENCE` (422), described in section 2.7.
7. **Linked-resource subject match** — `ERR_LINKED_RESOURCE_MISMATCH` (422), described in section 2.7.
8. **Ballot item resolution** — a ballot's `menu_item_code` resolves against the addressed cycle's items: `ERR_MENU_ITEM_NOT_IN_CYCLE` (422).
9. **State legality** — the addressed row is in a state from which the requested transition exists, and every row-shape precondition that transition declares holds: `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). A repeat of an already-applied transition falls here too.
10. **Stale parent version** — the optimistic-concurrency guard of section 2.8: `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
11. **Segregation of duties** — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403), as defined in section 2.1.
12. **Venue may serve at all** — the venue's `status` is `OPEN` and its `temp_compliance_posture` is not `SUSPENDED`: `ERR_VENUE_SERVICE_SUSPENDED` (409). Ticket service is the one endpoint that reads only the posture half of this tier and ignores `status`, as section 3.6 records.
13. **Posture ticket restriction** — while the venue's `temp_compliance_posture` is `WATCH`, only a `RESIDENT` ticket may be issued; `GUEST` and `TAKEOUT` issuance is refused with `ERR_VENUE_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (409).
14. **Tray safety** — `ERR_UNSAFE_TRAY_NO_SUBSTITUTION` (422).
15. **Texture liberalization gate** — `ERR_TEXTURE_REQUIRES_DIETITIAN` (422).
16. **Verified diet order required** — `ERR_DIET_ORDER_NOT_VERIFIED` (409).
17. **Resident participation** — `ERR_RESIDENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409).
18. **Care level served** — `ERR_CARE_LEVEL_NOT_SERVED` (422).
19. **Published menu cycle available** — `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_MENU_CYCLE` (409).
20. **Future-dated records** — `ERR_OBSERVATION_FUTURE_DATE` (422).
21. **Resident meal spacing** — `ERR_RESIDENT_MEAL_SPACING` (409).
22. **Publication date overlap** — `ERR_MENU_CYCLE_DATE_OVERLAP` (409).
23. **Item code uniqueness** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_ITEM_CODE` (409).
24. **Ballot uniqueness** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_BALLOT` (409).
25. **Therapeutic coverage at publication** — `ERR_THERAPEUTIC_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE` (409).
26. **Review round ceiling** — `ERR_REVIEW_ROUND_LIMIT` (409).
27. **Venue seat capacity** — `ERR_VENUE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).
28. **Closed billing period** — `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSED` (409).
29. **Sponsorship cap** — `ERR_SPONSOR_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (402).
30. **Reconciliation freshness** — `ERR_RECONCILIATION_STALE` (409).
31. **Variance explanation** — `ERR_VARIANCE_UNRESOLVED` (409).

The first three tiers apply to every endpoint without exception. Tier 4 applies to every endpoint carrying a path id, and tier 5 to every endpoint accepting a foreign key in its body. Tier 9 applies to every action endpoint. The remaining tiers apply only to the endpoints named in the workflow sections below, and each of those sections states its own applicable scope; the ordering above governs whenever two or more of them are live on the same request. Note in particular that tier 12's suspension check precedes tier 16's verified-order check, so a suspended venue reports the suspension rather than a missing order; and that tier 27's capacity check comes after tier 21's spacing check, so a candidate seating that violates both reports the resident-side spacing conflict.

### 2.7. Community Boundaries and Linked-Resource Integrity

The community is the tenancy boundary of the whole system, and cross-community references are refused rather than silently accepted. A seating reservation's resident must belong to the same community as the venue being booked. A meal ticket's resident must belong to the same community as the venue issuing the ticket. A committee ballot's resident must belong to the same community as the menu cycle being balloted. A venue's `care_levels_served` must be a subset of its parent community's `care_levels_offered`, and a resident's `care_level` must be a member of that same `care_levels_offered` set. All five violations report `ERR_CROSS_COMMUNITY_REFERENCE`, and they are enforced on venue registration, resident admission, reservation creation, ticket issuance and ballot casting.

A second family of integrity rules requires that a supplied link point at a row belonging to the same subject as the request. A ticket's `reservation_id`, when supplied, must name a reservation whose own resident and venue equal the ticket's `resident_id` and `venue_id`. A diet order's `supersedes_order_id` must name an order belonging to the same resident as the new order. A plan-of-care review's `trigger_observation_id` must name an observation belonging to that review's resident — a property the system-driven opener of section 3.8 guarantees by construction, since no endpoint accepts a client-supplied `trigger_observation_id`. And a `DISLIKE` substitution rule's `replacement_item_code` must differ from its `match_value`, because a rule that replaces an item with itself resolves nothing. All four violations report `ERR_LINKED_RESOURCE_MISMATCH`, and they are enforced on diet-order creation, substitution-rule creation and ticket issuance.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Guards on Shared Parents

Two creates hang off a parent row that other writers legitimately mutate, and both are protected by an optimistic-concurrency guard rather than by locking.

Creating a diet order requires the non-optional field `expected_resident_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Resident`. If that value does not equal the resident's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a new order legitimately supersedes the resident's currently `ACTIVE` order once approved, so a second writer working from a stale view of the resident's order history would otherwise succeed against apparently identical state and layer a second supersede on top of a picture it never saw. Since every diet-order create itself increments the resident's `version`, a client must re-read the resident before writing again.

Creating a seating reservation requires the non-optional field `expected_venue_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `DiningVenue`. If that value does not equal the venue's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Seat capacity is a genuinely shared pool, so a writer holding a stale venue view would otherwise be checking capacity numbers that no longer exist; the guard turns that into a deterministic stale-version rejection. Both reservation creation and reservation release increment the venue's `version`, so any client that observed the venue before either of those events must re-read it.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Sending one where it is not specified is simply an unrecognized field, and no other write is subject to a stale-version rejection.

### 2.9. Money, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in whole cents; there are no fractional-cent values anywhere in a response. All percentages and scores are decimal numbers carried to exactly 2 decimal places. Weights are decimal numbers carried to exactly 1 decimal place, and temperature readings likewise to exactly 1 decimal place. On the way in a declared precision is a ceiling rather than an exact width, so an integer weight or temperature is a perfectly valid payload.

Wherever a rule below says an amount is rounded, it means **rounded half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. Two properties of the rounding discipline are load-bearing and are restated where they apply. First, in ticket pricing each intermediate component is rounded to whole cents on its own before the components are combined; summing unrounded components and rounding once at the end produces a different, incorrect figure. Second, in the reconciliation fee waterfall there is exactly one rounding step, applied when the administrative fee is computed, and the fee tier is keyed on the pre-fee base rather than on the final total.

Percentage outputs are rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and every threshold comparison against them is performed on the rounded 2-decimal value, not on an unrounded intermediate. Boundary inclusivity is specified individually for every threshold in section 3; where a threshold is inclusive, a value sitting exactly on it does breach.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Derived Fields

A number of fields are never written by a client and are instead fully derived by the server. Section 3 says where each is computed, but three global properties hold for all of them.

Derived counters are recomputed, not incremented in place, wherever the scaffold of this specification says "recompute". A community's `pending_dietitian_review_count` is a denormalized count of that community's diet orders whose `review_status` is `AWAITING_DIETITIAN`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `REREVIEW_REQUIRED` **and** whose `clinical_status` is `PENDING_REVIEW` or `ACTIVE`; it is recomputed in full on every write that touches a diet order's `review_status`, including writes that leave the count unchanged. A community's `active_menu_cycle_id` holds the `id` of the single menu cycle of that community whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` and whose closed date range from `effective_start_date` through `effective_end_date` contains the logical clock date, and is null when no such cycle exists; uniqueness is guaranteed by the publication non-overlap rule, so this field never has to choose between candidates. Because it is a pure function of the clock date it is re-evaluated on every read of the community rather than latched at write time, so advancing the clock past a published cycle's `effective_end_date` empties the field with no write having occurred at all.

Cascades are transactional and complete. When an operation is described below as firing hops, all hops of that chain are applied within the same request, and the response reflects the state after the last hop. If any gate anywhere in the chain fails, nothing is written at all.

Cascade-induced state changes are not gated by the role that triggered them. A `ClinicalStaff` recording an intake observation causes a diet order's `review_status` to change and substitution rules to be superseded even though `ClinicalStaff` cannot make those transitions directly; those hops are system transitions.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Program Setup: Communities, Venues and Resident Admission

Setting up a program starts with registering the community itself. A `ProgramAdministrator` supplies the community's `name` and its `care_levels_offered`, which must be a non-empty subset of `INDEPENDENT_LIVING`, `ASSISTED_LIVING`, `MEMORY_CARE` and `SKILLED_NURSING`. Two financial policy dials may be supplied at the same time, and both default if omitted: `guest_meal_sponsor_cap_cents`, the per-resident open sponsorship ceiling for guest and takeout meals, defaults to 20000 cents — two hundred dollars — and `variance_tolerance_cents`, the absolute reconciliation variance the community will finalize without a written explanation, defaults to 5000 cents. Neither may be negative. A freshly registered community starts with `pending_dietitian_review_count` at 0 and `active_menu_cycle_id` at null.

Venues come next, and each is registered under a community by a `ProgramAdministrator` with a `community_id`, a `name`, a `venue_kind` of `MAIN_DINING`, `BISTRO` or `SATELLITE`, a `seat_capacity` of at least 1, and a `care_levels_served` list that must be non-empty and must not name a care level the parent community does not offer. `SATELLITE` venues are the ones served out of satellite kitchens and are the reason the temperature-compliance machinery exists, but posture applies uniformly to every venue kind. Two scheduling dials may be supplied: `seating_turn_minutes`, the length of one seating turn, defaults to 60 and must be at least 15; and `turnover_buffer_minutes`, the table-reset tail appended to an incumbent reservation's occupation interval, defaults to 20 and may be 0. A new venue opens with `temp_compliance_posture` at `COMPLIANT`, `consecutive_temp_violation_count` at 0, and `status` at `OPEN`. The `status` field distinguishes `OPEN` from `CLOSED`; a `CLOSED` venue cannot be reserved, seated or ticketed, and reports `ERR_VENUE_SERVICE_SUSPENDED` just as a suspended posture does.

Admitting a resident into the dining program is likewise a `ProgramAdministrator` action, taking `community_id`, `full_name`, a `care_level` from the same four-value enumeration, and a `baseline_weight_kg` that must be greater than zero and carry at most 1 decimal place. The clinical preference profile may be seeded at admission: `allergens` is a list of allergen tags the resident must never be served and defaults to empty; `dislikes` is a list of menu item codes the resident refuses and also defaults to empty; and `preference_strictness` decides whether dislike substitutions are mandatory or best-effort, taking `STRICT` or `FLEXIBLE` and defaulting to `FLEXIBLE`. A new resident starts with `dining_status` `ACTIVE`, `tray_card_state` `CLEAR`, `current_weight_kg` equal to `baseline_weight_kg`, `guest_meal_charge_balance_cents` at 0, and every monitoring metric at its default: `rolling_30d_weight_change_pct` and `baseline_weight_change_pct` at 0.00, `intake_risk_score` at 0.00, and `avg_intake_pct_7d` at null.

Participation changes over time, and a `ProgramAdministrator` moves a resident between `ACTIVE`, `ON_LEAVE` and `DISCHARGED` by supplying the target `dining_status`. The move is not cosmetic. Because the risk score carries a severity multiplier keyed on participation, changing `dining_status` recomputes the resident's `intake_risk_score` immediately. Moving a resident to `DISCHARGED` also removes them from the eligible-resident denominator that the committee refresh index is computed against, which can change a menu cycle's participation tier on the very next ballot. `ON_LEAVE` and `DISCHARGED` residents cannot be reserved, cannot have a `RESIDENT` ticket issued and cannot cast a committee ballot — all of which report `ERR_RESIDENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. Sponsorship is deliberately not gated the same way: a `GUEST` or `TAKEOUT` ticket naming a non-`ACTIVE` sponsoring resident is still issued and still charged against that resident's sponsorship ceiling, it merely loses the sponsorship discount of section 3.6. A `DISCHARGED` resident additionally cannot accept a new diet order, a new substitution rule, or a new intake observation, which is the same error; an `ON_LEAVE` resident can still accept all three, because clinical documentation continues while a resident is away.

### 3.2. Diet Order Intake and the Parallel Review Axis

A diet order is the clinical instruction that authorizes a tray, and it carries **two independent status dimensions on the same row**. The `clinical_status` axis takes `PENDING_REVIEW`, `ACTIVE`, `SUPERSEDED` or `DISCONTINUED` and describes where the order sits in the resident's clinical record. The `review_status` axis takes `AWAITING_DIETITIAN`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED` or `REREVIEW_REQUIRED` and describes where the order sits in the dietitian's queue. The two axes advance separately: an order that is already `ACTIVE` can be pushed back to `REREVIEW_REQUIRED` without leaving `ACTIVE`, and a `DISCONTINUED` order keeps whatever `review_status` it held at the moment it was discontinued. `SUPERSEDED` and `DISCONTINUED` are the terminal values of the clinical axis.

`ClinicalStaff` originate an order for a resident, naming the `resident_id`, the `texture_level`, the `liquid_consistency` and the mandatory `expected_resident_version` described in section 2.8. `texture_level` is one of `REGULAR`, `BITE_SIZED`, `MINCED_MOIST` or `PUREED`, and these four values carry a texture rank of 0, 1, 2 and 3 respectively, with `REGULAR` the most liberal. `liquid_consistency` is one of `THIN`, `SLIGHTLY_THICK`, `MILDLY_THICK`, `MODERATELY_THICK` or `EXTREMELY_THICK`, carrying liquid ranks of 0, 1, 2, 3 and 4. Optionally the order carries `therapeutic_codes`, a possibly-empty subset of `CARB_CONTROLLED`, `RENAL`, `LOW_SODIUM`, `HIGH_PROTEIN` and `GLUTEN_FREE`, defaulting to an empty list; and `supersedes_order_id`, naming the same resident's currently `ACTIVE` order that this one will replace once approved, defaulting to null for a first order. If `supersedes_order_id` is supplied, the named order must belong to the payload's resident and must itself be `ACTIVE` on the clinical axis — a link to an order sitting on any other clinical status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, which by the ordering of section 2.6 lands before the stale-version guard.

The resident must exist and must not be `DISCHARGED`. A newly created order lands with `clinical_status` `PENDING_REVIEW`, `review_status` `AWAITING_DIETITIAN`, `review_round` at 0, and `ordered_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`; `reviewed_by_user_id` and `activated_at` are null. Creation increments the resident's `version` by 1 and recomputes the community's `pending_dietitian_review_count`.

One safety gate is specific to intake, and it is deliberately narrow. Consider a resident whose active order is `MINCED_MOIST` and whose weight has just triggered a monitoring review: a new order proposing `REGULAR` would liberalize the texture at exactly the moment clinical concern is highest. So when the resident already has an `ACTIVE` order and the proposed `texture_level` has a **strictly lower** texture rank than that active order's `texture_level`, the resident must not have any plan-of-care review in `OPEN` status; if one is open, the create is refused with `ERR_TEXTURE_REQUIRES_DIETITIAN`. Ordering the same texture, or a more restrictive one, is always allowed. This same gate is re-applied on resubmission.

Review happens on the other axis. A `RegisteredDietitian` records a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REQUEST_CHANGES`, optionally with a `reviewer_note` that is stored on the order and echoed back in its `reviewer_note` field — a `REQUEST_CHANGES` decision always overwrites the stored note, clearing it to null when none is supplied, while an `APPROVE` decision writes it only when one is supplied and otherwise leaves whatever note was there; the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the order's `ordered_by_user_id` or the decision is refused for segregation of duties. A decision is only accepted when `review_status` is `AWAITING_DIETITIAN` or `REREVIEW_REQUIRED`; from any other value the request is an illegal transition and reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

An `APPROVE` decision from `AWAITING_DIETITIAN` moves `review_status` to `APPROVED` and, on the other axis, moves `clinical_status` from `PENDING_REVIEW` to `ACTIVE` with `activated_at` stamped from the logical clock; when the order has meanwhile left `PENDING_REVIEW` — a `DISCONTINUED` order still sitting in the dietitian's queue is the case that arises — the clinical axis and `activated_at` are left exactly as they are and only the review axis moves. `reviewed_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`. If the order named a `supersedes_order_id`, that predecessor's `clinical_status` moves from `ACTIVE` to `SUPERSEDED` while its `review_status` is left exactly as it was, and its `activated_at` is retained for audit. The community's `pending_dietitian_review_count` is recomputed, and so is the resident's `intake_risk_score`, because which order is `ACTIVE` has just changed and the score reads the active order's texture, therapeutic codes and liquid consistency.

An `APPROVE` decision from `REREVIEW_REQUIRED` is the other half of the review axis and is treated with equal weight. It moves `review_status` from `REREVIEW_REQUIRED` to `APPROVED` while `clinical_status` remains `ACTIVE` throughout — proof that the two axes are genuinely independent. It sets `reviewed_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, cascades the resident's `tray_card_state` from `HOLD_PENDING_REVIEW` back to `CLEAR` while leaving any other value of that field — `BLOCKED` included — exactly as it stands, recomputes the resident's `intake_risk_score` as every `APPROVE` decision on either branch does, and recomputes the community's `pending_dietitian_review_count`.

A `REQUEST_CHANGES` decision from `AWAITING_DIETITIAN` moves `review_status` to `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, increments `review_round` by 1, sets `reviewed_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, stores the supplied reviewer note and echoes it back in the response, and leaves `clinical_status` at `PENDING_REVIEW`. The loop is bounded: a `REQUEST_CHANGES` decision is only accepted while `review_round` is strictly less than 3, so the third such decision is the last one accepted and a fourth is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_ROUND_LIMIT`.

`ClinicalStaff` close the loop by amending and resubmitting an order that came back `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, optionally supplying replacement values for `texture_level`, `liquid_consistency` and `therapeutic_codes` — any field supplied replaces the stored value, any field omitted keeps it, and `ordered_by_user_id` is never rewritten. Resubmission requires that `review_status` be `CHANGES_REQUESTED` **and** `clinical_status` be `PENDING_REVIEW`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. It moves `review_status` back to `AWAITING_DIETITIAN` while retaining `review_round`, re-applies the texture liberalization gate against the resident's `ACTIVE` order using the resubmitted values, and recomputes the community's `pending_dietitian_review_count`, which in this case nets out unchanged. The cycle `CHANGES_REQUESTED` → `AWAITING_DIETITIAN` → `CHANGES_REQUESTED` is therefore a legal loop, bounded by the round ceiling.

Discontinuation is a clinical-axis action available to `ClinicalStaff`, with an optional free-text `reason` that is accepted but neither persisted nor echoed anywhere in the response. It is accepted while `clinical_status` is `PENDING_REVIEW` or `ACTIVE` and moves it to `DISCONTINUED`; an order that is already `DISCONTINUED` or `SUPERSEDED` reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Both the `ACTIVE` → `DISCONTINUED` and the `PENDING_REVIEW` → `DISCONTINUED` edges freeze `review_status` at its current value and recompute the community's `pending_dietitian_review_count`. Discontinuing an `ACTIVE` order additionally recomputes the resident's `intake_risk_score`, since with no active order the texture, therapeutic and liquid contributions all drop to zero; discontinuing a `PENDING_REVIEW` order does not change the score, because a pending order never contributed to it.

Reading a diet order returns both axes plus `review_round`, and the diet-order collection can be filtered by resident, by the self-referential supersede link, or by either axis independently.

### 3.3. Substitution Profiles and Tray-Card Resolution

The substitution profile is how a generic published menu becomes a specific resident's tray. `ClinicalStaff` add rules to a resident's profile, each carrying a `trigger_type` of `ALLERGEN`, `TEXTURE` or `DISLIKE`, a `match_value`, a `replacement_item_code` naming what gets served instead, and a `priority_rank` of at least 1 where a lower number wins within the same trigger type. What `match_value` means depends on the trigger: for an `ALLERGEN` rule it is an allergen tag; for a `DISLIKE` rule it is a menu item code, and it must differ from `replacement_item_code`; for a `TEXTURE` rule it is the literal string `ANY`. A new rule is created with `status` `ACTIVE`; the only other value is `SUPERSEDED`, and superseded rules are ignored entirely by tray-card resolution. Adding a rule immediately changes the resolved tray card for that resident. A `DISCHARGED` resident cannot receive new rules.

Resolving a tray card is a computed read for one resident and one `service_date` and `meal_period` pair. Resolution begins by locating the menu cycle to serve from: the resident's community must have a cycle in `PUBLISHED` status whose closed range from `effective_start_date` through `effective_end_date` contains the requested `service_date`. If no such cycle exists, the read fails with `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_MENU_CYCLE`. From that cycle the cycle day is derived: take the number of whole days from the cycle's `effective_start_date` to the requested `service_date`, reduce it modulo the cycle's `week_count` multiplied by seven, and add 1. The result is the `day_index` reported on the response, and the menu items considered are exactly the cycle's items whose `day_index` and `meal_period` match, taken in ascending `item_code` order — never in id order.

Each item becomes one line of the tray card, and each line is resolved against the resident's `ACTIVE` diet order, of which there is at most one. First the eligible candidate rules are gathered from the resident's `ACTIVE` substitution rules. An `ALLERGEN` rule is a candidate when its `match_value` appears in the item's `allergen_tags`. A `TEXTURE` rule is a candidate when the resident has an `ACTIVE` order and the item's `base_texture` has a **strictly lower** texture rank than that order's `texture_level` — that is, the kitchen's default preparation is more liberal than the order permits. A `DISLIKE` rule is a candidate when its `match_value` equals the item's `item_code` **and** the resident's `preference_strictness` is `STRICT`; a `FLEXIBLE` resident's dislikes never force a substitution.

The winning rule among the candidates is chosen by three ordered keys: trigger priority first, where `ALLERGEN` ranks 0, `TEXTURE` ranks 1 and `DISLIKE` ranks 2 and the lowest rank wins; then ascending `priority_rank`; then ascending `created_at`. Row ids are never an ordering input. When there is a winner, the line's `served_item_code` is the winner's `replacement_item_code` and the line's `substitution_rule_id` is the winner's `id`; when the candidate set is empty, the line falls back to the base item unchanged, with `served_item_code` equal to the item's own `item_code` and `substitution_rule_id` null. Every line reports the underlying item's `menu_item_id`, `item_code`, `name` and `base_texture` alongside `served_item_code`, `substitution_rule_id` and the `reason` described next.

Each line also carries a `reason`, and the reason is decided by a strict precedence rather than by which rule won. Two unsafe conditions are checked first. A line has an **unresolved allergen** when the item carries at least one allergen tag that also appears in the resident's `allergens` list and no candidate `ALLERGEN` rule matches that specific tag. A line has a **pending extension** when the resident has an `ACTIVE` order and at least one of that order's `therapeutic_codes` appears in the item's `required_therapeutic_codes` while no therapeutic extension of that item for that diet code has reached `APPROVED` review status. If the unresolved-allergen condition holds, `reason` is `ALLERGEN_UNRESOLVED`; otherwise if the pending-extension condition holds, `reason` is `EXTENSION_PENDING`; otherwise if there is no winner, `reason` is `NONE`; otherwise `reason` is `ALLERGEN_SUBSTITUTION`, `TEXTURE_SUBSTITUTION` or `DISLIKE_SUBSTITUTION` according to the winner's trigger type.

The response reports `resident_id`, the resolving `menu_cycle_id`, the requested `service_date` and `meal_period`, the derived `day_index`, the resolved `lines`, a `blocked_item_codes` list and an overall `tray_status`. `blocked_item_codes` holds the item codes of every line whose reason is `ALLERGEN_UNRESOLVED` or `EXTENSION_PENDING`, in ascending item-code order. `tray_status` is `BLOCKED` whenever `blocked_item_codes` is non-empty; otherwise it mirrors the resident's own `tray_card_state`, reporting `HOLD_PENDING_REVIEW` when that field is `HOLD_PENDING_REVIEW`, `BLOCKED` when that field is `BLOCKED`, and `CLEAR` otherwise. If the requested day and meal period have no items at all, `lines` is an empty list and `tray_status` derives from `tray_card_state` alone.

The resident-level `tray_card_state` is itself never written by a client. It takes `CLEAR`, `HOLD_PENDING_REVIEW` or `BLOCKED`, starts at `CLEAR`, moves to `HOLD_PENDING_REVIEW` when monitoring opens a plan-of-care review or when such a review is completed with a re-review outcome, moves to `BLOCKED` when a review is completed with an escalation outcome, and returns to `CLEAR` when a dietitian approves the order out of `REREVIEW_REQUIRED`.

### 3.4. Menu Cycle Authoring, Therapeutic Extensions and the Committee Refresh Loop

A menu cycle is the repeating rotation a community serves from. A `DiningServicesManager` creates one with a `community_id`, an operator-assigned `cycle_number` of at least 1, a `week_count` of at least 1 that determines how many cycle days exist, and the closed effective range `effective_start_date` through `effective_end_date` where the end must not precede the start. The cycle is born in `DRAFT` with `refresh_round` at 0, `refresh_index` at 0.00, `refresh_tier` at `ADVISORY`, `refresh_reason` null and `signed_off_by_user_id` null. Its lifecycle values are `DRAFT`, `PENDING_DIETITIAN_SIGNOFF`, `PUBLISHED`, `REFRESH_PENDING` and `ARCHIVED`, with `ARCHIVED` terminal, and the interesting property is that it is a **cycle**: `DRAFT` → `PENDING_DIETITIAN_SIGNOFF` → `PUBLISHED` → `REFRESH_PENDING` → `DRAFT`.

Items are added to a cycle by a `DiningServicesManager` — item creation carries no cycle-status gate whatsoever, so an item may be appended at any point in the cycle's lifecycle, a `PUBLISHED` or `ARCHIVED` cycle included — each with an `item_code` that must be unique among that cycle's items — a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ITEM_CODE` — a dish `name`, a `day_index` between 1 and the cycle's `week_count` multiplied by seven inclusive, a `meal_period` of `BREAKFAST`, `LUNCH` or `DINNER`, and a `base_texture` from the same four-value texture enumeration the diet orders use. Two optional lists shape safety and therapeutic handling: `allergen_tags`, the allergen tags present in the item as the kitchen produces it, defaulting to empty; and `required_therapeutic_codes`, the therapeutic diet codes that need an approved extension before the cycle can be published, also defaulting to empty and drawn from the same five-code set as a diet order's `therapeutic_codes`. A new item's `extension_coverage_status` is `NOT_REQUIRED` when `required_therapeutic_codes` is empty and `INCOMPLETE` otherwise; it becomes `COMPLETE` only when every code the item requires has a therapeutic extension in `APPROVED` review status.

Therapeutic extensions are authored by a `RegisteredDietitian` against one menu item — the item the extension belongs to is recorded as its `menu_item_id` — and one `diet_code`, optionally carrying `nutrient_overrides`, a free-form object pinning nutrient targets such as a sodium ceiling in milligrams and defaulting to an empty object. Authoring is likewise ungated by the owning cycle's status, and nothing prevents a second extension of the same item for the same `diet_code`; only submission is status-gated. The extension is created in `DRAFT` review status with `review_round` at 0, `authored_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and `reviewed_by_user_id` and `reviewer_note` null. Its lifecycle takes `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED` and `RETIRED`, with `RETIRED` terminal.

Submitting an extension for review is accepted while its review status is `DRAFT` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED` and the owning item's cycle is in `DRAFT` or `PENDING_DIETITIAN_SIGNOFF`; a cycle that has already been published, sent for refresh or archived can no longer accept extension submissions, and the attempt reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Submission moves the review status to `SUBMITTED` and changes nothing else beyond `updated_at` and `version`; when it is a resubmission out of `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `review_round` is retained, so the loop `SUBMITTED` → `CHANGES_REQUESTED` → `SUBMITTED` is bounded by the same ceiling of 3 that governs the diet-order loop.

Reviewing an extension requires review status `SUBMITTED` and an acting `X-User-ID` different from the extension's `authored_by_user_id`. The `decision` is `APPROVE` or `REQUEST_CHANGES`, with an optional `reviewer_note` that is stored on the extension and reflects the most recent decision. `APPROVE` moves the review status to `APPROVED`, sets `reviewed_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, and recomputes the owning item's `extension_coverage_status`, which flips to `COMPLETE` once every code in `required_therapeutic_codes` has an approved extension. `REQUEST_CHANGES` moves the review status to `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, increments `review_round` by 1, sets `reviewed_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, stores the note, and also recomputes the owning item's `extension_coverage_status`. As with diet orders, a `REQUEST_CHANGES` decision is only accepted while `review_round` is strictly below 3, and the fourth attempt is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_ROUND_LIMIT`.

Submitting the cycle itself for sign-off is a `DiningServicesManager` action, accepted only from `DRAFT` and only when the cycle owns at least one menu item — an empty cycle cannot be submitted and reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. It moves the cycle to `PENDING_DIETITIAN_SIGNOFF` and cascades nothing.

Publication is the `RegisteredDietitian`'s sign-off and is accepted only from `PENDING_DIETITIAN_SIGNOFF`. Two business gates apply. Every item of the cycle whose `required_therapeutic_codes` is non-empty must have `extension_coverage_status` equal to `COMPLETE`, or publication is refused with `ERR_THERAPEUTIC_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE`. And no other cycle of the same community that is currently `PUBLISHED` may have a closed effective range that intersects this cycle's closed range, or publication is refused with `ERR_MENU_CYCLE_DATE_OVERLAP`; because both ranges are closed, two cycles that merely share a single boundary date do overlap. On success the cycle moves to `PUBLISHED` with `signed_off_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`. Publication also cascades: every other cycle of the community that is `PUBLISHED` and whose `effective_end_date` falls strictly before this cycle's `effective_start_date` moves to `ARCHIVED`. A later cycle's publication is therefore the only way a `PUBLISHED` cycle becomes `ARCHIVED`. Finally the community's `active_menu_cycle_id` is recomputed, both by the publication itself and by each archival it triggers.

A published cycle is not the end of the story, because residents get a vote on the menu and that vote is what eventually sends the cycle back to `DRAFT`. A `ResidentRepresentative` casts one ballot against one item of a cycle, supplying the `resident_id` — which must be the resident their `X-User-ID` resolves to — a `menu_item_code`, and a `sentiment` of `POSITIVE`, `NEUTRAL` or `NEGATIVE`. The `menu_item_code` is a natural key resolved against the `item_code` values of the menu items belonging to the addressed cycle; a code that resolves nowhere in that cycle is refused with `ERR_MENU_ITEM_NOT_IN_CYCLE`. The balloting resident must belong to the cycle's community and must have `dining_status` `ACTIVE`. Duplicate balloting is prevented at the level of the triple of cycle, resident and item code: while a ballot on that triple is still in `SUBMITTED` status a second one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BALLOT`, but once the earlier ballot has moved to `ACKNOWLEDGED` a fresh ballot on the same triple is accepted.

A ballot is stored with `status` `SUBMITTED` and a `vote_weight` the server computes rather than accepts: the weight is 2 when the submitting resident's `care_level` is `MEMORY_CARE` or `SKILLED_NURSING`, and 1 for every other care level. Ballots are append-only records with no `updated_at` and no `version`.

After the ballot is persisted, the cycle's refresh index is recomputed, and the step order is normative: participation first, then tier, then index, then the threshold test. The eligible resident count is the number of residents of the cycle's community whose `dining_status` is `ACTIVE`. The ballot pool is every ballot of this cycle whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`; ballots already `ACKNOWLEDGED` by a prior refresh are excluded, which is exactly what lets a refreshed cycle start a fresh loop from zero. Participation is the number of distinct residents represented in that pool, multiplied by 100, divided by the eligible resident count or by 1 if that count is zero, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The `refresh_tier` is `QUORUM` when participation is at least 40.00 percent — exactly 40.00 does reach quorum — and `ADVISORY` otherwise. The negative weight is the sum of `vote_weight` over the pooled ballots whose `sentiment` is `NEGATIVE`. The `refresh_index` is that negative weight, multiplied by the tier weight — 1.0 for `QUORUM`, 0.6 for `ADVISORY` — multiplied by 100, divided by the eligible resident count or by 1 if that count is zero, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The cycle is eligible for refresh when `refresh_index` is at least the tier's threshold: 25.00 for `QUORUM` and 40.00 for `ADVISORY`, with a value sitting exactly on the threshold counting as eligible.

Both `refresh_index` and `refresh_tier` are persisted onto the cycle by every ballot, whether or not the threshold is reached, though the cycle's own `updated_at` and `version` move only when the ballot actually changes one of those two values or the cycle's `status`. When the cycle is `PUBLISHED` and the threshold **is** reached, the ballot itself auto-transitions the cycle from `PUBLISHED` to `REFRESH_PENDING`, and because the cycle is no longer `PUBLISHED` the community's `active_menu_cycle_id` is recomputed to null. A cycle in any other status simply keeps the recomputed index without transitioning.

Executing the refresh is a `DiningServicesManager` action accepted only from `REFRESH_PENDING`, and it requires a `refresh_reason` explaining the change. It returns the cycle to `DRAFT` so the next rotation can be authored, increments `refresh_round` by 1, stores the supplied `refresh_reason`, and resets `refresh_index` to 0.00 and `refresh_tier` to `ADVISORY`. It cascades twice. Every ballot of this cycle in `SUBMITTED` status moves to `ACKNOWLEDGED`, clearing the pool. And every therapeutic extension in `APPROVED` review status whose owning menu item belongs to this cycle moves to `RETIRED`, which in turn recomputes each affected item's `extension_coverage_status` — an item that still declares `required_therapeutic_codes` therefore falls back from `COMPLETE` to `INCOMPLETE`, and the cycle cannot be published again until its extensions are re-authored, resubmitted and re-approved.

### 3.5. The Reservation Book: Spacing, Capacity, Waitlists and Seating

Booking a resident into a seating window is a `DiningServicesManager` action carrying `resident_id`, `venue_id`, `service_date`, `meal_period`, the inclusive `seat_start_utc`, a `party_size` of at least 1 counting the resident plus any accompanying guests, the mandatory `expected_venue_version`, and optionally `allow_waitlist`, which defaults to false. The `service_date` must equal the UTC calendar date of `seat_start_utc`. The server computes `seat_end_utc` as `seat_start_utc` advanced by the venue's `seating_turn_minutes`; that end instant is the exclusive end of the venue-side interval. A reservation's lifecycle values are `RESERVED`, `WAITLISTED`, `SEATED`, `COMPLETED` and `RELEASED`, with `COMPLETED` and `RELEASED` terminal, and it is born `RESERVED` with `suspension_hold` false and `seated_at` null; the supplied `allow_waitlist` is itself persisted on the row and echoed back with it. Creation increments the venue's `version` by 1.

Before a row is written, a stack of gates runs, and it runs in the tier order of section 2.6, which is the order they are narrated in here. The venue's `status` must be `OPEN` and its `temp_compliance_posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`. The resident must have a diet order that is at once `ACTIVE` on the clinical axis and `APPROVED` on the review axis, or the booking is refused with `ERR_DIET_ORDER_NOT_VERIFIED`. The resident's `dining_status` must be `ACTIVE`. The resident's `care_level` must be a member of the venue's `care_levels_served`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_CARE_LEVEL_NOT_SERVED`. Then the two interval calculations run, resident-side spacing first and venue-side capacity second.

**Resident-side meal spacing** exists so that a resident is not booked into back-to-back sittings, and its geometry is deliberately different from the venue's. The candidate interval is the **closed** interval from `seat_start_utc` through `seat_end_utc`. Every other reservation of the same resident — at any venue, in any community — whose `status` is `RESERVED`, `SEATED` or `COMPLETED` occupies the **closed** interval running from 90 minutes before its `seat_start_utc` to 90 minutes after its `seat_end_utc`. If the candidate interval intersects any such occupation interval, the booking is refused with `ERR_RESIDENT_MEAL_SPACING`. Because both intervals are closed, merely touching endpoints do intersect: a candidate that starts exactly 90 minutes after an incumbent's `seat_end_utc` conflicts, and it must start at least one minute later than that to be accepted. `WAITLISTED` and `RELEASED` rows of the resident are ignored, while `COMPLETED` rows still count — a resident who has already finished lunch still cannot be booked into a sitting that crowds it.

**Venue-side capacity** is an aggregate check with a one-sided buffer and half-open intervals. The candidate occupies the **half-open** interval from `seat_start_utc` up to but not including `seat_start_utc` advanced by the venue's `seating_turn_minutes`. Each incumbent reservation of the same venue on the same `service_date` whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `SEATED` occupies the **half-open** interval from its `seat_start_utc` up to but not including its `seat_end_utc` advanced by the venue's `turnover_buffer_minutes` — the buffer is appended only to the incumbent's tail, never to the candidate's. The conflict set is every such incumbent whose occupation interval intersects the candidate's. Committed seats are the sum of `party_size` over the conflict set, and the booking passes capacity when committed seats plus the candidate's `party_size` is at most the venue's `seat_capacity`. Because the intervals are half-open, a candidate starting exactly at an incumbent's buffered end instant does not conflict at all. `COMPLETED`, `RELEASED` and `WAITLISTED` rows never occupy seats.

When capacity fails, the outcome depends on the caller's intent. With `allow_waitlist` false the booking is refused with `ERR_VENUE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. With `allow_waitlist` true — and with every other precondition satisfied, capacity being the only failure — the row is created in `WAITLISTED` status instead of erroring. A waitlisted row computes `seat_end_utc` identically, keeps `suspension_hold` false, increments the venue's `version` by 1 just like a reserved row, and is excluded both from committed-seat arithmetic and from the census projection used in billing.

Seat availability is exposed as a separate computed read, and it deliberately answers a coarser question than the capacity gate. For one venue, one `service_date` and one `meal_period` it rolls up the sum of `party_size` over every reservation of that venue, date and meal period whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `SEATED`, and reports `venue_id`, `service_date`, `meal_period`, `seat_capacity`, `committed_seats`, `open_seats` — the venue's `seat_capacity` minus committed seats, floored at 0 — and `blocking_reservation_ids`, the ids of the rows that were summed, in ascending `created_at` order. This roll-up spans the whole meal period rather than an interval, so when two disjoint seating turns are booked inside one meal period it can legitimately report more committed seats than the venue's capacity. That is precisely why the interval-level check, and not this roll-up, gates writes.

Seating a party moves a reservation from `RESERVED` to `SEATED` and stamps `seated_at` from the logical clock; it takes no payload. The venue's `status` must be `OPEN` and its `temp_compliance_posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`. The tray card for the reservation's `service_date` and `meal_period` is resolved at this moment, and its resolved status must not be `BLOCKED`: neither an `ALLERGEN_UNRESOLVED` line, nor an `EXTENSION_PENDING` line, nor a resident-level `tray_card_state` of `BLOCKED` may be present, or seating is refused with `ERR_UNSAFE_TRAY_NO_SUBSTITUTION`. The resident must still hold a diet order that is `ACTIVE` and `APPROVED`, or seating is refused with `ERR_DIET_ORDER_NOT_VERIFIED` — the order may have been superseded or pushed back to re-review since the booking was made; the tray-safety refusal is the earlier tier and wins whenever both apply. Since resolution needs a published cycle covering the date, seating also fails with `ERR_NO_PUBLISHED_MENU_CYCLE` when the community has none, and that refusal is the latest of the three, surfacing only once the diet-order check has passed. On success, seating cascades a meal ticket into existence: a ticket with `ticket_type` `RESIDENT`, its `reservation_id` pointing at this row, and `venue_id`, `service_date` and `meal_period` copied from it, created in `ISSUED` status with `billed_amount_cents` priced by the ticket pricing rules of section 3.6, `voided_amount_cents` at 0, `billed_reconciliation_id` null and `issued_by_user_id` set to the acting `DiningServicesManager`. The cascade prices the ticket but does not re-run the issuance gates of section 3.6, so the `WATCH` posture restriction, the future-date check, the closed-period check and the sponsorship cap play no part in seating.

Releasing a reservation is a `DiningServicesManager` action with an optional `reason`, accepted from `RESERVED`, `WAITLISTED` or `SEATED`, and it moves the row to `RELEASED` and increments the venue's `version` by 1. Release then runs the **waitlist promotion selector** for the freed combination of venue, `service_date` and `meal_period`. Promotion moves one `WAITLISTED` row to `RESERVED`, and it happens only when three conditions hold: the venue's `temp_compliance_posture` is `COMPLIANT` — a `WATCH` posture is not good enough for promotion — the committed seats after the release plus the candidate row's `party_size` is at most the venue's `seat_capacity`, and the candidate is the selector winner. The winner is chosen by ascending `created_at` and by nothing else. Committed seats in that middle condition are the meal-period roll-up of the seat-availability read rather than an interval calculation, and only the single winner is ever considered: if the earliest-created waitlisted row does not fit, the selector promotes nobody at all rather than looking further down the queue. A row carrying `suspension_hold` true is as eligible for promotion as any other. A promoted row has `suspension_hold` set to false and increments the venue's `version` by 1.

Two further reservation transitions are driven entirely by the system. When a linked ticket is served, a reservation in `SEATED` status moves to `COMPLETED`; a completed row still counts in the census projection and still counts for resident meal spacing, but it stops occupying seats. And when a linked ticket is voided, a reservation in `SEATED` or `COMPLETED` status moves to `RELEASED`, which fires the waitlist promotion selector for that venue, date and meal period and removes the row from both committed seats and the census projection.

### 3.6. Meal Tickets: Issuance, Pricing, Service and Voids

A meal ticket is the billing record for one meal at one venue. Some tickets are created by the seating cascade described above; others are issued directly by a `DiningServicesManager` or a `SatelliteKitchenLead` supplying `venue_id`, a `ticket_type` of `RESIDENT`, `GUEST` or `TAKEOUT`, a `service_date` and a `meal_period`, optionally with `resident_id` and `reservation_id`. For a `RESIDENT` ticket the `resident_id` is the resident being served; for a `GUEST` or `TAKEOUT` ticket it is the resident sponsoring the meal, and it may be omitted only for an unsponsored guest or takeout meal. `reservation_id` names the seating the ticket was issued from and is null for walk-in and takeout tickets. A ticket's lifecycle values are `ISSUED`, `SERVED`, `BILLED` and `VOIDED`, with `VOIDED` terminal; it is born `ISSUED` with `voided_amount_cents` at 0, `billed_reconciliation_id` null, and `issued_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`.

Issuance gates in the fixed tier order of section 2.6, which is the order narrated here. When `reservation_id` is supplied, that reservation's own resident and venue must equal the payload's `resident_id` and `venue_id`. The venue's `status` must be `OPEN` and its `temp_compliance_posture` must not be `SUSPENDED`. While the posture is `WATCH`, only `RESIDENT` tickets may be issued and a `GUEST` or `TAKEOUT` attempt is refused with `ERR_VENUE_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`. A `RESIDENT` ticket requires a non-null `resident_id` whose resident holds a diet order that is `ACTIVE` on the clinical axis and `APPROVED` on the review axis, failing which the issue reports `ERR_DIET_ORDER_NOT_VERIFIED` — a `RESIDENT` ticket with no `resident_id` at all reports that same error rather than a shape error; the resident must also be `ACTIVE` in `dining_status`. The `service_date` must not fall after the logical clock date, or the issue is refused with `ERR_OBSERVATION_FUTURE_DATE`. No finalized reconciliation of the venue's community may already cover the `service_date`, or the issue is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSED`. Finally, for a `GUEST` or `TAKEOUT` ticket with a sponsoring resident, the resident's `guest_meal_charge_balance_cents` plus the amount this ticket is about to bill must be at most the community's `guest_meal_sponsor_cap_cents`, or the issue is refused with `ERR_SPONSOR_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402) — note that the cap is tested against the newly computed amount, so pricing runs before the cap is evaluated. The sponsor's own `dining_status` is not gated here: an `ON_LEAVE` or `DISCHARGED` sponsor is accepted, priced without the sponsorship discount below, and still charged against the same ceiling.

**Pricing a ticket** produces `billed_amount_cents`, and the order of rounding is part of the contract. Start from the base rate, which depends on both `ticket_type` and `meal_period`. A `RESIDENT` meal is 400 cents at `BREAKFAST`, 650 at `LUNCH` and 800 at `DINNER`. A `GUEST` meal is 850, 1250 and 1650 respectively. A `TAKEOUT` meal is 700, 1100 and 1450 respectively. A ticket is **sponsorship-eligible** when its `ticket_type` is `GUEST` or `TAKEOUT`, its `resident_id` is not null, and that resident's `dining_status` is `ACTIVE`. For a sponsorship-eligible ticket the guest discount is the base rate multiplied by 12.5 percent and then **rounded half up to whole cents on its own**; for every other ticket the discount is 0. A packaging fee of 175 cents applies to `TAKEOUT` tickets and to nothing else. The subtotal is the base rate minus the already-rounded discount plus the packaging fee, and it is a whole-cent integer by construction. Meals tax of 7.25 percent applies to `GUEST` and `TAKEOUT` tickets only, is computed on that whole-cent subtotal, and is itself **rounded half up to whole cents**; for a `RESIDENT` ticket the tax is 0. The `billed_amount_cents` is the subtotal plus the tax. A `RESIDENT` ticket is therefore an internal transfer priced straight from the rate table with no discount, no packaging and no tax.

Rounding each component before combining, rather than combining first and rounding once, changes the answer, and this is the single most common way to get pricing wrong. Take a sponsored `TAKEOUT` `DINNER`: the base is 1450, twelve-and-a-half percent of that is 181.25 which rounds half up to 181, and adding 175 packaging gives a subtotal of 1444; 7.25 percent of 1444 is 104.69 which rounds half up to 105; the `billed_amount_cents` is 1549. Carrying the unrounded components through instead would give a subtotal of 1443.75 and a tax of 104.672, totalling 1548.42 and rounding to 1548 — which is wrong.

After issuance, a ticket with a sponsoring or served resident triggers a recomputation of that resident's `guest_meal_charge_balance_cents`, which is the sum of `billed_amount_cents` over that resident's tickets whose `ticket_type` is `GUEST` or `TAKEOUT` and whose `status` is `ISSUED`, `SERVED` or `BILLED`. Voided tickets drop out of the balance entirely.

Serving a ticket is a `DiningServicesManager` or `SatelliteKitchenLead` action taking no payload, accepted only from `ISSUED`, and it requires the venue's `temp_compliance_posture` not be `SUSPENDED`. Service is gated on the posture alone; a `CLOSED` venue `status` does not block the serving of a ticket that has already been issued. It moves the ticket to `SERVED`, and when the ticket carries a `reservation_id` whose reservation is `SEATED`, that reservation cascades to `COMPLETED` and stops occupying seats.

Voiding a ticket belongs to the `BillingSpecialist` and takes an optional `void_reason`. It is accepted from `ISSUED`, `SERVED` or `BILLED`; a ticket already `VOIDED` reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The void copies the ticket's current `billed_amount_cents` into `voided_amount_cents` and then forces `billed_amount_cents` to 0, so the amount that was charged remains visible for audit while the live charge disappears. The sponsoring resident's `guest_meal_charge_balance_cents` is recomputed, which is how a void frees sponsorship headroom. The void then cascades: a linked reservation in `SEATED` or `COMPLETED` status moves to `RELEASED`, and that release in turn runs the waitlist promotion selector for the freed venue, `service_date` and `meal_period` combination. Every void behaves this way regardless of which status the ticket was voided out of; voiding a `SERVED` ticket fires exactly the same chain as voiding an `ISSUED` one. Voiding a ticket that had already reached `BILLED` — which by definition carries a non-null `billed_reconciliation_id` — additionally becomes a **chargeback**: the voided amount enters the void-credit term of any later reconciliation whose period covers the ticket's `service_date`.

The system also defines a community-level billable figure as a conserved quantity across issue and void: for a community and a period it is the sum of `billed_amount_cents` over tickets at that community's venues with `service_date` inside the period whose `status` is `SERVED` or `BILLED`, minus the sum of `voided_amount_cents` over tickets at those venues with `service_date` inside the period whose `status` is `VOIDED` **and** whose `billed_reconciliation_id` is not null. Because the credit term is restricted to tickets a prior reconciliation already billed, voiding a never-billed ticket simply removes it from the first term without adding a credit.

### 3.7. Satellite Kitchen Temperature Compliance

Food safety is enforced through a probe log stream per venue. A `SatelliteKitchenLead` logs a reading with a `probe_point` of `HOT_HOLDING`, `COLD_HOLDING` or `REHEAT`, a `temperature_f` in degrees Fahrenheit carried to at most 1 decimal place and bounded by nothing else, and optionally a `corrective_action_code` describing the action taken — any supplied string counts as a present code, an empty string included, and only an omitted or null code counts as absent. The server stamps `logged_at_utc` from the logical clock and sets `logged_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`. Log rows are append-only and carry no `updated_at` and no `version`.

Whether a reading is `in_range` depends on its probe point and on nothing else: `HOT_HOLDING` is in range at 135.0 degrees or above, `COLD_HOLDING` at 41.0 degrees or below, and `REHEAT` at 165.0 degrees or above. Boundary readings — exactly 135.0, exactly 41.0, exactly 165.0 — are in range. The three limits are independent; a cold-holding reading is never judged against the hot-holding limit.

The venue's `consecutive_temp_violation_count` is a **sticky** counter with exactly one resetting path. A log that is in range **and** carries a non-null `corrective_action_code` resets the counter to 0. A log that is out of range increments the counter by 1. An in-range log **without** a corrective action code leaves the counter exactly where it was — it neither resets nor increments. The consequence is worth spelling out: an in-range reading logged with no corrective action between two violations still leaves the venue at a count of 2 and escalates it, because the intervening reading did not clear anything. Every log row records `violation_sequence_index`, the value of the venue's `consecutive_temp_violation_count` as it stands immediately after that row has been applied.

From the counter the venue's `temp_compliance_posture` is derived: `SUSPENDED` when the count is 3 or more, `WATCH` when the count is 2, and `COMPLIANT` when the count is 1 or 0. Crossing into `WATCH` closes the venue to guest and takeout business — those issuance attempts now report `ERR_VENUE_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` — while `RESIDENT` tickets continue to be issued and served.

Crossing into `SUSPENDED` stops the venue entirely: no ticket may be issued there and no ticket may be served there. It also cascades into the reservation book. Every reservation of that venue whose `status` is `RESERVED` and whose `seat_start_utc` is at or after the logical clock — that is, every future booking — moves to `WAITLISTED` with `suspension_hold` set to true, and those rows leave committed-seat arithmetic. Bookings whose seating window has already started are left alone.

Recovery requires an affirmative corrective log: an in-range reading carrying a non-null `corrective_action_code` resets `consecutive_temp_violation_count` to 0 and returns the posture to `COMPLIANT`, whether the venue was in `WATCH` or in `SUSPENDED`. Recovery from `SUSPENDED` does **not** automatically restore the reservations that were held: rows carrying `suspension_hold` true stay `WAITLISTED` until the promotion selector runs for their venue, date and meal period, which happens when some other reservation at that combination is released or when a linked ticket is voided.

The temperature stream is readable per venue in ascending `created_at` order and can be narrowed to a single `probe_point` or to only in-range or only out-of-range rows.

### 3.8. Intake Monitoring, Weight Trends and Plan-of-Care Review

Recording an intake observation is the head of a four-hop cascade and the most consequential write `ClinicalStaff` make. The payload carries an `observation_date`, a `meal_period`, an `intake_pct` integer between 0 and 100 inclusive expressing what fraction of the served meal was consumed, and optionally a `weight_kg` which, when present, must be greater than zero and carry at most 1 decimal place; omit it when only intake was observed. `recorded_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`. The `observation_date` must not fall after the logical clock date, or the write is refused with `ERR_OBSERVATION_FUTURE_DATE`, and the resident must not be `DISCHARGED`. Observations are append-only records with no `updated_at` and no `version`, and the resident's observation history reads back in ascending `observation_date` then ascending `created_at` order, optionally narrowed by `from_date`, `to_date` and `meal_period`.

**Hop 1** recomputes the resident's monitoring metrics. Two weight variants are always computed and they answer different questions.

The gating variant is the **rolling thirty-day** trend, published as `rolling_30d_weight_change_pct`. Take the resident's observations that carry a weight and whose `observation_date` falls inside the closed window from 30 days before the clock date through the clock date, sorted by ascending `observation_date` and then ascending `created_at`; ids are never an ordering input. If fewer than two such observations exist, the value is 0.00. Otherwise take the earliest weight and the latest weight in that ordering, subtract the earliest from the latest, divide by the earliest, multiply by 100, and round half up to 2 decimal places. Because it measures the newest weight against the **oldest weight still inside the window**, a gain followed by a loss shows the full intra-window swing.

The reported-only variant is the **baseline-anchored** trend, published as `baseline_weight_change_pct`, and it never gates anything. Take all of the resident's weight-carrying observations regardless of date, sorted by descending `observation_date` and then descending `created_at`; the first row's `weight_kg` is the resident's `current_weight_kg`, and when the resident has no weight-carrying observation at all `current_weight_kg` falls back to `baseline_weight_kg`. Subtract `baseline_weight_kg` from `current_weight_kg`, divide by `baseline_weight_kg`, multiply by 100, and round half up to 2 decimal places. The same set of observations can read a mild change here while the rolling window reads a much sharper one, and that divergence is intentional.

The seven-day intake average, `avg_intake_pct_7d`, is the mean of `intake_pct` over the resident's observations whose `observation_date` falls inside the closed window from 6 days before the clock date through the clock date — a seven-day window counting today — rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and null when that window holds no observation at all.

The composite `intake_risk_score` is then recomputed from five inputs. Weight points are 40 when `rolling_30d_weight_change_pct` is at most negative 10.00, otherwise 30 when it is at most negative 5.00, otherwise 15 when it is at most negative 2.00, otherwise 0. Intake points are 0 when `avg_intake_pct_7d` is null, otherwise 40 when it is below 25.00, otherwise 30 when it is below 50.00, otherwise 15 when it is below 75.00, otherwise 0. The remaining three inputs read the resident's single diet order whose `clinical_status` is `ACTIVE`, of which there is at most one; when there is none, all three contribute 0, and orders that are `PENDING_REVIEW`, `SUPERSEDED` or `DISCONTINUED` never contribute. Texture points are the active order's texture rank multiplied by 5, so `REGULAR` scores 0 and `PUREED` scores 15. Therapeutic points are 5 multiplied by the number of codes in the active order's `therapeutic_codes`, capped at 10 — a third and further codes add nothing. Liquid points are 10 when the active order's liquid rank is 2 or higher, meaning `MILDLY_THICK`, `MODERATELY_THICK` or `EXTREMELY_THICK`, and 0 otherwise. Sum the five contributions, then multiply by a severity multiplier of 0.50 when the resident's `dining_status` is `ON_LEAVE` and 1.00 otherwise, then clamp the product to be no less than 0.00 and no more than 100.00, and round half up to 2 decimal places. The multiplier is applied before the single rounding, not after. The score is also recomputed by any diet-order transition that changes which order is `ACTIVE` and by a change to `dining_status`.

**Hop 2** evaluates the plan-of-care trigger, and it runs only after the weight variants, the intake average and the risk score have been written. The weight breach holds when `rolling_30d_weight_change_pct` is at most negative 5.00 — exactly negative 5.00 does breach, negative 4.99 does not. The intake breach holds when `avg_intake_pct_7d` is not null and is strictly below 50.00 — exactly 50.00 does not breach. If either breach holds and the resident has no plan-of-care review in `OPEN` status, a review is opened. Its `trigger_observation_id` is the observation just created, its `risk_score_at_open` snapshots the resident's `intake_risk_score` as recomputed in hop 1, and its `trigger_reason` records which threshold fired: `COMBINED` when both breaches hold, `WEIGHT_LOSS_30D` when only the weight breach holds, and `LOW_INTAKE_AVG` when only the intake breach holds. Exactly one review may be open per resident at a time, so a further breach while a review is already open opens nothing and mutates nothing. Only the rolling thirty-day variant is consulted here; the baseline-anchored figure never gates.

**Hop 3** fires when hop 2 actually opened a review: the resident's diet order whose `clinical_status` is `ACTIVE` moves its `review_status` from `APPROVED` to `REREVIEW_REQUIRED`, while `clinical_status` stays `ACTIVE`. When the resident has no `ACTIVE` order, or that order's `review_status` is anything other than `APPROVED`, hop 3 and hop 4 both do nothing at all: the review stays open, the texture rules stay `ACTIVE` and `tray_card_state` stays where it was. **Hop 4** follows from that transition: every substitution rule of the resident whose `trigger_type` is `TEXTURE` and whose `status` is `ACTIVE` flips to `SUPERSEDED`, and the resident's `tray_card_state` moves from `CLEAR` to `HOLD_PENDING_REVIEW`. The community's `pending_dietitian_review_count` is recomputed as part of the review-status write. The practical effect is that the resident can still be seated — `HOLD_PENDING_REVIEW` is not `BLOCKED` — but their texture substitutions no longer apply until a dietitian re-approves the order, at which point `tray_card_state` returns to `CLEAR`.

Closing a review is a `RegisteredDietitian` action supplying an `outcome` and an optional `note`. It is accepted only from `OPEN`, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `recorded_by_user_id` of the triggering observation, and it moves `status` to `COMPLETED` with `completed_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`. The three outcomes fire three disjoint side-effect sets, and each is specified in full.

- `NO_CHANGE` records the clinical judgement that nothing needs to change. It mutates no other entity at all: the diet order is untouched and `tray_card_state` is left exactly as it stands.
- `DIET_ORDER_REREVIEW` sends the order back to the dietitian queue. It requires that the resident have a diet order whose `clinical_status` is `ACTIVE`, and refuses with `ERR_DIET_ORDER_NOT_VERIFIED` when there is none. That order's `review_status` is forced to `REREVIEW_REQUIRED` from whatever value it currently holds — `AWAITING_DIETITIAN` and `CHANGES_REQUESTED` included, not only `APPROVED` — and the write is idempotent, moving neither `updated_at` nor `version`, when it is already `REREVIEW_REQUIRED`. The resident's `tray_card_state` is set to `HOLD_PENDING_REVIEW`.
- `ESCALATED` halts service pending physician follow-up. The resident's `tray_card_state` is set to `BLOCKED`, which makes every tray card resolve as `BLOCKED` and therefore blocks seating with `ERR_UNSAFE_TRAY_NO_SUBSTITUTION`. No diet order is mutated.

A review reads back with its `trigger_reason`, its `risk_score_at_open` and its `outcome`, which is null until the review is completed; the `note` supplied at completion is stored but is not one of the review's response fields; the collection can be filtered by resident, by triggering observation, by `status` and by `outcome`.

### 3.9. Meal-Count Reconciliation and Period Closure

At period end a `BillingSpecialist` reconciles a community's meals by supplying a `community_id` and the closed period bounds `period_start_date` and `period_end_date`, where the end must not precede the start. A reconciliation is created in `RECONCILED` status with `variance_note` and `finalized_by_user_id` null; `FINALIZED` is the terminal status. The period may not intersect the closed range of any already-`FINALIZED` reconciliation of the same community, or the create is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSED`.

Creation computes **two independent and both-valid readings of the same period**, and stores both. The venues in scope are all venues of the named community, and both period bounds are inclusive.

The **ticket-ledger** reading is what the point of service actually recorded. Take the meal tickets at in-scope venues whose `service_date` lies inside the period and whose `status` is `SERVED` or `BILLED`; `ISSUED` and `VOIDED` tickets are excluded. `ticket_ledger_total_cents` is the sum of their `billed_amount_cents`, and `ledger_meal_counts` is the count of those tickets grouped by `ticket_type` with all three keys — `RESIDENT`, `GUEST` and `TAKEOUT` — always present and zero-filled.

The **census-projection** reading is what the reservation book projected. Take the seating reservations at in-scope venues whose `service_date` lies inside the period and whose `status` is `RESERVED`, `SEATED` or `COMPLETED`; `WAITLISTED` and `RELEASED` rows never count. Price each of those reservations at the `RESIDENT` transfer rate for its `meal_period` — 400 cents at `BREAKFAST`, 650 at `LUNCH`, 800 at `DINNER` — and sum. Then take the meal tickets at in-scope venues whose `service_date` lies inside the period, whose `ticket_type` is `GUEST` or `TAKEOUT`, and whose `status` is `SERVED` or `BILLED`, and add the sum of their `billed_amount_cents`. That total is `census_projection_total_cents`. `census_meal_counts` reports the count of those reservations under the `RESIDENT` key, and the counts of the guest-and-takeout ticket set split into the `GUEST` and `TAKEOUT` keys.

The two readings disagree in specific, expected ways, and the disagreement is the point. A walk-in `RESIDENT` ticket with no `reservation_id` raises the ledger only. A reservation that was booked but never seated raises the census only, because the census charges the transfer rate whether or not a ticket was ever issued. `variance_cents` records the disagreement as `ticket_ledger_total_cents` minus `census_projection_total_cents`, and it may be negative. All of these figures are whole-cent integers and no rounding occurs anywhere in this reading, because every input is already a whole-cent value.

Creation then runs the **fee waterfall**, and its step order is normative because it looks circular and is not. Step one takes the gross figure, which is `ticket_ledger_total_cents` — never the census projection. Step two computes `void_credit_cents`, the sum of `voided_amount_cents` over tickets at in-scope venues whose `service_date` lies inside the period, whose `status` is `VOIDED`, and whose `billed_reconciliation_id` is not null; that last condition restricts the credit to genuine chargebacks — voids of tickets some prior reconciliation had already billed. Step three computes `pre_fee_base_cents` as the gross figure minus the void credit, floored at 0. Step four selects the administrative fee tier **from `pre_fee_base_cents`**: the tier is small when `pre_fee_base_cents` is at most 3500, mid when it is at most 20000, and large above that. Step five computes `administrative_fee_cents` as `pre_fee_base_cents` multiplied by the tier's rate — 6 percent for small, 4.5 percent for mid, 3 percent for large — rounded half up to whole cents, and this is the only rounding step in the whole waterfall. Step six computes `net_billable_total_cents` as `pre_fee_base_cents` plus `administrative_fee_cents`.

The tier is keyed on step three and never on step six, and getting that backwards changes the answer. With a `pre_fee_base_cents` of 3386 the tier is small, the fee is 6 percent of 3386, which is 203.16 and rounds half up to 203, and the net billable total is 3589. Keying the tier on that 3589 total would select mid, produce a fee of 152, and yield a net of 3538 — which is wrong.

Finalizing is the `BillingSpecialist`'s closing act, accepted only from `RECONCILED` and taking an optional `variance_note`. Two gates run before the state moves. First, **freshness**: both readings and the whole fee waterfall are recomputed at finalize time, and the recomputation must reproduce the stored `ticket_ledger_total_cents`, `census_projection_total_cents` and `net_billable_total_cents`; if activity since the reconciliation was created has moved any of them, finalize is refused with `ERR_RECONCILIATION_STALE` and the specialist must reconcile afresh. Second, the **variance gate**: when the absolute value of `variance_cents` exceeds the community's `variance_tolerance_cents`, a non-empty `variance_note` must be supplied, or finalize is refused with `ERR_VARIANCE_UNRESOLVED`; a variance whose absolute value is at or under the tolerance needs no note. A note counts as supplied only when it holds at least one non-whitespace character, and an omitted note leaves whatever `variance_note` was already stored untouched rather than clearing it.

On success the reconciliation moves to `FINALIZED` with `finalized_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and it cascades into the ticket ledger: every meal ticket at in-scope venues whose `status` is `SERVED` and whose `service_date` lies inside the closed period moves to `BILLED` with its `billed_reconciliation_id` set to this reconciliation's `id`. That link is what turns a subsequent void of one of those tickets into a chargeback credit against a later period, and it is also what closes the period against new tickets: once finalized, any ticket issue for a `service_date` inside the range reports `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSED`.

Reconciliations read back with both variant totals, `variance_cents`, `ledger_meal_counts`, `census_meal_counts` and the full waterfall of `void_credit_cents`, `pre_fee_base_cents`, `administrative_fee_cents` and `net_billable_total_cents`; the community-scoped list can be narrowed by `status`.
