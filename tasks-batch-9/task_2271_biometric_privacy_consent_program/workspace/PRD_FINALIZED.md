# Biometric Consent Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Employers and retailers that capture fingerprints, face geometry, voiceprints, iris scans or hand geometry now operate inside one of the most punitive corners of US privacy law. A single timekeeping terminal installed without a published written policy, without a lead-timed notice, or without an executed written release can generate statutory damages per person per collection event — and in several states aggrieved individuals may sue directly rather than waiting for a regulator. The operational problem is rarely intent; it is bookkeeping. Nobody knows how many collection points exist, which policy version each one was activated under, whether the processor's contract actually forbids resale, or which of ten thousand enrolled workers ever signed anything.

This document specifies the Biometric Consent Program API, the system of record for that bookkeeping. The API tracks a biometric identifier inventory across timekeeping, access control, security camera and customer-facing surfaces; the written policy versions that publish a retention schedule and a destruction trigger; the notice packets that must reach employees and customers, in translation where the statute demands it, before consent may lawfully be solicited; the consent records themselves, with their release language and their own independent retention floor; the vendor contracts that must prohibit sale, restrict disclosure and compel destruction when the engagement ends; the alternative non-biometric processes owed to anyone who declines; the incident files that carry heightened notification duties when templates are exposed; and the state-by-state exposure model that prices private-right-of-action risk against a board-approved ceiling.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, the program carries a single derived **posture** that gates collection: when deletion obligations go overdue, or carried exposure approaches the board's ceiling, the program freezes and new collection stops until the underlying defect is cured. Second, every deadline, retention horizon, notification clock and sweep reads a server-controlled logical UTC clock rather than wall-clock time, so compliance timelines are reproducible and testable. The API is mounted at the root of the host: resources are addressed directly, for example at `/biometric-assets/{asset_id}` and `/consent-records/{consent_id}`, with relationships carried as explicit identifier fields in request bodies rather than as nested URL segments.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the rules that hold across every endpoint. Where §3 describes what a workflow means, this section fixes how every request is authenticated, validated, ordered, rounded and versioned.

### 2.1. Actors, Headers and Role Authority

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user acts. Six roles exist, and their spellings are exact: `SystemOperator`, `PrivacyOfficer`, `ComplianceApprover`, `VendorManager`, `HRAdministrator`, and `Subject`. A request that omits either header, or that supplies an `X-User-Role` value outside those six, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). A request whose role is well-formed but not permitted for the operation is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).

`SystemOperator` is the platform operator: it provisions programs and state statutory profiles, amends those profiles after legislative change, runs the scheduled destruction sweep, and drives the logical clock. `PrivacyOfficer` owns the inventory, the policy drafting loop, notice packets, incident containment and exposure drafting. `ComplianceApprover` is the independent second pair of hands: it publishes policies, kicks them back for revision, approves vendor contracts, grants notification exemptions and finalizes exposure assessments. `VendorManager` registers processors, records their attestations, drafts contracts and drives them from execution through termination and close-out. `HRAdministrator` is the front line: it registers data subjects, solicits and records consent, and requests and provisions alternative processes. `Subject` is the individual whose biometrics are at stake.

Identity semantics differ between the operator roles and the subject role, and the distinction is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `SystemOperator`, `PrivacyOfficer`, `ComplianceApprover`, `VendorManager`, or `HRAdministrator`, the `X-User-ID` value is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition asks it to — as `submitted_by_user_id` and `published_by_user_id` on a policy, `drafted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a contract, `created_by_user_id` and `finalized_by_user_id` on an exposure assessment — and it compares recorded operator ids against each other for the separation-of-duties rules in §2.7, but it never compares an operator `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Subject`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `DataSubject`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `DataSubject` compare that resource's subject reference to `X-User-ID`. A `Subject` may read its own subject profile, its own consent records and its own accommodations, and may revoke its own granted consent; when the referenced resource belongs to a different individual the request is rejected with `ERR_SUBJECT_MISMATCH` (403).

Several endpoints are shared by more than one role, and on those the authorization check is not merely "is this role listed" but "is this role listed for the requested `to_status`". Policy lifecycle moves accept `PrivacyOfficer` only for a `to_status` of `IN_REVIEW` or `RETIRED`, and `ComplianceApprover` only for a `to_status` of `DRAFT`. Contract lifecycle moves accept `ComplianceApprover` only for `APPROVED`, and `VendorManager` only for `EXECUTED`, `TERMINATED`, `CLOSED_OUT` or `VOID`. Exposure assessment moves accept `ComplianceApprover` for either `FINAL` or `VOID`, and `PrivacyOfficer` only for `VOID`. Consent lifecycle moves accept `Subject` only for `REVOKED`. Incident notification moves accept `PrivacyOfficer` for `ASSESSED` and `NOTIFIED`, and `ComplianceApprover` only for `EXEMPT`. A role-and-target combination outside these grants yields `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Filtering

Collection reads are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` skips rows from the front of the ordered result and defaults to 0; its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range of 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending.

Lists also accept domain filters, and every filter is optional. State statutory profiles may be narrowed by `program_id` and `state_code`. Vendors accept `program_id`. Vendor contracts accept `vendor_id` and `status`. Policies accept `program_id` and `status`. Notice packets accept `program_id`, `jurisdiction_id`, `policy_id`, `audience` and `status`. Collection points accept `program_id`, `jurisdiction_id`, `vendor_id`, `pinned_policy_id` and `status`. Data subjects accept `program_id`, `jurisdiction_id` and `enrollment_status`. Consent records accept `data_subject_id`, `asset_id`, `pinned_policy_id` and `status`. Accommodations accept `data_subject_id`, `asset_id`, `created_from_consent_id`, `status` and `origin`. Incident cases accept `program_id`, `containment_status` and `notification_status`. Exposure assessments accept `program_id`, `jurisdiction_id`, `incident_id` and `status`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource in this system — compliance programs, state statutory profiles, vendors, vendor contracts, policies, notice packets, collection points, data subjects, consent records, accommodations, incident cases and exposure assessments — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no embedded semantics.
- `created_at` (string): the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the row was first written, rendered with three fractional second digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`).
- `updated_at` (string): the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the most recent mutation, in the same format. Every mutation described anywhere in this document refreshes it.
- `version` (integer): a counter that increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. No entity in this system is exempt from `updated_at` or `version`; both are always present.

All time-dependent behaviour reads a single server-controlled **logical UTC clock**. Retention horizons, notice lead times, accommodation windows and SLAs, contract deletion deadlines, incident notification deadlines and the destruction sweep all evaluate against that clock, never against wall-clock time. Before the clock is ever set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; the server must never fall back to real time. A `SystemOperator` may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, and the response reports the resulting `current_utc`. Moving the clock mutates nothing: no entity is written, no `version` is incremented, and no deadline effect materializes until some later request or sweep actually reads the clock.

### 2.5. Validation Precedence

Because most operations in this system are guarded by several independent rules at once, the order in which those rules fire is normative. A request that violates more than one rule always reports the violation with the lowest priority number below. Implementations must evaluate in exactly this sequence:

1. **Header authentication.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present, and `X-User-Role` one of the six declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role authority.** The acting role is permitted for the endpoint, and on shared lifecycle endpoints permitted for the requested `to_status` per §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. **Body well-formedness.** Required fields present; enum values members of their declared sets; integers non-negative where constrained; datetimes parseable as ISO 8601; arrays non-empty where required; `to_status` a member of the target entity's status enum; pagination within range. This step also enforces the per-transition body requirements (`decline_reason`, `revocation_reason`, `containment_note`, `exemption_basis`, `void_reason`, `deletion_certificate_ref`, `notified_subject_count`, `signature_ref`) and the per-program uniqueness of a state profile's `state_code`, a data subject's `external_ref` and a policy's `version_label`. Any failure yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
4. **Reference existence**, resolved left to right per §2.11 — otherwise the entity-specific 404.
5. **Subject ownership.** When the acting role is `Subject`, `X-User-ID` must equal the `DataSubject` `id` that owns the target row — otherwise `ERR_SUBJECT_MISMATCH`.
6. **Stale-read rejection.** The supplied version-pinning field must match its parent's current `version` per §2.8 — otherwise the rule's declared 412.
7. **Separation of duties.** The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the recorded actor of the preceding step per §2.7 — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
8. **Transition legality.** The pair formed by the row's current status and the requested `to_status` must be a declared edge for that entity; and for consent lifecycle operations the target collection point must additionally be in a collectable status. An undeclared edge — including a repeated trigger against an already-advanced or terminal row — yields `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`; a legal edge blocked only because the collection point is `DISCOVERED`, `CLASSIFIED` or `DECOMMISSIONED` yields `ERR_ASSET_NOT_COLLECTABLE` instead.
9. **Program posture gating** per §2.6 — otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_POSTURE_BLOCKED`.
10. **Regulatory prerequisites** for beginning or continuing collection: a published in-scope policy (`ERR_POLICY_NOT_PUBLISHED`), a published notice covering the jurisdiction and audience (`ERR_NOTICE_NOT_PUBLISHED`), notice locales covering every required locale where translation is mandated (`ERR_TRANSLATION_LOCALE_MISSING`), an acknowledged written release where the statute demands one (`ERR_WRITTEN_RELEASE_REQUIRED`), and an operating processor whose risk score is inside the gate (`ERR_VENDOR_RISK_TOO_HIGH`).
11. **Time-window rules**: notice lead time not yet elapsed (`ERR_NOTICE_LEAD_NOT_MET`), overlapping published notice windows (`ERR_NOTICE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`), colliding accommodation windows (`ERR_ACCOMMODATION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`), and close-out attempted after the deletion deadline (`ERR_DELETION_DEADLINE_MISSED`).
12. **Threshold and configuration constraints**: policy retention above a statutory ceiling (`ERR_RETENTION_EXCEEDS_JURISDICTION_MAX`), contract compliance score below the floor (`ERR_CONTRACT_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR`), an accommodation method not permitted for the collection surface (`ERR_ACCOMMODATION_METHOD_NOT_PERMITTED`), and a notified count short of the exposed count (`ERR_NOTIFIED_COUNT_SHORTFALL`).
13. **Cross-entity referential coherence**: incomplete contract clauses (`ERR_VENDOR_CONTRACT_NONCOMPLIANT`), an open consent gap on re-activation (`ERR_CONSENT_GAP_OPEN`), a subject and collection point in different states (`ERR_JURISDICTION_MISMATCH`), a subject population that does not match the collection point's population (`ERR_SUBJECT_CLASS_MISMATCH`), a duplicate live consent for the same subject-and-point pair (`ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_EXISTS`), a second executed contract for one vendor (`ERR_DUPLICATE_EXECUTED_CONTRACT`), a second final assessment for one state (`ERR_ASSESSMENT_ALREADY_FINAL`), dependents still active (`ERR_ASSET_STILL_ACTIVE`), notification unresolved before remediation (`ERR_NOTIFICATION_OUTSTANDING`), and an exemption claimed where none is available (`ERR_EXEMPTION_NOT_AVAILABLE`).
14. **Fiscal ceiling.** Finalizing an exposure assessment must not push the program's carried exposure past its board-approved ceiling — otherwise `ERR_EXPOSURE_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

The practical consequence is worth stating plainly: a frozen program surfaces as `ERR_PROGRAM_POSTURE_BLOCKED` rather than as the missing-policy or missing-notice error that would also have applied, because posture is checked at step 9 and regulatory prerequisites at step 10. Equally, a separation-of-duties violation is reported even when the target row is in the wrong status for the requested move, because step 7 precedes step 8.

### 2.6. Program Posture as the Master Gate

The `ComplianceProgram` row carries a computed `posture` field, one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`, initialized to `OPEN` at registration. Posture is a derived gate, not a stored decision: it is recomputed at the end of every mutation that can change its inputs — collection point activation, gating and decommissioning; consent grant, decline, revocation, expiry and re-solicitation; contract termination and close-out; policy publication; exposure assessment finalization and voiding; the move of an incident's containment dimension from `CONTAINED` to `REMEDIATED`; and each run of the destruction sweep.

Recomputation gathers five inputs. `gated_asset_count` is the number of the program's collection points whose status is `GATED`. `consent_gap_count` on the program is the sum of the per-point `consent_gap_count` values across those of its collection points whose status is `ACTIVE` or `GATED`. The **open deletion obligations** are those vendor contracts, reachable through the program's vendors, whose status is `TERMINATED` and whose `deletion_certificate_ref` is still null; their count is published as `open_deletion_obligation_count`. The **overdue deletion obligations** are the subset of those whose `deletion_deadline_at` lies strictly before the current logical clock. `pra_exposure_cents` is the sum of `exposure_cents` across the program's exposure assessments whose status is `FINAL`.

The three postures are then tested in a pinned precedence — freeze conditions first, restriction second, and `OPEN` only as the fallthrough:

1. If at least one overdue deletion obligation exists, `posture` becomes `FROZEN`.
2. Otherwise, if `exposure_ceiling_cents` is greater than zero and carried exposure has reached at least eighty percent of it, `posture` becomes `FROZEN`. The comparison is evaluated entirely in integers — one hundred times `pra_exposure_cents` against eighty times `exposure_ceiling_cents` — so no floating-point drift is possible and exactly eighty percent does freeze the program.
3. Otherwise, if `gated_asset_count` is greater than zero, or the program's `consent_gap_count` is greater than zero, or at least one open deletion obligation exists, `posture` becomes `RESTRICTED`.
4. Otherwise `posture` becomes `OPEN`. A program with no collection points, no contracts and no final assessments is therefore `OPEN`.

Posture then gates operations asymmetrically, and the asymmetry is deliberate. `FROZEN` blocks the activation of a `CLASSIFIED` collection point, the re-activation of a `GATED` one, policy publication, notice publication, consent solicitation and consent granting. `RESTRICTED` blocks strictly less: only the activation of a `CLASSIFIED` collection point, and consent solicitation against a collection point whose status is anything other than `ACTIVE`. `RESTRICTED` deliberately does **not** block the re-activation of a `GATED` collection point — otherwise a gated point could never recover, because gating is itself one of the conditions that produces `RESTRICTED`. Any operation blocked by posture is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_POSTURE_BLOCKED` (409).

### 2.7. Separation of Duties

Three approvals in this system must be performed by someone other than the person who prepared the work, and the server enforces that mechanically by comparing the acting `X-User-ID` against a recorded operator id. Publishing a policy requires an `X-User-ID` different from the policy's `submitted_by_user_id`. Approving a vendor contract requires an `X-User-ID` different from the contract's `drafted_by_user_id`. Finalizing an exposure assessment requires an `X-User-ID` different from the assessment's `created_by_user_id`. Any of the three performed by the same user is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). The complementary ids — `published_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, `finalized_by_user_id` — are stamped from the acting `X-User-ID` when the approval succeeds, and are null before it.

### 2.8. Version Pinning and Stale-Read Rejection

Two operations in this system are priced or decided from a parent row that other writers can amend underneath them. Both therefore require the client to pin the parent `version` it last observed, and both reject a mismatch outright.

Publishing a policy requires `expected_program_version` in the request body. It is not optional. Its value must equal the current `version` of the `ComplianceProgram` that owns the policy; the canonical read of that number is the program's single-resource read. If any other writer has advanced the program's `version` since the approver last read it, the publication is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` (412). Publication legitimately supersedes whatever policy was published before it, so a second publication of a different version would succeed on its own merits — the stale pin is the only signal that the approver's picture of the program (its posture, its currently published policy, its consent-gap counters) predates another writer's completed publication.

Drafting an exposure assessment requires `expected_jurisdiction_version` in the request body. It is likewise not optional. Its value must equal the current `version` of the `Jurisdiction` named on the draft; the canonical read of that number is the state profile's single-resource read. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_JURISDICTION_VERSION` (412). A new assessment legitimately supersedes an earlier draft for the same state, so the create would otherwise succeed; the stale pin shows that the statutory parameters the estimate is priced from — the per-violation amounts, the cure period, the statutory cap — have been amended since the drafter read them, which would silently produce an exposure figure priced off withdrawn law.

No other endpoint takes a version-pinning field. Requests to endpoints outside these two must not be expected to carry one, and the presence of an unexpected pin is not itself an error condition defined by this document.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Derived Counter Maintenance

A great many mutations in this system touch more than one row. Two multi-hop chains are named and referred to by name throughout §3: **Cascade A**, fired by the termination of an executed vendor contract, and **Cascade B**, fired by the publication of a policy. Both are described hop by hop where the workflow lives — Cascade A in §3.2 and Cascade B in §3.3 — and neither is optional or partial. Three general rules govern all of them.

First, **every hop is a real mutation.** A row moved by a cascade refreshes its own `updated_at` and increments its own `version` by 1, exactly as if a client had moved it. Derived counters are not exempt: writing `posture`, `consent_gap_count`, `gated_asset_count`, `open_deletion_obligation_count`, `pra_exposure_cents` or `inventory_completeness_pct` onto a program during a cascade is a mutation of that program.

Second, **cascade order is deterministic.** When a hop applies to a set of rows, those rows are processed in `created_at` ascending order, and each hop is applied to its entire row set before the next hop begins. This matters most in Cascade B, where every affected consent record must be moved before any collection point re-pins its policy.

Third, **derived fields are recomputed, never patched.** `enrolled_subject_count` on a collection point is the count of its consent records whose status is `GRANTED`. `consent_gap_count` on a collection point is the count of its consent records whose status is `SOLICITED`, `RESOLICIT_REQUIRED` or `EXPIRED` — a `DECLINED` record and a `REVOKED` record are lawful outcomes and are explicitly **not** gaps, and a registered data subject who has no consent record at all is likewise not a gap until a record is actually solicited. `active_accommodation_count` on a data subject is the count of its accommodations whose status is `PROVISIONED` or `ACTIVE`. `enrollment_status` on a data subject is derived in a strict priority order: it is `OPTED_OUT` if any of that subject's consent records is `DECLINED` or `REVOKED` and none is `GRANTED`; otherwise `ENROLLED` if any is `GRANTED`; otherwise `ALTERNATIVE` if any of the subject's accommodations is `PROVISIONED` or `ACTIVE`; otherwise `NOT_ENROLLED`.

One read is deliberately a read-through: reading a single compliance program recomputes `inventory_completeness_pct` and `posture` from current child state before responding, but does **not** refresh `updated_at` and does **not** increment the program's `version`.

### 2.10. Numeric Precision and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary quantity in this system is an integer count of cents, and every field whose name ends in `_cents` is such an integer. Percentages, scores and multipliers are decimal numbers carried to exactly 2 decimal places using round-half-up, unless the rule that produces them says otherwise. Four precision commitments are normative and are restated where each calculation is described, because getting any of them wrong changes published figures by whole cents:

- The vendor contract compliance score rounds half-up to 2 decimal places **once per clause** and then **once more** on the final clamped total. Summing raw clause products and rounding only at the end produces a different figure and is wrong.
- The private-right-of-action exposure figure applies **exactly one** round-half-up, to zero decimal places, to the fully multiplied product — never to an individual factor and never to an intermediate product.
- The vendor indemnity credit ceiling on an incident is **truncated toward zero**, not rounded, so an odd gross notification cost yields the lower cent.
- The vendor risk score is an integer point total clamped to the inclusive range 0 through 100. The inventory completeness percentage is a 2-decimal round-half-up figure, and an empty inventory yields exactly `100.00` rather than a division by zero.

Day arithmetic adds whole days to an ISO 8601 UTC instant and preserves the time of day; the resulting instant is rendered with three fractional second digits like every other timestamp.

### 2.11. Reference Resolution Order and Not-Found Reporting

Requests are checked for referential existence before any business rule runs, and the checks proceed strictly left to right: first the identifier embedded in the URL path, then the foreign keys carried in the body, in the order they appear in the endpoint's input list — `program_id`, then `jurisdiction_id`, then `policy_id`, then `vendor_id`, then `asset_id`, then `data_subject_id`, then `incident_id`, and finally the members of `affected_asset_ids` in index order. The response reports the first reference that fails to resolve, and nothing further is evaluated.

The path identifiers are, respectively, `program_id` for a compliance program, `jurisdiction_id` for a state statutory profile, `vendor_id` for a vendor, `contract_id` for a vendor contract, `policy_id` for a policy, `notice_id` for a notice packet, `asset_id` for a collection point, `subject_id` for a data subject, `consent_id` for a consent record, `accommodation_id` for an accommodation, `incident_id` for an incident case and `assessment_id` for an exposure assessment. Each maps to its own 404: `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBJECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCOMMODATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`. Two array cases deserve explicit mention. An unresolvable identifier listed inside a policy's `jurisdiction_scope` is a state-profile reference, so it reports `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_FOUND` and not `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`; the members of that array are checked in index order. Likewise, any unresolvable member of `affected_asset_ids` reports `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Program and Its State Statutory Profiles

A `SystemOperator` opens the engagement by registering a **compliance program** — the root aggregate for one employer or retailer. Registration supplies the entity's legal name as `org_name`, an `org_type` of `EMPLOYER`, `RETAILER` or `HYBRID` describing whether biometrics are collected from workers, shoppers or both, and an `exposure_ceiling_cents` value: the board-approved maximum aggregate private-right-of-action exposure the program may carry across all of its final assessments. The ceiling must not be negative. A freshly registered program comes up with `posture` at `OPEN`, `inventory_completeness_pct` at exactly `100.00` under the empty-inventory convention of §3.5, and every derived counter — `consent_gap_count`, `gated_asset_count`, `open_deletion_obligation_count` and `pra_exposure_cents` — at zero.

Onto that program the operator layers one **state statutory profile** per state whose law the organization must satisfy. Each profile names its owning program through `program_id` and identifies itself by `state_code`, exactly two uppercase letters, unique within the program; a duplicate is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The profile records the controlling statute's short name as `statute_name`, whether private plaintiffs may sue through `private_right_of_action` (`NONE`, `LIMITED` or `FULL`), and whether damages are statutory or must be proved through `damages_model` (`STATUTORY_PER_VIOLATION` or `ACTUAL_ONLY`). Three consent-mechanics flags follow: `requires_written_release`, true when consent is valid only with an executed written release; `translation_required`, true when notices and consents must be issued in every locale the profile lists; and `required_locales`, the array of locale tags those notices must cover, defaulting to an empty array. The profile also fixes `retention_max_days`, the statutory ceiling on template retention measured in days from the start of collection, which must be at least 1, and `notice_lead_days`, the number of days that must elapse between publishing a notice and the first lawful consent solicitation under it, which may be zero. Finally it carries the pricing inputs for §3.9: `per_violation_negligent_cents` and `per_violation_reckless_cents`, the statutory damages per negligent and per reckless or intentional violation; `cure_period_days`, the notice-and-cure window in days, where zero means no cure right exists; and `statutory_cap_cents`, a hard cap on aggregate exposure for that state which defaults to null, meaning uncapped. All four must be non-negative when supplied.

Registering a state profile increments the owning program's `version` by 1 — which is exactly why the program's `version` is the thing a policy publication must pin, per §2.8. Statutory profiles are also amendable, since legislatures move: a `SystemOperator` may amend `private_right_of_action`, `damages_model`, `requires_written_release`, `translation_required`, `required_locales`, `retention_max_days`, `notice_lead_days`, `per_violation_negligent_cents`, `per_violation_reckless_cents`, `cure_period_days` or `statutory_cap_cents` on an existing profile. Every amendment increments that profile's own `version` by 1 — the number the exposure-assessment pin observes — and, because a statutory retention ceiling has just moved, it recomputes the destruction horizon (per §3.5) for every collection point in that state whose status is `ACTIVE` or `GATED`.

### 3.2. Vendor Due Diligence and Contract Governance

Most biometric collection is operated by somebody else's software, and the statute makes the organization answerable for what that processor does. A `VendorManager` therefore registers each processor as a **vendor** under a `program_id`, supplying the vendor's legal `name` and its disclosed posture: `sells_biometric_data`, true when the vendor's own disclosures admit sale or trade of biometric identifiers; `subprocessor_count`, the number of downstream subprocessors it discloses, which may not be negative; `deletion_sla_days`, the number of days it commits to destroying templates after a deletion instruction, also non-negative; and optionally `subprocessor_disclosure_complete`, which defaults to false and becomes true only once the vendor has named every subprocessor in writing. Registering a vendor increments the owning program's `version` by 1.

A vendor's written biometric-handling attestation has its own small lifecycle, carried in `attestation_status` and initialized to `NOT_REQUESTED`. Only the dedicated attestation operation advances it, and only along the permitted order `NOT_REQUESTED` → `REQUESTED` → either `RECEIVED` or `REJECTED`, plus a re-request edge `REJECTED` → `REQUESTED`. `RECEIVED` is terminal. Any other pair — jumping `NOT_REQUESTED` → `RECEIVED`, or backing `RECEIVED` → `REQUESTED` — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`. The same operation may also record that `subprocessor_disclosure_complete` has become true. It increments the vendor's `version` by 1, recomputes the vendor's `risk_score`, and recomputes the compliance score of every one of that vendor's contracts whose status is not `VOID` and not `CLOSED_OUT`: a contract that has been voided or closed out is frozen, and later attestation movement never re-prices it.

**The vendor risk score.** A vendor's `risk_score` is an integer point total, computed at registration and recomputed on every attestation change. It starts from a base of 20 points. Thirty points are added when the vendor admits selling biometric data. Five points are added for each disclosed subprocessor, counting at most six of them — so a vendor with nine subprocessors contributes the same thirty points as a vendor with six. Fifteen points are added when the vendor's committed deletion SLA is strictly longer than thirty days. The attestation itself contributes 20 points while `NOT_REQUESTED`, 10 points while `REQUESTED`, nothing once `RECEIVED`, and a punitive 35 points when `REJECTED`. A further 10 points are added while `subprocessor_disclosure_complete` is false. The total is then clamped into the inclusive range 0 through 100. The gate that matters is the one applied when a collection point is activated: the operating vendor's `risk_score` must be **at most 70**. A score of exactly 70 passes; a score of 75 fails and yields `ERR_VENDOR_RISK_TOO_HIGH`.

**The processing contract.** A `VendorManager` drafts a **vendor contract** against a `vendor_id`, and the draft is where the statutory clause requirements are captured: `prohibits_sale` for the clause forbidding sale, lease or trade of biometric identifiers; `restricts_disclosure` for the clause confining disclosure to named purposes and parties; and `requires_deletion_on_termination` for the clause compelling template destruction when the engagement ends. The draft also fixes `deletion_deadline_days`, the contractual number of days after termination by which the vendor must certify deletion; `indemnity_cap_cents`, the per-incident indemnity ceiling the vendor accepts, which feeds the incident credit in §3.8; and `effective_from`, the ISO 8601 UTC start of the contract term. Neither `deletion_deadline_days` nor `indemnity_cap_cents` may be negative. Drafting records the acting `X-User-ID` as `drafted_by_user_id`, computes `compliance_score` immediately, and leaves the contract in `DRAFT` with `approved_by_user_id`, `terminated_at`, `deletion_deadline_at` and `deletion_certificate_ref` all null.

**The compliance score.** A contract's `compliance_score` is a weighted clause score discounted by how far the vendor has got with its attestation, and its rounding order is normative. Four clauses carry point values: thirty-five points for `prohibits_sale`, twenty-five points for `restricts_disclosure`, thirty points for `requires_deletion_on_termination`, and ten points for a deletion deadline that is at most thirty days. A clause that is absent — a false flag, or a `deletion_deadline_days` value greater than thirty — contributes zero points. Each clause's point value is then multiplied by the vendor's attestation factor, which is 0.715 while the attestation is `NOT_REQUESTED`, 0.855 while `REQUESTED`, 1.000 once `RECEIVED`, and 0.000 when `REJECTED`. **Each of those four products is rounded half-up to two decimal places individually, and only then are the four rounded figures summed.** Summing the raw products and rounding once at the end produces a different cent figure and is not the specified behaviour. From that sum, two penalties are then subtracted, in this order: 7.5 points when the vendor admits selling biometric data, and 2.5 points for each disclosed subprocessor counting at most four of them (so a vendor with seven subprocessors incurs the same ten-point penalty as one with four). The penalized figure is clamped upward at zero and downward at 100, and the clamped result is rounded half-up to two decimal places to give `compliance_score`. The floor the score must clear, both to execute a contract and to be relied on when activating a collection point, is **82.50**.

**The contract lifecycle.** A contract begins in `DRAFT` and can leave it two ways. A `VendorManager` may abandon it, moving `DRAFT` → `VOID`, which is terminal. Otherwise a `ComplianceApprover` moves it `DRAFT` → `APPROVED`; that approval demands an `X-User-ID` different from `drafted_by_user_id` (else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`) and demands that all three clauses — `prohibits_sale`, `restricts_disclosure` and `requires_deletion_on_termination` — are true, rejecting an incomplete clause set with `ERR_VENDOR_CONTRACT_NONCOMPLIANT`. Approval stamps `approved_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and recomputes `compliance_score`.

A `VendorManager` then moves `APPROVED` → `EXECUTED`. Execution is refused if any other contract for the same vendor is already `EXECUTED` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_EXECUTED_CONTRACT`) — a vendor may carry at most one executed contract at a time — and refused if `compliance_score` is below 82.50 (`ERR_CONTRACT_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR`). Execution recomputes `compliance_score` once more.

**Cascade A: termination.** Moving `EXECUTED` → `TERMINATED` is the most consequential single action in the system, because collection that was lawful only through that processor stops being lawful the moment the engagement ends. A `VendorManager` triggers it. The contract's `terminated_at` is stamped from the current logical clock and its `deletion_deadline_at` is set to `terminated_at` plus `deletion_deadline_days`. Then five hops run in order:

- **Hop 1** is the contract's own move to `TERMINATED`, refreshing its `updated_at` and incrementing its `version` by 1.
- **Hop 2**: every collection point operated by that vendor whose status is `ACTIVE` transitions `ACTIVE` → `GATED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order.
- **Hop 3**: because those points became `GATED`, every consent record on them whose status is `GRANTED` transitions `GRANTED` → `EXPIRED`, again in `created_at` ascending order.
- **Hop 4**: because those consents expired, each expired consent whose data subject has a `subject_type` of `EMPLOYEE` spawns one alternative accommodation in `REQUESTED` status with `origin` set to `AUTO_CONSENT_EXPIRY`. Consents belonging to `CUSTOMER` subjects spawn nothing.
- **Hop 5**: because points became gated and consent gaps opened, the program's `gated_asset_count`, `consent_gap_count` and `open_deletion_obligation_count` are recomputed together with `posture`. In practice this alone is enough to drive an `OPEN` program to `RESTRICTED`, since the newly terminated contract has no destruction certificate yet.

From `TERMINATED`, the contract has one remaining move: a `VendorManager` closes it out, `TERMINATED` → `CLOSED_OUT`, which is terminal. Close-out requires `deletion_certificate_ref` in the request body — the reference to the vendor's signed destruction certificate, which is settable only here — and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when it is absent. Close-out is also time-bound: it is permitted only while the current logical clock is at or before `deletion_deadline_at`, and a clock strictly after that instant yields `ERR_DELETION_DEADLINE_MISSED`. This is where posture bites hardest, because a `TERMINATED` contract with a null certificate whose deadline has already passed is an *overdue* deletion obligation, and a single overdue obligation freezes the entire program. Successful close-out stores the certificate reference, recomputes `open_deletion_obligation_count` and recomputes `posture` — typically the step that lifts a program back out of `FROZEN`.

### 3.3. The Written Policy Lifecycle and the Supersession Cascade

The statute's threshold requirement is a written policy, publicly available, that states a retention schedule and destruction timelines. A `PrivacyOfficer` drafts one as a **biometric policy** under a `program_id`, labelling it with a `version_label` such as "v1.0" that must be unique within the program. The draft publishes `retention_days`, the retention period in days from the start of collection, which must be at least 1, and a `destruction_trigger` of either `FIXED_SCHEDULE` or `PURPOSE_SATISFIED_OR_MAX` — a choice that selects which of the two retention variants in §3.5 applies to every collection point pinned to this policy. It records `release_language_included`, true when the policy embeds the written-release language that consent validity depends on, and a `jurisdiction_scope`: a non-empty array of state-profile identifiers this version covers, each of which must resolve to a state profile under the same program. A collection point in a state outside the scope may not pin the policy. A new policy sits in `DRAFT` with `published_at`, `submitted_by_user_id` and `published_by_user_id` null and `revision_count` at zero.

Submission for review is a `PrivacyOfficer` action moving the policy `DRAFT` → `IN_REVIEW`. It enforces two substantive checks. If any state profile in `jurisdiction_scope` has `requires_written_release` true, then `release_language_included` must be true, or the submission fails with `ERR_WRITTEN_RELEASE_REQUIRED`. And for every state profile in the scope, the policy's `retention_days` must be at most that profile's `retention_max_days`; a policy promising to keep templates longer than any covered statute permits is rejected with `ERR_RETENTION_EXCEEDS_JURISDICTION_MAX`. Submission stamps `submitted_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`.

Review is genuinely two-way. A `ComplianceApprover` may kick a policy back, `IN_REVIEW` → `DRAFT`, which increments `revision_count` by 1 and clears `submitted_by_user_id` back to null so that the next submission re-stamps it — the officer who re-submits after a kick-back may be a different person, and separation of duties must be measured against the *latest* submitter. This revision edge is what makes the policy machine cyclic: `DRAFT` → `IN_REVIEW` → `DRAFT` can repeat indefinitely, with `revision_count` recording how often it has.

**Publication and Cascade B.** A `ComplianceApprover` publishes an `IN_REVIEW` policy, moving it `IN_REVIEW` → `PUBLISHED`. Four preconditions guard it: the policy must currently be `IN_REVIEW`; the request must carry an `expected_program_version` equal to the program's current `version`, or the publication is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` per §2.8; the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_user_id`, or `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; and the program's `posture` must not be `FROZEN`, or `ERR_PROGRAM_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. On success, `published_at` is stamped from the current logical clock and `published_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and then a four-hop cascade runs:

- **Hop 1**: the program's other policy whose status is `PUBLISHED` — at most one such row can exist — transitions `PUBLISHED` → `SUPERSEDED`. That transition itself refreshes the superseded policy's `updated_at` and increments its `version` by 1.
- **Hop 2**: because that policy was superseded, every consent record whose `pinned_policy_id` is the superseded policy and whose status is `GRANTED` transitions `GRANTED` → `RESOLICIT_REQUIRED`. Each such move decrements the owning collection point's `enrolled_subject_count` and increments its `consent_gap_count`, and re-derives the data subject's `enrollment_status`. Consent obtained under a policy that no longer governs is not consent; those individuals must be asked again.
- **Hop 3**: because those consents lost their grant, every collection point whose `pinned_policy_id` is the superseded policy and whose status is `ACTIVE` transitions `ACTIVE` → `GATED`, and re-pins its `pinned_policy_id` to the newly published policy when that point's state profile appears in the new policy's `jurisdiction_scope`. Hop 2 must be applied to **all** affected consent records before any collection point re-pins; otherwise a point would repin first and the consents pinned to the old policy would no longer be discoverable through it.
- **Hop 4**: because collection points became gated, the program's `posture` is recomputed — a positive `gated_asset_count` raises it to at least `RESTRICTED`.

Publication also recomputes the destruction horizon for every affected collection point, refreshing `destruction_due_at` under the newly pinned policy's retention terms, increments the program's `version` by 1, and refreshes the published policy's own `updated_at` and `version`.

The last edge closes the lifecycle: a `PrivacyOfficer` retires a superseded policy, `SUPERSEDED` → `RETIRED`, which is terminal. Retirement is refused while any collection point still pins that policy with a status of `ACTIVE` or `GATED`, reported as `ERR_ASSET_STILL_ACTIVE`. Note the asymmetry with notice withdrawal in §3.4: policy retirement is blocked by both `ACTIVE` and `GATED` dependents, because a gated point retains templates collected under that policy's schedule.

### 3.4. Publishing Notice to Employees and Customers

Before consent may be solicited, the affected population must have been told — in writing, in their language, with a channel for people who would rather not participate. A `PrivacyOfficer` drafts a **notice packet** naming its `program_id`, the `jurisdiction_id` whose notice rules it satisfies, and the `policy_id` of the published policy whose terms it reproduces. It declares an `audience` of either `EMPLOYEE` or `CUSTOMER`, a non-empty `locales` array recording the locale tags the packet was actually issued in, an `accommodation_channel` naming the contact route published for individuals who decline biometric enrollment, and an effective window: `effective_from`, an inclusive ISO 8601 UTC start, and an optional `effective_to`, an **exclusive** end that defaults to null, where null means the window is open-ended and extends to positive infinity. A packet begins in `DRAFT` with `published_at` and `consent_eligible_from` null.

Publication, `DRAFT` → `PUBLISHED`, is a `PrivacyOfficer` action with three substantive gates. The referenced policy must itself be `PUBLISHED`, or the request fails with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_PUBLISHED` — a notice cannot reproduce a policy that is still in draft or review. When the governing state profile has `translation_required` true, the packet's `locales` must be a superset of that profile's `required_locales`; a missing locale yields `ERR_TRANSLATION_LOCALE_MISSING`. And no other published packet for the same combination of `jurisdiction_id` and `audience` may occupy an overlapping window. Overlap is judged under **half-open** semantics on the interval from `effective_from` inclusive to `effective_to` exclusive, with a null `effective_to` treated as positive infinity; consequently two abutting windows, where the earlier packet's `effective_to` equals the later packet's `effective_from`, do **not** overlap and both may be published. A genuine intersection is rejected with `ERR_NOTICE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Publication is also posture-gated: a `FROZEN` program cannot publish notice, yielding `ERR_PROGRAM_POSTURE_BLOCKED`.

Successful publication stamps `published_at` from the current logical clock and computes `consent_eligible_from` as `published_at` plus the governing state profile's `notice_lead_days`. That derived instant is the statutory cooling-off boundary: it is the earliest instant at which a consent record may be solicited under this packet, and §3.6 enforces it.

Withdrawal, `PUBLISHED` → `WITHDRAWN`, is terminal and likewise a `PrivacyOfficer` action. It is refused while any collection point in the same state with a `subject_class` equal to the packet's `audience` is still `ACTIVE`, reported as `ERR_ASSET_STILL_ACTIVE`. Unlike policy retirement, a `GATED` point does not block withdrawal — gated points are not collecting, so the notice they relied on may lapse.

### 3.5. The Biometric Inventory and the Retention Clock

Everything else in this system hangs off knowing where biometrics are actually captured. A `PrivacyOfficer` inventories each capture surface as a **collection point**, supplying its `program_id`, the `jurisdiction_id` whose statute governs it, an optional `vendor_id` naming the processor that operates it (null when the surface is handled in-house), a `system_category` of `TIMEKEEPING`, `ACCESS_CONTROL`, `SECURITY_CAMERA` or `CUSTOMER_FACING`, a `modality` of `FINGERPRINT`, `FACE_GEOMETRY`, `VOICEPRINT`, `IRIS` or `HAND_GEOMETRY`, a `subject_class` of `EMPLOYEE` or `CUSTOMER` naming the population enrolled there, a `template_storage_location` identifying the system of record where templates or vectors persist, a `declared_retention_days` value of at least 1 recording the retention the surface owner claims before policy reconciliation, and a `retention_basis` of either `PURPOSE_LIMITED` or `SCHEDULE_ONLY`. A newly inventoried point is `DISCOVERED`, with `pinned_policy_id`, `collection_started_at`, `purpose_satisfied_at`, `destruction_due_at` and `templates_destroyed_at` all null and `enrolled_subject_count` and `consent_gap_count` at zero. Inventorying a point recomputes the program's inventory completeness and increments the program's `version` by 1.

A `PrivacyOfficer` may also update a non-decommissioned point's inventory attributes, revising `declared_retention_days`, `retention_basis` or `template_storage_location`, and — critically — reporting `purpose_satisfied_at`, the instant at which the collection purpose was actually fulfilled, which is null while the purpose remains live. Such an update recomputes `destruction_due_at` whenever the point already has both a pinned policy and a `collection_started_at`, recomputes the program's inventory completeness, and increments the point's `version` by 1.

**Inventory completeness.** The program's `inventory_completeness_pct` measures how much of the live inventory is properly documented. The denominator is every collection point of the program whose status is not `DECOMMISSIONED`; when that set is empty the figure is exactly `100.00` by convention rather than an undefined division. The numerator counts those points that satisfy three conditions simultaneously: status is `CLASSIFIED`, `ACTIVE` or `GATED`; `template_storage_location` is not the empty string; and either `vendor_id` is null or an executed contract exists for that vendor. The percentage is one hundred times the numerator divided by the denominator, rounded half-up to two decimal places. Because decommissioned points leave the denominator entirely, decommissioning an undocumented `DISCOVERED` point raises the figure. The measure is recomputed when a point is inventoried, on each of the classification, activation and decommission transitions, and as a read-through whenever the program itself is read.

**Classification.** Moving `DISCOVERED` → `CLASSIFIED` is the officer's confirmation that the surface has been analysed. Its one substantive gate is vendor analysis: either `vendor_id` is null, or the named vendor has at least one contract whose status is `APPROVED` or `EXECUTED`. A vendor with nothing but drafts and voids is not analysable, and classification fails with `ERR_VENDOR_CONTRACT_NONCOMPLIANT`. Classification recomputes inventory completeness.

**Activation.** Moving `CLASSIFIED` → `ACTIVE` is the moment lawful collection begins, and it carries the heaviest precondition set in the system. The point must be `CLASSIFIED`. The program's `posture` must be neither `RESTRICTED` nor `FROZEN`, else `ERR_PROGRAM_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. Exactly one policy under the point's program must have status `PUBLISHED` and list the point's `jurisdiction_id` in its `jurisdiction_scope`, else `ERR_POLICY_NOT_PUBLISHED`. A notice packet with status `PUBLISHED` whose `jurisdiction_id` equals the point's and whose `audience` equals the point's `subject_class` must exist, else `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_PUBLISHED`. Where the governing state profile has `translation_required` true, that packet's `locales` must be a superset of `required_locales`, else `ERR_TRANSLATION_LOCALE_MISSING`. If the point names an operating vendor, that vendor's `risk_score` must be at most 70, else `ERR_VENDOR_RISK_TOO_HIGH`; and that vendor must have a contract whose status is `EXECUTED` and whose `compliance_score` is at least 82.50, else `ERR_CONTRACT_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR`. Points with a null `vendor_id` skip both vendor gates.

Activation then pins `pinned_policy_id` to that single published in-scope policy, stamps `collection_started_at` from the current logical clock **only when it is currently null** — that is, on the very first activation and never again — computes `destruction_due_at`, and recomputes the program's `consent_gap_count`, its inventory completeness and its `posture`.

**The retention horizon.** A point's `destruction_due_at` is derived from its pinned policy, its state profile and its own retention basis. First, the **effective retention** in days is the smaller of the pinned policy's `retention_days` and the state profile's `retention_max_days` — a policy may promise a shorter life than the statute allows but never a longer one. The **schedule horizon** is `collection_started_at` plus that many whole days. Then one of two variants applies:

- The *fixed-schedule* variant governs whenever the pinned policy's `destruction_trigger` is `FIXED_SCHEDULE`, **or** the point's `retention_basis` is `SCHEDULE_ONLY`, **or** `purpose_satisfied_at` is null. Under it, `destruction_due_at` is simply the schedule horizon, and a reported `purpose_satisfied_at` is ignored entirely.
- The *purpose-satisfied-or-max* variant governs only when all three conditions hold together: the pinned policy's `destruction_trigger` is `PURPOSE_SATISFIED_OR_MAX`, the point's `retention_basis` is `PURPOSE_LIMITED`, and `purpose_satisfied_at` is not null. Under it, `destruction_due_at` is the earlier of two instants: `purpose_satisfied_at` plus a seven-day grace period, or the schedule horizon. Identical retention inputs therefore destroy templates strictly earlier under this variant whenever the graced purpose instant precedes the horizon.

A point whose `pinned_policy_id` is null or whose `collection_started_at` is null has a null `destruction_due_at` and is excluded from the calculation altogether. The horizon is recomputed on first activation, on re-activation, whenever inventory attributes are updated, whenever the supersession cascade touches the point, and on every destruction sweep. Because `collection_started_at` is stamped once and only once, a re-activation recomputes the horizon from the **original** start of collection and never restarts the retention clock — a point that is gated and revived does not get a fresh retention allowance.

**Gating and revival.** `ACTIVE` → `GATED` may be driven deliberately by a `PrivacyOfficer` or by the system through Cascade A hop 2 or Cascade B hop 3. When it is driven by Cascade A hop 2 specifically, it triggers hop 3 on every consent record for that point whose status is `GRANTED`, moving each `GRANTED` → `EXPIRED`. In all cases gating recomputes the program's `gated_asset_count` and `posture`. The reverse edge, `GATED` → `ACTIVE`, is a `PrivacyOfficer` revival and is what makes this machine cyclic. It requires that the program's `posture` is not `FROZEN` (else `ERR_PROGRAM_POSTURE_BLOCKED`); that the point's own `consent_gap_count` is exactly zero, so that no individual is left in an unresolved consent state at a point about to resume collecting (else `ERR_CONSENT_GAP_OPEN`); and, when a vendor operates the point, that the vendor still has an `EXECUTED` contract whose `compliance_score` is at least 82.50 (else `ERR_CONTRACT_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR`). Revival recomputes `destruction_due_at` from the original collection start, then `gated_asset_count` and `posture`.

**Decommissioning.** A point reaches the terminal `DECOMMISSIONED` state from either of two places, both `PrivacyOfficer` actions. `CLASSIFIED` → `DECOMMISSIONED` is permitted only while **no** consent record exists for that point at all; the move recomputes inventory completeness. `GATED` → `DECOMMISSIONED` is always permitted, and that path is a full teardown: `templates_destroyed_at` is stamped from the current logical clock; every consent record for the point whose status is `SOLICITED`, `GRANTED` or `RESOLICIT_REQUIRED` transitions to `EXPIRED`, and **no** accommodation is auto-created for any of those expiries because the collection point being bypassed no longer exists; every accommodation for the point whose status is `REQUESTED`, `PROVISIONED` or `ACTIVE` transitions to `ENDED`; and the program's `gated_asset_count`, `consent_gap_count`, inventory completeness and `posture` are all recomputed. There is no edge from `ACTIVE` straight to `DECOMMISSIONED`: a live point must be gated first.

**The scheduled destruction sweep.** Retention obligations are not self-executing, so a `SystemOperator` runs a program-scoped destruction sweep at the current logical clock. For every collection point of the program whose status is `ACTIVE` or `GATED`, whose `destruction_due_at` is not null, and whose `destruction_due_at` is at or before the current clock, the sweep stamps `templates_destroyed_at` and, when the point was `ACTIVE`, transitions it to `GATED`. Every consent record on those points whose status is `GRANTED` then transitions `GRANTED` → `EXPIRED` — and, unlike the vendor-termination path, the sweep auto-creates **no** accommodation for these expiries, because a retention-driven expiry is a scheduled destruction event rather than a refusal to participate. The sweep additionally sets `sla_breached` to true on every accommodation of the program whose status is still `REQUESTED` and whose `sla_due_at` has passed. Finally it recomputes `gated_asset_count`, `consent_gap_count` and `posture`, and increments the program's `version` by 1. The sweep reports what it did as `swept_asset_count`, `destroyed_template_count`, `expired_consent_count`, `sla_breached_accommodation_count` and the resulting `program_posture`.

### 3.6. Registering Data Subjects and Soliciting Consent

An `HRAdministrator` registers each individual whose biometrics may be captured as a **data subject** of the program, naming the `program_id` and the `jurisdiction_id` of the state whose statute protects them, a `subject_type` of `EMPLOYEE` or `CUSTOMER`, an `external_ref` such as an employee number or loyalty identifier that must be unique within the program, a `preferred_locale` recording the locale tag the individual reads, and optionally `accommodation_needed`, defaulting to false and set true when the individual asks for a non-biometric process at intake. A new subject has `enrollment_status` of `NOT_ENROLLED` and `active_accommodation_count` of zero, both derived thereafter per §2.9.

Soliciting consent creates a **consent record** against a `data_subject_id` and an `asset_id`, recording the `locale` the consent form was actually presented in and optionally `written_release_acknowledged` (defaulting to false) and `signature_ref` (defaulting to null). The solicitation is the point at which the whole regulatory stack is checked, in the precedence of §2.5:

- The collection point must be in a collectable status — `ACTIVE` or `GATED` — else `ERR_ASSET_NOT_COLLECTABLE`.
- Posture must permit it: a `FROZEN` program cannot solicit consent at all, and a `RESTRICTED` program cannot solicit consent against a collection point whose status is anything other than `ACTIVE`. Either way, `ERR_PROGRAM_POSTURE_BLOCKED`.
- A published in-scope policy must exist (`ERR_POLICY_NOT_PUBLISHED`), a published notice packet must cover the point's state and population (`ERR_NOTICE_NOT_PUBLISHED`), and where the state profile mandates translation the notice locales must cover every required locale (`ERR_TRANSLATION_LOCALE_MISSING`).
- The current logical clock must be at or after the covering published packet's `consent_eligible_from`. Soliciting inside the statutory lead time is rejected with `ERR_NOTICE_LEAD_NOT_MET`.
- The subject's `jurisdiction_id` must equal the collection point's `jurisdiction_id`, else `ERR_JURISDICTION_MISMATCH`; and the subject's `subject_type` must equal the point's `subject_class`, else `ERR_SUBJECT_CLASS_MISMATCH`. An employee may not be enrolled at a customer-facing lane, and a shopper may not be enrolled at a timekeeping terminal.
- No consent record whose status is `SOLICITED`, `GRANTED` or `RESOLICIT_REQUIRED` may already exist for the same pairing of `data_subject_id` and `asset_id`, else `ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_EXISTS`. Records that are `DECLINED`, `REVOKED` or `EXPIRED` do not block a fresh solicitation.

A successful solicitation lands the record in `SOLICITED`, copies the collection point's `pinned_policy_id` into the record's own `pinned_policy_id`, sets `solicitation_round` to 1, and leaves `signed_at`, `pinned_retention_days`, `record_retention_until`, `decline_reason` and `revocation_reason` null. It then recomputes the point's `consent_gap_count`, the program's `consent_gap_count` and the program's `posture` — a solicited-but-unanswered record is itself a gap, so soliciting consent at an otherwise clean program moves it to `RESTRICTED` until the individual answers.

**Granting.** An `HRAdministrator` moves a record `SOLICITED` → `GRANTED`. Three checks apply beyond the status itself: `signature_ref` must not be null, or the grant fails with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; where the subject's state profile has `requires_written_release` true, `written_release_acknowledged` must be true, or the grant fails with `ERR_WRITTEN_RELEASE_REQUIRED`; and the collection point's status must be `ACTIVE` or `GATED`, or `ERR_ASSET_NOT_COLLECTABLE`. Granting is additionally blocked in a `FROZEN` program with `ERR_PROGRAM_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. Both `written_release_acknowledged` and `signature_ref` may be supplied on the grant request itself as well as at solicitation time.

A successful grant does six things. It stamps `signed_at` from the current logical clock. It freezes `pinned_retention_days` to the `retention_days` of the policy named in the record's `pinned_policy_id`, capturing for evidentiary purposes the retention promise the individual actually consented to. It sets `record_retention_until` to `signed_at` plus 1,095 days — three years — which is the statutory floor for keeping the consent record itself, entirely independent of when the templates are destroyed. As a compensating action, every accommodation whose `created_from_consent_id` is this record and whose status is `REQUESTED`, `PROVISIONED` or `ACTIVE` transitions to `ENDED`, so that an individual who declined, was given a manual workaround, and later changed their mind does not retain both an enrollment and a bypass; this restores the point's `enrolled_subject_count` to its pre-decline value. It increments `enrolled_subject_count` on the collection point and recomputes that point's `consent_gap_count`. And it re-derives `enrollment_status` and `active_accommodation_count` on the subject, then the program's `consent_gap_count` and `posture`.

**The re-solicitation loop.** A record that has lost its grant can be brought back around. A `PrivacyOfficer` may drive `EXPIRED` → `RESOLICIT_REQUIRED` and `REVOKED` → `RESOLICIT_REQUIRED`, in both cases requiring that the collection point's status is `ACTIVE` or `GATED` (else `ERR_ASSET_NOT_COLLECTABLE`); both moves simply refresh `updated_at` and increment `version`. An `HRAdministrator` then drives `RESOLICIT_REQUIRED` → `SOLICITED`, which requires that the point is `ACTIVE` or `GATED` (else `ERR_ASSET_NOT_COLLECTABLE`) and that the point's `pinned_policy_id` is not null and names a policy whose status is `PUBLISHED` (else `ERR_POLICY_NOT_PUBLISHED`). That move increments `solicitation_round` by 1, re-pins the record's `pinned_policy_id` to the point's current pinned policy, and clears `signed_at`, `pinned_retention_days`, `record_retention_until`, `written_release_acknowledged` and `signature_ref` so the individual is genuinely asked afresh; it then recomputes the point's `consent_gap_count` and the subject's `enrollment_status`. The cycle `SOLICITED` → `GRANTED` → `RESOLICIT_REQUIRED` → `SOLICITED` may therefore repeat indefinitely, with `solicitation_round` recording how many times the individual has been asked.

Two system-driven edges also lead out of `GRANTED`. `GRANTED` → `EXPIRED` fires when the destruction sweep finds the clock at or past the point's `destruction_due_at`, when Cascade A hop 3 gates the point because its processor's contract was terminated, or when the point is decommissioned; each such expiry decrements the point's `enrolled_subject_count`, recomputes its `consent_gap_count`, and re-derives the subject's `enrollment_status`. `GRANTED` → `RESOLICIT_REQUIRED` fires as Cascade B hop 2 when the policy pinned on the record is superseded, decrementing `enrolled_subject_count` and incrementing `consent_gap_count` on the point and re-deriving the subject's `enrollment_status`.

### 3.7. Declines, Revocations and Alternative Processes

An individual who will not enrol must still be able to clock in, badge through a door or check out of a store. That is the purpose of the **alternative accommodation**, and the system creates them by two routes: automatically, as compensation for a refusal, and manually on request.

**Declining.** An `HRAdministrator` moves a record `SOLICITED` → `DECLINED`, which is terminal for that record. A `decline_reason` must be present in the request body, or the move fails with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The decline auto-creates exactly one accommodation with `origin` set to `AUTO_DECLINE`, `created_from_consent_id` set to the declining record, status `REQUESTED`, `method` defaulted from the collection point's `system_category` per the mapping below, `starts_at` at the current logical clock, `ends_at` at ninety days after `starts_at`, and `sla_due_at` at five days after the current logical clock. The decline then recomputes the point's `consent_gap_count` — a decline is a lawful outcome and closes the gap rather than opening one — and re-derives the subject's `enrollment_status` to `OPTED_OUT`.

**Revoking.** A granted consent may be withdrawn at any time, by an `HRAdministrator` or by the `Subject` acting on their own record; a `Subject` whose `X-User-ID` is not the record's `data_subject_id` is rejected with `ERR_SUBJECT_MISMATCH`. The move `GRANTED` → `REVOKED` requires a `revocation_reason` in the request body, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. It auto-creates one accommodation with `origin` set to `AUTO_REVOKE`, `created_from_consent_id` set to the revoked record, status `REQUESTED`, `method` from the same mapping, `starts_at` at the current logical clock and `ends_at` ninety days later. It then decrements the point's `enrolled_subject_count`, recomputes its `consent_gap_count`, re-derives the subject's `enrollment_status` to `OPTED_OUT`, and recomputes the program's `consent_gap_count` and `posture`.

**Expiry-driven creation.** As described in §3.2, Cascade A hop 4 spawns one accommodation with `origin` set to `AUTO_CONSENT_EXPIRY` for each consent expired by a processor's contract termination whose subject has a `subject_type` of `EMPLOYEE`. No accommodation is created for `CUSTOMER` subjects on that path, none is created for expiries driven by decommissioning a collection point, and none is created by the destruction sweep. `AUTO_DECLINE`, `AUTO_REVOKE` and `AUTO_CONSENT_EXPIRY` are written only by these cascades; a manually requested accommodation always carries `origin` of `MANUAL` with a null `created_from_consent_id`.

**Permitted methods.** The `method` an accommodation grants is not free-form: exactly one non-biometric process is permitted per collection surface, and the mapping is normative both for manual requests and for the method every cascade-created accommodation receives. A `TIMEKEEPING` surface maps to `MANUAL_TIMESHEET`. An `ACCESS_CONTROL` surface maps to `BADGE_PIN`. A `CUSTOMER_FACING` surface maps to `STAFFED_LANE`. A `SECURITY_CAMERA` surface maps to `MANUAL_LOG`. Any other pairing is rejected with `ERR_ACCOMMODATION_METHOD_NOT_PERMITTED`.

**Manual requests.** An `HRAdministrator` may request an accommodation directly, supplying the `data_subject_id`, the `asset_id` of the collection point being bypassed, the `method`, and the window bounds `starts_at` and `ends_at`, both ISO 8601 UTC and both **inclusive**. The request lands in `REQUESTED` with `origin` of `MANUAL`, `created_from_consent_id` null, `sla_breached` false, and `sla_due_at` set to five days after the current logical clock — the deadline for reaching `PROVISIONED`. Manual creation enforces a window-collision rule that is deliberately stricter than the notice-window rule of §3.4: the requested closed interval from `starts_at` through `ends_at` is inflated by a twenty-four-hour changeover buffer on **both** ends, and if the inflated interval intersects the window of any other accommodation for the same `data_subject_id` whose status is `REQUESTED`, `PROVISIONED` or `ACTIVE`, the request is rejected with `ERR_ACCOMMODATION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. The buffer inflates only the requested interval: the other accommodation's window is compared exactly as recorded, from its own `starts_at` through its own `ends_at`, and is not itself inflated. Because the intervals are closed and the requested one is buffered, windows that merely touch — or that sit within a day of each other — do collide.

**The accommodation lifecycle.** An `HRAdministrator` drives `REQUESTED` → `PROVISIONED`, which re-checks that the recorded `method` is the permitted method for the collection point's `system_category` (else `ERR_ACCOMMODATION_METHOD_NOT_PERMITTED`) and sets `sla_breached` to true when the current logical clock is **strictly after** `sla_due_at`; provisioning exactly at the deadline is on time. Provisioning recomputes the subject's `active_accommodation_count` and `enrollment_status`. The administrator then drives `PROVISIONED` → `ACTIVE`, which requires that the current logical clock is at or after `starts_at`; activating early is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`. Activation re-derives the subject's `enrollment_status`. `ACTIVE` → `ENDED` closes a running accommodation at the terminal state, recomputing `active_accommodation_count` and `enrollment_status`. Two shortcut edges also reach `ENDED`: `PROVISIONED` → `ENDED` and `REQUESTED` → `ENDED`, driven either by an `HRAdministrator` ending the accommodation deliberately or by the system, when the originating consent reaches `GRANTED` and the compensation of §3.6 fires, or when the collection point is decommissioned. `PROVISIONED` → `ENDED` recomputes both `active_accommodation_count` and `enrollment_status`; `REQUESTED` → `ENDED` re-derives `enrollment_status` only, since a `REQUESTED` row never counted toward the active tally in the first place.

### 3.8. Biometric Exposure Incidents

When templates leak, the obligations are heightened and the clock is short. A `PrivacyOfficer` opens an **incident case** against a `program_id`, listing the exposed collection points in a non-empty `affected_asset_ids` array whose every member must belong to that program, an `exposure_vector` of `VENDOR_BREACH`, `INSIDER_MISUSE`, `LOST_DEVICE` or `MISCONFIGURED_EXPORT`, a `description` narrating the exposure for the regulator file, an `exposed_subject_count` of at least 1 recording how many individuals' templates were exposed, and a `translation_locale_count` of at least zero recording how many non-default locales the notification must be issued in. Opening the case stamps `discovered_at` from the current logical clock — the origin of the notification clock — and initializes both state dimensions: `containment_status` to `DETECTED` and `notification_status` to `PENDING`.

An incident advances along **two parallel dimensions that move independently**, and only one join point couples them. The containment dimension runs `DETECTED` → `CONTAINED` → `REMEDIATED`, driven by a `PrivacyOfficer`. The move to `CONTAINED` requires a `containment_note` in the request body describing the containment or remediation step taken, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The notification dimension runs `PENDING` → `ASSESSED` and then either `ASSESSED` → `NOTIFIED` or `ASSESSED` → `EXEMPT`. Neither dimension waits on the other except at the terminal containment step.

**Assessment.** A `PrivacyOfficer` moves `notification_status` from `PENDING` to `ASSESSED`, and that single move runs the whole tier, deadline and cost calculation, writing `heightened_tier`, `notification_due_at`, `gross_notice_cents`, `regulator_surcharge_cents`, `vendor_indemnity_credit_cents` and `net_incident_cost_cents`.

The tier is `TIER_1` when either of two conditions holds: at least one affected collection point has a `modality` of `FACE_GEOMETRY` or `IRIS`, or `exposed_subject_count` is at least 500. Otherwise the tier is `TIER_2`. Face and iris templates are irrevocable in a way a badge number is not, and mass exposure is mass exposure, so both routes into `TIER_1` are independent. The deadline follows from the tier: fifteen days for `TIER_1`, forty-five days for `TIER_2`, and `notification_due_at` is `discovered_at` plus that many whole days.

The cost is then computed in a pinned four-step order. **Step one, the gross.** `gross_notice_cents` is 425 cents (US$4.25) for every exposed individual plus 15,000 cents (US$150.00) for every non-default locale the notification must cover — that is, `exposed_subject_count` multiplied by 425, plus `translation_locale_count` multiplied by 15,000. **Step two, the regulator surcharge**, keyed on the pre-credit gross and never on the net: `regulator_surcharge_cents` is zero when the gross is below 50,000 cents (US$500.00); it is 25,000 cents (US$250.00) when the gross is at least 50,000 cents and below 150,000 cents (US$1,500.00); and it is 75,000 cents (US$750.00) when the gross is at least 150,000 cents. **Step three, the vendor indemnity credit.** The indemnity pool is the sum of `indemnity_cap_cents` across those vendor contracts whose status is `EXECUTED` and whose vendor operates at least one of the affected collection points; contracts in `DRAFT`, `APPROVED`, `TERMINATED`, `CLOSED_OUT` or `VOID` contribute nothing, and affected points with a null `vendor_id` bring no pool with them. `vendor_indemnity_credit_cents` is the smaller of that pool and half of the pre-surcharge gross, where the half is **truncated toward zero** rather than rounded — an odd gross therefore yields the lower cent. **Step four, the net.** `net_incident_cost_cents` is `gross_notice_cents` plus `regulator_surcharge_cents` minus `vendor_indemnity_credit_cents`. Note carefully that the surcharge is keyed on the gross before any credit is applied, and the credit ceiling is computed on the gross before the surcharge is added; neither reads the other's output.

**Notifying.** A `PrivacyOfficer` moves `notification_status` from `ASSESSED` to `NOTIFIED`, supplying `notified_subject_count`, the number of individuals actually notified. That count must be present and must be at least `exposed_subject_count`; a shortfall is rejected with `ERR_NOTIFIED_COUNT_SHORTFALL`. The move stores the count, stamps `notified_at` from the current logical clock, and sets `late_notification` to true when `notified_at` is **strictly after** `notification_due_at` — notifying exactly at the deadline is timely.

**Exemption.** A `ComplianceApprover`, and only a `ComplianceApprover`, may move `notification_status` from `ASSESSED` to `EXEMPT` instead. Exemption is available only at `TIER_2`; claiming it on a `TIER_1` case is rejected with `ERR_EXEMPTION_NOT_AVAILABLE`. A written `exemption_basis` must be present in the request body, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and it is stored on the case.

**The guarded join.** The containment dimension's terminal move, `CONTAINED` → `REMEDIATED`, is where the two dimensions meet. It requires that `notification_status` is already `NOTIFIED` or `EXEMPT` — an outstanding `PENDING` or `ASSESSED` notification blocks remediation with `ERR_NOTIFICATION_OUTSTANDING` — and it requires that every collection point listed in `affected_asset_ids` has a status of `GATED` or `DECOMMISSIONED`, since an exposed surface that is still collecting has not been remediated at all; a still-`ACTIVE` point yields `ERR_ASSET_STILL_ACTIVE`. Reaching `REMEDIATED` recomputes the program's `posture`.

### 3.9. Private-Right-of-Action Exposure and the Program Ledger

The last piece is quantification: how much a state's private right of action could plausibly cost, under which damages theory, against a board-approved ceiling. A `PrivacyOfficer` drafts an **exposure assessment** for one `program_id` and one `jurisdiction_id`, optionally linking it to the `incident_id` that prompted it (null for a routine posture assessment), choosing a `variant` of `PER_VIOLATION_STACKED` or `PER_SUBJECT_CAPPED`, and pinning `expected_jurisdiction_version` per §2.8. Drafting records the acting `X-User-ID` as `created_by_user_id`, computes the full estimate immediately, leaves the assessment in `DRAFT` with `finalized_by_user_id` and `void_reason` null, and increments that state profile's `version` by 1.

**The estimate.** The scope of the estimate is those collection points of the assessment's program whose `jurisdiction_id` equals the assessment's and whose status is `ACTIVE` or `GATED`. Consent records on points that are `DISCOVERED`, `CLASSIFIED` or `DECOMMISSIONED` are outside the scope entirely.

Within that scope, violation units are counted in two buckets. The **negligent** bucket is every consent record on a scoped point whose status is `SOLICITED`, `RESOLICIT_REQUIRED` or `EXPIRED` — an individual who was asked and never answered, who must be asked again, or whose consent lapsed. The **reckless** bucket is every consent record on a scoped point whose status is `REVOKED` **and** whose data subject has no accommodation at all with status `PROVISIONED` or `ACTIVE` — taking away someone's consent and giving them no alternative process is the aggravated case. A `DECLINED` record is never a violation in either bucket: a lawful decline is exactly what the statute contemplates. The counts are published as `negligent_unit_count` and `reckless_unit_count`.

Two adjustment factors are then derived. `notice_gap_multiplier` is 1.25 when at least one scoped collection point has a `consent_gap_count` greater than zero, and 1.00 otherwise. `cure_credit_pct` is 0.30 when both conditions hold — the state profile's `cure_period_days` is at least 1, and a policy exists under the assessment's program whose status is `PUBLISHED` and whose `jurisdiction_scope` includes this state profile — and 0.00 otherwise. A third factor comes straight from the statute: the private-right-of-action factor is 0.00 when `private_right_of_action` is `NONE`, 0.50 when it is `LIMITED`, and 1.00 when it is `FULL`.

The figure is then produced in five pinned steps with **no intermediate rounding whatsoever**.

*Step one* fixes `base_cents`, and it is the only variant-sensitive step. If the state profile's `damages_model` is `ACTUAL_ONLY`, `base_cents` is zero regardless of variant — there are no statutory per-violation damages to stack. Otherwise, under the `PER_VIOLATION_STACKED` variant — the plaintiff theory that each unconsented collection event is its own violation — `base_cents` is the negligent unit count multiplied by the state profile's `per_violation_negligent_cents`, plus the reckless unit count multiplied by its `per_violation_reckless_cents`; every violation row is priced independently, so one individual with both an unconsented collection and a post-revocation collection contributes the full sum with no per-person ceiling. Under the `PER_SUBJECT_CAPPED` variant — the defence theory that recovery is capped per aggrieved person — each data subject contributing at least one row to either bucket is priced separately: that subject's own base is the count of their negligent rows multiplied by `per_violation_negligent_cents` plus the count of their reckless rows multiplied by `per_violation_reckless_cents`, and each subject's base is then clipped at 300,000 cents (US$3,000.00) before the per-subject figures are summed into `base_cents`. Identical consent data therefore yields a strictly lower figure under the capped variant whenever any single subject's base exceeds the cap. Where the per-subject accumulation is order-sensitive, rows are accumulated in consent-record `created_at` ascending order.

*Steps two, three and four* apply the three factors in sequence: `base_cents` is multiplied by the private-right-of-action factor, then by `notice_gap_multiplier`, then by one minus `cure_credit_pct`.

*Step five* rounds. The fully multiplied product is rounded half-up to zero decimal places — **exactly one rounding, applied to the product and never to any individual factor or intermediate**. The rounded figure is then subjected to the statutory cap: if the state profile's `statutory_cap_cents` is null the rounded figure stands as `exposure_cents`; otherwise `exposure_cents` is the smaller of the rounded figure and `statutory_cap_cents`.

**Finalization.** A `ComplianceApprover` moves an assessment `DRAFT` → `FINAL`, and this is the step that commits the number to the program's carried exposure. Four preconditions guard it: the assessment must be `DRAFT`; the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `created_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; no other assessment for the same pairing of `program_id` and `jurisdiction_id` may already be `FINAL`, else `ERR_ASSESSMENT_ALREADY_FINAL`; and the program's existing `pra_exposure_cents` plus this assessment's `exposure_cents` must not exceed `exposure_ceiling_cents`, else `ERR_EXPOSURE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Equality with the ceiling is permitted — it is the excess that is refused. Finalization stamps `finalized_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, adds `exposure_cents` into the program's `pra_exposure_cents`, recomputes the program's `posture` (a program whose carried exposure reaches eighty percent of its ceiling freezes, per §2.6), and increments the program's `version` by 1.

**Voiding.** Two void edges exist, and they differ in exactly one respect. A `ComplianceApprover` may void a `FINAL` assessment, `FINAL` → `VOID`, and that move is compensating: `exposure_cents` is subtracted back out of the program's `pra_exposure_cents`, restoring the pre-finalization value exactly, then `posture` is recomputed and the program's `version` is incremented by 1. A `PrivacyOfficer` may void a `DRAFT` assessment, `DRAFT` → `VOID`, and that move leaves the program's `pra_exposure_cents` untouched, because a draft figure was never added to it in the first place. Both edges require a `void_reason` in the request body, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and both land on the terminal `VOID` state. The estimate itself is re-evaluated on the way into `FINAL`, so a figure priced from consent data that has since moved is refreshed before it is committed.
