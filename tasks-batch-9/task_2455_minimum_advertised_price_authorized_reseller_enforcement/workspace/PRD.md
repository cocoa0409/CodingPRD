# Map Authorized Reseller Enforcement API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A consumer brand that sells through marketplaces, retailer sites and independent storefronts lives with a permanent tension. It wants a floor under the price at which its goods are *advertised*, because a race to the bottom on advertised price destroys the margin its authorized partners depend on. At the same time, it may not *agree* with any reseller about price — the moment price becomes a negotiated term, a unilateral pricing policy turns into a horizontal or vertical price-fixing arrangement. The system specified here exists to let a brand run a Minimum Advertised Price (MAP) programme that is aggressive on enforcement and scrupulously unilateral on process.

Three workstreams converge in this service. **Policy and contract** covers drafting a unilateral MAP policy, submitting it to legal counsel for antitrust review, publishing it, and papering authorized resellers with agreements that restrict sales channels and impose quality obligations — but never price terms. **Monitoring and enforcement** covers capturing advertised-price evidence from marketplaces and retailer sites, computing the advertised price the policy definition actually recognizes, comparing it against the floor in force at the moment of observation, and walking a violation up a notice ladder from warning to supply suspension to termination of supply. **Diversion and accountability** covers identifying sellers who were never authorized at all, tracing the serialized units they are selling back through the distributor that shipped them, and assessing contractual chargebacks against the distributor that let the goods leak.

Because every number in this system eventually appears in a legal notice, the specification is uncompromising about determinism. There is one logical clock, and it is settable. Points, floors and money are computed by pinned formulas with pinned rounding order. Every derived aggregate — a reseller's points ledger, a distributor's chargeback balance and diversion score, a product's open-exception count — must equal the sum of its contributors at every observable instant, so that a forward action followed by its compensating action leaves the books exactly where they started. And because enforcement decisions are grounded in a ledger that other operators are concurrently moving, the two writes that install irreversible consequences require the client to prove it read the current state of the counterparty before acting.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Channel Actors and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the actor, and `X-User-Role`, naming the actor's role. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well formed but the named role does not appear in the authorized-role list published for the requested endpoint in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `BrandChannelManager`, `BrandLegalCounsel`, `MonitoringAnalyst`, `PlatformAdmin`, `Reseller` and `Distributor`.

Four of these roles are **operator roles** whose identifiers are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `BrandChannelManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow calls for an audit stamp — for example as `requested_by_user_id` on a pricing exception — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `BrandLegalCounsel`, whose identifier is recorded as `reviewed_by_user_id` and `published_by_user_id` on a policy, as `approved_by_user_id` on an exception, and as `issued_by_user_id` on an enforcement notice; for `MonitoringAnalyst`, whose identifier is recorded as `evidence_captured_by_user_id` on a price observation and as `purchaser_user_id` on a test purchase; and for `PlatformAdmin`, whose identifier is recorded nowhere but which is nonetheless required for the clock and sweep capabilities. Opaque does not mean unused: the segregation-of-duties rule described in §3.7 compares two recorded `BrandLegalCounsel` identifiers to each other, even though neither is matched against an entity `id`.

Two roles are **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `Reseller`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Reseller` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a reseller compare that resource's reseller foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a reseller may only respond to, acknowledge, cure or dispute rows whose reseller reference is its own `id` — its own agreements, its own violations and its own notices — and may only read rows it owns. When `X-User-Role` is `Distributor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Distributor` row; a distributor may only record downstream shipments for serial units whose `shipped_distributor_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and may only dispute chargebacks whose `distributor_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and may only read rows it owns. A violation of either ownership rule is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection read accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400).

Collections are returned sorted by `created_at` ascending. When several rows share a `created_at` value, the ordering between them is not further constrained by this specification, but it must be stable across repeated reads of the same page. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`: `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters *before* the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows matching the filters remain beyond the current window. `response_type` is `"array"` for list reads and `"object"` for single-resource reads.

All filter parameters on a list endpoint are optional and combine conjunctively — supplying `status` and `reseller_id` together returns only rows matching both.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every persisted row in this system carries four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of creation, formatted with three fractional digits. `updated_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the most recent mutation, in the same format; at creation it equals `created_at`. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. All twelve entity types in this system — pricing policies, products, resellers, distributors, reseller agreements, price observations, violations, enforcement notices, pricing exceptions, serial units, test purchases and distributor chargebacks — carry all four properties.

The `version` counter is not decorative. Whenever a workflow described in §3 says that an operation "bumps" or "advances" a row's version, that means the row's `version` increases by 1 and its `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock. A cascade that touches five rows advances five separate counters. Conversely, a recomputation that leaves a row's stored values identical to what they already were must **not** advance that row's `version` — the derived-flag recomputation described in §2.11 is explicitly required to be idempotent in this sense.

Timestamps supplied by clients are ISO 8601 UTC instants. Timestamps minted by the server are always read from the logical clock described in §2.6, never from wall-clock time.

### 2.5. Money, Points and Rounding Discipline

Money in this system is always an integer number of cents. There are no fractional-cent intermediate values that survive into a stored field: wherever a computation produces a fractional amount, the specification names the exact rounding step and the exact point in the sequence at which it is applied. Where rounding is called for, the method is **round half up** — a value exactly halfway between two representable results rounds away from zero — unless the text explicitly says *floor* (truncate toward zero) instead.

Three precision rules apply across the whole system and are restated where they bite in §3. Advertised-price arithmetic is performed in whole cents throughout; the only rounding inside it is the *floor* applied to the discount-attribution cap. Bundle allocation rounds **each component's share to whole cents first** and then hands the arithmetic residual to a designated last component, so that the component shares sum to the advertised bundle price exactly; "sum first, then round" is explicitly not the algorithm. Violation points are computed as a chain of multiplications and rounded to a whole number of points **once, at the very end** — never factor by factor.

Two derived numbers carry declared sub-integer precision. A deviation fraction is carried to four decimal places, rounded half up. A distributor's diversion score is carried to one decimal place. Percentage-style configuration inputs — the discount-attribution cap fraction and the clearance discount fraction — are two-decimal fractions between 0 and 1, not integer percentages: a value of `0.40` means forty percent.

### 2.6. The Deterministic Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads the host's wall clock. There is a single logical clock, and every server-minted timestamp, every window comparison, every staleness check and every sweep decision reads it. Before any client pins it, the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`.

A `PlatformAdmin` may pin the clock to an arbitrary instant by supplying a `to_iso8601` value; the response reports the new `current_utc` and the `previous_utc` it replaced. This is a test-driving capability, not a business operation, and it is the only way the clock ever moves. Because the clock does not advance on its own, all the time-driven consequences in this system — agreement expiry, exception expiry, cure-window lapse, suspension service — are realized by an explicit sweep capability rather than by a background timer. The sweep is described in §3.9.

### 2.7. Antitrust Guardrails on Unilateral Process

The legal theory this system implements is that the MAP policy is **unilateral**: the brand announces it, resellers decide independently whether to comply, and no agreement about price is ever formed. Four request-level guardrails protect that theory, and they are checked ahead of every other business rule on the endpoints they guard — before referential checks, before window checks, before domain constraints — so that a request which attempts to create a price agreement is rejected on antitrust grounds rather than being masked by an unrelated failure.

An authorized reseller agreement may not declare that it contains price terms: if a draft carries `price_terms_included` set to true, it is rejected with `ERR_ANTITRUST_PRICE_TERM_IN_AGREEMENT` (HTTP 422). A reseller's response to an agreement may not carry a price counter-proposal: if the response supplies `counter_price_proposal_cents` at all, it is rejected with `ERR_ANTITRUST_NEGOTIATION_PROHIBITED` (HTTP 422), because entertaining a counter-proposal is exactly the negotiation the unilateral posture forbids. An enforcement notice may not open a pricing dialogue: if it carries `contains_pricing_discussion` set to true, it is likewise rejected with `ERR_ANTITRUST_NEGOTIATION_PROHIBITED`, because a notice must be a one-way communication rather than an invitation to discuss price. And a pricing exception may not be sourced from a dealer's price request: if `dealer_input_source` is `RESELLER_PRICING_REQUEST`, the request is rejected with `ERR_ANTITRUST_DEALER_PRICE_INPUT` (HTTP 422). The other three provenance values — `NONE`, `RETAILER_INVENTORY_REPORT` and `BRAND_MARKETING_PLAN` — are permitted, because inventory reporting and the brand's own marketing planning are not dealer input on price.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on the Two Ledgers

Two write operations in this system price their output from a counterparty's running ledger, and both therefore require the client to prove it is working from a current view of that counterparty.

Issuing an enforcement notice requires a non-optional `expected_reseller_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Reseller` row. If that value does not equal the reseller's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RESELLER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The rung a notice installs is derived from the reseller's cumulative points and its highest previously issued rung, so a client working from a stale reseller view would install a rung the current ledger does not support. Any earlier notice, any rescission, any sweep-driven reinstatement and any exception-driven point reversal advances the reseller's `version` and therefore invalidates a view read before it.

Assessing a distributor chargeback requires a non-optional `expected_distributor_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Distributor` row. If that value does not equal the distributor's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DISTRIBUTOR_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The per-unit tier rate is selected from the distributor's pre-increment diversion score, so a stale view would silently price the chargeback at the wrong tier; the concurrency check converts that into a deterministic rejection instead.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field. Requests to any other endpoint that happen to carry one are not rejected for it, and requests to these two that omit it are rejected as malformed with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 400).

### 2.9. The Order in Which a Request Is Rejected

When a single request violates several rules, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the first failure short-circuits the request:

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the six declared roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorization.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list in §4, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
3. **Request shape.** Required body fields present, types correct, enum members drawn from the declared sets, numeric ranges honoured, `evidence_hash` exactly 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters, `permitted_channels` members distinct, `bundle_components` well formed whenever `is_bundle` is true, a suspension window no longer than 90 days, and `diverted_units` at least 1 — else `ERR_VALIDATION` (400).
4. **Path existence.** Every resource named by a path parameter exists, validated left to right, so that on a nested path the leftmost missing resource determines the error. The code is the not-found code of the resource type that is missing: `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESELLER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OBSERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VIOLATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXCEPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERIAL_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEST_PURCHASE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CHARGEBACK_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404.
5. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to an existing row, validated in the order the fields are listed for that endpoint in §4's companion narrative, again yielding the not-found code of the missing type (and `ERR_SERIAL_NOT_REGISTERED`, HTTP 404, when a submitted `serial_number` matches no registered serialized unit). This step applies to product registration, reseller application, agreement drafting, observation capture, notice issuance, exception request, serial-unit registration, downstream shipment recording, test-purchase ordering and chargeback assessment.
6. **Resource ownership.** For the two entity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` equals the owning foreign key of the target row, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This guards agreement response, cure submission, violation dispute, notice acknowledgement, downstream shipment recording and chargeback dispute, and every single-row read of an owned row.
7. **Transition legality.** The target row is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, else one of `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_ALREADY_EVALUATED`, `ERR_VIOLATION_NOT_NOTICEABLE`, `ERR_RESELLER_TERMINATED`, `ERR_TERMINATION_NOT_RESCINDABLE` or `ERR_SUPPLY_BLOCKED_BY_AUTHORIZATION`, all HTTP 409. This step subsumes the specific state guards named throughout §3: an observation must be `CAPTURED` to be evaluated, a violation must be `DETECTED` to be noticed, `NOTICED` to be cured or disputed and `DISPUTED` to receive a dispute decision, a notice must be `ISSUED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` to be acknowledged or rescinded, an exception must be `REQUESTED` to be decided, and a chargeback must be `ASSESSED` or `UPHELD` to be disputed.
8. **Optimistic concurrency.** `expected_reseller_version` matches the reseller's `version` on notice issuance; `expected_distributor_version` matches the distributor's `version` on chargeback assessment. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_RESELLER_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_DISTRIBUTOR_VERSION`, HTTP 412.
9. **Antitrust guardrails.** The four declarations described in §2.7, yielding `ERR_ANTITRUST_PRICE_TERM_IN_AGREEMENT`, `ERR_ANTITRUST_NEGOTIATION_PROHIBITED` or `ERR_ANTITRUST_DEALER_PRICE_INPUT`, all HTTP 422. Note the consequence of this position: an antitrust failure is reported even when the request would also have failed a referential, temporal or domain check, but a stale-version failure is reported ahead of an antitrust failure.
10. **Cross-entity referential preconditions.** A product's governing policy must be `PUBLISHED` at product creation; an agreement's referenced policy must be `PUBLISHED` both at send and at activation; a violation's cited policy must be `PUBLISHED` at notice issuance — all three yielding `ERR_POLICY_NOT_PUBLISHED` (409). A notice's `reseller_id` must equal the cited violation's `reseller_id`, else `ERR_VIOLATION_RESELLER_MISMATCH` (422). A `CLEARANCE` exception's product must be in lifecycle stage `CLEARANCE`, else `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_IN_CLEARANCE` (409). A chargeback's referenced test purchase must be `CONFIRMED_UNAUTHORIZED`, else `ERR_TEST_PURCHASE_NOT_CONFIRMED` (409), and its traced serial unit's `shipped_distributor_id` must equal the chargeback's `distributor_id`, else `ERR_DIVERSION_NOT_ATTRIBUTED` (422). The non-negotiation acknowledgement must be present when the governing policy demands it, else `ERR_NON_NEGOTIATION_ACK_MISSING` (422). A governing policy must exist for the observation instant, else `ERR_NO_GOVERNING_POLICY` (422). A serialized unit named in a serial capture must be in a traceable custody state, else `ERR_SERIAL_NOT_TRACEABLE` (409), and a unit may only be registered against a product whose serialization is enabled, else `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_SERIALIZED` (422).
11. **Window and clock rules.** An observation's `observed_at` must lie within the trailing 30 days, else `ERR_OBSERVATION_STALE` (422). An exception window must not overlap another live window on the same product with an intersecting channel scope, else `ERR_EXCEPTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409); a retroactive exception may not reach back more than 30 days, else `ERR_RETROACTIVE_WINDOW_TOO_OLD` (422). A suspension window must respect the cooling-off buffer and must not intersect a live suspension, else `ERR_SUSPENSION_COOLING_OFF` (409). A cure submission must arrive on or before the cure deadline, else `ERR_CURE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (409).
12. **Domain constraints.** Uniqueness of `sku` and `serial_number` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERIAL`, both 409); channel permissions (`ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_PERMITTED`, 422); a client-asserted rung matching the computed rung (`ERR_LADDER_RUNG_MISMATCH`, 422); the two-round dispute cap (`ERR_DISPUTE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, 409); the two segregation-of-duties rules on termination (`ERR_TERMINATION_REQUIRES_COUNSEL` and `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, both 403); a final points assessment greater than zero (`ERR_NO_BREACH_AT_FINAL_FLOOR`, 422); and an override floor inside its permitted band (`ERR_OVERRIDE_FLOOR_OUT_OF_RANGE`, 422). These guard product registration, serial-unit registration, agreement drafting, observation capture, notice issuance, violation dispute, chargeback dispute and exception decision.
13. **Fiscal ceiling, checked last.** A distributor's chargeback balance plus the new chargeback's amount must not exceed the distributor's annual chargeback ceiling, else `ERR_CHARGEBACK_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402). Because this check runs last, a request that both breaches the cap and fails any earlier check reports the earlier failure.

### 2.10. Flat Addressing and Reference Discovery

Every entity in this system is a peer, addressed at its own top-level collection. There are no nested ownership URLs; relationships are carried as foreign-key fields in request bodies and are discovered afterwards through filtered list reads. A caller who wants a reseller's violations filters the violation collection by reseller; a caller who wants the notices grounded in a particular violation filters the notice collection by violation. Every declared foreign key in the system therefore has a corresponding filter parameter on the collection of the referencing entity, and the §4 table names them.

Where a path does contain more than one segment of resource identity, resolution proceeds strictly left to right, and the first unresolvable segment determines the 404 code returned.

### 2.11. Reconciliation Invariants on Derived Aggregates

Four aggregates in this system are stored as running fields *and* defined as sums or predicates over their contributors. Both readings must agree at every instant a client can observe, which is the strongest consistency requirement in this document.

A reseller's `violation_points_total` equals the sum of `assessed_points` over that reseller's violations whose `assessed_points` is not null and whose `status` is one of `NOTICED`, `DISPUTED`, `CURED`, `ESCALATED` or `CLOSED_MOOT`. A violation that was detected but never noticed contributes nothing, because its `assessed_points` is still null; a `VOIDED` violation contributes nothing, because voiding clears `assessed_points` back to null. The invariant that follows is the one the enforcement workflow depends on: after any forward action and its compensating action — issue then rescind, notice then sustained dispute, notice then retroactive-exception void — the sum is exactly what it was before the forward action.

A distributor's `chargeback_balance_cents` equals the sum of `amount_cents` over that distributor's chargebacks whose `status` is `ASSESSED`, `DISPUTED` or `UPHELD`, and its `diversion_score` equals the sum of `score_delta_applied` over the same filtered set. A `REVERSED` chargeback keeps its historical `amount_cents` and `score_delta_applied` on the row for audit but contributes to neither sum. The distributor's `standing` is re-derived from the restored score after a reversal, which means `PROBATION` can fall back to `GOOD`.

A product's `open_exception_count` equals the count of that product's pricing exceptions in status `APPROVED`. Exceptions in `REQUESTED`, `REJECTED`, `EXPIRED` or `VOIDED` are excluded, and the count is recomputed on every approval, expiry and voiding.

A reseller's `review_required` flag is a predicate rather than a sum: it is true when either the reseller declares a sourcing distributor whose `standing` is `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED`, or at least one serialized unit whose `downstream_reseller_id` is this reseller has been traced to an unauthorized seller. Only the reseller's own declared sourcing distributor is consulted for the first clause; units shipped by any other distributor can only matter through the second clause. This recomputation runs when a distributor's standing changes, when a serialized unit becomes traced, and when a reseller is created — and it is idempotent: if the recomputed value equals the stored value, the reseller row is not touched and its `version` does not advance.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Drafting, Reviewing and Publishing the Unilateral Pricing Policy

Everything downstream is anchored in a policy document. A `BrandChannelManager` opens a new one by supplying a `policy_code` — a human-readable label between 1 and 40 characters that must be unique across every policy the system holds; a second attempt to use the same code is rejected. The new policy is born in status `DRAFT` with `revision_number` set to 1, and its `effective_from`, `legal_review_notes`, `reviewed_by_user_id`, `published_by_user_id`, `superseded_by_policy_id` and `withdrawn_reason` all null.

The draft carries the brand's **advertised-price definition** — the part of the document that decides, for any listing the monitoring team later captures, what counts as the advertised price. Three booleans control it. `shipping_counts_as_advertised` decides whether a displayed shipping charge is added to the item price; it defaults to false, reflecting the common position that shipping is not part of the advertised price. `auto_coupon_counts_as_advertised` decides whether a coupon that the marketplace applies automatically on the listing page is treated as reducing the advertised price; it defaults to true, because a shopper sees that price without doing anything. `promo_code_counts_as_advertised` decides whether a discount reachable only by typing a code is treated as reducing the advertised price; it defaults to false, because a code the shopper must go and find is not what the page advertises. Alongside them, `max_discount_attribution_pct` caps how much of a listing's gross price any single discount may be credited with reducing for MAP purposes; it is a two-decimal fraction between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive and defaults to 0.40, meaning at most forty percent of the gross price.

Four further inputs configure the enforcement machinery rather than the price definition. `non_negotiation_ack_required` defaults to true and decides whether a reseller must acknowledge the policy's unilateral, non-negotiable character before its agreement can be activated. `cure_window_days` is the number of days a reseller has to cure a noticed violation before it escalates; it must be between 1 and 60 inclusive and defaults to 10. `suspension_points_threshold` is the cumulative points figure at or above which the ladder's points-based rung becomes suspension; it must be at least 1 and defaults to 6. `termination_points_threshold` is the cumulative points figure at or above which the points-based rung becomes termination; it defaults to 12 and must be strictly greater than `suspension_points_threshold`. Finally, `suspension_cooling_off_days` is the mandatory gap between the end of one suspension window and the start of the next; it must be between 0 and 180 inclusive and defaults to 14.

Submission for review is available to the `BrandChannelManager` and moves the document `DRAFT → IN_LEGAL_REVIEW`. It requires the policy to be in `DRAFT`, and it re-checks that `termination_points_threshold` is strictly greater than `suspension_points_threshold` — a draft whose ladder thresholds have been edited into an incoherent order cannot be handed to counsel. The transition advances the policy's `version`.

Review is a `BrandLegalCounsel` responsibility and forks on a `decision` value. A decision of `RETURN_FOR_REVISION` sends the document back, `IN_LEGAL_REVIEW → DRAFT`: it sets `revision_number` to one more than its previous value, records the supplied `legal_review_notes` and stamps `reviewed_by_user_id` with the reviewing counsel's `X-User-ID`, and advances `version`. This is a genuine loop — the same document may cycle `DRAFT → IN_LEGAL_REVIEW → DRAFT` any number of times, with `revision_number` climbing on every return. A decision of `APPROVE_FOR_PUBLICATION` moves the document `IN_LEGAL_REVIEW → APPROVED`: it records `legal_review_notes` and `reviewed_by_user_id` and advances `version`, but does **not** touch `revision_number`. Both branches require the document to be in `IN_LEGAL_REVIEW`.

Publication, also a `BrandLegalCounsel` act, moves the document `APPROVED → PUBLISHED`. The request supplies `effective_from`, which must be at or after the current logical clock — a policy may be scheduled forward but never backdated at publication. The transition stores `effective_from`, stamps `published_by_user_id` with the publishing counsel's `X-User-ID`, and advances the policy's `version`. It also performs the supersession cascade: if any *other* policy is currently in status `PUBLISHED` — and by construction there is at most one — that policy moves `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED`, has its `superseded_by_policy_id` set to the newly published policy's `id`, and has its own `version` advanced. Two rows are therefore mutated by a single publication, and both version counters move.

Withdrawal, again by `BrandLegalCounsel`, moves a policy `PUBLISHED → WITHDRAWN`, recording the supplied `withdrawn_reason` and advancing `version`. Withdrawal deliberately cascades to nothing: products keep their governing policy reference and open violations keep their citation. What withdrawal does is close the gate on *new* enforcement — a notice may not be issued for a violation whose cited policy is no longer `PUBLISHED`, which surfaces as `ERR_POLICY_NOT_PUBLISHED`. `SUPERSEDED` and `WITHDRAWN` are the two terminal states of a policy document; nothing moves out of them.

One resolution rule deserves stating on its own, because two different workflows depend on it. **Which policy governs a given instant** is answered by considering every policy whose `status` is `PUBLISHED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `WITHDRAWN` and whose `effective_from` is non-null and at or before the instant in question. If no policy qualifies, the caller is rejected with `ERR_NO_GOVERNING_POLICY`. Otherwise the qualifying policy with the latest `effective_from` wins, and if two share the same `effective_from`, the one created earliest wins — the tie is broken on `created_at` ascending, never on `id`. Note that superseded and withdrawn policies remain candidates: a listing observed last month is judged by the definition that was in force last month, even if that document has since been replaced. At notice issuance no re-selection occurs; the violation's `cited_policy_id`, frozen when the violation was detected, is reused verbatim.

Policies may be read one at a time by every role, and the collection may be filtered by `status`, by `policy_code`, or by `superseded_by_policy_id` to find the revision a given policy displaced.

### 3.2. The Product Catalogue and Its Standard Floors

A `BrandChannelManager` registers each branded item with a `sku` of 1 to 40 characters that must be unique across the catalogue — a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU` — plus a display `name`, an `msrp_cents` figure that must be strictly greater than zero, and a `standard_map_floor_cents` figure that must be strictly greater than zero and no greater than `msrp_cents`. Registration also names a `governing_policy_id`, and that policy must be in status `PUBLISHED` at the moment of registration, else `ERR_POLICY_NOT_PUBLISHED`.

Three optional inputs shape the product's later behaviour. `lifecycle_status` is the commercial stage and defaults to `ACTIVE`, with `CLEARANCE` and `DISCONTINUED` as its other values; it is the gate on which exception types may be approved. `clearance_discount_pct` is the fraction subtracted from the standard floor when a clearance exception is approved; it is a two-decimal fraction between 0.00 and 0.60 inclusive and defaults to 0.20, meaning a twenty-percent cut. `serialization_enabled` defaults to true and declares whether units of this item carry traceable serial numbers. Every newly registered product starts with `open_exception_count` at 0.

Products are adjustable in three respects by a `BrandChannelManager`: `lifecycle_status`, `clearance_discount_pct` and `standard_map_floor_cents` may each be supplied, individually or together, on an adjustment request, and the transition advances the product's `version`. Adjusting the standard floor is prospective only — an exception that was already approved carries a snapshotted floor and does not move when the standard floor is re-priced. Setting `lifecycle_status` to `DISCONTINUED`, however, triggers a cascade: every pricing exception on that product in status `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` moves to `VOIDED` with its `decision_reason` recorded as `PRODUCT_DISCONTINUED` and its own `version` advanced; where the exception was previously `APPROVED`, the product's `open_exception_count` is reduced by one as part of the same cascade.

Products may be read individually by every role, and the collection may be filtered by `governing_policy_id`, by `lifecycle_status` or by `sku`.

The product also exposes a **computed floor read**: given a required `channel` and, optionally, an `as_of` instant and an `is_bundle` flag, it reports the floor that would apply. The response carries `product_id`, the echoed `channel` and `as_of`, the resolved `effective_map_floor_cents`, a `floor_source` of either `STANDARD` or `EXCEPTION`, the `applied_exception_id` of the exception that supplied the floor (null when the source is `STANDARD`), and the product's current `standard_map_floor_cents` for comparison. When `as_of` is omitted it defaults to the current logical clock, and when `is_bundle` is omitted it is treated as false. The resolution rule behind this read is specified in §3.6, because it is the exception window machinery that makes it interesting.

### 3.3. Distributors, Reseller Applications and the Sourcing Link

Distributors enter the system through a `BrandChannelManager` registration carrying a `legal_name` and an `annual_chargeback_cap_cents` ceiling that must be strictly greater than zero. Every new distributor starts in `standing` `GOOD` with `diversion_score` at 0, `chargeback_balance_cents` at 0, `shipped_units_total` at 0 and `probation_entered_at` null. The three permitted standings are `GOOD`, `PROBATION` and `SUSPENDED`, and none of them is ever set directly by a client — standing is always re-derived from the diversion score, as described in §3.9. Whenever that re-derivation moves a distributor into `PROBATION`, `probation_entered_at` is stamped with the current logical clock; while a distributor is `GOOD`, the field is null. Distributors are readable one at a time and as a collection filtered by `standing`.

Resellers enter as applications. A `BrandChannelManager` records a `legal_name`, a `contact_email` that serves as the notice delivery address, and a `primary_channel` drawn from `MARKETPLACE`, `RETAILER_SITE`, `OWN_WEBSITE` and `PHYSICAL_RETAIL`. Optionally the application names a `sourcing_distributor_id` — the distributor this reseller buys from — which is left null for direct accounts. A newly recorded reseller starts with `authorization_status` `APPLIED`, `supply_status` `HOLD`, `hold_reason` `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION`, `violation_points_total` at 0 and `review_required` evaluated once at creation (which for a fresh row means false unless the named sourcing distributor is already in `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED`). The fields `suspension_ends_at`, `last_suspension_ended_at` and `terminated_at` all start null.

A reseller carries **two independent state axes on the same row**, and keeping them apart is the single most important structural idea in this domain. `authorization_status` is the contractual axis, moving `APPLIED → PENDING_AGREEMENT → AUTHORIZED`, then optionally cycling `AUTHORIZED → SUSPENDED → AUTHORIZED`, and finally `AUTHORIZED` or `SUSPENDED → TERMINATED`. `supply_status` is the physical axis, taking the values `OPEN`, `HOLD` and `CUT_OFF`, with `hold_reason` recording why supply is not open: `NONE`, `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION`, `QUALITY_OBLIGATION`, `SUSPENSION`, `AGREEMENT_EXPIRED` or `TERMINATION`. The axes are not locked together. The supply axis can move entirely on its own — for a quality-obligation hold, or when an agreement's term lapses — while the authorization axis stays exactly where it was. The authorization axis forces the supply axis only in two places: a suspension drags supply to `HOLD` with reason `SUSPENSION`, and a termination drags supply to `CUT_OFF` with reason `TERMINATION`. The combined initial condition of a reseller is `APPLIED` on the authorization axis and `HOLD` on the supply axis; the combined terminal condition is `TERMINATED` on the authorization axis and `CUT_OFF` on the supply axis, and nothing moves out of it.

The supply axis is driven directly by a `BrandChannelManager` supplying a target `supply_status`, optionally a `hold_reason` and optionally a free-text `note`. Two moves are legal on this route. Placing an `OPEN` reseller on hold moves `OPEN → HOLD` with `hold_reason` set to `QUALITY_OBLIGATION`, requires the reseller's `authorization_status` not to be `TERMINATED`, and advances the reseller's `version`; the authorization axis is untouched, so a reseller sitting on a quality hold is still `AUTHORIZED`. Releasing a hold moves `HOLD → OPEN` with `hold_reason` reset to `NONE`; it requires the reseller to currently be on `HOLD`, requires `hold_reason` to be either `QUALITY_OBLIGATION` or `AGREEMENT_EXPIRED`, and requires `authorization_status` to be `AUTHORIZED`. It also advances `version`. A hold whose reason is `SUSPENSION` or `TERMINATION` may **not** be released on this route — the attempt is rejected with `ERR_SUPPLY_BLOCKED_BY_AUTHORIZATION`, because lifting a suspension is a legal act on the authorization axis and not a warehouse decision. Likewise a `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION` hold is only cleared by activating an agreement.

Resellers are readable individually and as a collection filtered by `sourcing_distributor_id`, `authorization_status`, `supply_status` or `review_required`.

### 3.4. The Authorized Reseller Agreement

Drafting an agreement is a `BrandChannelManager` act. The payload names the counterparty by `reseller_id` and the unilateral document it references by `policy_id`, and it carries the two substantive commercial restrictions the brand *is* allowed to agree: `permitted_channels`, an array of between 1 and 4 distinct members drawn from `MARKETPLACE`, `RETAILER_SITE`, `OWN_WEBSITE` and `PHYSICAL_RETAIL`, and `quality_obligations`, an array of between 0 and 4 distinct members drawn from `AUTHENTICITY_LABELING`, `WARRANTY_HONORED`, `PACKAGING_INTACT` and `AUTHORIZED_SERVICE_ONLY`. `permitted_channels` must contain the reseller's own `primary_channel`, else the draft is rejected with `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_PERMITTED` — it would be incoherent to authorize a marketplace seller for everything except marketplaces. The payload also supplies `term_ends_at`, the expiry instant the sweep later enforces, and may declare `price_terms_included`, which defaults to false and whose only permitted value is false per §2.7. A fresh agreement is created in status `DRAFT` with `non_negotiation_acknowledged` false and with `sent_at`, `countersigned_at`, `countersigned_by_user_id`, `activated_at` and `closed_reason` all null.

Sending the agreement out for countersignature moves it `DRAFT → SENT`. The manager driving it must find the agreement in `DRAFT`, the referenced reseller with `authorization_status` exactly `APPLIED`, and the referenced policy in status `PUBLISHED`. The transition stamps `sent_at` with the current logical clock, advances the agreement's `version`, and moves the reseller's authorization axis `APPLIED → PENDING_AGREEMENT` while advancing the reseller's `version` as well. The reseller's supply axis is deliberately left on `HOLD` with reason `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION`.

The reseller's response forks the agreement, and the two branches have deliberately different consequences. A `decision` of `COUNTERSIGN` moves it `SENT → COUNTERSIGNED`: it stamps `countersigned_at` with the current clock, records `countersigned_by_user_id` from the acting reseller's `X-User-ID`, and sets `non_negotiation_acknowledged` from the supplied `non_negotiation_acknowledged` flag. It requires the agreement to be `SENT` and the reseller's `authorization_status` to be `PENDING_AGREEMENT`, and it requires the request to omit `counter_price_proposal_cents` entirely — supplying it at all is the antitrust rejection of §2.7. Countersignature changes **no** reseller field: the authorization axis stays on `PENDING_AGREEMENT` and advances only at activation, so only the agreement's `version` moves. A `decision` of `DECLINE` moves it `SENT → DECLINED`, recording `closed_reason` from the supplied `decline_reason` and advancing the agreement's `version`. Unlike countersignature, declining *does* touch the reseller: its `authorization_status` is rolled back to `APPLIED`, its `supply_status` set to `HOLD` and its `hold_reason` set to `PENDING_AUTHORIZATION`, with its `version` advanced. Both branches require the same two preconditions; the asymmetry is entirely in the side effects.

Activation is a `BrandChannelManager` act moving the agreement `COUNTERSIGNED → ACTIVE`. It requires the agreement to be `COUNTERSIGNED`, requires the referenced policy to still be in status `PUBLISHED`, and — when that policy has `non_negotiation_ack_required` set to true — requires `non_negotiation_acknowledged` on the agreement to be true, else the activation is rejected with `ERR_NON_NEGOTIATION_ACK_MISSING`. When the governing policy does not demand the acknowledgement, an unacknowledged agreement activates without complaint. Activation stamps `activated_at` with the current clock and advances the agreement's `version`, and it moves the reseller on both axes at once: `authorization_status` to `AUTHORIZED`, `supply_status` to `OPEN`, `hold_reason` to `NONE`, with the reseller's `version` advanced. This is the moment goods may legitimately flow.

An active agreement leaves the live set in two ways, both driven by system events rather than by a direct client action. When the sweep observes that `term_ends_at` is at or before the current clock, the agreement moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` with `closed_reason` recorded as `TERM_EXPIRED` and its `version` advanced, and the reseller's supply axis is pushed to `HOLD` with `hold_reason` `AGREEMENT_EXPIRED` while its `authorization_status` is left exactly as it was — the clearest demonstration of axis independence in the system, since a reseller with a lapsed agreement remains `AUTHORIZED` on paper but receives no goods. When the reseller's `authorization_status` becomes `TERMINATED`, the agreement moves `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` with `closed_reason` recorded as `SUPPLY_TERMINATED` and its `version` advanced, and this transition in turn recalls stock: every serialized unit whose `downstream_reseller_id` is this agreement's reseller and whose custody state is `SHIPPED_TO_RESELLER` moves to `RECALLED`, each with its own `version` advanced. The recall is caused by the agreement's termination, not directly by the notice that started the chain. `DECLINED`, `TERMINATED` and `EXPIRED` are the three terminal states of an agreement.

Agreements are readable individually, and the collection may be filtered by `reseller_id`, by `policy_id` or by `status`.

### 3.5. Channel Monitoring: Evidence Capture and Advertised-Price Evaluation

A `MonitoringAnalyst` is the only actor who may capture evidence. Each captured listing names the monitored SKU by `product_id`, the `channel` on which it was seen (one of `MARKETPLACE`, `RETAILER_SITE`, `OWN_WEBSITE` or `PHYSICAL_RETAIL`), the storefront's `seller_display_name`, the evidence `listing_url`, the `listed_price_cents` as displayed (which must be at least 0), the `observed_at` instant, and an `evidence_hash` that must be exactly 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters — the SHA-256 digest of the captured screenshot. Optionally the capture records the `marketplace_name` of the venue, which defaults to null. Three optional money fields all default to 0 and must each be at least 0: `shipping_cents` for the displayed shipping charge, `auto_coupon_cents` for a coupon the page applies automatically, and `promo_code_cents` for a discount reachable only by typing a code. The capture may also name `matched_reseller_id`, the authorized reseller whose storefront this is; it defaults to null, and a null value is the signal that the storefront could not be tied to anyone the brand has authorized. The row is created with `evaluation_status` `CAPTURED`, `unauthorized_seller_confirmed` false, `evidence_captured_by_user_id` stamped from the capturing analyst's `X-User-ID`, and all five computed money fields — `allocated_component_price_cents`, `effective_advertised_price_cents`, `effective_map_floor_cents`, `deviation_cents` and `deviation_pct` — still null.

Two capture-time rules protect the evidence chain. `observed_at` must be at or before the current logical clock and at or after the clock minus 30 days; anything outside that trailing month is rejected with `ERR_OBSERVATION_STALE`, because a screenshot older than a month is not a usable basis for a notice. And when a `matched_reseller_id` is supplied, the observed `channel` must be one of the members of that reseller's currently governing `permitted_channels`; a marketplace listing from a reseller authorized only for its own website is rejected with `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_PERMITTED`.

Bundles get extra evidence. When `is_bundle` is true — it defaults to false — the capture must also supply `bundle_total_price_cents`, the advertised price of the whole bundle, which must be strictly greater than zero, and `bundle_components`, an array of at least two distinct objects each carrying a `sku` string and an `msrp_cents` integer strictly greater than zero. The component array must include the observed product's own `sku`. Both fields default to null and are only meaningful for bundle listings.

**Allocating a bundle price across its components** is the first computation the evaluation performs, and its rounding order is pinned. Sum the `msrp_cents` of every member of `bundle_components` to obtain the total component MSRP. Order the components by `msrp_cents` descending, breaking ties by `sku` ascending — never by any row identifier. Walk that ordering, and for every component except the last, set its share to the advertised bundle price multiplied by that component's `msrp_cents`, divided by the total component MSRP, rounded half up to a whole number of cents; keep a running total of the shares assigned so far. The last component in the ordering receives the residual: the advertised bundle price minus the running total of every earlier share. Because the residual absorbs all the rounding drift, the component shares add up to `bundle_total_price_cents` exactly. The observation's `allocated_component_price_cents` is the share belonging to the component whose `sku` matches the observed product's `sku`. Note carefully that the component MSRPs used here are the ones captured in the evidence array, not the `msrp_cents` values on any sibling product row — the brand does not necessarily sell every item in a third party's bundle, and every evidenced member participates in the allocation regardless. Because the allocation reads only immutable fields of the observation itself, the server is free to compute `allocated_component_price_cents` eagerly at capture time as well; the value stored after evaluation is in either case exactly the figure this rule produces, and it remains null for any listing that is not a bundle.

**The effective advertised price** is what the policy definition recognizes, and it is computed from the observation together with the policy in force at `observed_at`. Start from a base: the allocated component share when the listing is a bundle, or the `listed_price_cents` otherwise. Add the `shipping_cents` to the base when the governing policy has `shipping_counts_as_advertised` set to true, and add nothing otherwise; call the result the gross price. Next, assemble the set of qualifying discounts: the `auto_coupon_cents` amount qualifies when the policy has `auto_coupon_counts_as_advertised` set to true, and the `promo_code_cents` amount qualifies when the policy has `promo_code_counts_as_advertised` set to true. Only the **single largest** qualifying amount is a candidate for attribution; qualifying discounts are never added together, so a listing with a qualifying auto-coupon of 500 cents and a qualifying promo code of 800 cents offers a candidate of 800 cents, not 1300. Separately, compute the attribution cap as the gross price multiplied by the policy's `max_discount_attribution_pct`, truncated downward to a whole number of cents. The `effective_advertised_price_cents` is the gross price minus the smaller of the discount candidate and the attribution cap. When no discount qualifies, the candidate is zero and the effective advertised price equals the gross price. Everything in this computation is integer cents; the downward truncation on the attribution cap is the only rounding step.

Evaluation is triggered by the `MonitoringAnalyst` and moves the observation out of `CAPTURED`. It requires `evaluation_status` to be `CAPTURED` — a second attempt on an already-evaluated row is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_EVALUATED` — and it requires a governing policy to exist for `observed_at` in the sense of §3.1, with the selected policy being one whose status is `PUBLISHED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `WITHDRAWN` and whose `effective_from` is at or before `observed_at`; otherwise `ERR_NO_GOVERNING_POLICY`. The evaluation resolves the floor as of `observed_at` (see §3.6), computes the effective advertised price as above, and then forks.

When the effective advertised price is greater than or equal to the resolved floor, the observation moves `CAPTURED → EVALUATED_COMPLIANT`. It stores `allocated_component_price_cents` (bundles only), `effective_advertised_price_cents` and `effective_map_floor_cents`, sets both `deviation_cents` and `deviation_pct` to 0, advances its own `version`, and creates no violation.

When the effective advertised price is strictly less than the resolved floor, the observation moves `CAPTURED → EVALUATED_BREACH`. It stores the same computed fields plus a genuine `deviation_cents` and `deviation_pct`, and advances its own `version`. From here the side effects diverge on whether the storefront was matched. If `matched_reseller_id` is not null, a violation row is opened in status `DETECTED` carrying `provisional_floor_cents` — the floor as resolved at `observed_at` — and `provisional_points`, together with `observation_id` pointing back at the evidence, `reseller_id` copied from the observation's `matched_reseller_id`, and `cited_policy_id` set to the policy the selection rule picked for `observed_at`. If `matched_reseller_id` is null, **no** violation row is created at all: there is nobody under contract to notice, `unauthorized_seller_confirmed` remains false, and the breach is instead routed into the test-purchase workflow of §3.8. Both branches land on the same target state; only the matched branch produces a violation.

The evaluation response reports `observation_id`, the resulting `evaluation_status`, `allocated_component_price_cents`, `effective_advertised_price_cents`, `effective_map_floor_cents`, the `floor_source` that produced the floor, `deviation_cents`, `deviation_pct`, `cited_policy_id`, the `violation_id` of the violation opened (null when none was), `provisional_points`, and `unauthorized_seller_confirmed`.

`deviation_cents` itself is defined as the resolved floor minus the effective advertised price, floored at 0 so it is never negative, and `deviation_pct` is `deviation_cents` divided by the resolved floor, rounded half up to four decimal places.

**Violation points** convert a deviation into a number the ladder can act on, and the same weighted formula is evaluated twice at two different instants. Begin with the floor as of the chosen instant and the deviation in cents; if the deviation is zero, the points are zero and no further factors are applied. Otherwise take the deviation fraction to four decimal places and read the base points off four inclusive tiers: 1 point when the fraction is at most 0.0500, 2 points when it is at most 0.1500, 4 points when it is at most 0.3000, and 6 points when it exceeds 0.3000. Multiply the base points by a channel weight — a `MARKETPLACE` listing weighs one and a half times, a `RETAILER_SITE` or `OWN_WEBSITE` listing weighs exactly once, and a `PHYSICAL_RETAIL` listing weighs only half, because a printed circular reaches fewer shoppers than a marketplace page. Then multiply by a repeat weight that starts at 1.00 and adds a quarter for each prior noticed violation of the **same** reseller, counting at most four of them, so the repeat weight tops out at 2.00. A prior violation counts toward that tally only if it is a different row, belongs to the same reseller, sits in status `NOTICED`, `DISPUTED`, `CURED`, `ESCALATED` or `CLOSED_MOOT`, has a non-null `assessed_points`, has a non-null `cure_deadline_at`, and was created no earlier than 180 days before the instant being evaluated. Violations still in `DETECTED` and violations that were `VOIDED` never count as repeats, because the former were never noticed and the latter had their points reversed. Finally, multiply base points by channel weight by repeat weight and round the product half up to a whole number of points — **once, at the end**. Never round after an individual factor.

The two evaluations of that formula differ only in the instant they use. The **provisional** figure is computed during evaluation with the instant set to `observed_at`, so the floor is the one that was in force when the listing was seen, and the repeat tally is counted as of `observed_at`; it is stored as `provisional_points` alongside `provisional_floor_cents`. The **final** figure is recomputed at notice issuance with the instant set to the logical clock at issuance, so a retroactive exception approved between detection and notice can move the floor, move the deviation tier and therefore move the points, and the repeat tally is recounted and may have grown; it is stored as `assessed_points` alongside `assessed_floor_cents`. The two figures are allowed to differ, and when they do, the final figure is the one that governs.

Observations are readable one at a time, and the collection may be filtered by `product_id`, by `matched_reseller_id`, by `evaluation_status` or by `channel`.

### 3.6. Exception Handling for Clearance, Bundles and Promotional Events

Not every price below the standard floor is a breach. A `BrandChannelManager` may request an exception naming the `product_id` it covers and an `exception_type` of `CLEARANCE`, `BUNDLE` or `PROMOTIONAL_EVENT`, together with a window: `starts_at` is inclusive and `ends_at` is exclusive, `ends_at` must be strictly after `starts_at`, and the window may not span more than 180 days. The optional `channel_scope` defaults to `ALL` and may otherwise be any single channel value — `MARKETPLACE`, `RETAILER_SITE`, `OWN_WEBSITE` or `PHYSICAL_RETAIL` — with `ALL` understood to intersect every channel. The request may supply `requested_override_floor_cents`, which is **required** for `BUNDLE` and `PROMOTIONAL_EVENT` exceptions and **ignored** for `CLEARANCE` exceptions, and it may declare a `dealer_input_source` which defaults to `NONE` and whose `RESELLER_PRICING_REQUEST` value is the antitrust rejection of §2.7. A new exception is created in status `REQUESTED`, with `requested_by_user_id` stamped from the requesting manager's `X-User-ID`, with `override_floor_cents`, `approved_by_user_id` and `decision_reason` all null, and with `voided_violation_count` at 0.

The `retroactive` flag is computed, not supplied: it is true exactly when `starts_at` is strictly before the logical clock at creation time. A retroactive exception is allowed — reaching back is precisely how a promotional event that legal cleared late gets applied to listings already captured — but it may not reach back arbitrarily far: a retroactive exception whose `starts_at` is earlier than the clock minus 30 days is rejected with `ERR_RETROACTIVE_WINDOW_TOO_OLD`.

Windows on the same product may not collide. A request is rejected with `ERR_EXCEPTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` when another exception on the same product, in status `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`, has a window that intersects the requested one under half-open interval semantics and has a channel scope that intersects the requested scope. Half-open means a window whose `starts_at` falls exactly on another's `ends_at` is adjacent, not overlapping — back-to-back promotional weeks are legal. Channel scopes intersect when they are equal or when either is `ALL`.

`BrandLegalCounsel` decides a requested exception, supplying a `decision` and a `decision_reason`, and the exception must be in `REQUESTED` for either branch to apply. A `decision` of `REJECT` moves it `REQUESTED → REJECTED`, recording `decision_reason` and stamping `approved_by_user_id` with the deciding counsel's `X-User-ID`, and advances the exception's `version`. A `decision` of `APPROVE` moves it `REQUESTED → APPROVED`, and carries considerably more weight.

Approval first requires the referenced product not to be in `lifecycle_status` `DISCONTINUED`, and additionally — when the `exception_type` is `CLEARANCE` — requires the product to be in `lifecycle_status` `CLEARANCE`, else `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_IN_CLEARANCE`. It then computes and snapshots `override_floor_cents`, and how it does so depends on the type. For a `CLEARANCE` exception the requested figure is ignored entirely: the installed floor is the product's `standard_map_floor_cents` multiplied by one minus the product's `clearance_discount_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. For a `BUNDLE` or `PROMOTIONAL_EVENT` exception the installed floor is exactly the `requested_override_floor_cents` supplied on the request, but it must sit inside a permitted band: it must be strictly less than the product's `standard_map_floor_cents` — an "exception" that raises the floor is not an exception — and it must be at least the product's `standard_map_floor_cents` multiplied by 0.50 and truncated downward to whole cents, so no exception may cut the floor by more than half. A figure outside that band is rejected with `ERR_OVERRIDE_FLOOR_OUT_OF_RANGE`. The floor is read off the product row as it stands at approval time and does not move afterwards if the product is later re-priced.

Approval then does three more things. It stamps `approved_by_user_id` and records `decision_reason`. It increases the product's `open_exception_count` by one and advances the product's `version`. And it runs the **retroactive void cascade**: every violation covered by this exception and lifted above the new floor is voided. A violation is covered when its status is `DETECTED`, `NOTICED` or `DISPUTED`, when its observation's `channel` falls inside the exception's scope, when the observation's `observed_at` lies inside the half-open window from `starts_at` up to but not including `ends_at`, and when the observation's `effective_advertised_price_cents` is greater than or equal to the exception's newly installed `override_floor_cents`. Each such violation moves to `VOIDED` with `resolution_reason` recorded as `RETROACTIVE_EXCEPTION` and its own `version` advanced; where its `assessed_points` was not null, that figure is subtracted from the reseller's `violation_points_total`, `assessed_points` is set back to null, and the reseller's `version` is advanced; and every enforcement notice for that violation in status `ISSUED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` is moved to `RESCINDED`. The number of violations the cascade voided is recorded on the exception as `voided_violation_count`, and the exception's own `version` is advanced. This cascade is the reason a retroactive exception is a compensating action in the sense of §2.11: the points it reverses come back out of the ledger exactly as they went in.

Two system events end an approved exception. When the sweep observes that `ends_at` is at or before the clock, the exception moves `APPROVED → EXPIRED`, the product's `open_exception_count` is reduced by one with the product's `version` advanced, and the exception's `version` is advanced. When a product is discontinued, exceptions in `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` move to `VOIDED` as described in §3.2, with the count decremented only where the prior status was `APPROVED`. `REJECTED`, `EXPIRED` and `VOIDED` are the three terminal states.

**Resolving the floor that applies** to a product, a channel, a bundle flag and an instant is the rule that ties this subsection back to monitoring. Consider every exception on that product whose status is `APPROVED`, whose window contains the instant under half-open semantics — `starts_at` at or before the instant and the instant strictly before `ends_at` — whose `channel_scope` is either `ALL` or exactly the channel in question, and which is either not a `BUNDLE` exception or else is being applied to a listing where `is_bundle` is true, since bundle exceptions only cover bundle listings. If nothing qualifies, the floor is the product's `standard_map_floor_cents` and the floor source is `STANDARD`. If something qualifies, order the candidates so that an exception whose `channel_scope` exactly equals the channel comes before one scoped to `ALL`; among equals, prefer the later `starts_at`; among those still tied, prefer the earlier `created_at` — never the identifier. The first candidate in that ordering supplies the floor from its `override_floor_cents`, the floor source is `EXCEPTION`, and its identifier is what the computed floor read reports as `applied_exception_id`.

Exceptions are readable individually, and the collection may be filtered by `product_id`, by `exception_type` or by `status`.

### 3.7. The Enforcement Ladder: Warning, Suspension, Termination

A detected violation is a fact; a notice is an act. Issuing one is open to a `BrandChannelManager` or a `BrandLegalCounsel`, and the payload names the target reseller by `reseller_id`, the grounding violation by `violation_id`, the `delivery_channel` used to deliver the unilateral communication (`EMAIL`, `CERTIFIED_MAIL` or `PORTAL`), and the `expected_reseller_version` demanded by §2.8. It may declare `contains_pricing_discussion`, whose only permitted value is false, and it may assert the `rung` the issuer believes applies. When a suspension is being installed, it must also supply `suspension_starts_at` and `suspension_ends_at`.

Four preconditions gate issuance. The violation must be in status `DETECTED`, else `ERR_VIOLATION_NOT_NOTICEABLE`. Its `cited_policy_id` must resolve to a policy still in status `PUBLISHED`, else `ERR_POLICY_NOT_PUBLISHED` — this is the practical bite of withdrawal from §3.1. The reseller's `authorization_status` must not be `TERMINATED`, else `ERR_RESELLER_TERMINATED`. And the recomputed final points must be strictly greater than zero, else `ERR_NO_BREACH_AT_FINAL_FLOOR` — a violation that a retroactive exception has already lifted back above the floor is not noticeable even if it is still sitting in `DETECTED`. Separately, the payload's `reseller_id` must equal the violation's own `reseller_id`, else `ERR_VIOLATION_RESELLER_MISMATCH`.

**Selecting the rung** is the heart of the ladder. Read the reseller's `violation_points_total` *before* this notice's points are added, then add the final points once to obtain the cumulative figure recorded on the notice as `cumulative_points_at_issue`. Compare that figure against the cited policy's two thresholds, with both boundaries inclusive at the lower bound: the points-justified rung is `WARNING` when the cumulative figure is strictly below `suspension_points_threshold`, `SUSPENSION` when it is at or above `suspension_points_threshold` but strictly below `termination_points_threshold`, and `TERMINATION` when it is at or above `termination_points_threshold`. Independently, find the highest rung the reseller has already been served: rank the rungs so that `WARNING` is first, `SUSPENSION` second and `TERMINATION` third, and take the highest rank appearing among that reseller's notices whose `status` is `ISSUED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `SATISFIED`; notices in `RESCINDED` are ignored entirely, and if no qualifying notice exists the highest served rank is treated as zero. The ceiling for this notice is one rank above that highest served rank, capped at the third rank. The rung actually issued is whichever of the points-justified rung and that ceiling ranks lower. **The ladder therefore advances at most one rung per notice**: a first-offence listing so far below the floor that the points justify termination still only earns a warning, because a reseller who has never been warned cannot be terminated. If the request asserted a `rung` and the asserted value differs from the computed one, the request is rejected with `ERR_LADDER_RUNG_MISMATCH`; when the request omits `rung`, the computed value is simply installed.

Issuance is a multi-row write. The violation moves `DETECTED → NOTICED`: its `assessed_floor_cents` and `assessed_points` are set from the final recomputation, its `cure_deadline_at` is set to the current logical clock plus the cited policy's `cure_window_days`, and its `version` is advanced. The reseller's `violation_points_total` is increased by the assessed points and the reseller's `version` is advanced. And the notice itself is created in status `ISSUED`, carrying the computed `rung`, the snapshotted `cumulative_points_at_issue`, an `assessed_points` mirroring the figure charged onto the violation, and `issued_by_user_id` stamped from the issuer's `X-User-ID`, with `acknowledged_at` and `closed_reason` null.

The rung then decides how far the consequences reach. A `WARNING` notice touches nothing beyond the violation and the ledger. A `SUSPENSION` notice additionally drives the reseller's authorization axis to `SUSPENDED`, its supply axis to `HOLD` and its `hold_reason` to `SUSPENSION`, and copies the notice's `suspension_ends_at` onto the reseller's own `suspension_ends_at`; this is legal from either `AUTHORIZED` or `SUSPENDED`, but never from `TERMINATED`. A `TERMINATION` notice — legal from `AUTHORIZED` or `SUSPENDED` — drives the reseller to `TERMINATED` on the authorization axis, `CUT_OFF` on the supply axis and `TERMINATION` as the `hold_reason`, stamps `terminated_at` with the current clock and clears `suspension_ends_at` to null. Termination then cascades twice over. Every violation of that reseller whose status is `DETECTED`, `NOTICED` or `DISPUTED` moves to `CLOSED_MOOT` with `resolution_reason` recorded as `RESELLER_TERMINATED` and its own `version` advanced — and critically, the `assessed_points` on those violations are **retained** in the ledger, because a terminated reseller's history is not forgiven. And that reseller's `ACTIVE` agreement moves to `TERMINATED` as described in §3.4, which in turn recalls its `SHIPPED_TO_RESELLER` serialized units. A single termination notice therefore advances version counters on the notice, the violation it cites, the reseller, every other open violation of that reseller, the agreement, and every recalled unit.

Because termination is irreversible, it carries a **four-eyes requirement** that no other rung does. A notice whose computed `rung` is `TERMINATION` may only be issued by a `BrandLegalCounsel`; an attempt by a `BrandChannelManager` — who is otherwise a legitimate issuer of warnings and suspensions — is rejected with `ERR_TERMINATION_REQUIRES_COUNSEL`. On top of that, the `X-User-ID` issuing the termination must be different from the `X-User-ID` recorded as `issued_by_user_id` on the reseller's governing suspension notice, so that the same individual cannot both suspend and then terminate the same partner; a match is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Both checks are evaluated among the domain constraints described in §2.9, after the antitrust, referential and window checks have passed.

Suspension windows are policed on top of all that. The window's duration, measured from `suspension_starts_at` to `suspension_ends_at`, may not exceed 90 days. The window must not intersect any existing non-rescinded suspension window for the same reseller, treated as a closed interval so that touching endpoints do count as an intersection. And `suspension_starts_at` must be at or after the reseller's `last_suspension_ended_at` advanced by the cited policy's `suspension_cooling_off_days` — with the default fourteen-day setting, a reseller whose previous suspension ended on the first of a month cannot be suspended again before the fifteenth. Either window failure is rejected with `ERR_SUSPENSION_COOLING_OFF`.

A reseller may acknowledge a notice, moving it `ISSUED → ACKNOWLEDGED` with `acknowledged_at` stamped from the current clock and the notice's `version` advanced. Acknowledgement is bookkeeping; it changes nothing about the underlying violation or the ladder, but an `ACKNOWLEDGED` notice still counts as served when the rung ceiling is computed.

There are three ways out of a noticed violation. **Curing** it is the reseller's own remedy: the reseller submits a `cure_evidence_url`, the `corrected_price_cents` now displayed, and an `evidence_hash` of the corrected screenshot in the same 64-lowercase-hex-character form as the original evidence. Cure requires the violation to be `NOTICED` and requires the current clock to be at or before `cure_deadline_at`, else `ERR_CURE_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. It moves the violation `NOTICED → CURED` with `resolution_reason` set to `CURE_ACCEPTED` and its `version` advanced, and it moves the latest non-rescinded notice for that violation to `SATISFIED` with `closed_reason` recorded as `VIOLATION_CURED` and that notice's `version` advanced. What curing does **not** do is refund points: the `assessed_points` stay in the reseller's `violation_points_total`, because a cured violation still counts toward the ladder. Fixing the listing stops the escalation; it does not erase the offence.

**Disputing** it is the reseller's other remedy. Supplying a `dispute_reason` moves the violation `NOTICED → DISPUTED`, recording that reason and advancing the violation's `version`; it requires the violation to be `NOTICED` and requires `dispute_rounds` to be strictly below 2, else `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. Note that the dispute itself does not increment `dispute_rounds` — that counter tracks disputes that have already been *rejected*. A `BrandChannelManager` then decides the dispute with a `decision` and a `decision_reason`, on a violation that must be in `DISPUTED`. A `decision` of `REJECT` moves it `DISPUTED → NOTICED`: `dispute_rounds` is increased by one, `cure_deadline_at` is pushed out by a further five days to give the reseller a fresh chance to comply, `resolution_reason` is cleared back to null, and the violation's `version` is advanced. Two rejected rounds exhaust the reseller's right to dispute. A `decision` of `SUSTAIN` moves it `DISPUTED → VOIDED`: `resolution_reason` is set to `DISPUTE_SUSTAINED`; the `assessed_points` are subtracted from the reseller's `violation_points_total` and then `assessed_points`, `assessed_floor_cents` and `cure_deadline_at` are all cleared to null with the reseller's `version` advanced; the latest non-rescinded notice for that violation moves to `RESCINDED`, which — when that notice's `rung` was `SUSPENSION` — reinstates the reseller as described below; and the violation's `version` is advanced. The `NOTICED → DISPUTED → NOTICED` cycle is therefore genuinely a loop, capped at two rejected rounds.

**Escalating** it is what happens when the reseller does nothing. When the sweep observes a violation in `NOTICED` whose `cure_deadline_at` is at or before the clock, the violation moves `NOTICED → ESCALATED` with `resolution_reason` recorded as `CURE_WINDOW_LAPSED` and its `version` advanced; the `assessed_points` are retained in the ledger. `CURED`, `ESCALATED`, `VOIDED` and `CLOSED_MOOT` are the four terminal states of a violation.

Rescission is counsel's tool for unwinding a notice the brand should not have issued. `BrandLegalCounsel` supplies a `rescinded_reason`, and the notice must be in `ISSUED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`. A notice whose `rung` is `TERMINATION` may not be rescinded at all — the attempt is rejected with `ERR_TERMINATION_NOT_RESCINDABLE`, because cutting off supply is a decision the brand must live with. Otherwise the notice moves to `RESCINDED` with `closed_reason` recorded from the supplied reason and its `version` advanced, and the reversal reaches back into the violation: the cited violation is returned to status `DETECTED` with `assessed_points`, `assessed_floor_cents` and `cure_deadline_at` all cleared to null, and the notice's `assessed_points` are subtracted from the reseller's `violation_points_total`. Returning the violation to `DETECTED` rather than voiding it is deliberate — the evidence is still good, so the brand may notice it again at the correct rung. When the rescinded notice's `rung` was `SUSPENSION` and the reseller is currently `SUSPENDED`, the reseller is reinstated in the same operation: `authorization_status` back to `AUTHORIZED`, `supply_status` to `OPEN`, `hold_reason` to `NONE`, `last_suspension_ended_at` stamped with the current clock, `suspension_ends_at` cleared to null, and the reseller's `version` advanced. `SATISFIED` and `RESCINDED` are the two terminal states of a notice.

The other route out of a suspension is simply serving it. When the sweep observes a reseller whose `authorization_status` is `SUSPENDED`, whose `hold_reason` is `SUSPENSION` and whose `suspension_ends_at` is at or before the clock, the reseller returns to `AUTHORIZED` with `supply_status` `OPEN` and `hold_reason` `NONE`; `last_suspension_ended_at` is set to the `suspension_ends_at` that just elapsed (not to the sweep instant), `suspension_ends_at` is cleared to null, and the reseller's `version` is advanced. The governing suspension notice moves to `SATISFIED` with `closed_reason` recorded as `SUSPENSION_SERVED` and its own `version` advanced. Unlike a rescission, serving a suspension does **not** refund points — the reseller has been punished, and the ledger remembers.

Violations are readable individually and as a collection filtered by `observation_id`, `reseller_id`, `cited_policy_id` or `status`. Notices are readable individually and as a collection filtered by `reseller_id`, `violation_id`, `rung` or `status`.

A **compliance summary** computed read reconciles a reseller's ledger against its contributors, optionally as of a supplied `as_of` instant which defaults to the current logical clock. It reports `reseller_id`, the stored `violation_points_total`, the `contributing_violation_count` of violations that qualify under the ledger definition of §2.11, the `summed_assessed_points` obtained by adding up those violations' `assessed_points` — which must equal the stored total, since that is the whole point of the read — the `highest_issued_rung` served so far (reported as the empty-history case when no non-rescinded notice exists), the `next_rung` the ladder would install on the next notice, and the two gaps `points_to_suspension` and `points_to_termination`, each being the reseller's own governing thresholds minus the current total, floored at zero when the threshold has already been passed.

### 3.8. Unauthorized Sellers: Serialized Tracing and Test Purchases

When a breach is detected on a storefront that could not be matched to any authorized reseller, enforcement has nowhere to send a notice. The remedy is physical: buy the goods, read the serial off the carton, and find out who shipped them.

Serialization begins with the brand. A `BrandChannelManager` registers each produced unit with a `serial_number` of 1 to 40 characters that must be unique across every unit the system holds — a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERIAL` — the `product_id` it belongs to, a `lot_code` of 1 to 40 characters identifying the shipment lot, and the `shipped_distributor_id` naming the distributor it went to. The product must have `serialization_enabled` set to true, else `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_SERIALIZED`. The unit is created in custody state `SHIPPED_TO_DISTRIBUTOR` with `downstream_reseller_id`, `shipped_downstream_at` and `traced_at` all null, and the named distributor's `shipped_units_total` — which counts every unit whose `shipped_distributor_id` is that distributor, in any custody state — is increased accordingly.

The distributor itself records the next hop. Supplying a `downstream_reseller_id` moves a unit `SHIPPED_TO_DISTRIBUTOR → SHIPPED_TO_RESELLER`, setting `downstream_reseller_id` and stamping `shipped_downstream_at` with the current clock, and advancing the unit's `version`. Three preconditions apply: the unit must be in `SHIPPED_TO_DISTRIBUTOR`, the acting distributor's `X-User-ID` must equal the unit's `shipped_distributor_id`, and the target reseller's `supply_status` must be `OPEN` — a distributor may not ship to a reseller the brand has put on hold or cut off, which is precisely the mechanism a supply hold is meant to be.

A `MonitoringAnalyst` orders a test purchase against a suspect listing by naming the `observation_id`, an `ordered_at` instant that must be at or before the current logical clock, and the `purchase_price_cents` actually paid, which must be strictly greater than zero. The purchase is created in status `ORDERED` with `purchaser_user_id` stamped from the analyst's `X-User-ID` and with `captured_serial_number`, `traced_serial_unit_id` and `outcome_reason` all null.

When the unit arrives, the analyst records the serial found on it. Supplying a `serial_number` moves the purchase `ORDERED → SERIAL_CAPTURED`, storing that value as `captured_serial_number` and resolving it to a `traced_serial_unit_id`, and advancing the purchase's `version`. The purchase must be in `ORDERED`. A serial that matches no registered unit is rejected with `ERR_SERIAL_NOT_REGISTERED`; a serial that resolves to a unit whose custody state is neither `SHIPPED_TO_DISTRIBUTOR` nor `SHIPPED_TO_RESELLER` is rejected with `ERR_SERIAL_NOT_TRACEABLE`, since a unit already traced or recalled cannot ground a fresh trace.

The analyst then confirms the finding, supplying an `outcome` and an `outcome_reason`, on a purchase that must be in `SERIAL_CAPTURED`. An `outcome` of `UNAUTHORIZED` moves the purchase `SERIAL_CAPTURED → CONFIRMED_UNAUTHORIZED`; it requires `traced_serial_unit_id` to be non-null, records `outcome_reason`, and advances the purchase's `version`. It also reaches into two other rows: the linked observation has `unauthorized_seller_confirmed` set to true with its `version` advanced, and the traced unit moves from `SHIPPED_TO_DISTRIBUTOR` or `SHIPPED_TO_RESELLER` to `TRACED_UNAUTHORIZED` with `traced_at` stamped from the current clock and its own `version` advanced. Where that unit has a non-null `downstream_reseller_id`, the affected reseller's `review_required` flag is recomputed per §2.11 and its `version` advanced only if the value actually changed. An `outcome` of `AUTHORIZED` moves the purchase `SERIAL_CAPTURED → CLEARED`, recording `outcome_reason` and advancing only the purchase's own `version`; the traced unit and the observation are left entirely untouched. `CONFIRMED_UNAUTHORIZED` and `CLEARED` are the two terminal states of a test purchase.

A serialized unit reaches `RECALLED` only through the agreement-termination cascade of §3.4, and `RECALLED` is its single terminal state. Units are readable individually and as a collection filtered by `product_id`, `shipped_distributor_id`, `downstream_reseller_id`, `lot_code` or `status`. Test purchases are readable individually and as a collection filtered by `observation_id`, `traced_serial_unit_id` or `status`.

### 3.9. Distributor Accountability, Diversion Exposure and the Sweep

A confirmed unauthorized sale is evidence that a distributor let goods leak into a channel the brand did not authorize, and the agreement with that distributor provides a contractual chargeback remedy. A `BrandChannelManager` assesses one by naming the accountable `distributor_id`, the originating `test_purchase_id`, the number of `diverted_units` charged for this event (at least 1), and the `expected_distributor_version` demanded by §2.8. The referenced test purchase must be in status `CONFIRMED_UNAUTHORIZED`, else `ERR_TEST_PURCHASE_NOT_CONFIRMED`, and the `shipped_distributor_id` of the unit it traced must equal the chargeback's `distributor_id`, else `ERR_DIVERSION_NOT_ATTRIBUTED` — a distributor is only accountable for units its own shipping record names.

**Pricing the chargeback** turns on a dependency that looks circular and is deliberately pinned. Take the distributor's `diversion_score` as it stands *before* this event. The `tier_rate_cents_per_unit` is 2500 cents when that pre-event score is strictly below 10.0, 4000 cents when it is at or above 10.0 but strictly below 25.0, and 6000 cents when it is at or above 25.0 — twenty-five, forty and sixty dollars a unit, with both tier boundaries inclusive at the lower bound. The `amount_cents` is that per-unit rate multiplied by `diverted_units`. The `score_delta_applied` is two and a half score points per diverted unit. Only after both figures are fixed is the distributor's state moved, and in this order: `amount_cents` is added to `chargeback_balance_cents`, then `score_delta_applied` is added to the pre-event score to give the new `diversion_score`, then `standing` is re-derived from that new score — `GOOD` when it is strictly below 10.0, `PROBATION` when it is at or above 10.0 but strictly below 25.0, and `SUSPENDED` when it is at or above 25.0. Evaluating the tier *after* the increment would price the very first chargeback one tier too high, which is why the order is fixed. The distributor's `version` is advanced once for the whole assessment, and where the re-derivation moved the standing into `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED`, every reseller whose `sourcing_distributor_id` names this distributor has its `review_required` flag recomputed and its `version` advanced where the value changed. The chargeback row itself is created in status `ASSESSED` with `dispute_rounds` at 0 and `dispute_reason` and `decision_reason` null.

The fiscal ceiling is checked last of all, after every other rule has passed: the distributor's current `chargeback_balance_cents` plus the new `amount_cents` must not exceed its `annual_chargeback_cap_cents`, with equality explicitly allowed. Exceeding it is rejected with `ERR_CHARGEBACK_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

Distributors may push back. Supplying a `dispute_reason` moves a chargeback `ASSESSED → DISPUTED` or `UPHELD → DISPUTED`, recording that reason and advancing the chargeback's `version`; it requires the chargeback to be in `ASSESSED` or `UPHELD`, requires `dispute_rounds` to be strictly below 2 (else `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`), and requires the acting distributor's `X-User-ID` to equal the chargeback's `distributor_id`. `BrandLegalCounsel` then decides, supplying a `decision` and a `decision_reason`, on a chargeback that must be in `DISPUTED`. A `decision` of `UPHOLD` moves it `DISPUTED → UPHELD`: `dispute_rounds` is increased by one, `decision_reason` is recorded, the chargeback's `version` is advanced, and both ledgers are left exactly as they are — the `amount_cents` stays in the distributor's balance and the `score_delta_applied` stays in its diversion score. An upheld chargeback may be disputed again until `dispute_rounds` reaches 2, which makes `UPHELD → DISPUTED → UPHELD` a genuine cycle. A `decision` of `REVERSE` moves it `DISPUTED → REVERSED`, its single terminal state: `decision_reason` is recorded, the chargeback's `version` is advanced, `amount_cents` is subtracted from the distributor's `chargeback_balance_cents`, `score_delta_applied` is subtracted from its `diversion_score`, `standing` is re-derived from the restored score with the distributor's `version` advanced, and — because a standing that falls back from `PROBATION` to `GOOD` changes who is under review — the `review_required` flag is recomputed for every reseller whose `sourcing_distributor_id` names this distributor, with each changed reseller's `version` advanced. The reversal is exact: assess-then-reverse leaves both conserved quantities at their pre-assessment values.

Chargebacks are readable individually and as a collection filtered by `distributor_id`, `test_purchase_id` or `status`.

A **diversion exposure** computed read lets either side see what is at stake before anyone bills anything, under a required `variant` selecting one of two attribution theories. Both variants first pick a per-unit rate from the distributor's *current* `diversion_score`, using the same three tiers as the assessment: 2500 cents below 10.0, 4000 cents from 10.0 up to but not including 25.0, and 6000 cents at 25.0 and above. Under the `TRACE_ATTRIBUTED` variant — the default theory the chargeback workflow bills from — every traced unit is charged in full to the distributor named on its shipping record, so the counted units are simply the number of serialized units whose `shipped_distributor_id` is this distributor and whose custody state is `TRACED_UNAUTHORIZED`, and the exposure is the per-unit rate multiplied by that count. Under the `LOT_PRORATED` variant — the distributor-facing settlement view for lots that were split across several distributors — traced units are shared out by shipped-unit proportion within each lot. Walk the distinct `lot_code` values appearing on units whose `shipped_distributor_id` is this distributor, in ascending `lot_code` order. For each lot, count the units of that lot in state `TRACED_UNAUTHORIZED` across *all* distributors; if that count is zero, skip the lot without performing any division. Otherwise count all units of that lot in any custody state, and count the units of that lot whose `shipped_distributor_id` is this distributor in any custody state, and add to the running tally the lot's traced count multiplied by this distributor's shipped count, divided by the lot's total shipped count, rounded half up to two decimal places. A distributor that received six of a lot's ten units therefore carries six tenths of that lot's traced units. Once every lot is folded in, the exposure is the per-unit rate multiplied by the accumulated tally, rounded half up once to whole cents at the very end. The response reports `distributor_id`, the echoed `variant`, the `traced_unit_count` of this distributor's own units in `TRACED_UNAUTHORIZED`, the `units_counted` the chosen variant arrived at (a whole number under `TRACE_ATTRIBUTED`, a two-decimal figure under `LOT_PRORATED`), the resulting `exposure_cents`, the `tier_rate_cents_per_unit` used, and the `diversion_score` the tier was read from. An empty candidate set yields a counted-unit figure of 0 and an exposure of 0, with no division attempted.

Finally, the **enforcement sweep** is how every time-driven consequence in this system is realized. A `BrandChannelManager` or a `PlatformAdmin` runs it, and it processes four phases in the order given below, taking the affected rows within each phase in ascending `created_at` order so that the outcome is fully deterministic. First, agreements in `ACTIVE` whose `term_ends_at` is at or before the clock become `EXPIRED` and push their reseller's supply axis to `HOLD` with `hold_reason` `AGREEMENT_EXPIRED`, leaving the authorization axis untouched. Second, exceptions in `APPROVED` whose `ends_at` is at or before the clock become `EXPIRED` and decrement their product's `open_exception_count`. Third, violations in `NOTICED` whose `cure_deadline_at` is at or before the clock become `ESCALATED`. Fourth, resellers in `SUSPENDED` whose `suspension_ends_at` is at or before the clock return to `AUTHORIZED` with `supply_status` `OPEN`, `hold_reason` `NONE` and `last_suspension_ended_at` set to the elapsed `suspension_ends_at`, and their governing suspension notice becomes `SATISFIED`. The sweep responds with the `swept_at` instant it ran at and four counters: `expired_agreements`, `expired_exceptions`, `escalated_violations` and `reinstated_resellers`. Running the sweep when nothing is due is legal and returns four zeroes.

Request and response payloads are not restated here: the fields each operation accepts, the fields it computes, and the rows it cascades into are specified in the §3 narratives.
